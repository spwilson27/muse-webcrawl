Kremlin

A kremlin (Russian: кремль, kremlʹ) is a fortified central complex found in historic Russian cities, typically the oldest part of the city and containing cathedrals and palaces within great walls and towers. The word is often used to refer specifically to the Moscow Kremlin — the seat of the President of Russia — and has by metonymy become a synonym for the government of Russia and, before it, of the Soviet Union, a usage popularised in the Cold War-era term "Kremlinology" for the study of Soviet politics.

The word

Kremlin derives from the Russian kremlʹ, "fortress" or "citadel", possibly related to kremenʹ, "flint". Such citadels — called detinets in the earliest Rus' sources — were built of wood in the Kievan period and progressively rebuilt in stone. Dozens of Russian cities retain kremlins, though many have lost their walls.

The Moscow Kremlin

The Moscow Kremlin stands on Borovitsky Hill above the Moskva River, adjoining Red Square and St Basil's Cathedral. Rebuilt in red brick under Ivan III in the late 15th century with the help of Italian architects, its triangular wall circuit (2,235 m, 20 towers, including the Spasskaya Tower with its famous clock) encloses Cathedral Square — with the cathedrals of the Dormition, the Annunciation and the Archangel — the Ivan the Great Bell Tower, the Tsar Cannon and Tsar Bell, and the palaces of the tsars, including the Terem Palace and the Great Kremlin Palace (1838–49). It served as the seat of the Soviet government from 1918 and is now the official residence of the President of Russia; in 1990 it was inscribed with Red Square on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Other kremlins

Major surviving kremlins include those of Novgorod (the Detinets, with St Sophia Cathedral, 1045–50), Kazan (a World Heritage Site combining Russian and Tatar elements), Suzdal, Rostov (the "Rostov Kremlin" of Metropolitan Jonah), Pskov, Nizhny Novgorod, Tula, Kolomna and Astrakhan. Each was a combined military, administrative and religious centre, often with the city's principal cathedral inside the walls.

Metonymic usage

In English and other languages "the Kremlin" commonly denotes the Russian (formerly Soviet) government and presidency, as in "the Kremlin announced…". The derivative Kremlinology named the Cold War practice of inferring Soviet politics from indirect signs — the order of leaders atop Lenin's Mausoleum, changes in official photographs and editorials.
