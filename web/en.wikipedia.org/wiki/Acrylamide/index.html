IUPAC nameprop-2-enamide

AppearanceWhite crystalline solid

Acrylamide (or acrylic amide) is an organic compound with the chemical formula CH2=CHC(O)NH2. It is a white, odourless solid, soluble in water and several organic solvents. It is produced industrially from the hydration of acrylonitrile and is used mainly as a precursor to polyacrylamides, which find many uses as water-soluble thickeners and flocculation agents.

[Image: Acrylamide crystals]

Uses

The majority of acrylamide is used to manufacture various polymers, principally polyacrylamide, which is used in water treatment, paper production, mining, and as a soil conditioner. Polyacrylamide gels are also widely used in molecular biology laboratories for electrophoresis of proteins and nucleic acids.

Toxicity

Acrylamide is a neurotoxin at high doses and is classified as a probable human carcinogen (Group 2A) by the International Agency for Research on Cancer. In 2002, Swedish researchers reported that acrylamide forms in starchy foods cooked at high temperatures, such as potato crisps and bread, through the Maillard reaction between asparagine and reducing sugars — a finding that attracted widespread public attention and prompted food-safety monitoring and mitigation efforts.

Regulation

Food safety authorities in Europe and elsewhere have set benchmark levels for acrylamide in foods and encourage manufacturers to reduce its formation, for example by lowering cooking temperatures and modifying recipes.
