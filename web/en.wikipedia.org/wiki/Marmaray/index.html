Marmaray is a 76.6 km commuter rail line in Istanbul, Turkey, linking Halkalı on the European side with Gebze on the Asian side. Its centrepiece is a 13.5 km Bosphorus crossing including a 1.4 km immersed tube tunnel beneath the strait — the deepest of its kind in the world.

History

A tunnel under the Bosphorus was first proposed in the Ottoman era. Construction began in 2004 but was delayed for years by major archaeological discoveries, including the Byzantine harbour of Theodosius at Yenikapı and thousands of artefacts. The first phase opened on 29 October 2013, the 90th anniversary of the Republic of Turkey.

Operations

The full line opened in March 2019 with 43 stations, integrating suburban services across the city and joining the high-speed rail network at Gebze and Söğütlüçeşme. The tunnel also carries intercity and freight trains at night, forming a rail link between Europe and Asia.
