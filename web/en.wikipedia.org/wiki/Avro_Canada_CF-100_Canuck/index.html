Avro Canada CF-100 Canuck

The Avro Canada CF-100 Canuck (affectionately known as the "Clunk") was a Canadian twin-engine, all-weather fighter-interceptor aircraft designed and produced by Avro Canada. It served with the Royal Canadian Air Force and Canadian Forces from 1953 to 1981, and was also operated by the Belgian Air Force.

Development

The Canuck was designed in the late 1940s to meet an RCAF requirement for a jet interceptor able to patrol Canada's vast northern airspace in all weathers. The prototype first flew on 19 January 1950. Production aircraft were powered by two Avro Canada Orenda turbojets. Five main marks were built, progressively improving the radar, armament of eight 0.50-inch machine guns and unguided rocket pods, and the wing design; 692 aircraft were produced in total.

CF-100 squadrons defended North America under NORAD and served in Europe with NATO's 1 Air Division. Belgium bought 53 Mk 5s, the type's only export. In later years Canadian Canucks were used for electronic warfare and target facilities work before retirement in 1981. The CF-100 has the distinction of being the only Canadian-designed fighter aircraft to enter mass production and operational service.
