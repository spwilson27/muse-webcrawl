Kenneth Douglas Taylor OC (5 October 1934 – 15 October 2015) was a Canadian diplomat and businessman, best known for his role as Canada's ambassador to Iran during the 1979–81 Iran hostage crisis, when he sheltered six American diplomats and helped engineer their escape from Tehran in the operation known as the "Canadian Caper."

Early life and career

Taylor was born in Calgary, Alberta, and educated at the University of Toronto and the University of California, Berkeley, where he earned an MBA. He joined the Canadian Trade Commissioner Service and served in Guatemala, Detroit, and Pakistan before postings in London, where he rose to deputy high commissioner. In 1977 he was appointed ambassador to Iran and High Commissioner to Pakistan; he was a fluent Farsi speaker and well regarded in Tehran.

The Canadian Caper

When Iranian militants stormed the United States embassy in Tehran on 4 November 1979, six American diplomats escaped and eventually found refuge with Canadian officials. Taylor hid two of them, Mark and Cora Lijek, in his own residence, while the others stayed with immigration officer John Sheardown. Taylor obtained Canadian passports for the six under a special Order in Council, gathered intelligence for Washington, and arranged the cover story of a Hollywood film crew scouting locations for a fictitious science-fiction film, Argo. On 27 January 1980 the six flew out of Tehran using the Canadian passports. The operation was revealed the following day by journalist Jean Pelletier, and Taylor was ordered out of Iran for his own safety.

Later life

Taylor received the Order of Canada and the United States Congressional Gold Medal, and became something of a celebrity in the United States. After leaving the foreign service he worked as a senior executive in the private sector in New York and served as consul general of Canada there, and as chancellor of Victoria University at the University of Toronto. The 2012 film Argo, which dramatised the escape with emphasis on the CIA's role, prompted Taylor to note publicly that Canada's contribution had been understated; director Ben Affleck later amended the film's postscript. Taylor died of colon cancer in New York in 2015.
