The Enguri (Georgian: ენგური) is a river in western Georgia, 213 kilometres (132 mi) long. It rises in the Greater Caucasus near the region of Svaneti and flows westwards into the Black Sea near the town of Anaklia. For part of its lower course it forms the de facto boundary between territory controlled by the Georgian government and the breakaway region of Abkhazia.

Enguri Dam

The river is best known for the Enguri Dam, a large arch dam completed in 1978 whose crest, at 271.5 metres, makes it one of the tallest concrete arch dams in the world. The associated hydroelectric station, with an installed capacity of about 1,300 MW, is the largest electricity-generating facility in Georgia and historically supplied a major share of the country's power. The dam lies upstream of the administrative boundary line, while most of the power station's generators are on the Abkhaz side, and the facility is jointly operated under informal arrangements between the two sides.

Geography

The upper Enguri drains the glaciers and high valleys of Svaneti, passing the town of Mestia. Downstream of the dam the river crosses the Colchian plain before reaching the Black Sea. The Enguri Bridge, closed to vehicles for periods after the 1992–93 war in Abkhazia, has been the principal pedestrian crossing point between Abkhazia and Georgia proper.
