Ethnoburb

An ethnoburb is a suburban residential and business area with a notable cluster of a particular ethnic minority population. The term was coined by geographer Wei Li in 1997 in her study of Chinese communities in the San Gabriel Valley of Los Angeles County, California. Although the group may constitute a local minority, ethnoburbs are typically multi-ethnic communities in which no single ethnic group forms a majority.

Ethnoburbs differ from traditional urban ethnic enclaves, such as the historic Chinatowns of the inner city, in several respects: they are located in the suburbs rather than the central city; their residents are often relatively affluent, well-educated professionals and entrepreneurs; and they are directly connected to the global economy through transnational business and family networks. Immigrants may settle in an ethnoburb directly upon arrival, bypassing the inner-city gateway neighbourhoods that characterised earlier waves of immigration.

Characteristics

Wei Li identified several defining features of ethnoburbs. They emerge in suburban municipalities rather than central cities; they are multiethnic, though one ethnic group is particularly visible; they contain substantial ethnic economies, including banks, restaurants, professional services, media and shopping centres oriented toward the ethnic community; and their formation is driven both by international geopolitical and economic change and by local factors such as housing availability, school quality and proximity to employment.

Examples

The San Gabriel Valley east of Los Angeles, including the cities of Monterey Park, Alhambra, San Gabriel, Rosemead, Arcadia, Rowland Heights and Hacienda Heights, is the paradigmatic example, sometimes described as the first suburban Chinatown. Other examples studied by geographers include Chinese communities in Silicon Valley and in Vancouver's suburbs such as Richmond, British Columbia; South Asian communities in suburban Toronto and in Edison, New Jersey; and Korean communities in the suburbs of northern New Jersey. Monterey Park, which in the 1980s became the first city in the continental United States with an Asian-majority population, figures prominently in the literature.

Related concepts

The ethnoburb is related to, but distinct from, the older concept of the ethnic enclave. Scholars have also applied the concept outside North America, to suburban ethnic concentrations in Australia and elsewhere. Critics note that the term may understate class diversity within such communities, which often include working-class service workers alongside affluent homeowners.
