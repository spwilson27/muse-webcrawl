Navier–Stokes equations

The Navier–Stokes equations are partial differential equations that describe the motion of viscous fluid substances such as liquids and gases. Named after Claude-Louis Navier and George Gabriel Stokes, they express the conservation of momentum and mass for Newtonian fluids.

ρ(∂u/∂t + u·∇u) = −∇p + μ∇²u + f

Form and assumptions

The equations balance the acceleration of a fluid element against pressure gradients, viscous stresses, and external body forces such as gravity. Together with a continuity equation for mass conservation, they govern flows from ocean currents and weather systems to airflow over aircraft wings and blood flow in arteries.

Existence and smoothness

Whether smooth solutions always exist in three dimensions is one of the great open problems of mathematics. The Clay Mathematics Institute designated the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem as one of its seven Millennium Prize Problems, offering a prize of one million US dollars for a solution.

Solutions and computation

Exact solutions are known only for simple geometries such as laminar pipe flow. In practice, engineers use computational fluid dynamics (CFD) to solve the equations numerically, often with turbulence models for the Reynolds-averaged equations.
