Astronomical spectroscopy

Astronomical spectroscopy is the study of astronomy using spectroscopy — measuring the spectrum of electromagnetic radiation from stars and other celestial objects to determine their composition, temperature, density, mass, distance, luminosity and motion.

Principles

A spectrograph splits light into wavelengths. Absorption and emission lines (Fraunhofer lines in the Sun) reveal chemical elements; line shifts reveal radial velocity via the Doppler effect and redshift; line broadening reveals rotation, pressure and magnetic fields (Zeeman effect).

History

Fraunhofer mapped solar lines in the 1810s; Kirchhoff and Bunsen explained them chemically; Huggins applied spectroscopy to stars and nebulae; Annie Jump Cannon's stellar classification and Cecilia Payne's work on stellar composition followed. Spectroscopy enabled discovery of helium in the Sun before Earth.

Applications

Used across optical, infrared, ultraviolet and radio wavelengths for stellar classification, galaxy evolution, exoplanet atmospheres, interstellar medium studies and cosmology, including redshift surveys.
