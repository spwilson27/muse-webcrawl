Hereford cattle

The Hereford is a British breed of beef cattle originally from Herefordshire in the West Midlands of England. It has spread to many countries — there are more than five million pedigree Hereford cattle worldwide — and is one of the most numerous beef breeds in temperate regions including the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Argentina and Uruguay.

Characteristics

Herefords are characterised by their deep red body colour with a distinctive white face, crest, dewlap, underline, tail switch and often white markings on the legs. Both horned and naturally polled (Polled Hereford) types exist. The breed is known for docility, hardiness, early maturity, good mothering ability and the ability to convert grass to beef efficiently.

History

The breed was developed from local red cattle of Herefordshire in the 18th century; Benjamin Tomkins (1745–1815) is generally credited as its founder. The first herd book was published in 1846. Herefords were exported to the United States from the 1840s onwards and became central to the range-cattle industry of the American West. Crossed with other breeds — notably the Aberdeen Angus to produce the black baldy — the Hereford remains a mainstay of commercial beef production.

Use

Herefords are raised almost exclusively for beef. Their meat is well regarded for flavour and marbling, and "Hereford Beef" is marketed as a premium branded product in the United Kingdom and elsewhere.
