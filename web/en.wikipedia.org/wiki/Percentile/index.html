Percentile

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A bell curve with vertical lines marking the 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles]

A percentile is a value below which a given percentage of observations fall: a test score at the 90th percentile is higher than 90% of the scores. The 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles are the quartiles; the 50th percentile is the median.

Percentiles are the standard language of growth charts (a child's height percentile), standardised testing and performance benchmarking, because they translate raw values into position within a reference population.

Definitions differ

Several interpolation conventions exist for computing percentiles from finite samples, and software packages implement slightly different defaults — a notorious source of small discrepancies in reported results.

Percentiles are not percentages: scoring at the 90th percentile says nothing about scoring 90% correct.
