Hasan ibn Ali al-Askari (c. 844 – 874) was a descendant of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and the eleventh of the Twelve Imams in Twelver Shia Islam. He succeeded his father, Ali al-Hadi, in 868 and was Imam until his death six years later. His epithet al-Askari derives from 'askar (military camp), referring to Samarra, the garrison city where he lived under Abbasid surveillance for most of his life.

Life

Born in Medina, Hasan moved as a child with his father to Samarra, the Abbasid capital, where the Imams were kept under close watch by the caliphs al-Mu'tazz, al-Muhtadi and al-Mu'tamid. His imamate coincided with repression of the Alids and the fragmentation of Abbasid power. He communicated with his followers largely through a network of deputies (the wikala), which also collected religious taxes on his behalf.

Death and succession

Hasan al-Askari died in Samarra in 874, aged about 29; Shia tradition holds that he was poisoned at the instigation of the caliph al-Mu'tamid. He was buried beside his father in what became the Al-Askari Shrine. Twelver doctrine holds that his son, Muhammad al-Mahdi, succeeded him and entered occultation, to return as the Mahdi. His death without a publicly acknowledged heir precipitated a succession crisis from which the Twelver community and its doctrine of occultation emerged.
