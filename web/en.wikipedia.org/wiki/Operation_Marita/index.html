Operation Marita

Date: 6–30 April 1941

Location: Greece

Part of: Balkans campaign, Second World War

Belligerents: Germany, Italy, Bulgaria vs. Greece, United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand

Result: Axis victory; occupation of Greece

Operation Marita (German: Unternehmen Marita) was the German invasion of Greece in April 1941, conducted as part of the Balkans campaign alongside the invasion of Yugoslavia (Operation 25). The operation followed Italy's failed invasion of Greece in October 1940, which had driven Italian forces back into Albania and brought British forces to Greek soil, prompting Adolf Hitler to intervene to secure Germany's southern flank before the planned invasion of the Soviet Union.

Background

Hitler issued Directive No. 20 in December 1940 for the conquest of Greece. German forces of Field Marshal Wilhelm List's 12th Army assembled in Romania and Bulgaria, which joined the Tripartite Pact in March 1941. The coup d'état in Belgrade on 27 March 1941 that overthrew the Yugoslav government led Hitler to add Yugoslavia to the operation.

The invasion

The attack began on 6 April 1941, with the Luftwaffe devastating Belgrade and Piraeus. German armoured thrusts outflanked the Metaxas Line in Greek Macedonia by advancing through Yugoslav territory down the Vardar valley, cutting off the Greek Army of Eastern Macedonia, which surrendered on 9 April. Thessaloniki fell, and German forces drove south through the Monastir Gap against the Greek and Commonwealth forces — the British, Australian, and New Zealand expeditionary force ("W Force") under General Henry Maitland Wilson. Athens was occupied on 27 April, and organized resistance on the mainland ended by 30 April, though the battle continued on Crete in May (Operation Mercury).

Aftermath

Commonwealth forces were evacuated by sea, largely to Crete and Egypt, losing much of their equipment. Greece was partitioned among German, Italian, and Bulgarian occupation zones, and endured a harsh occupation, famine, and a large resistance movement. Historians debate whether the Balkan campaign materially delayed Operation Barbarossa, scheduled weeks later.
