Generalitat de Catalunya

Palau de la Generalitat, seat of the institution.

The Generalitat de Catalunya (Catalan for "Government of Catalonia") is the institutional system around which the self-government of Catalonia, an autonomous community of Spain, is organized. It consists of the Parliament of Catalonia, the Executive Council (government), and the Presidency of the Generalitat, together with other institutions established by the Statute of Autonomy.

History

The Generalitat has medieval origins: the permanent deputation of the Catalan Courts (Corts Catalanes) established in the 13th–14th centuries to administer taxes granted to the Crown grew into a standing institution of government. It was abolished in 1714 by the Nueva Planta decrees of Philip V after the War of the Spanish Succession. The institution was restored in 1931 under the Second Spanish Republic, with Francesc Macià as its first modern president, and abolished again after the Spanish Civil War under Franco. Josep Tarradellas returned from exile in 1977, and the Generalitat was formally restored in the transition to democracy and the 1979 Statute of Autonomy.

Functions

The Generalitat exercises executive and legislative powers transferred or recognized by the Spanish Constitution and the Statute of Autonomy, including education, health, policing (through the Mossos d'Esquadra), transport, culture, language policy and civil law. Its seat is the Palau de la Generalitat in Plaça de Sant Jaume, Barcelona. The institution was the protagonist of the 2017 Catalan independence crisis, when the Spanish government temporarily imposed direct rule under Article 155 of the Constitution.
