Liahona (Book of Mormon)

In the Book of Mormon, the Liahona is a brass ball of "curious workmanship" that functioned as a compass or director for Lehi and his family as they travelled in the wilderness after leaving Jerusalem around 600 BC. According to the narrative, Lehi found the ball outside his tent door, and its two spindles pointed the way the party should travel, working according to the faith and diligence of its users. Writing also appeared on the ball from time to time, giving instructions and reproofs.

In the narrative

The Liahona guided Lehi's family through the wilderness of Arabia to the seashore they called Bountiful, and later directed Nephi in building and navigating the ship that carried them to the promised land. When the travellers were slothful or contentious, the pointers ceased to work. The prophet Alma later used the Liahona as an analogy for the words of Christ guiding believers to eternal life.

Legacy

The name "Liahona" has been adopted within the Latter Day Saint movement for various purposes, including the title of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints' international magazine (formerly the Ensign's companion publication), which took the name Liahona in 2021.
