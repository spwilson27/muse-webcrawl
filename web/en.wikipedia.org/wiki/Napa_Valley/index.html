Napa Valley

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Napa Valley is a valley in Napa County, California, United States, and one of the world's premier wine-producing regions. The valley is about 30 miles (48 km) long, running northwest from the city of Napa to Calistoga between the Mayacamas Mountains to the west and the Vaca Mountains to the east, drained by the Napa River. The Napa Valley AVA (American Viticultural Area), established in 1981 as California's first AVA, contains more than a dozen sub-appellations including Oakville, Rutherford, Stags Leap District, Howell Mountain, and Mount Veeder.

Geology and climate

The valley's soils are exceptionally varied — more than thirty distinct soil types, roughly half of the world's known soil orders — formed from volcanic activity of the Sonoma Volcanics, uplifted seabeds, and river alluvium. Its Mediterranean climate combines warm days with cool nights moderated by fog drawn in from San Pablo Bay, a pattern that ripens Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Chardonnay, and other classic varieties while retaining acidity.

History

The valley was home to the Wappo people for thousands of years. George C. Yount planted the first vineyard in 1839, and settlers including John Patchett, Charles Krug (1861), and Jacob Schram followed; by 1889 more than 140 wineries operated. Phylloxera in the 1890s and Prohibition (1920–1933) devastated the industry; only a handful of wineries survived by producing sacramental wine.

Modern era

The modern renaissance began with Robert Mondavi's winery in 1966 and the 1976 Judgment of Paris tasting, at which a Stag's Leap Wine Cellars Cabernet Sauvignon and a Chateau Montelena Chardonnay defeated top French wines, establishing Napa's world reputation. In 1968 Napa County created the nation's first Agricultural Preserve to protect vineyard land. Today the valley hosts over 400 wineries and about 3.8 million visitors a year; tourism and wine are balanced against conservation efforts and concerns over wildfires, which severely damaged the valley in the 2017 and 2020 fire seasons.

Towns

From south to north: Napa, Yountville, Oakville, Rutherford, St. Helena, and Calistoga, connected by Highway 29 and the scenic Silverado Trail.
