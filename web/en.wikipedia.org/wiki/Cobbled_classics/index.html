Cobbled classics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The cobbled classics are a group of prestigious one-day professional road cycling races held in northern Europe in early spring whose courses feature significant sections of cobblestone roads (pavé). They form a distinctive sub-season within the spring classics and are prized by specialists in rough, tactical racing.

The races

The two greatest cobbled classics are both among cycling's five Monuments: the Tour of Flanders (Ronde van Vlaanderen), famous for short, steep cobbled climbs such as the Oude Kwaremont and Paterberg, and Paris–Roubaix, the "Hell of the North," whose flat cobbled sectors through northern France — including the Trouée d'Arenberg — are among the sport's most feared tests. Supporting races include E3 Saxo Classic, Gent–Wevelgem, Dwars door Vlaanderen, and Omloop Het Nieuwsblad.

Characteristics

Cobbled racing rewards bike-handling, positioning, and durability; punctures and crashes are common, and races are often decided by attrition. Riders such as Tom Boonen, Fabian Cancellara, and Johan Museeuw — the "Lion of Flanders" — built legends on the cobbles, while more recently Mathieu van der Poel and Wout van Aert have dominated the spring campaign.
