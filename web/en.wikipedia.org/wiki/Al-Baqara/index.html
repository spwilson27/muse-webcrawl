Al-Baqara (Arabic: ٱلْبَقَرَة, "The Cow") is the second and longest chapter (surah) of the Quran, containing 286 verses (āyāt). It is a Medinan surah, revealed after the Hijrah, and addresses the early Muslim community as well as the Children of Israel, Christians, and the hypocrites of Medina. Its name derives from the story, told in verses 67–73, of the cow that Moses commanded the Israelites to sacrifice.

Contents

The surah opens by dividing people into believers, disbelievers, and hypocrites, then recounts episodes from the histories of Adam, Moses, and Abraham. Much of the chapter consists of legislation for the new community: fasting during Ramadan, the change of the qibla (direction of prayer) toward Mecca, pilgrimage rites, marriage, divorce, inheritance, finance and the prohibition of usury (riba), dietary rules, and regulations of warfare and retaliation.

Verse 282, dealing with the writing down of debts, is the longest verse in the Quran. The final verses (285–286) are among the most frequently recited in devotional practice.

Ayat al-Kursi

Verse 255, known as Āyat al-Kursī (the Throne Verse), is one of the most famous passages of the Quran. It proclaims God's sovereignty, knowledge, and everlasting life: "Allah — there is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of existence." It is widely memorized, recited for protection, and calligraphed in homes and mosques.
