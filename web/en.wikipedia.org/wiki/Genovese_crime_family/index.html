The Genovese crime family, also sometimes referred to as the Westside, is an Italian American Mafia crime family and one of the "Five Families" that dominate organized crime activities in New York City within the American Mafia. It has generally been considered the most powerful and influential of the Five Families, and has been nicknamed the "Ivy League" and the "Rolls-Royce" of organized crime.

History

The family originated in the 1890s among Sicilian immigrants in Harlem and the Lower East Side, coalescing under Giuseppe Morello. It passed through the hands of Joe Masseria, whose murder in 1931 ended the Castellammarese War, and then Charles "Lucky" Luciano, who modernized the organization and helped establish the Commission. Following Luciano's deportation, Frank Costello ran the family until an assassination attempt by Vincent Gigante, ordered by Vito Genovese, forced Costello's retirement in 1957. Genovese's own reign ended with his imprisonment in 1959.

Later decades

From the 1980s, Vincent "Chin" Gigante led the family, feigning mental illness for decades to deflect law enforcement — wandering Greenwich Village in a bathrobe — while directing affairs through messengers. Federal prosecutors used the RICO statute to convict much of the family's leadership, including Gigante in 1997, and the family's power declined relative to its peak, though it remains active in rackets including labor and construction racketeering, gambling, loansharking, and extortion.
