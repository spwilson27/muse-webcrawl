Escarpment

An escarpment is a steep slope or long cliff formed by erosion or faulting, separating two areas of different elevation.

An escarpment, or scarp, is a steep slope or long cliff that forms as a result of differential erosion or faulting and separates two relatively level areas having different elevations. The word derives from the French escarpe, originally the inner slope of a ditch dug below a fortress wall — a reminder that escarpments have long had defensive significance.

Formation

Erosional escarpments commonly develop where a resistant rock layer (such as limestone, dolomite or sandstone) overlies weaker rock such as shale or mudstone. The hard caprock protects the slope, while the soft strata beneath are undercut, so the scarp retreats while keeping a steep profile; the gentle slope on the other side, following the dip of the strata, is the dip slope. Such paired features are called cuestas when the dip is gentle. Fault scarps, by contrast, result from vertical displacement along a fault — though erosion quickly modifies them, and features such as the East African Rift escarpments are fundamentally tectonic in origin.

Notable escarpments

Famous examples include the Niagara Escarpment, a dolomite-capped cuesta running in a great arc from New York through Ontario (where it carries the Bruce Trail and forms the cliff over which Niagara Falls plunges) to Michigan and Wisconsin; the Great Escarpment of southern Africa, whose Drakensberg section rises over 3,000 m; the Darling Scarp of Western Australia; and the escarpments of the Cotswolds and the North and South Downs in England. On the Moon and other bodies, long cliff-like features called rupes are lobate scarps formed by thrust faulting as the interior cooled and contracted.

Significance

Escarpments influence settlement, transport and ecology: they channel rivers into waterfalls and gorges, create distinct microclimates and habitats along their faces, form natural defensive lines and boundaries, and expose geological strata conveniently to study.
