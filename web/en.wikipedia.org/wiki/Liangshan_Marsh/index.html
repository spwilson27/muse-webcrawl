Liangshan Marsh

Liangshan Marsh (Chinese: 梁山泊; Liángshān Pō) was a vast marshland in the low-lying country around Mount Liang in present-day Shandong, China, formed where the Yellow River and other waters spread across the plain. During the Northern Song dynasty its maze of lakes, reed beds, and channels — at its greatest extent covering hundreds of square kilometres — made it a classic refuge for fugitives and rebels beyond the easy reach of government troops.

In history

The marsh expanded dramatically after the Yellow River changed course in 944 and again during the Song period; contemporary sources describe an interconnected lake district adjoining Mount Liang. The bandit leader Song Jiang is recorded in Song-era histories as operating with a band of outlaws in the region during the reign of Emperor Huizong, raiding across the surrounding prefectures before surrendering or being captured (accounts differ).

In literature

Liangshan Marsh is immortalized as the stronghold of the 108 Stars of Destiny in Water Margin (水滸傳), one of the Four Great Classical Novels of Chinese literature, attributed to Shi Nai'an. In the novel the marsh's natural defences — water, mudflats, and hidden channels guarded by the Ruan brothers' fishermen — protect the outlaw fellowship led successively by Chao Gai and Song Jiang, who champion the slogan of "enforcing justice on behalf of Heaven". The image of the marsh stronghold became so durable that today the scenic area on Liangshan markets itself around the novel's heroes, with rebuilt strongholds and statuary on the mountain.

Disappearance

Successive shifts of the Yellow River and deliberate reclamation from the Ming dynasty onward drained the marshes. Today's Dongping Lake is considered a remnant of the once-enormous Liangshan lake system.
