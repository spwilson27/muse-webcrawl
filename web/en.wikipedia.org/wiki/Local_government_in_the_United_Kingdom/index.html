Local government in the United Kingdom

Local government in the United Kingdom is organised separately in its four constituent countries, because of devolution. Local authorities administer services such as education, social care, housing, transport, waste, and planning, within powers granted by Parliament and the devolved legislatures. The United Kingdom has no constitutional guarantee of local government; councils derive their powers from statute.

England

Local government in England is a patchwork of two-tier county and district councils, unitary authorities, metropolitan boroughs, and London boroughs under the Greater London Authority. Parish and town councils form the lowest tier in many areas. Recent years have seen combined authorities headed by elected mayors taking on devolved functions in city regions.

Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland

Scotland has 32 unitary council areas, Wales has 22 principal areas, and Northern Ireland has 11 local government districts with more limited powers. Devolved governments in Edinburgh and Cardiff supervise local government within their nations.

Finance and elections

Councils are funded by council tax, business rates, and central government grants. Councillors are elected by residents, generally every four years, using first-past-the-post in England and Wales and the single transferable vote in Scotland and Northern Ireland.
