Silmaril

The Silmarils (Quenya: Silmarilli) are three fictional jewels in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, appearing principally in The Silmarillion. Fashioned by the elf Fëanor in Valinor, they captured the light of the Two Trees, Laurelin and Telperion, and were the most prized creations of the Elves.

Story

Fëanor made the Silmarils from the substance silima, enclosing in them the mingled light of the Trees. The Vala Morgoth (Melkor), desiring them, destroyed the Trees with Ungoliant and stole the jewels, killing Fëanor's father Finwë. Fëanor and his seven sons swore a terrible oath to recover them, leading the Noldor into exile in Middle-earth and to the kinslayings and wars narrated in the Quenta Silmarillion.

One Silmaril was taken from Morgoth's crown by Beren and Lúthien — the jewel that the mortal Beren cut from the Iron Crown at great cost. At the end of the First Age, Eärendil bore that Silmaril into the sky, where it shines as a star. Of the other two, after Morgoth's defeat they were seized by Fëanor's sons Maedhros and Maglor, but the jewels burned their hands, for they had forfeited their right to them: Maedhros cast himself and his Silmaril into a fiery chasm, and Maglor threw his into the sea. Thus one Silmaril is in the sky, one in the earth, and one in the sea.

Significance

The Silmarils embody Tolkien's themes of light, possession, and the corrupting desire to own beautiful things; their fate structures the whole mythology of the First Age and gives The Silmarillion its name.
