Source Han Serif

Source Han Serif is a serif Pan-CJK typeface family developed by Adobe in collaboration with Google. It was released in April 2017 as the serif counterpart to Source Han Sans (known in Google's distribution as Noto Sans CJK). In Google's Noto fonts family, the font is distributed as Noto Serif CJK.

Design

Source Han Serif provides consistent support for Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Japanese and Korean in a single font family, with region-specific glyph variants selectable through OpenType language features. The Hangul glyphs were designed by the Korean foundry Sandoll Communications, and the Japanese kana and kanji designs were contributed by Iwata Corporation; Adobe coordinated the overall design under typeface designer Ryoko Nishizuka and project lead Ken Lunde. The family ships in seven weights, from ExtraLight to Heavy.

Licensing and distribution

Like Source Han Sans, the font is released under the SIL Open Font License, allowing free use, modification and redistribution. It is offered in several configurations, including language-specific OTF fonts, region-specific subsets, and large multilingual "Super OTC" collections combining all four languages and all weights. Source Han Serif forms part of Adobe's open-source Source type family, which also includes Source Sans, Source Serif and Source Code Pro.

Usage

The typeface is widely used for Chinese, Japanese and Korean text in publishing, user interfaces and e-readers where a Mincho/Song-style serif is required, and it underlies conversions of classical East Asian texts into digital editions.
