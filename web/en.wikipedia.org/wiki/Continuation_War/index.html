The Continuation War, also known as the Second Soviet–Finnish War, was a conflict fought by Finland and Nazi Germany against the Soviet Union during World War II, from 1941 to 1944. In Finland, the war was seen as a continuation of the Winter War of 1939–40 — hence the name — fought to recover territories ceded to the USSR in the Moscow Peace Treaty and, for a time, to advance into Soviet East Karelia. The United Kingdom declared war on Finland on 6 December 1941, but the United States did not.

Background

After the Winter War, Finland drifted toward Germany, permitting the transit of German troops and joining Germany's Operation Barbarossa against the USSR. The war began with Soviet air raids on Finnish targets on 25 June 1941, three days after Barbarossa began, whereupon Finland declared war.

Course of the war

In the offensive phase of 1941, Finnish forces retook the Karelian Isthmus and Ladoga Karelia and advanced into East Karelia, reaching the River Svir and Lake Onega and participating (controversially) in the northern pincer around besieged Leningrad. Thereafter the front stabilized into trench warfare until June 1944, when the massive Soviet Vyborg–Petrozavodsk Offensive shattered Finnish lines on the Isthmus and forced a fighting retreat. Finnish resistance, stiffened by German arms deliveries and victories at Tali-Ihantala — the largest battle in Nordic history — stabilized the front, and Finland negotiated an exit.

Outcome

The Moscow Armistice of 19 September 1944 required Finland to cede Petsamo, lease Porkkala to the USSR, pay heavy reparations ($300 million), and expel German troops from its soil — which led to the Lapland War (1944–45) against its former co-belligerent. Finland nevertheless preserved its independence and democratic institutions, unlike the Baltic states. Finnish casualties were about 63,000 dead; Soviet losses were several times higher.
