Magic hypercube

A magic hypercube is the n-dimensional generalisation of a magic square and a magic cube. It is an arrangement of distinct integers in an n-dimensional grid in which the numbers along every row, column, file and higher-dimensional axis line — and, in the "perfect" case, along every diagonal — sum to the same constant, the magic constant.

Definitions

An order-m magic hypercube in n dimensions uses the integers 1 through mn. Variants are classified by which sets of lines are required to be magic: "simple" hypercubes require only the axis-parallel lines, while pandiagonal and perfect hypercubes impose additional conditions on broken diagonals and space diagonals.

Existence

Magic cubes (the three-dimensional case) exist for all orders except 2, and constructions using methods such as the Siamese method and composition extend to all higher dimensions for most orders. The smallest perfect magic cube is of order 8.
