Simón Bolívar

Simón José Antonio de la Santísima Trinidad Bolívar y Palacios (24 July 1783 – 17 December 1830) was a Venezuelan military and political leader who led several South American countries to independence from the Spanish Empire. Known as El Libertador ("the Liberator"), he is one of the most influential figures in Latin American history; the country of Bolivia is named after him.

Born24 July 1783, Caracas, Venezuela
Died17 December 1830, Santa Marta, Colombia
Known forSpanish American wars of independence
OfficesPresident of Gran Colombia, Peru, Bolivia

Career

Educated in Europe and influenced by Enlightenment thought, Bolívar joined the independence movement after 1810. His campaigns included the crossing of the Andes and the victory at Boyacá (1819), which secured New Granada, and Carabobo (1821), which secured Venezuela. With Antonio José de Sucre he completed the liberation of Peru at Ayacucho (1824). His project of a united Spanish America (Gran Colombia) dissolved amid regional rivalries by 1830, the year of his death from tuberculosis.
