Hudson Heights is a residential neighborhood of Upper Manhattan, New York City, within the larger Washington Heights neighborhood. It is bounded generally by West 173rd Street to the south, Broadway to the east, Fort Tryon Park to the north and the Hudson River to the west, and is built on some of the highest natural ground in Manhattan.

Name

The name "Hudson Heights" was promoted in the 1990s by local residents and real-estate brokers to brand the area west of Broadway, in the same period that saw the creation of a Hudson Heights Owners Coalition. Earlier names for the area included "Fort Washington Heights" and "Frankfurt-on-the-Hudson", reflecting the large German-Jewish refugee community — including many from Frankfurt — that settled there in the 1930s and 1940s fleeing Nazi Germany. A substantial Orthodox and Modern Orthodox Jewish community remains, alongside a large Dominican population.

Character and landmarks

The neighborhood is notable for its pre-war Art Deco and Tudor Revival apartment buildings, including the Castle Village cooperative (1939), designed by George F. Pelham II on the former estate of painter Charles C. Abbott, whose landscaping incorporates remnants of the estate's grounds and views toward the George Washington Bridge, completed in 1931 just north of the area. At the neighborhood's northern edge, Fort Tryon Park contains The Cloisters, the Metropolitan Museum of Art's branch museum of medieval art. Bennett Park, on the site of Fort Washington, marks the highest natural point in Manhattan (265 feet / 80.8 m above sea level), the scene of the Battle of Fort Washington in 1776.

Hudson Heights is served by the A train at 175th and 181st Streets and by the 190th Street station, with deep-level elevators connecting street level to Fort Tryon Park. The neighborhood's largely residential, comparatively quiet streets and river views have attracted families and artists, and its main commercial strips run along 181st Street, Broadway and Fort Washington Avenue.
