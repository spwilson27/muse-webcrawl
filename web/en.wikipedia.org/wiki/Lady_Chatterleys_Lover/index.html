Lady Chatterley's Lover is a novel by the English author D. H. Lawrence, first published privately in 1928 in Florence, Italy. It tells the story of Constance Reid, Lady Chatterley, whose upper-class husband Sir Clifford Chatterley has been paralysed from the waist down in the First World War; feeling trapped in an intellectual, sexless marriage, she begins an affair with Oliver Mellors, the gamekeeper on their estate. The novel's explicit descriptions of sex and its use of then-unprintable words made it notorious and led to bans in many countries.

Publication history and obscenity trial

Unexpurgated editions were long unavailable in Britain and the United States; the book was banned in the US until a 1959 court ruling, and in Canada, Australia, India, and Japan it was also suppressed. In Britain, Penguin Books' 1960 publication of the full text was prosecuted under the Obscene Publications Act 1959 in the case R v Penguin Books Ltd. The trial at the Old Bailey featured testimony from literary figures including E. M. Forster and Helen Gardner; the jury's not-guilty verdict on 2 November 1960 was a landmark for freedom of publication and is often taken to mark a shift in British social attitudes. Penguin sold millions of copies within months.

Themes

Lawrence wrote three versions of the novel. Its themes include the contrast between the life of the mind and the life of the body, class division, and the dehumanising effects of industrialisation, embodied in Clifford's mechanised collieries in the Midlands. Lawrence intended the novel as a call for tenderness and physical renewal against an over-intellectualised modern world.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted many times for film and television, including a 1955 French film, the 1981 film with Sylvia Kristel, Ken Russell's 1993 BBC serial, and a 2022 Netflix film starring Emma Corrin and Jack O'Connell.
