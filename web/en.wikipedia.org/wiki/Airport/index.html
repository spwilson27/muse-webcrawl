Airport

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A modern airport terminal at dusk with aircraft tails at the gates]
A major international airport terminal.

An airport is an aerodrome with extended facilities for commercial air transport: runways, taxiways, aprons, passenger terminals, control towers and ground services. The world\'s busiest airport by passengers is usually Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta in the United States; by international passengers, Dubai leads. Airports are identified by IATA (three-letter) and ICAO (four-letter) codes, and range from single-strip regional fields to mega-hubs handling over 100 million travellers a year.

Facilities

The airside includes runways (whose length and strength determine which aircraft can operate), taxiways, aprons and jet bridges. The landside has terminals with check-in, security, immigration, baggage reclaim and retail; behind the scenes lie fuel farms, cargo villages, catering and maintenance hangars.

Operations

Air traffic control sequences arrivals and departures; ground handling covers marshalling, refuelling, cleaning and pushback. Slots at congested hubs are valuable assets traded between airlines. Airport charges and retail rents fund operations.

History

Early \'aerodromes\' were grass fields; College Park Airport in Maryland (1909) is often called the world\'s oldest continually operating airport. Purpose-built terminals grew in the 1930s (Berlin Tempelhof, closed 2008), and the jet age demanded long paved runways and vast terminals such as those at Atlanta, Beijing Daxing and Istanbul.
