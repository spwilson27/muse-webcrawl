Scottsdale is a city in Maricopa County, Arizona, United States, in the eastern part of the Phoenix metropolitan area. Founded on land bought in 1888 by U.S. Army chaplain Winfield Scott — for whom it is named — and incorporated in 1951, Scottsdale grew from a small farming town into an affluent resort and tourism centre known for luxury resorts, golf courses, spas, and a large winter ("snowbird") population.

Geography and character

The city stretches north–south for some 31 miles, from Tempe and Phoenix in the south to desert foothills in the north, and is loosely divided into Old Town (downtown arts and nightlife district), South Scottsdale (working-class roots adjoining Tempe), Central Scottsdale (residential and commercial corridors such as the "Shea Corridor"), and wealthy North Scottsdale, which includes master-planned communities, Scottsdale Airport, and the McDowell Sonoran Preserve — one of the largest urban nature preserves in the United States, protecting over 30,000 acres of Sonoran Desert.

Economy and culture

Tourism anchors the economy: more than 200 golf courses lie in and around the city, and the WM Phoenix Open at TPC Scottsdale is the best-attended event on the PGA Tour. Major annual events include the Barrett-Jackson Collector Car Auction and the Scottsdale Arabian Horse Show. Frank Lloyd Wright's winter home and studio, Taliesin West (1937), is a UNESCO World Heritage site within the city. Scottsdale is also home to spring training baseball (the San Francisco Giants at Scottsdale Stadium; the Diamondbacks and Rockies at nearby Salt River Fields) and corporate offices for companies in healthcare, technology, and finance.

History

The area was farmed by the Hohokam and later the Pima and Maricopa peoples, whose canal systems preceded modern irrigation. Olive and citrus groves planted by Scott and early settlers underpinned the early town; the 1930s brought artists and architects, and post-war growth, air conditioning, and resort development transformed Scottsdale after incorporation.
