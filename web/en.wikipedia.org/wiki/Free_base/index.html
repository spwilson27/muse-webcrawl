Free base

A free base (also freebase) is the unprotonated, neutral form of an amine, typically an alkaloid or pharmaceutical drug, as distinct from its salt forms (such as hydrochlorides or sulfates). The term is also used for the conjugate base of an ammonium compound.

Chemistry

Many drugs are amines that are formulated as salts to improve stability, solubility and handling. Converting the salt to the free base — for example by treatment with a base such as sodium hydroxide or sodium bicarbonate — yields the neutral amine. Free bases are generally less water-soluble but more soluble in organic solvents than their salts, and they typically have lower melting points and higher vapour pressures.

Pharmacology

The free base form can cross lipid membranes, including the blood–brain barrier, more rapidly, which historically led to the practice of "freebasing" cocaine: converting cocaine hydrochloride, which decomposes when heated, into the volatile free base form that can be smoked for a faster and more intense effect. Crack cocaine is a related, cruder form produced with baking soda. Similar salt/free-base distinctions matter in legitimate pharmacology — for instance in the design of transdermal and inhaled formulations, and in nicotine products, where free-base nicotine in e-liquids is harsher to inhale than protonated nicotine salts.
