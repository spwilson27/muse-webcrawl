Mourou in 2018.

Gérard Albert Mourou (born 22 June 1944) is a French optical physicist and pioneer in ultrafast lasers and strong-field physics. He shared the 2018 Nobel Prize in Physics with his former student Donna Strickland for chirped pulse amplification (CPA), and with Arthur Ashkin.

At the University of Rochester in the 1980s, Mourou led the group that invented CPA — stretch, amplify, compress — which freed laser science from a two-decade intensity plateau. He went on to found major ultrafast-laser centres at the University of Michigan and in Paris (the Institut de la Lumière Extrême), and championed Europe's Extreme Light Infrastructure (ELI), whose facilities aim at exawatt-class pulses for probing nonlinear quantum electrodynamics.

Legacy

Mourou's students seeded ultrafast optics worldwide, and CPA's consequences run from eye surgery and semiconductor inspection to laser-driven particle acceleration and laboratory astrophysics. A 2018 controversy over an earlier, ill-judged promotional video brought an apology and debate about sexism in physics, which Mourou acknowledged publicly.
