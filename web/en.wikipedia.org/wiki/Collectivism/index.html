Collectivism

Collectivism is a cultural perspective that emphasises the needs and goals of the group as a whole over the needs and desires of each individual. In collectivist cultures, relationships with other members of one's in-group play a central role in each person's identity; it is contrasted with individualism.

In psychology and anthropology

Geert Hofstede's cultural dimensions framework places cultures on an individualism–collectivism scale, with countries such as the United States scoring highly individualist and many East Asian, Latin American and African societies more collectivist. Harry Triandis distinguished horizontal and vertical forms of both orientations.

In politics

In political discourse, "collectivism" has been used for doctrines — including forms of socialism and communitarianism — that stress collective ownership or decision-making, though the cultural and political senses are distinct.
