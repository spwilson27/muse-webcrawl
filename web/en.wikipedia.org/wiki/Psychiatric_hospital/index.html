A psychiatric hospital, also known historically as a mental asylum or lunatic asylum, is a hospital specialising in the treatment of severe mental disorders. Psychiatric hospitals vary widely: some are units within general hospitals providing short-term crisis care, while others are large standalone institutions providing medium- or long-term treatment, rehabilitation and, in forensic facilities, care for patients detained under mental-health or criminal law. Admission may be voluntary or involuntary, under civil commitment procedures that differ by jurisdiction.

History

Specialised institutions for people with mental illness existed in the medieval Islamic world (bimaristans), and Bethlem Royal Hospital in London — founded in 1247 and admitting "distracted" patients by the 14th century — became notorious as "Bedlam". The 18th and 19th centuries saw the reform era of "moral treatment" associated with Pinel in Paris and the Tukes' York Retreat (1796), followed by the great age of asylum building: large rural asylums were erected across Europe and North America under the belief that orderly surroundings, work and routine were therapeutic. By the mid-20th century many such institutions were overcrowded and custodial, holding hundreds of thousands of patients in the United States and Britain.

Deinstitutionalisation

From the 1950s, the advent of antipsychotic medication, legal reforms strengthening patients' rights, exposés of abuse, and new policies favouring community care drove deinstitutionalisation: the large asylums emptied and mostly closed. Care shifted toward acute inpatient units, community mental-health teams, supported housing and outpatient treatment. Critics argue the shift was under-resourced in many countries, contributing to homelessness and the criminalisation of mental illness; supporters point to greater liberty and better outcomes for most patients.

Modern facilities

Contemporary psychiatric hospitals typically include acute admission wards, psychiatric intensive care units (PICUs), rehabilitation and forensic units, child and adolescent (CAMHS) and older-adult services, and mother-and-baby units. Treatment combines pharmacotherapy, psychological therapies, occupational therapy and structured nursing care, with detention and discharge governed by legislation such as the Mental Health Act 1983 in England and Wales.
