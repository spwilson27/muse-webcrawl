Exponential function

The graph of y = ex, increasing ever more steeply.

In mathematics, the exponential function is the unique real function that maps zero to one and has a derivative everywhere equal to its value. It is commonly denoted ex or exp(x), where e is Euler's number, approximately 2.71828. More generally, an exponential function is a function of the form a·bx, where b is a positive constant called the base.

The exponential function models processes in which a quantity grows or decays at a rate proportional to its current value — population growth, compound interest, radioactive decay, and cooling among them. Its inverse is the natural logarithm.

Definitions

The function ex can be characterised in several equivalent ways:

  • The power series ex = 1 + x + x²/2! + x³/3! + ⋯
  • The limit ex = limn→∞ (1 + x/n)n.
  • The unique solution of the differential equation y′ = y with y(0) = 1.
  • The inverse of the natural logarithm, ln x = ∫1x dt/t.

Complex exponential

Extended to complex arguments, the exponential function satisfies Euler's formula, eiθ = cos θ + i sin θ, which links it to the trigonometric functions and makes it fundamental to Fourier analysis, signal processing, and quantum mechanics.
