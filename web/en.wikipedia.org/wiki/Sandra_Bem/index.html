Sandra Lipsitz Bem (22 June 1944 – 20 May 2014) was an American psychologist best known for her work on gender studies, psychological androgyny, and gender schema theory. In 1974 she introduced the Bem Sex-Role Inventory (BSRI), one of the most widely used measures of gender-role orientation, which reconceptualised masculinity and femininity as independent dimensions rather than opposite ends of a single scale.

Early life and career

Bem was born in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, and studied psychology at the Carnegie Institute of Technology (now Carnegie Mellon University), earning her doctorate from the University of Michigan in 1968. With her husband Daryl Bem, also a social psychologist, she first taught at Carnegie Mellon and Stanford University, then from 1978 at Cornell University, where she later directed the women's studies programme and became professor of psychology and feminist, gender, and sexuality studies.

Work

The Bem Sex-Role Inventory asks respondents to rate themselves on personality traits culturally associated with masculinity (instrumental traits such as assertiveness and independence) and femininity (expressive traits such as warmth and tenderness). People scoring high on both dimensions are classified as "androgynous". Bem's research suggested that androgynous individuals were more flexible in their behaviour across situations, challenging the assumption that psychological health required conformity to one's sex role.

In the 1980s Bem developed gender schema theory, proposing that children learn culturally defined associations for maleness and femaleness and use these schemas to organise information about the world and the self. Her 1993 book The Lenses of Gender argued that three largely invisible assumptions — androcentrism, gender polarisation and biological essentialism — reproduce male power, and it won the Distinguished Publication Award of the Association for Women in Psychology.

With Daryl Bem, she also conducted early research and advocacy on sex discrimination in employment, including testimony on sex-segregated job advertisements.

Later life and death

Bem was diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease in 2010. She wrote candidly about her experience of the illness and, after four years, ended her own life at home in Ithaca, New York, on 20 May 2014, at the age of 69, a decision she had planned and discussed openly with her family while she retained the capacity to act on it.
