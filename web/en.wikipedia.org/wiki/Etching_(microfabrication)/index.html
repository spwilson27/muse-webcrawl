Etching (microfabrication)

Etching is used in microfabrication to chemically remove layers from the surface of a wafer during manufacturing. It is a critically important step in semiconductor device fabrication and in the manufacture of microelectromechanical systems (MEMS): along with deposition, photolithography, and doping, it is one of the fundamental processes by which integrated circuits are built up layer by layer.

Wet etching

In wet etching, the wafer is immersed in a liquid etchant that dissolves the exposed material. Classic examples include hydrofluoric acid (often buffered) for silicon dioxide, mixtures of nitric and hydrofluoric acids for silicon, and hot potassium hydroxide (KOH) or tetramethylammonium hydroxide (TMAH) for anisotropic etching of silicon. KOH etches the {111} crystal planes of silicon far more slowly than other planes, producing characteristic V-shaped grooves and pyramidal pits used in MEMS structures.

Most wet etches are isotropic — they etch in all directions at equal rates and undercut the mask, limiting the minimum feature size. Wet etching is nevertheless valued for its high selectivity, low cost, batch processing, and low damage to the wafer.

Dry etching

Dry etching removes material using plasmas or gases. In reactive-ion etching (RIE), a plasma of reactive species such as fluorine- or chlorine-based gases chemically attacks the surface while ion bombardment drives the reaction vertically, yielding anisotropic profiles with straight sidewalls. The Bosch process alternates etch and polymer deposition steps to produce very deep, high-aspect-ratio trenches (deep reactive-ion etching, DRIE). Other dry methods include sputter etching, ion milling, and vapour-phase etching with xenon difluoride, which releases MEMS structures by undercutting silicon.

Key parameters

Etch processes are characterised by etch rate, selectivity (the relative rate for the target versus mask and underlying materials), anisotropy, uniformity across the wafer, and the damage or contamination left behind. The choice between wet and dry etching balances these factors against cost and feature size.
