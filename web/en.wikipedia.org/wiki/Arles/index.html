Arles is a city in the Bouches-du-Rhône department of Provence, southern France, on the Rhône River near its delta in the Camargue. A major Roman city of Gaul, it preserves an exceptional group of Roman and Romanesque monuments inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

History

Founded by the Greeks of Marseille as Theline and refounded by Julius Caesar in 46 BC as a colony for veterans of the Sixth Legion, Arelate prospered as a river port and trading hub, rivalling Marseille. Constantine the Great made it an imperial residence; its amphitheatre (c. 90 AD, seating some 20,000), theatre, cryptoporticus, baths, and the Alyscamps necropolis date from its Roman heyday. In the Middle Ages Arles gave its name to the Kingdom of Arles and flourished as a pilgrimage stop on the Via Tolosana to Santiago de Compostela; the Church of St. Trophime is a masterpiece of Provençal Romanesque.

Van Gogh and modern Arles

Vincent van Gogh lived in Arles in 1888–1889, producing some 200 paintings including The Night Café, Starry Night Over the Rhône, and the Sunflowers series, before the ear incident and his departure for Saint-Rémy. Today the city hosts the Rencontres d'Arles, a major annual photography festival founded in 1970, and the LUMA Arles arts complex with its Frank Gehry-designed tower (opened 2021). Bullfighting in the Roman amphitheatre and rice, salt, and horse traditions of the Camargue remain central to local identity.
