Olympias (Greek: Ὀλυμπιάς; c. 375–316 BC) was a Greek princess of the Molossians, the eldest daughter of King Neoptolemus I of Epirus, and the fourth wife of Philip II, king of Macedon. She was the mother of Alexander the Great and of Cleopatra of Macedon.

According to the biographer Plutarch, her birth name was Polyxena, and she was also called Myrtale before taking the name Olympias, supposedly in honour of Philip's victory at the Olympic Games in 356 BC, the year of Alexander's birth.

Life

Olympias married Philip II in 357 BC in a union that sealed an alliance between Macedon and Epirus. Ancient sources describe the marriage as eventually strained, in part because of Philip's polygamy and Olympias's fierce temperament and devotion to the ecstatic cult of Dionysus, in whose rites she reportedly handled snakes.

After Philip's assassination in 336 BC, Olympias was implicated by hostile sources in the killing, though her involvement is unproven. During Alexander's campaigns in the east she remained a formidable political presence in Macedon and Epirus, corresponding with her son and clashing with the regent Antipater.

Wars of the Diadochi

Following Alexander's death in 323 BC, Olympias manoeuvred to protect the interests of her grandson Alexander IV. In 317 BC she returned to Macedon with an Epirote army, defeated and executed Philip III Arrhidaeus and his wife Eurydice, and ruled briefly as regent. In 316 BC she was besieged at Pydna by Cassander, forced to surrender, and put to death despite the terms of her capitulation.

Legacy

Olympias has been portrayed in numerous works of historical fiction and film, including Mary Renault's novels and the 2004 film Alexander, in which she was played by Angelina Jolie. The trireme reconstruction Olympias, launched by the Hellenic Navy in 1987, was named in her honour.
