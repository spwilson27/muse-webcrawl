Borg

The Borg are a fictional alien civilisation in the Star Trek franchise. Portrayed as a cybernetic collective of drones linked by a hive mind, the Borg travel the galaxy assimilating other species and technologies into their Collective, forcibly converting individuals with nanoprobes and surgically attached implants. Their characteristic greeting — "Resistance is futile" — has entered popular culture.

[ Image: Borg drones with cybernetic implants aboard a Borg cube ]

Appearances

The Borg were introduced in the Star Trek: The Next Generation episode "Q Who" (1989), in which the entity Q hurls the Enterprise-D across the galaxy into first contact. They returned in the acclaimed two-part episode "The Best of Both Worlds" (1990), in which Captain Jean-Luc Picard is assimilated as Locutus, and in the film Star Trek: First Contact (1996), which introduced the Borg Queen. A former drone, Seven of Nine, became a principal character in Star Trek: Voyager.

Characteristics

Borg society has no individuals: every drone is a node in a single consciousness, directed through subspace communications and ultimately personified by the Queen. Their vessels, typically vast cubes and spheres, are decentralised, with no bridge, engineering section or living quarters in the conventional sense. Borg technology adapts rapidly to weapons fire, making them among the Federation's most dangerous adversaries. Their goal is the pursuit of "perfection" through the accretion of distinctiveness — biological and technological — from assimilated cultures.

Reception

The Borg are consistently ranked among the greatest science-fiction villains. Commentators read them as metaphors for collectivism, totalitarianism, runaway technology and the loss of individuality. The phrase "resistance is futile" and the verb "to assimilate" in the Borg sense have passed into general usage.
