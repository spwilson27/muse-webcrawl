Points classification in the Tour de France

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The points classification in the Tour de France is a secondary competition in the Tour de France, which started in 1953. Points are given for high finishes in a stage and for winning intermediate sprints, and these are recorded in a points classification. It is considered a sprinters' competition. The leader is indicated by a green jersey (French: maillot vert), which has become a metonym for the points classification competition.

The system has inspired many other cycling races; the other two Grand Tours have also installed points classifications.

History

After scandals in the 1904 Tour de France, the rules of the 1905 Tour de France were changed: the winner was no longer decided by time, but by a points system. Cyclists received points based on their finishing position in each stage, and the cyclist with the fewest points was the winner. In 1913, the timing system returned, but points classifications were reintroduced permanently in 1953, the Tour's fiftieth year, with Fritz Schär of Switzerland its first winner. The green colour was chosen so the jersey would stand out from the leader's yellow jersey; the classification's first sponsor was a lawn mower manufacturer.

Points system

As of recent editions, points are awarded at the finish of each stage and at one intermediate sprint per road stage. Flat stages award the most points at the finish (50 for the winner), medium mountain stages fewer, and high mountain stages and time trials the fewest. The intermediate sprint awards 20 points to its winner. Riders who win several stages, or who are consistently placed on flat stages while also contesting intermediate sprints, dominate the competition.
