Water conservation

Drip irrigation conserves water by delivering it directly to the root zone.

Water conservation is the practice of using water efficiently to reduce unnecessary water usage. It includes all the policies, strategies and activities to sustainably manage the natural resource of fresh water, to protect the water environment, and to meet current and future human demand. According to the United States Environmental Protection Agency, water conservation also saves energy and reduces treatment and infrastructure costs.

Goals and benefits

The goals of water conservation efforts include ensuring water availability for future generations, energy conservation (since pumping, heating and treating water consumes energy), and habitat conservation by minimizing withdrawals from rivers, lakes and aquifers. Conservation can defer expensive new supply infrastructure and protect environmental flows during drought.

Strategies

At the household level, measures include fixing leaks, high-efficiency toilets, faucets and showerheads, water-efficient appliances, and landscaping with native or drought-tolerant plants (xeriscaping) instead of thirsty lawns. Rainwater harvesting and greywater reuse supply irrigation and flushing. In agriculture, which accounts for the majority of global freshwater withdrawals, drip and precision irrigation, canal lining, soil-moisture monitoring and drought-resistant crops reduce consumption. Utilities use metering, tiered pricing, leak detection on distribution networks and public education; programs such as the EPA's WaterSense label certify efficient products.
