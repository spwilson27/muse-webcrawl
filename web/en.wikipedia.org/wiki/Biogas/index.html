Biogas

Biogas is a mixture of gases, primarily methane and carbon dioxide, produced by the breakdown of organic matter in the absence of oxygen (anaerobic digestion). It can be produced from raw materials such as agricultural waste, manure, municipal waste, plant material, sewage, green waste and food waste. Biogas is a renewable energy source and can be used as a fuel for heating, electricity generation, or upgraded to biomethane for injection into gas grids and use as vehicle fuel.

Composition

Typical biogas contains 50–75% methane, 25–50% carbon dioxide, and small quantities of nitrogen, hydrogen sulfide, ammonia and water vapour. The exact composition depends on the feedstock and the digestion process. The methane content determines the energy value of the gas.

Production

Biogas is produced by micro-organisms through anaerobic digestion inside sealed reactors called digesters. The process proceeds in stages — hydrolysis, acidogenesis, acetogenesis and methanogenesis — carried out by successive communities of bacteria and archaea. Plants range from small household digesters, common in rural areas of China, India and Nepal, to large industrial facilities processing sewage sludge or energy crops. The solid residue, digestate, is nutrient-rich and used as fertiliser.

Uses

Biogas can be burned directly for cooking and heating, used in gas engines or turbines to generate electricity and heat, or refined to remove carbon dioxide and contaminants, yielding biomethane that is chemically equivalent to natural gas. Landfills also produce gas of similar composition (landfill gas) as buried waste decomposes.

Environmental aspects

Capturing methane that would otherwise escape from manure or landfill sites reduces greenhouse gas emissions. However, leaks in production and distribution can offset some of this benefit, and the use of purpose-grown energy crops raises land-use questions.
