History of Australia

The history of Australia spans at least 65,000 years of Aboriginal habitation, European exploration and colonisation from 1788, federation in 1901, and development into a modern multicultural nation.

Indigenous history

Aboriginal peoples arrived via Sahul land bridges, developing diverse languages, law, fire-stick farming and trade, including Macassan contact in the north. See Aboriginal art.

European exploration and colonisation

Dutch navigators charted coasts from 1606; James Cook charted the east coast in 1770. The First Fleet under Arthur Phillip founded Sydney in 1788 as a penal colony. Explorers such as Matthew Flinders, who circumnavigated the continent, and the ill-fated Burke and Wills expedition opened the interior. Colonisation brought dispossession, disease and frontier conflict devastating Indigenous populations. Gold rushes from 1851 transformed the colonies.

Federation and 20th century

The six colonies federated as the Commonwealth of Australia on 1 January 1901. Australians served in both world wars (Gallipoli, Kokoda); post-1945 mass migration ended the White Australia policy progressively. The 1967 referendum counted Aboriginal people in the census; Mabo (1992) recognised native title.

Contemporary

Australia became a prosperous, urbanised democracy; debates continue over republic, reconciliation and climate.
