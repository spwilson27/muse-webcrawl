N. G. Ranga

Born7 November 1900, Nidubrolu, Madras Presidency
Died9 June 1995, Ponnur, Andhra Pradesh
OccupationFreedom fighter, parliamentarian, peasant leader
HonoursPadma Vibhushan

N. G. Ranga (born Gogineni Ranga Nayukulu, 7 November 1900 – 9 June 1995) was an Indian freedom fighter, economist, parliamentarian, and a pioneering leader of the peasant (kisan) movement. He served in Parliament for a record five decades and is remembered as the father of the Indian peasant movement.

Career

Educated at Oxford, Ranga returned to India and became a professor of economics before joining the independence movement led by Mahatma Gandhi. He founded peasant schools and organisations across Andhra, co-founded the All India Kisan Sabha in 1936, and championed the rights of tenant farmers and agricultural labourers. Initially a socialist within the Congress Party, he later broke with it over agricultural policy, co-founding the Swatantra Party in 1959 with C. Rajagopalachari, advocating free enterprise and farmers' property rights.

Legacy

Ranga received the Padma Vibhushan and entered the Guinness records for his exceptionally long parliamentary service. The agricultural university of Andhra Pradesh, Acharya N. G. Ranga Agricultural University, is named in his honour.
