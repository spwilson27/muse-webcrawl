The Al-Askari Shrine is a Shia Muslim mosque and mausoleum in Samarra, Iraq, containing the tombs of Ali al-Hadi and Hasan al-Askari, the tenth and eleventh of the Twelve Imams. It is also significant as the site adjoining the cellar (sardab) associated with the occultation of the twelfth Imam, Muhammad al-Mahdi. The shrine is one of the holiest sites in Shia Islam, drawing millions of pilgrims.

History

The imams lived and died under Abbasid supervision in the garrison city of Samarra ("al-Askar", whence "al-Askari"). A shrine over their graves was developed from the tenth century onward and embellished under successive dynasties; its golden dome and minarets were completed in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries under Qajar-era patronage.

Bombings and reconstruction

On 22 February 2006, attackers bombed the shrine, destroying its golden dome; the attack ignited waves of sectarian violence across Iraq. A second bombing in June 2007 destroyed the two minarets. UNESCO assisted in reconstruction, and the dome and minarets were rebuilt, with the shrine reopening to pilgrims in stages from 2009.
