Radio Caroline

Radio Caroline is a British radio station founded in 1964 by Ronan O'Rahilly and Allan Crawford to circumvent the record companies' control of popular music broadcasting in the United Kingdom and the BBC's radio broadcasting monopoly. Broadcasting from ships in international waters in the North Sea, it became the most famous of the offshore "pirate" stations and played a major role in the development of British popular music and commercial radio.

Founded: 28 March 1964
Founder: Ronan O'Rahilly
Original base: MV Caroline, off the Essex coast
Ship (from 1983): MV Ross Revenge
Today: Licensed station on 648 AM, DAB and online

History

Radio Caroline began broadcasting on Easter weekend 1964 from the MV Caroline anchored off Felixstowe, playing pop music all day at a time when the BBC offered only a few hours of it per week. The station attracted millions of listeners and DJs such as Tony Blackburn, Johnnie Walker and Kenny Everett. The Marine Broadcasting Offences Act 1967 made it illegal for British citizens to supply or work for offshore stations, and most pirates closed; Caroline defied the Act and continued, supplied from the Netherlands.

Later years

Migrating between ships and frequencies, the station survived the sinking of the Mi Amigo in 1980 and resumed from the Ross Revenge in 1983. A raid by British and Dutch authorities in 1989 and a final shipwreck in 1991 ended the offshore era. The Ross Revenge is now preserved at Tilbury and used for special broadcasts. Radio Caroline later broadcast legally by satellite and online, and in 2017 it obtained an Ofcom licence to broadcast on 648 kHz AM — fittingly, from a transmitter at Orford Ness — along with DAB in several cities.

Cultural impact

Caroline and its fellow pirates transformed British radio, leading directly to the launch of BBC Radio 1 in 1967 (staffed largely by ex-pirate DJs) and to legal commercial radio in the 1970s. The story was dramatised in the 2009 film The Boat That Rocked.
