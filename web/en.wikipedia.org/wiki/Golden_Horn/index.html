Golden Horn

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Golden Horn
Location: Istanbul, Turkey
Type: Natural estuary of the Bosphorus, about 7.5 km long

The Golden Horn is a natural estuary and historic harbour dividing the European side of Istanbul. Formed where the Alibey and Kağıthane streams meet the Bosphorus, it curves inland for roughly 7.5 kilometres, separating the old city (the historic peninsula) from the districts of Beyoğlu and Galata to the north.

The Horn was the great harbour of Byzantium and Constantinople. A massive iron chain stretched across its mouth in wartime to bar enemy fleets — most famously in 1453, when Mehmed II circumvented it by hauling ships overland on greased timbers into the Horn, a decisive stroke in the conquest of the city. Its name is traditionally explained by the golden light of sunset reflected on its waters, or by the wealth of its fisheries and trade.

Today the Golden Horn is crossed by several bridges, including the Galata Bridge — famous for its lines of fishermen — and the Haliç Metro Bridge. Shipyards and industry once polluted it heavily; cleanup since the 1980s has restored much marine life, and its banks hold parks, museums and the historic Eyüp district at its head.
