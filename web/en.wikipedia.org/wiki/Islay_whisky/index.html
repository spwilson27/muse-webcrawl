Islay whisky is Scotch whisky made on the island of Islay ('eye-lah'), one of the southernmost of the Inner Hebridean Islands located off the west coast of Scotland. Islay is one of five whisky distilling localities and regions in Scotland whose identity is protected by law. The island has been famed for distilling since at least the 14th century, when Irish monks are said to have brought the craft across the North Channel.

Islay whiskies are among the most strongly flavoured of all Scotch malts, typically dry and peaty, with flavours commonly described as smoky, medicinal, tarry, and seaweedy — the product of peat used to dry the malted barley, the island's peaty water, and maturation in seaside warehouses.

Distilleries

The island's distilleries cluster in three groups. Along the south-east coast, the "Kildalton" distilleries — Laphroaig (founded 1815), Lagavulin (1816), and Ardbeg (1815) — make the most heavily peated whiskies, often at 40–55 ppm phenols. Bowmore, in the island's capital, is among Scotland's oldest distilleries (1779) and malts some of its own barley in traditional floor maltings. On the north coast, Caol Ila and Bunnahabhain (lighter and heavier styles respectively) overlook the Sound of Islay. In the west, Bruichladdich produces largely unpeated whisky alongside the heavily peated Port Charlotte and the exceptionally peaty Octomore, while the farm distillery Kilchoman (opened 2005) pioneered a new wave of Islay producers, followed by Ardnahoe (2018) and the celebrated reopening of the long-closed Port Ellen in 2024.

Peat and flavour

Peat levels are measured in phenol parts per million (ppm) in the malted barley. The smoke compounds (phenols) survive fermentation and distillation in proportion to still shape, cut points, and cask regime, so ppm figures are only a rough guide to perceived smokiness. Islay peat, formed largely of moss and seaweed-rich deposits, gives a distinctive maritime, iodine-like note compared with mainland peat.

Fèis Ìle

Each May the island hosts Fèis Ìle, the Islay Festival of Music and Malt, during which each distillery holds an open day and releases special festival bottlings that draw thousands of visitors.
