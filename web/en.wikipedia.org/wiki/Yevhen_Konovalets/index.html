Yevhen Konovalets

Born: 14 June 1891, Zashkiv, Galicia, Austria-Hungary
Died: 23 May 1938 (aged 46), Rotterdam, Netherlands
Occupation: Military commander, political leader
Known for: Founder of the Ukrainian Military Organization and the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists

Yevhen Mykhailovych Konovalets (14 June 1891 – 23 May 1938) was a Ukrainian military commander and political leader. He commanded the Corps of Sich Riflemen during the Ukrainian War of Independence and later founded the Ukrainian Military Organization (UVO) and the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN), becoming the central figure of the Ukrainian nationalist movement in exile between the world wars.

Early life and First World War

Born in the village of Zashkiv near Lemberg (Lviv) in Austrian Galicia, Konovalets studied law at Lviv University and was active in Ukrainian student organisations. Mobilised into the Austro-Hungarian army in 1914, he was captured by Russian forces in 1915. After the February Revolution of 1917 he escaped captivity, travelled to Kyiv, and helped organise the Galician-Bukovynian Battalion of Sich Riflemen, which became one of the most disciplined formations of the Ukrainian People's Republic.

War of independence

As commander of the Sich Riflemen, Konovalets supported the Central Rada, suppressed the Bolshevik Kyiv Arsenal January Uprising in 1918, and later backed Symon Petliura's Directory. After the defeat of the Ukrainian armies, he was interned in Poland and eventually released, turning to clandestine resistance against Polish rule in Galicia.

Exile and the OUN

In 1920 Konovalets founded the Ukrainian Military Organization, which carried out sabotage and assassinations in Polish-ruled western Ukraine. In 1929, at a congress in Vienna, the UVO merged with radical nationalist youth groups to form the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists, with Konovalets as its first leader (providnyk). Operating from exile in Switzerland, Germany, and elsewhere, he sought international support for an independent Ukrainian state and met with officials of several European governments.

Assassination

On 23 May 1938, Konovalets was killed in Rotterdam by a bomb concealed in a box of chocolates, handed to him in a café by Pavel Sudoplatov, an undercover officer of the Soviet NKVD who had infiltrated his circle. His death removed the OUN's unifying leader; within two years the organisation split into rival factions led by Andriy Melnyk and Stepan Bandera.
