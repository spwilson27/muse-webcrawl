Shrine of the Book
LocationIsrael Museum, Jerusalem
Opened20 April 1965
ArchitectsArmand Bartos, Frederick Kiesler
ContentsDead Sea Scrolls; Aleppo Codex

The Shrine of the Book (Hebrew: היכל הספר, Heikhal HaSefer) is a wing of the Israel Museum in Jerusalem that houses the Dead Sea Scrolls — ancient Jewish manuscripts discovered between 1946 and 1956 in the Qumran Caves near the Dead Sea — together with other rare early biblical manuscripts, including the Aleppo Codex.

Architecture

Designed by Armand Phillip Bartos and Frederick John Kiesler, the shrine opened in 1965. Two-thirds of the building is underground; above it rises a distinctive white dome whose shape echoes the lids of the jars in which the scrolls were stored for two thousand years. Opposite stands a black basalt wall, the two forms symbolising the "Sons of Light" and "Sons of Darkness" of the scrolls' War Scroll. Water flows over the dome to keep it cool, and the interior is engineered to keep light, temperature, and humidity within strict conservation limits — and, it was designed, to protect the scrolls even in wartime.

The exhibits

The centrepiece is a facsimile of the Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaa), about 7.3 metres long and the oldest complete copy of a biblical book, displayed wrapped around a central drum; the original is shown in short rotations to limit light exposure. Other scrolls exhibited include the War Scroll, the Community Rule, the Thanksgiving Hymns, and fragments displayed in the surrounding cases. The lower level presents the Aleppo Codex, the oldest complete Hebrew Bible codex (c. 10th century).
