Libra (Latin for "the balance" or "scales") is a constellation of the zodiac, lying between Virgo to the west and Scorpius to the east. It is a rather faint constellation with no first-magnitude stars. Unusually for a zodiacal constellation, it represents an inanimate object; its stars were once regarded by Babylonian and Greek astronomers as the claws of the scorpion, a memory preserved in the Arabic names of its two brightest stars.

Stars

Alpha Librae, Zubenelgenubi — from Arabic for "the southern claw" — is a multiple star system. The brightest star in the constellation is Beta Librae, Zubeneschamali, "the northern claw" (magnitude 2.6), which has sometimes been reported as appearing greenish. Sigma Librae was formerly designated Gamma Scorpii. Libra also contains Gliese 581, a red dwarf around which several exoplanets have been reported, and HD 140283, the "Methuselah star", one of the oldest known stars.

History and mythology

The Romans made the stars into the scales held by Astraea, goddess of justice, associated with neighbouring Virgo; the association with balance also fitted the period when the Sun passed through Libra near the autumnal equinox, when day and night are equal. The Sun now appears in Libra from late October to late November owing to precession.
