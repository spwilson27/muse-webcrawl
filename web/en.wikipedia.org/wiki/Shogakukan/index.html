Shogakukan Inc. (株式会社小学館, Kabushiki-gaisha Shōgakukan) is a Japanese publisher of magazines, manga, picture books and educational materials, founded in 1922 and headquartered in Hitotsubashi, Tokyo. Along with Shueisha — which was founded as its entertainment division in 1925 before becoming a separate company — and Hakusensha, it forms the Hitotsubashi Group, one of Japan's largest publishing families. Shogakukan is a major force in manga, publishing the magazine Weekly Shōnen Sunday and long-running franchises such as Doraemon and Detective Conan.

History

Shogakukan was founded by Takeo Ōga in 1922 to publish educational magazines for elementary-school children and teachers — its name means "elementary school building". Its grade-by-grade study magazines became a Japanese institution. The entertainment arm spun off as Shueisha in 1926. After the war, the company expanded into general magazines and manga, launching Weekly Shōnen Sunday in 1959 and the preschool magazine CoroCoro Comic in 1977. Fujiko F. Fujio's Doraemon, serialised from 1969, became one of the best-selling manga series of all time.

Publications and properties

  • Weekly Shōnen Sunday — home of Detective Conan (Gosho Aoyama), Inuyasha and Ranma ½ (Rumiko Takahashi), Urusei Yatsura.
  • CoroCoro Comic — children's magazine tied to Pokémon, Beyblade and game franchises.
  • Big Comic and its sister magazines — seinen manga, including Golgo 13 (with Leed), works by Naoki Urasawa and Taiyō Matsumoto.
  • Sho-Comi, Cheese! and Flowers — shōjo and josei manga.
  • Educational series, dictionaries, encyclopedias and the manga for Nintendo's Pokémon Adventures.

With Shueisha, Shogakukan co-owns Viz Media, the largest English-language manga publisher in North America, and operates the Shogakukan-Shueisha Productions (ShoPro) licensing business.

Shogakukan Manga Award

Established in 1955, the Shogakukan Manga Award is one of Japan's major manga prizes, awarded across children's, shōnen, shōjo and general categories.
