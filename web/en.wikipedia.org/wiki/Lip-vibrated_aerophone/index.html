Lip-vibrated aerophone

Lip-vibrated aerophones, commonly called brass instruments, are a family of musical instruments in which sound is produced by the vibration of the player's lips against a cup- or funnel-shaped mouthpiece, coupled to a resonating column of air. In the Hornbostel–Sachs classification they are category 423.

How sound is produced

The player presses the lips against the mouthpiece and blows, causing them to vibrate like a double reed. The standing waves in the tube reinforce selected harmonics from this buzz; the player changes pitch by varying lip tension and air speed, and in valved instruments by changing the tube length. Only notes of the harmonic series are available on a fixed tube length.

Instruments

The family includes the trumpet, cornet, flugelhorn, French horn, trombone, euphonium, baritone horn and tuba, as well as natural instruments such as the bugle, alphorn and didgeridoo (whose lips vibrate against a simple rim). Not all are made of brass, and not all brass-looking instruments are lip-vibrated — the saxophone, for example, is a reed aerophone despite its metal body.
