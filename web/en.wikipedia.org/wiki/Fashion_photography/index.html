Fashion photography is a genre of photography that portrays clothing and other fashion items, and by extension the models, styling and settings that present them in a desirable light. It is mostly conducted for advertisements or fashion magazines such as Vogue, Vanity Fair and Elle, and has evolved its own aesthetic in which clothes are complemented by exotic locations, striking poses and strong narrative moods.

History

Fashion illustration dominated the nineteenth century; photography gradually took over after Condé Nast hired Baron Adolph de Meyer as Vogue's first staff fashion photographer in 1913. Edward Steichen's work in the 1920s lent the genre artistic prestige. In the postwar decades Irving Penn's minimalist studio portraits and Richard Avedon's energetic, movement-filled images for Harper's Bazaar redefined the field; Avedon's 1955 "Dovima with Elephants" became one of the most famous fashion photographs ever made. Later figures — Helmut Newton, Guy Bourdin, Herb Ritts, Mario Testino, Annie Leibovitz and Steven Meisel — pushed fashion photography toward cinema, provocation and celebrity culture.

Practice and criticism

A fashion shoot combines the photographer with stylists, make-up artists, set designers and models, and may be editorial (for magazines), commercial (advertising) or high fashion (haute couture). The genre has been criticised for promoting narrow beauty standards and for the extensive retouching of models, leading in some countries to disclosure rules for altered images and debates about body image and diversity.
