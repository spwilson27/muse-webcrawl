Robert Betts Laughlin (born November 1, 1950) is an American theoretical physicist and the Anne T. and Robert M. Bass Professor of Physics and Applied Physics at Stanford University. He shared the 1998 Nobel Prize in Physics with Horst Störmer and Daniel Tsui for the discovery of a new form of quantum fluid with fractionally charged excitations.

Research

In 1983 Laughlin proposed a wavefunction describing the ground state of electrons in the fractional quantum Hall regime, showing that the fluid's quasiparticles carry fractions of the electron charge — the "Laughlin state". His wider work concerns high-temperature superconductivity, quantum criticality and the idea that physical laws are emergent; he set out the latter in his popular book A Different Universe: Reinventing Physics from the Bottom Down (2005).

Other activities

Laughlin served as president of the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST) from 2004 to 2006. He is also known for outspoken views on nuclear power and on the economics of science funding.
