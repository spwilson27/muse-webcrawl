Computational physics

Computational physics is the study and implementation of numerical methods to solve physical problems, often regarded as a subset of theoretical physics but one that bridges theory and experiment through simulation.

Methods

Common methods include numerical integration of differential equations, Monte Carlo simulations, molecular dynamics, lattice methods such as lattice QCD, finite element and finite difference methods, and fast Fourier transforms.

History

The field grew with wartime computing (including Monte Carlo work at Los Alamos), weather prediction, and the development of algorithms such as the Metropolis algorithm (1953) and particle-mesh methods.

Applications

Computational physics is applied in condensed matter, plasma physics and fusion, astrophysics, fluid dynamics, materials design, and climate modeling, and is now sometimes called the "third pillar" of physics alongside theory and experiment.
