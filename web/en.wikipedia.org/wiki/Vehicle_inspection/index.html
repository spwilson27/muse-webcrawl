Vehicle inspection

Vehicle inspection is a procedure mandated by national or subnational governments in many countries, in which a vehicle is inspected to ensure that it conforms to regulations governing safety, emissions, or both. Inspection can be required at various times, such as periodically or at transfer of title.

Safety inspection

Safety inspections typically check brakes, steering, tyres, lights, suspension, mirrors, seatbelts and structural corrosion. Examples include the MOT test in the United Kingdom (required annually for most vehicles over three years old), the German Hauptuntersuchung (TÜV), Japan's shaken system, and state-level inspection programs in parts of the United States and Canada. Frequencies vary from annual to biennial, and new vehicles are often exempt for their first years.

Emissions inspection

Emissions testing measures pollutants such as carbon monoxide, hydrocarbons and nitrogen oxides, increasingly administered through the vehicle's onboard diagnostics (OBD-II) system rather than tailpipe sampling. Such programs, for example in California and many U.S. metropolitan areas, aim to identify high-emitting vehicles for repair.

Effectiveness debates

Studies differ on how much periodic inspection reduces accidents or emissions; critics cite cost and inconvenience, while supporters point to removal of unsafe vehicles and deterrence of neglect. Some jurisdictions have repealed or narrowed their programs as vehicle fleets have become more reliable.
