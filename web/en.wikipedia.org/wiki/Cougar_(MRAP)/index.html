Cougar (MRAP)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cougar is a mine-resistant ambush-protected (MRAP) infantry mobility vehicle designed by Force Protection, Inc. (later General Dynamics Land Systems) for the United States armed forces. Built around a V-shaped hull that deflects under-vehicle blasts away from the crew compartment, it was fielded from 2004 in response to the improvised explosive device threat in Iraq and Afghanistan.

Design

The Cougar is produced in 4×4 (Category I) and 6×6 (Category II) variants, carrying a crew of two plus four or eight troops respectively. Its monocoque hull, heavy armour, and raised chassis protect occupants against mines, IEDs, and small-arms fire. Variants include the Joint EOD Rapid Response Vehicle (JERRV) used by explosive ordnance disposal teams.

Operators

Besides the United States, the vehicle was adopted by the United Kingdom as the Mastiff and Ridgback, and by Canada, Iraq, Poland, Ukraine, and other countries. Thousands were transferred to partner forces or state and local law-enforcement agencies as US operations wound down.
