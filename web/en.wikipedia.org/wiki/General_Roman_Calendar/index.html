The General Roman Calendar is the liturgical calendar of the Roman Rite of the Catholic Church, listing the celebrations of saints and mysteries of the life of Christ to be observed throughout the Latin Church. Particular churches and religious orders supplement it with their own calendars.

A page of a Roman Catholic liturgical calendar.

History

The calendar developed over centuries as feasts of martyrs and saints accumulated around the Roman sanctoral cycle. Following the Council of Trent, Pope Pius V issued a revised calendar in 1568–1570 that pruned many medieval additions. Further feasts were added over the following centuries, and Pope Pius X reorganised the ranking of feasts in 1911. Pope John XXIII issued a simplified rubrical code in 1960, and after the Second Vatican Council the calendar was thoroughly revised in 1969 under Paul VI by the motu proprio Mysterii Paschalis, which removed a number of saints of doubtful historicity and restored the primacy of Sundays and the major seasons.

Structure

Celebrations are ranked as solemnities, feasts, memorials (obligatory), and optional memorials. The highest solemnities include Christmas, Epiphany, Easter-related feasts of the Lord, Pentecost, Corpus Christi, and feasts of the Virgin Mary such as the Immaculate Conception and Assumption. When a celebration falls on a Sunday or higher-ranking day, rules of precedence determine which is observed or transferred.
