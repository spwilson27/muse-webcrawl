The Ryukyu Kingdom was a kingdom in the Ryukyu Islands from 1429 to 1879. Ruled by the kings of Chūzan from Shuri, it unified the island of Okinawa and dominated a maritime trade network linking China, Japan, Korea and Southeast Asia. Though formally independent for most of its history, the kingdom was a tributary of Ming and Qing China, and after 1609 was simultaneously subordinate to the Japanese domain of Satsuma. It was annexed by the Empire of Japan in 1879, becoming Okinawa Prefecture.

History

Unification

In the 14th century Okinawa was divided among three competing principalities (the Sanzan period). Shō Hashi of Chūzan conquered Hokuzan in 1416 and Nanzan in 1429, unifying the island and founding the First Shō dynasty. The Ming court recognised him as "King of Ryukyu".

Golden age of trade

Under the Second Shō dynasty (from 1470), especially King Shō Shin (r. 1477–1526), Ryukyu flourished as an entrepôt: its ships traded between Fuzhou, Japan, Korea, Malacca, Siam and Java, exchanging Chinese silk and porcelain for Japanese swords and silver and Southeast Asian spices and sappanwood. The kingdom developed a distinctive culture blending Chinese, Japanese and island traditions, visible in Shuri Castle, the gusuku fortresses and Ryukyuan music and dance.

Satsuma invasion and dual subordination

In 1609 the Shimazu clan of Satsuma invaded, capturing King Shō Nei and annexing the Amami Islands. Thereafter Ryukyu paid taxes to Satsuma while maintaining its tributary missions to China, a dual status that lasted more than 250 years and let Satsuma profit from the China trade.

Annexation

After the Meiji Restoration, Japan moved to absorb the kingdom: it was made the Ryukyu Domain in 1872 and formally annexed in 1879 (the Ryūkyū shobun), with King Shō Tai removed to Tokyo. Okinawa Prefecture was established in its place.

Culture

Ryukyuan culture developed the sanshin lute, karate and kobudō (said to have evolved partly under weapons restrictions), bingata stencil dyeing, and a unique court cuisine and pottery. The Gusuku Sites and Related Properties of the Kingdom of Ryukyu are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
