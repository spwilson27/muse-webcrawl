New Game Plus

New Game Plus (also written New Game+, NG+, or, in some games, New Game EX or playthrough 2) is an unlockable video game mode available in some games that allows the player to restart the game's story with their character's stats, items, abilities, or other progress from a previously completed playthrough retained. The feature is most common in role-playing games, action games, and roguelikes, and is intended to add replay value.

Features

In a typical implementation, finishing the game unlocks the option to begin again while keeping experience levels, equipment, skills, and collectibles. Some games scale up enemy difficulty to compensate — notably the Souls series, in which repeated New Game Plus cycles (NG+1, NG+2, and beyond) progressively increase enemy health and damage up to a capped cycle. Other games add exclusive content in New Game Plus, such as new items, bosses, endings, or narrative scenes unavailable in a first playthrough.

History and notable examples

The mode is generally traced to Chrono Trigger (1995), which popularized the term and allowed players to carry over levels and equipment to reach alternate endings. Its predecessor examples include the second quest in The Legend of Zelda (1986) and the looped, harder playthroughs of arcade games such as Ghosts 'n Goblins. Notable later users of the mode include the Persona series, Dark Souls and Elden Ring, the Borderlands series (as True Vault Hunter and Ultimate Vault Hunter modes), The Witcher 3, God of War, and indie titles such as Hades, which integrates repeated play into its story progression.

Criticism

Commentators praise New Game Plus for extending a game's lifespan and rewarding completion, but some argue that content locked behind multiple playthroughs can be inaccessible, and that starting overpowered can trivialise early-game challenge if difficulty is not scaled.
