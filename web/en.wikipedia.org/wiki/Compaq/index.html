Compaq

Compaq Computer Corporation was an American information technology company founded in 1982 that developed, sold, and supported computers and related products and services. Headquartered in Harris County, Texas, Compaq produced some of the first IBM PC compatible computers, being the second company to legally reverse engineer the IBM Personal Computer after Columbia Data Products. It rose to become the largest supplier of PC systems during the 1990s before being overtaken by Dell, and was acquired by Hewlett-Packard for $25 billion in 2002.

History

Compaq was founded by three former Texas Instruments executives. Its first product, the Compaq Portable (1983), was a suitcase-sized, all-in-one portable computer that ran all the software of the IBM PC; it sold over 53,000 units in its first year and set records for first-year sales in American business history. The name "Compaq" derived from "Compatibility and Quality".

In 1986 Compaq beat IBM to market with the first PC based on Intel's 80386 processor, the Deskpro 386, asserting leadership of the PC standard. In 1989 it launched the Systempro server line, and in 1992 the popular Presario home computer range. By 1994 Compaq had become the world's largest PC manufacturer, a position it held through most of the decade.

Decline and acquisition

The 1997 acquisition of Tandem Computers and the 1998 purchase of Digital Equipment Corporation for $9.6 billion strained the company, and intense price competition from Dell eroded its position. Compaq struggled with the integration of DEC and falling margins. In September 2001 HP announced its acquisition of Compaq; the controversial merger closed in May 2002 after a proxy fight led by Walter Hewlett. HP retained "Compaq" as a brand for lower-end systems, including the Compaq Presario line, until phasing it out around 2013.
