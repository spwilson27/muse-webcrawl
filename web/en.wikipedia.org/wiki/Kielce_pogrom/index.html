Kielce pogrom

The Kielce pogrom was an outbreak of violence against Jewish Holocaust survivors in the city of Kielce, Poland, on 4 July 1946. A mob, including soldiers and police, murdered 42 Jews and wounded more than 40 others, making it the deadliest post-war pogrom in Poland.

Events

The violence was triggered by the false claim of a nine-year-old boy that he had been held captive by Jews in the building at 7 Planty Street, which housed about 180 Jews — a revival of the blood libel. Security forces sent to "search" the building instead joined or enabled attacks, and workers from a nearby steel mill joined the mob.

Aftermath

Nine perpetrators were executed after a swift trial, though the full chain of responsibility remains debated by historians. The pogrom shocked Jewish survivors and hastened a mass emigration of Jews from Poland (the Bricha), with tens of thousands leaving in the following months. It remains a painful symbol of post-Holocaust antisemitism in Europe.
