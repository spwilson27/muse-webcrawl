Laytime

In commercial shipping under a voyage charter, laytime (also called laydays) is the period of time agreed between the shipowner and the charterer during which the vessel is made available for loading and discharging cargo without payment of additional charges. Laytime is usually expressed as a number of days or hours, or as a rate of loading or discharging per day.

Commencement

Laytime generally begins to run when the vessel has arrived at the agreed destination (an "arrived ship"), is ready in all respects to load or discharge, and the master has tendered a notice of readiness (NOR) to the charterer or shipper. The precise requirements are governed by the terms of the charterparty, commonly using standard wording such as "whether in berth or not" (WIBON) or "whether in port or not" (WIPON).

Exceptions and expiry

Charterparties often provide that laytime does not count during specified periods, for example weekends and holidays — expressed by phrases such as SHEX (Sundays and holidays excluded) or SHINC (Sundays and holidays included). Weather working days exclude time when weather prevents cargo work.

If loading or discharge is not completed within the allowed laytime, the vessel is on demurrage and the charterer must pay liquidated damages at the agreed rate for the excess time. Conversely, if cargo operations finish early, the charterparty may reward the charterer with despatch money for time saved.
