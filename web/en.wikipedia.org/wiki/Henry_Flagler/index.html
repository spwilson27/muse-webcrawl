Henry Flagler

Henry Morrison Flagler (January 2, 1830 – May 20, 1913) was an American industrialist and a co-founder of Standard Oil, which he built with John D. Rockefeller and William Rockefeller. In later life he became the leading developer of Florida's Atlantic coast: his Florida East Coast Railway and chain of grand hotels opened St. Augustine, Palm Beach, Miami, and ultimately Key West to tourism and settlement.

Early life and Standard Oil

Born in Hopewell, New York, Flagler left school at 14 and worked in grain and distilling businesses in Ohio before entering the oil trade in Cleveland. In 1870 he joined Rockefeller in founding the Standard Oil Company, serving as a director and as the partnership's principal strategist for transportation and refining agreements.

Florida development

Visiting St. Augustine in the 1880s, Flagler saw the city's potential but found its hotels and transport inadequate. He built the luxurious Hotel Ponce de León (opened 1888) and bought and extended railways down the coast, consolidating them as the Florida East Coast Railway. His Royal Poinciana Hotel and The Breakers made Palm Beach a winter resort; in 1896 his railway reached the small settlement that became Miami, and in 1912 the Overseas Railroad reached Key West — a feat of engineering across open water, destroyed by the 1935 Labor Day hurricane.

Death and legacy

Flagler died in Palm Beach in 1913. His estate, Whitehall, is now the Flagler Museum. He is remembered both as a Gilded Age oil magnate and as the man who effectively created modern tourist Florida.
