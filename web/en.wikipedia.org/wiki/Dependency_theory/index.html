Dependency theory

The core–periphery structure of the world economy

Dependency theory is a body of social science theory, emerging principally from Latin America in the 1960s and 1970s, which holds that resources flow from a "periphery" of poor and underdeveloped states to a "core" of wealthy states, enriching the latter at the expense of the former. It argues that the underdevelopment of poor countries is not an original condition or a stage on the way to development, but a product of their integration into the world capitalist system on unequal terms.

Origins

The theory grew out of the work of the United Nations Economic Commission for Latin America (ECLA) under Raúl Prebisch, whose "Prebisch–Singer thesis" argued that the terms of trade for primary-commodity exporters deteriorate over time relative to manufactured goods. Thinkers such as André Gunder Frank, Fernando Henrique Cardoso, Enzo Faletto, Theotonio Dos Santos and Samir Amin developed the approach, with Frank's notion of the "development of underdevelopment" its best-known slogan. Dependency theory was closely related to Immanuel Wallerstein's world-systems theory.

Main claims

  • The global economy is structured into core, semi-periphery and periphery.
  • Surplus is transferred from periphery to core through trade, profit repatriation and debt.
  • Local elites in the periphery ("comprador" classes) benefit from and sustain dependent relationships.
  • Development within dependency requires delinking, import-substitution industrialisation, or systemic change, depending on the variant.

Criticism and influence

Critics argued that dependency theory understated the possibility of development within capitalism, pointing to the rapid industrialisation of East Asian economies, and that it neglected internal causes of underdevelopment such as domestic policy and institutions. Nevertheless, the theory profoundly influenced development studies, Latin American politics and critiques of globalisation, and its vocabulary of core and periphery remains in wide use.
