Cardiac arrest

DefinitionSudden cessation of effective cardiac mechanical activity
Key actionsImmediate CPR and defibrillation (for shockable rhythms)
Contrasts withHeart attack (myocardial infarction) — a circulation problem that may cause arrest

Overview

Cardiac arrest is the sudden loss of effective heart function, breathing and consciousness, caused by an electrical malfunction of the heart (arrhythmias such as ventricular fibrillation or pulseless ventricular tachycardia, or by asystole/pulseless electrical activity) or by non-cardiac causes such as hypoxia, trauma or overdose. Without treatment it causes death within minutes.

Survival chain

Survival depends on the "chain of survival": early recognition and emergency call, immediate high-quality cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), rapid defibrillation — each minute of delay reduces survival by roughly 7–10% without CPR — and advanced life support and post-resuscitation care. Public-access defibrillators (AEDs) and community CPR training are major public-health measures. Out-of-hospital arrest survival to discharge is typically around 10% in many systems, higher for witnessed, shockable arrests with bystander CPR.

Causes

In adults the commonest cause is coronary artery disease; in children, respiratory failure predominates. Reversible causes are taught as the "4 Hs and 4 Ts" (hypoxia, hypovolaemia, hypo/hyperkalaemia and metabolic causes, hypothermia; tamponade, toxins, tension pneumothorax, thrombosis).
