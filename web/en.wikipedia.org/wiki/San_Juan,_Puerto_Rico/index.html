San Juan (Spanish: [saŋ ˈxwan], "Saint John") is the capital city and most populous municipality in the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, an unincorporated territory of the United States. As of the 2020 census, it is the 57th-largest city under the jurisdiction of the United States, with a population of 342,259. San Juan was founded by Spanish colonists in 1521, who called it Ciudad de Puerto Rico ("City of the Rich Port").

Puerto Rico's capital is the second oldest European-established capital city in the Americas, after Santo Domingo in the Dominican Republic, founded in 1496. Old San Juan, the historic colonial district on a small island at the entrance to the bay, is noted for its blue cobblestone streets, colonial architecture, and massive fortifications — Castillo San Felipe del Morro and Castillo San Cristóbal — built by Spain to protect the harbour, now part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The settlement was originally founded at Caparra in 1508 by Juan Ponce de León and moved to its present site on the islet in 1521. The names of the island and city were eventually swapped: the port (Puerto Rico) gave its name to the whole island, while the city took the name of Saint John the Baptist. San Juan withstood attacks by the English (including Sir Francis Drake in 1595) and the Dutch (1625), and its forts guarded the treasure fleets' route to Spain.

During the Spanish–American War of 1898, United States forces bombarded and occupied the city, and Puerto Rico was ceded to the United States under the Treaty of Paris. In the twentieth century the city expanded far beyond its walls into districts such as Santurce, Hato Rey (the financial district, the "Golden Mile"), and Río Piedras, home to the University of Puerto Rico. San Juan's port is among the busiest cruise-ship and cargo ports in the Caribbean, and Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport serves as the island's gateway.

Geography and climate

San Juan lies on the island's northern Atlantic coast. It has a tropical monsoon climate, with warm temperatures year-round averaging about 27 °C (81 °F), a wetter season from May to December, and exposure to Atlantic hurricanes — most devastatingly Hurricane Maria in September 2017, which caused catastrophic damage across Puerto Rico.

Culture and landmarks

The city hosts the Fiestas de la Calle San Sebastián in January, one of the Caribbean's largest festivals. Landmarks include La Fortaleza (the governor's residence, another World Heritage component), San Juan Cathedral, the Puerto Rico Museum of Contemporary Art, and the oceanfront resorts of Condado and Isla Verde.
