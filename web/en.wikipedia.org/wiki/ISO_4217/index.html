ISO 4217

TitleCodes for the representation of currencies
First published1978
PublisherISO
Maintenance agencyBSI (secretariat)

ISO 4217 is a standard published by the International Organization for Standardization that defines three-letter alphabetic codes and three-digit numeric codes for the representation of currencies, together with the number of minor unit digits for each currency.

Alphabetic codes

The alphabetic code is based on ISO 3166 country codes: the first two letters identify the country or territory and the third usually is the initial of the currency name — for example USD (United States dollar), EUR (euro), JPY (Japanese yen) and GBP (pound sterling). Codes for precious metals use the prefix X, as in XAU for gold and XAG for silver.

Use

ISO 4217 codes are used worldwide in banking, aviation ticketing, foreign exchange, and financial messaging such as SWIFT, to avoid ambiguity between currencies sharing names like "dollar" or "franc".
