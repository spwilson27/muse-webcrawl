The GSMA is a global trade association representing the interests of mobile network operators and the wider mobile ecosystem. Its members include more than 750 mobile operators and some 400 companies in the broader industry, including handset makers, software companies and equipment providers. The organisation unites the industry behind common initiatives, promotes mobile technology to governments and regulators, and runs the world's largest mobile-industry exhibitions.

History

The GSMA traces its origins to the 1982 Groupe Spécial Mobile, formed in Europe to develop a common digital mobile standard — the work that produced GSM — and to the 1987 memorandum of understanding signed by operators committed to deploying it. The GSM Association was formally established in 1995 as the technology spread worldwide, and later shortened its brand to GSMA as its remit expanded beyond GSM to 3G, 4G, 5G and industry-wide issues such as mobile money, identity and the Internet of Things.

Activities

The GSMA organises MWC Barcelona (Mobile World Congress), the industry's flagship annual event, along with regional editions in Shanghai, Los Angeles and Kigali. It runs working groups where operators agree on technical and commercial frameworks, such as roaming arrangements and the specifications behind eSIM. It administers the allocation of Type Allocation Codes used in IMEI numbers for mobile devices, maintains device databases used to block stolen phones, and publishes research including the annual State of the Industry Report on Mobile Money and its Mobile Connectivity Index. The GSMA also advocates for spectrum policy and supports the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals through initiatives such as Mobile for Development.

Governance

The association is led by a director general and governed by a board of senior executives drawn from member operator groups around the world. Its head office is in London, with regional offices on every continent.
