Intense pulsed light

Intense pulsed light (IPL) is a technology used in cosmetic and medical treatments in which a handheld device delivers high-intensity pulses of broad-spectrum visible light. Unlike a laser, which emits a single wavelength, IPL devices emit light across a range of wavelengths, typically filtered to roughly 500–1200 nm.

Uses

IPL is used for hair removal, treatment of sun-damaged and aging skin ("photorejuvenation"), acne, rosacea, and vascular and pigmented lesions such as age spots and broken capillaries. The light is absorbed by chromophores such as melanin and hemoglobin, heating and damaging the target structures.

Procedure and risks

Treatments are performed in a clinic or salon, with cooling gel and eye protection; several sessions are usually required. Possible side effects include temporary redness, blistering, burns, and changes in skin pigmentation, and the treatment is less suitable for darker skin tones because of the risk of pigment changes.

History

IPL systems were developed in the 1990s as a versatile alternative to single-purpose lasers, and home-use devices have since become widely available.
