The Kra–Dai languages (also called Tai–Kadai) are a language family spoken in Southeast Asia and southern China, including Thai, Lao and the Zhuang languages. Most are tonal languages with analytic grammar.

Branches

The family is usually divided into the Kra branch (in Guizhou and northern Vietnam), the Kam–Sui branch, the large Tai branch (including Thai, Lao and Shan), and the smaller Hlai and Ong Be branches of Hainan island.

History

Scholars generally locate the Kra–Dai homeland in southern China, possibly the Guangxi–Guizhou region, from which Tai-speaking peoples migrated southwestward into mainland Southeast Asia during the first and early second millennia AD. Proposed wider relationships, such as a link with Austronesian, remain debated.
