Siebengebirge

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siebengebirge (German for "Seven Hills") is a range of low volcanic hills on the east bank of the Rhine in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, in the area of Bonn, Königswinter and Bad Honnef. Despite its name, the range comprises more than forty hills and elevations. It is one of Germany's oldest nature reserves and forms the core of the Siebengebirge nature park.

Geography and geology

The hills are the eroded remnants of Oligocene and Miocene volcanism, part of the volcanic belt associated with the Eifel. The highest peak is the Großer Ölberg at 460.7 metres. Other prominent summits include the Drachenfels (321 m), the Petersberg (331 m), the Löwenburg (455 m) and the Lohrberg. The Drachenfels, topped by a ruined medieval castle, is one of the most visited mountains in Germany and was famously celebrated in Lord Byron's poetry.

History and protection

The range was quarried for trachyte and basalt from Roman times onward; stone from the Drachenfels was used in the construction of Cologne Cathedral. Concern over quarrying led to early conservation efforts, and the Siebengebirge was placed under protection in the 1920s, making it one of the oldest protected areas in Germany. In 1958 it became the centre of one of the first nature parks in North Rhine-Westphalia.
