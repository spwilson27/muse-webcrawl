Stanley, the robot vehicle that won the 2005 DARPA Grand Challenge.

Stanley was an autonomous car created by the Stanford Racing Team in cooperation with the Volkswagen Electronics Research Laboratory. It competed in, and won, the 2005 DARPA Grand Challenge, earning the Stanford Racing Team the $2 million prize.

The vehicle

Stanley was built from a stock Volkswagen Touareg SUV modified for computer control of steering, throttle, and braking. Its sensor suite included five roof-mounted lidar units, a colour camera for long-range vision, GPS receivers, an inertial measurement unit, and wheel encoders. Onboard computing consisted of six Pentium M-based computers running Linux.

Software

The software, written largely in C and C++, fused lidar and camera data to build a map of drivable terrain ahead. A key innovation was the use of machine learning: the system learned from recorded sensor data of a human driver crossing similar terrain, allowing it to classify distant terrain as drivable or non-drivable from the camera image. Probabilistic terrain analysis and a hierarchical control architecture allowed Stanley to travel at speed while avoiding obstacles.

The 2005 Grand Challenge

On 8 October 2005, Stanley completed the 131.2-mile Mojave Desert course in 6 hours, 53 minutes and 58 seconds, averaging about 19 mph, and was the first of five vehicles to finish. The team was led by Sebastian Thrun, then director of the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory. Stanley's success is widely regarded as a landmark in the development of self-driving cars; Thrun later led Google's autonomous car project.

Legacy

Stanley is now on display at the Smithsonian National Museum of American History. The Stanford team's successor vehicle, Junior, placed second in the 2007 DARPA Urban Challenge.
