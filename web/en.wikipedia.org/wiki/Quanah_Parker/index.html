Quanah Parker

Quanah Parker

Born: c. 1845
Died: 23 February 1911
Nationality: Comanche
Known for: Last principal chief of the Comanche

Quanah Parker (c. 1845 – February 23, 1911) was a war leader of the Kwahadi band of the Comanche Nation and the last principal chief of the Comanche. He was the son of the Comanche chief Peta Nocona and Cynthia Ann Parker, a white woman captured by the Comanche as a child.

Early life

Quanah was born into the Nokoni band around 1845. His mother, Cynthia Ann Parker, had been taken in a raid on Fort Parker, Texas, in 1836 and raised as a Comanche. After Texas Rangers recaptured her in 1860 at the Battle of Pease River, where Peta Nocona was killed, Quanah and his brother fled and eventually joined the Kwahadi ("Antelopes") band.

Resistance and surrender

During the Red River War of 1874–75, Quanah led attacks on buffalo hunters, including the second Battle of Adobe Walls in June 1874. After relentless pursuit by the U.S. Army under Ranald S. Mackenzie, Quanah surrendered at Fort Sill in June 1875, the last free band of Comanches to do so.

Later life

On the reservation in Indian Territory, Quanah adapted to the new order, becoming a successful rancher and businessman, building the Star House near Cache, Oklahoma. He advocateded education for Comanche children, served as a judge on the Court of Indian Offenses, and was a founder of the Native American Church, promoting the peyote religion. He died in 1911 and was buried beside his mother and sister at Fort Sill.
