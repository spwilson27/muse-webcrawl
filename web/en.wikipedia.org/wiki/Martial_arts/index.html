Martial arts are codified systems and traditions of combat practiced for a number of reasons such as self-defence; military and law enforcement applications; competition; physical, mental, and spiritual development; entertainment; and the preservation of a nation's intangible cultural heritage.

The term is ultimately derived from Latin, "arts of Mars", the Roman god of war, and was originally used for European fighting systems before being applied worldwide.

Classification

Martial arts may be categorized by technique (striking arts such as karate, taekwondo, boxing, and Muay Thai; grappling arts such as judo, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, and wrestling; weapon-based arts such as kendo, fencing, and escrima), by origin (Chinese kung fu, Japanese budo, Korean arts), or by intent (traditional arts emphasizing form and philosophy versus combat sports and mixed martial arts).

History

Combat systems are ancient: wrestling and boxing appear in Egyptian and Greek art, and formalized Asian traditions developed alongside trade and religious exchange along the Silk Road. Shaolin kung fu in China, the samurai arts of Japan, and Indian kalaripayattu are among the oldest continuously taught systems. The 20th century saw global dissemination, Olympic inclusion for judo (1964) and taekwondo (2000), and the rise of mixed martial arts competition in the 1990s.

Training and philosophy

Training typically combines forms (kata), sparring, conditioning, and etiquette. Many traditions incorporate ethical codes—such as the samurai's bushido or Confucian and Buddhist influences in East Asian arts—and are practiced as much for character development as for fighting skill.
