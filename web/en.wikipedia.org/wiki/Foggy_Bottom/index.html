Foggy Bottom

Foggy Bottom is a neighbourhood of Washington, D.C., west of the White House and north of the National Mall. It is home to the US Department of State — “Foggy Bottom” is a common metonym for the State Department — as well as George Washington University, the Kennedy Center and the Watergate complex.

Foggy Bottom
City: Washington, D.C.
Known for: State Department, GWU, Kennedy Center, Watergate
Name origin: Fog over the Potomac lowlands

History

The low-lying riverside area, prone to fog and swamp gas from nearby industry, was a 19th-century working-class district of breweries, gasworks and glass plants, home to Irish and German immigrants and African American communities. In the 20th century federal buildings and the university displaced much housing; the historic district preserves remaining rowhouses.

Landmarks

Besides the Harry S. Truman Building (State Department headquarters), the neighbourhood hosts the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, Constitution Hall, the headquarters of the World Bank and IMF nearby, and the notorious Watergate office-apartment complex of 1972 break-in fame.
