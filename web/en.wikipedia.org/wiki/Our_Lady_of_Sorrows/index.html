Our Lady of Sorrows (Latin: Beata Maria Virgo Perdolens), also called Mater Dolorosa ("Sorrowful Mother"), is a title of the Virgin Mary in Roman Catholic devotion, referring to the sorrows she suffered during the life, passion and death of her son Jesus. The devotion centres on the Seven Sorrows (or Dolours) of Mary and is traditionally associated with the Servite Order.

The Seven Sorrows

Catholic tradition meditates on seven events of grief in Mary's life: (1) the prophecy of Simeon at the Presentation; (2) the flight into Egypt; (3) the loss of the child Jesus in the Temple; (4) meeting Jesus on the way to Calvary; (5) standing at the foot of the Cross during the Crucifixion; (6) receiving the body of Jesus taken down from the Cross (the scene of the Pietà); and (7) the burial of Jesus. Devotions include the Rosary of the Seven Sorrows and the hymn Stabat Mater.

History and iconography

The devotion grew in the late Middle Ages, was spread especially by the Servites from the 13th century, and received a universal feast—now celebrated on 15 September, the day after the Exaltation of the Holy Cross. In art, Mary as Our Lady of Sorrows is often shown weeping, dressed in dark blue or black, with one or seven swords piercing her heart, an image rooted in Simeon's prophecy that "a sword will pierce your own soul too" (Luke 2:35). The title is attached to numerous shrines and to apparitions such as Our Lady of Kibeho in Rwanda.
