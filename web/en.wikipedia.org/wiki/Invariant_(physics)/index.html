Invariant (physics)

A quantity that is invariant under a transformation has the same value for all observers.

In theoretical physics, an invariant is an observable of a physical system which does not change under some transformation. Invariance, as a broader term, also applies to the no change of form of physical laws under a transformation, and is closer in scope to the mathematical definition. Invariants of a system are deeply tied to the symmetries imposed by its environment.

For example, under Galilean transformation, Newton's laws of motion are invariant. The speed of light is an invariant under Lorentz transformation, as is the spacetime interval between two events.

Examples

In the theory of special relativity, the proper time experienced along a world line and the rest mass of a particle are Lorentz invariants. In general relativity, the Ricci scalar and other curvature scalars constructed from the Riemann tensor are invariants under coordinate transformations.
