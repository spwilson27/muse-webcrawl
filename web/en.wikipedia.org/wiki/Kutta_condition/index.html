Kutta condition

The Kutta condition is a principle in steady-flow fluid dynamics, especially aerodynamics, applicable when a fluid flows past a body with a sharp corner, such as the trailing edge of an airfoil.

Statement

The condition states that the flow leaves the sharp trailing edge smoothly: the velocity is finite there, and the streamlines depart tangentially from both surfaces without wrapping around the edge. In potential-flow theory this requirement selects the unique value of circulation around the airfoil, fixing the strength of the bound vortex in the Kutta–Joukowski model and thereby determining the lift.

History and justification

The condition is named after the German mathematician Martin Kutta, who stated it in 1902; Nikolai Joukowski independently employed the same idea. Physically, viscosity justifies it: a real flow cannot turn around the infinitesimally sharp edge at infinite velocity, and experiments confirm that the rear stagnation point settles at the trailing edge once the starting vortex has been shed.
