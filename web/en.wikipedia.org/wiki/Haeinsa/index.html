Haeinsa (Korean: 해인사, "Temple of the Seal of the Sea") is a head temple of the Jogye Order of Korean Buddhism, situated on Mount Gaya (Gayasan) in Hapcheon County, South Gyeongsang Province, South Korea. It is one of the Three Jewels Temples of Korea, representing the Dharma (Buddha's teachings), and is world-famous as the repository of the Tripitaka Koreana, the most complete collection of Buddhist scriptures carved onto some 81,350 wooden printing blocks in the thirteenth century.

History

Tradition dates the temple's founding to 802, when the monks Suneung and Ijeong built it under the patronage of Queen Jinseong of Silla. The temple was enlarged under the Goryeo dynasty — the royal courtier Choe Chi-won is associated with its development — and rebuilt several times after fires, most of the present buildings dating from the Joseon period and later renovations.

The Tripitaka Koreana

The woodblocks were carved between 1237 and 1248 during the Mongol invasions of Korea, as an act of devotion intended to invoke the Buddha's protection; an earlier set of blocks had been destroyed by the invaders in 1232. The blocks — each block of birch wood bearing about 320 characters — were moved to Haeinsa in 1398 and have been housed there ever since in the Janggyeong Panjeon, a pair of storage halls engineered with carefully sized windows and raised floors to regulate ventilation, temperature and humidity. The halls and blocks were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1995, and the Tripitaka itself entered UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 2007. During the Korean War, the temple was famously spared bombing after an air force pilot, Kim Young-hwan, refused an order that would have destroyed it.
