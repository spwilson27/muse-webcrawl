Farm Sanctuary

Farm Sanctuary is an American animal protection organisation that provides lifelong care for rescued farmed animals and advocates for changes to laws and policies governing animal agriculture. Founded in 1986 by Gene Baur and Lorri Houston, it is regarded as the first sanctuary in the United States devoted specifically to farmed animals, and has grown into one of the country's leading farm animal advocacy groups.

History

The organisation began with the rescue of Hilda, a sheep found alive on a pile of dead animals at a stockyard in Pennsylvania; her rescue in 1986 is treated as Farm Sanctuary's founding moment. Early funding famously came in part from selling veggie hot dogs at Grateful Dead concerts. The group established sanctuaries in Watkins Glen, New York, and in California (Orland, and later Acton), sheltering hundreds of cattle, pigs, chickens, turkeys, goats and sheep rescued from farms, stockyards and cruelty cases.

Advocacy

Farm Sanctuary campaigns against factory farming practices such as gestation crates, battery cages and veal crates, and has supported ballot measures including California's Proposition 2 (2008) and Proposition 12 (2018). It operates rescue, education and advocacy programmes, runs visitor tours and a "living" vegan education curriculum, and has litigated and lobbied on farm animal welfare issues.
