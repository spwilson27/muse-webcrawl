César Lattes
Born: 11 July 1924, Curitiba, Brazil
Died: 8 March 2005, Campinas, Brazil
Known for: Co-discovery of the pion

Cesare Mansueto Giulio Lattes (11 July 1924 – 8 March 2005), better known as César Lattes, was a Brazilian experimental physicist, co-discoverer of the subatomic particle known as the pion (pi meson).

Work on the pion

Working at the University of Bristol with Giuseppe Occhialini and Cecil Powell in 1947, Lattes helped develop improved photographic emulsions exposed to cosmic rays at high altitude. Tracks in the emulsions gave evidence for the pion and its decay into a muon, a landmark result in particle physics; Powell received the Nobel Prize in 1950 for the photographic method.

Later career

Lattes returned to Brazil, becoming a leading figure in Brazilian physics, a founder of the Brazilian Center for Research in Physics (CBPF) and a long-serving professor at the University of Campinas. The Lattes Platform, Brazil's national curriculum database for researchers, is named after him.
