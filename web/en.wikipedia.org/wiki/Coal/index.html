Coal

Coal
TypeFossil fuel — sedimentary rock of ancient plant matter
GradesAnthracite, bituminous, sub-bituminous, lignite
Historic roleFuel of the Industrial Revolution
[Image: A seam of black coal in a mine wall, glossy and fractured]

Coal is a combustible black or brownish sedimentary rock formed from ancient plant matter compressed over millions of years. It fuelled the Industrial Revolution — the steam engines of factories, railways and ships — and still generates a large share of world electricity.

Decline and impacts

Coal is the most carbon-intensive major fuel, and mining it has scarred landscapes and miners' lungs for centuries; many countries are phasing it out for solar, wind and gas. Coke from coal remains essential to steel making, linking coal to the built world of bridges and towers.
