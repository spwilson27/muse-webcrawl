Urashima Tarō

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Urashima Tarō (浦島 太郎) is the protagonist of a Japanese fairy tale (otogi banashi), who, in a typical modern version, is a fisherman rewarded for rescuing a sea turtle with a visit to the undersea palace of the Dragon King. He stays for what seems a few days, but when he returns to his village he finds that centuries have passed. The tale is one of the best-known Japanese folk stories and an early literary example of time dilation in fiction.

The tale

Urashima saves a turtle from tormenting children (or catches and releases it), and the turtle returns to carry him beneath the sea to Ryūgū-jō, the Dragon Palace, where the princess Otohime entertains him. When homesickness drives him back, she gives him a jewelled box, the tamatebako, warning him never to open it. On shore he finds his home gone and everyone he knew long dead. In despair he opens the box; white smoke escapes and he ages instantly — the box contained his lost years. Early versions of the legend appear in 8th-century texts such as the Fudoki and the Man'yōshū.

Legacy

The story is retold in countless children's books in Japan, is the namesake of places and statues along the coast, and its name is used in Japanese science writing as an analogy (the "Urashima effect") for relativistic time dilation, similar to the twin paradox.
