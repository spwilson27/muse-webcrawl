The World Customs Organization (WCO) is an intergovernmental organisation headquartered in Brussels, Belgium, that works to improve the effectiveness and efficiency of customs administrations worldwide. Founded as the Customs Co-operation Council (CCC), it adopted the working name World Customs Organization in 1994. Its 186 members process about 98% of world trade.

Instruments

The WCO develops and maintains international standards for customs procedures, most importantly:

  • The Harmonized System (HS) — the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System, used by more than 200 economies as the basis for customs tariffs and trade statistics, classifying goods with six-digit codes.
  • The Revised Kyoto Convention — the blueprint for modern, simplified customs procedures.
  • The SAFE Framework of Standards (2005) — to secure and facilitate global trade, including the Authorized Economic Operator (AEO) concept.
  • The WCO Data Model and instruments on valuation (implementing the WTO Customs Valuation Agreement), rules of origin, and enforcement against counterfeiting and smuggling.

Activities

The organisation runs capacity-building programmes for developing-country customs services, publishes tariff classification opinions and valuation rulings, and coordinates enforcement operations with Interpol and the UN Office on Drugs and Crime. International Customs Day is celebrated each year on 26 January, the anniversary of the CCC's first session in 1953.

Membership and governance

The WCO Council, composed of the heads of member customs administrations, meets annually. The Secretariat in Brussels is led by a Secretary General elected for a five-year term; long-serving secretary general Kunio Mikuriya (2009–2023) was succeeded by Ian Saunders of the United States in 2024.
