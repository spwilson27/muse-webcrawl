The Peace of Thorn (Polish: Pokój toruński) was a peace treaty concluded on 1 February 1411 at Thorn (Toruń) between the Kingdom of Poland and the Grand Duchy of Lithuania on one side and the Teutonic Order on the other, ending the Polish–Lithuanian–Teutonic War that had culminated in the Order's defeat at Grunwald.

Terms

Given the Order's successful defence of Marienburg under Heinrich von Plauen, the terms were moderate. The Order returned the Dobrzyń Land to Poland, ceded Samogitia to Lithuania for the lifetimes of Jagiełło and Vytautas, gave up claims to certain border castles, and agreed to pay an enormous indemnity of 100,000 kopas of Prague groschen — roughly ten times the annual revenue of a great kingdom — in four instalments, to free prisoners and redeem pledged territory.

Consequences

To raise the indemnity the Order imposed heavy taxes on its Prussian towns and estates, provoking the discontent that produced the Prussian Confederation in 1440 and, in 1454, the Thirteen Years' War, which ended with the Second Peace of Thorn (1466) and the loss of western Prussia. The financial strain and the destruction of its military elite at Grunwald — including Grand Master Ulrich von Jungingen — mark 1411 as the turning point in the Order's decline.
