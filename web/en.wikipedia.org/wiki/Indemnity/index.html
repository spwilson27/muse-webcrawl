Indemnity

Indemnity is a contractual obligation of one party (the indemnitor) to compensate the loss incurred by another party (the indemnitee) due to the acts of the indemnitor or any other party. The duty to indemnify is usually, but not always, coextensive with the contractual duty to "hold harmless" or "save harmless". In contrast, a "guarantee" is an obligation of one party to assure another party that a third party will perform its obligation, while an indemnity is a primary obligation.

Common law

At common law, indemnity rights may arise in several ways: by express contract, by implied contract, and by operation of law where one party is compelled to pay damages for which another is primarily responsible. A classic example is the right of an agent to be indemnified by a principal for liabilities incurred while carrying out the principal's lawful instructions.

Insurance

The principle of indemnity underlies most insurance contracts: the purpose of insurance is to restore the insured to the financial position they occupied before the loss, and no better. This principle gives rise to the doctrines of subrogation, contribution, and average, and it is why the insured must generally have an insurable interest in the subject matter. Life insurance is a notable exception, since the value of a human life cannot be restored and such policies are instead "benefit" policies that pay a stated sum.

Indemnity clauses in contracts

Indemnity clauses are widely used in commercial agreements to allocate risk between parties — for example, a contractor may indemnify a client against claims arising from the contractor's negligence. Because such clauses can shift liability extensively, many jurisdictions regulate them; in the United States, several states limit indemnification for one's own negligence in construction contracts, and under English law the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 restricts some indemnity clauses in consumer and standard-form contracts.

War indemnities and reparations

Historically, indemnities were also payments exacted from a defeated state after a war, such as the five billion francs France paid Germany under the Treaty of Frankfurt (1871) ending the Franco-Prussian War, and the reparations imposed on Germany after the First World War by the Treaty of Versailles.
