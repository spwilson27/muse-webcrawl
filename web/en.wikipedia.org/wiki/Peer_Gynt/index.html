Peer Gynt is a five-act play in verse by the Norwegian dramatist Henrik Ibsen, published in 1867 and first performed in 1876. One of the most famous works of Scandinavian literature, it follows the lifelong wanderings of its boastful, evasive hero, who avoids commitment and responsibility until, in old age, he returns home to the faithful Solveig. Ibsen drew on Norwegian fairy tales collected by Asbjørnsen and Moe, in which a character named Peer Gynt appears.

Plot

Peer, the son of the formerly rich Jon Gynt, who squandered the family fortune, tries to restore his standing by abducting the bride Ingrid at her wedding, then abandons her and flees to the mountains, where he encounters the trolls of the Dovre-Master and their motto "To thyself be enough". He builds a hut where Solveig comes to live with him, but leaves when reminded of his past. After decades of adventures abroad — as a slave trader, a prophet in Morocco, and a madman proclaimed emperor in a Cairo asylum — the ageing Peer returns to Norway, confronts the Button Moulder who proposes to melt him down for never having been himself, and is finally redeemed by Solveig's love.

Grieg's music

For the 1876 premiere, Ibsen commissioned incidental music from Edvard Grieg. Grieg later extracted two orchestral suites from the score; movements such as "Morning Mood", "In the Hall of the Mountain King" and "Solveig's Song" are among the most widely recognised pieces of classical music. The complete incidental music is now also performed and recorded regularly.
