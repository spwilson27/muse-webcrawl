The Upanishads are late Vedic Sanskrit texts of religious teaching and philosophical speculation, forming the concluding part of the Vedas ('Vedanta' = 'end of the Veda') and the foundation of Hinduism's philosophy. In dialogues between teachers and students they teach that Atman, the inmost self, is identical with Brahman, the ground of all reality — 'That thou art' (tat tvam asi) — and that realising this identity liberates one from rebirth.

Influence

Of more than 200 Upanishads, about a dozen 'principal' ones are earliest and most authoritative, commented on by Shankara and every Vedanta school. Arthur Schopenhauer read a Latin translation nightly and called them 'the consolation of my life and of my death'; they influenced modern figures from Yeats to Schrödinger, and remain Hinduism's most philosophical layer, shifting religion from ritual sacrifice toward interior knowledge.
