Banking in Switzerland

Major centresZurich, Geneva, Basel, Lugano
RegulatorFINMA; Swiss National Bank (central bank)
StrengthCross-border private wealth management (world leader)

Banking in Switzerland is among the most developed and significant in the world. The Swiss financial sector, centred on Zurich and Geneva, is famed for political stability, the strong Swiss franc, and private banking and wealth management; Switzerland manages roughly a quarter of global cross-border private wealth, more than any other country.

Structure

The system includes the two large international banks—UBS and, until its 2023 collapse and takeover by UBS, Credit Suisse—a tier of cantonal banks owned by the cantons, Raiffeisen cooperative banks, regional banks, and hundreds of private bankers and foreign-controlled institutions. The Swiss National Bank acts as central bank, and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) supervises the sector.

Banking secrecy and its erosion

Swiss bank secrecy was codified in the Banking Law of 1934, which made it a criminal offence for bankers to disclose client information; combined with neutrality and stability, it drew large foreign deposits, alongside longstanding criticism over tax evasion and hidden assets. Since the 2008 financial crisis and the UBS tax scandal in the United States, Switzerland has adopted automatic exchange of tax information (from 2017–2018), effectively ending absolute secrecy for foreign clients, while domestic privacy protections remain comparatively strong.

Recent developments

The emergency takeover of Credit Suisse by UBS in March 2023, engineered by Swiss authorities to avert a banking crisis, reshaped the sector, leaving UBS as the dominant institution and prompting debate about concentration risk in a bank whose balance sheet is large relative to Swiss GDP.
