Godzilla is a 2014 American monster film directed by Gareth Edwards. It is a reboot of Toho's Godzilla franchise and the first film in Legendary Pictures' MonsterVerse. The film stars Aaron Taylor-Johnson, Ken Watanabe, Elizabeth Olsen, Juliette Binoche, Sally Hawkins, David Strathairn and Bryan Cranston, and depicts Godzilla battling parasitic creatures called MUTOs (Massive Unidentified Terrestrial Organisms).

Plot

In 1999, scientists Ishiro Serizawa and Vivienne Graham investigate a colossal skeleton and egg-like pods in the Philippines; one pod hatches and a creature escapes. The same year, at the Janjira nuclear plant in Japan, a seismic anomaly causes a reactor collapse that kills engineer Sandra Brody; her husband Joe becomes obsessed with proving the accident was no earthquake. Fifteen years later, Joe's son Ford, a US Navy bomb-disposal officer, bailes Joe out in Japan and accompanies him into the quarantine zone, where a winged MUTO emerges and escapes. The creature attacks Hawaii, a second, female MUTO awakens in Nevada, and Godzilla — an ancient apex predator studied by the secret organisation Monarch — emerges to hunt them, culminating in a battle in San Francisco in which Godzilla kills both MUTOs before returning to the sea.

Production and release

Legendary acquired the rights in 2010 and Edwards, fresh from Monsters, was hired in 2011. Principal photography took place in 2013 in Canada (British Columbia) and Hawaii. The film premiered in Los Angeles on May 8, 2014 and was released on May 16, grossing $529 million worldwide against a $160 million budget. Reviews were generally positive, praising the direction, visual effects and sound design while criticising the limited screen time of Godzilla and the human characters.

Sequels

It was followed by Godzilla: King of the Monsters (2019) and Godzilla vs. Kong (2021), within the shared MonsterVerse that also includes Kong: Skull Island (2017).
