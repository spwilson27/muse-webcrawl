The Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources (CCAMLR) is an international commission responsible for the conservation of Antarctic marine ecosystems. It was established by the Convention on the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources, which was signed in Canberra in 1980 and entered into force in 1982. The commission is based in Hobart, Tasmania, Australia.

Convention

The Convention applies to the marine living resources of the area south of 60° South latitude and to the resources of the area between that latitude and the Antarctic Convergence. It was negotiated out of concern that an increase in krill catches in the Southern Ocean could seriously affect populations of krill-dependent animals such as whales, seals, penguins and seabirds.

Approach

CCAMLR pioneered an ecosystem approach to conservation: harvesting is permitted only where it can be conducted sustainably, taking account of effects on dependent and related species, and the commission applies a precautionary approach in the face of uncertain scientific information. Its work covers fisheries for Antarctic krill, Patagonian toothfish (marketed as Chilean sea bass), Antarctic toothfish, and icefish.

Marine protected areas

In 2009 CCAMLR established the South Orkney Islands southern shelf marine protected area, the world's first high-seas MPA. In 2016 it agreed the Ross Sea region marine protected area, at the time the largest MPA in the world, covering about 1.55 million km² and entering into force in December 2017. Proposals for further MPAs in East Antarctica, the Weddell Sea and the Antarctic Peninsula region have been debated by members for a number of years.

Illegal fishing

CCAMLR operates a Catch Documentation Scheme for toothfish and maintains lists of vessels engaged in illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing in the Convention Area.
