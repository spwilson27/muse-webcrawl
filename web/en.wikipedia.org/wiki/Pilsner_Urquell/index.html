Pilsner Urquell (Czech: Plzeňský Prazdroj) is a pale lager brewed since 1842 in Plzeň, Bohemia (now the Czech Republic). It was the world's first pale lager and the prototype of the pilsner style of beer, which today accounts for the majority of beer produced worldwide. "Urquell" is German for "original source".

History

In 1838 the citizens of Plzeň, dissatisfied with the quality of the local top-fermented beer, publicly dumped barrels of it in the town square and founded a new Citizens' Brewery. They hired Bavarian brewer Josef Groll, who combined new techniques — pale malts dried with indirect heat, soft Plzeň water, Saaz hops, and Bavarian-style lagering in cool cellars — to produce the first clear, golden lager, first served on 5 October 1842. Its clarity, made possible by cheap glassware replacing stoneware, made it a sensation.

The brewing process

Pilsner Urquell is still brewed in Plzeň using a triple-decoction mash, direct-fire kettles, and open fermentation, and is lagered in the brewery's vast sandstone cellars, where a small parallel batch is still matured in oak barrels for comparison. The brewery passed from the citizens to various owners and is today owned by Asahi Group Holdings of Japan.

Influence

The pale lager style it created spread around the world and inspired countless imitations bearing the name "pilsner", "pilsener", or "pils". The original became a protected symbol of Czech brewing tradition.
