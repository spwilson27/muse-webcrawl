Édouard Dujardin (10 November 1861 – 31 October 1949) was a French novelist, playwright, poet and editor, associated with the Symbolist movement. He is best remembered for his novella Les Lauriers sont coupés (We'll to the Woods No More, 1888), widely credited as the first sustained use of the interior monologue — the stream of consciousness technique — in fiction.

Édouard Dujardin, pioneer of the stream-of-consciousness novel.

Life and work

Born near Blois, Dujardin studied music at the Paris Conservatoire and became an ardent Wagnerian, founding the Revue wagnérienne in 1885, an influential journal of the Symbolist circle that also published writers such as Stéphane Mallarmé. He wrote verse plays and prose poems before publishing Les Lauriers sont coupés in serial form in 1887 and in book form in 1888.

Influence

The novella follows six hours in the mind of a young Parisian, Daniel Prince, rendered almost entirely through his unspoken thoughts. The Irish writer James Joyce acknowledged the book as an influence on the interior monologues of Ulysses, and the critic Valery Larbaud championed its rediscovery in the 1920s. In 1931 Dujardin published a theoretical account of the method, Le Monologue intérieur, defining it as the direct introduction of the reader into a character's inner life without authorial intervention.
