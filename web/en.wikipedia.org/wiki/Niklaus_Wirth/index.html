Niklaus Wirth

Niklaus Emil Wirth (15 February 1934 – 1 January 2024) was a Swiss computer scientist who designed several influential programming languages, most notably Pascal, and pioneered concepts in software engineering and structured programming. He received the Turing Award in 1984.

Work

Wirth studied at ETH Zurich and the University of California, Berkeley, where he received his PhD in 1963. He taught at Stanford and Zurich before becoming professor of informatics at ETH Zurich in 1968. His first languages included Euler and PL360; with C. A. R. Hoare he developed ALGOL W.

Pascal, released in 1970, was designed for teaching structured programming and became one of the most influential languages ever created, dominating computer-science education and early personal-computer development. Wirth followed it with Modula and Modula-2, emphasizing modularity, and later the Oberon language and operating system, developed with Jürg Gutknecht. His textbook Algorithms + Data Structures = Programs (1976) is a classic of the field.

He is also associated with “Wirth's law”: the observation that software is getting slower more rapidly than hardware is becoming faster. Wirth consistently advocated simplicity, elegance, and efficiency in system design.
