Temporary Assistance for Needy Families

Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) is a United States federal assistance program created by the Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act of 1996, signed by President Bill Clinton. It replaced Aid to Families with Dependent Children (AFDC) and provides block grants to states to help families with children achieve self-sufficiency.

Provisions

TANF funds may be used for cash assistance, work activities, child care, and other supports. Recipients are generally required to work after two years of assistance, and federal law limits lifetime receipt of federally funded assistance to five years (60 months), though states may set shorter limits or continue support with state funds. States must meet work-participation rates for recipient families.

Criticism and effects

Caseloads fell sharply after 1996. Supporters credit TANF with promoting employment; critics argue the fixed block grant has eroded in real value, that assistance reaches a shrinking share of poor families, and that benefits are often too low to lift families out of poverty.
