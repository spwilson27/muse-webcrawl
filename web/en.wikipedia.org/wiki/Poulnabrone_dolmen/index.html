Poulnabrone dolmen (Irish: Poll na Brón) is a large portal tomb in the Burren, County Clare, Ireland. Situated on a high karst limestone plateau near the R480 road south of Ballyvaughan, it is one of Ireland's most iconic and most photographed megalithic monuments, and among the oldest dated megalithic tombs in the country.

Description

The tomb consists of a huge, thin capstone — roughly 3.5 metres long — supported about 1.8 metres above the ground on slender upright portal stones, forming a chamber open at one end. The monument stands within the remains of a low oval cairn. Portal tombs of this kind are found mainly in the northern half of Ireland, and Poulnabrone is an unusually southern and unusually well-preserved example.

Excavation

Excavations directed by Ann Lynch in 1986 and 1988, undertaken when the capstone required conservation, recovered the disarticulated remains of some 33 individuals — men, women and children — buried in the chamber during the Neolithic, along with grave goods including a polished stone axe, pottery, a bone pendant, and flint and chert arrowheads and scrapers. Only one adult appears to have lived beyond about 40 years, and the bones showed signs of hard physical lives. A later, Bronze Age burial of an infant was found in the portico outside the chamber. Radiocarbon dates place the burials broadly between about 3800 and 3200 BC.

Name

The name Poll na Brón is usually translated "hole of the quern stone" (bró meaning a quern), though it is popularly rendered "hole of the sorrows".
