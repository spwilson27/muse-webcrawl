Jim Thorpe

James Francis Thorpe (May 22 or 28, 1887 – March 28, 1953), a citizen of the Sac and Fox Nation, was an American athlete and Olympic gold medallist. He won the pentathlon and decathlon at the 1912 Stockholm Olympics, played professional American football, baseball and basketball, and was proclaimed the greatest athlete of the first half of the 20th century in an Associated Press poll of sportswriters.

Early life and the Olympics

Thorpe was born in Indian Territory (present-day Oklahoma) and educated at the Carlisle Indian Industrial School in Pennsylvania, where he was coached by the pioneering football coach Glenn "Pop" Warner. At Stockholm in 1912 he dominated both multi-event competitions, winning the pentathlon and setting a decathlon record that stood for nearly two decades. King Gustav V of Sweden is famously said to have told him, "You, sir, are the greatest athlete in the world."

Stripped and restored

In 1913 it emerged that Thorpe had played semi-professional summer baseball in North Carolina in 1909–10, breaching the era's strict amateurism rules. The International Olympic Committee stripped his titles and medals — a decision widely seen as harsh and tainted by the prejudices of the time. Thorpe went on to play Major League Baseball for the New York Giants and other clubs, and was a leading early star and the first president of the professional football league that became the NFL, starring for the Canton Bulldogs. Replicas of his medals were presented to his family in 1983; in 2022 the IOC formally reinstated Thorpe as the sole champion of both 1912 events.
