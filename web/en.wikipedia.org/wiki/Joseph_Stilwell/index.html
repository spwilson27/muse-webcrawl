Joseph Warren Stilwell (19 March 1883 – 12 October 1946) was a United States Army general who served in the China Burma India Theater during the Second World War. Nicknamed "Vinegar Joe" for his caustic wit and blunt manner, he was chief of staff to Chiang Kai-shek, commander of American forces in the theatre, and deputy commander in South East Asia, while also supervising Lend-Lease aid to China.

Early career

Stilwell graduated from West Point in 1904 and served in the Philippines, taught at West Point and the Infantry School, and was a staff officer in France in the First World War. Three tours in China made him one of the US Army's foremost China hands; he spoke fluent Chinese and served as military attaché in Beijing from 1935 to 1939, reporting on the Japanese invasion.

Burma and China

After Pearl Harbor, Stilwell was sent to lead Chinese forces in Burma, where the Japanese conquest of 1942 forced him to walk out to India with a party of 114 — declaring famously, "I claim we got a hell of a beating. We got run out of Burma and it is humiliating as hell." He rebuilt Chinese divisions in India, drove the building of the Ledo Road to reopen a land supply route to China, and in 1944 his Northern Combat Area Command captured Myitkyina. His relations with Chiang Kai-shek deteriorated badly over strategy and the control of Lend-Lease; in the "Stilwell incident" of autumn 1944 Chiang demanded his recall, and President Roosevelt complied in October 1944.

Later service and death

Stilwell commanded the Tenth Army briefly after the Okinawa campaign and then the Sixth Army in the United States. He died of stomach cancer in San Francisco in October 1946. His diaries, published posthumously, remain a classic source on the China-Burma-India theatre.
