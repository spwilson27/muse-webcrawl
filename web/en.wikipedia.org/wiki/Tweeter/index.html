Tweeter

A tweeter or treble speaker is a special type of loudspeaker (usually dome, inverse dome or horn-type) that is designed to produce high audio frequencies, typically from about 2,000 Hz to 20,000 Hz (generally considered to be the limit of human hearing). Tweeters are used in multi-driver loudspeaker systems alongside midrange drivers and woofers to reproduce the full audible spectrum, since a single driver cannot do so efficiently.

Design

To produce high frequencies, a tweeter's vibrating surface must be small, light and stiff, and it needs to move very little air. Most modern hi-fi tweeters use a dome diaphragm of about 25 mm (1 inch) diameter made of materials such as silk or other treated fabric, aluminium, titanium, beryllium, or ceramic. Because the highest frequencies are highly directional, dome geometry strongly influences dispersion: the tweeter tends to "beam" sound on-axis, so careful cabinet and crossover design is needed for even coverage.

Other designs include cone tweeters (older and cheaper), ribbon and planar magnetic tweeters, piezoelectric tweeters, electrostatic panels, and horn-loaded tweeters used in public address and high-efficiency systems. Tweeters are fed only the high-frequency part of the signal by a crossover network, which also protects them from damaging low-frequency energy.

Super tweeters

Super tweeters are designed to reproduce frequencies above the nominal range of human hearing, up to 40 kHz or more, and are marketed for use with high-resolution audio formats; their audible benefit is disputed, though they may affect the audible band through improved transient behaviour or reduced distortion near the top of the hearing range.
