Mohammed VI of Morocco

Mohammed VI (born 21 August 1963) is the King of Morocco, having ascended the throne on 23 July 1999 following the death of his father, King Hassan II. A member of the Alawi dynasty, he is the 18th king in the line of Sharifian monarchs who have ruled Morocco since the 17th century.

Early life and education

Mohammed was born in Rabat, the eldest son of Hassan II and Lalla Latifa. He was educated at the Royal Palace school and obtained a bachelor's degree in law from Mohammed V University in Agdal in 1985, followed by advanced studies in political science and public law, earning a doctorate from the University of Nice Sophia Antipolis in 1993. He married Salma Bennani in 2002, who became Princess Lalla Salma; they have two children, Crown Prince Moulay Hassan and Princess Lalla Khadija.

Reign

Early in his reign Mohammed VI introduced reforms including the Equity and Reconciliation Commission to address human rights abuses of the "Years of Lead", and a reformed family code (Moudawana) in 2004 improving women's legal status. Following the 2011 protests associated with the February 20 Movement, he introduced a new constitution, approved by referendum, which modestly expanded the powers of the elected parliament and prime minister while retaining the king's central authority. His reign has also seen large infrastructure projects, the normalisation of relations with Israel in 2020 under the Abraham Accords framework, and continued assertion of Morocco's claim to Western Sahara. Through the royal holding company Al Mada, the king is one of Africa's wealthiest monarchs.
