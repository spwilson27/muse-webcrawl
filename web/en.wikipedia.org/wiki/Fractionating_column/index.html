Fractionating column

A fractionating column or fractional column is equipment used in the fractional distillation of liquid mixtures to separate the mixture into its component parts, or fractions, based on differences in volatilities. Fractionating columns are used in small-scale laboratory distillations as well as large-scale industrial distillations.

Theory of operation

The column works by providing a large surface area and a temperature gradient along its height: the temperature is highest at the bottom and decreases toward the top. As vapor rises through the column, it repeatedly condenses and re-evaporates on the column's internal surfaces. Each condensation–vaporization cycle enriches the vapor in the more volatile component, so that by the time the vapor reaches the top of the column it consists largely of the most volatile fraction, while less volatile components remain lower down. The repeated cycles are described as theoretical plates; a taller or more efficient column provides more plates and a sharper separation.

Design

Laboratory fractionating columns include the Vigreux column, a tube with a series of downward-pointing glass indentations, and columns packed with glass helices, Raschig rings, or metal sponge. Industrial columns are large vertical cylinders, often tens of metres tall, containing either trays (such as bubble-cap, sieve, or valve trays) stacked at intervals, or structured packing. Rising vapor passes through the trays while condensed liquid flows downward, maximizing contact between the phases.

Applications

Fractionating columns are essential in petroleum refining, where crude oil is separated into fractions such as refinery gases, gasoline, kerosene, diesel, and bitumen. They are also used in petrochemical plants, natural gas processing, air separation, and the production of distilled spirits and laboratory reagents.
