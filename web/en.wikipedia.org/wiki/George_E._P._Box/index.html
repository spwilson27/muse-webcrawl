George E. P. Box

George Edward Pelham Box

Born: 18 October 1919, Gravesend, Kent, England

Died: 28 March 2013, Madison, Wisconsin, U.S.

Fields: Statistics, time series analysis

Institutions: University of Wisconsin–Madison

George Edward Pelham Box (18 October 1919 – 28 March 2013) was a British statistician who made important contributions to quality control, time-series analysis, design of experiments and Bayesian inference. He is famous for the aphorism "all models are wrong, but some are useful."

Career

Trained originally as a chemist, Box worked for Imperial Chemical Industries before moving to the United States, where he founded the statistics department at the University of Wisconsin–Madison in 1960. With Gwilym Jenkins he developed the Box–Jenkins approach to ARMA and ARIMA time-series models, set out in their influential 1970 book Time Series Analysis: Forecasting and Control.

Contributions

Box is associated with numerous methods bearing his name, including the Box–Muller transform, the Box–Behnken design, Box–Cox transformations, the Box–Jenkins model and the Box–M test. With J. Stuart Hunter and William G. Hunter he authored the widely used text Statistics for Experimenters. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1985.
