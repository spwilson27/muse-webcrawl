Goose

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Aves
Order: Anseriformes
Family: Anatidae
Genera: Anser, Branta, Cereopsis, Cnemiornis (extinct)

A goose (plural geese) is a bird of any of several waterfowl species in the family Anatidae. This group comprises the genera Anser (the grey geese and white geese) and Branta (the black geese). Some other birds, mostly related to the shelducks, have "goose" as part of their names. More distantly related members of the family Anatidae are swans, most of which are larger than true geese, and ducks, which are smaller.

The term "goose" can refer to either a male or female bird, but when paired with "gander", refers specifically to a female one. Young birds before fledging are called goslings. The collective noun for a group of geese on the ground is a gaggle; when in flight, they are called a skein, a team, or a wedge; when flying close together, they are called a plump.

True geese and their relatives

The two living genera of true geese are Anser, which include the greylag goose and domestic geese, and Branta, which include the Canada goose and barnacle goose. Geese are monogamous, living in permanent pairs throughout the year; however, unlike most other permanently monogamous animals, they are territorial only during the short nesting season.

Behaviour and ecology

Geese are primarily herbivorous, grazing on grasses, sedges and aquatic plants, and occasionally eating small insects and crustaceans. Many species are strongly migratory, flying in a characteristic V formation over long distances between northern breeding grounds and southern wintering areas. The V formation reduces drag and allows the birds to communicate and coordinate.

Domestic geese

Geese were domesticated in Egypt more than 3,000 years ago, and domestic geese are descended from the greylag goose (Anser anser) in Europe and the swan goose (Anser cygnoides) in Asia. They have been kept for their meat, eggs, and feathers — goose down is prized as insulation — and are famously vigilant, serving as "guard geese." According to Roman tradition, geese saved Rome in 390 BC by raising the alarm when Gauls attacked the Capitoline Hill.

In culture

Geese feature in folklore, nursery rhymes ("Mother Goose"), and idioms such as "wild goose chase" and "your goose is cooked." Roast goose is a traditional festive dish in many European and East Asian cuisines, and foie gras is produced from the specially fattened liver of a goose or duck.
