The Transcaucasian Democratic Federative Republic (TDFR) was a short-lived state in the South Caucasus that existed from April to May 1918, comprising the territory of the modern-day republics of Armenia, Azerbaijan and Georgia.

Background

After the February Revolution of 1917, the Russian Provisional Government established the Special Transcaucasian Committee to administer the region. Following the Bolshevik seizure of power, the Transcaucasian Commissariat was formed in Tiflis, and in January 1918 a regional legislature, the Seim, was convened. The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk ceded districts including Kars and Batum to the Ottoman Empire, which the Seim refused to accept, and Ottoman forces advanced into the region.

Independence and collapse

Under Ottoman military pressure, the Seim declared the independence of Transcaucasia as a federal republic on 22 April 1918. Negotiations at Batum failed to hold the federation together: Georgia, under German protection, declared its independence on 26 May, followed on 28 May by Armenia and Azerbaijan. The federation thus dissolved after barely five weeks, and the three successor republics pursued separate paths until their incorporation into the Soviet Union in the early 1920s.
