Sunset Boulevard is a major boulevard in the central and western parts of Los Angeles County, California, stretching some 22 miles (35 km) from Figueroa Street in downtown Los Angeles to the Pacific Coast Highway in Pacific Palisades, near the Pacific Ocean.

Route and districts

The boulevard follows an old route that once connected the Los Angeles pueblo with cattle ranches to the west. East to west it passes through Echo Park and Silver Lake, crosses Hollywood, then forms the famous Sunset Strip through West Hollywood — a stretch noted for its billboards, nightclubs, and music venues such as the Whisky a Go Go and the Roxy Theatre. It continues through Beverly Hills, famous for its palm-lined residential section, and through Holmby Hills, Bel-Air, and Brentwood before descending to the coast.

In culture

The boulevard is immortalised by Billy Wilder's 1950 film Sunset Boulevard, about a faded silent-film star, as well as the Andrew Lloyd Webber musical of the same name. It remains one of the most recognisable street names in the world, symbolising the glamour and illusions of Hollywood.
