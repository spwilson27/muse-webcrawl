Penrose transform

In theoretical physics, the Penrose transform, introduced by Roger Penrose, is a complex analogue of the Radon transform that relates massless fields on Minkowski space to cohomology classes on twistor space. It is a central tool of twistor theory.

Overview

The transform associates to a holomorphic sheaf cohomology class on (projective) twistor space a solution of a massless field equation — such as the wave equation, Maxwell's equations, or the Yang–Mills equations — on spacetime. Concretely, solutions of the zero-rest-mass equations of given helicity correspond to elements of the first sheaf cohomology group of twistor space with coefficients in a suitable line bundle, whose degree encodes the helicity of the field.

Significance

Penrose's nonlinear graviton construction extended the transform to curved self-dual backgrounds. The correspondence later became important in gauge theory, integrable systems, and, through the work of Witten and others, in twistor string theory and the calculation of scattering amplitudes.
