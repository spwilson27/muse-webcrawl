Romanian revolution

The Romanian revolution of December 1989 was a period of violent civil unrest that overthrew the communist regime of Nicolae Ceaușescu, ending 42 years of Communist Party rule in Romania. It was the only one of the revolutions of 1989 in Eastern Europe to turn violent and the only one in which the regime's leader was executed. Approximately 1,100 people were killed and several thousand wounded.

Background

During the 1980s Ceaușescu imposed severe austerity to repay Romania's foreign debt: food and energy were rationed, heating and electricity were cut, and exports took priority over domestic consumption. His regime combined a pervasive security apparatus (the Securitate) with a personality cult and grandiose building projects such as the Palace of the Parliament. Dissent erupted in the Brașov rebellion of 1987 and grew as communist governments fell across Eastern Europe in 1989.

Events

Protests began on 16 December 1989 in Timișoara, sparked by the attempted eviction of the Hungarian Reformed pastor László Tőkés. Security forces fired on demonstrators, but the unrest spread; on 20 December Timișoara declared itself free of the regime. On 21 December a staged rally in Bucharest turned against Ceaușescu, whose stunned reaction was broadcast live. The following day crowds stormed the Central Committee building and the Ceaușescus fled by helicopter. The army defected to the revolution, fighting loyalist Securitate units amid confusion and sniper fire. Nicolae and Elena Ceaușescu were captured, tried by an exceptional military tribunal on charges including genocide, and executed by firing squad on 25 December in Târgoviște.

Aftermath

Power passed to the National Salvation Front, led by former communist official Ion Iliescu, which won the elections of May 1990. The revolution's course — particularly the violence after 22 December and the role of "terrorists" — remains contested, and numerous trials and investigations into the deaths have continued for decades. Romania subsequently transitioned to a market economy and joined NATO (2004) and the European Union (2007).
