Temperance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Temperance is the practice of moderation and self-restraint, particularly with regard to consumption. In classical philosophy it is one of the four cardinal virtues (Greek: sōphrosynē), alongside prudence, justice, and fortitude. In modern usage the term is most closely associated with abstinence from alcohol and with the temperance movement of the 19th and early 20th centuries.

As a virtue

Plato and Aristotle treated temperance as the harmonious governance of appetite by reason. In Christian theology it became one of the seven virtues opposed to the sin of gluttony. The virtue personified often appears in art holding a bridle or mixing water with wine.

Temperance movement

Organized temperance movements arose in the United States and Britain in the 1820s–1830s, initially promoting moderation and later total abstinence ("teetotalism"). Organizations such as the Woman's Christian Temperance Union (founded 1874) and the Anti-Saloon League campaigned for legal prohibition, achieved nationally in the United States from 1920 to 1933 under the Eighteenth Amendment.

Other meanings

Temperance is also a Major Arcana card in the tarot, and the word appears in place names, lodges of fraternal "temperance" societies that provided mutual aid and recreation without alcohol.
