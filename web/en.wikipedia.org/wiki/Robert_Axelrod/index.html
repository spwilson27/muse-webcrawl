Robert Axelrod

Born: May 27, 1943, Chicago, Illinois
Field: Political science, game theory
Institution: University of Michigan
Known for: The Evolution of Cooperation; iterated prisoner's dilemma tournaments

Robert Marshall Axelrod (born May 27, 1943) is an American political scientist, professor emeritus at the University of Michigan. He is best known for his work on the evolution of cooperation, in which computer tournaments of the iterated prisoner's dilemma showed how cooperative behavior can emerge among self-interested agents.

The Evolution of Cooperation

Around 1980 Axelrod invited game theorists to submit strategies for a computer tournament of the iterated prisoner's dilemma. The winner, in both the first tournament and a larger second one, was the simplest entry: tit for tat, submitted by Anatol Rapoport, which cooperates on the first move and thereafter copies the opponent's previous move. In his 1984 book The Evolution of Cooperation, Axelrod analyzed the successful strategies—they were "nice" (never first to defect), retaliatory, forgiving, and clear—and explored applications from trench warfare in the First World War to international politics. The book became a classic of interdisciplinary social science.

Other work

Axelrod's earlier Structure of Decision (1976) pioneered cognitive mapping of political elites' belief systems. With Michael Cohen he wrote Harnessing Complexity (2000) on complex adaptive systems and organizations. His agent-based models have addressed cultural dissemination, coalition formation, and cybersecurity, and he has advised governments on cooperation and conflict. His honors include a MacArthur Fellowship (1987) and the National Medal of Science (2014).
