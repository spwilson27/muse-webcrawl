Terra Mariana
Established1207 (consecration by Pope Innocent III); confederation formalised 1228

Terra Mariana (Latin for "the Land of Mary") was the official name of medieval Livonia, the loose confederation of crusader states established in the eastern Baltic — modern Latvia and Estonia — during the Livonian Crusade of the early thirteenth century. In 1207 Bishop Albert of Riga dedicated the conquered lands to the Virgin Mary, and Pope Innocent III proclaimed them her dowry, a counterpart to the Holy Land of Christ.

Organisation

Terra Mariana was never a unitary state. Power was divided among the Livonian Brothers of the Sword (after 1237 a branch of the Teutonic Order), the Archbishop of Riga, several prince-bishops, the autonomous city of Riga, and — in northern Estonia — the king of Denmark. A general assembly, the Livonian Diet, occasionally coordinated the members. German-speaking nobles, clergy and merchants ruled over Latvian, Livonian, Estonian and Curonian peasants, a social order that persisted for centuries.

History

The confederation endured recurring wars among its members, conflicts with Lithuania, Novgorod and the Teutonic Order in Prussia, and uprisings such as Estonia's St George's Night revolt of 1343, after which Denmark sold its Estonian duchy to the Order. The Reformation reached Riga in the 1520s and dissolved much of the ecclesiastical structure. In 1561, as Russian armies invaded in the Livonian War, the last master of the Order, Gotthard Kettler, secularised his lands as the Duchy of Courland under Polish suzerainty, while Estonia submitted to Sweden and Livonia to Poland — ending Terra Mariana.

Legacy

The name was revived in the Latvian Terra Mariana album of 1888 and in modern Latvian historiography; Estonia's highest state decoration, the Order of the Cross of Terra Mariana, established in 1995, bears its name.
