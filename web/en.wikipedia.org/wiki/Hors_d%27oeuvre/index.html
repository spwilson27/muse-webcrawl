Hors d'oeuvre

An hors d'oeuvre (French, literally "outside the work") is a small dish served before a meal in European cuisine. Some hors d'oeuvres are served cold, others hot; they may be served at the dinner table as part of the meal, or before seating, such as at a reception or cocktail party.

Terminology

In French culinary usage the term denotes a first course distinct from the main body (œuvre) of the meal. In North America, similar small items served before a meal are often called appetizers, while the bite-sized items passed at parties are known as canapés.

Examples

Classic hors d'oeuvres include crudités, devilled eggs, bruschetta, vol-au-vent, smoked salmon, oysters, pâté, and cheese-based preparations. Elaborate banquet service historically featured grand displays of hors d'oeuvres on side tables.

See also

Antipasto, mezze, tapas, and zakuski are related traditions of small dishes served at the start of or alongside a meal.
