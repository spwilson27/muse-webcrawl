Bathyscaphe

A bathyscaphe (from Greek bathys, "deep", and skaphe, "ship") is a free-diving, self-propelled deep-sea submersible with a spherical pressure hull suspended beneath a large float filled with gasoline. The design was invented by Swiss scientist Auguste Piccard, applying his experience with high-altitude balloon floats to ocean depth.

Design

The float provides buoyancy through petrol, which is nearly incompressible and lighter than seawater. Descent is controlled by releasing iron-shot ballast held by electromagnets; if power fails, the shot drops automatically and the craft ascends. The crew occupy a thick-walled steel sphere hung beneath the float.

Notable vessels

Piccard's first bathyscaphe, FNRS-2, dived in 1948. Trieste, built in Italy and later purchased by the United States Navy, carried Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh to Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench on 23 January 1960 — the first descent to the deepest known point of the oceans, at about 10,911 metres. The French bathyscaphe Archimède explored the Kuril–Kamchatka Trench in 1962.
