Seymour Aubrey Papert (29 February 1928 – 31 July 2016) was a South African-born American mathematician, computer scientist, and educator, who spent most of his career teaching and researching at MIT. He was one of the pioneers of artificial intelligence and of the constructionist movement in education, and a co-inventor, with Wally Feurzeig and Cynthia Solomon, of the Logo programming language.

Early life and education

Papert was born in Pretoria, South Africa, and educated at the University of the Witwatersrand, where he earned a PhD in mathematics in 1952; he received a second PhD, also in mathematics, at the University of Cambridge in 1959. Active in anti-apartheid politics, he left South Africa and worked with Jean Piaget at the University of Geneva from 1958 to 1963, an experience that deeply shaped his theory of learning.

Career at MIT

Papert joined MIT in 1963, co-founding the Artificial Intelligence Laboratory with Marvin Minsky, with whom he co-authored the influential book Perceptrons (1969). Drawing on Piaget's constructivism, Papert developed "constructionism" — the idea that learning is most effective when learners actively construct meaningful products. Logo, created in 1967, embodied this philosophy, letting children programme a "turtle" and learn mathematics by teaching the computer.

His book Mindstorms: Children, Computers, and Powerful Ideas (1980) became a landmark of educational computing. Papert later influenced the development of LEGO Mindstorms (named in honour of his book), the MIT Media Lab's Lifelong Kindergarten group, and the One Laptop per Child initiative.

Death and legacy

Papert suffered a severe brain injury in 2006 while attending a conference in Hanoi and died in 2016 at his home in Maine. He is remembered as a visionary who saw computers not as teaching machines but as tools with which children could think, create, and learn by doing.
