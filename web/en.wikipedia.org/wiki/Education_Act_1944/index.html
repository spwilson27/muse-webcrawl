Education Act 1944

The Education Act 1944, commonly known as the Butler Act after its sponsor R. A. Butler, President of the Board of Education, was a landmark act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that transformed education in England and Wales. It made secondary education free for all pupils and raised the school leaving age to 15 (later 16).

Provisions

The Act established a system of primary, secondary and further education administered by local education authorities. In practice, most areas adopted the "tripartite system" of grammar schools, secondary technical schools and secondary modern schools, with children allocated by the eleven-plus examination. The Act also required local authorities to provide school meals and milk, made religious education and a daily act of worship compulsory in county schools, and reformed the governance of voluntary (church) schools. It created a Ministry of Education to oversee the system.

Legacy

The Butler Act formed the foundation of the post-war welfare state settlement in education. From the 1960s onward, most local authorities replaced the tripartite system with comprehensive schools, but the framework of free, compulsory state education established in 1944 endured.
