The Modern Lovers were an American rock band led by Jonathan Richman, formed in Boston in 1970. Strongly influenced by the Velvet Underground, the original line-up included future Talking Heads keyboardist Jerry Harrison and future Cars drummer David Robinson.

The debut album

The band recorded demos in the early 1970s, including sessions produced by John Cale, but their debut album The Modern Lovers was not released until 1976, after the group had effectively dissolved. It contained songs such as "Roadrunner", "Pablo Picasso", "Astral Plane", and "I'm Straight", whose plainspoken, suburban subject matter and minimalist drive made the album a cornerstone of proto-punk. The Sex Pistols covered "Roadrunner", and "Pablo Picasso" was later covered by David Bowie.

Later versions

Richman re-formed the band as Jonathan Richman and the Modern Lovers with a quieter, acoustic sound and a string of whimsical albums from the later 1970s onward.
