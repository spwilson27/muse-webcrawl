Grumman A-6 Intruder

Role: All-weather attack aircraft

Manufacturer: Grumman

First flight: 19 April 1960

Retired: 1997 (US Navy)

Crew: 2 (pilot and bombardier/navigator)

The Grumman A-6 Intruder was an American twinjet all-weather attack aircraft developed and manufactured by Grumman Aerospace for the United States Navy and Marine Corps. Its two crew members sat side by side; the bombardier/navigator operated its integrated radar and navigation-attack system (DIANE), which allowed low-level strikes in any weather, day or night.

Service history

The Intruder entered service in 1963 and became the Navy's principal medium attack aircraft, seeing extensive combat in the Vietnam War and later in Lebanon, Libya, the Gulf War and Bosnia. The four-seat EA-6B Prowler electronic-warfare derivative remained in service long after the attack variant retired in 1997.

Design

The A-6 had a side-by-side cockpit, two Pratt & Whitney J52 turbojets, and could carry a heavy bomb load on five stations, including nuclear weapons in its early role.
