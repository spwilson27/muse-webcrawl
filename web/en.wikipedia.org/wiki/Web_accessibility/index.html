Web accessibility

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Using a screen reader to browse the web.

Web accessibility is the inclusive practice of ensuring that websites, tools, and technologies are designed and developed so that people with disabilities can perceive, understand, navigate, and interact with the web, and can contribute to it. It encompasses visual, auditory, physical, speech, cognitive, language, learning, and neurological disabilities, and also benefits people without disabilities — for example, people using phones, older users, and people in noisy environments or with slow connections.

Guidelines

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), developed by the W3C's Web Accessibility Initiative (WAI), are the principal international standard. WCAG is organised around four principles — content must be Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, and Robust (POUR). WCAG 2.0 was published in 2008, WCAG 2.1 in 2018 (adding mobile and low-vision criteria), and WCAG 2.2 in 2023. Conformance levels A, AA, and AAA grade the degree of accessibility; AA is the level referenced by most legislation.

Techniques

Accessible design practices include providing text alternatives (alt text) for images, captions and transcripts for audio and video, sufficient colour contrast, logical heading structure, keyboard navigability with visible focus indicators, descriptive link text, and the use of ARIA attributes to expose dynamic content to assistive technologies such as screen readers. Accessibility can be tested with automated tools, manual review, and user testing with disabled people.

Legal requirements

Many jurisdictions mandate web accessibility: Section 508 of the US Rehabilitation Act for federal websites, the Americans with Disabilities Act as applied to businesses, the European Accessibility Act and EN 301 549 in the EU, and the Equality Act 2010 in the UK. High-profile litigation, such as the US Supreme Court's refusal to hear the Domino's Pizza v. Robles appeal in 2019, has reinforced the applicability of accessibility law to commercial websites and apps.
