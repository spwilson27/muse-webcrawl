The price-to-book ratio (P/B ratio) is a financial ratio used to compare a company's current market price to its book value. It is calculated by dividing the market price per share by the book value per share, where book value is the value of the company's net assets as shown on its balance sheet — total assets minus intangible assets and liabilities.

Interpretation

A P/B ratio below 1.0 can indicate that a stock is undervalued, or that the market expects the company's assets to be worth less than their stated book value; value investors in the tradition of Benjamin Graham have historically looked for such stocks. A high P/B ratio suggests investors expect the company to generate high returns on its assets, as with many growth and technology companies whose most valuable assets — intellectual property, brand, human capital — do not appear fully on the balance sheet.

Uses and limitations

The ratio is most informative for companies with substantial tangible assets, such as banks, insurers and manufacturers, and is widely used to value financial institutions. It is less useful for service companies with few physical assets. Book values depend on accounting conventions, depreciation schedules and historical cost, so they may diverge substantially from economic value; repurchases of shares at prices above book value also mechanically reduce book value per share.

Relationship to return on equity

Under the residual income model, a company's justified P/B ratio is linked to its return on equity (ROE): firms earning an ROE above their cost of equity should trade above book value, while firms earning less should trade below it. The ratio can be decomposed alongside the price-to-earnings ratio and ROE in fundamental analysis.
