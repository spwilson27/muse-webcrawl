Cask ale or cask-conditioned beer is unfiltered and unpasteurised beer which is conditioned (including secondary fermentation) and served from a cask without additional nitrogen or carbon dioxide pressure. In the United Kingdom it is widely promoted under the term real ale, coined by the Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA) in the 1970s.

Process

Beer is racked from the brewery's fermenter into casks while still containing live yeast and a small amount of fermentable sugar or wort. A secondary fermentation in the cask produces gentle natural carbonation and develops flavour. Finings — traditionally isinglass — are added to help yeast and protein settle as a sediment. In the pub cellar, kept cool at about 11–13 °C (52–55 °F), the cask is stillaged, vented through a soft spile to release excess gas, and tapped; once broached, the beer must be sold within a few days before oxidation spoils it.

Dispense

Cask ale is traditionally drawn by a hand pump (beer engine), which pulls beer from the cellar through the line, or served by gravity directly from a tapped cask on the bar, stillage or a cradle. A tight "sparkler" nozzle on the pump is customary in northern England to produce a creamy head, while southern serving typically uses a looser pour — a long-running point of debate among drinkers. Because it is served without extraneous gas, cask ale is lower in carbonation and served cooler than lager but warmer than keg beer.

History and the real ale campaign

All beer was once cask-conditioned, but during the 1960s British breweries shifted production to filtered, pasteurised keg beers pushed by gas pressure. CAMRA, founded in 1971, campaigned to preserve the cask tradition and is credited with saving it; its Good Beer Guide (first published 1974) became the standard directory of real-ale pubs. Cask ale survived to enjoy a revival alongside the craft beer movement, though it remains predominantly a British format — a small cask/handpump scene exists in North America, where cask versions of craft ales are served at festivals and specialist bars.
