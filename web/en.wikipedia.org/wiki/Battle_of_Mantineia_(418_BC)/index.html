Battle of Mantineia (418 BC)

The Battle of Mantineia was fought in 418 BC during the Peloponnesian War, near the city of Mantineia in Arcadia. A Spartan-led army under King Agis II defeated a coalition army of Argos, Mantineia, Elis, and a small contingent of Athenian cavalry. It was the largest land battle of the war and restored Spartan prestige after the humiliation of Sphacteria in 425 BC.

Background

After the Peace of Nicias (421 BC) proved fragile, Argos built an anti-Spartan coalition in the Peloponnese. In 418 BC the Spartans marched north to confront the coalition before it could threaten Laconia. Agis II, stung by criticism for an earlier abortive campaign, sought a decisive engagement.

The battle

According to Thucydides, whose account in Book 5 of his History is the principal source, the Spartan right wing enveloped the Argive left after the Mantineian troops on that flank were defeated. A gap opened in the Spartan centre, but the elite Argive troops caught inside it were overwhelmed. The Athenian cavalry covered part of the retreat; the Athenian generals Laches and Nicostratus were killed.

Aftermath

The victory re-established Spartan dominance in the Peloponnese. Argos abandoned its Athenian alliance and made peace with Sparta, and the democratic government at Argos was briefly overthrown. The battle is also famous in military history for Thucydides' detailed description of phalanx drift — the tendency of hoplite lines to edge rightward as each man sheltered behind his neighbour's shield.
