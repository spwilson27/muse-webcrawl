Gustáv Husák

Gustáv Husák (10 January 1913 – 18 November 1991) was a Slovak politician and the last communist leader of Czechoslovakia. He served as First Secretary (later General Secretary) of the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia from 1969 to 1987 and as President of Czechoslovakia from 1975 to 1989. His two decades in power coincided with the period known as "normalization" following the suppression of the Prague Spring.

Early life and imprisonment

Husák was born in Dúbravka, now part of Bratislava, and joined the Communist Party as a law student in the 1930s. During the Second World War he was a leading figure in the Slovak National Uprising of 1944 against the clerical-fascist Slovak state and Nazi Germany. After the war he held high office in Slovakia, but in 1951 he was arrested in the Stalinist purges, convicted of "bourgeois nationalism" in a show trial, and sentenced to life imprisonment, spending years at Leopoldov prison before his release in 1960 and rehabilitation in 1963.

Rise to power

During the Prague Spring of 1968 Husák initially presented himself as a reformist supporter of Alexander Dubček, but after the Warsaw Pact invasion of August 1968 he aligned with Moscow. In April 1969 he replaced Dubček as party leader and directed the normalization: purges of reformists, renewed censorship, and the restoration of orthodox communist rule, while maintaining a degree of consumer-oriented stability.

Fall and death

Husák ceded the party leadership to Miloš Jakeš in 1987. As the Velvet Revolution swept Czechoslovakia in November 1989, he appointed a largely non-communist government on 10 December 1989 and resigned the presidency the same day; Václav Havel succeeded him. Husák died in Bratislava in 1991.
