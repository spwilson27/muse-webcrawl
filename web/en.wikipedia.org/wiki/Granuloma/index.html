Granuloma

A granuloma is an organised collection of immune cells — principally macrophages, often transformed into epithelioid cells and sometimes fused into multinucleated giant cells — that forms when the immune system attempts to wall off a substance it perceives as foreign but is unable to eliminate. Granulomas are a hallmark of granulomatous inflammation.

Causes

Granulomas occur in many conditions, including tuberculosis (where they are often caseating, with central necrosis), leprosy, sarcoidosis, Crohn's disease, fungal infections such as histoplasmosis, berylliosis, and foreign-body reactions to materials such as sutures or talc.

Pathology

Granuloma formation is driven by a Th1-type immune response, with interferon-gamma activating macrophages and tumour necrosis factor (TNF) maintaining the structure — which is why TNF-blocking drugs can cause granulomatous infections such as tuberculosis to reactivate.
