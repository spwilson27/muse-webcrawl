Boyan Slat, Dutch inventor and founder of The Ocean Cleanup.

Boyan Slat (born 27 July 1994) is a Dutch inventor and entrepreneur, best known as the founder and chief executive of The Ocean Cleanup, a non-profit organisation developing systems to remove plastic pollution from the oceans and to intercept it in rivers before it reaches the sea.

Early life and the original idea

Slat grew up in Delft and showed an early interest in engineering, setting a Guinness World Record as a schoolboy for launching 213 water rockets simultaneously. The idea that became The Ocean Cleanup came to him at age 16 while scuba diving in Greece, where he saw more plastic bags than fish. A school project on ocean plastic pollution, followed by a widely viewed TEDx talk in 2012, developed his concept of a passive barrier array that would use ocean currents to concentrate floating plastic in the subtropical gyres, above all the Great Pacific Garbage Patch.

The Ocean Cleanup

Slat left his aerospace engineering studies at TU Delft to found The Ocean Cleanup in 2013. A crowdfunding campaign in 2014 raised over US$2 million, followed by tens of millions in donations. The organisation's first full-scale system was deployed in the Great Pacific Garbage Patch in 2018; after an initial structural failure, a redesigned system successfully retained plastic in 2019, and larger "System 03" arrays were deployed in the following years. The organisation also deploys "Interceptors" in polluted rivers, beginning in Indonesia and Malaysia, aiming to stop plastic inflow at the source.

Recognition and criticism

Slat was named a UN Champion of the Earth in 2014 and has received numerous environmental and innovation awards. Marine scientists have debated the feasibility, cost-effectiveness and ecological impact of open-ocean cleanup, criticism Slat has publicly engaged with through the organisation's research publications and iterative engineering programme.
