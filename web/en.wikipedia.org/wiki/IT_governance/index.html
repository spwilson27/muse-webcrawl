IT governance

IT governance (ITG) is defined as a subset of corporate governance focused on information technology (IT) and its performance and risk management. It ensures that investments in IT generate business value and that the risks associated with IT are mitigated. The interest in IT governance lay in the knowledge that technology projects can get out of control very quickly and affect the performance of an entire organization.

Definitions

The IT Governance Institute defines IT governance as "the responsibility of executives and the board of directors, and consists of the leadership, organisational structures and processes that ensure that the enterprise's IT sustains and extends the organisation's strategies and objectives." Frameworks such as COBIT and ITIL provide structured guidance for implementing IT governance.

Frameworks

Well-known frameworks include COBIT (Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies), published by ISACA; ITIL, focusing on IT service management; ISO/IEC 38500, the international standard for corporate governance of information technology; and Val IT, which addresses value delivery from IT investments.

Structures and processes

Effective IT governance typically involves a board-level technology committee, a chief information officer (CIO) reporting to senior management, and formal processes for project prioritisation, resource allocation, performance measurement and risk assessment. Decision rights are usually divided between business and IT leadership.

Criticism

Critics argue that heavy governance structures can slow innovation and create bureaucracy. Proponents respond that well-designed governance aligns IT spending with strategic goals and improves accountability.
