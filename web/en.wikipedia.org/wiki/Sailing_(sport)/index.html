Sailing (sport)

The sport of sailing involves a variety of competitive sailing formats that are sanctioned through various sailing federations and yacht clubs. Racing disciplines include matches within a fleet of sailing craft, between a pair thereof, or among teams. Additionally, there are specialized competitions that include setting speed records. Racing formats include both closed courses and point-to-point contests; they may be in protected waters, coastwise, or on the open ocean. The sport is governed internationally by World Sailing.

History

Sailing racing has been conducted since at least the 17th century in the Netherlands, and the sport spread through yacht clubs in Britain and elsewhere in the 18th and 19th centuries. The first America's Cup was contested in 1851. Sailing has been part of the Olympic Games since 1900 (it was scheduled for 1896 but cancelled due to weather), and Olympic classes have changed repeatedly over the years to reflect developments in yacht design and economics.

Forms of competition

Fleet racing

Fleet races take place with four or more boats, often many dozens, sailing a course that includes windward and leeward legs and finishing according to position, sometimes with a handicap system to equalise different designs.

Match racing

Match racing pits two boats against each other, as in the America's Cup; tactics emphasise controlling the opponent rather than simply sailing the course fastest.

Team racing

Team racing is most often between two teams of three boats each, requiring complex tactics to achieve a winning combination of places.

Offshore and ocean racing

Long-distance races such as the Fastnet Race, the Sydney to Hobart Yacht Race, and round-the-world events such as The Ocean Race test navigation, endurance and seamanship. Speed records over set routes are ratified by the World Sailing Speed Record Council.

Rules

Racing is conducted under the Racing Rules of Sailing, revised every four years, which include right-of-way rules between boats on different tacks and around marks, as well as rules against gaining advantage by propulsion other than wind and water currents.
