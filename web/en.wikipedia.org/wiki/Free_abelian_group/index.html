Free abelian group

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, a free abelian group is an abelian group with a basis, analogous to a vector space over the integers. Equivalently, a free abelian group is a direct sum of copies of the integers ℤ under addition. Free abelian groups are the free objects in the category of abelian groups, and they play a central role in algebra, particularly in the classification of finitely generated abelian groups and in algebraic topology, where they appear as chain and homology groups.

Definition and universal property

An abelian group F is free abelian if it has a subset B, called a basis, such that every element of F can be written uniquely as a finite integer linear combination of elements of B. Equivalently, F satisfies the universal property: for every abelian group A and every function from the basis B to A, there is a unique group homomorphism from F to A extending that function. The cardinality of a basis is called the rank of the group and is well defined.

Examples

The group ℤ itself is free abelian with basis {1}. The group ℤn of integer vectors under componentwise addition is free abelian of rank n, with the standard basis of unit vectors. The group of polynomials with integer coefficients under addition is free abelian with basis 1, x, x2, … and hence of countably infinite rank.

Properties

Every subgroup of a free abelian group is itself free abelian — a theorem that depends on the fact that ℤ is a principal ideal domain; the analogous statement for free groups is the Nielsen–Schreier theorem. Free abelian groups are exactly the projective modules over ℤ that are torsion-free, and every abelian group is a quotient of a free abelian group. The fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups states that such a group is the direct sum of a free abelian group and a finite (torsion) group.

Construction

Given a set S, the free abelian group on S can be constructed as the group of finitely supported functions from S to ℤ, under pointwise addition. It is the abelianisation of the free group on S.
