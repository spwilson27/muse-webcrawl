Leander-class cruiser (1931)

The Leander class was a class of eight light cruisers built for the Royal Navy in the early 1930s and named after mythological figures. The class comprised HMS Leander, Achilles, Ajax, Neptune, and Orion, together with a modified group of three ships—Sydney, Perth, and Hobart—built for the Royal Australian Navy.

Design

Displacing about 7,000–7,300 tons, the ships were armed with eight 6-inch guns in four twin turrets, supplemented by 4-inch anti-aircraft guns and torpedo tubes, and could reach 32.5 knots. Influenced by the York-class heavy cruisers, their design emphasized endurance for trade-protection duties throughout the British Empire. HMS Ajax gained fame at the Battle of the River Plate in 1939, and Achilles served with a largely New Zealand crew.

Service

The class served extensively in the Second World War. Neptune was lost with nearly all hands on an Italian minefield off Tripoli in December 1941, and Perth was sunk at the Battle of Sunda Strait in 1942. Surviving ships were sold or scrapped in the late 1940s and 1950s; HMS Leander herself gave her name to the later Leander-class frigates.
