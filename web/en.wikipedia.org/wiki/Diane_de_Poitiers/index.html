Diane de Poitiers (9 January 1500 – 25 April 1566) was a French noblewoman and courtier who wielded much power and influence as the favourite mistress of King Henry II of France. Celebrated for her beauty, intelligence, and political acumen, she was for twenty years effectively a co-ruler of the court, eclipsing the queen, Catherine de' Medici.

Early life and marriage

Diane was born at the Château de Saint-Vallier into the house of Poitiers and educated in the household of Anne de Beaujeu. At fifteen she married Louis de Brézé, grand seneschal of Normandy, some forty years her senior, whose death in 1531 left her a wealthy widow; she mourned him conspicuously, wearing black and white for the rest of her life — colours the king later adopted for his own livery.

Royal mistress

Diane's influence over Henry began when he was still dauphin and survived his marriage to Catherine de' Medici in 1533. On his accession in 1547 she was showered with gifts: the Château de Chenonceau, jewels, and the titles of Duchess of Valentinois and Duchess of Étampes. Her cipher of interlaced H and D initials appears across royal residences. She supervised the education of the royal children, encouraged the arts, and acted as a channel of patronage; her rivalry with Anne de Pisseleu d'Heilly and her feud-by-proxy with the neglected queen structured court politics.

Fall and death

When Henry was mortally wounded in a tournament in 1559, Catherine barred Diane from his deathbed and demanded the return of the crown jewels and Chenonceau. Diane retired to her Château d'Anet, where she died in 1566. Excavation of her remains in 2009 suggested chronic gold poisoning, plausibly from the drinkable gold (aurum potabile) she took to preserve her beauty.
