Wacław Sierpiński

Wacław Franciszek Sierpiński (14 March 1882 – 21 October 1969) was a Polish mathematician. He was known for contributions to set theory (research on the axiom of choice and the continuum hypothesis), number theory, theory of functions, and topology. He published over 700 papers and 50 books.

Life and career

Sierpiński was born in Warsaw and attended the University of Warsaw, graduating in 1904. During the First World War he was interned in Russia. After the war he took a chair at the University of Warsaw, where he spent most of his career and helped found the Polish School of Mathematics. He was one of the founders of the journal Fundamenta Mathematicae.

Contributions

Three well-known fractals are named after him: the Sierpiński triangle, the Sierpiński carpet, and the Sierpiński curve. He also worked on Sierpiński numbers and the Sierpiński problem. The lunar crater Sierpiński is named after him, as is the asteroid 159776 Sierpiński.
