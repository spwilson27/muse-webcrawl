Scheduled Tribes

Scheduled Tribes constitute about 8.6% of India's population

The Scheduled Tribes are officially designated groups of historically disadvantaged indigenous peoples in India, recognised under Article 342 of the Constitution of India. The term is a constitutional and administrative category: communities are specified ("scheduled") by Presidential order for each state and union territory. Together with the Scheduled Castes, they are the principal beneficiaries of India's reservation (affirmative action) system.

Definition and criteria

Scheduled Tribes are often identified by the criteria articulated by the Lokur Committee (1965): primitive traits, distinctive culture, geographical isolation, shyness of contact with the wider community, and backwardness — criteria that are debated by anthropologists but remain administratively influential. Many of these communities refer to themselves as Adivasi ("original inhabitants"), though the term has no official legal status. There are over 700 recognised Scheduled Tribe communities.

Demographics

According to the 2011 Census of India, Scheduled Tribes numbered about 104 million people, 8.6% of the population. They are concentrated in the "tribal belt" of central India — Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Jharkhand — and in the hill states of the Northeast, including Mizoram, Nagaland, Meghalaya, and Arunachal Pradesh, where many are in the majority. The largest groups include the Gond, Bhil, Santhal, Munda, and Oraon peoples.

Constitutional safeguards

  • Fifth and Sixth Schedules — special provisions for the administration of Scheduled Areas and autonomous tribal councils
  • Reservations — seats in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies, government jobs, and educational institutions proportional to population
  • PESA Act (1996) — extends self-governance through gram sabhas in Scheduled Areas
  • Forest Rights Act (2006) — recognises individual and community rights over forest land and produce
  • The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes, established by the 89th Amendment (2003), monitors safeguards

Issues

Scheduled Tribe communities experience disproportionate poverty, land alienation, displacement by development projects, and poorer health and literacy outcomes. At the same time, tribal movements have asserted political power, notably in the creation of Jharkhand state in 2000, and in cultural and language revivals across the country.
