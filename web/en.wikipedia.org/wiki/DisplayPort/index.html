DisplayPort

TypeDigital video and audio connector
Designed2006; first standard published May 2006
StandardVESA
SupersedesDVI, VGA (in new designs)
DataPacketized; up to 80 Gbit/s (DP 2.1, UHBR 20)

DisplayPort (DP) is a digital display interface developed by a consortium of PC and chip manufacturers and standardized by the Video Electronics Standards Association (VESA). The interface is primarily used to connect a video source to a display device such as a computer monitor. It can also carry audio and USB data. DisplayPort was designed to replace VGA and DVI as the primary interface for computer monitors, and it is royalty-free to implement.

Technical overview

Unlike HDMI and DVI, which transmit a continuous pixel stream with a dedicated clock, DisplayPort uses packetized data transmission similar to Ethernet and USB, with the clock embedded in the signal. The main link consists of one, two, or four differential pairs ("lanes"). Successive versions raised per-lane rates: DP 1.2 (2009) offers up to 21.6 Gbit/s aggregate (HBR2), supporting 4K at 60 Hz; DP 1.4 (2016) adds Display Stream Compression (DSC) and forward error correction for 8K support; DP 2.0/2.1 (2019/2022) introduces Ultra High Bit Rate modes up to 80 Gbit/s. Multi-Stream Transport allows daisy-chaining several monitors from one port.

Connectors and compatibility

The interface exists in full-size and Mini DisplayPort connectors; the latter was adopted as the physical connector for Thunderbolt 1 and 2, and DisplayPort alternate mode is now carried over USB-C connectors, including Thunderbolt 3 and later. Dual-mode (DP++) ports can output HDMI or single-link DVI signals through passive adapters. DisplayPort added adaptive sync (FreeSync) support in version 1.2a, and is the reference interface for high-refresh-rate gaming monitors and professional displays.
