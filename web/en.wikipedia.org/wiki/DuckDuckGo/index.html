DuckDuckGo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
DuckDuckGo
Founded2008
FounderGabriel Weinberg
TypeSearch engine and browser; privacy-focused
MascotDax the duck
[Image: The DuckDuckGo logo: a white duck's head with a red bow tie in a red circle]

DuckDuckGo is an American software company whose eponymous search engine is notable for not tracking its users. While other search engines log queries, build profiles and personalise results, DuckDuckGo does not store IP addresses or user information, does not create search profiles, and shows the same results to everyone — a stance summed up in its slogan and its duck mascot.

Founded by Gabriel Weinberg in 2008 and named (loosely) after the children's game 'duck, duck, goose', it grew slowly until the 2013 Snowden revelations made search privacy a mainstream concern, after which its traffic multiplied. Results draw on its own crawler and hundreds of sources including Microsoft's Bing index under licence, supplemented by instant answers and '!bang' shortcuts that redirect a query to another site's own search.

DuckDuckGo has extended into browser apps and extensions that block trackers, and a desktop browser; its share of searches remains a few percent at most against Google, but it is the most prominent proof that a search engine can run as a business on contextual advertising alone, without surveillance of its users.
