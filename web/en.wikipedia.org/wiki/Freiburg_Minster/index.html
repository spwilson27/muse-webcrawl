Freiburg Minster

The 116-metre spire of Freiburg Minster.

Freiburg Minster (German: Freiburger Münster) is the cathedral of Freiburg im Breisgau, Baden-Württemberg, Germany. Built in the Romanesque and Gothic styles, construction began around 1200 under the dukes of Zähringen and continued into the 16th century; the church became a cathedral in 1827, when Freiburg became the seat of an archbishopric.

The tower

The minster's most famous feature is its 116-metre west tower, completed around 1330, with an open-work spire of stone tracery often described — in a phrase attributed to art historian Jacob Burckhardt — as the most beautiful spire in Christendom. It is one of the few great Gothic towers completed in the Middle Ages. The tower houses 19 bells, the oldest of which, the Hosanna bell, dates from 1258.

History

The church is unusual in having been financed and owned by the citizens of Freiburg rather than the Church for much of its history. Remarkably, it survived the bombing of Freiburg on 27 November 1944 largely intact, even as the surrounding old town was destroyed; its medieval stained glass, largely removed for safekeeping, was reinstalled after the war. Notable artworks include altarpieces by Hans Baldung Grien in the choir.
