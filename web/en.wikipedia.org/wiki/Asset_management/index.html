Asset management

Asset management is a systematic approach to the governance and realization of value from the things that a group, entity or individual is responsible for, over their whole life cycles. It may apply both to tangible assets (such as complex process or production equipment, infrastructure, buildings, financial capital, human capital or logistics) and to intangible assets (such as intellectual property, goodwill or reputation).

Closely related to investment management, asset management in the financial sense refers more specifically to the practice of buying, holding and trading financial securities and other financial assets in order to meet specified investment goals for the benefit of investors. Individuals may manage their own assets, or they may delegate responsibility to asset managing companies or asset management companies, sometimes informally called money managers.

Definition

A broad definition of asset management involves the professional management of investments such as stocks, bonds, real estate and private equity. According to the ISO 55000 standard, asset management is "the coordinated activity of an organization to realize value from assets". Value realization involves balancing costs, risks, opportunities and performance benefits.

Overview

Asset management is a global financial services industry with hundreds of firms, including banks, insurance groups, broker-dealers and independent asset managers. The largest asset managers companies include firms such as BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street and Fidelity Investments, whose assets under management reach tens of trillions of US dollars.

Asset managers work with clients such as individuals, pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, endowments, foundations, central banks and corporations. Their services include creating pooled vehicles, such as mutual funds, exchange-traded funds and hedge funds, and managing model portfolios tailored to client objectives.

Types

Types of asset management include:

  • Infrastructure asset management — management of man-made and natural systems such as bridges, roads, drainage and pipelines.
  • Fixed asset management — management of things such as buildings, machinery, computing equipment and IT resources.
  • Enterprise asset management — information systems that support the management of an organization's assets.
  • Financial asset management — concerned mainly with the accumulation, control and preservation of money and securities.
  • Public asset management — management of the common wealth.
  • Digital asset management — management of the content of media and brands.

Asset management in finance

Financial asset management is part of the financial industry providing consumers and businesses with financial services. Asset managers provide investors with more diversification and investing options than they would have by themselves. The fiduciary responsibility of asset managers to their clients includes managing assets and tracing transactions.
