Edward de Bono (1933–2021)

Edward Charles Francis Publius de Bono (19 May 1933 – 9 June 2021) was a Maltese physician and commentator. He is known for originating the term lateral thinking and for the Six Thinking Hats technique, writing many books on thinking as a learnable skill.

Life and career

De Bono was born in Malta and studied medicine at the University of Malta. As a Rhodes Scholar at Christ Church, Oxford, he earned a doctorate in medicine. He held faculty appointments at Oxford, Cambridge, the University of London and Harvard, and founded the Cognitive Research Trust (CoRT) in 1969, whose thinking lessons were adopted in schools in many countries.

Ideas

De Bono argued that the brain is a self-organizing patterning system, so perception — not just logic — determines the quality of thinking. His term "lateral thinking," introduced in the 1960s, describes deliberately moving sideways from established patterns to generate new ideas. The Six Thinking Hats method assigns six coloured "hats" (facts, feelings, caution, benefits, creativity, process) to structure group discussion by separating modes of thought. He also proposed the word "Po" as a provocation operator and developed the CoRT programme of thinking tools for schools.

Criticism

Some academics have criticized de Bono's work for lacking rigorous empirical testing and for the commercial scale of his consulting business, while supporters credit him with popularizing creativity training in business and education.

Selected books

  • The Mechanism of Mind (1969)
  • Lateral Thinking (1970)
  • Six Thinking Hats (1985)
  • I Am Right, You Are Wrong (1990)
