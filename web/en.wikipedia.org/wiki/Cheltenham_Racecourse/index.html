Cheltenham Racecourse

Cheltenham Racecourse at Prestbury Park, near Cheltenham, Gloucestershire, is Britain's most important venue for National Hunt racing. Its natural amphitheatre below Cleeve Hill hosts the four-day Cheltenham Festival each March, one of the largest events in the British sporting calendar, with a capacity of about 67,500.

History

Racing in Cheltenham dates to 1815 on Nottingham Hill, moving to Cleeve Hill and finally to Prestbury Park, where the first meeting took place in 1831. The Festival developed in the early twentieth century; the Gold Cup was first run in 1924 and the Champion Hurdle in 1927.

The courses

The racecourse has three courses: the Old Course, used for the first two days of the Festival; the New Course, with its famous downhill fence and long run-in; and the Cross Country Course, used for the Glenfarclas Cross Country Chase. The uphill finish in front of the grandstands, known simply as "the hill," is one of the most demanding in jump racing.

The Festival

Held in March, the Festival attracts around 250,000 spectators over four days and is especially popular with Irish visitors. Feature races include the Champion Hurdle, Queen Mother Champion Chase, Stayers' Hurdle and the Cheltenham Gold Cup.
