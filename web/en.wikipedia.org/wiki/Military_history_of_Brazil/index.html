Military history of Brazil

Brazilian Expeditionary Force troops in Italy during the Second World War.

The military history of Brazil spans the colonial conflicts of Portuguese America, the wars of the Empire of Brazil, and the campaigns of the modern Brazilian Armed Forces, including Brazil's participation in both World Wars.

Colonial period

Portuguese colonization, begun in 1500, involved conflicts with Indigenous peoples and rival European powers. The Dutch invasions of the 17th century culminated in the Battles of Guararapes (1648–1649), celebrated as the founding moment of the Brazilian Army, when Portuguese, Indigenous, and Afro-Brazilian forces expelled the Dutch from Pernambuco.

Empire and the Paraguayan War

After independence in 1822, Brazil fought the Cisplatine War (1825–1828) against the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata and the Platine War (1851–1852). The largest conflict was the Paraguayan War (1864–1870), in which Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay defeated Paraguay under Francisco Solano López; Brazil bore the brunt of the fighting, and the war devastated Paraguay.

Republic and World Wars

The Proclamation of the Republic in 1889 was led by the military. Internal conflicts included the Canudos War (1896–1897) and the Tenente revolts of the 1920s. In the Second World War, Brazil declared war on the Axis in 1942 and sent the Brazilian Expeditionary Force (FEB), some 25,000 men, to fight in Italy, distinguishing itself at Monte Castello and Montese.

Military government and modern era

A coup in 1964 installed a military government that lasted until 1985. Since redemocratization, the armed forces have focused on border security, Amazon surveillance, and peacekeeping, notably leading the UN mission in Haiti (MINUSTAH) from 2004 to 2017.
