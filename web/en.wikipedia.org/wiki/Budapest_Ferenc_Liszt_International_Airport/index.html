Budapest Ferenc Liszt International Airport (IATA: BUD, ICAO: LHBP), formerly known as Ferihegy Airport, is the international airport of Budapest, the capital of Hungary, and the largest of the country's five international airports. It lies 16 kilometres (9.9 miles) south-east of the city centre.

History

The airport, designed in the late 1930s and built during the Second World War, opened for civil traffic in 1950 and was named after the nearby Ferihegy hill. It was the home base of the Hungarian national airline Malév until that carrier's collapse in February 2012. In 2011, marking the 200th anniversary of his birth, the airport was renamed in honour of the Hungarian composer Franz Liszt (Ferenc Liszt in Hungarian). Passenger traffic is concentrated in Terminal 2 (2A for Schengen and 2B for non-Schengen flights), joined by the SkyCourt shopping and food hall opened in 2011; the old Terminal 1, a notable example of early modernist architecture, closed to scheduled traffic in 2012 and is used for events and private aviation.

Traffic

The airport is a major base for the low-cost carrier Wizz Air, which was founded in Hungary, as well as for Ryanair and LOT Polish Airlines services. It handled about 16.2 million passengers in 2019 and recovered to record levels, around 17.6 million, in 2024.
