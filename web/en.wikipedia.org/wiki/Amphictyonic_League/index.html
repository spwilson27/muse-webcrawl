An amphictyony (Greek: ἀμφικτυονία, "league of neighbours"), or Amphictyonic League, was an ancient religious association of Greek tribes formed in the dim past, before the rise of the Greek poleis. The most famous was the Delphic Amphictyony, centred first on the sanctuary of Demeter at Anthela near Thermopylae and later on the sanctuary of Apollo at Delphi. Its purpose was the protection and administration of these shrines, and it administered the Pythian Games at Delphi.

Organisation

The League consisted of twelve tribes (ethnē), each sending two representatives called hieromnemones to the council, giving twenty-four votes in total. Members swore an oath not to destroy any member town, not to cut off its water supply in war or peace, and to punish anyone who robbed the temple of Apollo — principles sometimes described as an early form of international law.

The Sacred Wars

The League's authority over Delphi led to a series of conflicts known as the Sacred Wars. The First Sacred War (c. 595–585 BC) was fought against Cirrha. The Third Sacred War (356–346 BC), provoked by Phocian seizure of the Delphic treasury, ended with the intervention of Philip II of Macedon, who was awarded the Phocians' two votes — a major step in Macedon's rise to dominance over Greece.

Later history

The Amphictyony continued under Macedonian, Aetolian, and Roman dominance. The emperor Augustus reorganised it in 31 BC, and it faded from history in late antiquity with the decline of the Delphic sanctuary.
