Indira Gandhi

Indira Priyadarshini Gandhi (19 November 1917 – 31 October 1984) was an Indian politician who served as Prime Minister of India from 1966 to 1977 and again from 1980 until her assassination in 1984. She was the daughter of Jawaharlal Nehru, India's first prime minister.

Premiership

Her tenure saw bank nationalisation, the Green Revolution's expansion, victory in the 1971 war leading to Bangladesh's creation, and India's first nuclear test (1974). She imposed the Emergency (1975–77), suspending civil liberties — a deeply controversial period — lost the 1977 election, then returned to power in 1980.

Assassination

After ordering Operation Blue Star against militants in the Golden Temple, she was assassinated by her Sikh bodyguards on 31 October 1984, triggering anti-Sikh riots. Her son Rajiv Gandhi succeeded her.
