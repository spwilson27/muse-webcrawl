Reflex arc

TypeNeural pathway
ComponentsReceptor, sensory neuron, interneuron, motor neuron, effector
ExamplePatellar (knee-jerk) reflex

A reflex arc is a neural pathway that controls a reflex. In vertebrates, most sensory neurons do not pass directly into the brain but synapse in the spinal cord, allowing reflex actions to occur faster by activating spinal motor neurons without the delay of routing signals through the brain.

Components

An arc consists of a receptor that detects the stimulus, a sensory (afferent) neuron, an integration center in the spinal cord or brainstem (with or without interneurons), a motor (efferent) neuron, and an effector muscle or gland.

Types

Monosynaptic arcs, such as the patellar reflex, involve a single synapse between sensory and motor neurons. Polysynaptic arcs, like the withdrawal reflex, involve interneurons and produce more complex, coordinated responses. Reflexes are classified as somatic or autonomic (visceral).

Clinical use

Testing reflexes — knee, ankle, biceps, Babinski — helps localize lesions in the nervous system; hyperreflexia suggests upper motor neuron damage, hyporeflexia lower motor neuron or peripheral nerve damage.
