Amintore Fanfani (6 February 1908 – 20 November 1999) was an Italian politician and statesman, who served as Prime Minister of Italy in five separate periods. A leading figure of the Christian Democracy party, he was one of the dominant figures of Italian politics for the first three decades of the post-war republic, and is regarded as one of the founders of the modern Italian Republic.

Early life and academic career

Fanfani was born in Pieve Santo Stefano, in the province of Arezzo, Tuscany. He studied economics at the Catholic University of Milan, where he became a professor of economic history. His early academic work, including Capitalism, Catholicism and Corporatism (1934), examined the relationship between Catholic social teaching and economic organisation.

Political career

After the Second World War, Fanfani became a close collaborator of Alcide De Gasperi and held several ministerial posts, including Minister of Labour, where he promoted the INA-Casa housing plan for workers' homes. As secretary of Christian Democracy (1954–1959), he modernised and strengthened the party organisation. He served as Prime Minister in 1954, 1958–1959, 1960–1963, 1982–1983, and briefly in 1987.

His third government, formed in 1962 with the abstention of the Italian Socialist Party, enacted the nationalisation of the electricity industry and the unification of middle schools, and is considered a precursor of the "centre-left" formula (centro-sinistra) that reshaped Italian politics.

Later years

Fanfani was President of the United Nations General Assembly in 1965–1966, President of the Senate several times, and was appointed Senator for Life in 1972. He remained an influential figure in Italian politics into the 1980s. He died in Rome in 1999.
