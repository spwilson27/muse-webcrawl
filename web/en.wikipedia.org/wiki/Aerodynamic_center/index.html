Aerodynamic center

Aerodynamic center
FieldAerodynamics
Typical location (subsonic aerofoil)Near quarter-chord point (≈25% chord)
PropertyPitching moment coefficient about this point is ~constant with angle of attack
Related conceptsCenter of pressure, neutral point, static margin

The aerodynamic center of an aerofoil (airfoil) or wing is the point at which the pitching moment coefficient does not vary with the lift coefficient — that is, with angle of attack — provided the flow remains attached. Taking moments about the aerodynamic center greatly simplifies stability analysis, because the aerodynamic moment can be treated as a constant while lift acts at that point.

Location

Thin-aerofoil theory places the aerodynamic center exactly at the quarter-chord point (25% of the chord from the leading edge) for a subsonic aerofoil, and experiments on typical sections confirm a value close to 23–27% chord. In supersonic flow the aerodynamic center shifts rearward toward the half-chord point (about 50% chord), a change that historically caught designers by surprise as aircraft first flew transonically.

Significance

For a complete aircraft, the corresponding whole-aircraft concept is the neutral point: the centre-of-gravity position at which longitudinal static stability is neutral. The distance between the centre of gravity and the neutral point, expressed as a fraction of the mean aerodynamic chord, is the static margin — a primary measure of an aircraft's pitch stability and control feel.
