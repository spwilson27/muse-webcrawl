Kapodistrias reform

The Kapodistrias reform (Greek: Σχέδιο Καποδίστριας, Schedio Kapodistria) was a major reorganisation of the administrative divisions of Greece, enacted by Law 2539/1997 and implemented from 1998. Named after Ioannis Kapodistrias, the first governor of the modern Greek state, the reform drastically reduced the number of municipalities and communities.

Provisions

Before the reform, Greece had 5,775 local government units — 441 municipalities and 5,382 mostly tiny communities. The Kapodistrias plan amalgamated them into 1,033 units: 900 municipalities and 133 communities, aiming to create viable local authorities able to deliver modern services and absorb European Union development funds. The reform also redrew many municipal boundaries and gave the new municipalities broader responsibilities in planning, water supply and local development.

Aftermath

The plan was politically controversial, provoking resistance from villages that lost their independent status, and implementation was uneven in the quality of services delivered. In 2010 the Kapodistrias structure was superseded by the Kallikratis Programme, which reduced the number of municipalities further, to 325, and created thirteen elected regions.
