Tupolev

Tupolev (Russian: Туполев), officially the Tupolev Design Bureau, is a Russian aerospace and defence company founded in 1922 by aviation pioneer Andrei Tupolev. Headquartered in Moscow, it designs and manufactures military bombers, missiles, and civilian airliners; it is now part of the state-owned United Aircraft Corporation.

History

Founded as an experimental design bureau (OKB-156), Tupolev led Soviet development of all-metal aircraft. During the Second World War it produced the Tu-2 bomber. In the Cold War its designs included the Tu-95 turboprop strategic bomber — still in service — the Tu-16 and supersonic Tu-22 and Tu-160 bombers, and the Tu-144, one of only two supersonic airliners ever to enter passenger service. Andrei Tupolev was arrested during the Great Purge and worked from a prison design bureau (a sharashka); his son Alexey later led the bureau.

Aircraft

Civil types include the Tu-104 (an early jet airliner), Tu-124, Tu-134, Tu-154, and Tu-204/214 families. Military types include the Tu-22M swing-wing bomber and the Tu-160 "Blackjack," the largest combat aircraft ever built. The bureau continues to modernise the Tu-95MS and Tu-160 fleets and develops the future PAK DA stealth bomber concept.
