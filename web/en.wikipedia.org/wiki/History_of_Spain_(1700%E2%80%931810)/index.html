History of Spain (1700–1810)

The history of Spain from 1700 to 1810 begins with the extinction of the Spanish Habsburgs and the accession of the Bourbon dynasty. The death of the childless Charles II in 1700 triggered the War of the Spanish Succession (1701–1714), which installed Philip V but cost Spain its European possessions.

Bourbon reforms

The Bourbon monarchs — Philip V, Ferdinand VI, Charles III, and Charles IV — centralized government through the Nueva Planta decrees, reformed administration and the military, and promoted economic and Enlightenment reforms, especially under Charles III, whose ministers modernized infrastructure, trade, and colonial government.

Empire and war

Spain retained a vast American empire and participated in the Seven Years' War and the American Revolutionary War, recovering Florida and Menorca. Trade liberalization within the empire spurred colonial growth late in the century.

Crisis

Alliance with revolutionary and Napoleonic France led to disaster at Trafalgar (1805) and, in 1808, Napoleon's invasion and the outbreak of the Peninsular War, which also ignited independence movements in Spanish America. See also Enlightenment in Spain.
