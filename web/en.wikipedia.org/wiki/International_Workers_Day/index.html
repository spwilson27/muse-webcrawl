International Workers' Day

International Workers' Day
Also calledMay Day; Labour Day
Date1 May
SignificanceCommemorates labour movement achievements and the Haymarket affair

International Workers' Day, also known as Labour Day in some countries and often referred to as May Day, is a celebration of labourers and the working classes promoted by the international labour movement and occurs every year on 1 May, or the first Monday in May.

May Day demonstrations are held annually in cities around the world.

Origins

The date was chosen by the Second International in 1889 to commemorate the Haymarket affair in Chicago: on 1 May 1886, American unions launched a general strike demanding the eight-hour day, and on 4 May a bomb thrown at police in Haymarket Square killed several officers and civilians. Four anarchists were later executed, becoming martyrs of the labour movement. The first International Workers' Day demonstrations took place on 1 May 1890 across Europe and America.

Observance

1 May is an official public holiday in more than 80 countries, marked by union marches, rallies, and in some places festivities. In the Soviet Union and Eastern Bloc, large state-organized parades were held; the tradition continues in countries such as China, Russia and Cuba. In the United States and Canada, Labor Day is instead celebrated on the first Monday in September, partly to distance the holiday from its socialist associations; the US also designates 1 May as Law Day and Loyalty Day.

May Day traditions

May 1 also coincides with the older Celtic festival of Beltane and European spring traditions such as dancing around the maypole—customs that in some countries blend with or stand apart from the labour holiday.
