Huế

Country: Vietnam
Status: Centrally governed municipality (from 2025)
Known for: Imperial City, former capital of the Nguyễn dynasty

Huế is a city in central Vietnam, situated on the banks of the Perfume River (Sông Hương). It served as the capital of Vietnam under the Nguyễn dynasty from 1802 to 1945 and is renowned for its imperial citadel, royal tombs, and refined court culture.

History

Huế became the seat of the Nguyễn lords in the 17th century and then the imperial capital under Emperor Gia Long. The city was the scene of heavy fighting during the 1968 Tet Offensive, when much of the old citadel was damaged. The Complex of Huế Monuments was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993.

Culture

Huế is famous for its elegant cuisine, traditional music (nhã nhạc, recognized by UNESCO), and festivals such as the biennial Festival Huế. Landmarks include the Imperial City, the Thien Mu Pagoda, and the tombs of emperors Minh Mạng, Tự Đức, and Khải Định.
