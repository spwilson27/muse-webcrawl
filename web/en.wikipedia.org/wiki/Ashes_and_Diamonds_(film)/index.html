Ashes and Diamonds (Polish: Popiół i diament) is a 1958 Polish drama film directed by Andrzej Wajda, based on the 1948 novel by Jerzy Andrzejewski. It is the third film in Wajda's war trilogy (after A Generation and Kanał) and is widely regarded as a masterpiece of Polish cinema and one of the great films of the postwar period.

Plot

On 8 May 1945, the last day of the Second World War in Europe, Maciek Chełmicki, a young soldier of the Polish Home Army, is ordered to assassinate Szczuka, a local communist party secretary, in a provincial town — though Maciek has begun to long for a normal life as the war ends. Having blundered a first attempt (killing two innocent workers instead), Maciek waits in a hotel bar, where he falls for the barmaid Krystyna, while the town holds a banquet to celebrate the end of the war. He carries out the killing at dawn and is himself shot while fleeing, dying slowly on a rubbish heap as fireworks and music mark the victory celebrations and the polonaise that closes the film.

Among the film's famous scenes is one in which Maciek sets fire to a row of vodka glasses in memory of his fallen comrades, and the climactic burning fireworks that silhouette his flight.

Cast and production

The role of Maciek made Zbigniew Cybulski an icon — sometimes called the "Polish James Dean" — his modern dress of dark glasses, jeans, and a windbreaker contrasting with official heroic stereotypes and making the anti-communist resistance fighter the film's emotional centre, a remarkable ambiguity under censorship. Ewa Krzyżewska plays Krystyna and Wacław Zastrzeżyński plays Szczuka.

Reception and legacy

The film won the FIPRESCI prize at the 1959 Venice Film Festival and brought international attention to the Polish Film School. Directors including Martin Scorsese and Francis Ford Coppola have cited it as an influence. It regularly appears in polls of the greatest films ever made, and Cybulski's performance remains a touchstone of European screen acting.
