Simpson's rule

Simpson's rule is a method for numerical integration, the numerical approximation of definite integrals. It is named after the English mathematician Thomas Simpson (1710–1761), although the formula was known earlier to Johannes Kepler.

Formulation

For an interval [a, b] divided into an even number n of subintervals of width h = (b − a)/n, the composite Simpson's rule approximates the integral as (h/3)[f(x0) + 4f(x1) + 2f(x2) + 4f(x3) + … + f(xn)]. Intuitively, the rule fits a parabola through each successive triple of points.

The error is proportional to h4 times the fourth derivative of the integrand, making Simpson's rule exact for polynomials up to degree three — one degree higher than expected.

Variants

Simpson's 3/8 rule uses cubic interpolation over three subintervals. Adaptive Simpson's method recursively subdivides intervals where the estimated error is large.
