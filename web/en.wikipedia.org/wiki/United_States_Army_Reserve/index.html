United States Army Reserve

The United States Army Reserve (USAR) is the federal reserve component of the United States Army. It provides combat support, combat service support and training capabilities that complement the Regular Army and the Army National Guard. The United States Army Reserve Command, headquartered at Fort Liberty, North Carolina, exercises operational control over most Army Reserve units.

Distinction from the National Guard

Unlike the Army National Guard, whose units normally serve under state governors unless federalized, the Army Reserve is always a federal force. Its structure is weighted toward specialized enabling capabilities rather than large numbers of infantry and armor formations. These include logistics, transportation, engineering, medicine, legal services, civil affairs, psychological operations, chemical defence, aviation and sustainment.

History

The Army Reserve traces its origin to the Medical Reserve Corps created in 1908 and to the Officers' and Enlisted Reserve Corps formed around the First World War. Reservists served in both world wars, Korea, Vietnam-era mobilizations and the Cold War. After the Vietnam War, the Total Force policy integrated the reserve into war plans so that major operations could not be conducted without it. Large numbers of Army Reserve soldiers deployed during the Gulf War, the Iraq War, the war in Afghanistan and domestic emergencies.

Service

Most members train part time—traditionally one weekend a month and an annual training period—while maintaining civilian employment, and can be mobilized by the President and Congress. Others serve full time in Active Guard and Reserve, civilian or dual-status technician roles. The force emphasizes professional and technical skills that mirror civilian occupations, particularly in medicine, engineering and logistics.
