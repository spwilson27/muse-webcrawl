Lubart's Castle (Ukrainian: Замок Любарта), also known as Lutsk Castle, is a medieval castle in Lutsk, Ukraine, and the city's foremost landmark. It was raised in the 14th century by Liubartas, a son of the Lithuanian grand duke Gediminas, on the site of an earlier wooden fortress of Kievan Rus' overlooking the Styr River.

History

A fortified settlement existed at Lutsk from at least the 11th century. After Volhynia passed to the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, Liubartas — who ruled Lutsk from about 1340 — rebuilt the stronghold in brick. In 1429 the castle hosted a congress of European monarchs convened by Grand Duke Vytautas, attended by the Holy Roman Emperor Sigismund and numerous princes. The fortress later passed to the Polish Crown and served as a royal residence and administrative centre.

Architecture

The irregular triangular castle is defended by three towers: the Entrance (Gate) Tower, the Styr Tower, and the Bishops' (Vladychna) Tower, joined by brick curtain walls. Within the walls stand the former bishop's palace, the ruins of the cathedral of St John, and an 18th-century noble court building. The complex is built largely of brick in a style reflecting both Rus' and Western European military architecture.

Today

Lubart's Castle is preserved as a state historical and cultural reserve and houses several museums, including a museum of books and printing and a collection of bells. It hosts festivals and open-air events, and its silhouette — in particular the Entrance Tower — is depicted on the Ukrainian 200-hryvnia banknote.
