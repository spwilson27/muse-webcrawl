The 1912 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the V Olympiad, were an international multi-sport event held in Stockholm, Sweden, from May to July 1912. They are widely regarded as the first truly modern and well-organised Olympic Games, introducing innovations such as electronic timing, photo finishes, and the first Olympic art competitions.

Highlights

The Stockholm Games saw the debut of the modern pentathlon, devised by Pierre de Coubertin, and the first Olympic appearances of Japan, which sent two athletes. Finnish distance runner Hannes Kolehmainen became a star, winning the 5,000 and 10,000 metres and the individual cross-country race. American athlete Jim Thorpe dominated the pentathlon and decathlon, but was stripped of his medals in 1913 after it emerged he had played semi-professional baseball; the medals were restored posthumously in 1982, and he was reinstated as sole champion in 2022.

Organisation

A purpose-built Olympic Stadium, designed by Torben Grut, hosted the athletics and ceremonies. The Swedish organisers limited the Games to a compact programme and pioneered the use of a public-address system and official timing equipment, setting new standards for Olympic administration that influenced all subsequent Games.

Medal table

The United States topped the medal table with 25 gold medals, followed closely by host nation Sweden with 24 golds, though Sweden won the most medals overall. Great Britain placed third.
