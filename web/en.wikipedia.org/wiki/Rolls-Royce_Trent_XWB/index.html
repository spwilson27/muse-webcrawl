Rolls-Royce Trent XWB

The Rolls-Royce Trent XWB is a high-bypass turbofan aircraft engine developed exclusively for the Airbus A350 family. It is the most powerful variant of the Trent family, and Rolls-Royce describes it as the most efficient large aero-engine in service. The engine first ran in 2010, powered the A350's maiden flight on 14 June 2013, and entered service with Qatar Airways in January 2015.

Design

The Trent XWB retains the three-shaft architecture of earlier Trents. Its fan, 118 inches (3.0 m) in diameter with swept titanium blades, gives a bypass ratio of about 9.6:1. Two variants are produced: the XWB-84 for the A350-900, rated at 84,000 lbf (370 kN) of thrust, and the XWB-97 for the A350-1000 and A350F freighter, rated at 97,000 lbf (430 kN) with a strengthened core and higher-temperature materials.

Operations

The Trent XWB is the sole engine offered on the A350, and by the mid-2020s more than a thousand had been delivered. Rolls-Royce has proposed an UltraFan-derived upgrade to improve fuel burn further. The engine has a strong reliability record, though like other modern widebody engines it requires periodic shop visits for high-pressure turbine durability.

Specifications (XWB-97)

Type: three-shaft high-bypass turbofan. Fan diameter: 118 in (3.0 m). Maximum thrust: 97,000 lbf (431 kN). Overall pressure ratio: about 50:1. Dry weight: approximately 7,277 kg.
