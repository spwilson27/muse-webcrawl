Patriot movement

The patriot movement is a term used in the United States to describe a loose collection of conservative and libertarian groups, including militia organisations, tax protesters, and sovereign citizens, united by distrust of the federal government and a belief that it has exceeded its constitutional limits.

History

Analysts trace the movement's roots to earlier groups such as Posse Comitatus in the 1970s. It grew rapidly in the 1990s, reacting to events including Ruby Ridge (1992) and the Waco siege (1993) and to federal gun legislation, and was closely watched after the 1995 Oklahoma City bombing. It declined in the early 2000s and resurged later in that decade with the militia and Tea Party activism.

Beliefs

Common themes include defence of Second Amendment rights, opposition to federal land management and taxation, and various theories about an impending imposition of martial law or a "new world order". Researchers such as the Southern Poverty Law Center track groups they classify as antigovernment extremist.
