Vimy Ridge

Vimy Ridge is a long, prominent escarpment in northern France, west of the city of Arras and north-east of the town of Lens, in the department of Pas-de-Calais. Rising up to about 145 metres above the surrounding plain, the ridge commanded extensive views over the Douai plain and was a strategically important position on the Western Front during the First World War.

Battle of Vimy Ridge

The ridge was captured by German forces in 1914 and strongly fortified. French and British attempts to retake it in 1915 and 1916 failed with heavy casualties. In April 1917 the Canadian Corps, fighting together as a single formation for the first time, attacked as part of the Battle of Arras. Meticulous preparation, extensive tunnelling, a powerful creeping artillery barrage and rehearsed small-unit tactics allowed the Canadians to seize most of the ridge on 9 April 1917 and the remainder by 12 April, at a cost of 3,598 Canadian dead and some 7,000 wounded. The victory became a defining event in Canadian national history.

Memorial and preserved battlefield

France granted Canada the use of about 100 hectares of the ridge in perpetuity. On it stands the Canadian National Vimy Memorial, designed by Walter Seymour Allward and unveiled in 1936, inscribed with the names of 11,285 Canadians killed in France whose graves are unknown. The surrounding battlefield park preserves trenches, tunnels (including the Grange Subway) and shell craters, and is managed by Veterans Affairs Canada.
