Cash register

A cash register, or till, is a mechanical or electronic device for registering and calculating transactions at a point of sale. It is usually attached to a drawer for storing cash and valuables, and issues receipts for customers.

History

The first cash register was invented in 1879 by James Ritty, a Dayton, Ohio saloon keeper, to stop employees pilfering. John H. Patterson acquired the business and founded the National Cash Register Company (NCR), which sold registers under the slogan of the "incorruptible cashier". Early models were elaborate brass machines that added sales, rang a bell, and displayed the amount to the customer.

Modern systems

Electronic cash registers and computer-based point of sale (POS) systems added barcode scanning, inventory control, card payment, and reporting. The cash drawer, opened by a key or by a solenoid triggered by a sale, remains a standard feature, and many jurisdictions require certified fiscal registers that record tax data.
