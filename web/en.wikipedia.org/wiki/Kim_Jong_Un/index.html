Kim Jong Un

Born: 8 January 1982/1983/1984, Pyongyang, North Korea

Office: Supreme Leader of North Korea since 2011

Predecessor: Kim Jong-il

Spouse: Ri Sol-ju

Kim Jong Un (Korean: 김정은; born 8 January 1982, 1983 or 1984) is a North Korean politician who has been the supreme leader of North Korea since 2011 and the leader of the Workers' Party of Korea since 2012. He is the third son of Kim Jong-il, the second supreme leader, and the grandson of Kim Il-sung, the country's founder.

Early life and education

Little is known with certainty about Kim's early life. He is believed to have attended school in Switzerland under an assumed name. In North Korea he studied at Kim Il-sung University and the Kim Il-sung Military University. He was publicly unveiled as his father's successor in 2010, receiving senior party and military posts despite little prior public profile.

Leadership

Following Kim Jong-il's death in December 2011, Kim Jong Un consolidated power, removing or executing several senior figures, most notably his uncle Jang Song-thaek in 2013. In 2016 he assumed the title of Chairman of the State Affairs Commission. His rule has combined purges with a policy of simultaneously developing the economy and nuclear weapons, known as byungjin.

Nuclear programme and diplomacy

North Korea conducted several nuclear tests and long-range missile launches during Kim's tenure, including tests in 2016 and 2017 that Pyongyang said demonstrated intercontinental and thermonuclear capability. After a period of heightened tension, Kim met South Korean president Moon Jae-in in 2018 and became the first North Korean leader to meet a sitting United States president, holding summits with Donald Trump in Singapore (2018) and Hanoi (2019); the Hanoi summit ended without an agreement. His elder half-brother, Kim Jong-nam, was assassinated in Malaysia in 2017, an act widely attributed to his regime.

Personal life

Kim is married to Ri Sol-ju, with whom he is believed to have three children. Former basketball player Dennis Rodman, who visited North Korea several times and described Kim as a friend, is among the few foreigners known to have met his family informally.
