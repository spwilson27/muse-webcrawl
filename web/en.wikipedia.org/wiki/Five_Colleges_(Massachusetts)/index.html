Five College Consortium

The Five College Consortium (often styled Five Colleges, Incorporated) is a group of five colleges and universities in the Pioneer Valley of western Massachusetts: Amherst College, Hampshire College, Mount Holyoke College, Smith College, and the University of Massachusetts Amherst. The institutions, all within about a ten-mile radius, cooperate academically and administratively while remaining independent.

Members

  • Amherst College — a private liberal arts college in Amherst, founded 1821.
  • Hampshire College — a private liberal arts college in Amherst, founded 1970, known for self-designed study.
  • Mount Holyoke College — a women's liberal arts college in South Hadley, founded 1837, the oldest of the Seven Sisters.
  • Smith College — a women's liberal arts college in Northampton, founded 1871.
  • University of Massachusetts Amherst — the flagship public research university of Massachusetts, founded 1863.

Cooperation

The consortium began with cooperative arrangements in the 1950s and was formally incorporated in 1965; Hampshire College, founded as an experiment by the other four, joined on its creation. Students at any member institution may cross-register for courses at the others at no extra cost, choosing from roughly 6,000 courses. The colleges share a joint course catalogue, certificate programmes (such as those in Five College area-studies centres), a common library depository, inter-campus bus service (run with the Pioneer Valley Transit Authority), and joint departments and programmes, notably in astronomy, dance and film and media studies.

Impact

Together the five institutions enrol on the order of 38,000 students and form the cultural and economic centre of the Pioneer Valley. The consortium is regarded as a model of college cooperation, frequently cited alongside consortia such as the Claremont Colleges in California.
