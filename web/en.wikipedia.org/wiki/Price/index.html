Price

[Image: price tags attached to goods on a market stall]

A price is the quantity of payment — usually Money — given by one party to another in exchange for a unit of a good or service. Prices are the signals around which markets organise production and consumption: a rising price encourages more supply and rations demand; a falling price does the reverse.

Formation and roles

In competitive markets prices are formed by Supply and demand and tend toward the cost of production; where firms have market power they set prices above marginal cost (a markup analysed in Monopoly and Oligopoly theory). Friedrich Hayek stressed prices' informational role: a price aggregates dispersed knowledge about scarcity into a single number anyone can act on. Economists distinguish nominal prices from real (inflation-adjusted) ones, list from transaction prices, and administered prices set by firms or the state. Price indices such as the CPI average many prices to measure Inflation, and sticky prices — which adjust slowly — are a key friction in macroeconomic models of the business cycle.
