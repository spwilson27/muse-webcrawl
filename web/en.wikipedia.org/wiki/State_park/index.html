State park

State parks are parks or other protected areas managed by the individual US states, and in some other countries by sub-national governments, for the purposes of conservation and recreation. They are generally established by state governments to preserve locations for their natural beauty, historic interest, or recreational potential.

United States

There are more than 6,600 state park units in the United States, covering over 14 million acres and receiving hundreds of millions of visitors each year — more than the US national parks. The oldest state park is often considered to be Niagara Reservation in New York, established in 1885, though state-managed reserves such as Yosemite (initially a state grant) predate it. California, New York, and Ohio operate some of the largest state park systems. State parks are typically administered by a division of state parks within a state department of natural resources or equivalent agency.

Activities

Facilities commonly include campgrounds, hiking trails, picnic areas, boat launches, and interpretive programmes. Some state parks protect historic sites, battlefields, and archaeological resources as well as scenery and wildlife.

Other countries

Analogous sub-national park systems exist in countries such as Australia, Canada (where they are called provincial or territorial parks), Brazil, and Argentina.
