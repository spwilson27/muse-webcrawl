Television set

A television set, or television receiver, more commonly called a TV, is an electronic device for viewing and hearing television broadcasts, or a signal from other video sources. It combines a tuner, a display, and loudspeakers. Since the early 2010s most television sets sold have been flat-panel "smart TVs" with internet connectivity and the ability to run streaming applications.

History

Experimental mechanical television sets appeared in the late 1920s based on the Nipkow disk, but commercial television began with fully electronic sets using cathode-ray tubes (CRTs) in the late 1930s. After the Second World War, black-and-white sets spread rapidly through American and European homes; colour broadcasting arrived in the United States in the 1950s (NTSC) and in Europe in the 1960s (PAL and SECAM). Solid-state electronics replaced valves, remote controls became universal, and screen sizes grew.

The turn of the 21st century brought flat-panel displays — plasma, then LCD with LED backlighting, and later OLED and QLED — together with high-definition (HDTV), digital tuners, and the analogue switch-off. Smart TVs integrated operating systems and streaming services such as Netflix and YouTube, blurring the line between television receiver and computer display. Ultra-high-definition 4K, and later 8K, sets became common in the 2010s and 2020s.

Technology

A modern television set receives signals through terrestrial, cable, satellite, or IP connections; demodulates and decodes them (using standards such as ATSC, DVB, or ISDB); processes the picture and sound; and presents them on a display panel with built-in or external speakers. Important specifications include screen size, resolution, refresh rate, peak brightness, colour gamut, and support for high-dynamic-range formats such as HDR10 and Dolby Vision. Connectivity typically includes HDMI and USB ports, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth.

Market and social role

The television set was one of the defining consumer products of the 20th century, reshaping news, entertainment, advertising, and domestic life. Although younger audiences increasingly watch video on phones and computers, the large-screen TV remains the centrepiece of most living rooms, and global sales run at roughly 200 million units per year. Major manufacturers include Samsung, LG, TCL, Hisense, and Sony.
