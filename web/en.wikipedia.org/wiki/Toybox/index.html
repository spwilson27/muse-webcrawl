Toybox

For other uses, see Toybox (disambiguation).

A toybox, or toy chest, is a piece of furniture — typically a lidded box or chest — used to store children's toys. Toyboxes are commonly kept in children's bedrooms and playrooms and range from simple plastic bins and wooden chests to upholstered ottomans and bench-style chests that double as seating.

Design and safety

Traditional toyboxes are wooden chests with hinged lids. Because children have been injured — and in rare cases suffocated — by falling lids and by becoming trapped inside airtight chests, modern safety standards for toyboxes in many countries require features such as:

  • Spring-loaded or soft-close lid supports that prevent the lid from slamming shut
  • Ventilation holes so a child inside can breathe
  • No automatic latches that could trap a child inside
  • Hinge gaps sized to avoid pinching small fingers

Regulatory bodies such as the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission issue guidance and recalls concerning toy chest hazards, and parents are advised to remove locks and latches from older chests.

In popular culture

The toybox is a familiar symbol of childhood and appears in literature and film — notably as the home of the toys in Pixar's Toy Story franchise, where Andy's toy box houses Woody, Buzz Lightyear and the other characters. "Toy box" is also used metaphorically for any collection of playthings or tools.
