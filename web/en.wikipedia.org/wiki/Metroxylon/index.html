The sago palm, Metroxylon sagu

Metroxylon is a genus of flowering plants in the palm family (Arecaceae), native to Southeast Asia and the western Pacific, from the Malay Archipelago and New Guinea to Fiji, Samoa, and the Solomon and Caroline Islands. The genus contains about seven species of mostly monocarpic palms—each stem flowers once at the end of its life and then dies. The name derives from the Greek metra ("womb" or core/pith) and xylon ("wood"), referring to the starchy pith of the trunk.

The sago palm

The best-known species, Metroxylon sagu (the true sago palm), is one of the world's most important starch-producing plants. A single trunk can yield 150–300 kg of sago starch, extracted from the pith just before flowering, when starch reserves peak. Sago is a staple food for peoples of lowland New Guinea and the Moluccas, eaten as pearls, flatbreads, and porridge, and is traded internationally as an industrial starch. The palm also provides thatch (from its large pinnate leaves), building materials, and food for sago grubs, a local delicacy.

Other species

Other species include Metroxylon amicarum of the Caroline Islands, valued for its ivory-like seeds, and M. warburgii of Vanuatu and Samoa. Several species are grown as ornamentals in tropical gardens.
