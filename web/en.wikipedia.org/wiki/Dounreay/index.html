Dounreay is a nuclear research and development site on the north coast of Caithness, Scotland, built from 1955 on a former wartime airfield. For four decades it was Britain's centre for fast breeder reactor research, and it is now one of Europe's largest nuclear decommissioning projects.

Reactors

The site's best-known landmark is the steel sphere of the Dounreay Fast Reactor (DFR), which operated from 1959 to 1977 and was the first fast reactor in the world to supply electricity to a grid. The Dounreay Materials Testing Reactor (DMTR) ran from 1958 to 1969, and the larger Prototype Fast Reactor (PFR) generated power from 1974 until its closure in 1994, which ended the UK's fast reactor programme. A fuel fabrication and reprocessing plant supported the experimental programme.

Incidents and legacy issues

In 1977 an explosion occurred in a waste shaft contaminated with sodium and potassium residues, and it later emerged that radioactive fuel particles had escaped into the environment from historic practices, leading to long-running monitoring and retrieval programmes on the nearby seabed and at Sandside Bay. A second shaft, used for intermediate-level waste, presents one of the site's most difficult clean-up challenges.

Decommissioning

Since the 1990s the site has been progressively decommissioned under the Nuclear Decommissioning Authority, with the operating company now NRS Dounreay. The programme involves dismantling the reactors, treating exotic fuels, and remediating the shafts, with the site intended to reach an interim end state — largely cleared and capped — over the coming decades. Decommissioning remains a major employer in the Caithness economy, and the DFR sphere is planned to be preserved as an industrial heritage landmark.
