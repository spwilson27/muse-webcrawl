Webcam

A webcam is a video camera which is designed to record or stream to a computer or computer network. Webcams are typically small cameras that sit on a desk, attach to a monitor, or are built into the hardware of laptops and other devices. They are primarily used in video telephony, live streaming and security surveillance, and for recording video for upload to the internet.

History

The first webcam, the Trojan Room coffee pot camera, was created in 1991 at the University of Cambridge Computer Laboratory to let researchers check whether a shared coffee pot was full without walking to it; it went live on the web in 1993 and became a minor internet celebrity until its retirement in 2001. The first commercial webcam, the QuickCam, was released by Connectix in 1994. Webcams became mass-market peripherals in the 2000s and are now integrated into nearly all laptops, tablets and monitors.

Technology

Webcams capture images with a small CMOS image sensor, process them with on-board electronics, and connect to computers most commonly by USB using the USB video device class, requiring no specialised drivers. Resolutions range from standard definition to 4K, and higher-end models add autofocus, automatic low-light correction and built-in microphones. Software handles videoconferencing, streaming to services such as YouTube and Twitch, and motion detection for home security.

Privacy

Because webcams can be activated by malware without the user's knowledge, privacy concerns have led to advice to cover lenses when not in use, and to indicator lights and operating-system permission controls in modern systems.
