Caretaker government

A caretaker government, also known as a provisional or interim government, is a temporary administration that governs a state, usually until a new government can be formed after an election or the collapse of the previous administration.

Functions and conventions

Caretaker governments typically handle the routine business of the state but, by convention or law, refrain from making major policy decisions, significant appointments, or large financial commitments that would bind a successor. In Westminster systems, caretaker conventions apply from the dissolution of parliament until a new government is formed after an election.

Examples

Australia and New Zealand have well-documented caretaker conventions. In Bangladesh, a formal caretaker government system administered the country during elections between 1996 and 2011. Caretaker cabinets are also common in parliamentary systems such as the Netherlands and Belgium during prolonged coalition negotiations; Belgium was governed by a caretaker administration for 541 days in 2010–2011.
