Petersburg (novel)

Author: Andrei Bely

Original title: Петербург (Peterburg)

Published: 1913–1914 (serial); revised edition 1922

Genre: Symbolist novel

Petersburg (Russian: Петербург) is a novel by the Russian writer Andrei Bely, first published in serial form in 1913–1914 and issued in a substantially revised, shorter edition in 1922. Considered the masterpiece of Russian Symbolist prose, it has been called by Vladimir Nabokov one of the four greatest novels of the 20th century, alongside Joyce's Ulysses, Kafka's The Metamorphosis and Proust's In Search of Lost Time.

Plot

Set in St. Petersburg during the Revolution of 1905, the novel centres on Nikolai Apollonovich Ableukhov, a young philosophy student ensnared by a revolutionary cell. He has promised — half in jest, half in earnest — to assassinate his own father, Apollon Apollonovich, a high tsarist senator, using a sardine tin containing a time bomb given to him by the agent Dudkin, who is manipulated by the provocateur Lippanchenko. As the bomb ticks toward its deadline, father and son circle one another through a city of fog, red dominos and the menacing Bronze Horseman, in a narrative woven from rhythmical prose, puns, colour symbolism and hallucination.

Themes and style

The novel pits East against West, order against chaos, and the cerebral, geometry-loving senator against revolutionary dissolution. Its prose is famous for its musical rhythms, neologisms and synaesthetic imagery. Bely revised the text heavily for the 1922 edition, tightening its structure; the 1916 book version stands between the serial and final texts.
