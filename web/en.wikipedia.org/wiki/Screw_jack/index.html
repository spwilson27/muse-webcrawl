Screw jack

A mechanical screw jack used for lifting heavy loads.

A screw jack, also known as a jack screw, is a mechanical device used to lift heavy loads or apply very large forces. It uses a power screw — a screw thread with a large mechanical advantage — to convert rotary input into linear motion. Screw jacks range from small bottle-type car jacks to industrial units capable of lifting hundreds of tonnes.

Type: Lifting mechanism

Principle: Power screw / inclined plane

Actuation: Manual, electric, pneumatic, hydraulic motor

Typical uses: Vehicle lifting, aircraft maintenance, stage machinery, antenna positioning

Operation

Turning the screw — by hand lever, handwheel, or motor — advances the load-bearing spindle through a fixed nut (or rotates a nut along a fixed screw). Because the thread pitch is small compared with the lever arm, a modest input torque can raise a very heavy load. The friction of the thread usually makes the jack self-locking: the load does not drive the screw backwards when input torque is removed, so no brake is needed to hold position.

Types

Screw jacks are classified by the motion of their components: in a translating jack the screw moves axially through a fixed nut; in a rotating-screw (or keyed) design the screw rotates and a travelling nut moves the load. Multiple jacks can be linked by shafts and bevel gearboxes so that platforms rise evenly.

Applications

Screw jacks support aircraft during maintenance, adjust large antennas and telescopes, level heavy machinery, operate sluice gates, and move theatre stages. In the home, the familiar scissor jack and bottle jack in cars derive their lifting action from a screw thread.
