H. P. Bulmer Limited, trading as Bulmer's Cider, is a cider-making company founded in 1887 in Hereford, England. It is the world's largest cider maker, best known for the Strongbow brand, and since 2003 has been a subsidiary of Heineken UK, part of the Dutch brewing group Heineken N.V.

History

Henry Percival Bulmer, the younger son of the rector of Credenhill, founded the company in 1887 with a £1,760 loan from his father, following his mother's advice that food and drink would never go out of fashion. Using an old stone press at a rented mill in Hereford, he began making cider by traditional methods. His brother Fred, who had been selling wine and spirits, joined him and helped develop a national sales network.

Bulmers grew with the popularity of bottled cider. The company received a royal warrant in 1911. Percy Bulmer died in 1919, and the company was led for decades by his son Bertram. Strongbow — named after the Anglo-Norman knight Richard de Clare, 2nd Earl of Pembroke ("Strongbow") — was launched in 1960 and grew into the world's best-selling cider brand.

In 2003, after financial difficulties partly blamed on diversification and the costs of its disastrous acquisition strategy, Bulmers was acquired by Scottish & Newcastle. When Scottish & Newcastle was broken up in 2008, the UK business, including Bulmers, passed to Heineken.

Products

  • Strongbow – a dry cider launched in 1960; the world's top-selling cider.
  • Bulmers Original – premium bottled cider, relaunched in 2006 to be served "over ice".
  • Woodpecker – a sweeter cider.
  • Scrumpy Jack and Symonds brands.

Operations

The company operates from its historic site in Hereford, supplied by orchards across Herefordshire and the West Country. Around 40% of the UK's cider apples are grown within 50 miles of Hereford, and Bulmer's remains the region's largest buyer.
