Parol

Parol
Origin: Philippines
Occasion: Christmas season (Simbang Gabi through Epiphany)
Materials: Bamboo or rattan frame; papel de japon, capiz shell, cellophane, or modern LED lighting
Symbolism: Star of Bethlehem; triumph of light over darkness

A parol (pronounced [paˈɾol]) is a Filipino ornamental lantern displayed during the Christmas season. Traditionally constructed using bamboo and Japanese paper (papel de japon), modern parols are made from a variety of materials including plastic, shells, and glass, and are commonly illuminated by electric lights or LEDs. The lantern is a traditional part of the Philippines' Christmas celebrations, which are among the longest in the world, and its most recognisable symbol.

History

The parol's origins lie in the Spanish colonial period and the nine-day dawn Masses of Simbang Gabi: worshippers used lanterns to light their way to church in the early morning darkness. The lantern is closely related to the Mexican farol and to the luminaria traditions brought through the Manila–Acapulco galleon trade. The star shape recalls the Star of Bethlehem that guided the Magi.

Design and festivals

The classic parol is a five-pointed star with two "tails" or streamers. Large elaborate versions use capiz shells and rotating light effects (the trompo mechanism). The city of San Fernando, Pampanga — called the "Christmas Capital of the Philippines" — holds the annual Giant Lantern Festival (Ligligan Parul), where barangays compete with lanterns up to about 6 metres across, choreographed to music. Filipino communities abroad also display parols as a marker of cultural identity; a parol-lighting ceremony is held annually in San Francisco.
