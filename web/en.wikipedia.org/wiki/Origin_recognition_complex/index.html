Origin recognition complex

In molecular biology, the origin recognition complex (ORC) is a multi-subunit DNA-binding protein complex (six subunits, ORC1–ORC6) that binds at origins of replication in an ATP-dependent manner and forms a foundation for the assembly of the pre-replication complex. ORC is essential for the initiation of DNA replication in all eukaryotes and is highly conserved across species, from yeast to humans.

ORC marks origins of replication and recruits the replication machinery.

Discovery

ORC was discovered in the early 1990s in the budding yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae, where it was identified as the protein complex binding to the ARS (autonomously replicating sequence) consensus sequence at yeast origins. Homologous complexes were subsequently found in fission yeast, Drosophila, Xenopus and mammals.

Function in replication licensing

ORC remains bound to origins through much of the cell cycle and acts as a landing pad during late mitosis and G1 phase. It recruits Cdc6 and Cdt1, which together load the MCM2–7 helicase complex onto DNA, forming the pre-replication complex and "licensing" the origin. Once S phase begins, licensing factors are inactivated so that each origin fires at most once per cycle, preventing re-replication.

Species differences

In budding yeast, ORC binds specific DNA sequences; in metazoans, origins lack a strict consensus sequence, and ORC recruitment is influenced by chromatin structure, nucleosome positioning and DNA topology. The largest subunit, ORC1, contains a bromo-adjacent homology (BAH) domain and is degraded and re-synthesised each cell cycle in many organisms, providing an additional layer of control.

Other roles and disease

ORC subunits also participate in heterochromatin formation and transcriptional silencing (in yeast, through interaction with Sir1), sister-chromatid cohesion, and centrosome biology. Mutations in ORC subunit genes (including ORC1, ORC4 and ORC6) cause Meier-Gorlin syndrome, a form of primordial dwarfism characterised by small ears, absent or small kneecaps, and proportionate short stature.
