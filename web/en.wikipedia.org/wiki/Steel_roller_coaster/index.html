Steel roller coaster

A steel roller coaster is a roller coaster whose track is made of steel. Steel coasters offer smoother rides and a far wider range of elements than wooden roller coasters, including inversions, steep drops and launches, because tubular steel track can be bent into precise, complex shapes and steel trains run on polyurethane or nylon wheels with less friction.

History

Early steel coasters included the Matterhorn Bobsleds at Disneyland (1959), the first tubular steel track coaster, and the runaway mine train designs of Arrow Development in the 1960s. The first modern coaster with inversions, Corkscrew at Knott's Berry Farm (1975), demonstrated what steel track made possible. In the 1980s and 1990s manufacturers such as Bolliger & Mabillard, Intamin and Arrow pushed heights and speeds, leading to the first coasters over 200, 300 and 400 feet.

Characteristics

Steel coasters are classified by layout and features: sit-down, inverted, floorless, wing, flying, launched, spinning, and hyper/giga/strata coasters by height. Their smoothness and versatility have made them the dominant form of new large coaster installations worldwide.
