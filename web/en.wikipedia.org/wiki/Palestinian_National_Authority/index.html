Palestinian National Authority

The Palestinian National Authority (PNA; Arabic: السلطة الوطنية الفلسطينية, as-Sulṭa al-Waṭanīya al-Filasṭīnīya) is the formal, official name of the Palestinian Authority — the interim self-government body established in 1994 under the Oslo Accords between Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO). The two names refer to the same institution: "Palestinian National Authority" appears in the founding agreements and in official documents, while "Palestinian Authority" is the common short form. In January 2013, President Mahmoud Abbas decreed that the name "State of Palestine" would replace "Palestinian National Authority" on official documents, seals and stamps, following the UN General Assembly's grant of observer-state status, though the Authority's institutions and functions continued unchanged.

Terminology

The distinction between the PNA and the PLO matters in Palestinian politics: the PLO is the internationally recognised representative of the Palestinian people as a whole, including the diaspora, and it created the Authority to administer the territory from which Israel was to withdraw under Oslo. The PNA's president is elected (in principle) by residents of the West Bank and Gaza, while its Basic Law functions as an interim constitution. In practice the offices of PLO chairman and PNA president have been held by the same person — Yasser Arafat until 2004, then Mahmoud Abbas.

Functions

Under the Oslo framework the National Authority assumed responsibility for civil affairs and internal security in the areas transferred to it, gradually extending to the population centres of the West Bank (Areas A and B) and most of the Gaza Strip, until Hamas displaced it in Gaza in 2007. It operates ministries, a civil service, courts and security branches, and issues passports and identity documents in coordination with Israel. Its finances depend on donor assistance and on clearance revenues — taxes collected by Israel on its behalf — whose periodic withholding has repeatedly triggered fiscal crises.
