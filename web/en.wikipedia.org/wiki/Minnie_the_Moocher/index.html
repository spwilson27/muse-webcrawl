Minnie the Moocher

"Minnie the Moocher" is a jazz song first recorded in 1931 by Cab Calloway and his orchestra. Written by Calloway, Clarence Gaskill, and Irving Mills, it became Calloway's signature song and one of the best-known examples of scat singing, built around the call-and-response refrain "Hi-de-hi-de-hi-de-hi" answered by "Ho-de-ho-de-ho-de-ho".

Content

The lyrics tell of Minnie, a "red-hot hoochie-coocher" who falls in with a "cokey" who introduces her to opium, after which she dreams of the King of Sweden giving her things. The song was inspired in part by earlier songs about "moochers" — people who cadged drinks, food, and drugs — and by street slang of the Harlem jazz world. The recording sold over a million copies and reached number one.

In popular culture

The song appeared in Betty Boop cartoons of the 1930s, with Calloway's voice and rotoscoped dance moves for a ghostly walrus. Calloway performed it in the 1980 film The Blues Brothers, introducing it to a new generation, and it has been inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame.
