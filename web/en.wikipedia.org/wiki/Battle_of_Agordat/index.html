Battle of Agordat

The Battle of Agordat may refer to several engagements fought around Agordat in present-day Eritrea. The best-known are the First Battle of Agordat (or Battle of Serobeti, 1890) and the Second Battle of Agordat of 1893, both fought between the Kingdom of Italy and Mahdist Sudanese forces during the Italian colonisation of Eritrea; and the fighting of 26–31 January 1941 during the East African campaign of the Second World War, when Commonwealth forces advanced through Agordat on their way to the decisive Battle of Keren.

The fort at Agordat guarded the caravan routes between Massawa and Kassala.

First battle (1890)

In June 1890, a Mahdist raiding force moving toward the Italian-protected territories was intercepted near Serobeti, west of Agordat, by Italian-led askari under Captain Fara. The Mahdists were repulsed, and Italy consolidated its hold on the Agordat area, building a fort that anchored the western frontier of the Eritrean colony facing Mahdist-held Kassala.

Second battle (1893)

On 21 December 1893, a Mahdist army of some 10,000–12,000 men under Emir Ahmed Ali advanced from Kassala to attack Agordat. Colonel Giuseppe Arimondi marched from the fort with roughly 2,200 troops, most of them Eritrean askari, and met the Mahdists in a set-piece battle. Disciplined rifle and artillery fire broke the Mahdist attacks; Ahmed Ali was killed and his army routed, leaving around a thousand dead on the field. The victory was one of the most celebrated of Italy's early colonial campaigns, though Italy soon faced a far greater challenge from Ethiopia, culminating in the disaster at Adwa in 1896.

Second World War (1941)

In late January 1941, during the British-led invasion of Italian Eritrea, Indian and British units of the 4th and 5th Indian Infantry Divisions outflanked the Italian positions at Agordat — dominated by Mount Laquetat and the Cochen strongpoint — using the dry Baraka riverbed. After hard fighting on 26–31 January the Italian garrison withdrew toward Keren, and Agordat's airfield and stores fell to the Commonwealth force, opening the road to Asmara.
