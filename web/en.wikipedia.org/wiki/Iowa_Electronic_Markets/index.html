Iowa Electronic Markets

The Iowa Electronic Markets (IEM) are a group of real-money prediction markets (futures markets) operated by the University of Iowa's Tippie College of Business. Traders can buy and sell contracts based on the outcome of political elections, economic indicators, and other events, with payouts tied to actual results. The IEM is a non-profit research and teaching tool.

History

The markets were founded in 1988 by professors including George Neumann, Robert Forsythe, and Forrest Nelson, beginning with the 1988 U.S. presidential election. They operate under "no-action" letters from the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission, which permit the markets for academic purposes subject to limits on the number of traders and the size of accounts (investments are capped at a few hundred dollars).

Forecasting accuracy

The IEM's election markets have attracted sustained research attention because their prices have often predicted vote shares more accurately than opinion polls, particularly close to election day. In a widely cited comparison, the Iowa markets outperformed major national polls in a large majority of head-to-head comparisons over several election cycles. The markets are a leading practical example cited in the literature on prediction markets and the "wisdom of crowds".

Market types

Principal markets include winner-take-all markets, which pay according to which candidate wins, and vote-share markets, whose contracts pay in proportion to the popular-vote share received by each candidate.
