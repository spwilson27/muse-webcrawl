Maurice Richard

Joseph Henri Maurice "Rocket" Richard
Born: August 4, 1921, Montreal, Quebec
Died: May 27, 2000, Montreal
Position: Right wing
Team: Montreal Canadiens (1942–1960)

Joseph Henri Maurice Richard (August 4, 1921 – May 27, 2000), nicknamed "the Rocket," was a Canadian professional ice hockey player who played 18 seasons in the National Hockey League (NHL) for the Montreal Canadiens. He was the first player in NHL history to score 50 goals in one season (in 1944–45, in only 50 games), and the first to reach 500 career goals.

Career

Richard was known for his explosive speed, powerful shot, and fierce intensity. He won the Hart Trophy as league MVP in 1947 and helped Montreal win eight Stanley Cups. In 1955 his suspension by league president Clarence Campbell after a violent incident sparked the Richard Riot in Montreal on St. Patrick's Day, an event of lasting cultural significance in Quebec.

Legacy

Richard retired in 1960 as the NHL's all-time leading goal scorer. The Maurice "Rocket" Richard Trophy, awarded annually to the league's top goal scorer, was named in his honour. He remains an icon of French-Canadian identity and pride.
