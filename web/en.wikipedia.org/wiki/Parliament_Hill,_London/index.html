Parliament Hill, London

Parliament Hill
Location: Hampstead Heath, north-west London
Elevation: 98 m (322 ft)
Managed by: City of London Corporation (as part of Hampstead Heath)
Former name: Traitors' Hill

Parliament Hill is an area of open parkland in the south-east corner of Hampstead Heath in north-west London. The hill, rising to 98 metres (322 ft), offers one of the most celebrated protected views over the London skyline, taking in the Palace of Westminster, St Paul's Cathedral, the City, and Canary Wharf. The viewpoint is legally protected under the London View Management Framework.

Name and history

The hill was formerly known as Traitors' Hill. According to tradition, its present name derives from the English Civil War, when it was occupied by troops loyal to Parliament — alternatively, from the story that Guy Fawkes and his fellow conspirators planned to watch the destruction of Parliament from the hill during the Gunpowder Plot of 1605. Parliament Hill Fields, comprising the hill and surrounding sports grounds, were purchased for the public in 1888 and later administered as part of Hampstead Heath.

The hill today

Parliament Hill is popular for walking, kite flying, and cross-country running, and is the home of the Highgate Harriers' cross-country course; the National Cross Country Championships are held there regularly. The nearby Parliament Hill Lido (opened 1938) and athletics track serve the surrounding neighbourhoods of Gospel Oak, Highgate, and Hampstead. "Parliament Hill" also names a small residential area and a girls' secondary school at the foot of the hill.
