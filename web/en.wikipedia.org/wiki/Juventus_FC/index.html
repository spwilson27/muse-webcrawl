Juventus Football Club is a professional football club based in Turin, Piedmont, Italy, that competes in Serie A, the top flight of Italian football. Founded in 1897 by a group of Torinese students, the club has worn a black and white striped home kit since 1903 and has played home matches in different grounds around the city, since 2011 at the Juventus Stadium.

History

Juventus — from the Latin for "youth" — won its first league championship in 1905. The Agnelli family, founders of Fiat, took control in 1923, beginning an association that has lasted a century; the club won five consecutive titles in the early 1930s (the Quinquennio d'Oro). Under Giovanni Trapattoni in the late 1970s and 1980s, Juventus won six league titles and, in 1985, the European Cup — a triumph overshadowed by the Heysel Stadium disaster. Marcello Lippi's side won the UEFA Champions League in 1996.

The Calciopoli scandal of 2006 saw Juventus stripped of two titles and relegated to Serie B for the first time; the club returned immediately and, under Antonio Conte and then Massimiliano Allegri, won a record nine consecutive Serie A titles from 2011–12 to 2019–20, reaching two more Champions League finals (2015, 2017).

Honours

Juventus is Italy's most successful club, with a record number of Serie A championships (36 officially recognised), a record 15 Coppa Italia titles, and 9 Supercoppa Italiana titles. Internationally the club has won two European Cups/Champions Leagues, three UEFA Cups, two Intercontinental Cups and the European Cup Winners' Cup, becoming the first club to win all three major UEFA competitions.

Identity and support

Nicknamed La Vecchia Signora ("The Old Lady"), Juventus is widely regarded as Italy's most supported club, with a fan base extending far beyond Piedmont due to migration of Fiat workers. Its main rivalries are with city rivals Torino (the Derby della Mole), Inter Milan (the Derby d'Italia), AC Milan and Fiorentina. Famous players include Giampiero Boniperti, Michel Platini, Roberto Baggio, Alessandro Del Piero, Zinedine Zidane, Gianluigi Buffon and Cristiano Ronaldo.
