The Fields Medal is a prize awarded to two, three, or four mathematicians under 40 years of age at the International Congress of the International Mathematical Union (IMU), a meeting that takes place every four years. It is often regarded as the highest honour a mathematician can receive, and has been described as the Nobel Prize of mathematics.

History

The medal was first awarded in 1936, using funds bequeathed by the Canadian mathematician John Charles Fields, who organised the 1924 International Congress of Mathematicians in Toronto and endowed the prize. The age limit reflects Fields's wish that the award both recognise past work and encourage future achievement. The front of the medal depicts Archimedes and a Latin inscription; the reverse bears the phrase Congregati ex toto orbe mathematici ob scripta insignia tribuere — "the mathematicians having congregated from the whole world awarded [this] for outstanding writings".

Notable recipients and events

Recipients include Lars Ahlfors and Jesse Douglas (1936), Laurent Schwartz, Atle Selberg, Alexander Grothendieck — who declined to attend the 1966 ceremony in Moscow — Grigori Perelman, who declined the medal in 2006, and Maryam Mirzakhani (2014), the first woman to receive it. Maryna Viazovska (2022) was the second woman. In 2018 the medal awarded to Caucher Birkar was stolen shortly after the ceremony; the IMU presented a replacement.

Conditions

A candidate's 40th birthday must not occur before 1 January of the award year. A monetary award of CA$15,000 accompanies the medal.
