The Maliseet (also spelled Malecite; self-designation Wolastoqiyik, "people of the beautiful river") are an Indigenous people of the Northeastern Woodlands. Their traditional territory centres on the Saint John River valley — which they call the Wolastoq — in present-day New Brunswick and Quebec in Canada and Maine in the United States.

History

The Maliseet traditionally lived by hunting, fishing and farming along the river, growing corn, beans and squash, and travelling seasonally by birchbark canoe. They were members of the Wabanaki Confederacy alongside the Mi'kmaq, Passamaquoddy, Penobscot and Abenaki peoples. After European contact they participated in the fur trade and were drawn into colonial conflicts between France and Britain.

Communities

Today Maliseet First Nations in New Brunswick include Tobique, Woodstock, Kingsclear, Saint Mary's, Oromocto and Madawaska, and the Viger First Nation in Quebec. In Maine, the Houlton Band of Maliseet Indians is federally recognised.

Language

The Maliseet language, part of the Maliseet-Passamaquoddy language, is an Eastern Algonquian language. It is endangered, with revitalisation efforts under way in several communities.
