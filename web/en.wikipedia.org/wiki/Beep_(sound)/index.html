Beep (sound)

Beep

TypeShort single-tone sound
UsesAlarms, notifications, censorship

A beep is a short, single-tone sound typically produced by electronic devices as a signal or alert. Beeps are generated by equipment such as alarm clocks, timers, telephones, medical monitors, vehicle reversing alarms and computers, where they serve to attract attention or indicate the completion of an action.

In computing

In computing, a beep historically came from the PC speaker, controlled through the ASCII bell character (BEL, code 7), a legacy of teletype machines whose bell signalled the operator. Command-line interfaces and terminals still use the "system beep" for errors and notifications. Error beep codes emitted by a computer's firmware during power-on self-test can indicate hardware faults, with patterns of long and short beeps identifying the failing component.

Censorship

A sustained beep, usually a 1 kHz sine tone, is conventionally used in broadcasting to mask profanity or other content deemed unsuitable for transmission, a practice known as "bleeping". The technique originated in radio and television and remains common in edited programming. Beeps are also used in answering machines, voicemail prompts and hearing tests (audiometry).
