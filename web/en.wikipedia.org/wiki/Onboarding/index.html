Onboarding

Onboarding or organizational socialization is the mechanism through which new employees acquire the necessary knowledge, skills, and behaviors to become effective organizational members. The term was introduced in management literature in the 1970s and is now a central topic in human resources and organizational psychology.

Process

Onboarding typically begins before or on the first day of work and may extend for months. Common elements include paperwork and compliance, orientation to facilities and tools, introduction to colleagues and mentors, explanation of role expectations, and training in organizational culture and values. Some models describe the "four Cs" of onboarding: compliance, clarification, culture, and connection.

Outcomes

Research links effective onboarding to higher job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and performance, and to lower turnover. Poor onboarding, conversely, is associated with early attrition — a significant share of new hires who quit do so within the first months. Organizations increasingly use structured programs, buddy systems, and digital onboarding portals.

Related uses

In software, "user onboarding" describes the process by which new users learn an application and experience its core value, often through tutorials, guided tours, and progressive disclosure.
