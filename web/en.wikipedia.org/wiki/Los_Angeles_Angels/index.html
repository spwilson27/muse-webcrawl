The Los Angeles Angels are an American professional baseball team based in the Los Angeles metropolitan area. The Angels compete in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member club of the American League (AL) West Division. Since 1966 the team has played its home games at Angel Stadium in Anaheim, California.

History

The franchise was founded in Los Angeles in 1961 by singing cowboy Gene Autry as one of the first two expansion teams of the American League. The team moved to Anaheim in 1966 and has played under several names, including the California Angels (1965–1996), Anaheim Angels (1997–2004) and Los Angeles Angels of Anaheim (2005–2015), before adopting the current name in 2016.

2002 World Series

The highlight of the franchise's history came in 2002, when, under manager Mike Scioscia, the Angels won their first pennant and defeated the San Francisco Giants in seven games to win the World Series. The team was famous for its "Rally Monkey" and thundersticks, and for outfielder Darin Erstad, first baseman Scott Spiezio and pitcher Francisco Rodríguez.

Recent era

In the 2010s and 2020s the Angels have featured two of the game's biggest stars: center fielder Mike Trout, widely regarded as one of the greatest players of his generation, and two-way player Shohei Ohtani, who won the American League MVP award with the club in 2021 and 2023 before departing for the Los Angeles Dodgers after the 2023 season.
