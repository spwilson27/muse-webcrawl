Di (Five Barbarians)

The Di founded several states in northern China during the Sixteen Kingdoms period.

The Di (Chinese: 氐) were an ancient ethnic group that lived in western China during the Han dynasty and the Period of Disunion, and are counted among the "Five Barbarians" (Wu Hu) of Chinese historiography, alongside the Xiongnu, Xianbei, Qiang, and Jie. The Di inhabited the borderlands of present-day Gansu, Shaanxi, and Sichuan.

History

Di groups are recorded as early as the Zhou dynasty as peoples of the western frontiers. Under the Han and the Jin dynasty, many Di were resettled within China proper, and Di chieftains were given Chinese surnames such as Li, Yang, and Fu. During the collapse of the Western Jin in the early 4th century, Di leaders founded several of the Sixteen Kingdoms: Li Xiong established Cheng-Han in Sichuan (304), and Fu Jian of the Former Qin (351–394) briefly unified nearly all of northern China before his catastrophic defeat at the Battle of Fei River in 383. Later Di rulers founded the Later Chouchi and other small states, and the Di leader Yang Maosou's descendants ruled the Chouchi kingdoms for centuries.

Culture and fate

The Di spoke a language that is poorly attested and variously classified, often linked to the Qiangic or Tibeto-Burman sphere. Over the following centuries the Di were gradually assimilated into the Han Chinese and Tibetan populations and disappeared as a distinct people; some scholars connect them with the modern Baima people of Sichuan and Gansu.
