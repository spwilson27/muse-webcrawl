Funicular

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The two counterbalanced cars of a funicular pass each other at the midway loop.

A funicular (also called a cliff railway or inclined plane) is a cable railway in which a cable attached to a pair of tram-like vehicles on rails moves them up and down a steep slope; the ascending and descending vehicles counterbalance each other. The name derives from the Latin funiculus, a diminutive of funis, "rope".

Operation

Two cars are permanently attached to opposite ends of the same haulage cable, which passes around a pulley at the top station. As one car descends, it helps pull the other up, so the motor at the top station need only overcome friction and the difference in load. Many lines have a single pair of rails shared for most of their length, splitting into two tracks at the midpoint so the cars can pass; others have separate tracks throughout (four-rail or two-rail layouts). Historic systems used water ballast: tanks in the descending car were filled at the top to outweigh the ascending car, then emptied at the bottom — a system still operating on lines such as the Nerobergbahn in Wiesbaden.

History

The earliest funiculars served mines and canals: inclined planes hauled boats and tubs of coal in the 18th century. The Reisszug at Salzburg Fortress (c. 1515) is considered one of the oldest surviving cable railways. Passenger funiculars proliferated in the 19th century in hilly cities and resorts — examples include the Funiculaire de Lyon (1862), Lisbon's Glória Funicular (1885), Istanbul's Tünel (1875, underground), and the cliff railways of British seaside towns. Los Angeles's Angels Flight (1901) is a famous surviving urban example.

Notable funiculars

  • Gelmerbahn, Switzerland — one of the steepest, with a gradient of 106%.
  • Stoosbahn, Switzerland — opened 2017, gradient up to 110%, with rotating cylindrical cabins.
  • Duquesne Incline and Monongahela Incline, Pittsburgh, USA.
  • Valparaíso, Chile — a network of ascensores, several designated historic monuments.
  • Peak Tram, Hong Kong — in service since 1888.
