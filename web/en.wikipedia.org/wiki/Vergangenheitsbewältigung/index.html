Vergangenheitsbewältigung

Vergangenheitsbewältigung (German for "struggle of overcoming the past" or "coming to terms with the past") is the public and scholarly process by which post-war Germany has confronted the crimes of the Nazi regime, especially the Holocaust. It encompasses criminal trials, reparations, education, memorial culture (Erinnerungskultur), and continuing debates about German responsibility and identity.

Origins and phases

The term gained currency in the Federal Republic of Germany in the 1950s–60s. The immediate post-war years combined Allied-imposed denazification and the Nuremberg trials with widespread silence and reintegration of former Nazis. A generational shift came in the 1960s: the Eichmann trial (1961) and the Frankfurt Auschwitz trials (1963–65) forced public confrontation, and the student movement of 1968 accused the parent generation of complicity. In 1970 Chancellor Willy Brandt's Kniefall at the Warsaw Ghetto memorial symbolised contrition; the 1979 broadcast of the Holocaust miniseries reached a mass audience.

Historians' dispute and memory culture

The Historikerstreit of 1986–87 pitted historians who stressed the singularity of Nazi crimes against those who sought to relativise them in comparison with Stalinism. Germany built an extensive memorial landscape: the Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe in Berlin (2005), documentation centres, preserved concentration camps, and the decentralised Stolpersteine ("stumbling stones") set into pavements before victims' homes. School curricula make the Nazi period compulsory study.

Reunification and beyond

After 1990 a second Vergangenheitsbewältigung addressed the East German dictatorship, through the Stasi records authority and trials of border officials. Debates continue over colonial crimes (including recognition of the Herero and Nama genocide), far-right attempts to declare a Schlussstrich ("final line") under memory, and comparisons with other countries' reckonings, such as Austria's later confrontation with its Nazi past or South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission.
