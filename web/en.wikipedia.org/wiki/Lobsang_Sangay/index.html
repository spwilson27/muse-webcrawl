Lobsang Sangay

Lobsang Sangay (born 5 September 1968) is a Tibetan-American politician and legal scholar who served as the Sikyong (President) of the Central Tibetan Administration, the Tibetan government-in-exile based in Dharamsala, India, from 2011 to 2021. He was the first democratically elected political leader of the Tibetan exile community after the 14th Dalai Lama devolved his political authority in 2011.

Early life and education

Sangay was born in Darjeeling, West Bengal, to Tibetan refugee parents and grew up in a refugee settlement. He studied at the Central School for Tibetans in Darjeeling and earned BA and law degrees at the University of Delhi. As a Fulbright scholar he went to Harvard Law School, where he earned an LLM and, in 2004, a Doctor of Juridical Science (SJD), the first Tibetan to do so. His dissertation addressed democracy in exile and Chinese constitutional law regarding Tibet.

Political career

In the 2011 election for Kalon Tripa (the office retitled Sikyong in 2012) Sangay defeated Tenzin Tethong and Tashi Wangdi, and was sworn in on 8 August 2011. His election followed the Dalai Lama's decision to retire from political office, making Sangay the political face of the Tibetan movement. He advocated the "Middle Way Approach" of seeking genuine autonomy for Tibet within China, pursued international support for the Tibetan cause, and was re-elected in 2016. He was succeeded by Penpa Tsering in May 2021.
