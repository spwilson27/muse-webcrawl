Batman: Knightfall

"Knightfall" is a 1993–1994 Batman story arc published by DC Comics. It consists of three major storylines — "Knightfall", "Knightquest" and "KnightsEnd" — that ran across the Batman family of titles, written principally by Doug Moench, Chuck Dixon, Alan Grant and others. The arc introduces the villain Bane, a criminal mastermind who engineers the mass release of Gotham City's most dangerous criminals from Arkham Asylum, exhausting Batman before confronting and defeating him, breaking his back. Jean-Paul Valley (Azrael) takes up the mantle of Batman in Bruce Wayne's absence, but his increasingly violent methods force a recovered Wayne to reclaim the role.

Plot

Bane, raised in the prison of Peña Duro on the island of Santa Prisca and enhanced by the steroid Venom, escapes and comes to Gotham to destroy Batman, whom he sees as the city's ruling "king". Rather than face him directly, Bane blows open Arkham Asylum, forcing an already ailing Batman to recapture the Joker, the Scarecrow, the Mad Hatter, Poison Ivy, Firefly, the Ventriloquist and many others in a draining gauntlet. When Batman returns to Wayne Manor, Bane ambushes him, defeats him, and breaks his spine over his knee, then throws the crippled Batman from a rooftop into the street.

Paraplegic, Bruce Wayne appoints Jean-Paul Valley as his successor. Valley redesigns the Batsuit with built-in weaponry, defeats Bane, but becomes increasingly brutal and unstable, letting the serial killer Abattoir die and alienating Robin. In "KnightsEnd", Wayne — healed with the help of Dr. Shondra Kinsolving and Lady Shiva's training — defeats Valley after a final duel and reclaims the mantle, entrusting it temporarily to Dick Grayson in the follow-up "Prodigal" storyline.

Legacy

"Knightfall" is regarded as one of the defining Batman sagas of the 1990s, notable for its depiction of Batman as physically and psychologically fallible. The back-breaking scene has been repeatedly homaged, most prominently in the 2012 film The Dark Knight Rises, in which Bane similarly breaks Batman. The storyline has been adapted into a BBC radio dramatisation and novelisation, and its collected editions remain in print.
