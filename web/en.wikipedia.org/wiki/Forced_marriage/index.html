Forced marriage

A forced marriage is a marriage in which one or both of the parties is married without their consent or against their will. A marriage can also become forced when a person is not able (because of age, capacity, or other constraints) to consent and only has the option of leaving the marriage or staying. Forced marriage is distinct from arranged marriage, in which both parties consent to the assistance of their parents or a third party in choosing a spouse, although the line between the two can be blurred where consent is not freely given.

Overview

Forced marriage is recognised internationally as a human rights violation. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (Article 16) states that marriage shall be entered into only with the free and full consent of the intending spouses, and the practice is addressed by instruments including the 1956 Supplementary Convention on the Abolition of Slavery and CEDAW. UNICEF estimates that more than 650 million women alive today were married as children, and child marriage — marriage of a person under 18 — is widely regarded as a form of forced marriage because minors cannot give full, free and informed consent.

Causes and context

Factors associated with forced marriage include poverty and dowry or bride-price economics, traditions concerning family honour and female sexuality, the desire to control behaviour, settling debts or disputes, inheritance considerations, and immigration-related motives. Forced marriage occurs in many regions and communities worldwide, and can also occur through abduction (sometimes called marriage by capture) or in armed conflict, where armed groups abduct women and girls as "wives". Male victims also exist, particularly minors and men with disabilities, though they are less frequently reported.

Consequences and responses

Victims may face domestic violence, sexual abuse, early and repeated pregnancy with associated health risks, interrupted education, isolation, and psychological harm. Many countries have criminalised forced marriage or created civil protection remedies — for example, the United Kingdom's Forced Marriage (Civil Protection) Act 2007 and the criminalisation of forced marriage in England and Wales in 2014 — and specialist units and NGOs provide rescue, shelter, and legal support to people at risk.
