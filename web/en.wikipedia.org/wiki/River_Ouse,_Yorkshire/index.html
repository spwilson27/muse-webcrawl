River Ouse, Yorkshire

The River Ouse in Yorkshire, England, is a continuation of the River Ure, formed where the Ure is joined by the River Swale at Swale Nab near Linton-on-Ouse. It flows south-east through the city of York and Selby, and at Trent Falls meets the River Trent to form the Humber Estuary. Measured from the source of the Ure, the Ure–Ouse system is about 208 km (129 mi) long, one of the longest river systems wholly in one English county.

Course and tributaries

From Swale Nab the Ouse flows past Aldwark and into York, where it is crossed by Lendal, Ouse and Skeldergate bridges and joined by the small River Foss. Continuing south through Naburn — site of a lock that marks the head of the tidal river — it passes Cawood and Selby, receives the Derwent from the east coast hills and the Aire at Airmyn, and at Goole receives the Don. Below Goole it joins the Trent at Trent Falls to begin the Humber.

Navigation

The Ouse was for centuries a major trading highway: York was a significant inland port, and goods still move by water to the ports at Goole and Howdendyke. The river is navigable for commercial vessels as far as York for smaller craft; the tidal section below Naburn has strongly shifting channels. Together with the Aire and Calder Navigation it connects the Humber with Leeds and the trans-Pennine waterway network.

The York reach is noted for flooding; the Kings Arms pub on King's Staith famously records flood levels, and the York Flood Alleviation Scheme raised and extended defences following major floods including those of 2000 and December 2015.

Name

"Ouse" is a common English river name of Celtic origin, thought to mean simply "water" or "river"; several unrelated English rivers bear it, including the Great Ouse of East Anglia.
