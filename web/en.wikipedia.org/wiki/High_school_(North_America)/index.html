High school (North America)

A high school in North America is a school for secondary education, typically serving students in grades 9 through 12 (ages roughly 14 to 18) in the United States and Canada. It follows elementary and middle school (or junior high), and its completion — marked by a high school diploma — is generally the end of compulsory education and the usual qualification for entry to college or university.

Structure

Students are commonly classified by year: freshman (grade 9), sophomore (grade 10), junior (grade 11) and senior (grade 12). The curriculum combines required subjects (English, mathematics, science, social studies, and often a foreign language and physical education) with electives. Most public high schools are run by local school districts, funded largely by local and state taxes; private and charter schools also operate widely.

Culture

High schools are notable for their extracurricular programmes — interscholastic sports (notably American football and basketball), marching band, theatre, debate and student government — and for social events such as homecoming and prom. Standardised tests such as the SAT and ACT, taken in the junior and senior years, feature in US college admissions.

History

The first public high school in the United States, the English High School of Boston, opened in 1821. The "high school movement" of 1910–1940 rapidly expanded provision, transforming secondary education from an elite preparatory stage into mass education.
