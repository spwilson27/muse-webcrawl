Self-defense

Self-defense
TypeLegal justification; physical practice
ElementsImminent threat, proportionality, necessity

Self-defense (self-defence in Commonwealth spelling) is the use of force to protect oneself — or in many jurisdictions, others — from imminent harm. As a legal doctrine it justifies otherwise unlawful force when it is necessary and proportionate to an unlawful threat.

Legal principles

Common requirements include an honest and reasonable belief of imminent attack, proportionality of response, and, in some jurisdictions, a duty to retreat where safely possible — contrasted with "stand-your-ground" laws and the castle doctrine for the home. In international law, Article 51 of the UN Charter preserves states' inherent right of self-defence against armed attack.

Physical practice

As a practice, self-defense training draws on martial arts and combat systems (e.g., Krav Maga, jiu-jitsu, boxing), awareness and de-escalation, and lawful use of defensive tools whose legality varies widely by country. Women's self-defense courses and empowerment-based models are a significant branch of instruction.
