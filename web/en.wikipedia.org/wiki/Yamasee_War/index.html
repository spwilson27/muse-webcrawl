The Yamasee War (also spelled Yemassee War) was a conflict fought in colonial South Carolina from 1715 to 1717 between British settlers and a broad alliance of Native American peoples, principally the Yamasee, joined by the Lower Creek, Catawba, Apalachee, and others. It was one of the most severe challenges ever faced by a British colony in North America, killing roughly 400 colonists — about seven percent of South Carolina's European population.

Causes

Relations had deteriorated over abuses in the deerskin and Indian slave trades: traders cheated Native partners, debts mounted, and enslavement of Native people continued despite official promises. Fearing collective punishment and enslavement for unpaid debts, Yamasee leaders decided to strike first.

Course of the war

On Good Friday, 15 April 1715, Yamasee warriors killed the colonial emissaries who had come to negotiate, then attacked Port Royal and settlements across the lowcountry. Frontier settlements were abandoned as colonists crowded into Charles Town. The tide turned after the Cherokee, courted by both sides, decided in early 1716 to ally with the colonists and attack the Creek, shattering the Native coalition. Fighting dwindled through 1717.

Consequences

The surviving Yamasee fled south to Spanish Florida. The war devastated the Native slave trade, pushed South Carolina toward greater reliance on enslaved African labour, and contributed to the colonists' overthrow of the Lords Proprietors in 1719, after which South Carolina became a royal colony.
