Chukotko-Kamchatkan languages

Distribution of the Chukotko-Kamchatkan languages

The Chukotko-Kamchatkan or Chukchi–Kamchatkan languages are a language family of languages spoken in the Russian Far East, specifically the Chukotka Autonomous Okrug and Kamchatka Krai. The family is sometimes grouped with other Siberian languages under the Paleosiberian umbrella, a grouping of convenience rather than a demonstrated genetic relationship.

Branches

The family has two branches. The Chukotkan branch includes Chukchi, Koryak, Alutor, and the extinct Kerek. The Kamchatkan branch includes Western Itelmen, the only surviving Kamchatkan language; Southern and Eastern Kamchatkan languages are extinct. All of the languages are endangered, with fluent speakers concentrated among older generations.

Typology

Chukotko-Kamchatkan languages are polysynthetic and agglutinative, making extensive use of noun incorporation. The Chukotkan languages are ergative in alignment, while Itelmen differs in a number of structural respects, leading some scholars to question whether the two branches are truly related.
