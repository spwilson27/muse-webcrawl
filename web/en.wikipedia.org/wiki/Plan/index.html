Plan

A plan is typically a diagram or a list of steps with details of timing and resources, used to achieve an objective or goal. It is commonly understood as a temporal set of intended actions through which one expects to achieve a goal. Plans can be formal or informal, written or held in mind, detailed or sketchy.

Types of plans

Structured types of plans include business plans, strategic plans, project plans, contingency plans, and lesson plans. In centrally organised economies, an economic plan sets production and investment targets, as in the Soviet five-year plans. In urban and regional studies, planning determines land use and development; in architecture and engineering, a "plan" is specifically a drawing representing a horizontal section or a view from above, as in a floor plan or site plan.

Planning

Planning is the process of creating a plan: analysing a situation, setting goals, identifying actions and resources, sequencing steps, and anticipating obstacles and contingencies. Management theory since Henri Fayol has treated planning as a primary function of management. Psychologists distinguish planning (a cognitive process of selecting and ordering means to ends) from related activities such as scheduling and forecasting.

Quotations

Planning has generated many aphorisms. Dwight D. Eisenhower remarked that "plans are worthless, but planning is everything", emphasising the value of the process over the document; the Prussian field marshal Helmuth von Moltke observed that no plan of operations survives first contact with the enemy — a point echoed in the saying that everyone has a plan until they get punched in the mouth.
