Louis Majorelle

NationalityFrench
OccupationFurniture designer and manufacturer; decorator

Louis-Jean-Sylvestre Majorelle, usually known simply as Louis Majorelle (26 September 1859 – 15 January 1926), was a French decorator and furniture designer who manufactured his own designs in the traditions of the ébéniste (cabinetmaker). He was one of the outstanding designers of the Art Nouveau era and a leading figure of the École de Nancy.

Career

Majorelle was born in Toul, the son of Auguste Majorelle, a furniture designer and manufacturer. The family moved to Nancy in 1861, and Louis studied briefly in Paris, including at the École des Beaux-Arts, before returning to take over the family business on his father's death in 1879. The firm initially produced furniture in historicist styles, but in the 1890s Majorelle, influenced by Émile Gallé, turned to designs based on natural forms, with carved mahogany, exotic veneers and marquetry of flowers, water lilies and orchids.

By 1900 Majorelle's firm was one of the largest luxury furniture producers in France, with workshops and retail galleries in Nancy, Paris, Lille and London. At the Paris Exposition Universelle of 1900 his work achieved international acclaim. Majorelle also produced lamps and metalwork in collaboration with the Daum glassworks, and designed his own house in Nancy, the Villa Majorelle (1901–1902), built by architect Henri Sauvage — considered the first fully Art Nouveau house in Nancy.

Later years

After the First World War, Majorelle's style moved toward the cleaner lines that anticipated Art Deco. His workshops suffered from a fire in 1916 and the changed tastes of the 1920s, but the firm continued under his son Jacques until production ceased in 1931. Majorelle died in Nancy in 1926.
