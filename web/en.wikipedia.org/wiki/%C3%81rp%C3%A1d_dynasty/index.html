Árpád dynasty

Árpád dynasty
Country: Principality and Kingdom of Hungary
Founder: Álmos / Árpád
Ruled: c. 855/895 – 1301

The Árpád dynasty was the ruling dynasty of the Principality of Hungary in the 9th and 10th centuries and of the Kingdom of Hungary from 1000 to 1301. It is named after Árpád, the Grand Prince who led the Magyars into the Carpathian Basin around 895.

History

Árpád's descendant Stephen I was crowned the first King of Hungary in 1000 (or 1001) and Christianised the realm; he was later canonised, as were his son Emeric and King Ladislaus I. Árpádian kings such as Béla III and Béla IV presided over the medieval kingdom's expansion and its recovery from the Mongol invasion of 1241–42.

End of the dynasty

The male line died out with Andrew III in 1301, after which the crown passed, through female-line descendants, to the Capetian House of Anjou. The dynasty produced more canonised saints than any other European royal house, and the striped Árpád banner remains a Hungarian national symbol.
