Pressure vessel

A pressure vessel is a container designed to hold gases or liquids at a pressure substantially different from the ambient pressure. They are used throughout industry — in chemical and petrochemical plants, oil and gas production, power generation, diving cylinders, autoclaves, and compressed-gas storage.

Design

Pressure vessels are commonly cylindrical or spherical. A sphere distributes stress most evenly, but large spheres are costly to fabricate, so most vessels are cylinders with hemispherical or ellipsoidal heads (dished ends). Wall thickness is calculated so that membrane stresses remain within allowable limits for the material, with design codes specifying factors of safety. Vessels are equipped with pressure relief valves or rupture discs to prevent overpressure, along with nozzles, supports, and inspection openings.

Materials and construction

Most vessels are made of carbon or stainless steel, welded under strict quality control and tested hydrostatically before service. Composite overwrapped vessels — metal liners wrapped with carbon fibre — are used where light weight matters, as in aerospace and breathing apparatus. The ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code is the principal design standard in North America; the European Union uses the Pressure Equipment Directive.

Safety

Because a failed vessel can release enormous stored energy, pressure vessels are regulated and periodically inspected in most jurisdictions. Historical boiler explosions in the 19th and early 20th centuries were a major impetus for codified standards and inspection regimes.
