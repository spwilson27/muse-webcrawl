Indian literature


Indian literature across the ages

Indian literature refers to the literature produced on the Indian subcontinent from ancient times to the present, in dozens of languages and in oral as well as written traditions. India's 22 officially recognized languages each sustain major literary traditions, and the Constitution's Eighth Schedule reflects a multilingual heritage without parallel in scale.

Ancient and classical literature

The earliest Indian literature is the Sanskrit corpus of the Vedas (c. 1500–500 BCE), composed orally and transmitted with extraordinary fidelity. The great Sanskrit epics, the Mahābhārata and the Rāmāyaṇa, stand at the centre of the tradition, alongside classical poetry and drama by authors such as Kālidāsa. In the south, the Sangam literature of ancient Tamil (c. 300 BCE–300 CE) represents an independent classical tradition. Prakrit and Pali literatures preserve Jain and Buddhist texts, including the Jātaka tales.

Medieval and early modern literature

The medieval centuries saw the flowering of bhakti (devotional) poetry in regional languages — the works of Kabir, Mirabai, Tulsidas, and the Tamil Alvars and Nayanars — as well as Persian-influenced Urdu poetry at Mughal courts. Regional literatures in Bengali, Marathi, Kannada, Malayalam, Telugu, Assamese, and other languages developed distinctive genres and canons.

Modern literature

The nineteenth century brought print, the novel, and reformist writing, exemplified by Bankim Chandra Chatterjee in Bengali and culminated in Rabindranath Tagore, the first non-European Nobel laureate in Literature (1913). The twentieth century produced major writers in every Indian language, including Premchand in Hindi-Urdu, and a globally prominent Indian English literature — from R. K. Narayan, Mulk Raj Anand, and Raja Rao to Salman Rushdie, Arundhati Roy, and Amitav Ghosh.
