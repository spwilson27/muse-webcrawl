Bełżec extermination camp
Location: Near Bełżec, occupied Poland
Operated: March – December 1942
Part of: Operation Reinhard
Victims: approx. 430,000–500,000 Jews

Bełżec was a Nazi German extermination camp built by the SS during World War II near the village of Bełżec in occupied Poland. It was the first of the Operation Reinhard camps to begin gassing operations, in March 1942.

Operation

Jews from the General Government and elsewhere were deported by rail to Bełżec and murdered in gas chambers using engine exhaust. An estimated 430,000 to 500,000 people were killed there; only a handful survived the war.

Closure and concealment

The camp was closed in December 1942; the SS exhumed and burned the bodies and dismantled the site to conceal evidence.

Memorial

A memorial and museum opened on the site in 2004 following archaeological investigations that located the mass graves.
