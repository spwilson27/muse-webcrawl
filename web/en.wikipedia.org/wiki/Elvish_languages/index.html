Elvish languages

The Elvish languages are a family of constructed languages created by J. R. R. Tolkien for the Elves of his fictional world of Middle-earth. Tolkien, a philologist by profession, began developing his Elvish tongues as early as 1910–1911, and they evolved alongside his legendarium for the rest of his life.

Principal languages

The two most developed Elvish languages are Quenya, the ceremonial High-Elven tongue, and Sindarin, the everyday language of the Grey Elves of Beleriand. Both derive in Tolkien's internal history from a common ancestral language, Primitive Quendian, through Eldarin.

Quenya was influenced in its phonology by Finnish, Latin, and Greek, while Sindarin was modeled partly on Welsh, with its characteristic mutations of initial consonants. Tolkien also sketched many other Elvish languages, including Telerin, Nandorin, and the Avarin tongues.

Writing systems

The Elves are credited in the fiction with devising the Tengwar script of Fëanor and the earlier Cirth runes of Daeron. Tolkien provided detailed descriptions of these scripts in the appendices to The Lord of the Rings.

Influence

Tolkien's Elvish languages are among the most elaborate constructed languages created for fiction, and they have inspired extensive study, journals, and dictionaries maintained by enthusiasts and scholars.
