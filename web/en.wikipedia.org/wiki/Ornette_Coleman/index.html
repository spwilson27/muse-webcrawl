Randolph Denard Ornette Coleman (9 March 1930 – 11 June 2015) was an American jazz saxophonist, trumpeter, violinist, and composer. He was one of the founders of free jazz, and his 1960 album Free Jazz: A Collective Improvisation gave the movement its name. His melodic, blues-rooted improvisation freed jazz from fixed chord changes and profoundly influenced modern music.

Career

Born in Fort Worth, Texas, Coleman taught himself saxophone and worked in rhythm-and-blues bands before developing his distinctive approach in Los Angeles. His recordings for Contemporary Records, beginning with Something Else!!!! (1958), and his classic quartet albums for Atlantic — including The Shape of Jazz to Come (1959) and Change of the Century (1960) — featured trumpeter Don Cherry, bassist Charlie Haden, and drummers Billy Higgins and Ed Blackwell. His 1959 residency at New York's Five Spot Café was a sensation that sharply divided the jazz world.

Harmolodics and later work

Coleman called his philosophy of improvisation "harmolodics", in which harmony, melody, and rhythm are given equal weight and no player merely accompanies. In the 1970s he formed the electric band Prime Time, merging free improvisation with funk and rock rhythms on albums such as Dancing in Your Head (1977). He continued recording and touring into the 2000s.

Recognition

Coleman's album Sound Grammar (2006) won the Pulitzer Prize for Music in 2007, and he received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award the same year.
