Indirect effect

An indirect effect, in the context of ecology and community interactions, is the impact that one species has on another through the mediation of a third species or a chain of interactions. Unlike a direct effect, in which one species affects another through direct contact or consumption — for example a predator eating its prey — an indirect effect is transmitted through intermediate links in the interaction network.

Types

Indirect effects are commonly divided into several categories. In a trophic cascade, a change at one trophic level propagates through the food web: predators suppress herbivores, which in turn releases plants from grazing pressure. In apparent competition, two prey species negatively affect one another by increasing the population of a shared predator. Trait-mediated indirect effects occur when one species changes the behaviour or traits of another, altering its interactions with a third species — for instance, prey that hide in refuges when predators are present and therefore graze less.

Importance

Indirect effects can be as strong as, or stronger than, direct effects, and they are central to understanding keystone species, whose influence on a community is disproportionate to their abundance. The concept has also been applied beyond ecology, for example in economics and epidemiology, wherever effects propagate through intermediate steps.
