National Museum of Anthropology (Mexico City)

National Museum of Anthropology
Spanish: Museo Nacional de Antropología
Location: Avenida Paseo de la Reforma, Chapultepec, Mexico City
Opened: 17 September 1964
Visitors: Among Mexico's most visited museums

The National Museum of Anthropology in Mexico City (Spanish: Museo Nacional de Antropología) is Mexico's national museum of archaeology and ethnography, situated within Bosque de Chapultepec on Paseo de la Reforma. It houses the world's largest collection of ancient Mexican art and ethnographic exhibits on Mexico's Indigenous peoples.

Highlights

The museum's twenty-two halls surround a great courtyard shaded by a monumental concrete umbrella fountain. On the lower level, archaeological galleries present the cultures of Mesoamerica in roughly chronological and regional order, from early hunter-gatherers to the Mexica (Aztec) gallery at the center, which holds the Sun Stone. Upper-level ethnology halls display the material culture, dress and traditions of contemporary Indigenous groups across Mexico. A full-scale reproduction of the tomb of the Maya king Pakal the Great of Palenque is among the most popular exhibits.

Visiting

The museum is open Tuesday to Sunday and is a standard stop for visitors to Mexico City, lying close to the Chapultepec Zoo, the Museum of Modern Art and the Chapultepec Castle, seat of the National Museum of History.
