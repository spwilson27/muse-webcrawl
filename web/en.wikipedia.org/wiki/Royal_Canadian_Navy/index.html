Royal Canadian Navy

The Royal Canadian Navy (abbreviated RCN; French: Marine royale canadienne) is the naval force of Canada. It is one of three environmental commands within the Canadian Armed Forces, alongside the Canadian Army and the Royal Canadian Air Force. Formed by the Naval Service Act in 1910, the Navy has participated in the First and Second World Wars, the Korean War, the Persian Gulf War, and numerous United Nations peacekeeping missions and NATO operations.

History

Early years

Canada's naval service was established on 4 May 1910 with the Naval Service Act, creating the Department of the Naval Service under Minister Louis-Philippe Brodeur. The first warships, the protected cruisers HMCS Rainbow and HMCS Niobe, were acquired from Britain, arriving at Esquimalt, British Columbia, and Halifax, Nova Scotia, respectively. With the accession of George V in 1911, the service was renamed the Royal Canadian Navy.

World Wars

During the First World War the small navy patrolled Canada's coasts. In the Second World War the RCN expanded dramatically, from about 11 warships and roughly 3,700 personnel in 1939 to one of the world's largest navies by 1945, with hundreds of vessels, most of them corvettes and frigates escorting convoys in the Battle of the Atlantic. Canadian escort groups and aircraft helped sink numerous German U-boats, and the RCN made a vital contribution to the Allied victory in the Atlantic.

Cold War and beyond

After the war the RCN operated aircraft carriers, most notably HMCS Bonaventure, and developed a specialized anti-submarine role within NATO. In 1968 the Navy was amalgamated into the unified Canadian Armed Forces as Maritime Command; the name Royal Canadian Navy was restored in 2011. The modern fleet is centred on Halifax, Nova Scotia, and Esquimalt, British Columbia.

Fleet

The modern RCN operates twelve Halifax-class frigates, four Victoria-class submarines (the former British Upholder class), Kingston-class maritime coastal defence vessels, and the Harry DeWolf-class Arctic and Offshore Patrol Ships, the first of which entered service in 2021. Planned replacements include the River-class destroyers, derived from the British Type 26 design, intended to succeed the Halifax class, and up to twelve new submarines announced under Canada's defence renewal plans.

Structure and bases

The Navy is divided between Maritime Forces Atlantic (MARLANT), based at CFB Halifax, and Maritime Forces Pacific (MARPAC), based at CFB Esquimalt. The Naval Reserve, headquartered in Quebec City, maintains divisions across the country. Command of the RCN is exercised by the Commander of the Royal Canadian Navy, normally a vice-admiral.
