William Hesketh Lever, 1st Viscount Leverhulme (19 September 1851 – 7 May 1925) was an English industrialist, entrepreneur and politician. He co-founded Lever Brothers with his brother James, building it into one of the first multinational consumer-goods companies — the direct ancestor of today's Unilever. His Sunlight Soap, launched in 1884, was among the first branded, packaged household soaps and pioneered modern advertising and, according to some, the idea of building a brand.

Early life

Lever was born in Bolton, Lancashire, the son of a wholesale grocer, and joined the family business at sixteen. With his brother James Darcy Lever he expanded the grocery trade before deciding to manufacture soap himself.

Lever Brothers

In 1885 the brothers bought a soap works at Warrington and began producing Sunlight Soap from vegetable oils — notably glycerine and copra and palm oils — rather than tallow. Aggressive branding, wrapped and named product, and heavy advertising made Sunlight a global success. The company expanded into the United States, Europe and the British Empire; in 1929, four years after Lever's death, Lever Brothers merged with the Dutch margarine company Margarine Unie to form Unilever.

Port Sunlight

In 1888 Lever began building a new factory and model village, Port Sunlight, on marshland at Bromborough on the Wirral. The village eventually housed thousands of employees in architecturally varied cottages with allotments, a school, a church, the Lady Lever Art Gallery and other amenities — the archetype of the paternalist garden village and now a conservation area.

Colonial ventures and controversy

To secure raw materials, Lever obtained palm-oil concessions in the Congo under an agreement with Belgium, founding the Huileries du Congo Belge (1911). The concession system relied on coercive labour practices and drew criticism in his lifetime; its legacy remains controversial. He also bought the Scottish islands of Lewis and Harris in 1918, attempting development schemes that met local resistance.

Politics and later life

Lever sat as a Liberal MP for Wirral (1906–1910). He was created a baronet in 1911, Baron Leverhulme in 1917 and Viscount Leverhulme in 1922, taking his title from his surname and the "hulme" of his wife Elizabeth Hulme. He died in Hampstead in 1925.
