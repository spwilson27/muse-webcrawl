TypemRNA vaccine

The Pfizer–BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine, sold under the brand name Comirnaty, is an mRNA-based COVID-19 vaccine developed by the German biotechnology company BioNTech in partnership with the American pharmaceutical company Pfizer. Its international nonproprietary name is tozinameran. It was the first COVID-19 vaccine to be authorized by a stringent regulatory authority for emergency use and the first to receive full approval for regular use.

Technology

The vaccine uses nucleoside-modified messenger RNA (modRNA) encoding a mutated form of the spike protein of SARS-CoV-2, encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles. After injection, cells produce the spike protein, provoking an immune response that protects against future infection. The primary course originally consisted of two doses given about three weeks apart, with booster doses later recommended; updated formulations targeting Omicron-lineage variants were authorized from 2022 onward.

Authorization and use

The United Kingdom became the first country to authorize the vaccine, on 2 December 2020, and mass vaccination began days later. Authorizations in the European Union, the United States, and many other countries followed. In August 2021 it became the first COVID-19 vaccine to receive full approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for people aged 16 and older. Clinical trials showed efficacy of about 95% against symptomatic COVID-19 caused by the original virus strain.

Safety

Common side effects include pain at the injection site, fatigue, headache, muscle pain, and fever. Rare serious adverse events include anaphylaxis and myocarditis or pericarditis, particularly in adolescent and young adult males, at rates of a few cases per hundred thousand doses. Regulatory agencies worldwide concluded that the benefits of vaccination outweigh these risks.
