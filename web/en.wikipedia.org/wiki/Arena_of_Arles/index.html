Arena of Arles

The Arles Amphitheatre, known as the Arena of Arles.

The Arena of Arles (French: Arènes d'Arles) is a Roman amphitheatre in the southern French town of Arles. Built around 80/90 AD, shortly after the Colosseum in Rome, it measures about 136 m by 109 m and could seat more than 20,000 spectators for gladiatorial combats, chariot races and public spectacles. Its façade of two levels of sixty arches, with engaged Corinthian and Doric columns, is among the best preserved of any Roman amphitheatre.

History

After the fall of the Roman Empire, the amphitheatre was transformed into a fortified town: towers were added, and its interior filled with houses and chapels — more than 200 dwellings — earning it the nickname of a city within the arena. The houses were cleared in the nineteenth century following the advocacy of the writer Prosper Mérimée, and the monument was restored to use as a venue. Since 1981 it has been listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the "Arles, Roman and Romanesque Monuments" listing.

Modern use

Today the arena hosts bullfighting in both the Spanish style (corridas) and the Camargue style (course camarguaise), as well as concerts and festivals, with a modern capacity of around 12,500–14,000 for such events. It famously inspired Vincent van Gogh's 1888 painting Spectators in the Arena at Arles.
