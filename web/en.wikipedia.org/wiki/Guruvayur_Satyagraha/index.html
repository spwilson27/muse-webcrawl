Guruvayur Satyagraha

Guruvayur Satyagraha
Type: Civil disobedience
Location: Guruvayur, Kerala, India
Date: 1931–1932
Led by: K. Kelappan

The Guruvayur Satyagraha was a movement in 1931–1932 in present-day Kerala, India, demanding that the Guruvayur Temple be opened to Hindus of all castes, including those then classified as "untouchables" (avarna Hindus), who were barred from entering the temple.

Background

In early 20th-century Kerala, lower-caste Hindus were excluded from many temples and even from roads approaching them. Reformers sought to challenge caste discrimination through non-violent protest, inspired by Mahatma Gandhi's satyagraha campaigns.

The movement

Led by K. Kelappan, often called the "Kerala Gandhi," volunteers marched to the temple and demanded entry. Kelappan began a fast unto death in September 1932, which drew national attention and pressure from Gandhi. Following interventions and negotiations, Kelappan ended his fast on Gandhi's advice.

Outcome

Although the temple was not immediately opened to all Hindus, the satyagraha galvanised public opinion. A referendum among upper-caste Hindus in the area favoured entry, and temple entry was eventually granted through the Temple Entry Proclamation of Travancore in 1936 and later reforms in Malabar.
