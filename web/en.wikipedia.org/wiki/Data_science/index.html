Data science

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A dashboard of charts and visualisations on a data scientist's screen]

Data science is an interdisciplinary field that uses scientific methods, processes, algorithms, and systems to extract knowledge and insights from data. It combines statistics, mathematics, computer science, and domain expertise, and centres on analysing large and complex datasets ('big data').

The term gained currency in the 2000s–2010s as cheap storage and computing made data abundant; a widely cited 2012 Harvard Business Review article called data scientist 'the sexiest job of the 21st century'. The field's workflow spans data acquisition and cleaning, exploratory analysis, statistical modelling and machine learning, evaluation, and communication of results.

Methods and tools

Data scientists use programming languages especially Python (with pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn) and R; visualisation tools; SQL databases; and distributed systems such as Spark. Statistics — inference, experimental design (A/B testing), Bayesian methods — remains the field's intellectual core alongside machine learning.

Critiques note hype, ill-defined job boundaries with statistics and analytics, and reproducibility and ethics concerns (bias, privacy) when models affect people.

Origins

Precursors include John Tukey's advocacy of 'data analysis' (1962) and statistics' expansion toward computing; academic programmes and the title 'data scientist' spread from tech companies (LinkedIn, Facebook, around 2008) industry-wide.
