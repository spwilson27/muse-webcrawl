BB cream is an all-in-one cosmetic product marketed as combining moisturiser, primer, sunscreen and light foundation in a single step. "BB" stands for "blemish balm" or "beauty balm". The formula was originally developed in the 1960s by German dermatologist Christine Schrammek to protect patients' skin after facial peels and surgery.

Korean boom and global spread

BB cream was introduced to South Korea and Japan in the mid-1980s and became a phenomenon in Korea in the 2000s, promoted by actresses seeking a natural, pale "no make-up" look; it came to account for a substantial share of the Korean cosmetics market. Western brands launched their own BB creams from about 2011, usually reformulated as tinted moisturisers with SPF rather than the thicker, greyer Korean originals. Successors and spin-offs include CC cream (colour control/correcting) and DD creams. Typical ingredients include silicones, humectants such as glycerin and hyaluronic acid, UV filters, niacinamide and antioxidants; in Asia, skin-whitening claims are common.
