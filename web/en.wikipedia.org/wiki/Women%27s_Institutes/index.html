Women's Institutes

Women's Institutes are community organisations for women found in many countries, mostly within the Commonwealth. The movement began in Canada in 1897 and spread to Britain in 1915, and later to countries including Ireland, New Zealand, South Africa and Zimbabwe. Many national bodies are affiliated with the Associated Country Women of the World (ACWW), founded in 1933, which represents rural women's organisations internationally.

International spread

In Canada, the Federated Women's Institutes of Canada coordinates provincial organisations; the first institute at Stoney Creek, Ontario, is commemorated by the Erland Lee Museum. In Ireland, the Irish Countrywomen's Association (founded 1910 as the United Irishwomen) is a counterpart organisation. The Scottish Women's Institutes were formed in 1917, beginning at Longniddry.

Purpose

Women's Institutes provide education, social connection and campaigning opportunities for women, especially in rural communities, covering skills from cookery and crafts to public affairs and health.
