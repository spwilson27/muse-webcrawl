Kallikratis Programme

The Kallikratis Programme (Greek: Πρόγραμμα Καλλικράτης) is the common name of Greek Law 3852/2010, a sweeping reform of the administrative division of Greece that came into force on 1 January 2011. It is named after Kallikrates, the architect of the Parthenon, following the earlier "Kapodistrias" reform of 1997.

Main changes

The reform slashed the number of municipalities and communities from 1,034 to 325 through mergers, abolished the 54 prefectures, and replaced the 13 administrative regions' appointed leadership with directly elected regional governors and councils. Seven new "decentralized administrations" were created to exercise state oversight. Local elections, previously held every four years, moved to a five-year cycle aligned with European Parliament elections.

Context and reception

The reform was enacted by the government of George Papandreou in the opening phase of the Greek government-debt crisis, partly to cut the cost of local government. It was criticised for imposing mergers from above, weakening small island and mountain communities, and creating municipalities so large that local representation suffered. A successor reform, the "Kleisthenis I" programme of 2018 (Law 4555/2018), modified the system again from 2019, splitting several municipalities and changing local electoral rules.
