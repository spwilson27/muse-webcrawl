The Battle of Cholet was fought on 17 October 1793 during the War in the Vendée, part of the French Revolutionary Wars. It was the largest battle of the Vendée revolt, pitting the Catholic and Royal Army of the insurgents against the Republican armies converging on the town of Cholet. The Republican victory drove the Vendéens across the Loire and set in motion the campaign known as the Virée de Galerne.

Background

In the autumn of 1793 the Republican columns, reinforced by the veteran Army of Mainz transferred from the Rhine, pressed the Vendéen army into an ever-smaller territory south of the Loire. After Republican defeats at Coron and Saint-Fulgent and a Vendéen success at Torfou, both sides concentrated around Cholet, the principal town of the insurgent Mauges region.

The battle

The Vendéen army, some 30,000–40,000 strong under commanders including the Marquis de Bonchamps and Maurice d'Elbée, attacked the Republican positions around Cholet on 17 October. Republican generals Kléber and Marceau directed the defence and counter-attacks; the nominal commander Léchelle played little effective part. After hard fighting the Vendéens were repulsed with heavy losses. Both Bonchamps and d'Elbée were gravely wounded — d'Elbée never recovered — and the royalist army lost much of its cohesion.

Aftermath

Defeated, the Vendéens crossed the Loire at Saint-Florent-le-Vieil, famously aided when the dying Bonchamps ordered the release of several thousand Republican prisoners rather than their massacre. The ensuing march north of the Loire, the Virée de Galerne, ended in disaster at Le Mans and Savenay in December 1793, effectively destroying the main Vendéen army.
