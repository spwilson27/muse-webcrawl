Skye Terrier

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Skye Terrier is a Scottish breed of terrier dog, one of the oldest and most distinctive of the Scottish terrier breeds. It is a long-bodied, short-legged dog with a long, flowing double coat that falls over its face and body, bred originally on the Isle of Skye to hunt foxes, otters and badgers in their dens.

History

The breed takes its name from the Isle of Skye in the Inner Hebrides, where terriers of this type were kept for centuries to pursue vermin among the rocks and cairns. Queen Victoria was a noted admirer of the breed, and her patronage in the nineteenth century made the Skye fashionable. The most famous Skye Terrier is Greyfriars Bobby, the Edinburgh dog said to have kept watch over his master's grave for fourteen years after his death in 1858.

Characteristics

The Skye is a low, elongated dog, roughly twice as long as it is tall, standing about 25 cm at the shoulder. Its coat may be black, blue, grey, silver, fawn or cream, and the ears may be prick or drop. Beneath the profuse coat lies a keen, dignified and loyal temperament, though the breed is characteristically reserved with strangers.

Health and status

The breed is classed as vulnerable by the Kennel Club, with very few puppies registered each year. Health concerns include orthopaedic problems associated with its long back and short legs.
