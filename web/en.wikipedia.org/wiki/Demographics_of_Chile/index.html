Demographics of Chile

The demographics of Chile describe a population of roughly 19.5–20 million (2024 census results reported about 18.5 million residents, below earlier projections), with one of South America's highest levels of urbanisation: about 87% of Chileans live in cities, and around 40% live in the Santiago Metropolitan Region.

Ethnic composition

Most Chileans are of mixed Spanish and Indigenous (mestizo) descent, with substantial European immigration in the 19th and 20th centuries from Spain, Germany, Italy, Croatia, Britain, France and the Middle East. Indigenous peoples — principally the Mapuche, plus Aymara, Rapa Nui, Atacameño, Quechua, Diaguita and Kawésqar peoples — comprise roughly 10–13% of the population in census counts. In recent decades immigration has grown sharply from Venezuela, Peru, Haiti, Colombia and Bolivia, raising the foreign-born share to around 8%.

Language and religion

Spanish is the de facto national language, spoken by nearly everyone; minority languages include Mapudungun, Aymara, Quechua and Rapa Nui. Roman Catholicism remains the largest faith (about 42–45% in recent surveys), with a growing Evangelical minority (around 15–18%) and a rapidly rising share — roughly a third — reporting no religion.

Vital statistics

Chile has undergone a rapid demographic transition: fertility has fallen well below replacement (to around 1.0–1.3 children per woman in the early 2020s), life expectancy is the highest in Latin America at around 80–81 years, and the population is ageing quickly.
