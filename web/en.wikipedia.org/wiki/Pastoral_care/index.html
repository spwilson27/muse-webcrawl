Pastoral care

Placeholder image

Pastoral care is an ancient model of emotional, social and spiritual support that can be found in many religious traditions and more broadly in society. The term is generally credited to a shepherding imagery: the religious leader as shepherd and the congregation as flock.

Scope

In Christianity, pastoral care includes visiting the sick and bereaved, counselling, prayer, and the administration of sacraments. In modern settings the term has widened: school and hospital chaplains, military chaplains, and pastoral workers in many faiths provide support to people regardless of their own religious affiliation.

Pastoral counselling draws on both theological resources and insights from psychology. Practitioners may be ordained clergy or trained lay people. The concept is related to spiritual care in healthcare settings.
