A horror film is one that seeks to elicit fear or disgust in its audience for entertainment purposes. Horror films often explore dark subject matter and may deal with transgressive topics; the genre exists as a major category across a century of cinema and frequently overlaps with fantasy, supernatural fiction and the thriller.

History

German Expressionism produced early classics such as The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari (1920) and Nosferatu (1922). In the 1930s Universal Pictures built a cycle around Dracula, Frankenstein and The Mummy. Alfred Hitchcock's Psycho (1960) moved horror into everyday settings, and the 1970s brought an era of auteur horror: The Exorcist (1973), The Texas Chain Saw Massacre (1974), Jaws (1975), Halloween (1978) and Ridley Scott's Alien (1979), which screenwriter Dan O'Bannon pitched as a haunted-house film in space; its star Ellen Ripley became the model of the resourceful "final girl".

Later movements include the slasher cycle of the 1980s, Japanese horror (Ring, 1998), found-footage hits such as The Blair Witch Project (1999) and Paranormal Activity (2007), and the critically acclaimed "elevated horror" of the 2010s (Get Out, Hereditary, The Witch). Directors such as David Fincher, though classed with the thriller, have strongly influenced horror's visual language.

Subgenres

Scholars divide horror into categories including action horror, body horror, comedy horror, gothic horror, natural horror, psychological horror, slasher films, splatter films, supernatural horror and zombie films, each reflecting the anxieties of its era.
