Dairy farming

Dairy farming
Primary productsMilk, cheese, butter, yoghurt
Principal animalsCattle; also goats, sheep, buffalo

Dairy farming is a class of agriculture for the long-term production of milk, which is processed (either on the farm or at a dairy plant) for eventual sale of dairy products. Most dairy farms raise cattle, especially the Holstein Friesian breed prized for high milk yield, though goats, sheep, and water buffalo are also milked in many regions.

History

Dairying began with the domestication of cattle, sheep, and goats in the Neolithic period. For millennia milk was consumed locally as cheese and fermented products, since fresh milk spoiled quickly. Industrialisation brought railways, refrigeration, pasteurisation, and centrifugal cream separators, allowing milk to supply growing cities. The twentieth century added mechanical milking machines, artificial insemination, and bulk-tank collection.

Production

Modern dairy cows are typically milked two or three times daily in parlours or by robotic milking systems, and produce an average of more than 10,000 litres per lactation in intensive systems. India is the world's largest milk producer, followed by the United States; New Zealand dominates international trade in whole milk powder.

Issues

The sector faces criticism over animal welfare (including the separation of calves and the fate of male calves), the environmental footprint of methane emissions and manure, and the economics of small farms squeezed by volatile milk prices.
