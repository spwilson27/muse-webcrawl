The Plains of Abraham (French: Plaines d'Abraham) is a historic plateau in Quebec City, Canada, famous as the site of the Battle of the Plains of Abraham on 13 September 1759, a decisive engagement of the Seven Years' War in which both commanding generals, Britain's James Wolfe and France's Marquis de Montcalm, were mortally wounded.

Name and history

The name is popularly traced to Abraham Martin, a 17th-century pilot and fisherman who grazed cattle on the heights; land records also reference an Abraham in connection with the area. The plateau, a long, flat-topped ridge above the St. Lawrence River just west of the old city walls, was largely farmland at the time of the battle.

The battle

In the summer of 1759 a British expedition under Wolfe besieged Quebec. On the night of 12–13 September, British troops scaled the cliffs at Anse-au-Foulon and deployed on the plains. Montcalm attacked hastily that morning; the ensuing battle, though brief, broke French power in Canada — Quebec surrendered days later, and Montreal capitulated the following year. A second battle, at Sainte-Foy in April 1760, was won by the French but could not reverse the outcome.

Battlefields Park

In 1908 the site was acquired for the nation and became The Battlefields Park (Parc des Champs-de-Bataille), Canada's first national historic park, administered by the National Battlefields Commission. The park today is Quebec City's central green space, hosting monuments, the Joan of Arc garden, Martello towers, and major public celebrations including the Fête nationale du Québec (Saint-Jean-Baptiste Day) concerts.
