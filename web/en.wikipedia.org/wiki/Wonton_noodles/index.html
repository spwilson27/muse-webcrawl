Wonton noodles

CourseMain course, noodle soup
Place of originChina
RegionGuangdong, Hong Kong
Main ingredientsWheat noodles, wontons, broth, choy sum

Wonton noodles (Cantonese: wàhn-tān mihn) is a noodle dish of Cantonese origin, popular in Hong Kong, Guangzhou, and Southeast Asia. It consists of springy wheat noodles served in a hot broth, topped with wontons — small dumplings usually filled with shrimp and pork — and leafy greens such as choy sum.

History

The dish originated in Guangzhou during the Qing dynasty and spread to Hong Kong in the early 20th century, where it became a staple of dai pai dong stalls and tea houses. Wonton noodles were popularised in the West largely through Hong Kong emigrant communities.

Preparation

The noodles are typically made from wheat flour and egg, kneaded and sometimes pressed with a bamboo pole (juk sing min, "bamboo-pressed noodles") to achieve their characteristic elasticity. The broth is traditionally made from dried flounder, shrimp shells, and pork bones. Wontons are folded to resemble goldfish tails and are cooked briefly so that the shrimp filling remains firm and translucent.

A properly prepared bowl is judged by the noodles being "al dente" and springy, the wontons smooth and compact, and the broth clear and flavourful. A small spoonful of chili oil is sometimes added.

Variations

In Malaysia and Singapore, wonton noodles are often served "dry" with a dark soy-based sauce, accompanied by slices of char siu (barbecued pork) and pickled green chilies, with the soup served in a separate bowl. Thai-style bami haeng is a related dry version.
