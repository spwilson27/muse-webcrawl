Mythologies of the indigenous peoples of the Americas

The mythologies of the indigenous peoples of the Americas comprise the diverse body of myths and sacred narratives of Native American, First Nations, Inuit, Métis and Mesoamerican and South American indigenous peoples. There is no single pantheon or unified mythology: thousands of cultures developed distinct traditions, many transmitted orally over centuries.

Common themes

Despite their diversity, scholars note recurring motifs: creation stories, including earth-diver myths in which an animal brings up earth from primordial waters; trickster figures such as Coyote, Raven and Nanabozho; culture heroes; journeys to spirit worlds guided by shamans; and a close spiritual relationship between humans, animals and the land. Animism — the attribution of spiritual essence to natural phenomena — is a widespread feature.

Regional traditions

Mesoamerican traditions include the Aztec mythology of gods such as Huitzilopochtli, Quetzalcoatl and Tezcatlipoca, and Maya mythology recorded in the Popol Vuh. Andean mythology centres on the Inca pantheon of Inti the sun god and Pachamama the earth goddess. North American traditions range from the Great Spirit concepts of Algonquian peoples to the complex ceremonial mythologies of the Pueblo and Navajo peoples of the Southwest and the rich oral traditions of the Pacific Northwest Coast.
