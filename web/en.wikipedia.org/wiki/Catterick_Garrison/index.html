Catterick Garrison

Location: North Yorkshire, England
Established: 1908 (as Richmond Camp)
Garrison: British Army

Catterick Garrison is a major British Army garrison and town in North Yorkshire, England, near Richmond. It is the largest British Army garrison in the world, with a population of around 13,000, and home to the Infantry Training Centre (ITC) Catterick, where all British infantry soldiers complete initial training.

History

The site was established in 1908 as Richmond Camp, renamed Catterick Camp in 1915, and used extensively in both world wars. It was planned as a permanent garrison after the First World War under the influence of Lord Baden-Powell, who had been based at nearby Richmond Castle. The disused Catterick military railway once served the camp.

Units and facilities

Units based there include battalions of the Royal Yorkshire Regiment and the Royal Lancers, the School of Infantry, and the Army Medical Services training. The garrison town has its own shops, a cinema complex, and leisure facilities centred on the Princes Gate development.
