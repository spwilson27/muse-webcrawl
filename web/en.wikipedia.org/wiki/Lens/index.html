Lens

A lens is a transmissive optical device that focuses or disperses a light beam by means of refraction. A simple lens consists of a single piece of transparent material, while a compound lens consists of several simple lenses (elements), usually arranged along a common axis. Lenses are made from materials such as glass or plastic, ground and polished or moulded to the required shape. A lens can focus light to form an image, unlike a prism, which refracts light without focusing. Devices that similarly focus or disperse electromagnetic radiation in other parts of the spectrum are also called lenses, as are devices that focus sound or particle beams.

Types

Simple lenses are classified by the curvature of their two optical surfaces. A lens is biconvex (or double convex) if both surfaces bulge outward; such a lens converges parallel rays to a focal point and is called a converging or positive lens. A lens is biconcave if both surfaces curve inward; it diverges rays and is called a diverging or negative lens. Other forms include plano-convex, plano-concave, and meniscus lenses, which combine one convex and one concave surface. The distance from the lens to its focal point is the focal length, and the reciprocal of the focal length in metres is the lens power in dioptres.

History

The earliest known lens artefacts, such as the Nimrud lens from ancient Assyria (about 750–710 BC), may have been used as burning glasses or magnifiers. Reading stones — hemispherical magnifying lenses laid directly on text — were used in medieval Europe, and spectacles appeared in Italy in the late thirteenth century. The development of the telescope and microscope in the early seventeenth century drove rapid advances in lens-making, and the nineteenth and twentieth centuries brought photographic objectives corrected for optical aberrations.

Imaging and aberrations

A converging lens forms a real image of a distant object in its focal plane; the geometry of image formation is described by the thin-lens equation, 1/f = 1/u + 1/v. Real lenses depart from ideal behaviour through aberrations — including spherical aberration, chromatic aberration, coma, and astigmatism — which compound lens designs and coatings are used to correct.

Applications

Lenses are used in spectacles and contact lenses to correct refractive errors of the eye, and in cameras, telescopes, microscopes, binoculars, projectors, and lasers. The crystalline lens of the human eye is itself a biconvex lens whose shape is adjusted by the ciliary muscle to focus at different distances.
