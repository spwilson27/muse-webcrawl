The Libyan civil war (2014–2020), often called the Second Libyan Civil War, was a multilateral conflict in Libya between rival armed groups and governments claiming legitimacy following the fragmentation that followed the 2011 civil war and the fall of Muammar Gaddafi. The two principal camps were the internationally recognised Government of National Accord (GNA) in Tripoli, and the Tobruk-based House of Representatives backed by Khalifa Haftar's Libyan National Army (LNA).

Course of the conflict

Fighting began in May 2014 with Haftar's "Operation Dignity" against Islamist militias in Benghazi, while in Tripoli rival militias battled over the capital, which destroyed its international airport and split Libya between east and west. The Skhirat agreement of December 2015 created the GNA under Fayez al-Sarraj, but the Tobruk parliament never fully accepted it. Both sides drew in foreign backers: Turkey and Qatar supported the GNA, while Egypt, the United Arab Emirates, and Russia supported the LNA, turning the war into a proxy conflict fought with drones and mercenaries.

In April 2019 Haftar launched an offensive to capture Tripoli, which stalled at the city's outskirts. With direct Turkish military intervention in early 2020, GNA forces pushed the LNA back from western Libya. Front lines froze around Sirte, and on 23 October 2020 the parties signed a permanent ceasefire at UN-sponsored talks in Geneva. A Government of National Unity under Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh was formed in March 2021, though elections were postponed and political division persisted.
