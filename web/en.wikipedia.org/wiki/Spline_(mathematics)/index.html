Spline (mathematics)

In mathematics, a spline is a function defined piecewise by polynomials. In interpolating problems, spline interpolation is often preferred to high-degree polynomial interpolation because it yields smooth curves that pass through given points without the wild oscillations (Runge's phenomenon) of a single high-degree polynomial, even when low-degree pieces are used.

Definition

A spline of degree n on an interval is a function that is a polynomial of degree at most n on each subinterval between consecutive knots, and has continuous derivatives up to order n − 1 at the knots. The most widely used are cubic splines (degree 3), which are twice continuously differentiable. A natural cubic spline imposes zero second derivative at the endpoints. B-splines provide a numerically stable basis for representing spline curves, and NURBS generalize them for computer-aided design.

History

The name comes from the flexible drafting tool — a thin strip of wood or metal bent around weights called ducks — used by shipbuilders and draftsmen to draw smooth curves. Isaac Jacob Schoenberg introduced the term "spline" for the mathematical functions in 1946. With the rise of computing, splines became fundamental to computer graphics, animation, geometric modelling and numerical analysis.

Applications

Splines are used for interpolation of data, smoothing of noisy measurements (smoothing splines minimize a combination of fit error and curvature), solving differential equations, font design (TrueType and PostScript outlines use quadratic and cubic Bézier splines), and keyframe animation, where they produce smooth motion paths.
