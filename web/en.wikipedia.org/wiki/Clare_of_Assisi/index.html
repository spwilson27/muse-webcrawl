Clare of Assisi (born Chiara Offreduccio; 1194–1253) was an Italian saint and one of the first followers of Francis of Assisi. Inspired by Francis's preaching, she left her wealthy family's home on Palm Sunday night in 1212 and consecrated her life to Christ, founding the Order of Saint Clare (the Poor Clares), the female branch of the Franciscan movement.

Life

Born into Assisi's nobility, Chiara refused arranged marriage and, at eighteen, fled to the chapel of the Porziuncola, where Francis cut her hair and clothed her in a rough habit. She settled with her companions at the church of San Damiano, where she led a community devoted to prayer, manual work, and absolute poverty for more than forty years. Her sister Agnes soon joined her, defying family efforts—at times violent—to retrieve them.

The Poor Clares

Clare fought throughout her life to preserve the "privilege of poverty," insisting her sisters own nothing, individually or in common. Days before her death in 1253, Pope Innocent IV approved her own Rule—the first rule of life for a religious order written by a woman. The order spread widely across Europe during her lifetime.

Legacy and veneration

Clare was canonized in 1255. Tradition holds that in 1240 she repelled Saracen mercenaries attacking San Damiano by displaying the Blessed Sacrament, for which she is depicted holding a monstrance. Pope Pius XII named her patron saint of television in 1958, recalling a vision in which, confined to her sickbed, she saw and heard Christmas Mass on the wall of her room. The Basilica of Saint Clare in Assisi houses her incorrupt remains and the crucifix that, according to tradition, spoke to Francis.
