Chernobyl is a 2019 historical drama television miniseries created and written by Craig Mazin and directed by Johan Renck, co-produced by HBO and Sky UK. In five episodes it dramatises the Chernobyl nuclear disaster of April 1986 and the extraordinary clean-up effort that followed, focusing on the scientists and officials who confronted both the catastrophe and the Soviet system that had enabled it.

Plot and characters

The series centres on Valery Legasov (Jared Harris), the deputy director of the Kurchatov Institute, who is appointed to the government commission investigating the explosion of Reactor No. 4; Boris Shcherbina (Stellan Skarsgård), the bureaucrat who becomes his ally; and Ulana Khomyuk (Emily Watson), a fictional composite character representing the many scientists who assisted the investigation. Around them the series portrays the plant operators, the firefighters and miners, the evacuation of Pripyat, and the 1987 trial of the plant's management, culminating in Legasov's testimony exposing the fatal RBMK design flaw and the state's culture of secrecy.

Reception

Chernobyl received universal critical acclaim for its writing, direction, performances, and production design, and won ten Primetime Emmy Awards including Outstanding Limited Series. It also sparked a surge of tourism to the Chernobyl Exclusion Zone and public debate — in Russia and elsewhere — about its historical accuracy, with some compressions and inventions defended by its creators as dramatically necessary while the broad account of the disaster follows the historical record.
