Unified Payments Interface

Unified Payments Interface (UPI) is an Indian instant payment system as well as a protocol developed by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) in 2016. The interface facilitates inter-bank peer-to-peer (P2P) and person-to-merchant (P2M) transactions, allowing users to transfer money between bank accounts instantly using a mobile device, a virtual payment address, a phone number, or a QR code.

A merchant QR code used to accept UPI payments in India.

History

NPCI launched UPI with member banks in April 2016; it was formally launched by the then Governor of the Reserve Bank of India, Raghuram Rajan, in Mumbai. Apps built on UPI, such as BHIM (developed by NPCI), PhonePe, Google Pay, and Paytm, grew rapidly, especially following India's 2016 demonetisation, which spurred adoption of digital payments.

How it works

UPI runs on top of the Immediate Payment Service (IMPS) infrastructure and uses a Virtual Payment Address (VPA) — for example, name@bank — so users need not share bank details. Transactions are authenticated with a device binding and a UPI PIN. The system supports push (pay) and pull (request/collect) transactions, scheduled mandates, and QR-code-based payments both online and at physical stores.

Adoption

UPI has become one of the world's largest real-time payment systems by volume, processing well over ten billion transactions per month in recent years. NPCI has pursued international linkages — for example with Singapore's PayNow — and UPI acceptance has expanded to several countries for Indian travellers and merchants abroad.

Related services

UPI has been extended with features such as UPI Lite for small-value offline-ish payments, credit lines on UPI, and recurring-payment mandates (UPI AutoPay). The RuPay credit card has also been linked to UPI.
