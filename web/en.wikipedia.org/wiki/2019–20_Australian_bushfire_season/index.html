2019–20 Australian bushfire season

2019–20 Australian bushfires ("Black Summer")
Duration: June 2019 – May 2020
Area burnt: ~18.6 million hectares (46 million acres)
Buildings destroyed: ~9,300 (incl. ~2,800 homes)
Deaths: 33 direct; ~445 from smoke (estimated)
Location: Australia, esp. NSW and Victoria

The 2019–20 Australian bushfire season, known as the Black Summer, was a period of exceptionally severe bushfires in Australia, peaking during December 2019 and January 2020. It was Australia's worst recorded bushfire season.

Scale and causes

Fires burnt an estimated 18.6 million hectares, destroyed thousands of buildings and killed 33 people directly. Contributing factors included record-breaking temperatures, a severe drought, and a strongly positive Indian Ocean Dipole. Huge pyrocumulonimbus clouds generated their own lightning, and smoke reached South America; smoke also caused hazardous air quality in Sydney, Melbourne and Canberra, where smoke-related deaths were estimated in the hundreds.

Impact on wildlife and response

Estimates suggested nearly three billion animals were killed or displaced. The Australian Defence Force was deployed in Operation Bushfire Assist, and a royal commission (the National Natural Disaster Arrangements Royal Commission) was established. International firefighters and large charitable donations supported the response.

Aftermath

The season intensified debate about climate change and hazard-reduction burning in Australia. Heavy rain in February 2020 helped extinguish the remaining major fires.
