Color wheel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A traditional RYB colour wheel with primary, secondary and tertiary colours.

A color wheel or color circle is an abstract illustrative organization of color hues around a circle, which shows the relationships between primary colors, secondary colors, tertiary colors, and so on. Most color wheels are based on three primary colors, three secondary colors, and the six tertiary colors formed by mixing a primary with a secondary, giving a total of twelve main segments.

History

The first color circle was devised by Isaac Newton, who in his Opticks (1704) arranged the spectral colours around a circle to show their relationships and to predict the result of mixing lights. Later systems included Moses Harris's 18th-century wheel for painters and Michel Eugène Chevreul's chromatic circle, which influenced the Impressionists and theorists of simultaneous contrast.

Color schemes

The wheel is a standard tool for choosing harmonious colour combinations: complementary colours lie opposite each other; analogous colours are adjacent; triadic schemes use three colours evenly spaced 120° apart; and split-complementary and tetradic (rectangular) schemes build on the same geometry. Painters' wheels are usually based on the RYB (red, yellow, blue) primaries of subtractive mixing, while digital design uses the RGB additive wheel, in which hues are placed around a circle in HSV or HSL space.
