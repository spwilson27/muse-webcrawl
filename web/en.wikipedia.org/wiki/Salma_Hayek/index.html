Salma Valgarma Hayek Pinault (née Jiménez; born 2 September 1966) is a Mexican and American actress and film producer. She began her career in Mexico with starring roles in the telenovela Teresa (1989–1991) and the romantic drama Midaq Alley (1995). She soon established herself in Hollywood with appearances in films such as Desperado (1995), From Dusk till Dawn (1996), Wild Wild West (1999), and Dogma (1999).

Her breakthrough role was that of Mexican painter Frida Kahlo in the biographical film Frida (2002), which she also produced. For her performance, she received a nomination for the Academy Award for Best Actress, becoming the first Mexican actress nominated in that category. Hayek went on to star in a wide range of films, including Once Upon a Time in Mexico (2003), Grown Ups (2010), Puss in Boots (2011), The Hitman's Bodyguard (2017), and the Marvel film Eternals (2021), in which she played Ajak.

Early life

Hayek was born in Coatzacoalcos, Veracruz, Mexico. Her father, Sami Hayek Domínguez, is of Lebanese descent and ran an industrial-equipment firm; her mother, Diana Jiménez Medina, is an opera singer and talent scout of Spanish descent. She was raised in a wealthy Catholic family and studied at the Academy of the Sacred Heart in Louisiana before attending university in Mexico City, where she studied international relations. She left her studies to pursue acting.

Career

After success in Mexican television, Hayek moved to Los Angeles in 1991. Director Robert Rodriguez cast her opposite Antonio Banderas in Desperado (1995), which brought her to wider attention. She founded the production company Ventanarosa in 1999, through which she produced Frida and the television series Ugly Betty (2006–2010), which won a Golden Globe Award for Best Television Series – Musical or Comedy. As a producer she also worked on the Netflix series Monarca and the film El Coronel No Tiene Quien Le Escriba (1999).

Personal life and activism

Hayek married French billionaire François-Henri Pinault in 2009; they have a daughter, Valentina Paloma, born in 2007. She is an advocate for women's rights and immigrant communities, and has supported UNICEF campaigns against maternal and neonatal tetanus. In 2017 she published an opinion piece describing harassment she experienced from producer Harvey Weinstein during the making of Frida, becoming a prominent voice in the #MeToo movement.
