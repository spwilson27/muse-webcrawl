Franz Xaver Wolfgang Mozart

BornJuly 26, 1791, Vienna, Austria
DiedJuly 29, 1844 (aged 53), Karlsbad, Austrian Empire (now Karlovy Vary, Czech Republic)
OccupationComposer, pianist, teacher
ParentsWolfgang Amadeus Mozart, Constanze Weber

Franz Xaver Wolfgang Mozart (26 July 1791 – 29 July 1844), often referred to as W. A. Mozart Son, was an Austrian composer, pianist, and music teacher. He was the youngest child of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart and Constanze Weber, and one of only two of their six children to survive infancy, the other being his elder brother Carl Thomas Mozart.

Life

Born in Vienna five months before his father's death, Franz Xaver never knew him. He was educated by prominent musicians of the day, including Antonio Salieri, Johann Nepomuk Hummel, and Sigismund von Neukomm, and was marketed by his mother under the name "Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (son)" to capitalize on his father's fame, a burden that weighed on him throughout his career.

He made his debut as a pianist and composer in Vienna in 1805. In 1808 he moved to Galicia (in modern-day Ukraine), where he worked as a music teacher to noble families in Lemberg (Lviv) and surrounding estates. He spent much of his career in Lemberg, founding the city's first music society and concert life there, before returning to Vienna in 1838.

Music

His compositions — published under the name "W. A. Mozart fils" — include two piano concertos, piano sonatas, chamber music, songs, and variations, written in a polished early-Romantic idiom influenced by Hummel and his father. Though overshadowed by his father's genius, his works have been recorded and reassessed in modern times.

Death

He died in Karlsbad in 1844 and was buried there; like his father, he never married and had no children, so the direct Mozart family line ended with his generation.
