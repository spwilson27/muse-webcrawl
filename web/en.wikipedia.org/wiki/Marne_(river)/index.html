Marne (river)

The Marne is a river in France, a right-bank tributary of the Seine in the area east and southeast of Paris. At 514 kilometres (319 mi) it is the longest river wholly in France. The Marne gives its name to the departments of Haute-Marne, Marne, Seine-et-Marne, and Val-de-Marne.

Course

The river rises on the Langres Plateau in the Haute-Marne department and flows generally northwest, passing Saint-Dizier, Vitry-le-François, Châlons-en-Champagne, Épernay, Château-Thierry, and Meaux, before joining the Seine at Charenton-le-Pont, just upstream of Paris. Its main tributaries include the Blaise, the Saulx, the Ourcq, the Petit Morin, and the Grand Morin.

History

The Marne valley was the scene of two major battles of the First World War. The First Battle of the Marne (September 1914) halted the German advance on Paris; the famous "taxis of the Marne" ferried reinforcements from the capital to the front. The Second Battle of the Marne (July 1918) was the last major German offensive of the war and a turning point toward Allied victory.

Navigation and canals

The Marne is navigable by barges over much of its lower course and is linked by canals — including the Marne–Rhine Canal and the Canal latéral à la Marne — to the Rhine, the Saône, and the canal network of northern France. Its valley is also known for the vineyards of Champagne around Épernay.
