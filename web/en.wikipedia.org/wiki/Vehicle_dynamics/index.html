Vehicle dynamics

Vehicle dynamics is the study of vehicle motion, e.g., how a vehicle's forward movement changes in response to driver inputs, propulsion system outputs, ambient conditions, air/surface/water conditions, etc. Vehicle dynamics is a part of engineering primarily based on classical mechanics.

Components and forces

The motion of a vehicle is determined by the forces acting through its tyres at the contact patches with the road, together with aerodynamic forces and gravity. Analysis is conventionally divided into three areas:

  • Longitudinal dynamics — acceleration, braking, load transfer, traction, and rolling resistance.
  • Lateral dynamics — steering response, cornering, understeer and oversteer, yaw stability.
  • Vertical dynamics — ride comfort and road holding over bumps, governed by the suspension springs and dampers.

Tyres

Tyres generate force through slip: longitudinal slip during acceleration and braking, and slip angle during cornering. The relationship between slip and force is nonlinear and saturates at the limit of grip; models such as the Pacejka "Magic Formula" are used in simulation to capture this behaviour.

Handling characteristics

A vehicle is said to understeer when the front tyres saturate first and the car turns less than the steering input suggests; oversteer describes the opposite tendency of the rear to lose grip, rotating the car more. Engineers tune these behaviours through weight distribution, suspension geometry, anti-roll bars, and tyre selection. Electronic stability control systems intervene by braking individual wheels to correct unwanted yaw.

Simulation

Multibody dynamics software is widely used to predict ride and handling before prototypes are built, ranging from simple single-track ("bicycle") models to full vehicle models with detailed tyre, suspension, and aerodynamics representations.
