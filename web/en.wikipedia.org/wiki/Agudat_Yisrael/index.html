Agudat Yisrael (Hebrew: אגודת ישראל, "Union of Israel"; also spelled Agudath Israel) is a Haredi Jewish political movement and party, founded in 1912 as the political arm of Orthodox Judaism in Eastern Europe in opposition to secular Zionism and Reform Judaism. Guided by a council of senior rabbis, the Moetzes Gedolei HaTorah (Council of Torah Sages), Agudat Yisrael seeks to defend traditional Torah observance and the interests of the Haredi community in public life.

History

The movement was established at a conference in Kattowitz in 1912, uniting Hasidic and non-Hasidic Orthodox leaders from across Europe. In interwar Poland, Agudah-affiliated parties won seats in the Sejm and in Jewish community councils, and built networks of schools (including the Beis Yaakov schools for girls founded by Sarah Schenirer) and youth movements. The movement's European heartland was destroyed in the Holocaust.

In Israel

In Israel, Agudat Yisrael participated in the first governments of the state despite its theological ambivalence toward Zionism, and helped negotiate the "status quo" arrangements on religion and state, including Sabbath observance in the public sphere and Haredi military-service deferments for yeshiva students. Since 1992 it has usually run jointly with the Lithuanian-oriented party Degel HaTorah in the United Torah Judaism alliance, traditionally winning a handful of Knesset seats and joining right-leaning coalitions. Sister organizations exist in the United States (Agudath Israel of America) and elsewhere in the diaspora.
