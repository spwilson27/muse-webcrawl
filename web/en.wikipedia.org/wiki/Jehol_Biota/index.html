AgeEarly Cretaceous (Barremian–Aptian, ~131–120 Ma)
Principal unitsYixian Formation, Jiufotang Formation

The Jehol Biota is an Early Cretaceous terrestrial and freshwater ecosystem preserved in the rock formations of northeastern China, chiefly the Yixian and Jiufotang formations of Liaoning, Hebei, and Inner Mongolia. Its exquisitely preserved fossils — including feathered dinosaurs, early birds, mammals, and flowering plants — have transformed understanding of dinosaur evolution and the origin of birds.

Fauna

Notable Jehol fossils include the feathered theropods Sinosauropteryx, Microraptor, and Yutyrannus; the early birds Confuciusornis and Jeholornis; the giant mammal Repenomamus, one specimen of which preserves a baby dinosaur in its stomach; and early angiosperms such as Archaefructus.

Preservation

Fine-grained lake sediments and volcanic ashfalls (a "Cretaceous Pompeii" effect) preserved soft tissues, feathers, and even color-bearing melanosomes, allowing reconstruction of dinosaur plumage coloration.
