Treaty of Belgrade

Signed18 September 1739
LocationBelgrade, Ottoman Serbia
SignatoriesHabsburg Monarchy, Ottoman Empire

The Treaty of Belgrade, known also as the Peace of Belgrade, was the peace treaty signed on September 18, 1739 in Belgrade, Serbia (then in the Ottoman Empire), by the Ottoman Empire on one side and the Habsburg Monarchy on the other, that ended the Austro-Turkish War of 1737–39.

Background

The Habsburg Monarchy had entered the war in 1737 alongside its ally Russia, hoping to reverse some Ottoman gains. However, the Austrian campaigns in the Balkans went badly: after initial successes, Austrian forces suffered defeats, losing ground in Serbia and Bosnia. Meanwhile Russia had concluded its own separate peace negotiations, leaving Austria exposed.

Terms

Under the treaty, Austria was forced to cede back to the Ottomans the territories gained by the Treaty of Passarowitz in 1718 south of the Sava and Danube rivers:

  • Northern Serbia, including Belgrade itself;
  • The northern part of Bosnia;
  • Oltenia (Lesser Wallachia).

The Habsburgs retained the Banat of Temeswar and the part of Wallachia west of the Olt was returned to the Ottomans. The line of the Sava and the Danube remained the frontier between the two empires for the next half-century.

Consequences

The treaty marked a significant setback for Habsburg expansion in the Balkans and was greeted as humiliating in Vienna. It confirmed the Danube–Sava line as the border between Austria and the Ottoman Empire until the late 18th century. Belgrade remained in Ottoman hands until its temporary Austrian occupation in 1789 and finally passed to Serbian rebels in the early 19th century.

France, which mediated the negotiations through its ambassador the Marquis de Villeneuve, gained prestige as a diplomatic arbiter in European affairs.
