New product development

New product development (NPD) is the complete process of bringing a new product to market, from identifying an opportunity and generating ideas through design, testing, and commercial launch. A central aspect of NPD is product design, together with consideration of business, marketing, and manufacturing issues. New products are a vital source of growth for firms, yet most new product ideas fail before reaching the market.

Process models

The classic model developed by Booz, Allen and Hamilton (1982) describes seven stages: new product strategy, idea generation, screening and evaluation, business analysis, development, testing, and commercialization. Robert G. Cooper's Stage-Gate process refines this into stages separated by decision "gates" at which projects are continued, redirected, or killed. Many firms now supplement these linear models with agile and lean-startup methods — rapid prototyping, minimum viable products, and iterative customer feedback — particularly in software.

The fuzzy front end

The earliest phases of NPD, between opportunity recognition and the formal decision to develop, are often called the "fuzzy front end". Idea sources include customers, employees, competitors, suppliers, and research. Techniques for generating and refining concepts include brainstorming, lead-user research, ethnography, and quality function deployment (the "house of quality"), which translates customer requirements into engineering specifications.

Failure rates and success factors

Studies consistently report high attrition: of every hundred ideas, only a handful reach launch, and a substantial fraction of launched products fail commercially. Research on success factors points to a strong market orientation, a differentiated product advantage, early and sharp product definition, cross-functional teams, and disciplined go/kill decision-making as the strongest predictors of success.
