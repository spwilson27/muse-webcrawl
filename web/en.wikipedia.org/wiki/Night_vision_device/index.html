Night vision device

A night vision device (NVD), also known as a night optical/observation device (NOD) or night-vision goggles (NVG), is an optoelectronic device that allows visualization of images in low levels of light, improving the user's night vision. Such devices are widely used by military forces, law-enforcement agencies, hunters, and outdoor enthusiasts.

How it works

Most night vision devices operate by image intensification. Ambient light—photons from moonlight, starlight, or near-infrared illumination—is collected by an objective lens and focused onto a photocathode inside an image intensifier tube. The photocathode converts photons into electrons, which are multiplied by a microchannel plate and then strike a phosphor screen, producing a bright visible image, traditionally green because the human eye is most sensitive to shades of green.

Devices are classified into generations (Gen 0 through Gen 3 and beyond) according to the technology of their intensifier tubes, with later generations offering greater sensitivity, resolution, and operational life. Some modern systems fuse image intensification with thermal imaging, which detects infrared radiation emitted by objects themselves rather than reflected light.

History and use

Active infrared sights appeared during the Second World War, paired with infrared searchlights. Passive “starlight scopes” were fielded by the United States during the Vietnam War. Helmet-mounted binocular and monocular goggles are now standard infantry equipment, and aviation variants are integrated with cockpit lighting standards. Civilian uses include wildlife observation, boating, caving, and security surveillance.
