The Second Anglo-Afghan War (Pashto: د افغان-انګرېز دويمه جګړه) was a military conflict fought between the British Raj and the Emirate of Afghanistan from 1878 to 1880, when the latter was ruled by Sher Ali Khan of the Barakzai dynasty, the son of former Emir Dost Mohammad Khan. The war was part of the Great Game between the British and Russian empires, as Britain sought to prevent Afghanistan from falling under Russian influence following a Russian mission to Kabul in 1878.

The war was split into two campaigns. In the first, British forces invaded in November 1878 after Sher Ali refused to receive a British mission, and compelled him to sign the Treaty of Gandamak (May 1879), under which his son Yakub Khan ceded control of Afghanistan's foreign policy to Britain and admitted a British Resident in Kabul. In September 1879 the Resident, Sir Pierre Cavagnari, and his escort were massacred, provoking a second campaign in which General Frederick Roberts occupied Kabul. In July 1880 Afghan forces under Ayub Khan defeated a British brigade at the Battle of Maiwand; Roberts then marched from Kabul to relieve Kandahar and defeated Ayub Khan there on 1 September 1880.

Aftermath

Following the war, Abdur Rahman Khan was installed as Emir. Britain achieved its strategic aims — Afghanistan's foreign relations passed under British control and the frontier districts were held — while withdrawing from Kabul and abandoning attempts to station a Resident or annex Afghan territory. The settlement endured until the Third Anglo-Afghan War of 1919, after which Afghanistan regained full control of its foreign policy.
