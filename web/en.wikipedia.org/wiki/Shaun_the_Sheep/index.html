Shaun the Sheep

Shaun the Sheep is a British stop-motion animated television series created by Nick Park and developed by Richard Starzak for Aardman Animations. It began on CBBC in 2007 and centres on Shaun, a clever sheep who leads his flock into adventures at Mossy Bottom Farm, watched over — often ineffectually — by the sheepdog Bitzer and the oblivious Farmer.

Background

Shaun first appeared in the Wallace and Gromit short film A Close Shave (1995), in which Wallace's knitting machine sheared him — hence his name, a pun on "shorn". The television series dispenses with dialogue almost entirely: the animals communicate in bleats, grunts and expressive gestures, and the stories are told through visual slapstick in episodes of about seven minutes.

Films and reception

The franchise has produced two feature films: Shaun the Sheep Movie (2015), nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature, and A Shaun the Sheep Movie: Farmageddon (2019), as well as television specials including The Flight Before Christmas (2021). The series has been broadcast in some 180 countries, won multiple British Academy Children's Awards and an International Emmy, and is noted for references aimed equally at children and adults.
