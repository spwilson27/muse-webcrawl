Panchanga

A Panchanga (Sanskrit: पञ्चाङ्गम्, pañcāṅgam) is a Hindu calendar and almanac, which follows traditional units of Hindu timekeeping, and presents important dates and their calculations in a tabulated form. The word derives from pañca ("five") and aṅga ("limb"), referring to the five attributes of the day that it records. Panchangas are consulted across India and Nepal, and by Hindu communities worldwide, for fixing festivals, marriages and other auspicious activities (muhurta).

The five limbs

The five elements tabulated for each day are:

  • Tithi — the lunar day, defined by the angular separation of the Moon from the Sun.
  • Vara — the weekday, each ruled by a celestial deity/planet.
  • Nakshatra — the lunar mansion (one of 27 divisions of the ecliptic) occupied by the Moon.
  • Yoga — a division based on the combined longitude of the Sun and Moon.
  • Karana — a half-tithi, of which eleven recur in a lunar month.

Contents and use

A full Panchanga also gives the times of sunrise and sunset, moonrise, the positions and transits of the planets, eclipses, the ruling rashi (zodiacal sign), festival dates, fasting days (vrata), and auspicious and inauspicious periods such as Rahu kala. Regional editions follow either the solar or the lunar (lunisolar) reckoning — for example the Tamil and Malayalam almanacs are largely solar, while most North Indian panchangas are lunisolar, with either Amanta or Purnimanta month endings.

Traditionally printed annually by hereditary astrologer families and temple authorities, Panchangas are today also published by religious trusts and widely available as mobile apps and websites, but their underlying computations still follow the classical astronomical treatises and regional conventions.
