London Docklands

The London Docklands is the semi-official name for a large area in east and southeast London, England, forming part of the former Port of London. The docks were the world's largest port through much of the 19th and early 20th centuries. After their decline and closure between the 1960s and 1980s, the area was redeveloped from the 1980s onward, most famously with the Canary Wharf financial district on the Isle of Dogs.

The working docks

From the 1800s, enclosed docks were built along both banks of the Thames: the West India Docks (1802), London Docks (1805), East India Docks (1806), Surrey Commercial Docks, Millwall Dock, the Royal Docks (Royal Victoria, Royal Albert and King George V) and Tilbury further downstream. At their peak the Port of London employed tens of thousands of dockers and handled much of the trade of the British Empire.

Decline

Containerisation from the 1960s moved shipping to deep-water ports such as Tilbury and Felixstowe, which the cramped upriver docks could not serve. Between 1967 and 1981 nearly all the London docks closed, leaving some 21 km² of derelict land and severe local unemployment.

Regeneration

In 1981 the Secretary of State for the Environment, Michael Heseltine, designated the London Docklands Development Corporation (LDDC) to regenerate the area, with an Enterprise Zone on the Isle of Dogs offering tax breaks. The flagship Canary Wharf project — masterminded by Canadian developers Olympia & York — produced One Canada Square (1990), for a time Britain's tallest building, and a second financial centre rivalling the City of London. The Docklands Light Railway (opened 1987) and the Jubilee line extension (1999) connected the area to the Underground network. Other redeveloped areas include Wapping, Limehouse, Surrey Quays, the Royal Docks (now home to the ExCeL exhibition centre and London City Airport) and Greenwich Peninsula.

Criticism

Regeneration was controversial: new housing was often unaffordable to existing dockland communities, and the LDDC was criticised for its unelected status and for prioritising commercial development over local needs.
