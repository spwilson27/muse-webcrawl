Christopher Reeve

Born: September 25, 1952, New York City, U.S.
Died: October 10, 2004, Mount Kisco, New York, U.S.
Occupation: Actor, director, activist
Known for: Superman film series

Christopher D'Olier Reeve (September 25, 1952 – October 10, 2004) was an American actor, film director, and activist, best known for playing the title character in the film Superman (1978) and its three sequels.

Career

Trained at Cornell University and the Juilliard School, Reeve worked in theatre and the soap opera Love of Life before winning the role of Superman, which made him an international star. His other films include Somewhere in Time (1980), Deathtrap (1982), The Bostonians (1984), Street Smart (1987), and The Remains of the Day (1993).

Accident and activism

On May 27, 1995, Reeve was thrown from a horse during an equestrian competition in Virginia, sustaining a cervical spinal cord injury that left him paralyzed from the neck down (quadriplegic) and dependent on a ventilator. He became a prominent advocate for spinal cord injury research and for people with disabilities, co-founding the Christopher Reeve Foundation (later the Christopher & Dana Reeve Foundation) and lobbying for stem cell research. He continued to act and direct, starring in a television remake of Rear Window (1998). Reeve died of heart failure in 2004, aged 52.
