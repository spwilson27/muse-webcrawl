Potassium nitrate

Potassium nitrate is a chemical compound with the formula KNO3, commonly known as saltpetre (or saltpeter). It is the nitrate salt of potassium, a strong oxidizer, and one of the principal ingredients of gunpowder (black powder), in which it is mixed with charcoal and sulfur. A white crystalline solid, it also occurs naturally as the mineral niter, historically scraped from cave walls and produced in "niter beds" from manure and urine — a supply of strategic importance for centuries of warfare.

Production

Historically, saltpetre was produced in nitre plantations (niter beds) by mixing manure, earth, and urine, waiting for nitrifying bacteria to generate nitrates, then leaching and converting with potash. During the American Civil War and the Napoleonic Wars, such production was a state priority. Modern manufacture uses the reaction of potassium chloride with nitric acid or sodium nitrate (double decomposition).

Uses

Beyond black powder — still used in fireworks, model rocket motors, and fuses — potassium nitrate is a major fertilizer (supplying nitrogen and potassium, 13-0-44), a food preservative (E252) in cured meats, an ingredient in some toothpastes for sensitive teeth, and a component of stump removers and smoke bombs with sugar. Its endothermic dissolution was historically used in cooling mixtures.

Safety

As an oxidizer it intensifies combustion of flammable materials and must be stored away from fuels and reducing agents; it is of low acute toxicity but nitrate contamination of water is an environmental concern.
