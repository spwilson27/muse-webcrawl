Qaboos bin Said Al Said (18 November 1940 – 10 January 2020) was the Sultan of Oman from 1970 until his death in 2020. He came to power by deposing his father, Said bin Taimur, in a palace coup on 23 July 1970, and ruled for nearly fifty years — making him the longest-serving leader in the Middle East and the Arab world at the time of his death. He is widely credited with transforming Oman from an isolated, underdeveloped state into a modern, prosperous country.

Early life and accession

Qaboos was educated in Salalah and in England, graduating from the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst and serving briefly with a British infantry regiment before returning to Oman, where his father kept him under virtual house arrest. In the 1970 coup — supported by British officials and Omani officers — Said bin Taimur abdicated and went into exile in London. Qaboos inherited a country fighting the communist-backed Dhofar Rebellion, which he defeated by 1976 with British, Jordanian and Iranian support, combining military action with development programmes in the south.

Reign

Using oil revenues, Qaboos built roads, schools, hospitals and ports, ending Oman's isolation. In foreign policy he cultivated a reputation for pragmatic neutrality and quiet mediation, maintaining relations with both Iran and the West and hosting discreet diplomacy. In 1996 he promulgated the Basic Statute of the State, Oman's first written constitutional framework. During the 2011 Arab Spring, limited protests in Oman led him to grant some legislative powers to the elected Shura Council and increase public spending. A lifelong enthusiast of classical music, he founded the Royal Oman Symphony Orchestra. Childless and unmarried, he left the succession to a sealed envelope; on his death in January 2020 his cousin Haitham bin Tariq was proclaimed sultan.
