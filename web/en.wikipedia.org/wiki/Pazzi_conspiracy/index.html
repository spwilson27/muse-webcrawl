Pazzi conspiracy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Pazzi conspiracy (Congiura dei Pazzi) was a plot by members of the Pazzi family and others to displace the Medici family as rulers of Renaissance Florence. On 26 April 1478, during High Mass in Florence Cathedral, conspirators attacked Lorenzo de' Medici and his brother Giuliano. Giuliano was stabbed to death; Lorenzo escaped with a wound, and the ensuing popular fury destroyed the conspirators. The plot was backed by Pope Sixtus IV and Federico da Montefeltro, Duke of Urbino, and led to war between Florence and the papacy.

Background

The Pazzi were an old Florentine banking family, rivals of the Medici, enriched as bankers to the papal court — a role Sixtus IV transferred from the Medici to the Pazzi amid quarrels over loans for the pope's purchase of Imola, which Lorenzo opposed. Sixtus's nephew Girolamo Riario, who coveted Florentine territory, and Francesco Salviati, appointed Archbishop of Pisa against Lorenzo's wishes, joined the Pazzi in plotting to kill both Medici brothers and seize the government. Veteran condottiere Federico da Montefeltro was a silent partner, with troops ready outside the city.

The attack

The assassination was timed for the elevation of the Host during Mass in the Duomo on Sunday, 26 April 1478, with the young Cardinal Raffaele Riario present as unwitting cover. Two priests, Stefano da Bagnone and Antonio Maffei, were assigned to kill Lorenzo because the hired killer, the condottiere Giovan Battista da Montesecco, refused to murder in a church; Montesecco's confession later became the key source for the plot. Giuliano, who had been lured to the cathedral despite illness, was struck down by Francesco de' Pazzi and Bernardo Baroncelli, receiving nineteen wounds. Lorenzo was slashed in the neck but escaped into the sacristy, where friends barricaded the doors until the crowd rallied.

Aftermath

Florence erupted in support of the Medici. Jacopo de' Pazzi's attempt to raise the city failed; he was hanged from a window of the Palazzo della Signoria along with his nephew Francesco and Archbishop Salviati — whose hanging, in full vestments, scandalised Rome. Scores of conspirators and Pazzi relatives were executed or exiled, Pazzi property confiscated, the family name and arms suppressed, and even marriage into the family forbidden. Botticelli painted the hanged conspirators on a wall of the Palazzo (effigies later removed).

Sixtus IV excommunicated Lorenzo and placed Florence under interdict, and with Naples he made war on Florence (1478–1480). Lorenzo's daring personal diplomacy in Naples in 1479–80 split the alliance and ended the war, leaving his position stronger than before. Historians regard the conspiracy as a turning point that consolidated Medici rule from an oligarchic partnership toward a personal lordship.

In popular culture

The conspiracy features in the video game Assassin's Creed II (2009), the television series Da Vinci's Demons and Medici, and Thomas Harris's novel Hannibal, whose villain Mason Verger's ancestor the Pazzi detective commemorates the family's disgrace.
