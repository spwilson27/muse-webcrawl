Grassroots sport

Grassroots sport refers to sporting activity undertaken by ordinary people at a local, community level, as distinct from elite, professional, or high-performance sport. Grassroots sport is typically organized by volunteers, schools, community clubs, and local leagues, and emphasizes participation, enjoyment, and health rather than commercial success or elite competition.

Characteristics

Grassroots sport is characterized by open access regardless of ability, low-cost participation, and a reliance on volunteers for coaching, officiating, and administration. It forms the base of the sporting "pyramid," from which talented participants may progress into development pathways and eventually elite sport.

Importance

Governments and sporting federations promote grassroots sport for its public-health benefits — regular physical activity reduces the risk of cardiovascular disease, obesity, and diabetes — and for social benefits including community cohesion and youth development. Funding models in many countries channel a share of elite-sport or lottery revenue back into grassroots facilities and clubs.

Challenges

Grassroots sport commonly faces shortages of volunteers, ageing facilities, rising insurance and maintenance costs, and competition for participants' time. Governing bodies often run campaigns to recruit coaches and officials and to make community sport more inclusive for women, disabled people, and minority groups.
