Colour revolutions

Date2000–2005 (core wave)
LocationPost-communist states of Europe and Central Asia
MethodsNonviolent resistance, mass protest, election monitoring

Colour revolution (sometimes color revolution) is a term used widely by worldwide media to describe various protest movements and accompanying attempted or successful changes of government that took place in post-communist Europe and Central Asia in the early 2000s. The movements typically followed disputed elections and involved largely nonviolent resistance, adopted a specific colour or flower as their symbol, and sought democratic reforms and closer integration with the West.

Principal movements

  • Bulldozer Revolution (Serbia, 2000) — overthrow of Slobodan Milošević, often considered the prototype.
  • Rose Revolution (Georgia, 2003) — resignation of Eduard Shevardnadze and the rise of Mikheil Saakashvili.
  • Orange Revolution (Ukraine, 2004) — protests against electoral fraud that led to a re-run of the presidential election won by Viktor Yushchenko.
  • Tulip Revolution (Kyrgyzstan, 2005) — ousting of Askar Akayev.
  • Cedar Revolution (Lebanon, 2005) — sometimes grouped with the colour revolutions, it followed the assassination of Rafic Hariri and led to the withdrawal of Syrian troops.

Characteristics

Common features included a unified opposition coalition, student and youth movements (such as Serbia's Otpor!, Georgia's Kmara and Ukraine's Pora), independent election monitoring, foreign democracy-promotion funding, and sophisticated branding built around a single colour. Gene Sharp's writings on nonviolent action influenced activists, and successful movements often trained their successors elsewhere.

Interpretations

Supporters see the colour revolutions as genuine popular movements against corrupt, authoritarian rule. Critics, including the governments of Russia and China, have portrayed them as Western-orchestrated regime change, a view that contributed to restrictive "foreign agent" laws and suspicion of NGOs in several states. The concept has since been applied retrospectively and prospectively to movements elsewhere, including the Arab Spring.
