Pau (French pronunciation: [po]; Occitan: Pau) is a commune overlooking the Pyrenees, and the prefecture (capital) of the Pyrénées-Atlantiques department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region of south-western France. The city lies on the northern edge of the Pyrenees, astride the Gave de Pau river, and is famed for the view from its Boulevard des Pyrénées over the mountain chain. Pau was the historic capital of Béarn and the birthplace of Henry IV of France.

History

The town grew up around a castle founded in the 11th century by the viscounts of Béarn on a bluff above the Gave de Pau. The Château de Pau was remodelled over the centuries; Gaston III, Count of Foix ("Gaston Fébus"), added its great brick keep in the 14th century. In 1512 Pau became the capital of the kings of Navarre, and Henry of Navarre — the future Henry IV of France — was born in the château in 1553. According to legend, his cradle was a giant tortoise shell, preserved in the castle to this day.

In the 19th century Pau became one of Europe's most fashionable winter resorts, especially for the British, attracted by its mild climate; a British doctor, Alexander Taylor, promoted its health benefits, and the city acquired an English church, villas, the first golf course on the European continent (1856) and a celebrated horse-racing course. The Wright brothers opened a flying school at nearby Pont-Long in 1909, making Pau a cradle of French military aviation.

Geography and climate

Pau stands on a terrace about 165–245 metres above sea level, some 100 km from the Atlantic coast and 50 km from the Spanish border. Its climate is oceanic, mild in winter with abundant rainfall; on clear days the panorama from the Boulevard des Pyrénées takes in peaks from the Pic du Midi de Bigorre to the Pic d'Anie.

Sport and culture

The city hosts the Pau Grand Prix (first run in 1930, among the oldest street races in France), the Étoiles de Pau international eventing competition, and the Section Paloise rugby union club. The Musée national du Château de Pau, a national museum of France, preserves the birthplace of Henry IV. Pau is also the birthplace of the Bernadotte dynasty: Jean-Baptiste Bernadotte, born in Pau in 1763, became King Charles XIV John of Sweden.
