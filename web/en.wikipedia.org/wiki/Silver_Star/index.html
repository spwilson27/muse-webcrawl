The Silver Star Medal (SSM) is the United States Armed Forces' third-highest military decoration for valour in combat. It is awarded primarily to members of the United States Armed Forces for gallantry in action against an enemy of the United States, and may also be awarded to allied and foreign military members serving with American forces.

History

The Silver Star grew out of the "Citation Star," a small silver star device authorised in 1918 to be worn on campaign ribbons by soldiers cited for gallantry. In 1932 the Citation Star was converted into a full medal, retroactive to the Spanish–American War, at the urging of General Douglas MacArthur. The design — a large silver star encircled by a laurel wreath, with a smaller gold star at its centre — was created by the sculptor Bailey Banks.

Criteria and wear

The medal is awarded for gallantry that, while of a lesser degree than that required for the Distinguished Service Cross or its naval and air equivalents, must nevertheless have been performed with marked distinction. Multiple awards are denoted by oak leaf clusters (Army and Air Force) or gold and silver stars (Navy, Marine Corps, and Coast Guard).
