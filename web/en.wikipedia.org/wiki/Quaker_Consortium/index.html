The Quaker Consortium is an academic cooperative in the Philadelphia area of the United States linking four institutions founded by or historically associated with the Religious Society of Friends (Quakers): Bryn Mawr College, Haverford College, Swarthmore College and the University of Pennsylvania.

Cross-registration

Under the consortium, undergraduate and graduate students at any member institution may register for courses at the others without additional tuition, greatly widening the curriculum available at the three small liberal arts colleges and giving Penn students access to their intimate seminar settings. Bryn Mawr and Haverford form the closest partnership — the "Bi-College" community — with a shared course catalogue, joint departments and a dedicated bus service; adding Swarthmore creates the "Tri-College" community, whose students are linked by van services and joint library borrowing.

History

Formal cooperation between Bryn Mawr and Haverford dates to the mid-20th century and expanded in the 1960s and 1970s to include Swarthmore and the University of Pennsylvania. The arrangement takes its name from the Quaker heritage of all four schools, though only Haverford and Bryn Mawr retain formal Quaker ties today.
