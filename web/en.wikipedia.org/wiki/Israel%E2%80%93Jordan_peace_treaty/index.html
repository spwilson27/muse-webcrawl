Israel–Jordan peace treaty

Signed26 October 1994, Arava/Araba crossing
PartiesIsrael, Jordan
SignatoriesYitzhak Rabin, King Hussein, Abdel Salam al-Majali

The Israel–Jordan peace treaty (formally the Treaty of Peace Between the State of Israel and the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan), signed on 26 October 1994, normalised relations between the two countries and resolved long-standing territorial and water disputes. Jordan became only the second Arab country, after Egypt, to sign a peace treaty with Israel.

Background

The treaty followed the 1993 Oslo Accords between Israel and the Palestinians and a July 1994 Washington Declaration that formally ended the state of war in place since 1948. Talks were facilitated by the United States, and US President Bill Clinton attended the signing ceremony in the Arava valley on the border.

Terms

The treaty fixed the international boundary along the Jordan and Yarmouk rivers, arranged allocations of shared water resources, opened borders to tourism and trade, and addressed security cooperation. A special regime applied to the Baqura/Naharayim and Zofar/al-Ghamr enclaves, whose leasing arrangements ended in 2019.
