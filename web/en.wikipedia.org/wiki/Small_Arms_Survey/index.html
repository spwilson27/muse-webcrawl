Small Arms Survey

The Small Arms Survey (SAS) is an independent research project located at the Graduate Institute of International and Development Studies in Geneva, Switzerland. It provides publicly available information on all aspects of small arms and armed violence and acts as a resource centre for governments, policy-makers, researchers, and activists.

History

The Small Arms Survey was established in 1999. Its flagship publication, the Small Arms Survey Year Book, was published annually from 2001 to 2018 and has been translated into several languages including Arabic, Chinese, French, Portuguese, Russian, and Spanish. The project also publishes occasional papers, special reports, working papers, and briefing papers, as well as a podcast series.

Research

The Survey's research fields include the global distribution and effects of small arms, stockpile management, illicit trafficking, civilian firearm ownership, and armed violence reduction. Its widely cited estimates include a 2018 finding that there are over one billion firearms in the world, the great majority of them in civilian hands. The Survey maintains the Small Arms Trade Transparency Barometer, which evaluates countries' reporting on arms transfers.

Funding

The project receives financial support from a range of governments and foundations, and works with the United Nations and other international organisations on small arms control programmes.
