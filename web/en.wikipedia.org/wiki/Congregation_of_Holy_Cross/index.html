Congregation of Holy Cross

The Congregation of Holy Cross founded the University of Notre Dame in 1842.

The Congregation of Holy Cross (Latin: Congregatio a Sancta Cruce, abbreviated CSC) is a Catholic religious congregation of priests and brothers founded in 1837 in Le Mans, France, by Blessed Basil Moreau. It was formed from the merger of Moreau's Auxiliary Priests with the Brothers of St. Joseph, a community founded by Jacques-François Dujarié.

Holy Cross religious are dedicated especially to education and parish ministry, and work in Europe, North and South America, Africa and Asia. In 1842, Edward Sorin led a group of Holy Cross brothers to Indiana in the United States, where they founded the University of Notre Dame. The congregation also administers other colleges and universities, including the University of Portland and Stonehill College, and the Holy Cross Mission in Bangladesh and East Africa.

The congregation's motto is Ave Crux Spes Unica — "Hail the Cross, Our Only Hope." Related communities include the Marianites of Holy Cross, the Sisters of the Holy Cross, and the Sisters of Holy Cross, all tracing their roots to Moreau's foundation at Sainte-Croix, Le Mans.
