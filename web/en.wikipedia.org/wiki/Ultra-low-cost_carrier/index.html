Ultra-low-cost carrier

An ultra-low-cost carrier (ULCC) is a type of low-cost airline whose business model takes cost reduction and fare unbundling further than traditional low-cost carriers. ULCCs typically offer very low base fares and charge separately for nearly all ancillary services, including checked and carry-on baggage, seat selection, boarding priority, and onboard refreshments, deriving a large share of revenue from these fees.

ULCCs use high-density single-class cabins and rapid turnarounds to keep unit costs minimal.

Business model

Key features include operating a single aircraft type with high-density seating, flying point-to-point routes (often to secondary airports), high daily aircraft utilization with fast turnarounds, minimal free in-flight service, and aggressive direct online sales. Examples include Spirit Airlines and Frontier Airlines in the United States, Allegiant Air (often classified similarly), and European carriers such as Wizz Air and Ryanair that apply comparable models.

Reception

ULCCs are credited with stimulating new demand by making air travel affordable to price-sensitive passengers and forcing fare competition on contested routes. They are also frequently criticized for fees that can exceed the base fare, tight seating pitch, and poor rankings in customer-satisfaction surveys.
