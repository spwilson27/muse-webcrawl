Protein skimmer

A protein skimmer or foam fractionator is a device used in saltwater aquaria to remove organic compounds such as food and waste particles from the water before they break down into nitrogenous waste. Protein skimming is the only form of filtration that physically removes organic compounds before they begin to decompose, reducing the load on the biological filter.

Function

The skimmer injects large numbers of tiny air bubbles into a column of water. Dissolved organic molecules, being amphiphilic, adsorb onto the bubble surfaces; as the bubbles rise they form a stable foam that overflows into a collection cup, carrying the waste out of the system. Only dissolved organic compounds with both hydrophobic and hydrophilic regions are removed effectively, along with some suspended particles and bacteria.

Designs

Common designs include air-driven (counter-current) skimmers using wooden air stones, venturi skimmers, and needle-wheel or pinwheel pump skimmers, which chop air into very fine bubbles. Skimmers are rated by the tank volume they can service and may sit in a sump, hang on the tank, or stand externally.

Use

Protein skimmers are considered near-essential equipment for reef aquariums keeping corals and other invertebrates sensitive to nitrate and phosphate, and they are also used in aquaculture and public aquaria. They are less common in freshwater systems because foaming is weaker at lower ionic strength.
