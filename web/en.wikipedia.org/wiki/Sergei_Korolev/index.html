Sergei Korolev

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Korolev, whose identity was a state secret until his death.

Sergei Pavlovich Korolev (12 January 1907 – 14 January 1966) was the lead Soviet rocket engineer and spacecraft designer during the Space Race. As head of the OKB-1 design bureau he was the mastermind behind the R-7 rocket — the world's first intercontinental ballistic missile and the ancestor of the Soyuz launcher family — and the programme that launched Sputnik 1, Laika, and Yuri Gagarin.

Korolev's identity was a state secret: publicly he was known only as the 'Chief Designer', and his name was revealed only after his death. In the 1930s he had been arrested during the Great Purge and spent years in prison and a sharashka design bureau. His early death during surgery in 1966, amid the Soviet effort to beat America to the Moon, is often seen as a turning point after which the Soviet lunar programme faltered. The town near Moscow that hosts Russia's mission control is now named Korolyov in his honour.
