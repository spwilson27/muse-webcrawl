The Pikes Peak International Hill Climb (PPIHC), also known as the "Race to the Clouds", is an annual automobile hillclimb to the summit of Pikes Peak in Colorado, USA. First held in 1916, it is one of the oldest motor races in the United States.

Course

The course runs along the Pikes Peak Highway for 12.42 miles (19.99 km), climbing 4,720 ft (1,439 m) from a start at 9,390 ft to the finish on the summit at 14,115 ft, through 156 turns and switchbacks. The thin air at altitude robs internal-combustion engines of power and tests drivers' stamina. The road was partly gravel until 2001; paving proceeded in stages, and the course has been fully paved since 2012.

History and records

The race was founded by Spencer Penrose, who built the highway to promote the region. The Unser family dominated much of the 20th century, while in the rally era the event became famous through the short film Climb Dance (1989), featuring Ari Vatanen's record run. Sébastien Loeb set a record of 8:13.878 in 2013 in a Peugeot 208 T16. In 2018, Romain Dumas broke the eight-minute barrier with 7:57.148 in the all-electric Volkswagen I.D. R, helped by electric motors' immunity to altitude.

Classes

Competition is divided into divisions and classes for cars, motorcycles, and exhibition vehicles, including the Unlimited and Time Attack divisions. The event takes place in late June each year.
