Cape Tribulation is a headland and locality in the Douglas Shire of Far North Queensland, Australia, located within the Daintree National Park and the Wet Tropics World Heritage Area. The area is notable as one of the few places in the world where ancient tropical rainforest meets a coral reef system directly along the shoreline.

History

The cape was named by Captain James Cook on 10 June 1770, after his ship HMS Endeavour ran aground on a shoal of the Great Barrier Reef just off the coast the previous night. Cook wrote that "the north point was named Cape Tribulation because here began all our troubles." The Endeavour was careened further south for repairs. The area is the traditional land of the Eastern Kuku Yalanji people.

Environment

Cape Tribulation receives very high rainfall and supports lowland tropical rainforest containing many ancient lineages of plants, including cycads and the idiot fruit tree. Mount Sorrow and Mount Hemmant rise behind the cape. Beaches such as Myall Beach and Coconut Beach border the headland, and fringing reefs lie close offshore. The locality is a popular eco-tourism destination, reached by road from the Daintree River ferry crossing.

1960s–1980s development disputes

In the early 1980s, plans to build a sealed road through the rainforest north of the cape provoked a celebrated environmental protest, the Cape Tribulation blockade, which helped spur the area's eventual World Heritage listing in 1988.
