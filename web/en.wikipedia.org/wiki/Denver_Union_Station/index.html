Denver Union Station

Location1701 Wynkoop Street, Denver, Colorado
Opened1881 (original); 1914 (current headhouse)
StyleBeaux-Arts
ServicesAmtrak, RTD commuter rail and light rail, buses

Denver Union Station is the main railway station and central transportation hub in Denver, Colorado. Located in the Lower Downtown (LoDo) district, its historic Beaux-Arts terminal building is crowned by a large neon sign reading "Union Station — Travel by Train" and a clock tower.

History

The first station on the site opened in 1881 as the largest building in Colorado at the time. After a fire in 1894, the central portion was rebuilt; the present grand headhouse, designed by Gove & Walsh, opened in 1914. At its peak during the Second World War, the station handled more than 80 trains a day.

Redevelopment

Following a major redevelopment completed in 2014, the station reopened as a multimodal transit center and mixed-use destination. The Great Hall houses the Crawford Hotel, restaurants, and shops, while a new train hall, bus terminal, and light rail and commuter rail platforms (including the A Line to Denver International Airport) serve the Regional Transportation District and Amtrak's California Zephyr.
