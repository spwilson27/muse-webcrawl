Patient-centered medical home

The patient-centered medical home (PCMH) is a model of primary care organization and delivery in the United States that aims to provide comprehensive, coordinated, patient-centered care with improved access and quality. The term "medical home" was first used by the American Academy of Pediatrics in 1967 to describe a central source of a child's medical records, and was expanded in the 2000s into a broader model for all primary care.

Principles

The 2007 Joint Principles of the Patient-Centered Medical Home, issued by the major American primary care physician organizations, define its features: a personal physician for each patient; physician-directed, team-based practice; whole-person orientation; care coordinated and integrated across the health system; emphasis on quality and safety, including evidence-based medicine and health information technology; enhanced access to care; and payment reform recognizing the added value of the model. Recognition programs, most prominently that of the National Committee for Quality Assurance (NCQA), certify practices against PCMH standards.

Evidence and criticism

Evaluations have found mixed but generally positive effects: improvements in preventive services and patient experience, and in some programs reduced emergency department use and cost, particularly for patients with chronic conditions. Critics note the administrative burden of recognition and the mixed strength of savings evidence. PCMH concepts underpin later reforms such as accountable care organizations and advanced primary care models in Medicare.
