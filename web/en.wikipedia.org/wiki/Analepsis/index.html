Analepsis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Analepsis is a narrative device in which an earlier event is inserted into the chronological sequence of a story — commonly known as a flashback. The term was systematised by the narratologist Gérard Genette in his work Narrative Discourse (1972), as the counterpart to prolepsis, or flashforward.

Types

Genette distinguishes external analepsis, which reaches back to events before the start of the main narrative; internal analepsis, which revisits events within its timeframe, either filling gaps (completing analepsis) or repeating scenes from a different perspective (repeating analepsis); and mixed forms. Analepsis can be carried by a narrator's account, a character's memory or confession, dreams, or documents within the story.

Examples

Classical literature opens in medias res and supplies the past through analepsis, as when Odysseus narrates his wanderings in the Odyssey. In modern fiction and film, flashbacks structure works such as Wuthering Heights, Citizen Kane, and Memento — the latter telling its story in reverse through a chain of analepses. The device reveals character motivation, supplies exposition, or recontextualises earlier scenes with new meaning.
