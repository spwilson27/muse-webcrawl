Listeriosis

Listeriosis is a bacterial infection most commonly caused by Listeria monocytogenes. It is primarily a foodborne illness, acquired by eating food contaminated with the bacterium, such as unpasteurized milk and soft cheeses, deli meats, hot dogs, and refrigerated smoked seafood. In healthy people the infection may cause only mild flu-like symptoms or gastroenteritis, but in pregnant women, newborns, the elderly, and people with weakened immune systems it can become invasive, spreading to the bloodstream and central nervous system.

Invasive listeriosis is a serious disease with a high fatality rate, commonly estimated at 20 to 30 percent of cases. It is one of the leading causes of death from foodborne illness in the United States, even though it is relatively uncommon, with roughly 1,600 cases estimated annually.

Signs and symptoms

In people with normal immune function, listeriosis may present as a self-limited febrile gastroenteritis with fever, muscle aches, nausea, and diarrhea. Invasive disease can cause sepsis, meningitis, and meningoencephalitis, with symptoms including fever, headache, stiff neck, confusion, loss of balance, and convulsions. During pregnancy, infection may cause only mild illness in the mother but can lead to miscarriage, stillbirth, premature delivery, or life-threatening infection of the newborn.

Cause

Listeria monocytogenes is a Gram-positive, rod-shaped bacterium that is widespread in the environment, including soil, water, and decaying vegetation. Unusually among foodborne pathogens, it can grow at refrigerator temperatures, which makes refrigerated ready-to-eat foods a particular risk. It is killed by pasteurization and thorough cooking.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually made by culturing the bacterium from blood or cerebrospinal fluid. Treatment of invasive disease is with antibiotics, typically intravenous ampicillin, often combined with gentamicin. Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole is used in patients allergic to penicillin.

Prevention

Prevention relies on food safety: washing raw vegetables, keeping refrigerators at or below 4 °C (40 °F), avoiding unpasteurized dairy products, and heating deli meats and hot dogs until steaming, especially for people in high-risk groups. Public health advice commonly given to pregnant women includes avoiding soft cheeses such as feta, Brie, and queso fresco unless made with pasteurized milk.

Epidemiology

Notable outbreaks include the 2011 United States outbreak linked to cantaloupes from Jensen Farms in Colorado, which caused 33 deaths, and the 2017–2018 outbreak in South Africa linked to processed meat products, the largest listeriosis outbreak on record, with over 1,000 confirmed cases and around 200 deaths.
