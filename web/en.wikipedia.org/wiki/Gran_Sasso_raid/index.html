Gran Sasso raid

The Gran Sasso raid (German codename Unternehmen Eiche, "Operation Oak") was the German rescue of deposed Italian dictator Benito Mussolini from imprisonment at the Campo Imperatore hotel on the Gran Sasso massif in the Abruzzo Apennines on 12 September 1943. A glider-borne force of German paratroopers and Waffen-SS commandos, fronted by Otto Skorzeny, landed beside the hotel, overawed its Carabinieri guards without a shot, and flew Mussolini out in a light aircraft to be taken to Hitler and installed as head of the puppet Italian Social Republic.

Background

On 25 July 1943 the Fascist Grand Council voted Mussolini out; King Victor Emmanuel III had him arrested and moved between hiding places, finally the ski hotel at Campo Imperatore, reachable only by cable car. After Italy's armistice with the Allies on 8 September, Hitler ordered Mussolini located and rescued before he could be handed over.

The raid

Intelligence (and the interception of a coded message) pinpointed the hotel. On 12 September ten DFS 230 gliders crash-landed on the rough plateau beside it, while other troops seized the cable-car station below. The Italian guards — instructed not to shed blood and confronted by an Italian general brought along to order them to stand down — offered no resistance. Mussolini was flown off the mountain in a Fieseler Storch light plane, overloaded with Skorzeny aboard, and taken via Rome and Vienna to Hitler's headquarters in East Prussia.

Aftermath

The exploit was a propaganda triumph for Germany and Skorzeny, whose role was magnified over that of the paratroop commander Harald Mors. Mussolini was installed at Salò as head of the Italian Social Republic, the German-backed state in northern Italy, until his capture and execution by partisans in April 1945.
