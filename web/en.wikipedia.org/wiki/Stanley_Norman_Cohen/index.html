Stanley Norman Cohen

Stanley Norman Cohen (born 17 February 1935) is an American geneticist and professor at Stanford University School of Medicine. Together with Herbert Boyer, he performed the first successful experiments in recombinant DNA technology, work that founded the biotechnology industry.

Biography

Cohen was born in Perth Amboy, New Jersey. He studied at Rutgers University and earned his medical degree from the University of Pennsylvania in 1960. After training in internal medicine and research at institutions including Mount Sinai and the National Institutes of Health, he joined the Stanford faculty in 1968, where he became professor of medicine and, later, the Kwoh-Ting Li Professor in the School of Medicine.

Recombinant DNA

Cohen's laboratory studied bacterial plasmids, the small circular DNA molecules that carry antibiotic-resistance genes. In 1972–1973, in collaboration with Herbert Boyer of UC San Francisco, Cohen and his colleagues (including Annie Chang and Robert Helling) showed that DNA fragments cut with the restriction enzyme EcoRI could be joined to plasmid DNA and introduced into Escherichia coli, where the foreign genes were replicated and expressed. In 1974 the team cloned genes from the frog Xenopus laevis in bacteria, demonstrating that genes could be transferred across species boundaries.

The Cohen–Boyer patent, granted in 1980, became one of the most lucrative licensing programs in university history and helped launch the biotechnology industry, including Boyer's company Genentech. Cohen co-founded the biotechnology company DNAX.

Honors

Cohen's awards include the Lasker Award, the National Medal of Science (1988), the National Medal of Technology (1989), the Wolf Prize in Medicine, and the Shaw Prize in Life Science and Medicine.
