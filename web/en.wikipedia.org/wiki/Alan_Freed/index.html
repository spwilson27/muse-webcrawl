Alan Freed

Alan Freed
Born: 15 December 1921, Windber, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Died: 20 January 1965 (aged 43), Palm Springs, California
Occupation: Disc jockey, radio personality
Known for: Popularising the term "rock and roll"

Albert James "Alan" Freed (December 15, 1921 – January 20, 1965) was an American disc jockey. He internationally popularised the concept of rock and roll music in the 1950s, and is often credited with coining / popularising the phrase "rock and roll" to describe rhythm and blues records played to a broad, racially mixed audience.

Career

Freed began in radio in Ohio in the 1940s, first in sports and classical music. At WJW in Cleveland from 1951, sponsored by record-store owner Leo Mintz, his late-night Moondog show played rhythm and blues for white teenagers. His Moondog Coronation Ball of 21 March 1952 in Cleveland is widely regarded as the first major rock and roll concert; it was shut down early due to overcrowding.

In 1954 Freed moved to WINS in New York, then to WABC. He promoted tours and appeared in rock and roll films such as Rock Around the Clock (1956), Don't Knock the Rock and Rock, Rock, Rock, featuring performers including Chuck Berry and Frankie Lymon.

Payola and downfall

Freed's career collapsed in the payola scandal. He was dismissed in 1959, pleaded guilty in 1962 to commercial bribery, and faced tax charges. He died in 1965 of uraemia and cirrhosis brought on by alcoholism.

Legacy

Freed was among the first inductees into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame — itself established in Cleveland partly because of his legacy — in 1986. He also has a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.
