Energy density

SI unitJoule per cubic metre (J/m³)
Other unitsMJ/L, Wh/L
Related quantitySpecific energy (per unit mass)

In physics, energy density is the amount of energy stored in a given system or region of space per unit volume. It is sometimes confused with energy per unit mass (specific energy), which is the more relevant quantity for fuels and batteries. The SI unit is the joule per cubic metre (J/m³), dimensionally identical to pressure.

Stored energy

Hydrocarbon fuels have much higher energy densities than electrical storage: petrol stores roughly 34 MJ/L, while a lithium-ion battery stores only around 1–2.6 MJ/L, which is why electrochemical storage requires far greater volumes for vehicles. At the extreme, nuclear reactions release millions of times more energy per unit volume than chemical reactions, and matter–antimatter annihilation is higher still.

Fields

Energy density also describes the energy stored in electric and magnetic fields. For a magnetic field, the energy density is B²/(2μ0); for an electric field, ε0E²/2. In cosmology, the energy densities of matter, radiation and dark energy determine the expansion history of the universe.

Food

In nutrition, energy density means the amount of energy (calories) per unit mass or volume of food. Foods with low energy density, such as fruits and vegetables, provide bulk with relatively few calories, a property used in weight-management strategies.
