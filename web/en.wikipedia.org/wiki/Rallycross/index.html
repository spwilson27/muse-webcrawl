Rallycross

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Rallycross is a form of sprint-style automobile racing held on a closed mixed-surface racing circuit, combining sections of asphalt with gravel or dirt. Cars race against each other directly in short heats, rather than against the clock as in rallying. The sport is especially popular in Northern and Western Europe and North America.

History

Rallycross was created in 1967 for a British television programme, with the first event held at Lydden Hill in Kent, England. It spread quickly to the Netherlands and Scandinavia, and a European championship was established in the 1970s. In 2014 the FIA launched the World Rallycross Championship (World RX), raising the sport's profile. Rallycross also featured prominently in the X Games in the United States, and formats such as the American-based Nitro Rallycross series have followed.

Format

Events typically consist of a series of qualifying heats, semi-finals and a final, with four to six cars starting side by side. Races are short — usually four to six laps — and most formats include a "joker lap", a slightly longer alternate section of track that each driver must take once during the race, adding a tactical element. Top-level Supercar/RX1 machines are four-wheel-drive cars based on production models, producing around 600 horsepower and accelerating from 0–100 km/h in under two seconds. Electric cars have taken over the top class of the world championship in recent years.
