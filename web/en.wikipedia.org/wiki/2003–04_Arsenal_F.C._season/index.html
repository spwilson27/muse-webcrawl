The 2003–04 season was Arsenal Football Club's twelfth in the Premier League and one of the most celebrated in English football history. Managed by Arsène Wenger, Arsenal won the Premier League without losing a single match — 26 wins and 12 draws — earning the team the nickname "The Invincibles", after the Preston North End side of 1888–89, and a unique gold replica Premier League trophy.

Squad and tactics

The team's spine combined goalkeeper Jens Lehmann and a defence of Lauren, Kolo Touré, Sol Campbell and Ashley Cole; a midfield anchored by Patrick Vieira and Gilberto Silva with the flair of Robert Pires and Freddie Ljungberg; and an attack led by Thierry Henry — voted PFA and FWA Player of the Year — alongside Dennis Bergkamp. Arsenal's fluid, fast counter-attacking football produced 73 league goals.

Campaign

The league title was effectively sealed with a 2–2 draw at rivals Tottenham Hotspur on 25 April 2004, sparking celebrations at White Hart Lane. Arsenal finished 11 points clear of Chelsea. In Europe the season ended in disappointment: after a famous 5–1 win at Inter Milan in the group stage, Arsenal were knocked out of the Champions League quarter-finals by Chelsea, and lost both domestic cup semi-finals. The unbeaten league run ultimately stretched to a national record 49 matches before ending at Manchester United in October 2004.

Legacy

The Invincibles are routinely cited among the greatest club sides ever assembled in England. Henry's 30 league goals won the European Golden Shoe, and the team's achievement — a full top-flight season unbeaten — had not been repeated since Preston's short 22-game season of 1889, and has not been matched since.
