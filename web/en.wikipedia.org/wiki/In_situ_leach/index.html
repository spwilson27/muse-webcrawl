In situ leach

In situ leach (ISL), also called in situ recovery (ISR) or solution mining, is a mining process used to recover minerals such as copper and uranium through boreholes drilled into a deposit, rather than by excavating the ore body. A leaching solution is pumped into the deposit, where it dissolves the target mineral; the mineral-laden ("pregnant") solution is then pumped back to the surface and processed.

Process

ISL requires a permeable ore body confined between impermeable layers so that the leach solution can be contained. For uranium, the leachate is typically groundwater fortified with oxygen or hydrogen peroxide and carbon dioxide or sodium bicarbonate, which mobilises uranium as a soluble carbonate complex. Wellfields use patterns of injection and extraction wells, surrounded by monitoring wells to detect excursions of solution outside the mining zone.

Applications and concerns

In situ recovery accounts for a large and growing share of the world's uranium production, particularly in Kazakhstan, the United States and Australia, and is also used for copper. Its advantages include low surface disturbance and lower cost; its principal environmental concern is groundwater quality, so mined aquifers must be restored toward baseline conditions after production ends.
