Isis Unveiled: A Master-Key to the Mysteries of Ancient and Modern Science and Theology is a book of esoteric philosophy published in 1877 by Helena Petrovna Blavatsky, co-founder of the Theosophical Society. It was Blavatsky's first major published work and is considered a foundational text of modern Theosophy.

Contents

The work is divided into two volumes. The first, The "Infallibility" of Modern Science, criticises the materialism of nineteenth-century science and argues that genuine knowledge of nature requires recognition of spiritual forces. The second, Theology, attacks dogmatic religion and surveys comparative religion, presenting ancient Egyptian religion, Hermeticism, Kabbalah, Buddhism, Hinduism and other traditions as fragments of a single primordial "Wisdom-Religion". The title refers to the veiled goddess Isis of Sais, whose inscription declared that no mortal had lifted her veil.

Writing and publication

Blavatsky wrote the book in New York in 1875–1877, working closely with Henry Steel Olcott. She claimed that much of the material was dictated or inspired by hidden adepts, the "Masters" or Mahatmas. The first edition, published by J. W. Bouton in September 1877, sold out quickly and brought Blavatsky international attention.

Reception

Critics accused Blavatsky of plagiarism, noting the text's extensive unattributed borrowing from contemporary books on occultism, a charge assessed in William Emmette Coleman's famous analysis of her sources. Admirers nonetheless regard the book as a landmark in the Western esoteric tradition and in the modern revival of interest in Eastern religions. Blavatsky herself later acknowledged its flaws and described it as a preliminary work, superseded in part by The Secret Doctrine.
