Warlpiri people

The Warlpiri, sometimes written Walpiri, are an Aboriginal Australian people of the Tanami Desert area of the Northern Territory and Western Australia. They are one of the largest Aboriginal groups in central Australia, numbering roughly 5,000–6,000 people, and many Warlpiri continue to speak the Warlpiri language and maintain strong ceremonial traditions.

Country and communities

Warlpiri country lies northwest of Alice Springs, centred on the Tanami Desert. The largest Warlpiri community is Yuendumu, established as a government settlement in 1946; others include Lajamanu, Nyirripi, Yuelamu and Alekarenge, with significant populations also in Alice Springs and Katherine.

Culture

Warlpiri social organisation is structured by a system of eight "skin names" (subsections) governing kinship and marriage, and by patrilineal and matrilineal ceremonial moieties. Central to Warlpiri law and religion is Jukurrpa (the Dreaming), ancestral beings whose travels created the land and its law. Warlpiri also became prominent in the Western Desert art movement, notably through the Warlukurlangu Artists cooperative at Yuendumu, and they developed an influential system of sign language used especially by women during mourning.

History

Europeans entered Warlpiri country in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In 1928 the Coniston massacre — the last recorded massacre of Aboriginal people in Australia — killed many Warlpiri, Anmatyerre and Kaytetye people following the murder of a dingo trapper. Warlpiri were prominent in the land rights era; the Aboriginal Land Rights (Northern Territory) Act 1976 enabled claims over much of their traditional country.
