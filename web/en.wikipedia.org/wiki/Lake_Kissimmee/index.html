Lake Kissimmee, Florida

Lake Kissimmee is a freshwater lake in central Florida, United States, straddling Osceola and Polk counties southeast of the city of Kissimmee. With a surface area of roughly 35,000 acres (about 140 km²), it is the third-largest lake entirely within Florida and the largest of the Kissimmee Chain of Lakes, which drains southward via the Kissimmee River to Lake Okeechobee.

Environment and use

The shallow lake is ringed by marsh, cypress and oak hammocks, and its littoral grass beds make it one of Florida's best-known bass fisheries. Much of the eastern shore is protected within Lake Kissimmee State Park, noted for its nineteenth-century cracker cow camp re-enactments, sandhill cranes, bald eagles and Florida scrub-jays. In the twentieth century the lake's levels were regulated by water-control structures as part of the Central and Southern Florida flood-control project, and periodic drawdowns are conducted to consolidate sediments and restore fish habitat.
