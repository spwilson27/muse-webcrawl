Belfast Lough (Irish: Loch Lao) is a large intertidal sea inlet on the east coast of Northern Ireland, at the mouth of the River Lagan. It opens into the North Channel of the Irish Sea and gives the city of Belfast, at its head, access to the sea.

Geography

The lough is roughly 19 kilometres long. Its northern shore, in County Antrim, includes Carrickfergus and Whitehead, while the southern shore in County Down includes Bangor and Holywood — the latter area of large residences being nicknamed the "Gold Coast". Carrickfergus Castle has guarded the northern shore since the 12th century.

Use and environment

Belfast Harbour, including the docks where RMS Titanic was built, lies at the head of the lough. The inner lough's mudflats are an important feeding ground for overwintering wildfowl and waders and are protected as a Ramsar site and Special Protection Area. The outer lough is a busy shipping channel serving the port of Belfast. See also River Lagan.
