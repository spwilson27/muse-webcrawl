Pashalik of Yanina

The Pashalik of Yanina, sometimes called the Pashalik of Janina or Ioannina, was a de facto autonomous region of the Ottoman Empire in western Greece and Albania from 1787 to 1822, ruled by the Albanian Ottoman lord Ali Pasha of Ioannina from his capital at Ioannina (Yanina).

History

Ali Pasha, appointed pasha of Ioannina in 1788, expanded his power over Epirus, Thessaly, much of mainland Greece and southern Albania, building a court, army and administration largely independent of Constantinople, and conducting his own diplomacy with Britain and Napoleonic France. His rule combined patronage of Greek culture and commerce with notorious brutality against rivals and rebellious groups such as the Souliotes.

In 1820 Sultan Mahmud II moved to curb him; Ali rebelled, and Ottoman forces besieged Ioannina. His fall in January 1822 — he was killed on the island in Lake Pamvotis — diverted Ottoman resources on the eve of, and during, the Greek War of Independence, indirectly aiding the Greek revolt that had begun in 1821.
