Blue Helmet

[Image: United Nations peacekeeper wearing the distinctive light-blue helmet on patrol]

Blue Helmet (French: Casque bleu) is a nickname for a member of a United Nations peacekeeping force, derived from the light-blue helmets and berets worn by UN military personnel so that they can be readily identified as neutral international troops. The term is used especially in French-language media, where UN peacekeepers are commonly called Casques bleus.

Origin

The blue headgear dates to the first UN emergency force, UNEF I, deployed during the Suez Crisis of 1956 under Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld and Canadian foreign minister Lester B. Pearson. Because the peacekeepers needed to be visually distinguishable from the combatants, surplus helmet liners were painted UN blue. The colour, matching the United Nations flag, became the standard for UN forces; blue berets or helmets remain part of the uniform today.

Usage

"Blue Helmets" is used as a collective noun for UN peacekeeping troops in many languages, alongside the English terms "peacekeepers" and "UN troops". Peacekeeping operations are authorised by the UN Security Council, staffed by personnel contributed by member states, and traditionally operate on principles of host-state consent, impartiality, and non-use of force except in self-defence and defence of the mandate — a form of limited interventionism distinct from war-fighting. Peacekeepers collectively received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1988.
