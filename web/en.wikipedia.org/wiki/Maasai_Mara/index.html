Maasai Mara National Reserve, also known simply as the Masai Mara or the Mara, is a large national game reserve in Narok County, Kenya, contiguous with the Serengeti National Park in Tanzania. It is named in honour of the Maasai people, the ancestral inhabitants of the area, who call it Enkare Narok. The word "Mara" means "spotted" in the Maasai language Maa, referring to the short bushy trees dotting the landscape. It is one of the most famous and important wildlife conservation and wilderness areas in Africa, world-renowned for its exceptional populations of lions, leopards, cheetahs and elephants, and for the annual migration of wildebeest.

Wildlife and the Great Migration

The reserve's rolling savanna grasslands, acacia woodland and the Mara and Talek river systems support herds of zebra, gazelle, eland, giraffe, buffalo and both black and white rhinoceros, together with hippos and crocodiles in the rivers. The Mara has one of the highest densities of lions in the world and is a stronghold of the African leopard and the cheetah.

Each year roughly 1.5 million wildebeest, accompanied by hundreds of thousands of zebra and gazelle, move in a vast loop between the Serengeti and the Mara in the Great Migration, arriving in the Mara typically from July to October. The dramatic Mara River crossings, where animals face crocodiles and steep banks, are among the most sought-after spectacles in safari tourism.

History and management

The area was first protected as a wildlife sanctuary in 1961, initially covering 520 km² including the Mara Triangle, and was extended and given national reserve status in 1974. Unlike a national park, a Kenyan national reserve is managed by the local county government (Narok County), while the western Mara Triangle is managed by the non-profit Mara Conservancy. The reserve is surrounded by private and community conservancies, such as Olare Motorogi and Mara North, which lease land from Maasai owners and have become a significant model of conservation tourism.

Tourism and issues

The Mara is Kenya's flagship safari destination, accessed by road from Nairobi or by scheduled flights to its many airstrips, with accommodation ranging from public campsites to luxury lodges. Pressures include poaching, human–wildlife conflict around its unfenced borders, land subdivision in the wider ecosystem, and debate over tourism density at migration crossings.
