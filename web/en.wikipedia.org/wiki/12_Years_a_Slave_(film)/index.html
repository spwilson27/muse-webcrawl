12 Years a Slave is a 2013 biographical drama film directed by Steve McQueen and written by John Ridley, based on the 1853 slave narrative Twelve Years a Slave by Solomon Northup, a free Black man from New York who was kidnapped in Washington, D.C. in 1841 and sold into slavery in Louisiana. The film stars Chiwetel Ejiofor as Northup, with Michael Fassbender, Lupita Nyong'o, Sarah Paulson, Paul Dano, Benedict Cumberbatch and Brad Pitt in supporting roles.

Plot

In 1841, Solomon Northup is a free African-American violinist living with his wife and children in Saratoga Springs, New York. Lured by two men offering circus work, he is drugged, chained and sold into slavery under the name "Platt". He is transported to Louisiana, where he is bought first by planter William Ford and later by the brutal Edwin Epps. For twelve years Northup endures forced labour on cotton plantations, concealing his literacy and identity. After confiding in Canadian abolitionist Samuel Bass, Northup's friends in New York are alerted, and he is finally freed and reunited with his family.

Production

McQueen developed the film with screenwriter John Ridley; Northup's memoir, long out of print prominence, was adapted closely. Principal photography took place in Louisiana in the summer of 2012, on a budget of about $20 million. Hans Zimmer composed the score, with Nicholas Britell arranging period music.

Release and reception

The film premiered at the Telluride Film Festival in August 2013 and was released theatrically in November 2013. It received widespread critical acclaim for its direction, screenplay, and the performances of Ejiofor, Fassbender and Nyong'o.

Accolades

At the 87th Academy Awards season cycle for 2013 films (the 86th Academy Awards), the film won Best Picture, Best Supporting Actress for Nyong'o, and Best Adapted Screenplay for Ridley. It was the first Best Picture winner directed and produced by a Black filmmaker. It also won the Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Drama and the BAFTA Award for Best Film.
