Actors' Equity Association

The Actors' Equity Association (AEA, commonly called Equity) is an American labour union representing actors and stage managers working in professional theatre. Founded in 1913, it negotiates contracts with producers — including Broadway's Production Contract — setting minimum salaries, working conditions, health and pension benefits, and safety rules for its members.

History

Actors organised in 1913 against exploitative touring conditions and won recognition after the Actors' Strike of 1919, which closed theatres nationwide and forced producers to accept Equity contracts. The union later admitted its first Black members' chapter struggles with segregation, campaigned over non-traditional casting, and has struck periodically, including over touring contracts and the development of new work. Its chorus counterpart merged into Equity in 1955.

Membership and contracts

Actors typically join by working under an Equity contract (through the Equity Membership Candidate programme) or by membership in a sister union. Equity administers dozens of agreements ranging from Broadway and national tours to small regional and 99-seat theatres, and only Equity members may be hired for most professional stage work in the United States.
