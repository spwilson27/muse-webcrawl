Mehmed VI Vahideddin (Ottoman Turkish: محمد سادس; 14 January 1861 – 16 May 1926) was the 36th and last sultan of the Ottoman Empire, reigning from 1918 until the abolition of the sultanate in 1922. He was also the penultimate Ottoman caliph, the religious office surviving separately under his cousin Abdülmecid II until 1924.

Accession

Born at the Dolmabahçe Palace, Mehmed was the youngest son of Sultan Abdülmecid I. For most of his life he lived in relative seclusion, studying Islamic law and calligraphy. He succeeded his half-brother Mehmed V on 4 July 1918, in the closing months of the First World War, and personally girded himself with the Sword of Osman at the Eyüp Sultan Mosque.

Reign and the end of the empire

Defeated in the war, the empire signed the Armistice of Mudros in October 1918, and Allied forces occupied Constantinople. Mehmed VI dissolved parliament, relied on governments led by his brother-in-law Damat Ferid Pasha, and accepted the punitive Treaty of Sèvres in 1920, which partitioned Anatolia. In response, Turkish nationalists under Mustafa Kemal established a rival government in Ankara, fought the Turkish War of Independence, and rejected the settlement.

After nationalist victories, the Grand National Assembly in Ankara abolished the sultanate on 1 November 1922, separating it from the caliphate. Mehmed VI left Constantinople on 17 November 1922 aboard the British warship HMS Malaya.

Exile and death

Mehmed lived briefly in Malta and then in Sanremo, Italy, where he died on 16 May 1926. He was buried in the Tekkiye Mosque in Damascus, Syria. The Ottoman dynasty itself was sent into exile in 1924 after the abolition of the caliphate.
