Mashhad (Persian: مشهد, romanised Mašhad, literally "place of martyrdom") is the second-most populous city in Iran and the capital of Razavi Khorasan Province. It is located in the north-east of the country, close to the borders with Turkmenistan and Afghanistan. Mashhad is one of the holiest cities in Shia Islam, as it contains the shrine of Imam Reza, the eighth Shia Imam, which draws tens of millions of pilgrims annually.

History

The city grew around the village of Sanabad, near the ancient city of Tus, after the eighth Shia Imam, Ali al-Ridha (Imam Reza), died there in 818 — traditionally said to have been poisoned by the Abbasid caliph al-Ma'mun — and was buried beside the tomb of Harun al-Rashid. The shrine made the settlement a major pilgrimage destination; the name Mashhad, "place of martyrdom", gradually replaced Sanabad. The city was sacked by Mongols and later flourished under the Safavids, who promoted Shia pilgrimage to it as an alternative to Mecca and Karbala. Nader Shah Afshar made Mashhad the capital of Persia in the 18th century and is buried there.

Imam Reza shrine

The Imam Reza shrine complex, administered by the Astan Quds Razavi foundation, covers a vast area of courtyards, mosques (including the Safavid-era Goharshad Mosque), libraries, museums and seminaries, and is continually expanded. Pilgrimage is the mainstay of the city's economy, alongside trade, saffron and turquoise from the wider Khorasan region.

Geography and climate

Mashhad lies in the valley of the Kashaf River between the Binalud and Hezar Masjed mountain ranges, at an elevation of about 995 metres. It has a cold semi-arid climate with hot summers and cold winters.
