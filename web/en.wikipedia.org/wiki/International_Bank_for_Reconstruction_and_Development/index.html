International Bank for Reconstruction and Development

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) is an international development bank and the lending arm of the World Bank Group for middle-income and creditworthy low-income governments. Founded at the Bretton Woods conference of 1944 and making its first loans in 1947 (for post-war reconstruction in Europe, beginning with France), it was the original institution to which “World Bank” referred, and remains the Group's largest.

The IBRD raises most of its funds on world capital markets on the strength of its members' callable capital and preferred-creditor status, and on-lends at rates below what borrowing governments could obtain themselves, for infrastructure, health, education, public administration and climate projects, typically with policy conditions and technical assistance. It has 189 member states (as of the mid-2020s); voting power is weighted by shareholding, with the United States the largest shareholder and by convention supplier of the Bank's president. Together with the concessional International Development Association it forms the World Bank proper.
