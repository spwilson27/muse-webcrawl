Squaric acid

FormulaC4H2O4
Name3,4-dihydroxycyclobut-3-ene-1,2-dione
AppearanceWhite crystalline solid

Squaric acid is an organic compound with the formula C4H2O4. Its molecule contains a four-membered ring of carbon atoms, giving the compound its name. It is a dibasic acid whose dianion is notable for its aromatic character, with the negative charge and pi electrons delocalised around the square ring.

Properties and uses

Squaric acid is a white crystalline solid, first reported in 1959. Its esters, especially dibutyl squarate, have been used in medicine as topical sensitising agents in the treatment of warts and alopecia areata. Squarate dyes, formed by condensing squaric acid with electron-rich aromatics, are important near-infrared dyes used in optical recording and solar cells. Derivatives called squaramides are used in organocatalysis and medicinal chemistry as hydrogen-bond donors.
