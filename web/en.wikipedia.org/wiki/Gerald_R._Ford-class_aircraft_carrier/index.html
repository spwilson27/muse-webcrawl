Gerald R. Ford-class aircraft carrier

The Gerald R. Ford class is a class of nuclear-powered aircraft carriers of the United States Navy, designed to replace the Nimitz-class carriers. Named for the 38th President of the United States, the lead ship USS Gerald R. Ford (CVN-78) was commissioned in 2017. The class introduces a new nuclear reactor design, electromagnetic aircraft launch systems, advanced arresting gear, and greater automation intended to increase sortie rates and reduce crew size and lifetime operating costs.

Design features

The class is built around the new A1B reactor plant, which generates substantially more electrical power than the A4W plant of the Nimitz class. That power enables the Electromagnetic Aircraft Launch System (EMALS), which accelerates aircraft with a linear induction motor instead of steam catapults, and the Advanced Arresting Gear (AAG), which uses water turbines and digital control to recover aircraft. A redesigned, smaller island set further aft, a larger flight deck, and advanced weapons elevators are intended to raise the sustained sortie generation rate to about 160 per day (220 in surge), roughly 25 percent above the Nimitz class. The ships also carry the dual-band radar and are designed to operate the F-35C Lightning II and future unmanned aircraft.

Development issues

The program was criticized for concurrency — building the first ship while key technologies such as EMALS, AAG, and the weapons elevators were still maturing — which contributed to cost growth (the lead ship cost about $13 billion) and schedule delays. Gerald R. Ford completed shock trials in 2021 and made her first full deployment in 2023, including an extended presence in the eastern Mediterranean following the October 2023 outbreak of war in Gaza.
