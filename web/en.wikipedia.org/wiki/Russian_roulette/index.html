Russian roulette

Russian roulette is a potentially lethal game of chance in which a participant places a single round in a revolver, spins the cylinder, places the muzzle against their head or body, and pulls the trigger. With a six-chamber cylinder, each pull has a one-in-six chance of firing the loaded chamber, assuming the cylinder is re-spun before each attempt.

Origin of the term

Although the game is associated with Russia in popular imagination, documented references are late and largely literary. The term first appeared in print in a 1937 short story by Georges Surdez in Collier's magazine, which described Russian officers of the First World War playing the game; earlier Russian literature, such as Lermontov's A Hero of Our Time, describes similar fatalistic bets with single-shot pistols but not the revolver game itself.

In culture and statistics

Russian roulette has been depicted in films such as The Deer Hunter (1978) and has been cited in public-health literature in connection with accidental and deliberate deaths, including cases involving celebrities. The phrase is also used metaphorically for any recklessly dangerous gamble.
