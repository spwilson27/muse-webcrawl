Papa Legba

Papa Legba is a lwa in Haitian Vodou who serves as the intermediary between the lwa (spirits) and humanity. He stands at a spiritual crossroads and gives (or withholds) permission to speak with the spirits of Guinee, and is believed to speak all human languages. Legba facilitates communication, speech and understanding.

A vèvè for Papa Legba, drawn in ceremonies to summon him

Role and iconography

Legba is the first lwa saluted in any Vodou ceremony, since no other spirit can be approached without his opening the gate. He is usually depicted as an old man on crutches or with a cane, wearing a straw hat and smoking a pipe, accompanied by dogs. His colours and offerings, and his Catholic counterpart — often Saint Peter, who holds the keys to heaven, or Saint Lazarus or Saint Anthony — reflect his role as keeper of thresholds. His symbols are the crossroads, keys and cane, and his ritual diagram (vèvè) is traced in cornmeal on the temple floor.

Origins and counterparts

Legba derives from the Fon and Yoruba trickster-messenger deities — Legba in Dahomey and Eshu (Elegba) in Yorubaland — carried to the Caribbean through the African diaspora. Related figures appear in Louisiana Voodoo and in Afro-Brazilian traditions. Because of his crossroads associations, he is frequently invoked in blues and popular culture, though often conflated with other spirits.
