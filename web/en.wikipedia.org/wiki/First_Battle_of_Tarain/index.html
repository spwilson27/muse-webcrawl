The First Battle of Tarain, also known as the First Battle of Taraori, was fought in 1191 between the Ghurid forces of Muhammad of Ghor and the Rajput confederacy led by Prithviraj Chauhan, the Chahamana king of Delhi and Ajmer. The battle took place near Taraori, close to Karnal in present-day Haryana, about 110 kilometres north of Delhi.

The battle

Muhammad of Ghor had advanced into the Punjab and captured the fortress of Bathinda (Tabarhinda), provoking Prithviraj to march against him with a large confederate host. In the ensuing battle the Rajputs defeated the Ghurid army; Muhammad himself was wounded — according to tradition, struck in personal combat — and was carried from the field, his army retreating to Ghazni.

Aftermath

Prithviraj recovered Bathinda after a siege, but did not pursue the Ghurids into the Punjab. Muhammad returned the following year with a reorganized army and won the Second Battle of Tarain (1192), a decisive turning point that opened northern India to Ghurid conquest.
