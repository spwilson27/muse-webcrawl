The Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA) is a research and development agency of the United States Department of Defense responsible for developing emerging technologies for military use. Known for high-risk, high-payoff research managed by a small staff of programme managers, DARPA has played a foundational role in technologies including the internet, GPS, stealth aircraft and voice-assistant software.

History

The agency was created as ARPA in February 1958, in direct response to the Soviet launch of Sputnik 1 in 1957, to ensure the United States would not again be caught by a technological surprise. Its early work covered space, missile defence and nuclear-test detection before space programmes were transferred to NASA and the military services. It was renamed DARPA in 1972, reverted to ARPA in 1993, and became DARPA again in 1996.

Notable programmes and legacy

DARPA funded the ARPANET (1969), the forerunner of the internet, and the TCP/IP protocols. Other landmarks include the TRANSIT and GPS navigation programmes, stealth technology demonstrated by Have Blue (leading to the F-117), unmanned aerial vehicles, the Grand Challenge contests that launched the self-driving car industry, the CALO project whose technology fed into Apple's Siri, and rapid vaccine-platform research. Its projects range across artificial intelligence, hypersonics, microelectronics, synthetic biology and space systems.

Organisation and model

DARPA employs roughly 200 government staff, including about 100 programme managers serving limited terms, and works through contracts with universities, industry and laboratories rather than its own labs. Its model — small, autonomous teams pursuing ambitious goals with tolerance for failure — has been widely emulated, including by ARPA-E (energy), ARPA-H (health) and analogous agencies abroad.
