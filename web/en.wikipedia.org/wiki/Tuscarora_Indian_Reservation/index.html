The Tuscarora Indian Reservation is an Indian reservation in Niagara County, New York, home to the federally recognized Tuscarora Nation, one of the Six Nations of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy. The reservation lies east of Niagara Falls and Lewiston, near the Niagara River.

History

The Tuscarora migrated north from North Carolina following the Tuscarora War (1711–1715) and were adopted as the Sixth Nation of the Iroquois Confederacy around 1722. The Seneca granted them land in the Niagara region, and additional land was later set aside by the Holland Land Company and confirmed through treaty arrangements. The Tuscarora sided largely with the United States in the War of 1812, defending Lewiston against British attack.

In the 1950s the New York Power Authority sought reservation land for the reservoir of the Niagara Power Project, a fight the Tuscarora lost in the 1960 Supreme Court case Federal Power Commission v. Tuscarora Indian Nation, though the resistance became a landmark of Native activism.

Government and culture

The Nation is governed by a council of chiefs selected by clan mothers, alongside elected and federal-recognition structures. The Tuscarora language, though endangered, is taught through community programmes. The reservation is known for its raised beadwork tradition.
