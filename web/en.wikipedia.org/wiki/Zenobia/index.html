Zenobia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The great colonnade of Palmyra (Tadmor) in the Syrian desert]

Zenobia (c. AD 240 – c. 274), Septimia Zenobia (in Palmyrene: Bat-Zabbai), was queen of the Palmyrene Empire in Syria, who as regent for her young son Vaballathus broke with Rome and conquered its eastern provinces. Widow of Odaenathus, the Roman client-king whose victories over Persia had made Palmyra the shield of the Roman East, Zenobia from 270 seized Egypt, Anatolia as far as Ancyra, and all Syria and Arabia, while her armies and coinage styled her son a rival emperor. The Emperor Aurelian's counter-campaign (272) defeated her generals at Immae and Emesa, starved Palmyra into surrender, and captured Zenobia at the Euphrates as she fled toward Persia; she was displayed in Aurelian's triumph at Rome (golden chains, in the tradition) and pensioned at a villa near Tivoli, while Palmyra's revolt the next year brought its destruction.

The Palmyrene bid

Zenobia's court — advised by the philosopher Longinus, whom Aurelian executed — claimed descent from Cleopatra (whose name and image she cultivated), and her coinage and inscriptions in Greek and Palmyrene Aramaic proclaimed an eastern empire in Roman dress. Her expansion exploited Rome's crisis: Gallienus and Claudius II dead, Aurelian occupied with the Danube. Egypt's grain, Antioch's mints and Anatolia's roads fell to her generals Zabdas and Zabbai in two campaigning seasons — the largest secession the empire ever faced, rival to the Gallic Empire in the west.

Defeat and afterlife

Aurelian's recovery was methodical: Egypt retaken, Asia Minor cleared, the Palmyrene army broken in two pitched battles, and Palmyra besieged until Zenobia's flight. The sources divide on her end — triumph and retirement (Historia Augusta, embroidered), death on the journey (Zosimus) — and on her character, from scheming femme fatale to legitimate empress defending her son's inheritance. In Syria she is a national heroine; Tadmor (Palmyra), her ruined capital of colonnades, later suffered again in the 21st century's wars, making Zenobia's defiance a living emblem.
