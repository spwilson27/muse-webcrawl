Leech lattice

The Leech lattice is an even unimodular lattice Λ24 in 24-dimensional Euclidean space, E24. It is one of the best examples of an efficient sphere packing, providing the densest known sphere packing in 24 dimensions; its centre density is 1, meaning that the centres of unit spheres packed according to the lattice are spaced so that each occupies the minimum possible volume. Its kissing number is 196,560.

The lattice was discovered by John Leech in 1967. Its automorphism group is the Conway group Co0, of order 8,315,553,613,086,720,000, whose structure led John Horton Conway to discover three new sporadic simple groups.

Characterisation

The Leech lattice is the unique even unimodular lattice in 24 dimensions with no vectors of squared length 2 (no "roots"). Equivalently, it is the unique such lattice whose minimal non-zero vectors have squared length 4, of which there are exactly 196,560.

Constructions

The Leech lattice can be constructed in many ways: using the binary Golay code; by modifying the construction of the E8 lattice; via the complex or quaternionic Leech lattices with respect to the Eisenstein and Hurwitz integers; and using the "holy constructions" from Niemeier lattices. One standard construction starts from the extended binary Golay code, a 12-dimensional subspace of the 24-bit space whose codewords have weights divisible by 4.

Applications

The Leech lattice gives the optimal sphere packing and kissing configuration in 24 dimensions, results later proved optimal in full generality by Henry Cohn and collaborators building on Maryna Viazovska's work in dimension 8. The quotient of the lattice's symmetry group by its centre yields the Conway groups, and the lattice underlies constructions in vertex operator algebra theory, including the monster vertex algebra and monstrous moonshine.
