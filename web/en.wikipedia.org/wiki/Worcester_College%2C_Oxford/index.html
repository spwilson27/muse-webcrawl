Worcester College is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England. It stands on the site of Gloucester College, founded in 1283 as a house of study for Benedictine monks, and was refounded in 1714 with a bequest from Sir Thomas Cookes, a Worcestershire landowner.

History

Gloucester College served Benedictine abbeys for more than two and a half centuries until the Dissolution of the Monasteries under Henry VIII. The buildings later served as Gloucester Hall, a sometimes precarious academic hall whose principals included John Pakington and the playwright-adjacent scholar Benjamin Woodroffe; at one time the antiquary Thomas Hearne studied there. In 1714 the hall was elevated to a full college under Cookes's endowment and renamed Worcester College.

Buildings and grounds

The college is known for its unusually extensive grounds for a city-centre Oxford college — about 26 acres including a lake, playing fields and orchards. The main quadrangle mixes surviving medieval monastic cottages (the "Cambridge Terrace" row of monks' lodgings) with grand 18th-century classical buildings designed in the orbit of Nicholas Hawksmoor, including the hall, chapel and library blocks; further buildings were added in the 20th and 21st centuries.
