The Emscher is a river in the Ruhr district of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. It rises near Holzwickede, east of Dortmund, and flows westwards for about 83 kilometres through cities including Dortmund, Herne, Gelsenkirchen, Essen, Bottrop and Oberhausen before joining the Rhine near Dinslaken.

Industrial history

During the industrialisation of the Ruhr in the 19th and 20th centuries, the Emscher was converted into an open wastewater channel for coal mines, steelworks and growing cities. Because mining subsidence made underground sewers impractical, the river was straightened, deepened and lined with concrete, carrying untreated sewage for decades; it was often described as the dirtiest river in Germany.

Renaturalisation

Since the 1990s the Emschergenossenschaft water board has carried out one of Europe's largest river restoration projects, the Emscher Conversion. New underground sewers and modern treatment plants have progressively taken wastewater out of the river, allowing sections of the Emscher and its tributaries to be renaturalised with meanders, floodplains and public greenways. The conversion, costing several billion euros, was substantially completed in the early 2020s, and fish and other wildlife have returned to stretches of the river.
