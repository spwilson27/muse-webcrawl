Geography of North Korea

Location: East Asia
Area: 120,540 km²
Highest point: Mount Paektu (2,744 m)
Coastline: 2,495 km

The geography of North Korea covers the northern portion of the Korean Peninsula, which the Democratic People's Republic of Korea occupies above the 38th parallel. The country covers 120,540 km² and is bordered by China and Russia to the north and by South Korea along the Korean Demilitarized Zone to the south; it has coasts on the Yellow Sea (Korea Bay) to the west and the Sea of Japan (East Sea) to the east.

Terrain

North Korea is rugged and mountainous, with mountain ranges covering about 80 per cent of the land area. The Paektu-Changbai mountains run along the Chinese border and include Mount Paektu (2,744 m), the peninsula's highest peak, an active volcano whose crater holds Heaven Lake (Chonji). The Rangrim Mountains run north–south through the country's centre, and the Kangnam and Myohyang ranges branch to the southwest. The largest plains are the Pyongyang and Chaeryong plains on the west coast; the east coast is narrow, with plains interrupted by mountains descending steeply to the sea.

Rivers and climate

The longest river is the Yalu (Amnok), forming much of the Chinese border; others include the Tumen, Taedong, Chongchon and Yesong rivers. The climate is temperate continental with hot, humid summers under the East Asian monsoon and cold, dry winters dominated by Siberian air; winters are markedly colder than in South Korea at similar latitudes. Natural hazards include drought, flooding and occasional typhoons.

Resources and environment

North Korea has substantial mineral resources, including coal, iron ore, magnesite, graphite, tungsten and rare-earth elements. Arable land is limited (roughly 19–22 per cent of the area), and soil erosion and deforestation — driven by fuelwood collection and hillside farming — have aggravated flood damage since the 1990s.
