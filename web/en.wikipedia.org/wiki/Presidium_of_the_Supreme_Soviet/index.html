Presidium of the Supreme Soviet

Presidium of the Supreme Soviet

Established: 1938 (under the 1936 Constitution)

Abolished: 1991

Chairman: Head of state of the USSR (ex officio role)

The Presidium of the Supreme Soviet (Russian: Президиум Верховного Совета) was the standing body of the Supreme Soviet of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics, empowered to act on its behalf between sessions. Its chairman was, formally, the head of state of the Soviet Union.

Functions

Under the 1936 and 1977 Soviet constitutions, the Presidium interpreted laws, issued decrees (ukazy) with the force of law subject to later ratification, ratified international treaties, appointed and recalled diplomatic representatives, conferred awards and honorary titles, granted pardons, proclaimed martial law and mobilisation, and called elections and sessions of the Supreme Soviet.

Chairmen

Holders of the chairmanship included Mikhail Kalinin (1938–1946), Nikolay Shvernik, Kliment Voroshilov, Leonid Brezhnev, Anastas Mikoyan, Nikolai Podgorny, Vasily Kuznetsov, Yuri Andropov, Konstantin Chernenko, Andrei Gromyko, and finally Mikhail Gorbachev (1988–1989), after which the post was superseded by the Chairman of the Supreme Soviet and then the President of the USSR in 1990.

Equivalent bodies

Each union republic and autonomous republic also had its own Presidium of its Supreme Soviet with analogous functions at the republican level.
