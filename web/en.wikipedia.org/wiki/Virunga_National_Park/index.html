Virunga National Park

Virunga National Park (French: Parc National des Virunga) is a national park in the Albertine Rift Valley in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. Created in 1925 as Africa's first national park, it covers about 8,090 km2 (3,120 sq mi), stretching from the Virunga Mountains in the south to the Rwenzori Mountains in the north, along the borders with Rwanda and Uganda. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979.

Biodiversity

Virunga's extraordinary habitat range — savanna, lava plains, swamps, lowland forest, and glacier-capped peaks above 5,000 m — supports exceptional diversity, including a quarter of the world's remaining mountain gorillas, eastern lowland gorillas, chimpanzees, okapis, elephants, hippos, and over 700 bird species. The park also contains two active volcanoes, Nyiragongo, noted for its persistent lava lake, and Nyamuragira.

Threats and protection

Decades of regional conflict have placed the park and its rangers in danger; armed groups have engaged in poaching, illegal charcoal production, and encroachment, and more than 200 rangers have been killed in the line of duty. Oil exploration licences overlapping the park drew international opposition. The park is managed by the Congolese Institute for the Conservation of Nature (ICCN) in partnership with the Virunga Foundation. Mountain gorilla tourism and community development, including hydroelectric projects, underpin its conservation model; the park was the subject of the 2014 documentary Virunga.
