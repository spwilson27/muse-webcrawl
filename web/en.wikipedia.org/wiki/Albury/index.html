Albury is a major regional city in New South Wales, Australia. It is located on the Hume Highway and the northern side of the Murray River. Albury is the seat of local government for the council area which also bears the city's name – the City of Albury. It is on the Victoria–New South Wales border, across the river from its twin city of Wodonga in Victoria; together the two form an urban area of roughly 97,000 people.

History

The Wiradjuri people are the traditional owners of the land around Albury. The explorers Hamilton Hume and William Hovell passed through the area in 1824, naming the river the Hume (later renamed the Murray). A crossing place was established, and the town was surveyed in 1839 and named after Albury in Surrey, England. Albury grew as a river port and customs point between the colonies of New South Wales and Victoria, which levied different tariffs; federation in 1901 removed the customs barrier. The two colonies also built railways to different gauges, making Albury a famous break-of-gauge station where passengers changed trains — a quirk that persisted until standardisation in 1962.

Geography and climate

Albury lies on the floodplain of the Murray River in the foothills of the Great Dividing Range. Lake Hume, a large reservoir, lies a few kilometres upstream and is used for irrigation and recreation. The city has a temperate climate with hot, dry summers and cool winters.

Economy and culture

The city is a regional service, manufacturing and logistics centre for the Murray region. Notable landmarks include the Murray Art Museum Albury (MAMA), the Albury Botanic Gardens, Monument Hill war memorial, and the Hume Dam. The city is also known for the Albury railway station, opened in 1882, whose platform was for a time the longest in the Southern Hemisphere.
