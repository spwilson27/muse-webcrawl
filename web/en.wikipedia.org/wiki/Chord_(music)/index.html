Chord (music)

In music, a chord is a group of three or more notes sounded together, as a basis of harmony. Chords are built from a root note by stacking intervals, most commonly thirds: a triad consists of a root, a third and a fifth. Chords form the harmonic framework over which melody is heard, and sequences of chords (chord progressions) structure most Western music.

Triads and qualities

The four basic triad qualities are major (root, major third, perfect fifth), minor (root, minor third, perfect fifth), diminished (root, minor third, diminished fifth) and augmented (root, major third, augmented fifth). Adding further thirds produces seventh chords and extended chords (ninths, elevenths, thirteenths) common in jazz.

Notation and analysis

In Roman numeral analysis, chords within a key are labelled I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi and vii°, with upper- and lower-case indicating major and minor quality. Popular music uses chord symbols such as C, Am7 or G/B (slash chords indicating a specified bass note). Chords may be inverted by placing a note other than the root in the bass, and voiced in many spacings and registers.

Function

In tonal harmony, chords are classed by function — tonic, subdominant and dominant — and progressions such as ii–V–I in jazz or I–V–vi–IV in pop follow recognisable patterns. Voice leading governs how individual notes move between chords.
