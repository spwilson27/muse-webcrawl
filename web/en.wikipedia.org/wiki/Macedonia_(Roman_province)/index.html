Macedonia was a province of ancient Rome, encompassing the territory of the former Antigonid Kingdom of Macedon, which had been conquered by Rome in the Macedonian Wars. The province was officially established in 146 BC, after the Roman general Quintus Caecilius Metellus defeated Andriscus of Macedon, the last claimant to the Macedonian throne, during the Fourth Macedonian War. Its capital was Thessalonica.

History

Following the defeat of Perseus at the Battle of Pydna in 168 BC, Macedon was initially divided into four client republics. After the revolt of Andriscus in 149–148 BC, the territory was reorganised as a regular province in 146 BC. The original province was vast, extending over much of the southern Balkans, including Epirus, Thessaly, parts of Illyria and Thrace.

Macedonia was a senatorial province for much of its history, governed by a proconsul. The great Roman road, the Via Egnatia, crossed the province from Dyrrhachium on the Adriatic coast to Thessalonica and onward toward Byzantium, making it a vital link between Italy and the eastern empire.

Later reorganisation

Under Diocletian's reforms in the late 3rd century AD, the province was subdivided; Macedonia Prima and Macedonia Secunda (or Salutaris) were created in the 4th century, within the Diocese of Macedonia. The province was repeatedly overrun during the Gothic and Slavic invasions of late antiquity.

Economy

The province exported timber, grain, wine and metals; the gold and silver mines of Mount Pangaeum, once exploited by Philip II of Macedon, continued under Roman administration. Thessalonica and Philippi were major commercial and administrative centres.
