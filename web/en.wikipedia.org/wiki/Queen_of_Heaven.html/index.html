Queen of Heaven

Queen of Heaven (Latin: Regina Caeli) is a title given to the Virgin Mary in the Roman Catholic Church and other Christian traditions, based on the belief that Mary, having been assumed into heaven, shares in the glory of her son Jesus Christ.

Origins and devotion

The title developed in medieval devotion and liturgy; the Regina Caeli is one of the four Marian antiphons sung in the Easter season. The doctrine was affirmed by Pope Pius XII in Ad Caeli Reginam (1954), which instituted the feast of the Queenship of Mary, celebrated on 22 August. The phrase also appears in the Old Testament, where Jeremiah condemns offerings made to a "Queen of Heaven", a pagan goddess of the ancient Near East.
