The Hunt (Danish: Jagten) is a 2012 Danish psychological drama film directed by Thomas Vinterberg and starring Mads Mikkelsen. The story follows Lucas, a recently divorced kindergarten teacher in a small, close-knit Danish town, whose life disintegrates when a young girl — the daughter of his best friend — makes a false, half-imagined accusation of sexual abuse, and the community turns on him in a modern witch-hunt.

Plot

Lucas works at a nursery school and is popular with the children, among them Klara, the five-year-old daughter of his best friend Theo. After Lucas gently rebuffs the child's inappropriate gift of a heart and a kiss, Klara, repeating phrases she has heard and seen on her brother's tablet, tells the headmistress an ambiguous story implying Lucas exposed himself. Adults lead the child with suggestive questions and take her words as proof. Despite Klara's attempts to retract the story, and although the formal case collapses, the townspeople ostracise and violently persecute Lucas: he loses his job and girlfriend, his dog is killed, and he is assaulted in the supermarket and at the Christmas Eve church service, where he confronts Theo. A year later the men go hunting together in an apparent reconciliation — but an unseen shooter fires at Lucas, leaving the ending deliberately unsettled.

Production

Vinterberg and co-writer Tobias Lindholm drew on case material about false abuse accusations and child psychology, including cases in which suggestive interviewing produced fabricated memories. The film was shot in Zealand, Denmark, and produced by Zentropa.

Reception

The film premiered in competition at the 2012 Cannes Film Festival, where Mikkelsen won the Best Actor Award. It was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film and the Golden Globe for Best Foreign Language Film, and won the British Independent Film Award for Best International Independent Film. Critics praised Mikkelsen's restrained performance and the film's portrayal of mass hysteria and destroyed trust; it holds a high approval rating on review aggregators and is frequently cited among the finest Danish films of its decade.
