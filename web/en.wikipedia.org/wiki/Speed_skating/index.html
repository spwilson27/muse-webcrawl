Speed skating

Speed skating is a competitive form of ice skating in which participants race one another over set distances. It includes long track speed skating — raced on a 400-metre oval, typically in pairs — and short track speed skating, raced in packs on an Olympic-sized rink. Long track has been an Olympic sport since the first Winter Games in 1924; short track became a full Olympic sport in 1992.

History

Skating for transport and sport developed in the Netherlands and Scandinavia, and the first known organized races were held in the Netherlands in the 19th century. The International Skating Union (ISU), founded in 1892, governs the sport. The Netherlands has historically dominated long track competition; the introduction of clap skates in the 1990s dramatically lowered world records.

Events

Long track distances range from 500 m sprints to the 10,000 m, plus team pursuit and mass start events. Skaters race in lanes, crossing over on the back straight to equalize distance, and times are compared across pairs. Short track races (500 m, 1000 m, 1500 m, relays) feature direct head-to-head racing and frequent overtaking and falls.
