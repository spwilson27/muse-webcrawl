Akbarieh Garden

Akbarieh Garden (Persian: باغ اکبریه) is a historic Persian garden in Birjand, South Khorasan Province, Iran. In 2011 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as one of nine gardens representing the classical Persian garden tradition under "The Persian Garden" serial nomination.

Description

The complex occupies several hectares on a slope at the edge of the desert, organised around a two-storey mansion in the centre of the garden. Long rectangular pools and water channels — fed by a qanat — mirror the façade, and rows of pine, pomegranate and other trees line the axial paths. The main building combines Iranian architecture with European-influenced decoration, including mirrored halls, painted ceilings and a distinctive Russian-style service wing built in the early 20th century.

History

The garden was developed in stages from the late Zand and early Qajar periods and was used as the residence and seat of government of the local rulers, the Alam family. Under Shawkat al-Mulk it hosted official ceremonies. During the Second World War the estate received Polish refugees. Today parts of the complex house museums of archaeology, anthropology and wildlife, as well as a library and the Birjand office of Iran's cultural heritage organisation.
