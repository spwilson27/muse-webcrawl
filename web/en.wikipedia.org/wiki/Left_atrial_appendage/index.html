Left atrial appendage

The left atrial appendage (LAA) is a small, ear-shaped pouch in the muscle wall of the left atrium of the heart. It is a remnant of the embryonic left atrium, and its inner surface is lined by pectinate muscles that give it a trabeculated appearance visible on echocardiography.

Function

The appendage is more compliant than the rest of the atrium and acts as a decompression chamber during periods of raised left atrial pressure. It is richly supplied with natriuretic peptide granules and contributes to the release of atrial natriuretic peptide.

Clinical significance

In patients with atrial fibrillation, blood stasis within the left atrial appendage makes it the site of the great majority — over 90% in non-valvular AF — of intracardiac thrombi that can embolise and cause stroke. For patients who cannot take long-term anticoagulation, the appendage may be closed surgically or sealed with a percutaneous occlusion device such as the WATCHMAN implant, or clipped with the AtriClip, to reduce stroke risk.
