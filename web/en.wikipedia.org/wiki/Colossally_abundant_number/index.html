Colossally abundant number

In number theory, a colossally abundant number (CA number) is a natural number that, in a specific rigorous sense, has many divisors: for some ε > 0, the ratio σ(n)/n1+ε — where σ is the sum-of-divisors function — is maximal at n compared with all other natural numbers. The concept was introduced by Srinivasa Ramanujan in his 1915 work on highly composite numbers and developed by Alaoglu and Erdős in 1944.

Properties

Colossally abundant numbers are also superabundant and highly composite; their prime factorisations use the smallest primes with non-increasing exponents. They matter beyond pure curiosity: the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to a certain inequality (Robin's inequality, σ(n) < eγ n log log n) holding for all n > 5040, and colossally abundant numbers are the natural candidates where such bounds are tightest or could fail.
