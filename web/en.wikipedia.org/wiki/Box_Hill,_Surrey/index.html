Box Hill is a summit of the North Downs in Surrey, England, approximately 31 km (19 mi) south-west of London. The hill gets its name from the ancient box woodland found on its chalk slopes, and it is one of the best-known beauty spots in the south-east of England. Much of the hill is owned and managed by the National Trust, and the summit area is a Site of Special Scientific Interest noted for its chalk grassland, woodland, and populations of orchids and butterflies.

Geography

Box Hill rises to about 224 metres (735 ft) above sea level. Its western face overlooks the valley of the River Mole, which cuts through the chalk ridge in a steep-sided gap, and the town of Dorking lies to the south-west. The A24 London Road passes along the foot of the hill, and the Zig Zag Road, built in the early 20th century, climbs the scarp in a series of hairpin bends to the viewpoint near the summit.

Natural history

The hill supports some of the finest ancient box woodland in Britain, together with yew woods and species-rich chalk grassland. More than a dozen species of orchid have been recorded, and the grassland supports butterflies including the Adonis blue and chalkhill blue. Steep slopes are kept open by grazing, while the plateau carries beech and ash woodland. Box Hill forms part of the Mole Gap to Reigate Escarpment Special Area of Conservation.

History and culture

Box Hill has been a popular excursion destination since the 18th century, when day-trippers came out from London by coach and later by train to Dorking. The hill appears in literature: a picnic scene on Box Hill is a pivotal episode in Jane Austen's novel Emma (1815), and George Meredith lived in a cottage on the hill in the later 19th century. At the summit stands Salomons Memorial, a viewpoint tower commemorating Leopold Salomons, who gave the summit to the nation in 1914; from it there are wide views over the Weald to the South Downs.

Sport

The Zig Zag Road climb became internationally known when it was included in the road cycling races of the London 2012 Olympic Games, with the men's race completing nine laps of a circuit including Box Hill. The hill remains a celebrated climb for amateur cyclists, and its slopes host organised runs and walking events throughout the year.
