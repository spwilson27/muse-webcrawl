William Lee Shoemaker (August 19, 1931 – October 12, 2003) was an American jockey, widely regarded as one of the greatest in the history of Thoroughbred horse racing. Over a career spanning more than four decades he won 8,833 races, a world record he held for 29 years, and won eleven Triple Crown races, including four Kentucky Derbys.

Early life and career

Shoemaker was born in Fabens, Texas, and weighed barely two pounds at birth; he was placed in a shoebox in a warm oven to survive, a story often retold in profiles. Standing just 4 feet 11 inches and weighing about 105 pounds, he was ideally sized for a jockey. He began riding professionally in 1949 and quickly became the nation's leading rider, winning the George Woolf Memorial Jockey Award in 1951 and repeatedly topping the earnings standings.

Triple Crown success

Shoemaker won the Kentucky Derby four times: on Swaps (1955), Tomy Lee (1959), Lucky Debonair (1965), and Ferdinand (1986). The 1986 victory made him, at 54, the oldest jockey ever to win the Derby. He twice narrowly missed the Triple Crown's most famous sweep; in the 1957 Derby, his mount Gallant Man was leading when Shoemaker misjudged the finish line and stood in the stirrups momentarily, allowing Iron Liege to win by a nose — an error he openly acknowledged and that became one of racing's most famous mistakes.

Later career and injury

Shoemaker retired from riding in 1990 after a farewell tour and became a trainer. In April 1991 he was paralyzed in a drunk-driving car accident, which left him a quadriplegic; he continued training from a wheelchair, winning races with horses such as Diazo. He sued Ford Motor Company over the accident, settling for a substantial sum. He died in 2003 at his home in San Marino, California.

Legacy

Shoemaker was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1958, while still an active rider. His record of 8,833 wins stood until 1999, when it was surpassed by Laffit Pincay Jr. He is remembered for his quiet hands, tactical patience, and remarkable longevity at the top of the sport.
