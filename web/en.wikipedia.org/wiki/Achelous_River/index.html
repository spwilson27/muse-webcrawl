Achelous River

The Achelous (Greek: Αχελώος, Achelóös), also Acheloos, is a river in western Greece. It is 220 km (137 mi) long, making it the second-longest river entirely in Greece after the Aliakmon. It rises in the Pindus mountains near Mount Lakmos and flows south through Epirus, Acarnania and Aetolia, emptying into the Ionian Sea near the Gulf of Patras.

Course and dams

The river's upper course passes through deep gorges in the Pindus range. A series of hydroelectric dams — Kremasta, Kastraki and Stratos — were built on its middle course in the 1960s; the Kremasta Dam created Lake Kremasta, one of the largest artificial lakes in Greece. A long-debated diversion project to transfer Achelous water to the Thessaly plain has been repeatedly contested on environmental grounds.

Mythology

In Greek mythology, Achelous was the god of the river, the eldest of the river gods and son of Oceanus and Tethys. He wrestled Heracles for the hand of Deianeira and lost one of his horns, which became associated with the cornucopia, the horn of plenty. The river's shifting course and deposits made it a symbol of fertility in antiquity.

Ecology

The Achelous delta, with its lagoons near Mesolonghi, is an important wetland protected under the Ramsar Convention, hosting significant bird populations.
