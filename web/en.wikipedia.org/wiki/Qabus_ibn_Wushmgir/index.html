Qabus ibn Wushmgir

Reign977–981 and 998–1012
DynastyZiyarid
Died1012, Gorgan

Qabus ibn Wushmgir (Persian: قابوس بن وشمگیر), full name Abu'l-Hasan Qabus ibn Wushmgir ibn Ziyar (died 1012), was the Ziyarid ruler of Gorgan and Tabaristan in northern Iran. He reigned in two periods, 977–981 and 998–1012, being restored after years of exile.

Patronage and scholarship

Qabus was himself a scholar and poet who wrote in both Arabic and Persian. His court became a celebrated centre of learning: the polymath al-Biruni dedicated his Chronology of Ancient Nations (c. 1000) to Qabus, and the young Avicenna (Ibn Sina) visited his court. His grandson's mirror-for-princes, the Qabus-nama (1082), written by Keikavus, preserves his name.

Death and tomb

Known for harsh and arbitrary rule, Qabus was deposed by a military revolt in 1012 and was killed shortly afterwards, reportedly by being exposed to the cold. His tomb tower, the Gonbad-e Qabus near Gorgan — a 53-metre brick tower built in 1006 — is one of the finest monuments of early Islamic architecture in Iran and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
