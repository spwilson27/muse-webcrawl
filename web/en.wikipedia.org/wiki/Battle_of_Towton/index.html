Battle of Towton

The Battle of Towton was fought on 29 March 1461 (Palm Sunday) near Towton, Yorkshire, during the Wars of the Roses. It is commonly described as the largest and bloodiest battle ever fought on English soil. The Yorkist army of Edward IV destroyed the Lancastrians of Henry VI and Margaret of Anjou, securing Edward's throne.

Background

After victory at Mortimer's Cross, Edward was proclaimed king in London. He marched north to confront the Lancastrian host, crossing the Aire at Ferrybridge on 28 March after hard fighting.

Battle

In driving snow, Fauconberg's archers exploited the wind: Lancastrian arrows fell short, Yorkist volleys stung the enemy into advancing. Hours of brutal hand-to-hand fighting followed along the ridge. The late arrival of the Duke of Norfolk's fresh Yorkist troops on the Lancastrian flank broke their line; the rout toward the Cock Beck, where many drowned or were cut down, produced the field's grim name of Bloody Meadow.

Aftermath

Henry VI, Margaret and Prince Edward fled to Scotland. Edward IV was crowned in June 1461. A mass grave excavated near Towton in 1996 confirmed the violence of close combat on the dead. The battle remains a byword for the savagery of the Wars of the Roses.
