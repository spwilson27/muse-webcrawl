Battle of the Denmark Strait

The Battle of the Denmark Strait was a naval engagement on 24 May 1941 between ships of the Royal Navy and the German Kriegsmarine during the Second World War. The British battlecruiser HMS Hood and battleship HMS Prince of Wales intercepted the German battleship Bismarck and heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen in the strait between Iceland and Greenland. Hood was sunk with the loss of all but three of her crew of 1,418.

Background

Bismarck and Prinz Eugen had sortied into the Atlantic on Operation Rheinübung to raid Allied convoys. Shadowed by the cruisers Norfolk and Suffolk, they were intercepted at dawn by Vice-Admiral Holland's force approaching from the south-west.

The battle

The British ships opened fire at about 05:52. Holland's approach angle initially prevented his after turrets from bearing, and a misidentification of the German ships' order caused the British to engage Prinz Eugen — the leading ship — rather than Bismarck at first. German return fire was rapid and accurate: Prinz Eugen hit Hood, starting a fire on her boat deck, and at around 06:01 a salvo from Bismarck's fifth discharge struck Hood near her mainmast. A huge explosion tore the ship apart — most likely from a magazine detonation — and she sank in three minutes. Only three men survived. Prince of Wales, herself hit several times and suffering gun breakdowns, broke off the action under cover of smoke; she had nevertheless damaged Bismarck's fuel system, a hit that forced Lütjens to abandon the raiding mission and run for France.

Aftermath

The loss of Hood, the pride of the Royal Navy, provoked an enormous pursuit — "Sink the Bismarck!" — involving dozens of warships. Bismarck was crippled by Fleet Air Arm torpedo bombers from Ark Royal on 26 May and sunk the following morning.
