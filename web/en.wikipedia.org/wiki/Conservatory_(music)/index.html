Type: Music school

Conservatory (music)

A conservatory (also called a conservatoire) is a school for the advanced study of music, typically offering professional training in performance, composition, and conducting, often within or alongside a university. The term comes from the Italian conservatorio, originally an orphanage where children received musical training.

Origins

The earliest conservatories were the charitable institutions of Naples, such as Santa Maria di Loreto, which produced leading composers of the 17th and 18th centuries. The Paris Conservatoire, founded in 1795, became the modern model, followed by institutions in Prague (1811), Vienna (1817), and London (the Royal Academy of Music, 1822). In the United States, notable conservatories include the Juilliard School, the Curtis Institute of Music, and the New England Conservatory.

Training

Admission is by competitive audition. Study centers on private lessons with a principal teacher, ensemble playing, music theory, and ear training, culminating in degrees or diplomas. Many conservatories also teach dance and drama.
