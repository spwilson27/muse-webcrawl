Miss Saigon

Miss Saigon is a sung-through musical by Claude-Michel Schönberg and Alain Boublil, with lyrics by Boublil and Richard Maltby Jr., loosely inspired by Puccini's opera Madama Butterfly. It tells the tragic romance between Kim, a Vietnamese bargirl, and Chris, an American serviceman, during and after the Vietnam War.

Productions

The musical premiered at the Theatre Royal, Drury Lane, London in 1989, starring Lea Salonga as Kim and Jonathan Pryce as the Engineer, and opened on Broadway in 1991. Its staging famously includes a helicopter evacuation scene recreating the fall of Saigon in 1975. Songs include "The Movie in My Mind", "Sun and Moon", "I'd Give My Life for You" and "Bui Doi".

Reception and controversy

Miss Saigon was a major commercial success and has been revived internationally, including a 2014 West End revival. Its casting and portrayal of Vietnamese characters have also drawn sustained criticism and debate since the original production.
