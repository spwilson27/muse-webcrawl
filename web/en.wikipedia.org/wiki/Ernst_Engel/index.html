Christian Lorenz Ernst Engel (26 March 1821 – 8 December 1896) was a German statistician and economist, known for formulating Engel's law, an empirical relationship between household income and expenditure on food.

Life

Engel studied mining engineering at the Bergakademie Freiberg and in Paris. In 1850 he became head of the Royal Saxon Statistical Bureau in Dresden, and later directed the Royal Prussian Statistical Bureau in Berlin from 1860 to 1882. He also served in the Reichstag, representing the National Liberal Party.

Engel's law

Drawing on household budget data, Engel observed that as a family's income rises, the proportion of income spent on food decreases, even though absolute food expenditure may rise. This regularity, presented in 1857, became one of the earliest and most robust empirical laws in economics. The associated graphical device, the Engel curve, shows how demand for a good varies with income. Engel's law is still used today as a rough indicator of a population's standard of living: the smaller the share of spending devoted to food, the wealthier the population is generally considered to be.
