Haft-Sin

Haft-Sin (Persian: هفت‌سین, "seven S's") is an arrangement of seven symbolic items, each of whose names starts with the Persian letter sin (س), traditionally displayed on a table or cloth (sofreh) as part of the celebrations of Nowruz, the Persian New Year, which falls on the spring equinox.

The seven items

  • Sabzeh (سبزه) — sprouted wheat, barley, mung bean or lentils grown in a dish, symbolising rebirth and renewal.
  • Samanu (سمنو) — a sweet pudding made from germinated wheat, symbolising affluence, patience and fertility.
  • Senjed (سنجد) — dried oleaster or silverberry fruit, symbolising love.
  • Seer (سیر) — garlic, symbolising health and medicine.
  • Seeb (سیب) — apples, symbolising beauty and health.
  • Somaq (سماق) — sumac, symbolising the colour of the sunrise and the triumph of light over darkness.
  • Serkeh (سرکه) — vinegar, symbolising age and patience.

Additional items

Families commonly add other items to the table, including a mirror (symbolising reflection and honesty), candles (light and happiness), decorated eggs (fertility), goldfish (life), coins (wealth), hyacinth and other flowers, traditional sweets, and a book of significance to the household — often the Qur'an in Muslim families, or the Divan of Hafez or the Shahnameh.

The Haft-Sin table is prepared before the moment of the equinox and remains in the home during the Nowruz holiday period, traditionally thirteen days, ending with Sizdah Bedar, when families picnic outdoors.
