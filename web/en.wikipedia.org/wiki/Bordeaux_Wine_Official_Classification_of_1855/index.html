The Bordeaux Wine Official Classification of 1855 (French: Classification officielle des vins de Bordeaux de 1855) was drawn up at the request of Napoleon III for the Exposition Universelle de Paris. It ranked the wines of the Gironde according to the reputation of the château and the price its wine customarily commanded on the market. The resulting hierarchy — First Growths (Premiers Crus) down to Fifth Growths (Cinquièmes Crus) — remains one of the most famous and enduring wine classifications in the world.

History

When the Bordeaux Chamber of Commerce was asked to present the region's wines at the 1855 Paris exhibition, it passed the task to the Syndicat of Courtiers, the brokers who intermediated the wine trade. Working from decades of price records, the brokers produced a list organised not by terroir but by market value. The classification covered the red wines of the Médoc (with one estate from Graves) and the sweet white wines of Sauternes and Barsac.

Remarkably, the classification has been altered only a handful of times. Château Cantemerle was added as a Fifth Growth in 1856, and in 1973 Château Mouton Rothschild was elevated from Second to First Growth — the only promotion of a red wine estate, marked by Baron Philippe de Rothschild's motto change from "Premier ne puis, second ne daigne, Mouton suis" to "Premier je suis, second je fus, Mouton ne change."

Beneath the First Growths stand 14 Second Growths, 14 Third Growths, 10 Fourth Growths and 18 Fifth Growths, for a total of 61 classified red wine estates. The sweet wines of Sauternes and Barsac were ranked separately into a superior First Growth (Château d'Yquem alone as Premier Cru Supérieur), First Growths and Second Growths.

Criticism and legacy

Critics note that the classification reflects the market of 1855 rather than present-day quality: estates have changed hands, vineyards have been bought and sold, and some unclassified estates — notably many in Pomerol, which was omitted entirely — now outrank classified growths. Nevertheless, the 1855 hierarchy still shapes prices, labelling ("Grand Cru Classé en 1855") and Bordeaux's global image.
