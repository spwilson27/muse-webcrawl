Chiles en nogada

Chiles en nogada is a Mexican dish of poblano chiles stuffed with picadillo (a mixture of ground meat, fruits and spices), topped with a walnut-based cream sauce called nogada, and garnished with pomegranate seeds and parsley. Its green, white and red colours evoke the flag of Mexico, and it is traditionally served in late summer, especially around Mexican Independence Day in September.

History

According to legend, the dish was created in 1821 by Augustinian nuns of the Convent of Santa Mónica in Puebla to honour Agustín de Iturbide, who had signed the Treaty of Córdoba securing Mexican independence and whose Army of the Three Guarantees marched under a green, white and red banner. The nuns are said to have designed the dish to display those colours: green parsley, white walnut sauce, and red pomegranate seeds.

Preparation

The picadillo filling combines ground pork or beef with chopped seasonal fruits — apple, pear, peach, plantain — along with raisins, almonds, pine nuts and spices. The nogada is made from peeled walnuts blended with milk or cream, cream cheese or goat cheese, and sometimes sherry; it depends on fresh Castilian walnuts available only in season, which is why the dish is not made year-round. The stuffed chiles may be served plain or battered and fried (capeados) before being coated with the cold walnut sauce.

Cultural significance

Chiles en nogada is regarded as one of the emblematic dishes of Puebla and of Mexican national cuisine. An annual festival in Puebla and surrounding towns celebrates the start of the season each year.
