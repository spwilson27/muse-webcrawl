The Thrill Is Gone

"The Thrill Is Gone" is a slow minor-key blues song written by West Coast blues musician Roy Hawkins and Rick Darnell in 1951. It became the signature song of B. B. King, whose 1969 recording brought it to a mass audience and defined his career.

B. B. King's version

King had performed the song for years before recording it for his 1969 album Completely Well, with a lush string arrangement by producer Bill Szymczyk that set his plaintive vocal and the biting single-note runs of his guitar "Lucille" against a pop-orchestral backdrop. Released as a single, it reached No. 15 on the Billboard Hot 100 and No. 3 on the rhythm and blues chart — King's biggest crossover success — and won the Grammy Award for Best Male R&B Vocal Performance in 1970. A later live version from Live in Cook County Jail is also celebrated; the song was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1998.

Legacy

"The Thrill Is Gone" is ranked among the greatest blues songs of all time and was placed on Rolling Stone's list of the 500 greatest songs. King closed countless concerts with it, and it has been covered by numerous artists across blues, jazz and rock.
