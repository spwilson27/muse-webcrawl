Garmon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Garmon is the Welsh form of the name of Saint Germanus of Auxerre (c. 378 – c. 448), a 5th-century Gallo-Roman bishop venerated in Wales, where numerous churches, settlements and other places bear his name. In Welsh tradition he is remembered as Sant Garmon.

Germanus in Britain

Germanus, bishop of Auxerre in Gaul, twice visited Britain, most famously in 429, to combat the Pelagian heresy. According to later accounts, during his mission he led a force of newly baptised Britons to the "Alleluia Victory" over Saxon and Pictish raiders — a battle which Welsh tradition located at Maes Garmon ("Garmon's Field") near Mold in Flintshire, and from which the saint is said to have routed the enemy by having his men shout "Alleluia" three times.

Dedications in Wales

Many Welsh places are named after or dedicated to Garmon, reflecting the strength of his cult in medieval Wales. They include:

  • Llanarmon — several villages whose names mean "Garmon's church", including Llanarmon-yn-Iâl (Denbighshire), Llanarmon Dyffryn Ceiriog (Wrexham) and Llanarmon Mynydd Mawr
  • St Harmon (Powys), whose name is an anglicised form of Garmon
  • Capel Garmon, a village in Conwy County Borough, near the site of the famous Capel Garmon Firedog, an Iron Age iron firedog now in the National Museum of Wales
  • Maes Garmon, near Mold, traditional site of the Alleluia Victory, where a commemorative pillar stands

Feast day

The feast of Saint Garmon is traditionally kept in Welsh calendars in early August, corresponding to the feast of Germanus of Auxerre in the wider Church (31 July).
