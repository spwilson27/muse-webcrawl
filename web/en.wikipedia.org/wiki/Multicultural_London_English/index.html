Multicultural London English (MLE) is a dialect (or multiethnolect) of English that emerged in the late 20th century in the multicultural neighbourhoods of inner London. It is spoken predominantly by young working-class people of diverse ethnic backgrounds and has been the subject of extensive sociolinguistic research, notably by Jenny Cheshire, Paul Kerswill and colleagues.

Origins and features

MLE arose where children of immigrants from the Caribbean, South Asia, West Africa and elsewhere grew up together, blending features of their heritage languages with traditional London Cockney. Characteristic features include a distinctive vowel system (e.g. monophthongal FACE and GOAT vowels), quotative "this is me" alongside "be like", the pronoun use of "man" ("man's tired"), and a lexicon drawing on Jamaican patois and other sources — words such as bare ("very/many"), ting, safe (as greeting/approval), and peng.

Spread and perception

Related multiethnolects have been documented in other British cities and across northern Europe. Media coverage sometimes labels MLE "Jafaican" ("fake Jamaican"), a term linguists criticise as misleading because the dialect is a genuine new variety spoken natively by multi-ethnic youth, not an imitation. MLE has influenced mainstream youth slang and British popular culture, including grime music, while remaining stigmatised in some formal contexts.
