William V (Willem Batavus; 8 March 1748 – 9 April 1806) was Prince of Orange and the last Stadtholder of the Dutch Republic. He reigned from 1751 until his deposition and exile in 1795, when the Batavian Republic was proclaimed under French revolutionary protection.

Early life and regency

William was born in The Hague, the son of William IV, Prince of Orange, whom he succeeded as hereditary stadtholder in 1751 at the age of three. His mother, Anne, Princess Royal of Great Britain, acted as regent until her death in 1759, after which regencies continued under his grandmother and then Duke Louis Ernest of Brunswick-Lüneburg, whose tutelage bred later resentments. William assumed personal rule in 1766. In 1767 he married Princess Wilhelmina of Prussia, sister of Frederick the Great.

Stadtholderate and the Patriot era

William proved indecisive amid the decline of the Republic. The disastrous Fourth Anglo-Dutch War (1780–1784) discredited his government and fed the democratic Patriot movement, which drove him from The Hague in 1785. Only a Prussian military intervention in 1787 — prompted by Wilhelmina's arrest at Goejanverwellesluis — restored his authority, and many Patriots fled into exile.

Exile and death

When French revolutionary armies and returning Patriots invaded in the winter of 1794–95, William fled to England on 18 January 1795. From Kew he issued the Kew Letters instructing Dutch colonial governors to admit British forces. He was later compensated with the principality of Nassau-Orange-Fulda (1802–1806). He died at Brunswick on 9 April 1806. His son became King William I of the Netherlands in 1815.
