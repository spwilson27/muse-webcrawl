Stephen Daye

Stephen Daye (c. 1594–1668) was an English locksmith who became the first printer in colonial British North America. Employed by Harvard College's first president's circle and the Reverend Joseph Glover, Daye set up a printing press at Cambridge, Massachusetts, in 1638–1639.

The Cambridge Press

Working with his son Matthew, Daye printed the first items produced on a press in English America: the broadside The Freeman's Oath (1639), an almanac, and in 1640 the Bay Psalm Book (The Whole Booke of Psalmes Faithfully Translated into English Metre), the first full-length book printed in British North America. Eleven copies of the Bay Psalm Book survive; one sold at auction in 2013 for over $14 million, a record for a printed book.

Legacy

Daye's press laid the foundation of printing in New England, though he worked as an indentured locksmith-turned-printer and spent his later years in obscurity in Cambridge.
