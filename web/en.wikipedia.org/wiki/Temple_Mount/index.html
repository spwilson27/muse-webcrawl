The Temple Mount (Hebrew: Har haBayit; Arabic: al-Haram al-Sharif, 'the Noble Sanctuary') is a walled hilltop compound in the Old City of Jerusalem, among the most sacred and contested religious sites on earth. For Judaism it is the site of the First and Second Temples and, in tradition, of the binding of Isaac; for Islam it is the place of Muhammad's Night Journey and ascension, crowned by the Dome of the Rock (built 691–92 AD) and Al-Aqsa Mosque.

History and status quo

Herod the Great massively expanded the platform in the 1st century BC; Rome destroyed the Second Temple in 70 AD, leaving the Western Wall as its surviving retaining wall. Muslim rule from the 7th century built the shrines; Crusaders, Ayyubids, Mamluks and Ottomans succeeded one another. Since 1967 Israel controls security while the Jordanian-linked Waqf administers the compound; under the 'Status Quo', non-Muslims may visit but not pray there — rules whose every breach risks violence, making the Mount a perennial flashpoint of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.
