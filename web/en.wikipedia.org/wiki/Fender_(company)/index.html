Fender (company)

The Fender Musical Instruments Corporation is an American manufacturer of musical instruments and amplifiers, founded in Fullerton, California, by Leo Fender in 1946. Fender is famous for its solid-body electric guitars, including the Telecaster, Stratocaster, and Precision Bass, which transformed popular music.

History

Leo Fender's radio repair business led to amplifier and lap steel guitar production. In 1951 he introduced the Telecaster, the first mass-produced solid-body electric guitar, followed by the Precision Bass and, in 1954, the Stratocaster. In 1965 Fender sold the company to CBS; quality concerns in the CBS era led to a management buyout in 1985, after which Fender re-established itself, headquartered in Scottsdale, Arizona. Fender also owns brands including Squier, Gretsch, Jackson, Charvel, and EVH.

Products

Besides guitars and basses, Fender builds amplifiers such as the Twin Reverb and Bassman, effects pedals, acoustic guitars, and the Fender Custom Shop's high-end instruments.
