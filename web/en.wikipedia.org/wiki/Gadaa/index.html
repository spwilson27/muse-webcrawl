Gadaa

Gadaa (Oromo: Gadaa; Amharic: ገዳ) is the indigenous democratic socio-political system of the Oromo people of Ethiopia and northern Kenya. Long-standing in practice, it regulates the community's political, economic, social and religious life. In 2016, Gadaa was inscribed by UNESCO on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Structure

Under Gadaa, members of society are organised into grades or sets (hariyya) that pass together through a series of stages in life. Male members advance through eleven grades, each lasting eight years, so that a man belongs to a Gadaa class that assumes leadership of the community for an eight-year term. Leaders — headed by an elected Abbaa Gadaa (father of the Gadaa) and advised by retired elders — are chosen in assembly, and power is transferred peacefully at the end of each term in a ceremony known as baallii.

Principles

The system rests on principles of periodic succession, checks on power, and consensus decision-making in open assemblies held under the sacred sycamore (odaa) tree. Laws are debated and proclaimed at assemblies such as the Gumii Gaayo of the Borana Oromo. Women participate through the parallel siiqqee institution, which protects women's rights and provides a recognised channel for resolving conflicts.

History

Scholars such as Asmarom Legesse documented Gadaa as one of the oldest known democratic systems, developed by the Oromo over centuries and still practised, alongside modern state institutions, in Oromia and among Kenyan Oromo communities. Suppressed under imperial and Derg governments in Ethiopia, it has revived since the 1990s.
