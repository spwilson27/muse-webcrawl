Grubbs catalyst

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Image placeholder: Grubbs catalyst

Grubbs catalysts are a series of transition metal carbene complexes used as catalysts for olefin metathesis. They are named after Robert H. Grubbs, the chemist who supervised their synthesis, and were first reported in the early 1990s. Typically based on ruthenium, the catalysts are notable for their tolerance of many functional groups, their stability toward air and moisture relative to earlier metathesis catalysts, and their ease of handling, which made olefin metathesis a widely used method in organic synthesis and polymer chemistry.

First-generation catalyst

The first well-defined ruthenium catalyst reported by the Grubbs group was based on the diazo compound route to ruthenium carbenes. The first-generation Grubbs catalyst, often written as RuCl2(PCy3)2(=CHPh) where PCy3 is tricyclohexylphosphine, is a purple solid that initiates metathesis by dissociation of one phosphine ligand. It is effective for ring-closing metathesis, ring-opening metathesis polymerisation and cross metathesis, although it is less active than molybdenum-based Schrock catalysts.

Second-generation catalyst

The second-generation catalyst replaces one phosphine ligand with an N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC), typically the H2IMes ligand derived from 1,3-bis(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)imidazolidine. The stronger electron-donating NHC ligand greatly increases activity, enabling the formation of sterically demanding tetrasubstituted olefins and expanding the range of usable substrates.

Hoveyda–Grubbs catalysts

The Hoveyda–Grubbs catalysts replace the benzylidene ligand with a chelating isopropoxy-substituted benzylidene, in which an oxygen atom coordinates to ruthenium. These catalysts are more stable, can often be recovered and recycled, and are widely used in both academic and industrial settings.

Applications

Grubbs catalysts are used in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals, advanced materials and specialty chemicals, and in the production of polymers by ring-opening metathesis polymerisation. Grubbs shared the 2005 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Yves Chauvin and Richard R. Schrock for the development of the metathesis method in organic synthesis.
