Lake Sevan (Armenian: Սևանա լիճ) is the largest body of water in Armenia and the Caucasus region, and one of the largest high-altitude freshwater lakes in Eurasia. It covers about one-sixth of Armenia's territory and provides most of the country's fish catch and a large share of its irrigation water.

History

The lake's shores are rich in archaeology, from Bronze Age petroglyphs to the medieval Sevanavank monastery, founded in 874 on what was then an island. In the Soviet era, engineers drained the lake for irrigation and hydroelectric power on the Hrazdan River cascade: the level fell by nearly 20 metres, the island became a peninsula, and the ecosystem was badly damaged. After independence, Armenia raised the level again — partly with water diverted through the Arpa–Sevan tunnel — and established Sevan National Park to protect the recovering lake.

Ecology

Sevan is noted for the endemic Sevan trout (Salmo ischchan), once the backbone of its fishery but now endangered because of falling levels and introduced competitors such as whitefish. The lake's beaches make it Armenia's principal summer resort destination.
