Luciano Moggi (born 10 July 1937) is a former Italian football executive best known as the general director of Juventus from 1994 to 2006, and as the central figure of the 2006 Calciopoli scandal that shook Italian football.

Career

Moggi began as a scout and worked for several clubs, including Roma, Lazio, Torino, and Napoli, where he was part of the club that won the Serie A title in the Diego Maradona era. At Juventus he formed a powerful management trio with president Gianni Agnelli's executives Antonio Giraudo and Roberto Bettega, building teams that won multiple Serie A championships and reached four Champions League finals, winning the trophy in 1996.

Calciopoli

In May 2006, transcripts of intercepted telephone calls appeared to show Moggi exercising influence over referee appointments and football federation officials. Juventus were relegated to Serie B and stripped of the 2005 and 2006 league titles. Moggi resigned and was banned from football for life by the Italian federation. In the subsequent criminal trial in Naples he was convicted of criminal association at first instance in 2011, but the case was ultimately extinguished by the statute of limitations in 2015.

Later life

Since the scandal, Moggi has worked as a journalist, broadcaster, and columnist in Italy, frequently commenting on football and contesting the verdicts against him.
