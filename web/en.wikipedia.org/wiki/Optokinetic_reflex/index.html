Optokinetic response

The optokinetic response (also called the optokinetic reflex, or optokinetic nystagmus) is an eye-movement reflex that stabilizes the image of a moving visual scene on the retina. When the visual field moves across the eye — for example, when watching the landscape from a moving train — the eyes follow the movement with a slow tracking phase and then rapidly reset with a saccade in the opposite direction, producing a characteristic nystagmus pattern.

Mechanism

The reflex is driven by retinal slip: motion of the whole visual scene is detected by direction-selective neurons, and signals via the accessory optic system and visual cortex drive compensatory eye movements. It works together with the vestibulo-ocular reflex, which stabilizes gaze during head movements; the optokinetic response is especially important for sustained, low-frequency motion, where the vestibular signal decays.

Clinical testing

Clinicians test the response with a rotating optokinetic drum or moving striped tape. An intact response confirms function of the visual and ocular motor pathways and is used in assessing infants, detecting malingering visual loss, and localizing lesions of the parietal and occipital lobes and brainstem.
