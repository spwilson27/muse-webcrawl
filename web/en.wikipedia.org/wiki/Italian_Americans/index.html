Italian Americans

Total population: about 16–17 million (roughly 5% of the U.S. population)
Regions: Northeast (New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania), Midwest, California, Florida
Languages: English, Italian and regional Italian languages

Italian Americans are Americans of Italian ancestry. The largest wave of immigration came between 1880 and 1924, when more than four million Italians — mostly from southern Italy and Sicily — arrived in the United States, settling in cities such as New York, Boston, Philadelphia and Chicago.

History

Early arrivals included explorers, artisans and political exiles. Mass migrants often faced discrimination, including the 1891 New Orleans lynching of eleven Italians. During the Second World War some Italian nationals in the U.S. were classed as enemy aliens, while Italian Americans served in large numbers in the armed forces. Post-war decades saw suburbanisation and full integration into American public life.

Culture

Italian-American culture has strongly influenced American cuisine (pizza, pasta dishes adapted to local ingredients), religious festivals such as the Feast of San Gennaro, and entertainment, with prominent figures in film, music, sports and politics.
