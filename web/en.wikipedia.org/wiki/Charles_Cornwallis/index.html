Charles Cornwallis, 1st Marquess Cornwallis

Charles Cornwallis, 1st Marquess Cornwallis (31 December 1738 – 5 October 1805) was a British Army officer, Whig politician and colonial administrator. He is best known in the United States for his surrender at the Siege of Yorktown in October 1781, which effectively ended major fighting in the American Revolutionary War. Despite that defeat, Cornwallis's career flourished: he served twice as Governor-General of India, where he enacted lasting administrative and legal reforms, and as Lord Lieutenant of Ireland, where he suppressed the Irish Rebellion of 1798 and advanced the Act of Union.

American war

Cornwallis served with distinction in America from 1776, fighting at Long Island, Brandywine and Germantown, and in 1780 took command of the southern campaign. He won a costly victory at Camden and defeated Nathanael Greene at Guilford Court House in March 1781, but his weakened army moved into Virginia and was trapped at Yorktown by the combined American and French forces of George Washington and the Comte de Rochambeau, supported by the French fleet. Cornwallis surrendered on 19 October 1781; legend holds that his band played "The World Turn'd Upside Down".

India

As Governor-General of Bengal (1786–1793), Cornwallis defeated Tipu Sultan of Mysore in the Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790–92), reformed the East India Company's administration in the Cornwallis Code of 1793, and introduced the Permanent Settlement of land revenue in Bengal. His reforms professionalised the civil service, separated revenue collection from the judiciary, and barred Indians from the higher offices — a policy with long consequences.

Ireland and later career

As Lord Lieutenant and commander-in-chief in Ireland (1798–1801), Cornwallis defeated the United Irishmen's rebellion and the French expedition to County Mayo, while restraining reprisals against rebels. He supported the Act of Union of 1800 and resigned with Pitt when George III blocked Catholic emancipation. In 1805 he returned to India for a second term as governor-general but died at Ghazipur shortly after arriving.
