Lake Como

[Image: Villa balconies and gardens on the shore of Lake Como]
Villa balconies and gardens on the shore of Lake Como

Lake Como, in Lombardy, Italy, is a glacial lake of 146 square kilometres, the third-largest in Italy after Garda and Maggiore, and one of the deepest in Europe at over 400 metres. Shaped like an inverted Y, it lies at the foot of the Alps, with the city of Como at its southwestern tip and Lecco at the southeastern.

Como has been a retreat for aristocrats and celebrities since Roman times — Pliny the Younger had villas there, and today stars such as George Clooney own homes around Bellagio, Varenna and Menaggio. The lake's scenery of steep wooded mountains, gardens, and villages, backed by snow-capped Alps, has made it one of Italy's most romantic destinations; Alessandro Manzoni set the opening of The Betrothed (1827), Italy's most famous novel, on its shores.
