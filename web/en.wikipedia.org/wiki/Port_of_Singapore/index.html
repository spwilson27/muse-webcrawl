Port of Singapore

The Port of Singapore is the collection of facilities and terminals that conduct maritime trade handling for Singapore, one of the world's great transshipment hubs and busiest ports. It connects to more than 600 ports in over 120 countries, and most of its container throughput is transshipped between vessels.

History

Singapore's harbour at the crossroads of the Strait of Malacca grew rapidly after Stamford Raffles founded a free-trading post in 1819. The colonial port at Keppel Harbour expanded through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries; after independence, container terminals opened at Tanjong Pagar from 1972, followed by Brani, Keppel, Pasir Panjang and Jurong terminals.

Tuas mega port

Since 2022 operations have been progressively consolidated at the automated Tuas Mega Port in the west of the island, which is planned, when fully completed in the 2040s, to handle up to 65 million TEU a year, making it the world's largest fully automated container terminal.

Significance

The port is also the world's largest bunkering (ship refuelling) port and a major ship-repair centre, and the maritime sector contributes roughly 7 percent of Singapore's GDP.
