The Patriarch of Moscow and all Rus' (Russian: Патриарх Московский и всея Руси) is the primate and spiritual head of the Russian Orthodox Church, the largest autocephalous Eastern Orthodox church in the world by membership. The office was created in 1589, suppressed by Peter the Great in 1721, and restored by the Local Council of 1917–18. Between its two periods the patriarchate has been held by figures of central importance in Russian history, from the reforming and divisive Nikon to the martyr-confessor Tikhon.

History

Establishment (1589)

The Russian Church, dependent on Constantinople since the baptism of Rus' in 988, had been effectively autocephalous since 1448, when Russian bishops elected Metropolitan Jonah without Constantinople's consent. In 1589, during a visit to Moscow, Ecumenical Patriarch Jeremias II consecrated Metropolitan Job as the first Patriarch of Moscow, ranking fifth in the order of Orthodox patriarchates, after Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch and Jerusalem — an arrangement ratified by an Eastern council in 1593 and consonant with the doctrine of Moscow as the "Third Rome".

Suppression (1721–1917)

After the death of Patriarch Adrian in 1700, Peter the Great left the see vacant and in 1721 abolished the patriarchate outright, replacing it with the Most Holy Governing Synod, a state-supervised collegial body overseen by a lay Chief Procurator. For nearly two centuries the Church was governed as, in effect, a department of the imperial state.

Restoration (1917)

The Local Council of 1917–18, meeting in revolutionary Moscow, restored the patriarchate and elected Metropolitan Tikhon (Bellavin) by lot in November 1917. Under Soviet persecution Tikhon resisted state seizure of church property and died in 1925; his successors were appointed only under heavy state pressure until Stalin's wartime rapprochement permitted the election of Sergius in 1943. Since 1991 the patriarchate has regained public prominence, property and influence in Russian society.

List of patriarchs (selected)

  • Job (1589–1605), first patriarch, deposed in the Time of Troubles
  • Hermogenes (1606–1612), martyred during the Polish occupation of Moscow
  • Filaret (1619–1633), father of Tsar Michael, effective co-ruler of Russia
  • Nikon (1652–1666), author of the reforms that caused the Old Believer schism
  • Adrian (1690–1700), last patriarch before the Synodal period
  • Tikhon (1917–1925), first patriarch after the restoration; canonised 1989
  • Sergius (1943–1944), elected under Stalin's wartime thaw
  • Alexy I (1945–1970); Pimen (1971–1990)
  • Alexy II (1990–2008), patriarch through the post-Soviet revival
  • Kirill (from 2009), formerly Metropolitan of Smolensk and Kaliningrad

Role and election

The patriarch presides over the Holy Synod and the Bishops' Council, represents the Church externally, and is diocesan bishop of Moscow. He is elected for life by secret ballot of the Local Council, composed of bishops, clergy and laity, from among bishops of the Russian Church. Liturgically he is commemorated in all churches of the Moscow Patriarchate, and he wears the distinctive white kukol and green mantle of the office.
