Runoff

Runoff is the flow of water occurring on the ground surface when excess rainwater, meltwater, or other sources can no longer sufficiently infiltrate the soil. It is a major component of the water cycle and a principal agent of soil erosion, flooding, and the transport of pollutants into rivers, lakes, and oceans.

Types

Surface runoff (overland flow) occurs when rainfall intensity exceeds the soil's infiltration capacity, or when the soil is saturated. Subsurface runoff moves laterally through the upper soil layers. In urban areas, pavements and roofs create impervious surfaces that greatly increase runoff volume and speed, a stream called stormwater runoff.

Effects

Agricultural runoff can carry fertilizer, pesticides, and sediment, contributing to eutrophication of water bodies. Urban runoff carries oils, heavy metals, and road salt. Engineers mitigate runoff with detention basins, permeable paving, rain gardens, and green roofs, while hydrologists model it with methods such as the rational method and hydrograph analysis to design drainage and flood protection.
