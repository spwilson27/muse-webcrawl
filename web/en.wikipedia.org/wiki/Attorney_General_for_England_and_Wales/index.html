Attorney General for England and Wales

Attorney General for England and Wales
Office: Attorney General's Office
Member of: Cabinet (attends)
Appointer: The Monarch, on advice of the Prime Minister

The Attorney General for England and Wales is one of the Law Officers of the Crown and the chief legal adviser to the Crown and the Government of the United Kingdom. The Attorney General heads the Attorney General's Office, superintends the Crown Prosecution Service and the Serious Fraud Office, and answers for their work in Parliament. The office is a ministerial post, though the holder is not usually a full member of the Cabinet but attends its meetings. The Attorney General is assisted by the Solicitor General for England and Wales, the junior Law Officer.

Functions

The Attorney General advises ministers and departments on questions of law, represents the Crown in important litigation, and performs a number of public-interest functions, including consenting to certain prosecutions, referring unduly lenient sentences and points of law to the Court of Appeal, and bringing contempt of court proceedings. As "superintending minister" for the prosecuting authorities, the Attorney General is accountable to Parliament for their operation but is not involved in day-to-day prosecution decisions, which are taken independently by the Director of Public Prosecutions; the convention governing this relationship is described in the Shawcross doctrine.

History

The office developed in the 13th and 14th centuries, as the king increasingly required professional attorneys to represent his interests in court, and by the later Middle Ages the King's Attorney had become a recognisable office. Over the centuries its holders became leading figures of the legal profession and government; notable Attorneys General include Francis Bacon, Lord Eldon, and, in the 20th century, figures such as Sir Hartley Shawcross, lead British prosecutor at Nuremberg.

Devolution

The Attorney General's remit covers England and Wales. Scotland has its own Lord Advocate, and Northern Ireland has an Attorney General for Northern Ireland, a post revived in 2010 following the devolution of policing and justice powers.
