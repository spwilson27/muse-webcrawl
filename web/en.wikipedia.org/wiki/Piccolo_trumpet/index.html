Piccolo trumpet

The piccolo trumpet is the smallest member of the brass family, pitched one octave higher than a standard B♭ trumpet. Most piccolo trumpets are built to play in either B♭ or A, using separate leadpipes for each key; instruments pitched in C also exist. Unlike the standard trumpet, most piccolo trumpets have four piston valves instead of three: the fourth valve lowers the pitch by a perfect fourth (or, in some designs, a major third), extending the low range and improving intonation for notes that would otherwise be problematic.


A piccolo trumpet with four valves; the short tubing makes it sound an octave above the standard trumpet.

History and design

Modern piccolo trumpets developed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries to meet the demands of high Baroque trumpet parts — the so-called "Bach trumpet". Makers such as Scherzer, Selmer and later Schilke refined the design. Because the instrument's small size makes its upper harmonics closely spaced, it eases the production of very high notes, though tone production and intonation require a smaller mouthpiece and considerable skill.

Repertoire

The piccolo trumpet is standard for Baroque repertoire originally written for natural trumpet, including Bach's Brandenburg Concerto No. 2 and Magnificat, and Handel's Messiah ("The trumpet shall sound"). In popular music, its best-known appearance is the piccolo trumpet solo — played by George Martin, performed by David Mason — in the Beatles' song "Penny Lane" (1967). The instrument is also used in modern orchestral and brass-band writing for brilliant high passages.

Notable players

Leading exponents include Maurice André, who popularised the piccolo trumpet as a solo instrument; Adolf Scherbaum; Håkan Hardenberger; Wynton Marsalis; and Alison Balsom.
