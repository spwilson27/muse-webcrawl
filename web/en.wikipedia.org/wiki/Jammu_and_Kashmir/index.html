Jammu and Kashmir

Jammu and Kashmir is a region administered by India as a union territory and consists of the southern portion of the larger Kashmir region, which has been the subject of a dispute between India and Pakistan since 1947 and between India and China since 1959.

Geography

The union territory comprises two divisions: Jammu in the south, largely foothills and plains, and the Kashmir Valley in the north, an intermontane valley enclosed by the Pir Panjal and the main Himalayan ranges and famed for Dal Lake and its houseboats. The winter capital is Jammu and the summer capital is Srinagar.

History

The princely state of Jammu and Kashmir was created in 1846 under the Dogra dynasty; its ruler acceded to India in October 1947 amid invasion by tribal forces from Pakistan, sparking the first Indo-Pakistani war. The wider Kashmir region was divided by the Line of Control. From 1954 until 2019 the Indian-administered area formed the state of Jammu and Kashmir, which enjoyed special autonomy under Article 370 of the Indian Constitution. In August 2019 the government of India revoked that status and the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act split the state into two union territories: Jammu and Kashmir, with a legislature, and Ladakh.

Demographics and economy

The population is about 12.5 million (2011 census). The Kashmir division is majority Muslim; Jammu has a Hindu majority, with significant Sikh and Buddhist minorities. The economy depends on agriculture, horticulture (apples, saffron, walnuts), handicrafts such as pashmina and carpets, and tourism.
