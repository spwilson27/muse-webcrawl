Organised crime in India

Organised crime in India encompasses a range of criminal enterprises, from the Mumbai underworld syndicates that grew out of smuggling in the 1970s and 1980s to drug trafficking, extortion rackets, contract killing and illegal mining networks in various regions of the country.

The Mumbai underworld

The best-known syndicate, D-Company, grew under Dawood Ibrahim from gold smuggling and extortion into an international network. Its involvement in the 1993 Bombay bombings transformed it from a criminal gang into a national-security concern, and its leader remains one of India's most wanted fugitives. Rival gangs led by figures such as Chhota Rajan fought violent turf wars through the 1990s.

Government response

Indian states have enacted special organised-crime statutes, notably the Maharashtra Control of Organised Crime Act (MCOCA, 1999), providing stricter bail and evidence rules. Police "encounter" operations sharply reduced gang activity in Mumbai, though organised crime persists in forms such as narcotics, cybercrime and illegal resource extraction.
