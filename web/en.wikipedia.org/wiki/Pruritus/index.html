Pruritus

Pruritus
Other namesItch, itching
SpecialtyDermatology
SymptomsUnpleasant skin sensation provoking the desire to scratch

Pruritus, from the Latin prurire ("to itch"), is the medical term for itching — an unpleasant sensation of the skin that provokes the desire to rub or scratch the area. Itch has resisted many attempts at classification: physiologically it is a low-level pain signal, and scratching gives relief through mild counter-stimulation.

Causes

Itching may be localised or generalised. Common causes include dry skin (xerosis), eczema, contact dermatitis, urticaria, insect bites, fungal infections and scabies. Generalised pruritus without rash can signal systemic disease: chronic kidney disease, cholestatic liver disease, thyroid disorders, iron deficiency, polycythaemia vera and some cancers. Many medicines, especially opioids, provoke itch.

Mechanism

Itch is carried by dedicated unmyelinated nerve fibres distinct from pain fibres. Histamine from mast cells is the classic mediator, but many pathways — involving proteases, cytokines such as interleukin-31, and bile acids — act through receptors on sensory neurons. Chronic itch can involve sensitisation similar to chronic pain.

Treatment

Management targets the underlying cause, plus emollients, avoidance of triggers, topical corticosteroids or calcineurin inhibitors for dermatitis, and antihistamines mainly where urticaria or sedation is useful. Specific therapies include bile-acid sequestrants for cholestatic itch and kappa-opioid agonists for uraemic pruritus.
