Frank Serpico

Francesco Vincent Serpico (born April 14, 1936) is an American retired New York City Police Department detective, best known for whistleblowing on widespread corruption in the NYPD in the late 1960s and early 1970s — an act dramatised in the 1973 film Serpico starring Al Pacino.

Early life

Serpico was born in Brooklyn, New York, to Italian immigrant parents from Marigliano, near Naples. He attended Brooklyn College and joined the NYPD in 1959, becoming a patrolman and then a plainclothes detective working in Brooklyn and the Bronx.

Whistleblowing

In plainclothes enforcement, Serpico encountered systematic "pad" payments — regular bribes collected from gamblers and narcotics dealers and shared among officers. Refusing to accept money himself, he reported the corruption through official channels from 1966 onward but was met with indifference and obstruction. With fellow officer David Durk, he eventually took the story to the New York Times, whose April 1970 front-page report prompted Mayor John Lindsay to appoint the Knapp Commission to investigate police corruption.

Shooting

On February 3, 1971, Serpico was shot in the face during a drug raid in Williamsburg, Brooklyn, after the officers backing him failed to follow him through the door. He survived but was left deaf in one ear and with bullet fragments lodged in his brain. The circumstances of the shooting have been debated ever since. He testified before the Knapp Commission in October 1971, the first active-duty officer to do so against the department.

Later life

Serpico retired from the NYPD in 1972 and received the department's Medal of Honor. He lived for a time in the Netherlands and Switzerland before returning to New York State, where he has continued to lecture and campaign on police reform and whistleblower protection.
