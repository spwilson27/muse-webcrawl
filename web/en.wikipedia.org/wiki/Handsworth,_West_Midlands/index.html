Handsworth, West Midlands

Handsworth is an inner-city, urban area of northwest Birmingham, in the West Midlands, England. Historically in Staffordshire, it lies just outside Birmingham City Centre and is known for its diverse communities and Victorian heritage.

History

Handsworth developed rapidly during the Industrial Revolution. Matthew Boulton's Soho Manufactory stood nearby, and St Mary's Church contains memorials to Boulton, James Watt, and William Murdoch. The area's large Victorian houses later became home to successive waves of migrants, including Caribbean, South Asian, and other communities after the Second World War.

Culture

Handsworth has a strong musical heritage, including the reggae band Steel Pulse and singer-songwriter Joan Armatrading, who grew up in the area. The Handsworth Carnival and the annual Vaisakhi celebrations in Handsworth Park are major local events.

Notable places

Landmarks include Handsworth Park, St Mary's Church, the Soho House museum, and the Guru Nanak Gurdwara on Soho Road. The area was the scene of riots in 1981 and 1985, events that drew national attention to inner-city deprivation.
