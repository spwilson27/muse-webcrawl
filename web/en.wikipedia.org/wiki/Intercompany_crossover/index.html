Intercompany crossover

An intercompany crossover is a fictional story, most often in comic books, in which characters owned by two different publishers or media companies appear together. Such crossovers require licensing agreements between competitors and are marketed as special events. They are distinct from ordinary crossovers, in which characters from a single publisher's shared universe meet. Intercompany crossovers have most often paired characters of Marvel Comics and DC Comics, the two largest American publishers, but also occur between comics and other media, and in film, television, video games and prose fiction.

History

Unofficial or parody meetings appeared as early as the Golden Age, but the first modern authorised Marvel–DC crossover was Superman vs. the Amazing Spider-Man (1976), a oversized one-shot in which the heroes fought Lex Luthor and Doctor Octopus. It was followed by further treasury editions and, in the 1990s, an era of frequent crossovers including Batman/Punisher, Darkseid vs. Galactus, and the 1996 DC vs. Marvel miniseries, whose outcome was partly decided by reader votes and which spawned the Amalgam Comics imprint merging the two universes' characters. The last major Marvel–DC collaboration for many years was JLA/Avengers (2003–2004), by Kurt Busiek and George Pérez; relations between the publishers thereafter cooled, although reprints of earlier crossovers resumed in the 2020s.

Other media

Examples beyond Marvel and DC include comics crossovers between licensed properties such as Aliens versus Predator (which became a film franchise), Star Trek/Green Lantern and Archie vs. Predator. In video games, crossover fighting games and titles such as Fortnite routinely combine characters from many owners, while films such as Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1988) famously brought together cartoon characters from competing studios. Negotiating rights, creative control and canon status — crossovers are usually considered non-canonical for at least one of the participating franchises — are the principal obstacles to such projects.
