Philip Henslowe

Born: c. 1550, Lindfield, Sussex, England

Died: January 6, 1616, London, England

Occupation: Theatre impresario, property developer, financier

Philip Henslowe (c. 1550 – 6 January 1616) was an Elizabethan theatrical entrepreneur and impresario, the builder of the Rose Theatre on London's Bankside and the keeper of a famous diary that constitutes one of the most important primary sources for the history of early modern English theatre.

Life and business

Henslowe, originally from Sussex, settled in Southwark, London, where he made money in property, pawnbroking, starch-making, and other ventures, eventually serving as a groom of the chamber under Elizabeth I and James I. In 1587 he built the Rose, one of the first purpose-built playhouses in the Bankside theatre district. He later had interests in the Fortune Theatre (built 1600) with his son-in-law, the actor Edward Alleyn, and in the Hope Theatre (1614), as well as in bear-baiting and other entertainments at the Beargarden.

Henslowe's Diary

The manuscript known as Henslowe's Diary (kept roughly 1592–1603, with later entries, and now held at Dulwich College, which Alleyn founded) records in a recycled account book the Rose's daily box-office receipts, Henslowe's payments — usually in instalments — to playwrights including Ben Jonson, Thomas Dekker, Thomas Heywood, Henry Chettle, and George Chapman, and inventories of the Admiral's Men's costumes and playbooks. It reveals how repertory companies commissioned plays — often collaboratively, at prices of about £6–£10 per play — and lists many lost plays, including a Hamlet predating Shakespeare's. Because Shakespeare worked for the rival Lord Chamberlain's Men, he figures only indirectly in the Diary.

Legacy

Henslowe is a principal character in the 1998 film Shakespeare in Love, where he was played by Geoffrey Rush. Modern scholarship, beginning with W. W. Greg's editions of the Diary and the Henslowe-Alleyn papers, rests heavily on his records for knowledge of Elizabethan theatre finance and repertory.
