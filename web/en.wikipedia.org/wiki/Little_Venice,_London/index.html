Little Venice is a neighbourhood in the City of Westminster in London, England, centred on the junction of the Regent's Canal and the Grand Union Canal (Paddington Arm), just north of Paddington. The area is known for its canalside setting, white stucco Regency terraces and rows of moored narrowboats and houseboats, which lend it a resemblance — much exaggerated but charming — to Venice in Italy.

History

The canals arrived in the early nineteenth century: the Paddington Arm of the Grand Union Canal opened in 1801, and the Regent's Canal followed in 1820. The broad triangular pool at their junction, once called Browning's Pool after the poet Robert Browning, who lived nearby at Warwick Crescent, became the heart of the district. The name "Little Venice" is popularly attributed to Browning or to Lord Byron, though its first recorded uses date from the early twentieth century, and it only became the area's standard name after the Second World War. A regular waterbus service has run from Little Venice to London Zoo and Camden Lock since the 1950s, and the Canal Café Theatre, a well-known fringe and comedy venue, overlooks the junction bridge. The neighbourhood's stucco villas have been home to figures including the computer pioneer Alan Turing, who was born in Warrington Crescent.
