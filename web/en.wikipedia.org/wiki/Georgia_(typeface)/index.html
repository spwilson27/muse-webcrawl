Georgia is a serif typeface designed in 1993 by Matthew Carter and hinted by Tom Rickner for the Microsoft Corporation. It was released in 1996 as one of the core fonts for the Web and was named after a tabloid headline, "Alien heads found in Georgia."

Design

Georgia was designed for clarity on low-resolution computer screens of the 1990s: it has a large x-height, fairly wide letterforms, and old-style (text) figures by default in its original release. Despite being optimised for the screen, its forms were influenced by traditional book faces such as Scotch Roman. It pairs with the sans-serif Verdana, also designed by Carter for Microsoft. Georgia became one of the most widely used web serif fonts, adopted for body text by many news and publishing websites, and remains distributed with Windows and macOS.
