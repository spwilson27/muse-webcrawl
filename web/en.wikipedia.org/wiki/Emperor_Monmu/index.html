Emperor Monmu

Emperor Monmu (文武天皇, Monmu-tennō; 683 – 18 July 707) was the 42nd emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. He reigned from 697 until his death in 707. His personal name was Karu no Miko (Prince Karu), and he was the son of Prince Kusakabe and the grandson of Emperor Tenmu and Empress Jitō.

Reign

Monmu ascended the throne in 697 after the abdication of his grandmother, Empress Jitō, who continued to exert influence as a retired sovereign. His mother, Princess Abe — later Empress Genshō — was a daughter of Emperor Tenji, which made Monmu the focus of the union of the two imperial lines.

The outstanding achievement of the reign was the promulgation of the Taihō Code in 701, a comprehensive legal and administrative code modelled on Tang Chinese law that reorganised the Japanese state under the ritsuryō system. The code's compilation was directed by Prince Osakabe and Fujiwara no Fuhito. The reign also saw continued minting of coinage and the dispatch of missions to Tang China.

Death and succession

Monmu died in 707 at about twenty-five years of age, leaving an infant son, Prince Obito, the future Emperor Shōmu. Because the heir was a child, Monmu's mother succeeded him as Empress Genmei. His tomb is traditionally identified as the Hinokuma no Misasago in Asuka, Nara.
