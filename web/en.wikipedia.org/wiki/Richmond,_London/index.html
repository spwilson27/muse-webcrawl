Richmond, London

Richmond is a town in south-west London, 8.2 miles (13.2 km) west-southwest of Charing Cross. It stands on a meander of the River Thames and is the administrative centre of the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames.

History

Richmond grew around Richmond Palace, built by Henry VII, who named it after Richmond Castle in Yorkshire. Elizabeth I died at the palace in 1603. In the 18th century Richmond became a fashionable resort; Richmond Bridge, opened in 1777, is the oldest surviving Thames bridge in London.

Geography and landmarks

Richmond Hill offers a famous protected view across the Thames toward Twickenham, celebrated in paintings by Turner. Richmond Park, created by Charles I, is London's largest Royal Park and a National Nature Reserve, home to herds of red and fallow deer. Richmond Green, the riverside, Kew Gardens nearby, and Ham House are notable landmarks.

Transport and culture

Richmond station is served by District line, London Overground, and South Western Railway services. The town has a theatre, Richmond Theatre on the Green, and a strong association with literature and art, from Virginia Woolf's Hogarth Press to Mick Jagger, a long-time resident.
