Turkish rock

Turkish rock is a genre of music in Turkey that emerged in the 1960s, blending Western rock and roll and psychedelic rock with Turkish folk music and Anatolian themes. It gave rise to the subgenre known as Anatolian rock (Anadolu rock), pioneered by artists such as Erkin Koray, Cem Karaca, Barış Manço, Moğollar and Selda Bağcan.

History

Rock music reached Turkey in the late 1950s and 1960s through radio broadcasts and imported records. In the 1960s, competitions such as the Altın Mikrofon (Golden Microphone) encouraged bands to fuse folk melodies with electric instrumentation. Barış Manço and Cem Karaca became leading figures, while Erkin Koray developed a distinctly Turkish psychedelic sound, experimenting with the bağlama and Middle Eastern scales on electric guitar.

The 1970s saw the genre flourish, with groups such as Moğollar, Üç Hürel and Kurtalan Ekspres. Political turmoil in the late 1970s and the 1980 military coup restricted the scene, and arabesque and pop gained ground. In the 1990s and 2000s, Turkish rock experienced a revival through bands such as Duman, Mor ve Ötesi, maNga, Athena and Şebnem Ferah performing as a solo artist, with rock festivals drawing large audiences.

Characteristics

Anatolian rock typically combines rock instrumentation with Turkish folk rhythms, makam-influenced melodies and instruments such as the bağlama. Lyrics frequently address social and political themes, love, and Anatolian history and mythology.
