Regions of the Czech Republic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The 14 regions (kraje) of the Czech Republic

The regions of the Czech Republic (Czech: kraje, singular kraj) are the country's highest-level territorial self-governing units. There are 14 regions, including the capital city of Prague, which has the status of both a municipality and a region. The regions were established on 1 January 2000 under the Act on the Creation of Higher Territorial Self-Governing Units, implementing Chapter VII of the Czech Constitution.

Government

Each region is governed by an elected regional assembly (zastupitelstvo kraje), an executive council (rada kraje) and a governor (hejtman). Regional governments are responsible for secondary education, regional roads, hospitals and health care planning, social services, public transport coordination and regional development. Regions are subdivided into districts (okresy), which since 2003 have had no self-governing bodies but remain statistical and administrative units, and municipalities.

History

The kraj system has roots in the Habsburg-era circles (Kreise) and was used in Czechoslovakia from 1949 and again between 1960 and 1990, when ten large regions existed. After the restoration of regional self-government in 2000, the 14 smaller regions were drawn broadly along historic and functional boundaries.
