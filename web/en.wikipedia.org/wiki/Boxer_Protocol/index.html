Boxer Protocol

Signed7 September 1901
LocationBeijing, Qing China
PartiesQing Empire and the Eight-Nation Alliance

The Boxer Protocol was a diplomatic protocol signed on 7 September 1901 between the Qing Empire of China and the Eight-Nation Alliance — Austria-Hungary, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States — together with Belgium, Spain and the Netherlands, following the defeat of the Boxer Rebellion and the relief of the siege of the International Legations in Beijing.

Terms

The protocol's twelve articles required China to pay an indemnity of 450 million taels of silver (about US$333 million at the time) over 39 years, with interest — a total approaching one billion taels. Other terms included the execution or punishment of officials who had supported the Boxers, the destruction of the Taku Forts, a ban on arms imports, the stationing of foreign troops along the railway from Beijing to the sea, a fortified and exclusively foreign-occupied Legation Quarter in Beijing, an imperial apology mission to Germany for the murder of envoy Clemens von Ketteler, and the suspension of official examinations in cities where foreigners had been killed.

Consequences

The indemnity, secured on China's customs and salt revenues, deepened the Qing government's dependence on foreign lenders and is remembered in China as part of the "century of humiliation". The United States remitted part of its share to fund the Boxer Indemnity Scholarship programme, which educated Chinese students in America and helped establish what became Tsinghua University. The last payments were still being made when the indemnity era ended with the Second World War.
