Special municipality (Taiwan)

A special municipality is a large metropolitan municipality in Taiwan (the Republic of China) administered directly by the central government. Special municipalities form the top level of the country's administrative divisions, alongside provinces (now largely nominal) and are themselves divided into districts.

List

There are currently six special municipalities: Taipei, New Taipei, Taoyuan, Taichung, Tainan and Kaohsiung. Taipei was made the first special municipality in 1967, followed by Kaohsiung in 1979. In 2010, Taipei County was upgraded and renamed New Taipei, and the cities and counties of Taichung and Tainan were merged and upgraded. Taoyuan was upgraded in 2014.

Criteria and governance

Under the Local Government Act, an area with a population of more than 1.25 million that has special needs in political, economic and cultural development may be designated a special municipality. Each is headed by an elected mayor and a municipal council, and they receive a larger share of centrally allocated tax revenue than counties and provincial cities.
