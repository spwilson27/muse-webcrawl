The Australian Electoral Commission (AEC) is the independent federal agency in charge of organising, conducting, and supervising federal Australian elections, by-elections, and referendums. Established under the Commonwealth Electoral Act 1918 as amended in 1984, the AEC also maintains the Commonwealth electoral roll, determines electoral boundaries through redistributions, registers political parties, and administers public funding and disclosure of political donations.

Functions

  • Conducting federal elections for the House of Representatives and the Senate, and national referendums.
  • Maintaining the electoral roll of more than 17 million enrolled voters under Australia's compulsory enrolment and compulsory voting system.
  • Running redistribution processes that redraw divisional boundaries to keep enrolments roughly equal.
  • Registering political parties and administering the funding and disclosure scheme for election donations and expenditure.
  • Providing electoral education and information services, and conducting industrial and fee-for-service elections for other organisations.

Structure and independence

The Commission itself consists of three members: a chairperson who must be a judge or retired judge of the Federal Court, the Electoral Commissioner (the chief executive), and a non-judicial member, conventionally the Australian Statistician. The AEC is a statutory authority independent of government direction in the conduct of elections; its head office is in Canberra with offices in every state and territory.

Notable events

Following the 2013 federal election, the loss of 1,370 Senate ballot papers in Western Australia led to the result in that state being declared void and a fresh Senate election being held in April 2014—the only such re-run at a federal election—and prompted reforms to ballot handling and the resignation of the Electoral Commissioner. The AEC is also known internationally for its active and widely followed social media communication during election periods.
