Tripura Sundari

Tripura Sundari (Sanskrit: त्रिपुर सुन्दरी, "Beautiful Goddess of the Three Cities") is one of the ten Mahāvidyās and a principal form of the supreme goddess (Mahādevī) in the Śākta tradition of Hinduism. She is also worshipped as Lalitā, Rājarājeśvarī, Ṣoḍaśī, and Kāmeśvarī.

Iconography and worship

Tripura Sundari is depicted as a beautiful sixteen-year-old goddess (hence Ṣoḍaśī, "sixteen"), seated on a throne supported by Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra, and Īśvara, with Sadāśiva as the seat itself. She holds a sugarcane bow, flower arrows, a noose, and a goad, symbolising mind, senses, attachment, and aversion. Her central symbol is the Śrī Chakra (Śrī Yantra), a geometric diagram of nine interlocking triangles, and her worship forms the heart of the Śrī Vidyā tradition. The Lalitā Sahasranāma, a hymn of her thousand names from the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, is one of the most widely recited Śākta texts.

Temples

A major temple dedicated to her stands in Udaipur, Tripura, one of the 51 Śakti Pīṭhas, from which the state of Tripura is said to take its name.
