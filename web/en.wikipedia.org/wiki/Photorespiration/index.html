Photorespiration

The photorespiratory pathway spans three organelles: chloroplast, peroxisome and mitochondrion.

Photorespiration (also known as the oxidative photosynthetic carbon cycle, or C2 photosynthesis) is a process in plant metabolism where the enzyme RuBisCO oxygenates RuBP, causing some of the energy produced by photosynthesis to be wasted. The desired reaction is the addition of carbon dioxide to RuBP (carboxylation), a key step in the Calvin–Benson cycle, but approximately 25% of reactions by RuBisCO instead add oxygen to RuBP (oxygenation), creating a product that cannot be used within the Calvin–Benson cycle. In many plants this decreases photosynthetic efficiency.

Photorespiration involves a complex network of enzyme reactions that exchange metabolites between chloroplasts, leaf peroxisomes and mitochondria. The oxygenation of RuBP produces one molecule of 3-phosphoglycerate and one molecule of 2-phosphoglycolate. The 2-phosphoglycolate is toxic to the plant and is recycled at considerable cost into 3-phosphoglycerate through a series of reactions, releasing carbon dioxide and ammonia in the process.

Causes and consequences

Unlike cellular respiration, photorespiration does not produce useful energy in the form of ATP, and it releases previously fixed carbon dioxide, reducing the overall efficiency of photosynthesis. Estimates suggest that photorespiration can reduce the efficiency of photosynthesis in C3 plants by as much as 25–30%, a significant factor for crop yields under warm, dry conditions where stomata close and internal carbon dioxide concentrations fall.

Evolutionary significance

Photorespiration is considered an evolutionary relic arising from the fact that RuBisCO evolved in an atmosphere rich in carbon dioxide and poor in oxygen, so the enzyme's active site could not fully discriminate between the two gases. Some plants, including many grasses, have evolved C4 photosynthesis and CAM photosynthesis to minimise oxygenation by concentrating carbon dioxide around RuBisCO.

Despite its costs, photorespiration may play protective roles, dissipating excess energy under high-light stress and contributing to nitrogen metabolism.
