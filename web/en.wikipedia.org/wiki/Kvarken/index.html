Kvarken

Kvarken (Swedish: Kvarken; Finnish: Merenkurkku, literally "sea throat") is the narrow region in the Gulf of Bothnia separating the Bothnian Bay (the northern part of the gulf) from the Bothnian Sea to the south. The distance between the Swedish mainland and the Finnish mainland is around 80 km (50 mi), while the distance between the outermost islands is roughly 25 km (16 mi).

Geography and geology

The Kvarken Archipelago, on the Finnish side near Vaasa, comprises some 5,600 islands characterised by distinctive De Geer moraines—washboard ridges formed beneath the retreating ice sheet. The region exhibits some of the fastest post-glacial land uplift (isostatic rebound) in the world, about 8–9 mm per year, and new land continually emerges from the sea. The waters of the archipelago are shallow, with a maximum depth of around 25 m.

World Heritage Site

In 2006 the Kvarken Archipelago was added to UNESCO's World Heritage List as an extension of the High Coast site in Sweden, forming the transnational "High Coast / Kvarken Archipelago" property, recognised for its outstanding evidence of glacial uplift and land emergence.

Transport

Ferries cross Kvarken between Vaasa in Finland and Umeå in Sweden. A fixed link across Kvarken has been studied for decades; the proposed crossing would combine bridges and causeways via the archipelago's islands, but remains unbuilt. In winter the strait can freeze, and ice roads have occasionally been opened.
