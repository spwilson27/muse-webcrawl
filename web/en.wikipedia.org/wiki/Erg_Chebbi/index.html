Erg Chebbi (Arabic: عرق الشبي) is one of Morocco's two Saharan ergs — large seas of dunes formed by wind-blown sand — the other being Erg Chigaga. It lies in the Drâa-Tafilalet region of south-eastern Morocco, near the Algerian border and the village of Merzouga.

Geography

The erg measures roughly 22 kilometres from north to south and about 5 kilometres across, covering an area of around 110 square kilometres. Its highest dunes reach approximately 150 metres, making them among the tallest in Morocco. The distinctive orange colour of the sand results from its iron oxide content.

Tourism and local significance

Erg Chebbi is a major destination for desert tourism in Morocco: camel treks at sunrise and sunset, overnight camps among the dunes, and sandboarding are popular activities. Local tradition holds that the dunes were formed when a wealthy family was punished for refusing hospitality, and some Moroccans visit the sands for their supposed therapeutic effects on conditions such as rheumatism, burying themselves in the hot sand during the summer months.
