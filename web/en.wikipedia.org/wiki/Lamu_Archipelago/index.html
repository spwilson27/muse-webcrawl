The Lamu Archipelago is a group of islands situated on the north coast of Kenya, in the Indian Ocean. Its principal islands are Lamu Island, Manda Island and Pate Island, together with smaller islands and islets such as Kiwayu and Manda Toto. The archipelago lies offshore from the mainland port of Mokowe and forms part of Lamu County.

History

The archipelago has been inhabited for many centuries and forms one of the best-preserved centres of Swahili culture. Its towns traded with Arabia, Persia, India and China; Chinese porcelain and other imported wares have been found in local sites, and tradition holds that survivors of one of Zheng He's fleets may have settled on Pate Island in the 15th century. Lamu Town, on Lamu Island, is the oldest and best-preserved Swahili settlement in East Africa, with origins in the 14th century, and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001. Pate Island was the seat of the Pate Sultanate, and its ruins include the town of Siyu and the Shanga archaeological site.

Geography and life

The islands are low, sandy and fringed with mangroves, separated from the mainland by narrow channels. Transport on Lamu Island is famously by foot, donkey and dhow, with very few motor vehicles. The economy combines fishing, dhow building, mangrove-pole cutting, agriculture and tourism. The proposed Lamu Port, part of the LAPSSET corridor project on the mainland opposite Manda Island, is expected to change the region substantially.
