Positive feedback

Positive feedback (exacerbating feedback, self-reinforcing feedback) is a process that occurs in a feedback loop which increases the effects of a small disturbance. That is, the effects of a perturbation on a system include an increase in the magnitude of the perturbation. In contrast, a system in which the results of a change act to reduce or counteract it has negative feedback.

Examples

Examples include the audio feedback squeal from a microphone too close to a loudspeaker, blood clotting, the contractions of childbirth driven by oxytocin, runaway climate processes such as the ice–albedo feedback, and financial panics or bubbles. In electronics, positive feedback is exploited in oscillators and Schmitt triggers, while uncontrolled it causes latch-up and instability.

Mathematical behaviour

Positive feedback tends to cause system instability through exponential growth, oscillation or chaotic behaviour, until limited by nonlinearities or external constraints.
