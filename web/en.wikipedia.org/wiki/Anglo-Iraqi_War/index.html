Anglo-Iraqi War

Date2–31 May 1941
LocationKingdom of Iraq
CombatantsUnited Kingdom and allies vs. Government of Rashid Ali al-Gaylani (supported by Germany and Italy)
ResultBritish victory; regent restored

The Anglo-Iraqi War was a British-led Allied military campaign during the Second World War against the Kingdom of Iraq, then ruled by Rashid Ali al-Gaylani, who had seized power in a pro-Axis coup d'état in April 1941. The fighting lasted from 2 to 31 May 1941 and resulted in the restoration of the pro-British regent, Prince 'Abd al-Ilah.

Course of the war

Iraq had been formally independent since 1932, but the Anglo-Iraqi Treaty of 1930 preserved British bases at Habbaniya and Basra and transit rights. After the coup, Iraqi forces besieged the RAF station at Habbaniya; the outnumbered British garrison, using training aircraft pressed into service as bombers, broke the siege in early May. A relief column (Kingcol, part of Habforce) advanced across the desert from Transjordan, while German aircraft staged through Vichy-held Syria to Mosul and a small number of Axis planes intervened. British and Indian forces occupied Baghdad at the end of May; Rashid Ali and the Mufti of Jerusalem fled to Persia and then to Germany.

Aftermath

The campaign secured Middle Eastern oil supplies and the route to India and was followed by the Allied invasion of Syria and Lebanon and the Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran. The Farhud pogrom against Baghdad's Jews erupted in the vacuum of power on 1–2 June 1941.
