Charles de Bonchamps

Charles Melchior Artus de Bonchamps, Marquis de Bonchamps (10 May 1760 – 18 October 1793) was a French nobleman and a leader of the royalist War in the Vendée against the French Republic in 1793. He is celebrated for his dying act of clemency, ordering the release of several thousand Republican prisoners whom his men intended to kill.

Life

Bonchamps served as an officer in the French army and fought in the American War of Independence before retiring to his estate in Anjou. When the Vendée rose against the Republic in March 1793, the peasants of his district compelled him to take command of their forces.

War in the Vendée

Bonchamps became one of the principal generals of the Catholic and Royal Army, fighting at Thouars, Saumur and in the attacks on Nantes. Mortally wounded at the Battle of Cholet on 17 October 1793, he was carried to Saint-Florent-le-Vieil, where the Vendéans held some 5,000 Republican prisoners. Learning that his men planned to massacre them in reprisal, the dying Bonchamps ordered their release — "Grâce aux prisonniers!" ("Pardon for the prisoners!"). Among those freed was the father of the sculptor David d'Angers, who later carved Bonchamps's tomb monument depicting the pardon.
