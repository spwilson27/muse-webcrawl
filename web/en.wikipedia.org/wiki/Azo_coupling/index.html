Azo coupling

An azo coupling is an organic reaction between a diazonium compound and another aromatic compound (the coupling component) that produces an azo compound, containing the –N=N– linkage. It is the most widely used industrial reaction for producing azo dyes and pigments.

Mechanism

The reaction proceeds by electrophilic aromatic substitution: the diazonium ion, a weak electrophile, attacks an electron-rich aromatic ring such as a phenol or an aniline, usually at the para position. Phenols are typically coupled in mildly alkaline solution and amines in mildly acidic solution.

Applications

Azo coupling is used to manufacture dyes such as methyl orange, Congo red and the tartrazine family of food colorants. The reaction conditions — temperature near 0–5 °C for diazotisation, and controlled pH for coupling — are central to large-scale dye production.
