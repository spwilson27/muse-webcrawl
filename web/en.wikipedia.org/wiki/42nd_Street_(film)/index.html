42nd Street is a 1933 American pre-Code musical film directed by Lloyd Bacon, with musical numbers choreographed by Busby Berkeley, for Warner Bros. It is widely credited with reviving the Hollywood musical during the Great Depression and with establishing the "backstage musical" formula.

Plot

Director Julian Marsh (Warner Baxter), ill and desperate for one more hit, stages the musical Pretty Lady during the Depression. When leading lady Dorothy Brock (Bebe Daniels) breaks her ankle on the eve of the opening, inexperienced chorus girl Peggy Sawyer (Ruby Keeler) is thrust into the lead. Marsh's famous speech — telling her she must go on stage a youngster but "come back a star" — has become one of cinema's most quoted lines.

Music and legacy

The score by Harry Warren and Al Dubin includes "You're Getting to Be a Habit with Me", "Shuffle Off to Buffalo", "Young and Healthy" and the title song, "42nd Street". Berkeley's geometric, kaleidoscopic production numbers defined his style and influenced musicals for decades. The film was a major box-office success, was Oscar-nominated for Best Picture, and was selected for the US National Film Registry in 1998. A stage musical adaptation premiered on Broadway in 1980 and won the Tony Award for Best Musical.
