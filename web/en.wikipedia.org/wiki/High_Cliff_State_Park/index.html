High Cliff State Park

LocationCalumet County, Wisconsin, US
Area1,187 acres (480 ha)
Established1956
Administered byWisconsin DNR

High Cliff State Park is a Wisconsin state park on the eastern shore of Lake Winnebago, near Sherwood in Calumet County. It is the only state-owned recreational facility on Lake Winnebago, Wisconsin's largest inland lake. The park takes its name from the limestone cliff of the Niagara Escarpment that runs along its western edge, rising above the lake.

Features

The park covers about 1,187 acres of forest, prairie and cliff-top land, with hiking and equestrian trails, a campground, a marina and boat launch, swimming beach, picnic areas and an observation tower with views over the lake. Within the park are protected Native American effigy mounds built by Siouan-speaking peoples between roughly 1050 and 1300 AD, as well as the remains of a 19th- and early 20th-century lime quarrying and kiln operation. In winter the park offers snowshoeing, cross-country skiing and ice fishing access.

Geology

The cliffs are part of the Niagara Escarpment, a band of resistant Silurian dolomite that arcs from Wisconsin through Ontario and New York to Niagara Falls.
