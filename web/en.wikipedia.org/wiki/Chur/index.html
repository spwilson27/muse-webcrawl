Chur

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Chur (Romansh: Cuira) is the capital of the Swiss canton of the Grisons (Graubünden) and the seat of the municipality of Chur. Lying in the valley of the Alpine Rhine at an elevation of about 594 metres, it has a population of roughly 35,000 and is frequently described as the oldest town in Switzerland, with settlement evidence going back some 5,000 years and a Roman past as Curia Raetorum.

History

Chur became the seat of a bishopric in the 4th century — traditionally counted the first north of the Alps — and its bishops, as prince-bishops, dominated the region through the Middle Ages. The town joined the League of God's House in 1367 and, with the other leagues of the Grisons, became an associate of the Swiss Confederation before finally joining as a canton in 1803.

Geography and transport

Chur is a major transport node: it is the junction of the SBB line from Zürich with the narrow-gauge Rhaetian Railway, whose lines include the famous Bernina and Glacier Express routes. The A13 motorway passes nearby, and the local mountain Brambrüesch is served by a cable car from the town.

Culture

Landmarks include the Romanesque cathedral of St. Mary of the Assumption, the old town's bishop's court (Hof), and museums devoted to the Grisons and to the artist Angelika Kauffmann, who was born in Chur. The town is bilingual in German and Romansh heritage, though German now predominates.
