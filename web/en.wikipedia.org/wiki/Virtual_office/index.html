Virtual office

A virtual office is part of the flexible workspace industry that provides businesses with a combination of services, space and technology, without those businesses bearing the capital expenses of owning or leasing a traditional office.

Virtual office users typically do not hold a dedicated physical desk or office, but instead subscribe to services such as a professional business mailing address, mail forwarding, telephone answering by a receptionist, voicemail, and access to meeting rooms and day offices booked on demand. The concept allows small businesses, freelancers and remote workers to project a presence in a desirable location while working from elsewhere.

History

The idea is often traced to Ralph Gregory, who founded "The Virtual Office, Inc." in 1994, and to earlier serviced-office and executive-suite providers that began offering address and telephone services in the 1980s. The spread of the internet, mobile phones and cloud computing in the 2000s made virtual offices a mainstream option, and demand grew sharply with the rise of remote work in the 2020s.

Services

Common services include:

  • A business address for mail and, in some jurisdictions, company registration
  • Mail receipt, scanning and forwarding
  • A local telephone number with live answering or auto-attendant
  • Meeting and conference rooms rented by the hour
  • Administrative support and access to coworking lounges

Criticism and regulation

Because a virtual office address can be used to register a company, regulators in some countries require providers to verify customer identity and may restrict the use of virtual addresses for certain legal purposes. Critics note that the same features that make virtual offices attractive to legitimate remote businesses can also be exploited for mail fraud or shell companies.
