Frei Otto

Born31 May 1925, Siegmar, Germany
Died9 March 2015, Warmbronn
Known forLightweight tensile structures
AwardPritzker Prize 2015

Frei Paul Otto (1925–2015) was a German architect and structural engineer famed for lightweight tensile and membrane structures, seeking minimum material through form-finding experiments with soap films, models and gridshells.

Works

Key works include the German Pavilion at Expo 67 in Montreal (with Rolf Gutbrod), the cable-net roof of the Munich Olympic Stadium for the 1972 Games (with Behnisch & Partner), and the Multihalle Mannheim timber gridshell. He founded the Institute for Lightweight Structures at Stuttgart.

Recognition

Otto received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2015, announced shortly before — and confirmed after — his death, an unusual posthumous honour. His ecological, economical building philosophy influences sustainable architecture.
