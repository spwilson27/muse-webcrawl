The Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA) is a statutory office within the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) of the Executive Office of the President of the United States. It is the United States government's central authority for reviewing executive branch regulations, approving government information collections, and setting statistical policy.

History and authority

OIRA was established by the Paperwork Reduction Act of 1980, initially to review agency paperwork burdens. Its regulatory role expanded dramatically under Executive Order 12291 (1981) of President Ronald Reagan, which required agencies to submit major rules to OMB for cost-benefit review, and under Executive Order 12866 (1993) of President Bill Clinton, which remains the basic framework: OIRA reviews "significant regulatory actions", particularly economically significant rules with an annual effect of $100 million or more (a threshold later raised), before they are published.

Functions

OIRA reviews draft proposed and final rules for consistency with presidential priorities, principles of cost-benefit analysis set out in OMB Circular A-4, and proper consideration of alternatives. Its Administrator — sometimes nicknamed the "regulatory czar" — is appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate. Notable administrators include Cass Sunstein, who served under President Barack Obama and wrote extensively about the office's role.

Criticism

Supporters credit OIRA with coordinating policy and preventing unjustified burdens; critics argue its review process can delay or weaken public health, safety, and environmental rules and operates with limited transparency.
