Oxyrhynchus Papyri

The Oxyrhynchus Papyri are a vast body of manuscripts — well over half a million fragments — recovered from the rubbish dumps of the ancient Egyptian city of Oxyrhynchus (modern el-Bahnasa), some 160 km south-west of Cairo. Preserved by Egypt's dry climate for up to two thousand years, they range from lost masterpieces of Greek literature to tax receipts, private letters and magical spells, and constitute the largest and most important single find of papyri ever made.

Discovery and publication

Excavations for the Egypt Exploration Society began in the winter of 1896–97 under two young Oxford scholars, Bernard Grenfell and Arthur Hunt, who realised within days that the ancient mounds were stuffed with discarded papyrus. Grenfell and Hunt, and later their successors, shipped the material to Oxford, where the edition The Oxyrhynchus Papyri has been appearing since 1898; dozens of volumes have been published, yet only a small fraction of the collection has been edited. The project is now based at the Sackler Library, Oxford, and volunteers in the Ancient Lives project have helped transcribe fragments online.

Contents

By chance of survival, the dumps preserved works that had vanished elsewhere: substantial fragments of Sappho, Pindar's Paeans, the lost plays of Menander, Sophocles' satyr play Ichneutae, Bacchylides, and the Hellenica Oxyrhynchia, an anonymous Greek history. The biblical and early Christian material includes early fragments of the Gospels, the sayings-collection known as the Gospel of Thomas in Greek, and apocryphal gospels. Most of the papyri, however, are documentary: census returns, wills, marriage contracts, tax registers, petitions and letters that illuminate everyday life, administration and literacy in Roman and late antique Egypt.

Significance

The papyri revolutionised classical scholarship by restoring lost authors, transformed knowledge of the textual transmission of the New Testament, and — in their mundane majority — founded the modern discipline of papyrology, in which scholars reconstruct ancient society from its paperwork. Famous single pieces include P.Oxy. 1 (the first Sayings of Jesus), P.Oxy. 657, and the tiny 3rd-century fragment of Revelation known as P.Oxy. 4499, whose reading of the Number of the Beast as 616 attracted worldwide press attention.
