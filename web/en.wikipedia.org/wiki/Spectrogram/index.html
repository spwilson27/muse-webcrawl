Spectrogram

A spectrogram is a visual representation of the spectrum of frequencies of a signal as it varies with time. When applied to an audio signal, spectrograms are sometimes called sonographs, voiceprints, or voicegrams. When the data are represented in a 3D plot they may be called waterfall displays.

Format

A common format places time on the horizontal axis and frequency on the vertical axis, with the amplitude or energy of each frequency component at each instant shown by colour or intensity. Alternatively, a three-dimensional surface can plot amplitude as height. Spectrograms are usually computed from the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) of the signal, or from a bank of band-pass filters.

Parameters and trade-offs

The choice of analysis-window length faces a fundamental time–frequency trade-off: longer windows give finer frequency resolution but poorer time resolution, and vice versa. Wideband spectrograms (short windows) resolve individual pitch pulses in speech, while narrowband spectrograms (long windows) resolve harmonics.

Applications

Spectrograms are widely used in speech processing and phonetics (where "spectrogram reading" was developed at Bell Telephone Laboratories in the 1940s), music analysis, animal vocalization research, seismology, radar and sonar, and machine listening. Logarithmic frequency scales, such as the mel scale, are often used for audio to reflect human pitch perception.
