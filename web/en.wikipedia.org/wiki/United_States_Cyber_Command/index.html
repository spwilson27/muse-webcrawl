United States Cyber Command (USCYBERCOM) is one of the eleven unified combatant commands of the United States Department of Defense, responsible for military operations in cyberspace. It directs, synchronises and coordinates cyberspace planning and operations to defend US interests, conducts offensive and defensive cyberspace operations, and works to secure the Department of Defense Information Network. The command is headquartered at Fort Meade, Maryland, alongside the National Security Agency, with which it has historically shared a dual-hatted commander.

History

Cyber Command was created in 2009 by Secretary of Defense Robert Gates and reached full operational capability on 31 October 2010 under General Keith Alexander, who was simultaneously Director of the NSA. Its mission force, the Cyber Mission Force of 133 teams, reached full operational capability in 2018. On 4 May 2018 USCYBERCOM was elevated to a full unified combatant command. The command has publicly acknowledged operations including the campaign against the Islamic State's media and communications (Joint Task Force Ares) and measures to defend US elections against foreign interference from 2018 onward; its doctrine of "persistent engagement" and "defend forward" calls for contesting adversaries in cyberspace below the level of armed conflict.

Organisation

Each armed service provides a component command: Army Cyber Command, Fleet Cyber Command/Tenth Fleet, Air Force Cyber (Sixteenth Air Force), Marine Corps Forces Cyberspace Command, and Coast Guard Cyber Command. The Cyber National Mission Force, based at Fort Meade, is tasked with defending the nation against significant cyber threats.
