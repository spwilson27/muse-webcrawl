Nova music festival massacre

The Nova music festival massacre was a mass killing that took place on 7 October 2023 during the Hamas-led attack on Israel that opened the Gaza war. Gunmen attacked the Supernova Sukkot Gathering, an open-air electronic music festival held in the Negev desert near Kibbutz Re'im, close to the Gaza border. Of roughly 3,500 to 4,000 attendees and staff, 378 people were killed — 334 of them civilians — making it the largest single mass killing of the 7 October attacks and the deadliest concert attack in history. Dozens of festival-goers were taken hostage into the Gaza Strip.

Background

The Supernova festival was an outdoor trance music event organised by the Nova production company, timed to coincide with the Jewish holiday of Sukkot and the weekend of Simchat Torah. The site, in open farmland about 5 km (3 mi) from the Gaza barrier, had been moved at short notice from a location further south. Attendees were mostly young adults, many of them fans of psytrance music; international visitors were also present.

The attack

At about 6:29 a.m., as a rocket barrage from Gaza began, sirens sounded and the music stopped. Festival-goers initially moved toward their cars as rockets fell. Militants arrived by vehicle, motorcycle, and powered paraglider, and began shooting at fleeing crowds. Many attendees were killed in their cars on Route 232 and at nearby road junctions, where gunmen ambushed traffic; others were killed hiding in portable toilets, rubbish skips, orchards, and roadside bomb shelters, some of which were attacked with grenades.

Israeli police officers and armed security at the site fought the attackers, and many were killed. Survivors described hiding for hours in bushes and fields until Israeli forces secured the area in the afternoon. Forty-four of the dead at the festival were police and soldiers. Seventeen attendees were abducted to Gaza, including several who later became prominent hostage cases; some were subsequently released in exchanges or killed in captivity.

Aftermath

The site became one of the most recognisable symbols of the 7 October attacks. Memorials of photographs of the victims on poles were erected at the grounds, and the area has been visited by dignitaries and the public. The attack was investigated by Israeli authorities and journalists; disputes arose over the timeline of the military response and over friendly-fire incidents, with later reporting indicating that some festival-goers may have been killed by an Israeli combat helicopter firing at a vehicle near Re'im. Testimony collections, documentary films, and exhibitions about the festival-goers have been produced internationally.
