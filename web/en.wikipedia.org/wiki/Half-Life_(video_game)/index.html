Half-Life is a 1998 first-person shooter game developed by Valve and published by Sierra Studios for Windows. It was Valve's debut product. Players assume the role of Gordon Freeman, a theoretical physicist at the Black Mesa Research Facility, who must fight his way out after an experiment opens a portal to an alien dimension, triggering an invasion.

Gameplay and design

Rather than using cutscenes, Half-Life unfolds entirely in real time from the player's viewpoint, using scripted sequences and environmental storytelling. It was praised for its enemy artificial intelligence, level design and immersive narrative, and it ran on the GoldSrc engine, a heavily modified version of id Software's Quake engine.

Reception and legacy

The game won over fifty "Game of the Year" awards and is widely regarded as one of the most influential first-person shooters ever made. Its modding scene produced major titles such as Counter-Strike and Team Fortress Classic. Expansions Opposing Force (1999) and Blue Shift (2001) followed, along with the sequel Half-Life 2 (2004). A fan-made remake, Black Mesa, was completed in 2020 with Valve's approval.
