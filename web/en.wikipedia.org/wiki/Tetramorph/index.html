Tetramorph

Tetramorph
Four creaturesMan (angel), lion, ox, eagle
SourcesEzekiel 1; Revelation 4
Later associationThe four Evangelists

A tetramorph is a symbolic arrangement of four differing elements, most famously the four "living creatures" of the Book of Ezekiel and the Book of Revelation: a man (or angel), a lion, an ox, and an eagle. In Ezekiel's vision each creature has four faces — human, lion, ox, and eagle — and four wings, attending the throne-chariot (merkabah) of God.

Biblical sources

Ezekiel 1 describes the creatures emerging from a storm cloud beside the river Chebar during the Babylonian exile. Ezekiel 10 identifies them as cherubim. In Revelation 4, four creatures with six wings and covered in eyes stand around the throne, crying "Holy, holy, holy", and each has a single distinct face: lion, calf, man, and eagle.

The four Evangelists

From the second century, Church Fathers such as Irenaeus and later Jerome assigned the creatures to the four Evangelists: Matthew the winged man, Mark the lion, Luke the ox, and John the eagle. This scheme became one of the most common motifs of medieval Christian art, appearing in illuminated manuscripts such as the Book of Kells and in the tympana of Romanesque and Gothic churches.

Symbolism and antecedents

The four creatures have been linked to the four fixed signs of the zodiac (Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius), the four elements, and older Near Eastern imagery of composite guardian beings such as the Babylonian lamassu. In Byzantine and Orthodox iconography the tetramorph circles the Christ in Majesty.
