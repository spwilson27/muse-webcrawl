Parker Institute for Cancer Immunotherapy

The institute coordinates immunotherapy research across leading US cancer centres.

The Parker Institute for Cancer Immunotherapy (PICI) is a nonprofit research institute founded in 2016 by Sean Parker, the entrepreneur and philanthropist who co-founded Napster and served as the first president of Facebook. Parker committed an initial $250 million grant to establish the institute, then described as the largest single donation ever made to cancer immunotherapy.

Model

The institute brings together researchers at major cancer centres — including Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, MD Anderson Cancer Center, Stanford Medicine, UCLA, UCSF and the University of Pennsylvania — under an agreement to share data, intellectual property and clinical trial infrastructure. The model aims to remove bureaucratic and competitive barriers that slow collaborative science, allowing the network to run coordinated trials and move discoveries quickly from the laboratory to patients.

Research focus

PICI supports work on checkpoint inhibitors, personalised cancer vaccines based on neoantigens, cell therapies such as CAR-T cells, and the tumour microenvironment. It also funds early-career investigators and has helped launch clinical trials testing combinations of immunotherapies.
