Whittling

Whittling is the carving of wood with a knife, typically using only a small hand knife and a piece of soft wood, without the chisels, gouges, and mallets of formal woodcarving. It is one of the oldest forms of woodworking and is often practiced as a pastime, producing figures, utensils, toys, and decorative objects.

Technique

The basic cuts include the push cut, the pull (paring) cut, and the stop cut, which controls where a cut ends. Soft, fine-grained woods such as basswood, pine, and balsa are preferred by beginners because they cut cleanly and hold detail. Whittling knives have short, stout blades, and safety practice emphasizes cutting away from the body and keeping the blade sharp — a dull blade requires more force and is more likely to slip.

Culture

Whittling has deep roots in folk traditions, including Scandinavian flat-plane carving and the folk art of rural America, and remains a common scouting and leisure activity.
