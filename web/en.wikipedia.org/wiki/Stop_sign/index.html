Stop sign

A stop sign is a traffic sign designed to notify drivers that they must come to a complete stop and make sure the intersection or road ahead is clear of vehicles and pedestrians before continuing past the sign. More than any other traffic sign, its universally recognisable shape — a red octagon with the word "STOP" in white — allows drivers to identify it even in poor visibility or when the sign is snow-covered or seen from behind.

The first stop signs appeared in Detroit, Michigan, in 1915; they were originally square and yellow with black lettering. The octagonal shape was adopted in the United States in 1923 so that police officers could identify the sign at night by shape alone, and the red background became standard in 1954 after fade-resistant red porcelain enamel became available. The Vienna Convention on Road Signs and Signals prescribes the octagonal sign or a circular alternative.

Most countries use the word "STOP" in English or the local language. Stop signs are placed at intersections where traffic control requires it; an all-way stop has stop signs in every direction. Running a stop sign is a traffic offence in most jurisdictions.
