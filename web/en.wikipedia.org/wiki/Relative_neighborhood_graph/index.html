Relative neighborhood graph

FieldComputational geometry
Introduced1982 (Toussaint)
TypeGeometric graph

The relative neighborhood graph (RNG) is a geometric graph defined on a set of points in the plane or in higher-dimensional Euclidean space. Two points p and q are connected by an edge in the relative neighborhood graph if and only if there is no third point that is closer to both p and q than they are to each other.

Definition

Formally, for points p and q, the lune formed by the intersection of the two disks centered at p and q, each with radius equal to the distance between p and q, must contain no other point of the set for the edge (p, q) to exist. The relative neighborhood graph was introduced by Godfried Toussaint in 1980–1982 in connection with pattern recognition problems.

Properties

The relative neighborhood graph is a subgraph of the Delaunay triangulation and a supergraph of the Euclidean minimum spanning tree. It can be constructed in O(n log n) time in the plane. In higher dimensions it may be defined analogously using intersections of balls.

Applications

Relative neighborhood graphs are used in geographic information systems, wireless sensor networking, image analysis, and clustering, where they provide a sparse yet connected representation of proximity relationships among points.
