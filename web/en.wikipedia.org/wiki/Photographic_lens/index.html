Photographic lens

A photographic lens gathers and focuses light onto film or a sensor.

A photographic lens or camera lens is an optical lens or assembly of lenses used in conjunction with a camera body and mechanism to make images of objects either on photographic film or on an electronic image sensor. A lens is defined by properties including its focal length — usually expressed in millimetres — which determines the angle of view and magnification, and its maximum aperture (f-number), which determines light-gathering ability and depth of field.

Lens types

Lenses are classed by focal length relative to the image format: wide-angle lenses capture a broad field of view; "normal" lenses approximate the perspective of human vision; telephoto lenses magnify distant subjects; and macro lenses focus at very close range. A prime lens has a fixed focal length, while a zoom lens varies it. Specialised designs include fisheye lenses, tilt–shift lenses for perspective control, and catadioptric (mirror) lenses.

Construction

Modern lenses combine multiple glass elements in groups to correct aberrations such as chromatic aberration, spherical aberration, and distortion. Mechanisms control focus (manual or autofocus) and aperture via an iris diaphragm; many lenses incorporate optical image stabilisation. Lens mounts standardise the mechanical and electronic interface between lens and camera, and each major camera maker uses its own mount system.
