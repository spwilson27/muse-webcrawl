Vela Supernova Remnant

The Vela supernova remnant is a supernova remnant in the southern constellation Vela. Its source supernova exploded approximately 11,000–12,300 years ago at a distance of about 800 light-years, making it one of the closest known supernova remnants to Earth. In the sky it spans roughly 8 degrees, overlapping with the larger and more distant Gum Nebula.

The Vela Pulsar

At the centre of the remnant lies the Vela Pulsar (PSR B0833-45), a neutron star spinning about 11 times per second, discovered in 1968. The association of the pulsar with the remnant was one of the first direct proofs that supernovae give birth to neutron stars. The pulsar is a bright source of radio, optical, X-ray and gamma-ray pulses, and is surrounded by a pulsar wind nebula.

Structure

The remnant consists of tangled filaments of glowing gas visible in optical images and a large soft X-ray shell. Embedded within it is the smaller, younger remnant Vela Junior (RX J0852.0−4622), discovered in 1998, in front of the main Vela remnant. The fine optical filaments of the Vela remnant, imaged dramatically by Anglo-Australian Observatory photographs, trace shock waves still expanding into the interstellar medium.
