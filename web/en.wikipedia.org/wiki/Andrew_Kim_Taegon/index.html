Andrew Kim Taegon

Andrew Kim Taegon (Korean: 김대건 안드레아, August 21, 1821 – September 16, 1846) was the first Korean-born Catholic priest and is the patron saint of Korean clergy. He was martyred during the Joseon dynasty's persecution of Catholics and was canonized by Pope John Paul II in 1984 together with 102 other Korean martyrs.

Early life

Kim Taegon was born in Solmoe, in present-day Dangjin, South Chungcheong Province, into a family of yangban (aristocratic) converts to Catholicism. His great-grandfather and his father, Ignatius Kim Je-jun, were both martyred for the faith; his father was executed in the Gihae Persecution of 1839 and was also canonized in 1984. The family lived in poverty because their property was confiscated after their conversion.

Studies and ordination

At age fifteen, Kim was selected by French missionaries of the Paris Foreign Missions Society as a seminarian and traveled to Macau, then a Portuguese colony, to study for the priesthood. He also studied at Lolomboy, Bulacan, in the Philippines, where a statue of him now stands. After years of study interrupted by travel to Manchuria and Shanghai as an interpreter for French missionaries, he was ordained a priest on August 17, 1845, in Shanghai by Bishop Jean Joseph Ferréol — the first native Korean to be ordained.

Return to Korea and martyrdom

Kim returned secretly to Korea to organize the entry of French missionaries by sea, preparing charts of the coast and contacting local Christians. In 1846 he was arrested while preparing a sea route for the missionaries, after attempting to use a Chinese junk to reach Shanghai. He was imprisoned in Seoul, interrogated and tortured, and beheaded at Saenamteo on the Han River on September 16, 1846, at the age of twenty-five.

Veneration

Pope Pius XI beatified Kim Taegon in 1925, and Pope John Paul II canonized him with 102 other Korean martyrs in Seoul on May 6, 1984, during the first papal visit to Korea. His feast day is September 20, shared with Paul Chong Hasang and the Korean Martyrs. His birthplace at Solmoe is a major pilgrimage site, and in 2021 UNESCO recognized his bicentenary among its anniversary commemorations.
