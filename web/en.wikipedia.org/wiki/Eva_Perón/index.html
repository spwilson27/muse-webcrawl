María Eva Duarte de Perón (7 May 1919 – 26 July 1952), better known as Eva Perón or by the diminutive Evita, was an Argentine politician, activist, actress, and philanthropist who served as First Lady of Argentina from June 1946 until her death in July 1952, as the wife of President Juan Perón. She was a central figure of Peronism, championing labour rights and women's suffrage, and she remains one of the most iconic and controversial figures in Argentine history.

Early life and acting career

Eva Duarte was born in the rural village of Los Toldos in Buenos Aires Province, the youngest of five children. Her father, Juan Duarte, died when she was a child, and the family, of modest means and burdened by the stigma of her parents' informal union, struggled financially. At fifteen she travelled to Buenos Aires to pursue a career on the stage and radio. She gradually found work in theatre and film, and by the early 1940s she had become a successful radio actress, co-owning a radio company and earning a comfortable income, with a popular series of radio dramas portraying famous women of history.

Marriage and rise to prominence

She met Colonel Juan Perón in January 1944 at a charity gala for victims of the San Juan earthquake, and the two soon became a couple. When Perón was arrested by rival officers in October 1945, mass demonstrations by workers — remembered as Loyalty Day, 17 October — forced his release; Eva's role in organising the mobilisation has been debated, but she was by then a prominent public supporter. The couple married later that year, and Perón won the presidential election of February 1946.

First Lady

As First Lady, Evita played an unprecedentedly active role. Through the Ministry of Labour she built ties with the trade unions, the backbone of Peronist support. She campaigned successfully for women's suffrage, achieved with the law of September 1947, and founded the Female Peronist Party in 1949, which organised women voters on a large scale. Her Eva Perón Foundation, established in 1948, distributed food, clothing, medicine, and housing to the poor on a vast scale, funded by donations, lottery proceeds, and union contributions; it built hospitals, schools, and homes for children and the elderly, though its finances were never publicly audited and critics charged it was a vehicle for patronage.

In 1947 she undertook the celebrated "Rainbow Tour" of Europe, visiting Spain, Italy, France, and other countries, an effort to improve Argentina's international image. In 1951 the organised labour movement pushed for her to stand as vice-presidential candidate on Perón's re-election ticket; amid military opposition and her own failing health, she publicly renounced the candidacy on 31 August 1951, in a speech remembered as the Renunciamiento ("Renunciation").

Death and legacy

Evita died of cervical cancer on 26 July 1952, aged 33. The government declared national mourning; enormous crowds filled the streets of Buenos Aires for her funeral, and her embalmed body lay in state. After the 1955 coup that overthrew Perón, the military government hid her body, which was eventually buried in Milan under a false name; it was returned to Perón in Spain in 1971 and finally repatriated to Argentina, where it now rests in the Duarte family tomb in La Recoleta Cemetery.

Adored by many of Argentina's poor as "Santa Evita" and loathed by much of the elite and military, she has inspired an enormous cultural legacy, including the Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice musical Evita (1976) and its 1996 film adaptation starring Madonna. In 2012 her image appeared on the Argentine 100-peso note, the first woman so honoured on Argentine currency.
