Linoleic acid

FormulaC18H32O2
Molar mass280.45 g/mol
AppearanceColourless oil
TypeOmega-6 polyunsaturated fatty acid

Linoleic acid (LA) is an organic compound with the formula COOH(CH2)7CH=CHCH2CH=CH(CH2)4CH3. Both alkene groups are cis. It is a fatty acid sometimes denoted 18:2 (n-6) or 18:2 cis-9,12. A linoleate is a salt or ester of this acid.

Biological role

Linoleic acid is an essential fatty acid: humans and other animals must ingest it because the body requires it for various biological processes but cannot synthesise it from other food components. It is a precursor of arachidonic acid and thus of some prostaglandins, leukotrienes and thromboxanes, and it is a component of membrane lipids and of the ceramides that waterproof the skin.

Sources

Linoleic acid is abundant in many vegetable oils, especially safflower, sunflower, corn and soybean oils, and accounts for over half the fatty acids in many seed oils. Its name derives from the Greek linon (flax) and Latin oleum (oil).

Industrial use

Linoleic acid is used in making quick-drying oils, paints and varnishes, exploiting its ready reaction with oxygen in air (drying). It is also used in soaps, emulsifiers and beauty products.
