The 8×50mmR Lebel (8 mm Lebel) was the world's first smokeless powder rifle cartridge, adopted by France in 1886 for the Lebel Model 1886 rifle. Developed around Paul Vieille's Poudre B smokeless propellant, it revolutionized military small arms: its small-caliber, high-velocity bullet made obsolete the large black-powder cartridges of the previous generation and triggered an international race to re-equip with magazine rifles.

Design

The cartridge is a rimmed, bottlenecked design with an unusually prominent taper and thick rim, giving it a distinctive profile. The original Balle M loading used a 15 g (231 grain) round-nosed cupro-nickel-jacketed bullet at about 628 m/s. In 1898 France introduced the Balle D, a solid brass (latten) spitzer boat-tail bullet of 12.8 g at roughly 701 m/s — the first spitzer service bullet adopted by any army — which greatly extended effective range. A later Balle N loading (1932) with a heavier bullet served primarily in machine guns.

Service

The 8 mm Lebel served in the Lebel and Berthier rifles, the Chauchat light machine gun, and the Hotchkiss and St. Étienne machine guns through both world wars. Its heavily tapered case and rim caused feeding complications in automatic weapons, and its pointed Balle D bullet posed a hazard in the Lebel's tubular magazine — addressed by a groove in the case head and a deep primer pocket design — but the cartridge remained in production for over half a century. It was progressively replaced by the rimless 7.5×54mm French after 1929.
