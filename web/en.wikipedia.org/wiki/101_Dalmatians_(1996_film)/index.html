101 Dalmatians (1996 film)

101 Dalmatians is a 1996 American family comedy film produced by Walt Disney Pictures. It is a live-action remake of Disney's 1961 animated film of the same name, itself adapted from Dodie Smith's 1956 novel The Hundred and One Dalmatians. The film was directed by Stephen Herek, written and co-produced by John Hughes, and stars Glenn Close as Cruella de Vil, with Jeff Daniels, Joely Richardson and Joan Plowright.

Plot

Video game designer Roger Dearly and fashion designer Anita Campbell fall in love after their Dalmatians, Pongo and Perdy, meet in a London park; the dogs also pair off and Perdy gives birth to fifteen puppies. Anita's flamboyant boss, Cruella de Vil, obsessed with a coat made of Dalmatian fur, has the puppies stolen by her henchmen Jasper and Horace when Roger and Anita refuse to sell them. With the help of animals across the country, relayed through the "twilight barking", Pongo and Perdy rescue their puppies — and 84 others Cruella has bought or stolen — and foil her plans. Cruella and her accomplices are arrested, and Roger and Anita adopt all 101 Dalmatians, moving to a country estate.

Production and reception

Unlike the animated original, the animal characters do not speak. Hundreds of Dalmatian puppies were used during filming, and demand for the breed surged afterwards, to the concern of animal welfare groups. Glenn Close's performance as Cruella de Vil was widely praised and earned her a Golden Globe nomination for Best Actress in a Comedy or Musical. The film was a major commercial success and was followed by a sequel, 102 Dalmatians (2000), and later by the prequel Cruella (2021), starring Emma Stone.
