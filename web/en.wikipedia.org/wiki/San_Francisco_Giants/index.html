The San Francisco Giants are an American professional baseball team based in San Francisco, California. They compete in Major League Baseball as a member of the National League West Division. Founded in 1883 as the New York Gothams and later known as the New York Giants, the franchise moved to San Francisco in 1958, alongside the Dodgers' move to Los Angeles, bringing top-level baseball to the West Coast.

History in New York

The Giants were a powerhouse of the early National League under manager John McGraw, winning the World Series in 1905, 1921 and 1922. Later New York highlights included Carl Hubbell's screwball, Mel Ott's home runs, Bobby Thomson's "Shot Heard 'Round the World" in 1951, and the 1954 championship team featuring Willie Mays, whose over-the-shoulder catch in that year's Series became one of baseball's most famous plays.

San Francisco era

The club played at Candlestick Park from 1960 before moving to its waterfront ballpark (now Oracle Park) in 2000. Stars of the San Francisco era include Willie McCovey, Juan Marichal, Barry Bonds — who set the single-season (73, in 2001) and career (762) home run records in a Giants uniform — and Buster Posey. The Giants ended a 56-year championship drought by winning the World Series in 2010, then repeated in 2012 and 2014, an "even-year" run led by manager Bruce Bochy and pitchers Tim Lincecum and Madison Bumgarner.

Rivalries

The Giants' rivalry with the Los Angeles Dodgers, carried west from New York, remains among the fiercest in American sport. They also share a cross-bay rivalry with the Oakland Athletics, highlighted by the earthquake-interrupted 1989 "Bay Bridge" World Series.
