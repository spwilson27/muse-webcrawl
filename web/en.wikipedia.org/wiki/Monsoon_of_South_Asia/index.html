Monsoon of South Asia

The monsoon of South Asia is among several globally distributed systems of monsoons. It affects the Indian subcontinent, where it is one of the oldest and most anticipated weather phenomena and an economically important pattern that affects the agriculture, economy, and ecology of India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, and neighbouring regions. The word monsoon derives from the Arabic mausim ("season"), via Portuguese monção.

Mechanism

The monsoon is driven by the differential heating of land and ocean. In summer, the intense heating of the Tibetan Plateau and the Indian landmass creates low pressure that draws moist air from the Indian Ocean; the Intertropical Convergence Zone shifts north, and the cross-equatorial Somali jet streams moisture toward the subcontinent. Two branches — the Arabian Sea branch and the Bay of Bengal branch — bring rain to the west coast, the northeast, and the Indo-Gangetic plain. In winter the flow reverses: dry northeasterly winds dominate, though the retreating (northeast) monsoon brings significant rain to southeastern India, especially Tamil Nadu.

Onset and progression

The southwest monsoon typically "breaks" over Kerala around 1 June and advances north and west to cover the whole country by mid-July. Orographic lifting over the Western Ghats and the Khasi Hills produces some of the heaviest rainfall on Earth; Mawsynram and Cherrapunji in Meghalaya average more than 11 metres of rain a year. Withdrawal begins from the northwest in September and completes by October.

Importance and variability

Roughly 70–80% of India's annual rainfall falls in the southwest monsoon season, irrigating rain-fed crops that sustain a large share of the population. Year-to-year variability, influenced by phenomena such as ENSO and the Indian Ocean Dipole, alternates droughts and floods with major economic consequences. Weak monsoons have contributed historically to famines; modern forecasting by the India Meteorological Department is a closely watched national event. Climate change is expected to increase the variability and intensity of monsoon rainfall.
