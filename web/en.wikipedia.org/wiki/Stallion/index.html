Stallion

A stallion is a male horse that has not been gelded (castrated). Stallions follow the conformation and phenotype of their breed, but within that standard, the presence of testosterone gives stallions a thicker, "cresty" neck and a somewhat more muscular build than mares or geldings. A young male horse is a colt; a stallion used for breeding may also be called a stud.

An Arabian stallion

Temperament and management

Stallions are often more aggressive and self-assured than geldings, reflecting their natural role as herd defenders, and they require skilled handling in domestication. Properly trained stallions can perform in many disciplines, including racing and Olympic equestrian sport, but they are generally considered unsuitable mounts for inexperienced riders. Stallions are commonly kept separated from mares and other stallions to prevent fighting and unplanned breeding, although management systems vary by culture and era.

Breeding

Only a small proportion of male horses are kept entire as breeding stallions; most are gelded to make them easier to manage. Breeding stallions are selected for pedigree, conformation, athletic performance and the quality of their offspring. In Thoroughbred racing, famous stallions command very high stud fees and can sire hundreds of foals during a career at stud, in some cases shuttling between hemispheres to cover mares in both breeding seasons.

In the wild

In feral herds, a dominant stallion defends a band of mares against rival males, while surplus young males form bachelor groups until they are strong enough to challenge for mares of their own.
