The Bombay Presidency was an administrative subdivision (province) of British India, with its capital in the city of Bombay. It was one of the three original presidencies of the East India Company, alongside the Madras Presidency and the Bengal Presidency, and later became one of the major provinces of the British Raj.


Map of the Bombay Presidency, c. 1909

History

The first English settlement in western India was a factory at Surat, established in 1612–1618 following the Battle of Swally. In 1661 the island of Bombay was acquired by the English Crown as part of the dowry of Catherine of Braganza on her marriage to Charles II, and in 1668 it was leased to the East India Company for £10 a year. The Company's headquarters were moved from Surat to Bombay in 1687, and Bombay grew from a modest trading post into the commercial hub of western India.

Over the eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, the presidency expanded dramatically through wars with the Maratha Empire, culminating in the Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–1818), after which much of the Deccan came under Bombay's administration. Sindh was annexed in 1843 and administered as part of the presidency until it became a separate province in 1936. Aden, in modern Yemen, was also administered from Bombay between 1839 and 1932.

Geography and administration

At its greatest extent the presidency stretched along the western coast of India from Sindh in the north to North Kanara in the south, and inland across the northern Deccan. It comprised both directly ruled British districts and numerous princely states under British paramountcy, including Baroda, Kolhapur, and the states of Kathiawar. The province was governed by a Governor appointed from London, assisted by an executive council and, after the constitutional reforms of 1919 and 1935, an elected provincial legislature.

Economy and society

Bombay city became a great port and industrial centre, famous for its cotton mills, its docks, and the opium and cotton trades. The presidency was noted for its educational institutions, including the University of Bombay (founded 1857), and for the prominence of merchant communities such as the Parsis, Gujaratis, and Marwaris. It was also an early centre of the Indian nationalist movement.

After independence

With the independence and partition of India in 1947, the Bombay Presidency became Bombay State of the Dominion of India. In 1960 the state was divided along linguistic lines into Maharashtra and Gujarat.
