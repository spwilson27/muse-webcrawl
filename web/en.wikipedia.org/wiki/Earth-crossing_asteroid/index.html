Earth-crossing asteroid

An Earth-crossing asteroid is a near-Earth asteroid whose orbit crosses that of Earth as observed from the ecliptic pole. Such objects have a semi-major axis and an orbital path that intersect Earth's orbit, meaning their perihelion distance is less than Earth's aphelion distance (1.017 AU) and their aphelion distance is greater than Earth's perihelion distance (0.983 AU).

Earth-crossers are subdivided into groups including the Aten asteroids (with semi-major axes smaller than Earth's), the Apollo asteroids (semi-major axes larger than Earth's), and the Amor asteroids, which approach but do not strictly cross Earth's orbit. Because their orbits cross Earth's, these asteroids are monitored as potential impact hazards, and the largest are catalogued by programs such as Spaceguard.

Notable examples

  • 1566 Icarus
  • 1862 Apollo
  • 4179 Toutatis
  • 99942 Apophis
