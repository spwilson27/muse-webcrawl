Civil war era in Norway

The civil war era in Norway (Norwegian: borgerkrigstiden) is the period of Norwegian history from about 1130 to 1240, during which rival kings and pretenders repeatedly fought for the throne. The era began with the death of King Sigurd the Crusader in 1130 and ended with the accession of Haakon IV and the defeat of the last rival claimant, Duke Skule Bårdsson, in 1240.

Causes

Norwegian succession customs allowed all of a king's sons — legitimate or not — to share the kingship, which made disputed successions almost routine. Ambitious pretenders, often claiming (sometimes dubiously) to be royal sons, could raise support from regional factions, churchmen and magnates. The Church, growing in wealth and organisation, became a major political player.

Main phases

The first phase saw wars among the sons and grandsons of Harald Gille. King Sverre Sigurdsson, a priest from the Faroes who arrived in 1177 claiming royal birth, seized power with his Birkebeiner ("birch-legs") party and defeated the church-backed opposition, though he was excommunicated for his defiance of the clergy. His opponents, the Bagler, supported by the bishops, kept the war going into the reign of Sverre's son Haakon III and grandson Haakon IV. The famous ski flight that saved the infant Haakon IV from the Bagler in 1206 is a national legend, commemorated by the Birkebeinerrennet ski race. After battles and settlements in the 1210s–1230s, Haakon IV eliminated Skule in 1240, beginning a long period of stability and the Norwegian "golden age" of the high Middle Ages.
