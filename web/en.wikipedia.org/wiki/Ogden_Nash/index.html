Frederic Ogden Nash (August 19, 1902 – May 19, 1971) was an American poet well known for his light verse, of which he wrote more than 500 pieces. With his unconventional rhyming schemes, he was declared the country's best-known producer of humorous poetry.

Life and career

Nash was born in Rye, New York, and spent much of his life in Baltimore, Maryland. He briefly attended Harvard University before working in advertising and publishing; he joined the staff of The New Yorker in 1932, where many of his poems appeared. His first collection, Hard Lines (1931), was an immediate success.

Style

Nash's verse is famous for whimsical, deliberately irregular lines, playful misspellings, and surprising rhymes, as in "Candy / Is dandy / But liquor / Is quicker." He wrote on everyday subjects — animals, children, husbands and wives, and modern life — and also produced lyrics for the musical One Touch of Venus (1943) and verses for Saint-Saëns's The Carnival of the Animals.
