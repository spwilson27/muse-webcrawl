Studio Aalto

Studio Aalto's amphitheatre-like courtyard

The Studio Aalto (Finnish: Atelier Aalto) is the office building designed by the Finnish architect Alvar Aalto as the premises of his architectural practice, located at Tiilimäki 20 in the Munkkiniemi district of Helsinki, not far from Aalto's own house. Aalto designed the studio in 1954 and it was completed in 1955; an extension followed in 1962–63.

Design

The white-rendered building wraps around a sheltered courtyard shaped like a small amphitheatre, whose stepped, grassy slope Aalto used for outdoor presentations and staff gatherings. Aalto remarked that a studio must have its own yard: the arrangement closes the building to the street while opening workspaces to daylight and the garden. The main drafting room is lit by large windows and a distinctive arrangement of artificial lighting; the building also contains a meeting room, offices, and a taverna-style dining space decorated with Aalto furniture and lamps produced by Artek. The curving, free-form wall of the courtyard is a characteristic Aalto gesture, echoing the undulating forms of the Savoy Vase.

Later use

Aalto's practice continued in the building after his death in 1976 under his widow Elissa Aalto. Today the studio is administered by the Alvar Aalto Foundation and is open for guided tours; it remains a working architectural office for the foundation's building-protection and research staff, and together with the Aalto House it forms one of Finland's most visited modernist sites.
