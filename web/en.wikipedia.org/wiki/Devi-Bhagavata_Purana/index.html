Devi-Bhagavata Purana

Devi, the goddess as supreme reality in Shaktism

The Devi-Bhagavata Purana (Sanskrit: देवी भागवतपुराण), also known as the Devi Bhagavatam, is one of the major Puranas of Hinduism and the central scripture of the Shakta tradition. Composed in Sanskrit in twelve books (skandhas) and some 18,000 verses, it exalts the goddess Devi — identified with the goddess Bhuvanesvari and Durga — as the supreme being and source of all existence, including the gods Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva.

Contents

The text retells many myths familiar from other Puranas from a goddess-centred perspective, narrates the deeds of the goddess in her many forms, and includes cosmological, philosophical and devotional teaching. Its seventh book incorporates the Devi Gita, a dialogue in which the goddess reveals her universal form and teaches a synthesis of devotion, knowledge and yoga, modelled on the Bhagavad Gita.

Dating and status

Scholars generally date the work to roughly the 11th–12th centuries CE, though its traditions are older. The text itself claims the status of a Mahapurana ("great Purana"), a status debated with the Bhagavata Purana, which Vaishnavas regard as the fifth Mahapurana; the Devi-Bhagavata is revered above all by Shaktas, particularly in Bengal and Assam, and is recited during the Navratri festival.
