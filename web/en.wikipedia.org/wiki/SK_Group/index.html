SK Group (Korean: SK그룹) is a South Korean multinational manufacturing and services conglomerate (chaebol) headquartered in Seoul. It is one of South Korea's largest chaebols, alongside Samsung, Hyundai Motor Group and LG. The group's businesses span semiconductors, energy and chemicals, telecommunications, and an expanding portfolio in batteries, biopharmaceuticals and advanced materials.

History

The group traces its origins to 1953, when Chey Jong-hyon acquired the Sunkyong Textiles factory in Suwon in the aftermath of the Korean War. Under the name Sunkyong, the company grew from textiles into polyester film, petroleum refining and petrochemicals. In 1980 it acquired a controlling stake in Korea Oil Corporation, which became the cornerstone of its energy business, and in the 1990s it entered telecommunications by taking over Korea Mobile Telecommunications, the predecessor of SK Telecom.

The group adopted the name "SK" in 1997, during the Asian financial crisis, when it also restructured around its energy, telecom and trading arms. In 2012 SK acquired a controlling stake in Hynix Semiconductor, later renamed SK Hynix, which grew into one of the world's largest memory chip makers and a central asset of the group.

Businesses

Major affiliates include:

  • SK Hynix — DRAM and NAND flash memory semiconductors.
  • SK Innovation — refining, petrochemicals and lubricants; its battery arm was spun off as SK On in 2021.
  • SK Telecom — South Korea's largest mobile network operator.
  • SK Networks and SK Ecoplant — trading, construction and environmental services.
  • SK Biopharmaceuticals and SK Bioscience — drug development and vaccines.

Governance

The holding company is SK Inc. Chairman Chey Tae-won, son of the founder, has led the group since 1998. Since 2021 the group has promoted an ESG-driven strategy under the slogan of creating "social value" alongside economic value, with major investments in hydrogen, EV batteries and carbon reduction.
