Father of the House

Father of the House is a title in Westminster system parliaments bestowed on the member of a legislative body who has the longest continuous service. Typically it is a ceremonial honor with few formal duties, although in some jurisdictions the Father of the House presides during the election of a Speaker.

United Kingdom

In the House of Commons of the United Kingdom, the Father of the House is the MP with the longest unbroken service. The title dates from the early 19th century. Long-serving holders have included Winston Churchill, Bernard Weatherill, Peter Tapsell, Kenneth Clarke and Edward Leigh.

Other countries

Equivalent titles exist in Canada, Australia (Father of the House of Representatives / Senate), New Zealand, and many other Commonwealth parliaments. In the United States House of Representatives the analogous figure is the Dean of the House.
