Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions

In mathematical optimisation, the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions are first-order necessary conditions for a solution of a nonlinear programming problem to be optimal, generalising the method of Lagrange multipliers to problems with inequality constraints.

Statement

For a problem minimising f(x) subject to inequality constraints gi(x) ≤ 0 and equality constraints hj(x) = 0, the KKT conditions comprise stationarity of the Lagrangian, primal feasibility, dual feasibility (non-negative multipliers for the inequality constraints) and complementary slackness: each inequality multiplier times its constraint equals zero.

History

The conditions were first published in the 1939 master's thesis of William Karush and were later independently presented by Harold Kuhn and Albert Tucker in 1951. Under constraint qualifications such as LICQ or Slater's condition they are necessary for optimality, and for convex problems they are also sufficient.

References: Karush (1939); Kuhn & Tucker (1951); Boyd & Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization.
