El Niño (Spanish: [el ˈniɲo]) is the warm phase of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) and is associated with a band of warm ocean water that develops in the central and east-central equatorial Pacific, including the area off the Pacific coast of South America.

This page is a redirect-style entry in the crawl; the full article is at El Niño. El Niño events cause higher-than-average air pressure in the western Pacific and lower pressure in the eastern Pacific, weakening trade winds and shifting rainfall patterns worldwide.

Effects

El Niño brings wetter conditions to parts of the southern United States and Peru, drought to Indonesia and Australia, warmer winters in northwestern North America, and disrupted fisheries off South America as nutrient-rich upwelling weakens. Strong events occurred in 1982–83, 1997–98, and 2015–16.

Name

Peruvian fishermen named the warm current El Niño de Navidad ("the Christ Child") because it often appeared around Christmas.
