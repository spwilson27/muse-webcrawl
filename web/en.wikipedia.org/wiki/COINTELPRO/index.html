COINTELPRO (from Counter Intelligence Program) was a series of covert and often illegal projects conducted between 1956 and 1971 by the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation aimed at surveilling, infiltrating, discrediting, and disrupting American political organizations deemed subversive. Under director J. Edgar Hoover, the bureau authorised tactics including infiltration, psychological warfare, harassment via legal process, anonymous letters, and illegal surveillance.

Targets and methods

Programmes targeted the Communist Party USA (1956), the Socialist Workers Party (1961), white hate groups including the Ku Klux Klan (1964), "Black Nationalist–Hate Groups" (1967) — covering Martin Luther King Jr., the Nation of Islam, and later the Black Panther Party — and the New Left (1968). Documented operations included anonymous letters intended to break up marriages and provoke violence, the planting of hostile media stories, forged correspondence, IRS audits, and the FBI letter urging King to discredit himself before accepting the Nobel Peace Prize.

Exposure and aftermath

In March 1971, activists of the Citizens' Commission to Investigate the FBI burgled the FBI office in Media, Pennsylvania, and leaked documents revealing the programme's existence; Hoover ordered it terminated. The Church Committee (1975–76) investigated COINTELPRO alongside CIA and NSA programmes, concluding that the bureau had conducted a "secret war against those citizens it considers threats to the established order". The scandal led to reforms including the Levi guidelines restricting domestic intelligence investigations.
