Thoracic aorta

Thoracic aorta
SourceAortic arch (continuation)

LevelT4 to T12 vertebral levels

The thoracic aorta is the portion of the aorta located within the thorax. It begins at the lower border of the fourth thoracic vertebra, where it continues from the aortic arch, descends through the posterior mediastinum to the left of the vertebral column, and ends at the aortic hiatus of the diaphragm at the level of the twelfth thoracic vertebra, where it becomes the abdominal aorta.

Branches

The thoracic aorta gives off visceral branches — the bronchial, esophageal, mediastinal and pericardial arteries — and parietal branches, principally the paired posterior intercostal arteries that supply the intercostal spaces, and the subcostal and superior phrenic arteries.

Clinical significance

The thoracic aorta may be affected by aneurysm, dissection, and coarctation. Aortic dissection involving the descending (thoracic) aorta is classified as Stanford type B and is often managed medically or with endovascular stent-graft repair.
