Scuba set

A scuba set is a self-contained underwater breathing apparatus that supplies a diver with breathing gas at ambient pressure, independent of any surface supply. The name is an acronym for self-contained underwater breathing apparatus. The modern open-circuit set was developed in 1943 by Jacques-Yves Cousteau and Émile Gagnan as the Aqua-Lung, whose demand regulator made recreational and commercial diving practical.

Components

A typical recreational set comprises one or more high-pressure cylinders (usually compressed air at about 200–300 bar), a regulator with a first stage that reduces cylinder pressure and a second-stage demand valve at the mouthpiece, a pressure gauge, and a buoyancy compensator device (BCD) for controlling buoyancy. Alternatives include rebreathers, which recycle exhaled gas, scrubbing out carbon dioxide and adding oxygen, allowing longer, quieter dives.

Use

Scuba equipment is used in recreational diving, scientific and archaeological work, public-safety diving, and military operations. Training agencies certify divers to plan dives within decompression limits and to manage the risks of pressure, gas supply and the underwater environment.
