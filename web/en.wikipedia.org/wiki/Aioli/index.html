Aioli

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A mortar of golden aioli with raw garlic cloves and olive oil beside it]
A mortar of golden aioli with raw garlic cloves and olive oil beside it

Aioli (Provençal: alhòli; Catalan: allioli) is a sauce made of garlic and olive oil — its name literally means 'garlic and oil' — traditional to the northwestern Mediterranean coasts of Spain, France and Italy. In its purest form it is an emulsion pounded by hand in a mortar; many modern versions add egg yolk as an emulsifier, effectively making a garlic mayonnaise — a point purists dispute passionately.

Preparation and variants

Traditional allioli contains only garlic, salt and olive oil worked to a stiff emulsion; the emulsion is fragile and forms only with patience and good oil. French aioli typically includes egg yolk. It accompanies boiled vegetables, fish soups (with rouille, its saffron-and-chilli cousin, in Bouillabaisse), salt cod and grilled meats, and is the centrepiece of the Provençal aïoli garni, a feast of cod and vegetables around a bowl of the sauce.

Cultural notes

Garlic emulsions of this family are ancient around the Mediterranean; the sauce is a point of Catalan-Provençal identity, celebrated in village festivals where making it by hand remains a test of skill.
