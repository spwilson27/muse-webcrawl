Overview

Sexagesimal is the base-60 positional number system developed by the Sumerians in the third millennium BC and perfected by Babylonian astronomers. Sixty is a highly composite number — divisible by 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20 and 30 — which makes fractions unusually convenient, one reason the system was superb for astronomical calculation.

Greek astronomers, above all Ptolemy in the Almagest, adopted sexagesimal for angles and coordinates, and through them it passed into universal use. It survives today every time an angle is written in degrees, minutes and seconds — 60 arcminutes to a degree, 60 arcseconds to a minute — and in the division of the hour into 60 minutes and 60 seconds.
