Simonides of Ceos (c. 556 – c. 468 BC) was a Greek lyric poet, one of the nine lyric poets canonised by the scholars of Hellenistic Alexandria. He wrote choral lyric, epinician odes celebrating victors at the Panhellenic games, elegies, and epitaphs for the war dead of the Persian Wars.

Life and work

Simonides worked for patrons across the Greek world: the Pisistratid court at Athens, the rulers of Thessaly, and finally Hieron I of Syracuse, where he rivalled Pindar and Bacchylides (his nephew). He is credited with composing the famous epitaph for the Spartans who fell at Thermopylae: "Go tell the Spartans, stranger passing by, that here, obedient to their laws, we lie." Tradition also credits him with inventing the art of memory — the method of loci — after he survived the collapse of a banquet hall and identified the crushed bodies by recalling the seating arrangement.

Reputation

Ancient sources portray Simonides as the first poet to charge fees for his verse professionally, and as both wise and famously frugal. Only fragments of his poetry survive, quoted by later authors and recovered from papyri, including a substantial elegy on the Battle of Plataea published in the 1990s.
