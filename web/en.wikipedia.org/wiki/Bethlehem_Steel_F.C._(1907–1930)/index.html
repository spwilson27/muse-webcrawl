Bethlehem Steel Football Club (1907–1930) was an American professional soccer team based in Bethlehem, Pennsylvania, sponsored by the Bethlehem Steel Corporation. It was one of the most successful early American soccer clubs, and its record of five U.S. Open Cup titles stood for decades.

History

The works team was formed in 1907 and quickly rose to prominence, recruiting experienced British players, including many Scots, to the steel town. Bethlehem won the American Cup six times and the National Challenge Cup (now the U.S. Open Cup) five times between 1915 and 1926. In 1919 the club undertook a celebrated tour of Scandinavia and Denmark — one of the first tours abroad by an American team.

In 1921 the club was a founding member of the American Soccer League, winning the league championship in the 1926–27 season with a squad led by the great Scottish forward Archie Stark, whose 67 goals in the 1924–25 season remain a legendary total in American soccer. The "Soccer War" between the ASL and the U.S. Football Association, together with the onset of the Great Depression, ruined the club's finances, and Bethlehem Steel withdrew its sponsorship in 1930, ending the team.
