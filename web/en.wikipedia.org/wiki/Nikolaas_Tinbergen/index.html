Nikolaas "Niko" Tinbergen FRS (15 April 1907 – 21 December 1988) was a Dutch biologist and ornithologist who shared the 1973 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Karl von Frisch and Konrad Lorenz for their discoveries concerning the organization and elicitation of individual and social behaviour patterns in animals. He is regarded as one of the founders of modern ethology, the study of animal behaviour.

Early life and education

Tinbergen was born in The Hague, the third of five children. His elder brother Jan Tinbergen won the first Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 1969; they remain the only siblings both to have won Nobel Prizes. As a boy Niko spent long hours observing wildlife in the Dutch dunes, an interest that led him to study biology at Leiden University, where he received his PhD in 1932 for a short thesis on the homing behaviour of digger wasps.

Career

After fieldwork in Greenland and a period in Lorenz's institute in Austria, Tinbergen taught at Leiden. During the Second World War he was held in a German prisoner-of-war camp for refusing to cooperate with the occupation authorities. In 1949 he moved to the University of Oxford, where he built an influential animal behaviour research group and remained for the rest of his career. His books The Study of Instinct (1951) and The Herring Gull's World (1953) became classics of the field.

Key concepts

Tinbergen is known for the four questions that frame any complete explanation of behaviour: causation (mechanism), ontogeny (development), survival value (function), and phylogeny (evolution). He also developed the concepts of fixed action patterns, sign stimuli (releasers), innate releasing mechanisms, and supernormal stimuli, demonstrated experimentally in herring gull chicks and three-spined sticklebacks. His studies of the "hawk/goose" silhouette and of egg-rolling in greylag geese became textbook examples of experimental ethology.

Later life

Tinbergen suffered from depression in his later years and wrote candidly about it. He took British citizenship in 1955 and died in Oxford on 21 December 1988, aged 81.
