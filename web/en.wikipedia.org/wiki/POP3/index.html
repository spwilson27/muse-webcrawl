POP3

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
POP3
Full namePost Office Protocol version 3
Port110; 995 (POP3S)
StandardRFC 1939 (1996)
[Image: Diagram showing a mail client downloading messages from a POP3 server and deleting them from the server]

POP3, the Post Office Protocol version 3, is an application-layer Internet standard protocol used by e-mail clients to retrieve e-mail from a mail server. Together with SMTP (which sends mail) it formed the classic pair of email protocols for the dial-up era.

POP3 works like its namesake: the client connects, authenticates, downloads all waiting messages to the local machine, and — by default — deletes them from the server, then disconnects. This download-and-delete model assumed one user, one computer and expensive, intermittent connections. Its limitations became obvious when email moved to phones and multiple computers: messages lived on whichever device downloaded them first. IMAP, which keeps mail on the server and synchronises it across devices, therefore displaced POP3 for most users, though POP3 remains supported by mail providers and embedded in countless legacy setups.
