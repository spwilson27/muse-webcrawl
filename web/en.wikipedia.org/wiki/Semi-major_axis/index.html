Semi-major axis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The semi-major axis a and semi-minor axis b of an ellipse.

In geometry, the semi-major axis of an ellipse is its longest diameter: a line segment running through the centre and both foci, with ends on the widest points of the perimeter. The semi-major axis is one half of the major axis, and thus runs from the centre, through a focus, to the perimeter. The semi-minor axis is correspondingly one half of the minor axis, perpendicular to the major axis at the centre.

Ellipse

For an ellipse with semi-major axis a and semi-minor axis b, the eccentricity is e = √(1 − b²/a²) and the area is πab. The semi-major axis is the mean of the maximum and minimum distances from a focus to points on the ellipse.

Hyperbola

The semi-major axis of a hyperbola is half the distance between its two branches along the transverse axis. Unlike the ellipse case, the semi-minor axis of a hyperbola is defined through the conjugate axis and the asymptotes, and it does not in general touch the curve.

Astronomy

In orbital mechanics the semi-major axis is one of the most important orbital elements. For a body orbiting the Sun it determines, through Kepler's third law, the orbital period: T² ∝ a³. For planets the semi-major axis equals the average distance from the Sun, and it is conventionally measured in astronomical units. The semi-major axis also fixes the specific orbital energy of a two-body orbit.
