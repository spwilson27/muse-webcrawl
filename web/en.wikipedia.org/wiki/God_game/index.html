God game

A god game is a video game genre in which the player assumes the role of a god-like entity wielding supernatural powers over a world and its inhabitants, typically guiding or manipulating a population of simulated worshippers or creatures rather than controlling units directly.

Characteristics

God games emphasise indirect control: the player terraforms land, works miracles, punishes or rewards followers, and influences growth, while the simulated people retain a degree of autonomous behaviour. They overlap with simulation, strategy, and "sandbox" games.

History

The genre was defined by Bullfrog Productions titles, beginning with Populous (1989), designed by Peter Molyneux, and followed by Powermonger, Dungeon Keeper, and Black & White (2001). Earlier antecedents include Utopia (1981) and elements of SimCity. Later examples include From Dust, Reus, Godus, and WorldBox.
