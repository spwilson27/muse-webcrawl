Ixchel

Ixchel (Mayan pronunciation [iʃˈtʃel]) is the 16th-century name of the aged jaguar goddess of midwifery and medicine in the Maya pantheon. She was a major deity associated with childbirth, fertility, the moon, weaving, and water, and her cult centre was on the island of Cozumel off the Yucatán coast.

Attributes

In the Dresden Codex Ixchel appears as an old woman with jaguar ears and claws, wearing a serpent headdress and a skirt patterned with crossed bones. She pours water from a jar, an aspect linking her both to rainfall and life-giving waters and to destructive floods. In her younger aspect she is sometimes identified with the moon goddess.

Worship

Her oracle shrine on Cozumel drew pilgrims from across the Maya world, particularly women seeking blessings in pregnancy. A smaller shrine stood on Isla Mujeres, the "Island of Women", named for the goddess images found there by Spanish explorers in 1517.
