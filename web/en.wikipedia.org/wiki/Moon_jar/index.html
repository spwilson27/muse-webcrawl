Moon jar

TypeWhite porcelain jar
OriginKorea, Joseon dynasty
Periodlate 17th–18th century
Korean namedalhangari (달항아리)

A moon jar (Korean: 달항아리, dalhangari) is a type of white porcelain jar made during Korea's Joseon dynasty (1392–1910), named for its milky-white colour and round, moon-like shape. Moon jars were made by joining two hemispherical bowls, giving them a slightly irregular, asymmetric form prized in Korean aesthetics.

Production

Moon jars were produced at the royal kilns at Gwangju, Gyeonggi Province, in the late 17th and 18th centuries. They typically stand over 40 cm tall, have small mouths and bases, and were used to store rice, grains, or wine, or to hold flower arrangements. Their imperfect symmetry reflects the Neo-Confucian values of purity and modesty associated with Joseon white porcelain (baekja).

Legacy

Only about a dozen large early moon jars survive. The form strongly influenced modern studio pottery, notably through the British potter Bernard Leach, and contemporary Korean artists continue to reinterpret it.
