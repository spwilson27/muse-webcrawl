Pyrometallurgy

Pyrometallurgy is a branch of extractive metallurgy concerned with the treatment of ores and concentrates at high temperatures to bring about physical and chemical transformations that enable the recovery of valuable metals. It is one of the oldest forms of metallurgy and remains central to the production of copper, nickel, lead, zinc and iron and steel.

Processes

Key pyrometallurgical operations include roasting, in which ore is heated in air to convert sulfides to oxides; smelting, in which material is melted so that metal separates from slag; converting, which removes iron and sulfur from molten matte; and refining, including fire refining and electrorefining. Calcination, the thermal decomposition of carbonates and hydroxides, is also a pyrometallurgical step.

Comparison with other methods

Pyrometallurgy contrasts with hydrometallurgy, which uses aqueous solutions at modest temperatures, and electrometallurgy, which uses electrical energy for electrolysis or heating. Pyrometallurgical routes are often energy-intensive and produce sulfur dioxide emissions that must be captured, commonly by converting them to sulfuric acid.
