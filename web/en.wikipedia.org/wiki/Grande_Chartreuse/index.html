Grande Chartreuse (French: [ɡʁɑ̃d ʃaʁtʁøz]) is a monastery and the head house of the Carthusian order of monks. It is located in the commune of Saint-Pierre-de-Chartreuse in the department of Isère, southeastern France, in the Chartreuse Mountains from which the order takes its name.

History

The monastery was founded in 1084 by Saint Bruno of Cologne and his six companions, who sought a remote site for a life of solitude and prayer. The original buildings were destroyed by an avalanche in 1132 and rebuilt on the present, safer site. The monastery was repeatedly rebuilt after fires; the current complex largely dates from the 17th century and later. The monks were expelled during the French Revolution and again in 1903, when they moved to Spain, but returned in 1940.

Life and influence

The Carthusians at the Grande Chartreuse live in near-total silence and solitude, each monk in his own cell with a small garden, following the Statutes of the order. The community is forbidden to receive tourists inside the monastery; a museum at nearby La Correrie explains Carthusian life. The monks are also famous for producing Chartreuse, the green and yellow herbal liqueurs distilled from a secret recipe of 130 herbs, plants and flowers, held—according to tradition—by only two monks at a time. The monastery was the subject of the acclaimed 2005 documentary film Into Great Silence.
