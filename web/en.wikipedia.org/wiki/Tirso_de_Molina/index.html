Tirso de Molina


Tirso de Molina (c. 1579–1648)

Tirso de Molina (24 March 1579 – 12 March 1648) was a Spanish Baroque dramatist, poet and Roman Catholic monk, born Gabriel Téllez in Madrid. He is best known as the creator of Don Juan, one of the most enduring figures in Western literature, in his play El burlador de Sevilla y convidado de piedra (The Trickster of Seville and the Stone Guest).

Life and work

Tirso joined the Mercedarian order in 1600 and spent parts of his career in the Americas, serving in Santo Domingo, an experience reflected in some of his plays. He wrote some 300 to 400 plays, of which about 80 survive, encompassing comedies of intrigue, biblical dramas, and theological works. His comedy Don Gil de las calzas verdes and the theological drama El condenado por desconfiado (The Doubted Damned) are also highly regarded.

Don Juan

The Trickster of Seville, first published around 1630, introduced the libertine Don Juan Tenorio, who seduces women without conscience and mockingly invites the statue of a man he has killed to dinner — an invitation that leads to his damnation. The character went on to inspire works by Molière, Mozart and Lorenzo Da Ponte (Don Giovanni), Byron, and many others.
