The James River is a river in Virginia that flows 348 miles (560 km) from the Allegheny Mountains to the Chesapeake Bay. It is the longest river entirely within Virginia and one of the longest in the United States contained in a single state. Its drainage basin covers about 10,432 square miles (27,020 km²), roughly a quarter of the state's land area.

Course

The James is formed near Iron Gate, Virginia, by the confluence of the Cowpasture and Jackson rivers. It flows east through the Blue Ridge Mountains at Balcony Falls, past Lynchburg and the state capital, Richmond, where rapids at the Fall Line mark the head of navigation, and then widens into a tidal estuary for its final 100 miles through Williamsburg, Jamestown, and Newport News before joining Hampton Roads and the Chesapeake Bay at the Hampton Roads Bridge–Tunnel area.

History

The river was known to the Powhatan peoples as the Powhatan River; English settlers renamed it for King James I. Jamestown, founded on its banks in 1607, was the first permanent English settlement in North America, and the river corridor became the core of colonial Virginia, lined with tobacco plantations. During the Civil War the river saw naval actions around Drewry's Bluff and the operations against Richmond in 1864–65. The river suffered severe industrial pollution in the 20th century, notoriously the contamination of its sediments and fish with the insecticide Kepone in the 1970s, but water quality has substantially recovered, and the lower river now supports a notable population of Atlantic sturgeon and bald eagles; the James River Association's periodic report cards grade its health in the B range in recent years.
