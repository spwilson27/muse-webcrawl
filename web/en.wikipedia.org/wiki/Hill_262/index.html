Hill 262 (Mont Ormel)
LocationNear Coudehard, Normandy, France
Elevation262 m
ConflictBattle of the Falaise Pocket, August 1944

Hill 262, also known as Mont Ormel, is a height in Normandy, France, overlooking the valley of the Dives river near the villages of Chambois and Coudehard. In August 1944 it became a key position in the closing of the Falaise Pocket during the Normandy campaign of the Second World War.

Battle

On 19 August 1944 the Polish 1st Armoured Division under General Stanisław Maczek seized the northern summit of the Mont Ormel ridge, from which Polish artillery observers commanded the last road by which the German Seventh Army and Fifth Panzer Army were trying to escape eastward out of the closing pocket. Cut off and under sustained German counterattacks by the II SS Panzer Corps on 20–21 August, the Poles — nicknamed "the Mace" (la masse) from the appearance of the position on maps — held the hill at heavy cost until relieved by Canadian forces on 21 August. The Polish stand prevented the escape of thousands of German troops and effectively sealed the pocket, in which some 10,000 Germans were killed and 50,000 captured.

Memorial

Today the site is marked by the Mont Ormel Memorial and museum, opened for the 50th anniversary in 1994, which overlooks the battlefield; the Polish military cemetery at Urville-Langannerie lies nearby.
