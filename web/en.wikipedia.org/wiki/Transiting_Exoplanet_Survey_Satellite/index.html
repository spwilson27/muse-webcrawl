Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite

The Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) is a NASA space telescope designed to search for exoplanets using the transit method. Launched on 18 April 2018 aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9, TESS surveys the brightest stars near the Sun across nearly the whole sky, succeeding the Kepler mission with a focus on planets amenable to follow-up study.

Mission design

TESS occupies a highly elliptical 13.7-day orbit in 2:1 resonance with the Moon, giving it a stable thermal environment and long uninterrupted viewing. Its four wide-field cameras, each 24° × 24°, tile the sky into 26 sectors; each sector is observed for about 27 days. The primary two-year mission surveyed the southern then northern hemispheres, and extended missions have revisited sectors with faster cadences and covered the ecliptic plane.

Results

By the mid-2020s TESS had identified several thousand candidate planets (TESS Objects of Interest) and hundreds of confirmed planets, ranging from lava worlds such as LHS 3844 b to temperate sub-Neptunes and the young system HD 63433. TESS targets are prime candidates for atmospheric characterization by the James Webb Space Telescope. The mission is led by MIT with partners including NASA Goddard, and its principal investigator is George Ricker.
