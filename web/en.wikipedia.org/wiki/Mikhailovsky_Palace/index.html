Mikhailovsky Palace

The Mikhailovsky Palace (Russian: Михайловский дворец) is a grand Neoclassical palace in Saint Petersburg, Russia, facing Arts Square. Built between 1819 and 1825 to designs by Carlo Rossi for Grand Duke Michael Pavlovich, it is widely considered the masterpiece of late Russian Neoclassicism. Since 1895 it has housed the main building of the State Russian Museum.

History

The palace was commissioned by Emperor Paul I, who set aside funds for his youngest son, Grand Duke Michael, before his assassination in 1801; Alexander I fulfilled the plan two decades later. Rossi designed not only the palace — with its eight-column Corinthian portico, triumphal-arch entrances, and lavishly decorated interiors including the famous White Hall — but the entire surrounding ensemble of Arts Square and adjoining streets, one of the triumphs of Russian urban planning.

The palace remained the residence of Grand Duke Michael and later of his widow, Grand Duchess Elena Pavlovna, whose salon was a center of Petersburg cultural life. In 1895 Nicholas II purchased it for the state and it was converted, by architect Vasily Svinin, into the Russian Museum of Emperor Alexander III — today's Russian Museum, the largest museum of Russian art in the world. The adjoining Benois Wing was added in 1914–1919.
