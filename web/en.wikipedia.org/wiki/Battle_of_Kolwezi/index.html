Battle of Kolwezi

The Battle of Kolwezi was fought in May 1978 in the mining city of Kolwezi, in the Shaba province of Zaire (now the Democratic Republic of the Congo). Rebels of the Congolese National Liberation Front (FNLC), the "Tigers," invaded from Angola and seized the city, taking thousands of European residents hostage. A combined Franco-Belgian airborne operation, spearheaded by the French Foreign Legion's 2nd Foreign Parachute Regiment (2e REP), retook Kolwezi and evacuated the expatriates.

Background

The FNLC, made up largely of former Katangese gendarmes exiled in Angola, crossed into Shaba on 11–13 May 1978 in the second such invasion in two years. They quickly overran Kolwezi, a vital copper- and cobalt-mining centre, and began massacring Zairian officials and European residents; some 2,500–2,800 foreigners were trapped, and more than 100 Europeans and many hundreds of Zairians were killed in the days that followed.

The operation

France launched Operation Bonite (also called Operation Léopard): on 19 May, some 400 legionnaires of the 2e REP, airlifted by US transport aircraft to Kinshasa and then flown forward, parachuted onto Kolwezi in daylight. They seized the old town, the hospital and key points in fierce street fighting, freeing hostage groups. A second drop the next day brought reinforcements, while Belgian paracommandos landed at the airfield to organise the evacuation. By 22 May the city was secure and the rebels had withdrawn toward Angola.

Aftermath

About 2,700 Europeans were evacuated by air. French losses were five legionnaires killed; Belgian and Zairian forces also took casualties, and FNLC losses were estimated in the hundreds. The operation remains the French Foreign Legion's most famous post-Algeria combat jump, and it shored up Mobutu Sese Seko's regime in Zaire, though Shaba's instability and foreign intervention foreshadowed later Congolese wars.
