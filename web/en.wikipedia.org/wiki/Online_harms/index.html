Online harms

Online harms is a term used in policy and regulation to describe harmful content and behaviour encountered on the internet, particularly on social media and search services. The concept gained prominence through the United Kingdom's Online Harms White Paper of 2019, which led to the Online Safety Act 2023.

Scope

The category spans illegal content such as child sexual abuse material, terrorist content and fraud, as well as content that is legal but harmful, including cyberbullying, disinformation, content promoting self-harm or eating disorders, and online harassment. Frameworks typically impose a duty of care on platforms, enforced by a regulator such as Ofcom in the UK.

Criticism

Critics warn that broad "legal but harmful" duties risk chilling lawful speech, are technically hard to enforce at scale, and may conflict with encryption and privacy. Supporters argue that platform self-regulation proved insufficient and that risk-based duties are proportionate.
