152830 Dinkinesh

152830 Dinkinesh is a small main-belt asteroid, about 790 metres across, and the first target visited by NASA's Lucy spacecraft. On 1 November 2023 Lucy flew within about 430 kilometres of Dinkinesh and discovered that it has a satellite — which itself turned out to be a contact binary, the first such satellite found around an asteroid. The asteroid's name (Amharic: ድንቅነሽ) means "you are marvellous" and is the Ethiopian name for the Lucy fossil after which the mission is named.

Discovery and flyby

Dinkinesh was discovered in 1999 by the LINEAR survey but was added to Lucy's itinerary only in early 2023, as an engineering test ahead of the mission's main Trojan asteroid targets. The flyby tested Lucy's autonomous tracking system at a closing speed of about 4.5 km/s. It far exceeded expectations scientifically: images revealed an equatorial ridge and trough on Dinkinesh suggesting internal strength and past reshaping, and a satellite roughly 220 metres across.

Selam

The satellite, named Selam (Amharic for "peace", after the juvenile Australopithecus fossil), consists of two similar-sized lobes joined together — a contact binary — orbiting at about 3.1 km distance. How such a configuration formed and survived around so small a primary is an open question; both bodies may be rubble piles shed or re-accreted after a rotational disruption of the primary.
