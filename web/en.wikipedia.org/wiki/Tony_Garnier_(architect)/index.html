Tony Garnier (architect)

Born13 August 1869, Lyon, France
Died19 January 1948 (aged 78), Roquefort-la-Bédoule, France
OccupationArchitect and urban planner
Known forUne Cité Industrielle; architect of Lyon

Tony Garnier (13 August 1869 – 19 January 1948) was a celebrated French architect and city planner, known for the Cité Industrielle ("Industrial City"), a visionary scheme for a modern zoned city that profoundly influenced twentieth-century urban planning, and for a series of public buildings in his native Lyon.

Cité Industrielle

Trained at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris, Garnier won the Prix de Rome in 1899. During his stay at the Villa Medici he developed his design for an ideal industrial city of 35,000 inhabitants, exhibiting the drawings in 1901 and publishing them in 1917 as Une Cité Industrielle. The scheme separated functions — industry, housing, leisure, administration — connected by green space, and envisaged schools, hospitals and houses built in reinforced concrete with flat roofs, generous glazing and no enclosing walls or private gardens. Its rational zoning anticipated the Athens Charter and the planning of the modern movement, and Le Corbusier acknowledged its importance.

Work in Lyon

Under mayor Édouard Herriot, Garnier became the municipal architect of Lyon from 1905, realising parts of his urban vision: the enormous livestock market and slaughterhouse of La Mouche (now the Halle Tony Garnier concert venue), the Stade de Gerland stadium (1914–1926), the vast Édouard Herriot hospital, and the États-Unis housing district. His buildings combined classical planning with exposed modern construction.
