Overview

North Tyneside is a metropolitan borough in Tyne and Wear, North East England. It covers the area north of the River Tyne east of Newcastle, including the towns of Wallsend, North Shields, Tynemouth, Whitley Bay, and Killingworth.

History

The borough was created in 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972. Wallsend marks the eastern end of Hadrian's Wall and takes its name from that fact; it was home to the Swan Hunter shipyard, famous for building liners such as the Mauretania. North Shields developed as a fishing port at the mouth of the Tyne, while Tynemouth has a ruined priory and castle and Whitley Bay became a popular seaside resort.

Governance

North Tyneside Council is led by a directly elected mayor, a system adopted in 2002. The borough forms part of the North East Combined Authority.
