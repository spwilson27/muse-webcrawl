Quantum metrology

Quantum metrology is the study of high-precision measurements of physical parameters using quantum theory, in particular entanglement and squeezing, to surpass the precision achievable with classical probes. It underlies quantum sensing and much of quantum-enhanced interferometry.

Shot noise and the Heisenberg limit

With N independent classical probes, estimation uncertainty scales as 1/√N — the standard quantum or shot-noise limit. Using entangled states such as NOON states in an interferometer, the scaling can in principle reach 1/N, the Heisenberg limit. The ultimate precision for a given probe state is set by the quantum Fisher information.

Applications

Quantum metrology techniques are applied in gravitational-wave detectors such as LIGO, which inject squeezed light to reduce quantum noise; in atomic clocks; in magnetometry with nitrogen-vacancy centres in diamond; and in quantum imaging and lithography proposals. Decoherence and photon loss make the full Heisenberg scaling difficult to sustain in practice.
