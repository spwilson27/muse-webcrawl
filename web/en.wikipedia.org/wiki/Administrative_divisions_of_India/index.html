Administrative divisions of India

The administrative divisions of India are subnational administrative units of the country. India is a federal union comprising 28 states and 8 union territories, for a total of 36 entities. The states and union territories are further subdivided into districts and smaller administrative divisions, creating a hierarchy that extends from the national capital down to individual villages. The structure descends in part from the administrative systems of British India, reorganized after independence in 1947 and by the States Reorganisation Act of 1956, which redrew state boundaries largely along linguistic lines.

Hierarchy

  • States and union territories — the primary subdivisions. States have their own elected governments; union territories are administered by the central government, though Delhi, Puducherry, and Jammu and Kashmir have legislatures.
  • Divisions — in some states, groups of districts administered by a divisional commissioner.
  • Districts — more than 750 districts headed by a District Collector (Deputy Commissioner) of the Indian Administrative Service, responsible for revenue, law and order coordination, and development programs.
  • Sub-districts — known as tehsils, talukas, blocks, or mandals depending on the state, headed by a tehsildar or block development officer.
  • Local bodies — urban municipalities (municipal corporations, councils, and nagar panchayats) and rural Panchayati raj institutions (gram panchayats at village level, block panchayats, and district zilla parishads), given constitutional status by the 73rd and 74th amendments of 1992.

History

British India was organized into presidencies and provinces, with districts as the basic unit of administration under collector-magistrates — a framework independent India retained. The princely states were integrated into the union in the late 1940s, and the 1956 reorganisation replaced the old Part A/B/C/D state classifications with linguistically based states and union territories. Later changes created new states including Haryana (1966), the northeastern states, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, and Uttarakhand (2000), and Telangana (2014). In 2019 the state of Jammu and Kashmir was reorganized into two union territories, Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh.

Function

Under the Constitution's Seventh Schedule, legislative subjects are divided between the Union, the states, and a concurrent list, so administrative divisions are not merely territorial: states control police, public health, agriculture, and local government, while the Union controls defense, foreign affairs, and railways. The All-India Services — the IAS, IPS, and Indian Forest Service — staff key posts across both levels, linking the tiers of the administrative hierarchy.
