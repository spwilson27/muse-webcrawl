Juffureh

Juffureh
Country: The Gambia
Division: North Bank Division
Known for: Association with Alex Haley's Roots; Atlantic slave trade history

Juffureh (also spelled Jufureh) is a village in the North Bank Division of the Gambia, on the north bank of the Gambia River near Albreda and the former slave-trading post on James Island (Kunta Kinteh Island). It is internationally known as the ancestral village of Kunta Kinte, the central figure of Alex Haley's 1976 novel Roots: The Saga of an American Family.

History

Juffureh was a trading settlement connected with the commerce of the Gambia River, in a region where European traders — Portuguese, then French and British — dealt in goods and in enslaved Africans from the fifteenth century onward. Nearby James Island, established as a fortified trading post in the seventeenth century, was a holding point for captives shipped across the Atlantic. Juffureh functioned as a shore-side settlement associated with the trade, and its population included trading families and interpreters.

Roots and heritage tourism

Alex Haley traced his ancestry, through family oral tradition, to Kunta Kinte, who according to the story was captured near Juffureh in 1767 and transported to Maryland. The book and the 1977 television miniseries Roots made Juffureh a major destination for heritage tourism, particularly for African American visitors seeking ancestral connections. The village has a small museum devoted to the history of slavery, and guided visits commonly include Albreda and Kunta Kinteh Island, inscribed together by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site in 2003.

Historians have debated the accuracy of parts of Haley's account, including details of the Kinte genealogy, while acknowledging the story's enormous cultural impact in focusing attention on the slave trade and African diaspora heritage.
