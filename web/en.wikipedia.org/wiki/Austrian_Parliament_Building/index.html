Austrian Parliament Building

LocationDr.-Karl-Renner-Ring 3, Innere Stadt, Vienna, Austria
Construction started1874
Completed1883
ArchitectTheophil Hansen
Architectural styleGreek Revival (Neoclassical)

The Austrian Parliament Building (German: Parlamentsgebäude, colloquially das Parlament) in Vienna is the meeting place of the two houses of the Austrian Parliament. The building is located on the Ringstraße boulevard in the first district, Innere Stadt, near Hofburg Palace and the Palace of Justice. It was constructed to house the two chambers of the Imperial Council (Reichsrat), the bicameral legislature of the Cisleithanian part of Austria-Hungary.

The parliament building with the Pallas Athena statue and fountain in the foreground.

The foundation stone was laid in 1874, and the building was completed in 1883. The architect responsible for its Greek Revival style was Theophil Hansen. He designed the building holistically, aiming to have each element harmonise with the others and also be responsible for the interior decoration, including statues, paintings, furniture, chandeliers, and numerous other elements.

Architecture

The building covers 13,500 square metres and is one of the largest structures on the Ringstraße. Its design was inspired by ancient Greek architecture, reflecting the ideals of democracy born in Athens. A broad ramp leads to the main entrance, flanked by statues of Greek and Roman historians, philosophers, and statesmen. In front of the building stands the four-metre-high statue of Pallas Athena on a pillar, erected in 1902, with gilded figures of Nike and allegories of legislation and execution at its base.

Interior

The building contains the debating chambers of the National Council and the Federal Council, the historic chamber of the former House of Deputies, committee rooms, libraries, lobbies, dining rooms, bars, and a gymnasium. The grand Entrance Hall, with its Ionic columns, is used for ceremonial occasions.

History

The building was heavily damaged by bombing during the Second World War and was rebuilt after 1945. Between 2017 and 2023 it underwent a comprehensive renovation, during which parliament met in temporary quarters in the Hofburg. The renovated building reopened in January 2023 with a new visitors' centre and a glass dome over the National Council chamber.
