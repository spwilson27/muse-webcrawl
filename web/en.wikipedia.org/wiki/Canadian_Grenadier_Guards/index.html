Canadian Grenadier Guards

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Canadian Grenadier Guards (CGG) is a Canadian Army Primary Reserve infantry regiment based in Montreal, Quebec. It is Canada's second-most-senior reserve infantry regiment and, as a regiment of foot guards, contributes soldiers to the Ceremonial Guard that mounts the guard at Rideau Hall and Parliament Hill in Ottawa each summer.

History

The regiment traces its origin to the 1st Battalion Volunteer Militia Rifles of Canada, raised in Montreal in 1859, making it one of Canada's oldest militia units. It served in the Fenian Raids and contributed volunteers to the South African War. In the First World War, members served chiefly through the 87th Battalion (Canadian Grenadier Guards), CEF, which fought at the Somme, Vimy Ridge, Passchendaele, and in the Hundred Days. In the Second World War the regiment mobilised as the 22nd Armoured Regiment (Canadian Grenadier Guards), landing in Normandy in July 1944 and fighting through France, Belgium, the Netherlands, and into Germany — including Operation Totalize and the Falaise Pocket.

Today

The regiment trains at the Canadian Grenadier Guards Armoury in the Côte-des-Neiges district of Montreal and forms part of 34 Canadian Brigade Group. Its ceremonial dress mirrors that of the British Grenadier Guards, with a bearskin cap and scarlet tunic; its regimental motto is Nulli Secundus ("Second to None"). It is allied with the British Grenadier Guards.
