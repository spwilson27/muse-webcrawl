Mount Carmel Center

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Mount Carmel Center, site of the 1993 Waco siege.

The Mount Carmel Center was a large group of buildings used by the Branch Davidian religious group located on a 77-acre property near Waco, Texas, United States. The site was originally purchased in the 1930s by the Davidian Seventh-day Adventists under Victor Houteff and named after Mount Carmel in Israel.

History

After a leadership struggle in the 1980s, the community came under the control of David Koresh (born Vernon Howell). The Mount Carmel property contained a main chapel building with dormitory-style residences, an underground storm shelter, and a weapons store; followers lived communally there, trading in surplus and gun-show merchandise.

The 1993 siege

On February 28, 1993, agents of the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, and Firearms (ATF) attempted to serve search and arrest warrants at the compound; a shootout erupted in which four ATF agents and six Branch Davidians were killed. A 51-day standoff with the FBI followed. On April 19, 1993, during an FBI assault using tear gas, a fire destroyed the center; 76 Branch Davidians, including Koresh and 25 children, died. The Waco siege became a lasting subject of controversy over federal law-enforcement tactics. Little of the original complex survives; a small chapel and memorials now stand on the site.
