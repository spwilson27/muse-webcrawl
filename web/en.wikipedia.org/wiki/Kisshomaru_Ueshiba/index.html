Kisshomaru Ueshiba (1921–1999) was a Japanese martial artist and the second Doshu (hereditary head) of aikido, succeeding his father, the art's founder Morihei Ueshiba, on the latter's death in 1969. He had begun training under his father in the 1930s and taught at the Aikikai Hombu Dojo in Tokyo from the 1940s.

Role in spreading aikido

Kisshomaru is credited with systematising aikido teaching, standardising its curriculum and terminology, and organising its worldwide expansion. Under his leadership the Aikikai sent instructors abroad, the International Aikido Federation was founded (1976), and aikido grew into a global practice. He wrote several books, including The Spirit of Aikido. On his death in 1999 he was succeeded by his son Moriteru Ueshiba as third Doshu.
