Second Franco-Dahomean War

The Second Franco-Dahomean War (1892–1894) was a conflict between France, led by Colonel Alfred-Amédée Dodds, and the Kingdom of Dahomey under King Béhanzin. It followed the First Franco-Dahomean War of 1890 and French claims over the port of Cotonou and the Ouémé valley, which Dahomey disputed.

France assembled an expeditionary force of several thousand men, including Foreign Legionnaires, marine infantry, and Senegalese tirailleurs, equipped with modern artillery and machine guns. Dahomey fielded an army of regular soldiers that included the famous Agojie — the women warriors known to Europeans as the Dahomey Amazons.

Course of the war

Advancing inland from Cotonou in the autumn of 1892, Dodds' column fought hard engagements at Dogba, Poguessa, and Adégon against Dahomean forces. After further fighting through 1893, French forces occupied the sacred cities of Cana and entered the capital, Abomey, which Béhanzin had evacuated and burned. Béhanzin surrendered in January 1894 and was exiled (eventually to Algeria, where he died in 1906).

Aftermath

Dahomey became a French protectorate and then a colony within French West Africa; Béhanzin's brother Goutchili was installed as king Agoli-agbo under French supervision. The war is remembered for the fierce resistance of Dahomey's army, particularly the Agojie.
