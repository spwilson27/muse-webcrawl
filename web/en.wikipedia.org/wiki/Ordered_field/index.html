Ordered field

In mathematics, an ordered field is a field together with a total ordering of its elements that is compatible with the field operations: if a ≤ b then a + c ≤ b + c for all c, and if 0 ≤ a and 0 ≤ b then 0 ≤ ab. Equivalently, an ordering can be specified by a set of "positive" elements closed under addition and multiplication and containing exactly one of each pair {x, −x} for nonzero x.

Examples

The rational numbers and the real numbers, with their usual orderings, are ordered fields; the real numbers form the unique (up to isomorphism) complete ordered field, a characterisation central to real analysis. Other examples include the field of rational functions with a suitable ordering, and non-Archimedean examples such as fields of formal Laurent series and the hyperreal numbers.

Properties

Every ordered field has characteristic zero, since 1 > 0 implies n·1 > 0 for every positive integer n. Every square in an ordered field is non-negative, and −1 can never be a sum of squares; fields with this property are called formally real. The complex numbers cannot be made into an ordered field, because i² = −1 would have to be non-negative. Every ordered field contains a copy of the rationals, and its ordering extends that of ℚ.
