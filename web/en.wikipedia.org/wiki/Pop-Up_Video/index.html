Pop-Up Video

Pop-Up Video is an American television series created by Woody Thompson that aired on VH1 from October 27, 1996, to August 8, 2002, with a revival from 2011 to 2012. The show presented music videos accompanied by "pop-up" bubbles — called "info nuggets" — containing trivia, background information, and humorous commentary about the artist, the song, and the video's production. The format became one of VH1's signature programmes of the late 1990s and a widely imitated piece of television design.

Format

Each episode collected several music videos around a theme or artist. As a video played, cartoon thought-bubble graphics popped onto the screen with a "bloop" sound effect, displaying researched facts (for example, about a video's budget, director, or the artist's biography) alongside jokes, wordplay, and tongue-in-cheek statistics. A team of writers and researchers produced the nuggets, interviewing artists, directors, and crew; artists occasionally objected to the trivia or jokes, and some videos were pulled or edited after complaints.

History

Creator Woody Thompson pitched the concept as "videos with footnotes." The series premiered in 1996 and quickly became a hit, spawning specials and themed episodes devoted to single artists such as Madonna and Michael Jackson, a spin-off (Pop-Up Quiz), and imitators on other networks. Production of new episodes ended in 2002. VH1 revived the series in 2011 with new episodes applying the format to contemporary videos, and the format has since been licensed for international versions.

Influence

Pop-Up Video is credited with anticipating the annotation culture of the internet era: DVD commentary, annotated streaming, and social-media "fact" overlays all echo its approach. The phrase "pop-up video" entered general usage, and the show is frequently cited in accounts of 1990s popular culture and of VH1's shift toward celebrity- and nostalgia-driven programming.
