Marcellin Boule

Born: 1 January 1861, Montsalvy, France
Died: 4 July 1942, Montsalvy
Nationality: French
Fields: Palaeontology, anthropology, geology
Institutions: Muséum national d'histoire naturelle

Pierre Marcellin Boule (1 January 1861 – 4 July 1942) was a French palaeontologist and geologist who became the dominant figure in French human palaeontology in the early 20th century. He held the chair of palaeontology at the Muséum national d'histoire naturelle in Paris and founded the Institut de paléontologie humaine in 1920.

Neanderthal reconstruction

Boule is best known for his three-volume monograph on the Neanderthal skeleton discovered at La Chapelle-aux-Saints in 1908, published 1911–1913. He reconstructed the "Old Man" of La Chapelle as a stooped, brutish, ape-like creature with a shambling gait, and argued that Neanderthals were an extinct side branch unrelated to modern human ancestry. Later research showed the skeleton was of an elderly, arthritic individual and that Boule's posture was wrong, but his interpretation fixed the popular image of the brutish caveman for half a century.

Other work

Boule edited the journal L'Anthropologie, wrote the influential synthesis Les Hommes fossiles (1921), and was sceptical of the Piltdown fossils' supposed antiquity claims in some respects while accepting others. His authority shaped European palaeoanthropology between the wars.
