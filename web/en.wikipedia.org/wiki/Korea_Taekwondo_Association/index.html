Korea Taekwondo Association

The Korea Taekwondo Association (KTA; Korean: 대한태권도협회) is the national governing body for taekwondo in South Korea. Founded in 1961 (initially as the Korea Tae Soo Do Association, renamed in 1965), it is the founding member association of World Taekwondo and works alongside the Kukkiwon, the world headquarters responsible for dan certification and the codification of technique.

History and role

After the liberation of Korea in 1945, the various martial arts schools (kwan) that had taught Korean striking arts began to unify. The KTA was established to standardise competition rules, organise the national championships, train referees and coaches, and select the national team. Through the 1960s and 1970s, its instructors' demonstration teams toured the world, and the association's organisational model underpinned taekwondo's growth into a global sport, culminating in taekwondo becoming a full Olympic medal sport at the Sydney 2000 Games.

Activities

The KTA sanctions national tournaments in sparring (gyeorugi) and forms (poomsae), oversees regional and school associations across South Korea, and represents Korean taekwondo in dealings with World Taekwondo, the Asian Taekwondo Union, and the Korean Sport & Olympic Committee. It also supports the preservation of traditional training and the global dispatch of Korean master instructors.
