The Lion of Flanders (novel)

The Lion of Flanders, or the Battle of the Golden Spurs (Dutch: De Leeuw van Vlaenderen, of de Slag der Gulden Sporen) is an 1838 historical novel by the Belgian writer Hendrik Conscience. It is one of the most famous works of Dutch-language literature and a landmark of Flemish national consciousness.

AuthorHendrik Conscience
LanguageDutch
Published1838
GenreHistorical novel

Plot

Set in the early 14th century, the novel dramatises the Franco-Flemish War, culminating in the Battle of the Golden Spurs (1302) near Kortrijk, where Flemish militias defeated the French royal army. Its hero, the weaver Jan Breydel, and other figures of the Bruges Matins uprising frame a story of resistance to French domination.

Significance

Conscience wrote the novel to give Dutch-speaking Belgians a heroic past in their own language; he is often called "the man who taught his people to read". The book remains a symbol of the Flemish Movement.
