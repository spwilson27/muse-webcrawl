Donaueschingen Festival

The Donaueschingen Festival (German: Donaueschinger Musiktage) is a festival for contemporary music held annually in the town of Donaueschingen in Baden-Württemberg, Germany. Founded in 1921, it is one of the world's oldest festivals devoted to new music and is renowned for its premieres of works by leading avant-garde composers.

History

The festival began in 1921 under the patronage of the Prince of Fürstenberg, with the aim of promoting young and little-known composers. Early programmes featured works by Paul Hindemith (who was closely involved in its organisation), Arnold Schoenberg, Anton Webern and Alban Berg. After interruptions in the 1930s and the Second World War, the festival was revived in 1950 in association with the Südwestfunk (later SWR) broadcasting corporation, with Heinrich Strobel as artistic director.

Postwar importance

In the 1950s Donaueschingen became a central stage for the postwar avant-garde: works by Pierre Boulez, Karlheinz Stockhausen, Luigi Nono, Olivier Messiaen, John Cage, Mauricio Kagel and many others received their premieres there, performed by the SWR Symphony Orchestra. The festival remains a leading commissioning body; the SWR Experimentalstudio and numerous ensembles present new works each October.

Present day

The festival continues to present dozens of world premieres in a single weekend, alongside installations, sound art and improvised music, and is broadcast internationally by SWR2.
