The Jagiellonian University (Polish: Uniwersytet Jagielloński) is a public research university in Kraków, Poland. Founded in 1364 by King Casimir III the Great, it is the oldest university in Poland and the 13th oldest university in continuous operation in the world.

The university fell into decline after its founder's death and was refounded in 1400 by Queen Jadwiga and King Władysław II Jagiełło, whose name it now bears. Its oldest surviving building, the Collegium Maius, dates from this revival.

History

In the 15th and 16th centuries the university was a leading centre of astronomy, mathematics, and law; Nicolaus Copernicus studied there in the 1490s. During the Second World War, the Nazi occupiers arrested 184 academics in the Sonderaktion Krakau of November 1939, deporting many to concentration camps, and the university operated underground. It expanded greatly after 1945 and again after 1989.

Organisation

The university comprises faculties including Law and Administration, Medicine, Philosophy, Physics and Astronomy, Chemistry, Biology, and International and Political Studies. The Jagiellonian Library is one of Poland's largest, holding medieval manuscripts and the Copernican collection. The university is consistently ranked among Poland's top institutions.
