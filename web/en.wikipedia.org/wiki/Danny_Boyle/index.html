Danny Boyle

Daniel Francis Boyle (born 20 October 1956) is an English film and television director and producer. He won the Academy Award for Best Director for Slumdog Millionaire (2008) and was the artistic director of the opening ceremony of the 2012 London Olympic Games.

Early career

Boyle was born in Radcliffe, Lancashire, to Irish immigrant parents, and studied English and drama at Bangor University. He worked in theatre with the Joint Stock Theatre Company and the Royal Court before moving to television, directing episodes of Inspector Morse and the BBC drama Mr Wroe's Virgins.

Films

His feature debut Shallow Grave (1994), written by John Hodge, was followed by the landmark Trainspotting (1996), adapted from Irvine Welsh's novel, whose kinetic style and soundtrack defined 1990s British cinema. After the less successful A Life Less Ordinary (1997) and The Beach (2000), he revitalised the zombie genre with 28 Days Later (2002) and directed the family film Millions (2004) and the science-fiction film Sunshine (2007).

Slumdog Millionaire (2008), set in Mumbai, won eight Academy Awards including Best Picture. Boyle followed it with 127 Hours (2010), Trance (2013), the biopic Steve Jobs (2015), T2 Trainspotting (2017) and Yesterday (2019). He returned to television with Trust (2018) and Pistol (2022), and directed 28 Years Later (2025).

Olympic ceremony

Boyle's "Isles of Wonder" opening ceremony for London 2012 — a celebration of British history and culture featuring the Industrial Revolution, the NHS and James Bond escorting Queen Elizabeth II — was widely acclaimed as one of the finest in Olympic history.
