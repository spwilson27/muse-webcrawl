Lindsey Caroline Vonn (née Kildow; born October 18, 1984) is an American World Cup alpine ski racer. She won four World Cup overall championships — third most for a female skier — with three consecutive titles in 2008, 2009, and 2010, plus another in 2012. Vonn won the gold medal in downhill at the 2010 Winter Olympics, the first one for an American woman. She also won a record eight World Cup season titles in downhill, five titles in super-G, and three consecutive titles in the combined.

Career

Raised in Minnesota and developed through the Ski Club Vail program in Colorado, Vonn debuted in the World Cup at age 16. After early success and a silver-medal breakthrough at the 2007 World Championships, she dominated the speed events from 2008, winning 20 crystal globes in total. At the 2010 Vancouver Olympics she won downhill gold and super-G bronze despite a shin injury. Her 2013 season ended with a serious knee injury at the World Championships, and she missed the 2014 Sochi Olympics after re-injuring the knee.

Vonn returned to break the women's record for World Cup victories, surpassing Annemarie Moser-Pröll's 62 wins in 2015 and finishing with 82, second only to Ingemar Stenmark's 86 overall at the time. She won downhill bronze at the 2018 Pyeongchang Olympics and retired in February 2019 after winning a final World Championship bronze in Åre, her body worn down by repeated injuries. In late 2024, at age 40 and with a partial knee replacement, she made a widely noted comeback to World Cup racing with the 2026 Olympics in view, reaching the podium again in 2025.

Personal life

Vonn married fellow skier Thomas Vonn in 2007 and divorced in 2013, keeping his surname professionally. She founded the Lindsey Vonn Foundation, supporting girls' education and sports programs, and has worked as a television analyst and author.
