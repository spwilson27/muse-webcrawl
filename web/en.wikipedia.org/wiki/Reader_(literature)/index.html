Reader (literature)

In literature, a reader is a person who reads a text. Literary theory, particularly reader-response criticism, examines the role of the reader in creating the meaning of a work, holding that a text has no fully determinate meaning until it is read.

Reader-response theory

Theorists such as Wolfgang Iser and Stanley Fish argued that readers actively produce meaning: Iser through the filling of textual "gaps," Fish through the shared strategies of "interpretive communities." Related concepts include the implied reader — the reader a text presupposes — and the ideal reader.

Types of readers

Distinctions are drawn between the actual (real) reader, the implied reader, the narratee (the figure addressed by a narrator), and professional roles such as the publisher's reader or sensitivity reader, who evaluate manuscripts before publication.
