Sam (text editor)

Sam is a multi-file text editor based on structural regular expressions. It was originally designed in the early 1980s at Bell Labs by Rob Pike with the help of Ken Thompson and other Unix developers. Sam was the standard text editor for Plan 9 from Bell Labs and is included in most Plan 9 distributions, as well as in the Plan 9 from User Space utilities for Unix-like systems.

History

Sam was developed as the successor to the venerable ed line editor, bringing its terse command language to a bitmapped-display environment. Pike designed Sam for the Blit terminal and later for the DMD 5620, exploiting the mouse for pointing and text selection while retaining a powerful command language. The editor's design was described in Pike's 1987 paper "The Text Editor sam".

When Plan 9 was developed at Bell Labs in the late 1980s and early 1990s, Sam became its standard editor, splitting into a small terminal-side front end and a file-system-side back end that communicate over a protocol — an arrangement that makes remote editing natural on a distributed system.

Design

Sam has two editing modes: a command language accessed through a special command window, and direct mouse-driven editing. Its most influential feature is structural regular expressions, which extend the familiar regular-expression notation so that patterns can be composed and nested to describe hierarchical structure in text. Commands such as x (extract), y, g (guard) and v can be combined to select and transform regions precisely.

Like its predecessor ed, Sam treats files as sequences of characters rather than lines, and multiple files can be open simultaneously in a single session. The editor's minimal interface — essentially a set of resizable text panes — influenced later editors by Pike, notably Acme.
