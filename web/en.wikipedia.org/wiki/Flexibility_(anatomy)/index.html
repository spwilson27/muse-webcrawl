Flexibility (anatomy)

Flexibility is the anatomical range of movement in a joint or series of joints, and the length in muscles that cross the joints to induce a bending movement or motion. Flexibility varies between individuals, particularly in terms of differences in muscle length of multi-joint muscles. Flexibility in some joints can be increased to a certain degree by stretching and exercise.

Flexibility is one of the components of physical fitness, alongside strength, endurance and balance. It is influenced by genetics, age, sex, body composition, activity level and the structure of the joints themselves.

A gymnast demonstrating flexibility in a split leap.

Anatomical elements

Joints

The joints of the human body are surrounded by synovial membranes and articular cartilage, which cover, cushion and nourish the joint. Increasing muscular elasticity of the joint's range of mobility increases flexibility.

Ligaments

Ligaments are composed of two types of tissue: white fibrous connective tissue, which is not stretchy, and yellow fibrous connective tissue (elastin), which is much more elastic. Ligaments limit the range of motion of joints and contribute to stability, so they should not be overstretched.

Tendons

Tendons are not elastic and are even less stretchy than ligaments. They connect muscle to bone and transmit force; most of the change in length during stretching occurs in the muscle itself.

Muscles

Muscle tissue is made of a stretchy material. Each muscle cell contains contractile proteins (actin and myosin) that give the muscle its ability to contract and relax. The stretch response is regulated by muscle spindles and Golgi tendon organs, which modulate the reflex response to lengthening.

Limits of flexibility

Each joint has a normal range of motion. Factors limiting flexibility include bony structure, joint capsule tightness, muscle stiffness, and neural tension. People with generalised joint hypermobility, such as in Ehlers–Danlos syndrome, have unusually flexible joints, which can be associated with injury and early joint degeneration.

Stretching

Flexibility can be improved by regular stretching. Common methods include static stretching, dynamic stretching, ballistic stretching and proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation (PNF). Stretching is widely used in warm-ups, rehabilitation and disciplines such as yoga, gymnastics, dance and martial arts.
