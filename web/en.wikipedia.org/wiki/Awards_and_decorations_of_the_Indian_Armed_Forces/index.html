Awards and decorations of the Indian Armed Forces

The Indian Armed Forces are awarded a wide range of military decorations for gallantry, distinguished service and meritorious service, instituted after independence and awarded by the President of India. The wartime gallantry series is headed by the Param Vir Chakra, India's highest military decoration, followed by the Maha Vir Chakra and the Vir Chakra. The peacetime gallantry series is headed by the Ashoka Chakra, followed by the Kirti Chakra and Shaurya Chakra.

Gallantry awards

  • Param Vir Chakra — highest wartime gallantry award, equivalent in precedence to the Bharat Ratna among civil awards in protocol terms.
  • Maha Vir Chakra — second-highest wartime gallantry award.
  • Vir Chakra — third in precedence among wartime gallantry awards.
  • Ashoka Chakra — highest peacetime gallantry award.
  • Kirti Chakra and Shaurya Chakra — peacetime gallantry awards.

Distinguished service awards

These include the Sarvottam Yudh Seva Medal and Param Vishisht Seva Medal at the top tier, followed by the Uttam Yudh Seva Medal, Ati Vishisht Seva Medal, Yudh Seva Medal, Vishisht Seva Medal, and the Sena Medal, Nao Sena Medal and Vayu Sena Medal of the three services. Service and campaign medals, long-service medals, and the Mention in Despatches complete the system. Most of the awards were instituted in the 1950s and 1960s, with several decorations given retrospective effect to 1947.

Order of precedence

A formal order of wearing published by the President's Secretariat governs the sequence in which the medals are worn on uniform, from the Bharat Ratna and Param Vir Chakra down through campaign and long-service medals.
