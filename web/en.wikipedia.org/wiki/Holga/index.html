Holga

The Holga is a medium format 120 film camera, made in Hong Kong, known for its low-fidelity aesthetic. First introduced in 1981, its simple plastic lens, vignetting, light leaks and soft focus produce unpredictable, dreamlike images.

Design

The original Holga 120S uses a 60mm f/8 plastic meniscus lens, two apertures and a single shutter speed around 1/100s plus bulb. Later models added a glass lens, flash, and 35mm variants.

Cult following

Despite — or because of — its flaws, the Holga became a favourite of fine-art and "lomography" photographers. Its images have won press photography awards, demonstrating that the inexpensive toy camera could be used for serious work.

Variants

Models include the 120N, 120GN (glass lens), 120CFN (colour flash), pinhole versions, and the Holga 135 for 35mm film.
