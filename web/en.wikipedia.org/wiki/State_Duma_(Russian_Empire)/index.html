State Duma (Russian Empire)

The State Duma (Russian: Государственная дума, romanized: Gosudarstvennaya duma) was the lower house of the legislature of the Russian Empire, established by Tsar Nicholas II in the October Manifesto of 1905 during the Russian Revolution of 1905. It met in the Tauride Palace in St. Petersburg.

History

The Duma was intended initially as a consultative body, but the Fundamental Laws of 1906 left the Tsar with extensive powers, including the ability to dissolve the Duma and legislate by decree when it was not in session. Four Dumas were convened between 1906 and 1917. The first two, dominated by reformist and opposition parties, were dissolved quickly; after changes to the electoral law in 1907, the Third Duma (1907–1912) completed its full term, and the Fourth Duma was sitting at the outbreak of the First World War.

February Revolution

During the February Revolution of 1917, members of the Fourth Duma defied the Tsar's order of dissolution and formed the Provisional Committee of the State Duma, which became one of the foundations of the Russian Provisional Government. The Duma was formally dissolved in October 1917.
