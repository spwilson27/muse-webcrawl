Art Spiegelman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Art Spiegelman at his drawing board, with panels of Maus visible behind him]
Art Spiegelman at his drawing board, with panels of Maus visible behind him

Art Spiegelman (born Itzhak Avraham ben Zeev Spiegelman; 15 February 1948) is an American cartoonist, editor and comics advocate, best known for Maus (1980–1991), the graphic novel recounting his father Vladek's survival of The Holocaust — with Jews drawn as mice and Nazis as cats — which won a Pulitzer Prize in 1992, the first awarded to a work of comics. His work advanced comics as a literary and artistic medium more than any other single creator's.

Career

Born in Stockholm to Polish-Jewish survivors and raised in Queens, New York, Spiegelman worked for Topps (creating Wacky Packages and the Garbage Pail Kids concept) and joined the underground comix movement, co-founding the anthologies Arcade (with Bill Griffith) and, with his wife Françoise Mouly, RAW (1980), which introduced American readers to the international avant-garde of comics. His mother Anja's suicide in 1968 haunts his work, beginning with the strip 'Prisoner on the Hell Planet' (1972), later folded into Maus.

Maus

Serialised in RAW and published in two volumes (1986, 1991), Maus interweaves Vladek's testimony — Auschwitz, the death marches — with the fraught father–son interviews that produced it, interrogating memory, guilt and representation itself. Its animal allegory, at once distancing and devastating, made it a canonical Holocaust text taught worldwide; a 2022 Tennessee school-board ban perversely returned it to the bestseller lists.

Later work

Spiegelman covered 9/11 in In the Shadow of No Towers (2004) from his downtown vantage, drew many New Yorker covers with Mouly, edited children's comics (TOON Books) and chronicled Maus's creation in MetaMaus (2011). He remains comics' most prominent public intellectual in the United States.
