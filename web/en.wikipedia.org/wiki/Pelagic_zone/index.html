Pelagic zone

DefinitionOpen water column of the ocean, away from shore and bottom
LayersEpipelagic, mesopelagic, bathypelagic, abyssopelagic, hadalpelagic
Volume~1.37 billion km³ (the largest habitat on Earth)

The pelagic zone is the open ocean water column, home to everything from plankton to blue whales.

The pelagic zone consists of the open water column of the ocean, comprising all of the ocean's water other than that near the shore (the littoral zone) or the sea floor (the benthic zone). It is the largest habitat on Earth by volume. The word pelagic derives from the Greek πέλαγος (pelagos), meaning "open sea".

Vertical layers

The pelagic zone is divided by depth into layers of decreasing light and temperature:

  • Epipelagic (sunlight zone), 0–200 m: where photosynthesis occurs; most ocean life is concentrated here.
  • Mesopelagic (twilight zone), 200–1,000 m: faint light, no photosynthesis; home to lanternfish and vast daily vertical migrations.
  • Bathypelagic (midnight zone), 1,000–4,000 m: complete darkness except bioluminescence.
  • Abyssopelagic, 4,000–6,000 m, and hadalpelagic below 6,000 m in deep trenches.

Life

Pelagic organisms include plankton (drifting), nekton (strong swimmers such as tuna, sharks and whales) and many seabirds. The zone supports the world's great fisheries, though it is increasingly pressured by overfishing, plastic pollution and ocean warming.
