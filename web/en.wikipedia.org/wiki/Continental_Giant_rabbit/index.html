The Continental Giant, often called the "Conti", is a very large breed of domestic rabbit descended from the Flemish Giant, one of the oldest and largest rabbit breeds. Bred in continental Europe, Continental Giants are kept as show animals and, increasingly, as pets noted for their size and generally docile, dog-like temperament.

Characteristics

Continental Giants commonly weigh 6–12 kg (13–26 lb), with exceptional animals exceeding 16 kg, and body lengths can surpass 1 metre when fully stretched. They have a long, powerful body, broad hindquarters, upright ears of roughly 20 cm or more, and a dense rollback coat in colours recognised by the British Rabbit Council, including white, agouti, black, dark steel, and opal. The breed is recognised by the British Rabbit Council in the United Kingdom but not by the American Rabbit Breeders Association, which recognises the Flemish Giant instead. Individual "giant rabbit" record holders in the press, including famous Flemish-type giants such as Darius and Ralph, are often described in the same tradition as the Continental Giant.

Care

Their size demands correspondingly large housing, reinforced hutches or free-range accommodation, and careful handling to protect their spines. Like other giant breeds they mature slowly, may take 8–12 months or longer to reach full size, and have shorter average lifespans (around 4–6 years is commonly reported for giant rabbits) than smaller breeds, with susceptibility to sore hocks, arthritis, and heart conditions.
