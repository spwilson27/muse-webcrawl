Splice

A splice is a method of joining two pieces of material — rope, wire, cable, film, or tape — by interweaving or binding their ends so that the joint is continuous and, ideally, nearly as strong or seamless as the original material.

Rope splicing

In ropework, splicing interweaves the strands of the rope ends. Common forms include the eye splice (forming a permanent loop), the short splice (joining two ropes of similar size with a slight thickening), the long splice (used where the rope must still pass through a block), and the back splice (finishing an end to prevent fraying). Unlike knots, which can reduce rope strength by up to half, a well-made splice retains most of the rope's strength.

Other uses

Electrical conductors are joined with soldered or crimped splices, and fibre-optic cables by fusion splicing, in which an electric arc fuses the glass fibres. In film and audio editing, a splice physically joins lengths of film or tape with cement or adhesive tape — the origin of "splicing" as an editing term. In genetics, RNA splicing removes introns from pre-messenger RNA.
