The southern tamandua (Tamandua tetradactyla), also called the collared anteater or lesser anteater, is a species of anteater from South America and the island of Trinidad in the Caribbean. It is a solitary animal found in many habitats, from mature to highly disturbed secondary forests and arid savannas. It feeds on ants, termites, and bees. Its strong forelimbs are equipped with large claws that it uses to tear open the nests of insects and to defend itself.

Description

The southern tamandua is a medium-sized anteater, though it can vary considerably in size based on environmental conditions. It has a total length of 34 to 88 cm (13 to 35 in), with a tail 37 to 67 cm (15 to 26 in) long, and weighs 1.5 to 8.4 kg (3.3 to 18.5 lb). Males and females are similar in size and colour. They possess eyes and ears with poor sight and hearing, but a well-developed sense of smell.

The head and body are marked with a broad black band across the shoulders and midsection; the rest of the body ranges in colour from tan to blond. This distinctive pattern gives rise to the alternative name "collared anteater". The muscular tail is mostly hairless, and the pattern of irregular blotches visible on its underside allows tamanduas to be individually recognized, much like fingerprints.

Distribution and habitat

The southern tamandua is found from Venezuela and Trinidad south to northern Argentina, southern Brazil, and Uruguay, at elevations up to 2,000 m (6,600 ft). It inhabits both wet and dry forests, including tropical rainforest, savanna, and thorn scrub. It is most common in habitats near streams and rivers, especially those thick with vines and epiphytes, perhaps because such habitats offer the most food.

Behaviour and ecology

The tamandua is mainly nocturnal, but is occasionally active during the day. It spends much of its time arboreal; a study at one site in Venezuela found that 64% of observations were in trees. Its prehensile tail is essential for climbing, and it moves slowly on the ground because of its large foreclaws, which force it to walk on the outer edges of its hands.

Tamanduas forage for ants, termites, and bees, using their powerful claws to rip open nests and an elongated, sticky tongue — which can reach 40 cm (16 in) — to lap up the inhabitants. An individual may spend only short periods at any single nest, thereby avoiding the soldier caste of colonies that might injure it.

Conservation

The southern tamandua is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN because of its wide distribution, presumed large population, and occurrence in a number of protected areas. It is not a frequently hunted species, although it is sometimes killed by dogs or hit by vehicles.
