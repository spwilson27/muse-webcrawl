Northern Ballet

Northern Ballet is a ballet company based in Leeds, England. Founded in Manchester in 1969 as Northern Dance Theatre by the Canadian-born dancer Laverne Meyer, it is celebrated for its full-length narrative "dance theatre" productions — ballets that tell stories, often adapted from literature — and for extensive touring across the United Kingdom.

History

The company became Northern Ballet Theatre in 1976. Christopher Gable, a former Royal Ballet principal, became artistic director in 1987 and, with choreographer Michael Pink, reshaped the company around dramatic narrative works; their production of Dracula became a signature piece. After Gable's death in 1998, Stefano Giannetti and then David Nixon led the company; Nixon's long directorship (from 2001) produced popular adaptations including Wuthering Heights, Madame Butterfly, The Great Gatsby and Cinderella.

Home and repertoire

In 2010 the company moved into a purpose-built headquarters on Quarry Hill in Leeds, shared with Phoenix Dance Theatre and housing the Stanley & Audrey Burton Theatre. Northern Ballet tours widely to regional theatres and is known for accessible storytelling ballets, children's productions and live orchestral accompaniment by the Northern Ballet Sinfonia. Federico Bonelli, former Royal Ballet principal, became artistic director in 2022.
