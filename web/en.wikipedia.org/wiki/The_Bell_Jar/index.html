The Bell Jar

The Bell Jar is the only novel by American writer and poet Sylvia Plath. It was published in London in January 1963 under the pseudonym "Victoria Lucas", shortly before Plath's death, and first appeared under her own name in 1967; it was not published in the United States until 1971, over her family's objections.

Plot

The semi-autobiographical novel follows Esther Greenwood, a talented young writer who wins a summer internship at a New York fashion magazine but becomes increasingly disillusioned and descends into depression. Returning home, she undergoes insulin coma therapy and electroconvulsive therapy and attempts suicide, before gradual recovery in a private asylum under the care of a sympathetic female psychiatrist. The title metaphor describes Esther's feeling of being trapped and suffocating under a bell jar.

Legacy

The novel is regarded as a classic of American literature and a key text in discussions of mental illness, gender expectations in the 1950s and the parallels between Esther's story and Plath's own life.
