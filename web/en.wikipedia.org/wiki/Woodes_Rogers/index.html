Woodes Rogers

Bornc. 1679, Bristol, England
Died15 July 1732, Nassau, Bahamas
OccupationPrivateer, colonial governor

Woodes Rogers (c. 1679 – 15 July 1732) was an English sea captain, privateer and colonial administrator. He completed a privateering circumnavigation of the globe (1708–1711) that rescued the castaway Alexander Selkirk, and later served as Governor of the Bahamas, where he suppressed the pirates' republic at Nassau.

The circumnavigation

Backed by Bristol merchants, Rogers sailed in 1708 with the ships Duke and Duchess, with William Dampier as pilot. In February 1709 his expedition rescued Alexander Selkirk from the Juan Fernández Islands, where Selkirk had been marooned for over four years—an episode that inspired Daniel Defoe's Robinson Crusoe. Rogers captured prizes on the Pacific coast of South America, attacked Guayaquil, and fought a battle with a Manila galleon off Mexico before returning to England in 1711 via the Cape of Good Hope. His account, A Cruising Voyage Round the World (1712), was a popular success.

Governor of the Bahamas

Appointed the first Royal Governor of the Bahamas, Rogers arrived at Nassau in 1718 offering the King's pardon to pirates who surrendered. Many accepted, including some who then helped hunt their former comrades; Rogers repelled a Spanish attack in 1720 and is credited with expelling piracy from the colony. His motto for the islands, Expulsis Piratis – Restituta Commercia ("Pirates expelled, commerce restored"), reflected the achievement. He served two terms, dying at Nassau in 1732.
