IMEI

The International Mobile Equipment Identity (IMEI) is a numeric identifier, usually unique, for 3GPP and iDEN mobile phones, as well as some satellite phones. It is usually found printed inside the battery compartment of the phone, but can also be displayed on-screen on most phones by entering *#06# on the dialpad, or alongside other system information in the settings menu of smartphone operating systems.

GSM networks use the IMEI number to identify valid devices, and a lost or stolen phone can be blocked from accessing the network through the use of equipment identity registers. The IMEI is only used for identifying the device and has no permanent or semi-permanent relation to the subscriber; the subscriber is identified instead by an IMSI number stored on the SIM card.

Structure

The IMEI has 15 digits and consists of three parts:

  • Type Allocation Code (TAC) — the first eight digits, identifying the model and origin of the device. The first two digits give the Reporting Body Identifier.
  • Serial number — the next six digits, defined by the manufacturer.
  • Check digit — a single digit calculated using the Luhn algorithm, used to guard against erroneous inputs.

Before 2003, the structure also included a Final Assembly Code and a spare digit; these were folded into the modern TAC and serial number fields. A variant, the IMEISV, replaces the check digit with two digits of software version number.

Use on networks

When a mobile phone connects to a GSM or UMTS network, its IMEI can be checked against the network's Equipment Identity Register (EIR). EIRs may maintain white, grey and black lists; a blacklisted IMEI prevents the device from registering. Many countries maintain a shared Central Equipment Identity Register so that a phone reported stolen can be blocked across multiple operators. Law enforcement and intelligence services may also use the IMEI to track or locate devices.

Legality of changing IMEIs

Alteration or duplication of IMEI numbers ("IMEI reprogramming") is illegal in a number of jurisdictions, including the United Kingdom, because it defeats theft-blocking measures. Dual-SIM devices have two IMEI numbers, one for each SIM slot.
