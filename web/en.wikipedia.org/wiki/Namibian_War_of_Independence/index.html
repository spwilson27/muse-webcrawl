Namibian War of Independence
Date: 26 August 1966 – 21 March 1990
Location: Namibia, Angola, Zambia
Result: Namibian independence; SWAPO electoral victory
Combatants: SWAPO/PLAN and allies vs. South Africa

The Namibian War of Independence, also known in South Africa as the South African Border War, was an armed conflict between South Africa, which administered South West Africa (Namibia) under a League of Nations mandate it refused to relinquish, and the South West Africa People's Organisation (SWAPO) and its armed wing, the People's Liberation Army of Namibia (PLAN). The war began on 26 August 1966 with a South African police raid at Omugulugwombashe and lasted until Namibian independence on 21 March 1990.

Background

South Africa had governed the former German colony since the First World War and extended apartheid laws to the territory despite United Nations resolutions terminating the mandate. SWAPO, founded in 1960, turned to armed struggle after peaceful protests were suppressed, basing its forces in Zambia and later Angola.

Course of the war

PLAN guerrillas infiltrated the northern regions of Ovamboland and the Caprivi Strip, while South African forces mounted large cross-border operations into Angola, where the conflict merged with the Angolan Civil War and involved Cuban troops supporting the MPLA government. Fighting intensified in the 1980s, culminating in the battles around Cuito Cuanavale in 1987–88.

Settlement

The New York Accords of December 1988 linked Cuban withdrawal from Angola to implementation of UN Security Council Resolution 435. UN-supervised elections in November 1989 were won by SWAPO, and Sam Nujoma became the first President of independent Namibia on 21 March 1990.
