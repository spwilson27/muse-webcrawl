Nottoway River

The Nottoway River is a river in southern Virginia and northern North Carolina in the United States, approximately 249 km (155 miles) long. It rises in Prince Edward County, Virginia, and flows generally southeastward through the Piedmont and Coastal Plain past the towns of Victoria, Courtland, and Franklin, joining the Blackwater River at the North Carolina border to form the Chowan River, which empties into Albemarle Sound.

Course and character

The upper Nottoway is a modest Piedmont stream flowing over rocky shoals; as it descends to the Coastal Plain it becomes slower and darker, its waters stained by tannins from the surrounding swamps and bottomland hardwood forests. Major tributaries include the Meherrin River near its mouth and numerous smaller creeks. The river takes its name from the Nottoway (Cheroenhaka) people, an Iroquoian-speaking tribe historically resident along its banks whose descendants maintain a tribal community in Southampton County today.

Ecology and use

The Nottoway is valued for recreational fishing—largemouth bass, catfish, and shad runs in spring—and for paddling on its lower, scenic reaches. Its watershed supports diverse wildlife, including bald eagles and the endangered Roanoke logperch in connected drainages. Much of the lower river remains largely undeveloped, bordered by cypress-tupelo swamp forest. See also Chowan River, Blackwater River, and Nottoway people.
