Puthandu (Tamil: புத்தாண்டு), also known as Puthuvarudam or the Tamil New Year, is the first day of the year on the Tamil calendar, traditionally celebrated as a festival by Tamil Hindus. The festival date follows the solar cycle of the lunisolar Hindu calendar and falls on or about 14 April of the Gregorian year, the first day of the Tamil month of Chithirai.

Celebration

On the eve of Puthandu, families prepare a tray of auspicious items—three fruits (mango, banana, and jackfruit), betel leaves and nuts, flowers, gold and silver jewellery, coins, and a mirror—so that the first sight of the morning (kanni) brings prosperity for the year ahead. The day begins with a ritual bath, new clothes, and visits to temples. Homes are decorated with kolam (rice-flour designs) at the entrance.

A special dish called manga pachadi is prepared, combining raw mango, jaggery, tamarind, neem flowers, chillies, and salt to represent the sweet, sour, bitter, and spicy experiences of life. Elders give money (kai vishesham) to the young. The festival is also marked by the reading of the new almanac (panchangam) in some temples and households.

Regional variations

Puthandu coincides with new year festivals across South and Southeast Asia, including Vishu in Kerala, Bisu Parba in Karnataka, Pohela Boishakh in Bengal, and Songkran in Thailand. Tamil communities in Sri Lanka, Malaysia, Singapore, Mauritius, Réunion, and South Africa also celebrate it, and it is a public holiday in Tamil Nadu and Sri Lanka.
