The Brazilian Marine Corps (Portuguese: Corpo de Fuzileiros Navais, CFN) is the naval infantry branch of the Brazilian Navy. It is an elite amphibious and expeditionary force of roughly 18,000 marines, responsible for the security of naval installations, amphibious landings, riverine operations and rapid-reaction duties in support of Brazilian naval power.

History

The corps traces its origin to the Portuguese Royal Brigade of the Navy, which arrived in Rio de Janeiro on 7 March 1808 with the Portuguese royal family fleeing Napoleon. Marines fought in the independence campaigns, the Cisplatine War and the Paraguayan War, where they distinguished themselves at the Battle of Riachuelo. In the 20th century the corps modernised as an amphibious force, and Brazilian marines have served in United Nations peacekeeping missions, including in Haiti and Lebanon.

Organisation

The corps is headed by the Marine Corps General Command. Its principal operational formation is the Fleet Marine Force (Força de Fuzileiros da Esquadra), headquartered on Ilha do Governador in Rio de Janeiro, which includes infantry battalions, an amphibious vehicles battalion, an artillery group, a special operations battalion (GRUMEC, the naval combat divers, is a related Navy unit) and the Marine Special Operations Battalion (Tonelero). Regional marine groups guard naval districts throughout Brazil, including an Amazon riverine battalion at Manaus.

Roles

Missions include amphibious assault, security of naval bases and nuclear facilities, riverine and jungle operations in the Amazon, support to civil authorities, and ceremonial duties. The marines maintain a famous symphonic band and bagpipe band that perform at state occasions.
