Citizenship of the European Union

Citizenship of the European Union is afforded to all nationals of member states of the European Union (EU). It was formally created with the adoption of the 1992 Maastricht Treaty, at the same time as the creation of the EU. EU citizenship is additional to, as it does not replace, national citizenship.

Rights

EU citizens enjoy rights including freedom of movement and residence throughout the Union, the right to vote and stand in European Parliament and municipal elections in their member state of residence, consular protection by any EU member state abroad, and the right to petition the European Parliament and apply to the European Ombudsman.

Legal basis

The concept is codified in Article 20 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Article 9 of the Treaty on European Union. The Court of Justice has described EU citizenship as "destined to be the fundamental status of nationals of the Member States".
