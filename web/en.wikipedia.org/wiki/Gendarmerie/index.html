Gendarmerie

[Image placeholder: Gendarmerie patrol]

A gendarmerie (from French gens d'armes, "men-at-arms") is a military force with law enforcement duties among the civilian population, or the building and organisation of such a force. Members, called gendarmes, typically combine military status and discipline with police powers, and are often responsible for policing rural areas, highways, and public order in countries where the regular police are primarily urban.

History

The concept originated in France, where the Maréchaussée of the Ancien Régime was reorganised in 1791 as the Gendarmerie Nationale. The Napoleonic Wars spread the model across Europe; Italy's Carabinieri (founded 1814), Spain's Guardia Civil (1844), the Netherlands' Marechaussee, Belgium's former Gendarmerie, and Turkey's Jandarma all follow the French pattern. Gendarmerie-type forces remain common in Francophone Africa and in Latin America (e.g., Chile's Carabineros, Argentina's Gendarmería Nacional).

Role and organisation

Gendarmeries typically sit between the army and the civilian police: gendarmes are soldiers subject to military law, but spend most of their time on civilian policing — criminal investigation, traffic enforcement, border security, riot control, and protection of government buildings. In wartime they may act as military police. European gendarmeries coordinate through the European Gendarmerie Force (EUROGENDFOR), founded in 2006 for crisis-management deployments.
