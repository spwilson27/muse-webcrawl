Water loss

Water loss in a supply system is water produced but not delivered to paying customers, split into real (leakage) and apparent losses.

Water loss refers to water that is abstracted, treated and supplied by a water utility but never reaches a paying end user, or is not accurately measured. It is a major component of non-revenue water and is conventionally divided into real losses and apparent losses.

Real and apparent losses

Real losses are physical losses of water: leakage from distribution mains, service connections and valves, and overflows and leaks at storage tanks. Apparent losses are paper losses: customer meter inaccuracies, billing and data-handling errors, and unauthorised consumption such as theft or illegal connections. Distinguishing the two matters because the remedies differ — pressure management and pipe renewal reduce real losses, while metering and billing programmes reduce apparent losses.

Measurement and control

Utilities quantify losses through water audits and water balances, and increasingly express leakage using performance indicators such as the Infrastructure Leakage Index. Control measures include district metered areas with flow monitoring, acoustic leak detection, pressure reduction, active leakage control teams and systematic asset renewal. In many systems around the world, losses exceed 30–50% of water supplied, making loss reduction one of the cheapest sources of "new" water.
