The BCS National Championship Game was a postseason college football bowl game played each January from 1999 to 2014 to determine the national champion of NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) under the Bowl Championship Series system. The game matched the teams ranked No. 1 and No. 2 in the final BCS standings, which combined human polls (the Coaches Poll and Harris Interactive Poll) with computer rankings.

History

From 1998 to 2005 the championship was decided within one of the four traditional BCS bowls (Rose, Sugar, Fiesta, Orange) on a rotating basis. Beginning with the 2006 season, the National Championship became a separate game played at the site of one of the bowls a week later, so each host city staged two BCS games in those years. Memorable editions include the 2006 Rose Bowl, in which Texas, led by Vince Young, defeated USC 41–38 in the game deciding the 2005 title; the 2003 Fiesta Bowl, where Ohio State upset Miami in double overtime; and the final edition in January 2014, when Florida State beat Auburn 34–31 at the Rose Bowl. The BCS era ended after the 2013 season, replaced by the four-team College Football Playoff, whose championship game began in January 2015.

Criticism

The BCS was persistently controversial. Critics objected to deciding a championship by polls and computer formulas rather than a playoff, to split national titles (as in 2003, when LSU won the BCS game but USC was voted champion by the Associated Press), and to the exclusion of strong teams from undefeated non-power conferences. The system survived antitrust scrutiny and political pressure before conference commissioners agreed to replace it with the playoff format.
