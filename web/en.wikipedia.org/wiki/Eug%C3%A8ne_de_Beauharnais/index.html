Eugène Rose de Beauharnais (3 September 1781 – 21 February 1824) was a French nobleman, statesman and military commander of the Napoleonic Wars. He was the son of Joséphine de Beauharnais, and thus stepson and adopted son of Napoleon I, who made him Viceroy of the Kingdom of Italy. Renowned for his competence and loyalty, he was the only one of Napoleon's inner circle never to desert him.

Career

Eugène served as an aide-de-camp in Italy and Egypt, fought at Marengo, and in 1805 became viceroy of the newly created Kingdom of Italy, governing effectively from Milan. He commanded the IV Corps of the Grande Armée in the Russian campaign of 1812 and led the army's remnants during the disastrous retreat; in 1813 he held Italy against growing Austrian and Neapolitan pressure until Napoleon's abdication.

Later life

After 1814 he retired to Munich, where his father-in-law Maximilian I Joseph of Bavaria made him Duke of Leuchtenberg and Prince of Eichstätt. His descendants married into several European royal houses, including those of Sweden, Portugal, Brazil and Russia.
