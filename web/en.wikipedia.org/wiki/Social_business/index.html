Social business

A social business is a cause-driven business model originally articulated by Nobel Peace Prize laureate Muhammad Yunus, founder of the Grameen Bank in Bangladesh. In Yunus's definition, a social business is a non-loss, non-dividend company created to solve a social or environmental problem: investors may recover their original investment but take no profit beyond it, and all surpluses are reinvested in expanding the business's social impact.

Principles

Yunus set out seven principles of social business: the objective is to overcome poverty or another social problem rather than maximise profit; the company is financially and economically sustainable; investors get back only their investment amount, with no dividend beyond it; profits stay with the company for expansion; the company is environmentally conscious; employees receive market wages with better working conditions; and the work is done "with joy".

Yunus distinguishes two types: Type I social businesses focus on providing a product or service with a specific social goal (such as nutrition or clean water), while Type II social businesses are ordinary profit-making companies owned by the poor or disadvantaged, whose dividends flow to them, as with the Grameen Bank itself, which is majority-owned by its borrowers.

Examples

Well-known examples include Grameen Danone Foods in Bangladesh, which produces affordable fortified yogurt (Shokti Doi) to combat child malnutrition, and Grameen Veolia Water, which supplies safe drinking water in arsenic-affected villages. The model has been promoted through Yunus's books Creating a World Without Poverty (2007) and Building Social Business (2010), and through social business centres and competitions at universities worldwide.

Relation to social enterprise

"Social business" in Yunus's strict sense is narrower than the broader social enterprise concept, which may permit limited profit distribution. Critics have debated its scalability, its dependence on founders' charisma, and whether the non-dividend rule can attract sufficient capital.
