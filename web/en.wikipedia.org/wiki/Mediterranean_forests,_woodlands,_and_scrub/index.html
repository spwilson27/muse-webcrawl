Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub

Ecoregion: Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub
BiomeMediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub
RegionsMediterranean Basin; California; central Chile; Cape Province, South Africa; southern and southwestern Australia
Climate typeMediterranean climate (hot dry summer, mild wet winter)

Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub is a biome defined by the World Wide Fund for Nature. It is generally characterized by dry summers and rainy winters, although in some areas rainfall may be uniform. Summers are typically hot in low-lying inland locations but can be cool near colder seas. Winters are typically mild to cool in low-lying locations but can be cold in inland and higher locations. All these ecoregions are highly distinctive, collectively harboring about 10% of the Earth's plant species.

The five Mediterranean-climate regions of the world — the Mediterranean Basin, the California chaparral and woodlands, the Chilean matorral, the Cape fynbos of South Africa, and the mallee and woodlands of southern and southwestern Australia — share a convergent vegetation structure despite being geographically isolated. Landscapes are frequently a mosaic of forest, woodland, savanna, shrubland, and grassland patches determined by soils, aspect, and fire history.

Plant adaptations

Dominant plants are typically evergreen and sclerophyllous, with small thick leaves that conserve moisture. Many shrubs are adapted to fire: some resprout from lignotubers, others regenerate from seedbanks stimulated by heat or smoke. Bulbs and annual wildflowers take advantage of the wet winter and spring, producing celebrated spring blooms in several regions.

Biodiversity

Mediterranean-climate regions cover roughly two percent of the world's land area but harbor around ten percent of its plant species, with exceptionally high endemism. The Cape Floristic Region, with thousands of species found nowhere else, and Southwest Australia are among the world's biodiversity hotspots.

Human use and conservation

These regions have supported agriculture — olives, grapes, wheat, citrus — for millennia, and their landscapes have been shaped by grazing, coppicing, and burning. Today they face pressure from development, invasive species, altered fire frequency, and climate change.
