Hungarian Rhapsodies

The Hungarian Rhapsodies, S.244, R.106 (French: Rhapsodies hongroises) are a set of 19 piano pieces based on Hungarian folk themes, composed by Franz Liszt between 1846 and 1853, and later in 1882 and 1885. They are among Liszt's most famous works and helped popularize Hungarian and Romani musical idioms throughout Europe.

Structure

Most of the rhapsodies follow the slow–fast structure of the Hungarian verbunkos and csárdás: a slow, expressive lassan followed by a fast, exuberant friss. Liszt drew many themes from folk melodies he had heard performed by Romani bands in Hungary.

Famous examples

Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2 in C-sharp minor, with its famous opening cadenza, is the best known of the set and has been widely used in films and cartoons, including The Cat Concerto (1947). Several rhapsodies, including Nos. 2, 5, 6, 9, 12, and 14, were also arranged for orchestra, some by Liszt himself and Franz Doppler.
