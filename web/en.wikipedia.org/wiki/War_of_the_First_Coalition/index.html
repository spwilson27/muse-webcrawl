The War of the First Coalition (1792–1797) was the first major effort by several European monarchies to contain and defeat Revolutionary France. The coalition included Austria, Prussia, Great Britain, Spain, the Dutch Republic, and the Kingdom of Sardinia, among others. It forms the opening phase of the French Revolutionary Wars.

Europe during the War of the First Coalition

Course of the war

France declared war on Austria in April 1792. The initial invasion by Prussian and Austrian forces under the Duke of Brunswick was checked at the Battle of Valmy (September 20, 1792), and French armies advanced into the Austrian Netherlands, the Rhineland, Savoy, and Nice. The execution of Louis XVI in January 1793 widened the conflict, bringing in Britain, Spain, and the Netherlands. France responded with the levée en masse, raising mass conscript armies that by 1794–95 had overrun the Low Countries and forced Prussia and Spain out of the war by the Treaties of Basel (1795).

Italian campaign and end

In 1796 the young general Napoleon Bonaparte took command of the Army of Italy and, in a dazzling campaign, knocked Sardinia out of the war, defeated the Austrians repeatedly (Lodi, Arcole, Rivoli), and advanced into Austria itself. The resulting Treaty of Campo Formio (October 1797) gave France the Austrian Netherlands and recognised French control of the left bank of the Rhine, leaving Britain alone at war with France and ending the First Coalition.
