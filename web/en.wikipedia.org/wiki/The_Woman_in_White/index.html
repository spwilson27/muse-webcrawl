The Woman in White is an 1860 novel by Wilkie Collins, considered an early example of the sensation novel and a prototype of detective fiction. It was serialised in Charles Dickens's magazine All the Year Round from 1859 to 1860.

Plot

Drawing master Walter Hartright encounters a mysterious woman dressed entirely in white on a road at night; she later proves to be Anne Catherick, escaped from an asylum. Hartright falls in love with his pupil Laura Fairlie, who bears a striking resemblance to Anne and is forced into marriage with the sinister Sir Percival Glyde. With Laura's half-sister Marian Halcombe, Hartright unravels a conspiracy involving Glyde and the charismatic villain Count Fosco, who has Laura confined in an asylum under Anne's identity.

Significance

The novel's multiple narrators, legalistic structure and themes of identity, marriage law and women's rights made it a sensation; it has been adapted many times for stage and screen, including an Andrew Lloyd Webber musical (2004).
