The Rolls-Royce Trent 500 is a British high-bypass turbofan engine developed exclusively for the long-range Airbus A340-500 and A340-600 airliners. A member of the Trent family derived from the RB211's three-spool architecture, it was announced in the late 1990s, first ran in 1999, was certificated in 2000, and entered service with Virgin Atlantic on the A340-600 in 2002. Two thrust ratings were offered: the Trent 556 at 56,000 lbf for the A340-600 and the derated Trent 553 at 53,000 lbf for the A340-500.

Design

The Trent 500 scaled the Trent 800 core down and paired it with a 97.4-inch (2.47 m) diameter wide-chord fan derived from the Trent 700. It shares the family layout of three coaxial shafts driving the fan, an eight-stage intermediate compressor and a six-stage high-pressure compressor, with a single-stage high-pressure turbine and multi-stage low-pressure turbine. Because it is the sole engine type on its aircraft, Rolls-Royce could optimise it directly for the A340's four-engine installation; the engine also introduced improvements in turbine materials and aerodynamics that fed back into other Trent variants.

Service history

Trent 500 production followed A340-500/600 deliveries for airlines including Virgin Atlantic, Lufthansa, Emirates, Qatar Airways, Singapore Airlines, Thai Airways, South African Airways and Iberia. When Airbus ended A340 production in 2011, Trent 500 production ended with it, though a large fleet remained in service into the 2020s, declining as operators retired the four-engine type in favour of twin-engine aircraft such as the A350 and Boeing 777.

Specifications (Trent 556)

  • Type: three-spool, high-bypass turbofan
  • Thrust: 56,000 lbf (249 kN)
  • Fan diameter: 97.4 in (2.47 m)
  • Bypass ratio: about 7.6:1
  • Length: about 3.91 m; dry weight about 5,000 kg
