Bayan Obo

Bayan Obo
Location: Inner Mongolia, China
Known for: World's largest rare-earth deposit
Discovered: 1927 (iron); rare earths 1930s

Bayan Obo is a mining district in Inner Mongolia, China, containing the largest known deposit of rare-earth elements in the world. The Bayan Obo mines supply a dominant share of global production of light rare earths such as cerium, lanthanum and neodymium.

Geology

The orebodies are hosted in dolomitic rocks of the Bayan Obo Group and are associated with carbonatite magmatism. Iron, niobium and fluorite are also mined.

History

Iron was identified in 1927; rare earths were recognised in the 1930s, and large-scale mining developed after the 1950s under the Baotou steel complex.

Environmental issues

Decades of mining have produced vast tailings containing thorium and other contaminants, raising environmental and health concerns in the region.
