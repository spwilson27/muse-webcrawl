El Capitan

El Capitan

Elevation: 7,569 ft (2,307 m)

Vertical relief: About 3,000 ft (900 m) of granite wall

Location: Yosemite National Park, California, U.S.

Rock type: Granite (El Capitan Granite)

El Capitan (Spanish: El Capitán, "the Captain") is a vertical rock formation in Yosemite National Park, on the north side of Yosemite Valley, near its western end. The granite monolith is about 3,000 feet (914 m) from base to summit along its tallest face and is a world-renowned site for big wall climbing.

Geology

El Capitan is composed almost entirely of a pale, coarse-grained granite, the El Capitan Granite, intruded about 100 million years ago during the Cretaceous. Glaciers carved the valley and polished the cliff, leaving the nearly sheer face that rises abruptly from the valley floor.

Climbing history

The summit can be reached by a trail, but the face itself long seemed unclimbable. The Nose, the great prow at the centre of the wall, was first climbed in 1958 by Warren Harding, Wayne Merry and George Whitmore in 47 days of siege-style climbing spread over 18 months. The Salathé Wall followed in 1961. In the 1970s and 1980s climbers such as Lynn Hill — who made the first free ascent of the Nose in 1993 — transformed standards on the wall. In June 2017 Alex Honnold made the first free solo ascent of El Capitan, climbing the Freerider route in just under four hours without ropes, an achievement documented in the film Free Solo.

BASE jumping and popular culture

El Capitan was an early venue for BASE jumping in the 1960s and 1970s, though jumping is now illegal in the park. The formation's name was also used by Apple for its 2015 macOS release, OS X El Capitan, and its image appears in default desktop backgrounds.
