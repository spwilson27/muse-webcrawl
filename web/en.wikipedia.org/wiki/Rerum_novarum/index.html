Rerum Novarum

Rerum novarum ("of new things") is an encyclical issued by Pope Leo XIII on 15 May 1891, subtitled "On Capital and Labor" (On the Condition of the Working Classes). It addressed the social upheavals of industrialization, defended the right to private property against socialism, affirmed the right of workers to form unions and to receive a just wage, and laid the foundations of modern Catholic social teaching.

Context

By 1891 industrial capitalism had created vast urban working classes living in poverty, while Marxist socialism offered a revolutionary alternative and laissez-faire liberalism resisted state intervention. Catholic thinkers such as Bishop Wilhelm von Ketteler had pressed Rome to respond; Leo XIII, advised among others by Cardinal Manning's example in the London dock strike, issued the encyclical as a middle way.

Teaching

The encyclical argues that private property is a natural right flowing from human labor and necessary for family independence, condemning socialism's abolition of it as unjust and harmful to workers themselves. At the same time it insists capital has duties: wages must suffice for a frugal worker to support a family (the "just wage"), hours and conditions must respect human dignity, child labor is condemned, and the state has a duty to protect the weak. Workers may form associations — unions — as a natural right. Neither class, Leo taught, should be hostile to the other: "capital cannot do without labor, nor labor without capital."

Legacy

Rerum novarum is regarded as the charter of Catholic social teaching. Successive popes marked its anniversaries with major encyclicals — Pius XI's Quadragesimo anno (40 years, 1931) and John Paul II's Centesimus annus (100 years, 1991) — and it influenced Christian democratic parties and labor movements across Europe and Latin America.
