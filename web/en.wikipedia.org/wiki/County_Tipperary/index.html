County Tipperary (Irish: Contae Thiobraid Árann, "county of the well of Ara") is a county in the south of Ireland, in the province of Munster. It is one of Ireland's largest inland counties and is known as the "Premier County", a title reflecting its historic importance and its leading role in the Gaelic Athletic Association, whose founding meeting took place in Thurles in 1884. From 1838 the county was divided into North and South Ridings for administrative purposes; the two were reunified under a single Tipperary County Council in 2014.

The Rock of Cashel, seat of the kings of Munster, overlooking the Golden Vale.

Geography

The county is dominated by the rich farmland of the Golden Vale, drained by the River Suir, which rises in the Devil's Bit mountain and flows south through Thurles, Cashel, Cahir and Clonmel. Mountain ranges include the Galtee Mountains (Galtymore, 919 m, the county's highest point) on the Cork border, the Silvermine Mountains, Slievenamon in the south-east, and the Arra Mountains near Lough Derg, the large lake on the River Shannon forming the county's north-western boundary.

History

Tipperary contains some of Ireland's most famous historic sites. The Rock of Cashel was the traditional seat of the Kings of Munster for centuries before being granted to the Church in 1101; its buildings include Cormac's Chapel (1127–1134), a high cross and a round tower. Cahir Castle is one of Ireland's largest and best-preserved castles. During the Irish War of Independence the county was a centre of IRA activity (the Soloheadbeg ambush of January 1919 is often taken as the war's opening action), and during the Civil War it saw heavy fighting. The county town alternated historically between Clonmel and Cashel; today the council sits in Nenagh with offices in Clonmel.

Towns and sport

Principal towns include Clonmel, Nenagh, Thurles, Cashel, Tipperary town, Cahir, Carrick-on-Suir and Roscrea. Tipperary is one of Ireland's leading hurling counties, traditionally part of the "big three" with Kilkenny and Cork, and its senior team, the Premier County side, has won numerous All-Ireland titles; Semple Stadium in Thurles is regarded as one of the spiritual homes of the game. The county is also famous for horse breeding — the Coolmore Stud near Fethard is among the world's leading thoroughbred operations.
