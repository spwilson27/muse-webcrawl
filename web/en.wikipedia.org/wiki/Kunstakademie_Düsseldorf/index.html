The Kunstakademie Düsseldorf (Düsseldorf Academy of Fine Arts) is a public academy of fine arts in Düsseldorf, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. Founded in 1773, it is one of the most influential art schools in the world, home successively to the Düsseldorf school of painting in the 19th century and to leading figures of post-war German art.

History

The academy traces its origin to a drawing school founded in 1762 by Lambert Krahe and to the Academy of Painting, Sculpture and Architecture of the Elector Palatine established in 1773. Reorganised under Prussian rule in 1819, it became the Royal Prussian Academy of Art. Under director Friedrich Wilhelm von Schadow, the Düsseldorf school of painting gained an international reputation, attracting students from Scandinavia, Russia and the United States, including artists of the Hudson River School.

In the 20th century the academy moved into architecture (Peter Behrens), and after 1945 it became a hub of the German avant-garde. Joseph Beuys, appointed professor of sculpture in 1961, was famously dismissed in 1972 after occupying the academy's offices in protest over admissions limits. The Düsseldorf School of Photography — Bernd and Hilla Becher and their students Andreas Gursky, Thomas Struth, Thomas Ruff and Candida Höfer — redefined contemporary photography from the 1970s onward.

Building

The academy's main building on Eiskellerstraße in the Altstadt, designed by Hermann Riffart in Italian Renaissance Revival style, was erected in 1875–79. Above the portal runs a frieze with the names of artists from antiquity to the 19th century. Each February the academy holds its famous public Rundgang exhibition of student work.

Notable teachers and alumni

Teachers and students include painters Gerhard Richter, Sigmar Polke, Jörg Immendorff, Markus Lüpertz and Anselm Kiefer; ZERO founders Heinz Mack and Otto Piene; photographers of the Becher school; and conceptual artists including Blinky Palermo and Katharina Fritsch.
