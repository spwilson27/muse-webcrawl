Ext functor

In mathematics, the Ext functors are the derived functors of the Hom functor, central to homological algebra. For modules A and B over a ring R, Ext1(B, A) classifies extensions of B by A — short exact sequences 0 → A → E → B → 0 — up to equivalence, and the higher Extn are defined by taking a projective (or injective) resolution of one argument and computing cohomology of the resulting Hom complex.

Ext0 is just Hom itself. Ext groups vanish in positive degree when the first argument is projective or the second is injective, which makes them measures of how far modules are from those properties. Ext plays a role analogous to the Tor functor, and the two are connected in duality results. The notation was introduced in the 1950s in the foundational work of Cartan and Eilenberg; "Ext" is short for "extension".

Ext also has a sheaf version used throughout algebraic geometry.
