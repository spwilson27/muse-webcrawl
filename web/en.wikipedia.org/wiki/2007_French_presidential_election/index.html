The 2007 French presidential election was held on 22 April and 6 May 2007 to elect the successor to Jacques Chirac, who did not seek a third term. Nicolas Sarkozy of the centre-right Union for a Popular Movement (UMP) defeated Ségolène Royal of the Socialist Party in the run-off, becoming the sixth president of the Fifth Republic.

First round

Twelve candidates stood. Sarkozy led with 31.18 percent of the vote, followed by Royal with 25.87 percent. The centrist François Bayrou achieved a strong third place with 18.57 percent on a platform of independence from the two main blocs, while Jean-Marie Le Pen of the National Front fell to 10.44 percent, far below his shock second place in 2002. Turnout, at nearly 84 percent, was among the highest of the Fifth Republic, reflecting voters' determination to avoid a repeat of the 2002 result.

Second round and aftermath

A televised debate between Sarkozy and Royal drew over 20 million viewers. In the run-off Sarkozy won 53.06 percent to Royal's 46.94 percent. He took office on 16 May 2007, promising economic reform and a "rupture" with past policies; his UMP went on to win the legislative elections of June 2007, though with a smaller majority than expected.
