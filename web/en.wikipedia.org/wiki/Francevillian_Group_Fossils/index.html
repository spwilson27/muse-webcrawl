Francevillian Group Fossils

The Francevillian Group Fossils are a collection of macroscopic fossil structures found in the Paleoproterozoic Francevillian Group of Gabon, West Africa, dated to about 2.1 billion years ago. Reported in 2010 by a team led by Abderrazak El Albani, the pyritized, centimetre-scale forms were interpreted as possible early multicellular organisms — far older than the Ediacaran biota.

Context

The fossils occur in black shales deposited shortly after the Great Oxidation Event, during a temporary rise in atmospheric oxygen sometimes called the Lomagundi Event. Specimens show lobed, disc-like and elongated morphologies with apparent coordinated growth.

Debate

The interpretation remains debated: some researchers regard the structures as colonies of single-celled organisms or even abiotic/pyrite concretions rather than true multicellular eukaryotes. If confirmed as complex multicellular life, they would push back its emergence by hundreds of millions of years, possibly representing an early evolutionary experiment that left no descendants.
