Modena

Region: Emilia-Romagna
Country: Italy
Population: approx. 185,000

Modena is a city and comune on the south side of the Po Valley, in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy. It is known for its medieval old town, its Romanesque cathedral, and its central place in Italy's luxury sports-car industry.

History

Ancient Mutina was an Etruscan and then Roman settlement, famous in antiquity for its wool and pottery. From 1289 the city was ruled, with interruptions, by the Este family, and it served as the capital of the Duchy of Modena until Italian unification. The cathedral, begun in 1099, together with the Ghirlandina bell tower and the Piazza Grande, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Economy and culture

Modena and its province sit at the heart of Italy's "Motor Valley": Ferrari is based in nearby Maranello, and Maserati, Pagani, and Lamborghini are all located in or near the city. The tenor Luciano Pavarotti was born in Modena. The city is also celebrated gastronomically as the home of traditional balsamic vinegar (Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale di Modena) and of the chef Massimo Bottura's Osteria Francescana.

Education

The University of Modena and Reggio Emilia, founded in 1175, is one of the oldest universities in Italy.
