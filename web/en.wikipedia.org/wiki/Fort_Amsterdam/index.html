Fort Amsterdam

LocationSouthern tip of Manhattan, New York City
Built1625–1626 (Dutch)
Later namesFort George (under English/British rule)
Demolished1790

Fort Amsterdam was a fortified stronghold built in 1625–1626 by the Dutch West India Company at the southern tip of Manhattan Island, the administrative and military centre of the colony of New Netherland and of the settlement of New Amsterdam, which grew into New York City. Designed by engineer Cryn Fredericksz, it was a four-pointed star fort with bastions, enclosing barracks, a church, the governor's house, and company warehouses.

History

Directors of the colony, including Peter Minuit and later Peter Stuyvesant, governed from the fort. When English forces took the colony in 1664, the fort was renamed Fort George (after brief Dutch recapture in 1673, when it was briefly Fort Willem Hendrick). Under British rule it was repeatedly rebuilt and served as the seat of colonial government and a symbol of royal authority; in 1776 it was a focal point at the start of the American Revolutionary War.

The fort was demolished in 1790 after independence, and the site was filled and developed; the United States Custom House (now the National Museum of the American Indian) stands on part of the site at Bowling Green. The fort's footprint is remembered as the nucleus from which New York City grew.

Other forts of the name

Other Fort Amsterdams include a fort in Curaçao (1634), the capital-era fort at New Amsterdam in Suriname, and a fort in Ghana (on the Gold Coast), originally built by the English and captured by the Dutch.
