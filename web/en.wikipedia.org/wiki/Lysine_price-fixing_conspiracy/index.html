Lysine price-fixing conspiracy

Period1992–1995
ProductLysine (animal feed additive)
Principal companyArcher Daniels Midland (ADM)
Key figureMark Whitacre (FBI informant)
OutcomeADM fined $100 million; executives imprisoned

The lysine price-fixing conspiracy was an international cartel of the mid-1990s in which Archer Daniels Midland (ADM) and several Asian and European producers conspired to fix the price and allocate sales volumes of lysine, an amino acid used as an additive in animal feed. The conspiracy became one of the most famous corporate antitrust cases of the decade, largely because ADM executive Mark Whitacre secretly recorded the conspirators' meetings for the FBI.

The conspiracy

From 1992 to 1995, ADM and competitors including Ajinomoto and Kyowa Hakko of Japan and Sewon and Cheil Jedang of South Korea met to set prices and divide the world lysine market. Whitacre, president of ADM's BioProducts division, became an informant after his wife threatened to expose his involvement; his tapes captured executives declaring that "the competitor is our friend, and the customer is our enemy."

Prosecution

The FBI raided ADM's headquarters in June 1995. In October 1996 ADM pleaded guilty and paid a $100 million criminal fine — then the largest antitrust fine in US history — including $70 million for the lysine conspiracy and $30 million for a related citric acid conspiracy. Senior executives Michael Andreas and Terrance Wilson were convicted and sentenced to prison. Whitacre himself was convicted of embezzling millions from ADM during his time as an informant and served over eight years in prison. The case inspired the book and 2009 film The Informant!
