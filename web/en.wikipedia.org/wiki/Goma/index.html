Goma

Goma is the capital and largest city of North Kivu Province in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. It is located on the northern shore of Lake Kivu and shares a border with the Rwandan city of Gisenyi (Rubavu). It sits next to the active Nyiragongo volcano.

Geography and volcanic activity

The city lies in the East African Rift at an elevation of about 1,500 metres. In January 2002, an eruption of Nyiragongo sent lava flows through the centre of Goma, destroying roughly a third of the city, including much of the commercial district and part of the airport runway, and displacing hundreds of thousands. Another major eruption in May 2021 forced the evacuation of much of the population.

Conflict

Goma has repeatedly been at the centre of conflict in the eastern Congo. It absorbed huge refugee flows after the 1994 Rwandan genocide, was held by rebel movements during the First and Second Congo Wars, and was briefly captured by the M23 rebellion in November 2012. In late January 2025, M23 rebels again seized the city after an offensive, a major escalation of the conflict in North Kivu.

Economy

Goma is a regional trade and transport hub for minerals, agricultural produce and cross-border commerce with Rwanda, though insecurity has heavily constrained its economy. Its population is estimated in the millions when displaced people in surrounding camps are counted.
