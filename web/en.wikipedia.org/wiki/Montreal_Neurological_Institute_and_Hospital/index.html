The Montreal Neurological Institute and Hospital (French: Institut et hôpital neurologiques de Montréal), often called the Neuro, is a research and medical centre in Montreal, Quebec, Canada, devoted to neuroscience and the treatment of neurological diseases. Founded in 1934 by neurosurgeon Wilder Penfield with funding from the Rockefeller Foundation and the city and province, it forms part of McGill University and the McGill University Health Centre.

History

Penfield's work at the Neuro on the surgical treatment of epilepsy produced major advances in understanding the localization of brain function, including the mapping of the sensory and motor cortices known through the "homunculus" diagrams, and the Montreal procedure for epilepsy surgery. Scientists at the institute, including Brenda Milner — whose studies of patient H.M. began in connection with the Neuro — made foundational contributions to neuropsychology and memory research.

Open science

In 2016 the Neuro became the first academic institution of its kind to adopt an open science model, committing to share its research data and biological samples freely and to forgo patenting its discoveries, under an initiative supported by the Tanenbaum Open Science Institute.
