Kamehameha IV (born Alexander ʻIolani Liholiho; 9 February 1834 – 30 November 1863) was the fourth monarch of the Kingdom of Hawaii, reigning from 1855 until his death. Adopted as heir by his uncle Kamehameha III, he ascended the throne at the age of 20.

Reign

Kamehameha IV sought to limit American influence in the kingdom, counterbalancing it with closer ties to Britain, and worked to curb the power of American missionaries and merchants. With his wife Queen Emma he founded the Queen's Hospital in Honolulu (1859) to serve Native Hawaiians, and championed the introduction of the Anglican Church to Hawaii, inviting the first Church of England mission in 1862.

Death and legacy

The death of his only son, Prince Albert, in 1862 devastated the king; he died the following year, reportedly of asthma and grief. He was succeeded by his brother, Kamehameha V.
