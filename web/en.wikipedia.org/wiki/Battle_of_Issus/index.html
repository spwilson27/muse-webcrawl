Battle of Issus

The Battle of Issus (also Issos) was a decisive engagement fought in November 333 BC in southern Anatolia between the forces of Alexander the Great of Macedon and the Achaemenid Persian Empire under King Darius III, whom Alexander defeated for the first time. The battle took place near the town of Issus, close to the Pinarus River, in what is now the Turkish province of Hatay.

Background

After his victory at the Granicus in 334 BC, Alexander advanced along the Anatolian coast. Darius III, raising a great army, marched through the Amanus Mountains and came down behind Alexander at Issus, cutting his supply lines and forcing him to turn back and fight on a narrow coastal plain that neutralised Persian numbers.

The battle

Alexander led the Companion cavalry across the river against the Persian left, while the phalanx struggled in the riverbed against Greek mercenary hoplites in Persian service. Wheeling inward, Alexander drove toward Darius himself; the Great King, seeing his army collapsing around him, fled the field, abandoning his chariot, his camp, and his family.

Aftermath

The Persians lost Syria and the Levantine coast; Darius's mother, wife and children were captured and treated honourably by Alexander. The encounter is immortalised in the Alexander Mosaic from the House of the Faun at Pompeii. Alexander went on to besiege Tyre and Gaza, then entered Egypt before the final confrontation at Gaugamela in 331 BC.
