Destructive distillation
Type: Chemical process
Feedstocks: Wood, coal, oil shale
Products: Charcoal, coke, tar, gases
Related: Pyrolysis, Distillation

Destructive distillation is a chemical process involving the decomposition of unprocessed material by heating it to a high temperature in the absence, or limited supply, of air. The process breaks large molecules into smaller volatile substances and a solid residue.

Wood

Heating wood yields charcoal plus methanol (once called wood alcohol), acetic acid, acetone and wood tar. It was historically an important source of chemicals before petrochemicals.

Coal

Destructive distillation of coal produces coke, coal tar, ammonia and coal gas, foundational to 19th-century steel, dye and gas-lighting industries.

Relation to pyrolysis

The term is essentially an older name for pyrolysis applied to natural materials such as wood and coal.
