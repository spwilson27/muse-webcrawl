Logbook

A logbook (or log book) is a record used to log sequential information about operations, observations or events. The term originates in maritime practice: a ship's log records important events in the management, operation and navigation of a vessel, and takes its name from the chip log, the instrument formerly used to measure a ship's speed.

A traditional bound logbook.

Uses

Maritime: a ship's official log records courses steered, speeds, weather, positions and significant occurrences; it can be a legal document in inquiries after accidents. Aviation: pilots keep personal logbooks of flight hours and experience, and aircraft have technical logs recording defects and maintenance. Driving: professional truck drivers log hours of service to demonstrate compliance with rest regulations. Diving: scuba divers record dives as evidence of experience. Science and industry: laboratory notebooks, plant operation logs, radio station logs (including amateur radio contact logs) and vehicle logbooks (registration records) all follow the same pattern.

Digital logbooks

Many logbooks are now electronic: electronic flight bags, electronic logging devices (ELDs) mandated for trucks in some jurisdictions, dive computers that export logs, and software logging frameworks in computing all descend from the paper original. In computing, though, the related term "log file" refers to automatically generated records of events in a system or application.
