The second lady of the United States or second gentleman of the United States is the informal title held by the spouse of the vice president of the United States during the vice president's term in office. The title, coined in contrast to the first lady (the spouse of the president), is of relatively recent coinage; the term "second gentleman" came into use when Doug Emhoff became the first male spouse of a vice president upon Kamala Harris's inauguration in 2021.

Unlike the first lady, the second spouse has traditionally maintained a lower public profile, although several have pursued prominent public roles in literacy, mental health, military families and other causes. The role is not an elected or appointed office and carries no official duties or salary.

History

The first second lady was Abigail Adams, wife of Vice President John Adams. For much of American history the vice presidential spouse was little noticed publicly. The role grew in visibility during the twentieth century. Since 1977, the vice president and family have lived at Number One Observatory Circle on the grounds of the United States Naval Observatory in Washington, D.C., and the second spouse typically hosts official and charitable events there.

Notable modern second ladies include Lady Bird Johnson, who became first lady upon the assassination of President Kennedy; Tipper Gore, known for her advocacy on music labelling and mental health; Lynne Cheney, an author and historian; Jill Biden, the first second lady to hold a paying job (as a community-college professor) while her husband was in office, and later first lady; and Karen Pence, a schoolteacher and artist. Usha Vance became second lady in 2025.
