Three-number summary

In descriptive statistics, the three-number summary of a data set is a compact summary consisting of three values: the median of the data, and the two quartiles (the lower or first quartile and the upper or third quartile). Together with the minimum and maximum, these values form the better-known five-number summary.

Definition

For a sample, the three-number summary is written (Q1, median, Q3). The median splits the ordered data in half, while the quartiles give the middle values of the lower and upper halves, so the summary shows both the central tendency and the spread of the middle 50 percent of observations, known as the interquartile range.

Use

Three-number summaries are robust to outliers and are used for quick comparisons between groups when the extremes of a distribution are unreliable or uninformative. They are closely related to the box plot, in which the box itself is drawn from the three numbers of the summary.
