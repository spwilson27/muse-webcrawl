A440 (also known as Stuttgart pitch) is the musical pitch corresponding to an audio frequency of 440 Hz, which serves as a tuning standard for the musical note of A above middle C, or A4 in scientific pitch notation. It is designated by the International Organization for Standardization as standard ISO 16.

Before standardization on 440 Hz as the predominant pitch, many countries and organizations followed the French standard since the 1860s of diapason normal, which set A at 435 Hz. Historical pitches varied widely: Baroque performance practice commonly used values near A = 415 Hz, while "pitch inflation" during the nineteenth century pushed some orchestras' reference A above 450 Hz, to the frustration of singers.

History

In 1834, a congress of physicists in Stuttgart recommended A = 440 Hz, following the work of Johann Heinrich Scheibler, who had invented a tonometer for measuring pitch precisely. The standard gained ground slowly. An international conference in London in 1939 recommended 440 Hz, and the ISO formally adopted it in 1955, reaffirming it in 1975.

Modern practice

Although A440 is the official standard, actual practice varies. Many orchestras tune slightly sharp — A = 441–443 Hz — for a perceived brilliance of sound; the Berlin Philharmonic has at times used 443 Hz. Period-instrument ensembles use lower standards such as A = 415 Hz for Baroque repertoire. In electronic music and MIDI, 440 Hz remains the default reference, with A4 assigned note number 69.
