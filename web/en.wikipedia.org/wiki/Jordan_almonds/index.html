Jordan almonds

Other names: Dragées; confetti (Italian usage)
Type: Confectionery
Main ingredients: Almonds, sugar coating
Typical colours: White, pastels, gold, silver

Jordan almonds, also known as dragées, are a confection consisting of whole almonds coated in a hard sugar shell, often brightly coloured. They are a traditional celebratory sweet across Mediterranean and Middle Eastern cultures and are particularly associated with weddings, baptisms, and other rites of passage.

History

Sugar-coated nuts and seeds were made in ancient Rome, where honeyed almonds called dragata were thrown at weddings. The name "Jordan almond" is commonly traced either to the French word jardin ("garden"), to a variety of almond once grown along the Jordan River, or to the Spanish town of Córdoba's prized almonds; food historians debate the origin. The hard-panning technique used to build up the smooth shell was industrialised in France in the 19th century, centred on Verdun, famous for its dragées.

Symbolism and use

At Italian weddings, five Jordan almonds are given to guests in a tulle bundle (bomboniera), representing five wishes for the couple: health, wealth, happiness, fertility, and longevity. The bittersweet flavour of the almond beneath the sweet coating is said to symbolise the mixture of bitter and sweet in married life. Similar customs exist in Greek, French, and Middle Eastern celebrations, where sugared almonds are given in odd numbers—indivisible, and therefore symbolic of the unbreakable bond. White almonds are used for weddings, while coloured versions mark anniversaries, baby showers, graduations, and holidays.
