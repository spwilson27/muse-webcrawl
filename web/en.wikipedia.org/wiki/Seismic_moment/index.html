Seismic moment

The seismic moment is a quantity used by seismologists to measure the size of an earthquake. It is proportional to the area of the fault that ruptured multiplied by the average displacement and by the rigidity of the rocks. The seismic moment, denoted M0, is defined by M0 = μ·A·D̄, where μ is the shear modulus of the rocks involved, A is the area of the rupture along the fault, and D̄ is the average slip on the fault.

Units and measurement

Seismic moment is measured in units of energy-like torque, newton-metres (N·m) in SI units, or dyne-centimetres in older usage (1 N·m = 107 dyn·cm). It can be estimated from seismograms: the amplitude of very low-frequency seismic waves is directly related to the seismic moment, which makes it a robust measure even for very large earthquakes.

Moment magnitude scale

The seismic moment is the basis of the moment magnitude scale (Mw), introduced by Thomas C. Hanks and Hiroo Kanamori in 1979. Moment magnitude is calculated from the seismic moment and does not saturate for the largest earthquakes, unlike earlier scales such as the Richter scale.
