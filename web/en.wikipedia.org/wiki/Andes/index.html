Andes

[Image: High peaks of the Andes above the Altiplano]
High peaks of the Andes above the Altiplano

The Andes are the longest continental mountain chain in the world, forming a continuous highland along the western edge of South America. The range is about 8,900 kilometres long and 200 to 700 kilometres wide, running through seven countries: Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Chile and Argentina. Its average height is about 4,000 metres.

The Andes were formed by the subduction of the Nazca and Antarctic plates beneath the South American plate. Their highest peak, and the highest mountain outside Asia, is Aconcagua at 6,961 metres. The range contains the Altiplano, the most extensive high plateau in the world after Tibet, and Lake Titicaca, the world's highest navigable lake.

Regions

The Andes are commonly divided into the Northern Andes of Venezuela, Colombia and northern Ecuador; the Central Andes of Peru and Bolivia, the widest part of the range; and the Southern Andes of Chile and Argentina, which include Patagonia. Glaciers in the tropical Andes supply water to millions of people and have been shrinking rapidly.

People and history

The Andes were home to the Inca Empire, the largest empire in pre-Columbian America, and to earlier cultures such as the Norte Chico, Chavín, Moche, Tiwanaku and Wari. Crops domesticated in the Andes include the potato, quinoa and the tomato. Highland peoples domesticated llamas and alpacas.
