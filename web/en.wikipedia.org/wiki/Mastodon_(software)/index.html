Mastodon (software)

Mastodon is free and open-source software for running self-hosted social networking services. It has microblogging features similar to those of Twitter, but is built on a decentralised network of independently operated servers, called instances, whose users can interact with each other across servers. Mastodon is part of the Fediverse: instances communicate using the ActivityPub protocol, which allows interoperability with other compatible software such as PeerTube and Pixelfed. The project was created by German developer Eugen Rochko in 2016 and is developed by the non-profit Mastodon gGmbH.


The Mastodon web interface, showing the multi-column layout of the advanced web view.

Features

Posts on Mastodon, historically called "toots", have a default character limit of 500, exceeding Twitter's original 140 and later 280 characters. Users can adjust the visibility of each post (public, unlisted, followers-only, or direct), attach media with accessibility descriptions, add content warnings, and use hashtags and custom emoji. Timelines are chronological rather than algorithmic. Each instance sets its own moderation policies and code of conduct, and instances may block ("defederate") other instances whose content or behaviour they reject.

History

Rochko began developing Mastodon in 2016, motivated by dissatisfaction with centralised platforms, and launched the flagship instance mastodon.social in October of that year. The software gained successive waves of attention, notably in April 2017 and again during controversies over moderation on Twitter. Its largest growth came in late 2022, following Elon Musk's acquisition of Twitter, when millions of users created Mastodon accounts and the network passed ten million registered accounts in early 2023. Major organisations, including the European Commission and several news outlets and governments, have established official instances.

Technology

Mastodon is written with Ruby on Rails for the server, React and Redux for the web interface, and uses PostgreSQL, Redis, and Sidekiq for its background processing. Mobile and desktop access is available through official apps for iOS and Android as well as many third-party clients. Development is funded through crowdfunding, principally Patreon, and grants.
