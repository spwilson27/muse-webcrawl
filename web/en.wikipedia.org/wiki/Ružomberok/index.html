Ružomberok

Ružomberok Country: Slovakia
Region: Žilina
District: Ružomberok
First mentioned: 1233
Elevation: 494 m
Population: approx. 27,000

Ružomberok (German: Rosenberg; Hungarian: Rózsahegy) is a town in northern Slovakia, in the historical Liptov region, situated on the Váh River at the meeting point of the Great Fatra, Choč Mountains and Low Tatra ranges. It is an important crossroads of Slovak road and rail transport and a centre of the paper and textile industries.

History

Settled in the Bronze Age, the site was first mentioned in documents in 1233. German colonists founded the settlement in the 13th century, and it received town privileges in 1318. Ružomberok grew as a market and craft town, noted for pottery, weaving and timber rafting on the Váh. In the 19th and 20th centuries it industrialised, with cotton mills and, above all, paper production; the large SCP paper mill (now Mondi SCP) became one of the biggest employers in the region.

Nearby Černová was the birthplace of Andrej Hlinka, the interwar Slovak nationalist leader. In 1907 the village was the scene of the Černová tragedy, in which gendarmes shot dead 15 villagers during a dispute over the consecration of the local church—an event that drew international attention to Slovak grievances within the Kingdom of Hungary.

Sights

The town's landmarks include the historic main square with the town hall, the Gothic-Baroque Church of St Andrew, and the mausoleum of Andrej Hlinka. On a hill above the town stands the Calvary church of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross. A few kilometres away, the village of Vlkolínec—a remarkably preserved mountain settlement of log houses—is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and administratively part of Ružomberok. The surrounding mountains, including the Malinô Brdo ski area, draw tourists year-round.
