West Lulworth is a village and civil parish in Dorset, England, situated on the English Channel coast about 9 miles (14 km) west of Swanage. The village lies immediately inland from Lulworth Cove, a near-perfectly circular bay cut into the strata of the Jurassic Coast, and is a centre for visitors to the cove and to the natural limestone arch of Durdle Door, about a mile to the west.

Geography and geology

The cove was formed where the sea breached a band of hard Portland limestone and eroded the softer clays and sands behind it, leaving bands of chalk and limestone as enclosing arms; nearby Stair Hole shows further folded strata (the "Lulworth crumple") at an early stage of the same process. The coastline east of the village forms part of the Lulworth Ranges, used by the British Army for armoured vehicle training, so public access to that stretch of coast path is restricted to published firing-free days. West Lulworth parish church, Holy Trinity, was largely rebuilt in the 19th century; the village has a heritage centre explaining the geology of the cove.

History

The Lulworth estate, centred on Lulworth Castle in neighbouring East Lulworth, has been owned by the Weld family since the 17th century. Smuggling was common along this coast in the 18th and early 19th centuries, and the village later developed a modest fishing and tourist trade. Today tourism dominates: Lulworth Cove receives several hundred thousand visitors a year, making it one of Dorset's most-visited rural attractions.
