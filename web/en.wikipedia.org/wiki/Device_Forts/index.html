Device Forts

The Device Forts are a chain of artillery castles built for Henry VIII between 1539 and 1547 along the south and east coasts of England. They were constructed in response to the threat of invasion from France and the Holy Roman Empire following England's break with the Roman Catholic Church.

History

The fortifications were authorised under Henry's "device" (plan) of 1539, a national programme of defence works. The castles were built in two main waves and included sites at Deal, Walmer, Sandown, Hurst, Calshot, Sandgate and Portland, as well as blockhouses defending harbours such as Falmouth and the Thames estuary.

Design

The forts were designed for the age of gunpowder artillery, with concentric, rounded bastions presenting curved surfaces to deflect shot and providing overlapping fields of fire. Many were built of stone robbed from dissolved monasteries.

Later use

Several forts remained in military use into the twentieth century; many are now in the care of English Heritage and open to the public.
