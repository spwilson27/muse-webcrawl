Academy format

Aspect ratio1.375:1 (commonly called 1.37:1)
Film stock35 mm
Standardised1932, Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences

The Academy format is a standard film format with an aspect ratio of 1.375:1, adopted by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences in 1932. It modified the earlier silent-era full aperture ratio of about 1.33:1 by shrinking the frame to make room for an optical soundtrack on the film strip.

History

With the arrival of sound-on-film, the soundtrack strip displaced part of the image area, producing a nearly square picture unless the frame height was reduced. The Academy standardised a camera aperture of 0.868 × 0.631 inches (22.1 × 16.0 mm) with the image offset to accommodate the soundtrack, yielding the 1.375:1 ratio that defined most studio filmmaking from the 1930s to the early 1950s.

Decline and legacy

After the introduction of widescreen processes such as CinemaScope (1953) and Cinerama, theatrical films moved to wider ratios, but Academy ratio remained common in television until the widescreen era, since the 4:3 TV frame approximated it. Some modern directors deliberately shoot in or near Academy ratio for artistic effect, as in Ida, The Grand Budapest Hotel (in part) and First Reformed.
