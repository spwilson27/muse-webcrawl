Hohhot, formerly known as Kweisui, is the capital of Inner Mongolia in northern China. It is a prefecture-level city and the region's political, economic and cultural centre. Its name comes from the Mongolian for "blue city", referring to the colour associated with the sky and with local temples.

History

The city was founded in the late 16th century by Altan Khan, a Mongol leader, around the Dazhao Temple. It grew as a trading post on routes between China proper and Mongolia. After the founding of the People's Republic it became capital of Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region in 1952.

Geography

Hohhot lies in south-central Inner Mongolia on the Tumochuan Plain, north of the Dahei River, with the Yin Mountains to the north. It has a cold semi-arid continental climate with hot summers and very cold, dry winters.

Culture and sights

Landmarks include Dazhao Temple, the Five Pagoda Temple (Wuta Si), Xilitu Zhao, and the tomb of Wang Zhaojun. The Inner Mongolia Museum displays dinosaur fossils and Mongol history.

Economy

Dairy processing (Yili and Mengniu are headquartered here), energy, petrochemicals and textiles are major industries. The city is a rail and air hub for the region.
