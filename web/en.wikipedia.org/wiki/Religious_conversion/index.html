Religious conversion

Religious conversion is the adoption of a set of beliefs, or membership in a religious community, identified with one particular religion to the exclusion of others. Conversion may involve a profound transformation of worldview, ritual initiation such as baptism, or formal declaration such as the Shahada in Islam.

Causes and processes

Scholars distinguish sudden, dramatic conversions — such as Paul's on the road to Damascus — from gradual processes of affiliation through social networks, marriage, migration or intellectual persuasion. Conversion experiences are studied by psychologists, sociologists and historians of religion, and are central to the history of missionary religions such as Christianity, Islam and Buddhism.

Legal and social dimensions

Freedom to change religion is recognised in Article 18 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. In some countries, conversion — especially from the majority religion — is restricted by anti-conversion laws or punishable as apostasy, making it a contested human rights issue.
