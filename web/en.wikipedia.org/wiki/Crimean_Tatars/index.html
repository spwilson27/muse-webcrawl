Crimean Tatars

Crimean Tatars (Qırımtatarlar)
RegionsCrimea, Ukraine; large diaspora in Turkey, Uzbekistan, Romania
PopulationRoughly 250,000–500,000 in Crimea; millions of descendants in Turkey
LanguageCrimean Tatar, Russian, Ukrainian
ReligionPredominantly Sunni Islam

The Crimean Tatars are a Turkic ethnic group and an indigenous people of Crimea, formed from the mixing of Turkic peoples (especially Cumans/Kipchaks) with the peninsula's earlier inhabitants — Greeks, Goths, Scythians, and others. Their language, Crimean Tatar, belongs to the Kipchak branch of Turkic languages.

The Khan's Palace in Bakhchysarai.

History

From 1441 the Crimean Tatars were ruled by the Crimean Khanate under the Giray dynasty, a vassal of the Ottoman Empire from 1478, which controlled Crimea and the northern Black Sea steppe for over three centuries. The Russian Empire annexed Crimea in 1783 under Catherine the Great, prompting waves of Tatar emigration to the Ottoman Empire. After the Crimean War and again in the 20th century, the community shrank drastically.

Deportation and return

On 18 May 1944, on Stalin's orders, the entire Crimean Tatar population — roughly 190,000 people — was deported to Central Asia, chiefly Uzbekistan, accused collectively of collaboration with Nazi Germany. A large share died in transit and exile. The event, the Sürgünlik, is commemorated annually and recognised by Ukraine and several other states as genocide. Barred from returning for decades, Crimean Tatars mounted one of the Soviet Union's most persistent dissident movements, led by figures such as Mustafa Dzhemilev; mass return began only in the late 1980s.

Since 2014

After Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, the Tatars' representative body, the Mejlis, was banned as "extremist" in 2016, and activists have faced prosecution and pressure; many left Crimea. Ukraine's 2021 law on indigenous peoples formally recognised Crimean Tatars, Karaites, and Krymchaks as indigenous to Ukraine.
