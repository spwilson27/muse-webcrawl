The Segway is a two-wheeled, self-balancing battery-electric vehicle invented by Dean Kamen. Unveiled in 2001 amid extraordinary hype as the Segway HT (Human Transporter), later renamed the Segway PT (Personal Transporter), it carries a standing rider on a platform between two parallel wheels, using gyroscopes and tilt sensors to balance like an inverted pendulum; the rider steers and changes speed by leaning, and later models by a tilting handlebar column.

Development and reception

Kamen's company developed the balancing technology from his iBOT wheelchair. Before launch, the secret project (code-named "Ginger") was predicted by prominent technologists to transform urban transport. On release, the PT cost about US$5,000, reached 12.5 mph (20 km/h), and had a range of roughly 15–24 miles (24–39 km) per charge. Sales fell far short of forecasts — around 140,000 units over two decades — hampered by price, weight, and ambiguous legal status: many jurisdictions treated it as neither pedestrian nor vehicle, restricting sidewalk or road use.

Use and legacy

Segways found niches in guided city tours, warehouse work, and patrolling by police and security staff. Chinese firm Ninebot acquired Segway in 2015, and the brand continued on smaller self-balancing scooters and e-scooters; production of the original PT ended in 2020. Though a commercial disappointment relative to its hype, the Segway pioneered practical self-balancing transport and popularised the personal-mobility category.
