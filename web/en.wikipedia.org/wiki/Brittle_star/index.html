Brittle star

Brittle star
Class: Ophiuroidea
Phylum: Echinodermata
Habitat: marine, worldwide

Brittle stars, or ophiuroids, are echinoderms of the class Ophiuroidea, closely related to starfish. They have a distinct central disc and five long, slender, flexible arms, which they use to row themselves along the sea floor. The arms readily break off when attacked — hence the name — but can regenerate.

Anatomy and behaviour

Unlike starfish, brittle stars have no anus and a closed digestive system, and their internal organs are confined to the central disc. Most are scavengers or detritus feeders; some are suspension feeders or predators of small organisms. They are found from the intertidal zone to abyssal depths, sometimes in dense aggregations.

Diversity

More than 2,000 species are known, making ophiuroids the most diverse class of echinoderms. Basket stars are a subgroup with intricately branching arms.
