Lipschitz quaternion

Named for: Rudolf Lipschitz

Also called: Lipschitz integer

Field: Algebra, number theory

Related concept: Hurwitz quaternion

In mathematics, a Lipschitz quaternion (or Lipschitz integer) is a quaternion whose components are all integers. The set of all Lipschitz quaternions forms a subring of the ring of all quaternions, and was studied by Rudolf Lipschitz. Lipschitz quaternions were historically important in the development of the arithmetic of quaternions and in proofs about sums of four squares.

Definition

A quaternion is an expression of the form a + bi + cj + dk, where a, b, c, d are real numbers and i, j, k are the fundamental quaternion units satisfying i² = j² = k² = ijk = −1. A Lipschitz quaternion is one for which all of a, b, c, d are integers. The Lipschitz quaternions form a ring under quaternion addition and multiplication, but this ring lacks some desirable properties of the integers: in particular, it is not a unique factorization domain, and not every element with norm a rational prime can be factored nicely.

Relation to Hurwitz quaternions

Adolf Hurwitz introduced the Hurwitz quaternions, which allow the components to be either all integers or all half-integers. The Hurwitz quaternions form a maximal order containing the Lipschitz quaternions with index 2, and they enjoy a Euclidean domain structure that makes the arithmetic of sums of four squares much cleaner. Lagrange's four-square theorem, which states that every natural number is the sum of four integer squares, can be proved using the arithmetic of Hurwitz quaternions.
