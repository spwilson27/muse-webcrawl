Speed is a 1994 American action thriller film directed by Jan de Bont in his directorial debut, written by Graham Yost, and starring Keanu Reeves, Dennis Hopper, and Sandra Bullock. The film follows Los Angeles police officer Jack Traven, who must prevent a bomb on a city bus from exploding — the bomb is armed once the bus exceeds 50 miles per hour and will detonate if its speed drops below that threshold.

Plot

After SWAT officers Jack Traven and Harry Temple foil a bomber's elevator extortion plot, the bomber — embittered former bomb-squad officer Howard Payne (Hopper) — retaliates with the bus device in pursuit of a $3.7 million ransom. Jack boards the moving bus and, with passenger Annie Porter (Bullock) taking the wheel after the driver is shot, races through Los Angeles traffic, leaping a 50-foot gap in an unfinished freeway ramp and eventually reaching Los Angeles International Airport, where the passengers are rescued and the bus crashes into a cargo plane. Payne escapes, kidnaps Annie, and rigs her with explosives in the subway, leading to a final confrontation on a runaway train.

Reception and legacy

Speed was a major critical and commercial success, grossing about $350 million worldwide on a budget of roughly $30–37 million, and won two Academy Awards, for Best Sound and Best Sound Effects Editing. It propelled Bullock to stardom and established de Bont as an action director. A sequel, Speed 2: Cruise Control (1997), set aboard a cruise ship, was a critical and commercial failure; Reeves did not return. The premise has made the original a frequently cited benchmark of the high-concept action genre.
