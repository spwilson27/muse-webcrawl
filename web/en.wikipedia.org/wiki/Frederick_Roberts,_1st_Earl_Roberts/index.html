Field Marshal Frederick Sleigh Roberts, 1st Earl Roberts (30 September 1832 – 14 November 1914), popularly known as "Bobs," was a British soldier of the Victorian era who became one of the most successful commanders of the British Army and a national hero.

Early career and the Victoria Cross

Born in India, the son of a general, Roberts was commissioned into the Bengal Artillery. During the Indian Rebellion of 1857 he won the Victoria Cross for gallantry at Khudaganj in January 1858, where he pursued mutineers, captured a standard, and cut down a sepoy about to kill a wounded officer.

Afghanistan and Burma

Roberts won fame commanding in the Second Anglo-Afghan War (1878–80), capturing Kabul and making the celebrated march from Kabul to Kandahar — some 300 miles in 23 days — to relieve the besieged garrison and defeat Ayub Khan's army. He later commanded the expedition that occupied Rangoon in the Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885).

Commander-in-Chief and the Boer War

Roberts served as Commander-in-Chief of India (1885–1893) and then of Ireland. After early British reverses in the Second Boer War, the elderly Roberts was sent to South Africa in December 1899, relieving Kimberley, forcing the surrender of General Cronjé at Paardeberg, and occupying Bloemfontein, Johannesburg, and Pretoria before handing over to Kitchener. He was the last Commander-in-Chief of the Forces (1901–1904) before the office was abolished.

Later years

In retirement Roberts campaigned tirelessly for military preparedness and compulsory military training through the National Service League. He died of pneumonia in November 1914 while visiting Indian troops serving in France during the First World War, and received a state funeral, lying in state at Westminster Hall.
