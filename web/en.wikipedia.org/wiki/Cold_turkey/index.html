Cold turkey

Meaning: Abrupt, unaided cessation of a habit or substance use

Cold turkey refers to the abrupt discontinuation of a habit-forming drug or addictive behaviour, rather than gradually tapering use or using medical assistance. The phrase is most often used about smoking, alcohol, opioids and other drugs, but can also describe quitting habits such as gambling or a food.

Withdrawal

Abrupt cessation can produce withdrawal symptoms whose severity depends on the substance, dose, duration of use and the person's health. Symptoms may include anxiety, irritability, sweating, tremor, nausea, insomnia and cravings. For alcohol and some sedatives, sudden withdrawal can be medically dangerous and supervised detoxification may be recommended.

Etymology

The origin is uncertain. Explanations link the pale, clammy, goose-bumped skin of withdrawal to cold turkey flesh, or the idea of quitting plainly and without preparation, as a dish served cold and unadorned. The expression was in use in the early 20th century.

In culture

The phrase appears in songs and films, including John Lennon's 1969 single "Cold Turkey", written about heroin withdrawal, and the 1971 film Cold Turkey.
