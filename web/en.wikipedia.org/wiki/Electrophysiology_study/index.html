Electrophysiology study (EPS)
Field: cardiology
Access: catheter via femoral vein
Uses: arrhythmia diagnosis, ablation guidance

An electrophysiology study (EPS) is an invasive cardiac procedure in which electrode catheters are threaded through blood vessels into the heart to record its electrical activity and to provoke and analyse abnormal rhythms (arrhythmias) under controlled conditions.

Procedure

Under local anaesthesia and sedation, catheters are usually introduced through the femoral vein and positioned in the right atrium, near the bundle of His, at the right ventricular apex, and often in the coronary sinus. Programmed electrical stimulation — timed extra beats — is used to induce tachycardias so their mechanism and origin can be identified.

Indications

EPS is used to investigate unexplained syncope, to classify supraventricular and ventricular tachycardias, to assess the conduction system in bundle branch block, and to guide catheter ablation, in which the tissue causing the arrhythmia is destroyed by radiofrequency or cryotherapy energy during the same procedure.

Risks

Risks are low but include bleeding at the access site, blood vessel or heart perforation, blood clots, and rarely the need for urgent surgery or a pacemaker.
