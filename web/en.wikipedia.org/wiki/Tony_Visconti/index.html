Anthony Edward Visconti (born April 24, 1944) is an American record producer, musician, and singer. He is best known for his decades-long collaboration with David Bowie, producing many of Bowie's albums from Space Oddity (1969) through the Berlin Trilogy to Blackstar (2016).

Early career

Born in Brooklyn, Visconti played guitar in bands as a teenager and worked as an assistant at New York studios before moving to London in the late 1960s to work for music publisher and producer Denny Cordell. There he produced Tyrannosaurus Rex (later T. Rex), becoming the architect of Marc Bolan's glam-rock sound on albums such as Electric Warrior (1971) and The Slider (1972), and worked with the Move, Procol Harum, and Badfinger.

Work with David Bowie

Visconti first worked with Bowie as bassist and arranger on Space Oddity (1969) and produced The Man Who Sold the World (1970) and Diamond Dogs (1974). He co-produced the Berlin Trilogy with Bowie and Brian Eno—Low (1977), "Heroes" (1977), and Lodger (1979)—and produced Scary Monsters (and Super Creeps) (1980). After a long interval he returned for Heathen (2002), Reality (2003), The Next Day (2013), and Bowie's final album Blackstar (2016), released two days before Bowie's death.

Other work

Visconti has produced albums for the Moody Blues, Thin Lizzy, Sparks, Morrissey, the Kaiser Chiefs, and Angélique Kidjo, among many others, and won Grammy Awards for his work with Kidjo and for Blackstar. He also served as a judge/mentor on television talent programs and published the autobiography Bowie, Bolan and the Brooklyn Boy (2007). From 2016 he co-led the Bowie tribute project Holy Holy as bassist and producer.
