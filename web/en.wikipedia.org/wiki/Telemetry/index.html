Telemetry

Telemetry is the in situ collection of measurements or other data at remote points and their automatic transmission to receiving equipment for monitoring. The word is derived from the Greek roots tele, "remote", and metron, "measure". Systems may be guided by instructions and data transferred through it, in which case it overlaps with telecontrol.

History and uses

Telemetry began with wired systems in the 19th century — for example, monitoring of electrical parameters and weather instruments — and expanded with radio in the early 20th century. Modern telemetry is essential to spacecraft and missiles, weather balloons and sonobuoys, motorsport (Formula One cars send thousands of parameters), wildlife tracking collars, medical monitoring, utility metering, and industrial process control.

Standards

International standards for telemetry are maintained by bodies such as the Inter-Range Instrumentation Group (IRIG) for test ranges and the Consultative Committee for Space Data Systems (CCSDS) for space missions.
