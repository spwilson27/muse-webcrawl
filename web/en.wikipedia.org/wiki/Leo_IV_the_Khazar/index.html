Leo IV the Khazar (Greek: Λέων ὁ Χάζαρος; 25 January 750 – 18 June 780) was Byzantine emperor from 775 to 780. His epithet derives from his mother, the Khazar princess Tzitzak (baptised Irene), first wife of Constantine V.

Reign

Crowned co-emperor as an infant in 751, Leo succeeded his father Constantine V in 775. Though raised in the iconoclast tradition of the Isaurian dynasty, he was comparatively moderate: he relaxed persecution, allowed monks to return to office, and appointed an iconophile-leaning patriarch. Nevertheless, in 780 he acted harshly when icons were discovered in the possession of his wife Irene's attendants, confiscating property and punishing officials.

Militarily his reign saw successful campaigns against the Abbasid Caliphate in 776–778, including a victory near Germanikeia, and continued pressure against the Bulgars. He died suddenly in June 780 — later sources claim from a fever brought on by wearing a jewelled crown once dedicated in Hagia Sophia — and was succeeded by his nine-year-old son Constantine VI under the regency of Irene, who would reverse iconoclasm.
