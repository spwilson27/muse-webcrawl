Shandur Polo Festival

The Shandur Polo Festival is an annual sports festival held at the Shandur Pass in northern Pakistan, at an altitude of about 3,700 metres (12,200 feet), on what is often described as the highest polo ground in the world. The festival, held each July, centres on freestyle polo matches between teams from Chitral and Gilgit, a rivalry dating back to the 1930s.

History

Polo has been played in the mountain valleys of the Hindu Kush and Karakoram for centuries, and the high pastures of Shandur, lying between Chitral and Gilgit, were a natural meeting ground. The annual tournament was formalised in 1936, when the British political agent in Gilgit is said to have encouraged regular matches between the neighbouring districts. The festival lapsed at times during periods of conflict and hardship but was revived and promoted as a tourist event from the 1980s onward.

The game

The polo played at Shandur follows traditional local rules rather than international ones. There are few restrictions, no umpire-enforced fouls of the kind seen in regulated polo, and the game is fast and physical. Each team fields six players, and matches are played in two halves. A goal is celebrated with music and dancing, and the scoring player is traditionally honoured by the crowd.

The festival

Besides the polo, the festival features folk music, dancing, traditional food, and camping on the plateau beside Shandur Lake. It attracts thousands of spectators, including foreign tourists, who travel over the Shandur Pass road from either the Chitral or the Ghizer side. The event is organised with the support of the provincial government and the Pakistan Army and has become a significant part of the region's tourism calendar.
