Texas Education Agency

Formed: 1949

Headquarters: William B. Travis State Office Building, Austin, Texas

Agency executive: Commissioner of Education

Jurisdiction: State of Texas

The Texas Education Agency (TEA) is the branch of the government of Texas responsible for the state supervision and oversight of public primary and secondary education. It is headed by the Commissioner of Education, who is appointed by the Governor of Texas. The agency oversees more than 1,000 independent school districts and charter schools serving over five million students.

History

The modern agency was created by the Gilmer–Aikin Laws of 1949, which reorganized Texas public education, replacing the elected State Superintendent of Public Instruction with an appointed commissioner and establishing the State Board of Education as an elected policy-making body.

Functions

TEA administers the state's accountability system, rating schools and districts under the A–F rating framework; oversees the Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS) curriculum standards; administers statewide assessments including the State of Texas Assessments of Academic Readiness (STAAR); distributes state funding to districts; and certifies educators through the State Board for Educator Certification. The agency also monitors district compliance with state and federal law and may intervene in persistently low-performing districts, including appointing conservators or boards of managers.

Headquarters

The agency is headquartered in the William B. Travis State Office Building at 1701 North Congress Avenue in Austin, Texas.
