Sustainable finance
Sustainable finance integrates environmental, social and governance criteria into investment
FieldFinance, investment
Key frameworksESG criteria; UN Principles for Responsible Investment (2006); EU Taxonomy
InstrumentsGreen bonds, social bonds, sustainability-linked loans, ESG funds

Sustainable finance is the process of taking environmental, social and governance (ESG) considerations into account when making investment and lending decisions, directing capital toward activities considered sustainable and away from harmful ones. It encompasses ESG investing, green and social bonds, sustainability-linked loans, impact investing, climate finance and the integration of climate risk into prudential regulation.

Development

Roots lie in ethical and socially responsible investing — religious investors avoiding "sin stocks" — but the modern field dates from the mid-2000s: the UN-backed Principles for Responsible Investment (2006) committed signatory investors to incorporate ESG issues, and the European Investment Bank and World Bank issued the first green bonds in 2007–2008. The Paris Agreement (2015) and the EU's Action Plan on Sustainable Finance (2018) and Taxonomy Regulation (2020) moved the field into mainstream regulation, alongside disclosure regimes and central-bank climate stress tests through networks such as the NGFS.

Instruments and markets

Labelled green, social and sustainability bonds grew into a market of several trillion US dollars in cumulative issuance; sustainability-linked instruments tie borrowing costs to the issuer's performance against ESG targets. Asset managers market thousands of ESG funds, and global sustainable investment was estimated in the tens of trillions of dollars, though totals depend heavily on definition.

Criticism

The field faces persistent criticism of "greenwashing", inconsistent ESG ratings (different agencies rate the same company very differently), and doubts that portfolio strategies change real-world emissions. From the early 2020s it also met a political backlash in parts of the United States, where some states restricted ESG considerations in public funds, while regulators in Europe and elsewhere tightened anti-greenwashing rules and fund-labelling standards.
