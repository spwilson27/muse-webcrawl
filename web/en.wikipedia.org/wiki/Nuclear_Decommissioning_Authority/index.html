Nuclear Decommissioning Authority

The Nuclear Decommissioning Authority (NDA) is a non-departmental public body of the United Kingdom, sponsored by the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero. Established under the Energy Act 2004 and formally launched on 1 April 2005, it is responsible for the decommissioning and clean-up of Britain's civil public-sector nuclear sites and for ensuring that the country's nuclear legacy is managed safely.

Role and sites

The NDA owns and oversees a portfolio of sites across the United Kingdom, including Sellafield, the Magnox reactor stations, Dounreay, and the Low Level Waste Repository. Its work is carried out through subsidiary companies and site licence companies, including Sellafield Ltd, Magnox Ltd, Dounreay Site Restoration Ltd and Nuclear Waste Services. The programme deals with some of the most complex environmental clean-up challenges in Europe, including the retrieval and treatment of legacy wastes stored in ageing facilities at Sellafield.

Strategy and funding

The NDA publishes a strategy, revised periodically, setting out how it will manage decommissioning, spent fuel, nuclear materials and waste over a programme expected to span more than a century. It is funded primarily by government grant, supplemented historically by commercial income from reprocessing and electricity generation. It also oversees arrangements for geological disposal of higher-activity waste through Nuclear Waste Services and the search for a host community for a geological disposal facility.
