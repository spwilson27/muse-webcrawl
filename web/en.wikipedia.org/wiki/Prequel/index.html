Prequel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
A prequel tells a story that precedes an earlier work in its fictional chronology.

A prequel is a work of literature, film, television, theatre, video games, or other creative form that is set chronologically before the events of an earlier-published work, focusing on events that occur before the original narrative. Like sequels, prequels may or may not concern the same plot as the work from which they are derived, but often they do.

Prequels are usually careful not to contradict facts established in the original work, but they sometimes do, usually for reasons of artistic licence. The term "prequel" is a 20th-century coinage, a portmanteau of the prefix "pre-" (from Latin prae, "before") and "sequel".

Usage

Rather than implying a general concept of a successor work, the term "prequel" has come to be applied to a work that is released after a previous work, but is chronologically before it, or otherwise in some sense a predecessor or prelude to it. Although the term is of recent origin, works fitting this concept existed long before. The Cypria, an epic of the Trojan cycle, presupposes knowledge of the events of the Homeric Iliad, and was thus composed as a kind of introduction to it—that is, a prequel—although the Iliad itself was committed to writing much earlier.

History

The concept of the prequel gained widespread currency in popular culture with the success of later installments of major franchises. The Star Wars prequel trilogy (1999–2005), beginning with Star Wars: Episode I – The Phantom Menace, is often credited with popularizing the term, although earlier examples such as The Godfather Part II (1974)—which interleaved sequel and prequel material—and the novel The Magician's Nephew (1955) in C. S. Lewis's Chronicles of Narnia predate it.

Related concepts

A prequel differs from a reboot, which discards continuity, and from a remake, which retells the same story. Other related terms include "sidequel" (a work set during the same time frame as a previous work), "interquel" (set between two earlier works), and "midquel".
