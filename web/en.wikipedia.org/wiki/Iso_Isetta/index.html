Iso Isetta

ManufacturerIso S.p.A.
Production1953–1956 (Italy)
ClassMicrocar
Engine236 cc single-cylinder two-stroke
DesignerErmenegildo Preti and Pierluigi Raggi

The Iso Isetta is an Italian microcar produced by Iso S.p.A. of Bresso between 1953 and 1956. Designed by Ermenegildo Preti and Pierluigi Raggi, the tiny "bubble car" seated two passengers behind a single front-opening door to which the steering column and instrument panel were attached.

Design

The Isetta used a 236 cc single-cylinder two-stroke motorcycle engine mounted at the rear, giving a top speed of about 85 km/h (53 mph) and very low fuel consumption. Its egg-like body, small wheels placed close together at the rear, and compact dimensions suited post-war Italy's narrow streets and scarce fuel.

Licensed production

Although Iso's own production was modest, the design was licensed abroad with great success — most famously to BMW in Germany, which built the BMW Isetta in large numbers from 1955 and was effectively saved from bankruptcy by it. Versions were also produced in France (VELAM), Spain, Belgium, Brazil (Romi-Isetta) and the United Kingdom. Iso sold the licence and tooling to BMW in 1954 and turned to sports cars such as the Iso Rivolta and Grifo.
