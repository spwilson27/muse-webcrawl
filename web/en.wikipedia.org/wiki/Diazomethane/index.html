Diazomethane

Diazomethane is the chemical compound CH2N2, discovered by Hans von Pechmann in 1894. It is a yellow gas at room temperature and is one of the most common diazo compounds used in the laboratory.

Uses

Diazomethane is a versatile methylating agent: it converts carboxylic acids to methyl esters cleanly and is used in the Arndt–Eistert synthesis to homologate acids. It also serves as a 1,3-dipole in cycloaddition reactions and as a source of methylene in cyclopropanation.

Safety

Diazomethane is highly toxic and dangerously explosive — it can detonate on contact with rough surfaces, ground-glass joints or strong light. It is therefore prepared and used only in dilute ethereal solution, in special smooth-glass apparatus, and increasingly replaced by the safer (trimethylsilyl)diazomethane.
