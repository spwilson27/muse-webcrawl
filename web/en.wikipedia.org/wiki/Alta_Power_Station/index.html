Alta Power Station

The Alta Power Station (Norwegian: Alta kraftverk) is a hydroelectric power station on the Altaelva river in Finnmark, northern Norway. It comprises the Virdnejávri dam — an arch dam about 110 metres high — and a powerhouse with two turbines totalling 150 MW, producing roughly 655 GWh per year. It is owned by Statkraft.

Construction and controversy

The project was approved in 1978 and completed in 1987. Its construction provoked one of the largest environmental protests in Norwegian history: the Alta controversy (Alta-saken) of 1979–1982. Opponents — environmentalists and Sámi activists defending reindeer grazing lands and indigenous rights — mounted demonstrations, hunger strikes and civil disobedience, and the dispute brought down a government and delayed the works. Although the dam was built, the case transformed Norwegian environmental politics and Sámi policy, contributing to the creation of the Sámi Parliament in 1989.

Operation

The reservoir Virdnejávri stores water from the Finnmarksvidda plateau, and management of releases seeks to protect the Altaelva's famous Atlantic salmon fishery downstream in the Sautso canyon.
