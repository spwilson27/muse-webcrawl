Rochester, New York

Rochester is a city in Monroe County, New York, United States, on the southern shore of Lake Ontario. It is the seat of Monroe County and, with a population of about 211,000 at the 2020 census, the third-most populous city in New York State outside the New York City metropolitan area. The Rochester metropolitan area has roughly 1.1 million inhabitants.

History

Rochester grew rapidly after the opening of the Erie Canal in the 1820s as a flour-milling centre powered by the Genesee River falls — the "Flour City" — and later became known as the "Flower City" for its nurseries. It was a centre of abolitionism and the women's rights movement, home to Frederick Douglass and Susan B. Anthony, and later became a major industrial city as the headquarters of Eastman Kodak, Xerox, and Bausch + Lomb, earning the nickname "Image Capital of the World".

Economy and culture

Following the decline of Kodak, the economy has shifted toward education, health care, and optics and photonics; major institutions include the University of Rochester and Rochester Institute of Technology. The city hosts the Eastman School of Music, the Strong National Museum of Play, and the Lilac Festival.
