Tom Wolfe

Thomas Kennerly Wolfe Jr. (March 2, 1930 – May 14, 2018) was an American author and journalist associated with the New Journalism movement. Recognizable by his trademark white suit, he wrote bestselling nonfiction such as The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test and The Right Stuff, and the novel The Bonfire of the Vanities.

Career

Wolfe earned a PhD in American studies from Yale and worked as a newspaper reporter, including at the New York Herald Tribune. His exuberant, scene-driven reporting — rich in status detail, onomatopoeia, and free indirect style — helped define New Journalism, a mode he championed in a 1973 anthology. The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test (1968) chronicled Ken Kesey and the Merry Pranksters; The Right Stuff (1979), about test pilots and the first astronauts, was adapted into a major film.

Fiction and later work

His first novel, The Bonfire of the Vanities (1987), a satire of 1980s New York greed and racial politics, was a major bestseller. Later novels include A Man in Full (1998), I Am Charlotte Simmons (2004), and Back to Blood (2012). Wolfe coined or popularized phrases such as "radical chic," "the Me Decade," and "masters of the universe."
