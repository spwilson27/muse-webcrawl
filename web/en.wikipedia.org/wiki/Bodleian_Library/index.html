The Bodleian Library is the main research library of the University of Oxford and one of the oldest libraries in Europe. Founded in its modern form by Sir Thomas Bodley and reopened in 1602, it holds more than 13 million printed items and, as one of the six legal deposit libraries of the United Kingdom, is entitled to receive a free copy of every book published in the UK and Ireland. Its historic buildings around Radcliffe Square — including Duke Humfrey's Library, the Divinity School, and the Radcliffe Camera — are icons of the city.

History

Oxford's first university library, established in the 14th century and enriched by Humphrey, Duke of Gloucester, was dispersed during the Reformation. Bodley, a retired diplomat and fellow of Merton College, refounded it in 1598–1602, furnishing the building, buying books, and securing in 1610 an agreement with the Stationers' Company of London to deposit copies of new books — the seed of its legal-deposit status. Readers famously include kings, prime ministers, and writers from Tolkien to Oscar Wilde; books may not be borrowed and must be consulted in the reading rooms, a rule tradition says even Charles I could not break.

Collections and sites

The Bodleian's treasures include four copies of Magna Carta, a Shakespeare First Folio, the Codex Mendoza, and vast archives and manuscripts from around the world. Since 2015 its public face has been the Weston Library, exhibiting treasures and special collections, while most storage sits in facilities including the Book Storage Facility in Swindon.
