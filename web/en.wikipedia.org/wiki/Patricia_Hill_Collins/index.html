Patricia Hill Collins

Patricia Hill Collins (born May 1, 1948) is an American academic specializing in race, class, and gender. She is a Distinguished University Professor of Sociology Emerita at the University of Maryland, College Park, and the former head of the Department of African American Studies at the University of Cincinnati. In 2009 she became the 100th president of the American Sociological Association, the first African-American woman to hold that position.

Work

Collins is best known for her book Black Feminist Thought: Knowledge, Consciousness, and the Politics of Empowerment (1990; second edition 2000), which won the American Sociological Association's Jessie Bernard Award and became a foundational text of Black feminist sociology. In it she developed the concepts of the "matrix of domination" and the "outsider within", analysing how race, class, and gender form interlocking systems of oppression.

Collins is widely credited with helping to develop and popularise the concept of intersectionality, building on the work of Kimberlé Crenshaw and the Black feminist tradition. Her later books include Black Sexual Politics (2004), which won the ASA's Distinguished Publication Award, From Black Power to Hip Hop (2006), On Intellectual Activism (2012), and Intersectionality as Critical Social Theory (2019).

Education and honours

Collins earned her BA from Brandeis University in 1969, a master's degree from Harvard University, and her PhD in sociology from Brandeis in 1984. In 2022 she was awarded the Berggruen Prize for Philosophy and Culture. Her work emphasises the knowledge produced within Black women's communities and the role of scholarship as a form of social justice activism.
