Shanghai Tower

Height: 632 m (2,073 ft)
Floors: 128 above ground
Construction: 2008–2015
Architect: Gensler (Jun Xia, design principal)
Location: Lujiazui, Pudong, Shanghai

The Shanghai Tower (Chinese: 上海中心大厦; pinyin: Shànghǎi Zhōngxīn Dàshà) is a 128-storey, 632-metre (2,073 ft) supertall skyscraper in Lujiazui, Pudong, Shanghai. It is the tallest building in China and the third-tallest building in the world, after Dubai's Burj Khalifa and Malaysia's Merdeka 118. It was designed by the American architectural firm Gensler, with Chinese architect Jun Xia as design principal, and was completed in 2015, opening fully to tenants in the following years.

Design

The tower is organized as nine cylindrical buildings stacked on top of each other and enclosed by a twisting glass outer facade that rotates about 120 degrees as it rises — a form that reduces wind loads by roughly a quarter and evokes a dragon or a traditional Chinese concept of the Earth (round) over Heaven. Between the inner cylindrical block and the outer triangular facade are sky lobbies with atrium gardens at each nine-storey "neighbourhood." The building was conceived as a vertical city, mixing offices, the J Hotel (among the highest hotels in the world), retail, cultural space, and observation decks near the summit, which contain one of the world's highest observation platforms and a large tuned mass damper visible to visitors.

Engineering and sustainability

Shanghai Tower holds LEED Platinum certification and was designed with geothermal exchange, rainwater recycling, cogeneration, and a double-skin facade that insulates the building. Its Mitsubishi Electric elevators travel at up to 20.5 metres per second (74 km/h), among the fastest in the world. Construction on Shanghai's soft alluvial soil required a massive raft foundation on nearly a thousand bored piles and one of the largest single concrete pours ever undertaken at the time.

Context

The tower completes the trio of supertalls in Lujiazui planned in the 1990s master plan, standing beside the Jin Mao Tower (421 m, 1999) and the Shanghai World Financial Center (492 m, 2008), which it dwarfs. Together they symbolise Shanghai's emergence as a global financial centre.
