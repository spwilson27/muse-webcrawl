Alton Glenn Miller (March 1, 1904 – December 15, 1944) was an American big band conductor, arranger, composer, and trombonist, and one of the most popular and commercially successful dance bandleaders of the swing era. His Glenn Miller Orchestra recorded signature hits including "In the Mood", "Moonlight Serenade", "Pennsylvania 6-5000", "Chattanooga Choo Choo" and "Tuxedo Junction".

Career

Miller played with bands including those of Benny Goodman and the Dorsey brothers before forming his own orchestra in 1937. After initial failure, a reorganised band found success in 1939 with engagements at the Glen Island Casino and hit records built on Miller's distinctive reed sound — a clarinet leading four saxophones. From 1939 to 1942 the band was one of the best-selling recording acts in the world, and "Chattanooga Choo Choo" (1941) became the first record certified as a gold disc.

Military service and disappearance

In 1942 Miller enlisted, joining the Army Air Forces, where he led the Army Air Force Band, modernised military music, and raised morale with broadcasts and concerts. On December 15, 1944, Major Miller boarded a UC-64 Norseman aircraft in England to fly to Paris to arrange concerts; the plane disappeared over the English Channel and was never found. His death remains officially missing in action, and numerous theories have been proposed.

Legacy

The 1954 film The Glenn Miller Story, starring James Stewart, cemented his legend. The official Glenn Miller Orchestra continues to tour, and his recordings remain staples of the big band repertoire.
