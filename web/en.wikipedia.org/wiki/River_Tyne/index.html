River Tyne

SourcesSouth Tyne and North Tyne
MouthNorth Sea at Tynemouth / South Shields
Lengthabout 73 km (45 mi) from the confluence at Hexham
CitiesNewcastle upon Tyne, Gateshead

The River Tyne is a river in North East England. It is formed by the confluence of the North Tyne and South Tyne at Warden Rock near Hexham. The North Tyne rises on the Scottish border and flows through Kielder Water, England's largest artificial lake by capacity; the South Tyne rises in Cumbria and passes Alston and Haltwhistle. From Hexham the combined river flows east through Corbridge, Newcastle upon Tyne and Gateshead to the North Sea at Tynemouth.

Bridges and industry

The Tyne at Newcastle is crossed by a celebrated series of bridges, including the Tyne Bridge (1928), the High Level Bridge designed by Robert Stephenson (1849), the Swing Bridge and the Gateshead Millennium Bridge. The lower Tyne was one of the world's great shipbuilding and coal-exporting rivers; colliers carried "Newcastle coal" to London for centuries, and yards at Wallsend and Jarrow built famous ships such as the Mauretania and the Carpathia.

Culture

The river is celebrated in the music-hall song "Blaydon Races" and in the annual Great North Run, which crosses the Tyne Bridge. Kielder Water supplies much of the region's drinking water by transfer.
