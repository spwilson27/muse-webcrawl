Messiah (Handel)

Messiah, HWV 56
ComposerGeorge Frideric Handel
LibrettistCharles Jennens
Composed1741
Premiere13 April 1742, Dublin
ScoringSoloists, choir, orchestra

Messiah (HWV 56) is an English-language oratorio composed in 1741 by George Frideric Handel, with a scriptural text compiled by Charles Jennens from the King James Bible and the Coverdale Psalter. It was first performed in Dublin on 13 April 1742 and received its London premiere nearly a year later. It has become one of the best-known and most frequently performed choral works in Western music.

Structure

The work is divided into three parts. Part I presents prophecies of the coming of the Messiah and the Nativity. Part II concerns Christ's Passion, Resurrection, and Ascension, ending with the famous "Hallelujah" chorus. Part III is a meditation on redemption and the promise of eternal life, closing with the choruses "Worthy is the Lamb" and "Amen".

Handel composed the music in roughly 24 days in the summer of 1741. Although written for modest vocal and orchestral forces, it was later re-orchestrated by others, including Mozart, and in the 19th and 20th centuries was often performed by very large ensembles.

Traditions

Audiences traditionally stand during the "Hallelujah" chorus, a custom often attributed — probably apocryphally — to King George II rising at a London performance. Messiah is a mainstay of the Christmas season, although its subject matter spans the whole life of Christ and its first performances were given near Easter.
