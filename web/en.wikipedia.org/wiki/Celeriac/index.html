Celeriac (Apium graveolens Rapaceum Group), also called celery root, knob celery or turnip-rooted celery, is a variety of celery cultivated for its edible stem base and roots — a large, bulbous hypocotyl — rather than for its stalks. It has a mild, celery-like flavour with nutty notes and is widely used in European cooking.

Description and cultivation

The plant grows a rough, knotted, brown-skinned bulb roughly 10–14 cm in diameter at soil level, topped with celery-like foliage. It requires a long growing season (around 200 days), rich moist soil and cool weather; the bulbs are harvested in autumn and can be stored for several months. Celeriac was developed from wild celery of Mediterranean marshlands and has been grown in Europe since the 17th century.

Culinary use

Peeled celeriac can be eaten raw, grated into salads and slaws (classically in the French céleri rémoulade, dressed with mustard mayonnaise), or cooked: roasted, boiled and mashed with potatoes, blended into soups, or baked. It is low in calories and starchy carbohydrates relative to other root vegetables, making it a popular substitute in low-carbohydrate cooking. Like celery, it is a recognised allergen in some jurisdictions.
