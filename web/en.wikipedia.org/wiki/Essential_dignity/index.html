Essential dignity

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In astrology, essential dignity is the strength or weakness of a planet arising from its placement by zodiac sign and degree, as distinct from accidental dignity, which arises from a planet's house position, aspects and other circumstances of a particular chart. The system was elaborated in Hellenistic astrology and codified by Ptolemy and later authors of the medieval and Renaissance traditions.

The five dignities

Traditional astrology recognises five levels of essential dignity, scored by astrologers such as William Lilly with a point scheme:

  • Domicile (rulership): a planet in the sign it rules — e.g. the Moon in Cancer, Mars in Aries — considered the strongest dignity (+5).
  • Exaltation: a planet in the sign (and traditionally the exact degree) of its exaltation, e.g. the Sun in Aries (+4).
  • Triplicity: a planet in a sign of the same element as one it rules by triplicity, varying by day or night (+3).
  • Term (bound): a planet within a division of a sign assigned to it in the tables of terms (+2).
  • Face (decan): a planet in the ten-degree decan it rules (+1).

Debilities

The opposites of the two greatest dignities are weaknesses: a planet in the sign opposite its domicile is in detriment, and one in the sign opposite its exaltation is in its fall. A planet with no essential dignity is termed peregrine. Dignity assessments play a central role in traditional horary and natal astrology, indicating how effectively a planet can act.
