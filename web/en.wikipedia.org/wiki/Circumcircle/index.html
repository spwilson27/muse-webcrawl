Circumcircle

In geometry, the circumcircle (or circumscribed circle) of a polygon is a circle that passes through all of the polygon's vertices. The centre of this circle is called the circumcenter and its radius the circumradius. Not every polygon has a circumcircle; those that do are called cyclic polygons.

Triangles

Every triangle has a unique circumcircle. Its centre is the point where the perpendicular bisectors of the three sides meet. For an acute triangle the circumcenter lies inside the triangle; for an obtuse triangle it lies outside; for a right triangle it is the midpoint of the hypotenuse. If the triangle has sides a, b, c and area K, the circumradius is R = abc / 4K. By the extended law of sines, each side equals 2R times the sine of the opposite angle.

Other polygons

A quadrilateral has a circumcircle if and only if its opposite angles are supplementary; such quadrilaterals are called cyclic. All rectangles and isosceles trapezoids are cyclic. The concept generalises to circumscribed spheres for polyhedra in three dimensions.
