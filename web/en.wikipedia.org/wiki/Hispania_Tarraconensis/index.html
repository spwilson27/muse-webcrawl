Hispania Tarraconensis

Hispania Tarraconensis was one of three Roman provinces in Hispania, created by the emperor Augustus in 27 BCE from the former province of Hispania Citerior. It was the largest of the Iberian provinces, covering the northern, eastern, and central parts of the peninsula, with its capital at Tarraco (modern Tarragona).

Administration

As an imperial province, Tarraconensis was governed by a legate of the emperor. It was subdivided into conventus for judicial administration, including those at Tarraco, Carthago Nova, Caesaraugusta, and Bracara Augusta. Important cities included Barcino (Barcelona), Valentia (Valencia), and Pompaelo (Pamplona).

Economy

The province exported wine, olive oil, and garum (fish sauce) around the Roman world, and its mines, such as the gold workings at Las Médulas, were worked on a large scale using hydraulic techniques.
