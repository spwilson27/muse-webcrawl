Accommodation (vertebrate eye)

Accommodation is the process by which the vertebrate eye changes optical power to maintain a clear image, or focus, on an object as its distance varies. In humans and most mammals this is achieved by changing the shape of the crystalline lens: when the ciliary muscle contracts, tension on the zonular fibers (zonules of Zinn) is released, allowing the elastic lens to become more rounded and powerful for near vision; when the muscle relaxes, the lens flattens for distance vision.

Mechanism

The Helmholtz mechanism, proposed in the 19th century, remains the accepted account for primates: parasympathetic stimulation via the oculomotor nerve contracts the ciliary muscle, the zonules slacken, and the lens capsule molds the lens into a more convex form, increasing its refractive power by up to about 15 diopters in a young eye. Accommodation is normally linked with convergence of the eyes and constriction of the pupil — the near triad.

Amplitude and ageing

The amplitude of accommodation declines steadily with age as the lens grows and stiffens, producing presbyopia: by the mid-40s most people cannot focus comfortably at reading distance and require reading glasses or other correction. A young child may have 14–16 diopters of amplitude, while a 60-year-old may have only about 1 diopter of measurable ("apparent") accommodation, mostly from pupil effects and depth of field.

Other vertebrates

Different vertebrate groups focus by different means. Many fish and amphibians move the lens rigidly toward or away from the retina; snakes and some others move the lens by altering pressure in the eye; birds can change both lens shape and corneal curvature; chameleons achieve remarkably fast, large-amplitude accommodation using a lens with negative power in its center.

Disorders

Disorders of accommodation include accommodative insufficiency, accommodative spasm (which can produce pseudomyopia), and paralysis of accommodation from drugs or neurological disease. Anticholinergic agents such as atropine temporarily block accommodation (cycloplegia), which is used in refraction of children.
