Sural nerve
FromTibial nerve; common fibular (peroneal) nerve
InnervatesSkin of the posterolateral leg and lateral foot
Identifiers (Latin)nervus suralis

The sural nerve is a purely sensory nerve of the lower leg in humans and other animals, formed from branches of the tibial and common fibular (peroneal) nerves. It supplies the skin of the posterior and lateral lower third of the leg and the lateral border of the foot and little toe.

Course of the sural nerve down the posterior calf

Course

The nerve is typically formed by the union of the medial sural cutaneous nerve (a branch of the tibial nerve) and a communicating branch from the lateral sural cutaneous nerve (from the common fibular nerve), though this pattern varies considerably between individuals. It descends between the two heads of the gastrocnemius, pierces the deep fascia in the middle of the leg, runs with the small saphenous vein, passes behind the lateral malleolus, and continues along the lateral foot as the dorsal digital nerves.

Clinical significance

Because it is subcutaneous, easily located and purely sensory, the sural nerve is the most commonly used site for nerve conduction studies of sensory neuropathy and the most popular donor nerve for nerve grafting and biopsy (for example in the diagnosis of vasculitic neuropathy). Injury, which may occur during Achilles tendon surgery or ankle procedures, causes numbness along the lateral foot. Sural neuritis can also cause lateral ankle and foot pain.
