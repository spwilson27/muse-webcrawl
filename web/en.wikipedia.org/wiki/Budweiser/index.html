Budweiser is an American-style pale lager, part of the AB InBev family of brands, produced by Anheuser-Busch in the United States and internationally by local breweries, often under licence. Introduced in 1876 by Carl Conrad & Co. of St. Louis, Missouri, it has grown to become one of the largest-selling beers in the world and is available in over 80 markets. Budweiser is a filtered beer, available on draft and in bottles and cans, made with a blend of two-row and six-row barley malt, rice, water, hops, and yeast.

History

Budweiser was introduced in 1876 when company founder Adolphus Busch set out to create the United States' first national beer brand, brewed to be universally popular and transcend regional tastes. The beer took its name from Budweis (now České Budějovice), a Bohemian town known for its brewing tradition, where Busch had encountered similar lagers. The brand was a pioneer in pasteurisation, artificial refrigeration, and refrigerated rail cars, which allowed it to be distributed nationally.

During Prohibition (1920–1933), Anheuser-Busch survived by selling near beer, yeast, malt syrup, and other products. After repeal, Budweiser regained its position and by 1957 had become the world's best-selling beer, a position it held for decades.

Production

Budweiser is brewed using a process that includes kräusening and beechwood lagering. The beechwood chips, placed in the bottom of the lagering tank, provide surface area for yeast to settle during secondary fermentation; Anheuser-Busch states the chips do not add flavour. The beer uses rice as an adjunct, which the company says gives it a crisp, clean taste, along with a blend of American and European aroma hops.

Name dispute

The name Budweiser is the subject of one of the longest-running trademark disputes in the world, between Anheuser-Busch InBev and the Czech state-owned brewery Budějovický Budvar, which also produces a lager sold as Budvar or Czechvar in some markets. The dispute, dating to the early 20th century, has resulted in different naming arrangements by country: in much of Europe Anheuser-Busch's beer is sold simply as "Bud", while in North America Budvar is sold as Czechvar.

Marketing

Budweiser has been marketed with slogans such as "The King of Beers" and is closely associated with American sports sponsorships, the Budweiser Clydesdale horses, and famous advertising campaigns including the "Whassup?" ads and the Budweiser Frogs. The Clydesdales, introduced in 1933 to celebrate the repeal of Prohibition, remain an enduring symbol of the brand.
