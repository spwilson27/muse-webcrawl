ALARA

ALARA stands for "as low as reasonably achievable", a principle of radiation protection which states that radiation doses to workers and the public should be kept as far below regulatory limits as is practical, taking economic and social factors into account.

Application

ALARA underpins radiation safety programmes in nuclear power, medicine and industry. Its practical tools are the classic trio of time (minimising exposure duration), distance (maximising separation from the source) and shielding. In the United Kingdom the equivalent formulation ALARP ("as low as reasonably practicable") is used in general safety law.

Regulatory basis

The principle was promoted by the International Commission on Radiological Protection and is embedded in regulations such as those of the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission. See also Radiation protection.
