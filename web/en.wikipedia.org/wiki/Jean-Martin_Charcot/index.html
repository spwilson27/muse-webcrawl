Jean-Martin Charcot

Jean-Martin Charcot (29 November 1825 – 16 August 1893) was a French neurologist and professor of anatomical pathology, often called the founder of modern neurology.

Work at the Salpêtrière

At the Pitié-Salpêtrière Hospital in Paris, Charcot created a renowned school of neurology. He described and named multiple sclerosis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (Charcot's disease), Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease, and Parkinson's disease (which he renamed), and pioneered the clinical use of hypnosis in studying hysteria.

Influence

His Tuesday lectures drew students from across Europe, including Sigmund Freud, who translated his works. Charcot's insistence on careful clinical observation and clinicopathological correlation shaped the discipline. See also neurology.
