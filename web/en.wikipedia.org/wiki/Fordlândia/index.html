Fordlândia is a district and former company town in the municipality of Aveiro, Pará, Brazil, on the east bank of the Tapajós River. It was established by American industrialist Henry Ford in 1928 as a prefabricated industrial town intended to secure a supply of rubber for the Ford Motor Company's automobile production.

History

Ford obtained a concession of some 10,000 km² from the Brazilian government. Workers cleared jungle and planted rubber trees, but the trees, planted in dense rows unlike their wild spacing, were devastated by South American leaf blight and pests. Brazilian workers also resisted the imposed American lifestyle — Prohibition, hamburgers, square dancing and time clocks — culminating in the 1930 "Breaking of the Pans" riot in the cafeteria.

Decline

Operations moved to Belterra in 1934, and in 1945, with synthetic rubber ascendant and the project having lost some $20 million, Ford sold the holdings back to Brazil for a fraction of the investment. Ford himself never visited Fordlândia. A small population remains among the ruins.
