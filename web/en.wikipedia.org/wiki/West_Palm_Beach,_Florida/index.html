West Palm Beach is a city in and the county seat of Palm Beach County, Florida, United States. It is located immediately to the west of the adjacent Palm Beach, from which it is separated by the Lake Worth Lagoon. With a population of about 117,000 at the 2020 census, it is the principal city of the Miami metropolitan area's northern part and one of the oldest incorporated municipalities in South Florida.

History

The city was founded by railroad magnate Henry Flagler in 1893–94 as a community to house the servants and workers building his hotels on the barrier island of Palm Beach, and it was incorporated on 5 November 1894. It grew as Flagler's Florida East Coast Railway reached the area, and boomed during the Florida land boom of the 1920s before the devastating 1928 Okeechobee hurricane struck the region.

Geography and climate

West Palm Beach lies on Florida's Atlantic coast about 68 miles (109 km) north of Miami. It has a tropical rainforest climate with hot, humid, rainy summers and warm, drier winters, and it is served by Palm Beach International Airport. The Intracoastal Waterway runs along its eastern edge.

Culture and economy

The city is home to the Norton Museum of Art, the Kravis Center for the Performing Arts, the Armory Art Center, and the waterfront Clematis Street entertainment district. Nearby attractions include the Palm Beach Zoo and, across the lagoon, the mansions and resorts of Palm Beach. Tourism, real estate, and services dominate the economy.
