Fast food

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A fast food meal of burger, fries and a soft drink]

Fast food is a type of mass-produced food designed for quick service and commercial resale, prioritising speed, standardisation and price. It is typically served in carton or bag packaging for eating in, take-out or drive-through, from chains such as McDonald's, Burger King, KFC and Subway — among the world's largest restaurant companies.

The modern industry began in the United States in the early 20th century (White Castle, 1921; McDonald's, 1940, franchised from 1955) and globalised after the Second World War, standardising the Hamburger, French fries and soft drinks as a global menu, adapted locally in each market.

Contents
1. Criticism and response

Criticism and response

Fast food is criticised for high energy density, salt, sugar and fat, aggressive marketing, low-wage labour and environmental impact; it features in debates on obesity and public health. Chains have responded with salads, smaller portions, calorie labelling and, more recently, plant-based options, while 'fast casual' and premium burger chains blur the category.
