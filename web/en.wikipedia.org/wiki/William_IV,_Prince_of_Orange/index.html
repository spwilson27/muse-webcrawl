William IV, Prince of Orange

Born: 1 September 1711, Leeuwarden, Dutch Republic
Died: 22 October 1751, The Hague, Dutch Republic
Reign: Stadtholder of all Dutch provinces, 1747–1751
Spouse: Anne, Princess Royal

William IV (Willem Karel Hendrik Friso; 1 September 1711 – 22 October 1751) was Prince of Orange and, from 1747, the first hereditary stadtholder of all seven provinces of the Dutch Republic.

Early life

Born weeks after his father's death, William succeeded as Prince of Orange and stadtholder of Friesland, Groningen, Drenthe, and Gelderland, though the other provinces had abolished the stadtholderate after 1702. In 1734 he married Anne, Princess Royal, the eldest daughter of George II of Great Britain.

Restoration of the stadtholderate

During the War of the Austrian Succession, French invasion in 1747 triggered popular unrest — the Orangist revolution — which swept William to power. He was proclaimed stadtholder of Holland, Zeeland, and Utrecht, and in 1747 the office was made hereditary in both the male and female lines across all provinces, uniting the Republic under one stadtholder for the first time.

Reign and death

Expectations of reform ran high, but William moved cautiously, retaining much of the regent oligarchy's power and personnel. His health was poor, and he died in 1751, leaving the stadtholderate to his three-year-old son, William V, under the regency of Princess Anne.
