Staten Island is the southernmost of the five boroughs of New York City, coextensive with Richmond County and situated in the southwest portion of the city. It lies in New York Harbor at the entrance to the Verrazzano-Narrows, separated from Long Island by the Narrows and from New Jersey by the Arthur Kill and the Kill Van Kull. With a population of about 495,000, it is the least populous of the boroughs but the third largest in land area.

History

The island was inhabited by the Lenape people for thousands of years. Giovanni da Verrazzano sailed into the harbor in 1524, and Henry Hudson explored it in 1609. The Dutch named it Staaten Eylandt after the States General of the Netherlands; early Dutch settlement attempts were repeatedly destroyed in conflicts with the Lenape, including the Peach War of 1655. The English took control in 1664, and Richmond County was established in 1683. During the American Revolutionary War, the island was a major British staging area; the Battle of Staten Island was fought in 1777. In 1898, Staten Island was consolidated into the City of New York.

Geography and transport

The island's highest point, Todt Hill, is the highest natural point on the U.S. Atlantic coastal plain south of Maine. Much of the island is suburban in character, protected in part by the Staten Island Greenbelt, a network of parks and nature preserves. Staten Island is connected to Brooklyn by the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge (opened 1964, then the longest suspension bridge in the world) and to New Jersey by the Bayonne Bridge, the Goethals Bridge, and the Outerbridge Crossing. The free Staten Island Ferry has carried passengers between Whitehall Terminal in Manhattan and St. George Terminal since 1905 and is a major tourist attraction. The Staten Island Railway provides the borough's only rapid transit service.

The Fresh Kills Landfill, once the largest landfill in the world, operated from 1948 to 2001 and is being transformed into Freshkills Park.
