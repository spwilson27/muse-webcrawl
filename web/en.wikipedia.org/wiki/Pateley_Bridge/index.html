Pateley Bridge

Pateley Bridge
County: North Yorkshire, England
Population: about 1,400 (parish)
Known for: Nidderdale; oldest sweet shop in England

Pateley Bridge is a market town in the parish of High and Low Bishopside, in the borough of Harrogate, North Yorkshire, England. It lies on the River Nidd at the end of the Nidd Valley Railway's former line, and is the principal settlement of Nidderdale, an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. The town takes its name from a bridge over the Nidd; "Pateley" is thought to derive from Old Norse and Old English elements meaning a clearing by the paths.

History and attractions

Pateley Bridge received a market charter in the 14th century and developed around lead mining, quarrying, and textiles. Local attractions include the Nidderdale Museum, St Cuthbert's Church, the ruins of St Mary's Church, Brimham Rocks a few miles away, and the cold baths and How Stean Gorge nearby. The town's High Street hosts what is certified as the oldest sweet shop in England, established in 1827. The annual Nidderdale Show, an agricultural show held in September, is one of the area's major events.

Transport

The town was once the terminus of the Nidd Valley Railway from Harrogate, closed to passengers in 1951. Today Pateley Bridge is served by buses and is a popular base for walkers, including those following the Nidderdale Way long-distance path.
