Korean drama (Korean: 한국 드라마), widely known as K-drama, refers to television series produced in South Korea. Exported across Asia from the 1990s as part of the Korean Wave (Hallyu) and globally in the streaming era, K-dramas are among South Korea's most successful cultural exports.

Format and production

K-dramas are usually produced as closed series with a single season, unlike open-ended Western soap operas. The dominant format is the miniseries of 12 to 16 episodes, supplemented by daily dramas, weekend family series of 50 or more episodes, and historical epics (sageuk). Scripts are frequently written as filming proceeds ("live shooting"), allowing producers to respond to ratings and audience reaction, a practice criticised for punishing production schedules. Major producers include the terrestrial networks KBS, MBC, and SBS, cable channels such as tvN and JTBC, and global streaming services, notably Netflix, which has invested heavily in Korean originals since the late 2010s.

Global popularity

Early international breakthroughs included Winter Sonata (2002), which sparked a tourism boom in Japan, and the historical drama Jewel in the Palace (2003), broadcast in dozens of countries. Global streaming made series such as Crash Landing on You (2019) and Squid Game (2021)—Netflix's most-watched series on release—worldwide phenomena, boosting interest in Korean language, food, tourism, and beauty products.
