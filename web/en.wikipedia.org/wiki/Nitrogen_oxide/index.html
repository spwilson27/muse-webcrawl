Nitrogen oxide

Nitrogen oxides are chemical compounds of nitrogen and oxygen. The term covers several gases of very different properties: nitric oxide (NO), nitrogen dioxide (NO2), nitrous oxide (N2O, "laughing gas"), dinitrogen trioxide (N2O3), dinitrogen tetroxide (N2O4) and dinitrogen pentoxide (N2O5). In air pollution science, the abbreviation NOx refers specifically to the sum of NO and NO2, the two oxides emitted in quantity by combustion.

Nitric oxideNO — colourless, signalling molecule
Nitrogen dioxideNO2 — reddish-brown, toxic
Nitrous oxideN2O — anaesthetic, greenhouse gas
Dinitrogen tetroxideN2O4 — rocket propellant oxidiser

Formation and sources

NOx is produced whenever fuel is burned in air at high temperatures — chiefly in vehicle engines, power stations and industrial furnaces — because atmospheric nitrogen and oxygen react above roughly 1,300 °C. Lightning and soil microbes are the main natural sources. Emissions have been reduced in many countries since the 1970s through three-way catalytic converters, low-NOx burners and selective catalytic reduction (SCR) using ammonia or urea.

Environmental effects

NO and NO2 interconvert rapidly in sunlight and drive the photochemistry of urban smog: NOx reacting with volatile organic compounds in sunlight produces ground-level ozone and peroxyacetyl nitrate, the eye-stinging components of photochemical smog. Nitrogen oxides also contribute to acid rain (via nitric acid formation), to eutrophication of water bodies when nitrogen deposits, and to fine particulate matter through nitrate aerosols. NO2 itself irritates the lungs and is linked to asthma exacerbation and respiratory disease, prompting air-quality limits in the EU, US and elsewhere.

Individual compounds

Nitric oxide is a key biological signalling molecule: produced by the endothelium, it relaxes blood vessels — the discovery of this role won the 1998 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine — and it is used medically as inhaled therapy for pulmonary hypertension in newborns. Nitrous oxide is a long-lived greenhouse gas and ozone-depleting substance as well as an anaesthetic and aerosol propellant. Dinitrogen tetroxide is a storable oxidiser widely used in rocket propellants with hydrazine fuels.
