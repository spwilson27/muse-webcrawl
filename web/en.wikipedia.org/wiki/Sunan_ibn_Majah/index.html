Sunan Ibn Majah

Sunan Ibn Majah (Arabic: سنن ابن ماجه) is one of the six major hadith collections (Kutub al-Sittah) of Sunni Islam, compiled by the Persian scholar Ibn Majah (824–887 CE) of Qazvin. It contains over 4,300 hadith in 37 books, organised by legal topic, and is especially valued for hadith not found in the other five collections — roughly 1,300 unique narrations.

Author: Ibn Majah
Language: Arabic
Subject: Hadith; Islamic law

Status

The Sunan of Ibn Majah was added later than the other five books to the canonical six; some scholars, such as Ibn al-Athir, initially preferred the Muwatta of Malik or the Sunan of al-Darimi for the sixth position. Critics note that the collection contains more weak — and a small number of fabricated — narrations than the other five, a point addressed in commentaries that grade its hadith. Its inclusion among the six is generally attributed to its useful organisation and its many additions on legal topics.

Author

Muhammad ibn Yazid ibn Majah al-Qazvini travelled through Iraq, the Hijaz, Syria and Egypt collecting traditions before returning to Qazvin, where he taught and wrote history and Qur'anic exegesis in addition to his Sunan.
