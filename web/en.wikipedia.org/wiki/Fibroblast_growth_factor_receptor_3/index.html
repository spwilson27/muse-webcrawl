Fibroblast growth factor receptor 3

Fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the FGFR3 gene on chromosome 4. It is a receptor tyrosine kinase that binds fibroblast growth factors and plays an important role in regulating bone growth, acting as a negative regulator of endochondral ossification in the growth plates of long bones.

Structure and function

The protein consists of an extracellular ligand-binding region with three immunoglobulin-like domains, a single-pass transmembrane helix, and an intracellular tyrosine kinase domain. Activation by FGF ligands triggers signalling pathways including MAPK/ERK, which restrain chondrocyte proliferation in the growth plate.

Clinical significance

Gain-of-function mutations in FGFR3 cause several skeletal dysplasias. A single recurrent point mutation (Gly380Arg) is responsible for almost all cases of achondroplasia, the most common form of dwarfism. Other mutations cause thanatophoric dysplasia, hypochondroplasia and Muenke syndrome. Activating alterations and fusions of FGFR3 are also found in cancers, including bladder cancer, and FGFR inhibitors are used therapeutically in some settings.
