Flange

Flange
TypeMechanical component
UsesPipe joints, wheels, fasteners

A flange is a protruded ridge, lip or rim, either external or internal, used for strength, lightening, guiding, or for attachment to another object. Flanges are found on beams (such as I-beams), pipes, railway wheels, camera lens mounts, and many other engineered components.

Pipe flanges

In piping, a flange is a disc or ring fitted to the end of a pipe so that two pipe sections, or a pipe and a valve or vessel, can be bolted together, usually with a gasket between the faces to form a pressure-tight seal. Common types include weld-neck, slip-on, socket-weld, lap-joint, threaded and blind flanges, standardised by bodies such as ASME and DIN.

Other uses

The flange of a railway wheel is the projecting rim on the inner side that keeps the wheel on the rail. In beam theory, the flanges of an I-beam carry most of the bending stress. Flanged bushings, couplings and toilet flanges apply the same principle of a rim used to locate and secure a component.
