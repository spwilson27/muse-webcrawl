The Piccadilly line is a deep-level London Underground line running from the north to the west of London. It has two branches, which split at Acton Town, and serves 53 stations. The line is known for serving Heathrow Airport, as well as tourist destinations including Hyde Park Corner, Piccadilly Circus, Leicester Square and Covent Garden. It is coloured dark blue (corporate blue, Pantone 072) on the Tube map.

A Piccadilly line 1973 Stock train

Between Acton Town and Hammersmith, the Piccadilly line tracks run alongside those of the District line. The line is the fifth-busiest line on the Underground network, and its deep-level route through central London was opened in 1906 as the Great Northern, Piccadilly and Brompton Railway.

History

The Piccadilly line began as the Great Northern, Piccadilly and Brompton Railway (GNP&BR), which opened on 15 December 1906 between Finsbury Park and Hammersmith. It was one of the lines promoted by the American financier Charles Yerkes and his Underground Electric Railways Company of London. Extensions followed: to South Harrow and Uxbridge in the 1930s, north to Arnos Grove and Cockfosters (1932–33), and the Heathrow branch reached Hatton Cross in 1975, Heathrow Central in 1977, Terminal 4 in 1986 and Terminal 5 in 2008.

Route

From Cockfosters in the north, the line runs south through Finsbury Park, King's Cross St Pancras, Holborn and the West End, then west through Hammersmith and Acton Town. West of Acton Town it splits: one branch runs to Heathrow Airport via Boston Manor and Hounslow; the other, shared with the Metropolitan line between Rayners Lane and Uxbridge, serves Sudbury, Rayners Lane and Ruislip.

Rolling stock and upgrade

The line is operated by 1973 Stock trains, among the oldest on the Underground. A fleet of new walk-through, air-cooled trains built by Siemens (the 2024 Stock, part of the Inspiro family) is being introduced under the Piccadilly line upgrade, accompanied by new signalling to raise frequencies in the central section.
