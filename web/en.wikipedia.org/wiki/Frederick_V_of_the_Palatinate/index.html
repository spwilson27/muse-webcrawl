Frederick V of the Palatinate

Frederick V (German: Friedrich V.; 26 August 1596 – 29 November 1632) was the Elector Palatine of the Rhine in the Holy Roman Empire from 1610 to 1623, and reigned as King of Bohemia from 1619 to 1620. He is known as the "Winter King" (Winterkönig) because his Bohemian reign lasted only one winter. His acceptance of the Bohemian crown was a major factor in the outbreak of the Thirty Years' War.

Early life and marriage

Frederick was born at Deinschwang in the Upper Palatinate, the son of Frederick IV, Elector Palatine, and Louise Juliana of Orange-Nassau. He succeeded his father in 1610. In 1613 he married Elizabeth Stuart, daughter of James I of England, a prestigious match that tied the Palatinate to the English crown. The couple resided at Heidelberg Castle, where Frederick expanded the gardens (the Hortus Palatinus).

King of Bohemia

In 1618 the Protestant Bohemian estates revolted against the Catholic Habsburg Ferdinand II (the Defenestration of Prague) and in 1619 offered their crown to Frederick, the leading Calvinist prince of the Empire and head of the Protestant Union. Against the advice of many allies, he accepted and was crowned in Prague on 4 November 1619. His forces were crushed by Imperial and Bavarian troops at the Battle of White Mountain on 8 November 1620, and he fled — earning the mocking epithet "Winter King".

Exile and death

The Imperial ban was placed on Frederick in 1621; the Palatinate was overrun, Heidelberg fell in 1622, and the electoral dignity was transferred to Maximilian I of Bavaria in 1623. Frederick lived in exile mainly at The Hague, supported by his uncle Maurice of Nassau and English subsidies. He died at Mainz in 1632. His son Charles I Louis regained the (Lower) Palatinate and a new electoral title in the Peace of Westphalia (1648).

Issue

Frederick and Elizabeth had thirteen children, including Charles I Louis, Elector Palatine; Rupert, Prince of the Rhine, the Royalist cavalry commander of the English Civil War; and Sophia, Electress of Hanover, heiress to the English throne under the Act of Settlement 1701 and mother of George I of Great Britain.
