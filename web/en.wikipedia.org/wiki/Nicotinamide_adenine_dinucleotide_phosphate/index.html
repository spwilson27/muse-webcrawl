Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate

AbbreviationsNADP+, NADPH
FormulaC21H29N7O17P3
RoleCoenzyme in anabolic reactions

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate, abbreviated NADP+ in its oxidised form and NADPH in its reduced form, is a cofactor used in anabolic reactions such as the Calvin cycle and lipid and nucleic acid synthesis, which require NADPH as a reducing agent. It is used by all forms of cellular life.

Structure

NADP+ differs from NAD+ only by the presence of an additional phosphate group on the 2' position of the ribose ring that carries the adenine moiety. This single modification allows enzymes to distinguish between the two cofactors: NAD+/NADH is used mainly in catabolism, while NADP+/NADPH is used mainly in anabolism.

Synthesis and sources

In animals and other non-photosynthetic organisms, NADPH is produced chiefly by the oxidative phase of the pentose phosphate pathway, catalysed by glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase and 6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase. In photosynthetic organisms, NADPH is generated in the light reactions of photosynthesis by ferredoxin–NADP+ reductase, and is consumed in the Calvin cycle to fix carbon dioxide. Deficiency of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase impairs NADPH production in red blood cells and is the most common human enzyme defect, causing susceptibility to haemolytic anaemia.

Functions

NADPH provides the reducing equivalents for biosynthetic reactions, including fatty acid and cholesterol synthesis, and for the regeneration of reduced glutathione, which protects cells against oxidative stress. It is also the substrate of NADPH oxidases used by phagocytes to generate reactive oxygen species during the respiratory burst.
