Ōtsu (大津市, Ōtsu-shi) is the capital city of Shiga Prefecture, Japan. It lies on the southern shore of Lake Biwa, the largest lake in Japan, and borders Kyoto to the west. As of recent estimates the city has a population of roughly 343,000. Ōtsu was the capital of Japan briefly in the 7th century and has been a transport and cultural centre for the Kansai region for over a millennium.

History

Ōtsu first rose to prominence in 667, when Emperor Tenji moved the capital to Ōmi Ōtsu Palace, where it remained until 672. The city developed as a port on Lake Biwa and a post station on the Tōkaidō and Nakasendō highways during the Edo period, famous for the "Ōtsu-e" folk paintings sold to travellers. On 11 May 1891, Ōtsu was the site of the Ōtsu incident, an assassination attempt on the visiting Russian Tsarevich Nicholas (later Nicholas II) by a Japanese policeman, which caused a diplomatic crisis and led to a landmark assertion of judicial independence in Japan.

Geography

The city stretches along the southwestern shore of Lake Biwa, from the Hira Mountains in the north to the border with Kyoto in the south. Much of its historic centre sits on a narrow strip between the lake and the mountains, through which run the JR Biwako and Kosei lines, the Keihan Keishin Line and the Meishin Expressway.

Sights

  • Enryaku-ji – the great Tendai monastery on Mount Hiei, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, overlooks the city.
  • Mii-dera (Onjō-ji) – one of the four great temples of Japan and head temple of the Jimon sect.
  • Ishiyama-dera – temple traditionally associated with Murasaki Shikibu, who is said to have begun The Tale of Genji there.
  • Lake Biwa – cruises on the paddle steamer Michigan depart from Ōtsu Port.
  • Ōtsu Matsuri – an annual October festival with ornate floats, designated an Important Intangible Folk Cultural Property.
