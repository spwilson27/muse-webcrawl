South African Army

Founded: 1912 (as part of the Union Defence Forces)

Country: South Africa

Branch: Land warfare arm of the SANDF

Headquarters: Pretoria

Motto: "Pride of the Nation"

The South African Army is the principal land warfare force of South Africa, a part of the South African National Defence Force (SANDF) alongside the Navy, Air Force and Military Health Service. Its roots lie in the Union Defence Forces created in 1912, drawing on the traditions of the colonial forces and Boer commandos of the predecessor states.

History

South African land forces fought in both world wars, including at Delville Wood (1916), in the East and North African campaigns, and in Italy with the 6th South African Armoured Division. South African infantry also served in the Korean War era's ground commitments, and during the apartheid period the Army expanded greatly for the Border War in South West Africa (Namibia) and Angola (1966–1989), relying heavily on national-service conscription.

In 1994 the old South African Defence Force was integrated with the liberation armies (Umkhonto we Sizwe and APLA) and homeland forces to form the SANDF. Conscription ended and the Army became an all-volunteer professional force.

Modern role and structure

The Army is organised into formations for infantry, armour, artillery, air defence artillery, engineers, signals and intelligence, supported by training and logistics formations. Since 1994 it has deployed extensively on African peace-support operations under the United Nations and African Union — including in Burundi, the Democratic Republic of the Congo (as part of MONUSCO's Force Intervention Brigade), Sudan and Mozambique — as well as on domestic disaster-relief and border-safeguarding tasks. Chronic budget constraints have limited its size and equipment renewal in recent decades.
