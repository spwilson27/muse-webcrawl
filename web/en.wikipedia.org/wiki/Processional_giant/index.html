Processional giant

Regions: Belgium, northern France, Spain, and elsewhere

UNESCO: Processional giants and dragons of Belgium and France inscribed 2008

Processional giants are giant costumed figures, several metres tall, carried by one or more bearers (porteurs) during festivals, carnivals and religious or civic processions. They represent founders, saints, trades, mythical heroes or local characters and are a hallmark of folk celebrations in Belgium, the Nord-Pas-de-Calais region of France, and parts of Spain, notably Catalonia, where they are called gegants.

Construction

A traditional giant combines a head and torso sculpted from wood, papier-mâché or modern composites with a lightweight wicker or aluminium frame covered by clothing. The bearer, hidden inside, supports the structure on a harness and makes the giant walk, turn and sometimes dance.

Traditions

Famous giants include Gayant of Douai, Reuze Papa of Cassel, the giants of Ath — where the Ducasse d'Ath features Goliath and his family — and the Gigantes y cabezudos of Spanish fiestas. In 2005 UNESCO proclaimed the processional giants and dragons of Belgium and France a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, later inscribed on the Representative List in 2008.

Outside Europe

Similar traditions exist in other parts of the world, including the giants of Burundian and Latin American festivals, and modern artist-built figures used in parades and protest theatre.
