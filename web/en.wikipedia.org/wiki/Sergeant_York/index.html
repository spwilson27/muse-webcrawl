Sergeant York is a 1941 American biographical film directed by Howard Hawks and starring Gary Cooper as Alvin C. York, the Tennessee backwoodsman and conscientious objector who became one of the most decorated American soldiers of the First World War. Cooper won the Academy Award for Best Actor for the role, and the film was one of the highest-grossing of its year — a stirring piece of patriotic filmmaking released on the eve of America's entry into the Second World War.

Plot

The film follows York from his hard-drinking youth in the mountains of Tennessee through his religious conversion and his struggle, after being drafted in 1917, between his church's pacifist teaching and his duty as a soldier. In its climax it recreates the Meuse–Argonne action of 8 October 1918, in which York helped capture 132 German prisoners and silence a machine-gun position, earning the Medal of Honor.

Production and legacy

York had refused for years to sell his story, relenting only on conditions that included Cooper playing him. The film was nominated for eleven Academy Awards, winning for Best Actor and Best Film Editing. In 2008 it was selected for preservation in the National Film Registry of the Library of Congress as culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant.
