Tamil people

The Tamil people, also known as Tamilars, are a Dravidian ethnolinguistic group native to southern India and northern Sri Lanka, speaking the Tamil language, one of the longest-surviving classical languages in the world. The majority live in the Indian state of Tamil Nadu and the union territory of Puducherry, with a large indigenous population in Sri Lanka and a worldwide diaspora of several million.

History

Tamil civilisation is attested from the Sangam era, when the Chera, Chola and Pandya kingdoms flourished and traded with the Roman world. Medieval empires, above all the Cholas under Rajaraja I and Rajendra I, extended Tamil power across South and Southeast Asia between the 9th and 13th centuries. Tamil inscriptions in Tamil-Brahmi script are among the oldest in India.

Culture

Tamil culture is centred on the Tamil language and its literature, classical Carnatic music, Bharatanatyam dance, monumental temple architecture, and festivals such as Pongal and Puthandu observed according to the Tamil calendar. Most Tamils are Hindus, with significant Christian and Muslim minorities and a historic Jain presence.

Population

There are an estimated 75–80 million Tamil speakers worldwide. Tamil is an official language of India, Sri Lanka and Singapore, and is recognised as a classical language by the Government of India.
