South Pass City

Status: Ghost town / state historic site

South Pass City is a ghost town in Fremont County, Wyoming, United States, near the historic South Pass over the Rocky Mountains. Founded during an 1868 gold-mining boom, it is preserved today as the South Pass City State Historic Site.

History

Gold was discovered at the nearby Carissa lode in 1867, drawing thousands of miners. At its peak the town had roughly 2,000 residents, hotels, stores, and a newspaper. South Pass City played a notable role in women's suffrage history: its representative William Bright introduced the 1869 bill that made Wyoming Territory the first place in the United States to grant women the vote, and resident Esther Hobart Morris became the first female justice of the peace in the nation in 1870.

Decline and preservation

As the mines played out, the population collapsed; by the early 1900s only a handful of residents remained. More than twenty original structures survive on the site, which is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and interpreted by Wyoming State Parks.
