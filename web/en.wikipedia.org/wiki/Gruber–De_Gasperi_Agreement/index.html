Gruber–De Gasperi Agreement

Signed: 5 September 1946, Paris

Parties: Austria and Italy

Signatories: Karl Gruber, Alcide De Gasperi

The Gruber–De Gasperi Agreement, also known as the Paris Agreement, was an accord signed on 5 September 1946 by the foreign ministers of Austria (Karl Gruber) and Italy (Alcide De Gasperi), then Italy's prime minister. It concerned the status of the German-speaking population of South Tyrol, which had been annexed by Italy after the First World War.

Terms

Italy undertook to guarantee the German-speaking inhabitants of the province of Bolzano (Bozen) and neighbouring bilingual townships equality of rights with Italian speakers, including elementary and secondary schooling in German, parity of the German and Italian languages in public offices and official documents, and the use of German family names. Italy also promised autonomous legislative and executive powers for the region.

Aftermath

The agreement was annexed to the 1947 peace treaty with Italy. Austria regarded its implementation as inadequate for decades, raising the South Tyrol question before the United Nations; fuller autonomy was eventually granted by the Second Autonomy Statute of 1972.
