National Security Act of 1947
Enacted by80th United States Congress
Signed26 July 1947, by President Harry S. Truman
Effective18 September 1947
CitationsPublic Law 80-253; 61 Stat. 495

The National Security Act of 1947 was a landmark American law that restructured the United States military and intelligence agencies in the aftermath of World War II and laid the institutional foundations of the Cold War national-security state. It was signed by President Harry S. Truman on 26 July 1947, aboard his presidential aircraft, and most provisions took effect on 18 September 1947.

Principal provisions

The Act created the National Security Council to advise the president on security policy; the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA), the first peacetime American intelligence agency; and the National Military Establishment, a unified structure (renamed the Department of Defense in 1949) bringing the Departments of the Army, Navy, and the newly independent United States Air Force under a Secretary of Defense. It also established the position of Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff.

Legacy

Amendments in 1949 strengthened the Secretary of Defense and converted the military departments into subordinate components of the Department of Defense. The architecture created in 1947 — a unified defence establishment, a central intelligence agency, and a White House security council — endures today, supplemented by later creations such as the Department of Homeland Security (2002) and the Director of National Intelligence (2004).
