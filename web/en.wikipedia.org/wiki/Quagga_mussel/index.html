Quagga mussel

Quagga mussel
Scientific nameDreissena rostriformis bugensis
Native rangeDnieper River basin, Ukraine
StatusInvasive in North America and Western Europe

The quagga mussel (Dreissena rostriformis bugensis) is a freshwater bivalve mollusc native to the Dnieper River drainage of Ukraine. It is named after the quagga, an extinct subspecies of zebra, because its shell bears similar fading stripes. A close relative of the zebra mussel, it has become a major invasive species.

Invasion

Quagga mussels were first found in North America's Great Lakes in 1989, having arrived in ship ballast water, and have since spread widely, including to the western United States. They filter large volumes of phytoplankton, clarifying water but disrupting food webs, and they foul water intakes, boats and infrastructure.

Differences from zebra mussels

Quaggas tolerate deeper, colder and softer substrates than zebra mussels, which has allowed them to dominate the deep waters of Lakes Michigan and Huron, reaching enormous densities on the lake bed.
