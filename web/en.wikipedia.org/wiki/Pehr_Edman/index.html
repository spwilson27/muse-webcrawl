Pehr Edman.

Pehr Victor Edman (14 April 1916 – 19 March 1977) was a Swedish biochemist. He developed the Edman degradation, a method of sequencing amino acids in a peptide, which became a cornerstone technique of protein chemistry for several decades.

Life and career

Edman studied medicine at the Karolinska Institute in Stockholm, graduating in the 1940s, then worked on protein chemistry at Lund University. He published the stepwise degradation method that bears his name in 1950. In 1957 he moved to Australia to direct research at the St Vincent's School of Medical Research in Melbourne, where he and his collaborators, notably Geoffrey Begg, built the first automated protein sequencer (the "sequenator") in the 1960s. In 1972 he moved to the Max Planck Institute for Biochemistry in Munich, where he worked until his death in 1977.

Edman degradation

In the Edman degradation, phenyl isothiocyanate reacts with the N-terminal amino group of a peptide under mildly alkaline conditions; the derivatized terminal residue is then cleaved under acid as a phenylthiohydantoin (PTH) derivative, which can be identified by chromatography, leaving the rest of the peptide intact for the next cycle. Repeated cycles read off the sequence one residue at a time. Although largely superseded by mass spectrometry for large-scale work, the method remains in use for specific applications such as confirming N-terminal sequences.
