MALDI imaging

Ion images generated by MALDI imaging mass spectrometry

MALDI imaging, also known as imaging mass spectrometry (IMS) using matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization, is a technique that combines mass spectrometry with spatial information to map the distribution of molecules — such as proteins, peptides, lipids, metabolites and drugs — directly in tissue sections and other samples.

Method

A thin tissue section is coated with a chemical matrix that absorbs laser energy and assists ionization. A laser then raster-scans the surface in a grid, and a mass spectrum is recorded at each x–y position. Software reconstructs ion images showing where each detected mass-to-charge (m/z) value is located, typically at spatial resolutions of tens of micrometres. The technique was pioneered in the mid-1990s, notably by Richard Caprioli and colleagues.

Applications

MALDI imaging is used in biomedical research and pharmaceutical studies to visualize drug and metabolite distribution, identify tumor margins and molecular signatures of disease, and study proteins in neuroscience and pathology. Because it is label-free and multiplexed — hundreds of molecules can be mapped in one experiment — it complements immunohistochemistry and other imaging methods. Related ionization approaches used in imaging include DESI and SIMS.
