Thomas Tafirenyika Mapfumo (born 2 July 1945) is a Zimbabwean musician. He is nicknamed "The Lion of Zimbabwe" and "Mukanya" (the praised one) for his immense popularity and for the political influence he wields through his music, including his sharp criticism of the government of President Robert Mugabe. He both created and popularized Chimurenga music — a style of Zimbabwean popular music that adapts the traditional mbira (thumb piano) to electric guitars and modern instruments, combined with lyrics supporting the Shona culture and the struggle for independence.

Early life and career

Mapfumo was born in Marondera, east of Salisbury (now Harare), and was raised partly in the capital and partly in the rural area of Mhondoro. As a young man he sang in cover bands performing American rock and roll and rhythm and blues, including the Cosmic Dots and the Springfields. In the 1970s he began singing in Shona and adapting traditional mbira melodies to the electric guitar, a sound developed most fully with his band the Acid Band and, from 1978, the Blacks Unlimited.

Chimurenga music and the liberation struggle

The word chimurenga means "struggle" or "liberation war" in Shona. During the Rhodesian Bush War, Mapfumo's songs — sung in Shona and rich in proverb and allegory — gave poetic voice to black Zimbabweans' grievances against the white minority government of Ian Smith. Songs such as "Pamuromo Chete" and "Tumirai Vana Kuhondo" became anthems of the liberation movement. The Rhodesian authorities banned his records from state radio and in 1977 imprisoned him for several months without charge, a detention that only increased his stature.

After independence in 1980, Mapfumo initially supported the new ZANU-PF government, but his later albums, such as Corruption (1989), attacked the corruption of the ruling elite. Under escalating pressure and harassment, he left Zimbabwe in the late 1990s and settled in Oregon in the United States, returning to perform in Zimbabwe only after Mugabe's removal in 2017.

Legacy

Mapfumo is regarded as one of the most important figures in the history of Zimbabwean music. His fusion of mbira tradition with the electric band, and his use of music as political commentary, shaped generations of Zimbabwean artists.
