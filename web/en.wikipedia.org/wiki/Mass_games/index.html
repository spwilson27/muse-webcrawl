Mass games

Mass games, or mass gymnastics, are large-scale performances combining synchronised gymnastics, dance, acrobatics, and mosaic-like human tableaux performed by tens of thousands of participants. They developed in nineteenth- and twentieth-century Europe — notably in the German Turners and Czech Sokol movements, and in the Soviet Union's physical culture parades — and became most famous in North Korea.

North Korean mass games

North Korea's mass games are staged in the Rungrado May Day Stadium in Pyongyang, the largest stadium in the world. More than 100,000 performers may take part: synchronised dancers and gymnasts fill the field while some 18,000 schoolchildren in the stands flip coloured cards to create a vast animated "human mosaic" backdrop. The best-known production, the Arirang Mass Games (2002–2013, and revived in later variants), tells a nationalist narrative of Korean history, the leadership of the Kim family, and the ideal of reunification. Preparations involve months of gruelling training, and the games are regarded abroad as both an artistic spectacle and an instrument of state ideology and collective discipline.

Elsewhere

Similar traditions include the Czechoslovak Spartakiads, held every five years in Prague's Strahov Stadium; the opening ceremonies of Olympic Games, which have adopted mass choreography on a grand scale; and China's mass performances for national celebrations, descending from the spectacles of the Mao era.
