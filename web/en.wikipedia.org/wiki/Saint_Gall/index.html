Saint Gall

Saint Gall (Gallus)
Born: c. 550, Ireland
Died: c. 646, Arbon, Switzerland
Feast day: 16 October
Known for: Founder of the Abbey of St. Gall

Saint Gall (Latin: Gallus; c. 550 – c. 646) was an Irish disciple of St Columbanus and one of the great missionaries of the early medieval Church in the Frankish and Alemannic lands. His hermitage became the nucleus of the Abbey of St. Gall and the city of St. Gallen in present-day Switzerland.

Life

Educated at Bangor Abbey in Ireland, Gall accompanied Columbanus to the continent around 590 and helped found monasteries at Annegray, Luxeuil and Bobbio. After falling out with Columbanus over remaining behind due to illness, Gall settled as a hermit by the Steinach river around 612, where tradition tells of his friendship with a bear, which appears in the city's coat of arms.

Veneration

Gall is the patron saint of St. Gallen, and his feast day is 16 October. The Plan of St. Gall, the famous early medieval monastery plan, was drawn up at the abbey founded on his hermitage site.
