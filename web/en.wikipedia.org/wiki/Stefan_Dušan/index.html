Stefan Dušan

Stefan Uroš IV Dušan (Serbian Cyrillic: Стефан Урош IV Душан; c. 1308 – 20 December 1355), known as Dušan the Mighty, was the King of Serbia from 8 September 1331 and Emperor and Autocrat of the Serbs, Greeks, Bulgarians and Albanians from 16 April 1346 until his death. Dušan is considered one of the most powerful European rulers of his time and the greatest king in Serbian history.

Reign

Dušan came to the throne after the Serbian nobility, dissatisfied with his father's rule, supported his taking power; Stefan Dečanski was deposed and later died in captivity. Taking advantage of the Byzantine civil war of 1341–1347, Dušan conquered much of southeastern Europe, annexing Macedonia, Albania, Epirus and Thessaly, and roughly doubling the extent of the Serbian state. In 1345 he proclaimed himself Emperor in Serres and was crowned in Skopje on Easter 1346 by the newly elevated Serbian Patriarch Joanikije II, together with the Bulgarian Patriarch and the Archbishop of Ohrid.

Dušan's Code

Dušan is remembered as a lawgiver: his Code (Dušanov zakonik), promulgated in 1349 and supplemented in 1354, is one of the few surviving monuments of medieval Serbian law and placed Serbia among the most organized states of Europe at the time. He also founded the Monastery of the Holy Archangels near Prizren, where he was buried.

Death and legacy

Dušan died suddenly in 1355 while leading a campaign toward Constantinople. His empire, which stretched from the Danube to the Gulf of Corinth, was inherited by his son Stefan Uroš V and gradually disintegrated under regional lords in the following decades, before the Ottoman conquest of the Balkans.
