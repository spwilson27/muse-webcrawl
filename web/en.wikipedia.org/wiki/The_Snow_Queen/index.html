"The Snow Queen" (Danish: Snedronningen) is an original fairy tale by Danish author Hans Christian Andersen. It was first published on 21 December 1844 in New Fairy Tales Told for Children, First Collection, Third Booklet. The story centres on the struggle between good and evil as experienced by two children, Gerda and Kay (Kai), and is one of Andersen's longest and most highly acclaimed tales.

Plot

A wicked troll creates a magic mirror that distorts everything reflected in it, making good and beautiful things appear ugly. When the mirror shatters, splinters fly across the world; one lodges in young Kay's eye and another in his heart, turning him cold and cruel. The Snow Queen takes Kay away to her palace in the far north. His friend Gerda journeys through many lands—meeting a sorceress, a prince and princess, a robber girl, and the wise woman of Lapland and Finnmark—to rescue him. In the Snow Queen's palace she finds Kay spellbound, arranging ice blocks to spell "eternity". Her warm tears melt the splinter in his heart, and his own tears wash the splinter from his eye. The two return home, grown up yet children at heart.

Structure

The tale is divided into seven stories or chapters: "About the Mirror and Its Pieces"; "A Little Boy and a Little Girl"; "The Flower Garden of the Woman Who Could Conjure"; "The Prince and Princess"; "The Little Robber Girl"; "The Lapland Woman and the Finn Woman"; and "What Happened at the Snow Queen's Palace and What Happened Afterwards".

Adaptations and influence

The tale has been adapted into numerous films, ballets, operas, and stage works, and it influenced later fantasy writing including C. S. Lewis's The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe. Disney's Frozen (2013) was loosely inspired by the story.
