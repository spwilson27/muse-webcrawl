Author: Arkady and Boris Strugatsky
Original title: Обитаемый остров
Published: 1969 (serial); 1971 (book)
Genre: Science fiction

Inhabited Island (Russian: Обитаемый остров) is a science fiction novel by Arkady and Boris Strugatsky, first published in serial form in 1969 and in book form in 1971. It belongs to the authors' "Noon Universe" cycle and follows the young Earth explorer Maxim Kammerer, who crash-lands on the planet Saraksh and becomes entangled in the affairs of a totalitarian state.

Plot

Maxim's ship is destroyed on arrival, stranding him on Saraksh, a world poisoned by war and ruled by the anonymous "Unknown Fathers". He discovers that the regime controls the population through a network of towers broadcasting radiation that induces fanatical loyalty — and agony in the small minority, the "degenerates", who react differently. Joining the military and then the resistance, Maxim gradually learns that the towers' true purpose is mind control, and the novel ends with his ambiguous alliance with the intelligence chief known as the Wanderer (revealed to be the Earth progressor Rudolf Sikorski).

Themes and reception

The novel is a political allegory about propaganda, mass manipulation, and the ethics of intervention, published under Soviet censorship that forced cuts and changes. It inspired a two-part Russian film adaptation directed by Fyodor Bondarchuk (2008–2009) and video games. Kammerer returns in the sequels Beetle in the Anthill and The Time Wanderers.
