Hardy–Ramanujan theorem

In number theory, the Hardy–Ramanujan theorem, proved by G. H. Hardy and Srinivasa Ramanujan in 1917, states that the normal order of the number of distinct prime factors ω(n) of a number n is log log n.

Statement

Roughly, most numbers near n have about log log n distinct prime factors. More precisely, for any function ψ(n) tending to infinity, the count of integers ≤ n with |ω(m) − log log n| > ψ(n)√(log log n) is o(n).

Significance

The theorem was a founding result of probabilistic number theory, later strengthened by the Erdős–Kac theorem, which shows that ω(n) is asymptotically normally distributed with mean and variance log log n. See also the Hardy–Ramanujan number, 1729.
