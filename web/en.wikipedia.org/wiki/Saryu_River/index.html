The Saryu River (also spelled Sarayu or Sarju) is a river that flows through the Indian states of Uttarakhand and Uttar Pradesh. In Uttarakhand it rises in the Himalayas in the Bageshwar district, where it is joined by the Gomati at Bageshwar, a town famed for its ancient temples, before flowing southeast to join the Sharda (Kali) River at Pancheshwar near the Nepal border.

Course in the plains

The name Saryu is also traditionally associated with the river that flows past the holy city of Ayodhya. The river at Ayodhya — in modern hydrology a channel of the Ghaghara (Karnali) system — holds immense religious significance as the river of Rama's birthplace, and its banks host major festivals such as Ram Navami, when pilgrims bathe in its waters.

Religious significance

In Hindu tradition the Saryu is one of the sacred rivers of India, mentioned in the Ramayana and in Vedic hymns. Lord Rama is said to have ended his earthly life by entering the waters of the Saryu at Guptar Ghat in Ayodhya.
