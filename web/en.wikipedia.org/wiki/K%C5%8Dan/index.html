Kōan

TraditionZen Buddhism (Chan)
LanguageJapanese: 公案; Chinese: gōng'àn
PurposeMeditation training; provoking insight

A kōan (Japanese: 公案; Chinese: gōng'àn) is a story, dialogue, question, or statement used in Zen practice to provoke doubt and test a student's progress. The word originally meant a "public case" in Chinese law, implying an authoritative precedent to be realised.

Practice

In Rinzai Zen, students are assigned kōans by their teacher and present their understanding in private interviews (dokusan). Famous examples include Zhaozhou's "Mu" (the answer to "Does a dog have Buddha-nature?"), Hakuin's "What is the sound of one hand?", and "What was your original face before your parents were born?" Working with such phrases is meant to exhaust conceptual thinking and trigger awakening (kenshō).

Collections

Classical collections of kōans with commentary include the Blue Cliff Record (1125), the Gateless Barrier (1228), and the Book of Equanimity. Sōtō Zen traditionally emphasises shikantaza ("just sitting") but also studies kōans in its own way.
