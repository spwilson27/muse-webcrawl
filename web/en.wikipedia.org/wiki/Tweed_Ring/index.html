Tweed Ring

The Tweed Ring was a group of Democratic Party politicians led by William M. "Boss" Tweed who controlled New York City politics in the late 1860s and early 1870s and defrauded the city of an enormous sum of money — estimates range from $25 million to $200 million (several billion in modern terms). At the height of their power, the Ring dominated Tammany Hall, the Board of Supervisors, the mayoralty, and much of the state government of New York.

Rise to power

William M. Tweed, a former volunteer fireman and alderman, built his influence through the Democratic political machine of Tammany Hall. By 1868–1869 the core Ring — Tweed as Grand Sachem of Tammany and commissioner of public works; Mayor A. Oakey Hall; Comptroller Richard B. Connolly; and Treasurer Peter B. Sweeny — controlled the city's finances. Their principal instrument of fraud was the systematic padding of bills for public works and supplies: contractors charged hugely inflated prices and kicked back a share to the Ring. The New York County Courthouse (still known as the Tweed Courthouse) became the emblem of the scheme: authorised at $250,000, its cost ballooned past $12 million, with individual items such as $170,000 reportedly charged for 40 chairs and tables.

Exposure and downfall

The Ring began to unravel in 1871. The New York Times, having obtained copies of the city's secret account books from a disgruntled former bookkeeper, published detailed evidence of the thefts beginning in July 1871. At the same time, the cartoonist Thomas Nast of Harper's Weekly waged a relentless visual campaign against Tweed, producing some of the most famous political cartoons in American history. Reformers led by Samuel J. Tilden prosecuted the Ring's members. Tweed was arrested, convicted of forgery and larceny in 1873, escaped to Spain in 1875, was recognised and extradited, and died in the Ludlow Street Jail in New York in 1878.

Legacy

The Tweed Ring became a byword for municipal corruption in the United States, and its exposure spurred reforms in city accounting, auditing, and charter law. The Tweed Courthouse in Manhattan still stands as a monument to the era.
