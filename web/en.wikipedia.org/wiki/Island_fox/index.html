Island fox

Island fox

Scientific name: Urocyon littoralis

Family: Canidae

Habitat: Channel Islands, California

Conservation status: Least Concern (recovered)

The island fox (Urocyon littoralis) is a small fox endemic to six of the eight Channel Islands of California. It is a dwarf descendant of the mainland gray fox, and is one of the smallest canid species in North America, roughly the size of a domestic cat. There are six subspecies, each unique to the island it lives on.

Decline and recovery

In the 1990s, island fox populations on the northern Channel Islands crashed by over 90 percent, largely due to predation by golden eagles that had colonized the islands after bald eagles (which deterred them) declined from DDT contamination. Several subspecies were listed as endangered in 2004.

Conservation success

Captive breeding, golden eagle relocation, bald eagle reintroduction, and pig removal led to a dramatic rebound. The subspecies were delisted or downlisted in 2016 in what the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service described as the fastest recovery of any mammal under the Endangered Species Act.
