Treaties of Tilsit

Signed: 7 and 9 July 1807

Location: Tilsit, East Prussia (now Sovetsk, Russia)

Parties: France, Russia, Prussia

Context: War of the Fourth Coalition

The Treaties of Tilsit, also collectively known as the Peace of Tilsit, were two agreements signed by Napoleon I of France in the town of Tilsit in July 1807 in the aftermath of his victory at the Battle of Friedland. The first, signed on 7 July, was between Napoleon and Tsar Alexander I of Russia; the second, signed on 9 July, was between France and Prussia.

Terms

The Franco-Russian treaty converted Russia from an enemy into Napoleon's ally: Alexander recognised Napoleon's rearrangements of Europe, joined the Continental System against Britain, and accepted French mediation in Russia's war with the Ottoman Empire, in return for a free hand against Sweden and the Ottomans in the Danubian principalities. Secret clauses envisaged joint action against Britain.

Prussia, by contrast, was treated with deliberate harshness. It lost roughly half of its territory and population: its lands west of the Elbe went to the new Kingdom of Westphalia and other French satellites; most of its Polish territories formed the Duchy of Warsaw under the King of Saxony; and it was subjected to a heavy indemnity and a French occupation until payment, with its army capped at 42,000 men. Queen Louise of Prussia famously pleaded with Napoleon for milder terms at Tilsit, in vain.

Consequences

Tilsit marked the zenith of Napoleon's power and divided Europe into French and Russian spheres. The humiliation of Prussia spurred the Stein–Hardenberg reforms and Prussian military renewal. The Franco-Russian alliance gradually frayed over the Continental System, Poland and the Near East, culminating in Napoleon's invasion of Russia in 1812.
