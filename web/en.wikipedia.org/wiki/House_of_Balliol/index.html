House of Balliol

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Barnard Castle in County Durham

The House of Balliol was a noble family of Norman origin, from Bailleul-en-Vimeu in Picardy, which rose to prominence in England and Scotland in the eleventh to fourteenth centuries and produced two kings of Scotland: John Balliol (r. 1292–1296) and his son Edward Balliol (r. intermittently 1332–1356).

Origins and rise

The family took its name from Bailleul in northern France. Guy I de Balliol received lands in northern England from William II around 1090, centred on what became Barnard Castle in County Durham. His descendants intermarried with the Scottish royal house: John I de Balliol married Dervorguilla of Galloway, an heiress descended from David I of Scotland, and together they founded Balliol College, Oxford (c. 1263). Their son John became a Competitor for the Scottish crown on the death of Margaret, Maid of Norway, in 1290.

Kings of Scotland

John Balliol was chosen as king by Edward I of England in the Great Cause in 1292, but his subordination to Edward provoked resistance, alliance with France (the beginning of the Auld Alliance), and invasion; he was defeated at Dunbar in 1296, forced to abdicate, and remembered by the derisive nickname Toom Tabard ("empty coat"). His son Edward Balliol, backed by Edward III of England and the "Disinherited", invaded in 1332, won at Dupplin Moor, and was crowned at Scone, but was repeatedly driven out by supporters of David II and finally ceded his claim to Edward III in 1356, dying in England in 1364 without heirs, ending the Balliol royal line.
