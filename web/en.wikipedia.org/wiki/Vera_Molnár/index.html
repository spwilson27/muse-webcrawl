Vera Molnár (5 January 1924 – 7 December 2023) was a Hungarian-French media artist living and working in Paris. She is widely considered a pioneer of computer art and generative art, and was one of the first women in the field. Her work explores geometric abstraction generated by simple rules, interrupted by what she called a small amount — "1%" — of disorder.

Life and career

Molnár studied painting and art history at the Budapest College of Fine Arts, where she met her husband François Molnár. The couple moved to Paris in 1947. From the late 1950s she produced systematic abstract works by hand, inventing what she called the machine imaginaire — following sets of instructions as if she were a computer, before she had access to one. In 1968 she began working with a real computer at the Sorbonne's computing centre, writing programs in Fortran and outputting drawings with a plotter. She co-founded the research group Art et Informatique at the University of Paris in 1976 and later taught at Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne.

Work

Molnár's art is built from elementary geometric forms — lines, squares, rectangles — arranged by algorithms and then perturbed by controlled randomness. Series such as Interruptions and her homages to Dürer (based on the magic square in Melencolia I), Mondrian and Monet explore how slight deviations transform an ordered grid. She continued to work in drawing, painting, collage and plotter prints for more than seven decades.

Recognition

Long under-recognised, Molnár received major attention late in life: her work was included in the 2022 Venice Biennale, and the Centre Pompidou in Paris held a major retrospective, Parler à l'œil, in 2024, shortly after her death at the age of 99.
