Yamada Nagamasa

Yamada Nagamasa
Japanese: 山田 長政
Born: c. 1590, Suruga, Japan
Died: 1630, Siam (Thailand)
Occupation: Adventurer, trader, commander of the Japanese corps in Siam

Yamada Nagamasa (Japanese: 山田 長政; c. 1590–1630) was a Japanese adventurer and military commander who rose to prominence in the Ayutthaya Kingdom (Siam, now Thailand) in the early 17th century. He became the head of the Japanese settlement at Ayutthaya and leader of the Japanese guard corps in Siamese service.

Career

Yamada arrived in Siam around 1612, during the great age of the Japanese red seal ship trade. He gained the favor of King Songtham and was appointed head of the Japanese community (the Nihonmachi) at Ayutthaya and commander of the Japanese volunteer corps, which fought for the Siamese crown against rebels and rival kingdoms. He received the noble rank of Okya Senaphimuk and was reportedly appointed governor of Nakhon Si Thammarat in southern Siam.

Death

After the succession crisis of 1629, in which King Chetthathirat and Athittayawong were overthrown by Prasat Thong, Yamada's position became precarious; he had supported rivals of the new king. He was sent south, wounded in battle, and died in 1630 — by some accounts poisoned. The Japanese village at Ayutthaya was subsequently attacked and destroyed, ending the large Japanese presence in Siam. His dramatic career became famous in Japan, where he is the subject of novels and films.
