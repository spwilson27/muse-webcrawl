List of Liverpool docks

The Port of Liverpool in England stretches along both banks of the River Mersey estuary: the main system of docks on the Liverpool (Lancashire) side historically ran for over 7 miles from Bootle in the north to Garston in the south, with further docks at Birkenhead on the Wirral (Cheshire) side. The world's first commercial enclosed wet dock, the Old Dock, opened in Liverpool in 1715.

Docks on the Liverpool bank (north to south, selected)

  • Seaforth Dock (1971) — the northernmost and one of the few still in commercial use; expanded as Liverpool2 (2016).
  • Gladstone Dock (1927), Alexandra Dock (1881), Langton Dock (1879), Brocklebank Dock, Canada Dock (1859), Huskisson Dock (1852), Sandon Dock, Wellington Dock.
  • Bramley-Moore Dock (1848) — site of Everton FC's new stadium (opened 2025); Nelson Dock, Salisbury Dock, Collingwood Dock, Stanley Dock (1848) — with the vast Stanley Dock tobacco warehouse.
  • Prince's Dock (1821), Waterloo Dock, Victoria Dock, Trafalgar Dock, Albert Dock (1846) — designed by Jesse Hartley; now the Royal Albert Dock, a major leisure and cultural complex housing Tate Liverpool.
  • Canning Dock (1737/1821), Salthouse Dock (1734–1753), Duke's Dock (1773), King's Dock, Wapping Dock, Queen's Dock (1785/1902), Coburg Dock, Brunswick Dock (1832), Toxteth Dock, Harrington Dock, Herculaneum Dock (1866).
  • Garston Dock (1853) — a separate dock system to the south, still operational.

Birkenhead docks

Across the Mersey, the Birkenhead system designed by James Meadows Rendel included the Great Float (East and West Floats), Morpeth Dock, Egerton Dock, Alfred Dock, and Wallasey Dock, together with the Twelve Quays ro-ro terminal.

History

The dock system was administered from 1858 by the Mersey Docks and Harbour Board, and from 1971 by the Mersey Docks and Harbour Company. The southern central docks closed in 1972 and many were infilled or redeveloped, notably for the Liverpool Maritime Mercantile City World Heritage Site (2004–2021).
