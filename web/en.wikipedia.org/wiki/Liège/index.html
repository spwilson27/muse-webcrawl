Liège (French: Liège; Dutch: Luik; German: Lüttich; Walloon: Lidje) is a major city in Belgium, the capital of the province of the same name and the largest city of the French-speaking Walloon Region. It lies in the valley of the River Meuse in the east of the country, close to the Dutch and German borders. Liège is the third-largest urban area in Belgium and has been a major economic, industrial and cultural centre since the Middle Ages.

History

Liège grew around the shrine of St Lambert, martyred there around 705. From the late 10th century its bishops ruled it as the capital of the Prince-Bishopric of Liège, an ecclesiastical state of the Holy Roman Empire lasting until the 1790s. The medieval city was famed — and repeatedly fought over — for its liberties, codified in charters such as the Charter of Huy (1066) and the Peace of Fexhe (1316); in 1468 Charles the Bold of Burgundy had the city sacked and largely destroyed in punishment for revolt.

Standing on rich coal measures, Liège became a cradle of the Industrial Revolution on the European continent: coal mining, steel, glass (Val Saint-Lambert), arms manufacture (FN Herstal), and John Cockerill's integrated works at Seraing made the Meuse valley an industrial powerhouse in the 19th century. In August 1914 the city's ring of forts made the first stand against the German invasion, delaying the Schlieffen Plan timetable; the city received the French Légion d'honneur for its resistance. Heavy industry declined from the 1960s, and the region has since reinvented itself around logistics, aeronautics, biotech and the University of Liège (founded 1817).

Sights and culture

Landmarks include the vast Prince-Bishops' Palace (now law courts) on Place Saint-Lambert, the collegiate churches of St Bartholomew (with its famous baptismal font, a masterpiece of Mosan art) and St James, the Montagne de Bueren staircase of 374 steps, the modern Liège-Guillemins railway station designed by Santiago Calatrava (2009), and the Grand Curtius museum. Liège is the birthplace of the writer Georges Simenon, creator of Inspector Maigret, and of the composer César Franck. The city hosts the Sunday La Batte market, one of Europe's oldest and largest, and the cycling classic Liège–Bastogne–Liège, the oldest of cycling's Monuments, first run in 1892.
