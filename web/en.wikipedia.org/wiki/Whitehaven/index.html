Whitehaven is a town and civil parish on the west coast of Cumbria, England, on the Irish Sea. It was one of Britain's earliest planned towns, laid out on a grid pattern in the late 17th century by the Lowther family, and grew into a major port for the coal trade and the import of tobacco and rum from the Americas. By the mid-18th century it rivalled Bristol and briefly Liverpool as a port.

History

In April 1778, during the American Revolutionary War, John Paul Jones led a raid on Whitehaven — the last invasion of England by American forces — attempting to burn the shipping in the harbour. The town's mining heritage is marked by numerous coal pits, including undersea workings. Whitehaven's Georgian architecture survives extensively in its town centre, and its maritime and rum-trading history is told at the Rum Story visitor attraction. The nearby Sellafield nuclear site is a major regional employer.
