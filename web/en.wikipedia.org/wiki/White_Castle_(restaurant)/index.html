White Castle (restaurant)

White Castle is an American regional hamburger restaurant chain with about 345 locations across 13 states, with its greatest presence in the Midwest and New York metropolitan area. Founded on September 13, 1921, in Wichita, Kansas, by Billy Ingram and Walter Anderson, it is generally credited with being the world's first fast-food hamburger chain.

The slider

White Castle is known for its small, square hamburgers, called "sliders" (originally priced at five cents into the 1940s). The patties are steam-grilled on a bed of onions with the buns on top, absorbing the flavour, and are sold singly or in multi-pack "Cravers" boxes and "Crave Cases" of thirty.

Innovations

The chain standardised the look, construction, and operation of fast-food restaurants: clean white porcelain-enamel buildings, visible kitchens, uniformed staff, and take-out service. It sold the first fast-food coupons in newspapers and operated its own paper goods and building subsidiaries, Porcelain Steel Buildings. Its headquarters are now in Columbus, Ohio.

Cultural impact

White Castle was named the most influential burger of all time by Time magazine in 2014, and features prominently in the 2004 film Harold & Kumar Go to White Castle.
