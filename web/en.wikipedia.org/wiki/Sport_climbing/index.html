Sport climbing

Sport climbing is a form of rock climbing that relies on permanent anchors fixed to the rock, typically steel bolts, for protection rather than removable gear placed by the climber. Unlike traditional climbing, in which climbers must place and remove their own protection as they ascend, sport climbing emphasizes the gymnastic and athletic aspects of the ascent, because the climber is freed from the strenuous and often dangerous task of placing protection.

History

The origins of sport climbing lie in the free climbing movement of the 1970s, when climbers sought to climb routes using only their hands and feet on the rock, with ropes and hardware used only to catch falls. In France, climbers such as Patrick Edlinger popularized the style on the limestone cliffs of Verdon Gorge and Buoux, pre-inspecting routes, rehearsing moves, and placing closely spaced bolts. In the United States, areas such as Smith Rock in Oregon became early sport climbing centres in the 1980s. The practice of "redpointing" — climbing a route from bottom to top without falling after prior rehearsal — became the standard measure of a successful ascent.

Equipment and technique

A sport climber carries a rope, a harness, climbing shoes, a belay device, and a set of quickdraws, which are clipped into the bolts along the route; the rope is then clipped through the quickdraws as the climber ascends. Routes are typically climbed "on lead", with the rope running below the climber, or on top rope. Bolt spacing is generally close enough that falls are frequent and comparatively safe, which allows climbers to attempt moves at the limit of their ability.

Grading

Sport routes are graded using systems such as the French numerical scale (common in Europe), the Yosemite Decimal System (common in North America), and the UIAA scale. Grades measure the difficulty of the hardest moves and the overall sustained difficulty of the route.

Competition climbing

Sport climbing in its competitive form comprises three disciplines: lead climbing, bouldering, and speed climbing. The sport made its Olympic debut at the Tokyo 2020 Games (held in 2021) and returned at Paris 2024, where boulder-and-lead was separated from speed. World Cup events are organized by the International Federation of Sport Climbing (IFSC), founded in 2007.

Ethics and environmental issues

The drilling of bolts into natural rock has been controversial since the sport's beginnings. Debates over bolting — sometimes called the "bolt wars" in the United States — concern land management rules, wilderness preservation, and respect for traditional climbing ethics. Many areas restrict or prohibit power drills, and access organizations work with land managers to maintain climbing access.
