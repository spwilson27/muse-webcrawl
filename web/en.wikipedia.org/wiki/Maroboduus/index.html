Maroboduus

Maroboduus (c. 30 BC – AD 37) was a king of the Germanic Marcomanni tribe. He established a powerful kingdom in Bohemia that for a time posed a serious challenge to the Roman Empire.

Rise

A nobleman who had spent time in Rome and received military training, Maroboduus led the Marcomanni from their homeland between the Main and Danube into Bohemia, where he built a federation of tribes and a strong army modelled partly on Roman lines.

Relations with Rome

In AD 6 Rome planned a two-pronged invasion of his kingdom, but the campaign was abandoned because of the Great Illyrian Revolt. Maroboduus avoided joining Arminius's coalition that destroyed three legions at the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest in AD 9, and later refused Arminius's request for alliance. Overthrown by the chieftain Catualda in AD 19, he fled to Rome and lived in exile at Ravenna for the rest of his life.
