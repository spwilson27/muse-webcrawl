Sine wave

A sine wave, or sinusoid, is a mathematical curve that describes a smooth periodic oscillation. It is a type of continuous wave and also a fundamental waveform in mathematics, physics, engineering and signal processing. The sine wave is important because, by Fourier analysis, any periodic waveform can be decomposed into a sum of sine waves, and systems governed by linear equations respond to a sinusoidal input with a sinusoidal output of the same frequency.

y(t) = A sin(2πft + φ) = A sin(ωt + φ)

where A is the amplitude (peak deviation from zero), f the ordinary frequency in hertz, ω = 2πf the angular frequency, and φ the phase specifying where in the cycle the oscillation begins.

Properties

The sine wave retains its shape when added to another sine wave of the same frequency, and its derivatives and integrals are also sinusoids of the same frequency. These properties make it the natural basis for analysing alternating current electricity (mains power is sinusoidal), sound (a pure tone is a sine wave), and simple harmonic motion: the projection of uniform circular motion onto a diameter traces a sine wave in time.

Fourier analysis

Fourier's theorem states that any periodic signal can be represented as a sum of sine and cosine waves at harmonics of the fundamental frequency. The cosine wave is identical in shape to the sine wave, shifted by a quarter period; they are collectively called sinusoids.
