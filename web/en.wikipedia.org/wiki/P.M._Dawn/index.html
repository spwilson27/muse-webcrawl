P.M. Dawn was an American hip hop and R&B duo formed in Jersey City, New Jersey, in 1988 by brothers Attrell Cordes (known as Prince Be) and Jarrett Cordes (known as DJ Minutemix). Renowned for blending hip hop with psychedelic and pop influences, lush sampling, and spiritually themed lyrics, the duo achieved major commercial success in the early 1990s.

History

The Cordes brothers began recording together in the late 1980s, funding their first single, "Ode to a Forgetful Mind" (1989), with money saved from jobs. After signing with Gee Street Records in the UK, they released their debut album, Of the Heart, of the Soul and of the Cross: The Utopian Experience (1991). Its single "Set Adrift on Memory Bliss," built on a sample of Spandau Ballet's "True," reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100.

Their second album, The Bliss Album...? (1993), yielded the hits "I'd Die Without You" — also featured in the film Boomerang — and "Looking Through Patient Eyes," which sampled George Michael's "Father Figure." Subsequent albums included Jesus Wept (1995), Dearest Christian, I'm So Very Sorry for Bringing You Here. Love, Dad (1998), and F*cked Music (2000).

Legal dispute and later years

The duo was famously sued over the sample of "True" in "Set Adrift on Memory Bliss," a dispute that became part of the shifting legal landscape for sampling in hip hop. Prince Be's health declined due to diabetes and a stroke in 2005; he died of renal disease on June 17, 2016, at the age of 46. DJ Minutemix continued to perform the group's material with a cousin, Doc.G.

Style and legacy

P.M. Dawn's introspective, melodic approach — sometimes called "hippie hop" or psychedelic hip hop — set them apart from the harder-edged acts of their era and anticipated later alternative hip hop. Prince Be frequently cited the Beatles and Prince among his influences, and the duo's dreamlike production style drew on extensive sampling of rock, soul, and new wave records.
