Robert Cleckler Bowden (November 8, 1929 – August 8, 2021) was an American college football coach. He coached the Florida State Seminoles from 1976 to 2009, building the program into a national power and winning national championships in 1993 and 1999. At his retirement he was the second-winningest coach in major college football history, behind only Joe Paterno, with 377 wins (some later vacated by NCAA sanctions). He was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame in 2006.

Early life and early coaching

Bowden was born in Birmingham, Alabama, and played quarterback at Howard College (now Samford University), where he later returned as head coach from 1959 to 1962. After assistant coaching roles including a stint as an assistant at Florida State under Bill Peterson and at West Virginia under Jim Carlen, he became head coach at West Virginia in 1970, compiling a 42–26 record and winning the 1975 Peach Bowl before taking the Florida State job.

Florida State

When Bowden arrived in Tallahassee in 1976, Florida State had won four games in the previous three seasons. He embraced scheduling anyone anywhere — earning the Seminoles the nickname "Road Warriors" — and by the 1980s the program was a perennial contender. From 1987 to 2000, Florida State finished in the top five of the AP poll for fourteen consecutive seasons, an unmatched streak. The 1993 team, led by Heisman Trophy winner Charlie Ward, won Bowden's first national title; the 1999 team, with Chris Weinke, went wire-to-wire ranked No. 1.

Bowden was famous for trick plays, a folksy public manner, and producing NFL talent. His rivalry games against Miami and Florida (the latter against Steve Spurrier) defined the era. His sons Terry, Tommy, and Jeff all became football coaches.

Retirement and death

After declining results in the 2000s and a succession plan naming Jimbo Fisher, Bowden retired following the Seminoles' Gator Bowl victory over West Virginia on January 1, 2010. Diagnosed with pancreatic cancer, he died in Tallahassee on August 8, 2021, aged 91.
