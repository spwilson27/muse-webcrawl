Annona (goddess)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Annona is the divine personification of the grain supply in ancient Roman religion. Closely associated with Ceres, the goddess of the harvest, Annona embodied the annual harvest (annona from annus, "year") and, under the Empire, the regular importation and distribution of grain that fed the city of Rome.

Role and significance

Rome depended on imported wheat from Sicily, North Africa, and Egypt, shipped to the port of Ostia and later Portus. The state administration known as the cura annonae supervised this supply, and free or subsidised grain distributions (the frumentationes) were a cornerstone of imperial popularity. Annona, as a personification, represented this system on coinage and public monuments, expressing the emperor's care for the people's food supply.

Iconography

Annona is typically shown as a standing female figure holding ears of grain and a cornucopia, often with a modius (grain measure) at her feet and sometimes next to the prow of a grain ship, alluding to the maritime supply routes. The type Annona Augusti became especially common on coins of the emperors from the first century CE onward, and she also appears together with Ceres, who holds a torch or grain on related issues.
