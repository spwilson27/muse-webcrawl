Final Fantasy VII is a 1997 role-playing video game developed and published by Square for the PlayStation. It is the seventh main instalment in the Final Fantasy series and the first to use 3D computer graphics with fully rendered characters over pre-rendered backgrounds, and the first released on CD-ROM. The game follows Cloud Strife, a mercenary who joins the eco-terrorist group AVALANCHE to fight the Shinra megacorporation, and becomes entangled in a struggle against the fallen hero Sephiroth, who seeks to wound the planet and attain godhood.

Gameplay

Players guide Cloud and a party of up to three characters through towns, dungeons, and a world map. Battles use the series' Active Time Battle system, augmented by the Materia system: magical orbs slotted into weapons and armour grant spells, abilities, and summoned monsters, allowing flexible character customisation. Characters who fill their Limit gauge unleash unique Limit Break attacks. Mini-games, chocobo breeding and racing, and numerous side quests supplement the main story.

Plot

In the city of Midgar, the Shinra Electric Power Company drains the planet's life energy, Mako, for profit. Cloud, hired by AVALANCHE leader Barret Wallace, helps destroy a reactor and befriends flower seller Aerith Gainsborough, the last of the ancient Cetra. When the legendary SOLDIER Sephiroth resurfaces — having learned he was created from the cells of the alien Jenova — he sets out to summon Meteor and absorb the planet's energy. Aerith's death at Sephiroth's hands became one of gaming's most famous scenes. In the finale, Aerith's summoned Holy, aided by the Lifestream itself, destroys Meteor and saves the planet.

Development

Development began in 1994, briefly for Nintendo hardware, before Square moved the project to the PlayStation because of the storage capacity of CD-ROM, a shift that ended its partnership with Nintendo. Hironobu Sakaguchi produced, Yoshinori Kitase directed, Tetsuya Nomura designed the characters, and Nobuo Uematsu composed the score, including the celebrated "One-Winged Angel", the series' first track with recorded vocals. Themes of life, death, and environmentalism were informed in part by the death of Sakaguchi's mother.

Reception and legacy

Final Fantasy VII was a massive commercial and critical success, selling over 14 million copies worldwide across its releases and earning acclaim for its story, characters, music, and cinematic presentation. It popularised Japanese role-playing games with a global mainstream audience, boosted PlayStation sales, and is frequently cited among the greatest video games of all time. Its success spawned the Compilation of Final Fantasy VII multimedia project, including the film Advent Children (2005), and a multi-part remake series beginning with Final Fantasy VII Remake (2020) and continuing with Final Fantasy VII Rebirth (2024).
