Umayyad dynasty

The Umayyad dynasty (Arabic: Banū Umayya) was the ruling family of the Umayyad Caliphate, the first hereditary dynasty in Islam, reigning from 661 to 750 CE with its capital at Damascus. Founded by Mu'awiya I after the First Fitna, it oversaw the caliphate's greatest territorial expansion.

History

Mu'awiya, governor of Syria, became caliph after Hasan ibn Ali's abdication. Under his successors the empire expanded: North Africa, Iberia (al-Andalus from 711), Sindh, and Transoxiana were conquered, making the caliphate one of the largest empires in history. Caliphs such as Abd al-Malik arabised administration, minted Islamic coinage, and built the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem (691) and the Great Mosque of Damascus.

Fall and legacy

Discontent over taxation, tribal factionalism (Qays–Yaman), and mawali grievances fed the Abbasid Revolution; the last caliph, Marwan II, was defeated at the Battle of the Zab in 750. One survivor, Abd al-Rahman I, fled to Iberia and founded the Emirate (later Caliphate) of Córdoba, where Umayyads ruled until 1031.
