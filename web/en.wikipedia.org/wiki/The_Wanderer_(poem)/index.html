The Wanderer (poem)

The Wanderer is an Old English poem preserved in the Exeter Book, a tenth-century manuscript. At 115 lines, it is an elegy spoken by an exiled warrior, an anhaga ("solitary one"), who mourns his dead lord, his lost companions and the hall-joys of his youth while wandering over the icy sea.

Content

The poem moves from the wanderer's lament to generalised reflections on the transience of earthly life — the famous ubi sunt passage asking "Where is the horse? Where the rider?" — amid ruins of "the work of giants". It closes with a turn to Christian consolation, concluding that "it will be well for him who seeks grace, comfort from the Father in heaven, where for us all stability resides."

Interpretation

Scholars debate the poem's structure and voice: whether the lament and the moralising conclusion belong to a single speaker, and how its pagan-heroic ethos combines with its Christian frame. Together with The Seafarer, it is among the best-known Old English elegies, and its ruins and exile imagery influenced J. R. R. Tolkien, notably the lament of the Rohirrim in The Two Towers.
