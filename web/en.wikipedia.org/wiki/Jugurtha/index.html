Jugurtha

Jugurtha (c. 160 – 104 BC) was a king of Numidia, in present-day Algeria, who fought the Jugurthine War (112–106 BC) against the Roman Republic. An illegitimate grandson of Masinissa, he seized the Numidian throne by murdering his rivals, resisted Roman armies for years through guerrilla warfare and bribery, and was finally betrayed by his father-in-law Bocchus of Mauretania and delivered to Sulla. His war, chronicled by Sallust, exposed corruption in the late Roman Republic and launched the career of Gaius Marius.

Rise to power

Jugurtha served with distinction under Scipio Aemilianus at the siege of Numantia in Spain, where he impressed Roman officers including the young Gaius Marius. Adopted by his uncle Micipsa as co-heir alongside Hiempsal and Adherbal, he had Hiempsal murdered after Micipsa's death (118 BC), then besieged Adherbal in Cirta and massacred him together with the Italian traders sheltering there (112 BC) — an outrage that forced a reluctant Roman Senate to declare war.

Jugurthine War

Jugurtha defeated or bought off successive Roman commanders — Sallust has him call Rome "a city for sale" — until Quintus Caecilius Metellus, and then Marius, waged a methodical campaign through Numidia. In 105 BC Bocchus, king of Mauretania and Jugurtha's ally, was persuaded by Marius's quaestor Sulla to hand him over. Jugurtha was paraded in Marius's triumph on 1 January 104 BC and starved to death in the Tullianum prison.
