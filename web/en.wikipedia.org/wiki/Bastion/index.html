Bastion

A bastion or bulwark is an angular projecting structure forming part of a fortification, designed to allow defensive fire in multiple directions and to eliminate dead ground at the base of the walls. Bastions are the characteristic element of the trace italienne (star fort) fortifications developed in Renaissance Italy.

Design

A typical bastion is a low, broad, arrowhead- or pentagon-shaped platform of earth and masonry projecting from a curtain wall. Its two long faces meet at a salient angle facing the enemy, while its flanks, often recessed with "orillons", housed guns that swept the ditch and the face of the neighbouring bastion with flanking fire. The design emerged in the late 15th and early 16th centuries in response to mobile siege artillery, replacing tall medieval round towers that were vulnerable to cannon fire.

History and spread

Italian engineers such as Francesco di Giorgio Martini pioneered bastioned systems, which spread across Europe and its colonies through engineers like Vauban and Menno van Coehoorn. Bastioned fortresses dominated warfare from the 16th to the 19th century, until rifled artillery and explosive shells made them obsolete.
