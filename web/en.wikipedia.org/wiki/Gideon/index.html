Gideon (Hebrew: גִדְעוֹן, Gīdʿōn), also named Jerubbaal, is a military leader, judge, and prophet in the Hebrew Bible whose story is told in chapters 6–8 of the Book of Judges. He is remembered for defeating a vast Midianite army with only 300 men.

Biblical narrative

According to the narrative, Israel suffered seven years of Midianite raids because of its idolatry. An angel appeared to Gideon, the youngest son of Joash of the clan of Abiezer, while he was threshing wheat in a winepress to hide it, and greeted him as a "mighty man of valor." After destroying his father's altar to Baal — earning the name Jerubbaal, "let Baal contend against him" — Gideon sought confirming signs, including the famous test of the fleece: dew on a wool fleece while the ground stayed dry, then the reverse.

God reduced Gideon's army from 32,000 to 300 men, selected partly by how they drank water at a spring. Armed with trumpets, empty pitchers, and torches, the 300 attacked the Midianite camp at night, throwing the enemy into confusion and rout. Gideon then pursued the Midianite kings Zebah and Zalmunna across the Jordan and captured them. After the victory, the Israelites asked Gideon to rule over them; he refused, saying the Lord would rule them, though he accepted gold earrings from the spoil and made an ephod that became an object of idolatry.

Family and legacy

Gideon had seventy sons by many wives, plus a son, Abimelech, by a concubine in Shechem; Abimelech later murdered his brothers and briefly ruled Shechem. Gideon died at a good old age and was buried at Ophrah. He is listed among the heroes of faith in the New Testament's Epistle to the Hebrews (11:32). The name Gideon is associated with the Gideons International, the organization known for placing Bibles in hotels, founded in 1899 and named partly for him.
