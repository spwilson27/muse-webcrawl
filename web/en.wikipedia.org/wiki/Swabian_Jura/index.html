The Swabian Jura (German: Schwäbische Alb), sometimes also called the Swabian Alps, is a mountain range in Baden-Württemberg, Germany, extending about 200 km from southwest to northeast. It is a high limestone plateau, averaging 700–800 m elevation, bounded by the upper Danube valley to the southeast and the Neckar valley escarpment to the northwest.

Geology and landscape

The range is formed of Jurassic limestones deposited in a tropical sea roughly 200–145 million years ago; its fossils, notably from the Solnhofen-type deposits and the ammonite-rich quarries, are famous. Karst processes have produced dry valleys, sinkholes, caves, and underground rivers; water is scarce on the plateau surface, shaping its distinctive settlement pattern and juniper heathlands.

Prehistory

The caves of the Ach and Lone valleys (including Hohle Fels, Vogelherd, and Hohlenstein-Stadel) preserve the earliest known figurative art and music in the world, made by Aurignacian hunter-gatherers some 40,000 years ago: the Venus of Hohle Fels, the Lion-man figurine, animal carvings, and bone and ivory flutes. In 2017 these sites were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, "Caves and Ice Age Art in the Swabian Jura".
