Barry Diller

Barry Charles Diller (born February 2, 1942) is an American businessman, chairman and senior executive of IAC and of Expedia Group. Over a career of more than five decades he has run Paramount Pictures, created the Fox Broadcasting Company, and built a portfolio of internet businesses including Match Group, Vimeo, Expedia and the home-shopping network HSN.

Career

Diller started in the mailroom of the William Morris Agency, then moved to ABC, where as a young programming executive he pioneered the made-for-television movie format with ABC Movie of the Week. In 1974, aged 32, he became chairman of Paramount Pictures, which during his decade in charge released hits from Saturday Night Fever and Grease to Raiders of the Lost Ark and Beverly Hills Cop, while its television arm produced Cheers and Taxi.

Moving to 20th Century Fox in 1984, Diller persuaded Rupert Murdoch to launch Fox as a fourth US television network in 1986, green-lighting The Simpsons and Married... with Children. After leaving Fox in 1992 he acquired the QVC shopping channel stake that led to control of Silver King Communications and HSN; from these assets he assembled IAC/InterActiveCorp, spinning off Expedia and other holdings to shareholders. A celebrated mentor, his protégés — the "Dillerettes" — include Michael Eisner, Jeffrey Katzenberg and Dawn Steel.

Personal life

Diller is married to fashion designer Diane von Fürstenberg. He was a major funder of Little Island, the public park on Pier 55 in the Hudson River in New York, which opened in 2021.
