Samaria (ancient city)

Location: Near modern Sebastia, West Bank

Founded: c. 880/879 BCE by King Omri

Abandoned: Gradually during the early medieval period

Region: Samaria, central Levant

Samaria (Hebrew: שֹׁמְרוֹן, Šōmrōn; Greek: Σαμάρεια) was an ancient city in the land of Israel and the capital of the northern Kingdom of Israel from its foundation by King Omri around 880 BCE until its destruction by the Assyrian Empire in 722/720 BCE.

History

According to the Hebrew Bible, Omri purchased the hill from Shemer for two talents of silver and built the city upon it, naming it after the former owner. His son Ahab developed Samaria into a wealthy royal capital with an ivory-decorated palace, attested by the celebrated Samaria ivories recovered by excavators.

Samaria withstood a famous siege described in the Book of Kings during the time of Elisha. In 722/720 BCE, after a three-year siege, the city fell to Sargon II of Assyria, and much of its population was deported, an event that ended the northern kingdom.

Hellenistic and Roman periods

The city recovered under Persian, Hellenistic and Roman rule. Herod the Great rebuilt it lavishly around 30 BCE and renamed it Sebaste in honour of Augustus (Greek Sebastos), erecting a temple to the emperor on the acropolis. Sebaste became an important town of Roman Palestine and later a Byzantine bishopric.

Archaeology

Excavations by Harvard University (1908–1910) and the Joint Expedition (1931–1935) uncovered palaces, fortifications, ivories, ostraca, and remains of Herodian and Roman temples, streets and a stadium. The Samaria ostraca provide key evidence for Hebrew names and administration in the 8th century BCE.
