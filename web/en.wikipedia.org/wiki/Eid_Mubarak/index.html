Eid Mubarak

Eid Mubarak (Arabic: عِيد مُبَارَك, romanized: ʿīd mubārak) is an Arabic term meaning "Blessed feast/festival". The term is used by Muslims all over the world as a greeting to celebrate Eid al-Fitr (marking the end of Ramadan) and Eid al-Adha. This exchange of greetings is cultural tradition and not part of any religious obligation.

Usage

Throughout the Muslim world there are numerous other greetings for Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha. In Turkey, people wish each other Bayramınız mübarek olsun; in Malaysia and Indonesia, Selamat Hari Raya; in Nigeria and other Hausa-speaking regions, Barka da Sallah. The customary reply to "Eid Mubarak" is "Khair Mubarak" (خير مبارك), which wishes goodness on the one who has greeted you.

Regional variations

In South Asia, the greeting is often accompanied by a hug (Eid gale milna) after the Eid prayers. In the Levant, "Kul 'am wa-antum bi-khair" ("may every year find you in good health") is also common. During the month of Ramadan itself, the related greeting "Ramadan Mubarak" or "Ramadan Kareem" is used.
