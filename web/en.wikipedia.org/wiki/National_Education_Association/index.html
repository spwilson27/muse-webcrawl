National Education Association

NEA
Founded: 1857, Philadelphia
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Membership: approx. 3 million

The National Education Association (NEA) is the largest labor union in the United States, representing public school teachers, support personnel, faculty and staff at colleges and universities, retired educators, and students preparing to become teachers. With around three million members, it is headquartered in Washington, D.C., and has affiliate organizations in every state.

History

The NEA was founded in 1857 in Philadelphia as the National Teachers Association; Zalmon Richards was its first president. It received a congressional charter in 1906. Originally an organization led largely by school administrators, it transformed in the 1960s and 1970s into a collective-bargaining union representing classroom teachers. It is credited with coining and promoting Education Week celebrations and, through its Read Across America program (launched 1998), promoting children's literacy on March 2, the birthday of Dr. Seuss.

Activities

The NEA advocates for public education funding, smaller class sizes, and professional pay, and it is active in electoral politics, generally supporting Democratic candidates. Its Representative Assembly, held annually, is one of the largest deliberative bodies in the world. The association is frequently compared with the smaller American Federation of Teachers, with which it has considered (but not completed) merger.
