Districts of Portugal

The districts of Portugal (Portuguese: distritos de Portugal) were the first-level administrative subdivisions of mainland Portugal from 1835 until the administrative reforms of 2013–2015, which left them with a reduced role. There are 18 districts on the mainland; the autonomous regions of the Azores and Madeira do not use districts.

List

The districts are Aveiro, Beja, Braga, Bragança, Castelo Branco, Coimbra, Évora, Faro, Guarda, Leiria, Lisbon, Portalegre, Porto, Santarém, Setúbal, Viana do Castelo, Vila Real, and Viseu. Each takes its name from its capital city.

History and role

Districts were headed by a civil governor appointed by the central government, a post abolished in 2011. Although districts lost most administrative functions, they remain in use for electoral constituencies, statistical purposes, football associations, and the territorial organization of some public services such as the police and civil protection.
