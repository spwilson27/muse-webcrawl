Orders, decorations, and medals of Japan

Japanese honours system

Country: Japan

Supreme order: Order of the Chrysanthemum

Founded: 1875 (Order of the Rising Sun)

The Japanese honours system comprises the orders, decorations and medals of Japan, established during the Meiji Restoration as Japan built the institutions of a modern state. The first order, the Order of the Rising Sun, was founded in 1875; the supreme Order of the Chrysanthemum followed in 1876. Decorations are conferred by the Emperor on the advice of the Cabinet, and most are announced twice a year, on 29 April and 3 November.

The orders

  • Order of the Chrysanthemum — the highest order, reserved for royalty and heads of state, and awarded only exceptionally to Japanese subjects.
  • Order of the Paulownia Flowers — established in 1888 as the highest class of the Rising Sun and separated in 2003, awarded for outstanding achievements in public life.
  • Order of the Rising Sun — awarded for distinguished service to the state in fields other than the military since 1945; the first national decoration established in Japan.
  • Order of the Sacred Treasure — founded in 1888, awarded for long and meritorious public service.
  • Order of Culture — a single-class order founded in 1937, presented personally by the Emperor for major contributions to art, literature and science.
  • Order of the Precious Crown — historically awarded to women and now chiefly to foreign royalty.

Medals of honour

Alongside the orders, Japan confers Medals of Honour (hōshō) distinguished by ribbon colour: the Medal with Red Ribbon for saving life, Green Ribbon for moral exemplarity, Yellow Ribbon for diligence in work, Purple Ribbon for contributions to the arts and scholarship, Blue Ribbon for public service, and Dark Blue Ribbon for philanthropy. Campaign and commemorative medals were issued extensively before 1945.

Modern system

The system was reviewed comprehensively in 2002–2003: classes were renumbered, the award of orders to women was equalised, and the Paulownia Flowers class became a separate order. Japanese orders are notable for their craft — enamelled badges of paulownia, chrysanthemum, sun-ray and sacred-mirror motifs produced by the Japan Mint.
