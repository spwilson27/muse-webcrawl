Geography of Australia

Continent: Australia
Area: 7,692,024 km²
Coastline: 59,736 km
Highest point: Mount Kosciuszko (2,228 m)
Largest lake: Lake Eyre

The geography of Australia encompasses a wide variety of biogeographic regions. Australia is the world's smallest continent but its sixth-largest country, with a land area of about 7.7 million square kilometres. It is the lowest, flattest and (after Antarctica) driest inhabited continent, lying between the Pacific and Indian Oceans in the Southern Hemisphere.

Physical geography

Australia's landscape is dominated by the Outback, the vast arid interior that covers roughly 70 per cent of the landmass. The Great Dividing Range runs roughly parallel to the east and southeast coasts for more than 3,500 km and contains the country's highest peak, Mount Kosciuszko (2,228 m). To the west lies the Western Plateau, including the Great Victoria, Great Sandy and Gibson deserts. The central lowlands contain Lake Eyre, an endorheic salt lake that is the lowest point of the continent at about 15 m below sea level, and the Artesian Basin, one of the largest artesian groundwater systems in the world.

Climate

The climate ranges from tropical in the north, with a monsoonal wet season, through arid and semi-arid conditions in the interior, to temperate in the southeast and southwest and alpine conditions in the high country of the southeast. Rainfall is highly variable and strongly influenced by the El Niño–Southern Oscillation, the Indian Ocean Dipole and drought cycles.

Biodiversity

Long geographic isolation since the breakup of Gondwana has produced a distinctive flora and fauna, including marsupials such as kangaroos and koalas, monotremes (platypus and echidnas), and eucalypt-dominated forests. Off the northeast coast lies the Great Barrier Reef, the world's largest coral reef system, stretching more than 2,300 km.

Human geography

Australia's population of roughly 26 million is concentrated overwhelmingly in coastal urban centres in the southeast and east — Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide — reflecting the aridity of the interior. The country comprises six states and two mainland territories, and its surrounding maritime claims include one of the world's largest exclusive economic zones.
