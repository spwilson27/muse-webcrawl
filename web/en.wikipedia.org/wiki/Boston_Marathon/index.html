The Boston Marathon is an annual marathon race hosted in the Boston metropolitan area, traditionally held on Patriots' Day, the third Monday in April. First run in 1897, inspired by the success of the marathon at the 1896 Olympic Games, it is the world's oldest annual marathon and one of the six World Marathon Majors. Entry is famously selective: most runners must meet age- and gender-based qualifying standards.

Course

The point-to-point course runs 26 miles 385 yards from Hopkinton through Ashland, Framingham, Natick, Wellesley — whose college students form the cheering "Scream Tunnel" — and Newton, whose four hills culminate in Heartbreak Hill, before finishing on Boylston Street in Boston beside the public library. Because the course is net downhill and point-to-point, times set at Boston are not eligible for world records.

History

Organised by the Boston Athletic Association, the race began with 15 starters in 1897; John J. McDermott won in 2:55:10. Women were long excluded officially: Roberta Gibb ran unofficially in 1966, Kathrine Switzer registered and finished in 1967 despite an official's attempt to remove her, and women were admitted officially in 1972. Clarence DeMar won seven times; Bill Rodgers and Catherine Ndereba won four each. In 2013 two bombs exploded near the finish line, killing three spectators and injuring hundreds; the 2014 race became a symbol of civic resilience under the phrase "Boston Strong". The race was held virtually in 2020 and in October in 2021 owing to the COVID-19 pandemic, before returning to April.
