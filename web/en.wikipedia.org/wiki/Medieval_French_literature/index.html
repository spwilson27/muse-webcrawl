Medieval French literature

Medieval French literature is literature written in Old French and Middle French during the period from the 9th century — the Strasbourg Oaths of 842 are the earliest surviving French text — to the end of the 15th century.

Genres

Its earliest masterpieces are the chansons de geste, epic poems of heroic deeds such as The Song of Roland (c. 1100). In the 12th century, troubadour-influenced courtly lyric and the romances of Chrétien de Troyes introduced the Arthurian legend and the ideal of courtly love. Other major forms include the beast epic Roman de Renart, the allegorical Roman de la Rose, saints' lives, chronicles such as Froissart's, mystery plays and farces.

Later Middle Ages

The 14th and 15th centuries produced the poetry of Christine de Pizan, the first professional woman of letters in France, and François Villon, whose Testament remains a landmark of French lyric. By 1500, printing and Renaissance humanism were transforming French letters.
