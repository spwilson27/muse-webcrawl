Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality is one of the most widely used inequalities in mathematics. It states that for vectors u and v in an inner product space, |⟨u, v⟩| ≤ ‖u‖‖v‖, with equality if and only if u and v are linearly dependent. In coordinates for real numbers it reads (Σaᵢbᵢ)² ≤ (Σaᵢ²)(Σbᵢ²).

FieldLinear algebra, analysis
Named afterAugustin-Louis Cauchy, Viktor Bunyakovsky, Hermann Schwarz
First formsCauchy 1821; Bunyakovsky 1859; Schwarz 1888

History

Cauchy proved the finite-sum version in 1821. Viktor Bunyakovsky obtained the integral version in 1859, and Hermann Amandus Schwarz gave the general form in 1888, so the result is also called the Cauchy–Bunyakovsky–Schwarz inequality.

Applications

The inequality proves the triangle inequality for inner product spaces, bounds correlations and probabilities, underlies the Heisenberg uncertainty principle in its general Robertson form, and appears throughout analysis, geometry, statistics and quantum mechanics. It is a special case of Hölder's inequality. Compare Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem.
