Xu Beihong

Xu Beihong (Chinese: 徐悲鴻; 19 July 1895 – 26 September 1953) was a Chinese painter regarded as one of the founders of modern Chinese painting. He is best known for his ink paintings of galloping horses, which became an emblem of the Chinese national spirit, and for his large-scale oil paintings of historical and national subjects. Xu argued that Chinese art should be reformed by absorbing Western techniques of drawing, anatomy, and perspective, and as an influential teacher and administrator he reshaped art education in Republican and early People's Republic China.

Life

Xu was born in Yixing, Jiangsu, the son of a self-taught portrait painter; he began studying classical literature and painting as a child. In 1917 he travelled to Japan to study art, and from 1919 to 1927 he studied in Paris at the École Nationale Supérieure des Beaux-Arts and in Berlin, mastering Western oil painting and drawing. Returning to China, he taught at National Central University in Nanjing and led the art department at Peking University. He organised major exhibitions of Chinese painting abroad, including in Paris and Moscow.

During the Second Sino-Japanese War he painted patriotic works and raised funds for the war effort through exhibitions in Southeast Asia and India. After 1949 he became president of the Central Academy of Fine Arts in Beijing and chairman of the Chinese Artists' Association, promoting socialist realism in art education. He died of a stroke in 1953; his Beijing home became the Xu Beihong Memorial Museum, housing his major works.

Art

Xu's horse paintings, executed in bold ink washes with sparing, dynamic brushwork, drew on close anatomical study of real horses rendered with traditional Chinese media. His monumental history oils — Tian Heng and His Five Hundred Followers (1928–1930), Waiting for Deliverance (1933), and The Foolish Old Man Removes the Mountains (1940) — applied academic realism to themes of sacrifice and national perseverance. He also painted lions, eagles, cats, and portraits. His advocacy of drawing from life as the foundation of all art remains central to Chinese academy training.
