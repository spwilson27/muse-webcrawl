Bern Convention

"Bern Convention" may refer to several treaties concluded at Bern (Berne), Switzerland. This article is about the 1979 Council of Europe wildlife convention. For the copyright treaty, see Berne Convention for the Protection of Literary and Artistic Works.

The Bern Convention on the Conservation of European Wildlife and Natural Habitats, also known as the Berne Convention, is a binding international legal instrument of the Council of Europe in the field of nature conservation, covering most of the natural heritage of Europe and extending to some African states. It was adopted in Bern, Switzerland, on 19 September 1979 and came into force on 1 June 1982.

Aims and provisions

The convention aims to conserve wild flora and fauna and their natural habitats, especially species requiring the cooperation of several states, and to promote European cooperation in conservation. Its appendices list strictly protected flora (Appendix I), strictly protected fauna (Appendix II), protected fauna that may be regulated (Appendix III), and prohibited means and methods of killing and capture (Appendix IV). Parties undertake to protect habitats, with particular emphasis on endangered and migratory species.

Implementation

The convention's Standing Committee monitors implementation, opens case files on alleged breaches — often following complaints from NGOs — and issues recommendations. The Emerald Network of Areas of Special Conservation Interest, launched under the convention, is the counterpart for non-EU parties of the EU's Natura 2000 network; the EU implements the convention largely through its Birds and Habitats Directives.
