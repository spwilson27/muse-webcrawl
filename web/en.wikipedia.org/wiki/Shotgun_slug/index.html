Shotgun slug

A shotgun slug is a heavy, single projectile fired from a shotgun, in contrast to the multiple small pellets of shot normally fired by such weapons. Slugs are used for hunting large game, for self-defence and law enforcement, and for breaching doors. They combine the handling of a shotgun with single-projectile terminal performance broadly comparable to a large-calibre rifle bullet at short to moderate ranges.

Types

  • Foster slug — the classic American design: a hollow-based lead slug with rifling-like external grooves, intended to be fired from smoothbore barrels. The grooves reduce friction and the hollow base shifts the centre of gravity forward, giving the slug stability in flight in the manner of a shuttlecock.
  • Brenneke slug — a German design with an attached wad that remains fixed to the projectile, providing stability and improved accuracy from smoothbores.
  • Sabot slug — a smaller-calibre projectile held in a plastic sabot, designed for rifled shotgun barrels; it offers the best accuracy and flattest trajectory, approaching rifle performance.
  • Breaching rounds — frangible slugs of compressed metal powder used by police and military units to destroy door locks and hinges while minimising the risk of ricochet.

Performance and use

A typical 12-gauge slug weighs about one ounce (28 g) and leaves the muzzle at roughly 500 m/s, delivering several thousand joules of energy. Effective range is generally within 100 metres, extended somewhat by sabot slugs from rifled barrels. Because hunting regulations in some regions prohibit rifles for deer hunting — often in flat, populated country — slugs provide a legal alternative with a shorter maximum range than centre-fire rifle bullets.
