Kähler manifold

In mathematics and especially differential geometry, a Kähler manifold is a manifold with three mutually compatible structures: a complex structure, a Riemannian structure, and a symplectic structure. On a Kähler manifold, the Riemannian metric, the complex structure, and the symplectic form determine one another, and every complex projective space, complex torus, and smooth complex projective variety carries natural Kähler metrics.

Definition

A Kähler manifold is a Hermitian manifold whose associated (1,1)-form, called the Kähler form, is closed. Equivalently, the complex structure is parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection, so holonomy lies in the unitary group.

Examples and importance

Important examples include complex projective space with the Fubini–Study metric, flat complex tori, and the Calabi–Yau manifolds that appear in string theory. Kähler geometry links algebraic geometry, symplectic geometry, and Riemannian geometry: Hodge theory on compact Kähler manifolds constrains their topology strongly.
