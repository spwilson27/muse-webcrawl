Legal identity

Legal identity is the recognition, in law, of a person's existence and identity, typically evidenced by official registration and documentation such as a birth certificate and a national identity document. The United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 16.9 commits states to provide legal identity for all, including birth registration, by 2030.

Importance

Without proof of legal identity, people may be unable to open a bank account, enrol in school, obtain healthcare, vote, travel across borders, or access social protection. The World Bank has estimated that hundreds of millions of people worldwide lack any officially recognised identification, with children whose births are never registered forming a large share.

Systems

Legal identity is usually built on civil registration and vital statistics (CRVS) systems, which record births, marriages and deaths, and on national ID programmes that issue credentials to residents. Modern programmes increasingly use biometrics and digital identity wallets, raising questions about privacy, exclusion errors and data security.

Statelessness

Legal identity is distinct from nationality, but lack of documentation is both a cause and a symptom of statelessness: people who cannot prove where they were born or to whom may be unable to claim citizenship anywhere.
