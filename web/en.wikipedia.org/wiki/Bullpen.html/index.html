Bullpen

In baseball, the bullpen (sometimes spelled bull pen) is the area where relief pitchers warm up before entering a game. A team's roster of relief pitchers is also metonymically referred to as "the bullpen". These relievers usually wait in the bullpen if they have yet to play in a game, rather than in the dugout with the rest of the team.

Location

In Major League Baseball, most bullpens are located in foul territory beyond the outfield fences, though some parks have them along the foul lines. A typical bullpen contains one or two pitcher's mounds and home plates at regulation distance, allowing a pitcher and a bullpen catcher to warm up. A bullpen coach supervises the warm-up and communicates with the dugout by telephone.

Etymology

The origin of the term is debated. One theory holds that standing-room areas for fans in the late 19th century resembled cattle pens; another links it to Bull Durham tobacco advertisements once commonly posted on outfield fences near the warm-up areas. The term was in use for the warm-up area by the early twentieth century.

Usage

Managers decide when to have a reliever begin warming up based on the game situation. "Getting the bullpen up" or "loosening up" describes pitchers beginning to throw. Modern bullpens are staffed by specialists, including closers, setup men, middle relievers and long relievers.
