Lead vocalist

The lead vocalist, lead singer, or frontman/frontwoman is the member of a band who sings the main vocal parts of a song, as opposed to backing vocalists who sing harmonies and supporting lines. In many rock and pop groups the lead vocalist is also the public face of the band and its principal focal point during live performance.

Role

The lead vocalist typically carries the melody and the main lyrical content of a group's songs. In performance, the lead singer often addresses the audience between songs, introduces the band, and shapes the visual identity of the act. Many lead vocalists also play an instrument — guitar and piano being common — while others concentrate solely on singing and stagecraft.

Frontperson

When a lead vocalist is regarded as the band's leader or public representative, they are often described as the group's "frontman" or "frontwoman". Charismatic frontpersons such as Freddie Mercury of Queen, Mick Jagger of the Rolling Stones, and Robert Plant of Led Zeppelin became cultural icons whose stage personas were inseparable from their bands' identities. In some groups, vocal duties are shared between two or more members, or rotate from song to song, as with The Beatles.

In other genres

In classical music the nearest equivalent is the soloist; in hip hop the lead performer is the MC. Idol groups in K-pop and J-pop formally designate positions including "main vocalist" and "lead vocalist", the latter typically the second-strongest singer who often leads verses and harmonies.
