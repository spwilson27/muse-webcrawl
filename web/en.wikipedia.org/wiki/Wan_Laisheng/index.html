Wan Laisheng (Chinese: 万籁声; 1903–1992) was a Chinese martial artist, author, and educator, regarded as a leading exponent of the Natural School (Ziran Men) and a prominent figure in the modernisation of wushu during the Republic of China period.

Wan Laisheng demonstrating a Ziran Men posture

Life and training

Wan was born in Wuchang and took a degree in agriculture in Beijing, where he studied martial arts alongside his formal education. He became a disciple of the itinerant master Du Xinwu, famed as the "Great Hero of the North and South", from whom he inherited Ziran Men, a style characterised by relaxed, natural movement and evasion. Wan also trained in Northern Shaolin and other northern systems.

Career

In 1928 Wan competed in the first national martial arts examination and emerged among its top performers. That year he was appointed head coach of the Guangdong–Guangxi Guoshu institutes, and he helped establish martial arts academies in southern China. At the second national examination in 1933 his students performed strongly, enhancing his reputation.

Wan wrote extensively, publishing instructional works such as Common Knowledge of Martial Arts (Wushu Huizong) that systematised Chinese boxing for a modern readership. After 1949 he settled in Fujian, teaching at Fujian Agricultural College and later holding a professorship, while continuing to teach martial arts and traditional medicine until his death in 1992.
