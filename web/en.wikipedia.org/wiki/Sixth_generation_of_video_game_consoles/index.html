The sixth generation of video game consoles began on November 27, 1998, with the Japanese release of Sega's Dreamcast, followed by the Sony PlayStation 2 in 2000, the Nintendo GameCube in 2001, and Microsoft's Xbox in 2001. The era is notable for the transition to online gaming, DVD-based media, and more powerful 128-bit-era graphics processing.

Dreamcast (1998)

Sega's Dreamcast was the first console of the generation, featuring a built-in modem for online play, the Windows CE-compatible operating system option, and the VMU memory card with its own screen. Despite a strong launch and acclaimed titles such as Sonic Adventure, Soulcalibur, and Shenmue, competition from the forthcoming PlayStation 2 and Sega's accumulated losses forced its discontinuation in 2001, ending Sega's history as a console manufacturer after roughly 9.13 million units sold.

PlayStation 2 (2000)

Sony's PlayStation 2 launched in Japan on March 4, 2000, powered by the Emotion Engine CPU and offering backward compatibility with the original PlayStation and DVD movie playback, which helped drive adoption. It became the best-selling video game console of all time, with over 160 million units sold worldwide and a library of more than 4,000 games, including Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas, Gran Turismo 3, and Final Fantasy X. Production continued until January 2013, an unusually long lifespan.

GameCube (2001)

Nintendo's GameCube, released in September 2001, was a compact cube-shaped console using proprietary miniDVD-based discs of 1.5 GB capacity. It emphasised local multiplayer and connectivity with the Game Boy Advance. Acclaimed titles included Super Smash Bros. Melee, The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker, and Metroid Prime. It sold about 21.74 million units, finishing third in the generation.

Xbox (2001)

Microsoft's Xbox, launched in North America on November 15, 2001, was the most powerful console of the generation, with a built-in hard drive and Ethernet port as standard. Its launch title Halo: Combat Evolved became a system seller, and the Xbox Live service, launched in 2002, set the standard for console online gaming. The Xbox sold over 24 million units and established Microsoft as a major platform holder.

Handhelds and legacy

The generation's dominant handheld was Nintendo's Game Boy Advance (2001). The sixth generation marked the end of Sega's hardware business, the arrival of Microsoft, and the mainstreaming of online play and optical media; it also saw the rise of emulation and homebrew scenes around the Dreamcast and Xbox in particular.
