Content management system

A content management system (CMS) is computer software used to manage the creation and modification of digital content, typically in a collaborative environment. A CMS typically supports multiple users, allowing them to create, edit, organise and publish content — usually stored in a database — through an administration interface, without needing to write code or edit raw files. The term is most often used for web content management systems that run websites.

Structure

A conventional CMS consists of two major components: the content management application (CMA), the authoring back-end in which content is entered, edited and approved through forms and WYSIWYG editors; and the content delivery application (CDA), which assembles the stored content with templates to generate the pages visitors see. Common features include user roles and permissions, workflow and versioning, media libraries, search-engine-friendly URLs, templating and themes, and extension through plugins or modules.

Popular systems

WordPress, launched in 2003, is by far the most widely used CMS, powering a large share of all websites, and is used for blogs, news sites, shops (via WooCommerce) and general-purpose sites. Other widely used open-source systems include Joomla and Drupal, the latter favoured for complex, structured content and government sites; commercial and hosted platforms include Adobe Experience Manager, Sitecore, and hosted builders such as Squarespace and Wix. "Headless" CMS products (such as Contentful or Strapi) separate the back-end entirely, delivering content over APIs to websites, mobile apps and other channels.

History and security

Web content management emerged in the mid-1990s as websites outgrew hand-written static HTML; early commercial systems included Vignette StoryServer and Interwoven TeamSite. The open-source wave of the early 2000s made CMS software free and widely deployable. Because CMS installations are so numerous, their plugins and themes are frequent targets of attacks, and keeping them updated is a central operational concern.
