Perfectionism (psychology)

Perfectionism, in psychology, is a personality trait or disposition characterised by striving for flawlessness and setting excessively high performance standards, accompanied by overly critical self-evaluation and concern about the evaluations of others. It is studied as a multidimensional construct and is associated both with achievement striving and with a range of psychological difficulties.

Dimensions

Two influential models dominate research. The Frost Multidimensional Perfectionism Scale distinguishes personal standards, concern over mistakes, doubts about actions, parental expectations and criticism, and organisation. The Hewitt–Flett model distinguishes self-oriented perfectionism (demanding perfection of oneself), other-oriented perfectionism (demanding it of others) and socially prescribed perfectionism (believing others demand it of oneself). Researchers commonly group these into "perfectionistic strivings" and "perfectionistic concerns", the latter being the more consistently maladaptive cluster.

Clinical associations

Perfectionism is a transdiagnostic risk and maintenance factor implicated in depression, anxiety disorders, obsessive–compulsive disorder, eating disorders, chronic fatigue and suicidality, and it can interfere with treatment by discouraging disclosure of difficulties. At the same time, high personal standards alone, without harsh self-criticism, correlate with conscientiousness and achievement, leading to debate about "healthy" versus "unhealthy" perfectionism. Cognitive behavioural therapy adapted for perfectionism (CBT-P) targets self-worth's dependence on achievement and has shown efficacy in trials.

Trends

A widely cited 2017 meta-analysis by Thomas Curran and Andrew Hill found that levels of perfectionism among American, Canadian and British college students rose substantially between 1989 and 2016, with socially prescribed perfectionism increasing the most; the authors linked the trend to competitive social and economic environments and social media.
