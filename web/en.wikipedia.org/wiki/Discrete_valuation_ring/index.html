In abstract algebra, a discrete valuation ring (DVR) is a principal ideal domain (PID) with exactly one non-zero maximal ideal. This means a DVR is an integral domain which satisfies any one of the following equivalent conditions:

  1. It is a local principal ideal domain and not a field.
  2. It is a valuation ring with value group isomorphic to the integers under addition.
  3. It is a Dedekind domain with exactly one nonzero prime ideal.
  4. It is a Noetherian local domain of Krull dimension one whose maximal ideal is principal.
  5. It is an integrally closed Noetherian local ring of dimension one.

Discrete valuation

Given a DVR R, its fraction field carries a discrete valuation: a surjective homomorphism v : K* → Z such that R consists of zero together with the elements of nonnegative valuation. An element π with v(π) = 1 is called a uniformizer; it generates the maximal ideal, and every nonzero element of the fraction field can be written uniquely as uπn for a unit u and integer n.

Examples

  • The localization of the integers at a prime p, Z(p), consisting of rationals whose denominator is not divisible by p.
  • The ring of p-adic integers Zp, the completion of the above.
  • The ring of formal power series k[[t]] over a field k, with valuation given by the order of vanishing at t = 0.
  • Local rings of nonsingular points on algebraic curves.

Properties

DVRs are the local building blocks of Dedekind domains and of one-dimensional schemes: localizing a Dedekind domain at a nonzero prime yields a DVR. The completion of a DVR at its maximal ideal is again a DVR, and complete DVRs are classified up to isomorphism largely by their residue field and whether they have characteristic zero or p.
