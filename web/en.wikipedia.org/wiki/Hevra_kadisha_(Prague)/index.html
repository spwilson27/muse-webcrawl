Hevra kadisha (Prague)

The Hevra kadisha of Prague (Hebrew: חברה קדישא, "holy society") was the Jewish burial society of Prague, founded in 1564 under the patronage of Mordechai Maisel, one of the wealthiest members of the Prague Jewish community. Burial societies were voluntary associations responsible for preparing the dead for burial according to Jewish law, maintaining cemeteries, and supporting the bereaved. The Prague society became one of the most famous and long-lived in Europe.

History

Prague's Hevra kadisha was established during the 16th-century flowering of the Jewish ghetto under Rudolf II. Mordechai Maisel, court Jew and communal leader, endowed the society and helped it build its ceremonial hall near the Old Jewish Cemetery. The society enrolled prominent rabbis and communal figures; members fasted and studied on the society's annual fast day, the 15th of Kislev, and held a festive banquet afterward. The artistically decorated registers (pinkasim) of the Prague society, some dating to the 17th and 18th centuries, record its rules, membership and charitable activities and are now held by the Jewish Museum in Prague.

Functions

The society washed and purified the corpse (tahara), provided shrouds of plain white linen, watched over the body until burial, dug graves, and conducted funerals. It also visited the sick, comforted mourners and gave charity to the poor. Membership was considered a great honour and was often restricted to learned and prosperous householders.

Legacy

The Prague Hevra kadisha served as a model for burial societies throughout Bohemia and Moravia. Its ceremonial hall, built in 1906 in the neo-Romanesque style beside the Old Jewish Cemetery, now forms part of the Jewish Museum in Prague.
