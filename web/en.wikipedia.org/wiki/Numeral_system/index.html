Numeral system

A numeral system is a writing system for expressing numbers: a mathematical notation using digits or other symbols in a consistent manner to represent numbers of a given set. The same sequence of symbols may denote different numbers in different systems.

Positional and non-positional systems

In positional (place-value) systems, the value of a digit depends on its position, scaled by powers of a base. The Hindu–Arabic numeral system, with base ten and a symbol for zero, is now universal. Other bases include binary (base 2) and hexadecimal (base 16) in computing, and the Babylonian sexagesimal system (base 60) surviving in time and angle measurement. Non-positional systems, such as Roman numerals and tally marks, add or (in subtractive notation) subtract symbol values.

History

Counting systems developed independently in Egypt, Mesopotamia, China, Mesoamerica (where the Maya had positional notation with zero) and India, whose decimal positional system spread via Arabic scholarship to Europe by the late Middle Ages, displacing Roman numerals for computation.
