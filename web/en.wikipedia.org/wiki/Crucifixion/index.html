Crucifixion

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Crucifixion is a method of capital punishment in which the victim is tied or nailed to a large wooden cross or beam and left to hang until eventual death from exhaustion, asphyxiation, or shock. It was used as a form of punishment by the Persians, Carthaginians, and Romans, among others, and is most famous as the method by which Jesus was executed in Jerusalem in the 1st century AD.

History

The practice appears to have originated in Persia and spread through the Hellenistic world; Alexander the Great is reported to have crucified prisoners after the siege of Tyre. Rome adopted crucifixion as a punishment particularly for slaves, pirates, and rebels — Cicero called it the cruellest and most disgusting penalty — and Roman citizens were normally exempt. After the revolt of Spartacus in 71 BC, some 6,000 captured slaves were crucified along the Appian Way. During the siege of Jerusalem in AD 70, Roman forces crucified large numbers of Jewish prisoners.

Method

The condemned typically carried the horizontal crossbar (the patibulum) to the place of execution, where it was fixed to an upright stake. The victim's arms were tied or nailed to the bar and the feet to the upright. Death could take hours or days; Roman practice sometimes hastened it by breaking the victim's legs. Modern medical analyses attribute death chiefly to a combination of blood loss, dehydration, shock, and eventual asphyxiation.

The crucifixion of Jesus

The execution of Jesus under Pontius Pilate, described in the four canonical Gospels, is central to Christian theology, and the cross became the principal symbol of Christianity. The Emperor Constantine abolished crucifixion in the Roman Empire in the 4th century out of veneration for it.

Later use

Crucifixion or similar suspension punishments persisted in some places into the modern era, including in Japan during the Tokugawa period and, as an extrajudicial atrocity, in several 20th-century conflicts. In rare contemporary cases, judicial crucifixion remains on the statute books in a few countries, though it is seldom if ever carried out.
