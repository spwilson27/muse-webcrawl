Armenia–Azerbaijan relations are the bilateral relations between the Republic of Armenia and the Republic of Azerbaijan in the South Caucasus. The two states have no formal diplomatic relations, and their shared border has been closed since the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, as relations have been dominated by the conflict over Nagorno-Karabakh.

Background

After brief independence in 1918–1920 — during which the two republics fought over disputed territories — both were incorporated into the Soviet Union. Nagorno-Karabakh, an Armenian-majority region, was an autonomous oblast within Soviet Azerbaijan. As the USSR dissolved, competing claims escalated into full-scale war (1992–1994), ending with Armenian forces controlling Nagorno-Karabakh and surrounding Azerbaijani districts, and mass displacement on both sides.

Wars and aftermath

Decades of OSCE Minsk Group mediation failed to produce a settlement. Azerbaijan retook significant territory in the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War of 2020, ended by a Russian-brokered ceasefire. In September 2023 an Azerbaijani offensive dissolved the self-proclaimed Republic of Artsakh, prompting the flight of nearly the entire Armenian population of the region to Armenia. Border clashes and negotiations over delimitation, transport links, and a peace treaty continued afterward, with the two sides announcing agreement on a draft peace text in 2025.
