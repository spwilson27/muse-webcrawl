Australia at the Olympics

Australia at the Olympics
IOC code: AUS
NOC: Australian Olympic Committee
First Games: 1896 (Athens)

Australia has sent athletes to all but one edition of the modern Olympic Games. Australia has competed in every Summer Olympic Games, as well as every Winter Olympics except 1924–32 and 1948. In 1908 and 1912 Australia competed with New Zealand under the name Australasia. The Australian Olympic Committee, founded in 1895, organises the country's participation.

History

Australia's first Olympian was Edwin Flack, who won the 800 m and 1500 m running events at the inaugural Athens Games in 1896. Swimming has been Australia's most successful sport, producing stars such as Dawn Fraser, Murray Rose, Ian Thorpe, Grant Hackett, Leisel Jones, Emma McKeon and Ariarne Titmus. Australia has also excelled in track cycling, rowing, hockey, athletics and equestrian events. The country's best-known athletics champions include Betty Cuthbert, Debbie Flintoff-King, Cathy Freeman — whose 400 m victory at Sydney 2000 was an iconic moment — and Sally Pearson.

Australia has hosted the Summer Olympics twice: Melbourne in 1956 and Sydney in 2000, and will host a third time in Brisbane in 2032. The Sydney Games are widely regarded as among the most successful ever held.

Performance

Australia has traditionally finished high on the medal table relative to its population, peaking at the Athens 2004 Games with 17 gold medals and 50 medals overall, and reaching a record gold tally again at Paris 2024. Government funding through the Australian Institute of Sport, established in 1981 after a disappointing Montreal 1976 performance, underpins the elite programme. Australia is one of only a few nations to have won medals at every Summer Games it has attended, and one of two (with Great Britain) to have won gold at every Summer Olympics.
