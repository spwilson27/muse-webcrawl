New Zealand Rugby (NZR), formerly the New Zealand Rugby Union, is the governing body for the sport of rugby union in New Zealand. Founded in 1892, it administers the game at all levels — from community and provincial rugby to the professional competitions and national teams — and is headquartered in Wellington. Rugby is New Zealand's national sport, and NZR is best known globally as the home of the All Blacks, the most successful national team in the sport's history.

National teams

The All Blacks, named for their all-black kit, have won the Rugby World Cup three times (1987, 2011, and 2015) and hold a winning record against every other national team; their pre-match haka has become one of world sport's most famous rituals. The women's national team, the Black Ferns, are six-time Women's Rugby World Cup champions. NZR also fields the All Blacks Sevens and women's sevens teams, the Māori All Blacks, and age-grade sides such as the "Baby Blacks" under-20 team.

Structure and competitions

The domestic game is organised through 26 provincial unions. NZR runs the National Provincial Championship (NPC, now branded the Bunnings NPC) and the amateur Heartland Championship, the women's Farah Palmer Cup, and — with its SANZAAR partners — the Super Rugby Pacific competition, in which New Zealand's five franchises (Blues, Chiefs, Crusaders, Highlanders, Hurricanes) compete. The Ranfurly Shield, contested since 1904, is among the most prized trophies in domestic rugby.

History

Rugby was introduced to New Zealand in 1870 by Charles Monro, and the first provincial unions formed in the 1870s–80s before the national union's founding in 1892. The 1905 "Originals" tour of Britain established New Zealand's rugby reputation. In the professional era (from 1995), NZR became noted for its centralised contracting of leading players, and in 2022 it sold a minority commercial stake to the US private equity firm Silver Lake.
