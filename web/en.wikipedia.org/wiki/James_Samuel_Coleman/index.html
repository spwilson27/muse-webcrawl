James Samuel Coleman

James Samuel Coleman (May 12, 1926 – March 25, 1995) was an American sociologist, theorist and empirical researcher at the University of Chicago, best known for the Coleman Report (1966) on equality of educational opportunity, his theory of social capital, and Foundations of Social Theory (1990) on rational choice.

James S. Coleman
Born: May 12, 1926, Bedford, Indiana
Died: March 25, 1995, Chicago, Illinois
Fields: Sociology of education, social theory
Institutions: Johns Hopkins University, University of Chicago

Career

Coleman studied chemical engineering at Purdue and sociology at Columbia under Robert Merton and Paul Lazarsfeld. At Johns Hopkins he produced The Adolescent Society (1961), showing peer status systems in high schools valued athletics over scholarship.

Coleman Report

Commissioned under the Civil Rights Act of 1964, Equality of Educational Opportunity (1966), based on some 650,000 pupils, found that school resources explained little achievement variance compared with family background and peer composition — a finding that reshaped debates on desegregation, busing and later Coleman's own studies favouring Catholic schools' effects in High School Achievement (1982).

Social capital and theory

Coleman's 1988 article "Social Capital in the Creation of Human Capital" popularised social capital — obligations, information channels and norms in social structures. Foundations of Social Theory built a general theory from purposive actors, bridging micro and macro levels, and influenced rational-choice sociology.
