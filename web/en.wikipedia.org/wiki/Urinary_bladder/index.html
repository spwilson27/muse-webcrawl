Urinary bladder

The urinary bladder is a hollow muscular organ in humans and other vertebrates that stores urine from the kidneys before disposal by urination. In humans, the bladder is a distensible organ situated on the pelvic floor. Urine enters the bladder via the two ureters and exits through the urethra.

Structure

The bladder is a hollow muscular organ with a capacity in adults of roughly 400–600 ml. Its wall contains the detrusor muscle, composed of smooth muscle fibers arranged in longitudinal and circular layers, lined internally by transitional epithelium (urothelium) that allows the organ to stretch as it fills. Anatomically it is described as having an apex, a body, a fundus, and a neck. The trigone is a smooth triangular region between the two ureteric openings and the internal urethral orifice.

Function

The bladder stores urine until the act of micturition. As it fills, stretch receptors in its wall signal the central nervous system; during voiding, parasympathetic stimulation contracts the detrusor while the internal and external urethral sphincters relax, allowing urine to pass out through the urethra.

Clinical significance

Disorders of the bladder include urinary tract infections and cystitis, bladder stones, urinary retention, urinary incontinence, overactive bladder, and bladder cancer. The bladder may be emptied artificially by catheterization.
