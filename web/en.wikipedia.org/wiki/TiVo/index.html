TiVo

TiVo
Type: Digital video recorder / brand
Developer: TiVo Corporation (now part of Xperi)
First released: 1999
Founded: 1997 (as Teleworld)
Founders: Mike Ramsay, Jim Barton

TiVo is a digital video recorder (DVR) developed and marketed by TiVo Corporation, and the brand name of its service and user interface. Introduced in 1999, the TiVo set-top box recorded television programmes onto a hard drive, allowing viewers to pause and rewind live TV, record whole seasons automatically with "Season Pass", and skip commercials — capabilities that fundamentally changed television viewing habits and made "to TiVo" a common verb for recording a programme.

A TiVo DVR with its "peanut" remote, whose thumbs-up and thumbs-down buttons profiled viewer tastes.

Features and technology

TiVo boxes downloaded programme-guide data and recorded analogue or digital television to an internal hard disk. Signature features included WishList searches by actor or keyword, TiVo Suggestions — automatic recordings based on ratings given with the remote's thumbs-up and thumbs-down buttons — and, in later models, multi-room streaming, broadband video apps, and voice search. The service required a subscription or lifetime plan in addition to the hardware.

Company history

TiVo Inc. was founded in 1997 by Mike Ramsay and Jim Barton as Teleworld, and shipped its first DVR in 1999, competing with ReplayTV. Although the TiVo brand became iconic, the company increasingly profited from licensing its patents rather than from hardware sales, winning major litigation against EchoStar (Dish Network). TiVo merged with Rovi Corporation in 2016, the combined company taking the TiVo name; in 2020 it merged with Xperi Corporation, and the TiVo brand continues in licensed set-top boxes, smart-TV software (TiVo OS), and streaming devices.

Cultural impact

TiVo pioneered time-shifting for the mass market and popularised the notion of television on the viewer's schedule rather than the broadcaster's — a precursor to the on-demand streaming era. Its patented advertising and audience-measurement data businesses also made it an early example of data collection from television viewers.
