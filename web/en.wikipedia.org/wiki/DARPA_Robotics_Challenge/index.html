DARPA Robotics Challenge

The DARPA Robotics Challenge (DRC) was a competition funded by the US Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency, held from 2012 to 2015, to develop semi-autonomous ground robots capable of performing complex tasks in dangerous, degraded, human-engineered environments. The competition was prompted by the 2011 Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster, in which robots able to operate in a damaged human facility might have mitigated the accident. The total prize fund was over US$4 million.

Format

The challenge proceeded in stages: a Virtual Robotics Challenge (June 2013) conducted in simulation, the DRC Trials (December 2013, Florida), and the DRC Finals (June 2015, Pomona, California). In the finals, robots — tethered neither for power nor, except through a degraded communications link, for control — had about one hour to complete a circuit of tasks simulating disaster response: driving a utility vehicle, exiting the vehicle, opening a door, turning a valve, using a power tool to cut through a wall, connecting a hose or manipulating a plug, climbing stairs, and crossing rubble.

Results

The winning team of the 2015 finals was Team KAIST of South Korea, whose humanoid robot DRC-HUBO completed all eight tasks fastest, winning the $2 million grand prize. Second and third places went to Team IHMC Robotics (running a Boston Dynamics Atlas robot) and Tartan Rescue of Carnegie Mellon (with the CHIMP robot). Many robots famously fell over during the competition, and the falls — plus the slow, cautious pace of the machines at the time — became a widely shared illustration of both the progress and the limits of humanoid robotics.

Legacy

The DRC drove rapid advances in humanoid robot locomotion, manipulation and supervised autonomy software, and many of the participating teams and technologies went on to shape commercial humanoid robotics in the following decade.
