Prolepsis

Prolepsis (from the Greek prolambanein, "to anticipate") is a rhetorical and literary device in which an anticipated event, objection, or idea is represented as though it had already occurred or been settled. The term covers several related figures of speech and narrative techniques united by the idea of jumping forward in time or argument.

A narrative timeline with a proleptic flashforward

As a figure of speech

In rhetoric, prolepsis most commonly denotes the anticipation and refutation of an opponent's arguments before they have been raised. By answering an objection before it is made — "You may object that this is too costly, but consider..." — a speaker neutralises the counterargument and presents it in its weakest form. Classical rhetoricians treated it as a powerful tool of persuasion, and it survives in modern legal advocacy and political speech.

In narrative

In narratology, prolepsis is an anachrony in which the narration leaps ahead to events that occur later in story-time and then returns to the present moment. Gérard Genette, in his study Narrative Discourse (1972), defined prolepsis against its opposite, analepsis (flashback). Prolepsis is what is popularly called a "flashforward": a scene, image, or statement that foreshadows a future event. Writers use it to create suspense, dramatic irony, or a sense of fate. Famous examples include the prologues of Greek tragedy, which often foretell the catastrophe to come, and the opening of Gabriel García Márquez's One Hundred Years of Solitude, which begins "Many years later, as he faced the firing squad..."

Other senses

  • Grammatical prolepsis: the anticipatory use of an adjective or noun, as in "they were afraid the night would be long," where a quality is applied before it strictly takes effect.
  • Botany: the premature development of a shoot from a bud that would normally remain dormant until the following season; contrasted with syllepsis.
  • Historical writing: the anachronistic representation of an earlier period in terms of later ideas; also called proleptic description.
