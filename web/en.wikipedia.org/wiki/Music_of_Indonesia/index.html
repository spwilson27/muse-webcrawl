Music of Indonesia

The music of Indonesia is as diverse as the archipelago's hundreds of ethnic groups and languages. Its best-known tradition is the gamelan orchestras of Java and Bali, but the country also sustains traditions ranging from the kroncong of the colonial era to dangdut, the most popular mass-market genre of recent decades.

Gamelan

Gamelan ensembles consist mainly of tuned percussion instruments — metallophones, gongs, kettle gongs, drums and xylophones — playing interlocking cyclical melodies in sléndro and pélog tuning systems. Javanese gamelan is generally serene and courtly, while Balinese gamelan is brighter, faster and more dynamic, accompanying temple ceremonies and dances such as the kecak. Gamelan influenced Western composers including Claude Debussy, who heard it at the Paris Exposition of 1889, and later Steve Reich and other minimalists.

Regional traditions

Other notable traditions include the talempong and saluang of the Minangkabau in West Sumatra, the kolintang of North Sulawesi, the sasando of Rote Island, angklung bamboo ensembles of West Java (inscribed by UNESCO), and the vocal and string traditions of Sunda, Batak lands, and eastern Indonesia.

Popular music

Kroncong, played with Portuguese-derived string instruments, flourished in the early twentieth century. In the 1970s dangdut — blending Malay, Indian film, Arabic and rock elements — became hugely popular through stars such as Rhoma Irama and Elvy Sukaesih. Indonesian pop, rock and indie scenes thrive in Jakarta, Bandung and Yogyakarta, while campursari mixes gamelan with contemporary styles.
