Controlled demolition is the strategic placing of explosive material and timing of its detonation so that a structure collapses on itself in a matter of seconds, minimising damage to adjacent structures — generally called implosion.

A building imploding into its own footprint.

Terminology

"Implosion" is technically a misnomer: structures are not drawn inward by vacuum, but gravity pulls them down after key supports are destroyed in sequence.

Technique

Engineers first weaken a building ("pre-weakening"), remove non-load-bearing walls, and place charges — often RDX or dynamite in drilled columns, steel cut with linear shaped charges. Detonations are staggered over milliseconds so the structure folds inward and downward. Months of preparation precede a collapse lasting seconds.

Records

Large implosions include Seattle's Kingdome (2000), among the largest structures ever demolished by implosion by volume, and numerous Las Vegas casino towers.

Alternatives

Where explosives are impractical, structures are dismantled by high-reach excavators ("nibbling"), top-down deconstruction, or the wrecking ball.
