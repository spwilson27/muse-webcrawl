Boeing 777

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Boeing 777-300ER taking off with heat haze from its big engines]
A Triple Seven departing.

The Boeing 777 (\'Triple Seven\') is an American long-range wide-body airliner developed and manufactured by Boeing, seating 301–426 passengers with a range of 5,240–9,380 nautical miles depending on variant. Launched with United Airlines in 1990 and entering service in 1995, it was the first airliner designed entirely by computer (CATIA), the first Boeing with fly-by-wire, and the largest twinjet until its successor, the 777X. Over 1,700 have been delivered; the -300ER is the best-selling variant and the 777F a leading freighter.

Design for ETOPS

Eight airlines (\'Working Together\') shaped the design: a big circular-section fuselage, the largest turbofans then made (GE90, Trent 800, PW4000) and certification for twin-engine extended-range operations from entry into service — a first that rewrote long-haul economics away from trijets and quads.

Variants

The 777-200/ER and stretched -300/ER, the ultra-long-range -200LR (holder of the longest nonstop airliner flight record, 2005) and the 777F freighter followed. The 777X (777-9) with folding wingtips and GE9X engines, first flown in 2020, has suffered years of certification delay, with entry into service expected in the later 2020s.

Safety and notable events

The 777\'s record is excellent, marred by three hull losses: British Airways 38 (2008, ice in fuel lines), Asiana 214 (2013, San Francisco) and Malaysia Airlines MH17 (2014, shot down over Ukraine); MH370 (2014) remains missing. Its economics made it the default long-haul twin for two decades.
