Radio format

A radio format or programming format (not to be confused with broadcast programming) describes the overall content broadcast on a radio station. The radio format emerged mainly in the United States in the 1950s, at a time when radio was compelled to develop new and exclusive ways to deliver programs as a challenge to traditional television. Formats are tailored to a target demographic and are used to position a station in the advertising market.

Common formats

  • Contemporary hit radio (CHR / Top 40) — current chart hits aimed at younger listeners.
  • Adult contemporary (AC) — soft rock and pop ballads aimed at adults.
  • Country — modern and classic country music, one of the most popular U.S. formats.
  • News/talk — news bulletins, talk shows, and sports commentary.
  • Classic rock and oldies — rock and pop catalogues from past decades.
  • Urban contemporary, alternative rock, jazz, classical, and religious and ethnic formats.

Format clocks and dayparts

Stations structure each hour with a "format clock" or "hot clock" that schedules music, news, advertising, traffic, and DJ talk at fixed positions, and vary programming by daypart — morning drive, midday, afternoon drive, evenings, and overnights — according to audience habits.

Syndication and automation

Many formats are supplied centrally by networks and syndicators, and since the 1990s automation and voice-tracking have allowed a single presenter to serve many stations, a trend accelerated by consolidation after the U.S. Telecommunications Act of 1996.
