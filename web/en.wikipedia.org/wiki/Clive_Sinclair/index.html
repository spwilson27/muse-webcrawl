Clive Sinclair

Sir Clive Marles Sinclair (30 July 1940 – 16 September 2021) was an English entrepreneur and inventor, best known for bringing affordable home computers to the British public through Sinclair Research and for creations ranging from the first slimline pocket calculator to the ill-fated Sinclair C5 electric vehicle.

Early career

Sinclair founded Sinclair Radionics in 1961, selling miniature transistor radios and hi-fi kits by mail order. In 1972 his company launched the Sinclair Executive, the first slim pocket calculator. A later venture produced the Black Watch digital watch, whose failures damaged the business, which was eventually absorbed by the National Enterprise Board.

Home computers

Through Sinclair Research, he launched the ZX80 (1980), the first personal computer priced under £100 in the UK, followed by the ZX81 and the hugely successful ZX Spectrum (1982), which sold millions and helped seed a generation of British programmers and games developers. The ambitious Sinclair QL (1984) fared less well, and in 1986 Sinclair sold the computer business to Amstrad.

Later ventures

The battery-assisted Sinclair C5 (1985) was a commercial failure that became a cult object. Sinclair continued inventing into old age, working on folding bicycles (the A-bike) and other compact transport designs. He was knighted in 1983 and was a prominent member of British Mensa.
