Arnulf the Bad (German: Arnulf der Böse; died 14 July 937) was Duke of Bavaria from 907 until his death. A member of the Luitpolding dynasty, he succeeded his father Luitpold, who was killed at the Battle of Pressburg (907), in which the Bavarian army was annihilated by the Hungarians.

Reign

Faced with repeated Hungarian incursions, Arnulf rebuilt Bavaria's defences, fortified towns and raised new forces. To finance this he confiscated church lands and distributed them to his followers, an expropriation that earned him the epithet "the Bad" in monastic chronicles. In 909, 910 and 913 his forces defeated Hungarian raiding armies, notably at the River Rott in 909 and near Neuching in 913, after which he concluded a peace that eased pressure on Bavaria.

Conflict with the kings

Arnulf resisted the authority of King Conrad I, defeating a royal invasion, and in 919 his Bavarian supporters reportedly proclaimed him king of the East Franks in opposition to Henry the Fowler. The two men reconciled in 921: Henry recognised Arnulf's quasi-royal rights in Bavaria, including control over bishoprics and mints, while Arnulf acknowledged Henry's kingship. Arnulf later supported Henry against Hungary and fought in campaigns in Bohemia and Italy.

Death and succession

Arnulf died at Regensburg on 14 July 937 and was buried in St. Emmeram's Abbey. He was succeeded by his son Eberhard, who was soon deposed by King Otto I, and Bavaria passed to Arnulf's brother Berthold. His daughter Judith married Henry I, Duke of Bavaria, Otto's brother.
