Camarasaurus ("chamber lizard") is a genus of quadrupedal, herbivorous sauropod dinosaur that lived in North America during the Late Jurassic period. It is the most common and frequently best-preserved sauropod found in the Morrison Formation.

Description

Camarasaurus had a distinctive short, boxy skull with large nasal openings, a relatively short neck for a sauropod, and a short tail counterbalancing the body. Its spoon-shaped teeth were suited to cropping coarse vegetation. Adults reached about 15 to 23 metres in length.

Discovery

The genus was named by Edward Drinker Cope in 1877 during the "Bone Wars" with Othniel Charles Marsh. Hundreds of specimens are known, including near-complete skeletons and evidence that the animals may have lived in herds.
