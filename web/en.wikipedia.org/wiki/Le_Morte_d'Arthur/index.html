Le Morte d'Arthur

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Le Morte d'Arthur (originally spelled Le Morte Darthur; "The Death of Arthur") is a 15th-century Middle English prose compilation of Arthurian legends by Sir Thomas Malory. Written while Malory was imprisoned around 1469–1470 and printed by William Caxton in 1485, it is the best-known work of Arthurian literature in English and the source from which most later retellings derive.

Contents

Malory condensed and reworked French and English sources — principally the French Vulgate Cycle — into a continuous narrative: Arthur's birth and rise, the founding of the Round Table, the romances of Lancelot, Tristram, Gareth, and Perceval, the quest for the Holy Grail, the love of Lancelot and Guinevere, and the final civil war and Arthur's death at the hands of Mordred. Caxton divided the work into 21 books and 507 chapters.

Manuscript and text

A manuscript version, the Winchester Manuscript, was rediscovered in 1934 at Winchester College; it differs in places from Caxton's edition and is now held by the British Library. Modern editions by Eugène Vinaver and others have debated how far Malory intended a single unified work versus a series of separate romances.

Influence

Le Morte d'Arthur shaped the Arthurian tradition in English, influencing writers from Tennyson (Idylls of the King) to T. H. White (The Once and Future King) and countless adaptations in film, theatre, and popular culture.
