Vitaly Dmitrievich "Vitalik" Buterin (born 31 January 1994) is a Russian-Canadian computer programmer and writer best known as a co-founder of Ethereum, the decentralised, open-source blockchain platform with smart-contract functionality. He previously co-founded Bitcoin Magazine in 2011, one of the first publications devoted to cryptocurrency.

Early life

Buterin was born in Kolomna, Russia, and emigrated to Canada with his family at the age of six, settling in Toronto. A gifted child placed in a programme for gifted children, he excelled in mathematics, programming, and economics. He learned about Bitcoin from his father, a computer scientist, at age 17, and soon began writing for the blog Bitcoin Weekly and then co-founding Bitcoin Magazine with Mihai Alisie. He briefly attended the University of Waterloo before dropping out in 2014 upon receiving a Thiel Fellowship grant of $100,000.

Ethereum

After travelling and meeting Bitcoin developers in 2013, Buterin concluded that the Bitcoin scripting language was too limited for general applications and proposed a new platform with a Turing-complete programming language built in. He published the Ethereum white paper in late 2013; development was funded by a crowdsale in mid-2014, and the network launched on 30 July 2015 with co-founders including Gavin Wood, Joseph Lubin, and Charles Hoskinson. Buterin has remained the public face of Ethereum research, championing the transition from proof-of-work to proof-of-stake — completed in "The Merge" in September 2022 — and a rollup-centric scaling roadmap. His ether holdings made him, at age 27, one of the world's youngest self-made billionaires during the 2021 crypto boom, and he has donated large sums to causes including pandemic relief in India and support for Ukraine after Russia's 2022 invasion, as well as to effective-altruism-aligned charities.

Writing and views

Buterin writes extensively on his blog about cryptography, mechanism design, and governance. He has promoted concepts such as quadratic voting and funding, soulbound tokens, and "d/acc" (defensive accelerationism). In 2021 he received an honorary doctorate from the University of Basel's Faculty of Business and Economics; Time named him among its 100 most influential people of 2021.
