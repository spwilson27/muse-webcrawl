Ammonoidea

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ammonoids are a group of extinct marine mollusc animals in the subclass Ammonoidea of the class Cephalopoda. These molluscs, commonly referred to as ammonites, are more closely related to living coleoids (octopuses, squid, and cuttlefish) than they are to shelled nautiloids such as the living Nautilus species.

Subclass: Ammonoidea
Class: Cephalopoda
Range: Devonian – Cretaceous (about 409–66 million years ago)

Evolution and extinction

The earliest ammonoids appeared during the Devonian, and the last species vanished during or soon after the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event about 66 million years ago. Ammonoids are excellent index fossils, and it is often possible to link the rock layer in which a particular species or genus is found to specific geologic time periods.

Shell anatomy

Their fossil shells usually take the form of planispirals, although some helically spiraled and nonspiraled forms (heteromorphs) have been found. The shell is divided by septa into chambers; the lines where septa meet the outer shell — the sutures — evolved from simple folds to intricate, fern-like patterns that help distinguish ammonoid groups. The animal lived in the largest, outermost chamber, and gas in the empty chambers regulated buoyancy via a tube called the siphuncle.
