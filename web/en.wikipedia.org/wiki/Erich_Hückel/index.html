Erich Armand Arthur Joseph Hückel (9 August 1896 – 16 February 1980) was a German physicist and physical chemist. His name is attached to three foundational contributions: the Debye–Hückel theory of electrolyte solutions (with Peter Debye), the Hückel molecular orbital method for π-electron systems, and Hückel's rule for aromaticity.

Career

Hückel studied physics and mathematics at Göttingen, worked with Debye at the ETH Zürich, and held posts at Stuttgart and Marburg, where he became professor of theoretical physics in 1937 and remained until retirement. His brother Walter Hückel was also a prominent chemist.

Contributions

The Debye–Hückel theory (1923) explained the behaviour of strong electrolytes through the ionic atmosphere surrounding each ion. In the early 1930s Hückel developed a simple quantum-mechanical treatment of conjugated molecules, the Hückel molecular orbital (HMO) method, which for planar monocyclic systems yields his famous (4n + 2) rule: rings with 4n + 2 π electrons are aromatic. Though Hückel's quantum-chemical work was undervalued during his lifetime, it became standard textbook material and he received recognition late in life.
