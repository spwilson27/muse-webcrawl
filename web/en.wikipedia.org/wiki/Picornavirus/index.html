Picornavirus

Picornaviruses are a family of small, positive-sense, single-stranded RNA viruses with a 30-nm nonenveloped icosahedral capsid. The family Picornaviridae contains many important human and animal pathogens. The name derives from pico- (very small) and RNA, referring to the small RNA genome of about 7.2 to 8.5 kilobases.

Structure of a picornavirus capsid.

Genome and replication

The genome is translated as a single polyprotein, which is cleaved by virus-encoded proteases into structural capsid proteins (VP1–VP4) and nonstructural proteins including an RNA-dependent RNA polymerase. A small protein, VPg, is covalently attached to the 5′ end and primes RNA synthesis; translation is initiated at an internal ribosome entry site (IRES) rather than by cap-dependent scanning.

Important genera

  • Enterovirus — poliovirus, coxsackieviruses, echoviruses, and rhinoviruses (the main cause of the common cold; rhinoviruses are also classified in this genus).
  • Hepatovirus — hepatitis A virus.
  • Aphthovirus — foot-and-mouth disease virus of livestock.
  • Cardiovirus — including encephalomyocarditis virus and Theiler's virus.
  • Parechovirus and others, many recognised as molecular methods have expanded the family to dozens of genera infecting vertebrates.
