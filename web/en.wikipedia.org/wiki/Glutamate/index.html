Glutamate

Glutamate (sometimes GLU or E) is the anion of glutamic acid — its deprotonated, negatively charged form in aqueous solution — and one of the most important compounds in cellular metabolism and neuroscience. In vertebrates, glutamate is the most abundant excitatory neurotransmitter in the nervous system, and it also serves as the metabolic precursor for the synthesis of the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA.

Because glutamic acid loses a proton at physiological pH, "glutamate" is the form actually present in the body; in food science, its sodium salt, monosodium glutamate (MSG), is used as a flavour enhancer responsible for the savoury taste known as umami.

Biochemistry

Glutamate sits at the crossroads of nitrogen metabolism. It is formed by transamination of α-ketoglutarate, an intermediate of the citric acid cycle, and can donate its amino group to synthesise other amino acids. The enzyme glutamate dehydrogenase can release its nitrogen as ammonia for disposal via the urea cycle. Glutamate is a non-essential amino acid and a constituent of virtually all proteins.

Neurotransmission

Glutamate is released from synaptic vesicles at the great majority of excitatory synapses in the brain and spinal cord, and acts on ionotropic receptors (NMDA, AMPA and kainate receptors) and metabotropic glutamate receptors. It is central to synaptic plasticity and hence to learning and memory. Excess extracellular glutamate, however, is neurotoxic — a process called excitotoxicity — and contributes to neuronal death in stroke, traumatic brain injury and possibly neurodegenerative diseases.

Flavour

Free glutamate occurs naturally in tomatoes, cheese, mushrooms, fermented products and human breast milk. Kikunae Ikeda isolated it in 1908 from kombu seaweed and identified its taste as umami, leading to industrial production of MSG. Despite popular association with so-called "Chinese restaurant syndrome", controlled studies have not established MSG as harmful at normal dietary levels, and major food-safety bodies regard it as safe.
