Cemetery

FromGreek koimeterion, "sleeping place"
PurposeBurial and memorialisation of the dead
TypesMunicipal, churchyard, military, garden/rural, lawn

A cemetery, burial ground, gravesite, or graveyard is a place where the remains of dead people are buried or otherwise interred. The word cemetery (from the Greek koimeterion, "sleeping place") implies that the land is specifically designated as a burial ground and originally was a term applied to the Roman catacombs. The term graveyard is often used interchangeably, but a graveyard primarily refers to a burial ground within a churchyard.

History

For most of Christian-era Europe, burial took place in churchyards, which became severely overcrowded by the early 19th century and were blamed for disease. Reformers promoted new suburban cemeteries, beginning with Père Lachaise Cemetery in Paris (1804), which pioneered the landscaped "garden cemetery". Britain followed with the "Magnificent Seven" commercial cemeteries around London, and Mount Auburn Cemetery (1831) in Massachusetts began the rural cemetery movement in the United States, influencing public park design. In the later 19th and 20th centuries, municipal ownership, lawn cemeteries with flat markers, and crematoria became common.

Types and practices

Cemeteries may be religious or secular, public or private, and include sections for military burials (such as national cemeteries and Commonwealth war graves), children, or particular communities. Plots are usually sold or leased, sometimes in perpetuity; where space is scarce, remains may be exhumed after a period and placed in an ossuary. Memorials range from simple plaques to elaborate mausolea and monuments. Many jurisdictions regulate burial depth, coffin materials and cemetery record-keeping, and require perpetual care funds for maintenance.

Modern developments

Rising cremation rates, land scarcity and environmental concerns have driven new forms: natural or "green" burial grounds, woodland cemeteries, columbaria for ashes, and memorial reefs. Historic cemeteries are also valued as heritage sites, wildlife refuges and places of recreation and tourism.
