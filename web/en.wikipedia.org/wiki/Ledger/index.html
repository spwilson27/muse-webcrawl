Ledger

A ledger is a book or digital record used to store bookkeeping entries. In accounting, the general ledger is the principal book of final entry: it contains accounts for assets, liabilities, equity, revenues and expenses, summarising all transactions recorded first in journals (books of first entry). Historically, ledgers were large bound volumes in which clerks posted entries by hand; today they are almost always maintained in accounting software.

Structure

Each account in a ledger occupies its own page or record, traditionally arranged as a T-account with debits on the left and credits on the right. Posting transfers entries from the journal to the relevant ledger accounts, and at the end of an accounting period a trial balance is extracted from the ledger to verify that total debits equal total credits. Subsidiary ledgers, such as sales and purchase ledgers for individual customers and suppliers, support the control accounts in the general ledger.

Other uses

The word is used more broadly for any systematic record: in building, a ledger is a horizontal board supporting floor joists; in fishing, "ledgering" is a technique using a weighted line resting on the bottom. In computing, distributed ledger technology — of which a blockchain is one example — applies the accounting concept to records shared and synchronised across many computers without a central administrator.
