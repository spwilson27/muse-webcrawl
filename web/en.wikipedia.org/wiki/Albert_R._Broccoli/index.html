Albert R. Broccoli

Albert Romolo Broccoli (April 5, 1909 – June 27, 1996), nicknamed "Cubby", was an American film producer who made more than 40 films and is best known, with Harry Saltzman, for launching and producing the James Bond film series. Through their company Eon Productions, Broccoli produced Dr. No (1962) and nearly every subsequent Bond film, building the longest-running franchise in cinema history.

Cubby Broccoli on set at Pinewood Studios.

Career

Broccoli was born in Queens, New York, to Italian immigrant parents whose family gave its name to the broccoli vegetable, introduced to America by relatives. He entered the film business in the 1940s, worked his way up to assistant director, and in the 1950s formed Warwick Films in London with Irving Allen, producing films such as The Red Beret. In 1961 he and Harry Saltzman acquired the film rights to Ian Fleming's Bond novels (excepting Casino Royale) and founded Eon Productions; the series began with Sean Connery in Dr. No. Broccoli also produced the family film Chitty Chitty Bang Bang (1968). After Saltzman's departure in the mid-1970s, Broccoli ran the series alone, and he progressively handed control to his daughter Barbara Broccoli and stepson Michael G. Wilson, who continue to produce the films. He was appointed CBE in 1987 and received the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award in 1982. See also Eon Productions, James Bond and Barbara Broccoli.
