QI

QI (Quite Interesting) is a British comedy panel game television programme created and co-produced by John Lloyd. The show features permanent panellists and guests answering questions on general knowledge and trivia, with points awarded not only for correct answers but for answers that are interesting, while points are deducted for answers that are wrong and obvious — typically forfeits flagged by flashing lights and klaxon sounds.

Format

Each episode is hosted by a chair who poses obscure questions prepared by the show's researchers, known as the "QI Elves". Contestants are encouraged to answer in entertaining ways; the emphasis is on interesting facts rather than strictly correct responses. Most answers given on the show are connected to a series theme, and episodes in each series are organised by letter of the alphabet, with topics beginning with that letter — Series A, B, C, and so on.

History

QI was first broadcast on BBC Two in 2003, hosted by Stephen Fry, with Alan Davies as the permanent panellist appearing in nearly every episode. Fry stepped down after Series M in 2015, and Sandi Toksvig took over as host from Series N in 2016. The programme has also been shown on BBC Four and BBC One, and extended "XL" editions of episodes are regularly broadcast. It has spawned numerous books, including The Book of General Ignorance, as well as podcasts such as No Such Thing as a Fish, produced by the QI Elves.

Reception

The show has been praised for combining comedy with factual content and for debunking common misconceptions — a central theme of its "General Ignorance" round, in which seemingly obvious answers are usually wrong. QI has won several awards, including BAFTA nominations, and has been adapted in several other countries.
