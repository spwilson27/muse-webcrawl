Igor Ansoff

Born: 12 December 1918, Vladivostok, Russia

Died: 14 July 2002, San Diego, California

Known for: Ansoff matrix; strategic management

Harry Igor Ansoff (Russian: Игорь Ансов; 12 December 1918 – 14 July 2002) was a Russian-American applied mathematician and business manager, widely regarded as the father of strategic management. He is best known for the product–market growth matrix that bears his name.

Life and career

Ansoff emigrated with his family from Russia to New York as a child. He studied engineering at the Stevens Institute of Technology and earned a doctorate in applied mathematics from Brown University. He served in the US Naval Reserve during the Second World War, then worked at the RAND Corporation and at Lockheed, where he became a vice president. He later held professorships at Carnegie Mellon, Vanderbilt, and the United States International University, and founded a consulting practice in strategic management.

Contributions

In a 1957 Harvard Business Review article, "Strategies for Diversification", Ansoff introduced the Ansoff matrix, which classifies growth strategies along two axes — products and markets, each existing or new — yielding market penetration, market development, product development and diversification. His book Corporate Strategy (1965) laid the foundations of strategic planning, and later works such as Strategic Management (1979) addressed management under conditions of environmental turbulence, including the concepts of weak signals and strategic issue management.
