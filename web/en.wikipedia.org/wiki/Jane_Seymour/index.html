Jane Seymour (c. 1508/1509 – 24 October 1537) was Queen of England from 1536 until her death, as the third wife of King Henry VIII. She succeeded Anne Boleyn, whose execution took place eleven days before Jane's marriage to the king. Jane died shortly after giving birth to Henry's long-desired male heir, the future Edward VI, and was reportedly the wife Henry considered his "true" queen; he was buried beside her at Windsor.

Early life

Jane was the daughter of Sir John Seymour of Wulfhall, Wiltshire, and Margery Wentworth. She served as a maid of honour to both Catherine of Aragon and Anne Boleyn. Unlike many court ladies, she received little formal education, being more skilled in needlework and household management. Contemporaries described her as gentle, shy and demure — qualities that contrasted with Anne Boleyn's assertiveness and appealed to Henry by 1536.

Queenship

Henry became betrothed to Jane on 20 May 1536, the day after Anne Boleyn's execution, and married her on 30 May at Whitehall. Jane adopted the motto "Bound to obey and serve". As queen she was politically conservative and reportedly interceded for participants in the Pilgrimage of Grace, though Henry warned her not to meddle. She worked to restore Henry's daughter Mary to favour, a process completed in 1536.

Death

On 12 October 1537 Jane gave birth at Hampton Court to Prince Edward after a long, difficult labour. She appeared to recover but died on 24 October, probably of puerperal sepsis or a retained placenta fragment. She was given a queen's funeral and buried in St George's Chapel, Windsor Castle. Henry mourned her deeply, wore black for months, and did not remarry for over two years; in 1547 he was interred in the same vault.

Legacy

Jane's brothers Edward and Thomas Seymour rose to power during Edward VI's minority. In popular tradition Jane is remembered — as in the rhyme of Henry's wives — as the queen who "died", and as the mother of Henry's only legitimate surviving son.
