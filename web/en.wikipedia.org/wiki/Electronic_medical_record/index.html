Electronic medical record

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An electronic medical record (EMR) is a digital version of a patient's paper chart, maintained by a single health care provider or practice. EMRs contain the medical and treatment history of patients in one practice, including diagnoses, medications, laboratory results, immunisation dates and allergies.

The term is often contrasted with the electronic health record (EHR), which is designed to be shared across organisations and to follow a patient between providers. In everyday usage the two terms are frequently interchanged.

Adoption

Adoption accelerated sharply in the United States after the Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health (HITECH) Act of 2009, which funded incentive payments for "meaningful use" of certified EHR technology. By the mid-2010s the large majority of office-based physicians used some form of electronic record.

Benefits and criticism

Supporters cite legibility, remote access, clinical decision support such as drug-interaction alerts, and easier quality reporting. Critics point to documentation burden, time taken from patient contact, alert fatigue, interoperability problems between vendors' systems, and the risk of errors propagating through copied notes.
