The conquest of Mecca (Arabic: Fath Makkah) was the largely bloodless takeover of the city of Mecca by the Muslims of Medina in January 630 AD (Ramadan, 8 AH), led by the Islamic prophet Muhammad. It followed the Quraysh tribe's violation of the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah when their allies, the Banu Bakr, attacked the Muslim-allied Khuza'ah.

The campaign

Muhammad marched on Mecca with a force traditionally numbered at about 10,000 men, the largest Muslim army to date. The advance was kept secret, and Mecca's leaders, including Abu Sufyan, negotiated surrender. Muhammad entered the city and declared a general amnesty for the Quraysh — sparing all but a handful of individuals who had opposed Islam — an act widely remembered as demonstrating clemency.

Aftermath

Immediately after taking the city, Muhammad cleansed the Kaaba of its idols, rededicating it to monotheism; the event cemented the Kaaba as the qibla of Islam and established Mecca as the religion's spiritual centre. Within two years, most of Arabia had accepted Islam or entered into alliance with the Muslim community.
