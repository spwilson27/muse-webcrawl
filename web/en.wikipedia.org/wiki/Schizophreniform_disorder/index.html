Schizophreniform disorder

Schizophreniform disorder is a mental disorder diagnosed when symptoms of schizophrenia are present for a significant portion of the time within a one-month period, but signs of disruption are not present for the full six months required for a diagnosis of schizophrenia.

Signs and symptoms

The symptoms of schizophreniform disorder are the same as those of schizophrenia and may include delusions, hallucinations, disorganised speech, grossly disorganised or catatonic behaviour, and negative symptoms such as diminished emotional expression or avolition. By definition the total duration of the illness, including prodromal and residual phases, is at least one month but less than six months.

Causes

The exact cause is unknown, but like schizophrenia the condition is thought to arise from a combination of genetic vulnerability and environmental factors affecting brain development and function, particularly involving dopamine signalling.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis follows the criteria of the DSM-5. Clinicians must rule out schizoaffective disorder, bipolar disorder and major depressive disorder with psychotic features, as well as symptoms attributable to substances or another medical condition. Specifiers indicate whether good prognostic features are present, such as rapid onset of prominent psychotic symptoms and confusion or perplexity at the height of the episode.

Treatment and prognosis

Treatment typically involves antipsychotic medication together with psychotherapy and psychosocial support. About two-thirds of people initially diagnosed with schizophreniform disorder go on to receive a diagnosis of schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder; the remainder recover fully.
