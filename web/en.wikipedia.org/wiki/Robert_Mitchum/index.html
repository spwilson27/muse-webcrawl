Robert Mitchum

Robert Charles Durman Mitchum
Born: 6 August 1917, Bridgeport, Connecticut
Died: 1 July 1997 (aged 79), Santa Barbara, California
Occupation: Actor
Years active: 1942–1996

Robert Charles Durman Mitchum (August 6, 1917 – July 1, 1997) was an American actor. He is known for his antihero roles and understated performing style, and is ranked 23rd on the American Film Institute's list of the greatest male stars of classic American cinema.

Career

After a troubled youth riding freight trains, Mitchum broke into films in Hopalong Cassidy westerns and earned an Oscar nomination for The Story of G.I. Joe (1945). He became a defining star of film noir in Out of the Past (1947) and gave celebrated performances as the menacing preacher Harry Powell in The Night of the Hunter (1955) — with "LOVE" and "HATE" tattooed on his knuckles — and as Max Cady in Cape Fear (1962).

Later life

Mitchum worked steadily for five decades, in westerns, war films and dramas such as Ryan's Daughter (1970) and Farewell, My Lovely (1975), also recording music including the hit "The Ballad of Thunder Road." A 1948 marijuana arrest briefly jailed him without derailing his career. He died of complications from lung cancer and emphysema in 1997.
