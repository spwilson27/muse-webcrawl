Takata Corporation (タカタ株式会社) was a Japanese manufacturer of automotive safety systems, founded in 1933 by Takezo Takada as a maker of parachute cords and lifelines. It grew into one of the world's largest suppliers of seat belts, airbags, and steering systems, with manufacturing plants around the globe.

Airbag recall crisis

From 2013 onward, Takata airbag inflators using ammonium nitrate propellant were found to degrade over time, especially in hot and humid conditions, and could rupture on deployment, spraying metal fragments into the vehicle cabin. The defect was linked to numerous deaths and injuries worldwide and triggered the largest automotive recall in history, affecting on the order of 100 million inflators across many manufacturers.

Bankruptcy

In 2017 Takata pleaded guilty in the United States to criminal wire fraud related to the concealment of the defect and agreed to a $1 billion settlement. In June 2017 the company filed for bankruptcy protection in Japan and the United States. Its viable assets and operations were acquired in 2018 by Key Safety Systems (later part of Joyson Safety Systems), while the Takata entities remained responsible for recall obligations.
