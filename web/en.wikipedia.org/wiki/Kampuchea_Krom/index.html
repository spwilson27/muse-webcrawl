Kampuchea Krom (Khmer: កម្ពុជាក្រោម, "Lower Cambodia") is the Khmer name for the region of the Mekong Delta in what is now southern Vietnam. Historically part of the Khmer Empire and its successor Cambodian kingdoms, the territory was gradually absorbed into Vietnam between the 17th and 19th centuries through the Vietnamese southward expansion known as Nam tiến, and was formally assigned to Vietnam under French colonial administration in 1949, when it was incorporated into the State of Vietnam rather than French Cambodia.

History

The delta was once dominated by the early kingdom of Funan and later by Angkor-era Cambodia; the port of Óc Eo was a major trading centre of the ancient world. From the 1620s, Vietnamese settlers were permitted to settle at Prey Nokor (later Saigon, now Ho Chi Minh City), and over the following two centuries Vietnamese administration extended across the delta, while Cambodian kings — weakened by wars with Siam — progressively lost control. A decisive grievance was the 1949 French decision transferring the region (then known as part of Cochinchina) to Vietnam, an act protested by Cambodia ever since.

The Khmer Krom

The Khmer Krom ("Khmer of the lowlands") are the indigenous Khmer population of the delta, numbering roughly 1.3 million in Vietnam. They are predominantly Theravada Buddhists, maintain hundreds of Khmer pagodas that serve as centres of education and culture, and speak a southern dialect of Khmer. Khmer Krom advocacy groups, including exiled organisations and the Khmers Kampuchea-Krom Federation, have raised concerns about religious freedom, land rights and the preservation of Khmer-language education; Vietnamese authorities maintain that the community's rights are protected, and the region celebrates festivals such as Ok Om Bok and the ghe ngo (boat racing) festival shared with Cambodia.

Political significance

Kampuchea Krom remains a sensitive symbol in Cambodian nationalism: many Cambodians regard its loss as a historic injustice, and irredentist claims surface periodically in Cambodian politics and protests. Cambodia and Vietnam have nonetheless delimited most of their land border by treaty, and the area is today administered as Vietnam's Mekong Delta provinces, including An Giang, Kien Giang, Ca Mau, Soc Trang, Tra Vinh, Vinh Long, Can Tho and Ho Chi Minh City.
