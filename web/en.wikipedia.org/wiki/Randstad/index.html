The Randstad (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈrɑntstɑt]; "rim" or "edge" city) is a roughly crescent- or ring-shaped conurbation in the Netherlands, consisting of a circle of cities in the central-western part of the country. It contains the four largest Dutch cities — Amsterdam, Rotterdam, The Hague and Utrecht — as well as their surrounding suburbs, satellites and medium-sized cities, including Almere, Haarlem, Leiden, Dordrecht, Delft, Gouda, Amersfoort, Hilversum, Hoofddorp, Alphen aan den Rijn and Zaanstad.

The Randstad has a population of about 8.4 million people, roughly half the population of the Netherlands, and is one of the largest metropolitan regions in Europe. The name was coined, according to tradition, by KLM founder Albert Plesman, who, flying over the region in the 1930s, saw a ring of cities surrounding a green, open centre — the Groene Hart ("Green Heart") of agricultural and recreational land that planners have since worked to preserve from development.

Geography and structure

The region is often divided into two wings. The North Wing (Noordvleugel), centred on Amsterdam and Utrecht, is oriented toward finance, media, creative industries and services, and includes Amsterdam Airport Schiphol, one of Europe's busiest airports. The South Wing (Zuidvleugel), centred on Rotterdam and The Hague, is dominated by the Port of Rotterdam — Europe's largest seaport — and the petrochemical and logistics industries around it, together with the seat of the Dutch government, the royal court, most foreign embassies, and international institutions in The Hague such as the International Court of Justice.

Much of the Randstad lies below sea level on reclaimed polder land, making flood protection and water management central planning concerns.

Economy and transport

The Randstad generates the majority of the Netherlands' economic output. It is served by a dense network of motorways and by the busiest rail system in the country, anchored by Amsterdam Centraal, Rotterdam Centraal, Utrecht Centraal (the national rail hub) and Den Haag Centraal stations. Schiphol Airport lies between Amsterdam and Leiden. Because the area is not a formal administrative unit, its governance is a recurring subject of debate, with proposals over the years for a Randstad province or stronger metropolitan authorities in the Amsterdam and Rotterdam–The Hague regions.
