Falkland Islands

The Falkland Islands are an archipelago in the South Atlantic Ocean on the Patagonian Shelf, about 500 kilometres east of mainland South America. The principal islands are East Falkland and West Falkland, separated by Falkland Sound, with 776 smaller islands. The capital is Stanley, and the population is about 3,800.

The islands' sovereignty is disputed between the United Kingdom, which administers them as a British Overseas Territory, and Argentina, which claims them as the Islas Malvinas. Argentina's invasion in April 1982 led to the Falklands War, in which British forces recaptured the islands after 74 days, at the cost of 907 lives. Islanders voted overwhelmingly in 2013 to remain British. The economy rests on fishing (squid), sheep farming and tourism, with oil exploration in surrounding waters.
