Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) is a medical code set maintained by the American Medical Association (AMA) that describes medical, surgical, and diagnostic services and procedures. It is the standard system used in the United States to report procedures to insurers for payment, and its codes form Level I of the Healthcare Common Procedure Coding System (HCPCS).

Structure

CPT codes are five characters long and organised into three categories. Category I codes, the largest group, cover established procedures and are arranged in sections including evaluation and management, anaesthesia, surgery, radiology, pathology and laboratory, and medicine. Category II codes are supplemental tracking codes used for performance measurement, and Category III codes are temporary codes for emerging technologies and services. Two-digit modifiers appended to a code report special circumstances, such as a reduced or bilateral service.

Use

First published in 1966 and now updated annually, CPT is used on claim forms such as the CMS-1500 alongside diagnosis codes from the ICD. Because the AMA licenses CPT commercially, its use in American healthcare billing is effectively mandatory for reporting physician services to Medicare, Medicaid, and private insurers.
