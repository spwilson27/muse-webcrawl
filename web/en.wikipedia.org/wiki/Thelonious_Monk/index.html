Thelonious Sphere Monk (October 10, 1917 – February 17, 1982) was an American jazz pianist and composer. He had a unique improvisational style and made numerous contributions to the standard jazz repertoire, including "'Round Midnight", "Blue Monk", "Straight, No Chaser", "Ruby, My Dear", "In Walked Bud", and "Well, You Needn't". Monk is the second-most-recorded jazz composer after Duke Ellington.

Style

Monk's compositions and improvisations feature dissonances and angular melodic twists, often using flat ninths, elevenths, and thirteenths, consistent with his approach to jazz as a pianist. His percussive, sparse playing—marked by dramatic pauses, stabbing chords, and whole-tone runs—was instantly recognisable, as was his stage persona of hats, sunglasses, and dancing away from the piano during other soloists' turns.

Career

Raised in New York City, Monk was house pianist at Minton's Playhouse in the early 1940s, where bebop was forged in jam sessions with Dizzy Gillespie, Charlie Parker, Kenny Clarke, and others. His early Blue Note recordings (1947–1952) were commercially unsuccessful but are now classics. After a period with Prestige, and the loss and restoration of his New York cabaret card, he signed with Riverside Records; the album Brilliant Corners (1957) and his 1957 Five Spot residency with John Coltrane brought him wide acclaim. Signing with Columbia in 1962, he formed a long-running quartet with tenor saxophonist Charlie Rouse, appeared on the cover of Time magazine in 1964, and toured the world. Ill health led him to withdraw from public life in the mid-1970s.

Legacy

Monk was posthumously awarded a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award and a special Pulitzer Prize citation for his "significant and innovative contributions to the world of music". The Thelonious Monk Institute of Jazz (now the Herbie Hancock Institute) was founded in his honour.
