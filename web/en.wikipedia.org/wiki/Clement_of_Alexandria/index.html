Titus Flavius Clemens, better known as Clement of Alexandria (c. 150 – c. 215 AD), was a Christian theologian and philosopher who headed the Catechetical School of Alexandria. He sought to harmonize Greek philosophy with Christian faith.

Life

Converted from paganism, Clement traveled widely studying under Christian teachers before settling in Alexandria under Pantaenus. He fled the Severan persecution of 202 and spent his later years in Cappadocia. His most famous pupil was Origen.

Works

  • Protrepticus (Exhortation to the Greeks) — a call to abandon pagan religion.
  • Paedagogus (The Tutor) — Christian ethics and daily conduct.
  • Stromata (Miscellanies) — his great unfinished work on the true gnosis, interweaving Scripture, Plato, and Stoicism.

Theology

Clement held that philosophy was a "schoolmaster" preparing the Greeks for Christ, defended free will, and developed an influential account of scriptural allegory and of the Logos. He was long venerated as a saint; his name was removed from the Roman Martyrology in 1586 over doctrinal reservations, and restored in some traditions later.
