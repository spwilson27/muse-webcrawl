Respiratory system

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The respiratory system is the biological system that introduces respiratory gases to the body and performs gas exchange — taking in oxygen for cellular respiration and expelling carbon dioxide. In humans it comprises the airways (nose, pharynx, larynx, trachea and bronchi) and the lungs, where exchange occurs across millions of tiny air sacs called alveoli.

Breathing is driven mainly by the diaphragm, a dome of muscle below the lungs, under automatic control from the brainstem responding to blood carbon dioxide levels.

Gas exchange

Alveoli are wrapped in capillaries; oxygen diffuses into the blood and binds haemoglobin in red cells, while carbon dioxide diffuses out. The total alveolar surface is on the order of a tennis court, folded into a chest-sized volume.

Disorders

Respiratory diseases include asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), pneumonia, tuberculosis, lung cancer and the pneumonias caused by influenza and coronaviruses. Smoking and air pollution are dominant risk factors worldwide.
