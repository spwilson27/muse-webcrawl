Atlantic City Conference

The Atlantic City Conference was a historic summit of leaders of organized crime in the United States, held in Atlantic City, New Jersey, from May 13 to 16, 1929. Hosted by Atlantic City political boss and racketeer Enoch "Nucky" Johnson, it is generally regarded as the earliest organized-crime summit in American history and an important step toward the formation of a national crime syndicate.

Background

By 1929, Prohibition had made bootlegging enormously profitable but violent competition between gangs — especially in Chicago and New York — threatened the trade. The conference was convened under the cover of a birthday celebration for Chicago boss Al Capone, who was among the attendees, along with Meyer Lansky, Charles "Lucky" Luciano, Benjamin "Bugsy" Siegel, Frank Costello, Johnny Torrio, and Dutch Schultz.

Discussions and outcomes

According to later accounts, the delegates discussed dividing territories, cooperating in bootlegging and gambling, investing in legitimate businesses, and reducing the gang warfare that drew public and police attention. Capone was reportedly pressed to moderate the violence in Chicago. The meeting is seen as a precursor to the Commission and the National Crime Syndicate formed in the early 1930s. Immediately afterwards, Capone travelled to Philadelphia and was arrested on a weapons charge, beginning the legal troubles that ended with his imprisonment for tax evasion.

Historical assessment

Because no minutes were kept and details come largely from journalists and later memoirs, historians debate the conference's exact agenda and importance; some regard it as more of a social gathering than a founding convention. It has nevertheless become a staple of popular depictions of the American Mafia.
