Cancer vaccine

A cancer vaccine is a vaccine that either treats existing cancer or prevents cancer from developing. Vaccines that treat cancer are a form of immunotherapy.

Preventive vaccines

Some cancers are caused by viruses, so vaccines against those viruses can prevent cancer. The HPV vaccine protects against human papillomavirus strains that cause most cervical cancers and several other cancers, and the hepatitis B vaccine reduces the risk of liver cancer.

Therapeutic vaccines

Therapeutic vaccines aim to train the immune system to recognise tumor-associated antigens. Approaches include peptide and protein vaccines, dendritic cell vaccines, whole-cell vaccines, viral vectors, and personalised neoantigen vaccines made from the mutations in an individual patient's tumor, including mRNA-based designs.

Sipuleucel-T (Provenge), a dendritic-cell-based therapy, was approved in 2010 for metastatic prostate cancer. BCG, originally a tuberculosis vaccine, is used as an immunotherapy instilled into the bladder for early bladder cancer.

Challenges

Tumors vary between patients, can suppress the immune system, and often present few antigens that are truly tumor-specific, making vaccine development difficult. Many candidates are tested in combination with checkpoint inhibitors.
