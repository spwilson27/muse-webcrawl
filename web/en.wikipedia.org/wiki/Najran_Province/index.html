Najran Province (Arabic: منطقة نجران) is a province (mintaqah) of Saudi Arabia in the south-west of the country, bordering Yemen. Its capital is the city of Najran. The province is known for its mountain and desert landscapes, its historic oasis, and its distinctive multi-storey mud-brick tower houses.

History

The Najran oasis was an important centre on the incense trade routes of ancient South Arabia and was the site of a famous early Christian community; in 523 CE its Christians were massacred by the Himyarite king Dhu Nuwas, an event recalled in the Quran's story of the "People of the Ditch" in Surah Al-Buruj. The region was long contested between Yemen and the Saudis and was assigned to Saudi Arabia under the 1934 Treaty of Taif; a border agreement with Yemen was concluded in 2000.

People and culture

Many inhabitants belong to the Yam tribe and to the Ismaili Shia community, whose traditional heartland the province is. Landmarks include the ruined ancient city of al-Ukhdud, the Emarah Palace, and the Najran Valley Dam, one of the largest in the kingdom. Agriculture in the valley produces citrus fruit, especially oranges, alongside dates and grains.
