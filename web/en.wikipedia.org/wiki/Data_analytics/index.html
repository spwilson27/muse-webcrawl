Data analytics (DA) is the process of examining data sets in order to find trends and draw conclusions about the information they contain. Increasingly performed with specialised software and algorithms, it is used in commercial industries to support better-informed decisions, and in the sciences to verify or revise models and theories. The term covers a continuum of practice from traditional business intelligence to advanced statistical modelling.

Types of analytics

Four types are commonly distinguished. Descriptive analytics summarises what has happened, for example monthly revenue by region. Diagnostic analytics asks why it happened, drilling into causes. Predictive analytics uses statistical models and machine learning to forecast what is likely to happen. Prescriptive analytics goes further, recommending actions and estimating their likely outcomes.

Process

A typical analytics workflow involves defining the question, collecting relevant data (often from multiple systems), cleaning and validating it, exploring and modelling it with techniques ranging from aggregation and regression to clustering and classification, and communicating results through reports and visualisations. Because much real-world data is messy, data preparation commonly consumes the majority of an analyst's effort.

Applications

Data analytics is used in marketing (customer segmentation), finance (credit scoring and fraud detection), healthcare (patient outcomes and hospital operations), sport, logistics and government. The growth of "big data" — data sets too large or complex for traditional tools — drove the adoption of distributed computing platforms and, in the 2010s, the emergence of the data scientist as a distinct professional role.
