Uyghur (ئۇيغۇر تىلى, Uyghur tili) is a Turkic language written primarily in a Perso-Arabic alphabet and spoken by the Uyghur people. It is the official language of the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region of China, where it has roughly 10 million native speakers, with additional speaker communities in Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, and Uzbekistan and a worldwide diaspora.

History

Modern Uyghur descends from the Karluk branch of Turkic, whose literary predecessor was the Chagatai language, the lingua franca of Central Asia from the Timurid period into the early 20th century. The Old Uyghur of the Uyghur Khaganate and the Qocho kingdom, written in the vertically written Old Uyghur alphabet derived from Sogdian, is a more distant relative associated with a different branch. The name "Uyghur" was revived for the modern language and people in the 1920s–1930s.

Phonology and grammar

Uyghur has a vowel inventory of about eight vowels and is marked by vowel reduction and extensive vowel harmony in native words. Like other Turkic languages, it is agglutinative, with suffixes marking case (six cases), possession, tense, and person; word order is typically subject–object–verb, and there is no grammatical gender. The lexicon contains many loanwords from Persian, Arabic, Russian, and Mandarin Chinese.

Writing systems

In China, Uyghur is officially written in the Uyghur Arabic alphabet (Uyghur Ereb Yëziqi), a fully vocalized adaptation of the Perso-Arabic script standardized in the 1980s after a period in which a Latin alphabet (Yëngi Yëziq, promoted from the 1960s) was favored. Uyghurs in the former Soviet Union use Cyrillic, while diaspora communities and online usage also employ a Latin alphabet (Uyghur Latin Yëziqi).
