Eurodac

Eurodac compares the fingerprints of asylum applicants across European states.

Eurodac (European Dactyloscopy) is the European Union's fingerprint database for identifying asylum seekers and irregular migrants. Established by the Eurodac Regulation adopted in December 2000 and operational since 15 January 2003, it was the first common automated fingerprint identification system in the EU. It consists of a central system operated by the EU agency eu-LISA and national access points in the participating states.

Purpose

Eurodac was created to support the operation of the Dublin system, which determines which member state is responsible for examining an application for international protection. When a person aged 14 or over applies for asylum in a participating country, their fingerprints are taken and transmitted to the central system. A "hit" against a record from another state indicates that the person has previously applied there or crossed a border irregularly, and may trigger a transfer procedure.

Categories of data

The system stores three categories of fingerprints: those of asylum applicants (retained for ten years), those of third-country nationals apprehended crossing an external border irregularly (retained for eighteen months), and those of persons found illegally present, whose prints are compared but not stored. Since a 2013 recast of the regulation, designated law-enforcement authorities and Europol may query Eurodac under strict conditions for the prevention and investigation of serious crime and terrorism.

Participating states

All EU member states participate, along with the four European Free Trade Association countries—Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, and Liechtenstein—under association agreements. The system has drawn criticism from refugee and privacy advocates concerned about function creep and the retention of biometric data of people not accused of any crime.

Reform

As part of the EU's New Pact on Migration and Asylum, a recast Eurodac regulation adopted in 2024 expands the system to include facial images, lowers the minimum fingerprinting age to six, and extends its role to registering beneficiaries of temporary protection and resettled persons.
