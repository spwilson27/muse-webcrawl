Energy management system

An energy management system (EMS) is a system of computer-aided tools used by operators of electric utility grids to monitor, control and optimise the performance of the generation or transmission system. The term is also used for software applications that help building and industrial facility managers monitor and reduce energy consumption.

Electric utilities

In power systems, an EMS forms the core of a control centre, working together with SCADA (supervisory control and data acquisition) systems. Its functions include state estimation, contingency analysis, optimal power flow, load forecasting, automatic generation control and economic dispatch of generating units. Modern EMS platforms process thousands of measurements per second to keep the grid within secure operating limits.

Buildings and industry

In buildings, an energy management system (sometimes called a building energy management system, BEMS) monitors and controls mechanical and electrical equipment such as heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC), lighting and power systems. By collecting data from meters and sensors, such systems identify waste, verify savings from efficiency measures, and support compliance with standards such as ISO 50001.
