The Old Course at St Andrews is one of the oldest golf courses in the world, located in St Andrews, Fife, Scotland, and widely regarded as the "home of golf". It is a public course administered by the St Andrews Links Trust, played over common land beside the North Sea. Golf has been played on the links since the early 15th century; Archbishop Hamilton's charter of 1552 confirming the townspeople's right to play golf on the links is the earliest surviving written reference.

History

The course originally had 22 holes; in 1764 the first four and last four holes were combined into two, creating the 18-hole layout that became the world standard. The course's essential features — huge double greens, more than 110 bunkers, and the out-and-back routing along a narrow strip of links land — evolved naturally. Famous hazards include the Road Hole bunker at the 17th and Hell Bunker at the 14th; the stone Swilcan Bridge on the 18th fairway has become one of the sport's most photographed landmarks.

The Open Championship

The Old Course has hosted The Open Championship a record 30 times, most recently the 150th Open in July 2022, won by Cameron Smith. Champions at St Andrews include Jack Nicklaus (1970, 1978), Seve Ballesteros (1984), Nick Faldo (1990), John Daly (1995), and Tiger Woods (2000, 2005). The course also hosts the Alfred Dunhill Links Championship and the St Andrews Trophy.

Access

Unlike most championship venues, the Old Course is public: visiting golfers can book tee times in advance or enter a daily ballot (lottery), and the course is closed to golf on most Sundays, when the public may walk it.
