Sloop-of-war

In the age of sail, a sloop-of-war was a warship with a single gun deck that carried up to eighteen guns. In the rating system of the British Royal Navy, a sloop-of-war was an unrated vessel commanded by an officer with the rank of master and commander, ranking below the post ships (frigates and ships of the line) and above gun-brigs. The term did not refer to the sloop rig: naval sloops-of-war were usually ship-rigged (three masts) or brig-rigged (two masts).

Sloops-of-war were fast and handy, used for cruising, convoy escort, dispatch carrying, reconnaissance, and anti-privateer patrols. In the United States Navy, notable examples include USS Wasp and USS Hornet of the War of 1812 era, and the big ship-sloops of the Civil War period such as USS Kearsarge, which sank the Confederate raider CSS Alabama in 1864.

Steam sloops

From the 1840s, steam propulsion transformed the type: paddle and then screw sloops combined sail and engine power for global cruising duties. By the late 19th century, steel-hulled screw sloops performed colonial patrol and survey work, and the classification gradually gave way to cruisers and, in the 20th century, to the revived "sloop" of the Second World War — an anti-submarine escort vessel epitomized by the Black Swan class.
