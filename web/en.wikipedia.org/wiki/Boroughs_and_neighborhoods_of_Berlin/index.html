Boroughs and neighborhoods of Berlin

Boroughs (Bezirke)12
Localities (Ortsteile)96
Reform date1 January 2001
CityBerlin, Germany

Berlin is divided into twelve boroughs, called Bezirke, each with its own borough assembly and borough mayor. These are subdivided into a total of 96 officially recognized localities (Ortsteile), many of which correspond to historically independent towns and villages absorbed into the city, notably under the Greater Berlin Act of 1920.

The twelve boroughs

The twelve boroughs are: Mitte, Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg, Pankow, Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf, Spandau, Steglitz-Zehlendorf, Tempelhof-Schöneberg, Neukölln, Treptow-Köpenick, Marzahn-Hellersdorf, Lichtenberg, and Reinickendorf. Each borough administers local services such as schools, cultural facilities, and local planning, while the Senate of Berlin handles city-wide matters.

History

When Greater Berlin was created in 1920, the city was divided into 20 districts. During the division of the city after the Second World War, West Berlin had 12 districts and East Berlin 11. Following reunification, an administrative reform effective 1 January 2001 merged the 23 districts into the present twelve, pairing districts across the former East–West boundary in most cases.

Localities

Localities within boroughs retain distinct identities — examples include Prenzlauer Berg in Pankow, Charlottenburg and Wilmersdorf in the west, and Köpenick in the southeast. Informal neighborhoods (Kieze) further subdivide localities in everyday usage.
