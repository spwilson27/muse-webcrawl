Pinduoduo

Pinduoduo, founded in 2015 in Shanghai

Pinduoduo Inc. (Chinese: 拼多多; pinyin: Pīnduōduō) is a Chinese e-commerce platform operated by PDD Holdings. Founded in September 2015 by Colin Huang (Huang Zheng), it pioneered "social commerce" and team purchasing, growing within a few years into one of China's largest online retailers, rivalling Alibaba and JD.com.

Business model

Pinduoduo's signature feature is the team purchase: shoppers share product links with friends and family, typically over the WeChat messaging app, and unlock lower prices when enough buyers join a group order. The model, combined with gamified promotions, coupons and bargain features, proved especially popular in lower-tier cities and rural areas, and with price-sensitive consumers. The platform built a large business connecting farmers and manufacturers directly to consumers, cutting out intermediaries.

Growth and listing

The company listed on the Nasdaq in July 2018 under the ticker PDD, raising about $1.6 billion. By 2020 its annual active buyers numbered in the hundreds of millions, and in 2021 founder Colin Huang stepped down as chairman. PDD Holdings later redomiciled its principal executive offices to Ireland. In September 2022 PDD launched Temu, an overseas cross-border platform applying a similar low-price model, which expanded rapidly in the United States, Europe and many other markets.

Criticism

Pinduoduo has faced persistent criticism over counterfeit and low-quality goods, which it has addressed with brand-protection programmes and penalties for merchants. Its Chinese app was the subject of a 2023 security controversy after researchers alleged the app contained code capable of exploiting Android vulnerabilities, an affair that led to its removal from Google Play; the company denied wrongdoing.
