William Laws Calley Jr. (June 8, 1943 – April 28, 2024) was a United States Army officer convicted by court-martial for the premeditated murder of Vietnamese civilians in the My Lai massacre of March 16, 1968, during the Vietnam War. He was the only soldier convicted in connection with the massacre, in which troops of Charlie Company killed hundreds of unarmed villagers at My Lai.

My Lai and trial

Calley, a platoon leader in the Americal Division, maintained that he had acted on orders from his company commander, Captain Ernest Medina. News of the massacre became public in 1969 through the reporting of Seymour Hersh. Calley was charged with the murder of 109 Vietnamese civilians. His court-martial at Fort Benning, Georgia, in 1970–71 drew intense national attention and sharply divided American opinion. On March 29, 1971, he was convicted of the premeditated murder of 22 villagers and sentenced to life imprisonment at hard labour.

Aftermath

President Richard Nixon intervened to have Calley placed under house arrest pending appeal. His sentence was progressively reduced, and he was paroled in November 1974 after serving about three and a half years, most of it under house arrest. Calley later lived a private life in Georgia and Florida, working in the jewellery business. In 2009 he publicly apologised for his role at My Lai, saying there was "not a day that goes by that I do not feel remorse".
