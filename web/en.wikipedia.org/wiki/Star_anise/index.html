Star anise is a spice made from the fruit of the Chinese evergreen tree Illicium verum. It is named for the distinctive star shape of its fruit, which typically has eight points, each containing a single seed. Star anise is a key ingredient in traditional Chinese five-spice powder and is widely used in Chinese, Vietnamese, Indian and Indonesian cooking.

The flavour derives largely from anethole, the same compound found in aniseed and fennel, giving star anise a sweet, liquorice-like taste. Whole dried pods are added to braises, soups such as Vietnamese phở, and the spiced broth for tea eggs; ground star anise appears in garam masala and other spice blends.

Medicinal and industrial uses

Star anise is the principal commercial source of shikimic acid, a chemical intermediate used in the manufacture of the antiviral drug oseltamivir (Tamiflu). Demand during influenza outbreaks has periodically driven up prices. In traditional Chinese medicine the fruit is used to treat digestive complaints.

Toxicity confusion

Japanese star anise (Illicium anisatum) is toxic and should not be consumed; contamination incidents involving it mixed into star anise teas have prompted regulatory warnings, notably in the early 2000s.
