Northern Ireland Act 1998

The Northern Ireland Act 1998 (c. 47) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom which established a devolved legislature for Northern Ireland, the Northern Ireland Assembly, implementing the Belfast (Good Friday) Agreement of 10 April 1998. It repealed the Government of Ireland Act 1920 in relevant part and remains the constitutional foundation of devolution in Northern Ireland.

Provisions

The Act declares that Northern Ireland remains part of the United Kingdom and shall not cease to be so without the consent of a majority voting in a referendum—the “principle of consent”. It establishes the Assembly, elected by the single transferable vote, and a power-sharing Executive Committee in which ministerial portfolios are allocated by the d'Hondt method. The First Minister and deputy First Minister are elected jointly on a cross-community basis.

The Act distinguishes excepted, reserved, and transferred matters, defining the Assembly's legislative competence. It also created the British–Irish Council in domestic law, imposed equality duties on public authorities (notably section 75), and provided for the Northern Ireland Human Rights Commission. Following the St Andrews Agreement, the Northern Ireland (St Andrews Agreement) Act 2006 amended several of its provisions.

Referendum and operation

The Act's settlement was endorsed in simultaneous referendums in Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland in May 1998. The Assembly was first elected in June 1998; devolution has since been suspended and restored several times, most notably from 2002 to 2007 and from 2017 to 2020, but the Act's framework has endured as the basis of government.
