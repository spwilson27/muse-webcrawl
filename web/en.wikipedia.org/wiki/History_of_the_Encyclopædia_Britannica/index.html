History of the Encyclopædia Britannica

The history of the Encyclopædia Britannica spans more than 250 years, from its first publication in Edinburgh in 1768–1771 to the end of its print edition in 2012 and its continuation as an online encyclopedia. The Britannica was conceived during the Scottish Enlightenment by printer Colin Macfarquhar, bookseller Andrew Bell, and editor William Smellie, who wrote much of the first edition himself. Fifteen print editions appeared, including the celebrated eleventh edition (1910–11), long regarded as a pinnacle of scholarship.

Edinburgh origins

Issued in 100 weekly numbers and bound in three volumes, the first edition (1768–1771) contained some 2,400 pages. The second edition (1777–1784) expanded to ten volumes, and the third (1788–1797) to eighteen, establishing the work's commercial model. Contributors to early editions included James Tytler, who wrote major treatises while living as a fugitive debtor.

Nineteenth century

The fourth through eighth editions added scholarly contributors such as Sir Walter Scott, Thomas Malthus, David Ricardo and James Mill. The ninth edition (1875–1889), nicknamed the "Scholar's Edition", featured articles by James Clerk Maxwell and Lord Kelvin. Financial difficulties led to American involvement: the ninth edition was marketed in the United States, and in 1901 the rights were bought by American publishers.

The eleventh edition and after

The eleventh edition (1910–11), edited by Hugh Chisholm and published with Cambridge University Press, comprised 29 volumes and remains famous for literary, authoritative articles by some 1,500 experts. The fourteenth edition (1929) adopted "continuous revision". Mortimer J. Adler's influence shaped the fifteenth edition (1974), reorganised into the Micropædia, Macropædia and Propædia. Encyclopædia Britannica, Inc. was bought by the Swiss billionaire Jacqui Safra in 1996.

Digital era

Britannica issued a CD-ROM edition in 1994 and launched Britannica.com in 1999. Facing competition from free online resources, notably Wikipedia (founded 2001), the company announced in March 2012 that the 2010 printing would be the last; the work continues online by subscription.
