Port of Eilat

The Port of Eilat is a seaport in the city of Eilat, at the northern tip of the Red Sea's Gulf of Aqaba. It is Israel's only port on the Red Sea, giving the country access to shipping routes to Asia and the Far East via the Strait of Tiran and the Bab el-Mandeb without passing through the Suez Canal.

History

The port was established in the 1950s after Israel's occupation of the area in 1949 and the reopening of the Strait of Tiran to Israeli shipping following the 1956 Suez Crisis. It was formally inaugurated in 1965. For many years a key cargo was oil imported from Iran and unloaded for transport through the Trans-Israel pipeline to Ashkelon on the Mediterranean.

Operations

The port handles bulk cargo — notably phosphates and potash from the Dead Sea region — as well as vehicle imports, which have formed a large share of traffic, and general cargo. In the 2010s the port was privatised and operated by a private concessionaire. Traffic declined sharply after 2023, when Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping deterred vessels from calling at Eilat.
