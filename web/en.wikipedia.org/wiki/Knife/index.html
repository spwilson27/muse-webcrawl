A knife (pl.: knives) is a tool or weapon with a cutting edge or blade, usually attached to a handle. One of humanity's oldest tools, the knife has been used since the Stone Age as both an implement and a weapon. It consists of a blade — historically made of flint, obsidian, bronze, and iron, and today usually of steel or ceramic — and a handle into which the blade's tang extends.

History

Knapped stone blades are among the earliest artefacts of the hominin record, appearing over two million years ago. Metalworking brought copper and bronze knives in the Bronze Age, iron knives in the Iron Age, and increasingly specialised forms in the medieval period: table knives became common in European dining only from the 17th century, when pointed tips were sometimes rounded by law or custom — Cardinal Richelieu is often credited with ordering blunt table knives to discourage picking teeth and violence at table.

Types

Knives are classified by function: kitchen knives (chef's knife, paring knife, bread knife, cleaver), table knives, utility and pocket knives (including the Swiss Army knife and multi-tools), hunting and survival knives, and combat knives and bayonets. Blade geometry — point shape (clip, drop, tanto), grind, and edge profile — is adapted to each purpose, as is the distinction between fixed-blade and folding designs.

Materials and regulation

Modern blades are made from stainless or carbon steels, sometimes laminated or pattern-welded (Damascus steel), and increasingly from advanced ceramics. Because knives can serve as weapons, many jurisdictions regulate their sale, possession, and public carry, restricting blade length or locking and automatic mechanisms.
