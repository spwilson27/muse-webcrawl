1958 Pakistani coup d'état

Date7–27 October 1958
LocationPakistan
LeadersIskander Mirza; Muhammad Ayub Khan
ResultConstitution abrogated; martial law; Ayub Khan takes power

The 1958 Pakistani coup d'état was the first military coup in Pakistan's history. On 7 October 1958, President Iskander Mirza abrogated the 1956 constitution, dissolved the assemblies, and appointed army commander-in-chief General Muhammad Ayub Khan as Chief Martial Law Administrator. Twenty days later, on 27 October, Ayub Khan deposed Mirza himself and assumed the presidency, inaugurating more than a decade of military rule.

Background

Pakistan's first decade of independence was marked by political instability: seven prime ministers in eleven years, factional infighting, and tensions between East and West Pakistan. The 1956 constitution made Pakistan an Islamic republic with Mirza as its first president, but no general election had yet been held under it, and one scheduled for 1959 threatened the position of the ruling establishment.

The coup

Citing political chaos and the danger of the coming elections, Mirza — acting with Ayub Khan's support — proclaimed martial law on 7 October 1958, banned political parties, and imposed press censorship. Mirza soon attempted to assert control over the military administration and manoeuvre against Ayub, but on 27 October senior generals compelled Mirza to resign; he was exiled to London, where he died in 1969.

Aftermath

Ayub Khan introduced the "Basic Democracies" system of indirect elections, promulgated a new constitution in 1962, and pursued economic modernisation and an alliance with the United States. The 1958 coup set the precedent for repeated military interventions in Pakistani politics, followed by further coups in 1977 and 1999.
