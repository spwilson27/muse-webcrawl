Fisher Building

Location3011 W. Grand Blvd., Detroit, Michigan
Completed1928
ArchitectAlbert Kahn Associates

The Fisher Building is a landmark Art Deco skyscraper in the New Center area of Detroit, Michigan, United States. Completed in 1928, the building is often called "Detroit's largest art object". It houses the Fisher Theatre, one of Detroit's oldest live theatre venues, along with offices and retail space.

History

The building was commissioned by the Fisher brothers, founders of Fisher Body, and designed by Albert Kahn Associates, with chief designer Joseph Nathaniel French. The Fishers intended the complex to include a second, even taller tower and a third building, but the Great Depression halted those plans; only the first tower was built. It was constructed of limestone, granite, and marble, with a roof originally tiled in gold — the tiles were removed during World War II out of concern they would guide enemy bombers.

Architecture

The 30-story tower rises 428 feet (130 m) and is richly decorated with marble, mosaics, painted ceilings, and bronze work. Its lavish three-story arcade, known as one of the finest interior spaces in Detroit, features work by sculptor Corrado Parducci and Hungarian artist Géza Maróti. The building was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1989.

Fisher Theatre

The Fisher Theatre opened in 1928 as a movie palace and was remodeled in 1961 as a live theatre, seating around 2,000 patrons. It hosts touring Broadway productions and is operated by the Nederlander Organization.
