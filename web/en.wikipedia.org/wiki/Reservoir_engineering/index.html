Reservoir engineering

Reservoir engineering is a branch of petroleum engineering that applies scientific principles to the fluid flow through porous medium during the development and production of oil and gas reservoirs so as to obtain a high economic recovery. The working tools of the reservoir engineer are subsurface geology, applied mathematics, and the basic laws of physics and chemistry governing the behavior of liquid and vapor phases of crude oil, natural gas, and water in reservoir rock.

Scope

Reservoir engineers assess the amount of oil and gas in place, estimate recoverable reserves, forecast production rates over the life of a field, and design development plans — including well placement, injection of water or gas for pressure maintenance, and enhanced oil recovery methods such as chemical, thermal, or CO2 flooding. Key techniques include material balance analysis, decline curve analysis, well testing, and numerical reservoir simulation.

Reservoir simulation

Modern reservoir engineering relies heavily on computer simulation: the reservoir is divided into grid blocks, each assigned properties such as porosity and permeability, and the flow of oil, water, and gas is modelled over time. History matching adjusts the model until it reproduces past production, after which it is used to predict future performance under alternative development scenarios.
