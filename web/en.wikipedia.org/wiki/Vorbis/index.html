Vorbis is a free and open-source software project headed by the Xiph.Org Foundation. The project produces an audio coding format and software reference encoder/decoder (codec) for lossy audio compression. Vorbis is most commonly used in conjunction with the Ogg container format and is therefore often referred to as Ogg Vorbis.

History

Vorbis development began in 1998, led by Chris Montgomery, after licensing changes made the popular MP3 format costly for developers. The name derives from Vorbis, a character in Terry Pratchett's novel Small Gods. Version 1.0 was released in July 2002. The format was designed to be completely free of patent encumbrances and typically achieves better sound quality than MP3 at comparable bitrates, using variable bitrate encoding and a modified discrete cosine transform with sophisticated psychoacoustic modeling.

Usage

Vorbis saw wide adoption in open-source software, video games, and streaming: Spotify used Ogg Vorbis for its desktop streaming for many years, and the format is used by Wikipedia and other Wikimedia projects for audio. Support in consumer hardware was mixed, however, and the rise of AAC and later Xiph's own Opus codec — which outperforms Vorbis at all bitrates — led to Vorbis being largely superseded, though it remains in maintenance and use in legacy and web content.

Technical details

Vorbis supports sampling rates from 8 kHz to 192 kHz and more than two channels (including surround configurations). Encoding quality is governed by a quality setting from −1 to 10 rather than a fixed bitrate. Metadata is carried in Vorbis comments, a flexible key–value tagging system.
