Union Pacific Big Boy

The Union Pacific Big Boy is a type of simple articulated 4-8-8-4 steam locomotive manufactured by the American Locomotive Company (ALCO) for the Union Pacific Railroad between 1941 and 1944. Twenty-five were built to haul heavy freight trains over the Wasatch Range between Green River, Wyoming, and Ogden, Utah. The Big Boys are the heaviest and among the longest steam locomotives ever built, measuring about 40 metres (132 feet) with tender and weighing roughly 540 tonnes.

Design and service

The 4-8-8-4 wheel arrangement combined two sets of eight driving wheels under one boiler, designed to sustain 100 km/h (60 mph) while pulling trains of up to 4,200 tons. They were coal-fired, fitted with large 68-inch drivers, and developed over 6,000 horsepower. Dieselization displaced them by the late 1950s; the last revenue run was in 1959, and the fleet was retired by 1962.

Preservation

Eight Big Boys survive. Most famously, No. 4014 was reacquired by Union Pacific in 2013, restored to operating condition at Cheyenne, Wyoming, and returned to excursion service in May 2019 for the 150th anniversary of the transcontinental railroad, making it the world's largest operational steam locomotive.
