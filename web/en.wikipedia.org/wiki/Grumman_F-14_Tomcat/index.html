Grumman F-14 Tomcat

F-14 Tomcat
Role: Carrier-based air superiority fighter / interceptor
Manufacturer: Grumman
First flight: 21 December 1970
Retired: 2006 (US Navy)

The Grumman F-14 Tomcat is an American carrier-capable supersonic, twin-engine, two-seat, twin-tail, variable-sweep wing fighter aircraft. It served as the United States Navy's primary maritime air superiority fighter, fleet defense interceptor, and tactical aerial reconnaissance platform from 1974 to 2006.

Design

The F-14 was designed around the AWG-9 radar and the long-range AIM-54 Phoenix missile, able to track up to 24 targets and engage six simultaneously. Its variable-geometry wings sweep automatically between 20° and 68°, giving both low-speed carrier approach handling and high-speed intercept performance. A two-person crew—pilot and Radar Intercept Officer—sits in tandem.

Service history

Tomcats scored air-to-air victories over Libyan aircraft in 1981 and 1989 (the Gulf of Sidra incidents), flew combat in the Gulf War, and in later life were adapted for precision strike as "Bombcats" with LANTIRN targeting pods. The F-14 was retired by the US Navy in 2006, replaced by the F/A-18E/F Super Hornet. Iran, which received 79 aircraft before the 1979 revolution, has kept Tomcats in service for decades. The type achieved lasting fame through the 1986 film Top Gun.
