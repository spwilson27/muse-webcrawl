North American X-15

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The X-15, the fastest crewed aircraft ever flown.

The North American X-15 is an American hypersonic rocket-powered research aircraft operated by the United States Air Force and NASA as part of the X-plane series of experimental aircraft. The X-15 set speed and altitude records in the 1960s, crossing the edge of outer space, and reached a top speed of Mach 6.7 (about 4,520 mph / 7,274 km/h), which remains the highest speed ever recorded by a crewed, powered aircraft. Its pilots included Neil Armstrong, who later became the first person to walk on the Moon.

Design and development

Designed and built by North American Aviation, the X-15 was a long, cylindrical aircraft built largely of Inconel X, a nickel alloy able to withstand the extreme heating of hypersonic flight. It was carried aloft under the wing of a modified B-52 Stratofortress and released at about 45,000 feet, after which its XLR-99 rocket engine ignited. Because it could not take off conventionally, the X-15 landed on retractable skids at high speed on dry lakebeds at Edwards Air Force Base in California. Three aircraft were built.

Operational history

Between 1959 and 1968 the X-15 made 199 flights with twelve pilots. Eight pilots flew above 50 miles (80 km), qualifying for astronaut wings under the US definition of spaceflight, and two flights by Joseph A. Walker in 1963 exceeded 100 kilometres, the Kármán line used internationally. On 3 October 1967, William J. "Pete" Knight flew X-15A-2 to Mach 6.7, the programme's speed record; the aircraft had been fitted with a dummy ramjet and an ablative coating for the flight. The programme ended in tragedy once: on 15 November 1967, Michael J. Adams was killed when his X-15 broke up during re-entry. Data from the programme contributed directly to the design of the Mercury, Gemini, Apollo and Space Shuttle vehicles, as well as to knowledge of hypersonic aerodynamics and high-altitude physiology.
