Media franchise

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A media franchise, also simply called a franchise, is a collection of related media in which several derivative works have been produced from an original creative work of fiction, such as a film, a work of literature, a television program, or a video game. Typically, a franchise work consists of a cross-marketing across multiple distinct media.

Development

Media franchises usually arise when a work becomes successful enough to sustain sequels, prequels, spin-offs, adaptations, and merchandise. The fictional universe may expand across films, television series, novels, comics, video games, and theme park attractions — a strategy known as transmedia storytelling, in which each medium contributes distinct elements to a shared story world. Rights holders such as Disney, Warner Bros., and Nintendo manage franchises as long-term commercial properties, coordinating releases and licensing.

Examples

Among the highest-grossing media franchises of all time are Pokémon (the highest-grossing overall, driven heavily by merchandise), Mickey Mouse and Disney properties, Star Wars, the Marvel Cinematic Universe, Harry Potter, and Hello Kitty. Franchise revenues are often dominated not by the original medium but by licensed merchandise, games, and home entertainment.

Franchise fatigue

Critics and analysts discuss "franchise fatigue" — declining audience interest when sequels and spin-offs are released too frequently or perceived as formulaic — which has affected several major film series. Nonetheless, franchise productions dominate global box office receipts and publisher release schedules.
