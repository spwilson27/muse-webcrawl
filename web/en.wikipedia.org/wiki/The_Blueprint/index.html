The Blueprint is the sixth studio album by American rapper Jay-Z, released on September 11, 2001, through Roc-A-Fella Records and Def Jam Recordings. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest hip hop albums of all time and a landmark in Jay-Z's career.

Unlike many of Jay-Z's previous records, which relied on club-oriented production, The Blueprint features soul-based sampling and production handled primarily by Kanye West, Just Blaze, and Bink, marking a shift that helped bring chipmunk soul production to mainstream prominence.

Background and recording

Jay-Z recorded the album in less than two weeks during the summer of 2001, while awaiting trial on weapons and assault charges. Nine of the album's songs were reportedly recorded in two days. The album's release date coincided with the September 11 attacks, yet it still debuted at number one on the Billboard 200, selling more than 427,000 copies in its first week.

Singles and notable tracks

The album produced the singles "Izzo (H.O.V.A.)", "Girls, Girls, Girls", "Jigga That Nigga", and "Song Cry". "Izzo (H.O.V.A.)", built around a sample of the Jackson 5's "I Want You Back", became Jay-Z's first top-ten single as a lead artist. The track "Takeover", produced by Kanye West, is a diss track aimed at rappers Prodigy of Mobb Deep and Nas, and fueled one of hip hop's most famous feuds; Nas responded on "Ether".

Critical reception and legacy

The Blueprint received universal acclaim from music critics and has appeared on numerous lists of the greatest albums of all time, including Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time. In 2019, it was selected by the Library of Congress for preservation in the National Recording Registry for being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant". The album spawned two sequels: The Blueprint 2: The Gift & The Curse (2002) and The Blueprint 3 (2009).
