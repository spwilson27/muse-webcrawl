Birsa Munda (15 November 1875 – 9 June 1900) was an Indian tribal independence activist and folk hero from the Munda tribe of the Chota Nagpur Plateau region, in present-day Jharkhand. He led a millenarian tribal religious and political movement against British colonial rule in the late 19th century.

Early life and movement

Born into a poor sharecropper family, Birsa was educated briefly at a German mission school before rejecting missionary influence. In the 1890s he emerged as a preacher and faith healer, founding a movement that preached monotheism, moral reform and rejection of superstition. Followers revered him as Bhagwan and as Dharti Aba ("Father of the Earth").

The Ulgulan

His movement turned militant as Munda lands were alienated to outsiders (dikus) under colonial land and forest laws. The rebellion known as the Ulgulan ("Great Tumult") of 1899–1900 saw attacks on police stations, landlords and missionaries across the Ranchi and Singhbhum districts. Birsa was captured in February 1900 and died in Ranchi jail on 9 June 1900, officially of cholera, aged 24. The uprising led to the Chotanagpur Tenancy Act of 1908, which restricted transfer of tribal land to non-tribals.

Legacy

Birsa Munda is honoured as a national hero in India; his birth anniversary, 15 November, is celebrated as Janjatiya Gaurav Divas, and the day in 2000 that the state of Jharkhand was created. Airports, universities and institutions bear his name, and he remains the only tribal leader whose portrait hangs in the Central Hall of the Indian Parliament.
