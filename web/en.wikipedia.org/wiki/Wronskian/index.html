Wronskian

In mathematics, the Wronskian (or Wrońskian) is a determinant used in the study of differential equations, where it can sometimes show the linear independence of a set of solutions. It is named after the Polish mathematician Józef Hoene-Wroński, who introduced it in 1812.

Definition

For n functions f1, …, fn that are (n−1) times differentiable, the Wronskian is the determinant of the matrix whose rows are the functions and their successive derivatives, evaluated at a point x. For two functions, W(f, g) = f g′ − g f′.

Linear independence

If the Wronskian of a set of functions is nonzero at some point of an interval, the functions are linearly independent on that interval. The converse is not true in general for arbitrary functions, but for solutions of a single linear homogeneous differential equation the Wronskian is either identically zero or never zero — a consequence of Abel's identity, which expresses the Wronskian in terms of the equation's coefficients.

Generalisations

Analogues include the Casoratian for difference equations and Wronskian determinants in differential algebra, where Wronskians characterise linear dependence over the field of constants.
