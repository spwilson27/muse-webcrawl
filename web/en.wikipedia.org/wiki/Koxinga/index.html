Koxinga

Zheng Chenggong, the Ming loyalist commander known in the West as Koxinga

Koxinga (1624 – 23 June 1662), born Zheng Chenggong (Chinese: 鄭成功), was a Chinese Ming loyalist general and admiral who resisted the Qing conquest of China and, in 1662, expelled the Dutch East India Company from Taiwan, founding the Kingdom of Tungning there. The name "Koxinga" derives from the Hokkien pronunciation of his title Kok-sèng-iâ ("Lord of the Imperial Surname"), granted because the Longwu Emperor bestowed on him the imperial surname Zhu.

Life

Zheng was born in Hirado, Japan, to the Chinese maritime magnate and pirate-trader Zheng Zhilong and a Japanese mother, Tagawa Matsu. Raised in Fujian, he passed the Confucian examinations and was presented at the Ming court. When the Qing captured Fujian, his father defected, but Zheng Chenggong refused, taking command of the family fleet and holding coastal enclaves for the Southern Ming for over fifteen years. In 1659 he sailed up the Yangtze with a vast armada and besieged Nanjing, but was defeated and driven back to the coast, retaining Xiamen (Amoy) and Kinmen.

Conquest of Taiwan

Needing a secure base, in April 1661 he crossed the Taiwan Strait with some 25,000 men and besieged the Dutch at Fort Zeelandia. After a nine-month siege the Dutch governor Frederick Coyett surrendered in February 1662, ending 38 years of Dutch rule; Koxinga made Taiwan the base of his anti-Qing kingdom and promoted Chinese settlement and agriculture. He died only months later, in June 1662, aged 37, reportedly of malaria amid news that his father had been executed by the Qing and family tombs desecrated. His son Zheng Jing succeeded him.

Legacy

Koxinga is venerated as a hero in China, Taiwan, and Japan: as a Ming loyalist patriot, as the founder of Chinese Taiwan, and — through Chikamatsu's puppet play The Battles of Coxinga (1715) — as a Japanese national hero. Temples across Taiwan honour him as a deity, and National Cheng Kung University in Tainan bears his name.
