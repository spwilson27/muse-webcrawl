Joseph Bazalgette

Sir Joseph William Bazalgette CB (28 March 1819 – 15 March 1891) was an English civil engineer who, as chief engineer of London's Metropolitan Board of Works, designed and oversaw the construction of the Victorian London sewerage system — a network of intercepting sewers, pumping stations, and river embankments that transformed public health in the capital after the Great Stink of 1858.

Early career

Bazalgette was born in Enfield, the son of a retired Royal Navy captain. He was articled to the engineer Sir John Macneill, worked on railway and land-drainage projects in England and Ireland, and set up his own consulting practice in Westminster in 1842. In 1852 he became chief engineer of the Metropolitan Commission of Sewers, and in 1856 engineer to the new Metropolitan Board of Works.

London's main drainage

Victorian London's waste flowed into the Thames, fouling the river and fuelling cholera epidemics. After the Great Stink of 1858 forced Parliament to act, Bazalgette's plan was approved: some 82 miles (132 km) of great intercepting sewers running west to east on both banks of the Thames, fed by over 1,000 miles of street sewers, carrying sewage to outfalls at Beckton and Crossness east of the city. Grand pumping stations at Abbey Wood (Crossness, opened 1865) and elsewhere lifted the flow. The system was officially opened by the Prince of Wales in 1865 and substantially completed by 1875.

Bazalgette famously doubled the diameter of his pipes beyond his calculated requirement, reasoning that the density of the future city could not be foreseen — a decision that left London's sewers able to cope for well over a century.

Other works and legacy

His embankments — the Victoria, Albert, and Chelsea Embankments — reclaimed land from the Thames, carrying sewers, roads, and underground railways. He also designed or remodelled bridges including Putney, Battersea, and Hammersmith Bridges, and many London streets. He was knighted in 1875. His great-great-grandson Peter Bazalgette became a noted television executive.
