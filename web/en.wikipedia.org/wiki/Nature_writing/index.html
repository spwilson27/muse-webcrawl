Nature writing

Nature writing is a form of nonfiction or fiction prose that draws on the natural environment as its subject, combining observation of the natural world with personal reflection. It spans natural history essays, journals, memoirs of place and works of environmental advocacy.

History

The genre has roots in the natural history writing of Gilbert White, whose The Natural History and Antiquities of Selborne (1789) recorded careful observations of his parish. In the United States it developed through Henry David Thoreau's Walden (1854), John Muir's writings on the Sierra Nevada and John Burroughs' essays. In the twentieth century, Aldo Leopold's A Sand County Almanac (1949) and Rachel Carson's Silent Spring (1962) gave nature writing an explicitly ecological and conservationist cast.

Contemporary writing

Modern nature writing includes authors such as Barry Lopez, Annie Dillard, Robert Macfarlane and Helen Macdonald, and often engages with climate change, extinction and the human place in the more-than-human world.
