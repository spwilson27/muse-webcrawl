Bench grinder

A bench grinder is a benchtop machine used to drive abrasive wheels for grinding, sharpening, shaping and polishing metal tools and parts. It typically features two wheels of differing grain coarseness on either end of an electric motor shaft, with adjustable tool rests and eye shields in front of each wheel.

Description

Common wheel diameters are 150 mm (6 in) and 200 mm (8 in). One wheel is usually coarse for rough shaping and the other finer for sharpening and finishing. Wheels may be replaced with wire brushes for rust removal or with buffing wheels for polishing. Bench grinders are standard equipment in machine shops, garages and school workshops.

Uses

Typical tasks include sharpening drill bits, chisels, lathe tools, axes and lawn mower blades, removing burrs, and shaping metal stock. The workpiece is held against the rotating wheel at the tool rest, and cooled periodically in water to avoid overheating the edge.

Safety

Grinding produces sparks and debris; eye protection is essential. Wheels must be ring-tested and correctly mounted, guards kept in place, and the gap between tool rest and wheel kept small (about 3 mm) to prevent workpieces jamming.
