Soar (cognitive architecture)

Soar is a cognitive architecture, originally created by John Laird, Allen Newell, and Paul Rosenbloom at Carnegie Mellon University. Development began in the early 1980s; Newell described Soar in his 1990 book Unified Theories of Cognition as a candidate for a unified theory of the human mind. The name originally stood for "State, Operator, And Result", reflecting the theory that all goal-directed behaviour consists of selecting and applying operators to states.

Architecture

Soar represents knowledge as production rules (if–then rules) that fire in parallel against a working memory of symbolic structures. Decisions proceed by proposing, evaluating and applying operators; when knowledge is insufficient, an impasse occurs and Soar creates a substate in which it can reason about the problem — a mechanism used for planning and problem solving. Successful problem solving is compiled into new rules by a learning procedure called chunking. Later versions added reinforcement learning, episodic memory, semantic memory and mental imagery.

Applications

Soar has been used to model human behaviour in psychology experiments and to build intelligent agents, including TacAir-Soar, a system that flew simulated military air missions in large-scale distributed exercises, and agents for video games such as Quake II and Descent 3. It remains in active development, primarily at the University of Michigan.
