Bhopal

Bhopal is the capital city of the Indian state of Madhya Pradesh and the administrative headquarters of Bhopal district. Known as the "City of Lakes" for its numerous natural and artificial lakes, including the Upper and Lower Lakes, it is one of India's greenest cities. Its population is roughly two million.

History

According to tradition, the city was founded in the 11th century by the Paramara king Bhoja. The modern city was established in the early 18th century by Dost Mohammad Khan, an Afghan soldier who founded the princely State of Bhopal. The state was notable for being ruled in the 19th and early 20th centuries by a succession of women rulers, the Nawab Begums, who promoted education and public works. Bhopal acceded to India in 1949 and became a state capital in 1956.

Bhopal disaster

On the night of 2–3 December 1984, a leak of methyl isocyanate gas from the Union Carbide India Limited pesticide plant killed thousands of residents and injured hundreds of thousands, making it the world's worst industrial disaster. The catastrophe and its long aftermath — contested compensation, ongoing groundwater contamination, and criminal proceedings — remain defining issues for the city.

Geography and culture

Bhopal lies on the Malwa plateau at an average elevation of about 500 metres. The old city is known for its mosques, including the Taj-ul-Masajid, among the largest in India, while the new city hosts institutions such as the Bharat Bhavan arts centre and the Tribal Museum.
