A manga artist (Japanese: 漫画家, mangaka) is a person who creates manga, Japanese comics and print cartoons. Most professional mangaka both write and draw their works, though some work in writer–artist partnerships or studios with assistants. Manga artists typically debut by winning competitions or submitting one-shot stories to magazine editors, and then serialise works in weekly or monthly anthology magazines such as Weekly Shōnen Jump, with successful series collected into paperback volumes called tankōbon.

Work process

Production begins with the name, a rough thumbnail storyboard approved by the artist's editor, who plays a central creative role in commercial manga. The artist then draws the chapter in pencil, inks it — traditionally with G-pens and maru pens on large-format manuscript paper — and applies screentone, backgrounds and finishing effects, increasingly using digital tools. Weekly serialisation imposes famously demanding schedules, and established artists employ assistants for backgrounds, inking and tones. Reader surveys run by the magazines heavily influence whether a series continues.

Notable artists

Osamu Tezuka, creator of Astro Boy, is revered as the "God of Manga" for establishing the post-war industry and its visual grammar. Other widely influential artists include Akira Toriyama (Dragon Ball), Eiichiro Oda (One Piece, the best-selling manga series in history), Rumiko Takahashi (Ranma ½, Inuyasha), Takehiko Inoue, Naoki Urasawa and the all-female group CLAMP. Many artists began in the amateur doujinshi scene, which also functions as a pathway into professional work.

Employment and income

Mangaka are usually paid a modest per-page rate plus royalties on collected volumes, so income depends overwhelmingly on a series' popularity and on licensing for anime and merchandise. Copyright is generally retained by the artist, while publishers control serialisation. The profession is known for extreme working hours and consequent health problems, an enduring subject of debate in Japan.
