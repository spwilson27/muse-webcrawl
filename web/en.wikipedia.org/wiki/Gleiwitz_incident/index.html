The Gleiwitz incident (German: Überfall auf den Sender Gleiwitz) was a false flag attack on the German radio station Sender Gleiwitz in Gleiwitz (then Germany, now Gliwice, Poland) staged by Nazi Germany on the night of 31 August 1939. Together with about twenty other staged incidents in Operation Himmler, it was used by Adolf Hitler as a pretext to justify the invasion of Poland the following morning, which began the Second World War in Europe.

The operation

The operation was organised by Reinhard Heydrich and Gestapo chief Heinrich Müller, and carried out by Alfred Naujocks, an SS officer who testified about it at Nuremberg. SS men dressed in Polish uniforms seized the radio station, broadcast a short anti-German message in Polish, and fired shots. To make the attack seem convincing, the Gestapo murdered Franciszek Honiok, a Silesian farmer known for sympathising with Poland, and left his body at the scene dressed in a Polish uniform; concentration camp prisoners killed as "evidence" were referred to by the code phrase "canned goods" (Konserve).

Aftermath

German media publicised the "attack" and Hitler mentioned border violations in his speech to the Reichstag on 1 September 1939 announcing that Germany had returned fire. The incident became a textbook example of a false-flag provocation and featured in the Nuremberg trials' evidence of Nazi conspiracy to wage aggressive war.
