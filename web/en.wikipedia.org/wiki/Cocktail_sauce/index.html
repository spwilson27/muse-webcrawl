Cocktail sauce

Cocktail sauce, also known as seafood sauce, is one of several types of cold or room-temperature sauces often served as part of a dish referred to as a seafood cocktail, or as a condiment with other seafoods. In the United States it is generally a mixture of tomato-based sauce—usually ketchup—and prepared horseradish, spiced with lemon juice, Worcestershire sauce, and hot sauce. The sauce, and the dish after which it is named, was created by British celebrity chef Fanny Cradock.

Variations

In Britain and much of the Commonwealth, the sauce served with a prawn cocktail is Marie Rose sauce: a blend of mayonnaise, tomato ketchup, Worcestershire sauce, lemon juice, and sometimes brandy or Tabasco, giving a pale pink sauce resembling Thousand Island dressing. In Belgium, a similar sauce is frequently served with tomatoes stuffed with shrimp (tomate-crevette). Australian seafood sauce typically combines mayonnaise with tomato sauce and lemon.

Use

The American horseradish version is the classic accompaniment to a shrimp cocktail, raw oysters, clams, and fried seafood. Its pungency comes from horseradish rather than chillies, distinguishing it from Mexican coctel sauces, which are often more like a spicy tomato-vegetable salsa served with shrimp in a glass.
