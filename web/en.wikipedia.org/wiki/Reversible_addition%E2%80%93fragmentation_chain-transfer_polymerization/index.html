Reversible addition−fragmentation chain-transfer polymerization

FieldPolymer chemistry
TypeReversible-deactivation radical polymerization
Introduced1998 (CSIRO)

The RAFT mechanism

Reversible addition−fragmentation chain-transfer (RAFT) polymerization is a method of controlled radical polymerization developed at CSIRO in 1998. It uses a thiocarbonylthio chain-transfer agent to mediate the growth of polymer chains, giving good control over molecular weight and narrow molecular-weight distributions.

Mechanism

A growing radical chain adds to the C=S bond of the RAFT agent, forming an intermediate radical that fragments to release a new radical and a dormant chain capped with the thiocarbonylthio group. Rapid exchange between active and dormant chains means all chains grow intermittently and to similar lengths.

Applications

RAFT is versatile across many monomer families and tolerant of functional groups and aqueous conditions. It is widely used to make block copolymers, star and graft architectures, and materials for drug delivery and coatings. Its main drawbacks are the colour and odour of the sulfur-containing end groups, which may need removal.
