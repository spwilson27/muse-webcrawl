Turnhout is a city and municipality in the province of Antwerp, Flanders, Belgium, near the Dutch border. It is the capital of the arrondissement of Turnhout and the principal town of the Kempen region. Turnhout is internationally known as a centre of the playing card industry.

History

Turnhout grew up around the hunting castle of the Dukes of Brabant, first mentioned around the 12th century. The town received city rights in the early 13th century. In 1597 the Battle of Turnhout was fought here during the Eighty Years' War, in which Maurice of Nassau defeated a Spanish force. The castle later served as a hunting lodge for Mary of Hungary.

Playing cards

From the 19th century, Turnhout became a world centre of playing-card manufacture, most famously through the company Cartamundi, founded in 1970 from a merger of local printers and now the world's largest playing card manufacturer. The National Museum of Playing Cards is located in the city.

Landmarks

Sights include the Castle of the Dukes of Brabant, the late-Gothic St Peter's Church, the 13th-century beguinage (a UNESCO World Heritage site as part of the Flemish Béguinages), and the Taxandria Museum.
