Ripple is a real-time gross settlement system, currency exchange and remittance network created by Ripple Labs Inc., a US-based technology company. Released in 2012, Ripple is built upon a distributed open source protocol and supports tokens representing fiat currency, cryptocurrency, commodities, or other units of value. Ripple's native cryptocurrency is XRP.

History

The concept behind Ripple was first developed by Ryan Fugger in 2004 as Ripplepay, a decentralised monetary system based on trust lines between participants. In 2011 the system was redesigned by Arthur Britto and David Schwartz to eliminate the energy-intensive mining used by Bitcoin. Jed McCaleb, Chris Larsen and others founded the company that became Ripple Labs in 2012, and the XRP Ledger was launched that year.

Design

Ripple purports to enable "secure, instantly and nearly free global financial transactions of any size with no chargebacks." The network uses a common distributed ledger, the XRP Ledger, maintained by a network of independent validating servers that constantly compare their transaction records. Rather than proof of work, the ledger uses a consensus protocol in which validators reach agreement on the order and outcome of transactions.

Transactions settle in roughly three to five seconds. The network can sustain around 1,500 transactions per second. XRP can be sent directly without a central intermediary, and is used by Ripple's commercial products as a bridge currency to source liquidity for cross-border payments.

Adoption

Banks and payment providers that have experimented with or adopted Ripple's technology include Santander, American Express, Standard Chartered, SBI Holdings, and MoneyGram (a partnership that ended in 2021). Ripple's product suite has included xCurrent, xRapid (rebranded as On-Demand Liquidity, later Ripple Payments), and xVia.

SEC lawsuit

In December 2020 the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission filed a lawsuit against Ripple Labs and two of its executives, alleging that XRP was an unregistered security. In July 2023 a federal judge ruled that XRP sold on public exchanges did not meet the legal definition of a security, while institutional sales did constitute investment contracts — a partial victory for Ripple widely noted in the cryptocurrency industry.
