Magnificent Mile

The Magnificent Mile, sometimes referred to as The Mag Mile, is an upscale section of Chicago's Michigan Avenue, running from the Chicago River to Oak Street in the Near North Side. The district is located adjacent to downtown, and one block east of Rush Street. The Magnificent Mile serves as the main thoroughfare between Chicago's Loop business district and its Gold Coast.

It is generally the western boundary of the Streeterville neighbourhood, to the east of which is the community area of Near North Side. Real estate developer Arthur Rubloff of Rubloff Company gave the district its nickname in the 1940s.

History

Following the Great Chicago Fire of 1871, State Street in the Loop remained the city's primary commercial street, but was surpassed in the pre-Depression years by the upscale North Michigan Avenue, which was widened between 1918 and 1920 and saw the opening of the Michigan Avenue Bridge in 1920. The avenue was lined with new landmarks such as the Tribune Tower (1925) and the Wrigley Building (1924), and developers promoted it as an elegant shopping and residential boulevard modelled in part on Parisian precedents.

Today

Today, the Magnificent Mile contains a mixture of upscale department stores, restaurants, luxury retailers, residential and commercial architecture, financial services companies, and hotels, catering primarily to tourists and the wealthy. The area also has a high concentration of the city's advertising agencies.

The district is home to a number of tall buildings and landmarks, including the John Hancock Center (now 875 North Michigan Avenue), the historic Chicago Water Tower, the Tribune Tower, and the Wrigley Building. Major stores and shopping centres along the avenue include Water Tower Place, the Shops at North Bridge, and flagship stores of major national and international brands.

Location

The Magnificent Mile runs along Michigan Avenue from the Chicago River north to Oak Street, marking the transition from the Loop to the Near North Side and the Gold Coast neighbourhood beyond.
