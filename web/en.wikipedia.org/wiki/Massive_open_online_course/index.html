Massive open online course

A massive open online course (MOOC) is an online course aimed at unlimited participation and open access via the web. MOOCs provide interactive course materials — video lectures, readings, problem sets and quizzes — along with forums for interaction among students, professors and teaching assistants. They are a prominent recent development in distance education.

History

The term MOOC was coined in 2008 by Dave Cormier, in response to a course run by Stephen Downes and George Siemens based on connectivism (the "cMOOC" model). In 2011–2012, courses from Stanford, MIT and Harvard drew enrolments in the hundreds of thousands: Sebastian Thrun's artificial intelligence course attracted some 160,000 students. The New York Times called 2012 "The Year of the MOOC". Major providers that emerged include Coursera, edX (founded by MIT and Harvard) and Udacity, generally called "xMOOCs" for their more conventional instructor-led format.

Features and criticism

MOOCs are typically free to audit, with paid verified certificates and, later, full online degrees. Common criticisms include very low completion rates (often under 10 per cent), concerns about assessment integrity and plagiarism, and questions about sustainable business models. Nevertheless, MOOCs expanded access to university-level material worldwide and influenced campus teaching through blended and flipped-classroom models.
