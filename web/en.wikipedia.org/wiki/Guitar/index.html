Guitar

The guitar is a stringed musical instrument that is usually fretted (with some exceptions) and typically has six or twelve strings. It is usually held flat against the player's body and played by strumming or plucking the strings with the dominant hand, while simultaneously pressing selected strings against frets with the fingers of the opposite hand. A plectrum or individual finger picks may also be used to strike the strings. The sound of the guitar is projected either acoustically, by means of a resonant hollow chamber and wooden soundboard, or through an electrical amplifier and a speaker.

There are three main types of modern guitar: the classical guitar (Spanish guitar), the steel-string acoustic guitar or electric guitar, and the Hawaiian guitar (played across the player's lap). Traditional acoustic guitars include the flat top guitar (typically with a large body, steel strings, and a modern folk guitar shape) and the archtop guitar (typically with steel strings and slightly smaller body, commonly played in jazz).

History

The guitar likely originated in Spain in the early 16th century, derived from the late-medieval guitarra latina. By the 16th century, most of the features of the modern classical guitar were already emerging, although the instrument of the period was smaller and had five courses of strings. The modern six-string classical guitar was developed in the late 18th century and was standardised by builders such as the Spanish luthier Antonio de Torres Jurado in the 19th century.

Steel-string acoustic guitars emerged in the United States in the late 19th century, notably through the work of Christian Frederick Martin. The electric guitar, developed in the early 1930s, used an electromagnetic pickup to amplify string vibrations; solid-body designs by Leo Fender and Les Paul in the 1950s transformed popular music, giving rise to rock and roll, blues-rock, and heavy metal.

Construction

A guitar consists of a body with a rigid neck to which the strings, generally six in number, are attached. The top of the body (soundboard) is typically spruce or cedar; the back and sides are often rosewood or mahogany. The neck carries a fingerboard (fretboard) inlaid with metal frets that divide the string into fixed pitches. Tuning machines on the headstock adjust string tension.

Tuning

The standard tuning assigns the pitches E2, A2, D3, G3, B3, and E4 to the six strings, from low to high. Numerous alternative tunings exist, including open tunings and drop D. Twelve-string guitars double each course with an octave or unison string.
