Penal battalion

A penal battalion (or penal unit) is a military unit composed of personnel drawn from military prisons, convicts, or soldiers punished for disciplinary offences, typically assigned to especially dangerous or undesirable duties. Service in such a unit is often presented as an opportunity to redeem oneself, sometimes with the promise of restored rank or a cleared record for survivors.

The best-known historical examples are the Soviet shtrafbaty of the Second World War, created under Joseph Stalin's Order No. 227 of 1942 ("Not a step back"), and the German Bewährungsbataillone and Dirlewanger Brigade of Nazi Germany. Penal units were frequently used for tasks with very high casualty rates, such as clearing minefields, frontal assaults, and serving as expendable rearguards.

Penal and probationary units have also appeared in other conflicts and armies, and the concept remains controversial because of the coercion involved and the frequently brutal conditions under which such soldiers served.
