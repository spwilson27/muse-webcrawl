Aridity is a climatic condition characterized by a severe deficiency of available water, in which precipitation is far exceeded by potential evapotranspiration. Arid and semi-arid lands (drylands) cover roughly 40% of Earth's land surface and are home to more than two billion people.

Measurement

The UNEP aridity index (AI) is the ratio of mean annual precipitation to potential evapotranspiration. Regions are classified as hyper-arid (AI < 0.05), arid (0.05–0.20), semi-arid (0.20–0.50), and dry sub-humid (0.50–0.65). The Köppen classification similarly defines arid (BW) and semi-arid steppe (BS) climates by precipitation thresholds adjusted for temperature and rainfall seasonality.

Causes and distribution

Aridity results from subtropical high-pressure belts, rain shadows, continental interiors far from moisture sources, and cold ocean currents along coasts. Great arid regions include the Sahara, the Arabian Peninsula, central Australia, the Atacama, and the southwestern United States.

Ecology and human impact

Arid ecosystems support specially adapted plants and animals, with low and unpredictable productivity. Dryland livelihoods face water scarcity, and land degradation in drylands — desertification — is a major environmental concern, intensified in many regions by climate change.
