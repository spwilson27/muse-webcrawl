Margarete Steiff

Margarete Steiff (24 July 1847 – 9 May 1909) was a German seamstress and founder of the Steiff company, famous worldwide for its stuffed animals, in particular the teddy bear. Despite being confined to a wheelchair after contracting polio in childhood, she built a successful toy-manufacturing business in Giengen an der Brenz.

Early life

Margarete Steiff was born in Giengen an der Brenz in 1847. At eighteen months old she contracted polio, which paralysed both legs and caused pain in her right arm. She attended a sewing school in defiance of doctors' advice and became a skilled seamstress, eventually training other women and, with her sisters, running a dressmaking business.

In 1877 she opened a shop selling felt articles. In 1880, following a pincushion pattern in a fashion magazine, she made small stuffed felt elephants — the "Elefäntle" — which children adopted as toys. The elephant pincushions proved so popular that Steiff began producing other stuffed animals, laying the foundation of the company catalogued in 1892.

The company and the teddy bear

With the help of her brother Fritz and later her nephews, especially Richard Steiff, the business grew steadily. In 1902 Richard designed a jointed bear made of mohair plush, model "Bear 55 PB", which was presented at the Leipzig Toy Fair in 1903. An American buyer ordered 3,000 of them, and the bear became an international sensation, coinciding with the popularity of President Theodore "Teddy" Roosevelt's hunting story in the United States — hence "teddy bear".

In 1904 the company introduced the famous "button in ear" (Knopf im Ohr) trademark to protect its products from imitation. By 1907 Steiff produced nearly a million bears in a single year.

Death and legacy

Margarete Steiff died of pneumonia on 9 May 1909, aged 61. The company, Margarete Steiff GmbH, remains based in Giengen and continues to produce high-quality stuffed animals bearing her name and the button-in-ear trademark. The Steiff Museum in Giengen commemorates her life and work.
