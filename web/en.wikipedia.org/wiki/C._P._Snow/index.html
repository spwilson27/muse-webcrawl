C. P. Snow

Charles Percy Snow, Baron Snow (15 October 1905 – 1 July 1980) was an English novelist and physical chemist who also served in the UK government. He is best known for the Strangers and Brothers sequence of novels and for his 1959 Rede Lecture, The Two Cultures, which lamented the gulf between scientists and "literary intellectuals" in British culture.

Life

From a working-class Leicester background, Snow won a scholarship to Cambridge, took a doctorate in physics, and became a fellow of Christ's College. During the Second World War he recruited scientists for the war effort, later becoming a civil service commissioner and, in 1964, a life peer and junior minister in Harold Wilson's government.

The Two Cultures

Snow argued that the breakdown of communication between the sciences and the humanities hindered solving the world's problems. F. R. Leavis's fierce rebuttal made the exchange one of the most famous literary controversies of the century.
