Mendoza Province

Mendoza (Spanish pronunciation: [menˈdosa]), officially the Province of Mendoza, is a province of Argentina in the western Cuyo region, bordering Chile across the Andes. Its capital and largest city is Mendoza. The province covers about 148,827 km² and has a population of around two million.

Geography

Mendoza is dominated by the Andes to the west, including Aconcagua (6,961 m), the highest mountain in the Americas and outside Asia. The climate is arid, and agriculture depends on irrigation oases fed by rivers from snowmelt. The province is prone to the warm, dry zonda wind and to seismic activity.

Economy

Mendoza is Argentina's principal wine-producing province, responsible for roughly two-thirds of the country's wine output and internationally renowned for its Malbec wines. Other important activities include olive oil, fruit production, petroleum refining, and tourism — wine tourism in the Uco Valley and Maipú, mountaineering around Aconcagua, and ski resorts such as Las Leñas.

History

The city of Mendoza was founded in 1561 by Pedro del Castillo from Chile. The province played a central role in the independence wars: José de San Martín, as governor of Cuyo, organised the Army of the Andes here before the famous Crossing of the Andes in 1817 to liberate Chile and Peru.
