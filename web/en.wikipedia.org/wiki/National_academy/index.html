National academy

A national academy is an organisational body, usually operating with state financial support and approval, that co-ordinates scholarly research activities and standards for academic disciplines, most notably the sciences, within a particular country. National academies typically consist of the country's most distinguished scholars, elected by their peers, and serve as honorific societies, advisory bodies to government, and in some countries as direct operators of research institutes and universities. The model descends from the learned academies of early modern Europe, such as the Royal Society of London (founded 1660) and the French Academy of Sciences (1666).

The National Academy of Sciences building in Washington, D.C.

Functions

National academies commonly perform several roles:

  • Honour and recognition — electing leading scholars as fellows or members, widely regarded as among the highest honours in a discipline.
  • Advice — providing governments and the public with independent expert advice via consensus studies and reports.
  • Research — in many continental European and Asian systems, operating networks of research institutes employing thousands of scientists; the Chinese Academy of Sciences and the institutes descended from the Soviet Academy of Sciences are prominent examples.
  • International representation — representing the country's scholarly community in international unions and in the InterAcademy Partnership, the global network of academies.

Examples

United States

The United States National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine — comprising the National Academy of Sciences (chartered by Congress in 1863 under Abraham Lincoln), the National Academy of Engineering and the National Academy of Medicine — are private, non-profit societies that provide independent advice through the National Research Council. They do not run laboratories.

United Kingdom

The United Kingdom has four national academies: the Royal Society (natural sciences), the British Academy (humanities and social sciences), the Royal Academy of Engineering, and the Academy of Medical Sciences, all publicly funded learned societies.

Other countries

Examples elsewhere include the French Academy of Sciences within the Institut de France; the German National Academy of Sciences Leopoldina (founded 1652, the world's oldest continuously existing academy of its kind); the Russian Academy of Sciences; the Chinese Academy of Sciences and Chinese Academy of Engineering; the Japan Academy; the Australian Academy of Science; and India's three national science academies (INSA, IASc and NASI).

Interacademy organisations

National academies cooperate through bodies such as the InterAcademy Partnership, ALLEA (the European Federation of Academies of Sciences and Humanities), and regional networks, issuing joint statements on issues such as climate change, pandemics and research integrity.
