Cappoquin (Irish: Ceapach Choinn, meaning "Conn's tillage plot") is a town in west County Waterford, Ireland, situated on the River Blackwater at the foot of the Knockmealdown Mountains. The town developed at a strategic crossing point of the river and is noted for salmon fishing, rowing and its dramatic setting beneath wooded hills.

History

Cappoquin grew around the castle and estate of the Keily and later families; Cappoquin House, an 18th-century Georgian mansion overlooking the town and river, remains a landmark. The town's "cornerstone" layout and its narrow, twisting bridge over the Blackwater are well-known features. Industries historically included milling and bacon curing, and the town was served by the now-closed railway line between Waterford and Mallow.

Sport and culture

Cappoquin has a long rowing tradition — Cappoquin Rowing Club was founded in 1870 — and the town is home to Cappoquin GAA. The surrounding Blackwater valley, with Lismore Castle nearby, is a noted angling and touring area.
