Saronic Gulf

The Saronic Gulf, looking toward Aegina.

The Saronic Gulf (Greek: Σαρωνικός κόλπος, Saronikós kólpos) is an inlet of the Aegean Sea in Greece, lying between the Attica and Argolis peninsulas. It is named after the mythical king Saron, and its waters wash the eastern coast of the Isthmus of Corinth, the port of Piraeus, and the southern suburbs of Athens.

Geography

The gulf contains the Saronic Islands: Salamis, Aegina, Agistri, Poros, and the peninsular Methana, along with smaller islands such as Dokos and Hydra lying near its southern approaches. The Corinth Canal, cutting through the Isthmus of Corinth, connects the Saronic Gulf with the Gulf of Corinth to the west. The gulf is traversed by busy ferry routes from Piraeus to the islands and the Peloponnese.

History

The Saronic Gulf was the setting of the Battle of Salamis in 480 BC, in which the Greek fleet defeated the Persians in the straits between Salamis and the mainland. In antiquity the shores of the gulf were lined with important ports and sanctuaries, including the Temple of Aphaea on Aegina and the sanctuary of Poseidon on Poros. The volcanic Methana peninsula and the offshore island formations record the gulf's position along the Hellenic volcanic arc.
