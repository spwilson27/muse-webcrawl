Ismail Ibn Sharif

Moulay Ismail Ibn Sharif
Sultan of Morocco
Reign: 1672 – 1727
Born: c. 1645, Sijilmasa, Morocco
Died: 22 March 1727 (aged 81–82), Meknes, Morocco
Dynasty: Alaouite
Successor: Abu'l Abbas Ahmad II

Moulay Ismail Ibn Sharif (c. 1645 – 22 March 1727) was the second ruler of the Alaouite dynasty of Morocco, reigning from 1672 to 1727. His 55-year reign was one of the longest in Moroccan history, during which he unified the country, expelled the Spanish and English from their Moroccan outposts, and made Meknes a grand imperial capital.

Reign

Ismail succeeded his half-brother Moulay Rashid and spent his reign subduing rival factions and tribal rebellions. He recaptured Tangier from the English in 1684 and Larache from the Spanish in 1689, and drove the Spanish from their coastal enclaves except for Ceuta, Melilla and the Alhucemas. He built the great walls, palaces and stables of Meknes, which he made his capital, and maintained a large army of Black Guards (Abid al-Bukhari), whose descendants formed the backbone of his military power.

Diplomacy and legacy

Ismail cultivated diplomatic relations with European powers, most famously exchanging embassies with Louis XIV of France — an episode celebrated in the French play Le Bourgeois gentilhomme's Turkish ceremony — and with Charles II of England. He is also reputed to have had an exceptionally large family: a son count of several hundred is commonly cited, though the figures are disputed. After his death in 1727, Morocco fell into a period of civil war among his sons until the accession of Sidi Mohammed III in 1757.
