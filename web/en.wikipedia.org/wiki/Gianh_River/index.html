Gianh River

The Gianh River (Vietnamese: Sông Gianh) is a river in Quảng Bình Province, in the North Central Coast region of Vietnam. It flows from the Annamite Range eastward to the South China Sea, and is about 268 kilometres long.

Historically, the Gianh River marked the boundary between Đàng Ngoài (the north, ruled by the Trịnh lords) and Đàng Trong (the south, ruled by the Nguyễn lords) during the Trịnh–Nguyễn War in the 17th and 18th centuries, when Vietnam was effectively divided at the river.

The river valley is largely agricultural, and the river is known for seasonal flooding during the monsoon.
