Moons of Neptune

The planet Neptune has 16 known moons, the largest of which is Triton — discovered by William Lassell just 17 days after Neptune itself in 1846. Triton accounts for over 99.5% of the mass orbiting Neptune and is the only large moon in the Solar System with a retrograde orbit, indicating it is a captured Kuiper belt object similar to Pluto.

Inner and irregular moons

Seven small regular moons orbit close to Neptune in prograde, near-equatorial orbits, several of them associated with Neptune's faint rings: Naiad, Thalassa, Despina, Galatea, Larissa, Hippocamp, and Proteus. Proteus, about 420 km across, is the second-largest Neptunian moon and is heavily cratered. Beyond Triton lie the irregular moons — Nereid, with one of the most eccentric orbits of any moon, and distant captured bodies such as Halimede, Sao, Laomedeia, Psamathe, and Neso, whose orbit is among the most distant of any planetary satellite.

Triton

Triton is a geologically active world with nitrogen geysers, a thin nitrogen atmosphere, and a young, cantaloupe-textured surface imaged by Voyager 2 in 1989 — still the only spacecraft to visit the Neptune system. Tidal interactions are slowly spiralling Triton inward; in several billion years it is expected to cross Neptune's Roche limit and break up into a spectacular ring system.
