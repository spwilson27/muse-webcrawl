Rovaniemi (Finnish: [ˈroʋɑˌniemi]; Northern Sami: Roavvenjárga) is a city in Finland and the regional capital of Lapland. It lies at the confluence of the Kemijoki and Ounasjoki rivers, about 6 km south of the Arctic Circle, and serves as the commercial and administrative centre of Finnish Lapland.

History

The area has been settled since the Stone Age and grew with the 19th-century timber trade. In October 1944, during the Lapland War, retreating German forces burned Rovaniemi almost completely. Post-war reconstruction followed a plan by Alvar Aalto, who laid out the street grid in the shape of reindeer antlers; Aalto also designed the Lappia Hall and library. The modern municipality was formed in 2006 by merging the town with the surrounding rural municipality.

Santa Claus and tourism

Rovaniemi markets itself as the official hometown of Santa Claus. Santa Claus Village, on the Arctic Circle 8 km north of the centre, includes Santa's main post office and draws hundreds of thousands of visitors a year; the SantaPark attraction is carved into bedrock nearby. The Arktikum science centre and museum presents Arctic nature and Sámi culture. The city is also a base for aurora viewing, husky and reindeer safaris, and the University of Lapland.

Climate

Rovaniemi has a subarctic climate with long, snowy winters — snow cover typically lasts from November into May — and mild summers with midnight sun in June and the polar-night period (kaamos) around the winter solstice.
