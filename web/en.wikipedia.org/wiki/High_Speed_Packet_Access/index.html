High Speed Packet Access

High Speed Packet Access (HSPA) is a family of 3G mobile telephony protocols that improved UMTS data rates: HSDPA for the downlink and HSUPA for the uplink, together marketed as 3.5G.

Technology

HSDPA introduced fast scheduling at the base station, hybrid ARQ and adaptive modulation, initially reaching 14.4 Mbit/s downlink; HSUPA raised uplink rates to 5.76 Mbit/s. Evolved HSPA (HSPA+) added MIMO and 64-QAM, reaching 42 Mbit/s and beyond, bridging the gap to LTE.

Deployment

HSPA networks were rolled out worldwide from 2005–2006 onward and carried most mobile data before LTE became dominant in the 2010s.
