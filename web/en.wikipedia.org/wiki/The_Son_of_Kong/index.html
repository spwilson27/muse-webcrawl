The Son of Kong is a 1933 American adventure film produced by RKO Radio Pictures, the sequel to King Kong, released the same year. Directed by Ernest B. Schoedsack, it features stop-motion animation by Willis H. O'Brien and a score by Max Steiner.

Plot

A month after the destruction caused by Kong in New York, showman Carl Denham is bankrupt and facing lawsuits and indictment. Fleeing the city with Captain Englehorn, he ends up in the Dutch port of Dakang, where he meets singer Hilda Petersen and learns of a rumoured treasure on Skull Island. Returning to the island, the party encounters Kong's offspring: a friendly, twelve-foot albino gorilla, "Little Kong", whom Denham rescues from quicksand. Little Kong protects the group from the island's prehistoric creatures as they find a temple treasure, but when an earthquake sinks the island, Little Kong sacrifices himself — holding Denham above the waves until rescue arrives, then drowning.

Production

The film was made quickly and on a much smaller budget than its predecessor to capitalise on King Kong's success, reusing models and footage techniques. Its lighter, more comic tone and shorter running time distinguish it from the original. Though less celebrated, it is notable for O'Brien's animation, including the endearing characterisation of Little Kong, whose expressions were an advance in stop-motion personality animation.
