Junípero Serra

Saint Junípero Serra Ferrer (November 24, 1713 – August 28, 1784) was a Spanish Catholic priest and Franciscan missionary who founded a chain of missions in Alta California, beginning with San Diego de Alcalá in 1769. He is known as the "Apostle of California."

Early life

Serra was born Miquel Josep Serra in Petra, Mallorca, and joined the Franciscan Order in 1730, taking the name Junípero. A gifted scholar, he taught philosophy at the Lullian University in Palma before sailing for New Spain in 1749. In Mexico he served in the Sierra Gorda missions and Mexico City.

The California missions

In 1769 Serra accompanied the Portolá expedition north and founded Mission San Diego, the first of nine missions he established personally, including San Carlos Borromeo (Carmel), San Antonio, San Gabriel, San Luis Obispo, San Francisco de Asís, San Juan Capistrano, Santa Clara, and San Buenaventura. He died at Carmel in 1784.

Controversy and canonization

The mission system relied on coercive conversion, forced labour, and corporal punishment of Native Californians, and mission populations suffered devastating mortality from disease; Serra's legacy is thus bitterly contested by many indigenous people. Statues of Serra were toppled during the protests of 2020. Nevertheless, Pope Francis canonized him in Washington, D.C., in September 2015, the first canonization of a saint on United States soil, praising his defence of natives against some colonial abuses.
