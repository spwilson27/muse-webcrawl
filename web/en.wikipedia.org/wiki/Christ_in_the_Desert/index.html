Christ in the Desert

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christ in the Desert (Russian: Христос в пустыне) is an 1872 painting by Russian artist Ivan Kramskoi, depicting Jesus Christ during his forty-day fast and temptation in the desert as described in the Gospels of Matthew, Mark and Luke. It is one of the most celebrated Russian religious paintings of the nineteenth century and is held by the State Tretyakov Gallery in Moscow.

Description

The painting shows Christ seated alone on a grey stone at dawn in a rocky, barren landscape, wearing a dark red robe and a dark blue cloak. His hands are tightly clasped, his face is downcast and thoughtful, and the horizon divides the canvas so that the figure seems pressed between earth and an empty sky. Unlike traditional devotional images, there is no depiction of Satan or of the temptations themselves; Kramskoi instead concentrated on Christ's inner moral struggle and moment of choice.

History

Kramskoi, a leading figure of the Peredvizhniki (Wanderers), worked on the theme of Christ in the wilderness from the 1860s, making studies during travels that included a trip to Europe and the Crimea. The finished canvas was exhibited in 1872 and was purchased by Pavel Tretyakov for his collection. Contemporaries debated its psychological realism and humanized portrayal of Christ.
