Haltere

Halteres (singular haltere) are small knobbed organs found in flies (order Diptera), modified from the hindwings. They oscillate during flight and act as gyroscopic sense organs, detecting rotations of the body and helping the fly maintain stability and perform rapid manoeuvres.

Function

Sensory cells at the base of each haltere detect Coriolis forces as the beating haltere is deflected by body rotation. This information feeds into the flight motor system with very short latency. In Strepsiptera, it is the forewings that are modified into similar structures called halteres or pseudohalteres.

Evolution

Halteres evolved from wings about 200 million years ago and are a defining feature of Diptera ("two wings"). Their development is controlled by the Hox gene Ultrabithorax, a classic case in evolutionary developmental biology.
