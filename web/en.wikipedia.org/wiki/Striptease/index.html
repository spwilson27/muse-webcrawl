Striptease

A performer in the burlesque striptease tradition

A striptease is an erotic or exotic dance in which the performer gradually undresses, either partly or completely, in a seductive and sexually suggestive manner. The person who performs a striptease is commonly known as a "stripper" or exotic dancer. The emphasis is on the act of undressing — the tease — rather than on the state of being undressed; traditionally the performance ends before complete nudity or stops just short of it, prolonging anticipation.

History

Elements of striptease appear in ancient dance traditions, but the modern form developed in the late 19th century in Parisian venues such as the Moulin Rouge, and in American burlesque theatres. Famous performers of the golden age of burlesque included Gypsy Rose Lee, whose witty, teasing style made her a mainstream celebrity, and Sally Rand, known for her fan dance at the 1933 Chicago World's Fair. Theatrical striptease declined with the rise of explicitly sexual entertainment in the 1960s and 1970s, but a "neo-burlesque" revival began in the 1990s, treating the form as performance art with elaborate costumes and choreography.

Venues and styles

Striptease is performed in strip clubs, where styles range from stage shows to table dances and lap dances. Pole dancing, which developed in clubs in the 1980s, combines striptease with athletic acrobatics on a vertical pole and has since spread into fitness classes and international competitions. Male striptease acts, such as the Chippendales revue founded in 1979, follow a similar pattern of choreographed undressing for an audience.
