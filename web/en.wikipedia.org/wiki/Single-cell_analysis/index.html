Single-cell analysis

Single-cell analysis is the study of genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics and metabolomics at the level of individual cells. Because cells within a tissue differ from one another — even when genetically identical — bulk measurements that average across millions of cells can mask rare cell types, transient states and heterogeneity that are essential to understanding development, immunity and disease.

Methods

Isolation

Individual cells can be isolated by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS), microfluidic droplet systems that encapsulate cells in oil droplets with barcoded beads, micromanipulation, laser capture microdissection, and limiting dilution.

Genomics and transcriptomics

Single-cell DNA sequencing reveals copy-number variation and somatic mutations, while single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) measures gene expression in thousands to millions of cells. Key amplification approaches include Smart-seq, which sequences full-length transcripts, and droplet-based methods such as Drop-seq and commercial 10x Genomics platforms that use unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) and cell barcodes.

Proteomics and multi-omics

Mass cytometry (CyTOF) and antibody-based methods measure dozens of proteins per cell, and newer single-cell mass spectrometry approaches are extending proteome coverage. Multi-omic methods simultaneously profile, for example, chromatin accessibility (scATAC-seq) and gene expression in the same cell.

Applications

Single-cell analysis underpins large atlasing projects such as the Human Cell Atlas, helps resolve tumour heterogeneity and drug resistance, traces developmental lineages, and reveals rare immune cell states in infection and autoimmunity.

Challenges

Technical noise from low-input amplification, dropout of low-abundance transcripts, batch effects and the cost of analysing very large numbers of cells remain active areas of methodological development.
