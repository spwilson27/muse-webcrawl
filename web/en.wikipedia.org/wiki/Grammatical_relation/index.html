Grammatical relation

Subject and object are core grammatical relations

In linguistics, grammatical relations (also called grammatical functions or syntactic functions) are the roles that nominal elements play in relation to the predicate of a clause. The most central grammatical relations are the subject, the direct object and the indirect object. These relations are often expressed through case marking, word order, or verbal agreement.

Defining relations

Grammatical relations are traditionally defined by a cluster of syntactic behaviours: which element controls agreement, which can be promoted in the passive, which binds reflexive pronouns, and so on. Theories differ in how fundamental they take these relations to be. In Relational Grammar and Lexical Functional Grammar, grammatical relations are primitives of the theory, whereas in generative grammar they are typically derived from phrase structure positions.

Core and oblique relations

Beyond subjects and objects, languages distinguish oblique relations—such as instruments, locations and beneficiaries—which are typically expressed by adpositions rather than by core cases. The set of relations a language grammatically distinguishes relates closely to its alignment system, such as nominative–accusative or ergative–absolutive patterning.
