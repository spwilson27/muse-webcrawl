Branch: Fluid mechanics, aerodynamics

Center of pressure (fluid mechanics)

In fluid mechanics, the center of pressure is the point where the total sum of a pressure field acts on a body, causing a force to act through that point. The total force vector acting at the center of pressure is the value of the integrated vectorial pressure field; the resulting torque at the center of pressure equals the moment due to the pressure distribution, so no residual moment acts about that point.

Aeronautical significance

For an airfoil, the distribution of pressure over the chord determines the center of pressure, which generally moves forward as angle of attack increases. Because this movement complicates stability analysis, aeronautical engineers more often use the aerodynamic center — the point about which the pitching moment coefficient remains nearly constant — located close to the quarter-chord point for subsonic airfoils. The relationship between the center of pressure, the aerodynamic center, and the aircraft center of gravity governs longitudinal static stability.

General fluid mechanics

In hydrostatics, the center of pressure on a submerged plane surface is the point where the resultant hydrostatic force acts; it always lies below the centroid of the surface (at greater depth) because pressure increases with depth. The concept is used in dam design, naval architecture, and the analysis of submerged structures.
