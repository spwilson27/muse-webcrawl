Surface area

The surface area of a solid object is a measure of the total area that the surface of the object occupies. It is measured in square units, such as square metres (m²). For flat-faced solids (polyhedra) it is the sum of the areas of all the faces; for curved surfaces it is defined rigorously using the mathematics of calculus, as the integral of infinitesimal surface elements.

The surface area of a cube is the sum of the areas of its six faces

Common formulas

ShapeSurface area
Cube (side a)6a²
Sphere (radius r)4πr²
Closed cylinder (radius r, height h)2πr(r + h)
Cone (radius r, slant height l)πr(r + l)
Rectangular box (l × w × h)2(lw + lh + wh)

Surfaces of revolution

When a plane curve is revolved around an axis, the area of the resulting surface can be computed by integrating the circumference of infinitesimal bands along the curve — a technique associated with the theorems of Pappus. For general parametric surfaces, the area is the integral of the magnitude of the cross product of the partial derivatives over the parameter domain.

Applications

Surface area is central to chemistry and biology: reaction rates, catalysis, heat transfer and exchange processes all scale with surface area, while volume scales with the cube of linear size. This square–cube relationship explains why small animals lose heat faster, why cells remain small, and why finely divided materials (such as dust) can be explosively reactive. The surface-area-to-volume ratio is a key quantity in these fields.
