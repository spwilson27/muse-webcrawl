Peroxisome

Peroxisome
Diameter: about 0.1–1.0 µm
Membrane: single
Key enzymes: oxidases, catalase

A peroxisome is a membrane-bound organelle found in the cytoplasm of virtually all eukaryotic cells. Peroxisomes are oxidative organelles that play key roles in the oxidation of specific biomolecules, including the beta-oxidation of very long chain fatty acids, the metabolism of reactive oxygen species, and the synthesis of plasmalogens, a class of ether phospholipids important in brain and lung function.

Function

Peroxisomal oxidases generate hydrogen peroxide as a by-product, and the organelle contains catalase, which decomposes the peroxide into water and oxygen, protecting the cell. Peroxisomes also contribute to bile acid synthesis, amino acid catabolism and, in plants, photorespiration and the glyoxylate cycle (in specialised peroxisomes called glyoxysomes).

Biogenesis and disorders

Peroxisomes can grow and divide and can also form from the endoplasmic reticulum; their matrix proteins are imported post-translationally using targeting signals recognised by peroxins. Defects in peroxisome assembly cause peroxisome biogenesis disorders such as Zellweger syndrome, while defects in individual enzymes cause diseases such as X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy.
