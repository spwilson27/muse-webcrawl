The Lantingji Xu (蘭亭集序; "Preface to the Poems Composed at the Orchid Pavilion"), also rendered Lanting Xu, is a work of Chinese calligraphy and prose by the celebrated calligrapher Wang Xizhi (303–361), composed in 353 AD. Regarded as the most famous piece of Chinese calligraphy in the semi-cursive ("running") script, it is traditionally held to be the finest running-script work ever produced; Wang Xizhi himself was canonised in later tradition as the "Sage of Calligraphy".

Background

On the third day of the third month of 353, Wang Xizhi gathered forty-one friends and family members at the Orchid Pavilion near Shanyin (modern Shaoxing, Zhejiang) for the Spring Purification Festival. The guests played the drinking game of floating wine cups down a winding stream, composing poems as the cups passed; twenty-six of their poems were collected into an anthology, for which Wang, slightly drunk, dashed off the preface in running script. Sober the next day, he tried repeatedly to rewrite it — and by tradition could never equal the original.

Content and calligraphy

The text, 324 characters in 28 lines, begins as a joyful description of the gathering — "this day the sky is clear, the air fresh, and a gentle breeze blows" — and deepens into a meditation on the brevity of life and the inevitability of death, echoing but gently revising the pessimism of earlier thinkers. Calligraphically, the piece is admired for its unforced variation: characters repeat (such as 之, appearing twenty times) yet no two are written alike; corrections and deletions were left in the draft and copied faithfully, adding to its spontaneity.

Transmission

The original is lost. According to a famous story, the Tang emperor Taizong prized it so highly that he had it buried with him in the Zhaoling mausoleum; what survive are tracing copies and stone-engraved rubbings, of which the most esteemed is the "Shenlong version", traditionally attributed to the copyist Feng Chengsu, now in the Palace Museum, Beijing.
