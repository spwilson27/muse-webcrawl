Shia LaBeouf

Shia Saide LaBeouf (born June 11, 1986) is an American actor, performance artist, and filmmaker. He played Louis Stevens in the Disney Channel series Even Stevens, a role for which he received Young Artist Award nominations in 2001 and 2002 and won a Daytime Emmy Award in 2003. He made his film debut in The Christmas Path (1998). In 2004, he made his directorial debut with the short film Let's Love Hate and later directed a short film titled Maniac (2011).

Career

LaBeouf achieved mainstream success starring in the thriller Disturbia and the surf documentary-style drama of 2007, and rose to international prominence as Sam Witwicky, the main protagonist of Michael Bay's Transformers (2007) and its sequels Revenge of the Fallen (2009) and Dark of the Moon (2011). He also played Mutt Williams in Indiana Jones and the Kingdom of the Crystal Skull (2008). His other credits include Wall Street: Money Never Sleeps (2010), Lawless (2012), Nymphomaniac (2013), Fury (2014), American Honey (2016), Borg vs McEnroe (2017), and the critically acclaimed semi-autobiographical film Honey Boy (2019), which he wrote. He portrayed tennis player John McEnroe and received praise for The Peanut Butter Falcon (2019).

Performance art

Since 2014, LaBeouf has pursued a series of public performance art projects with artists Nastja Säde Rönkkö and Luke Turner, working under the collective name LaBeouf, Rönkkö & Turner. Projects have included #IAMSORRY (2014), #ALLMYMOVIES (2015), in which he livestreamed himself watching all of his films in reverse chronological order over three days, and HE WILL NOT DIVIDE US (2017), a participatory livestream installation launched on the day of Donald Trump's first inauguration. His 2014 paper-bag appearance at the Berlin premiere of Nymphomaniac, bearing the words "I am not famous anymore", became a widely reported cultural moment.

Personal life

LaBeouf was born in Los Angeles to a Jewish mother, a visual artist and jewelry designer, and a Cajun father, a Vietnam veteran who worked variously as a mime and a clown. He has spoken about a difficult childhood marked by his father's drug addiction and the family's poverty. He converted to Catholicism while researching his role as Padre Pio in the 2022 film of that name. His career has been accompanied by legal troubles and public controversy, including arrests for disorderly conduct and a 2020 lawsuit alleging abusive behaviour, which he has addressed publicly while discussing addiction and PTSD.
