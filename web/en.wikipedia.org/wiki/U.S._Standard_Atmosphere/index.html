U.S. Standard Atmosphere

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The U.S. Standard Atmosphere is a standard atmospheric model of how the pressure, temperature, density, and viscosity of the Earth's atmosphere change with altitude. First published in 1958 by the U.S. Committee on Extension to the Standard Atmosphere, it has been updated several times, most notably in 1962, 1966, and 1976.

Model

The 1976 version defines the atmosphere in layers up to 1,000 km, assuming a sea-level temperature of 288.15 K (15 °C), pressure of 101,325 Pa, and a defined temperature lapse rate in each layer — for example, −6.5 K/km through the troposphere and an isothermal stratosphere. The first 86 km of the model are essentially identical to the ICAO International Standard Atmosphere used in aviation.

Applications

The model is used as a reference in aerospace engineering, aircraft performance calculations, altimeter calibration, ballistics, and atmospheric science. It represents mid-latitude average conditions and does not describe actual weather at any given time or place.
