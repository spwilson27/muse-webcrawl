Molecular geometry

Molecular geometry is the three-dimensional arrangement of the atoms that constitute a molecule. It includes the general shape of the molecule as well as bond lengths, bond angles, torsional angles and other geometrical parameters that determine the positions of the atoms. Molecular geometry strongly influences a substance's reactivity, polarity, phase, colour, magnetism and biological activity.

Prediction: VSEPR theory

The valence-shell electron-pair repulsion (VSEPR) theory predicts geometry from the principle that electron pairs around a central atom — bonding pairs and lone pairs — arrange themselves to minimise repulsion. Lone pairs repel more strongly than bonding pairs, which explains the progressively compressed bond angles from methane (109.5°) through ammonia (≈107°) to water (≈104.5°).

Determination

Geometries are determined experimentally by X-ray and electron diffraction, microwave and vibrational spectroscopy, and NMR, and computationally by quantum-chemical methods such as density functional theory. Molecules are not rigid: bonds vibrate and rotate, so geometry is usually understood as an equilibrium or time-averaged structure; isomers — species with the same formula but different arrangements — can have dramatically different properties.
