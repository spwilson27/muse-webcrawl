Texas A&M University


Founded: 1876
Location: College Station, Texas
Type: Public land-grant research university
Students: Over 70,000 (among the largest in the US)

Texas A&M University is a public land-grant research university in College Station, Texas. Founded in 1876 as the Agricultural and Mechanical College of Texas under the Morrill Land-Grant Act, it was the first public institution of higher education in the state and is the flagship of the Texas A&M University System.

History

The college began as an all-male military school; membership in the Corps of Cadets was long required and remains a distinctive feature. The university opened fully to women and adopted its current name in 1963, and later became one of the few institutions designated a land-, sea- and space-grant university.

Campus and traditions

Texas A&M is known for traditions including the Aggie Ring, Midnight Yell and the 12th Man, as well as Kyle Field, one of the largest stadiums in the world. The George H.W. Bush Presidential Library sits on its west campus.
