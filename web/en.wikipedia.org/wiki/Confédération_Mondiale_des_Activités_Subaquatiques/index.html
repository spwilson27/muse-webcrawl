Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


CMAS is the world federation for underwater sports and diver training

The Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques (CMAS; English: World Underwater Activities Federation) is an international federation that represents underwater activities in underwater sport and underwater sciences, and oversees an international system of recreational snorkel and scuba diver training and recognition. It is also known by its English name, the World Underwater Federation, and its Spanish name, Confederación Mundial de Actividades Subacuáticas.

History

CMAS was founded in Monaco on 9–11 January 1959 by delegates from fifteen national underwater federations, following an initiative led by Jacques-Yves Cousteau, who became its first president. Luigi Ferraro of Italy was among its founders. CMAS succeeded the underwater sports committee of the International Confederation of Sport Fishing and took responsibility for international underwater sport.

Activities

CMAS organizes world championships in underwater sports including finswimming, spearfishing, underwater hockey, underwater rugby, underwater orienteering, apnoea (freediving), aquathlon, and underwater target shooting and visual sports. Its scientific committee promotes underwater research in fields such as archaeology, geology, and marine biology.

The CMAS diver training system uses a "star" grading from one-star diver to three-star diver (and instructor grades above), and CMAS standards have served as a reference for ISO standards on recreational diving. National member federations issue CMAS certification cards recognized worldwide.
