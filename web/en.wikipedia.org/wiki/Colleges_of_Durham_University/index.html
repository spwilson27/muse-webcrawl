Colleges of Durham University
Number of colleges: 17
Oldest: University College (1832)
Locations: Bailey, Hill, Queen's Campus origins

Durham University is a collegiate university in England, and its colleges are residential and social communities to which every student and many staff belong. Durham has seventeen colleges, ranging from the historic University College, founded in 1832 and housed partly in Durham Castle, to colleges founded in the twenty-first century.

Role of the colleges

Unlike the colleges of Oxford and Cambridge, Durham's colleges do not teach undergraduates—teaching is organised by the university's academic departments—but they provide accommodation, dining, pastoral care, sports, and social life. Every student is a member of a college throughout their degree, whether or not they live in college accommodation.

The colleges

The colleges include Castle (University College), Hatfield, St Chad's and St John's on the historic Bailey; the Hill colleges such as Grey, Collingwood, Trevelyan, Van Mildert, St Aidan's, St Mary's, and Stephenson; and newer colleges including Josephine Butler, South College, and John Snow College. Two colleges, St Chad's and St John's, are recognised rather than maintained colleges, with independent foundations.

Traditions

Colleges have strong individual identities, with their own colours, sports teams, formal dinners, and balls. Intercollegiate rivalry, particularly in rowing and other sports, is a prominent feature of student life.
