Geostatistics

Geostatistics is a branch of statistics focusing on spatial or spatiotemporal datasets. Originally developed to predict probability distributions of ore grades for mining operations, it is now applied in many fields, including petroleum geology, hydrogeology, hydrology, meteorology, oceanography, geochemistry, environmental science, forestry and epidemiology.

History

The field grew from the work of the South African mining engineer Danie Krige in the 1950s, formalised by the French mathematician Georges Matheron, who is generally credited with founding geostatistics as a discipline. Related methods were independently developed elsewhere, notably in Soviet meteorology.

Methods

The central tools of geostatistics are the variogram (or semivariogram), which quantifies how dissimilarity between measurements grows with distance, and kriging, a family of best linear unbiased interpolation methods that use the variogram to estimate values at unsampled locations together with an estimate of uncertainty. Other methods include indicator kriging for categorical variables, cokriging for multivariate data, and geostatistical simulation, which generates multiple equiprobable realisations of a spatial field for risk assessment.

Applications

Geostatistics underpins mineral resource and petroleum reservoir estimation, groundwater and contaminant plume modelling, weather and rainfall mapping, and precision agriculture. Related spatial-statistical methods are implemented in geographic information systems (GIS).
