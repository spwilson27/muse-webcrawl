Methyl red (2-(N,N-dimethyl-4-aminophenyl)azobenzenecarboxylic acid) is an azo dye used as a pH indicator in chemistry and microbiology. It is red in solutions below pH 4.4, passes through orange shades, and becomes yellow above pH 6.2, with a pKa of about 5.1. It is one of the indicators in the classic MR–VP test pair used in the IMViC series to identify enteric bacteria.

Methyl red test

In the methyl red test, bacteria are grown in a glucose broth (MR–VP broth). Organisms that ferment glucose by the mixed-acid pathway produce enough stable acid to drop the pH below 4.4, so addition of methyl red gives a red colour (a positive result, typical of Escherichia coli). Organisms that instead use the butylene glycol pathway, such as Enterobacter, neutralise the acids and give a yellow (negative) result. Methyl red is also sometimes used in acid–base titrations, though indicators with sharper endpoints in the relevant range are usually preferred.

Properties and safety

Methyl red is sparingly soluble in water and usually prepared as a solution in ethanol. Like many azo dyes it has been investigated for possible mutagenicity in laboratory tests and should be handled as a hazardous chemical; it is not used as a food colouring.
