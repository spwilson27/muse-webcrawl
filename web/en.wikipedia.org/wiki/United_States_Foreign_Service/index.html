U.S. Foreign Service
Established1924 (Rogers Act)
MembersAbout 13,000-14,000
Employer agenciesState, USAID, Commerce, Agriculture, USAGM

The United States Foreign Service is the primary personnel system for the diplomatic service of the United States federal government. Its members — Foreign Service officers and specialists — staff U.S. embassies, consulates, and diplomatic missions around the world, representing American interests and assisting U.S. citizens abroad.

The modern Foreign Service was created by the Rogers Act of 1924, which merged the diplomatic and consular services into a single merit-based corps. The Foreign Service Act of 1980 provides its current statutory basis.

Career tracks

Foreign Service officers serve in five career tracks (cones): consular, economic, management, political, and public diplomacy. Entry is through competitive examinations including the Foreign Service Officer Test and oral assessments. Officers spend most of their careers overseas, rotating between posts every two to three years, and the highest rank, Career Ambassador, is conferred on a small number of senior diplomats.
