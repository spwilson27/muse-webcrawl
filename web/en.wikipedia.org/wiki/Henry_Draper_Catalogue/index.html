The Henry Draper Catalogue (HD) is an astronomical star catalogue published between 1918 and 1924 by Harvard College Observatory, giving spectroscopic classifications for 225,300 stars, later extended to 359,083. Stars are still routinely identified by their HD numbers. The project was funded by Anna Palmer Draper in memory of her husband Henry Draper, a pioneer of astronomical photography and spectroscopy.

Classification

The catalogue's lasting legacy is the Harvard spectral classification — the O, B, A, F, G, K, M sequence with decimal subdivisions — refined by Annie Jump Cannon, who personally classified the great majority of the spectra. The scheme, developed by Cannon, Antonia Maury, and other women "computers" of the observatory under director Edward Charles Pickering, orders stars by the strength of their hydrogen absorption lines, later understood as fundamentally a temperature sequence.

Legacy

The HD catalogue made stellar spectroscopy a statistical science and underpins the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram's empirical basis. Its star designations persist in modern databases such as SIMBAD alongside Hipparcos and Gaia identifiers.
