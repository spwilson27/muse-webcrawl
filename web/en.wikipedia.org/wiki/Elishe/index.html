Elishe

Elishe (Armenian: Եղիշե, Yeghishe; c. 410 – 475), also known as Yeghishe Vardapet, was a prominent Armenian historian and theologian of the 5th century. He is best known as the author of the History of Vardan and the Armenian War, an account of the Armenian rebellion against Sasanian Persia in 450–451.

Life

Elishe was a student of Mesrop Mashtots, the inventor of the Armenian alphabet, and studied in Alexandria. Tradition holds that he served as a secretary or aide to Vardan Mamikonian, the leader of the Armenian revolt, and that he accompanied the Armenian army. After the Battle of Avarayr (451), he reportedly withdrew to a hermitage in the province of Mokkʿ.

Works

His History, written at the request of a priest named David Mamikonian, describes the causes of the Armenian–Persian war, the Battle of Avarayr, and its aftermath. It is regarded as a masterpiece of classical Armenian literature, though scholars debate whether it was written in the 5th century or somewhat later. Other works attributed to him include commentaries on biblical books and homilies, such as a commentary on Joshua and Judges and an explanation of the Lord's Prayer.
