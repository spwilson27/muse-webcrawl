Kartarpur Corridor

The Kartarpur Corridor is a visa-free border crossing and religious corridor connecting the Gurdwara Darbar Sahib in Kartarpur, Pakistan, with the shrine of Dera Baba Nanak in Punjab, India. It allows Sikh pilgrims from India to visit the Kartarpur gurdwara — where Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, spent his final years and died in 1539 — without a visa, travelling for the day through a dedicated 4.1-kilometre corridor.

Background

After the Partition of India in 1947, Kartarpur fell on the Pakistani side of the new border, barely a few kilometres from India; for decades Indian Sikhs could only view the shrine through binoculars from an elevated platform on the Indian side. Demands for a visa-free corridor were raised for many years before India and Pakistan agreed to build it in 2018, with foundation stones laid on both sides in November of that year.

Opening and operation

The corridor was inaugurated on 9 November 2019, days before the 550th birth anniversary of Guru Nanak, with ceremonies led by Pakistan's prime minister Imran Khan and India's prime minister Narendra Modi on their respective sides. Pilgrims register in advance, carry an electronic travel authorisation, and pay a US$20 service fee levied by Pakistan. The corridor closed in March 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic and reopened in November 2021; it was suspended again during periods of India–Pakistan tension and subsequently restored.
