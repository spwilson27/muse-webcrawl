Photographic filter

Filters screw or slide in front of the lens to modify the recorded image.

A photographic filter is a camera accessory consisting of an optical filter that can be inserted into the optical path. Filters are typically made of glass or resin in a threaded ring that screws onto the front of a photographic lens, or in square formats held in a bracket. They are used to modify the light reaching the film or sensor — changing colour balance, contrast, polarization, or exposure — and to protect the lens.

Types

Common types include ultraviolet (UV) filters, which block ultraviolet light and often serve as protective covers; skylight filters, which slightly reduce blue casts; polarizing filters, which cut reflections and darken skies by blocking polarized light; neutral-density (ND) filters, which uniformly reduce light to allow long exposures or wide apertures in bright conditions; graduated ND filters for balancing bright skies against darker landscapes; and colour filters used for contrast control in black-and-white photography or for colour correction with film.

Filter factors and use

Most filters absorb some light, requiring exposure compensation expressed as a filter factor. In digital photography many colour effects can be reproduced in software, but polarizing and ND effects cannot be fully simulated after capture, so physical filters remain standard tools in landscape and video work.
