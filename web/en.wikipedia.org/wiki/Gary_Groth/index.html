Gary Groth (born 1954) is an American comic book editor, publisher, and critic. He is a co-founder of Fantagraphics Books and of the magazine The Comics Journal, and has served as Fantagraphics' president and publisher. Groth is regarded as one of the most influential figures in alternative comics and comics journalism.

Early life and fandom

Groth was born in Buenos Aires, Argentina, where his father was working, and grew up in the Washington, D.C. area. As a teenager he was active in comics fandom, publishing the fanzine Fantastic Fanzine beginning in the late 1960s. In 1974 he co-founded a rock-music convention, and used its proceeds toward later publishing ventures.

Fantagraphics and The Comics Journal

In 1976 Groth, with Mike Catron, acquired The Nostalgia Journal and relaunched it as The Comics Journal, a magazine devoted to serious criticism of comics, combative editorials, and long, in-depth interviews with creators. In 1977 he co-founded Fantagraphics Books with Catron, and Kim Thompson joined the company in 1977, becoming Groth's longtime co-publisher until Thompson's death in 2013. The company relocated to Los Angeles in 1984 and then to Seattle in 1989.

Under Groth and Thompson, Fantagraphics became a leading publisher of alternative and literary comics, publishing creators such as Daniel Clowes, Peter Bagge, Gilbert and Jaime Hernandez (Love and Rockets), Chris Ware, and Joe Sacco, as well as prestige reprint collections of classic newspaper strips and comic books, including Peanuts, Krazy Kat, and the works of Carl Barks.

Interviews and criticism

Groth is known for his lengthy, probing interviews in The Comics Journal, which are considered some of the most substantive records of comics creators' lives and working methods. He has also been a polarizing figure for his outspoken criticism of mainstream comics industry practices, and Fantagraphics has been involved in several notable legal disputes, including a 1980s libel suit with writer Michael Fleisher, which Fantagraphics won, and the 2007 Harlan Ellison lawsuit, which was settled.
