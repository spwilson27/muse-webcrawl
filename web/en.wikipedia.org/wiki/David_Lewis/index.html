David Lewis

David Kellogg Lewis (28 September 1941 – 14 October 2001) was an American philosopher, one of the most influential analytic philosophers of the 20th century. He is best known for modal realism — the view that all possible worlds are as real as the actual world.


Born: 28 September 1941, Oberlin, Ohio, US
Died: 14 October 2001, Princeton, New Jersey
Institutions: UCLA, Princeton University

Philosophy

Educated at Swarthmore and Harvard (PhD under W. V. O. Quine), Lewis taught at UCLA and Princeton. In On the Plurality of Worlds (1986) he defended modal realism and counterpart theory. His Humean supervenience held that the world is a mosaic of local matters of fact, with laws, causation, chance, and minds supervening on it. He made major contributions to philosophy of language (Convention, 1969), counterfactuals, philosophy of mind, and metaphysics.

Legacy

Lewis's systematic, reductive style shaped generations of metaphysicians; his papers are collected in his Philosophical Papers.
