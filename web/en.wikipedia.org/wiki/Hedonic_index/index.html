Hedonic index

A hedonic index is a price index constructed using hedonic regression, a method that decomposes the price of a good into the implicit prices of its characteristics. Such indexes are used to measure pure price change while adjusting for changes in the quality and composition of the goods being priced — a central problem in producing inflation statistics for products whose features change rapidly.

Method

In a hedonic regression, the observed price of each product is regressed on its measurable characteristics (for a computer: processor speed, memory, storage; for a house: floor area, number of rooms, location). The estimated coefficients give the implicit market value of each characteristic. A price change can then be split into a part caused by a change in characteristics (quality change) and a residual pure price change, which is what the index aims to capture.

Applications

Statistical agencies such as the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics use hedonic adjustment in the consumer price index for items including computers, televisions, apparel and rental housing. Hedonic methods are also standard in constructing house price indexes, where the mix of homes sold varies from period to period, and in valuing environmental amenities such as clean air and quiet neighbourhoods, where prices of nearby housing reveal willingness to pay.

Criticism

Hedonic adjustment is controversial: critics argue that modelled quality improvements may overstate the value consumers place on new features and thereby understate measured inflation, while defenders respond that failing to adjust would ignore genuine improvements in what a given expenditure buys.
