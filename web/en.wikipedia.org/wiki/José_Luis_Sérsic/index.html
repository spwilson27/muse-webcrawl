José Luis Sérsic (6 May 1933 – 19 July 1993) was an Argentine astronomer best known for the Sérsic profile, the mathematical function bearing his name that describes how the surface brightness of a galaxy varies with radius.

Career

Sérsic spent his career at the Córdoba Observatory and the National University of Córdoba, where he directed the observatory from 1982 to 1983. Working largely with photographic plates of southern galaxies, he produced, with his wife Pastoriza and collaborators, the Atlas de Galaxias Australes (1968), a landmark survey of galaxy morphology in the southern sky.

The Sérsic law

In papers of the 1960s, notably his 1963 and 1968 works on the structure of galaxies, Sérsic proposed generalising de Vaucouleurs's r1/4 law with a free exponent: surface brightness falls as exp[−bn((r/re)1/n − 1)], where re is the half-light radius and n is the Sérsic index. The law unifies the exponential discs of spiral galaxies (n ≈ 1) and the concentrated profiles of ellipticals (n ≈ 4) in a single family. Long under-appreciated, the Sérsic profile became, from the 1990s onward, the standard tool for describing galaxy light distributions, bulges, dwarf galaxies and globular clusters in observational astronomy.

Legacy

The main-belt asteroid 2691 Sersic is named in his honour, and the Sérsic index remains a fundamental parameter in extragalactic astronomy.
