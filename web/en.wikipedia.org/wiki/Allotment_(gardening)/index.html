Allotment (gardening)

An allotment garden (British English), also called simply an allotment, is a parcel of land made available for individual, non-commercial gardening or for growing food plants. Allotments are typically formed by dividing a field into small plots — traditionally measured in rods, with a full plot of 10 poles (about 250 m²) — rented to local residents, often through a local council or allotment association.

History

The modern British allotment system grew out of 19th-century efforts to provide land to the labouring poor displaced by enclosure. The Allotments Acts of 1887–1908 obliged local authorities to provide land where demanded, and the system expanded enormously during both World Wars under campaigns such as "Dig for Victory". Comparable traditions exist across Europe: the German Kleingarten or Schrebergarten, Dutch volkstuin, and French jardins familiaux; North American community gardens are a close analogue.

Today

Although plot numbers declined in the later 20th century, interest revived strongly from the 2000s, with long waiting lists in many cities driven by concerns about food prices, sustainability and wellbeing. Allotment sites also have recognised value for urban biodiversity.
