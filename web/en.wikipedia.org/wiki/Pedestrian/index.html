A pedestrian is a person travelling on foot, whether walking or running. In some communities, those travelling on small wheels such as roller skates, skateboards, and scooters, as well as wheelchair users, are also included as pedestrians. The term derives from the Latin pedester ("on foot").

History

Walking is the oldest mode of human transport. Dedicated pedestrian infrastructure dates to antiquity: Pompeii had raised sidewalks and stepping-stone crossings. With the rise of the automobile in the 20th century, pedestrians were progressively pushed to the margins of road space, and the offence of "jaywalking" was created in the United States in the 1920s. Since the late 20th century, urban planning movements such as New Urbanism and Vision Zero have sought to restore pedestrian priority.

Safety

The World Health Organization estimates that pedestrians account for roughly 23% of the world's road traffic deaths—over 300,000 people per year—with the greatest burden in low- and middle-income countries. Risk is shaped by vehicle speed, road design, lighting, and alcohol use. Countermeasures include sidewalks, signalised crossings, traffic calming, and pedestrianised streets.

Health and environment

Walking is associated with reduced risk of cardiovascular disease, obesity and type 2 diabetes, and pedestrian-friendly design is promoted as a way to cut transport emissions and improve urban liveability.
