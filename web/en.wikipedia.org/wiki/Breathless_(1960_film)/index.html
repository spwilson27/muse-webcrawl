Breathless (French: À bout de souffle, lit. "at breath's end") is a 1960 French crime drama film written and directed by Jean-Luc Godard, the former Cahiers du Cinéma critic. It stars Jean-Paul Belmondo as Michel Poiccard, a small-time criminal modelled on Humphrey Bogart's screen persona, and Jean Seberg as Patricia Franchini, an American journalism student in Paris. When Michel impulsively kills a policeman, he hides out with Patricia while trying to persuade her to flee with him to Italy; she ultimately betrays him, and he is shot in the street.

Style and influence

The film is one of the earliest and most influential examples of the French New Wave. Godard shot it on location in Paris with a handheld camera, natural light and improvised dialogue, and famously used bold jump cuts to compress time — a technique that became a signature of modern cinema. Its mix of American film noir homage (to crime pictures in the tradition of The Maltese Falcon), pop-culture digressions and flaunted disregard for continuity rules signalled the auteur as cinema's creative centre. Breathless made Belmondo a star and is routinely ranked among the greatest films ever made; a Hollywood remake starring Richard Gere appeared in 1983.
