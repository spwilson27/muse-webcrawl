Flavivirus

Flavivirus is a genus of positive-strand RNA viruses in the family Flaviviridae. The genus includes the West Nile virus, dengue virus, tick-borne encephalitis virus, yellow fever virus, Zika virus and several other viruses which may cause encephalitis, as well as insect-specific flaviviruses (ISFs) such as cell fusing agent virus (CFAV), Palm Creek virus (PCV), and Parramatta River virus (PaRV).

Flaviviruses are named for the yellow fever virus; the word flavus means "yellow" in Latin, and yellow fever in turn is named because it causes jaundice. Most flaviviruses are arthropod-borne viruses (arboviruses), transmitted to vertebrate hosts by infected mosquitoes or ticks.

Structure and genome

Flavivirus particles are spherical and enveloped, about 40–60 nm in diameter. The genome is a single strand of positive-sense RNA of roughly 10,000–11,000 bases encoding a single polyprotein, which is cleaved into three structural proteins (capsid C, membrane prM/M, envelope E) and seven non-structural proteins (NS1, NS2A, NS2B, NS3, NS4A, NS4B, NS5). The envelope protein E mediates entry into host cells and is the main target of neutralising antibodies.

Diseases

Human flavivirus infections range from mild febrile illness to haemorrhagic fever (dengue, yellow fever), encephalitis (West Nile, Japanese encephalitis, tick-borne encephalitis) and congenital malformations (Zika syndrome). Vaccines exist for yellow fever, Japanese encephalitis, tick-borne encephalitis and dengue; most other flavivirus diseases are managed with supportive care.
