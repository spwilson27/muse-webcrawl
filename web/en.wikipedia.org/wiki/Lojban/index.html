Lojban (pronounced [ˈloʒban]) is a constructed, syntactically unambiguous human language based on predicate logic. It was developed by the Logical Language Group, which began the project in 1987, succeeding the earlier project Loglan. The name Lojban is a compound of the Lojban words loj (from logji, "logic") and ban (from bangu, "language").

Features

Lojban was designed to be culturally neutral, to have an exceptionally regular grammar, and to allow unambiguous parsing by computer. Its grammar is defined by a formal grammar (originally a YACC grammar, later a parsing expression grammar) that makes every well-formed sentence parse in exactly one way. The language has no irregular verbs, no exceptions to its spelling and phonology rules, and a one-to-one correspondence between letters and sounds.

Lojban's root vocabulary was drawn from the six most widely spoken languages at the time of its creation — Mandarin Chinese, English, Hindi, Spanish, Russian, and Arabic — weighted by number of speakers, in an effort at cultural neutrality.

Grammar

Content words in Lojban are divided into three classes: brivla (predicate words), cmavo (structure words, functioning much like function words and particles), and cmevla (name words). A central concept is the bridi, a claim that a relation holds between arguments, which occupy numbered places in the predicate's place structure. Attitudinals — cmavo expressing the speaker's attitude or emotion — are a distinctive feature, allowing fine distinctions of feeling to be marked grammatically.

Community

The Logical Language Group (LLG) oversees the language's development and published the reference grammar The Complete Lojban Language in 1997. A small international community of speakers exists, with learners' materials, translations, and original literature in the language.
