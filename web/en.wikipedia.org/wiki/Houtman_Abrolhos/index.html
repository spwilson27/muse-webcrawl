Houtman Abrolhos

The Houtman Abrolhos archipelago, Western Australia.

The Houtman Abrolhos (often informally called the Abrolhos Islands) is a chain of 122 islands and associated coral reefs in the Indian Ocean off the west coast of Australia, about 80 kilometres (50 mi) west of the port city of Geraldton in Western Australia. It is one of the world's most important seabird breeding sites and is the centre of Western Australia's western rock lobster fishery.

The archipelago is named after Frederick de Houtman, the Dutch explorer who came upon the islands in 1619 aboard the Dordrecht. The word "Abrolhos" is Portuguese, from abre os olhos — "open your eyes" — a warning to navigators of dangerous reefs.

Geography

The islands fall into three main groups, separated by channels: the Wallabi Group in the north, the Easter Group in the middle, and the Pelsaert Group in the south. The islands are low-lying, seldom more than a few metres above sea level, and are built on platforms of coral limestone. The surrounding reefs support diverse marine life, including extensive coral communities unusual at such a high latitude, sustained by the warm Leeuwin Current.

History

The Abrolhos are infamous in Australian maritime history as the site of several Dutch shipwrecks. The most notorious was the Batavia, wrecked on Morning Reef in the Wallabi Group in 1629. Survivors under Jeronimus Cornelisz mutinied and massacred more than 120 of their fellow castaways before rescue arrived. The Zeewijk was wrecked in the Pelsaert Group in 1727; its crew built a vessel, the Sloepie, from the wreckage and sailed to Java. In the 19th century the islands were mined for guano.

Ecology and economy

The islands host large breeding colonies of seabirds, including wedge-tailed shearwaters, lesser noddies, sooty terns and crested terns, and are a habitat for the tammar wallaby. The western rock lobster (Panulirus cygnus) fishery centred on the islands is one of Australia's most valuable single-species fisheries. A small seasonal population of fishers occupies camps on some islands.
