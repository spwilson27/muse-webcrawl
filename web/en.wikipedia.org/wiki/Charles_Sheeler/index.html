Charles Sheeler

Charles Rettew Sheeler Jr. (July 16, 1883 – May 7, 1965) was an American artist recognized as one of the founders of American modernism, a leading figure of Precisionism, and a prominent commercial photographer. His paintings and photographs of American industry — factories, machinery, and architecture rendered with photographic clarity — are among the defining images of the machine age.

Career

Sheeler studied at the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts under William Merritt Chase and travelled to Europe, where the 1913 Armory Show era's modernism reshaped his work. He established a successful commercial photography practice in New York, producing architectural and advertising photographs, and collaborated with Paul Strand on the avant-garde film Manhatta (1921).

River Rouge and mature work

In 1927 the Ford Motor Company commissioned Sheeler to photograph its River Rouge plant in Dearborn, Michigan. The resulting photographs, including the famous Criss-Crossed Conveyors, celebrated industrial power, and the plant became the subject of paintings such as American Landscape (1930) and Classic Landscape (1931). His later work also included the "Interior with Stove" photographs of his Bucks County home and the photomontage-like Power series for Fortune magazine (1940).

Legacy

Sheeler's fusion of photography and painting — he called photographs his "subconscious" studies for canvases — made him a central figure in American modern art; major retrospectives have been held at the Museum of Modern Art and the National Gallery of Art.
