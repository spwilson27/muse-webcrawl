Ginan

Ginans are devotional hymns of the Nizari Ismaili tradition

Ginans (Gujarati: જ્ઞાન, from Sanskrit jñāna, "knowledge") are devotional hymns or poems recited by Shia Ismaili Muslims, particularly the Nizari Ismailis of South Asia. Composed between roughly the 13th and 19th centuries by pirs — missionaries who spread the Ismaili faith in the Indian subcontinent — ginans form a sacred literature central to the religious life of the Khoja Ismaili community.

Content and form

The ginan corpus comprises several hundred compositions attributed to pirs such as Pir Sadardin, Pir Shams, Pir Nur Muhammad Shah and Pir Hasan Kabiruddin. Written in a mix of South Asian languages (Gujarati, Hindi, Sindhi, Punjabi, Multani and others) and traditionally recorded in the Khojki script, ginans express Ismaili doctrine through imagery drawn from Hindu, Sufi and local folk traditions — including narratives equating the Ismaili Imam with the awaited tenth avatar (Dashavatara) of Vishnu. They are sung to specific ragas in the jamatkhana (prayer hall), often with distinctive melodies for morning and evening recitation.

Transmission

For centuries ginans were transmitted orally and in Khojki manuscripts; in the 20th century they were printed in Gujarati and transliterated and translated for the global Ismaili diaspora. Scholars study them as a major source for the history of religious interaction in western India. Other Ismaili groups, such as the Imamshahi (Satpanth) tradition, have related but distinct hymnodies.
