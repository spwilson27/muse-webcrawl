Burke and Wills expedition

Date1860–1861
LeaderRobert O'Hara Burke
OutcomeFirst south–north crossing; leaders died

The Burke and Wills expedition (Victorian Exploring Expedition, 1860–61) was an Australian expedition of 19 men led by Robert O'Hara Burke, with William John Wills as surveyor, intended to cross the continent from Melbourne to the Gulf of Carpentaria (~3,250 km).

Expedition

Departing Royal Park, Melbourne, in August 1860 with camels imported for desert travel, the party split at Menindee and Cooper Creek. Burke, Wills, Charley Gray and John King dashed north, reaching tidal Gulf swamps in February 1861 — the first Europeans to cross the continent south to north.

Tragedy

On return, Gray died; the three reached Cooper Creek depot hours after the depot party left. Burke and Wills died of starvation and thiamine poisoning (beri-beri from improperly prepared nardoo) around June 1861 despite Yandruwandha Aboriginal help. King survived with Aboriginal care and was rescued by Alfred Howitt. The disaster prompted multiple relief expeditions and a royal commission; Burke and Wills received a state funeral in Melbourne.
