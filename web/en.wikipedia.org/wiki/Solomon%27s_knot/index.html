Solomon's knot


Solomon's knot — two interlaced closed loops, a link of two components with four crossings.

Solomon's knot (Latin: sigillum Salomonis, "Solomon's seal") is a decorative motif of two interlaced closed loops. In knot theory terms it is not a true knot but a two-component link with four crossings. The motif is found in ancient Roman mosaics, early Christian and medieval church decoration, Jewish and Islamic art, and many other traditions.

Symbolism

The interlaced loops, with no beginning and no end, have been read as symbols of eternity, unity, and the inseparability of opposites. The association with King Solomon reflects his legendary wisdom and his reputed power over spirits, sealed in tradition by his signet.

Occurrence

Solomon's knots appear in floor mosaics at sites such as Pompeii and in Roman villas, in Coptic and Byzantine textiles, in medieval manuscripts, and in carved stone crosses and church pavements across Europe. Elaborated multi-loop versions form part of the vocabulary of Celtic and Lombardic interlace.
