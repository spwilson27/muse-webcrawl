The Petersberg Agreement is an international agreement signed on 22 November 1949 between the government of West Germany under Chancellor Konrad Adenauer and the Allied High Commission. It substantially extended the rights of the young Federal Republic's government and is regarded as a first step toward West German sovereignty after the Second World War.

Terms

Negotiated at the Petersberg hotel, then the seat of the Allied High Commission near Bonn, the agreement allowed West Germany to join the Council of Europe as an associate member and to conduct its own consular and commercial relations with other countries. The dismantling of German industrial plants (the "dismantling" programme) was moderated, with a number of factories removed from the dismantling list. In return, the Adenauer government agreed to join the International Authority for the Ruhr, accept Allied controls on the Ruhr's coal and steel, commit to demilitarisation, and cooperate with occupation authorities.

Significance

The agreement was the first international treaty concluded by the Federal Republic, then only a few months old, and marked the beginning of its integration into Western institutions — a path that led to the European Coal and Steel Community in 1951 and to the restoration of sovereignty (with qualifications) under the Bonn–Paris conventions of 1952–1954. Within Germany the agreement drew criticism from the SPD opposition, which accused Adenauer of making concessions, but it also greatly eased Allied restrictions on the West German economy.
