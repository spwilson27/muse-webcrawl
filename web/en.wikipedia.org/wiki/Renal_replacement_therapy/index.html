Renal replacement therapy

Renal replacement therapy (RRT) is therapy that replaces the normal blood-filtering function of the kidneys. It is used when the kidneys are not working well, a condition known as renal failure, which includes acute kidney injury and chronic kidney disease. Renal replacement therapy includes dialysis (hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis), hemofiltration, and hemodiafiltration, which are various ways of filtration of blood with or without machines. Renal replacement therapy also includes kidney transplantation, which is the ultimate form of replacement in that the old kidney is replaced by a donor kidney.

Indications

RRT is typically initiated in acute kidney injury when complications such as hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, fluid overload, or uremic complications cannot be managed conservatively. In chronic kidney disease, it is begun when symptoms of kidney failure appear or the glomerular filtration rate falls very low.

Modalities

  • Hemodialysis — blood is circulated through an external filter (dialyzer) across a semipermeable membrane.
  • Peritoneal dialysis — the peritoneum in the abdomen is used as the filter, with dialysis fluid exchanged through a catheter.
  • Continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) — slower continuous techniques used mainly for hemodynamically unstable patients in intensive care.
  • Kidney transplantation — surgical placement of a functioning donor kidney.
