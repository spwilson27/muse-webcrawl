Astrophotographer

An astrophotographer is a photographer who specializes in astrophotography, the imaging of astronomical objects, celestial events, or areas of the night sky. Astrophotographers may be professional astronomers, who use specialized cameras attached to observatory telescopes, or amateur astronomers, who form a large and active community imaging deep-sky objects, planets and the Milky Way with consumer equipment.

Equipment and technique

Typical equipment includes a telescope or camera lens on an equatorial mount that tracks the sky's apparent rotation, a sensitive camera (increasingly a cooled CMOS or CCD sensor), and autoguiding to correct tracking errors during long exposures. Images are usually built by stacking many sub-exposures and calibrated with dark, flat and bias frames to reduce noise.

History

Henry Draper photographed the Orion Nebula in 1880, one of the first deep-sky photographs. In the digital era, falling sensor prices and image-processing software have let amateurs produce images rivalling professional work of a generation earlier. Annual competitions such as Astronomy Photographer of the Year showcase the field.
