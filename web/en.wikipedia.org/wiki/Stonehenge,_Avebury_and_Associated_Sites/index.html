Stonehenge, Avebury and Associated Sites is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Wiltshire, England, comprising two separate areas of chalk downland some 30 km apart, each dense with prehistoric monuments built between about 3000 and 1600 BC. It was inscribed in 1986 for the outstanding Neolithic and Bronze Age ceremonial landscapes it preserves.

The Stonehenge area

Centred on Stonehenge itself, this area includes the Avenue, the Cursus, Durrington Walls, Woodhenge and hundreds of Bronze Age barrows, as well as the causewayed enclosure at Robin Hood's Ball, among the earliest Neolithic monuments in Britain.

The Avebury area

Around the village of Avebury stands the world's largest megalithic stone circle, Avebury henge, together with West Kennet Long Barrow, Silbury Hill (the largest artificial prehistoric mound in Europe), the Sanctuary and the West Kennet Avenue.

Significance

The two landscapes demonstrate the evolution of monumental ceremonial architecture over two millennia and have inspired antiquarians and archaeologists since John Aubrey and William Stukeley surveyed them in the 17th and 18th centuries. The site is managed under a joint management plan coordinated with English Heritage, the National Trust and local authorities.
