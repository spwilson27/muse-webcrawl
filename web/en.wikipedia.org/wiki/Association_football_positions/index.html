Association football positions

In the sport of association football (soccer), each of the two teams has eleven players on the field at a time. Players are assigned to positions that define their primary area of responsibility. Apart from the goalkeeper, who is governed by special rules (notably the handling of the ball), outfield positions are not fixed by the Laws of the Game—they are conventions shaped by tactics and tradition.

Outfield players are broadly grouped into defenders, midfielders, and forwards, and a team's arrangement of these players is described by its formation, written as a sequence of numbers (e.g. 4–4–2 means four defenders, four midfielders, and two forwards, in addition to the goalkeeper).

Goalkeeper

The goalkeeper (GK) defends the team's goal and is the only player permitted to handle the ball within their own penalty area. Goalkeepers wear a shirt colour distinct from both teams and the officials.

History

In the game's early decades, formations were heavily attacking: the 2–3–5 "pyramid" was standard by the 1880s. The 1925 change to the offside law shifted the balance toward the 3–2–5 "WM" formation, and over the 20th century defensive numbers grew, producing the 4–4–2, 4–3–3, and 3–5–2 systems common today. Shirt numbers, originally assigned by position in the 1928 scheme, became personalised in most leagues in the 1990s, though traditional numbering still carries symbolic meaning.
