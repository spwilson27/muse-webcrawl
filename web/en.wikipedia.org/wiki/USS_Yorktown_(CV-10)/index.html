USS Yorktown (CV/CVA/CVS-10) is an Essex-class aircraft carrier built for the United States Navy during World War II. Initially to have been named Bon Homme Richard, she was renamed Yorktown while under construction to commemorate USS Yorktown (CV-5), sunk at the Battle of Midway in June 1942.

World War II service

Commissioned in April 1943, Yorktown joined the Pacific Fleet and took part in the island-hopping campaign, including raids on Marcus and Wake Islands, the Gilbert and Marshall Islands operations, the Battle of the Philippine Sea, the Battle of Leyte Gulf, and strikes on Iwo Jima and Okinawa. Nicknamed "The Fighting Lady" (also the title of a 1944 documentary film about her crew), she earned 11 battle stars and the Presidential Unit Citation.

Later service

Modernised in the 1950s with an angled flight deck, Yorktown served first as an attack carrier and then as an anti-submarine warfare carrier. During the Vietnam War she made several deployments to the Tonkin Gulf. In December 1968 she served as the recovery ship for Apollo 8, the first crewed spacecraft to orbit the Moon, recovering its crew and capsule after splashdown in the Pacific.

Museum ship

Decommissioned in 1970, Yorktown was towed to Charleston Harbor in 1975 and opened as the centerpiece of the Patriots Point Naval & Maritime Museum in Mount Pleasant, South Carolina. She was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986.
