Peer review is the evaluation of work by one or more people with similar competences to its producers. In academic publishing, editors send submitted manuscripts to outside experts ('referees') who recommend acceptance, revision or rejection and report errors and weaknesses; it is the main quality-control mechanism of scholarly journals.

History and criticism

Elements of peer review date to early scientific societies, but systematic external refereeing became standard only in the 20th century. It is criticised for slowness, bias, conservatism and failure to catch fraud, and reforms include open (signed) review, preprints and post-publication review. Related vetting ideas appear in open access publishing too.
