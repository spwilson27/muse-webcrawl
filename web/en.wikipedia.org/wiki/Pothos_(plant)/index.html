Pothos is a common name for several trailing aroid plants, most often Epipremnum aureum, a species in the family Araceae native to Mo'orea in the Society Islands. It is one of the most popular houseplants in the world, valued for its hardiness and glossy, heart-shaped leaves.

Description

In the wild, pothos climbs tree trunks by means of aerial roots, and mature leaves on tall plants can become large and fenestrated. Indoors it is usually grown as a trailing or climbing plant with smaller, entire leaves, often variegated with yellow or white, as in the cultivar 'Golden Pothos'.

Cultivation

Pothos tolerates low light and irregular watering, making it a forgiving indoor plant. It is readily propagated from stem cuttings, which root easily in water or moist soil. It rarely flowers in cultivation; the species was long thought to be incapable of flowering indoors until a flowering specimen was reported in 1964.

Names and confusion

The plant has been classified at various times in the genera Pothos, Rhaphidophora and Scindapsus before being placed in Epipremnum. Other species of the true genus Pothos are climbing aroids of tropical Asia and the Pacific.

Toxicity

Like many aroids, pothos contains calcium oxalate crystals and is toxic if eaten by pets or humans, causing irritation of the mouth and digestive tract.
