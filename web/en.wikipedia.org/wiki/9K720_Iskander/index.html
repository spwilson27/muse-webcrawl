The 9K720 Iskander (NATO reporting name SS-26 Stone) is a Russian mobile short-range ballistic missile system developed by the Kolomna Machine Design Bureau to replace the OTR-21 Tochka. Named after Alexander the Great ("Iskander" in Arabic and Turkic usage), it entered service with the Russian Armed Forces in 2006.

Design

The transporter-erector-launcher carries two single-stage, solid-fuel quasi-ballistic missiles that maneuver in flight and fly a depressed, non-parabolic trajectory, complicating interception. Missiles can carry conventional warheads of several types — cluster, thermobaric, and bunker-penetrating — and the system is assessed as nuclear-capable. Guidance combines inertial navigation, satellite updates, and terminal optical or radar seekers for accuracy of a few metres.

Variants and use

Variants include the Iskander-M for Russian forces, the export Iskander-E, and the Iskander-K, which launches cruise missiles. The system was used by Russia in the 2008 Russo-Georgian War, deployed to Kaliningrad Oblast, and employed extensively during the Russian invasion of Ukraine beginning in 2022.
