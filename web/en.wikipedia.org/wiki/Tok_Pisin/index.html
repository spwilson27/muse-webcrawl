Tok Pisin (Tok Pisin: [ˌtok piˈsin]; often referred to in English by its earlier name, Pidgin) is a creole language spoken throughout Papua New Guinea. It is an official language of Papua New Guinea and the most widely used language in the country. In parts of the provinces of Western, Gulf, Central, Oro, and Milne Bay, the use of Tok Pisin has a shorter history and is less universal, especially among older people.

Origins

Tok Pisin developed as a Pacific pidgin in the 19th century, when workers from New Guinea and other Pacific islands were recruited (sometimes forcibly) to plantations in Queensland and Samoa. On returning home, labourers brought with them a plantation pidgin based largely on English vocabulary with Melanesian grammar. During the German colonial period in New Guinea (1884–1914), the language stabilized and expanded; it is sometimes called by its German-era name, Unserdeutsch-era pidgin, though that term applies to a distinct Rabaul variety. During the Second World War, contact with Australian and American troops added vocabulary and spread the language widely.

Features

Although its vocabulary is drawn mainly from English, Tok Pisin has a distinctly Melanesian grammar shaped by Austronesian and Papuan substrate languages. Characteristic features include the predicate marker i, plural pronouns distinguishing inclusive and exclusive "we" (yumi vs mipela), a dual and trial number in pronouns, the transitive suffix -im (as in lukim, "to see"), and reduplication for emphasis or plurality. Famous words include wantok ("one who speaks the same language"; a close social bond), and haus sik (hospital).

Status and use

Tok Pisin is one of three official languages of Papua New Guinea, alongside English and Hiri Motu. It is the language of parliamentary debate (translated), of national media such as the Wantok newspaper, and increasingly a first language for urban children, which marks its transition from pidgin to creole. A Tok Pisin Bible and a Wikipedia edition exist. Its name derives from the English words "talk pidgin".
