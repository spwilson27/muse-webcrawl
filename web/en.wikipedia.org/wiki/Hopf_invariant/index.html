Hopf invariant

In mathematics, and more specifically in algebraic topology, the Hopf invariant is a homotopy invariant of certain maps between spheres. It was introduced by Heinz Hopf in 1931 in his study of the map now known as the Hopf fibration.

Definition

Let f : S2n−1 → Sn be a continuous map. Consider the mapping cone of f, obtained by attaching a 2n-cell to Sn along f. The cohomology ring of this space has generators in degrees n and 2n, and the square of the degree-n generator is a certain integer multiple of the top generator. That integer is the Hopf invariant of f.

Equivalently, the Hopf invariant can be described using the linking number of the preimages of two distinct regular values of f. For the classical Hopf map S3 → S2, each preimage is a circle, and any two such circles are linked exactly once in S3, so the Hopf invariant is ±1.

Properties

The Hopf invariant is a homomorphism from the homotopy group π2n−1(Sn) to the integers. Its most famous application is the theorem of J. Frank Adams, proved in 1960 using secondary cohomology operations, that maps with Hopf invariant one exist only for n = 1, 2, 4, and 8. This result is deeply connected with the existence of normed division algebras — the real numbers, complex numbers, quaternions, and octonions — in exactly those dimensions.

The maps of Hopf invariant one give rise to fibrations S0 → S1 → S1, S1 → S3 → S2, S3 → S7 → S4, and S7 → S15 → S8, known collectively as the Hopf fibrations.
