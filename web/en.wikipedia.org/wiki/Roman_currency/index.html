Roman currency

Roman currency for most of Roman history consisted of gold, silver, bronze, orichalcum, and copper coins. The system was revised repeatedly, from the heavy cast bronze aes grave of the early Republic to the gold solidus of late antiquity, and coins served throughout as instruments of political propaganda bearing portraits and slogans.

Republican coinage

Rome's standard silver coin, the denarius, was introduced in 211 BC during the Second Punic War and was initially tariffed at 10 asses, later 16. Gold coinage was rare in the Republic. The silver quinarius and the bronze denominations completed the system.

Imperial coinage

Augustus reformed the coinage around 23 BC: the gold aureus was struck at 40 to the pound, the silver denarius at 84, and orichalcum sestertii and dupondii and copper asses provided smaller change, with four sestertii to the denarius. Successive debasements eroded the denarius's silver content, and inflation in the 3rd century led to Diocletian's reforms and eventually to Constantine's solidus, a stable pure-gold coin that endured in the Byzantine Empire for centuries.
