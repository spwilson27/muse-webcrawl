Treblecross

Treblecross is a one-dimensional positional game played on a strip of squares. Both players place an X on an empty square on their turn; no X may be placed adjacent to an existing X. The player who completes a row of three consecutive X's wins.

Analysis

Treblecross is a classic example in combinatorial game theory. Because a move forbids the neighbouring squares, each placement splits the strip into independent smaller strips, allowing the game to be analysed with Sprague–Grundy theory by treating the resulting octal game. The impartial game equivalent is the octal .007, one of the classic octal games studied by Guy and Smith.

Misère and misère-like subtleties, and the pattern of P-positions (previous-player wins) for strips of increasing length, have been computed extensively; for sufficiently long boards the first player has a winning strategy in most cases, with known exceptions for particular lengths.
