Order of magnitude

An order of magnitude is a class in a system of classification determined by size, each class being a fixed multiple of the previous one. In the most common scheme the ratio is ten, so numbers differing by one order of magnitude differ by a factor of about 10; two numbers differ by orders of magnitude when one is roughly a power of ten times the other.

Use

Order-of-magnitude estimates are used throughout science and engineering to compare quantities spanning enormous ranges — from the mass of an electron to the mass of the observable universe — and to make rough "back-of-the-envelope" calculations, such as Fermi estimates, before precise data are available.

Scales

Reference lists of orders of magnitude organise quantities such as length, mass, time, energy and numbers by powers of ten, giving an intuitive grasp of scales that ordinary experience cannot supply directly.
