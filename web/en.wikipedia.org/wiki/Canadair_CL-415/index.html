Canadair CL-415

Canadair CL-415
Role: amphibious water bomber
Manufacturer: Canadair / Bombardier / De Havilland Canada
First flight: 6 December 1993
Water capacity: 6,137 litres (1,350 imp gal)

The Canadair CL-415 (later Bombardier 415, and revived as the De Havilland Canada DHC-515) is a Canadian amphibious aircraft purpose-built for aerial firefighting. A turboprop development of the piston-engined CL-215, it entered service in 1994 and is instantly recognisable in the yellow-and-red colours of the many fire services that operate it around the Mediterranean and in North America.

Operation

The CL-415 refills its tanks by skimming a lake or sea surface at about 80 knots for roughly 12 seconds, scooping over 6,000 litres through retractable probes without landing. Its two Pratt & Whitney Canada PW123 turboprops, high wing, and four-compartment tank system allow precise drops on wildfires, and it can also serve in search-and-rescue and utility roles.

Production

Ninety-five CL-415s were built before production ended in 2015. Viking Air and De Havilland Canada subsequently acquired the program, upgrading existing aircraft to CL-415EAF standard and launching the new-build DHC-515 Firefighter, with production restarted in Canada.
