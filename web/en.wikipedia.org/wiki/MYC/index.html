MYC (also called c-Myc) is a human gene that encodes a transcription factor of the basic helix-loop-helix leucine zipper family. It is a proto-oncogene: in normal cells it regulates the expression of a large fraction of the genome involved in cell growth, metabolism, ribosome production and the cell cycle, but when its expression becomes deregulated it drives uncontrolled proliferation and is implicated in a majority of human cancers.

Discovery and function

MYC was identified through its viral counterpart v-myc in an avian retrovirus and through the chromosomal translocation t(8;14) characteristic of Burkitt lymphoma, which places MYC under the control of the immunoglobulin heavy-chain enhancer. The MYC protein heterodimerises with MAX, binds E-box sequences in target-gene promoters, and acts both as a general amplifier of actively transcribed genes and as a regulator of specific programmes including apoptosis and differentiation.

Role in cancer

MYC is amplified, translocated or otherwise overexpressed in a wide range of tumours, including Burkitt and other aggressive B-cell lymphomas (where rearrangements also define "double-hit" lymphomas together with BCL2), breast, lung, cervical and colorectal cancers. Together with Oct4, Sox2 and Klf4, ectopic MYC expression is one of the four Yamanaka factors capable of reprogramming adult cells into induced pluripotent stem cells.

Therapeutic interest

Because the MYC protein is largely intrinsically disordered, it was long considered "undruggable", but strategies such as inhibiting the MYC–MAX interaction, the dominant-negative mini-protein Omomyc, and targeting transcriptional machinery that MYC-driven tumours depend on have entered experimental and early clinical study.
