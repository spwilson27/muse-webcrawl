The Miami metropolitan area, officially the Miami–Fort Lauderdale–West Palm Beach metropolitan statistical area, is the urban region centred on Miami in South Florida, United States. It comprises Miami-Dade, Broward and Palm Beach counties and is Florida's largest metropolitan area and one of the most populous in the United States.

Geography

The metropolis forms a nearly continuous urban corridor along Florida's Atlantic coast, squeezed between the Everglades to the west and the ocean to the east, stretching roughly 100 miles from Palm Beach County in the north to metropolitan Miami in the south. Its principal cities are Miami, Fort Lauderdale, West Palm Beach, Hialeah, Boca Raton, Sunrise and Hollywood.

Demographics and economy

South Florida has one of the largest Hispanic and Latin American populations in the United States and large Caribbean communities, giving the region a bilingual (English–Spanish) character and earning Miami the nickname "Capital of Latin America." The economy rests on international trade and finance, tourism, real estate, ports (PortMiami and Port Everglades) and Miami International Airport, a major cargo and passenger gateway to Latin America and the Caribbean.

Climate and risks

The area has a tropical climate and is highly exposed to hurricanes, storm surge and sea-level rise, with extensive research and adaptation programmes addressing flooding of low-lying coastal land.
