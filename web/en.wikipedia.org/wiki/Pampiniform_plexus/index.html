Pampiniform plexus

The pampiniform plexus (from Latin pampinus, a tendril, and forma, form) is a network of many small veins in the male spermatic cord, and a corresponding network in the female broad ligament. It is formed by the union of veins emerging from the testis and the epididymis, and constitutes the greater part of the bulk of the spermatic cord.

Function

In males, the plexus surrounds the testicular artery and acts as a countercurrent heat exchanger: cool venous blood returning from the testis absorbs heat from arterial blood entering it, helping maintain the testis a few degrees below core body temperature, which is necessary for normal spermatogenesis.

Clinical significance

Dilation and tortuosity of the plexus produces a varicocele, often described as feeling like a "bag of worms", which is common on the left side because the left testicular vein drains into the left renal vein. Varicoceles are associated with reduced fertility and may be treated by embolisation or surgical ligation. In females, the plexus drains the ovary via the ovarian veins.
