Saturday Night Live (SNL) is an American late-night live sketch comedy and variety television series created by Lorne Michaels and developed by Dick Ebersol. It premiered on NBC on 11 October 1975 under the title NBC's Saturday Night. Broadcast live from Studio 8H in New York, each episode is hosted by a celebrity guest who delivers an opening monologue and performs in sketches with the ensemble cast, interspersed with performances by a musical guest.

History

Michaels assembled the original "Not Ready for Prime Time Players" — including Dan Aykroyd, John Belushi, Chevy Chase, Jane Curtin, Garrett Morris, Laraine Newman and Gilda Radner. Chase's "Weekend Update" and characters such as the Blues Brothers and the Coneheads became cultural phenomena. Michaels left in 1980, the show declined under Jean Doumanian and then Ebersol, and Michaels returned in 1985, rebuilding the cast repeatedly over subsequent decades.

The show launched or propelled the careers of Eddie Murphy, Bill Murray, Tina Fey, Amy Poehler, Will Ferrell, Kristen Wiig, Kate McKinnon and many others. Its 50th season in 2024–25 was marked by major anniversary specials.

Format

A typical episode opens with a cold-open sketch ending with the line "Live from New York, it's Saturday Night!", followed by credits and the host's monologue, commercial parodies, recurring characters, digital shorts, "Weekend Update" — a satirical news segment — and two musical performances.

Production and influence

The week is structured around a Wednesday read-through, writing through Tuesday night, and rehearsals leading to a Saturday dress rehearsal before a live audience. SNL has won dozens of Emmy Awards and is widely credited with shaping American political satire, from Chevy Chase's Gerald Ford to Tina Fey's Sarah Palin and Alec Baldwin's Donald Trump.
