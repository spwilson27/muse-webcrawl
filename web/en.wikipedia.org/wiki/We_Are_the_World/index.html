"We Are the World" is a charity single recorded by the supergroup USA for Africa in 1985. It was written by Michael Jackson and Lionel Richie and produced by Quincy Jones, and was recorded to raise money for famine relief in Africa, particularly Ethiopia, during the 1983–1985 famine. Inspired by Band Aid's "Do They Know It's Christmas?", the project was initiated by Harry Belafonte and fundraiser Ken Kragen.

Recording

The song was recorded on the night of January 28, 1985, at A&M Recording Studios in Los Angeles, immediately following the American Music Awards, when most of the performers were already in town. More than 45 of America's top artists took part, including Stevie Wonder, Bruce Springsteen, Bob Dylan, Ray Charles, Diana Ross, Tina Turner, Billy Joel, Paul Simon, Kenny Rogers, Dionne Warwick, Willie Nelson, Cyndi Lauper, and Huey Lewis. A famous sign posted at the studio read "Check your egos at the door."

Release and impact

Released on March 7, 1985, the single became the fastest-selling American pop single in history at the time, eventually selling more than 20 million copies worldwide. The project, including the album, merchandise, and a simultaneous radio broadcast, raised more than $80 million (equivalent to well over $200 million today) for humanitarian aid in Africa and the United States. It won four Grammy Awards in 1986, including Record of the Year and Song of the Year.

Legacy

"We Are the World" defined the celebrity charity single format and was re-recorded in 2010 as "We Are the World 25 for Haiti" following the Haiti earthquake, with a new generation of artists. The 1985 recording session was documented in the 2024 film The Greatest Night in Pop.
