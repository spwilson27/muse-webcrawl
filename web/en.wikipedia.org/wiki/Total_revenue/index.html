Total revenue

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Total revenue is the total receipts a seller obtains from selling a good or service. It is calculated by multiplying the price of the good by the quantity sold:

TR = P × Q

where TR is total revenue, P is the price of the product, and Q is the quantity of products sold. In more formal terms, total revenue is the sum over all units sold of the price received for each unit.

Relationship with elasticity

Total revenue changes in response to price changes in a way governed by the price elasticity of demand. When demand is elastic, a price decrease increases total revenue, because the quantity demanded rises proportionally more than the price falls. When demand is inelastic, a price decrease reduces total revenue. Total revenue is maximised at the point where demand is unit elastic and marginal revenue is zero.

Marginal and average revenue

Marginal revenue is the additional revenue gained from selling one additional unit of output; in competitive markets it equals the price, while a monopolist faces a marginal revenue curve below the demand curve. Average revenue is total revenue divided by quantity sold and is equal to the price per unit in most cases.

Perfect competition

In a perfectly competitive market, firms are price takers: the price is constant regardless of the quantity the individual firm sells, so total revenue rises proportionally with output and marginal revenue equals average revenue equals price.
