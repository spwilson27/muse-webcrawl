Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode

The Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode (FHCM) is the governing body of the French fashion industry. Based in Paris, it brings together fashion houses that meet its standards, coordinates the official calendar of Paris Fashion Week, and oversees the legally protected designation haute couture — deciding each season which houses are entitled to use the term.

History

The federation traces its origins to the Chambre Syndicale de la Couture, founded in 1868 by Charles Frederick Worth, the English-born couturier credited with inventing haute couture as a modern business. In 1945 the term haute couture was given legal protection in France, with strict criteria administered by the Chambre Syndicale de la Haute Couture, including making made-to-order garments for private clients, maintaining a Paris atelier with a minimum number of full-time staff, and presenting collections of a minimum number of original designs twice a year. The present federation was formed in 1973, uniting the couture, ready-to-wear (prêt-à-porter), and menswear chambers, and adopted its current name in 2017.

Structure and role

The FHCM has roughly one hundred member brands across three chambers: haute couture, womenswear, and menswear. A small number of houses hold full haute couture membership; foreign houses may be invited as "correspondent members", and a rotating cast of guest members presents collections each season. The federation sets the official Paris Fashion Week schedules for womenswear, menswear, and haute couture, promotes French fashion internationally, supports emerging designers, and increasingly co-ordinates industry action on sustainability and digital showcases.
