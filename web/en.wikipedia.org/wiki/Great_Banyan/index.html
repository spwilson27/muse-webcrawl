The Great Banyan is a banyan tree (Ficus benghalensis) located in the Acharya Jagadish Chandra Bose Indian Botanic Garden in Howrah, near Kolkata, India. The great banyan tree draws more visitors to the garden than its own collection of plants from five continents. Its canopy covers roughly 1.9 hectares (about 4.7 acres), with a circumference of nearly 486 metres, making it among the largest trees in the world by canopy area — from a distance it resembles a whole forest.

History

The tree is over 250 years old and was already noted in 19th-century travel accounts. It survived the great cyclones of 1864 and 1867, though some of its main branches were damaged. Its original main trunk, which had become diseased after fungal attack, was removed in 1925; the tree lives on through its thousands of aerial prop roots — more than 3,600 of them had reached the ground in recent surveys — which thicken into accessory trunks and support the ever-spreading canopy. A road around its circumference was built for visitors, but the tree continues to spread beyond it.

Biology

Botanically, the Great Banyan is a single genetic individual: a banyan begins life as an epiphyte and sends aerial roots downward that eventually become woody supporting trunks. The tree produces small red figs eaten by birds, bats, and monkeys, and its canopy shelters a rich community of animals. It remains the emblem and best-known specimen of the botanic garden, founded in 1787.
