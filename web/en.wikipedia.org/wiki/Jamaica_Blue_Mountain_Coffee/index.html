Jamaica Blue Mountain Coffee

Jamaica Blue Mountain Coffee is a classification of coffee grown in the Blue Mountains of Jamaica, one of the most expensive and sought-after coffees in the world. The coffee is noted for its mild flavour, sweetness, and lack of bitterness, qualities attributed to the region's cool, misty climate, high rainfall, rich volcanic soil, and elevations between roughly 900 and 1,700 metres (3,000–5,500 feet).

History

Coffee was introduced to Jamaica in 1728, when Governor Sir Nicholas Lawes received plants from Martinique. Cultivation spread from the foothills into the Blue Mountains, and by the early 19th century Jamaican coffee had gained an international reputation. After a long decline in quality and prices following emancipation and the loss of preferential markets, the industry was reorganised in the 20th century with the establishment of the Coffee Industry Board of Jamaica in 1950.

Certification and grades

"Jamaica Blue Mountain" is a protected certification mark. Only coffee grown within the legally defined Blue Mountain area — in the parishes of Saint Andrew, Saint Thomas, Portland, and Saint Mary, at the prescribed elevations — and inspected and certified by the Jamaica Agricultural Commodities Regulatory Authority may bear the name. Coffee grown at lower elevations in Jamaica is classified as Jamaica High Mountain or Jamaica Prime. Much of the certified crop is exported, with Japan historically purchasing the great majority of production. Estates such as Mavis Bank and Clifton Mount are among the best known. Beans are traditionally shipped in wooden barrels as well as bags.
