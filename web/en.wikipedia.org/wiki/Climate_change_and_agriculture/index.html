Climate change and agriculture

Climate change and agriculture
Shared concerns: Food security, crop yields, livestock
Agriculture's share of emissions: roughly 10–14% directly (more incl. land use)
Key bodies: IPCC, FAO

Climate change and agriculture are interrelated processes, both of which take place on a global scale. Climate change affects agriculture in a number of ways, including through changes in average temperatures, rainfall patterns, and climate extremes; while agriculture contributes to climate change through greenhouse gas emissions and land-use change. The IPCC assesses that climate change has already slowed the growth of global agricultural productivity.

Effects of climate change on agriculture

Rising temperatures and heatwaves stress crops and livestock, shift growing zones poleward, and alter the ranges of pests and diseases. Changes in precipitation and the loss of glacial meltwater threaten irrigation supplies, while extremes — droughts, floods, and storms — cause severe episodic losses. Some high-latitude regions may initially gain longer growing seasons, and elevated atmospheric CO2 can stimulate plant growth (the "CO2 fertilisation effect"), though often with reduced nutritional quality and not enough to offset heat and water stresses in most regions.

Tropical and subtropical regions, where many low-income countries depend on rain-fed agriculture, face the largest projected yield declines, particularly for maize and wheat, raising concerns about food security and prices.

Agriculture's contribution to climate change

Agriculture emits methane from ruminant livestock and rice paddies, nitrous oxide from fertilised soils, and carbon dioxide from deforestation and land conversion. Together with forestry and other land use, the sector accounts for roughly a fifth of anthropogenic emissions.

Adaptation and mitigation

Adaptation options include drought- and heat-tolerant crop varieties, improved irrigation, diversified farming systems and climate services for farmers. Mitigation options include agroforestry, soil carbon sequestration, better manure and fertiliser management, and dietary shifts. "Climate-smart agriculture" seeks to combine productivity, adaptation and mitigation.
