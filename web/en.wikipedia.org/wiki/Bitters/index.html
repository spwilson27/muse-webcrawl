A bitters (plural also bitters) is traditionally an alcoholic preparation flavoured with botanical matter for a bitter or bittersweet flavour. Originally numerous longstanding brands of bitters were developed as patent medicines, but now are sold as digestifs, aperitifs, and cocktail flavourings.

History and ingredients

Bitter herbal infusions date to ancient Egypt and medieval monastic medicine. Commercial bitters emerged in the 19th century: Johann Gottlieb Benjamin Siegert created Angostura bitters in 1824 in Venezuela as a medicinal tonic, and Antoine Peychaud's bitters became associated with New Orleans and the Sazerac cocktail. Common bittering agents include gentian root, quassia, wormwood, cinchona bark (quinine), cascarilla, cassia, orange peel and aromatic spices, macerated in high-proof spirit.

Types and uses

Cocktail bitters are used in drops or dashes to season drinks such as the Old Fashioned and Manhattan; because they are used in tiny quantities, their high alcohol content contributes negligibly to the finished drink. Bitter liqueurs and amari, such as Campari, Fernet-Branca and Aperol, are potable bitters consumed in larger quantities as aperitifs or digestifs. The modern craft-cocktail revival has produced a wide range of artisanal bitters in flavours such as celery, chocolate, cardamom and grapefruit.
