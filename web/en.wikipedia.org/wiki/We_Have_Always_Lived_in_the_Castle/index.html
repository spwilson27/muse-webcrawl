We Have Always Lived in the Castle

AuthorShirley Jackson
PublishedSeptember 21, 1962 (Viking Press)
Pages214
GenreGothic fiction, mystery

We Have Always Lived in the Castle is a 1962 mystery novel by American author Shirley Jackson. It was Jackson's final work, published with a dedication to Pascal Covici, her editor at Viking Press, three years before her death in 1965. A Gothic psychological novel, it is written in the voice of eighteen-year-old Mary Katherine "Merricat" Blackwood, who lives with her older sister Constance and their ailing Uncle Julian in isolation after the rest of their family died from arsenic poisoning six years earlier.

Plot

The Blackwood family once lived in a large house in a small New England village, where the rest of the family — parents, a younger brother, and an aunt — were poisoned with arsenic in the sugar bowl. Constance, who prepared the meal, was tried and acquitted, but the villagers remain hostile to the surviving sisters. Merricat protects the household with sympathetic magic, burying objects around the property and nailing a book to a tree. When their cousin Charles Blackwood arrives and begins to insinuate himself into the household and its fortune, the fragile order collapses: a fire destroys much of the house, the villagers loot it openly, and the sisters withdraw permanently behind the ruins, accepting food left by villagers too frightened to approach.

Themes and reception

The novel explores isolation, ritual, persecution, and the psychology of the outsider. Critics have read Merricat as an early example of the unreliable narrator in American Gothic fiction and the book as a study of a small community's cruelty toward difference. Time magazine later named it one of the "TEN Best Novels" of 1962. A stage adaptation premiered in 1966, and a film adaptation starring Taissa Farmiga and Alexandra Daddario premiered in 2018.
