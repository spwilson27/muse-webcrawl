The O2 Arena, commonly known as the O2, is a multi-purpose indoor arena in the centre of The O2 entertainment complex on the Greenwich Peninsula in southeast London. It opened in 2007 inside the former Millennium Dome and, with a capacity of 20,000, is the second-largest indoor arena by seating capacity in the United Kingdom, and one of the busiest music venues in the world by ticket sales.

History

The arena was built under the roof of the Millennium Dome, the vast structure erected for the year 2000 celebrations. Construction of the arena began in 2003, and it opened on 24 June 2007 with a concert by Bon Jovi. Its naming rights have been held by the telecommunications company O2 (Telefónica) since the venue's redevelopment.

Events

The O2 has hosted concerts by many of the world's top-grossing artists, including record-setting residencies, and major sporting events: basketball and gymnastics at the 2012 Summer Olympics (when it was temporarily called the North Greenwich Arena), the annual ATP Finals tennis tournament from 2009 to 2020, NBA regular-season games, boxing, darts, MMA, and the BRIT Awards. Michael Jackson's planned 50-show "This Is It" residency in 2009 was cancelled following his death. In February 2022 Storm Eunice tore parts of the dome's roof, closing the venue for several days.

The O2 complex

Within the dome's canopy, the arena is surrounded by an entertainment avenue of restaurants, bars, the indigo at The O2 club venue, a cinema, and exhibition spaces such as the venue that hosted the Tutankhamun exhibition, making the complex one of London's major leisure destinations.
