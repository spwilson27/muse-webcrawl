Taedong River

The Taedong River (Korean: 대동강) is a large river in North Korea. It rises in the Rangrim Mountains of the country's north and flows southwest for about 439 kilometres, passing through the capital, Pyongyang, before emptying into Korea Bay of the Yellow Sea at Nampo.

Course and features

The river is one of the five longest on the Korean Peninsula and drains a basin of some 20,000 square kilometres. In Pyongyang it flows past landmarks including the Juche Tower, Kim Il-sung Square, the May Day Stadium on Rungra Island, and the Yanggakdo Hotel. The West Sea Barrage, an 8-kilometre barrage completed in 1986 near the river's mouth at Nampo, regulates the estuary and supports irrigation, shipping and flood control. Other dams and locks, such as those at Mirim and Bonghwa, make much of the river navigable.

History

The Taedong basin was a centre of early Korean civilization; the ancient kingdom of Goguryeo moved its capital to Pyongyang on the river in 427 CE. The river also appears in foreign history: the American merchant ship General Sherman was burned and sunk in the river in 1866, and the USS Pueblo, captured in 1968, is moored on the river in Pyongyang as a museum ship.
