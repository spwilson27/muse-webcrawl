Super Bowl XXXVIII halftime show controversy

The halftime show at Reliant Stadium, Houston, 1 February 2004

The Super Bowl XXXVIII halftime show controversy, widely known as "Nipplegate", centred on the halftime show of Super Bowl XXXVIII on 1 February 2004 at Reliant Stadium in Houston, Texas. Near the end of the performance, singer Justin Timberlake removed part of Janet Jackson's costume, exposing her right breast — adorned with a nipple shield — for about half a second before an audience of some 140 million viewers. The incident, attributed by the performers to a "wardrobe malfunction", ignited a national debate about broadcast decency and led to major regulatory and industry changes.

The incident

The MTV-produced show featured Jackson, Timberlake, P. Diddy, Nelly, Kid Rock and Jessica Simpson. During the closing duet of Timberlake's "Rock Your Body", at the lyric "gonna have you naked by the end of this song", Timberlake tore at Jackson's bodice, revealing her breast. CBS, MTV and the NFL all issued apologies, calling it an unplanned malfunction of the costume design. The disparity in consequences became a lasting point of controversy: Jackson's career suffered lasting damage — her music was blacklisted by major broadcasters and she was disinvited from the Grammy Awards — while Timberlake, who presented at the Grammys a week later, recovered quickly.

Regulatory fallout

The Federal Communications Commission received some 540,000 complaints and fined CBS a record $550,000; after years of litigation, a federal court voided the fine in 2011, ruling the agency had acted arbitrarily. Congress passed the Broadcast Decency Enforcement Act of 2005, raising maximum indecency fines tenfold to $325,000 per incident. Broadcasters responded by adopting multi-second delays for live events, including award shows and subsequent Super Bowls.

Cultural legacy

The controversy changed the Super Bowl halftime show for a decade: the NFL turned to veteran rock acts (Paul McCartney, the Rolling Stones, Prince, Tom Petty, Bruce Springsteen) judged "safe". The incident also famously spurred the founding of YouTube — co-founder Jawed Karim has cited his inability to find the clip online as inspiration — and became one of the most searched events in early internet history. The phrase "wardrobe malfunction" entered the language, and later documentaries and reporting have reassessed the gendered and racial dimensions of the backlash against Jackson.
