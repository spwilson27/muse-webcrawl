Alf Niels Christian Ross (10 June 1899 – 17 August 1979) was a Danish legal philosopher, judge, and leading figure of Scandinavian legal realism. He served as a judge of the European Court of Human Rights from 1959 to 1971.

Life and career

Ross studied law at the University of Copenhagen, graduating in 1922, and pursued further study in Uppsala, where he was influenced by the philosopher Axel Hägerström, and in Vienna, where he encountered Hans Kelsen. His doctoral thesis, Theorie der Rechtsquellen (1929), written under Kelsen's influence, was initially rejected in Copenhagen; he earned his doctorate there in 1934. Ross became professor of constitutional law at the University of Copenhagen in 1938 and later taught international law and legal philosophy.

Legal philosophy

Drawing on logical positivism and Hägerström's critique of metaphysics, Ross argued that legal concepts such as "rights" and "duties" have no independent reference in the world — his famous example compares them to the meaningless word "tû-tû" of a fictional tribe. Statements about valid law, for Ross, are predictions that courts will apply certain norms, grounded in the psychological and institutional reality of judicial practice. His principal works include On Law and Justice (1953 in Danish, 1958 in English) and Directives and Norms (1968). He is also known for the Ross paradox in deontic logic, concerning the apparently valid inference from "you ought to post the letter" to "you ought to post the letter or burn it".

Judicial service

From 1959 to 1971 Ross served as the Danish judge on the European Court of Human Rights in Strasbourg. He also contributed to Danish constitutional debates and served on public commissions.
