Hyundai

Hyundai (현대)
Founded: 1947 (Hyundai Group); 1967 (Hyundai Motor Company)
Founder: Chung Ju-yung
Headquarters: Seoul, South Korea
Industry: Conglomerate (historical); automotive, construction, shipbuilding (successor groups)

Hyundai (Korean: 현대; Hanja: 現代; IPA: [ˈhjəːndɛ], roughly "hyun-day") is a South Korean multinational conglomerate headquartered in Seoul. The name derives from the Korean word for "modernity." Founded by Chung Ju-yung in 1947 as a construction firm, Hyundai grew into one of the largest chaebol (family-controlled conglomerates) in South Korea, playing a central role in the country's rapid industrialization.

History

Chung Ju-yung founded Hyundai Engineering and Construction in 1947. The group expanded into shipbuilding (Hyundai Heavy Industries, founded 1972, which built the world's largest shipyard at Ulsan), automobiles (Hyundai Motor Company, 1967, whose Pony of 1975 was Korea's first mass-produced car), electronics, steel, elevators, and finance. Hyundai became a symbol of South Korea's "Miracle on the Han River." Following the 1997 Asian financial crisis, the group was broken up under government restructuring: Hyundai Motor Group, Hyundai Heavy Industries Group, Hyundai Department Store Group, and others became independent, while the rump Hyundai Group retained construction, elevator, and North Korea–related businesses such as the Mount Kumgang tourism project.

Hyundai Motor Group

The best-known successor, Hyundai Motor Group, comprises Hyundai Motor Company and Kia, together one of the world's largest automakers by sales. Its models include the Elantra, Sonata, Tucson, and Santa Fe, and the dedicated electric Ioniq line. The group also includes the Genesis luxury brand, Hyundai Mobis (parts), and Hyundai Steel. Hyundai and Kia built major plants in the United States (Alabama and Georgia), Europe, India, and elsewhere.

Other successor companies

HD Hyundai (formerly Hyundai Heavy Industries) is the world's largest shipbuilder. Hyundai Engineering and Construction remains a major builder, known for projects across the Middle East. Hyundai Elevator, Hyundai Department Store, and Hyundai Rotem (rail and defense) are other prominent affiliates. Chung Ju-yung's sons and grandchildren lead the various successor groups; Chung Mong-koo long led the Motor Group, succeeded by his son Chung Eui-sun.
