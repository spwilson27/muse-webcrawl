Pitch Lake

Pitch Lake
LocationLa Brea, Trinidad and Tobago
TypeNatural asphalt lake
AreaAbout 40 hectares (100 acres)
Estimated reservesAbout 10 million tonnes of asphalt

The Pitch Lake at La Brea in southwest Trinidad is the largest natural deposit of asphalt in the world. Covering some 40 hectares and estimated to hold ten million tonnes of pitch, it has been mined commercially for well over a century, and its asphalt has paved roads in cities across the globe.

Formation

The lake is thought to be the surface expression of a deep fault along which oil seeps upward from subterranean reservoirs. Volatile components evaporate in the tropical sun, leaving heavy asphalt mixed with sand, clay, and water. The surface is firm enough in places to walk on, yet slowly churns like a viscous liquid, and objects swallowed by the pitch can resurface decades later.

History

Indigenous peoples used the pitch to waterproof canoes, and legend links the lake to the Amerindian village said to have been punished by the gods. Sir Walter Raleigh caulked his ships with the asphalt in 1595 and praised it as superior to Norwegian pitch. Commercial extraction began in the nineteenth century; Trinidad lake asphalt was used to pave Pennsylvania Avenue in Washington, D.C., and many other famous streets.

Tourism

Today the lake is a tourist attraction, where visitors walk on the surface with guides and bathe in sulphurous pools believed locally to have curative properties.
