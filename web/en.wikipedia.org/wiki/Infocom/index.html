Infocom, Inc. was an American software company based in Cambridge, Massachusetts, that produced some of the most celebrated text adventure games (interactive fiction) of the 1980s. Founded in 1979 by MIT-affiliated computer scientists, it became famous for the sophistication of its parser, the quality of its writing, and the physical extras — "feelies" — packed into its game boxes.

History

Infocom's first product was Zork I (1980), adapted from the mainframe game Zork written at MIT's Laboratory for Computer Science. To sell the same game on many incompatible home computers, the company built the Z-machine, a virtual machine whose story files were portable across platforms — an early example of "write once, run anywhere." Hits followed in every genre: the mystery Deadline, the science-fiction comedy The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy (with Douglas Adams), Planetfall, Enchanter, Trinity, and the dystopian A Mind Forever Voyaging. Infocom games were written in ZIL, a Lisp-like language.

Decline

An expensive attempt to enter business software with the Cornerstone database failed, and as graphics-driven games displaced text adventures the company declined. Activision acquired Infocom in 1986 and closed the Cambridge studio in 1989. The Infocom catalogue remains influential, and its works are central to the study and preservation of interactive fiction.
