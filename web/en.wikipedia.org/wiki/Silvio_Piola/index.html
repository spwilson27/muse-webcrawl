Silvio Piola (29 September 1913 – 4 October 1996) was an Italian footballer who played as a striker. He is the all-time top scorer in the history of Italy's top flight, with 290 goals in Serie A — a record that has stood for decades — and he was a central figure in Italy's victory at the 1938 FIFA World Cup.

Piola with the Italy national team in the 1930s.

Club career

Piola began his career at Pro Vercelli, debuting in Serie A in 1930 while still a teenager, and scoring prolifically despite the club's modest standing. In 1934 he moved to Lazio, where he twice finished as Serie A's top scorer (1937 and 1943) without winning the league title. After wartime football with Torino, he played for Juventus (1945–47) and finished his career at Novara, whom he helped win promotion to Serie A, retiring in 1954 at the age of forty.

International career

For Italy, Piola scored 30 goals in 34 appearances. At the 1938 World Cup in France he scored five goals, including two in the final against Hungary, which Italy won 4–2 to retain the title. His international tally places him among Italy's highest scorers despite the interruption of the Second World War, which robbed him of a second World Cup in his prime.

Style and legacy

Piola was a complete centre-forward: strong, ambidextrous, an outstanding header of the ball, and famed for his spectacular overhead and diving finishes — the bicycle kick is known in Italian as the "rovesciata alla Piola." He is one of the very few players to be top scorer for three different Serie A clubs' seasons, and his 290 league goals remain unmatched. Stadia in Vercelli and Novara bear his name.
