University of Montpellier

The University of Montpellier (French: Université de Montpellier) is a public research university in Montpellier, France. Established in 1220 by papal bull and formally confirmed in 1289, it is one of the oldest universities in the world, particularly renowned for its medical school, which is among the oldest still operating.

History

Medicine was taught at Montpellier from the twelfth century, drawing students from across Europe; figures associated with the university include Arnaldus de Villa Nova, Guy de Chauliac, Nostradamus, and François Rabelais, who studied and taught medicine there. During the French Revolution the university was suppressed, and its faculties were later re-established under Napoleon. In 1970 the university was split into three institutions (Montpellier I, II, and III), which were partially reunified in 2015 when Montpellier I and II merged to form the present university, with Paul-Valéry University Montpellier III remaining separate.

Present day

The university enrolls roughly 50,000 students in faculties of law, economics, pharmacy, medicine, dentistry, sciences, and sport sciences, among others. Its Faculty of Medicine occupies historic buildings beside Montpellier Cathedral, including a famous anatomical museum, and the university maintains the Jardin des plantes de Montpellier, France's oldest botanical garden (founded 1593).
