The Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics (German: Max-Planck-Institut für Plasmaphysik, IPP) is a German research institute devoted to plasma physics and the development of nuclear fusion as an energy source. Founded in 1960 in Munich as the Institute for Plasma Physics, it became part of the Max Planck Society in 1971 and is one of the largest fusion research centres in Europe, contributing strongly to the European fusion programme and to ITER.

Sites and experiments

The institute's headquarters are at Garching near Munich, home of the ASDEX Upgrade tokamak, in operation since 1991, which studies plasma-wall interaction and divertor physics under conditions close to those of a future power plant, including a full tungsten first wall. A second site at Greifswald in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, founded after German reunification, hosts Wendelstein 7-X, the world's largest stellarator, which began operation in 2015 and is designed to demonstrate the suitability of optimised stellarators for steady-state fusion power.

Research

IPP research covers plasma heating, plasma diagnostics, theory and numerical simulation, materials and wall components for fusion devices, and systems studies of future fusion power plants (DEMO). It is a member of the EUROfusion consortium and has close historical ties to the Joint European Torus (JET) programme.
