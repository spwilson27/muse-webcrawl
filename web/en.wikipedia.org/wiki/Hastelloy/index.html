Hastelloy

Hastelloy is the registered trademark of Haynes International for a family of highly corrosion-resistant metal alloys. The Hastelloy alloys are primarily nickel-based superalloys alloyed with molybdenum, chromium, iron, cobalt and other elements, and are widely used in the chemical process industries, pollution control, geothermal, and oil and gas applications where resistance to aggressive acids and high temperatures is required.

History

The Hastelloy trademark was applied to a series of alloys developed beginning in the early 20th century by the Haynes Stellite Company (now Haynes International) of Kokomo, Indiana. Early grades such as Hastelloy B (a nickel–molybdenum alloy) and Hastelloy C (a nickel–chromium–molybdenum alloy) became standards for equipment handling hydrochloric and other reducing acids.

Common grades

  • Hastelloy B-2 and B-3 — nickel–molybdenum alloys with outstanding resistance to hydrochloric acid at all concentrations and temperatures.
  • Hastelloy C-276 — a versatile nickel–chromium–molybdenum alloy with tungsten, one of the most widely used corrosion-resistant alloys; UNS N10276.
  • Hastelloy C-22 — improved resistance to pitting, crevice corrosion and stress corrosion cracking.
  • Hastelloy X — a high-temperature alloy used in gas turbine engines and industrial furnace applications.
  • Hastelloy G-30 — developed for phosphoric acid and fertilizer industry service.

Properties

Hastelloy alloys resist uniform attack, localized pitting and crevice corrosion, and stress corrosion cracking in chloride-bearing environments. They retain mechanical strength at elevated temperatures and can be fabricated by standard welding and forming methods, though they work-harden rapidly.
