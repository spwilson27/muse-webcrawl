The Council of the District of Columbia is the legislative branch of the government of the District of Columbia, the federal capital of the United States. Often described as functioning as both a state legislature and a city council, the council enacts local laws, approves the district's budget, and confirms mayoral appointments. It was established under the District of Columbia Home Rule Act of 1973, and its first members took office in 1975.

The John A. Wilson Building on Pennsylvania Avenue houses the council and the mayor's office.

Composition

The council consists of thirteen members: a chairperson elected at large, four at-large members, and eight members each elected from one of the district's eight wards. Members serve four-year terms. To encourage minority-party or independent representation, no more than three of the five at-large seats (including the chair) may be held by members of the same political party; in practice this has reserved at-large seats for non-Democrats in the heavily Democratic district.

Powers and limitations

The council legislates on local matters including taxation, public safety, education oversight, land use and social services. Its authority is limited by the constitutional power of the United States Congress over the district: all council legislation is subject to a congressional review period (30 legislative days for most acts, 60 for criminal-code changes), during which Congress may disapprove it by joint resolution. The council also may not impose a commuter tax on income earned in the district by non-residents, nor tax federal property.

The council enacts legislation through bills passed at legislative meetings and signed by the mayor (subject to veto and override). It also holds oversight hearings over district agencies and confirms the mayor's nominees to boards, commissions and agency leadership posts.

Committees

Work is organised through standing committees — including the Committee of the Whole, and committees covering finance and revenue, education, health, housing, public safety, transportation and the environment, and business and economic development — which hold hearings and mark up legislation before it reaches the full council.

History

Before home rule, the district was governed directly by Congress and presidentially appointed commissioners. Earlier experiments in local self-government included an elected territorial legislature (1871–1874), abolished after financial scandals. The Home Rule Act restored an elected mayor and council in 1975. Walter Washington served as the first home-rule mayor; Sterling Tucker was the first council chair. Subsequent chairs have included David A. Clarke, Linda Cropp, Kwame Brown, Vincent C. Gray (who later became mayor), and Phil Mendelson.
