Elbląg (German: Elbing) is a city in northern Poland, in the Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship, located on the Elbląg River near its outlet into the Vistula Lagoon. With around 115,000 inhabitants, it is an industrial, port, and tourist city, and one of the oldest cities in the region.

History

Elbląg was founded in 1237 by the Teutonic Knights near an earlier Old Prussian trading settlement and became a member of the Hanseatic League, flourishing as a Baltic port. In 1466, after the Thirteen Years' War, it passed to the Polish Crown and was one of the major cities of Royal Prussia. Devastated in later wars, it declined as its harbour silted up. Following the First Partition of Poland in 1772 it became part of Prussia and then Germany, known as Elbing, an industrial and shipbuilding centre. Heavily destroyed in 1945, the city returned to Poland after the Second World War and its population was resettled.

Sights and transport

The Old Town, largely destroyed in 1945, has been rebuilt with reconstructed burgher houses; its landmark is the Gothic St. Nicholas Cathedral. The nearby Elbląg Canal (Kanał Elbląski), built in the 19th century, is famous for its unique system of slipways that carry boats overland between lakes, and remains a popular tourist attraction.
