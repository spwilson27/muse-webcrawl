Cheeger constant

In Riemannian geometry, the Cheeger constant (also called the isoperimetric constant) of a compact Riemannian manifold M is a positive real number h(M) defined by

h(M) = infS area(S) / min(vol(A), vol(B))

where the infimum is taken over all smooth hypersurfaces S that divide M into two disjoint open subsets A and B. The constant is named after the American mathematician Jeff Cheeger, who introduced it in 1970.

Cheeger inequality

Cheeger proved that the first nonzero eigenvalue λ1 of the Laplace–Beltrami operator satisfies λ1 ≥ h(M)²/4. The reverse comparison, bounding the Cheeger constant in terms of λ1, is known as Buser's inequality, proved by Peter Buser in 1982 under a lower bound on the Ricci curvature.

Graph theory

An analogous quantity for graphs — the ratio of the number of edges leaving a vertex set to the size of the set — underlies the theory of expander graphs and the discrete Cheeger inequality relating it to the spectral gap of the graph Laplacian.
