Western Desert cultural bloc

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Western Desert cultural bloc (also known simply as the Western Desert) is a grouping of Aboriginal Australian peoples united by a shared cultural tradition across the arid interior of Western Australia, the south-west Northern Territory and north-west South Australia. It covers roughly 600,000 square kilometres, including the Gibson, Great Victoria and Great Sandy deserts.

Peoples and languages

Peoples of the bloc include the Martu, Pintupi, Pitjantjatjara, Yankunytjatjara, Ngaanyatjarra, Luritja and many others. Their languages form the Wati subgroup of the Pama-Nyungan family and are largely mutually intelligible as a dialect chain, sometimes treated as a single language, Western Desert Language.

Culture

The bloc shares a common religious tradition centred on Tjukurpa (the Dreaming), a section and subsection ("skin name") system of kinship, and similar ceremonial and artistic practices. Communities of the region became internationally known from the 1970s through the Western Desert art movement centred on Papunya, whose acrylic dot paintings brought Aboriginal art to global attention.
