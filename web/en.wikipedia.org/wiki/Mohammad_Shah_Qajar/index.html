Mohammad Shah Qajar

Mohammad Shah Qajar (born Mohammad Mirza, 5 January 1808 – 5 September 1848) was the third shah of Qajar Iran, reigning from 1834 until his death in 1848. The grandson and successor of Fath-Ali Shah, his reign was dominated by the influence of his Sufi-minded prime minister Haji Mirza Aqasi and by the rivalry of Britain and Russia over Iran.

Accession

Mohammad Mirza was the son of crown prince Abbas Mirza, whose death in 1833 left his son heir to the throne. When Fath-Ali Shah died in 1834, rival princes contested the succession, but with British and Russian support — and the energy of his minister Mirza Abolqasem Qa'em-Maqam — Mohammad Shah was enthroned in Tehran. He soon had Qa'em-Maqam executed (1835) and raised his former tutor, the Sufi Haji Mirza Aqasi, to the premiership, which he held for the rest of the reign.

Reign

The shah twice campaigned against Herat (1837–38 and through proxies thereafter), a city Iran claimed but whose possession brought confrontation with Britain, which occupied Kharg Island and forced a withdrawal. Internally, the government struggled with empty coffers, tribal unrest, and the growing influence of the foreign legations. Mohammad Shah suffered from gout and poor health, and died in 1848, succeeded by his son Naser al-Din Shah.
