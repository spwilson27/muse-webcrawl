Sa'd ibn Mu'adh (Arabic: سعد بن معاذ, c. 590–627) was a prominent companion of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and the chief of the Banu Abd al-Ashhal clan of the Aws tribe in Medina. His conversion to Islam was a turning point that brought most of his clan into the new faith shortly before the Hijrah.

Life

Sa'd was converted by Mus'ab ibn Umair, the envoy Muhammad sent to Medina before the migration. As leader of the Aws, Sa'd played a leading role in the battles of Badr (624) and Uhud (625), where he was among those who stood firm around Muhammad. He was a close adviser whose opinion the Prophet frequently sought on questions of war.

Battle of the Trench and Banu Qurayza

During the Battle of the Trench (627), Sa'd was struck in the arm by an arrow that severed a vein; he was carried to a tent in the mosque to be treated. After the siege, the Jewish tribe of Banu Qurayza, accused of treasonous dealings with the besieging confederates, surrendered on the condition that their fate be decided by Sa'd, their former ally. Sa'd ruled that the fighting men be executed and the women and children enslaved and property divided — a judgement that Muhammad endorsed. Sa'd's wound reopened shortly afterwards and he died; Islamic tradition holds him in high esteem as a martyr, and a well-known hadith relates that the Throne of God shook at his death.
