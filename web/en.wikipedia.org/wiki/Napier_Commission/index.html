Napier Commission

The Napier Commission, officially the Royal Commission of Inquiry into the Condition of Crofters and Cottars in the Highlands and Islands of Scotland, was a royal commission appointed in 1883 to investigate the grievances of crofters in the Scottish Highlands and Islands. It was chaired by Francis Napier, 10th Lord Napier, and its report, published in 1884, led directly to the Crofters' Holdings (Scotland) Act 1886.

Background

During the 1880s the crofting communities of the Highlands and Islands were in a state of unrest sometimes called the Crofters' War. Crofters, small-scale tenant farmers, faced high rents, insecure tenure, evictions, and the loss of common grazing land to sheep farms and deer forests created during the Highland Clearances. Agitation included rent strikes and land raids, and in April 1882 disorder at Braes on the Isle of Skye led to the so-called Battle of the Braes, in which crofters resisted sheriff officers attempting to serve eviction notices.

The government of William Ewart Gladstone responded by appointing the commission under Lord Napier, a former diplomat and colonial governor. Other members included Sir Donald Cameron of Lochiel, a major Highland landowner and MP, and Professor Donald MacKinnon, the first holder of the chair of Celtic at the University of Edinburgh.

Inquiry and evidence

The commission travelled throughout the crofting counties — Shetland, Orkney, Caithness, Sutherland, Ross and Cromarty, Inverness-shire and Argyll — taking evidence at dozens of sittings. In an unusual step for the time, it heard testimony directly from crofters themselves, over 700 of whom gave evidence, alongside factors, landlords, ministers and officials. Crofters complained of arbitrary rent increases, eviction without compensation for improvements, the confiscation of hill grazing, and the small size and poor quality of their holdings.

Report and recommendations

The report of 1884 was sympathetic to the crofters' complaints, though its recommendations fell short of what the crofters' movement demanded. It proposed security of tenure for crofters paying fair rents, compensation for improvements, the enlargement of small holdings, and the creation of a body to fix fair rents. Landlords on the commission dissented from some of the more far-reaching proposals, and the crofters' leaders were disappointed that the report did not recommend the restoration of land lost to sheep farms and deer forests.

Legacy

Although the government initially failed to legislate, the election of four crofter MPs in 1885 strengthened the movement's hand, and the Crofters' Holdings (Scotland) Act 1886 gave crofters security of tenure, the right to a fair rent fixed by the new Crofters Commission, and compensation for permanent improvements. The Napier Commission's evidence remains a major source for the social history of the Highlands, and the crofters' township evidence in particular is valued as a record of ordinary people's experience of the Clearances and their aftermath.
