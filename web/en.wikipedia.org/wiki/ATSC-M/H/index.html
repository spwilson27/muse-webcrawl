ATSC-M/H

TypeDigital terrestrial television standard
Developed byAdvanced Television Systems Committee
StandardATSC A/153
AdoptedOctober 2009
RegionNorth America

ATSC-M/H (Advanced Television Systems Committee — Mobile/Handheld) is a standard for broadcasting digital television signals to mobile and handheld receivers, developed as an extension of the North American ATSC terrestrial television system. Standardized as ATSC A/153 in October 2009, it allows broadcasters to devote part of their 6 MHz channel to robust mobile services receivable in moving vehicles and portable devices.

Technical design

The standard ATSC 8VSB signal is difficult to receive in motion because multipath interference and Doppler effects disrupt reception. ATSC-M/H addresses this by adding extra training sequences, stronger forward error correction — including a low-density parity-check-style additional coding and a Reed–Solomon frame protection scheme — and by organizing mobile data into periodically transmitted "parades" of frames that let receivers power-cycle between bursts to save battery. M/H data occupies a backward-compatible portion of the transport stream, so existing fixed receivers ignore it.

Deployment

U.S. broadcasters organized ventures such as the Mobile Content Venture, which launched the Dyle mobile TV service, and receiver products included USB tuners, portable TVs, and a small number of phones. Adoption was limited: consumer awareness was low, compatible devices were scarce, and the rise of IP streaming over 4G networks offered a more flexible way to watch video on mobile devices. Most M/H services were wound down by the mid-2010s, and the industry's attention shifted to ATSC 3.0, whose IP-based design supports mobile reception natively.
