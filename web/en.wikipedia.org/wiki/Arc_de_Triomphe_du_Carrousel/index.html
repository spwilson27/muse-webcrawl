Arc de Triomphe du Carrousel

Arc de Triomphe du Carrousel
Location:Place du Carrousel, Paris, France
Architects:Pierre-François-Léonard Fontaine and Charles Percier
Construction:1806–1808
Height:19 m (62 ft)

The Arc de Triomphe du Carrousel (pronounced [aʁk də tʁijɔ̃f dy kaʁusɛl]) is a triumphal arch in Paris, located in the Place du Carrousel at the western end of the Louvre courtyard, between the Louvre Palace and the Tuileries Garden. It was built between 1806 and 1808 to commemorate Napoleon's military victories of the previous year.

Description

Modelled on the ancient Roman Arch of Septimius Severus, the monument is a triple-arched structure about 19 metres high, 23 metres wide, and 7.3 metres deep. Its eight Corinthian columns of pink marble, taken from the Château de Meudon, are each topped by a statue of a soldier of the Grande Armée in the dress of a different corps — cuirassier, dragoon, chasseur, carabinier, grenadier, and others. Bas-reliefs on the faces depict Napoleon's triumphs of 1805, including the battles of Austerlitz and Ulm and his entries into Munich and Vienna.

The quadriga

The arch is crowned by a quadriga — four horses with a chariot and figures of Peace and Victory. The original group used the famous bronze Horses of Saint Mark, looted from Venice in 1798; after Napoleon's fall in 1815 the originals were returned to Venice, and in 1828 a new quadriga by François Joseph Bosio took their place.

History

The arch originally served as the monumental entrance to the Tuileries Palace, aligned on the historical axis of Paris with the larger Arc de Triomphe de l'Étoile to the west. When the Tuileries Palace was burned during the Paris Commune in 1871 and later demolished, the view through the arch opened to the Champs-Élysées. The arch gave its name to the Carrousel du Louvre shopping mall and stands above the inverted glass pyramid designed by I. M. Pei.
