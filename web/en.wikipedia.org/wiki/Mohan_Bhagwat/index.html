Mohan Madhukar Bhagwat (born 11 September 1950) is an Indian social leader who has served since 2009 as the Sarsanghchalak (chief) of the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS), the Hindu nationalist volunteer organisation that is the ideological parent of the Sangh Parivar, including the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP). As head of the RSS — arguably the world's largest voluntary organisation — Bhagwat is considered one of the most influential figures in Indian public life.

Early life and RSS career

Bhagwat was born in Chandrapur, Maharashtra, into a family steeped in the RSS: his father, Madhukar Rao Bhagwat, was a senior RSS functionary, and his mother, Malati, was active in the Rashtra Sevika Samiti, the women's counterpart. He studied veterinary science at the Government Veterinary College, Nagpur, then abandoned a postgraduate course in 1975 — the year the Emergency was imposed — to become a full-time RSS pracharak (organiser). He worked underground during the Emergency, and rose through regional posts in Maharashtra and Bihar before becoming Akhil Bharatiya Sharirik Pramukh (national physical-training head) in 1991, prachar chief for the Northeast, and general secretary (Sarkaryavah) in 2000.

Sarsanghchalak

On 21 March 2009 Bhagwat succeeded K. S. Sudarshan as the sixth Sarsanghchalak, an unusually young appointee at 58. His tenure coincided with the RSS's centenary (2025) and the political ascendancy of the BJP under Narendra Modi, himself a former RSS pracharak. Bhagwat's annual Vijayadashami addresses from the RSS headquarters at Reshimbagh, Nagpur, are treated as significant political and social statements. His public remarks have sometimes suggested a moderating public face for the Sangh — including statements that the RSS accepts the Constitution, that it does not oppose any religion, remarks on caste and reservation, calls for a population policy, and in 2023 a statement that, following the opening of the Ram temple at Ayodhya, temple disputes should not be raked up afresh. At the same time he has reaffirmed core Sangh positions on Hindutva, cultural nationalism and social organisation. Bhagwat has also fronted outreach efforts, such as a three-day lecture series in Delhi in 2018 ("Future of Bharat") attended by politicians, diplomats and public figures.
