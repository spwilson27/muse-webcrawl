African-American studies
Field: Interdisciplinary academic discipline
Also called: Black studies, Africana studies
Focus: History, culture and politics of people of African descent
Key precursor: W. E. B. Du Bois

African-American studies (also called Black studies or Africana studies) is an interdisciplinary academic field devoted to the study of the history, culture, literature and politics of peoples of African descent, particularly in the United States and the African diaspora.

History

Scholarly study of African-American life traces to W. E. B. Du Bois and Carter G. Woodson in the early 20th century. The field was institutionalised in universities following student activism of the late 1960s: the first Black studies department was established at San Francisco State University in 1968 after the longest student strike in US history.

Scope

The discipline draws on history, sociology, literature, political science and the arts, examining slavery, emancipation, the Great Migration, the civil rights movement, and contemporary questions of race and inequality.
