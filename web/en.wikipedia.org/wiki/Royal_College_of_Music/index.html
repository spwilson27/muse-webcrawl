Royal College of Music

The RCM building on Prince Consort Road, South Kensington

The Royal College of Music (RCM) is a conservatoire located in South Kensington, London. Founded in 1882, it offers training from undergraduate to doctoral level in performance, composition, conducting, music theory and music history. The college is one of the four conservatoires of the Associated Board of the Royal Schools of Music.

History

The college grew out of the National Training School for Music, founded in 1876. In 1882 the Prince of Wales (later Edward VII) founded the Royal College of Music, which opened in 1883 with George Grove as its first director. Its purpose was to raise the standard of musical education in Britain and to provide serious training comparable to the great conservatoires of continental Europe. The main building on Prince Consort Road, designed by Arthur Blomfield, was opened in 1894.

Teaching and facilities

The college educates around 800 students from more than 50 countries. Facilities include the Amaryllis Fleming Concert Hall, the Britten Theatre for opera productions, and a museum of musical instruments holding some 800 items, including one of the earliest dated clavicyords. Its library and archives include the manuscript of Mozart's Piano Concerto No. 24 in C minor and Elgar's Cello Concerto.

Notable alumni

Alumni include Gustav Holst, Ralph Vaughan Williams, Benjamin Britten, Joan Sutherland, Thomas Allen, John Williams and Andrew Lloyd Webber.
