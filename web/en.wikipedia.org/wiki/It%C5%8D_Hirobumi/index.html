Prince Itō Hirobumi (16 October 1841 – 26 October 1909) was a Japanese statesman who served as the first Prime Minister of Japan and was the principal architect of the Meiji Constitution. A samurai of Chōshū Domain and member of the Meiji oligarchy, he was one of the most influential figures in Japan's transformation into a modern state.

Early life and Meiji Restoration

Born Hayashi Risuke, Itō was adopted into the Itō family and studied under Yoshida Shōin alongside other future leaders of the Meiji Restoration. In 1863 he secretly travelled to London with four other Chōshū students — the "Chōshū Five" — and returned convinced that Japan must modernise. He served in the new Meiji government in posts concerning finance, foreign affairs and public works, and led the Iwakura Mission's secretariat in 1871–73.

Prime Minister and constitution

Itō headed the first modern cabinet in 1885 and presided over the drafting of the Constitution of the Empire of Japan, promulgated in 1889 after his study of European constitutional systems, particularly that of Prussia. He served four terms as prime minister and founded the Rikken Seiyūkai party in 1900.

Korea and assassination

After the Russo-Japanese War, Itō became the first Resident-General of Korea in 1905, overseeing its reduction to a Japanese protectorate, though he opposed outright annexation. He was assassinated at Harbin railway station in 1909 by the Korean independence activist An Jung-geun — an event that accelerated Japan's annexation of Korea the following year.
