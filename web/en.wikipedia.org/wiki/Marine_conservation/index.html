Marine conservation

Marine conservation, also known as ocean conservation, is the protection and preservation of ecosystems in oceans and seas through planned management in order to prevent the over-exploitation of these marine resources. It focuses on limiting human-caused damage to marine ecosystems, restoring damaged ecosystems, and preserving vulnerable species of marine life, in response to threats including overfishing, pollution, habitat destruction, ocean acidification, and climate change.

Threats

Human activities have intensified over the past half-century: roughly a third of assessed fish stocks are fished at unsustainable levels; plastic pollution enters the ocean in millions of tonnes each year; nutrient runoff creates coastal dead zones; and warming waters drive coral bleaching events of increasing frequency and severity. Destructive fishing practices such as bottom trawling damage seabed habitats, while shipping introduces noise pollution and invasive species via ballast water.

Strategies

Marine conservation employs a range of tools. Marine protected areas (MPAs), including fully protected no-take marine reserves, restrict extractive activity in key habitats; as of the mid-2020s about 8% of the ocean was within designated MPAs, against the "30x30" target of protecting 30% by 2030 adopted under the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework. Other strategies include fisheries management (catch limits, gear restrictions, bycatch reduction), pollution control under conventions such as MARPOL, coral reef restoration, and international law, notably the 2023 High Seas Treaty (BBNJ Agreement) governing biodiversity beyond national jurisdiction.

Organisations

Key actors include the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN), UNESCO's marine programmes, national agencies such as NOAA, and non-governmental organisations such as Oceana, the Marine Conservation Society, and the Sea Shepherd Conservation Society. The discipline of marine conservation biology emerged as a distinct field in the late twentieth century.
