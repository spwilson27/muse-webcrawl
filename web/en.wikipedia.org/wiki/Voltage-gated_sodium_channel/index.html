Voltage-gated sodium channel

Voltage-gated sodium channels (VGSCs or NaV channels) are integral membrane proteins that conduct sodium ions (Na⁺) through a cell's plasma membrane in response to changes in membrane potential. They are responsible for the rising phase of action potentials in neurons, muscle, and other excitable cells.

Structure

Eukaryotic channels consist of a single large α subunit with four homologous domains (I–IV), each containing six transmembrane segments; the S4 segments act as voltage sensors and the re-entrant P loops form the selective pore with its DEKA signature motif. Auxiliary β subunits modulate gating and localization. Humans have nine α-subunit isoforms, NaV1.1–1.9, encoded by the SCN gene family.

Function and pharmacology

Channels cycle through closed, open, and fast-inactivated states within milliseconds. They are blocked by tetrodotoxin and targeted by local anesthetics, antiarrhythmics, and anticonvulsants. Mutations cause channelopathies including epilepsy syndromes, periodic paralysis, myotonia, and inherited pain disorders such as erythromelalgia; NaV1.7 loss produces congenital insensitivity to pain, making these channels major drug targets.
