Florida Bay is the bay located between the southern end of the Florida mainland (the Florida Everglades) and the Florida Keys in the United States. It is a shallow estuary, about 850 square miles (2,200 km²) in area, consisting of a network of basins separated by mud banks and mangrove islands. Most of the bay lies within Everglades National Park.

The shallow basins and mud banks of Florida Bay, seen from the air.

Ecology

The bay's seagrass meadows — dominated by turtle grass — are among the most productive estuarine habitats in North America, supporting shrimp, spiny lobster, spotted seatrout, tarpon, and wading birds including the roseate spoonbill. Fresh water flowing south from the Everglades historically moderated the bay's salinity; diversions of water for agriculture and cities to the north reduced these flows during the twentieth century.

Environmental problems

Beginning in 1987, a massive die-off of turtle grass affected tens of thousands of acres, followed by algal blooms and sponge die-offs. Hypersalinity, caused by reduced freshwater inflow and drought, is considered a principal stressor. Restoration of freshwater flows to the bay is a central goal of the Comprehensive Everglades Restoration Plan (CERP), approved in 2000.
