Theaster Gates

Theaster Gates (born August 28, 1973) is an American artist, urban planner and academic based in Chicago. His practice blends sculpture, installation, performance and large-scale urban redevelopment, focusing on Black history, African-American culture and the revitalisation of disinvested neighbourhoods.

Work

Trained in ceramics and urban planning, Gates founded the non-profit Rebuild Foundation in 2009. His Dorchester Projects converted abandoned buildings in Chicago's Greater Grand Crossing neighbourhood into cultural venues — including the Listening House, the Archive House and the Black Cinema House — and culminated in the Stony Island Arts Bank (2015), a rescued 1923 bank building housing archives of Black culture, including the Johnson Publishing Company library and the record collection of house-music pioneer Frankie Knuckles.

In his gallery work, Gates transforms salvaged materials — tarred roofing felt, decommissioned fire hoses, bound copies of Ebony and Jet magazines — into sculptures and paintings charged with histories of labour and civil rights. He also performs with his ensemble the Black Monks, drawing on gospel, blues and Eastern monastic chant.

Recognition

Gates is a professor at the University of Chicago. His work has been shown at documenta 13, the Whitney Biennial, the Venice Biennale, Tate Modern and the Serpentine (where his Black Chapel pavilion opened in 2022). He received the Artes Mundi Prize in 2015. His model of "art-led" neighbourhood investment is influential — and debated — in discussions of gentrification and community development.
