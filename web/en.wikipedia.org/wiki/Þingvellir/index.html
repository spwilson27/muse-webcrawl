Þingvellir

Þingvellir ("Thing Fields") is a national park in southwestern Iceland, about 40 km northeast of Reykjavík. It is a site of profound historical, cultural, and geological importance: the Icelandic parliament, the Althing, was founded there in 930 AD, making it one of the oldest parliamentary sites in the world, and the plain sits directly on the rift between the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates.

History

Chieftains and their followers gathered at Þingvellir each summer for the Althing, where the lawspeaker recited the law from the Lögberg (Law Rock), disputes were settled, and trade and marriages were arranged. The assembly met there until 1798. In 1944, Iceland's independence from Denmark was proclaimed at Þingvellir, and in 2000 the country celebrated the millennium of its conversion to Christianity at the site.

Geology

Þingvellir lies in a rift valley formed by the drifting apart of the North American and Eurasian plates, which separate by roughly 2.5 cm per year. The Almannagjá gorge marks the edge of the North American plate. The Silfra fissure, filled with glacial meltwater filtered through lava fields, offers exceptionally clear water popular with divers who can touch both continents at once. The park borders Þingvallavatn, Iceland's largest natural lake.
