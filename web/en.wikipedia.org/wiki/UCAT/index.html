UCAT

The University Clinical Aptitude Test (UCAT) is an admissions test used by a consortium of universities in the United Kingdom, Australia and New Zealand for entry to medical and dental degree programmes. It was introduced in the United Kingdom in 2006 as the UK Clinical Aptitude Test (UKCAT) and was renamed UCAT in 2019. The test is run by the UCAT Consortium and delivered at Pearson VUE test centres.

Format

The UCAT is a computer-based test of about two hours, comprising five sections: Verbal Reasoning, Decision Making, Quantitative Reasoning, Abstract Reasoning, and Situational Judgement. The first four sections are scored from 300 to 900 each, giving a total cognitive score of 1200–3600; Situational Judgement is reported in bands 1–4. The test is designed to assess aptitude and professional behaviours rather than academic knowledge, and is taken once per application cycle, typically in the summer before applying.

Use in admissions

Universities use UCAT scores in differing ways: some rank applicants by score for interview selection, some apply a threshold cut-off, and others use it as one factor alongside academic grades and personal statements. More than 30 UK medical and dental schools require the UCAT; the Australian and New Zealand version, UCAT ANZ, replaced the UMAT in 2019 for universities including those in the consortium across both countries.
