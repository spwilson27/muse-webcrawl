Multimedia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Multimedia is content that uses a combination of different media forms — typically text, audio, images, animation, video and interactivity. It contrasts with content that uses only rudimentary displays, such as text-only material or traditional printed matter. Multimedia can be recorded and played, displayed, interacted with or accessed by information-processing devices such as computers and smartphones.

Terminology and history

The term was coined in the 1960s — singer and artist Bob Goldstein used "multi-media" in 1966 to promote a show combining music, light and visual art — and became widespread in the 1990s with the arrival of CD-ROM encyclopedias, multimedia PCs, and authoring tools that let users combine media on a single disc. The World Wide Web then made multimedia a routine part of everyday communication, and streaming audio and video largely superseded physical multimedia products.

Categories and uses

Multimedia is commonly divided into linear content, which progresses without user navigation control (such as a film or presentation), and nonlinear or interactive content, such as video games, websites and interactive training, which (in the case of hypermedia) builds on hypertext structures. Applications span entertainment and the arts, education (computer-based training and edutainment), journalism, advertising, engineering and scientific visualisation, medicine, and business presentations.

Learning research

Cognitive research on multimedia learning, notably Richard Mayer's cognitive theory, suggests that people learn better from words and pictures combined than from words alone, but that extraneous material, redundancy and split attention can hinder learning — findings distilled into design principles widely applied in instructional media.
