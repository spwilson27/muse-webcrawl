Externship

Externship
TypeExperiential learning placement
Typical fieldsLaw, medicine, business, education
DurationUsually days to a semester
RelatedInternship, job shadowing, clerkship

Externships are experiential learning opportunities, similar to internships but generally shorter in duration, provided by educational institutions to give students short practical experiences in their field of study. The term is especially common in legal education, where law school externships place students with courts, government agencies, non-profit organisations and, in some cases, private firms for academic credit under faculty supervision.

Comparison with internships

Unlike internships, which involve working actively for an employer over weeks or months, externships often emphasise observation and job shadowing — sometimes lasting only a few days — though semester-long, credit-bearing externships in law and medicine involve substantial supervised work. Externships are frequently unpaid and undertaken for academic credit, whereas internships are more often paid employment-like positions.

In specific fields

  • Law: judicial externships (clerking for a judge) and agency externships are a standard part of many JD programmes in the United States.
  • Medicine: "externship" may refer to clinical rotations undertaken at an institution other than one's own medical school, particularly by international medical graduates seeking US residency experience.
  • Business and education: short shadowing placements for students and for teachers learning about industry workplaces.
