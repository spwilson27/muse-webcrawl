Zachary Taylor

Zachary Taylor (24 November 1784 – 9 July 1850) was an American military leader and statesman who served as the 12th president of the United States from 1849 until his death in 1850. A career army officer, "Old Rough and Ready" became a national hero for his victories in the Mexican–American War, including the Battle of Buena Vista. The Whig Party drafted the politically inexperienced Taylor as its 1848 presidential candidate. As president he confronted the crisis over slavery in the territories won from Mexico, urging California and New Mexico to enter the Union as free states; he died suddenly, probably of a gastrointestinal illness, after sixteen months in office.

President Zachary Taylor

Military career

Taylor joined the army in 1808 and served in the War of 1812, the Black Hawk War, and the Second Seminole War, earning his nickname for his indifference to uniforms and hardship. In 1846, as war with Mexico began, he won victories at Palo Alto, Resaca de la Palma and Monterrey. In February 1847, outnumbered at Buena Vista, he repelled Santa Anna's army—the triumph that made him a leading presidential prospect despite his having never voted in an election.

Presidency

Taylor encouraged settlers in California and New Mexico to draft state constitutions bypassing the territorial stage, anticipating they would exclude slavery. His stance enraged Southern leaders and complicated Henry Clay's efforts at a compromise. In July 1850, after ceremonies at the Washington Monument on a hot Independence Day, Taylor fell ill—tradition blames cherries and iced milk—and died on 9 July, probably of cholera morbus or acute gastroenteritis. Fillmore succeeded him and signed the Compromise of 1850 that Taylor had resisted. In 1991 Taylor's remains were exhumed and tested, ruling out long-rumoured arsenic poisoning.
