Vega (Italian: Vettore Europeo di Generazione Avanzata, "European Vector of Advanced Generation") was a European expendable small-lift launch vehicle developed by the Italian Space Agency (ASI) and the European Space Agency (ESA), built by Avio and operated by Arianespace from the Guiana Space Centre in Kourou, French Guiana. It was designed to carry small satellites — payloads of roughly 300 to 2,500 kg — into polar and low Earth orbits, complementing the heavier Ariane and Soyuz launchers flown from Kourou.

Design

Vega had four stages: three solid-propellant stages (the P80 first stage, Zefiro 23 second stage, and Zefiro 9 third stage) topped by a liquid-propellant AVUM upper stage capable of multiple restarts for precise orbit insertion and multiple-payload deployment. The P80's filament-wound carbon-epoxy motor casing was, at its introduction, the largest single-piece solid rocket motor of its type.

Operational history

Vega's qualification flight launched on 13 February 2012 carrying the LARES satellite and several CubeSats. It went on to fly Earth-observation missions such as Sentinel-2 and the PRISMA and Aeolus satellites, accumulating a record of successes marred by two failures: the 2019 loss of the UAE's FalconEye 1 and the 2020 loss of the Spanish SEOSat-Ingenio and French TARANIS satellites, the latter traced to incorrectly connected cables in the AVUM stage. The original Vega flew its final mission in September 2024; its role passed to the more powerful Vega C, which entered service in July 2022.
