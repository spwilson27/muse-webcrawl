Western Satraps
Existedc. 35 – 405 CE
TerritoryMalwa, Gujarat, Saurashtra, parts of Rajasthan and Maharashtra
Notable rulerRudradaman I

The Western Satraps, or Western Kshatrapas, were Saka rulers who governed a large territory in western India from about 35 to 405 CE. They were contemporaries and frequent rivals of the Satavahanas, and at different times contended with the Kushan Empire to the north.

History

The dynasty's most famous king, Rudradaman I (r. c. 130–150 CE), is known from the Junagadh rock inscription, among the earliest major Sanskrit inscriptions, which records his victories and his repair of the Sudarshana lake dam. The Western Satraps issued a long-lived silver coinage modelled on Indo-Greek drachms, which circulated widely in the region's trade.

Decline

The last Western Satrap ruler was defeated around 405 CE by Chandragupta II of the Gupta Empire, ending nearly four centuries of Saka rule in western India.
