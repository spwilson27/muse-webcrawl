Unit injector

A unit injector (UI) is a high-pressure integrated direct fuel injection system for diesel engines, combining the injector nozzle and an injection pump in a single component in each cylinder. The absence of high-pressure fuel lines allows for much higher injection pressures — up to about 2,200 bar (220 MPa) — than earlier pump-line-nozzle systems.

Operation

The unit injector is usually driven by the engine's camshaft, either directly or via a rocker arm. An electronic control unit meters fuelling by means of a solenoid or piezoelectric spill valve, timing injection and controlling quantity. Volkswagen Group marketed its version as the Pumpe Düse system, used widely in its TDI diesel cars until common-rail systems took over.

Applications

Unit injectors have been used in heavy trucks and locomotives by manufacturers such as Detroit Diesel and Caterpillar, as well as in passenger-car diesel engines. Their disadvantages include noise from high mechanical drive forces, packaging constraints, and cost relative to common rail.
