Crown jewels
Objects: Crowns, sceptres, orbs, regalia
Symbol of: Monarchy, sovereignty

Crown jewels are the ceremonial objects — crowns, sceptres, orbs, swords, rings and robes — belonging to a monarch or state, symbolising royal authority and continuity. Many collections also include historic plate and vestments used at coronations.

By country

The Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom, kept at the Tower of London, include St Edward's Crown, the Imperial State Crown, and the Cullinan diamonds set in sceptre and crown. France's remaining jewels are displayed at the Louvre after most were sold in 1887. Denmark, Sweden, the Netherlands, Iran (at the Central Bank in Tehran) and Thailand maintain notable collections.

Loss and dispersal

Revolutions, wars and sales have scattered many collections: England's medieval regalia were destroyed under Oliver Cromwell, Russia's were partly sold by the Soviet government, and Austria's are preserved at the Imperial Treasury in Vienna.

Significance

Beyond material value, crown jewels embody legitimacy; their capture or destruction has historically marked regime change.
