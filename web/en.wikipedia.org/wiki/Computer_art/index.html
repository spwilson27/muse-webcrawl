Computer art

Computer art is any art in which computers play a role in producing or displaying the artwork. Produced art can be digital works displayed on screen, or traditional works (such as prints, sculptures, or installations) whose creation involves computers. Computer art often involves algorithmically generated material, as in fractal art and generative art, and includes fields such as digital painting, computer animation, and demoscene graphics.

History

Early experiments date to the 1950s, when Ben Laposky produced oscilloscope "Oscillon" images (1950) and researchers at Bell Labs, including A. Michael Noll, began producing computer graphics. The first exhibitions of computer art were held in 1965 at the Technische Hochschule in Stuttgart (works by Georg Nees) and at the Howard Wise Gallery in New York. The Cybernetic Serendipity exhibition at London's Institute of Contemporary Arts in 1968 brought the field wide attention. The development of personal computers, painting software, and later the internet and AI image generators greatly expanded the practice.

Forms

Computer art encompasses algorithmic and generative art, digital painting and photo manipulation, 3D computer graphics and animation, interactive and internet art, and pixel art.
