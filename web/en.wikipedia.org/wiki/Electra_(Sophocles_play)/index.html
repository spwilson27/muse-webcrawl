Electra (Sophocles play)

Electra
Written by: Sophocles
Chorus: Women of Mycenae
Characters: Electra, Orestes, Clytemnestra, Chrysothemis, Aegisthus, Pylades, the Paedagogus
Place premiered: Athens
Date premiered: c. 420–410 BC

Electra (Ancient Greek: Ἠλέκτρα, Ēléktra) is a Greek tragedy by Sophocles, probably written in the late 410s BC. It dramatises the return of Orestes to Mycenae to avenge the murder of his father Agamemnon, focusing on the suffering and resolve of his sister Electra.

Plot

Electra, daughter of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra, lives in misery in the palace at Mycenae, where her mother and her mother's lover Aegisthus rule after murdering Agamemnon. She mourns openly and waits for her exiled brother Orestes to return and take revenge. Orestes arrives with Pylades and his aged tutor, who spreads a false report of Orestes' death in a chariot accident. Electra, believing her brother dead, despairs; but when an urn said to contain his ashes is brought in, brother and sister recognise each other in one of the play's most famous scenes. Orestes then enters the palace and kills Clytemnestra, and the play ends as Aegisthus returns to meet his own death.

Themes and reception

The play treats the same story as Aeschylus' Libation Bearers and Euripides' Electra, but centres it on Electra's character — her grief, endurance and moral isolation — rather than on the ethical dilemma of matricide. It has been praised for its psychological intensity and its recognition scene, and it influenced later treatments of the myth, including Hugo von Hofmannsthal's play and Richard Strauss's opera Elektra.
