Diesel-electric powertrain

A diesel-electric powertrain (or drivetrain) transmits power from a diesel engine to the wheels or propeller through electrical machinery rather than a mechanical gearbox: the engine drives a generator or alternator, and the electricity powers traction motors. The arrangement is a form of series hybrid transmission even when no battery is fitted.

Advantages

Electric transmission lets the diesel run at efficient speeds independent of road or track speed, delivers high torque from standstill, avoids a complex multi-ratio gearbox, and permits flexible placement of machinery. Regenerative or dynamic braking can be incorporated.

Applications

Diesel-electric transmission dominates mainline railway locomotives, and is used in large mining trucks, ships and submarines, buses and some heavy off-road vehicles. Ferdinand Porsche's early twentieth-century designs were among the first applications.
