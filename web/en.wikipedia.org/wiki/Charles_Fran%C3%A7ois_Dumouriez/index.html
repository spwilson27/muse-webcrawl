Charles-François du Périer Dumouriez (1739–1823) was a French general during the French Revolutionary Wars. As commander of the Army of the North, together with Kellermann he won the Battle of Valmy (20 September 1792), which saved the young French Republic, and went on to victory at Jemappes, conquering the Austrian Netherlands.

Career

Dumouriez had served as a soldier, spy and diplomat under the Ancien Régime, and became foreign minister in 1792, helping push France into war with Austria. After the execution of Louis XVI and his defeat at Neerwinden in March 1793, he tried to march on Paris to restore a constitutional monarchy; when his troops refused, he defected to the Austrians in April 1793, handing over the commissioners sent to arrest him.

Exile

He spent the rest of his life in exile, advising various governments, and died in England in 1823.
