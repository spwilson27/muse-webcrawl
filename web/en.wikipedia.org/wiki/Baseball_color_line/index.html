Baseball color line


Jackie Robinson

The baseball color line was the unwritten exclusion of players of Black African descent from Major League Baseball and its affiliated minor leagues in the United States. Never formally codified in league rules, it was maintained from the late 19th century by a "gentlemen's agreement" among owners until Jackie Robinson's debut with the Brooklyn Dodgers on 15 April 1947.

Origins

Black players had appeared in the early professional game: Moses Fleetwood Walker played for the Toledo Blue Stockings of the American Association in 1884, and his brother Welday and pitcher George Stovey were prominent contemporaries. But in 1867 the National Association of Base Ball Players had already voted to exclude clubs with Black members, and by the 1890s pressure from players such as Cap Anson and owners' mutual understandings had driven Black players from organised white baseball. Barred athletes instead built their own institutions, culminating in the Negro leagues founded under Rube Foster in 1920.

Breaking the line

Commissioner Kenesaw Mountain Landis, in office from 1920 to 1944, was widely accused of blocking integration. After his death, Happy Chandler proved more permissive, and Dodgers executive Branch Rickey signed Robinson in 1945, testing him with the Montreal Royals before his 1947 debut. Larry Doby integrated the American League weeks later. Integration proceeded unevenly — the Boston Red Sox were the last team to field a Black player, in 1959 — and the end of the colour line also drained the Negro leagues of talent, leading to their decline.
