Paul Lévy (mathematician)

Paul Pierre Lévy (15 September 1886 – 15 December 1971) was a French mathematician who made fundamental contributions to probability theory, including stable distributions, martingales and the study of Brownian motion.

Born15 September 1886, Paris, France
Died15 December 1971, Paris
FieldsProbability theory

Work

Lévy was a professor at the École Polytechnique from 1920 to 1959. His book Calcul des probabilités (1925) and later works introduced the characteristic-function approach to limit theorems. Concepts named for him include the Lévy distribution, Lévy flights, Lévy processes, Lévy's continuity theorem and Lévy's arcsine law.

Contributions

With Jarl Lindeberg and William Feller, Lévy established the modern theory of stable laws. His Processus stochastiques et mouvement brownien (1948) deeply influenced the study of stochastic processes and Brownian motion.
