Lumbar spinal stenosis
SpecialtyOrthopedics, neurosurgery, rheumatology
SymptomsLower back pain, leg pain and numbness on walking (neurogenic claudication)

Lumbar spinal stenosis (LSS) is a medical condition in which the spinal canal narrows in the lower (lumbar) back and compresses the nerves and blood vessels of the cauda equina and exiting nerve roots. It is most commonly caused by degenerative, age-related changes and is a leading cause of spinal surgery in older adults.

Signs and symptoms

The hallmark symptom is neurogenic claudication: pain, heaviness, numbness or weakness in the buttocks and legs that comes on with standing and walking and is relieved by sitting or bending forward (as when leaning on a shopping cart). This distinguishes it from vascular claudication, which is relieved simply by rest. Back pain, sciatica-like radicular pain and, rarely, bladder or bowel dysfunction may also occur.

Causes

Degenerative stenosis results from a combination of intervertebral disc bulging and height loss, facet joint osteoarthritis and hypertrophy, and thickening (buckling) of the ligamentum flavum. Less common causes include congenital short pedicles, achondroplasia, Paget's disease, tumours and trauma. The narrowing may be central, in the lateral recess, or in the intervertebral foramen.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is based on the clinical history and confirmed by MRI, which shows the narrowed canal and compressed thecal sac. Initial treatment is conservative: exercise and physiotherapy, weight management, simple analgesia and neuropathic pain agents, and sometimes epidural steroid injections. For persistent disabling symptoms, surgical decompression — usually a laminectomy, with or without fusion — can improve leg pain and walking distance, though benefits vary.
