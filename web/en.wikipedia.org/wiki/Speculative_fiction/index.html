Speculative fiction

Speculative fiction is a broad category of fiction encompassing genres with elements that do not exist in the real world, recorded history, nature, or the present universe. Such fiction covers various themes in the context of science fiction, fantasy, horror, alternate history, and other genres.

Speculative fiction
Major genres: Science fiction, Fantasy, Horror, Alternate history, Utopian and dystopian fiction
Related: Weird fiction, Slipstream

Definition

The term has been used as an umbrella for works that depart from realism, and sometimes as a more literary alternative to the label science fiction. Writers such as Margaret Atwood have used it to describe works including science fiction elements set in a recognisably real world, while others apply it to all fiction that asks "what if?"

History

Antecedents include ancient mythology, Lucian's True History, and early modern utopias. The modern genres coalesced in the pulp magazines of the early 20th century, notably Hugo Gernsback's science fiction magazines. In the 1960s and 1970s the New Wave and writers associated with Dangerous Visions (1967), edited by Harlan Ellison, promoted "speculative fiction" as a term for experimental, socially engaged work.

Genres

Subgenres include fantasy, horror, alternate history, apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic fiction, superhero fiction, and dystopian fiction. Cross-genre hybrids are common.
