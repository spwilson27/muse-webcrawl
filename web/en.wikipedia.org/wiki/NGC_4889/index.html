NGC 4889

Constellation: Coma Berenices
Type: cD; E4
Distance: ~300 million light-years
Group: Coma Cluster
Other name: Caldwell 35

NGC 4889 (also known as Caldwell 35) is a supergiant elliptical galaxy in the constellation Coma Berenices. It is the brightest galaxy in the Coma Cluster and lies roughly 300 million light-years away. Together with NGC 4874 it dominates the cluster's core.

Central black hole

NGC 4889 is notable for hosting one of the largest supermassive black holes known. In 2011, astronomers using the Hubble Space Telescope and ground-based observatories measured its mass at about 21 billion solar masses — comparable to the most massive black holes ever measured. Despite this, the black hole is currently quiescent, and stars at the galaxy's centre move in a relatively quiet environment.

Characteristics

The galaxy is a class cD elliptical with an extended diffuse envelope. It was discovered by William Herschel in 1785. Its enormous globular cluster system and hot X-ray-emitting halo are typical of the giant galaxies that grow at cluster centres through mergers.
