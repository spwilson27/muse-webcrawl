Fuerteventura

LocationCanary Islands, Spain
Nearest toAfrica (~100 km)
Known forBeaches, wind sports
[Image: Corralejo dunes and the island of Lobos]
Corralejo dunes and the island of Lobos

Fuerteventura is the second-largest of Spain's Canary Islands and the closest to Africa, some 100 km from Morocco. It is the oldest Canary island geologically and famed for endless white-sand beaches and wind sports.

Character

The great dune sea of Corralejo in the north, the surf and kite beaches of the Jandía peninsula in the south, and the old capital Betancuria in a green inland valley (founded 1404 by Jean de Béthencourt, for whom the island's name "strong fortune" is often explained) structure the island. Constant trade winds made it a windsurfing and kitesurfing world-cup venue; its arid interior of lava fields and goat herds contrasts with the resort coasts of Corralejo, Caleta de Fuste and Costa Calma. The whole island is a UNESCO biosphere reserve.
