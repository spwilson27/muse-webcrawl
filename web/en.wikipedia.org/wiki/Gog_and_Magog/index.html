Gog and Magog

RoleEschatological enemies of God's people

Gog and Magog (Hebrew: גוג ומגוג) appear in the Hebrew Bible and the Quran as names associated with an eschatological enemy of God's people. In the Book of Ezekiel, Gog is a prince of the land of Magog who leads a vast invasion of Israel in the latter days and is destroyed by divine intervention. In the New Testament Book of Revelation, "Gog and Magog" together denote the nations summoned by Satan for the final battle after the millennium.

In Jewish and Christian tradition

Ezekiel chapters 38–39 describe Gog's invasion and defeat, followed by a great burial of the slain. Josephus identified Magog with the Scythians, and later interpreters variously equated the pair with the Goths, Huns, Mongols, Turks, and other peoples pressing on the settled world. In the Alexander legends, Alexander the Great was said to have built a great gate or wall in the Caucasus to pen in Gog and Magog until the end of time.

In Islam

In the Quran, Ya'juj and Ma'juj (Arabic: يأجوج ومأجوج) are a destructive people restrained by a barrier of iron and copper built by Dhu al-Qarnayn (often identified with Alexander). Their release is a sign of the approaching Day of Judgement; in Islamic eschatology they will swarm over the earth after the coming of Jesus (Isa) and be destroyed by God's decree.

British folklore

In London tradition, Gog and Magog are also the names of two giant wicker effigies — guardians of the City of London — carried in the Lord Mayor's Show since the 16th century; the current figures date from 1953, replacing ones destroyed in the Blitz.
