Beihai Park

Beihai Park is a public park and former imperial garden in the Imperial City, Beijing, China. First built in the 12th century, it is among the oldest and best-preserved imperial gardens in China. The park covers about 71 hectares, more than half of it a lake, and is dominated by Jade Flower Island (Qiónghuá-dǎo), topped by the White Dagoba.

History

The lake was excavated and palaces built under the Jurchen Jin dynasty in the 1160s–1170s, modelled on the legendary Isles of the Immortals in the Bohai Sea. Kublai Khan made the island the centre of his Yuan dynasty capital, Dadu. The Ming and Qing dynasties repeatedly rebuilt and embellished the garden; the Qianlong Emperor undertook particularly extensive works in the 18th century.

Features

The White Dagoba (Bái Tǎ), a Tibetan-style stupa about 40 metres high, was first built in 1651 to honour a visit by the 5th Dalai Lama and rebuilt after earthquake damage. Other landmarks include the Nine-Dragon Wall (1761), with colourful glazed dragons; the Five Dragon Pavilions on the northern shore; the Round City (Tuánchéng) near the south gate, holding a huge jade bowl of the Yuan dynasty; and numerous temples and pavilions around the lake. The park opened to the public in 1925.
