Ivy Mike

Ivy Mike was the codename given to the first successful test of a full-scale thermonuclear device, detonated by the United States at Enewetak Atoll in the Pacific on November 1, 1952 (local time). It was the first test of a hydrogen bomb employing the Teller–Ulam staged design, and one of two tests in Operation Ivy.

The device

Nicknamed "the Sausage", the device used a fission primary to ignite a secondary filled with liquid deuterium, requiring a large cryogenic plant. It weighed some 82 tonnes and was housed in a multi-storey aluminium building on the island of Elugelab — an experimental apparatus rather than a deliverable weapon.

Yield and aftermath

The explosion yielded 10.4 megatons of TNT, far exceeding predictions, and completely vaporised Elugelab, leaving an underwater crater nearly two kilometres wide. Debris collected from the fallout led to the discovery of two new elements, einsteinium and fermium. The Soviet Union tested a deliverable thermonuclear weapon less than a year later, escalating the arms race.
