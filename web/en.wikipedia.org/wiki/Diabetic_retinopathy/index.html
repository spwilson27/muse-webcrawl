Diabetic retinopathy

Diabetic retinopathy is a complication of diabetes that affects the eyes. It is caused by damage to the blood vessels of the light-sensitive tissue at the back of the eye (retina). Initially it may cause no symptoms or only mild vision problems, but eventually it can cause blindness.

Diabetic retinopathy
SpecialtyOphthalmology
SymptomsOften none early; later blurred vision, floaters, vision loss
CausesLong-standing diabetes mellitus
PreventionBlood sugar control, regular eye screening

Stages

In non-proliferative retinopathy, weakened retinal vessels leak fluid and blood, forming microaneurysms and exudates, and the macula may swell (diabetic macular oedema). In proliferative retinopathy, abnormal new vessels grow on the retina; these may bleed into the vitreous or cause retinal detachment.

Diagnosis

It is detected by dilated eye examination and fundus photography; screening programmes photograph the retinas of people with diabetes regularly. Optical coherence tomography assesses macular swelling.

Treatment

Management includes tight control of blood glucose, blood pressure and lipids. Laser photocoagulation can seal leaking vessels and shrink abnormal ones. Intravitreal injections of anti-VEGF agents such as ranibizumab or aflibercept, and vitrectomy surgery, are used in advanced disease.

Epidemiology

It affects up to 80 percent of those who have had diabetes for 20 years or more and is a leading cause of blindness in working-age adults in developed countries.
