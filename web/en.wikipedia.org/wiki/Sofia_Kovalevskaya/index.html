Sofia Kovalevskaya

Sofia Vasilyevna Kovalevskaya (Russian: Софья Васильевна Ковалевская; 15 January 1850 – 10 February 1891) was a Russian mathematician who made significant contributions to analysis, partial differential equations and mechanics. She was a pioneer for women in science – the first woman to obtain a doctorate in mathematics in modern Europe and the first appointed to a full professorship in Northern Europe.

Life

Barred from Russian universities, she entered a nominal marriage with Vladimir Kovalevsky to study abroad, attending lectures in Heidelberg and privately studying with Karl Weierstrass in Berlin, earning her doctorate from Göttingen in absentia in 1874. After years without a post, and her husband's death, she was appointed at Stockholm University (1884), becoming full professor in 1889 and editor of Acta Mathematica.

Work

The Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem gives conditions for existence of analytic solutions to partial differential equations. Her 1888 paper on rotation of a rigid body about a fixed point – the Kovalevskaya top, a third integrable case – won the Prix Bordin of the French Academy of Sciences. She also wrote a memoir (A Russian Childhood) and a novel. She died of influenza/pneumonia in 1891, aged 41.
