Shaka era

The Shaka era (also Śaka era or Salivahana era) is a historical Hindu calendar era originating in India, with its epoch (year zero) in 78 CE. Years in the Shaka era are counted from the spring equinox of 78 CE, so a Shaka year is roughly 78–79 years behind the corresponding Gregorian year.

Origins

The era's origin is debated. Tradition associates it with the legendary king Shalivahana and a victory over the Shaka (Indo-Scythian) ruler Vikramaditya. Historically, the epoch of 78 CE coincides with the accession of the Kushan emperor Kanishka by one influential scholarly reckoning, and the era was used extensively by the Western Satraps and later by the Gupta Empire and many medieval Indian dynasties, becoming widespread in inscriptions across India and Southeast Asia.

Use

The Shaka calendar was adopted as India's national civil calendar in 1957, following the recommendations of the Calendar Reform Committee chaired by Meghnad Saha. In its reformed form it is a tropical solar calendar beginning with Chaitra, used alongside the Gregorian calendar in official contexts such as the Gazette of India and All India Radio news broadcasts. Variants of the Shaka era also underpin the traditional calendars of Bali (the Saka calendar) and were used historically in Cambodia, Java and the Philippines.

Conversion

To convert a Shaka year to a Gregorian year, add 78 for dates after the start of the Shaka new year (around 22 March), or 79 for dates before it.
