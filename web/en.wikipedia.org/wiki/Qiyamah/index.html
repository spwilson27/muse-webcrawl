Qiyamah (Arabic: قيامة) is the Islamic doctrine of the resurrection of the dead and the Day of Judgement (Yawm al-Qiyamah), one of the fundamental articles of faith in Islam. Belief in the Last Day follows only belief in God and His angels in the Quran's ordering of the faith, and the Quran treats the certainty of the Hour with repeated, vivid emphasis.

Quranic account

The Quran describes the Hour as arriving suddenly and without forewarning. The trumpet will be blown, the heavens will be rolled up, the mountains scattered like carded wool, and every soul will rise to be judged according to a record of its deeds—the "book" given in the right hand to the saved and behind the back to the damned. Paradise (jannah) and Hell (jahannam) await as the final destinations.

Signs and stages

Hadith literature elaborates a sequence of signs preceding the Hour: the appearance of the Dajjal (Antichrist), the descent of Jesus, the emergence of Gog and Magog, and the rising of the sun from the west. Traditions describe the blowing of the trumpet by the angel Israfil, a period of death and silence, then the general resurrection, the gathering, the weighing of deeds on the scales (mizan), and the crossing of the bridge (sirat) finer than a hair.

Theological significance

Qiyamah grounds Islamic ethics in ultimate accountability; it is listed among the six articles of faith (arkan al-iman). Denial of the resurrection is condemned in the Quran as a mark of the disbelievers. The doctrine parallels the Last Judgement of Christianity and the resurrection themes of Judaism, though with its own detailed eschatological imagery.
