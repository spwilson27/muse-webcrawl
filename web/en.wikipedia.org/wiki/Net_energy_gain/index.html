Net energy gain

Net energy gain (NEG) is a concept used in energy economics that refers to the difference between the energy expended to harvest an energy source and the amount of energy gained from that harvest. The net energy gain, which can be expressed in joules, differs from the net financial gain that may result from an energy harvesting process, in that various sources of energy (e.g. natural gas, coal, etc.) can be priced differently for the same amount of energy.

Net energy gain is the surplus of harvested energy over the energy used to obtain it.

Calculation

A net energy gain is achieved by expending less energy acquiring a source of energy than is contained in the source to be consumed. That is:

NEG = Energyconsumable − Energyexpenditure

Factors to consider when calculating NEG are the type of energy, the way energy is used and acquired, and the methods used to store or transport the energy. It is also possible to complicate the calculation by a weighting of the energy qualities, which can be done using thermodynamic principles or by considering the maximum work that could be extracted.

Energy sources

When evaluating energy sources, all energy inputs over the full life cycle must be accounted, including exploration, extraction, refining, transport, and decommissioning. Conventional oil production historically had a very high NEG, while sources such as oil shale and some biofuels have much lower, sometimes disputed, values. The related concept of energy returned on energy invested (EROEI) expresses the same balance as a ratio rather than a difference.
