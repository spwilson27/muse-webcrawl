Prithviraja III (IAST: Pṛthvī-rāja; c. 1166 – 1192), popularly known as Prithviraj Chauhan, was a Rajput king from the Chahamana (Chauhan) dynasty who ruled Sapadalaksha, centred on Ajmer in present-day Rajasthan, and later also Delhi. He is celebrated in Indian tradition for resisting the Ghurid invasions of Muhammad of Ghor.

Reign

Prithviraj inherited the throne as a minor around 1178 and expanded Chahamana power against neighbouring kingdoms, including the Chandelas of Jejakabhukti and the Chaulukyas of Gujarat. His court at Ajmer, and subsequently Delhi, was a centre of Sanskrit learning; the court poet Jayanaka composed the Prithviraja Vijaya in his honour.

Battles of Tarain

At the First Battle of Tarain (1191), Prithviraj defeated Muhammad of Ghor, who was wounded and withdrew. Muhammad returned the next year with a larger force and, at the Second Battle of Tarain (1192), decisively defeated and captured Prithviraj, who was executed. The victory opened northern India to Ghurid — and soon Delhi Sultanate — rule.

Legend

The later epic Prithviraj Raso, attributed to his court poet Chand Bardai, embellishes his life with the elopement of Sanyogita and tales of revenge against Muhammad; historians treat much of it as legend, but it fixed Prithviraj's image as a heroic last defender in popular memory.
