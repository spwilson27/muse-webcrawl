Potter Palmer

Potter Palmer
Born: May 20, 1826, Albany County, New York
Died: May 4, 1902, Chicago
Occupation: businessman, real-estate developer
Spouse: Bertha Honoré Palmer

Potter Palmer (May 20, 1826 – May 4, 1902) was an American businessman and real-estate developer who transformed Chicago's State Street into a great shopping district and built the Palmer House hotel.

Career

Palmer opened a dry-goods store in Chicago in 1852, pioneering practices such as money-back guarantees and elaborate window displays. He sold a controlling interest to Marshall Field and others, then turned to real estate, developing State Street.

Palmer House and the fire

His first Palmer House hotel opened in 1870 as a wedding present to his wife Bertha and burned thirteen days after its grand opening in the Great Chicago Fire of 1871. Palmer borrowed heavily to rebuild a grander, fireproof hotel on the same site.

Later life

The Palmers' Lake Shore Drive mansion anchored Chicago's Gold Coast. Palmer is buried in Graceland Cemetery.
