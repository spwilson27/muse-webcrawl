2006 Lebanon War

Date12 July – 14 August 2006
LocationLebanon and northern Israel
BelligerentsIsrael; Hezbollah
ResultCeasefire under UN Security Council Resolution 1701
Casualties~1,200 Lebanese and 165 Israelis killed (estimates vary)

The 2006 Lebanon War, also known as the July War in Lebanon and the Second Lebanon War in Israel, was a 34-day military conflict in Lebanon and northern Israel between Israel and the Shia militant group Hezbollah. The conflict began on 12 July 2006 when Hezbollah fighters crossed the border, attacked an Israeli patrol, killed three soldiers and captured two others, Ehud Goldwasser and Eldad Regev.

Course of the war

Israel responded with airstrikes on Hezbollah positions and Lebanese infrastructure, a naval and air blockade, and eventually a ground invasion of southern Lebanon. Hezbollah fired several thousand rockets into northern Israel, reaching as far as Haifa. Heavy fighting took place in towns such as Maroun al-Ras, Bint Jbeil and Ayta ash-Shab, and Israeli forces struggled to suppress the rocket fire. An Israeli airstrike on a building in Qana on 30 July killed at least 28 civilians, drawing widespread international condemnation.

Ceasefire and aftermath

The war ended with UN Security Council Resolution 1701, which took effect on 14 August 2006 and called for the deployment of the Lebanese Army and an expanded UNIFIL force in the south. Approximately 1,200 Lebanese and 165 Israelis were killed. In Israel, the Winograd Commission criticised the political and military leadership's conduct of the war; Hezbollah leader Hassan Nasrallah claimed a "divine victory" while later admitting he would not have ordered the capture operation had he anticipated the scale of Israel's response. The bodies of the two captured soldiers were returned in a 2008 prisoner exchange.
