Hyperbolic excess velocity

In astrodynamics and celestial mechanics, the hyperbolic excess velocity or v-infinity (v∞) is the velocity of an object travelling on a hyperbolic trajectory relative to a central body, measured when the object is at an infinite distance from the body and the body's gravitational influence has become negligible. It is the speed the object retains "at infinity" after escaping the body's gravity well.

A hyperbolic trajectory with its asymptotic excess velocity v∞.

Calculation

The hyperbolic excess velocity is related to the specific orbital energy and the characteristic energy C3 by v∞2 = C3 = −μ/a, where μ is the standard gravitational parameter and a is the (negative) semi-major axis of the hyperbola. For a spacecraft departing a planet, the excess velocity determines the heliocentric trajectory it will follow once outside the planet's sphere of influence.

Applications

In patched-conic mission design, v∞ is the interface quantity between the planet-centred and Sun-centred legs of an interplanetary trajectory. In a gravity assist, the magnitude of the incoming and outgoing hyperbolic excess velocities relative to the assisting planet are equal (in the planet's frame), while the direction changes, altering the spacecraft's heliocentric energy. Incoming interstellar objects such as 1I/ʻOumuamua and 2I/Borisov are characterised by their hyperbolic excess velocity relative to the Sun — about 26 km/s and 32 km/s respectively.
