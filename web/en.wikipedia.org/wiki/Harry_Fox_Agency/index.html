The Harry Fox Agency (HFA) is a provider of rights-management, licensing, and royalty services for the U.S. music industry. It was founded in 1927 by Harry Fox, an agent for theatrical and music publishers, and historically acted as the principal licensing agent for mechanical licences — the licences required to reproduce and distribute musical compositions on records, CDs, and downloads — on behalf of music publishers, collecting and distributing mechanical royalties to its affiliated publishers.

History

HFA was established as a subsidiary of the National Music Publishers' Association (NMPA). For most of the twentieth century, anyone wishing to record a cover version of a published song obtained a compulsory mechanical licence under U.S. copyright law, and HFA issued the great majority of these licences and processed the statutory royalty payments. In 2015 the performing-rights organisation SESAC acquired HFA from the NMPA.

Services

Beyond mechanical licensing, HFA has provided royalty examination, catalogue administration, and rights databases — including its Songfile service for online licensing of limited quantities — and services supporting digital music distribution and streaming. Following the U.S. Music Modernization Act of 2018, the Mechanical Licensing Collective assumed blanket licensing for streaming services, while HFA continued to serve publishers with licensing and administration offerings.
