Advocate

An advocate is a professional in the field of law. Different countries and legal systems use the term with somewhat varying meanings. In many civil-law jurisdictions, an advocate is a lawyer who specialises in representing clients in court; in Scots law, advocates are the equivalent of barristers in England and Wales, members of the Faculty of Advocates with rights of audience in the higher courts.

[Image: an advocate in robes addressing a court]

In different jurisdictions

  • Scotland — Advocates are members of the Faculty of Advocates, instructed mainly through solicitors, and appear in the Court of Session and High Court of Justiciary; senior members may become King's Counsel.
  • England and Wales (historical) — "Advocates" practised in the ecclesiastical and admiralty courts at Doctors' Commons until the nineteenth century; court representation is now the province of barristers and solicitor-advocates.
  • South Africa — Advocates form the referral Bar, broadly equivalent to barristers, in a profession split between advocates and attorneys.
  • India and Pakistan — Lawyers enrolled with a Bar Council are styled advocates; senior advocates are designated by the courts.
  • Scandinavia and civil-law Europe — The title (e.g. Danish and Norwegian advokat, French avocat, German Rechtsanwalt in function) is the standard protected title for a practising lawyer.

Advocate General

In the Court of Justice of the European Union, Advocates General are senior legal officers who deliver independent, reasoned opinions to assist the Court; the title is also used for government law officers in Scotland (the Lord Advocate) and for the chief legal adviser in some other systems.
