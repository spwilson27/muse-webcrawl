Potto


Labelled image placeholder: potto (Perodicticus potto)

The potto (Perodicticus potto) is a strepsirrhine primate of the family Lorisidae. It is native to the tropical forests of West and Central Africa and is closely related to the angwantibos and the lorises of Asia. The name "potto" possibly comes from the Wolof word pata.

Description

The potto grows to a length of about 30–40 cm, with a short tail of 3–10 cm, and weighs between 850 and 1,600 grams. It has dense, woolly brown fur, large forward-facing eyes adapted to nocturnal life, and strongly developed thumbs and big toes that give it a vice-like grip. Uniquely, the cervical vertebrae have elongated spines that protrude through the skin of the neck, forming a defensive shield of blunt points when the animal curls up against predators.

Behaviour

Pottos are strictly nocturnal and arboreal, moving slowly and deliberately through the canopy and almost never descending to the ground. They feed mainly on fruit, supplemented by tree gum and insects. They are solitary, communicate by scent marking with urine, and sleep during the day curled in dense foliage. Females give birth to a single infant (occasionally twins) after a gestation of about six months.

Conservation

The species is widespread and listed as Least Concern overall, although local populations are threatened by deforestation and hunting for bushmeat. A gland on the inside of the elbow secretes a pungent fluid that the potto licks onto its neck spines, possibly adding an irritant defence.
