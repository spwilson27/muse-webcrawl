Macon, Georgia

Macon, officially Macon–Bibb County, is a consolidated city-county in the U.S. state of Georgia. Located near the state's geographic centre, on the Ocmulgee River, it had a population of about 157,000 at the 2020 census and is known as the "Heart of Georgia".

History

The area was inhabited for thousands of years by Indigenous peoples; the Ocmulgee Mounds, now a National Historical Park, preserve earthworks built around 1000 CE by the Mississippian culture. The modern city was chartered in 1823 across the river from Fort Hawkins.

Culture

Macon has a rich musical heritage: it was home to Little Richard and Otis Redding, and Capricorn Records, the label behind the Allman Brothers Band, was based there. The city also hosts an International Cherry Blossom Festival each spring.
