CYP3A4

Cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4) is the most abundant cytochrome P450 enzyme in the human liver and intestine and the single most important drug-metabolizing enzyme in clinical pharmacology. It is encoded by the CYP3A4 gene on chromosome 7 and participates in the metabolism of roughly half of all marketed drugs.

Function

CYP3A4 has a large, flexible active site that accommodates structurally diverse substrates, including many statins, calcium channel blockers, benzodiazepines, macrolide antibiotics, immunosuppressants such as ciclosporin and tacrolimus, and HIV protease inhibitors. In the intestinal wall it contributes substantially to first-pass metabolism, limiting the oral bioavailability of many compounds.

Interactions

Because of its central role, CYP3A4 is a major source of drug–drug interactions. Potent inhibitors include ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir, and grapefruit juice (whose furanocoumarins irreversibly inactivate the intestinal enzyme). Inducers such as rifampicin, carbamazepine, and St John's wort increase expression via the pregnane X receptor and can cause therapeutic failure of substrates. The related enzyme CYP3A5 is expressed polymorphically and contributes variably to the same pathways.
