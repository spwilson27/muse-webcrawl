Johannes Ciconia

Johannes Ciconia (c. 1370 – between 10 June and 13 July 1412) was an important Franco-Flemish composer and music theorist of the late medieval era. He worked in Italy, particularly in Padua, and his music bridges the styles of the ars subtilior and the early Renaissance.

Life

Ciconia was born in Liège, the son of a priest also named Johannes Ciconia. By the 1390s he was in Italy, in the service of Cardinal Francesco Zabarella in Padua, where he became a singer and later a canon at the cathedral. He remained in Padua for the rest of his life.

Music

His output includes Mass movements, motets (several written for civic and ecclesiastical occasions in Padua and Venice), French virelais and ballades, and Italian madrigals and ballate. His ceremonial motet O felix templum jubila was written for the dedication of Padua's cathedral. Ciconia also wrote treatises on music theory, including Nova musica and De proportionibus.

Significance

Ciconia's synthesis of French and Italian styles, and his clear, melodious writing, made him one of the most influential composers of his generation, anticipating the international style of Guillaume Du Fay.
