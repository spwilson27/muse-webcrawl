History of education

The history of education extends from informal transmission of knowledge in prehistoric societies to the formal schools of the ancient world, the universities of medieval Europe and the Islamic world, and the mass public schooling systems created in the 19th and 20th centuries, which made education a near-universal experience.

Ancient education

Sumerian and Egyptian scribal schools (from around 3000 BCE) trained administrators in writing and reckoning. In China, Confucian teaching emphasised moral cultivation and mastery of classical texts; imperial examinations, systematised under the Sui and Tang dynasties, selected officials for over a thousand years. India's gurukula system and the monastic university of Nalanda drew students from across Asia. In Greece, Sparta's agoge trained soldiers, while Athens developed private teachers — sophists, Socrates, Plato's Academy and Aristotle's Lyceum — whose ideas on liberal education shaped Western thought. Rome adapted Greek models to rhetoric and law.

Medieval and early modern education

In the Islamic world, madrasas endowed from the 11th century (such as the Nizamiyya) taught law, theology and sciences; mosque schools spread Qur'anic literacy. In Europe, cathedral and monastic schools preserved learning until the rise of universities at Bologna, Paris and Oxford in the 11th–13th centuries. Renaissance humanism, the printing press (c. 1440) and reformers such as Comenius transformed expectations: Comenius advocated universal schooling and wrote pioneering illustrated textbooks. Religious orders, notably the Jesuits, built extensive school networks.

Mass schooling

Prussia introduced compulsory primary schooling in the 18th century; in the 19th, industrialising states created public systems — Horace Mann's common schools in the United States, England's Education Act 1870, and France's free, compulsory, secular laws of 1881–82. Girls' access expanded slowly and unevenly. Kindergarten (Froebel), Montessori and progressive methods broadened pedagogy. The 20th century brought comprehensive secondary education, mass higher education after 1945, and the 1948 Universal Declaration of Human Rights' proclamation of education as a right. Late-20th and 21st-century developments include international assessments such as PISA, expansion of schooling in the developing world, and digital and online learning.
