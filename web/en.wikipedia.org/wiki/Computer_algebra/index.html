Computer algebra

In computational mathematics, computer algebra, also called symbolic computation or algebraic computation, is a scientific area that refers to the study and development of algorithms and software for manipulating mathematical expressions and other mathematical objects. Whereas numerical computation works with approximate floating-point values, computer algebra works with exact symbolic representations of numbers, variables, and expressions.

Overview

Software that performs symbolic calculations is called a computer algebra system. Such systems can expand and factor polynomials, differentiate and integrate functions symbolically, solve equations exactly, simplify expressions, and manipulate matrices with symbolic entries. Major systems include Mathematica, Maple, MATLAB's Symbolic Math Toolbox, and free systems such as Maxima and SageMath.

History

Computer algebra began in the early 1960s, driven partly by the needs of theoretical physics for symbolic manipulation. Early systems included FORMAC, MATHLAB, and Reduce. The field developed alongside advances in algorithms for polynomial greatest common divisors, factorization, and Gröbner bases.

Methods

Key techniques include algorithms for arithmetic on integers, rational numbers, and polynomials; resultants; factorization over finite fields and the integers; symbolic integration (including the Risch algorithm); and solving polynomial systems. Because symbolic intermediate expressions can grow extremely large ("expression swell"), efficiency and canonical representations are central concerns.
