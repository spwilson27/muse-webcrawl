The Russo-Georgian War was a war between Georgia, on one side, and Russia together with the Russian-backed self-proclaimed republics of South Ossetia and Abkhazia, on the other. It took place in August 2008, following a long period of worsening relations between Russia and Georgia, and is regarded as the first European war of the 21st century.

After heavy fighting in South Ossetia between Georgian forces and South Ossetian separatists, Georgia launched a large-scale military operation against the regional capital, Tskhinvali, on the night of 7–8 August 2008. Russia responded with a large-scale land, air, and sea invasion of Georgia, intervening in South Ossetia and Abkhazia and advancing into undisputed Georgian territory, occupying the cities of Gori, Poti, Senaki, and Zugdidi. A French-brokered ceasefire was agreed on 12 August and signed by both presidents by 16 August.

Background

South Ossetia and Abkhazia had been de facto independent from Georgia since wars in the early 1990s, with Russian "peacekeepers" stationed in both regions and most residents holding Russian passports. Relations deteriorated after Georgia's pro-Western Rose Revolution government under Mikheil Saakashvili pursued NATO membership — a prospect endorsed in principle at the April 2008 Bucharest summit — while Russia recognised Kosovo's independence as a precedent, shelled Georgian positions in the Kodori Gorge disputes, and in spring 2008 increased its forces in Abkhazia. Clashes along the South Ossetian boundary escalated sharply in early August 2008.

Course of the war

Georgian artillery and rocket fire struck Tskhinvali late on 7 August and ground forces entered the city on 8 August. Russian forces of the 58th Army moved through the Roki Tunnel into South Ossetia, destroyed much of the Georgian force in and around Tskhinvali by 10 August, and pushed beyond the region's boundary toward Gori. On the western front, Russian and Abkhaz forces opened a second offensive from Abkhazia, took the Kodori Gorge from Georgia, and occupied positions deep in western Georgia. The Russian Black Sea Fleet sank a Georgian missile boat, and Russian aircraft bombed targets across Georgia, including near Tbilisi.

Ceasefire and aftermath

French President Nicolas Sarkozy, holding the EU presidency, negotiated a six-point ceasefire plan on 12 August. Russian forces withdrew from most undisputed Georgian territory by October 2008 but retained positions in Akhalgori and Perevi and expanded their bases in Abkhazia and South Ossetia. On 26 August Russia recognised both regions as independent states — a step followed by only a handful of countries and condemned by Georgia and most of the international community, which continues to regard the territories as Russian-occupied.

An EU-commissioned fact-finding mission (the Tagliavini Report, 2009) concluded that open hostilities began with Georgia's shelling of Tskhinvali, while also finding that Russia's response went far beyond defence of its peacekeepers, that much Russian action was disproportionate, and that ethnic cleansing of Georgians from South Ossetia had occurred. Georgia's NATO aspirations were set back, and the war is widely seen as an early demonstration of Russia's willingness to use force to defend its claimed sphere of influence.
