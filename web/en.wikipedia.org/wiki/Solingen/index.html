Solingen

Solingen is a city in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, located in the Bergisches Land south of the Ruhr area and east of Düsseldorf. With a population of roughly 160,000, it is known worldwide as the "City of Blades" (Klingenstadt) for its centuries-old production of swords, knives, scissors and razors.

History

Solingen was first mentioned in 1067 and received town privileges in the 14th century. Bladesmiths, hardeners and grinders settled along the Wupper river, whose water powered their grinding stones; by the late Middle Ages Solingen swords were exported across Europe, and the city's name became a mark of quality. The name "Solingen" is legally protected for cutlery made in the city. In November 1944 the old town was largely destroyed in air raids.

In May 1993 Solingen gained international attention for an arson attack by right-wing extremists on the home of a Turkish family, in which five people died — one of the most severe acts of xenophobic violence in post-war Germany.

Industry

Major manufacturers headquartered in Solingen include Zwilling J. A. Henckels and Wüsthof, and the city hosts the German Blade Museum (Deutsches Klingenmuseum). The name of the city is protected by a 1938 ordinance, one of the earliest geographical indications for industrial products.

Sights

Landmarks include Burg Castle (Schloss Burg), the ancestral seat of the counts of Berg; the Müngsten Bridge, Germany's highest railway bridge at 107 metres over the Wupper; and one of the few remaining trolleybus networks in Germany, operating since 1952.
