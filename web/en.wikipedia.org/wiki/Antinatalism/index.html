Antinatalism

Antinatalism
PositionProcreation is morally wrong or unjustified
Notable proponentDavid Benatar

Antinatalism is the philosophical position that assigns a negative value to birth and holds that procreation is morally wrong or unjustifiable. It has ancient roots in Buddhism and pessimist philosophy and has been developed by modern thinkers such as David Benatar.

Arguments

Benatar's asymmetry argument holds that the presence of pain is bad and the presence of pleasure good, but the absence of pain is good even if no one experiences it, while the absence of pleasure is not bad unless someone is deprived of it — so coming into existence is always a net harm. Other arguments appeal to the impossibility of consent from the unborn, the inevitability of suffering, and environmental costs.

Criticism

Critics respond that life's goods can outweigh its harms, that the asymmetry is implausible, and that antinatalism's conclusions conflict with widely held moral intuitions.
