Empress Shōken (昭憲皇太后, Shōken Kōtaigō, 9 May 1849 – 9 April 1914), born Ichijō Masako and later renamed Haruko, was the wife and empress consort of Emperor Meiji of Japan. She was the first imperial consort of the modern era to bear the title kōgō (empress) in public life and played a visible, influential role as a symbol of Japan's modernisation during the Meiji period.

Life

Haruko was the daughter of Ichijō Tadaka, a high-ranking court noble (sadaijin) of the Ichijō branch of the Fujiwara clan. Three years older than the emperor, she was selected as his consort partly for her intelligence and composed character; they were betrothed in 1867 and married in 1869. She had no children of her own, but formally adopted Meiji's son by a concubine, the future Emperor Taishō, and raised him.

Role as empress

Shōken broke with the seclusion traditionally expected of imperial consorts. She accompanied the emperor on public occasions, promoted girls' education and women's charitable work, encouraged the Japanese silk industry, and was a prominent supporter of the Japanese Red Cross; the Empress Shōken Fund, established with her donation in 1912, still supports Red Cross and Red Crescent welfare work worldwide. She composed waka poetry and, during the First Sino-Japanese and Russo-Japanese wars, set an example of wartime austerity and relief work.

On Meiji's death in 1912 she became empress dowager. She died in 1914 at the imperial villa in Numazu and is buried beside the emperor at the Fushimi Momoyama mausoleum in Kyoto. By custom she received the posthumous name Shōken, drawn from the name of an ancient Chinese empress.
