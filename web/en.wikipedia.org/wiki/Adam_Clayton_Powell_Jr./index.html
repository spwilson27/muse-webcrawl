Adam Clayton Powell Jr. (November 29, 1908 – April 4, 1972) was an American Baptist pastor and politician who represented the Harlem neighborhood of New York City in the United States House of Representatives from 1945 until 1971. He was the first African American to be elected from New York to Congress.

Ministry and activism

Powell succeeded his father as pastor of the Abyssinian Baptist Church in Harlem, building it into one of the largest Protestant congregations in the United States. During the Great Depression he led boycotts and pickets under the slogan "Don't Buy Where You Can't Work," pressuring Harlem stores to hire Black employees.

Congressional career

In Congress, Powell became chairman of the powerful House Committee on Education and Labor in 1961, overseeing the passage of major legislation including minimum-wage increases, federal aid to education, and programs of the Great Society. His career ended amid controversy over his finances and absences; the House voted to exclude him in 1967, and although the Supreme Court ruled the exclusion unconstitutional in Powell v. McCormack, he was defeated in the 1970 Democratic primary by Charles Rangel. Adam Clayton Powell Jr. Boulevard in Harlem is named in his honor.
