Microwave oven

A microwave oven is an electric oven that heats and cooks food by exposing it to electromagnetic radiation in the microwave frequency range, typically 2.45 GHz. This induces polar molecules in the food to vibrate and produce thermal energy in a process known as dielectric heating.

History

The heating effect of microwaves was noticed by Percy Spencer at Raytheon in 1945, leading to the large commercial Radarange in 1947. Countertop domestic models became widespread from the late 1960s after Amana introduced a compact, cheaper design.

Operation and safety

A magnetron generates microwaves that are distributed through the cooking cavity, often with a turntable for even heating. Metal shielding and door interlocks prevent leakage; regulatory limits govern emissions. Metal objects can arc, and superheating of water is a recognised hazard.
