Amitabha Sutra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Amitabha Sutra (Sanskrit: Sukhāvatīvyūha; Chinese: 阿彌陀經, Āmítuó Jīng), also known as the Smaller Sukhavati Sutra, is one of the principal scriptures of Pure Land Buddhism. It describes the adornments of Sukhavati, the Western Pure Land of the Buddha Amitabha, and explains the practice by which beings may be reborn there.

Content

In the sutra, the Buddha Shakyamuni, residing at Jetavana monastery in Sravasti, addresses the elder Sariputra and describes the splendours of Amitabha's land: jewelled trees, lotus ponds, celestial music, and birds that sing the Dharma. He teaches that beings who hold fast to the name of Amitabha Buddha with an undistracted mind for a period from one to seven days will, at death, be received by Amitabha and reborn in Sukhavati, from which they will not regress.

Translation and influence

The Sanskrit text was translated into Chinese by the Kuchean monk Kumarajiva in 402 CE. Because of its brevity and its focus on recitation of the Buddha's name (nianfo or nembutsu), the Amitabha Sutra became one of the most widely recited texts in East Asian Buddhism, forming with the Longer Sukhavati Sutra and the Contemplation Sutra the "three Pure Land sutras."
