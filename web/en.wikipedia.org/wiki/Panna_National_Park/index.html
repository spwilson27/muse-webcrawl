Area542 km² (core)

Panna National Park is a national park and tiger reserve in the Panna and Chhatarpur districts of Madhya Pradesh, India, spread over the Vindhyan ranges along the Ken River. It was declared a national park in 1981 and became the 22nd tiger reserve of India in 1994, under Project Tiger. The park's dry deciduous forests, plateaus, gorges and waterfalls once formed part of the hunting preserves of the rulers of Panna state.

Geography and flora

The terrain consists of plateaus and gorges of the Vindhyan range, drained by the Ken River, which flows through the park from south to north and is famous for its deep gorges (the Raneh and Pandav falls are nearby attractions). Vegetation is mainly dry deciduous forest dominated by teak, tendu, mahua, salai and bamboo, opening into grasslands and riverine strips along the Ken.

Fauna

The park supports Bengal tigers, leopards, sloth bears, wild dogs (dhole), wolves, chital, sambar, nilgai, chinkara and four-horned antelope, along with many bird species and gharials and muggers in the Ken. Panna is also known for its vulture populations and for the nearby Ken Gharial Sanctuary.

Tiger reintroduction

Panna became internationally notable when its tiger population collapsed through poaching, and by 2009 the reserve was found to have lost effectively all its tigers. A pioneering reintroduction programme then translocated tigers and tigresses from other Madhya Pradesh reserves (Bandhavgarh, Kanha and Pench). The animals bred successfully in the wild, and the population recovered to several dozen tigers, making Panna one of the most cited examples of tiger reintroduction in India. In 2020 the wider Panna landscape was inscribed in UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves.
