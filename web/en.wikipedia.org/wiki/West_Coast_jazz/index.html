West Coast jazz

Stylistic originsBebop, cool jazz, swing
Cultural originsLate 1940s and 1950s, California, United States
Typical instrumentsSaxophone, trumpet, piano, double bass, drums, French horn, tuba

West Coast jazz refers to styles of jazz that developed in Los Angeles and San Francisco during the 1950s. West Coast jazz is often seen as a subgenre of cool jazz, which featured a less frenetic, more lyrical and often more arranged sound than the bebop being played on the East Coast. Musicians associated with the style include Gerry Mulligan, Chet Baker, Dave Brubeck, Paul Desmond, Shorty Rogers and Shelly Manne.

History

Many musicians who defined West Coast jazz had played in the big bands that toured California during the Second World War and settled in Los Angeles, where studio work was plentiful. The Lighthouse Café in Hermosa Beach, under bassist Howard Rumsey's Lighthouse All-Stars, became a famous venue for the style. Record labels such as Pacific Jazz and Contemporary Records documented the scene.

The Gerry Mulligan Quartet, featuring trumpeter Chet Baker and no piano, became one of the most popular groups in jazz in 1952–53. Dave Brubeck's quartet, based in the Bay Area, achieved mainstream success with the album Time Out (1959), which included the hit "Take Five".

Criticism

The term "West Coast jazz" has been criticized as an imprecise marketing label, since musicians in California played in many styles, and some critics argue the label was applied along racial lines that obscured the contributions of Black musicians on the Central Avenue scene in Los Angeles.
