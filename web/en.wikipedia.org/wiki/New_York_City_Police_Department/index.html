New York City Police Department (NYPD)
Founded: 1845
Headquarters: One Police Plaza, Manhattan
Uniformed members: roughly 33,000–36,000

The New York City Police Department (NYPD) is the primary law enforcement agency of New York City. Established in 1845, it is the largest municipal police force in the United States, responsible for policing the city's five boroughs through 78 precincts and specialized bureaus for transit, housing, intelligence and counterterrorism.

Organization

The department is headed by the Police Commissioner, appointed by the mayor. Below the commissioner, the force is organized into patrol, detective, intelligence and counterterrorism, transit, housing and support bureaus, along with units such as the Emergency Service Unit, the aviation and harbor units, and the Strategic Response Group. Police headquarters is at One Police Plaza in Lower Manhattan, near City Hall.

History

The NYPD replaced an older watch and constable system in 1845, modelling itself partly on London's Metropolitan Police. Its history includes the Draft Riots of 1863, the reform era of commissioner Theodore Roosevelt in the 1890s, the crime declines associated with CompStat management in the 1990s, the department's role in the September 11 attacks — in which 23 NYPD officers died — and recurring public debates over tactics such as stop-and-frisk.
