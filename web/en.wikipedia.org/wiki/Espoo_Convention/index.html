Espoo Convention

Convention on Environmental Impact Assessment in a Transboundary Context
Signed25 February 1991
LocationEspoo, Finland
Effective10 September 1997
DepositarySecretary-General of the United Nations
LanguagesEnglish, French, Russian

The Convention on Environmental Impact Assessment in a Transboundary Context, commonly known as the Espoo Convention, is a United Nations Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE) convention signed in Espoo, Finland, in 1991. It lays down the general obligation of states to notify and consult each other on all major projects under consideration that are likely to have a significant adverse environmental impact across national boundaries.

History

The convention was adopted and signed in Espoo at the fourth session of the Senior Advisers to ECE Governments on Environmental and Water Problems, held in February 1991. It entered into force on 10 September 1997, ninety days after the deposit of the sixteenth instrument of ratification. The convention grew out of growing awareness in the 1970s and 1980s that industrial development, energy production, and large infrastructure projects could produce pollution and other environmental effects far beyond the borders of the state in which they were located.

Provisions

The Espoo Convention sets out the obligations of parties to assess the environmental impact of certain activities at an early stage of planning. It requires that, for listed activities likely to cause significant adverse transboundary impact, the party of origin notify any party it considers may be affected. The affected party may then participate in the environmental impact assessment procedure, which includes the preparation of assessment documentation, consultations between the parties, and a final decision that takes due account of the assessment and the comments received.

Appendix I to the convention lists activities subject to its provisions, including crude oil refineries, thermal power stations, installations for the production or enrichment of nuclear fuels, large dams and reservoirs, major mining operations, and large-diameter oil and gas pipelines. The convention also encourages parties to apply its principles to policies, plans, and programmes, an idea later developed in a separate protocol.

Protocol on Strategic Environmental Assessment

A Protocol on Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA) was adopted in Kyiv, Ukraine, in 2003 and entered into force in 2010. The Kyiv Protocol extends the assessment approach from individual projects to plans, programmes, and, as appropriate, policies and legislation, requiring parties to evaluate the environmental consequences of their official draft plans and programmes early in the decision-making process. It provides for extensive public participation and, uniquely among multilateral environmental agreements of its generation, opens accession to states outside the UNECE region upon approval by the Meeting of the Parties.

Amendments

Two amendments to the convention have been adopted. The first amendment, adopted in Sofia in 2001, opened the convention to accession by states outside the UNECE region and revised the list of activities in Appendix I. The second amendment, adopted in Cavtat, Croatia, in 2004, introduced provisions on scoping, the review of compliance, and revised lists of activities and substances. The amendments entered into force progressively as ratifications accumulated.

Meetings of the Parties and implementation

The Meeting of the Parties, held roughly every three years, reviews the implementation of the convention and its protocol. An Implementation Committee, established in 2001, examines compliance issues and can consider submissions by parties as well as information gathered by the secretariat. Well-known cases considered under the convention include disputes over the Danube Delta navigation canal in Ukraine and the Ostrovets nuclear power plant in Belarus, both of which generated findings and recommendations on transboundary assessment obligations.
