Public awareness of science

Public awareness of science (PAwS) refers to the attitudes, behaviors, opinions, and activities that comprise the relations between the general public and scientific knowledge and organizations. It encompasses science communication, science journalism, science museums, festivals, and other forms of outreach aimed at non-specialists.

Development

The modern movement is often dated to the Royal Society's 1985 Bodmer Report, The Public Understanding of Science, which argued that a scientifically literate public benefits both democracy and the economy. Early efforts followed a "deficit model," assuming public skepticism stemmed from ignorance to be remedied by one-way communication of facts.

From deficit to dialogue

Experience with controversies such as genetically modified food and nuclear power led scholars and institutions toward "dialogue" and "engagement" models, in which publics are consulted early and local knowledge is valued. Related concepts include public understanding of science (PUS), public engagement with science and technology (PEST), and scientific literacy.

Institutions and activities

Activities include science festivals, café scientifique events, citizen science projects, open days at laboratories, and media programming. National surveys, such as those by the US National Science Foundation, periodically measure public knowledge of and attitudes toward science.
