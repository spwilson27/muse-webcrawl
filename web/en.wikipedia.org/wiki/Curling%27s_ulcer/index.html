Curling's ulcer

A Curling's ulcer is an acute gastric erosion or peptic ulcer that develops as a complication of severe burns. It is named after Thomas Blizard Curling, an English surgeon who described the condition in 1842 in patients with extensive burn injuries.

Pathophysiology

Severe burns cause a systemic stress response with reduced blood flow to the gastrointestinal mucosa. The resulting ischemia weakens the protective mucosal barrier of the stomach and duodenum, allowing gastric acid to erode the lining and form ulcers, sometimes multiple and deep.

Clinical features and treatment

Patients may present with gastrointestinal bleeding, perforation, or no symptoms until a complication occurs. Management includes acid suppression with proton pump inhibitors or H2 blockers, supportive burn care, and surgery in cases of uncontrolled bleeding or perforation. It is related to Cushing's ulcer, which follows head injury.
