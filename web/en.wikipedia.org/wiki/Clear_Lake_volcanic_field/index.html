Clear Lake volcanic field

The Clear Lake volcanic field is a volcanic field in northern California, in the vicinity of Clear Lake. The volcanic field consists of lava domes, cinder cones, and maars distributed around the lake, with Mount Konocti the most prominent feature. Volcanic activity began about 2.1 million years ago and the most recent eruptions occurred around 10,000 years ago.

Geology

The field lies near the junction of the San Andreas Fault system and is thought to be related to the passage of the Mendocino Triple Junction and the resulting slab window, which allows hot asthenosphere to rise beneath the crust. Erupted compositions range from basalt to rhyolite.

Geothermal activity

The field hosts The Geysers, the world's largest geothermal field, which generates substantial electricity for northern California. Because of its young eruptions and ongoing seismicity and hydrothermal activity, the USGS monitors the field as a potentially active volcanic system.
