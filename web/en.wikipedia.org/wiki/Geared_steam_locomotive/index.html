Geared steam locomotive

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A geared steam locomotive is a type of steam locomotive which uses gearing, usually reduction gearing, in the drivetrain, as opposed to the directly driven rod-and-crank design common to most conventional steam locomotives. Geared locomotives could deliver high tractive effort at very low speeds, with all wheels driven, making them ideally suited to logging, mining and industrial railways with steep grades, sharp curves and poor, temporary track.

Characteristics

Because the pistons drive the wheels through gears rather than directly through connecting rods, the cylinders can run fast while the wheels turn slowly, multiplying torque. Geared locomotives typically have all axles driven and mounted in pivoting trucks (bogies), giving excellent adhesion and flexibility. Their drawbacks were low top speed — often under 20 mph (32 km/h) — high maintenance of the exposed gearing and shafting, and violent vibration at speed.

The three main types

Shay

The Shay locomotive, patented by Ephraim Shay and built in large numbers by the Lima Locomotive Works of Ohio, has two or three vertical cylinders on one side of the boiler driving a longitudinal shaft along the locomotive, which drives each axle through bevel gears. Nearly 2,800 Shays were built between 1878 and 1945, making it the most numerous geared type.

Climax

The Climax locomotive, built by the Climax Manufacturing Company of Corry, Pennsylvania, uses two inclined (later horizontal) cylinders driving a central transverse shaft, connected by gears to a longitudinal driveshaft powering the trucks. Climaxes are recognisable by their cylinders mounted at an angle partway along the boiler.

Heisler

The Heisler locomotive, built by the Stearns Manufacturing Company of Erie, Pennsylvania, has two cylinders arranged in a V beneath the boiler, driving a central crankshaft and longitudinal driveshaft. Heislers were generally the fastest of the three types and popular with many logging companies.

Other designs

Smaller numbers of geared locomotives of other designs were built elsewhere, including geared types in New Zealand, Australia and South America, and the specialised Kitson-Meyer and Garratt articulated designs (which are rod-driven rather than geared). A handful of Shays, Climaxes and Heislers survive in preservation, hauling tourist trains in the United States, Taiwan and elsewhere.
