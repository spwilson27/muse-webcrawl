Grapheme–color synesthesia

Grapheme–color synesthesia is a form of synesthesia in which an individual's perception of numerals and letters is associated with the experience of colors. It is one of the most common and most studied forms of synesthesia, first described scientifically in the late 19th century.

Characteristics

For grapheme–color synesthetes, each letter or digit consistently evokes a specific color — for example, "A" may always appear red — although the particular associations are idiosyncratic to the individual. The colors may be experienced in the mind's eye ("associators") or projected onto the written character itself ("projectors"). Associations are typically stable over a lifetime and can depend on factors such as the first letter of color words, letter frequency, and childhood toys like colored letter magnets.

Research

Neuroimaging studies suggest increased structural and functional connectivity between grapheme-recognition regions and the color area V4 in the fusiform gyrus. The condition runs in families and is thought to have a genetic component.
