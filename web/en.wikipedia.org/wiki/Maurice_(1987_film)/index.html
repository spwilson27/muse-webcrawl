Maurice is a 1987 British romantic drama film directed by James Ivory, based on E. M. Forster's novel Maurice, which Forster wrote in 1913–14 but withheld from publication until after his death because of its sympathetic treatment of homosexuality. Produced by Merchant Ivory Productions, the film stars James Wilby as Maurice Hall, Hugh Grant as Clive Durham, and Rupert Graves as Alec Scudder.

Plot

At Cambridge before the First World War, Maurice falls in love with fellow student Clive Durham, but after a friend is arrested for homosexual offences, Clive chooses social safety, marries, and repudiates his past. Maurice seeks "cure" through hypnosis before finding love with Clive's gamekeeper, Alec Scudder, and ultimately chooses to live openly with him, defying Edwardian convention — a rare happy ending for a gay love story of its period.

Reception and legacy

The film premiered at the Venice Film Festival, where Ivory won the Silver Lion and Wilby and Grant shared the Best Actor award. Though made when gay-themed cinema was uncommon in the mainstream, it was praised for its fidelity to Forster and its lavish period craft, and it has since been recognised as a landmark of LGBTQ cinema, receiving a restored re-release in 2017.
