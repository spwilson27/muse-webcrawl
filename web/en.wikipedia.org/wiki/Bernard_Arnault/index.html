Bernard Jean Étienne Arnault is a French businessman, investor and art collector. He is the co-founder, chairman and chief executive of LVMH, the world's largest luxury goods company, and has repeatedly ranked as the richest person in the world.

Early life and career

Arnault was born in Roubaix in northern France and graduated in engineering from the École Polytechnique in 1971. He joined his father's construction company, Ferret-Savinel, steered it into property development, and spent several years in the United States building condominiums in Florida.

Building LVMH

Returning to France, Arnault bought the bankrupt textile group Boussac in 1984 — mainly to obtain Christian Dior — selling off most assets and keeping the fashion house. He then manoeuvred through the merger that created LVMH, emerging as its chairman by 1989, and spent the following decades acquiring dozens of luxury houses, a strategy that earned him the nickname "the wolf in the cashmere coat". His five children all hold senior roles across the group, making succession a closely watched question.

Art and philanthropy

Arnault is a major art collector and patron, responsible for the Fondation Louis Vuitton museum in Paris, designed by Frank Gehry and opened in 2014. He pledged €200 million toward the restoration of Notre-Dame Cathedral after the 2019 fire.
