National Democratic Party (Suriname)

The National Democratic Party (Dutch: Nationale Democratische Partij, NDP) is a political party in Suriname. It was founded on 4 July 1987 by Dési Bouterse, the former military ruler of Suriname, and for much of its history was closely associated with him.

History

The NDP emerged from the political structures created after the 1980 sergeants' coup led by Bouterse. It first contested elections in 1987. The party won power through elections in 1996, forming a coalition that elected Jules Wijdenbosch president.

Under Bouterse, the NDP won the 2010 elections and Bouterse became president; the party was re-elected in 2015, winning an outright majority in the National Assembly. The party lost the 2020 elections to the Progressive Reform Party-led coalition of Chan Santokhi. Bouterse was convicted in 2019 (and on appeal in 2023) by a Surinamese court for his role in the December murders of 1982.

Ideology

The NDP has been described as nationalist and populist, with a broad, multi-ethnic support base in a country whose politics are often organised along ethnic lines.
