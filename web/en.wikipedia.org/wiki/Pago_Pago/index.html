Pago Pago (Samoan: Pago Pago, pronounced "pango pango") is the territorial capital of American Samoa. It is in Maʻoputasi County on Tutuila, the main island of American Samoa. Pago Pago is one of a grouping of villages around Pago Pago Harbor on the island's south-central coast, and the name is commonly applied to the whole harbour area and port.

Harbour

Pago Pago Harbor is widely regarded as one of the finest deepwater natural harbours in the South Pacific — the drowned caldera of an ancient volcano, nearly two miles long and sheltered by steep hills, with Rainmaker Mountain (Mount Pioa) at its entrance. The harbour's strategic value led the United States to establish a naval coaling station there in the late nineteenth century, formalised by treaties in 1878 and the cession of Tutuila in 1900.

History and economy

The United States Naval Station Tutuila operated until 1951, when administration passed to the Department of the Interior. Tuna canning became the economic mainstay from the 1950s, with canneries processing fish caught across the Pacific. On 29 September 2009 an earthquake south of Samoa triggered a tsunami that struck Pago Pago, destroying villages, killing more than thirty people in American Samoa and damaging the canneries and harbour facilities.

Culture

The village is the setting of W. Somerset Maugham's short story "Rain" (1921), featuring Sadie Thompson, later adapted for stage and film. The Jean P. Haydon Museum displays Samoan artefacts, and the annual Flag Day celebrations on 17 April mark the first raising of the US flag in 1900.
