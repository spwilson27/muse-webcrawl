Excusable homicide

Excusable homicide
Area of lawCriminal law
Related conceptsJustifiable homicide, manslaughter, self-defence

Excusable homicide is a legal category of killing, in common law jurisdictions, in which the death of a person occurs under circumstances of accident or misadventure and without negligence or unlawful intent. Historically, English common law distinguished between justifiable homicide (for example, execution under lawful sentence or killing in lawful self-defence) and excusable homicide, where the killer was blameless but traditionally required a royal pardon and forfeiture of property.

Categories

Classical common law recognised two main forms of excusable homicide: homicide per infortunium (by misadventure), where a lawful act is performed without intent to injure but death accidentally results, and homicide se defendendo (in self-defence), where the killer had no reasonable alternative. Modern statutes in many jurisdictions have abolished the pardon and forfeiture consequences and treat such killings simply as non-criminal.

Modern treatment

In contemporary law, the label "excusable homicide" survives in the statutes of some US states and other jurisdictions, generally covering accidental killings in the course of lawful conduct without criminal negligence. Where negligence is present, the killing is instead prosecuted as involuntary manslaughter, and where justification applies (such as true self-defence) it is classified as justifiable homicide and no offence at all.
