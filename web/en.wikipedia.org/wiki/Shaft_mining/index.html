Shaft mining

Shaft mining or shaft sinking is a method of excavating a vertical or near-vertical tunnel from the top down, providing access to deep underground mineral deposits. It is used when ore lies too deep for open-pit mining or horizontal adits.

Process

Sinking a shaft involves drilling, blasting, mucking out broken rock and lining the shaft with concrete or timber. The finished shaft is fitted with a headframe and hoisting equipment to raise ore, lower workers and materials, and pump water and ventilation air. Large mines may have separate shafts for personnel, ore and ventilation.

Depth records

The deepest mines in the world, South Africa's gold mines such as TauTona and Mponeng, reach nearly 4 kilometres below the surface and depend on some of the deepest single-lift shafts ever sunk. Shaft sinking is among the most hazardous and capital-intensive operations in mining.
