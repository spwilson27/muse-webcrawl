Bow tie

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The bow tie is a type of necktie. A modern bow tie is tied using a common shoelace knot, which is also called the bow knot for that reason. It consists of a ribbon of fabric tied around the collar of a shirt in a symmetrical manner so that the two opposite ends form loops.

Bow ties may be made of any fabric, but most are made of silk, polyester, cotton, or a mixture of fabrics. They are available in three main forms: pre-tied (with the bow sewn into a fixed shape and a strap fastened around the neck), clip-on, and the traditional self-tie or "freestyle" bow tie, which many wearers consider the most elegant because its slight irregularities evidence hand-tying.

History

The bow tie originated among Croatian mercenaries during the Thirty Years' War of the 17th century: the Croats used a scarf around the neck to hold together the opening of their shirts. This was soon adopted (under the name cravat, derived from the French Croate) by the upper classes in France, and in the 18th and 19th centuries cravats gave rise to both the modern long necktie and the bow tie. By the late 19th century the bow tie had become a standard item of formal and semi-formal dress.

Use

The bow tie is conventionally worn with formal attire: with a dinner jacket (tuxedo) in black-tie dress — where a black bow tie is standard — and historically with white tie, the most formal evening dress code. Outside formal wear, bow ties are associated with particular professions and styles, including waiters, sommeliers, and various academics, physicians, and architects, for whom the bow tie has the practical advantage of not dangling into one's work.

Tying

The self-tie bow tie is knotted with a shoelace (bow) knot: the two ends are folded into loops and one loop is passed through the knot hole formed behind them, producing the familiar two-winged bow. Sizes are adjusted so that the finished bow is roughly the width of the wearer's face, its ends aligned approximately with the outer corners of the eyes.

Types

  • Butterfly — the classic shape, about 2.25–2.75 inches tall.
  • Batwing — narrower, with straight ends, similar to a bat's wing.
  • Diamond point — ends pointed like a diamond.
  • Club round — with rounded ends.
