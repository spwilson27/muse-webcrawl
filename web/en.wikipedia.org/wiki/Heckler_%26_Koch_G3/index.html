The Heckler & Koch G3 (Gewehr 3) is a 7.62×51mm NATO battle rifle developed in the 1950s by the German arms manufacturer Heckler & Koch in collaboration with the Spanish state-owned design agency CETME. It uses a roller-delayed blowback operating system derived from wartime German designs and the CETME rifle.

Service

The G3 was adopted by the Bundeswehr in 1959 and became one of the most widely used military rifles of the Cold War, exported to and licence-produced in dozens of countries including Norway, Sweden, Portugal, Greece, Turkey, Iran and Pakistan. It served as the basis for the HK33, the MP5 submachine gun family mechanisms, sniper variants such as the PSG1, and numerous civilian semi-automatic versions.

Replacement

In German service the G3 was largely replaced by the 5.56mm G36 from the late 1990s, though G3 variants remain in service worldwide and in reserve and designated-marksman roles.
