Sophia (wisdom)

Sophia (Greek: σοφία, sophía, "wisdom") is a central idea in Hellenistic philosophy and religion, Platonism, Gnosticism, and Christian theology. Originally carrying a meaning of "cleverness, skill", the later meaning of the term, close to the meaning of phronesis ("wisdom, intelligence"), was significantly shaped by the philosophy of Plato.

In Judaism and Christianity

In the Septuagint and in the deuterocanonical Wisdom literature, Wisdom (Chokhmah in Hebrew, Sophia in Greek) is personified as a female figure who was with God before creation and through whom the world was made (Proverbs 8; Wisdom of Solomon 7–9). The Book of Proverbs portrays Wisdom crying aloud in the streets and inviting the simple to her feast. This personification influenced later Jewish philosophy, such as Philo of Alexandria, and early Christian reflection on the Logos.

In Gnosticism

In many Gnostic systems, Sophia is a feminine aeon and the lowest emanation of the divine Pleroma. In Valentinian myth, Sophia's desire to know the Father directly leads to a disturbance from which the material world and the Demiurge arise; her restoration and the redemption of the divine spark in humanity form a central part of the Gnostic myth of salvation.

In Orthodox theology

Sophia is venerated conceptually in Eastern Orthodoxy through the dedication of great churches such as Hagia Sophia ("Holy Wisdom") in Constantinople. In the 19th and 20th centuries, Russian religious philosophers including Vladimir Solovyov, Pavel Florensky, and Sergei Bulgakov developed "sophiology", meditating on Sophia as the divine wisdom and the world's soul — a teaching that proved controversial within the Orthodox Church.

Personification

In iconography, Sophia appears as a female figure, sometimes enthroned and crowned, in Byzantine, medieval Western, and Russian icon traditions.
