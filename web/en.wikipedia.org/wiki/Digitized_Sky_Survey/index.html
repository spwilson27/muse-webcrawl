The Digitized Sky Survey (DSS) is a digital version of several photographic atlases of the night sky, produced by scanning the glass photographic plates of major sky surveys. It was created at the Space Telescope Science Institute (STScI) in Baltimore, principally to support the guiding of the Hubble Space Telescope.

History

The original survey plates were taken with large Schmidt telescopes: the Samuel Oschin telescope at Palomar Observatory for the northern sky (the Palomar Observatory Sky Survey, POSS), and the UK Schmidt Telescope in Australia for the southern sky. The plates were scanned with high-precision microdensitometers at STScI.

The first version, DSS-I, based on the first-epoch Palomar plates and the SERC-J survey, was released in 1994 as a set of 102 CD-ROMs. A second generation, DSS-II, added the second-epoch Palomar survey (POSS-II) in blue, red and near-infrared photographic bands, giving much deeper and more uniform coverage.

Uses

The DSS underpins the Guide Star Catalog used to point Hubble, and its images are served worldwide through sky-atlas software and web services such as Aladin and WorldWide Telescope. It remains one of the most widely used astronomical image archives, providing a photographic record of the sky at epochs from the 1950s to the 1990s, against which the motions and variability of stars can be measured.
