Semi-acoustic guitar

A semi-acoustic guitar, also known as a hollow-body electric guitar, is a type of electric guitar that combines an acoustic sound chamber with electromagnetic pickups. True semi-acoustic designs have a solid centre block running through the body, which reduces feedback while retaining some acoustic character.

History

The type was pioneered by Gibson with the ES-335 of 1958, whose thinline hollow body with a maple centre block became a standard. Earlier archtop guitars, such as the Gibson ES-150 of 1936, were fully hollow.

Characteristics

Semi-acoustic guitars produce a warmer, rounder tone than solid-body instruments and are sensitive to feedback at high volume, a quality some players exploit. They are widely used in jazz, blues and rock; famous players include B. B. King (with his "Lucille" ES-355) and Chuck Berry.

Related designs

Chambered solid-body guitars hollow out portions of a solid body, while acoustic-electric guitars are primarily acoustic instruments fitted with pickups for amplification.
