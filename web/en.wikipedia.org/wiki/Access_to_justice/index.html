Access to justice

Access to justice is a principle of the rule of law holding that people should be able to seek and obtain a remedy for grievances through formal or informal institutions of justice, in compliance with human rights standards. It encompasses not only physical and financial access to courts, but also legal awareness, the availability of legal aid and representation, fair procedures, and the effective enforcement of decisions.

Barriers

Common barriers to access to justice include the cost of legal services and court fees, complexity and delay in legal procedures, geographic distance from courts, language barriers, and lack of knowledge of legal rights. These barriers fall disproportionately on poor and marginalised people, producing what scholars call a "justice gap" between legal needs and the services available to meet them.

Responses

Responses include publicly funded legal aid, pro bono work by lawyers, simplified court procedures and small-claims courts, alternative dispute resolution such as mediation, community paralegals, and legal technology. The right of access to justice is reflected in international human rights instruments including the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, and promoting access to justice is part of United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 16.
