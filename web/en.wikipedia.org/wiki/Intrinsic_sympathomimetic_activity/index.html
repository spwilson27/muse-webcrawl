Intrinsic sympathomimetic activity

Intrinsic sympathomimetic activity (ISA) is a property of some beta blockers whereby the drug partially activates the beta-adrenergic receptor while simultaneously blocking the effects of stronger agonists such as noradrenaline. Such agents act as partial agonists: they produce a modest level of sympathetic stimulation at rest but prevent excessive stimulation during exercise or stress.

Pharmacology

Beta blockers with ISA, including pindolol, acebutolol, carteolol, and penbutolol, tend to cause less resting bradycardia and may have smaller adverse effects on bronchial tone, peripheral circulation, and plasma lipid profiles than beta blockers without ISA. Their resting heart-rate reduction is smaller, which can be advantageous or disadvantageous depending on the indication.

Clinical significance

Because of their weaker effect on resting heart rate and concerns about outcomes after myocardial infarction, beta blockers with ISA are generally not first-line agents for secondary prevention after heart attack. They may, however, be preferred in patients prone to symptomatic bradycardia or bronchospasm.
