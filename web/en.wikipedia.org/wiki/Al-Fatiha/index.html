Al-Fatiha

Al-Fatiha (Arabic: ٱلْفَاتِحَةُ, "The Opener") is the first chapter (surah) of the Quran. It consists of seven verses (ayat) and is among the most widely recited passages in Islam: it forms an essential part of each unit (rak'ah) of the daily prayers, so observant Muslims recite it many times every day.

Text and meaning

The chapter opens with the basmala — "In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful" — followed by praise of God as Lord of the worlds and Master of the Day of Judgment, a declaration of worship and dependence ("You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help"), and a petition: "Guide us along the Straight Path" — the path of those God has blessed, not of those who have earned anger or gone astray. Worshippers conclude with amin (amen).

Significance

Al-Fatiha is also called Umm al-Qur'an ("Mother of the Quran") because it is held to summarise the whole book's themes. A hadith in Sahih Muslim describes it as a dialogue between God and the worshipper. It is recited at marriages, funerals, and for healing, and begins most printed copies of the Quran with elaborate illumination.
