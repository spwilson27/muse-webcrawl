Highlands and Islands

The Highlands and Islands is an area of Scotland broadly covering the Scottish Highlands plus the island groups of Orkney, Shetland, and the Outer Hebrides (Na h-Eileanan Siar), together with the Inner Hebrides and parts of the mainland west and north. It is one of the eight electoral regions of the Scottish Parliament and the area served by Highlands and Islands Enterprise.

Geography

The region is mountainous and sparsely populated, with a population of under 500,000 spread over more than half of Scotland's land area. It contains Ben Nevis, the highest mountain in the British Isles, and the Great Glen fault running from Inverness to Fort William. The island groups are reached by ferry and air services, with Kirkwall (Orkney), Lerwick (Shetland), and Stornoway (Lewis) the principal island towns.

Culture and language

The region is the heartland of Scottish Gaelic, which remains a community language especially in the Outer Hebrides. Its history includes the clan system, the Jacobite risings, and the Highland Clearances of the 18th and 19th centuries, when many inhabitants were displaced or emigrated. Traditional industries include crofting, fishing, whisky distilling, and, increasingly, tourism and renewable energy.

Governance

Most of the mainland area is administered by the Highland Council, one of the largest local authorities in Europe by area, with separate island councils for Orkney, Shetland, and Comhairle nan Eilean Siar. The University of the Highlands and Islands, granted university status in 2011, serves the whole region.
