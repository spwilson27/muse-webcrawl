Rent regulation in New York

Rent regulation in New York consists principally of two systems administered by the New York State Division of Housing and Community Renewal (DHCR): rent control and rent stabilization. Together they cover roughly one million apartments in New York City and additional units in parts of Nassau, Rockland and Westchester counties. The systems limit how much landlords may increase rents and give tenants rights to lease renewal and required services.

History

Rent control began with federal wartime price controls in 1943 and continued under state law; it now applies mainly to tenants in continuous occupancy since 1971 in buildings built before 1947, and covers a shrinking number of apartments. Rent stabilization was introduced in New York City in 1969 in response to rising rents, and the Emergency Tenant Protection Act of 1974 extended the system and brought in post-1973 buildings via tax-abatement programmes. Vacancy decontrol, introduced in the 1990s, allowed apartments to leave regulation when rents passed a threshold and units became vacant.

Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act of 2019

The HSTPA of 2019 made major changes: it ended vacancy decontrol and high-income deregulation, sharply limited rent increases for major capital improvements and individual apartment improvements, restricted preferential-rent practices, and made the rent laws permanent rather than subject to periodic renewal. Critics among landlord groups argued the law discouraged maintenance and investment; tenant advocates praised the expanded protections.

Administration

Annual rent increases for stabilized apartments in New York City are set by the Rent Guidelines Board. Disputes over registration, overcharges and services are handled by the DHCR and the courts.
