Telex

Telex is a telecommunication service that provides text communication through a switched telegraphy network of teleprinters. Developed commercially from the 1930s, it became — before the ubiquity of fax and email — the standard means by which businesses, governments, news agencies, and ships exchanged written messages quickly and with a printed record. A subscriber could dial another telex terminal anywhere in the world and type a message that printed out at the destination, typically at 50 baud using the five-bit Baudot–Murray code (ITA2).

Operation

Each telex subscriber had a unique number and an "answerback" code that the terminal transmitted automatically at the start and end of a call, verifying the identity of the receiving machine — a feature that gave telex messages a degree of legal standing as evidence of receipt. Messages could be prepared in advance on punched paper tape and transmitted at full line speed. Exchanges switched calls automatically, much like a telephone network, and store-and-forward services and overseas gateways linked national networks into a global system.

History

The first commercial telex services were established in Germany in 1933 and soon spread through Europe; the United States' comparable TWX service began in the 1930s–40s. At its height in the 1970s and 1980s, the international telex network connected hundreds of thousands of subscribers in nearly every country, and telex addresses appeared on business letterheads alongside telephone numbers. The service's reliability made it crucial for shipping, banking (where telex transfers and tested messages were standard), diplomacy, and journalism.

Decline

Fax machines in the 1980s and then email and the internet in the 1990s displaced telex almost entirely. Western Union ended its telex-based services in the 1990s–2000s, and most national carriers shut down their networks in the 2000s and 2010s; a few niche uses lingered in aviation, maritime, and developing-world contexts, along with email-to-telex gateways. Nevertheless, telex remains a landmark in the history of data communications — the first mass switched text network and a direct ancestor of modern messaging.
