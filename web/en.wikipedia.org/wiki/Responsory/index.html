Responsory

A responsory is a type of chant in the Western Christian liturgy, sung responsorially — that is, a soloist (or small group) sings a verse and the choir or congregation responds, typically by repeating part of the respond. Responsories form a major part of the sung Divine Office (Liturgy of the Hours) and also occur in the Mass.

Forms

In the Office, the "great responsory" (responsorium prolixum) follows the lessons at Matins and consists of a respond, a verse (often from a psalm), and a partial repetition of the respond, frequently concluding with the Gloria Patri. The "short responsory" (responsorium breve) is used at the other hours after the short lesson. In the Mass, the Gradual is a responsorial chant of similar origin.

Musical development

Responsories in the Gregorian chant repertory are often highly melismatic, especially in the solo verse. From the Renaissance onward, composers such as Tomás Luis de Victoria, William Byrd and Carlo Gesualdo set the responsories for Holy Week (the Tenebrae responsories) polyphonically; these sets remain among the best-known examples of the genre.
