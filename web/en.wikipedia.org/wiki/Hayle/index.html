Hayle (from Cornish heyl, "estuary") is a port town and civil parish in west Cornwall, England, at the mouth of the Hayle River on St Ives Bay. Once a major centre of Cornish mining engineering and copper smelting, it is now a holiday destination noted for three miles of sandy beaches running from the estuary toward Gwithian and Godrevy.

Hayle harbour, at the mouth of the Hayle estuary.

Industrial history

Hayle grew in the 18th and 19th centuries around two rival firms: Harvey's of Hayle, a celebrated maker of large steam engines for Cornish mines and drainage schemes worldwide, and the Cornish Copper Company at Copperhouse. The town's foundries cast some of the largest beam engines ever built, and bitter rivalry between the two ends of the town — famously said to be remembered in local disputes — shaped its development. A wharf and, later, the Hayle Railway (1837) linked the port to the mines of Camborne and Redruth.

Modern town

With the decline of mining, Hayle's economy shifted to tourism and light industry. The Hayle Estuary nature reserve, managed by the RSPB, is an important overwintering site for wading birds and wildfowl. Regeneration of the harbour area in the 2010s added a supermarket, housing, and marine-renewables businesses while preserving listed structures such as the North Quay.
