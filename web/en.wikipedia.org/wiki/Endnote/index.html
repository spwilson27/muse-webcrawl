Endnote

An endnote is a note placed at the end of a book, chapter, article or other written work, rather than at the foot of the page on which the reference appears (in which case it is called a footnote). Endnotes are typically used to give bibliographic citations, supplementary commentary, or acknowledgements without interrupting the flow of the main text.

Endnotes collected at the end of a scholarly volume.

Usage

In the body of the text, an endnote is indicated by a superscript number or symbol. The corresponding notes are gathered in a separate section, often titled "Notes", at the end of the document or of each chapter. Endnotes are common in academic publishing because they keep pages visually clean and simplify typesetting, although readers must flip back and forth between text and notes.

EndNote software

The term is also associated with EndNote, a commercial reference management software package developed by Clarivate, used to organise bibliographies and references when writing essays and articles. It should not be confused with the typographic concept described above.
