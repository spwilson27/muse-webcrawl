Earl Campbell

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Born: 29 March 1955, Tyler, Texas, U.S.

Position: Running back

College: Texas (1974–1977)

NFL draft: 1978, Round 1, Pick 1 (Houston Oilers)

Teams: Houston Oilers (1978–1984); New Orleans Saints (1984–1985)

Honours: Heisman Trophy (1977); NFL MVP (1979); Pro Football Hall of Fame (1991)

Earl Christian Campbell (born 29 March 1955), nicknamed "The Tyler Rose", is an American former professional football running back who played in the National Football League (NFL) for the Houston Oilers and New Orleans Saints. Renowned for his punishing, tackle-breaking running style, he is regarded as one of the greatest power backs in football history.

College career

At the University of Texas, Campbell rushed for 4,443 yards over four seasons. As a senior in 1977 he led the nation with 1,744 rushing yards and won the Heisman Trophy, despite Texas losing the Cotton Bowl — and the national championship — to Notre Dame.

Professional career

The Houston Oilers traded up to select Campbell first overall in the 1978 NFL draft. He was named NFL Offensive Rookie of the Year and led the league in rushing in each of his first three seasons, winning the NFL Most Valuable Player Award in 1979 and earning five Pro Bowl selections. His 1980 season, with 1,934 rushing yards, included four 200-yard games. Traded to the New Orleans Saints in 1984, he retired after the 1985 season with 9,407 career rushing yards and 74 touchdowns.

Later life and honours

Campbell was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1991 and the College Football Hall of Fame in 1990; his number 20 was retired by Texas and his number 34 by the Oilers' successor franchise. The physical toll of his style of play left him with serious health problems, and he has spoken publicly about his post-career struggles and recovery. He founded a food company, Earl Campbell Meat Products, and the Earl Campbell Tyler Rose Award honours college football's top offensive player with Texas ties.
