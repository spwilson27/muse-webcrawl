Children's Laureate

The Children's Laureate, now known as the Waterstones Children's Laureate, is a position awarded in the United Kingdom once every two years to a distinguished writer or illustrator of children's books. The post is intended to celebrate outstanding achievement and to promote children's literature and reading.

History

The role was established in 1999, inspired by the Poet Laureate, through the efforts of the then Poet Laureate Ted Hughes and children's author Michael Morpurgo. It is administered by BookTrust and sponsored by Waterstones. The laureate receives a bursary and a silver medal, and undertakes a personal programme of projects during the two-year term.
