Kai Tak Airport

Kai Tak Airport was the international airport of Hong Kong from 1925 until 1998. Located on the west side of Kowloon Bay in Kowloon City District, it was surrounded by dense high-rise development and was famous — and notorious — for its demanding approach, in which aircraft flew low over the rooftops of Kowloon before a sharp right turn onto short final.

The approach

The approach to runway 13, guided by the Instrument Guidance System, required pilots to make a 47-degree right turn at low altitude over Kowloon, aligning with the runway while passing a checkerboard marker painted on a hillside (the famous "checkerboard turn"). Strong crosswinds from typhoons and the terrain made Kai Tak one of the world's most challenging airport approaches, and landings there became iconic among aviation enthusiasts, with a popular plane-spotting site at the runway threshold.

Closure

By the 1990s Kai Tak was operating far beyond its design capacity. It closed on 6 July 1998, its operations transferred overnight to the new Hong Kong International Airport at Chek Lap Kok. The final flight departed shortly after midnight, and the control tower lights were ceremonially switched off.

Redevelopment

The former airport site has been redeveloped under the Kai Tak Development plan, including the Kai Tak Cruise Terminal built at the end of the old runway, sports facilities, housing and parkland.
