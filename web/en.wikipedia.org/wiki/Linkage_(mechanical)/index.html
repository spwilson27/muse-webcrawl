Linkage (mechanical)

A mechanical linkage is an assembly of systems connected to manage forces and movement. The bodies or links are connected via joints, and the motion of one or more links, driven by an input, produces a predictable output motion in the rest.

Analysis

The simplest closed-loop linkage is the four-bar linkage, with three moving links and four revolute joints; its behavior is classified by the Grashof condition relating the link lengths. Degrees of freedom are counted using the Kutzbach criterion. Famous designer linkages include the Chebyshev linkage and the Peaucellier–Lipkin linkage, which generates an exact straight line, as well as Watt's linkage used in vehicle suspensions.

Applications

Linkages appear in engines (slider-crank mechanisms), windshield wipers, folding structures, robotic arms, prosthetic limbs, and pantographs for copying drawings at a different scale.
