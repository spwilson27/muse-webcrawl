Maschke's theorem, named after the German mathematician Heinrich Maschke, is a theorem in group representation theory stating that a finite-dimensional representation of a finite group over a field whose characteristic does not divide the group's order is completely reducible: every invariant subspace has an invariant complement.

Statement and proof

If V is a representation of a finite group G and W is a G-invariant subspace, the theorem guarantees a complementary invariant subspace, so V decomposes as a direct sum of irreducible representations. The proof averages an arbitrary projection over the group, an operation requiring division by |G|, which is why the characteristic condition is needed.

Consequences

Complete reducibility underpins the standard theory of characters and the decomposition of group algebras into matrix blocks; it fails in modular representation theory when the characteristic divides the group order.
