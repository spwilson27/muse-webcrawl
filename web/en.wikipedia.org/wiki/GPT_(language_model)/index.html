Generative pre-trained transformers (GPT) are a family of large language models developed by the American artificial intelligence company OpenAI. GPT models are artificial neural networks based on the transformer architecture, pre-trained on large corpora of text and then fine-tuned for specific tasks. The name reflects the two-stage approach: generative pre-training followed by discriminative fine-tuning.

First introduced in 2018, successive GPT models — GPT-2 (2019), GPT-3 (2020), GPT-4 (2023) and GPT-5 (2025) — grew dramatically in parameter count and capability. GPT models underpin the chatbot product ChatGPT, whose release in November 2022 brought large language models to widespread public attention.

History

OpenAI published the first GPT model in June 2018 alongside the paper "Improving Language Understanding by Generative Pre-Training". GPT-1 had 117 million parameters and was trained on the BookCorpus dataset. GPT-2, announced in February 2019, scaled to 1.5 billion parameters; OpenAI initially withheld the full model citing misuse concerns before releasing it in November 2019.

GPT-3, released in 2020 with 175 billion parameters, demonstrated striking few-shot learning abilities: it could perform tasks given only a few examples in the prompt, without further training. GPT-4, released in March 2023, added multimodal input, accepting images as well as text. GPT-5 followed in August 2025, marketed as a unified system that routes between fast responses and deeper reasoning.

Architecture and training

GPT models are decoder-only transformers trained to predict the next token in a sequence, a form of self-supervised learning that requires no manual labelling. After pre-training on internet-scale text, models are adapted using supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), which shapes outputs toward helpful and harmless responses.

Impact

The GPT family is widely credited with launching the era of general-purpose AI assistants. The underlying approach — scaling a single architecture trained with next-token prediction — influenced competing model families such as Google's Gemini and Anthropic's Claude. Critics have raised concerns about hallucinated outputs, bias, energy consumption, and the use of copyrighted training data.
