Dose–response relationship

The dose–response relationship, or exposure–response relationship, describes the magnitude of an organism's response as a function of exposure (or dose) to a stimulus or stressor, usually a chemical, after a certain exposure time. Dose–response relationships are central to pharmacology, toxicology and epidemiology.


A typical sigmoidal dose–response curve.

Curve shape

Plotted semi-logarithmically, the relationship is usually sigmoidal. Key parameters include the EC50 (the concentration producing half the maximal effect), the ED50, TD50 and LD50 in toxicology, and the Hill slope describing steepness. Four-parameter logistic (Hill) equations are routinely fitted to experimental data.

Concepts

The threshold model assumes effects appear only above a certain dose, while the linear no-threshold model, used for some carcinogens and radiation, assumes risk proportional to dose down to zero. Hormesis describes biphasic responses in which low doses stimulate and high doses inhibit. Responses may be measured in individuals (graded) or as the fraction of a population responding (quantal).
