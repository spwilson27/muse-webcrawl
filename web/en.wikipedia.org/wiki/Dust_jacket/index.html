Dust jacket

The dust jacket (also called a dust wrapper or dust cover) is the detachable outer cover of a book, usually made of paper and printed with text and illustrations. It folds around the front and back covers with flaps tucked inside, protecting the binding and serving as the book's principal marketing surface.

MaterialPrinted paper, sometimes laminated
PurposeProtection; marketing and design
PartsFront, spine, back, front and rear flaps

History

Early 19th-century jackets were plain wrappers sealed around publishers' bindings and were usually thrown away. By the late 1800s jackets carried printed advertising and reviews, and in the 1920s–30s they became a major art form, with bold graphic designs encouraging buyers to keep them. The front flap typically carries a synopsis and the price; the back flap often an author biography.

Collecting

Because so many jackets were discarded, the presence of an original jacket in good condition greatly increases the value of a collectible book. The jacket of a first edition of F. Scott Fitzgerald's The Great Gatsby, with Francis Cugat's famous "celestial eyes" artwork, is among the most prized; copies with the jacket sell for many times the price of jacketless copies. Collectors use terms such as "price-clipped" to describe flaps whose printed price has been cut off.

Libraries

Libraries often preserve jackets by covering them in clear polyester film, or discard them after recording their information; the jacket's blurbs and author photos can be a useful bibliographic source.
