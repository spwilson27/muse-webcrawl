Innsmouth

Innsmouth is a fictional town in Essex County, Massachusetts, created by American author H. P. Lovecraft as a setting for his horror fiction. It first appeared in Lovecraft's novella The Shadow over Innsmouth (written 1931, published 1936) and is a cornerstone of the Cthulhu Mythos. Lovecraft based the town partly on the real fishing port of Newburyport, Massachusetts, and placed it near the mouth of the fictional Manuxet River, south of Plum Island.

Description

Innsmouth is depicted as a once-prosperous seaport and fishing town that has fallen into decay: crumbling Georgian houses, an almost deserted waterfront, and a population marked by the "Innsmouth look" — bulging, unblinking eyes, narrow heads, flat noses, and skin that becomes grey and scaly with age. Offshore lies Devil's Reef, where the townspeople are rumoured to consort with the Deep Ones, amphibious beings who worship Father Dagon and Mother Hydra.

The Shadow over Innsmouth

In the novella, an antiquarian travelling through New England visits Innsmouth against local advice and uncovers its secret: in the 1840s, sea captain Obed Marsh brought back from the South Seas the Esoteric Order of Dagon and a pact with the Deep Ones, who provided fish and gold in exchange for human sacrifices and interbreeding. The narrator escapes the town but discovers, horrifyingly, that he himself carries Marsh blood and is beginning to transform. The story ends shortly before a 1927–28 federal raid in which the government arrests the population and torpedoes Devil's Reef.

Legacy

Innsmouth has become one of the most widely reused settings in Lovecraftian fiction, appearing in stories by August Derleth and later Mythos writers, in role-playing games such as Call of Cthulhu, in video games including Call of Cthulhu: Dark Corners of the Earth and The Sinking City, and inspiring the town of Innsmouth Academy in other authors' work. Together with Arkham and Dunwich, it forms the heart of Lovecraft's fictional "Lovecraft Country" in Essex County.
