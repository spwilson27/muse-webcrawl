Tom Michael Mitchell (born August 9, 1951) is an American computer scientist and a founder of the field of machine learning. He is the Founders University Professor at Carnegie Mellon University (CMU), where he founded the Machine Learning Department, the first academic department devoted to the subject.

Early life and education

Mitchell received his bachelor's degree in electrical engineering from Stanford University in 1973 and a Ph.D. in electrical engineering, with a minor in computer science, from Stanford in 1979. His doctoral research, supervised in part within the Stanford artificial intelligence community, concerned the problem of learning search heuristics.

Career

After receiving his doctorate, Mitchell joined the faculty of Rutgers University, where he worked on machine learning and artificial intelligence. In 1986 he moved to Carnegie Mellon University, where he later founded and chaired the Machine Learning Department within the School of Computer Science. He has also been involved in research on learning in biological systems and on the interface between machine learning and cognitive neuroscience, including work using brain imaging data to study how humans process language.

Mitchell is the author of the textbook Machine Learning (McGraw-Hill, 1997), one of the most widely used introductions to the field. He is a member of the National Academy of Engineering and a fellow of the American Association for Artificial Intelligence and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.

Research

Mitchell's research covers a broad range of topics in machine learning, including inductive learning of concepts, explanation-based learning, reinforcement learning, semi-supervised learning, and "never-ending learning" systems that read the web continuously to build large knowledge bases, such as the NELL (Never-Ending Language Learner) project at CMU.
