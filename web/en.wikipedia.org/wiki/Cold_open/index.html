Cold open

A cold open drops viewers into a scene before any titles or credits appear.

A cold open (also called a teaser sequence) is a narrative tactic used in television and film in which the story jumps directly into a scene at the beginning of an episode or programme before the opening titles or credits are shown. Cold opens are designed to hook the audience immediately and prevent channel-switching.

Television

Although antecedents exist earlier, the technique became standard in American television from the 1960s onward, in dramas such as Star Trek and in crime procedurals, whose teasers often depict the discovery of the week's crime. Sitcoms frequently use a short unrelated comedy sketch as a cold open, a practice epitomised by Saturday Night Live (whose sketches end with "Live from New York, it's Saturday Night!") and The Office. Long prestige-drama cold opens, sometimes running ten minutes or more, are a hallmark of series such as Breaking Bad, which often used flash-forward teasers.

Film

In film, pre-title sequences serve a similar function; the James Bond films' gun-barrel and action pre-title sequences are famous examples.

Streaming era

Streaming platforms' "skip intro" features and autoplay have further entrenched the cold open, since programmes must capture viewers within seconds to prevent them abandoning an episode.
