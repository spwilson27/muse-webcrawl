Classes préparatoires aux grandes écoles

The classes préparatoires aux grandes écoles (CPGE), commonly known as prépas, are a two-year (in principle) post-secondary course of study in France, provided in dedicated sections of lycées, whose purpose is to prepare students for the competitive entrance examinations (concours) of the grandes écoles — the elite French institutions for engineering, business, the civil service, teaching, and research. Admission to a prépa, which is selective and based on the student's school record, follows the baccalauréat. Although physically located in secondary schools, CPGE are part of higher education, and their students are registered in parallel at a university so that they can transfer with credit if they leave or fail the concours.

Organisation

There are three broad streams:

  • Scientific prépas (prépas scientifiques), such as MPSI, PCSI, PTSI in the first year and MP, PC, PSI, PT in the second, prepare for engineering schools such as the École Polytechnique, the écoles normales supérieures, and the Centrale and Mines groups. Mathematics dominates the timetable.
  • Literary prépas (hypokhâgne in the first year and khâgne in the second) prepare for the écoles normales supérieures in humanities and for some business and translation schools.
  • Economic and commercial prépas (ECG, ECT) prepare for the top business schools such as HEC Paris, ESSEC, and ESCP.

There are also preparatory classes for veterinary schools, military academies, and art schools, and technological streams (TSI, TPC, ATS) for holders of technological baccalauréats.

Student life

Prépas are notorious for their punishing workload — often described as 50 to 60 hours of study a week — weekly written examinations, and the oral interrogations called khôlles (or colles). Students who repeat the second year are called cinq-demi ("five-halves") in the argot of the system, in a hierarchy of nicknames derived from the fractions 1/2 (demi) for first-years and 3/2 (trois-demi) for second-years — an allusion to integrating into the École Polytechnique (the "X"). Famous prépas are concentrated in a handful of prestigious Parisian lycées, including Louis-le-Grand and Henri-IV, alongside strong provincial lycées such as Le Parc (Lyon) and Clemenceau (Nantes).

Criticism

The system is criticised for social reproduction: students from privileged backgrounds are heavily overrepresented, and prépas absorb a disproportionate share of spending per student compared with universities. Defenders reply that prépas are free or nearly free in public lycées, meritocratic in selection, and provide an intensive education whose graduates staff much of France's scientific, industrial, and administrative elite. The state has responded with outreach schemes, targets for scholarship students, and the opening of prépas in less advantaged areas.
