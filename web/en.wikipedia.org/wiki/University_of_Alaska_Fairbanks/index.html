University of Alaska Fairbanks

The University of Alaska Fairbanks (UAF) is a public land-grant research university in Fairbanks, Alaska. Founded in 1917 as the Alaska Agricultural College and School of Mines, it is the flagship campus of the University of Alaska system and the oldest university in the state.

Academics and research

UAF enrols roughly 9,000 students across its Fairbanks campus and community campuses throughout rural Alaska. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity" and is especially known for Arctic and northern research through institutes such as the Geophysical Institute (home of the Poker Flat Research Range, the only university-owned rocket range in the United States), the International Arctic Research Center, and the Institute of Northern Engineering. UAF is the only university in the world located at 200 miles south of the Arctic Circle with such a broad Arctic research programme, and it administers the Alaska Sea Grant and Cooperative Extension services statewide.

Campus and athletics

The main Troth Yeddha' campus sits on a ridge overlooking the Tanana Valley. Its athletic teams are the Alaska Nanooks. The university also operates the University of Alaska Museum of the North.
