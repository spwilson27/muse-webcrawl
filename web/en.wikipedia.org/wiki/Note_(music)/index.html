Note (music)

In music, a note is a symbol denoting a musical sound. In common usage, "note" can refer both to the symbol used in musical notation and to the pitched sound itself, characterised by its pitch and duration. Notes are the building blocks of melody and harmony in most Western music.

Note names and pitch

In the English- and Dutch-speaking traditions, pitches are named with the letters A through G, modified by sharps (♯) and flats (♭) for the chromatic intermediate pitches. A note's pitch class recurs at every octave; by convention, the A above middle C is tuned to 440 Hz (A440) as a standard reference. Other naming systems include the solfège syllables (do, re, mi…) and German notation, in which H denotes B natural and B denotes B flat.

Note values

In written music, the duration of a note is shown by its note value — whole note (semibreve), half note (minim), quarter note (crotchet), eighth note (quaver) and so on — each normally lasting half the value of the previous one. Dots extend a note's length by half, and ties join durations together. The visual form combines a notehead, a stem and, for shorter values, flags or beams.

History

Systems of notation developed from the neumes of medieval chant through mensural notation to the modern staff notation standardised in the Baroque period. Modern notation also encompasses graphic scores and tablature for particular instruments.
