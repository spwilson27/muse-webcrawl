The Barbican Centre is a performing arts centre in the Barbican Estate of the City of London, and the largest of its kind in Europe. Opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 3 March 1982, it presents classical and contemporary music, theatre, film, dance and visual art, and is home to the London Symphony Orchestra and associate home of the BBC Symphony Orchestra.

Facilities

The complex contains Barbican Hall (about 1,950 seats), the Barbican Theatre (about 1,160 seats), the smaller Pit theatre, cinemas, the Barbican Art Gallery and the Curve gallery, as well as a library, restaurants and a conservatory — the second largest in London. It forms part of the wider Brutalist Barbican Estate of housing built on a bombsite cleared during the Blitz.

Reputation

The Centre's labyrinthine layout has long been notorious, but its programming is internationally acclaimed, and the estate is Grade II listed. A major long-term renewal programme of the Centre was developed in the 2020s.
