Karl Lagerfeld

Karl Otto Lagerfeld (10 September 1933 – 19 February 2019) was a German fashion designer, creative director, artist and photographer. He is best known for reviving the house of Chanel, whose creative director he was from 1983 until his death, and for his long tenure at Fendi (from 1965) alongside his own eponymous label. His powdered white ponytail, dark glasses, high starched collars and fingerless gloves made him one of the most recognisable figures in fashion.

Career

Lagerfeld moved to Paris as a teenager and won the coats category of the 1954 International Wool Secretariat competition (Yves Saint Laurent won for dresses). He worked at Balmain and Jean Patou, freelanced for Chloé and others, and joined Fendi in 1965, a collaboration that lasted the rest of his life. In 1983 he took over Chanel, then widely considered moribund, and rebuilt it into the most profitable luxury house in the world by reworking Coco Chanel's codes — tweed, camellias, pearls and the interlocked-C logo — for contemporary tastes.

Personal life and image

An obsessive reader and collector, Lagerfeld amassed vast libraries and homes filled with art and design. His companion of 18 years, Jacques de Bascher, died in 1989. In later life his Birman cat Choupette became a celebrity in her own right. He was famously outspoken, and some remarks — on weight, immigration and the #MeToo movement — drew controversy. He died in Paris in February 2019 after treatment for pancreatic cancer; the 2023 Met Gala, "Karl Lagerfeld: A Line of Beauty", was held in his honour.
