Operation Jupiter (1944)

Date: 10–11 July 1944

Location: Hill 112, near Caen, Normandy, France

Part of: Battle for Caen, Second World War

Belligerents: United Kingdom vs. Germany

Result: Inconclusive; Hill 112 contested

Operation Jupiter was an offensive by the British VIII Corps during the Normandy campaign of the Second World War. Conducted on 10–11 July 1944, immediately after Operation Charnwood, it aimed to recapture Hill 112 and the villages of Baron-sur-Odon, Fontaine-Étoupefour, Château de Fontaine, and Éterville, and to secure a bridgehead over the River Odon.

Background

Hill 112, southwest of Caen, had first been seized by British forces during Operation Epsom in late June but abandoned in the face of German counterattacks. The height dominated the ground between the Orne and Odon rivers and was regarded by both sides as key terrain for any advance south of Caen. Its German defenders included elements of the Waffen-SS panzer divisions that had fought there during Epsom.

The battle

The attack was led by the 43rd (Wessex) Infantry Division, supported by tanks of the 31st Tank Brigade — including Churchill tanks and Crocodile flame-throwers — and a heavy artillery program planned by General Richard O'Connor. On 10 July British infantry fought their way into the villages and onto the hill's summit in bitter close combat. German counterattacks from the far side of the hill, where German positions enjoyed dead ground immune to British artillery, repeatedly retook the crest. Fighting continued through 11 July, with the British holding the northern slopes and villages but unable to secure the entire hill.

Aftermath

Jupiter gained ground and inflicted heavy losses, tying down German armoured units, but Hill 112 was not fully cleared until early August, after the German withdrawal following the American breakout in Operation Cobra. The hill is remembered as one of the most bitterly contested positions of the Normandy campaign, memorialized by veterans of the 43rd Division.
