Semiochemical

A semiochemical is a chemical substance or mixture that carries a message — it mediates interactions between organisms. The term comes from the Greek semeion, meaning "sign" or "signal." Semiochemicals are central to chemical ecology and are especially well studied in insects.

Classification

Semiochemicals are divided by whether they act within a species or between species. Pheromones act intraspecifically — between members of the same species — and include sex, aggregation, alarm, and trail pheromones. Chemicals acting between species are called allelochemicals, subdivided by who benefits: a kairomone benefits the receiver (for example, a predator locating prey by its odour), an allomone benefits the emitter (such as a defensive secretion), and a synomone benefits both parties (as when floral scents attract pollinators).

Applications

Semiochemicals are widely used in integrated pest management. Synthetic pheromones are deployed in traps to monitor pest populations, in mass trapping, and in mating-disruption programmes in which orchards or forests are saturated with a pest's sex pheromone so that males cannot locate females. Because they are species-specific and used in tiny quantities, they offer an environmentally benign alternative or supplement to broad-spectrum insecticides.
