Fama–French five-factor model

The Fama–French five-factor model is an asset pricing model developed by Eugene Fama and Kenneth French in 2015. It extends their earlier three-factor model by adding profitability and investment factors to the market, size, and value factors, in an attempt to explain the cross-section of average stock returns more completely.

The factors

The five factors are: the market risk premium; SMB (small minus big, the size factor); HML (high minus low, the value factor based on book-to-market equity); RMW (robust minus weak, based on operating profitability); and CMA (conservative minus aggressive, based on investment or asset growth).

Motivation and reception

The added factors were motivated by the dividend discount model, which implies that expected returns increase with book-to-market and profitability and decrease with investment. Empirical tests find the five-factor model improves on the three-factor model for many portfolios, though the value factor becomes partly redundant, and the model still struggles with small, unprofitable, heavily investing growth stocks.
