Tamar of Georgia

Tamar (Georgian: თამარ მეფე, Tamar Mepe, "King Tamar"; c. 1160 – 18 January 1213) was Queen of Georgia from 1184 until her death. Her reign is regarded as the pinnacle of Georgia's Golden Age, when the kingdom reached its greatest territorial extent and cultural flourishing in the Middle Ages.

Early life and accession

Tamar was the daughter of King George III. She was crowned co-ruler in 1178 and succeeded her father in 1184 — the first woman to rule Georgia in her own right. The powerful nobility initially forced her to accept limitations on royal power and a husband, the Rus' prince Yuri, whom she later divorced, an unprecedented step.

Reign

Under Tamar, Georgia expanded across much of the Caucasus and eastern Anatolia. Georgian armies won celebrated victories at Shamkor (1195) and Basian (c. 1202), and in 1204 Georgia helped establish the Empire of Trebizond. She married David Soslan, an Alan prince, who served as her consort and military commander.

Legacy

Tamar's court patronised the poet Shota Rustaveli, traditionally credited with the national epic The Knight in the Panther's Skin, dedicated to her. She is venerated as a saint in the Georgian Orthodox Church, and churches, monasteries such as Vardzia, and countless legends preserve her memory. Her burial place remains unknown.
