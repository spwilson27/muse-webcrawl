SNOMED CT (Systematized Nomenclature of Medicine Clinical Terms) is a systematically organised collection of medical terms providing codes, terms, synonyms and definitions used in clinical documentation and reporting. It is the most comprehensive multilingual clinical healthcare terminology in the world and is a core standard for representing clinical information in electronic health records.

Structure

SNOMED CT consists of three kinds of component. Concepts represent clinical meanings — disorders, procedures, body structures, organisms, substances and many others — each identified by a permanent numeric code. Descriptions link human-readable terms, including synonyms and translations, to concepts. Relationships link concepts to one another, forming a hierarchy through is-a relationships and defining concepts through attributes, which allows computers to reason over clinical data: for example, to determine that viral pneumonia is both a lung disorder and an infectious disease. Content is organised into top-level hierarchies including clinical finding, procedure, body structure, organism, substance, pharmaceutical product, observable entity and environment.

History

SNOMED CT originated in 1965 with the Systematized Nomenclature of Pathology (SNOP), published by the College of American Pathologists, which expanded into the multi-axial SNOMED of the 1970s–1990s. SNOMED CT itself was created in 2002 by merging SNOMED Reference Terminology (SNOMED RT) with the United Kingdom's Clinical Terms Version 3 (the Read Codes). Ownership passed in 2007 to the International Health Terminology Standards Development Organisation (IHTSDO), renamed SNOMED International in 2016, a not-for-profit body owned by its member countries.

Use

SNOMED CT is used in dozens of countries, is mandated for clinical records in systems such as England's NHS, and underpins coding of problems, procedures and findings in electronic health record systems. It is cross-mapped to classifications such as ICD-10 and ICD-11 and to laboratory terminology such as LOINC, supporting interoperability, decision support, research and health statistics. Member countries receive the terminology free at the point of use; non-members license it through SNOMED International.
