The Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS), an agency of the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA), is the public health regulatory agency responsible for ensuring that the United States' commercial supply of meat, poultry, and egg products is safe, wholesome, and correctly labeled and packaged. The FSIS was established by the Secretary of Agriculture in 1977 following reorganisation of the USDA's meat and poultry inspection functions.

Responsibilities

FSIS derives its authority from the Federal Meat Inspection Act of 1906, the Poultry Products Inspection Act of 1957, and the Egg Products Inspection Act of 1970. Federal inspectors are present in slaughterhouses and processing plants to examine animals before and after slaughter, monitor sanitation, and verify that products meet federal standards. The agency also sets requirements for labeling, food additives, and recalls contaminated or adulterated products from commerce.

Organisation and staff

FSIS employs roughly 9,000 people, the great majority of whom are inspectors and veterinarians stationed in approximately 6,000 federal slaughter, food processing, distribution, and import establishments. The agency's headquarters are in Washington, D.C., with district offices across the country.
