London commuter belt

CountryUnited Kingdom
RegionSouth East, East and parts of the South West and East Midlands, England
Core cityLondon
Approximate extentUp to about 80 km (50 miles) from central London, varying by definition

The London commuter belt is the area surrounding Greater London from which a significant number of people commute into the capital for work. It extends well beyond the Greater London boundary into the home counties — Hertfordshire, Buckinghamshire, Berkshire, Surrey, Kent and Essex — and further into parts of Hampshire, Oxfordshire, Bedfordshire, Cambridgeshire, Sussex and beyond. It broadly corresponds to the London metropolitan area as defined for statistical purposes.

Extent and definitions

There is no single official boundary. The Office for National Statistics defines a London travel-to-work area based on commuting flows, while the wider "Greater South East" is used in planning contexts. The Metropolitan Green Belt, established to contain London's sprawl, separates the built-up area from many commuter towns, which grew up along railway lines radiating from the capital — a pattern sometimes described as the "stockbroker belt" in the affluent south-western sector.

Characteristics

Towns in the belt include St Albans, Guildford, Reading, Maidstone, Chelmsford, Hemel Hempstead, High Wycombe and Tunbridge Wells. New towns such as Milton Keynes, Harlow, Basildon and Stevenage were built partly to house London's overspill population. House prices in the belt are among the highest in the United Kingdom, reflecting demand from commuters, and rail services into London terminals are among the busiest commuter routes in Europe.
