The Towans (from Cornish tewyn, sand dunes) is a system of coastal sand dunes extending for about three miles along St Ives Bay on the north coast of Cornwall, England, from the mouth of the River Hayle northeastwards towards Gwithian and the Red River. Individual sections are known by local names including Hayle Towans, Riviere Towans, Mexico Towans, Upton Towans, and Gwithian Towans.

Landscape and history

The dunes formed over centuries from sand blown ashore from the bay, and in places reach substantial heights. Parts of the Towans overlie the submerged remains of older land surfaces and buried settlements; St Gothian's chapel at Gwithian was famously excavated from the sand. In the 19th century the area behind the dunes was an industrial hub: the Harveys foundry and the Cornish Copper Company operated at Hayle, and dynamite works were established among the dunes at Upton Towans — the remote location chosen for safety — leaving scattered ruins. During the Second World War the Towans were used for military training and defended against invasion.

Nature and recreation

Much of the dune system is designated for its wildlife value, supporting dune grassland plants, butterflies, and breeding birds, and the Gwithian to Hayle section falls within a Site of Special Scientific Interest. The beaches fronting the Towans are popular for surfing and bathing, and the South West Coast Path crosses the dunes. The area contains numerous holiday camps, chalets, and beach cafés.
