Cem Karaca

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cem Karaca
Born: 5 April 1945, Istanbul, Turkey
Died: 8 February 2004 (aged 58), Istanbul
Genres: Anatolian rock, progressive rock, folk rock
Occupations: Singer-songwriter, musician

Cem Karaca (5 April 1945 – 8 February 2004) was a Turkish rock musician and one of the most important figures in the Anatolian rock movement. He was a graduate of Robert College in Istanbul. Together with groups such as Moğollar and Kurtalan Ekspres, and musicians such as Barış Manço and Erkin Koray, he helped create a distinctly Turkish form of rock music that combined Western rock instrumentation with Turkish folk melodies, scales and instruments such as the bağlama.

Career

Karaca began his career in the early 1960s performing with cover bands such as the Dinamikler and the Apaşlar, singing rock and roll songs in English. He subsequently moved toward performing his own Turkish-language material. With the band Cem Karaca ve Apaşlar he released the single "Emrah" in 1967, one of the earliest recordings in the Anatolian rock style. In the 1970s he worked with the groups Kardaşlar, Moğollar and Dervişan, producing songs such as "Tamirci Çırağı", "Namus Belası" and "Bu Son Olsun", which combined social commentary with rock arrangements.

Exile and return

Karaca's lyrics addressed poverty, inequality and workers' rights, and after the 1980 Turkish coup d'état he was unable to return to Turkey from a concert tour in Germany; an arrest warrant was issued and his Turkish citizenship was later revoked. He lived in exile in West Germany through most of the 1980s, recording the German-language album Die Kanaken (1984). Following a meeting with Prime Minister Turgut Özal and an amnesty, he returned to Turkey in 1987 and resumed recording and performing, releasing albums such as Merhaba Gençler ve Her Zaman Genç Kalanlar (1987) and Nerede Kalmıştık? (1992).

Death and legacy

Karaca died of a heart attack on 8 February 2004 in Istanbul. He is remembered as one of the pioneers of Turkish rock, and his songs remain widely covered and broadcast in Turkey.
