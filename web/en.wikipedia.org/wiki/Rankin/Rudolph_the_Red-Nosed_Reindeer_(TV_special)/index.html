Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer (TV special)

Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer is a 1964 American stop-motion animated Christmas television special produced by Videocraft International (later Rankin/Bass Productions) and directed by Larry Roemer. Based on the 1949 Johnny Marks song and the 1939 Robert L. May story, it first aired on NBC on 6 December 1964 and has been broadcast annually ever since, making it one of the longest-running Christmas specials in US television history; it moved to CBS in 1972.

Plot and production

The special follows Rudolph, a young reindeer shunned for his glowing red nose, and Hermey, an elf who wants to be a dentist. They run away to the Island of Misfit Toys, encounter the prospector Yukon Cornelius and the Abominable Snow Monster, and Rudolph ultimately guides Santa's sleigh through a storm. Burl Ives narrates as Sam the Snowman. The special was filmed in Rankin/Bass's trademark "Animagic" stop-motion process in Japan, with music by Johnny Marks, including "A Holly Jolly Christmas" and "The Most Wonderful Day of the Year".

Legacy

The characters' designs and the special's story expansions (Hermey, Yukon Cornelius, the Island of Misfit Toys) have become inseparable from the Rudolph story in popular culture, and the special is regularly ranked among the greatest Christmas television programmes.
