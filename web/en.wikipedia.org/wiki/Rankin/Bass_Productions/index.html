Rankin/Bass Productions, Inc. was an American production company founded in 1960 by Arthur Rankin Jr. and Jules Bass. It is famous for its seasonal television specials, particularly its stop-motion "Animagic" Christmas productions, including Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer (1964), Frosty the Snowman (1969, cel-animated), Santa Claus Is Comin' to Town (1970), and The Year Without a Santa Claus (1974), which remain holiday broadcasting staples.

Productions

The studio's animation was largely produced in Japan, first with Tadahito Mochinaga's MOM Production and later with studios that fed talent into Topcraft and ultimately Studio Ghibli. Beyond holiday specials, Rankin/Bass produced animated features and series such as The Hobbit (1977), The Last Unicorn (1982), The Flight of Dragons (1982), and the syndicated series ThunderCats and SilverHawks in the 1980s.

Closure and legacy

The company ceased producing new material in the late 1980s and was dissolved in 2001; its library passed through several owners, much of the pre-1974 catalogue now being held by NBCUniversal/DreamWorks Classics and the later catalogue by Warner Bros. The studio's distinctive puppet specials continue to define the look of American Christmas television, and their characters — including the Island of Misfit Toys — are enduring pieces of popular culture.
