Formula: C16H17NO4
Class: Amaryllidaceae alkaloid
Sources: Lycoris, daffodils, snowdrops and relatives

Lycorine

Lycorine is a crystalline alkaloid found in various species of the plant family Amaryllidaceae, including spider lilies (Lycoris), daffodils (Narcissus), snowflakes (Leucojum), and Clivia. It is one of the most widespread of the Amaryllidaceae alkaloids.

Effects

Lycorine is toxic: it acts as an emetic and, in sufficient doses, can cause nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and more serious poisoning; ingestion of daffodil bulbs (sometimes mistaken for onions) commonly involves lycorine. In cell studies lycorine inhibits protein synthesis and shows antiviral and antitumour activity in vitro, which has made it a subject of pharmacological research, though it has no established clinical use.
