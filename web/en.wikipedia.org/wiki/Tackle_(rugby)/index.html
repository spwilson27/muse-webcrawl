Tackle (rugby)

In rugby union and rugby league, a tackle occurs when a player carrying the ball is held and brought to the ground by one or more opponents. The tackle is the principal means by which the defending team halts the progress of the ball carrier, and the laws governing it are among the most detailed and strictly enforced in the sport, largely because of concerns over player safety.

Laws

Under the laws of rugby union, a tackle occurs when the ball carrier is held by one or more opponents and is brought to ground, meaning that one or both knees touch the ground, the player is sitting on the ground, or the player is on top of another player who is on the ground. A tackler must attempt to grasp the ball carrier below the shoulders; tackles above the line of the shoulders (high tackles) are illegal and are penalised, with sanctions ranging from a penalty to a yellow or red card depending on the degree of danger. A player may not tackle an opponent whose feet are off the ground, nor use a shoulder charge without attempting to wrap the arms.

Once tackled, the ball carrier must immediately pass or release the ball, and the tackler must release the tackled player and roll away. Players arriving at the resulting contest for possession form a ruck if the ball is on the ground, or a maul if the ball carrier remains on their feet and is bound together with opponents.

Rugby league differences

In rugby league, the tackle has a different function: each team is allowed a set of six tackles (a "set") in possession, and when a tackle is completed the defending team must retreat ten metres while the tackled player stands and plays the ball with the foot. There is no contest for the ball at the tackle in the union sense, and rucks and mauls do not form.

Technique and safety

Coaches teach tacklers to keep the head to one side of the ball carrier ("cheek to cheek"), to drive with the legs, and to wrap the arms around the carrier's thighs or waist. In recent decades the governing bodies have progressively lowered the legal tackle height and increased sanctions for head contact, in response to research linking repeated head impacts with long-term brain injury.
