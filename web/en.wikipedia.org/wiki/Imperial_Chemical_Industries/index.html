Imperial Chemical Industries

IndustryChemicals
Founded1926
Defunct2008 (acquired by AkzoNobel)

Imperial Chemical Industries (ICI) was a British chemical company, once the largest manufacturer in Britain and for much of the twentieth century one of the world's leading producers of chemicals, paints, plastics and pharmaceuticals. It was formed in 1926 by the merger of four major British chemical firms: Brunner Mond, Nobel Explosives, the United Alkali Company and British Dyestuffs, largely in response to competition from Germany's IG Farben and the American DuPont.

At its height ICI employed more than 100,000 people worldwide and was a bellwether of British industry, its market capitalisation used as a barometer of the UK economy. Its researchers were responsible for major discoveries and inventions including polyethylene (1933), the acrylic plastic Perspex, the herbicide paraquat, the beta-blocker propranolol and the agricultural and pharmaceutical products that underpinned its later business.

History

Through the twentieth century ICI diversified into paints (through its Dulux brand), explosives, agrochemicals and pharmaceuticals. In 1993 the company demerged its pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals businesses into a new company, Zeneca, which later became part of AstraZeneca. The remaining ICI refocused on speciality chemicals and paints. In 2008 ICI was acquired by the Dutch company AkzoNobel for about £8 billion, ending its independence after more than eighty years.

Legacy

ICI's former sites, such as Billingham and Wilton on Teesside and Runcorn in Cheshire, remain important centres of the British chemical industry, and its name survives in legacy businesses and pension funds.
