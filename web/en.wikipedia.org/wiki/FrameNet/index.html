FrameNet

FrameNet is a lexical database of English based on frame semantics, a theory of meaning developed by Charles J. Fillmore. The project, housed at the International Computer Science Institute in Berkeley, California, documents the semantic frames evoked by words and the syntactic patterns in which they occur, using corpus evidence.

Concepts

A semantic frame is a schematic representation of an event, relation or object, together with its participants (frame elements). For example, the Commercial_transaction frame involves a Buyer, a Seller, Goods and Money, and is evoked by lexical units such as buy, sell, pay and charge, each profiling the frame from a different perspective. Annotators label example sentences from corpora (principally the British National Corpus) for frame elements and grammatical realisations.

Coverage and use

The database contains over 1,200 frames, more than 13,000 lexical units and some 200,000 annotated sentences. FrameNet data is used in natural language processing for semantic role labelling, information extraction and question answering, and FrameNets have been built for many other languages, including Spanish, German, Japanese and Brazilian Portuguese.
