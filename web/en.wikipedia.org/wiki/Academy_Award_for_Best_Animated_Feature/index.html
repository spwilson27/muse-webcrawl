The Academy Award for Best Animated Feature is an Academy Award (Oscar) given annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences. It was introduced at the 74th Academy Awards in 2002, honouring animated films from 2001 onward. The first winner was Shrek (2001).

History

For decades, animated films were eligible for Best Picture but rarely nominated; Beauty and the Beast (1991) was the first animated Best Picture nominee, followed by Up (2009) and Toy Story 3 (2010). Growing output of animated features led the Academy to create a dedicated category, initially requiring at least eight eligible films in a year.

Winners and records

Pixar has dominated the category, winning with films including Finding Nemo, The Incredibles, Ratatouille, WALL-E, Up, Toy Story 3, Brave, Inside Out, Coco, Toy Story 4 and Soul. Other studios' winners include Spirited Away (Studio Ghibli; the first non-English-language winner), Happy Feet, Rango, Frozen, Big Hero 6, Zootopia, Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse, Encanto and Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio. Hayao Miyazaki won twice, for Spirited Away and The Boy and the Heron.

Criticism

The category has been criticised for allegedly ghettoising animation and for voting patterns favouring big-studio family films, since many Academy voters historically did not watch all the nominees.
