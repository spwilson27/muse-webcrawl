Xin Zhui (died 163 BCE), also known as Lady Dai, was the wife of Li Cang, Marquis of Dai and Chancellor of the feudal kingdom of Changsha during China's Western Han dynasty. She is famous for her corpse, discovered in 1971 at the Mawangdui archaeological site near Changsha, which after more than 2,100 years remained astonishingly well preserved — her skin soft and elastic, joints flexible, and internal organs largely intact.

Life

Xin Zhui lived a life of aristocratic luxury in the Han capital region of Changsha. Grave goods and medical texts buried with her suggest she suffered from a range of ailments in later life, including coronary heart disease, gallstones and diabetes; an autopsy found melon seeds in her stomach, indicating she died shortly after a meal, most likely of a heart attack at around fifty years of age.

Burial and discovery

Her tomb, designated M1 at Mawangdui, contained over a thousand artefacts: lacquerware, silk garments, cosmetics, musical instruments and the famous Mawangdui Silk Texts. Her body lay in the innermost of four nested coffins, immersed in a slightly acidic reddish liquid within an airtight, charcoal-and-clay sealed tomb, conditions that inhibited decay and bacterial growth.

Legacy

Xin Zhui's remains are held by the Hunan Museum, where she is a centrepiece exhibit. Her preservation is considered one of the most remarkable examples of a "wet" mummy anywhere in the world and has provided extensive information about Han dynasty diet, medicine and funerary practice.
