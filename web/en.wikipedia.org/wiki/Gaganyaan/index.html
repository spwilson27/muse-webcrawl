Gaganyaan (from Sanskrit gagana, "celestial", and yāna, "craft" or "vehicle") is India's crewed orbital spacecraft programme, developed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). The programme aims to make India the fourth nation to independently launch humans into space, after the Soviet Union/Russia, the United States and China.

Spacecraft

The Gaganyaan system consists of a crew module carrying up to three astronauts and an attached service module, together forming the orbital module. It is launched by the HLVM3, a human-rated version of ISRO's LVM3 rocket. The crew module is equipped with life-support systems developed with Indian industry, parachutes for a sea recovery, and a crew escape system designed to pull the capsule clear of the rocket in an emergency at any point in the ascent.

Development and tests

Preparatory milestones included the 2014 CARE experiment, in which a crew module prototype made a suborbital flight and splashdown; a successful pad abort test in 2018; and the TV-D1 abort demonstration flight in October 2023, which tested the escape system in flight. A series of uncrewed orbital test flights (the "G" missions) precedes the first crewed flight (H1), in which vyomanauts — astronaut candidates drawn from the Indian Air Force and trained in Russia and at Bengaluru — will spend several days in orbit at around 400 km.

Significance

The programme, whose budget was sanctioned at roughly ₹9,023 crore, anchors India's longer-term human spaceflight ambitions, including a planned Indian space station (Bharatiya Antariksha Station) and a longer-duration crewed lunar mission by 2040. One of the selected astronaut candidates flew to the International Space Station as part of the Axiom Mission 4 in 2025, gaining operational experience ahead of the first Gaganyaan crewed flight.
