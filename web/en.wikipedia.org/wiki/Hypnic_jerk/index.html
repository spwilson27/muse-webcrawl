Hypnic jerk

A hypnic jerk, also called a hypnagogic jerk, sleep start, or night start, is a brief and sudden involuntary contraction of the muscles of the body which occurs when a person is beginning to fall asleep, often causing the person to jump and awaken suddenly for a moment. Hypnic jerks are one form of myoclonus, a category of rapid involuntary muscle movements.

Physically, hypnic jerks resemble the "jump" experienced by a person when startled, sometimes accompanied by a falling sensation or a vivid dream-like image. They are more frequent in childhood and become less common with age; some surveys suggest that between 60 and 70 percent of people experience them. They appear to be a normal physiological phenomenon and are not in themselves a sign of disease.

Causes

The exact cause is not fully understood. Theories include a misinterpretation by the brain of the relaxation of the muscles during the transition to sleep — as motor systems wind down, the brain may mistake the resulting slackness for falling and trigger a protective jerk. Contributing factors include stress and anxiety, fatigue and sleep deprivation, vigorous exercise late in the day, and stimulants such as caffeine and nicotine.

Hypnic jerks occur in the transition between wakefulness and sleep, principally during the hypnagogic state and light NREM sleep. When they occur very frequently and cause significant sleep disturbance or anxiety about falling asleep, they may merit medical evaluation to distinguish them from conditions such as restless legs syndrome or periodic limb movement disorder.
