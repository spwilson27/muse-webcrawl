Dado rail

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A dado rail, also known as a chair rail or surbase, is a type of moulding fixed horizontally to the wall around the perimeter of a room. It caps and protects the dado—the lower portion of the wall, traditionally finished differently from the upper wall, for example with panelling, wainscoting, plaster or wallpaper.

Purpose

The dado rail serves both decorative and practical functions. Originally it protected the wall surface from damage caused by chairs being pushed back against it—hence the North American name "chair rail"—and from other furniture in heavily used rooms. It also provides a visual boundary that allows the wall below and above to be decorated differently, a treatment common in Georgian and Victorian interiors.

Height and materials

Dado rails are traditionally fitted at around 900 mm (36 inches) above floor level, approximately the height of a chair back, though in classical proportion the dado corresponds to the pedestal of a column and may be placed between one quarter and one third of the wall height. Rails are commonly made of timber (MDF or softwood and hardwood in modern practice) and historically also of plaster or composition material, and may carry profiles ranging from a simple bevelled strip to elaborate classical mouldings.
