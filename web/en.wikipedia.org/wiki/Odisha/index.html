Odisha (Odia: ଓଡ଼ିଶା), formerly known as Orissa, is a state in eastern India located along the Bay of Bengal. It is the eighth-largest state by area and the eleventh-largest by population, with over 41 million inhabitants. The state shares borders with West Bengal and Jharkhand to the north, Chhattisgarh to the west, and Andhra Pradesh to the south. Odisha has a coastline of 485 kilometres (301 mi).

History

The region corresponds in part to the ancient kingdom of Kalinga, which was invaded by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka in 261 BCE, resulting in the Kalinga War. Modern Odisha was established on 1 April 1936 as a province in British India, consisting predominantly of Odia-speaking regions — the first Indian state formed on a linguistic basis. The state's name was officially changed from Orissa to Odisha in 2011, alongside the language's renaming from Oriya to Odia.

Culture

Odisha is home to the Jagannath Temple in Puri, one of the Char Dham pilgrimage sites, the Konark Sun Temple, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the classical dance form Odissi, one of India's major classical dances. Chilika Lake, Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon, lies on its coast.
