Chlorargyrite

Chlorargyrite specimen

Chlorargyrite is the mineral form of silver chloride (AgCl), a halide mineral in the chlorargyrite group. It is also known historically as horn silver or cerargyrite. The name derives from the Greek chloros, "pale green," and argyros, "silver," referring to its composition and typical coloration.

Properties

Chlorargyrite crystallizes in the isometric system, though well-formed crystals are uncommon; it more often occurs as crusts, masses, or horn-like aggregates. It is very soft (Mohs hardness 1.5–2.5), sectile—it can be cut like wax or horn—and has a high specific gravity of about 5.5. Fresh material is pale green, grey, or colorless, but it is photosensitive and darkens to violet-brown or black on exposure to light, a property related to the photochemistry once exploited in photography.

Occurrence

The mineral forms in the oxidized zones of silver deposits, especially in arid regions where it may be an important ore of silver. Notable localities include Broken Hill in Australia, mines in Chile, Bolivia, Mexico, and the western United States. Bromian varieties (embolite) contain appreciable bromine substituting for chlorine.
