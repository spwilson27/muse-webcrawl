Monterey International Pop Festival

DatesJune 16–18, 1967
LocationMonterey County Fairgrounds, Monterey, California
Founded byJohn Phillips, Lou Adler, Alan Pariser, Derek Taylor
Attendance25,000–90,000 (estimated)

The Monterey International Pop Festival was a three-day music festival held June 16–18, 1967, at the Monterey County Fairgrounds in Monterey, California. It is widely regarded as the first major American rock festival and a defining event of the Summer of Love.

Performances

The festival featured the first major American appearances by the Jimi Hendrix Experience — who famously set his guitar on fire — and the Who, who destroyed their instruments on stage. Otis Redding gave a breakthrough performance to a largely white audience, Janis Joplin stunned the crowd with Big Brother and the Holding Company, and Ravi Shankar performed a celebrated set of Indian classical music. Other acts included Jefferson Airplane, the Grateful Dead, Buffalo Springfield, Simon & Garfunkel, the Byrds, the Animals, and the Mamas & the Papas.

Legacy

The festival was filmed by D. A. Pennebaker and released as the concert film Monterey Pop (1968). It became a model for later festivals such as Woodstock, and its theme song, "San Francisco (Be Sure to Wear Flowers in Your Hair)" performed by Scott McKenzie, captured the spirit of the counterculture summer.
