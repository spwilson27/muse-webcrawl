Spawn (biology)

Spawn is the eggs and sperm released or deposited into water by aquatic animals. As a verb, to spawn refers to the process of releasing the eggs and sperm, and the act of both sexes is called spawning. Most aquatic animals, except for aquatic mammals and reptiles, reproduce through the process of spawning.

Spawn are the unfertilized or fertilized eggs of fish, amphibians, molluscs, crustaceans, and other invertebrates such as corals and sea urchins. In most species, spawning involves external fertilization: the female releases eggs and the male releases sperm (milt) into the water, where fertilization occurs. The eggs of many species are buoyant and develop into free-swimming larvae, while others are demersal and adhesive, attached to plants, rocks, or nests guarded by a parent.

Strategies

Spawning strategies vary enormously. Broadcast spawners, such as most marine fish and corals, release huge numbers of gametes synchronously, often timed to lunar cycles, tides, or season. Many freshwater and anadromous fish migrate to specific spawning grounds: salmon famously return to the streams where they were born, leaping rapids and waterfalls, and Pacific salmon die after a single spawning (semelparity). Other fish, such as cichlids and sticklebacks, build nests and guard their eggs and fry.

Amphibian spawn

Frogs and toads lay their spawn as gelatinous strings or clusters of eggs in ponds; the eggs hatch into tadpoles. Amphibian breeding is usually triggered by temperature and rainfall, and many species gather in explosive breeding aggregations.

Ecology and fisheries

Spawning aggregations are ecologically important food sources and are heavily targeted by fisheries, making protection of spawning grounds and seasons a key conservation measure. Artificial spawning in aquaculture and hatcheries supports restocking of species such as salmon and trout.
