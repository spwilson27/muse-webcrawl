Håkon Wium Lie

Håkon Wium Lie (born 26 July 1965) is a Norwegian web pioneer, software engineer, and activist, best known as the inventor of Cascading Style Sheets (CSS). He proposed CSS while working at CERN in 1994 and developed the specification together with Bert Bos at the World Wide Web Consortium. He later served as chief technology officer of Opera Software.

Career

Wium Lie studied at Østfold University College, the University of West Georgia, and MIT's Media Lab, earning a master's degree in visual studies in 1991. He worked at the Norwegian Telecom Research institute and at CERN, where Tim Berners-Lee had created the World Wide Web. In October 1994 he published "Cascading HTML Style Sheets: A Proposal", arguing that style sheets for web documents should combine author, reader, and browser preferences in a "cascade". With Bert Bos he developed the proposal into the CSS1 specification, a W3C Recommendation in December 1996, and he co-authored the standard reference book Cascading Style Sheets: Designing for the Web with Bos.

Opera and later work

As CTO of Opera Software from 1998, Wium Lie promoted web standards, small-screen rendering for mobile browsers, and the Acid2 test for standards compliance. He campaigned for browsers' support of downloadable fonts and for the adoption of the <video> element. In 2005 he was a co-founder of YesLogic, the company behind the Prince XML formatter, and he has been active in Norwegian politics and cultural debates, including opposition to software patents and support for preserving Norway's bokhylla digitised book collection.
