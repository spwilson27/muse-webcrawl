Hyperpituitarism

Hyperpituitarism is the condition of excessive secretion of one or more of the hormones of the pituitary gland. It is most commonly caused by a benign pituitary adenoma that autonomously produces a specific anterior pituitary hormone, and the clinical syndrome depends on which hormone is in excess.

Most hyperpituitarism is caused by hormone-secreting pituitary adenomas.

Types

Excess growth hormone causes gigantism in children and acromegaly in adults; excess prolactin (prolactinoma, the commonest functioning adenoma) causes galactorrhoea and hypogonadism; excess ACTH causes Cushing's disease; and, rarely, excess TSH or gonadotrophins causes secondary hyperthyroidism or sex-hormone effects. Large adenomas of any type can also cause symptoms by compressing the optic chiasm and normal pituitary tissue.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines measurement of the relevant hormone (for example IGF-1 for growth hormone excess) with magnetic resonance imaging of the sella. Treatment is usually transsphenoidal surgery to remove the adenoma, supplemented by medical therapy — dopamine agonists for prolactinomas, somatostatin analogues and growth-hormone receptor antagonists for acromegaly — and, in some cases, radiotherapy.
