Canoe sprint

A kayak four (K-4) race at an international regatta

Canoe sprint is a water sport in which athletes race in canoes or kayaks on calm water over set distances. It is one of the two main disciplines of the International Canoe Federation, alongside canoe slalom, and has been an Olympic sport for men since 1936 and for women since 1948.

Boats and events

Races are held in kayaks (K), in which the paddler sits and uses a double-bladed paddle, and canoes (C), in which the paddler kneels and uses a single-bladed paddle. Boats are raced as singles, doubles and fours (K-1, K-2, K-4, C-1, C-2). Olympic and world championship distances are 200 m, 500 m and 1000 m, raced in nine lanes on a straight, buoyed course. Women's canoe events were added to the Olympic programme at Tokyo 2020, long after women's kayak events had been established.

Competition

The discipline's premier events are the Olympic Games and the annual ICF Canoe Sprint World Championships. Germany, Hungary, and the Soviet Union have historically been the most successful nations, with Hungary especially dominant in women's kayak racing; Birgit Fischer of Germany, winner of eight Olympic gold medals between 1980 and 2004, is among the most decorated athletes in the sport.

Related disciplines

Canoe sprint is distinct from canoe marathon, raced over long distances with portages, and from paracanoe, which was added to the Paralympic programme in 2016 for kayak and 2020 for va'a (outrigger) boats.
