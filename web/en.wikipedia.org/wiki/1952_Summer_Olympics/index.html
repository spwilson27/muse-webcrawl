The 1952 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XV Olympiad, were held from 19 July to 3 August 1952 in Helsinki, Finland. Helsinki had originally been awarded the 1940 Games, which were cancelled because of the Second World War. The 1952 Games are remembered for the Olympic debut of the Soviet Union, whose rivalry with the United States inaugurated the sporting dimension of the Cold War.

Highlights

Czechoslovak long-distance runner Emil Zátopek achieved an unprecedented treble, winning the 5,000 metres, 10,000 metres, and the marathon — the last in his first attempt at the distance. Hungarian boxer László Papp won the second of his three Olympic gold medals, and the Hungarian "Golden Team" won the football tournament. The Games also marked the first Olympic participation of Israel and the return of Germany and Japan after the war.

Cold War context

The Soviet Union, competing for the first time since the Russian Empire in 1912, housed its athletes in a separate Olympic Village at Otaniemi and engaged in a fierce, unofficial points contest with the United States. The two nations finished nearly level, with the United States winning more gold medals (40 to 22) while the USSR accumulated a comparable overall total.

Organisation

The centrepiece venue was the Helsinki Olympic Stadium, completed in 1938 for the cancelled 1940 Games. Despite Finland's small population and the strains of post-war reparations to the Soviet Union, the Games were praised for their efficient, friendly organisation.
