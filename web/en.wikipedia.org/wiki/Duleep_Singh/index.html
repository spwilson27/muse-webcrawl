Duleep Singh

Maharaja Duleep Singh (6 September 1838 – 22 October 1893), also spelled Dalip Singh, was the last Maharaja of the Sikh Empire. He was Maharaja Ranjit Singh's youngest son, proclaimed king at age five after a period of court intrigue, and deposed at age ten following the British annexation of the Punjab in 1849. Exiled to Britain, he became a favourite of Queen Victoria but later sought to regain his throne and died in Paris.

Early life and reign

Duleep Singh was born in Lahore to Maharani Jind Kaur, the youngest wife of Ranjit Singh. After the assassinations of his half-brothers Kharak Singh, Nau Nihal Singh and Sher Singh, he was proclaimed Maharaja in 1843 with his mother as regent. Following the First and Second Anglo-Sikh Wars, the Treaty of Lahore and the annexation of 1849 forced the boy king to renounce his sovereignty and surrender the Koh-i-Noor diamond to Queen Victoria.

Life in Britain

Placed under the guardianship of Dr John Login, Duleep Singh converted to Christianity in 1853 and settled in England, purchasing the Elveden estate in Suffolk, which he rebuilt as a grand country house. He was a frequent guest of Queen Victoria at Osborne House. In later years, disillusioned and pressing claims for his lands and pension, he re-embraced Sikhism in 1886 and attempted to return to India, but was arrested at Aden. He died in Paris in 1893; his body was buried at Elveden beside his wife and son.
