Étendue

Étendue Symbol: G
SI unit: m²·sr (square metre steradians)
Definition: G = n² A Ω
Field: Nonimaging optics, radiometry
Étendue measures how "spread out" a beam of light is.

Étendue (from the French étendue géométrique, "geometric extent") is a property of light in an optical system that measures how spread out the light is in area and angle. It is defined as the product of the area of the source (or aperture) and the solid angle subtended by the system's entrance pupil, multiplied by the square of the refractive index: G = n²AΩ.

The central result of the theory is the conservation of étendue: in a lossless optical system, the étendue of a beam can never decrease as the light propagates through the system. This is a consequence of Liouville's theorem in Hamiltonian mechanics and of the second law of thermodynamics. Because brightness is flux divided by étendue, it follows that no passive optical device can make an image brighter (in radiance) than the source — a result of practical importance in illumination design, solar concentrators, fibre-optic coupling and projector optics.

Applications

Étendue is a core concept of nonimaging optics, the branch of optics concerned with transferring light efficiently rather than forming images. The maximum possible concentration of sunlight, for example, is set by the étendue of the Sun's disc as seen from Earth, imposing a fundamental limit on solar concentrator design.
