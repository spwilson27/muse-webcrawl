The News International phone hacking scandal was a controversy involving News International (the British arm of Rupert Murdoch's News Corporation), its newspaper the News of the World, and the illegal interception of voicemail messages ("phone hacking") by journalists and private investigators. The scandal erupted publicly in July 2011 and led to the closure of the 168-year-old News of the World, senior resignations, criminal trials, and a judge-led inquiry into British press culture and ethics.

Background

In 2005–07, the News of the World's royal editor Clive Goodman and private investigator Glenn Mulcaire were jailed for intercepting voicemail messages of royal aides. News International maintained that hacking was the work of one "rogue reporter," and the paper's former editor Andy Coulson resigned. Reporting by The Guardian from 2009 onward, along with civil lawsuits by celebrities and others, revealed that hacking had been widespread, targeting politicians, sports figures, actors, and crime victims.

The Milly Dowler revelation and consequences

In July 2011 it was reported that the paper had hacked the voicemail of murdered schoolgirl Milly Dowler in 2002 and deleted messages, giving her family false hope she was alive. Public revulsion forced News International to close the News of the World after its final edition on 10 July 2011. Rebekah Brooks, the paper's editor at the time of the Dowler hacking and by then chief executive of News International, resigned, as did senior Metropolitan Police officers amid criticism of the police's earlier investigations. The government set up the Leveson Inquiry into press practices, which reported in 2012 recommending a new system of press regulation.

Trials and settlements

Police investigations — Operation Weeting (phone hacking), Operation Elveden (payments to officials), and Operation Tuleta (computer hacking) — led to numerous arrests. In 2014 Andy Coulson, who had become Prime Minister David Cameron's director of communications, was convicted of conspiracy to intercept communications and jailed; Brooks and others were acquitted. News Group Newspapers has paid out hundreds of millions of pounds in settlements and costs to hundreds of civil claimants, with litigation continuing for more than a decade.
