Osaka Castle (大坂城, Ōsaka-jō) is a Japanese castle in Chūō-ku, Osaka, Japan. One of the most famous landmarks in the country, it played a major role in the unification of Japan during the Azuchi–Momoyama period of the 16th century. Its main tower, surrounded by moats, massive stone walls, and parkland, is an iconic symbol of the city of Osaka.

History

Construction began in 1583 on the orders of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, who intended it to be the largest and finest castle in Japan, modelled on Azuchi Castle but surpassing it. The central keep was completed within a few years, its gold-leaf ornamentation proclaiming Hideyoshi's wealth and power. After his death, the castle became the stronghold of his son Hideyori and was besieged by Tokugawa Ieyasu in the Siege of Osaka (1614–1615); it fell in the summer campaign of 1615 and the Toyotomi clan was destroyed.

Later history

The Tokugawa shogunate rebuilt the castle in the 1620s with an even taller main tower, which was destroyed by lightning in 1665. The castle remained a symbolically important government post until the Meiji Restoration. The present main tower is a 1931 concrete reconstruction, built with funds raised by the citizens of Osaka; it survived the Second World War air raids and was extensively renovated in 1995–1997. It now houses a museum on Hideyoshi and the castle's history.

The grounds

The castle park covers about 106 hectares and contains turrets, gates, and storehouses designated Important Cultural Properties, along with some 600 cherry trees that make it one of Osaka's most popular cherry-blossom viewing spots.
