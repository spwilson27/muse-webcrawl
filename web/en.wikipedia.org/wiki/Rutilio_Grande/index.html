Rutilio Grande

Rutilio Grande García, SJ (5 July 1928 – 12 March 1977) was a Salvadoran Jesuit priest. He served as parish priest of Aguilares, where his ministry among poor campesinos, influenced by liberation theology and the reforms of the Second Vatican Council, brought him into conflict with landowners and the military government. He was assassinated in 1977 along with two companions, becoming the first priest killed in the violence preceding the Salvadoran Civil War and a pivotal figure in the conversion of his friend Archbishop Óscar Romero to a defender of the oppressed. He was beatified by the Catholic Church in January 2022.

Life

Grande was born in El Paisnal to a poor family and entered the seminary at a young age despite fragile health. He joined the Society of Jesus and was ordained a priest in 1959 in Spain. After further formation he returned to El Salvador, teaching at the Externado San José seminary and serving in pastoral roles.

In 1972 he became parish priest of Aguilares, a sugar-growing town. There he promoted "base ecclesial communities" and the training of lay "Delegates of the Word", encouraging peasants to read the Gospel and reflect on their conditions of poverty and repression. His sermons denounced the injustices of the landowning oligarchy. On 13 February 1977, after denouncing the expulsion of a Colombian priest, he preached an influential sermon—the "Apopa sermon"—declaring that "it is dangerous to be truly Christian in our country".

Assassination

On 12 March 1977, Grande was travelling by jeep to El Paisnal to celebrate Mass when gunmen ambushed the vehicle on the road between Aguilares and El Paisnal, killing him together with Manuel Solórzano, an elderly sacristan, and Nelson Rutilio Lemus, a teenage boy. The killings were widely attributed to security forces or death-squad elements linked to landowners, though no one was prosecuted.

Grande's death profoundly affected Archbishop Romero, who had been considered a conservative. Romero celebrated a single national funeral Mass (the famous misa única) attended by tens of thousands, and from then until his own assassination in 1980 he became the nation's outspoken defender of human rights.

Beatification

Pope Francis recognised Grande's martyrdom in 2020, and Grande was beatified on 22 January 2022 in San Salvador together with his companions Solórzano and Lemus and the Franciscan priest Cosme Spessotto.
