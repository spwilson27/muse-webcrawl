The Dying Swan (French: La Mort du cygne) is a short ballet solo choreographed by Michel Fokine (Mikhail Fokine) in 1905 for the ballerina Anna Pavlova, set to "Le Cygne" (The Swan), the cello solo from Camille Saint-Saëns's The Carnival of the Animals. It is one of the most famous pieces in the ballet repertory and became Pavlova's signature role.

History

Fokine created the solo, at Pavlova's request, for a charity gala in St. Petersburg in 1905; it took only a few minutes to choreograph, drawing on Pavlova's observations of swans at her home and on Alfred Tennyson's poem "The Dying Swan". Fokine later revised the choreography, deepening the swan's struggle against death; the piece in its familiar form emerged around 1907 and was retitled after the dying rather than merely the swan.

The dance

Lasting only a few minutes, the solo consists largely of bourrée steps on pointe with flowing, fluttering arm movements (port de bras) evoking the beating of wings, as the swan glides across a moonlit lake, falters, and sinks to the ground in death. Fokine wrote that the dance was "a symbol of the everlasting struggle in this life and all that is mortal" and a protest against the purely technical virtuosity then dominating ballet.

Legacy

Pavlova performed the solo some 4,000 times around the world, and it shaped the public image of the ballerina for generations. Later interpreters including Maya Plisetskaya, Alicia Markova, and Margot Fonteyn made the role their own. The solo inspired countless imitations and parodies, most famously in Les Ballets Trockadero de Monte Carlo, and remains a rite of passage for prima ballerinas.
