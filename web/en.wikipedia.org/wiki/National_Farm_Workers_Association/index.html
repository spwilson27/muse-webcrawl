National Farm Workers Association (NFWA)
Founded: 1962, Delano, California
Founders: César Chávez, Dolores Huerta, Gilbert Padilla
Successor: United Farm Workers

The National Farm Workers Association (NFWA) was a labor union for farm workers in the United States, founded in Delano, California in 1962 by César Chávez, Dolores Huerta and Gilbert Padilla. It was a predecessor of the United Farm Workers (UFW).

Founding

Chávez had previously worked as an organizer with the Community Service Organization. The NFWA began as a community of mutual aid, offering members services such as a credit union, life insurance and a cooperative, while slowly building toward collective bargaining for the largely Mexican-American farm workforce of California's Central Valley.

Delano grape strike

In September 1965 the NFWA voted to join Filipino farm workers of the Agricultural Workers Organizing Committee in the Delano grape strike, demanding higher wages from grape growers. The strike and the accompanying nationwide grape boycott became a defining campaign of the farm labor movement. In 1966 the NFWA and AWOC merged to form the United Farm Workers Organizing Committee, which became the United Farm Workers union.
