Yoshiki (musician)

Born: Yoshiki Hayashi, 20 November 1965, Tateyama, Chiba, Japan
Occupations: Musician, songwriter, composer, record producer
Instruments: Drums, piano
Known for: Co-founder and leader of X Japan

Yoshiki Hayashi (Japanese: 林 佳樹, born 20 November 1965), known mononymously as Yoshiki, is a Japanese musician, songwriter, composer, and record producer. He is the drummer, pianist, and leader of the heavy metal band X Japan, of which he is the primary composer, and a leading figure of the visual kei movement. Classically trained from childhood, he is known for combining extreme metal drumming with symphonic piano composition.

Early life and X Japan

Yoshiki began piano lessons at four and took up drums at ten after his father's suicide, channelling his grief into music. In 1982 he formed X (later X Japan) with vocalist Toshi in Tateyama. The band became one of Japan's best-selling acts, pioneering visual kei with its flamboyant costumes and hair, and selling tens of millions of records. Yoshiki's compositions, including the ballad "Endless Rain" and the 30-minute "Art of Life", defined the band's sound. After disbanding in 1997 and the death of guitarist hide in 1998, the band reunited in 2007.

Classical and other work

Beyond the band, Yoshiki composed the theme for the 2005 World Expo in Japan, a piano concerto performed for the Emperor's enthronement anniversary, and the theme for the 69th Golden Globe Awards. In 2023 he debuted the supergroup The Last Rockstars and premiered his classical project Yoshiki Classical on world tours including Carnegie Hall and the Royal Albert Hall. His drumming style, performed in a neck brace after years of injury, has required repeated surgeries, including artificial disc replacement in 2017.

Business and philanthropy

Yoshiki has launched wine, kimono, and energy-drink lines, and his Yoshiki Foundation America channels donations to disaster relief, including for the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami. He is frequently cited among the most influential musicians in Japanese popular culture.
