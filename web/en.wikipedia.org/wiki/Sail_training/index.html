Sail training
Related organisationsSail Training International

Sail training is the practice of learning seamanship, teamwork and self-reliance aboard sailing vessels, traditionally large square-rigged "tall ships", as a form of character-building and experiential education. Participants, called trainees, are not passengers: they form part of the crew, standing watches, steering, handling sails, climbing aloft, and helping with navigation and domestic duties.

Purpose

Modern sail training is aimed primarily at young people, though programmes exist for adults and for specific groups. Its goals are personal and social development — confidence, resilience, cooperation and leadership — rather than vocational training for a career at sea, although many navies and merchant marine academies still use sail training vessels for initial officer training.

History

The movement grew out of naval training ships of the 19th and early 20th centuries and the survival, after the Second World War, of a number of national school ships. Civilian sail training organisations formed in many countries in the 1950s–1970s, and in 1956 the first Tall Ships Race was held. Today, events coordinated by Sail Training International bring together fleets of training vessels from dozens of nations.

Vessels

Sail training vessels range from ocean-going square riggers such as barques and full-rigged ships to brigantines, schooners and large yachts. Notable examples include Norway's Sørlandet (1927), Germany's Gorch Fock, Poland's Dar Młodzieży, and Britain's Jubilee Sailing Trust ships designed for mixed-ability crews.
