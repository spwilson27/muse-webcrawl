Parsing, syntax analysis, or syntactic analysis is the process of analysing a string of symbols, either in natural language, computer languages or data structures, conforming to the rules of a formal grammar. The term parsing comes from Latin pars (orationis), meaning part (of speech).

In computer science

In compilers and interpreters, a parser is the component that takes input data (often source code text), preceded by lexical analysis which breaks it into tokens, and builds a data structure — often a parse tree or abstract syntax tree (AST) — that reflects the grammatical structure of the input. Subsequent phases, such as semantic analysis and code generation, operate on this structure.

Parsers are commonly classified as top-down (e.g. recursive descent, LL parsers) or bottom-up (e.g. LR, LALR parsers). Parser generator tools such as yacc, Bison, and ANTLR automatically construct parsers from a grammar specification, often written in Backus–Naur form (BNF).

In linguistics

In linguistics and psycholinguistics, parsing refers to the way humans analyse sentences, assigning syntactic structure to words as they are heard or read. Phenomena such as garden-path sentences, which initially mislead the reader, illuminate the incremental nature of human sentence processing.
