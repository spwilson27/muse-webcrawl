Composite boson

A composite boson is a bound state of particles that behaves as a boson — that is, a particle with integer spin obeying Bose–Einstein statistics — even though its constituents may include fermions.

Examples

Examples include mesons (quark–antiquark pairs), nuclei with even numbers of nucleons (such as the alpha particle and carbon-12 nucleus), atoms with integer total spin, and Cooper pairs of electrons in superconductors.

Behavior

Whether a composite particle acts bosonically depends on the scale of observation: at distances large compared with its internal structure, it behaves as a boson; at short distances its fermionic constituents become apparent. Composite bosons such as helium-4 atoms can undergo Bose–Einstein condensation.
