Tribe of Ephraim

According to the Hebrew Bible, the Tribe of Ephraim was one of the Twelve Tribes of Israel. It descended from Ephraim, the younger son of Joseph; though younger than Manasseh, Ephraim received the greater blessing from Jacob, who crossed his hands to place his right hand on Ephraim's head (Genesis 48). Together Ephraim and Manasseh made up the House of Joseph.

Allotment

The tribe's territory lay in the central hill country north of Benjamin and west of the Jordan, including the important cultic centres of Shiloh and Bethel and later the capital of the northern kingdom at Samaria. Joshua, the successor of Moses, is presented as an Ephraimite, and the tribe was among the most populous and influential in the period of the Judges.

The northern kingdom

After the division of the monarchy, Jeroboam — an Ephraimite — became the first king of the northern Kingdom of Israel, and "Ephraim" in the prophets often stands for the whole northern kingdom. Hosea in particular addresses Ephraim as a wayward child of God. The tribe is listed among the "lost tribes" after the Assyrian conquest of Israel in 722 BCE, though groups such as the Samaritans claim descent from Ephraim and Manasseh.
