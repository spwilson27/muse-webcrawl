The suprachiasmatic nucleus or nuclei (SCN) are a tiny region of the brain in the hypothalamus, situated directly above the optic chiasm. It is responsible for controlling circadian rhythms. The neuronal and hormonal activities it generates regulate many different body functions in a 24-hour cycle.

The SCN sits just above the optic chiasm

Structure and inputs

The human SCN contains roughly 20,000 neurons per side. It receives information about illumination through the retinohypothalamic tract, a direct projection from intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells containing the photopigment melanopsin, which entrains the clock to the light–dark cycle. The SCN is conventionally divided into a ventrolateral "core" receiving retinal input and a dorsomedial "shell".

Molecular clockwork

Individual SCN neurons are cell-autonomous oscillators driven by transcription–translation feedback loops of clock genes such as Period (Per1/2), Cryptochrome, Bmal1, and Clock. Intercellular coupling, largely via the neuropeptide VIP, synchronises the population so the nucleus keeps coherent time even in constant darkness, with a period close to but not exactly 24 hours. Outputs from the SCN time the release of melatonin by the pineal gland, body temperature rhythms, and sleep–wake cycles; lesions of the SCN abolish circadian rhythmicity.
