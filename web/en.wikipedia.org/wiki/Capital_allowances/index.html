Capital allowances

JurisdictionUnited Kingdom
Governing statuteCapital Allowances Act 2001
TypeTax relief for capital expenditure

Capital allowances are a form of tax relief in the United Kingdom that allow a business to deduct the cost of certain capital expenditure — principally plant and machinery — from its taxable profits over time. Because depreciation charged in accounts is not generally allowable for tax purposes, capital allowances provide the statutory substitute, prescribed mainly by the Capital Allowances Act 2001.

Main categories

The principal allowances include:

  • Annual Investment Allowance (AIA) — 100% relief on qualifying expenditure up to a statutory annual limit (set at £1 million), available to most businesses.
  • Writing-down allowances — annual allowances on the reducing balance of pooled expenditure, at 18% for the main pool and 6% for the special rate pool (including integral features of buildings and long-life assets).
  • First-year allowances — enhanced 100% relief for specified environmentally beneficial plant, such as zero-emission vehicles and charging points.
  • Full expensing — introduced in 2023 for companies, allowing 100% first-year relief on main-rate plant and machinery and 50% on special-rate assets.
  • Structures and Buildings Allowance (SBA) — relief for construction costs of non-residential structures, given at a flat annual rate over 33⅓ years.

Pooling and disposals

Expenditure is generally allocated to pools. When an asset is disposed of, disposal proceeds are brought into the pool, and a balancing allowance or balancing charge may arise on cessation or where proceeds exceed the pool balance.
