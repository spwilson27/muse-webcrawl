Battle of Waterloo

The Battle of Waterloo was fought on Sunday 18 June 1815 near Waterloo in Belgium, then part of the United Kingdom of the Netherlands. A French army under Napoleon was defeated by two of the armies of the Seventh Coalition: an Anglo-allied army under the Duke of Wellington, and a Prussian army under Field Marshal von Blücher. The battle marked the end of the Napoleonic Wars and of Napoleon's Hundred Days return to power.

Background

Returning from Elba in March 1815, Napoleon regained power in France while the allies at the Congress of Vienna declared him an outlaw and mobilised. Napoleon struck first into Belgium, hoping to defeat the Anglo-allied and Prussian armies separately before they could combine. On 16 June he beat Blücher at Ligny while Ney fought Wellington to a draw at Quatre Bras. Wellington then fell back to a prepared ridge south of Waterloo — Mont-Saint-Jean — trusting Blücher's promise to march to his support.

The battle

Heavy rain delayed the French attack until about 11:30 a.m. The day's fighting turned on the allied strongpoints: the château of Hougoumont, which absorbed French attacks all day; the farmhouse of La Haye Sainte, which fell late in the afternoon; and the great French cavalry charges against unbroken allied infantry squares. Around 4:30 p.m. Prussian troops began arriving on Napoleon's right flank at Plancenoit. A final attack by the Imperial Guard in the early evening was repulsed, the French army broke, and the Prussians conducted a ruthless night pursuit.

Aftermath

Napoleon abdicated four days later; the allies entered Paris on 7 July, and Napoleon was exiled to Saint Helena. "Waterloo" entered the language as a byword for decisive defeat — to "meet one's Waterloo". The battlefield, with its Lion's Mound, remains one of Europe's most visited historic sites.
