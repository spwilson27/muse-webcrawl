Buru (formerly spelled Boeroe) is the third largest island within the Maluku Islands of Indonesia. It lies between the Banda Sea to the south and Seram Sea to the north, west of Ambon and Seram. The island belongs to Maluku province and includes the Buru and South Buru regencies. Its largest town and port is Namlea on the northeastern coast.

Geography

Buru is largely mountainous and covered in tropical rainforest. Mount Kapalatmada, the highest peak, rises to about 2,428 metres. The island is known for its endemic wildlife, including the Buru babirusa and several bird species found nowhere else.

History

The island was contested by the Portuguese, the Makassarese, and the Dutch East India Company in the 16th and 17th centuries because of its spice trade; the Dutch eventually built Fort Kayeli to control it. In the 1960s and 1970s, under Suharto's New Order, Buru was used as a prison island where thousands of political prisoners — alleged communists and dissidents, including the writer Pramoedya Ananta Toer — were exiled and forced to work without trial. Pramoedya composed his Buru Quartet while imprisoned there.

Economy

Agriculture, fishing, and forestry (including teak and eucalyptus plantations and distillation of eucalyptus oil) are the mainstays of the island's economy.
