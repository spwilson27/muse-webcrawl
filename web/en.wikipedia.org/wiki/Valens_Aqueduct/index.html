Valens Aqueduct


Location: Istanbul, Turkey
Completed: late 4th century AD

The Valens Aqueduct (Turkish: Bozdoğan Kemeri) is a Roman aqueduct that was the major water supply system of Constantinople (modern Istanbul). Completed around 373 AD under the emperor Valens, the aqueduct system ultimately stretched more than 250 kilometres from springs in Thrace, making it one of the longest water supply lines of the ancient world.

The surviving section

The best-known surviving stretch, two tiers of arches nearly a kilometre long and up to about 29 metres high, crosses the valley between the city's third and fourth hills, today straddling Atatürk Boulevard in the Fatih district. The structure supplied the Nymphaeum and the great cisterns of Constantinople, including the Basilica Cistern.

Later history

The aqueduct remained in use through the Byzantine and Ottoman periods, with repairs by several sultans, and parts of the system supplied Istanbul into the modern era. Earthquakes damaged the arches several times, most recently in the 20th century, but the monument was restored and remains an emblem of the city.
