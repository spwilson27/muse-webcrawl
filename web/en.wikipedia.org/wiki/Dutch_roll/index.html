Dutch roll

Dutch roll is a type of aircraft motion consisting of an out-of-phase combination of "tail-wagging" (yaw) and rocking from side to side (roll). This yaw-roll coupling is one of the basic flight dynamic modes (the others include phugoid, short period, and spiral divergence).

The motion is usually well damped in most light aircraft, though some aircraft with well-damped Dutch roll modes can experience a degradation in damping as airspeed decreases and altitude increases. Dutch roll stability can be artificially increased by the installation of a yaw damper. Wings placed well above the center of mass, swept wings, and dihedral wings tend to increase the roll restoring force, and therefore increase the Dutch roll tendency; this is why high-wing aircraft often have slight anhedral, and transport-category swept-wing aircraft are equipped with yaw dampers.

Cause

Dutch roll is triggered by any asymmetric input, such as a gust or an uncoordinated turn. The aircraft yaws, the outer wing speeds up and produces more lift, causing roll; the dihedral effect and vertical fin then act to restore the aircraft, but the yaw and roll responses are out of phase, producing a sustained oscillation that repeats until damped.

Name

The name is said to derive from the resemblance of the motion to the side-to-side movement of a Dutch ice skater, or alternatively from Dutch sailors moving unsteadily on deck.
