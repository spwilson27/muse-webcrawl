Turán's theorem

Turán's theorem is a result in extremal graph theory proved by Pál Turán in 1941. It determines the maximum number of edges in a graph on n vertices that contains no complete subgraph (clique) on r + 1 vertices.

Statement

Every graph on n vertices that does not contain a clique of size r + 1 has at most (1 − 1/r)·n²/2 edges, and this bound is achieved uniquely (up to isomorphism) by the Turán graph: the complete r-partite graph whose parts are as equal in size as possible.

Special cases and significance

For r = 2 the theorem reduces to Mantel's theorem (1907): a triangle-free graph on n vertices has at most ⌊n²/4⌋ edges. Turán's theorem launched the field of extremal graph theory, and many generalisations — including the Erdős–Stone theorem and stability results — build on it.
