Howl's Moving Castle (film)

Howl's Moving Castle (Japanese: ハウルの動く城, Hauru no Ugoku Shiro) is a 2004 Japanese animated fantasy film written and directed by Hayao Miyazaki and produced by Studio Ghibli. It is loosely based on the 1986 novel of the same name by British author Diana Wynne Jones. The film was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature and grossed over $235 million worldwide.

Plot

Sophie, a young milliner in a kingdom at war, is cursed by the Witch of the Waste and transformed into an elderly woman. Seeking to break the spell, she journeys into the hills and enters the walking castle of the wizard Howl, becoming its cleaning lady. Living alongside Howl, his young apprentice Markl, and the fire demon Calcifer — who powers the castle and is bound by a contract to Howl — Sophie becomes entangled in Howl's resistance to being drafted into the war, and gradually uncovers the secret of his heart, which Calcifer literally holds.

Production and themes

Miyazaki took over direction after the original director, Mamoru Hosoda, left the project. The film carries strong anti-war themes influenced by Miyazaki's opposition to the Iraq War, and explores old age portrayed positively — Sophie's transformation grants her freedom and confidence. The castle itself, a lumbering patchwork of turrets and legs, was realised through a combination of traditional animation and computer graphics.

Reception

The film premiered at the Venice Film Festival in 2004 and was a major commercial success in Japan and internationally. The English dub, produced by Walt Disney Pictures, featured the voices of Jean Simmons, Christian Bale, Lauren Bacall and Billy Crystal. It is frequently ranked among the greatest animated films of all time.
