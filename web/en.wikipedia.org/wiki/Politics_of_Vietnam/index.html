Politics of Vietnam

The politics of Vietnam are defined by a one-party socialist republic framework, in which the Communist Party of Vietnam (CPV) is the sole legal party and the leading force of state and society under the constitution. Vietnam's political system combines the party, the state presidency, the government headed by a prime minister, and the National Assembly, formally the highest organ of state power.

Communist Party

The CPV, founded in 1930 and led for decades by Ho Chi Minh, holds a National Congress every five years, which elects a Central Committee. The Central Committee elects the Politburo and the General Secretary, the country's most powerful figure. Key decisions are made through consensus within the leadership, traditionally balanced among the "four pillars" (tứ trụ): the General Secretary of the Party, the President, the Prime Minister, and the Chair of the National Assembly.

State institutions

The National Assembly, elected for five-year terms, passes laws and budgets and elects the President and Prime Minister — in practice ratifying candidates selected by the Party. The President is head of state and commander of the armed forces; the Prime Minister heads the government and the executive ministries. Vietnam is a unitary state divided into provinces and centrally governed municipalities.

History

Following reunification in 1976 after the Vietnam War, the country pursued centralized socialist planning. In 1986 the Sixth Party Congress launched Đổi Mới (renovation), introducing market-oriented reforms while retaining one-party rule, a model often compared to China's reform and opening up. Recent decades have seen an extensive anti-corruption drive (the "blazing furnace" campaign) and assertive leadership transitions, including Tô Lâm becoming General Secretary in 2024 following the death of Nguyễn Phú Trọng.
