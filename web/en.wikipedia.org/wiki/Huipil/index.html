Huipil


A richly brocaded huipil from Guatemala.

A huipil (Spanish pronunciation: [ˈwipil]; Nahuatl: huīpīlli) is the most common traditional garment worn by indigenous women from central Mexico to Central America. It is a loose-fitting tunic, generally made from two or three rectangular pieces of fabric, joined together with stitching, ribbons, or fabric strips, with an opening for the head and, if the sides are sewn, openings for the arms.

Variations

Traditional huipils are woven on a backstrap loom and are heavily decorated with designs woven into the fabric, embroidery, ribbons, lace, and more. The designs and colors vary from community to community and often identify the wearer's village, marital status, and personal beliefs. Ceremonial huipils may be worn only for special occasions, while everyday huipils are simpler.

History

The huipil has pre-Hispanic origins, with depictions dating back centuries in Mesoamerican art. It remains a strong symbol of indigenous identity in Mexico, Guatemala, and elsewhere in Central America.
