Eastern Air Lines was a major American airline that operated from 1926 to 1991. Headquartered in Miami, Florida, at Miami International Airport, it was one of the so-called "Big Four" domestic airlines that dominated American commercial aviation for much of the twentieth century, alongside American Airlines, United Airlines, and Trans World Airlines (TWA). At its peak, Eastern was the largest airline in the free world by passengers carried, serving the eastern United States, the Caribbean, and Latin America.

History

Early years and Rickenbacker era

The company traced its origins to Pitcairn Aviation, founded in 1926 by Harold Frederick Pitcairn. It was acquired by North American Aviation in 1929 and renamed Eastern Air Transport; the name Eastern Air Lines was adopted in 1934. Under the leadership of World War I flying ace Eddie Rickenbacker, who became general manager in 1935 and later chief executive, Eastern grew into a highly profitable carrier famous for its Florida routes and its "Great Silver Fleet." Rickenbacker ran the airline with military discipline for nearly three decades.

Postwar growth

After World War II, Eastern expanded aggressively along the U.S. East Coast, pioneering the air shuttle concept in 1961 with hourly no-reservation flights between New York, Boston, and Washington, D.C. The airline was an early operator of jet aircraft including the Douglas DC-8, Boeing 727, and the Lockheed L-1011 TriStar, and it was the launch customer for the Boeing 757. Astronaut Frank Borman became president in 1975 and worked to modernize the fleet and finances.

Decline and shutdown

Deregulation in 1978 intensified competition, and years of labor conflict, heavy debt, and fuel price shocks weakened the company. In 1986 Eastern was acquired by Frank Lorenzo's Texas Air Corporation, owner of Continental Airlines. Asset sales, including the lucrative Air Shuttle, failed to restore profitability. A bitter machinists' strike beginning in March 1989 crippled operations, and Eastern filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection. Unable to recover, Eastern Air Lines ceased all operations at midnight on January 18, 1991, one of the most prominent casualties of the deregulation era.

Fleet and legacy

Eastern operated a large fleet of Lockheed Electras, DC-9s, Boeing 727s and 757s, Airbus A300s, and L-1011 TriStars. Its final livery, introduced in the 1960s, paired a white fuselage with a blue "hockey stick" cheatline. The Eastern name was briefly revived in the 2010s by an unrelated charter operator, and later by Eastern Airlines, LLC, but neither had a corporate connection to the original carrier.
