Bayou Classic

SportCollege football
TeamsGrambling State Tigers vs. Southern Jaguars
First meeting1974 (series dates to 1932)
StadiumCaesars Superdome, New Orleans

The Bayou Classic is an annual college football game between the Grambling State Tigers of Grambling State University and the Southern Jaguars of Southern University, two historically Black universities in Louisiana. Played in New Orleans over Thanksgiving weekend, it is the most famous of the "classic" football games between HBCUs and regularly draws crowds among the largest in the Football Championship Subdivision.

The Bayou Classic at the Louisiana Superdome.

History

Grambling and Southern first played in 1932, but the rivalry was transformed in 1974 when the game was moved to New Orleans and relaunched as the Bayou Classic at Tulane Stadium. It moved to the Louisiana Superdome the following year. Under the promotion of Grambling coach Eddie Robinson and others, the event grew into a weekend-long celebration including a parade, a job fair, and concerts.

Battle of the Bands

The game's halftime showdown between Grambling's Tiger Marching Band and Southern's Human Jukebox — the "Battle of the Bands" — is itself a celebrated spectacle, often drawing as much attention as the football. The game has been nationally televised since the early 1990s, first by NBC.

Impact

The Bayou Classic is a major economic event for New Orleans and a showcase for Black college culture. The 2005 game was moved to Houston because of Hurricane Katrina, the only year it was played outside Louisiana.
