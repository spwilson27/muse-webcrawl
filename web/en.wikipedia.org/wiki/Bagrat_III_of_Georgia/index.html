Bagrat III of Georgia

Bagrat III (Georgian: ბაგრატ III; c. 960 – 7 May 1014) was the first king of a politically unified Kingdom of Georgia, reigning from 1008 until his death. A member of the Bagrationi dynasty, he united the crowns of Abkhazia and Kartli together with several other Georgian lands, laying the foundation of the medieval Georgian state.

Rise to power

Bagrat was the son of Gurgen, king of Kartli, and Gurandukht, daughter of George II of Abkhazia. Raised at the Abkhazian court, he was adopted by the childless David III of Tao and became king of Abkhazia in 978. On his father's death in 1008 he inherited Kartli as well, uniting the two crowns in one person for the first time.

Reign

Bagrat subdued the recalcitrant nobility, notably the Liparitid clan, annexed Kakheti and Hereti, and campaigned in Tao. He is remembered as a builder-king: he founded the great Bagrati Cathedral in Kutaisi, as well as the Nikortsminda and Bedia cathedrals, monuments of the Georgian golden age of church architecture.
