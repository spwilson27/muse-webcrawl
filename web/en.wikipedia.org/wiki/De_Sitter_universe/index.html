De Sitter universe

A de Sitter universe is a cosmological solution to the Einstein field equations of general relativity, named after Willem de Sitter. It models the universe as spatially flat, homogeneous and isotropic, containing no ordinary matter — only a positive cosmological constant (vacuum energy) — and expanding exponentially.

Background

De Sitter derived the solution in 1917, shortly after Einstein's own static model. Although unrealistic as a description of the early matter-filled universe, it is a good approximation in two eras: during cosmological inflation in the very early universe, and in the far future, when dark energy dominates and matter has been diluted away.

Properties

De Sitter space is the maximally symmetric Lorentzian manifold with constant positive curvature. Its expansion rate is constant, with Hubble parameter H determined by the cosmological constant, H² = Λ/3 (in units c = 1). An observer in de Sitter space is surrounded by a cosmological event horizon, which radiates at the Gibbons–Hawking temperature. The geometry is the Lorentzian analogue of a sphere, just as anti-de Sitter space corresponds to constant negative curvature.
