Languages of Middle-earth

The languages of Middle-earth are the fictional languages constructed by J. R. R. Tolkien for his legendarium, the body of work set in the fictional world of Middle-earth. Tolkien, a philologist by profession, regarded the invention of languages as the foundation of his stories: he famously remarked that the tales were made rather to provide a world for the languages than the reverse. The most developed of these languages are the Elvish tongues Quenya and Sindarin, for which Tolkien devised extensive vocabularies, grammars, and histories of sound change.

Elvish languages

The Elvish languages form the largest family in the legendarium. They descend from Primitive Quendian, the speech of the first Elves at Cuiviénen. After the Sundering of the Elves, the languages diverged into the Eldarin branch, which includes Quenya (the High-elven tongue of the Noldor and Vanyar) and the Telerin languages, among them Sindarin, the everyday speech of the Grey-elves of Beleriand and, by the Third Age, of most Elves remaining in Middle-earth. Quenya came to be used chiefly for lore, song, and ceremonial purposes, while Sindarin was the living vernacular. Other Elvish speeches include the Silvan dialects of Lothlórien and the Woodland Realm.

Other languages

  • Adûnaic — the language of the Men of Númenor, influenced by Elvish and by Khuzdul.
  • Westron (Common Speech) — the lingua franca of the western lands of Middle-earth in the Third Age, descended largely from Adûnaic; rendered as English in Tolkien's "translation" of the Red Book.
  • Khuzdul — the secret language of the Dwarves, said to have been devised by Aulë; rarely taught to outsiders.
  • Entish — the slow, sonorous, and extraordinarily complex speech of the Ents.
  • Black Speech — devised by Sauron in the Dark Years for the use of his servants; the Ring inscription is written in it.
  • Rohirric — the language of the Rohirrim, represented in the text by Old English.
  • Valarin — the language of the Valar, largely unintelligible and alien to Elvish ears.

Writing systems

Tolkien devised several scripts for his languages, most notably the Tengwar of Fëanor, used for Quenya and Sindarin, and the Cirth, the runic alphabet originally devised by the Sindar and later adopted and expanded by the Dwarves of Khazad-dûm. The Sarati of Rúmil are an earlier Elvish script mentioned in the lore.
