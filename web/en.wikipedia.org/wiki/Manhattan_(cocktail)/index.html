Manhattan

A Manhattan is a classic cocktail made with whiskey, sweet vermouth and bitters, stirred with ice, strained into a chilled cocktail glass and traditionally garnished with a maraschino cherry. Generally considered one of the great pre-Prohibition cocktails, it is listed among the IBA official cocktails in "The Unforgettables" category.

TypeCocktail
Base spiritRye whiskey (or bourbon)
Ingredients5 cl rye whiskey, 2 cl sweet red vermouth, dash Angostura bitters
PreparationStirred with ice, strained, served straight up
GarnishMaraschino cherry

History

The drink is conventionally dated to the 1870s in New York City. One famous but likely apocryphal story attributes it to a banquet at the Manhattan Club hosted by Jennie Jerome (Lady Randolph Churchill) in honour of presidential candidate Samuel J. Tilden, where a bartender named Iain Marshall supposedly created it. What is certain is that by the 1880s the Manhattan — originally often made with American rye whiskey — was a standard of bar manuals, and it survived Prohibition, when Canadian whisky was commonly substituted.

Variations

A Perfect Manhattan splits the vermouth between sweet and dry; a Dry Manhattan uses dry vermouth only; the Rob Roy substitutes Scotch whisky; the Brooklyn adds maraschino liqueur and Amer Picon or amaro. Modern versions variously use bourbon for a softer, sweeter profile, or add a bar spoon of cherry syrup. The classic ratio of two parts whiskey to one part vermouth is often adjusted toward 2½:1.
