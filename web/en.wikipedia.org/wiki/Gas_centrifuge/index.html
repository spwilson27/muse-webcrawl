Gas centrifuge

A gas centrifuge is a device that separates isotopes of a gas by spinning it at very high speed in a cylindrical rotor. The heavier molecules are thrown toward the wall of the rotor while the lighter ones concentrate near the axis, so that enriched and depleted streams can be drawn off separately. The method is most importantly used to enrich uranium: uranium hexafluoride (UF6) gas is processed so that the fissile isotope uranium-235 is increased relative to uranium-238.

Principle

Centrifuge rotors spin at peripheral speeds of several hundred metres per second, generating centrifugal accelerations hundreds of thousands of times gravity. A single machine achieves only a modest separation factor, so centrifuges are connected in cascades — series and parallel arrangements of hundreds or thousands of machines — to reach the desired assay. A countercurrent flow inside the rotor, driven by a scoop or thermal gradient, multiplies the elementary separation effect along the rotor's length.

History

Centrifugal isotope separation was proposed in 1919 by Lindemann and Aston and demonstrated in the 1930s by Jesse Beams at the University of Virginia. During the Second World War the method was investigated but not industrialised by the Manhattan Project. German scientists and engineers in the Soviet Union, notably Gernot Zippe, developed the practical short-bowl design after the war; the "Zippe-type" centrifuge became the basis of modern plants. The Urenco consortium industrialised the technology in Europe from the 1970s.

Use and proliferation

Gas centrifuges are far more energy-efficient than the older gaseous diffusion process and now dominate world enrichment capacity. Because the same technology can produce low-enriched reactor fuel or, by reconfiguring cascades, highly enriched weapon-grade uranium, centrifuge programmes in states such as Iran and North Korea have been central issues in nuclear non-proliferation. Notorious proliferation episodes include Abdul Qadeer Khan's theft of Urenco designs, which seeded the Pakistani programme and a black-market network.
