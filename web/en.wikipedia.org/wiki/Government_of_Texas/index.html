Government of Texas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Texas State Capitol in Austin, seat of the state government.

The government of Texas operates under the Constitution of Texas of 1876, which divides power among executive, legislative, and judicial branches and deliberately fragments executive authority among numerous independently elected officials. Texas is the second most populous state in the United States, and its state government administers a budget comparable to that of a medium-sized nation.

Executive branch

The governor of Texas is the chief executive, but unlike most states Texas has a "plural executive": the lieutenant governor, attorney general, comptroller of public accounts, commissioner of the General Land Office, and commissioner of agriculture are all elected separately and are not answerable to the governor. The lieutenant governor, who presides over the state Senate, is often described as the most powerful official in Texas government. The governor serves a four-year term with no term limit, wields a line-item veto over appropriations, and appoints members of state boards and commissions.

Legislative branch

The Texas Legislature is bicameral, consisting of a Senate of 31 members and a House of Representatives of 150 members. It meets in regular session for 140 days in odd-numbered years, reflecting the state's tradition of a part-time, citizen legislature. The governor may call special sessions limited to subjects of the governor's choosing.

Judicial branch

Texas has a bifurcated supreme court system: the Supreme Court of Texas handles civil matters while the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals is the final authority on criminal cases. Judges at all levels are chosen in partisan elections, a feature Texas shares with only a few states.

Local government

Texas has 254 counties, more than any other state, governed by commissioners courts. Cities may operate under home rule charters, and thousands of special districts provide services such as water, schools, and emergency response.
