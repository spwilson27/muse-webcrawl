Sydney is a former city and urban community on the east coast of Cape Breton Island in Nova Scotia, Canada, within the Cape Breton Regional Municipality. Founded in 1785 by the British, it served as the capital of the Colony of Cape Breton Island until 1820, when the colony merged with Nova Scotia and the capital moved to Halifax.

History

Sydney was founded by Colonel Joseph Frederick Wallet DesBarres and named in honour of Thomas Townshend, 1st Viscount Sydney, who was then the British Home Secretary. It was settled by Loyalists after the American Revolutionary War. Coal mining in the surrounding Sydney Coal Field made the area an industrial centre; a large steel plant, operated for much of the twentieth century by the Sydney Steel Corporation (Sysco), opened in 1901 and defined the city's economy. The plant's closure in 2001 and the subsequent cleanup of the Sydney Tar Ponds, one of Canada's most notorious contaminated sites, marked the city's transition to a service and tourism economy.

Today

Sydney is the largest urban centre on Cape Breton and a port of call for cruise ships. Its waterfront features the world's largest fiddle and bow, a tribute to the island's Celtic musical heritage. The city is served by JA Douglas McCurdy Sydney Airport and by the Marine Atlantic ferry terminal at nearby North Sydney, which connects to Newfoundland.
