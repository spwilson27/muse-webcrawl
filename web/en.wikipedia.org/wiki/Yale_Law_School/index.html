Yale Law School

Yale Law School (YLS) is the law school of Yale University, a private Ivy League research university in New Haven, Connecticut. Founded in 1824, it has consistently ranked among the top law schools in the United States.

Yale Law School
Established: 1824
Location: New Haven, Connecticut, US
Dean: Heather Gerken (since 2017)
Students: ~600
Parent: Yale University

History

The school grew out of a proprietary law school affiliated with Yale in the 1820s, rooted in the Litchfield Law School tradition. It awarded its first degrees in 1826 and became part of Yale University formally thereafter. Its Sterling Law Building, completed in 1931, is modelled on English Inns of Court.

Academics

Yale Law offers the JD, LL.M., JSD, and PhD in Law, plus joint degrees. It is known for small classes, a pass/fail first term, influential clinics, and an emphasis on legal scholarship and public service. The Yale Law Journal, founded in 1891, is one of the most cited law reviews.

Notable alumni

Alumni include US presidents Gerald Ford and Bill Clinton, and numerous Supreme Court justices including Clarence Thomas, Samuel Alito, Sonia Sotomayor, and Brett Kavanaugh.
