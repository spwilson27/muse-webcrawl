Empress Shōken

Empress Shōken (昭憲皇后, Shōken-kōgō, 9 May 1849 – 9 April 1914) was the wife and empress consort of Emperor Meiji of Japan. Born Ichijō Masako (一条勝子), she was the daughter of Ichijō Tadaka, a court noble, and was adopted into the Imperial Household as the first empress consort to receive public education and to play a visible public role in the modernizing Meiji state.

Role as empress

Masako married the Emperor in 1869 and was proclaimed empress (kōgō). Although she bore no children, she was formally the adoptive mother of Emperor Taishō. Breaking with court tradition, she appeared in public, promoted girls' education, and supported charitable and welfare work, notably the Japanese Red Cross Society, and championed the sericulture and silk industries as models of women's work. She was known for composing waka poetry and for supporting the translation and publication of educational materials.

Death and remembrance

Empress Shōken survived her husband by less than two years, dying at the imperial villa in Numazu in 1914. She was posthumously given the name Shōken and is enshrined with Emperor Meiji at the Meiji Shrine in Tokyo and at their mausolea at Fushimi Momoyama in Kyoto. The Empress Shōken Fund, established with her donation to the International Red Cross in 1912, still makes grants to national Red Cross and Red Crescent societies.
