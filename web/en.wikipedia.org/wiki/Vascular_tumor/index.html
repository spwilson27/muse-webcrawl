Vascular tumor

SpecialtyPathology, dermatology, oncology
ExamplesHaemangioma, haemangioendothelioma, angiosarcoma

A vascular tumor is a growth of cells that forms from blood vessels or lymphatic vessels. Vascular tumors range from benign lesions to aggressive malignant cancers; under the ISSVA classification they are distinguished from vascular malformations, which are structural errors of vessel development rather than true proliferative tumors.

Types

Benign vascular tumors include infantile haemangioma — the common "strawberry mark" of infancy, which proliferates then involutes — congenital haemangiomas, cherry angiomas, and tufted angiomas. Borderline or locally aggressive tumors include kaposiform haemangioendothelioma and retiform haemangioendothelioma. Malignant vascular tumors include angiosarcoma, a cancer of vessel-lining endothelium that can arise in skin, breast, liver, and soft tissue and carries a poor prognosis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is based on appearance, imaging (ultrasound, MRI), and histopathology with endothelial markers such as CD31 and ERG. Treatment depends on the type: many infantile haemangiomas need no treatment or respond to propranolol, while other tumors may require surgical excision, laser therapy, embolisation, or, for angiosarcoma, wide surgery combined with radiotherapy and chemotherapy.
