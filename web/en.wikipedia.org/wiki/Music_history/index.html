History of music

The history of music is the study of music across human cultures and time periods. Music is a universal human phenomenon: evidence of musical instruments, such as bone flutes over 40,000 years old, suggests that music-making predates recorded history. The academic discipline concerned with the history of music is musicology.

Prehistoric music

The earliest known musical instruments include flutes made from bird bones and mammoth ivory found in caves in Germany, dated to around 42,000–43,000 years ago. Music likely developed from rhythmic patterns and vocalizations used in ritual, communication, and communal activity.

Ancient music

In ancient Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, China, and India, music was central to religious and civic life. The Greeks developed theories of harmony and notation; the oldest surviving complete musical composition is the Seikilos epitaph from the 1st or 2nd century CE. Ancient Chinese music was based on the pentatonic scale and closely tied to court ritual.

Medieval music

In medieval Europe, plainchant (Gregorian chant) dominated sacred music. Polyphony emerged with organum in the 9th–12th centuries, developed by composers of the Notre-Dame school such as Léonin and Pérotin. Secular music flourished through troubadours and trouvères. Musical notation became increasingly precise, enabling complex works.

Renaissance music

During the Renaissance (c. 1400–1600), composers such as Josquin des Prez, Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina, and William Byrd created rich polyphonic sacred and secular works. The invention of music printing by Ottaviano Petrucci in 1501 spread music widely.

Baroque music

The Baroque era (c. 1600–1750) saw the birth of opera, the concerto, and tonal harmony. Composers included Claudio Monteverdi, Antonio Vivaldi, George Frideric Handel, and Johann Sebastian Bach.

Classical period

The Classical period (c. 1750–1820) emphasized clarity, balance, and form, with the symphony, string quartet, and sonata as major genres. Joseph Haydn, Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, and Ludwig van Beethoven were its leading figures.

Romantic music

Romantic music (c. 1800–1910) stressed emotion and individuality, expanding orchestras and harmonic language. Composers included Franz Schubert, Frédéric Chopin, Richard Wagner, Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky, and Gustav Mahler.

20th and 21st centuries

The 20th century brought atonality, jazz, electronic music, recording technology, and an explosion of popular genres including blues, rock, hip-hop, and electronic dance music. Composers such as Igor Stravinsky, Arnold Schoenberg, and later minimalists like Philip Glass reshaped art music, while recorded media transformed how music is created and consumed worldwide.
