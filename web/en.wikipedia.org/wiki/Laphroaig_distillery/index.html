Laphroaig distillery

Laphroaig distillery
Location: Port Ellen, Islay, Argyll and Bute, Scotland
Founded: 1815
Owner: Beam Suntory (Suntory Global Spirits)
Type: Single malt Scotch whisky

Laphroaig distillery (pronounced lə-FROYG) is an Islay single malt Scotch whisky distillery on the south coast of the island, near Port Ellen. Its name derives from the Gaelic Lag a' Mhòir, "the hollow of the broad bay". Laphroaig is renowned for producing one of the most strongly flavoured of all Scotch whiskies, intensely peaty, smoky and medicinal in character—an acquired taste it famously markets with the slogan that you will "love it or hate it".

History

The distillery was formally established in 1815 by brothers Donald and Alexander Johnston, whose family had farmed the land and probably distilled there earlier in the century. The Johnston family ran Laphroaig for well over a century; the last family member in charge was Ian Hunter, who expanded the distillery and its export markets, and who on his death in 1954 left the business to his secretary and manager, Bessie Williamson—one of the first women to own and run a Scotch whisky distillery. The company passed through several owners before becoming part of Beam Suntory (now Suntory Global Spirits).

During American Prohibition, Laphroaig was reportedly sold in the United States for "medicinal purposes", its iodine-like flavour helping to convince officials of its therapeutic character.

Production

Laphroaig malts part of its own barley on traditional malting floors and dries it over peat fires, imparting the distinctive smoky, seaweedy and medicinal notes; its water comes from the Kilbride Dam reservoir. The whisky is distilled in seven stills and matured mainly in ex-bourbon casks in warehouses exposed to the Atlantic weather. Standard expressions include the 10 Year Old and Quarter Cask, and the distillery is unusual in holding a Royal Warrant, granted in 1994 by the then Prince of Wales, whose "Friends of Laphroaig" programme grants loyal drinkers a lifetime lease of a square foot of Islay land by the distillery.
