Discography

A discography is the study and cataloging of published sound recordings, often of a particular performer or in a particular musical genre. A discography may also be the resulting catalogue itself — the organised list of an artist's recordings, typically giving titles, release dates, label and catalogue numbers, formats, personnel, and chart performance.

History

Discography developed in the 1930s among jazz collectors, who compiled session-by-session records of hot jazz recordings; works such as Charles Delaunay's Hot Discography (1936) were foundational. Brian Rust's jazz and blues discographies set standards for identifying recording sessions, matrix numbers, and personnel. Classical discographies catalogued competing recordings of standard repertoire, and the rise of rock and pop fandom produced comprehensive artist discographies in books, magazines, and later databases.

Contents of a discography

Artist discographies are conventionally divided into studio albums, live albums, compilation albums, singles, extended plays, and other appearances. Entries may include release dates, record labels, catalogue numbers, formats (LP, CD, download, streaming), track listings, certifications and sales figures, and peak chart positions in various countries. Session discographies for jazz and studio musicians additionally record recording dates, locations, producers, engineers, and take or matrix numbers.

Online discographies

The internet transformed discographic work: collaborative databases such as Discogs and MusicBrainz hold tens of millions of release entries built from user submissions and label data, while AllMusic and Rate Your Music combine discographies with reviews and ratings. Streaming services provide partial discographies within their catalogues, though licensing gaps can omit recordings.
