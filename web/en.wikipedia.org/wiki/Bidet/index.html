Bidet

A bidet is a bowl or receptacle designed to be sat on for washing the human genitalia, perineum, inner buttocks, and anus. The modern variety has a plumbed-in water supply and drainage, and is a type of plumbing fixture in many European, South American, Middle Eastern, and East Asian bathrooms. Variants include standalone bidets, toilet seats with integrated spray nozzles (as in the Japanese washlet), handheld bidet showers, and add-on attachments.

History

The bidet appears to have been invented in France in the late 17th or early 18th century; the word means "pony" in French, referring to the straddling posture. Early bidets were furniture-like basins on stands. Napoleon Bonaparte famously travelled with a silver bidet. Bidets became standard fixtures in much of Europe during the 20th century; in Italy and Portugal they became legally required in bathrooms. In Japan, high-tech bidet toilets (washlets), introduced by Toto in 1980, achieved mass adoption.

Usage and environmental aspects

Bidets are promoted by advocates as more hygienic and environmentally friendly than toilet paper alone, since they reduce paper consumption and the water used in paper manufacture; manufacturers and health writers also note benefits for people with limited mobility or certain medical conditions. Adoption remains low in the United States and some other countries, though sales of bidet attachments surged during the 2020 toilet paper shortages.
