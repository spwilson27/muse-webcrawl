Mohawk Nation

The Mohawk Nation (Mohawk: Kahniá:ken) is an Indigenous people of North America, one of the five — later six — founding nations of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy). Their traditional homeland is the Mohawk Valley in present-day upstate New York, where they were the easternmost nation of the confederacy and hence its "Keepers of the Eastern Door".

History

The Mohawk were a powerful agricultural and trading people, organized in three clans — Bear, Wolf and Turtle — and governed by the Great Law of Peace. In the seventeenth century they were central to the fur trade and the Beaver Wars, allied with the Dutch and then the English. Figures such as the chief Hendrick Theyanoguin and the leader Joseph Brant (Thayendanegea) shaped their history. After the American Revolution, in which most Mohawk sided with the British, many resettled in Canada at Tyendinaga and the Grand River, while others remained or re-established communities in New York.

Communities and culture

Today Mohawk communities include Akwesasne (St. Regis), Kahnawake, Kanesatake, Tyendinaga, Wahta, and the Six Nations of the Grand River. Kahnawake's ironworkers famously helped build North America's skyscrapers and bridges. Language revitalization programmes, the 1990 Oka Crisis in Quebec, and assertions of sovereignty and gaming rights have kept Mohawk affairs prominent in Canadian and American public life. The mohawk hairstyle takes its name from the nation, though it simplifies a more varied historical reality.
