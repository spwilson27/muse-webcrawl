Diyarbakır Fortress

The Diyarbakır Fortress is a historic fortification in the Sur district of Diyarbakır, Turkey, consisting of an inner fortress and massive outer city walls built largely of black basalt. Together with the adjacent Hevsel Gardens, it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2015.

History

The site, ancient Amida, was fortified from the Roman period; the Romans under Constantius II strengthened the walls in the 4th century CE. The walls were rebuilt and extended under Byzantine and later Islamic rulers, including the Marwanids and Artuqids, whose inscriptions and reliefs adorn many towers. At various times the city was contested by Persians, Arabs, Seljuks, and Ottomans.

Description

The outer walls run for nearly six kilometres and include some 82 towers and four principal gates — the Harput, Yenikapı, Mardin, and Urfa gates — facing the cardinal directions. The inner fortress (İçkale) occupies a bluff above the Tigris River.

Damage

Parts of the historic Sur district were heavily damaged during fighting in 2015–2016, prompting international concern and subsequent reconstruction work within the World Heritage area.
