Ventrilo

Ventrilo is a proprietary voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) application, developed by Flagship Industries, that allows users to talk over the Internet, primarily used by online gamers for voice communication within multiplayer games. It operates on a client–server architecture: users connect with the Ventrilo client to a server, which may be hosted commercially or privately.

Features

Ventrilo supports voice communication in hierarchical "channels", private and global chat, text-to-speech notifications, per-user volume adjustment, sound effects, and overlays displaying who is speaking while in a game. It uses codecs such as GSM and Speex to balance sound quality and bandwidth, and was designed to have a small CPU footprint so as not to interfere with gameplay.

History and use

First released in 2002, Ventrilo became one of the most popular voice chat programs for PC gaming through the 2000s, particularly among guilds in massively multiplayer online games such as World of Warcraft, where users nicknamed it "Vent". It competed with TeamSpeak and later with Discord, which surpassed it in popularity in the later 2010s. Versions were released for Windows and macOS.
