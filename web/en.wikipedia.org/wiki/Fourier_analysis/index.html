Fourier analysis

In mathematics, Fourier analysis is the study of the way general functions may be represented or approximated by sums of simpler trigonometric functions. Fourier analysis grew from the study of Fourier series, and is named after Joseph Fourier, who showed that representing a function as a sum of trigonometric functions greatly simplifies the study of heat transfer.

Overview

The subject of Fourier analysis encompasses a vast spectrum of mathematics. In engineering, decomposing a function into oscillatory components is often called Fourier analysis, while rebuilding the function from those components is known as Fourier synthesis. The Fourier transform converts a function of time (or space) into a representation in terms of frequencies, and its inverse recovers the original function. For discrete, sampled data, the discrete Fourier transform (DFT) is used, computed efficiently by the fast Fourier transform (FFT) algorithm.

Applications

Fourier methods are used in signal processing (audio compression such as MP3, image compression such as JPEG), solving partial differential equations, spectroscopy, crystallography, quantum mechanics, and the analysis of tides, vibrations and electrical circuits.
