Iron Dome (Hebrew: כִּפַּת בַּרְזֶל, romanized: Kippat Barzel) is an Israeli mobile all-weather air defense system developed by Rafael Advanced Defense Systems and Israel Aerospace Industries. The system is designed to intercept and destroy short-range rockets and artillery shells fired from distances of 4 to 70 kilometres away and whose trajectory would take them to an Israeli populated area. Israel hopes to increase the range of Iron Dome's interceptions, from the current maximum of 70 km to up to 250 km, and make it able to intercept rockets coming from two directions simultaneously.

Iron Dome was declared operational and initially deployed on 27 March 2011 near Beersheba. On 7 April 2011, the system successfully intercepted a BM-21 Grad rocket launched from Gaza for the first time.

Background

During the 2006 Lebanon War, Hezbollah fired roughly 4,000 rockets into northern Israel, and throughout the 2000s Hamas and other groups fired Qassam and Grad rockets from Gaza at southern Israeli towns such as Sderot and Ashkelon. In February 2007, Defense Minister Amir Peretz selected Rafael's proposal as Israel's short-range rocket defense, over alternatives including the U.S. Nautilus laser system.

System description

An Iron Dome battery consists of three main elements:

  • Detection and tracking radar — the EL/M-2084 multimission radar built by IAI's Elta division.
  • Battle management and weapon control (BMC) — computes the incoming projectile's trajectory and impact point, and assigns interceptors only to rockets predicted to strike defended areas, ignoring those headed for open ground.
  • Missile firing unit — launchers each carrying 20 Tamir interceptors, equipped with electro-optic sensors and steering fins, which detonate a proximity-fuzed warhead near the target.

Combat performance

Iron Dome has been used extensively since 2011, including during Operation Pillar of Defense (2012), Operation Protective Edge (2014), the May 2021 Gaza conflict — when some 4,300 rockets were fired at Israel — and the October 2023 war, which saw mass salvos numbering thousands of rockets. Israeli officials have claimed interception success rates of 85–90% against rockets engaged; analysts debate the true figure, but the system is widely credited with sharply reducing casualties and damage in Israeli cities. Its limitations include saturation by very large salvos and the cost asymmetry between the ~$50,000 Tamir interceptor and cheap unguided rockets.

Funding and exports

The United States has provided over $1.6 billion in funding for Iron Dome production and co-production since 2011, and the U.S. Army purchased two batteries for interim cruise-missile defense. Variants include the naval C-Dome, declared operational in 2017. Iron Dome forms the lowest layer of Israel's multi-tier missile defense, beneath David's Sling and the Arrow systems, and alongside the laser-based Iron Beam under development.
