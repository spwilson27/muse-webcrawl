Right of abode (United Kingdom)

The right of abode is a statutory immigration status in the United Kingdom. A person with the right of abode is free to live in, and to come and go into and from, the United Kingdom without let or hindrance, subject only to the requirement to present proof of the status and to certain exclusion powers on conducive grounds. It is the most secure immigration status under British nationality law and is held automatically by all British citizens.

History

The right of abode was created by the Immigration Act 1971, which introduced the concept of "patriality". As originally enacted, it also belonged to certain Commonwealth citizens with a parent born in the UK and to women married to men with the right of abode. The British Nationality Act 1981, in force from 1983, restricted the right to British citizens, phasing out most of the older categories, though some Commonwealth citizens who held it before 1983 retained it.

Proof and certificates

The usual proof is a British citizen passport. Others who hold the right may apply for a certificate of entitlement to the right of abode, which is placed in their passport. The status cannot be lost through absence, unlike indefinite leave to remain.
