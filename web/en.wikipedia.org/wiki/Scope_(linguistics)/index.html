Scope (linguistics)

For other uses, see Scope (disambiguation).

In semantics and formal linguistics, the scope of an operator is the semantic range over which it applies. Quantifiers, negation, modals, and adverbs interact in scope: the sentence "Every student read a book" is ambiguous between a reading where one book was read by all (the existential quantifier has narrow scope) and one where each student read some possibly different book (wide scope for the universal, or surface scope).

Scope ambiguity

Sentences like "Every man loves a woman" or "I didn't leave because I was angry" (negation scoping over or under the because-clause) demonstrate scope ambiguity. Generative grammar often models such readings with logical forms in which quantifiers "raise" (Quantifier Raising) to positions from which they take scope.

Related phenomena

Scope freezing, inverse scope, scope islands, and interactions with binding and intensional contexts are central topics in semantic theory, with variation across languages in how readily inverse-scope readings are available.
