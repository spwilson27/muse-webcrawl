Akihabara

Akihabara (秋葉原)
District in Chiyoda and Taitō, Tokyo, Japan
Known for: Electronics retail, otaku culture, anime, manga, video games, maid cafés

Akihabara (Japanese: 秋葉原) is a district in central Tokyo, roughly spanning parts of Chiyoda and Taitō wards around Akihabara Station. It is famous as a shopping district for electronics and as a centre of Japanese otaku culture, with many shops devoted to anime, manga, video games and collectibles. Its nickname is Akiba; the electronics area is also called Akihabara Electric Town.

History

The name derives from Akiba, a fire-controlling deity, and a shrine built after a fire in 1869. After World War II the area became a black market for radio parts, then a household-appliance and electronics market during Japan's postwar growth. Small shops under the railway tracks sold components to students and engineers.

Electronics to otaku culture

From the 1980s and 1990s, as household electronics moved to large suburban retailers, Akihabara shops shifted toward personal computers, then video games, anime and hobby goods. Landmarks include Radio Kaikan, Sofmap, LaOX, Yodobashi-Akiba, arcades such as GiGO, and shops for figures, trading cards and retro games. Maid cafés and idol venues became prominent in the 2000s.

Geography and transport

The district is served by Akihabara Station on the JR Yamanote, Keihin–Tōhoku and Chūō–Sōbu lines, the Tokyo Metro Hibiya line and the Tsukuba Express. Chūō-dōri, the main street, is closed to vehicles on Sunday afternoons, becoming a pedestrian paradise.

Incidents

The Akihabara massacre of 8 June 2008, in which a man drove a truck into a crowd and stabbed pedestrians, killed seven people and injured ten; Sunday road closures were suspended for several years afterwards.
