Luxeuil Abbey

Luxeuil Abbey
Location: Luxeuil-les-Bains, Haute-Saône, France
Founded: c. 590
Founder: Saint Columbanus
Order: Columbanian, later Benedictine

Luxeuil Abbey was one of the most important monasteries of early medieval Francia, founded around 590 by the Irish missionary Saint Columbanus at Luxeuil-les-Bains in present-day eastern France, on the site of a ruined Roman spa town.

History

Columbanus and his companions established the community under a strict rule combining prayer, manual labour, and study. Luxeuil quickly became a mother house of Columbanian monasticism, sending monks to found or reform houses across the Frankish kingdoms and supplying many bishops and missionaries. After Columbanus's expulsion in 610, his successor Eustace restored the house's fortunes, and the mission of Saints Amandus and others spread from its orbit.

Scriptorium

Luxeuil was famous for its scriptorium, which gave its name to the Luxeuil minuscule, an early medieval book hand, and produced works such as the Luxeuil Lectionary. The abbey adopted the Benedictine Rule in the eighth century, was repeatedly damaged by invasions and wars, and was dissolved during the French Revolution.

Surviving buildings

The Gothic abbey church of Saint-Pierre, the cloister, and several conventual buildings survive; the church still functions as the parish church of Luxeuil-les-Bains.
