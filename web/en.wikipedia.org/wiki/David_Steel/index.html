David Martin Scott Steel, Baron Steel of Aikwood (born 31 March 1938) is a British politician. He was leader of the Liberal Party from 1976 to 1988 and a Member of Parliament for constituencies in the Scottish Borders for 32 years. He later served as the first Presiding Officer of the modern Scottish Parliament.

Early life

Steel was born in Kirkcaldy, the son of a Church of Scotland minister also named David Steel. He was educated in Nairobi, Kenya, where his father was a missionary, at Dumbarton Academy and at George Watson's College in Edinburgh, then read law at the University of Edinburgh, where he was president of the Liberal Club and of the Students' Representative Council. He worked in journalism and for the Scottish Liberal Party before his election to Parliament.

Parliamentary career

Steel won the Roxburgh, Selkirk and Peebles by-election of March 1965 at the age of 26, becoming the youngest MP in the Commons — the "baby of the House". Two years later he piloted through Parliament, as a private member's bill, the Abortion Act 1967, which legalised abortion in Britain under defined conditions and remains his most durable legislative achievement. He became Liberal Chief Whip in 1970.

Liberal leader

After Jeremy Thorpe's resignation amid scandal, Steel was elected Liberal leader in 1976. Facing a minority Labour government in 1977, he negotiated the Lib–Lab Pact, sustaining James Callaghan in office for over a year in exchange for consultation on policy. In the 1983 general election he led the SDP–Liberal Alliance alongside Social Democratic leader David Owen; the Alliance won 25.4% of the vote but only 23 seats, and Steel's televised exhortation to "go back to your constituencies and prepare for government" became a byword for thwarted ambition. He championed the merger of the two parties and stepped down as leader in 1988, becoming the merged Liberal Democrats' first (joint interim) leader with Robert Maclennan.

Later career

Steel retired from Westminster in 1997 and was created a life peer as Baron Steel of Aikwood. In 1999 he was elected to the new Scottish Parliament and chosen as its first Presiding Officer, presiding until 2003. He has also served as president of the Liberal International and on European and Commonwealth bodies.
