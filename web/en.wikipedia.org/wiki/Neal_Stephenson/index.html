Neal Town Stephenson (born October 31, 1959) is an American writer known for his works of speculative fiction and historical fiction. His novels often explore mathematics, cryptography, linguistics, philosophy, currency, and the history of science.

Career

Stephenson's early novels include The Big U (1984) and Zodiac (1988). He rose to prominence with Snow Crash (1992), which popularized the term "metaverse" and the use of "avatar" for online personas, and The Diamond Age (1995), which won the Hugo Award. Cryptonomicon (1999) interweaves Second World War codebreaking with a contemporary data-haven plot. The Baroque Cycle (2003–2004) — Quicksilver, The Confusion, and The System of the World — is a vast historical epic of the 17th and early 18th centuries. Later novels include Anathem (2008), Reamde (2011), Seveneves (2015), The Rise and Fall of D.O.D.O. (2017, with Nicole Galland), Fall; or, Dodge in Hell (2019), and Termination Shock (2021).

Other work

Stephenson has worked in technology ventures, including as a founder of Subutai Corporation, an advisor to Blue Origin, and chief futurist at Magic Leap. His non-fiction includes the essay "In the Beginning... Was the Command Line" (1999).
