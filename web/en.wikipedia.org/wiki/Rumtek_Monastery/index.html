Rumtek Monastery, also called the Dharma Chakra Centre, is a Tibetan Buddhist monastery located at Rumtek, about 23 kilometres from Gangtok, the capital of the Indian state of Sikkim. It is the largest monastery in Sikkim and the seat in exile of the Gyalwang Karmapa, head of the Karma Kagyu school of Tibetan Buddhism.

History

The site was originally established in the mid-18th century under the direction of the 12th Karmapa, Changchub Dorje, when Sikkim's ruler invited the Kagyu lineage to build monasteries in the kingdom. After fleeing Tibet in 1959 following the Tibetan uprising against Chinese rule, Rangjung Rigpe Dorje, the 16th Karmapa, chose Rumtek as his seat in exile. With support from the Sikkimese royal family and the Indian government, a new monastery was built on the site in traditional Tibetan style and inaugurated in 1966.

The complex houses the Karma Shri Nalanda Institute for advanced Buddhist studies, a golden stupa containing the relics of the 16th Karmapa, and a rich collection of rare scriptures, thangkas, and ritual objects brought from Tsurphu monastery in Tibet. Annual masked dances (cham) are held before the Tibetan New Year (Losar).

Karmapa controversy

Since the death of the 16th Karmapa in 1981, Rumtek has been at the centre of the Karmapa controversy, a dispute over the identity of his reincarnation in which rival candidates have been recognised by different senior lamas. The monastery itself has been administered under court supervision while the rival parties contest the succession and control of the Karmapa's charitable trust.
