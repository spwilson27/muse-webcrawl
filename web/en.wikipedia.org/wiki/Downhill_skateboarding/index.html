Downhill skateboarding

Downhill skateboarding is a discipline of longboarding in which riders descend paved hills and mountain roads at high speed, racing head-to-head or against the clock. Riders commonly reach speeds of 50–80 km/h, and elite racers have exceeded 130 km/h; the standing world speed record on a skateboard is over 143 km/h. The sport emphasizes aerodynamics, controlled sliding and protective equipment.

Technique

Riders adopt a low, tucked stance to reduce drag. Speed is controlled by foot braking, air braking (standing upright to catch the wind), and sliding — most importantly the Coleman slide and other "pre-drifts" performed before corners to shed speed and control the line. Races are usually held in heats of four riders on closed roads.

Equipment

Modern downhill setups use stiff decks, precision reverse-kingpin trucks and large soft wheels with square lips for grip. Riders wear full-face helmets, leather suits and slide gloves with plastic pucks that allow hands-down sliding. Safety gear is mandatory in sanctioned competition.

Competition

Organized racing grew out of outlaw runs in California in the 1970s and developed into world circuits, including the International Downhill Federation's World Cup and later the World Downhill Skateboarding Championship. Famous races include the Maryhill Festival of Speed in Washington state and Newton's Playground at Mount Panorama, Australia.
