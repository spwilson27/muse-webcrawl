Malayan Campaign

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Malayan Campaign, also known as the Battle of Malaya, was a military campaign fought by Allied and Axis forces in British Malaya from 8 December 1941 to 15 February 1942 during the early stages of the Pacific War of the Second World War. The Japanese 25th Army under General Tomoyuki Yamashita invaded from Indochina and Siam, rapidly overran the peninsula against British, Indian, Australian and Malayan forces under Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival, and culminated in the fall of Singapore.

Course of the campaign

Japanese forces landed at Kota Bharu and in southern Thailand hours before the attack on Pearl Harbor, and quickly gained air superiority, destroying much of the RAF's strength on the ground. On 10 December the British capital ships Prince of Wales and Repulse were sunk by Japanese land-based aircraft, ending Allied naval power in the region. Yamashita's troops advanced rapidly down both coasts of the peninsula, repeatedly outflanking prepared positions, using light tanks, bicycles and infiltration through jungle the defenders had considered impassable. Engagements included the Battle of Jitra, the fight at Kampar, and the disastrous ambush at Gemas and the Battle of Muar during the retreat to Singapore.

Outcome

By 31 January 1942 the Allies had withdrawn across the Johore Strait to Singapore Island. After a week-long battle, Percival surrendered on 15 February 1942; some 80,000 Allied troops became prisoners of war in what Winston Churchill called the worst disaster in British military history. The campaign exposed failures in Allied planning, training, air power and assumptions about jungle warfare, and destroyed the myth of Singapore as an impregnable fortress.
