Elton John

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Elton John at the piano in the 1970s in a glittering stage costume and oversized glasses, mid-performance]

Sir Elton Hercules John (born Reginald Kenneth Dwight; 25 March 1947) is an English singer, songwriter and pianist, one of the best-selling music artists of all time. With lyricist Bernie Taupin he wrote a catalogue that soundtracked the 1970s and never left the charts thereafter: 'Your Song' (1970), 'Rocket Man' (1972), 'Goodbye Yellow Brick Road' (1973), 'Don't Let the Sun Go Down on Me' (1974), and 'Candle in the Wind 1997', reworked for the funeral of Diana, Princess of Wales, and the best-selling single of the chart era.

John's flamboyant stage costumes and spectacles, and later his AIDS philanthropy (the Elton John AIDS Foundation, 1992), knighthood (1998) and farewell tour (2018–23, the highest-grossing tour in history at its close), frame a career of more than 300 million records sold.

Taupin and the seventies

A Royal Academy of Music scholar from Pinner who played pubs as 'Bluesology's' pianist, Dwight answered a Liberty Records advert in 1967 and was paired, by the label's files, with Taupin's lyrics — a partnership conducted largely by post and never in the same room at the writing stage. The Troubadour show in Los Angeles in August 1970 ignited America; seven consecutive US No. 1 albums followed, a run unmatched at the time.

Later life

Addiction and recovery (sober from 1990), the Diana funeral performance watched by a global billions, the Lion King songs (an Academy Award, 1994), a civil partnership and then marriage to David Furnish, two sons, and the biopic Rocketman (2019) made him a national institution. His final tour, Farewell Yellow Brick Road, closed in Stockholm in July 2023; an EGOT was completed with the 2024 Emmy for his live-streamed Dodger Stadium concert film.
