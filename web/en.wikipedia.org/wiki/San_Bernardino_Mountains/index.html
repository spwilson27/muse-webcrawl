The San Bernardino Mountains are a high mountain range in Southern California, part of the Transverse Ranges, located in San Bernardino County north and east of the city of San Bernardino. Their highest point, San Gorgonio Mountain at about 11,503 feet (3,506 m), is the tallest peak in Southern California. Most of the range lies within the San Bernardino National Forest.

Geography

The range runs roughly east–west for about 60 miles, bounded by Cajon Pass and the San Gabriel Mountains to the northwest, the Mojave Desert to the north, and San Gorgonio Pass — with the San Jacinto Mountains beyond — to the southeast. Mountain resort communities include Lake Arrowhead, Big Bear Lake, Crestline and Running Springs. Big Bear Lake, a large reservoir in the range's northeastern section, is a major recreation area with ski resorts (Snow Summit and Bear Mountain); Lake Arrowhead is a private reservoir community on the range's western side.

Geology and ecology

The mountains were uplifted along faults related to the San Andreas system, which runs along the range's southern and northern bases. Lower slopes carry chaparral, giving way to oak woodland and then coniferous forest of pine, fir and cedar; alpine conditions occur near the summit of San Gorgonio, within the San Gorgonio Wilderness. Wildlife includes black bears (for which Big Bear is named, after grizzlies once present), mountain lions, bighorn sheep and bald eagles at Big Bear Lake.

Human use

The range is a critical watershed for the Inland Empire, and its resorts serve millions of visitors from metropolitan Los Angeles. It is periodically affected by major wildfires, and its proximity to the San Andreas Fault makes it a focus of earthquake monitoring.
