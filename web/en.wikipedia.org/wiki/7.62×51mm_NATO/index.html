The 7.62×51mm NATO is a rimless, bottlenecked rifle cartridge developed in the 1950s as the standard service cartridge for NATO forces. It was introduced in the M14 rifle and M60 machine gun and remains in widespread use in designated marksman rifles, sniper rifles, and general-purpose machine guns.

Development

The cartridge grew out of the American T65 experimental series, itself derived from the .300 Savage case and designed to replicate the ballistics of the .30-06 Springfield in a shorter case suitable for automatic weapons. NATO adopted it in 1954; the United States adopted it in 1957. Its commercial twin is the .308 Winchester, released by Winchester in 1952.

Use

Although largely replaced in the assault-rifle role by the smaller 5.56×45mm NATO from the 1960s onward, the 7.62×51mm continues to serve in weapons such as the M240 machine gun, the FN SCAR-H, the M110 semi-automatic sniper system, and numerous bolt-action sniper rifles, valued for its range and barrier penetration.

Variants

Standard loadings include ball (M80), tracer (M62), armour-piercing (M61), match (M118 and M118LR) and frangible training ammunition, among many national variants produced across NATO member states.
