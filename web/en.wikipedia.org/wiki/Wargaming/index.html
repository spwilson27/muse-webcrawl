Wargaming

Wargaming is a hobby and professional discipline in which players simulate armed conflict, usually with models, maps, counters or computers, under a set of rules that model movement, combat and logistics. It encompasses tabletop miniature games, board wargames, and computer wargames, and it is distinct from, though related to, military staff exercises.

History

Modern wargaming descends from Kriegsspiel, developed by the Prussian army in the early 19th century. Georg von Reisswitz's 1824 version used a gridded map, wooden blocks representing units, and umpires to adjudicate outcomes, and it was adopted for officer training. In the 20th century, H. G. Wells's Little Wars (1913) popularised recreational wargaming with toy soldiers, while Charles S. Roberts's Tactics (1954) founded the commercial board wargame industry. Computer wargames followed from the 1970s onward.

Forms

Miniature wargaming uses painted figures on modelled terrain, with rulers and dice resolving combat; popular systems include Warhammer 40,000 and historical rules such as Flames of War. Board wargames use hex maps and cardboard counters, often at operational or strategic scale. Computer wargames range from turn-based titles such as Panzer General to real-time strategy games, and include professional simulations used by armed forces for training and analysis.

Professional use

Militaries continue to use wargames — now called "wargames" or "exercises" — to test doctrine, explore scenarios and train commanders. Business and policy schools have adopted analogous "serious games" for decision-making under uncertainty.
