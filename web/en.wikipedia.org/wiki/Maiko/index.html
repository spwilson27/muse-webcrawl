Maiko

OccupationPerforming artist / entertainer
RegionKyoto, Japan (principally Gion)
Stage of careerApprentice to geiko (geisha)

A maiko (舞妓) is an apprentice geisha in Kyoto and Western Japan. Their jobs consist of performing songs, dances, and playing the shamisen or other traditional Japanese instruments for visitors during feasts. Maiko are usually aged 17 to 20 years old, and graduate to geiko status after a period of training that includes traditional dance, the shamisen, singing, and speaking the Kyoto dialect.

Appearance

Unlike fully-fledged geisha (geiko in Kyoto), who wear a wig, a maiko uses her own hair styled each week by a specialist hairdresser. The hairstyle of the first two to three years is called wareshinobu, decorated with seasonal hair ornaments (kanzashi). A maiko's long, dangling obi sash, the darari, is worn hanging down the back, and she walks in tall wooden clogs known as okobo.

Make-up is applied heavily in the first year, making the maiko a striking sight. The white base, red lips and elaborate hairstyle distinguish apprentices clearly from full geiko, whose appearance is more subdued.

Life and training

Maiko live in an okiya, a lodging house run by an okaa-san ("mother"), who finances their training. Before becoming maiko, girls may serve a period as shikomi (house maids and students). Daily life is strictly regulated: maiko attend lessons in dance, music and other arts, and entertain at banquets in the evenings.

The ceremony marking the transition from maiko to geiko is known as erikae ("turning of the collar").

Regional variants

The term maiko is specific to Kyoto. In Tokyo and elsewhere, apprentice geisha are called hangyoku, and in some regions other terms such as minarai are used for girls in earlier stages of training.
