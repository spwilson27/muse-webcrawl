Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union

The Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) is one of two treaties forming the constitutional basis of the European Union, the other being the Treaty on European Union (TEU). It derives from the Treaty of Rome of 1957, which established the European Economic Community, and was renamed and extensively amended by the Treaty of Lisbon, which entered into force on 1 December 2009.

Where the TEU sets out the Union's principles and institutional framework, the TFEU details how the Union operates: it categorises EU competences (exclusive, shared and supporting), and contains the detailed policy provisions on the internal market, free movement of goods, persons, services and capital, agriculture, transport, competition, taxation, economic and monetary policy, employment, social policy, the environment, consumer protection and other fields.

Structure

The treaty is divided into seven parts covering principles; non-discrimination and citizenship of the Union; Union policies and internal actions; association of the overseas countries and territories; the Union's external action; institutional and financial provisions; and general and final provisions. It is accompanied by protocols and declarations, and by the Charter of Fundamental Rights, which the Lisbon Treaty gave the same legal value as the treaties themselves.

Key provisions

Notable articles include Article 101 and 102 on competition (prohibiting cartels and abuse of a dominant position), Article 107 on state aid, Article 123 prohibiting monetary financing of governments, and Article 258, which empowers the European Commission to bring infringement proceedings against member states before the Court of Justice of the European Union.
