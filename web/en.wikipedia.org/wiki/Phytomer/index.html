Phytomer

A phytomer is a functional unit of a plant shoot, consisting of a node, the leaf (or leaves) attached at that node, the internode below it, and the axillary bud (or buds) in the leaf axil. Phytomers are the repeating modular units from which the shoot system of a plant is constructed.

Structure and development

Phytomers are produced sequentially by the shoot apical meristem. New phytomers are initiated as leaf primordia on the flanks of the meristem; the time interval between the initiation of successive phytomers is called a plastochron. Each phytomer may pass through phases of cell division, expansion and maturation, and its development is influenced by environmental signals such as light, temperature and water availability, as well as by phytohormones.

Modularity and plant architecture

Because plants grow by the repeated addition of phytomers, plant form can be described in modular terms. The number, size and orientation of phytomers determine branching patterns, canopy structure and the display of leaves for photosynthesis. In grasses, the phytomer is sometimes described as including the leaf blade, leaf sheath, node, internode and tiller bud, and the concept is widely used in crop modelling to simulate growth of cereals such as maize, wheat and rice.

Senescence

Individual phytomers have finite lifespans: older leaves at lower nodes typically senesce and are shed as the shoot grows, while their axillary buds may remain dormant, grow out into branches, or develop into reproductive structures such as flowers and inflorescences.
