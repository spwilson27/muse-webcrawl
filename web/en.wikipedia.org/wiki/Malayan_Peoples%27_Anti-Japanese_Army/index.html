The Malayan Peoples' Anti-Japanese Army (MPAJA) was a paramilitary guerrilla force formed to resist the Japanese occupation of Malaya during the Second World War. Composed largely of ethnic Chinese communists, it was the military arm of the Malayan Communist Party.

Formation

On the eve of the Japanese invasion in December 1941, the British authorities released communist prisoners and accepted the party's offer of military cooperation. Volunteers were given brief training at the 101 Special Training School in Singapore before Singapore fell in February 1942; the MPAJA then organized itself into independent regiments in the jungle.

Wartime activity

The MPAJA conducted ambushes, sabotage, and intelligence work, and from 1943 cooperated with the British Force 136, which supplied arms, training, and liaison officers by submarine and air drop. Figures such as Chin Peng emerged as leading commanders. The force also carried out reprisals against alleged collaborators, a controversial aspect of its record.

Disbandment and aftermath

The MPAJA formally disbanded on 1 December 1945, with members receiving gratuities and medals. However, many weapons were secretly cached, and veterans of the MPAJA formed the core of the Malayan National Liberation Army during the Malayan Emergency that began in 1948.
