The Civil Rights Memorial is an American memorial in Montgomery, Alabama, created by designer Maya Lin. It honours the martyrs of the civil rights movement — 41 men, women and children who died between 1954 and 1968 in the struggle for racial equality. The memorial stands on an open plaza next to the headquarters of the Southern Poverty Law Center (SPLC), a few blocks from the church where Martin Luther King Jr. served as pastor during the Montgomery bus boycott.

Design

Lin, who had designed the Vietnam Veterans Memorial in Washington, D.C., conceived the memorial around water. A curved black granite wall is inscribed with King's paraphrase of Amos 5:24 — "...until justice rolls down like waters and righteousness like a mighty stream" — and water flows gently over its face. Below it sits a circular black granite table, 12 feet (3.7 m) across, over which a thin sheet of water also flows; engraved around its rim are the names of the martyrs and key events of the movement, arranged chronologically. Visitors are encouraged to touch the names, like braille, making the water ripple.

History

The memorial was dedicated on November 5, 1989, at a ceremony attended by veterans of the movement including Rosa Parks. The names range from Emmett Till, the 14-year-old murdered in Mississippi in 1955, to King himself, assassinated in Memphis in 1968. Adjacent to the plaza, the SPLC's Civil Rights Memorial Center, opened in 2005, interprets the names and houses the Wall of Tolerance. The memorial plaza has been guarded and periodically targeted because of the SPLC's work; the building behind it was firebombed in 1983, an attack that helped spur the memorial's creation.
