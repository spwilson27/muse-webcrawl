Technical school

Technical school
TypeSecondary or post-secondary vocational institution
FocusApplied trades, technology, applied sciences
EquivalentsVocational school, trade school, polytechnic, Fachschule

A technical school is an educational institution — at secondary, post-secondary or further-education level — that emphasises practical, occupation-oriented instruction in trades, applied technology and applied sciences, rather than a purely academic curriculum. Students typically combine general subjects with workshop, laboratory or workplace training leading to a certificate, diploma or applied degree.

By country

In the United States, technical schools and career and technical education (CTE) programmes prepare students for fields such as automotive technology, health care, construction and information technology; the federal Perkins Acts have funded vocational education since the twentieth century. In Germany, the dual system combines part-time attendance at a Berufsschule with apprenticeship in a company. Japan's kōsen (colleges of technology) admit students at fifteen for five-year engineering programmes. In the United Kingdom, the short-lived secondary technical schools of the post-1944 tripartite system were mostly absorbed into comprehensive schools, while institutes of technology and polytechnics supplied higher technical education before many became universities in 1992.

Curriculum

Programmes commonly cover mathematics and science applied to the trade, technical drawing and computer-aided design, materials, safety, and extended hands-on practice. Many schools maintain partnerships with local employers and offer internships or cooperative placements.

Debate

Supporters argue technical schools match education to labour-market demand and provide routes to skilled employment; critics warn that early tracking can narrow students' options and reproduce social inequalities.
