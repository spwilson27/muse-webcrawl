Kolyma River

The Kolyma (Russian: Колыма) is a river in north-eastern Siberia, flowing through Magadan Oblast and the Sakha Republic (Yakutia) into the East Siberian Sea. It is about 2,129 km long with a drainage basin of some 647,000 km². Its name became synonymous in the Soviet era with the Gulag camp system of the Kolyma region, described by writers such as Varlam Shalamov in his Kolyma Tales.

Geography

The Kolyma rises in the Kolyma Highlands and flows first north-east through the gold-bearing uplands of Magadan Oblast, then north across the Kolyma Lowland — a vast, swampy permafrost plain — to its delta on the East Siberian Sea near Chersky. The river is frozen from October to early June; the spring breakup brings extensive flooding. Its basin holds rich deposits of gold, tin and other minerals, and the lower river supports fishing communities of Russians, Yakuts, Evens and Yukaghirs.

History

Russian explorers, including Mikhail Stadukhin, reached the Kolyma in the 1640s, and Semyon Dezhnev sailed from its mouth around Chukotka in 1648. In the 1930s the Dalstroy organisation developed the basin as a giant forced-labour mining enterprise; prisoners were shipped to Magadan and up the river to camps along its tributaries. The Kolyma hydroelectric station at Sinegorye, built from the 1970s, supplies power to the region.
