The Lord Lyon King of Arms is the chief heraldic authority of Scotland and the judge of the Court of the Lord Lyon, the oldest heraldic court in the world still in daily operation. The Lord Lyon regulates all matters of arms and pedigrees in Scotland, where heraldry remains a branch of law, and unauthorised use of arms is a criminal offence punishable in the Lyon Court.

History

The office dates from the 14th century, its title deriving from the lion rampant in the royal arms of Scotland. By Acts of the Scottish Parliament in 1592, 1669, and 1672, the Lyon was given wide powers to inspect and record all arms, to grant arms to "virtuous and well deserving persons", and to fine or imprison those who usurped arms. The 1672 Act established the Public Register of All Arms and Bearings in Scotland, in which all Scottish arms must be recorded.

Functions

The Lord Lyon grants and matriculates coats of arms, recognises clan chiefs and chiefs of names and arms, and advises the Crown and government on heraldic and ceremonial matters, including royal proclamations in Scotland. The Lyon Court also has jurisdiction over the name and arms of clan chiefs, and the Lord Lyon's recognition is the official means by which a chiefship is established. Unlike in England, where arms descend to all male-line descendants with difference marks prescribed by custom, in Scotland arms are the property of one person at a time and cadets must matriculate differenced versions.

The Lord Lyon is assisted by three heralds and pursuivants, including Rothesay Herald and Marchmont Herald, and by the Lyon Clerk and Keeper of the Records. Macers of the court carry out its decrees. At ceremonial occasions the Lord Lyon wears a tabard of the Scottish royal arms and, at coronations, Scotland's heraldic crown.
