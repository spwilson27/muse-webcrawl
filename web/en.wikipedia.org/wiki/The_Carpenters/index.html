The Carpenters

The Carpenters were an American vocal and instrumental duo consisting of siblings Karen Carpenter (1950–1983) and Richard Carpenter (born 1946). Their melodic pop sound, built around Karen's contralto voice and Richard's arrangements, produced a string of hits on the Billboard charts during the 1970s.

Career

Signed to A&M Records in 1969, the duo broke through in 1970 with "(They Long to Be) Close to You" and "We've Only Just Begun". Further hits included "Rainy Days and Mondays", "Superstar", "Yesterday Once More" and "Top of the World". Their albums, including Close to You, A Song for You and Now & Then, sold tens of millions of copies worldwide.

End and legacy

The duo's career ended with Karen's death in 1983 from complications of anorexia nervosa, which brought public attention to eating disorders. The Carpenters have sold more than 100 million records and were inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame; Karen Carpenter is widely ranked among the greatest singers of all time.
