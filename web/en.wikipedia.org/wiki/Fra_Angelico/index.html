Fra Angelico, O.P. (born Guido di Pietro; c. 1395 – 18 February 1455), also called Beato Angelico ("the Blessed Angelic One"), was an Italian painter of the Early Renaissance and a Dominican friar. He is remembered for the serene frescoes he painted in his own convent of San Marco in Florence and for his many depictions of the Annunciation.

Life and works

Guido entered the Dominican convent at Fiesole, taking the name Fra Giovanni. Vasari's nickname for him, "Angelico", reflected both his piety and the luminous quality of his painting. In Florence, Cosimo de' Medici had the convent of San Marco rebuilt, and Angelico and his workshop decorated its cells, chapter house and corridors with frescoes for the friars' meditation — among them a celebrated Annunciation at the head of the dormitory stairs, and the great Crucifixion in the chapter house. He also painted altarpieces such as the Linaioli Tabernacle and the San Marco Altarpiece.

Summoned to Rome by Pope Eugenius IV and later Nicholas V, he frescoed the Niccoline Chapel in the Vatican with scenes from the lives of Saints Stephen and Lawrence. He died in Rome in 1455 and is buried in Santa Maria sopra Minerva.

Style and reputation

Fra Angelico combined the Gothic grace of his Florentine training with Renaissance innovations in perspective and naturalistic space, using clear colour and gentle light to devotional ends. Pope John Paul II beatified him in 1982 and named him patron of Catholic artists.
