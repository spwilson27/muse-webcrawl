Indoor air quality

Indoor air quality
Common pollutantsParticulate matter, VOCs, carbon monoxide, radon, mould, allergens
Key controlsVentilation, source control, filtration

Indoor air quality (IAQ) is the air quality within and around buildings and structures, especially as it relates to the health and comfort of building occupants. Understanding and controlling common pollutants indoors can help reduce the risk of indoor health concerns. People in developed countries spend roughly 90 percent of their time indoors, so IAQ has a major influence on total exposure to air pollution.

Pollutants and sources

Major indoor pollutants include particulate matter from cooking, heating, and tobacco smoke; volatile organic compounds (VOCs) emitted by paints, furnishings, and cleaning products; carbon monoxide from faulty combustion appliances; nitrogen dioxide from gas stoves; radon seeping from the ground; formaldehyde from pressed-wood products; and biological contaminants such as mould, dust mites, pet dander, and bacteria including Legionella. In low-income settings, smoke from solid-fuel cooking fires is a leading cause of respiratory disease, particularly among women and children.

Health effects

Effects range from irritation of the eyes, nose, and throat, headaches, and fatigue, to respiratory diseases, heart disease, and cancer after long-term exposure. The cluster of symptoms associated with time spent in a building is known as sick building syndrome. Elevated indoor carbon dioxide, used as a proxy for ventilation adequacy, has been associated in studies with reduced cognitive performance.

Control

The standard strategies are source control (removing or reducing emissions), ventilation with outdoor air (governed in many countries by standards such as ASHRAE 62.1), and filtration or air cleaning. Interest in IAQ monitoring grew sharply during the COVID-19 pandemic, which highlighted the role of ventilation in controlling airborne infection.
