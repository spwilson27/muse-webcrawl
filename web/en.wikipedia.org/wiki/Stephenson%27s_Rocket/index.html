Stephenson's Rocket is an early steam locomotive of 0-2-2 wheel arrangement, designed by Robert Stephenson and built in 1829 at the Forth Street Works of Robert Stephenson and Company in Newcastle upon Tyne, England. It won the Rainhill Trials held by the Liverpool and Manchester Railway in October 1829.

Design

Although not the first steam locomotive, Rocket combined several innovative features that became standard: a multi-tube boiler, which greatly increased heating surface and steam generation; a separate firebox; and a blast pipe that used exhaust steam to draw the fire. Its single pair of driving wheels was driven by two inclined cylinders.

Rainhill Trials

At the Rainhill Trials, Rocket was the only locomotive to complete the course, averaging 12 mph (19 km/h) and reaching 30 mph (48 km/h), and was chosen to power the new railway. The original locomotive, much modified, is preserved at the Science Museum in London.
