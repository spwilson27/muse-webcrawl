Reichsmark

The Reichsmark (sign: ℛℳ; code: RM) was the currency in Germany from 1924 until 1948 in the Trizone and 1949 in East Germany, when it was replaced by the Deutsche Mark in West Germany and the East German mark in East Germany. The Reichsmark was subdivided into 100 Reichspfennig.

History

The Reichsmark was introduced in 1924 as part of the currency reform carried out under the Dawes Plan, replacing the Rentenmark at par and ending the hyperinflation of the Papiermark that had destroyed savings in 1923. It was pegged to the gold standard at a rate of 4.2 Reichsmark to the US dollar. Under Nazi Germany, extensive controls and the use of MEFO bills financed rearmament, while the currency remained nominally stable. After the Second World War the Reichsmark circulated amid a collapsed economy and cigarette black markets until the currency reform of 20 June 1948 replaced it in the Western zones with the Deutsche Mark.
