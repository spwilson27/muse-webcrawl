Costco Wholesale Corporation is an American multinational corporation which operates a chain of membership-only big-box warehouse club stores. Founded from the merger of Price Club (1976, San Diego) and Costco (first warehouse 1983, Seattle), it is one of the world's largest retailers and the largest warehouse club chain, with roughly 900 warehouses and well over 100 million members worldwide. Costco sells a deliberately limited selection of about 4,000 products in bulk sizes at thin margins, making most of its profit from annual membership fees, and sells online through costco.com.

Business model

Costco caps markups at roughly 14–15 percent, pays comparatively high retail wages, and relies on a "treasure hunt" assortment in which discretionary items rotate. Its private label, Kirkland Signature (named after its former headquarters city), frequently matches or exceeds national-brand quality and accounts for a large share of sales. Food courts are famous for the $1.50 hot dog combo, whose price has been unchanged since 1985 and is defended by management as a matter of principle. Costco also sells petrol at many locations, operates optical and hearing-aid centres, and runs a significant travel business.

History

Sol Price pioneered the warehouse-club model with Price Club in 1976, initially serving small businesses. James Sinegal, a Price protégé, co-founded Costco in 1983 with Jeffrey Brotman; the two companies merged in 1993 as PriceCostco before becoming Costco Wholesale in 1997. The company expanded into Canada, Mexico, the UK, East Asia, Australia, Spain, France and elsewhere, and has consistently ranked near the top of US retailers by revenue, behind Walmart and Amazon.
