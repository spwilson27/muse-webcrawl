Tower block

A tower block, or high-rise residential block, is a tall building constructed as a block of flats (apartments). The term is most commonly used in the United Kingdom, while the equivalent buildings in North America are usually called high-rise apartment buildings or apartment towers. Tower blocks became widespread in Britain during the post-war reconstruction period of the 1950s and 1960s, when local authorities built large numbers of them to replace housing destroyed in the Blitz and to clear Victorian slums.

Early tower blocks were seen as a modern, hygienic alternative to overcrowded terraced housing, offering residents indoor bathrooms, central heating and views. However, by the 1970s many had become associated with social problems, poor maintenance and design flaws, and a number were demolished from the 1980s onwards. Interest in tower block living has revived in recent decades, particularly for privately built luxury developments.

History

The first residential tower blocks in Britain were built in the early 1950s, influenced by the modernist ideas of architects such as Le Corbusier, whose Unité d'Habitation in Marseille demonstrated high-density housing in a single slab block. Government subsidies in the Housing Subsidies Act 1956 encouraged councils to build taller blocks, paying more subsidy per flat the higher the building. By the mid-1960s thousands of tower blocks had been approved.

The partial collapse of Ronan Point in east London in 1968, caused by a gas explosion that blew out load-bearing walls, badly damaged public confidence in system-built towers. Building regulations were subsequently tightened.

Design

Tower blocks are typically constructed from a reinforced concrete frame or using large-panel system building, clad in concrete, brick or metal panels. Access to flats is usually by internal corridors or deck access ("streets in the sky"). Notable examples include Trellick Tower and Balfron Tower in London, designed by Ernő Goldfinger, and Park Hill in Sheffield.

Safety

Fire safety in tower blocks became a major public issue after the Grenfell Tower fire in London in June 2017, in which 72 people died. The fire spread rapidly via combustible aluminium composite cladding, prompting a nationwide programme of cladding remediation and new building safety legislation.
