The Secret Doctrine, the Synthesis of Science, Religion and Philosophy is a two-volume work by Helena Petrovna Blavatsky, first published in 1888. It is the foundational text of Theosophy, the esoteric movement Blavatsky co-founded through the Theosophical Society in 1875. The two volumes are titled Cosmogenesis and Anthropogenesis.

Contents

Blavatsky claimed the work was based on an archaic manuscript, the Book of Dzyan, written in a sacred language called Senzar, which she said she had studied in Tibet. The first volume, Cosmogenesis, presents a cosmology in which the universe periodically manifests and dissolves in vast cycles, emanating from an eternal, unknowable Absolute. The second volume, Anthropogenesis, describes the spiritual and physical evolution of humanity through a succession of "root races" over immense periods of time, on a scheme drawing from Hindu and Buddhist concepts such as karma and reincarnation.

Influence and criticism

The Secret Doctrine profoundly influenced modern Western esotericism, and its ideas about cycles, karma, and hidden masters spread into many later occult and New Age movements. It has also been widely criticized: scholars have questioned the existence of the Book of Dzyan, noted extensive unattributed borrowings from contemporary works on science and comparative religion, and criticized the root-race doctrine for its racial hierarchies.
