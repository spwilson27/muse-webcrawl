WRN (Werner syndrome RecQ-like helicase) is a gene in humans that encodes the Werner syndrome ATP-dependent helicase, an enzyme belonging to the RecQ family of DNA helicases. The protein has both helicase and exonuclease activities and participates in DNA repair, replication, recombination, and the maintenance of telomeres.

Function

The WRN protein unwinds DNA structures such as replication forks, Holliday junctions, and G-quadruplexes, and it digests DNA from the 3′ end with its exonuclease domain. It interacts with many partners in genome maintenance, including replication protein A (RPA), proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA), p53, and the shelterin complex at telomeres. Loss of WRN function leads to genomic instability, accelerated cellular senescence, and sensitivity to DNA-damaging agents.

Clinical significance

Mutations in WRN that abolish the protein's function cause Werner syndrome, an autosomal recessive disorder characterized by premature aging (a progeroid syndrome). Affected individuals typically develop normally until puberty, after which they show features including short stature, graying and loss of hair, cataracts, skin changes, type 2 diabetes, atherosclerosis, and an increased risk of cancer, particularly sarcomas and thyroid carcinoma.

More recently, WRN has attracted interest as a cancer drug target: tumor cells with microsatellite instability are highly dependent on WRN helicase activity, making the protein a candidate for synthetic-lethal therapy with small-molecule WRN inhibitors.
