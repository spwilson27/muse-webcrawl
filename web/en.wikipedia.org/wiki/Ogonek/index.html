Ogonek

Example letters: ą ę į ǫ ų

Used in: Polish, Lithuanian, Navajo, and other languages

Unicode: U+0328 combining ogonek

The ogonek (Polish for "little tail", pronounced roughly "oh-GOH-nek") is a diacritic hook placed under the lower right corner of a vowel letter in the Latin alphabet. It looks like a small reversed cedilla or a tiny tail curving to the right, as in ą and ę.

Use

In Polish, the letters ą and ę denote nasal vowels (historically pronounced with nasal resonance, though in modern Polish their realization varies with context). In Lithuanian, ą, ę, į, and ų historically indicated long vowels and are now pronounced as long vowels. The ogonek marks nasal vowels in several Native American languages written in the Latin alphabet, notably Navajo (ą, ę, į, ǫ and their nasalized-long counterparts) and Western Apache, and it is used in the transcription of Old Norse and in some editions of Old English. In the International Phonetic Alphabet-related traditions and in older Slavic philology, it may transcribe vowel nasalization generally.

Origin and encoding

The mark originated in medieval manuscripts, where an "e caudata" (tailed e) stood for the digraph ae; the tail survived in Polish orthography for nasal vowels. In Unicode the ogonek is encoded both in precomposed letters (for example Ą U+0104, Ę U+0118) and as a combining character, U+0328 COMBINING OGONEK, which can be attached to other base letters. It should not be confused with the cedilla, which attaches to consonants such as ç, or with the comma below used in Romanian.
