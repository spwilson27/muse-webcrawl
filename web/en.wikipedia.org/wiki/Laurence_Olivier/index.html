Laurence Olivier

Laurence Kerr Olivier, Baron Olivier
Born: 22 May 1907, Dorking, Surrey
Died: 11 July 1989, Steyning, West Sussex
Occupation: Actor, director

Laurence Kerr Olivier, Baron Olivier (22 May 1907 – 11 July 1989) was an English actor and director who, along with Ralph Richardson and John Gielgud, was one of a trio of male actors who dominated the British stage of the mid-20th century. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest actors of the 20th century.

Olivier as Hamlet (1948).

Career

Olivier's career encompassed Shakespearean stage triumphs at the Old Vic, celebrated film adaptations of Henry V (1944), Hamlet (1948) — which won the Academy Award for Best Picture, with Olivier winning Best Actor — and Richard III (1955). He was the founding artistic director of the National Theatre of Great Britain, whose main auditorium now bears his name.

Personal life

He was married to actresses Jill Esmond, Vivien Leigh, and Joan Plowright. Knighted in 1947, he became in 1970 the first actor to be made a life peer. His ashes are buried in Poets' Corner in Westminster Abbey.
