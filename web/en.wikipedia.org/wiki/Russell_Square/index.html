Russell Square

Russell Square is a large garden square in Bloomsbury, in the London Borough of Camden, built predominantly by the firm of James Burton. It is named after the Russell family, Earls and Dukes of Bedford, who developed the family's Bloomsbury estates in the 17th and 18th centuries, and it was laid out in the early 1800s to designs associated with Humphry Repton.

Surroundings

The square is overlooked by major institutions, including the University of London's Senate House and the School of Oriental and African Studies, as well as the large Hotel Russell (now the Kimpton Fitzroy London). Russell Square tube station, on the Piccadilly line, serves the area. The square's gardens, with a café and a fountain featuring water jets at ground level, are open to the public and are Grade II listed on the Register of Historic Parks and Gardens.

History

Russell Square was one of the locations affected by the 7 July 2005 London bombings: a bomb exploded on a Piccadilly line train between King's Cross St Pancras and Russell Square, and another on a bus in nearby Tavistock Square. A statue of Francis Russell, 5th Duke of Bedford, stands on the south side of the square.
