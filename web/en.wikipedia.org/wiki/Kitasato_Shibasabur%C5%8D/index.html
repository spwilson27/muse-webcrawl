Kitasato Shibasaburō

Kitasato Shibasaburō (29 January 1853 – 13 June 1931) was a Japanese physician and bacteriologist, regarded as a founder of modern Japanese medicine. Working in Robert Koch's laboratory in Berlin, he became the first to grow the tetanus bacillus in pure culture (1889) and, with Emil von Behring, developed the concept of antitoxin serum therapy.

Career

In 1894, during a Hong Kong outbreak, Kitasato identified the bacterium that causes bubonic plague (Yersinia pestis), an achievement shared with Alexandre Yersin. On returning to Japan he founded the Institute for Infectious Diseases and later the Kitasato Institute. He was nominated for the first Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (1901) for the diphtheria antitoxin work, though the prize went to Behring alone.
