Rail freight transport

Rail freight transport is the use of railways and trains to transport cargo, as opposed to passengers. A freight train is a group of freight cars hauled by one or more locomotives, transporting cargo between the shipper and the intended destination as part of the logistics chain.

Rail is particularly suited to bulk commodities such as coal, iron ore, grain and aggregates, and to intermodal traffic in which shipping containers are carried between ports and inland terminals. Rail freight is generally more energy-efficient per tonne-kilometre than road haulage, though it is less flexible. Major freight rail systems operate in North America, China, India, Russia and Australia.
