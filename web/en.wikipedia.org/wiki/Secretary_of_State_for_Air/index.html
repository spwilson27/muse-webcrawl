Secretary of State for Air

The Secretary of State for Air was a cabinet-level position in the British government, responsible for the affairs of the Royal Air Force through the Air Ministry. The post existed from 1919 until 1964, when it was merged, along with the offices of First Lord of the Admiralty and Secretary of State for War, into the new office of Secretary of State for Defence.

The Air Ministry in Whitehall administered British air power under the Secretary of State for Air.

History

Military aviation in Britain was reorganized during the First World War. The Air Board and later the Air Council preceded the creation, on 10 January 1919, of the Air Ministry with a Secretary of State for Air. Winston Churchill was the first holder, combining the post with that of Secretary of State for War until 1921. The office presided over the independent Royal Air Force, formed in April 1918, and over the expansion of British air power in the interwar period, the Battle of Britain era, and the early Cold War.

Responsibilities

The Secretary of State for Air was the political head of the Air Ministry and chairman of the Air Council. He answered to Parliament for the RAF's estimates, policy, and administration, while professional military advice came from the Chief of the Air Staff.
