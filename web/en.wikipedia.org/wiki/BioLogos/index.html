BioLogos is an American nonprofit organization that advocates the compatibility of Christian faith with the scientific consensus on evolution and the age of the Earth. It was founded in 2007 by geneticist Francis Collins, leader of the Human Genome Project, following the publication of his book The Language of God (2006). BioLogos promotes the view it calls "evolutionary creationism" — the belief that God created life through the evolutionary process.

BioLogos promotes dialogue between science and Christian faith.

History

Collins launched BioLogos first as a website answering questions he had received after The Language of God, with support from the John Templeton Foundation. It grew into an organization hosting conferences — including the 2009 "Darwin Was Wrong?"–era workshops with theologians and scientists — publishing books and curricula, and convening scholars such as Karl Giberson, Darrel Falk (its first president), and Deborah Haarsma, who became president in 2013. BioLogos later developed resources aimed at pastors, Christian schools, and homeschooling families.

Positions

BioLogos affirms mainstream findings of biology, geology, and cosmology, including common descent and an ancient universe, while maintaining orthodox Christian doctrine as expressed in its statement of belief. It distinguishes its position from both young Earth creationism and intelligent design, which it regards as scientifically unsound, and from atheistic accounts of evolution, which it regards as philosophically overextended.

Reception

The organization has been welcomed by many mainline and evangelical scholars as a bridge between faith and science, and criticized from two directions: by creationist organizations such as Answers in Genesis, which accuse it of compromising biblical authority, and by some atheist commentators who reject any reconciliation of religion with science. BioLogos materials are widely used in Christian colleges and in public discussions of science education in the United States.
