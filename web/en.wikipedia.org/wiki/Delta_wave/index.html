Delta wave

An EEG showing delta waves during deep sleep

A delta wave is a high-amplitude brain wave with a frequency of oscillation between 0.5 and 4 hertz, recorded with an electroencephalogram (EEG). Delta waves are characteristically associated with stage N3 of non-REM sleep, also called slow-wave sleep, as well as with infancy and certain brain lesions.

Characteristics

Delta activity is the dominant rhythm in infants and young children during sleep, and it increases after sleep deprivation, reflecting sleep "pressure". In healthy adults, prominent delta activity during wakefulness is abnormal and may indicate brain injury, tumors, or metabolic disturbances. Delta waves are generated through interactions between the cortex and the thalamus.

Clinical significance

Excess delta activity has been reported in various neurological and psychiatric conditions. Focal delta slowing over one region of the scalp can help localize structural brain lesions in clinical EEG interpretation.
