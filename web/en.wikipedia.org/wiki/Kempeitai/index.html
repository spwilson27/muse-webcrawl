Kempeitai

Kempeitai (憲兵隊)
Active: 1881–1945
Country: Empire of Japan
Role: Military police, secret police

The Kempeitai was the military police arm of the Imperial Japanese Army from 1881 to 1945. Modelled partly on the French gendarmerie, it also functioned as a secret police force in Japan and in occupied territories.

History

Founded in 1881 during the Meiji period, the Kempeitai expanded with Japan's empire. During the Second World War it enforced security in occupied Asia, ran counter-espionage and suppressed dissent. It became notorious for torture, executions and abuse of prisoners of war and civilian internees, including operations in Singapore, Malaya and the Dutch East Indies.

Dissolution

The Kempeitai was disbanded under the Allied occupation in 1945. A number of its officers were prosecuted for war crimes after the war.
