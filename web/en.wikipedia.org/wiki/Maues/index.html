Maues

Maues (Greek: Μαύης), also spelled Moga, was the earliest known Indo-Scythian (Saka) king, ruling in the northwestern Indian subcontinent — principally Gandhara and the Punjab — during the early first century BCE, conventionally placed around 98/85 to 60/57 BCE. His rise marks the establishment of Scythian power in India after the decline of the Indo-Greek kingdoms.

Reign

Maues seems to have first ruled in the region of the Indus and Hazara before extending his power over Gandhara, with Taxila as a principal city. He took the title "Great King of Kings" (maharaja rajatiraja), imitating the imperial style of the Parthian and earlier Achaemenid rulers. He is often associated with the era used in the Taxila copper-plate inscription of Patika, dated in the year 78 of "the great king Moga," though the starting point of that reckoning remains debated. His coins imitate Indo-Greek types, pairing Greek legends on the obverse with Kharoshthi legends on the reverse, and depict Greek deities such as Zeus, Nike, and Tyche as well as Indian symbols.

Successors

Coins of Maues were overstruck by the Indo-Greek kings Apollodotus II and Hippostratus, indicating a contested succession in parts of the Punjab. He was followed in Gandhara by Azes I, whose era — beginning around 58/47 BCE — became the standard dating system of the Indo-Scythian realm.
