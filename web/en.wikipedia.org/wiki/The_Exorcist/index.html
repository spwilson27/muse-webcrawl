The Exorcist is a 1973 American supernatural horror film directed by William Friedkin, adapted by William Peter Blatty from his 1971 novel, which was itself inspired by a 1949 case of alleged demonic possession involving a Maryland boy. The film follows Regan MacNeil (Linda Blair), a 12-year-old girl in Georgetown, Washington, D.C., whose demonic possession leads her mother (Ellen Burstyn) to seek an exorcism performed by two Catholic priests, the aging archaeologist Father Merrin (Max von Sydow) and the doubting psychiatrist-priest Father Karras (Jason Miller).

Production and release

Friedkin pursued documentary realism: Regan's bedroom set was refrigerated so the actors' breath would show, and special effects — the spider walk, the head-turning dummy, pea-soup vomit — were practical. Mercedes McCambridge voiced the demon. The production was dogged by accidents that fed legends of a "curse." Released the day after Christmas 1973, the film provoked fainting and vomiting in audiences and lines around the block; it became the highest-grossing film of its time for Warner Bros. and, adjusted for inflation, one of the top-grossing films ever.

Reception and legacy

The Exorcist was the first horror film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture; it won Best Adapted Screenplay and Best Sound and received ten nominations overall. In 2010 it was inducted into the US National Film Registry. It spawned sequels and prequels of mixed quality, a 2016 television series, and a 2023 sequel, The Exorcist: Believer. Its cultural impact — like that of other genre-defining releases such as The Chronic in music — is a standard benchmark for the horror genre.
