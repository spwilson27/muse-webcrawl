Wold's theorem

Wold's decomposition theorem, named after Herman Wold, is a result in time series analysis stating that every covariance-stationary time series can be written as the sum of two time series: a deterministic component and a stochastic component represented as an infinite moving average of uncorrelated "innovations".

Statement

Formally, a stationary process {Yt} can be decomposed as Yt = Σj=0∞ bj εt−j + ηt, where {εt} is a sequence of uncorrelated random variables, {bj} are square-summable coefficients, and ηt is a deterministic component perfectly predictable from its own past.

Significance

The theorem provides the theoretical foundation for the use of ARMA models in time series analysis: any stationary series can be approximated arbitrarily well by moving-average processes, and combined with invertibility results this justifies modelling stationary data with autoregressive moving-average models.
