Momine Khatun Mausoleum

The Momine Khatun Mausoleum (Azerbaijani: Möminə Xatın türbəsi) is a mausoleum in Nakhchivan, the capital of the Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic in Azerbaijan. It is one of the best-known monuments of Azerbaijani architecture and a landmark of the Nakhchivan school of architecture founded by the architect Ajami ibn Abubakr Nakhchivani.

History

The mausoleum was commissioned in 1186 by Muhammad Jahan Pahlavan, the Eldiguzid atabeg of Azerbaijan, for his mother Momine Khatun, and was completed in 1186–87. Its architect was Ajami ibn Abubakr Nakhchivani, who also designed the nearby Yusuf ibn Kuseyir Mausoleum. The mausoleum was originally part of a larger complex that included a mosque and a madrasa, of which only the tower survives. In the 19th and 20th centuries the monument was repeatedly restored; it was placed on UNESCO's tentative list of World Heritage Sites as part of the Nakhchivan mausoleums nomination.

Architecture

The mausoleum is a ten-sided (decagonal) brick tower about 25 metres high, rising from a low platform. Each face is framed by pointed-arch niches, and the surfaces are decorated with carved geometric patterns and Kufic inscriptions executed in glazed turquoise tiles inserted into the brickwork. A band of Quranic inscriptions in Kufic script runs around the top of the tower. The tower is crowned by a pyramidal roof. Inside, the burial vault below ground is covered by a dome, and the upper chamber has a ribbed, star-pattern vault. The refined brick decoration and harmonious proportions make it a masterpiece of 12th-century Islamic architecture in the Caucasus.
