Kernmantle rope

Kernmantle rope is a type of rope built from two elements: an inner core, the kern, and a woven outer sheath, the mantle (from German Mantel, meaning coat or sheath). The design protects the load-bearing core from abrasion and dirt while giving the rope good handling qualities, and it is the standard construction for modern climbing and safety ropes.

Construction

The kern, made of parallel or braided synthetic fibres — usually nylon — provides most of the rope's tensile strength and, in dynamic ropes, its stretch. The mantle is a tightly braided sheath that shapes the rope, protects the core from cuts, ultraviolet light and abrasion, and contributes a smaller share of strength. Kernmantle ropes largely displaced traditional laid (twisted) ropes in mountaineering from the 1950s onwards, when they were adopted and standardised under UIAA safety standards.

Types

Dynamic kernmantle ropes are designed to stretch under load to absorb the energy of a climber's fall, elongating significantly to reduce impact forces. Static (low-stretch) kernmantle ropes elongate only a few percent and are used for rappelling, hauling, caving, rescue work and rope access, where stretch is undesirable. Standards such as EN 892 (dynamic) and EN 1891 (low-stretch) govern their performance and testing.
