Henderson Field (Guadalcanal)

Henderson Field is a former military airfield on Guadalcanal, Solomon Islands, built by the Imperial Japanese Army and captured by United States Marines in August 1942 during the Guadalcanal campaign of the Second World War. It is named for Major Lofton R. Henderson, a Marine aviator killed at the Battle of Midway. The airfield's site is now occupied by Honiara International Airport.

History

Japanese construction began in mid-1942 near Lunga Point. On 7 August 1942, the 1st Marine Division landed and seized the nearly complete airfield with little resistance. Marine engineers finished the runway, and on 20 August the first aircraft of the "Cactus Air Force" — named for the Allied codename for Guadalcanal — arrived.

Control of Henderson Field was the pivot of the six-month campaign. Japanese forces mounted repeated ground, air, and naval attacks to retake it, including the battles of the Tenaru, Edson's Ridge, and the Naval Battle of Guadalcanal. Aircraft operating from the field dominated the surrounding seas by day, forcing Japanese resupply into dangerous night runs dubbed the "Tokyo Express".

Legacy

After the war the field was expanded and became the principal airport for the Solomon Islands capital, Honiara.
