The Bundeswehr (German for "Federal Defence") is the armed forces of the Federal Republic of Germany. Founded in 1955 as the military of West Germany during the Cold War, it absorbed members of the East German National People's Army after reunification in 1990. The Bundeswehr is administered by the Federal Ministry of Defence and is divided into the military branches and civilian organisational areas. Its guiding ethos, Innere Fuhrung ("leadership and civic education"), conceives of soldiers as "citizens in uniform" bound by the democratic constitution.

History

Ten years after the end of the Second World War, West Germany's rearmament within NATO led to the founding of the Bundeswehr on 12 November 1955 — the 200th birthday of the Prussian reformer Gerhard von Scharnhorst, a deliberately symbolic choice. The first volunteers were sworn in at Bonn. The force grew rapidly into one of NATO's largest conventional militaries, fielding nearly half a million soldiers at its Cold War peak. After 1990 it shrank substantially, integrated some personnel and equipment of the former East German forces, and progressively took on out-of-area missions.

Structure

The service branches are the Army (Heer), Navy (Marine), and Air Force (Luftwaffe), supported by the Joint Support Service (Streitkraftebasis), the Cyber and Information Domain Service (established 2017), and the Joint Medical Service. Supreme command in peacetime rests with the Federal Minister of Defence; upon a state of defence being declared, it passes to the Federal Chancellor. The Inspector General of the Bundeswehr is the highest-ranking soldier.

Conscription and reform

For most of its history the Bundeswehr relied on conscription for male citizens. Conscription was suspended in 2011, converting the Bundeswehr into an all-volunteer force of roughly 170,000-185,000 personnel, and recruitment shortfalls and equipment readiness became recurring political issues. Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Chancellor Olaf Scholz proclaimed a Zeitenwende ("turning point") in security policy, creating a EUR 100 billion special fund to modernise the Bundeswehr and committing Germany to NATO's two-percent-of-GDP defence spending target.

Missions abroad

From the 1990s the Bundeswehr participated in international operations including peacekeeping in the Balkans (KFOR), the NATO mission in Afghanistan (ISAF/Resolute Support), anti-piracy operations off the Horn of Africa, and training missions in Iraq and Mali — a major shift from its Cold War posture of territorial defence only.
