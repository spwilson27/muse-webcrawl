Spin-off

This article is about the general concept. Spin-off may refer to a work derived from an existing media franchise, a new company created from a parent organisation, or a technology transferred from research to commercial use.

A spin-off (or spinoff) is the creation of something new from an existing parent entity, retaining a connection to the original while operating independently.

In media

In entertainment, a spin-off is a television series, film, book, video game, or other work derived from an existing one, typically focusing on a character, setting, or element from the parent work. Examples include Frasier, developed from Kelsey Grammer's character on Cheers, and Better Call Saul, a spin-off of Breaking Bad. Spin-offs differ from sequels and prequels in that they run parallel to, or branch away from, the original narrative rather than simply continuing it.

In business

A corporate spin-off is a type of divestiture in which a company creates a new, independent company by distributing shares of a subsidiary or business division to its existing shareholders. Unlike a sell-off, the parent receives no cash; shareholders end up owning stock in both companies. Well-known examples include the 2013 spin-off of AbbVie from Abbott Laboratories and eBay's 2015 spin-off of PayPal. Companies undertake spin-offs to unlock shareholder value, sharpen managerial focus, or separate businesses with different growth profiles.

In research and technology

Research spin-offs are companies or products created to commercialise technology developed by a university or public research programme. NASA's spinoff programme has publicised technologies such as memory foam that originated in space research and found consumer applications.
