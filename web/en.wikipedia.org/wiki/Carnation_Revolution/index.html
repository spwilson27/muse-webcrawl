The Carnation Revolution (Portuguese: Revolução de 25 de Abril or Revolução dos Cravos) was a military coup by military officers in Lisbon, Portugal, on 25 April 1974 that overthrew the authoritarian Estado Novo regime, which had ruled Portugal since 1933. The revolution began as a coup organised by the Armed Forces Movement (Movimento das Forças Armadas, MFA), composed of military officers who opposed the regime and the colonial war in Africa.

Background

By 1974 Portugal had been fighting colonial wars in Angola, Mozambique, and Portuguese Guinea for thirteen years, consuming a huge share of the state budget and conscripting generations of young men. The regime founded by António de Oliveira Salazar and continued after 1968 by Marcelo Caetano refused either decolonization or democratization. In February 1974, General António de Spínola published Portugal and the Future, arguing the colonial wars could not be won militarily — a public crack in the regime.

The coup

The signal to begin was the broadcast of two songs: "E depois do adeus" late on 24 April, and, at 00:20 on 25 April, the banned song "Grândola, Vila Morena" by José Afonso on Rádio Renascença. MFA units seized strategic points in Lisbon. Prime Minister Marcelo Caetano took refuge in the Carmo barracks of the National Republican Guard and surrendered to General Spínola. Despite radio appeals from the MFA for civilians to stay home, thousands poured into the streets, fraternizing with the soldiers; a flower seller's red carnations were placed in rifle barrels and on uniforms, giving the revolution its name. Only a handful of people were killed, by the regime's political police, the DGS/PIDE.

Aftermath

The National Salvation Junta took power, promising democratization, decolonization, and development. The following two years — the Ongoing Revolutionary Process (PREC) — brought land reform, nationalizations, the independence of Portugal's African colonies in 1975, and intense political competition. A constitution was adopted in 1976, establishing Portugal as a democratic republic; the first free legislative elections in fifty years were held on 25 April 1976. The date is celebrated annually in Portugal as Freedom Day.
