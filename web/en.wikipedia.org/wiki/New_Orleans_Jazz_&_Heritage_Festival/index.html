New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival

The New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival, commonly called Jazz Fest, is an annual celebration of the music and culture of New Orleans and Louisiana. Held over two weekends in late April and early May at the Fair Grounds Race Course, it is one of the largest music festivals in the United States, drawing several hundred thousand visitors.

History

The festival was founded in 1970 by George Wein, the founder of the Newport Jazz Festival, and produced by Festival Productions with the New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Foundation. The first festival featured Mahalia Jackson, Duke Ellington, Pete Fountain and the Preservation Hall Jazz Band. Gospel singer Mahalia Jackson's impromptu joining of a second line parade became an emblematic moment of the festival's early years.

Music and culture

Despite its name, Jazz Fest presents a broad range of Louisiana music — jazz, blues, R&B, gospel, Cajun, zydeco, Afro-Caribbean, folk, funk and brass bands — alongside national and international headliners on a dozen stages. The festival is also known for its Louisiana food stalls, crafts demonstrations and cultural exhibits celebrating the region's heritage.
