Virtual restaurant

Virtual restaurants fulfil orders exclusively through delivery apps

A virtual restaurant, also known as a ghost kitchen, cloud kitchen or dark kitchen when referring to the facility, is a food service business that serves customers exclusively by delivery, without a storefront, dining room or walk-in trade. Orders are placed through third-party food delivery apps or the brand's own website and fulfilled from a conventional restaurant kitchen, a shared commissary kitchen, or a dedicated delivery-only facility.

Business model

The model removes the costs of prime retail rent, front-of-house staff and table service, allowing operators to launch with lower capital and to experiment with menus quickly. A single kitchen may operate many virtual brands simultaneously — for example separate listings for burgers, salads and desserts — each tailored to search behaviour on delivery platforms. Existing restaurants also run virtual brands from their own kitchens to use spare capacity. Critics note dependence on the commissions and ranking algorithms of delivery platforms, inconsistent food quality, and consumer confusion when several differently named brands share one address.

History

Delivery-only concepts grew with the spread of app-based ordering in the 2010s. Companies such as CloudKitchens (founded by former Uber CEO Travis Kalanick), Kitchen United and Reef Technology raised substantial venture funding to build networks of kitchen spaces leased to restaurant operators. The COVID-19 pandemic sharply accelerated adoption as dine-in service closed. Some prominent ventures later retrenched, while established chains continued to use the model for delivery-focused offshoots, such as Chili's "It's Just Wings" and Applebee's "Cosmic Wings". Celebrity-linked ventures, including MrBeast Burger launched in 2020, demonstrated the reach of purely virtual brands, though some ended amid disputes over quality control.

Regulation

Some municipalities have debated rules requiring virtual restaurants to disclose the physical kitchen behind an app listing, citing food-safety inspection and transparency concerns; health permits generally apply to the operating kitchen regardless of brand name.
