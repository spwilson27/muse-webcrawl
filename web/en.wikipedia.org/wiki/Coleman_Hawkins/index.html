Coleman Hawkins

Coleman Randolph Hawkins

Born: 21 November 1904, St. Joseph, Missouri, U.S.
Died: 19 May 1969, New York City
Instrument: Tenor saxophone
Genres: Jazz, swing, bebop
Nickname: "Hawk", "Bean"

Coleman Randolph Hawkins (November 21, 1904 – May 19, 1969), nicknamed "Hawk" and sometimes "Bean", was an American jazz tenor saxophonist. He is widely credited with establishing the tenor saxophone as a major solo instrument in jazz, rather than a novelty or comic instrument.

Career

Hawkins played piano and cello as a child before taking up saxophone. He toured with Mamie Smith's Jazz Hounds and then joined Fletcher Henderson's Orchestra in 1923, remaining until 1934 and helping define the swing saxophone style with a full tone, rich vibrato and harmonic sophistication. He then worked in Europe for several years.

Returning to the United States, he recorded "Body and Soul" in 1939 — an largely improvised performance over the chord changes that became one of the most famous jazz recordings and a landmark for harmonic improvisation. In the 1940s he embraced bebop early, hiring and recording with young musicians including Thelonious Monk, and led sessions often cited among the first bebop recordings.

Legacy

Hawkins remained active and modern-sounding into the 1960s, recording with contemporaries and younger players alike. His influence on virtually every tenor saxophonist who followed, from Lester Young (whose contrasting lighter style defined the other pole) onward, is foundational to jazz history.
