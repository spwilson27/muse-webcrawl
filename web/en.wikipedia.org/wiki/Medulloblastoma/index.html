Medulloblastoma

Medulloblastoma
SpecialtyNeuro-oncology, neurosurgery
Usual onsetChildhood (peak 3–8 years)
LocationCerebellum / fourth ventricle
ClassificationWHO grade IV embryonal tumour

Medulloblastoma is a common type of primary brain cancer in children. It originates in the part of the brain that is towards the back and bottom, on the floor of the skull, in the cerebellum, or posterior fossa. It is an invasive, rapidly growing tumour that, unlike most brain tumours, spreads through the cerebrospinal fluid and frequently metastasizes to different locations along the surface of the brain and spinal cord.

Medulloblastoma is the most common malignant brain tumour of childhood, accounting for roughly 20% of paediatric brain tumours. It also occurs in adults, where it is much less common. Tumours typically arise in the cerebellar vermis in children and in the cerebellar hemispheres in adults.

Molecular subgroups

Modern classification divides medulloblastoma into four principal molecular subgroups: WNT-activated, SHH-activated, Group 3, and Group 4. These subgroups differ in their genetics, demographics, and prognosis — WNT tumours have an excellent prognosis, while Group 3 tumours, often with MYC amplification, fare worst. The World Health Organization classification of CNS tumours incorporates these molecular categories.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms result mainly from raised intracranial pressure due to blockage of the fourth ventricle and from cerebellar dysfunction: morning headaches, nausea and vomiting, unsteady gait, clumsiness, and visual disturbances such as double vision.

Treatment

Treatment generally combines maximal safe surgical resection, craniospinal irradiation (in children old enough to receive it), and chemotherapy. Risk stratification by residual tumour, metastatic spread, and molecular subgroup guides the intensity of therapy. Long-term survival has improved substantially, though survivors may face significant late effects from treatment.
