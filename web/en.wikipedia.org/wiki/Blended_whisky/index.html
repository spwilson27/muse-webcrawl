Blended whisky
TypeWhisky
ComponentsMalt whisky and grain whisky
Famous examplesJohnnie Walker, Chivas Regal, Ballantine's, Dewar's

Blended whisky (or blended whiskey) is the product of blending different types of whiskies and sometimes also neutral grain spirits, colourings, and flavourings. It is generally the product of mixing one or more higher-quality straight or single malt whiskies with less expensive spirits and other ingredients, allowing for a more consistent, less costly product.

Blended Scotch whisky

Blended Scotch whisky constitutes the great majority of Scotch sold worldwide. It is a blend of one or more single malt Scotch whiskies with one or more single grain Scotch whiskies, all distilled and matured in Scotland; by law the blend must be aged in oak casks for at least three years. Pioneers such as Andrew Usher in the 1860s created the first successful blends, and brands built by John Walker, James Chivas and George Ballantine made blended Scotch a global commodity.

Other traditions

American blended whiskey may contain a high proportion of neutral spirits. Canadian whisky is inherently a blended style, and Japanese whisky includes highly regarded blends such as those of Nikka and Suntory. Irish blends combine pot still and grain whiskey.

The blender's craft

The master blender selects from dozens of component whiskies to maintain a house style year after year despite variation in individual casks. Premium blends emphasise a high malt content and older components, and may carry age statements reflecting the youngest whisky in the blend.
