Glass-to-metal seal

A glass-to-metal seal is a hermetic bond between glass and metal used to pass electrical conductors through the envelope of a vacuum tube, lamp, semiconductor package, or other sealed device while preventing the passage of gas or moisture.

Types

In a matched seal, the glass and metal have nearly equal coefficients of thermal expansion, so the bond remains sound across temperature changes; alloys such as Kovar are matched to borosilicate glass. In a compression seal, a metal housing with higher expansion contracts around the glass on cooling, holding it in mechanical compression, in which glass is strong.

Requirements

A good seal requires the glass to wet the metal (usually via a controlled oxide layer on the metal surface) and the joint to withstand thermal cycling and pressure differences. Applications include feedthroughs in refrigeration compressors, hermetic transistor and diode packages, and vacuum electronics.
