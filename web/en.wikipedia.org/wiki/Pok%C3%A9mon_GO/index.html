Pokémon Go


Catching a Pokémon in AR mode

Pokémon Go (stylized as Pokémon GO) is a 2016 augmented reality (AR) mobile game, part of the Pokémon franchise, developed and published by Niantic in collaboration with Nintendo and The Pokémon Company for iOS and Android devices. It uses mobile devices with GPS to locate, capture, train, and battle virtual creatures, called Pokémon, which appear as if they are in the player's real-world location.

Gameplay

Players create an avatar and walk around the real world; Pokémon appear on a map of the player's surroundings and can be caught by throwing Poké Balls on the touch screen, optionally viewing the creature through the phone's camera in AR mode. PokéStops at real-world landmarks dispense items, and Gyms allow team-based battles. The game launched with the original 151 Pokémon and has added species from later generations, raids, trading, and player-versus-player battles through regular updates and live events.

Reception and impact

Pokémon Go was released in July 2016 to mixed reviews but became a global phenomenon, surpassing 500 million downloads by the end of 2016 and popularizing location-based and AR gaming. It was credited with increasing physical activity among players, boosting local businesses near PokéStops, and causing various controversies and incidents involving trespassing and distracted walking and driving. The game has grossed billions of dollars in revenue and remains one of the most successful mobile games ever.
