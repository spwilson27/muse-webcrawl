Ziranmen (simplified Chinese: 自然门; traditional Chinese: 自然門; pinyin: Zìránmén; literally "Natural Gate" or "School of Spontaneity") is a Chinese martial art that traces its lineage to the Sichuan region in the late 19th century. It is associated with the mysterious master Xu Aishi, whose student Du Xinwu (1869–1953) became its most famous exponent; Du in turn taught Wan Laisheng, who did much to popularise the style.

Characteristics

Ziranmen emphasises natural, relaxed movement, rapid changes of direction, and evasive footwork (bu fa) combined with close-range striking, palm techniques, and low kicks. Rather than fixed forms as the centrepiece of training, practitioners build skill through repetitive stepping drills, circle walking-like exercises, heavy qigong and conditioning methods — famously including striking sandbags and practising with iron rings on the arms in related northern Shaolin-derived curricula. The guiding ideal, drawn from Daoist thought, is action that appears effortless and unforced: ziran, "self-so", or spontaneity.

Lineage

Du Xinwu served as bodyguard to Sun Yat-sen and was celebrated as a hero of the late Qing and early Republic for his fighting ability. Wan Laisheng, a famous martial arts author and champion of the 1928 national examinations, systematised Ziranmen alongside his studies of Shaolin, Wudang, and other arts, and taught widely in southern China.
