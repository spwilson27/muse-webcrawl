German diaspora

German emigration destinations worldwide

The German diaspora (German: Deutschstämmige) consists of ethnic Germans and their descendants who live and have lived outside German-speaking Central Europe, whether through historic settlement in Eastern Europe or through emigration overseas in the 19th and 20th centuries. Estimates of the German diaspora worldwide range from about 12 million to over 30 million people of full or partial German ancestry, depending on the criteria used.

History

From the Middle Ages, German settlers moved eastward in the Ostsiedlung, founding communities across East Prussia, Silesia, Transylvania and the Volga region of Russia, where Volga Germans settled at the invitation of Catherine the Great. Large-scale overseas emigration began in the 18th century and peaked in the 19th, driven by economic hardship, political unrest after the revolutions of 1848–49, and the pull of land in the Americas.

Distribution

The largest population of German ancestry is in the United States, where German Americans form the largest self-reported ancestry group. Significant communities also exist in Brazil (especially in the southern states of Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul), Argentina, Canada, Australia, South Africa and Namibia — a former German colony. In Europe, historic German minorities lived in Poland, the Czech lands, Hungary, Romania and the former Soviet Union, many of whom were expelled westward after the Second World War.

Expulsion and return

Between 1944 and 1950 some 12–14 million ethnic Germans fled or were expelled from Eastern Europe. Later, ethnic Germans from the former Soviet Union (Aussiedler and Spätaussiedler) migrated to the Federal Republic of Germany in large numbers under Germany's right-of-return provisions.
