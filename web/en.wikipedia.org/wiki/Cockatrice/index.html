A cockatrice is a mythical beast, essentially a two-legged dragon or serpent-like creature with a rooster's head. Described by Laurence Breiner as "an ornament in the drama and poetry of the Elizabethans", it was featured prominently in English thought and myth for centuries.

Legend

The cockatrice was said to be hatched from an egg laid by a rooster and incubated by a toad or serpent. Its gaze or breath was reputedly lethal, able to kill or turn victims to stone, similar to the basilisk, with which it is often conflated. According to legend, a cockatrice could be killed by hearing a rooster crow, or by looking at itself in a mirror.

In heraldry

The cockatrice appears in heraldry, though it is rarer than the basilisk. It is depicted with a dragon's body, wings, and a cockerel's head and legs.

In popular culture

Cockatrices appear in numerous works of fantasy literature, role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons, and video games, usually retaining their petrifying gaze.
