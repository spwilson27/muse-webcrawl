Dae Joyeong (대조영, 大祚榮; died 719), posthumously known as King Go of Balhae, was the founder of the kingdom of Balhae in Manchuria and the northern Korean Peninsula. A former general of Goguryeo, he led Goguryeo and Mohe refugees to found a new state after Goguryeo's fall to Tang China and Silla in 668.

Founding of Balhae

After Goguryeo's collapse, Dae Joyeong's father Dae Jung-sang and the Khitan rebel Li Jinzhong rose against Tang in 696. When the Khitan revolt was crushed, Dae Joyeong led the remnants eastward. In 698 he defeated a pursuing Tang army at the Battle of Tianmenling (Cheonmunryeong), a victory that secured his independence. He established his state at Dongmou Mountain, initially calling it Zhen (Jin); in 713 the Tang court formally invested him as king of Balhae (Bohai), the name by which the kingdom is known.

Reign

Dae Joyeong expanded Balhae's territory over the Mohe tribes of Manchuria and consolidated control of former Goguryeo lands, balancing diplomacy with Tang while resisting subordination. Balhae grew into a prosperous state trading with Tang, Silla and Japan. He died in 719 and was succeeded by his son Dae Muye, under whom Balhae reached further prominence. Korean historical tradition remembers Dae Joyeong as a national hero; he is also the subject of the popular 2006–2007 KBS television drama Dae Jo-yeong.
