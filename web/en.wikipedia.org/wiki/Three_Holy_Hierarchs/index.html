Three Holy Hierarchs

The Three Holy Hierarchs are Basil the Great, Gregory the Theologian (Gregory of Nazianzus) and John Chrysostom, three fourth-century bishops and theologians who shaped Eastern Christianity. They are venerated together in the Eastern Orthodox Church on the feast of the Three Hierarchs, 30 January.

Background

Basil, bishop of Caesarea, organised monastic life and defended Nicene orthodoxy; his friend Gregory served briefly as archbishop of Constantinople and is famed for his theological orations; Chrysostom, also archbishop of Constantinople, was renowned for his preaching and wrote extensively on the liturgy and ethics. Together they exemplify the Cappadocian synthesis of Hellenic learning and Christian theology.

Origin of the feast

In eleventh-century Constantinople, disputes arose among the faithful over which of the three was greatest. According to tradition, the hierarchs appeared together in a vision to Bishop John Mauropous of Euchaita, declaring that they were equal before God, and a common feast was instituted to end the rivalry.

Patronage of education

The Three Hierarchs are regarded in Greece and the Orthodox world as patrons of learning and letters. Their feast day, 30 January, is celebrated in Greece as a school holiday honouring education and the teaching profession.
