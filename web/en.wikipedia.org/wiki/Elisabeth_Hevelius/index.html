Elisabeth Hevelius

Born: January 17, 1647, Gdańsk, Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth
Died: December 22, 1693 (aged 46), Gdańsk
Fields: Astronomy

Elisabeth Catherina Koopmann Hevelius (Polish: Elżbieta Heweliusz; January 17, 1647 – December 22, 1693) was a Polish–Lithuanian astronomer from Gdańsk (Danzig). She was the wife and collaborator of the astronomer Johannes Hevelius, and she is considered one of the first female astronomers; she is sometimes called the "mother of lunar charting."

Life and work

Elisabeth was born into the wealthy Koopmann merchant family. Fascinated by astronomy from a young age, she married Johannes Hevelius in 1663, when she was 16 and he was 52. She learned Latin and worked alongside her husband in their observatory, measuring star positions with sextants and quadrants.

After Hevelius's death in 1687, she completed and published his works, including the Prodromus Astronomiae (1690), which contained a catalogue of 1,564 stars and the Firmamentum Sobiescianum, an atlas of 56 star charts. The lunar crater Hevelius and asteroid 12625 Koopman are named in honor of the couple and her family name respectively.
