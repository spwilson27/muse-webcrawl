Entrepôt

Entrepôt
TypePort / trading post
FunctionImport, storage and re-export of goods
Classic examplesSingapore, Hong Kong, Rotterdam, Dubai

An entrepôt (French for "warehouse") is a port, city or trading post where merchandise may be imported, stored and re-exported, often without paying import duties — a practice known as entrepôt trade. Such hubs flourish where major trade routes intersect and where customs regimes or free-port status encourage transshipment.

History

Entrepôts were central to early modern commerce: Malacca, Venice and later Amsterdam acted as staple ports where goods from distant producers were exchanged and redistributed. In the 19th century Singapore and Hong Kong grew rich as free-port entrepôts linking China, India and Europe.

Modern entrepôts

Today the largest transshipment hubs — Singapore, Rotterdam, Busan, Jebel Ali (Dubai) — handle vast container flows, much of it merely transferring between vessels. Free-trade zones and bonded warehouses are the legal descendants of the classic entrepôt regime.
