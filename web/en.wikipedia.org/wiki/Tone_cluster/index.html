Tone cluster

A tone cluster is a musical chord comprising at least three adjacent tones in a scale. Typically, the term is used for a chord made up of adjacent notes separated by semitones or whole tones, producing a dense, dissonant sound.

Clusters are characteristic of 20th-century music. The American composer Henry Cowell pioneered their use on the piano — struck with the fist, palm, or forearm — and gave them the name "tone clusters" in his New Musical Resources. Charles Ives had used similar effects earlier, and composers such as Béla Bartók, György Ligeti, and Iannis Xenakis later made extensive use of clusters in orchestral and choral writing.

Notation and technique

Clusters are often notated with a thick black bar or a block symbol indicating the range of notes rather than each pitch. On the piano they can be diatonic (white keys), chromatic (black and white), or pentatonic, and their effect ranges from a muffled rumble to a shimmering, bell-like resonance.
