Lead zirconate titanate

Lead zirconate titanate (PZT)
FormulaPb[ZrxTi1−x]O3, 0 ≤ x ≤ 1
StructurePerovskite ceramic
PropertiesStrongly piezoelectric, ferroelectric
Common gradesPZT-4, PZT-5A, PZT-5H, PZT-8

Lead zirconate titanate, usually called PZT, is an inorganic ceramic perovskite material — a solid solution of lead zirconate (PbZrO3) and lead titanate (PbTiO3) — that shows a marked piezoelectric effect. Developed in the 1950s, it is by far the most widely used piezoelectric ceramic in industry.

Properties

PZT's piezoelectric coefficients peak near the morphotropic phase boundary at about 52% zirconate, where its crystal structure sits between tetragonal and rhombohedral phases. After sintering, the ceramic is poled — a strong electric field applied at elevated temperature — to align its ferroelectric domains. Commercial grades divide into "hard" PZTs (high power, low loss, e.g. PZT-4, PZT-8) and "soft" PZTs (high sensitivity, e.g. PZT-5A, PZT-5H), tuned by dopants.

Applications

PZT is used in ultrasound transducers for medical imaging and sonar, piezoelectric buzzers and speakers, gas igniters, inkjet print heads, precision actuators and nanopositioners, accelerometers, fuel injectors, and energy-harvesting devices. Its lead content is exempted under RoHS rules for many uses, but lead-free alternatives such as potassium sodium niobate (KNN) and bismuth sodium titanate (BNT) are actively researched.
