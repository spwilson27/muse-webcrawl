Porterhouse steak

Type: Beef steak
Cut from: Short loin
Contains: Strip steak and tenderloin

The porterhouse is a cut of beef steak taken from the rear end of the short loin. It consists of a T-shaped lumbar vertebra with sections of the strip steak (New York strip) on one side of the bone and the tenderloin (filet mignon) on the other. It is very similar to the T-bone steak, but is cut thicker and contains a larger portion of tenderloin.

Porterhouse vs. T-bone

Both cuts come from the short loin and have the same T-bone structure. Under United States Department of Agriculture regulations, a steak may be sold as a porterhouse only if the tenderloin portion is at least 1.25 inches (32 mm) wide at its widest point; a T-bone requires only 0.5 inches (13 mm). Because the porterhouse is cut from further back on the loin, it has more tenderloin and is generally larger, often served as a shared steak for two.

Name

The name derives from the porter houses (ale houses and chophouses) of 19th-century America, where porter ale was served and such steaks became a speciality. A popular account credits a New York City porter house keeper, Zachariah B. Porter or Martin Morrison, with popularising the cut in the early 1800s.

Florentine steak

The Italian bistecca alla fiorentina is a related cut from Chianina cattle, traditionally cooked rare over a wood fire and served very thick. In Italy, a ban on the sale of bone-in beef during the BSE crisis in the early 2000s temporarily made the traditional preparation illegal.
