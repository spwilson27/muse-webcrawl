The Sukhoi Su-30 (Russian: Сухой Су-30; NATO reporting name: Flanker-C/G/H) is a twin-engine, two-seat supermaneuverable fighter aircraft developed in the Soviet Union by Russia's Sukhoi Aviation Corporation. It is a multirole fighter for all-weather, air-to-air and air interdiction missions. The Su-30 started as an internal development project in the Sukhoi Su-27 family by Sukhoi as a dedicated interceptor and long-range strike aircraft; the two-seat design was intended to manage pilot workload on long missions.

Development and variants

The type first flew in 1989 as the Su-27PU; it entered service with the Russian Air Force in 1996. The type was developed in two main production branches: the Komsomolsk-on-Amur (KnAAPO) line, which produced the Su-30MKK and Su-30MK2 for China and other customers, and the Irkutsk (IAPO) line, which developed the Su-30MKI for India with thrust-vectoring engines, canards, and international avionics. The MKI led in turn to the Su-30MKM for Malaysia and Su-30MKA for Algeria, and to Russia's own Su-30SM, widely used by the Russian Aerospace Forces and offered for export as the Su-30SME. India's Su-30MKI fleet, licence-built by Hindustan Aeronautics Limited, numbers over 270 aircraft, making it one of the largest Flanker variants.

Design

The Su-30 retains the Su-27's aerodynamic layout with a blended wing-body, twin widely spaced engines, and twin tails, adding a second crew station, an in-flight refuelling probe, and strengthened structure for greater take-off weight. It carries a 30 mm GSh-30-1 cannon and up to about 8,000 kg of weapons on 12 hardpoints, including R-27 and R-77 air-to-air missiles and a variety of precision-guided and unguided air-to-surface weapons depending on variant. Operational range is about 3,000 km unrefuelled, extendable by aerial refuelling.

Operators

Operators include Russia, India, China, Vietnam, Venezuela, Algeria, Malaysia, Indonesia, Kazakhstan, Belarus, Armenia, Uganda, Ethiopia, Myanmar, and Angola.
