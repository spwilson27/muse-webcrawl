Diplodocus

KingdomAnimalia
OrderSaurischia
FamilyDiplodocidae
GenusDiplodocus Marsh, 1878
Lived154 – 152 million years ago (Late Jurassic)

Diplodocus is a genus of diplodocid sauropod dinosaur that lived in what is now North America during the Late Jurassic period, about 154 to 152 million years ago. Its fossils were first discovered in 1877 and named by Othniel Charles Marsh in 1878. The name means "double beam", referring to the paired chevron bones beneath its tail.

Description

Diplodocus was a very large, long-necked herbivore, reaching around 24–26 metres (79–85 ft) in length. It had a long, whip-like tail, a relatively small head with peg-like teeth suited to stripping vegetation, and four sturdy legs, the hind legs slightly longer than the front. Its neck vertebrae bore forked neural spines that supported strong ligaments.

Discovery and species

Several species have been named, with Diplodocus carnegii the best known thanks to a nearly complete skeleton found in Wyoming in 1899. Andrew Carnegie funded plaster casts of this skeleton, nicknamed "Dippy", which were donated to museums around the world and became among the most famous dinosaur displays.

Palaeobiology

Diplodocus lived in the semi-arid floodplains of the Morrison Formation alongside dinosaurs such as Stegosaurus, Allosaurus and Apatosaurus. It probably fed on low- to mid-level vegetation, sweeping its neck from side to side.
