Breakup of Yugoslavia

Breakup of Yugoslavia
Date: 1990–1992 (wars to 2001)
Successor states: Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Kosovo (partially recognised)

The breakup of Yugoslavia was the dissolution of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia in the early 1990s, as its six republics seceded amid rising nationalism, economic crisis and the collapse of communism in Europe, triggering the Yugoslav Wars.

Causes

After Tito's death in 1980, debt, inflation, constitutional gridlock in the 1974 system, and nationalist mobilisation — notably Slobodan Milošević's rise in Serbia and the weakening of federal authority — undermined the federation. The fall of the Berlin Wall removed its strategic Cold War rationale.

Dissolution and wars

Slovenia and Croatia declared independence in June 1991, followed by Macedonia and Bosnia and Herzegovina in 1992. Wars followed: the Ten-Day War in Slovenia, the Croatian War of Independence (1991–95), the Bosnian War (1992–95, ending with the Dayton Agreement after atrocities including the Srebrenica genocide), and the Kosovo War (1998–99) ending with NATO intervention. Serbia and Montenegro remained as the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia until peacefully separating in 2006; Kosovo declared independence in 2008.

Legacy

The wars killed roughly 130,000–140,000 people, displaced millions, and led to the ICTY war-crimes tribunal in The Hague.
