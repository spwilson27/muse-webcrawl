2016 Canadian census

The 2016 Canadian census was a detailed enumeration of the Canadian population with a reference date of 10 May 2016. It was conducted by Statistics Canada and counted a total population of 35,151,728, an increase of 5.0 percent over the 2011 census. The census marked the return of the mandatory long-form questionnaire, replacing the voluntary National Household Survey used in 2011.

Country: Canada

Background

In 2010 the federal government had replaced the mandatory long-form census with a voluntary survey, a decision criticised for reducing data quality. After the 2015 federal election, the new Liberal government of Justin Trudeau reinstated the mandatory long form for 2016. Response rates were high: the overall collection response rate reached about 98.4 percent, and 68.3 percent of households completed the questionnaire online.

Findings

Ontario remained the most populous province with 13.4 million people, followed by Quebec (8.2 million) and British Columbia (4.6 million). The fastest-growing province was Alberta, up 11.6 percent. The census also released detailed releases on language, immigration, Indigenous peoples, income, housing, education and labour. Population growth was driven mainly by immigration, which accounted for roughly two-thirds of the increase.
