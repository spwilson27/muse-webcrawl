Sir Roger Harry Daltrey (born 1 March 1944) is an English singer and actor best known as the lead vocalist and a founding member of the rock band the Who. Famous for his powerful voice and for swinging his microphone by its lead as a stage trademark, he fronted the band through classics including "My Generation", "Pinball Wizard" and "Won't Get Fooled Again".

Early life

Daltrey was born in Hammersmith, London, during the Second World War and grew up in Acton. Expelled from Acton County Grammar School, he worked as a sheet-metal worker while building his own first guitar. In 1959 he formed the skiffle group the Detours, which — recruiting schoolmates Pete Townshend and John Entwistle, and then drummer Keith Moon — became the Who in 1964.

Career with the Who

Initially the band's leader, Daltrey ceded creative control to guitarist-songwriter Pete Townshend as the group's ambition grew, becoming the interpreter and voice of Townshend's writing, including the rock operas Tommy (1969) and Quadrophenia (1973). In Ken Russell's 1975 film of Tommy Daltrey played the title role, launching a parallel acting career that included Lisztomania (1975) and McVicar (1980).

Solo work and charity

Daltrey's solo career began with the album Daltrey (1973), featuring Leo Sayer's songwriting and the hit "Giving It All Away"; later albums include Ride a Rock Horse (1975) and Under a Raging Moon (1985). His 2018 album As Long as I Have You returned him to the UK top ten. Since 2000 he has been a leading patron of the Teenage Cancer Trust, organising its annual Royal Albert Hall benefit concerts. He published his memoir, Thanks a Lot Mr Kibblewhite, in 2018, and was knighted in 2025 for services to music and charity.
