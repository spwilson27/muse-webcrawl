Dimension stone

Dimension stone is quarried in large blocks before being sawn to size.

Dimension stone is natural stone that has been selected and trimmed or cut to specified sizes or shapes for use in building and monuments. Unlike crushed stone or aggregate, dimension stone is valued for the appearance, durability and workability of individual pieces; the quarrying operation aims to recover intact blocks with as little damage as possible.

Types

The principal dimension stones are granite, marble, limestone, sandstone, slate, travertine and quartzite. Each has characteristic colours, textures and weathering behaviour that suit it to particular uses: granite for kerbs, paving and polished cladding; marble for sculpture and interiors; slate for roofing.

Production

Blocks are separated from the quarry face by drilling, diamond-wire sawing, channel cutting or controlled splitting along natural bedding and joint planes. The blocks are transported to mills where gang saws or diamond circular saws cut them into slabs, which may then be polished, honed, flamed or bush-hammered, and finished to final dimensions.

Uses

Dimension stone is used for building facades and ashlar masonry, flooring and stair treads, countertops, paving, monuments and memorials, and kerbing. Historic buildings constructed of dimension stone define the character of many cities, and matching the original stone is a central concern of conservation work.
