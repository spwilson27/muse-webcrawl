Military working dog

Military working dog
Roles: Detection, patrol, tracking, sentry
Common breeds: German Shepherd, Belgian Malinois, Labrador Retriever

A military working dog (MWD) is a dog trained for use in military and law-enforcement work, in roles such as explosives and narcotics detection, tracking, patrol and sentry duty, guarding installations, and search and rescue. Dogs have served alongside soldiers since antiquity, but modern military dog programmes were formalised during the First and Second World Wars.

Roles and training

Detection dogs are trained to find explosives, mines or drugs by scent — a capability that remains unmatched by machines for many field conditions. Patrol dogs are trained to bite and hold on command and to protect their handler. Dogs work with a single handler with whom they form a close bond; training in the US programme is centred at Lackland Air Force Base, Texas. Favoured breeds include the German Shepherd and Belgian Malinois for patrol work and Labrador Retrievers and spaniels for detection.

Recognition

Famous military dogs include Sergeant Stubby of the First World War, Chips of the Second World War, and Cairo, the Belgian Malinois who accompanied US Navy SEALs in the 2011 raid that killed Osama bin Laden. In Britain, exceptional animals can receive the Dickin Medal, regarded as the animals' Victoria Cross. Modern welfare rules increasingly require that retired working dogs be offered adoption, often to their former handlers.
