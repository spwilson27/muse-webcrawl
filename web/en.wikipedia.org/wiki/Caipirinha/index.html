Caipirinha

The caipirinha is Brazil's national cocktail, made with cachaça (sugarcane spirit), lime, and sugar, served over ice. Prepared by muddling lime wedges with sugar and topping with cachaça and crushed ice, it is one of the most widely known Brazilian drinks worldwide and is listed among the IBA official cocktails.

History

The drink's origins are debated: one account traces it to 19th-century São Paulo state, where cachaça with lime was a folk remedy; another links its popularity to the 1918 flu pandemic, when lime, honey, and spirit mixtures were taken medicinally. The name is a diminutive of caipira, a term for country folk.

Variations

Replacing cachaça with vodka gives the caipiroska; with rum, the caipirissima. Fruits such as passion fruit, strawberry, or kiwi are commonly muddled in as variations in Brazil.
