New Zealand cuisine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pavlova topped with kiwifruit — an emblematic New Zealand dish.

New Zealand cuisine is driven largely by local produce and seasonal variation. It developed from the food traditions of Māori, the indigenous people of New Zealand, and of British settlers, and has since been reshaped by immigration from Europe, Asia, and the Pacific Islands. Similarity to British and Australian cuisine is strong in the country's traditional dishes, but contemporary New Zealand cooking is known for fresh seafood, lamb, and fusion influences.

Māori food traditions

Māori brought Polynesian staple crops — kūmara (sweet potato), taro, and yams — and made use of native birds, fish, shellfish, and plants such as the root of the bracken fern. The most distinctive Māori cooking method is the hāngī, an earth oven in which meat and vegetables are slow-cooked on heated stones in a pit covered with earth, producing a smoky, steamed result. Traditional foods also include seafood such as pāua (abalone), kina (sea urchin), and whitebait, and dishes made from native birds are now largely prohibited as those species are protected.

Settler and modern food

British settlement introduced meat pies, roast lamb, fish and chips, and baking traditions such as ANZAC biscuits, Afghans, and the pavlova — the meringue dessert whose origin is disputed between New Zealand and Australia. Meat, especially lamb and beef from the country's pastoral farms, and dairy products are central to the traditional diet, and the barbecue is a fixture of summer social life.

From the late 20th century, immigration and a thriving café culture transformed urban food. Flat white coffee, claimed by both Wellington and Sydney, became a national symbol. New Zealand's wine industry — especially Marlborough Sauvignon blanc, Central Otago Pinot noir, and Hawke's Bay reds — gained international prominence, and chefs developed a Pacific Rim style combining local seafood and produce with Asian and Mediterranean techniques. Distinctively local products include mānuka honey, feijoas, golden kiwifruit (developed in New Zealand from the Chinese gooseberry), and L&P (Lemon & Paeroa) soft drink.
