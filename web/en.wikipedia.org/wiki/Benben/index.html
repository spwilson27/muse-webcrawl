Benben

In ancient Egyptian religion, the benben was the mound that arose from the primordial waters (Nu) and on which the creator god Atum settled. It is one of the central images of the creation mythology developed at Heliopolis. The word "benben" is thought to derive from the verb weben, "to rise" or "to shine", and is also associated with the benu bird — the Egyptian phoenix, herald of creation and of the rising sun.

Mythology

According to the Heliopolitan creation account preserved in the Pyramid Texts, before creation there existed only the dark, formless waters of Nu. The benben, the first dry land — a primordial hill — emerged from the waters, and upon it Atum came into being and began the creation of the other gods. The benben thus symbolised the first sunrise, the emergence of order from chaos, and renewal after the annual Nile flood, when mounds of silt emerged from the receding waters.

The Benben stone and architecture

A sacred stone called the benben was kept in the great temple of the sun at Heliopolis, in a special sanctuary sometimes called the "House of the Benben". The stone — probably conical or pyramidal in form — was believed to be the petrified seed of Atum and the point on which the first rays of the sun had fallen. It is thought to have inspired the gilded capstones (pyramidia, called benbenet) placed on pyramids and obelisks, which caught the dawn sun; the pyramid form itself is often interpreted as a monumental, stylised benben, a mound of creation that also served as a stairway for the king's ascent to the heavens.
