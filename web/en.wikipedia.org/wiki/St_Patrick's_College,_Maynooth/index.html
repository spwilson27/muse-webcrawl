St Patrick's College, Maynooth

St Patrick's College, Maynooth (Irish: Coláiste Phádraig, Má Nuad) is the national seminary of Ireland and a pontifical university, located in the town of Maynooth, County Kildare. Founded in 1795, it was for much of its history the largest seminary in the world, and it shares its campus with Maynooth University, from which it is legally distinct.

St Joseph's Square and the College Chapel, Maynooth

History

The college was established by an Act of the Irish Parliament in 1795, at a time when the Penal Laws were being relaxed and the British government wished to prevent Irish Catholic clergy from being educated in revolutionary France. For its first century and a half, Maynooth trained most of Ireland's diocesan priests, with ordinations commonly numbering in the hundreds each year at its peak. In 1871 it lost its government grant following disestablishment of the Church of Ireland and became fully independent.

Campus and chapel

The campus is noted for its Gothic Revival architecture, much of it designed by Augustus Welby Northmore Pugin and his followers. The College Chapel, completed in the 1890s, is one of the largest choir-stalled chapels in the world, with over 450 stalls. St Joseph's Square is surrounded by the college's principal residential and teaching buildings.

Present day

Following a sharp decline in vocations from the late 20th century, the number of seminarians fell dramatically, though the college continues to train seminarians for Irish dioceses and offers degrees in theology and philosophy through its pontifical university status. Its library holds important collections of Irish historical and theological material.
