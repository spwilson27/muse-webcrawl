Keshav Baliram Hedgewar (1 April 1889 – 21 June 1940), also known by his moniker Doctorji, was an Indian physician and Hindu nationalist activist who founded the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) in Nagpur in 1925, serving as its first chief (Sarsanghchalak) until his death.

Early life and activism

Hedgewar was born in Nagpur into a Telugu-speaking Brahmin family. Orphaned by plague as a boy, he studied medicine at the Calcutta Medical College, where he joined the revolutionary Anushilan Samiti and was influenced by V. D. Savarkar's writings. Returning to Nagpur, he participated in the Indian National Congress's Non-cooperation Movement and was jailed in 1921, but grew disillusioned with the Congress and with communal violence of the period.

Founding of the RSS

On Vijayadashami day, 27 September 1925, Hedgewar founded the RSS as a volunteer organisation to train Hindus in character, discipline and physical culture through daily local branches (shakhas), with the aim of uniting Hindu society. He deliberately kept the RSS out of direct participation in the independence struggle's political campaigns, focusing instead on cadre-building; notably, he nevertheless joined the 1930 civil disobedience (forest satyagraha) in a personal capacity. Under his leadership the RSS spread beyond Maharashtra, sending full-time organisers (pracharaks) across India.

Death and legacy

Hedgewar's health declined during a 1940 tour, and he died in Nagpur on 21 June 1940. He was succeeded by M. S. Golwalkar. The RSS, today one of the world's largest volunteer organisations and the parent body of the Sangh Parivar, reveres him as its founder; his memorial, the Smruti Mandir, stands at the RSS headquarters' Reshimbagh grounds in Nagpur.
