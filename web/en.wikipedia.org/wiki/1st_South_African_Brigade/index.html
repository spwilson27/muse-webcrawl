The 1st South African Brigade was an infantry brigade of the Union of South Africa that served in both world wars. In the First World War it formed the core of the South African Overseas Expeditionary Force on the Western Front and in Egypt.

First World War

Raised in 1915 under Brigadier-General Henry Lukin, the brigade first fought in Egypt, defeating a Senussi force at the Battle of Agagia in February 1916. Transferred to France, it was attached to the 9th (Scottish) Division and became famous for its defence of Delville Wood during the Battle of the Somme in July 1916, where it held the wood for five days at the cost of most of its strength. The brigade later fought at Arras, Ypres, and in the German spring offensives of 1918.

Second World War

Re-formed in 1940, the 1st South African Brigade served with the 1st South African Division in East Africa and in the North African campaign, including the fighting around Sidi Rezegh during Operation Crusader in November 1941, where it suffered heavy casualties.
