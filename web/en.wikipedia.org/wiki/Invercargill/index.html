Invercargill (Māori: Waihōpai) is the southernmost and westernmost city in New Zealand, and one of the southernmost cities in the world. It is the commercial centre of the Southland region. The city lies in the heart of the wide expanse of the Southland Plains on the Oreti or New River some 18 km north of Bluff, which is the southernmost town in the South Island. It sits amid rich farmland that is bordered by large areas of conservation land and marine reserves, including Fiordland National Park covering the south-west corner of the South Island and the Catlins coastal region.

History

The area was first settled by Māori, and the settlement of Waihōpai grew near the confluence of the Waihopai and Oreti rivers. European settlement began in the 1850s, and the town was named by Governor Sir Thomas Gore Browne: "Inver" from the Scottish Gaelic word inbhir meaning a river's mouth, and "Cargill" in honour of Captain William Cargill, who at the time was the superintendent of Otago, of which Southland was then a part. Invercargill was proclaimed a borough in 1871 and a city in 1930.

Geography and climate

Invercargill is situated on flat land near the southern coast of the South Island. Its climate is temperate oceanic, with cool summers and mild winters for its latitude, influenced by its proximity to the ocean. The city experiences changeable weather and relatively high rainfall spread throughout the year.

Economy

The economy is based largely on agriculture and related processing industries, including meat works, dairying and the Tiwai Point aluminium smelter near Bluff, one of the region's largest employers. The Southern Institute of Technology, known for its "Zero Fees" scheme, is a significant institution in the city.

Transport

Invercargill is the southern terminus of State Highway 1 and of the Main South Line railway. Invercargill Airport serves the city with domestic flights. The port of Bluff handles exports from the region, including aluminium, timber and agricultural products.

Notable people

The city is the birthplace of motorcycle racer and land-speed record holder Burt Munro, whose story was told in the film The World's Fastest Indian. Other notable residents have included author Dan Davin and cricketer Jeff Wilson.
