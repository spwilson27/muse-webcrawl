Metafiction

Metafiction is a form of fiction that self-consciously addresses the devices of fiction itself, drawing attention to its status as an artefact and posing questions about the relationship between fiction and reality.

History

The term was coined by William H. Gass in 1970. Precursors include Cervantes' Don Quixote, Sterne's Tristram Shandy, and Diderot. It flourished in postmodern literature.

Techniques

Unreliable narrators addressing readers, stories within stories, footnotes, characters aware they are fictional, and authors appearing as characters. Examples: John Fowles' The French Lieutenant's Woman, Italo Calvino's If on a winter's night a traveler, Vladimir Nabokov's Pale Fire.
