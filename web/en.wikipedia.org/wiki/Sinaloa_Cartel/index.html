The Sinaloa Cartel (Spanish: Cártel de Sinaloa), also known as the CDS or the Pacific Cartel, is a large international organised crime syndicate based in the city of Culiacán, in the Mexican state of Sinaloa. It emerged from the fragmentation of the Guadalajara Cartel in the late 1980s and became one of the most powerful drug trafficking organisations in the world, supplying cocaine, heroin, methamphetamine, marijuana and, later, fentanyl to markets principally in the United States.

History

After the 1985 murder of DEA agent Enrique "Kiki" Camarena and the subsequent arrest of Guadalajara Cartel leader Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo in 1989, the organisation split into regional factions. The Sinaloa faction, led by figures including Joaquín "El Chapo" Guzmán and Ismael "El Mayo" Zambada, consolidated control of smuggling corridors along Mexico's Pacific coast and at border crossings such as Tijuana and Ciudad Juárez, fighting rival groups including the Tijuana and Juárez cartels. The cartel became notorious for its sophistication, including the use of tunnels, semi-submersibles, and corrupt official networks.

El Chapo and fragmentation

Guzmán escaped from Mexican prisons in 2001 and again in 2015 before being recaptured in 2016 and extradited to the United States in 2017; he was convicted in New York in 2019 and sentenced to life imprisonment. His arrest intensified internal struggles between factions associated with his sons (known as "Los Chapitos") and with Zambada. Zambada was arrested in the United States in July 2024 after arriving on a flight to Texas, an event followed by serious factional violence in Sinaloa.

Impact

United States authorities have repeatedly described the Sinaloa Cartel as one of the largest and most powerful drug trafficking organisations globally, and in 2025 the United States designated it a Foreign Terrorist Organization. Its activities have been a central factor in the Mexican drug war and in the North American opioid epidemic.
