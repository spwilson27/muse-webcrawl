Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum

The Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum (Chinese: 中山陵; pinyin: Zhōngshān Líng) is the tomb of Sun Yat-sen, the founding father of the Republic of China, located at the foot of Purple Mountain in Nanjing, Jiangsu. Designed by architect Lü Yanzhi in a blend of traditional Chinese imperial tomb forms and modern Western construction, it was built between 1926 and 1929, and Sun's remains were interred there on 1 June 1929. The mausoleum is one of China's most visited patriotic sites and a national cultural heritage monument.

Location: Purple Mountain, Nanjing, China
Architect: Lü Yanzhi
Built: 1926–1929

Design and layout

The mausoleum complex covers about 80,000 square metres on a mountainside, arranged along a central axis shaped like a bell — said to symbolise a wake-up call to the Chinese people. A marble memorial archway inscribed with Sun's calligraphy "博愛" (bo'ai, universal love) leads to a 480-metre ceremonial stairway of 392 steps, symbolising China's 392 million people at the time. At the summit stands the sacrificial hall, fronted by a marble statue of Sun, and behind it the circular burial chamber, where a marble effigy of Sun lies above the crypt containing his coffin.

History

Sun died in Beijing in March 1925 and his body was temporarily kept at the Temple of Azure Clouds while the mausoleum was constructed. Lü Yanzhi won the design competition but died in 1929 shortly before the interment. The site survived the Second Sino-Japanese War and the Cultural Revolution, during which it was protected by order of Zhou Enlai, and it remains a place of pilgrimage, visited by leaders from both sides of the Taiwan Strait.
