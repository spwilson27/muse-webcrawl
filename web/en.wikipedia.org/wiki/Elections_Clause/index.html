Elections Clause

Elections Clause
LocationArticle I, Section 4, Clause 1, U.S. Constitution
SubjectTimes, places and manner of elections for Senators and Representatives
Key casesSmiley v. Holm (1932); Arizona State Legislature v. Arizona Independent Redistricting Commission (2015); Moore v. Harper (2023)

The Elections Clause (also known as the Times, Places and Manner Clause) is a provision of Article I, Section 4, Clause 1 of the United States Constitution. It provides that "The Times, Places and Manner of holding Elections for Senators and Representatives, shall be prescribed in each State by the Legislature thereof; but the Congress may at any time by Law make or alter such Regulations, except as to the Places of chusing Senators."

Interpretation

The clause gives state legislatures primary authority over the mechanics of federal elections — including the drawing of congressional district lines — while reserving to Congress the power to override state rules. In Smiley v. Holm (1932), the Supreme Court held that a governor's veto applies to redistricting legislation, and in Arizona State Legislature v. Arizona Independent Redistricting Commission (2015) it upheld an independent redistricting commission created by ballot initiative.

Moore v. Harper and the independent state legislature theory

In Moore v. Harper (2023), the Court, in an opinion by Chief Justice John Roberts, rejected the broad "independent state legislature theory", holding that state courts may review state election laws under state constitutions, while noting federal courts retain a role in policing whether state courts exceed ordinary bounds of judicial review. The clause remains central to litigation over partisan gerrymandering and state election administration.
