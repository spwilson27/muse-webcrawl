Tripoli is the capital and largest city of Libya, a Mediterranean port in the northwest of the country, and one of the ancient "three cities" (Tripolis) of Tripolitania alongside Oea (itself), Sabratha and Leptis Magna.

History

Founded by Phoenicians as Oea, Tripoli passed through Roman, Vandal, Byzantine, Arab, Norman, Spanish and Ottoman rule (including the corsair era of Dragut), Italian colonisation (1911–1943), and Libyan independence. Under Muammar Gaddafi (1969–2011) it was the seat of his regime; its fall in August 2011 during the Libyan Civil War ended his rule.

Geography and landmarks

The old medina, Red Castle (Assaraya al-Hamra), Arch of Marcus Aurelius, Gurgi and Karamanli mosques, and Martyrs' Square (formerly Green Square) mark the historic centre. Since 2011 rival governments and militia conflicts have centred on the capital, seat of the internationally recognised Government of National Unity.
