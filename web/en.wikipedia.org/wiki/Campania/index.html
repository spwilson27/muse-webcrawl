Campania

Campania is an administrative region of Italy, located in the south of the country on the Tyrrhenian Sea. Its capital and largest city is Naples. With about 5.6 million inhabitants and an area of 13,590 km², it is Italy's third-most-populous region and its most densely populated. Campania was anciently called Campania felix ("happy countryside") for the fertility of its volcanic soil.

Geography

Campania stretches from the Garigliano river in the north to the Gulf of Policastro in the south. Its coast includes the Amalfi Coast, the Sorrentine Peninsula and the islands of Capri, Ischia and Procida. Inland lie the Campanian Apennines; the dominant volcanic features are Mount Vesuvius and the Phlegraean Fields caldera.

History

Settled by Italic peoples and Greek colonists (Magna Graecia) and dominated in turn by Samnites, Rome, Byzantines, Lombards, Normans, Angevins, Aragonese and Bourbons, Campania was the heart of the Kingdom of Naples and later the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies until Italian unification in 1861. The Allied landings at Salerno and the Four Days of Naples marked its Second World War history.

Culture and economy

The region is home to the archaeological sites of Pompeii and Herculaneum, the royal palace of Caserta, and the birthplace of pizza. Agriculture (San Marzano tomatoes, buffalo mozzarella), food processing, aerospace and tourism anchor the economy, while Naples' Camorra syndicates remain a persistent social problem.
