Legibility

Legibility is the ease with which a reader can recognise individual characters in a piece of text or typeface. It is distinct from readability, which concerns how easily whole passages of text can be read and understood. Legibility depends on characteristics of the letterforms themselves — such as x-height, stroke contrast, aperture, spacing, and the distinctness of similar characters like I, l, and 1.

Measurement

Legibility is studied through reading-speed tests, recognition at a distance or at threshold sizes, and error rates. Research by typographers and psychologists since the early twentieth century has shown that generous x-heights, open apertures, and moderate stroke contrast generally improve recognition, especially at small sizes and in poor viewing conditions.

Applications

Legibility research informs the design of road signage typefaces such as Highway Gothic and Clearview, cockpit and medical labelling, and user-interface fonts. See also the Flesch–Kincaid readability tests, which measure a related property of running text.
