Appomattox Court House

LocationAppomattox County, Virginia, U.S.
Known forSurrender of Robert E. Lee, 9 April 1865
Preserved asAppomattox Court House National Historical Park

Appomattox Court House is a historic village in Appomattox County, Virginia, United States. It is best known as the site where, on 9 April 1865, Confederate General Robert E. Lee surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia to Union General Ulysses S. Grant, effectively ending the American Civil War. Despite its name, the surrender took place not in the courthouse building but in the nearby home of Wilmer McLean.

The surrender

After the fall of Petersburg and Richmond, Lee's army was trapped near Appomattox. Following the Battle of Appomattox Court House on the morning of 9 April, Lee met Grant in the parlor of the McLean House and agreed to generous terms: Confederate soldiers were paroled and allowed to return home, officers kept their sidearms, and men could keep their horses for spring planting. Grant also provided rations to the starving Army of Northern Virginia.

Preservation

The village, which declined after the county seat moved in 1892, is preserved as Appomattox Court House National Historical Park, established in 1935. The McLean House was dismantled in the 1890s for a planned exhibition that never occurred, then reconstructed on its original site by the National Park Service in the 1940s.
