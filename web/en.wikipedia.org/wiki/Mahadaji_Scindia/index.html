Mahadaji Scindia

Mahadaji Scindia (1730 – 12 February 1794), also known as Mahadji Sindhia, was a Maratha statesman and ruler of Gwalior in North India. He was the youngest son of Ranoji Rao Scindia, the founder of the Scindia dynasty. Mahadaji was instrumental in resurrecting Maratha power in North India after the Third Battle of Panipat in 1761, and rose to become the de facto ruler of the Mughal Empire's affairs as regent (Vakil-ul-Mutlaq) on behalf of the emperor Shah Alam II.

Early life and Panipat

Mahadaji accompanied his brothers in the Maratha campaigns in the north. At the Third Battle of Panipat (1761), he was wounded and escaped the battlefield; the injury left him with a lifelong limp. His elder brothers were killed in the catastrophe, and in the following years he rebuilt the Scindia army and influence from his base around Gwalior and Malwa.

Restoration of Maratha power

During the 1770s Mahadaji re-established Maratha authority in northern India. In 1771 he escorted the Mughal emperor Shah Alam II back to Delhi, restoring him to the throne under Maratha protection. He subdued the Rohillas, Jats and Rajput states, and by the Treaty of Salbai (1782), which ended the First Anglo-Maratha War, the British recognised his position; he emerged from the war as one of the most powerful rulers in India and a mediator between the Peshwa and the British.

In 1784 he was appointed Vakil-ul-Mutlaq (Regent Plenipotentiary) of the Mughal Empire, making him the effective manager of imperial affairs. He employed European officers, most famously Benoît de Boigne, to train and modernise his army with disciplined infantry brigades and artillery, which made his forces among the most formidable in India.

Later campaigns and death

Mahadaji defeated Rajput coalitions at battles such as Patan (1790) and Merta, and asserted Scindia dominance over much of northern and central India. His growing power alarmed other Maratha sardars, particularly the Holkars. He died at Wanowrie near Pune on 12 February 1794 and was succeeded by his grand-nephew Daulat Rao Scindia.
