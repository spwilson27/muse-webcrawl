Video game culture is a worldwide subculture formed by video game players and those who identify with gaming as a hobby, profession, or social identity. It encompasses the customs, language, art, music, media, and social structures that have developed around video games since their commercial emergence in the 1970s. What began in arcades and early home computers has grown into a global phenomenon involving billions of players, professional esports leagues, conventions, and an enormous body of fan-created content.

History

Early video game culture formed around arcade games such as Space Invaders (1978) and Pac-Man (1980), where players competed for high scores and gathered in arcades that functioned as social hubs. The home console and personal computer eras of the 1980s and 1990s broadened participation, while the rise of the internet enabled multiplayer gaming, online communities, forums, and fan sites. LAN parties, where players connected computers in local networks, became a hallmark social ritual of the 1990s and 2000s.

Practices and communities

Video game culture includes a wide range of participatory practices. Speedrunning involves completing games as quickly as possible, often exploiting glitches, and is showcased at charity marathons such as Games Done Quick. Modding communities alter and extend games, sometimes producing entirely new titles. Live streaming on platforms such as Twitch has made watching others play a major form of entertainment. Cosplay, fan art, fan fiction, and chiptune and game-inspired music further extend the culture beyond the games themselves.

Esports

Competitive gaming, or esports, professionalized many aspects of game culture. Titles such as League of Legends, Counter-Strike, and Dota 2 support professional leagues, salaried players, and tournaments with audiences in the tens of millions and prize pools reaching tens of millions of dollars.

Demographics and criticism

Although gaming was long stereotyped as a pastime of young males, industry surveys consistently show a broad demographic, with women making up roughly half of players in many markets and the average player age in the thirties. The culture has also been the subject of debate, including controversies over representation, online harassment, and the 2014 Gamergate controversy, as well as long-running public discussion of video game violence and addiction.
