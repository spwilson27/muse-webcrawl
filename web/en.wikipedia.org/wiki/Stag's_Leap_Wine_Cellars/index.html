Stag's Leap Wine Cellars

Stag's Leap Wine Cellars is a winery in the Stags Leap District of Napa Valley, California, founded in 1970 by Warren Winiarski. It achieved international fame at the 1976 Judgment of Paris, when its 1973 S.L.V. Cabernet Sauvignon was ranked first among ten French and Californian red wines by French judges in a blind tasting — an outcome that transformed global perceptions of California wine.

Judgment of Paris

Organised by wine merchant Steven Spurrier, the Paris tasting of 24 May 1976 pitched top Bordeaux against upstart California Cabernets. The Stag's Leap 1973 placed first, ahead of Château Mouton Rothschild 1970. A bottle of the wine is held by the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History, and the result is credited with opening world markets to New World wines. In a 30th-anniversary re-tasting in 2006 the Californian reds again took the top places.

The estate

The winery farms the S.L.V. and Fay vineyards on volcanic and alluvial soils beneath the Stags Leap palisades. Its flagship wines are the Cask 23 Cabernet Sauvignon, named for a notable fermentation cask, and the Artemis Cabernet. In 2007 Winiarski sold the winery to a partnership of Ste. Michelle Wine Estates and Marchesi Antinori; in 2023 Ste. Michelle sold it on to the Antinori family, ending a long legal dispute over the sale.

Note the apostrophe: the neighbouring Stags' Leap Winery is a separate estate; decades of litigation settled the similar names in 1986, with the two distinguished by the placement of the apostrophe.
