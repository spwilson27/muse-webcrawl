Dominica, officially the Commonwealth of Dominica, is an island country in the Caribbean, the largest of the Windward Islands, lying between the French islands of Guadeloupe to the north and Martinique to the south. It is not to be confused with the Dominican Republic, a separate and much larger country on Hispaniola.

Geography

Dominica is one of the most mountainous and rainy islands of the Lesser Antilles, formed by volcanic activity; its highest peak, Morne Diablotins, rises to 1,447 m. Lush rainforest covers much of the interior, protected in part by Morne Trois Pitons National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site containing the Boiling Lake — the world's second-largest hot spring — and the Emerald Pool and Trafalgar Falls. The island is marketed as the "Nature Island of the Caribbean", with notable whale watching and hundreds of rivers.

History

The island was long inhabited by the Kalinago (Island Caribs), whose descendants retain a territory on the east coast — the last such community in the Caribbean. Columbus sighted the island on a Sunday in November 1493 and named it for the day (dies Dominica). France and Britain contested the island through the eighteenth century; it passed finally to Britain in 1783, and slavery was abolished in 1834. Dominica became an associated state in 1967 and an independent republic on 3 November 1978, with Dame Eugenia Charles serving as prime minister from 1980 to 1995. In September 2017 Hurricane Maria devastated the island as a Category 5 storm, damaging the great majority of buildings; the government subsequently pledged to make Dominica the world's first climate-resilient nation.

Economy and culture

The economy rests on agriculture (historically bananas), tourism — particularly eco-tourism — offshore services and a citizenship-by-investment programme. English is official, while Dominican Creole (Kwéyòl), a French-based creole, is widely spoken; the island's music includes bouyon, and its national bird is the endemic sisserou parrot, which appears on the flag.
