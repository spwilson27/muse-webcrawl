Lachin corridor


The Lachin corridor road

The Lachin corridor (Armenian: Լաչինի միջանցք; Azerbaijani: Laçın dəhlizi) is a mountain road route in the South Caucasus that for more than three decades served as the only overland connection between Armenia and the ethnic Armenian population of Nagorno-Karabakh. Legally part of Azerbaijan's Lachin District, the roughly 5-kilometre-wide corridor runs through the town of Lachin (Berdzor) and the Hakari river valley.

History

Armenian forces captured the Lachin district in May 1992 during the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, opening a land link between Armenia and the self-declared Nagorno-Karabakh Republic. Under the ceasefire statement that ended the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War in November 2020, the corridor was placed under the control of a Russian peacekeeping contingent, with Azerbaijan guaranteeing the security of movement along it; a new bypass road around the town of Lachin opened in 2022.

Blockade and 2023 offensive

From December 2022 the corridor was blocked by Azerbaijani activists and later by an official checkpoint, producing a humanitarian crisis in Nagorno-Karabakh. In September 2023 Azerbaijan launched a military offensive that ended the de facto republic; virtually the entire Armenian population fled through the corridor to Armenia, and the route thereafter came under full Azerbaijani control.
