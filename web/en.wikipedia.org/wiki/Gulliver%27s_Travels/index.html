Gulliver's Travels

Author: Jonathan Swift

Published: 1726

Full title: Travels into Several Remote Nations of the World

Gulliver's Travels (1726), published under the full title Travels into Several Remote Nations of the World. In Four Parts. By Lemuel Gulliver, is a prose satire by the Anglo-Irish writer and clergyman Jonathan Swift. It recounts the voyages of Lemuel Gulliver to imaginary lands.

The four voyages

Gulliver visits Lilliput, inhabited by tiny people; Brobdingnag, a land of giants; Laputa, a flying island of impractical scholars, with Balnibarbi and Glubbdubdrib; and the country of the Houyhnhnms, rational horses served by brutish human-like Yahoos. Each voyage satirises human pride, politics, science and colonialism.

Legacy

The book has never been out of print. The first two voyages are often retold as children's stories, while the work as a whole is regarded as a classic of satire; the words "Lilliputian" and "Yahoo" entered the English language.
