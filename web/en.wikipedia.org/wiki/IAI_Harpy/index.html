IAI Harpy

IAI Harpy
Manufacturer: Israel Aerospace Industries
Type: Loitering munition / anti-radiation drone
First deployed: 1990s
Warhead: 32 kg high-explosive

The IAI Harpy is a loitering munition produced by Israel Aerospace Industries. It is designed as an autonomous "fire and forget" anti-radiation weapon for the suppression of enemy air defences (SEAD): launched from ground- or ship-based containers, it flies to a designated area, loiters for hours while searching for radar emissions, and dives onto any emitter it detects, destroying itself and the target.

Design

The Harpy has a distinctive cropped-delta wing with a rear pusher propeller driven by a rotary engine, giving it an endurance of several hours over the target area and a range of several hundred kilometres. Its passive radar seeker homes on hostile air-defence radars; if the radar shuts down, the Harpy resumes loitering and attacks when emissions restart, or selects another target. This persistence forces defenders to keep radars silent, degrading the air defence network even when no shot is fired. The aircraft is about 2.7 metres long with a wingspan of about 2.1 metres and carries a high-explosive warhead of roughly 32 kg.

Exports and successors

The Harpy was exported to several countries, including South Korea, India, Turkey, and China; the sale to China became a diplomatic dispute in 2004–2005 when the United States objected to Israel upgrading Chinese Harpies, an episode that led to tightened Israeli arms-export controls. IAI subsequently developed the improved IAI Harop, which adds an electro-optical sensor and a man-in-the-loop control option, allowing attacks on non-emitting targets and abort after launch.
