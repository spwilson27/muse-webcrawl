Hastings is a seaside town and borough in East Sussex on the south coast of England. It lies on the English Channel, about 85 km (53 miles) south-east of London. The town gives its name to the Battle of Hastings of 1066, which in fact took place several miles north-west at the site of the present town of Battle.

History

Hastings was one of the original Cinque Ports, a confederation of coastal towns providing ships for the Crown, and became an important fishing port. Its fleet of tall black net huts and fishing boats drawn up on the Stade beach survives today. Hastings Castle, built by William the Conqueror, stands in ruins on the West Hill. In the nineteenth century the town developed as a fashionable seaside resort, with a pier opened in 1872.

The Old Town and culture

The Hastings Old Town, set in a valley between the East and West Hills, is noted for its narrow lanes (locally "twittens"), historic pubs, and antique shops. The town has a strong artistic and musical community and hosts events including the Jack-in-the-Green festival each May. Hastings Pier, destroyed by fire in 2010, was rebuilt and reopened in 2016, winning the RIBA Stirling Prize in 2017.
