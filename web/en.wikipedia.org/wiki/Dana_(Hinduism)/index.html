Dana (Hinduism)

Dāna (Sanskrit: दान) is a Sanskrit and Pali word meaning "giving" or "generosity". In Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism, dāna is the practice of cultivating generosity and giving without expecting anything in return. It can refer to giving alms, charity, donations of food, clothing, money, knowledge, or shelter to those in need.

In Hinduism

In Hindu tradition, dāna is considered one of the essential virtues and a form of dharma. Ancient texts such as the Rigveda praise generosity, and the epics and Puranas recount gifts by kings and heroes. The Bhagavad Gita classifies giving into three types according to the gunas: sattvic giving (given selflessly, at the right time and place, to a worthy person), rajasic giving (given for reward or recognition), and tamasic giving (given grudgingly or to unworthy recipients). Charitable practices include anna-dāna (giving food), vidya-dāna (giving knowledge), and go-dāna (giving cows).

Forms and significance

Dāna is one of the traditional means of generating merit (punya) and progressing spiritually. Festivals and life-cycle rituals often include acts of giving to priests, ascetics, the poor and guests. In modern India, the concept underlies much religious philanthropy, including temple feeding programs (annadanam) and charitable trusts.
