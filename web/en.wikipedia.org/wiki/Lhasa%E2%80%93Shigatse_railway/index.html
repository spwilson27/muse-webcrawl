Lhasa–Shigatse railway

The Lhasa–Shigatse railway, also known as the Lari railway, is a railway line in the Tibet Autonomous Region of China, linking the capital Lhasa with Shigatse (Xigazê), the region's second city. It is an extension of the Qinghai–Tibet railway and opened on 15 August 2014.

Route and construction

The line is 251 kilometres long and runs west from Lhasa South station, following the Yarlung Tsangpo (Brahmaputra) valley through Qüxü, Rinbung, and Nyêmo counties. Construction began in September 2010 and cost about 13.3 billion yuan. The route includes 29 tunnels and 116 bridges, which together account for nearly half its length, and passes through the Yarlung Tsangpo gorge area. The highest point is about 3,850 metres above sea level.

Services

Trains travel at up to 120 km/h, and the journey between Lhasa and Shigatse takes under three hours, compared with about four or more hours by road. Fuxing high-speed trainsets adapted for the plateau began operating on the line in 2021. A further extension from Shigatse toward the Nepal border at Gyirong has been planned as part of a proposed China–Nepal railway.
