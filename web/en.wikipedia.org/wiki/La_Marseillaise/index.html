La Marseillaise is the national anthem of France. It was written in 1792 by Claude Joseph Rouget de Lisle in Strasbourg during the French Revolutionary Wars as a war song for the Army of the Rhine.

History

It gained its name after volunteers from Marseille marched into Paris singing it. It became the anthem of the French Republic in 1795, was banned under Napoleon and the Restoration, and was permanently reinstated in 1879.

Lyrics

The famous refrain begins 'Aux armes, citoyens, formez vos bataillons, marchons, marchons!' Its vivid, martial imagery has made it an enduring symbol of revolution and liberty, referenced in works from Tchaikovsky's 1812 Overture to the film Casablanca. See Olympique de Marseille for the city it is named after.
