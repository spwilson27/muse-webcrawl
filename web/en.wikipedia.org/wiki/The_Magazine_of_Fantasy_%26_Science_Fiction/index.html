The Magazine of Fantasy & Science Fiction

The Magazine of Fantasy & Science Fiction (usually called F&SF) is an American fantasy and science fiction magazine, first published in 1949. It is one of the longest-running and most respected genre magazines, noted for publishing literary work alongside genre staples.

First issue1949 (as The Magazine of Fantasy)
FoundersAnthony Boucher, J. Francis McComas
FrequencyBimonthly
CountryUnited States

History

Launched as The Magazine of Fantasy, the title added science fiction from the second issue. Under editors including Boucher, Avram Davidson, Edward Ferman and Gordon Van Gelder, it published landmark stories such as Daniel Keyes's "Flowers for Algernon", Robert Heinlein's Starship Troopers (serialised as "Starship Soldier") and Stephen King's Dark Tower tales.

Recognition

Stories from F&SF have won numerous Hugo, Nebula and World Fantasy Awards, and the magazine has been praised for the breadth of its fiction, from hard science fiction to slipstream and horror.
