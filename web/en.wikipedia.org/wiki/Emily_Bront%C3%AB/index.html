Emily Brontë

Emily Jane Brontë (30 July 1818 – 19 December 1848) was an English novelist and poet, best known for her only novel, Wuthering Heights, now considered a classic of English literature. She also published a book of poetry with her sisters Charlotte and Anne, titled Poems by Currer, Ellis and Acton Bell. She wrote under the pen name Ellis Bell.

Early life

Emily Brontë was born in Thornton, near Bradford, the fifth of six children of Patrick Brontë, an Irish Anglican clergyman, and Maria Branwell. In 1820 the family moved to Haworth, where Patrick served as perpetual curate. After her mother's death, Emily and her siblings created elaborate fantasy worlds—Angria and Gondal—and filled tiny handwritten books with their stories and poems.

Wuthering Heights

Published in 1847, a year before her death, Wuthering Heights tells, through the narrators Nelly Dean and Mr Lockwood, the story of the intense, destructive love between Heathcliff, an orphan taken in by the Earnshaw family, and Catherine Earnshaw, and of the rivalry between two families on the Yorkshire moors. Its structure and moral ambiguity shocked early reviewers; its reputation has since risen until it is regarded as one of the greatest novels in English.

Death and legacy

Emily caught a severe cold at the funeral of her brother Branwell in September 1848, declined treatment, and died of tuberculosis that December, aged 30. Her poems, including "No coward soul is mine," are now considered among the finest in 19th-century English poetry. Her sister Charlotte edited and republished Wuthering Heights in 1850, helping secure its posthumous fame.
