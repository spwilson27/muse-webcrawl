Steve McQueen (filmmaker)

Sir Steve Rodney McQueen CBE (born 9 October 1969) is a British film director, film producer, screenwriter, and video artist. He is known for his award-winning film 12 Years a Slave (2013), an adaptation of Solomon Northup's 1853 slave narrative. For his work on the film, McQueen won the Academy Award for Best Picture and the BAFTA Award for Best Film, becoming the first black filmmaker to win the Academy Award for Best Picture.

Early life and education

McQueen was born in London to a Grenadian mother and a Trinidadian father. He grew up in Hanwell, west London, and attended Drayton Manor High School. He studied art and design at Chelsea College of Art and Design and fine art at Goldsmiths, University of London, where he became interested in film. He later studied briefly at New York University's Tisch School of the Arts but found its approach too restrictive.

Visual art career

McQueen first gained recognition as a video artist. His early works, often shot in black-and-white 16mm film and projected in gallery spaces, included Bear (1993), Five Easy Pieces (1995), and Deadpan (1997), in which he re-enacted a Buster Keaton stunt. In 1999 he won the Turner Prize. He represented Britain at the Venice Biennale in 2009 and was appointed a Commander of the Order of the British Empire (CBE) in 2011; he was knighted in 2020.

Feature films

McQueen's first feature, Hunger (2008), dramatised the 1981 Irish hunger strike and starred Michael Fassbender as Bobby Sands; it won the Caméra d'Or at Cannes. He reunited with Fassbender for Shame (2011), a study of sex addiction. 12 Years a Slave (2013) followed, winning three Academy Awards including Best Picture. His subsequent features include the heist thriller Widows (2018) and Blitz (2024), set in wartime London.

Small Axe and documentary

In 2020 McQueen released Small Axe, a five-film anthology for the BBC about London's West Indian community between the 1960s and 1980s, including Mangrove, Lovers Rock, and Red, White and Blue. His documentary work includes Uprising (2021), about the 1981 New Cross fire, and Occupied City (2023), an adaptation of his wife Bianca Stigter's book about Amsterdam under Nazi occupation.

Style

McQueen's films are noted for long, unbroken takes, minimal dialogue, an emphasis on the body and endurance, and a gallery artist's attention to the duration and framing of images. He has frequently addressed histories of race, slavery, and state violence.
