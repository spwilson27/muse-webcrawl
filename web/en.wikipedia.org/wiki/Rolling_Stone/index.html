Rolling Stone is an American monthly magazine that focuses on music, politics, and popular culture. It was founded in San Francisco, California, in 1967 by Jann Wenner and the music critic Ralph J. Gleason. The magazine's name comes from the Muddy Waters song "Rollin' Stone", the English rock band the Rolling Stones, and Bob Dylan's song "Like a Rolling Stone".

History

The first issue was published on November 9, 1967, featuring John Lennon on the cover. In its early years the magazine was associated with the counterculture and distinguished itself from the underground press by striving for journalistic standards and avoiding the era's radical politics. It later became known for its political coverage, beginning in the 1970s with the work of Hunter S. Thompson, whose Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas was first serialized in the magazine.

In the 1990s the magazine shifted toward a younger readership and a greater focus on television, film actors, and pop music. In 2016 Wenner Media announced it would sell a 49% stake in the magazine to BandLab Technologies; in 2017 the remaining stake was sold to Penske Media Corporation, which took full ownership in 2019.

Cover features

Rolling Stone is known for its cover photographs of musicians, politicians, athletes, and actors, frequently shot by renowned photographers such as Annie Leibovitz. Its lists of the greatest albums and songs of all time, first published in the 2000s, have been widely discussed and influential.
