Shine–Dalgarno sequence

The Shine–Dalgarno (SD) sequence is a ribosomal binding site in bacterial and archaeal messenger RNA, generally located about eight bases upstream of the start codon AUG. Proposed by Australian scientists John Shine and Lynn Dalgarno, the purine-rich sequence (consensus AGGAGG) helps recruit the ribosome to the mRNA by base-pairing with a complementary pyrimidine-rich region — the anti-Shine–Dalgarno sequence — at the 3′ end of the 16S rRNA of the small ribosomal subunit.

Mechanism

Pairing between the SD sequence and the 16S rRNA positions the ribosome so that the start codon sits correctly in the P site, enabling initiation of translation. The spacing between the SD sequence and the start codon, as well as the strength of the pairing, influence translation efficiency and are important considerations in designing expression systems.

Distribution

The canonical Shine–Dalgarno mechanism is characteristic of bacteria and archaea; eukaryotes instead typically use the Kozak sequence context around the start codon, and chloroplasts retain SD-like interactions inherited from their cyanobacterial ancestors. Some bacterial mRNAs are "leaderless," beginning directly at the start codon, and many genes use weaker or non-canonical SD motifs.
