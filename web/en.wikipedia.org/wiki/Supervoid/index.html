Supervoid

The Supervoid is an extremely large void — a region of the universe containing far fewer galaxies than average — located in the constellation Eridanus. With a radius of roughly 250 megaparsecs (about 800 million light-years), it is one of the largest known structures in the observable universe. It was identified in 2015 by István Szapudi and collaborators using data from the Pan-STARRS telescope and NASA's WISE satellite.

Relation to the CMB Cold Spot

The Supervoid lies in the direction of the CMB cold spot, an unusually cool region of the cosmic microwave background first detected by the WMAP satellite. Through the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect, light crossing a vast, expanding underdensity loses energy, so the Supervoid has been proposed as a partial explanation for the cold spot — though studies suggest it accounts for only part of the observed temperature decrement.

Properties

The void's centre is approximately 3 billion light-years away. Galaxy density within it is estimated to be around 25–30 percent below the cosmic mean, and it is aligned with a smaller void closer to Earth, together forming an extended line-of-sight underdensity more than a billion light-years long.
