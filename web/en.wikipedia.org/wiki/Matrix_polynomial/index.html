Matrix polynomial

A matrix polynomial is a polynomial whose variables are square matrices, or, in the most common usage, the result of evaluating an ordinary scalar polynomial at a matrix argument. Given a polynomial p(x) = a₀ + a₁x + ⋯ + akxk and a square matrix A, the matrix polynomial p(A) is defined by replacing powers of x with powers of A and the constant term with a₀I, where I is the identity matrix.

Characteristic and minimal polynomials

Two polynomials are attached to every square matrix. The characteristic polynomial det(xI − A) encodes the eigenvalues of A. By the Cayley–Hamilton theorem, a matrix satisfies its own characteristic polynomial: pA(A) = 0. The minimal polynomial is the monic polynomial of least degree annihilating A; it divides the characteristic polynomial and determines, among other things, whether A is diagonalizable — it is, exactly when the minimal polynomial splits into distinct linear factors.

Polynomials of matrices in equations

A matrix polynomial equation asks for a matrix X satisfying an equation such as X² = A (a square root of a matrix) or the algebraic Riccati equation of control theory. A polynomial in A always commutes with A itself, and the set of all polynomials in an n × n matrix forms a commutative algebra of dimension equal to the degree of the minimal polynomial.

Matrix-valued polynomials

In a second sense, a matrix polynomial (or polynomial matrix) is a matrix whose entries are polynomials in a variable such as λ — the setting of λ-matrices in linear algebra and of polynomial eigenvalue problems (λ²M + λC + K)x = 0, which arise in vibration analysis and are solved by linearization into larger ordinary eigenvalue problems.
