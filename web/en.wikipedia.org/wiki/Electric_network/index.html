An electrical network is an interconnection of electrical components — such as resistors, inductors, capacitors, voltage sources and current sources — or a model of such an interconnection. An electrical circuit is a network forming a closed loop that provides a return path for current. Networks are studied in network analysis, a foundation of electrical engineering, using idealised ("lumped") elements when the physical dimensions are small compared with the wavelength of the signals involved.

Analysis

The behaviour of a network is governed by Kirchhoff's laws: the currents entering any node sum to zero (KCL), and the voltages around any closed loop sum to zero (KVL), together with the constitutive relations of the elements, such as Ohm's law for resistors. Systematic techniques include nodal analysis, mesh analysis, superposition, and the Thévenin and Norton equivalent circuits, which reduce any linear network to a single source and resistance as seen from a pair of terminals. In the frequency domain, impedance generalises resistance for AC analysis, and network functions and transfer functions describe filtering behaviour.

Types and terminology

Networks may be linear or nonlinear, passive or active, and are often described as two-terminal (one-port) or two-port networks, the latter characterised by impedance, admittance, hybrid or transmission parameters. Large-scale "networks" also include electric power grids, though those are treated in power-system engineering. Graph theory provides the mathematical language of nodes, branches and meshes used throughout the subject.
