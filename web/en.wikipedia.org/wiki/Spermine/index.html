Spermine

Spermine
Chemical formula: C10H26N4
Molar mass: 202.35 g/mol
Appearance: colourless liquid / crystals
Class: polyamine

Spermine is a polyamine involved in cellular metabolism that occurs in all eukaryotic cells. It is synthesised from putrescine via spermidine and is named for its first isolation from semen, where Antonie van Leeuwenhoek observed crystals of spermine phosphate in 1678. Spermine should not be confused with sperm cells themselves; it is a chemical constituent of seminal fluid and of nearly all tissues.

Biological role

At physiological pH, spermine is polycationic and binds to negatively charged molecules such as DNA and RNA, stabilising their structures. It is essential for cell growth and differentiation, modulates ion channels (notably inward-rectifier potassium channels and NMDA receptors), and participates in the regulation of gene expression.

Synthesis and derivatives

Spermine is produced by spermine synthase from spermidine and decarboxylated S-adenosylmethionine. Synthetic analogues of spermine are used as biochemical reagents and investigated as anticancer agents. The compound also acts as a free-radical scavenger and is used in molecular biology for DNA precipitation and transfection protocols.
