Chiller

A chiller is a machine that removes heat from a liquid coolant via a vapor-compression, adsorption, or absorption refrigeration cycle. The cooled liquid is circulated through a heat exchanger to cool equipment or dehumidify air in buildings. Chillers are a core component of commercial HVAC systems and many industrial processes.

Types

Chiller types
By cycleVapor-compression; absorption
By condenserAir-cooled; water-cooled
CompressorsReciprocating, scroll, screw, centrifugal
Typical useAir conditioning, process cooling

Vapor-compression chillers use a mechanical compressor — reciprocating, scroll, screw, or centrifugal — and are classified as air-cooled or water-cooled according to how heat is rejected at the condenser. Water-cooled chillers are generally more efficient but require a cooling tower.

Absorption chillers use a heat source (steam, hot water, or direct-fired gas) instead of a mechanical compressor, with lithium bromide–water or ammonia–water as the working pair. They are used where waste heat or cheap thermal energy is available.

Efficiency

Chiller efficiency is commonly expressed as the coefficient of performance (COP), in kW per ton of refrigeration, or by integrated part load value (IPLV). Modern centrifugal chillers with variable-speed drives can achieve COP values above 6 at favourable conditions.
