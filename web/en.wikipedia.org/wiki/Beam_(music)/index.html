Beam (music)

In musical notation, a beam is a horizontal or diagonal line used to connect a group of notes of short duration — eighth notes (quavers) and shorter — in place of individual flags on their stems. The number of beams corresponds to the number of flags the notes would otherwise carry: one beam for eighth notes, two for sixteenth notes, three for thirty-second notes, and so on.

Function and grouping

Beaming serves both to save space and to clarify rhythm. Notes are usually beamed in groups corresponding to the beats of the time signature, so that the metric structure of a bar can be read at a glance. In 4/4 time, for example, eighth notes are commonly beamed in pairs (one per quarter-note beat) or in groups of four (one per half bar). In compound metres such as 6/8, notes are typically beamed in two groups of three eighth notes.

Special conventions

Broken or partial beams (sometimes called feathered or subsidiary beams) indicate subdivisions within a group, such as a sixteenth note among eighths. In older vocal music, notes were beamed or flagged according to the syllables of the text; modern vocal notation generally beams notes sung to a single syllable. In instrumental music of the Baroque and Classical periods, beaming could also suggest phrasing or articulation. Beams are angled to reflect the general melodic contour of the group, sloping upward for rising figures and downward for falling ones.
