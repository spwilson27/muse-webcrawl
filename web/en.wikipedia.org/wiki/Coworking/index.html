Coworking


A typical coworking space

Coworking is an arrangement in which workers for different companies share an office space. It allows cost savings and convenience through the use of common infrastructure, such as equipment, utilities, receptionist and custodial services, and in some cases refreshments and parcel acceptance services. Coworking is distinct from serviced offices and executive suites in its emphasis on community, collaboration, and open, shared work areas.

History

The modern coworking movement is usually traced to 2005, when Brad Neuberg opened a space in San Francisco often described as the first coworking space, followed by the opening of venues such as the Citizen Space and, in Vienna, Schraubenfabrik (2004), whose founders helped popularise the term. Large commercial operators — most prominently WeWork, founded in 2010 — industrialised the model during the 2010s, while independent spaces continued to emphasise community values articulated in a "coworking manifesto": collaboration, openness, community, accessibility, and sustainability.

Users and models

Coworking spaces serve freelancers, remote employees, start-ups, and increasingly large companies seeking flexible space. Memberships range from hot-desking and dedicated desks to private offices, usually sold monthly with short commitments. The growth of remote work during and after the COVID-19 pandemic broadened demand further.
