Battle of Dien Bien Phu

The Battle of Dien Bien Phu (13 March – 7 May 1954) was the climactic battle of the First Indochina War. Viet Minh forces under Võ Nguyên Giáp besieged and destroyed a French Union garrison in a valley in north-west Vietnam, leading to the end of French rule in Indochina.

Background

In November 1953 French paratroops seized Dien Bien Phu (Operation Castor) to block Viet Minh routes into Laos and lure Giáp into a set-piece battle France expected to win with air power. The French built a central airstrip ringed by strongpoints named after women — Beatrice, Gabrielle, Anne-Marie, Dominique, Huguette, Claudine and Isabelle.

Siege

Giáp instead hauled artillery and anti-aircraft guns through jungle hills, dug them in, and supplied the siege with hundreds of thousands of porters and bicycles. On 13 March 1954 massive bombardment destroyed Beatrice, then Gabrielle and Anne-Marie. The airstrip became unusable; supplies fell by parachute, increasingly into Viet Minh hands. Monsoon rain flooded trenches. After weeks of attrition, final assaults on 6–7 May overran the command bunker; de Castries surrendered on 7 May. The isolated southern strongpoint Isabelle fell the next day.

Aftermath

The defeat, announced as the Geneva Conference opened, led to the Geneva Accords, partitioning Vietnam at the 17th parallel and ending the First Indochina War. Thousands of French prisoners endured a brutal march to camps; many did not survive.
