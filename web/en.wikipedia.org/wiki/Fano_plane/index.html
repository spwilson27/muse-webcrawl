Fano plane

In finite geometry, the Fano plane (after Gino Fano) is a finite projective plane with the smallest possible number of points and lines: 7 points and 7 lines, with 3 points on every line and 3 lines through every point. Its standard notation is PG(2, 2). The Fano plane is an example of a finite incidence structure and is widely used as an introductory example in combinatorics and geometry.

Properties

The Fano plane has seven points and seven lines. Each line passes through exactly three points, and each point lies on exactly three lines. Any two distinct points determine a unique line, and any two distinct lines intersect in a unique point. The configuration is symmetric: interchanging the roles of points and lines yields an isomorphic structure, an illustration of projective duality.

The seven points can be labelled with the nonzero binary triples (001, 010, 011, 100, 101, 110, 111). A line consists of three points whose coordinatewise sum (mod 2) is zero; equivalently, three points are collinear exactly when one is the sum of the other two. This gives the Fano plane the structure of the projective plane over the two-element field F2.

Symmetries

The automorphism group of the Fano plane is the simple group PSL(2,7), also isomorphic to GL(3,2), of order 168. It acts transitively on the points and on the lines. The Fano plane is closely related to the octonions: the multiplication of the seven imaginary octonion units can be encoded by the lines of the Fano plane, each oriented line indicating the product of two units giving the third.

Applications

The Fano plane appears in the construction of the Hamming (7,4) code in coding theory, where its incidence structure underlies the parity-check matrix. It also serves as a counterexample or minimal example in many combinatorial settings, including block designs: it is the unique Steiner system S(2,3,7), the smallest nontrivial Steiner triple system.
