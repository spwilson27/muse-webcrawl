Joseph-Armand Bombardier

Joseph-Armand Bombardier (April 16, 1907 – February 18, 1964) was a Canadian inventor and businessman who founded Bombardier Inc. Motivated by the isolation of rural Quebec in winter, he developed tracked vehicles for travel over snow and, in 1959, launched the Ski-Doo, the first modern snowmobile, creating a new industry.

Early life

Bombardier was born in Valcourt in Quebec's Eastern Townships. A self-taught mechanic fascinated by machinery from childhood, he built his first crude snow vehicle as a teenager by mounting an engine and propeller on a sleigh-like frame. The determination to conquer winter travel deepened after his young son died in 1934 when the family could not reach a hospital through the snow.

Inventions and company

In 1937 Bombardier patented a sprocket-and-track system for snow vehicles and began producing the B7, a seven-passenger enclosed snowmobile used by doctors, priests, and mail carriers across rural Quebec. His company, L'Auto-Neige Bombardier Limitée, founded in 1942, produced larger tracked carriers, including the B12 and vehicles used by the Canadian military.

Seeking a smaller, affordable recreational machine, he developed the Ski-Doo, launched in 1959. Lightweight, with an endless rubber track and front skis, it became a commercial phenomenon and founded the modern snowmobile industry. The company he created grew into Bombardier Inc., a multinational manufacturer of trains and aircraft.

Death and legacy

Bombardier died of cancer in Sherbrooke in 1964, at age 56. The Musée de l'ingéniosité J. Armand Bombardier museum in Valcourt preserves his workshop and inventions, and he is regarded as one of Canada's great inventor-entrepreneurs.
