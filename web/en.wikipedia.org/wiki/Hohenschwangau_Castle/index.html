Hohenschwangau Castle (German: Schloss Hohenschwangau, lit. "High Swan County Palace") is a 19th-century palace in southern Germany. It was the childhood residence of King Ludwig II of Bavaria and was built by his father, Maximilian II of Bavaria. It is located in the village of Hohenschwangau near Füssen in southwestern Bavaria, close to the border with Austria, and near the larger and more famous Neuschwanstein Castle across the valley.

History

The castle stands on the site of the medieval fortress of Schwanstein, built by the Knights of Schwangau in the 12th century. The fortress fell into ruin and changed hands several times, coming under attack during the Napoleonic Wars. In 1832 Crown Prince Maximilian (later Maximilian II) acquired the ruins and rebuilt the castle in the Gothic Revival style to designs by the painter and architect Domenico Quaglio the Younger.

Hohenschwangau served as the summer and hunting residence of Maximilian and his wife Marie of Prussia, and their sons, the future kings Ludwig II and Otto I, spent much of their youth there. Queen Marie's fondness for mountain hiking helped shape Ludwig's love of the surrounding landscape. After his accession in 1864, Ludwig II continued to use the castle, and it was from here that he watched the construction of his own Neuschwanstein Castle on the opposite hill. He never married, and after his death in 1886 his mother lived at Hohenschwangau until her death in 1889.

Interior and today

The interior is decorated with more than 90 murals depicting scenes from German sagas and medieval poetry, including the Lohengrin legend of the Swan Knight, executed by artists such as Moritz von Schwind. The castle remained a residence of the Bavarian royal family (the House of Wittelsbach) and is now administered by the Wittelsbach Compensation Fund. It is open to the public as a museum and receives hundreds of thousands of visitors annually, many of whom combine it with a visit to Neuschwanstein.
