Dicarboxylic acid

A dicarboxylic acid is an organic compound containing two carboxyl functional groups (–COOH). The general formula can be written HOOC–R–COOH, where R is an organic group; the simplest example is oxalic acid, where the two carboxyl groups are joined directly.

Common examples

Important dicarboxylic acids include oxalic, malonic, succinic, glutaric and adipic acids (linear saturated series), maleic and fumaric acids (unsaturated), and phthalic acid (aromatic). Many occur naturally as intermediates in metabolism — succinate and fumarate are steps in the citric acid cycle.

Uses

Dicarboxylic acids are industrially important as monomers. Adipic acid is polymerised with hexamethylenediamine to make nylon 6,6; terephthalic acid is used in polyester (PET) production; and dicarboxylic acid salts and esters serve as plasticisers, lubricants and corrosion inhibitors.
