Dualism

Dualism
FieldPhilosophy of mind / metaphysics
Key figureRené Descartes
AlternativesMonism, physicalism
[Image: A silhouette of a head split between a glowing mind and a mechanical brain]

Dualism in the philosophy of mind is the view that mind and body — or mental and physical properties — are fundamentally different kinds of thing. Descartes's substance dualism posits thinking substance distinct from extended matter, raising the interaction problem Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia pressed on him: how can an immaterial mind move a material body? Property dualists hold instead that one substance has irreducibly mental properties.

Debate

Dualism draws support from the apparent gap between subjective experience and physical description (qualia, the knowledge argument) and faces objections from neuroscience's tight mind–brain correlation, causal closure of the physical, and Occam's razor. It remains a minority but persistent position, defended by philosophers such as David Chalmers in property-dualist form.
