Chillwave

The chillwave aesthetic evokes faded summer memories
Chillwave
Stylistic originsDream pop, shoegaze, synth-pop, lo-fi, ambient, psychedelic music
Cultural originsLate 2000s, United States, internet music blogs
Key artistsWashed Out, Neon Indian, Toro y Moi
Other namesGlo-fi, hypnagogic pop

Chillwave (originally considered synonymous with glo-fi and hypnagogic pop) is a music microgenre that emerged in the late 2000s. It is characterized by a faded or dreamlike retro pop sound, escapist lyrics about nostalgia and summer, and lo-fi recording aesthetics, often produced by solo artists with laptops and vintage synthesizers.

History

The term "chillwave" was coined in 2009, half-jokingly, on the blog Hipster Runoff. The summer of 2009 saw breakthrough releases by Washed Out ("Feel It All Around"), Neon Indian (Psychic Chasms), and Toro y Moi, whose music defined the style: woozy synths, sampled or echoing vocals, and an atmosphere likened to faded photographs and late-afternoon sunlight.

Legacy

The microgenre's moment was brief, but its hazy, nostalgia-saturated aesthetic fed into later internet-born styles including vaporwave and remained an influence on indie pop production in the 2010s.
