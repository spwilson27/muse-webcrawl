Rin Tin Tin or Rin-Tin-Tin (September 1918 – August 10, 1932) was a male German Shepherd born in Flirey, France, who became an international star in motion pictures. He was rescued from a World War I battlefield by an American soldier, Lee Duncan, who nicknamed him "Rinty". Duncan trained him and obtained silent film work for him. Rin Tin Tin was an immediate box-office success and went on to appear in 27 Hollywood films, gaining worldwide fame. Along with the earlier canine film star Strongheart, Rin Tin Tin was responsible for greatly increasing the popularity of German Shepherd dogs as family pets. The immense profitability of his films contributed to the success of Warner Bros. Pictures and advanced the career of Darryl F. Zanuck from screenwriter to producer and studio executive.

Origin

Following advances by American forces during the Battle of Saint-Mihiel, Corporal Lee Duncan of the U.S. Army Air Service was assigned to scout a suitable airfield site. At the village of Flirey on September 15, 1918, he found a bombed-out kennel that had once supplied German Shepherd dogs to the Imperial German Army. The only dogs left alive were a starving mother and her litter of five nursing puppies. Duncan rescued the dogs, keeping two of the puppies for himself and naming them Rin Tin Tin and Nénette, after the pair of good-luck charm dolls that French children often gave to soldiers. Nénette died in America, but Rin Tin Tin survived and was brought back to Duncan's home in Los Angeles.

Film career

Duncan trained Rin Tin Tin intensively and entered him in dog shows, where the dog's athletic jumping—he could leap over 11 feet—attracted attention from filmmakers. After a small role in The Man from Hell's River (1922), the dog was cast as the lead in Where the North Begins (1923), which was a large success and is often credited with rescuing the young Warner Bros. studio from financial trouble. A string of silent features followed, and the dog received tens of thousands of letters from fans, a star billing, and (by studio publicity) an endorsement salary reputed to rival human stars'. Rin Tin Tin made the transition less easily into sound films but continued working until his death in 1932. Legend holds that he died in the arms of actress Jean Harlow, a neighbour of Duncan's.

Successors and legacy

Several related dogs assumed the role. Rin Tin Tin Jr. appeared in films in the 1930s, and Rin Tin Tin III was credited with helping to train military dogs during the Second World War. The name reached a new generation through the 1954–1959 ABC television series The Adventures of Rin Tin Tin, featuring a descendant of the original dog alongside the boy Rusty, played by Lee Aaker, in a frontier cavalry setting. The original Rin Tin Tin is buried at the Cimetière des Chiens, the famous pet cemetery in Asnières-sur-Seine, France. In 2011, Susan Orlean published Rin Tin Tin: The Life and the Legend, tracing the dog's story and Duncan's lifelong devotion to his memory.
