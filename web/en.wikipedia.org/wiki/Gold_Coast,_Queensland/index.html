The Gold Coast is a coastal city and region in the south-east corner of Queensland, Australia, about 66 kilometres south-southeast of Brisbane. It is Australia's sixth-largest city and second-largest in Queensland, famous for its long surfing beaches, high-rise-dominated skyline along suburbs such as Surfers Paradise, an extensive system of canals, theme parks and a lush rainforest hinterland.

History

The region is the traditional land of the Yugambeh people. Europeans first charted the coast with Captain James Cook in 1770; settlement followed in the 19th century around timber-getting, sugar and farming. The area, earlier called the South Coast, developed as a holiday destination for Brisbane residents, and the name "Gold Coast" — originally a real-estate flourish — was officially applied in 1958. The city hosted the 2018 Commonwealth Games.

Geography and attractions

The Gold Coast stretches roughly 57 kilometres along the Coral Sea coast from Beenleigh and the Logan River south to Coolangatta at the New South Wales border. Inland, the McPherson Range hinterland includes Lamington and Springbrook National Parks, part of the Gondwana Rainforests World Heritage area. Major attractions include the theme parks Warner Bros. Movie World, Sea World and Dreamworld, the Q1 tower (among the world's tallest residential buildings when completed), an extensive canal network of over 260 kilometres of waterways, and the Gold Coast light rail (G:link). Tourism, construction, education and health are the dominant industries.
