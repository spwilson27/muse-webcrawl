Science of science

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The science of science is an interdisciplinary field that studies science itself — its structure, dynamics, and social and intellectual organisation — using quantitative and computational methods. Drawing on large-scale data about publications, citations, patents, funding and careers, together with tools from network science, statistics and machine learning, it seeks empirical laws governing how discoveries are made, how scientists collaborate, and how ideas spread. The field is also called metascience in some of its applications, though metascience often emphasises evaluating and improving research practices.

Origins

Precursors include the Polish scholar Stanisław Ossowski's and Maria Ossowska's 1935 proposal for a "science of science" (nauka o nauce), Derek J. de Solla Price's quantitative history of science in Little Science, Big Science (1963), Robert K. Merton's sociology of science, and Eugene Garfield's creation of citation indexing. The modern field crystallised in the 2000s as digitised bibliographic databases (Web of Science, Scopus, Google Scholar, Dimensions) and cheap computing made it possible to analyse millions of papers and patents as a single system.

Main topics

  • Structure of knowledge: mapping fields through co-citation and bibliographic coupling; detecting emerging topics and the recombination of distant ideas.
  • Careers and credit: how impact is distributed over a scientist's career (including the "random impact rule" and hot streaks), and biases in recognition such as the Matthew effect and the Matilda effect.
  • Teams: the rising dominance of team science, and findings that small teams tend to disrupt while large teams develop existing lines of work.
  • Citations and impact: citation dynamics, delayed recognition ("sleeping beauties"), and the use and abuse of metrics such as the impact factor and h-index.
  • Science policy: evaluating funding schemes, peer review, and the reproducibility of published results.

Methods

Science-of-science research typically combines bibliometrics and scientometrics with network analysis, natural language processing of full texts, and causal inference. A landmark statement of the field's ambitions was the 2018 Science review "The science of science" by Santo Fortunato and colleagues, which surveyed quantitative regularities across careers, collaboration, citation and discovery.

Applications and criticism

Funders and universities use science-of-science tools for portfolio analysis, expert finding and strategic planning. Critics warn that metric-driven management invites gaming (Goodhart's law), reinforces existing inequalities, and may misread exploratory research; researchers in the field generally argue for using its findings to improve — not merely measure — the scientific enterprise, for example by supporting risk-taking, diversity of approaches and replication.
