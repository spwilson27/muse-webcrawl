Ziaur Rahman (19 January 1936 – 30 May 1981) was a Bangladeshi military officer and politician who served as President of Bangladesh from 1977 until his assassination in 1981. A decorated war hero, he is remembered for broadcasting the declaration of Bangladesh's independence from Chittagong in March 1971 during the Liberation War, and for founding the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP).

Military career and Liberation War

Commissioned in the Pakistan Army in 1955, Zia served in the Indo-Pakistani wars of 1965. When the Pakistan Army cracked down in East Pakistan on 25 March 1971, Major Zia revolted with his regiment, the 8th East Bengal Regiment, and read out the declaration of independence over Swadhin Bangla Betar radio at Kalurghat, Chittagong — first on 26 March and again on 27 March on behalf of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman. He commanded Sector 1 and later the regular "Z Force", receiving the Bir Uttom gallantry award.

Presidency

After the coups and counter-coups of 1975, Zia emerged as Chief of Army Staff and Chief Martial Law Administrator, formally becoming president in April 1977. He founded the BNP in 1978, won the 1978 presidential and 1979 parliamentary elections, ended martial law, and promoted multi-party politics, Bangladeshi nationalism, market-oriented economic reforms, and a foreign policy oriented toward the wider Muslim world and China. He was a founding figure of the South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC).

Assassination and legacy

Zia was assassinated on 30 May 1981 in Chittagong during an abortive military coup led by Major General Manzur. He was buried with state honours in Dhaka. His widow, Khaleda Zia, led the BNP and later served as Prime Minister. His rule remains debated — praised for restoring order and growth, criticised for rehabilitating collaborators of 1971 and consolidating military influence in politics.
