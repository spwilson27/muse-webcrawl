Dronedarone, sold under the brand name Multaq, is an antiarrhythmic medication used to reduce the risk of hospitalization in patients with paroxysmal or persistent atrial fibrillation who have been restored to sinus rhythm. It was developed by Sanofi-Aventis as a chemically related but modified alternative to amiodarone, designed to lack iodine and thus to reduce thyroid and other organ toxicities.

Medical use

Dronedarone is approved for patients with non-permanent atrial fibrillation and additional cardiovascular risk factors. It is contraindicated in permanent atrial fibrillation and in severe or recently decompensated heart failure, after the PALLAS trial found increased rates of stroke, heart failure and cardiovascular death in permanent AF patients.

Pharmacology

The drug blocks multiple cardiac ion channels (sodium, potassium and calcium) and has anti-adrenergic activity, giving it properties of all four Vaughan-Williams classes. It is metabolized mainly by CYP3A and also inhibits that pathway, creating important interactions with other drugs and with grapefruit.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include diarrhoea, nausea, fatigue and raised serum creatinine. Rare cases of liver failure have led regulators to recommend periodic liver function monitoring.
