Glutathione peroxidase

Glutathione peroxidase (GPx) (EC 1.11.1.9) is the general name of an enzyme family with peroxidase activity whose main biological role is to protect the organism from oxidative damage. The biochemical function of glutathione peroxidase is to reduce lipid hydroperoxides to their corresponding alcohols and to reduce free hydrogen peroxide to water.

Several isozymes are encoded by different genes, which vary in cellular location and substrate specificity. In mammals, GPx1 through GPx4 and GPx6 are selenoproteins, containing the amino acid selenocysteine in their active sites. The reaction consumes reduced glutathione (GSH), which is regenerated by glutathione reductase at the expense of NADPH.

Glutathione peroxidase was discovered in 1957 by Gordon C. Mills. GPx4 is unique in reducing phospholipid hydroperoxides within membranes, and its inhibition triggers the iron-dependent cell death pathway known as ferroptosis.
