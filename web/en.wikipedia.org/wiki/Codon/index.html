Codon

[Image placeholder: A codon table mapping triplets to amino acids]

A codon is a DNA or RNA sequence of three nucleotides (a trinucleotide) that forms a unit of genetic information encoding a particular amino acid or signalling the termination of protein synthesis (stop signals). There are 64 different codons: 61 specify amino acids and 3 act as stop signals.

The genetic code

The mapping of codons to amino acids constitutes the genetic code, which is nearly universal across living organisms. The code is degenerate: most amino acids are encoded by more than one codon. AUG serves both as the codon for methionine and as the start signal for translation.

Translation

During translation, transfer RNA (tRNA) molecules with complementary anticodons deliver amino acids to the ribosome according to the sequence of codons in messenger RNA. Stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA) are recognised by release factors instead of tRNAs.

Mutations

Changes in codon sequence can be silent (synonymous), missense (changing one amino acid), or nonsense (creating a premature stop codon), with effects ranging from none to severe genetic disease.
