Daniel Robert Elfman (born 29 May 1953) is an American film composer, singer, songwriter, and musician. He first came to prominence as the singer-songwriter for the new wave band Oingo Boingo in the early 1980s. Since the late 1980s he has scored more than 100 feature films, and he has frequently collaborated with directors Tim Burton, Sam Raimi, and Gus Van Sant.

Career

Elfman grew up in Los Angeles and in the 1970s performed with his brother Richard's musical theatre troupe, the Mystic Knights of the Oingo Boingo, which evolved into the rock band Oingo Boingo. Tim Burton and Paul Reubens hired him — with no prior film-scoring experience — for Pee-wee's Big Adventure (1985), beginning one of cinema's most durable composer-director partnerships.

For Burton he scored Beetlejuice (1988), Batman (1989), whose main theme became the franchise's signature, Edward Scissorhands (1990), and The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993), for which he also wrote the songs and provided Jack Skellington's singing voice. His other scores include Dick Tracy, Mission: Impossible, Men in Black, Good Will Hunting, Spider-Man and Spider-Man 2, Milk, Big Fish, Fifty Shades of Grey, Avengers: Age of Ultron (with Brian Tyler), and Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness. For television he composed the themes to The Simpsons (1989) — perhaps his most-heard music — Desperate Housewives, and Wednesday.

Style and honours

Elfman's style mixes orchestral writing with choral textures, waltzes, and macabre playfulness, influenced by Bernard Herrmann, Nino Rota, and classical composers such as Bartók and Stravinsky. He has been nominated for four Academy Awards and has won Grammy and Emmy Awards. Since 2013 his Burton scores have been performed worldwide in the live concert series Danny Elfman's Music from the Films of Tim Burton, and in 2022 he performed a career-spanning set at Coachella.
