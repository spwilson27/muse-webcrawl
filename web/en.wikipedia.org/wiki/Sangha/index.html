Sangha

Sangha (Pali: saṅgha; Sanskrit: saṃgha) is a term in Buddhism meaning "community" or "assembly". In its narrowest sense it is the monastic community of ordained monks (bhikkhus) and nuns (bhikkhunis); more broadly it includes lay followers, and in the ideal sense the community of noble ones who have attained stages of awakening.

Type: Buddhist monastic community
One of: Three Jewels (Buddha, Dharma, Sangha)

Three Jewels

The Sangha is one of the Three Jewels or Refuges of Buddhism, alongside the Buddha and the Dharma. Buddhists take refuge in the Sangha as a support for practice.

Monastic life

Monastics follow the Vinaya, rules of discipline, live on alms, and devote themselves to meditation, study and teaching. Ordination lineages differ among Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana traditions.

Fourfold assembly

Traditionally the sangha comprises monks, nuns, laymen and laywomen — the fourfold assembly founded by the Buddha, including the bhikkhuni order established at the request of Sanghamitta's predecessors such as Mahapajapati Gotami.
