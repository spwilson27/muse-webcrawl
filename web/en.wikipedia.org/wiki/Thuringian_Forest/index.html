The Thuringian Forest (German: Thüringer Wald) is a mountain range in the southern part of the German state of Thuringia, running northwest to southeast. It forms a natural border in its northwestern part between the regions of Thuringia and Franconia, and separates the drainage basins of the Elbe and Weser/Rhine systems. About 120 kilometres long and 35 kilometres wide, it is one of Germany's most-visited upland regions for recreation.

Geography

The range extends from the Werra valley near Eisenach in the northwest to the Franconian Forest in the southeast, where it continues geologically. Its highest summits are the Großer Beerberg (982.9 m), the Schneekopf (978 m) and the Großer Finsterberg (944 m). The famous Rennsteig ridge trail, some 170 km long, follows the crest of the range and is among the oldest long-distance hiking paths in Germany.

Geology

Geologically the Thuringian Forest is a horst uplifted along faults, exposing rocks of very varied ages: Precambrian gneisses and schists in the Ruhla area, Carboniferous and Permian (Rotliegend) volcanic rocks and sediments in its central part, and porphyry ridges that give many summits their form.

Tourism and culture

Towns such as Oberhof, a winter-sports centre, Friedrichroda, Ilmenau and Schmalkalden serve as bases for hiking, cycling and skiing. The Wartburg castle stands at the northwestern edge of the forest near Eisenach. The region is also known for glassmaking and for the Thuringian Forest Nature Park and the UNESCO-listed Vessertal–Thuringian Forest Biosphere Reserve.
