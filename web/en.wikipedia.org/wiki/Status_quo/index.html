Status quo

Status quo is a Latin phrase meaning "the existing state of affairs", particularly with regard to social or political issues. In the sociological sense, it generally applies to maintaining or changing existing social structures and values. To maintain the status quo is to keep things the way they presently are; the phrase is related to the concepts of status quo ante ("the state before") and the diplomatic formula of preserving an existing arrangement.

Usage

The phrase is used in law (where a court may order the parties to preserve the status quo pending a decision), in politics (where parties or movements may be described as defenders of, or challengers to, the status quo), and in international relations, where a "status quo power" is a state satisfied with the existing distribution of power, as opposed to a revisionist power seeking to change it.

Status quo bias

In behavioural economics and decision theory, status quo bias is the tendency for people to prefer the current state of affairs, perceiving losses involved in change as outweighing equivalent gains. It is related to the endowment effect and loss aversion described by Daniel Kahneman and others.
