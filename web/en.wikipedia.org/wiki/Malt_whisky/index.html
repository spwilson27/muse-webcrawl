Malt whisky

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Malt whisky is whisky made from a fermented mash consisting primarily of malted barley. If the product is made exclusively at a single distillery (along with other restrictions), it is called a single malt whisky. Although malt whisky can be made using other malted grains besides barley, those versions are not called malt whisky without specification of the grain, such as rye whisky.

Production

Barley is malted — germinated and then dried, in Scotland sometimes over peat smoke, which imparts the smoky flavour characteristic of many Islay whiskies. The malt is ground to grist, mashed with hot water to produce wort, fermented into a beer-like wash, and distilled, traditionally in copper pot stills for Scotch single malts. The spirit is matured in oak casks, for a minimum of three years in Scotland.

Single malt Scotch

Single malt Scotch whisky is distilled at a single distillery in pot stills, made only from water and malted barley, and aged in Scotland in oak casks for at least three years. Scotland's whisky regions include Speyside, the Highlands, Islay, the Lowlands and Campbeltown.

Other countries

Malt whisky is produced in many countries, including Ireland, Japan, India, Taiwan, Australia, Canada and the United States, each with its own regulations governing the term.
