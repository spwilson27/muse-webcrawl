Attack helicopter

An attack helicopter is an armed helicopter with the primary role of an attack aircraft, with the offensive capability of engaging ground targets such as enemy infantry, military vehicles and fortifications. Due to their heavy armament they are sometimes called helicopter gunships. They typically carry a combination of autocannon, guided anti-tank missiles, unguided rockets and air-to-air missiles, and are fitted with sophisticated sensors and target-acquisition systems for day, night and all-weather operations.

History

Armed helicopters appeared during the Korean and Algerian wars as improvisations, and in Vietnam the US Army widely fielded gunship versions of the UH-1 Iroquois. The limitations of conversions led to the first purpose-built attack helicopter, the Bell AH-1 Cobra (1967), with a slim tandem-seat fuselage, stub wings for weapons and armour. The Soviet Union took a different route with the Mil Mi-24 "Hind", which combined heavy armament with a cabin for eight troops.

Cold War development

Facing Warsaw Pact armoured masses, NATO and Soviet designers optimised helicopters as flying tank destroyers. The US Army's AH-64 Apache (in service from 1986) integrated the AGM-114 Hellfire missile, a 30 mm chain gun slaved to the pilot's helmet sight, and the TADS/PNVS sensor turrets for night fighting. Europe produced the Franco-German Eurocopter Tiger, and Italy the Agusta A129 Mangusta, the first attack helicopter designed and built in Western Europe.

Modern role

Attack helicopters proved both powerful and vulnerable in Iraq, Afghanistan and Ukraine: devastating against armour and in close support, but exposed to man-portable air-defence systems and even small-arms fire when operating low and slow. Modern practice emphasises stand-off engagements with long-range missiles, operation alongside unmanned reconnaissance drones ("manned–unmanned teaming"), and integration into networked battle-management systems. Survivability features include redundant systems, crashworthy structures, armoured crew seats, infrared suppressors and radar/laser warning receivers.
