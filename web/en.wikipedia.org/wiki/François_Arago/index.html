François Arago

Dominique François Jean Arago (26 February 1786 – 2 October 1853), known simply as François Arago, was a French mathematician, physicist, astronomer, freemason, and politician. He made important contributions to optics, electromagnetism, and geodesy, served as director of the Paris Observatory, and briefly headed the French government as chairman of the Executive Commission in 1848.

Scientific work

Born in Estagel in Roussillon, Arago studied at the École Polytechnique. As a young man he joined the expedition to Spain to complete the measurement of the meridian arc used to define the metre. In optics he verified Fresnel's wave theory of light, discovered the chromatic polarization of light, and investigated the magnetization of iron by electric currents with Ampère. His measurement of the speed of light with Fizeau and Foucault helped settle the wave theory's triumph. Arago also popularized astronomy through his celebrated public lectures and his Popular Astronomy.

Political career

A republican deputy from 1830, Arago served as minister of war and of the navy in the provisional government of 1848 and signed the decree abolishing slavery in the French colonies. He opposed Louis-Napoléon's coup of 1851 and refused the oath of allegiance to the Second Empire. He is one of the 72 names inscribed on the Eiffel Tower.
