Eucharistic prayer

The Eucharistic prayer (also called the anaphora, especially in Eastern Christianity) is the central prayer of the Eucharist, in which, according to the teaching of most Christian traditions, the bread and wine are consecrated and become the Body and Blood of Christ. It is the climax of the Liturgy of the Eucharist within the Mass and the Divine Liturgy.

Structure

Although the wording differs among traditions, a Eucharistic prayer typically includes:

  • an opening dialogue and preface of thanksgiving, varying with the season;
  • the Sanctus ("Holy, holy, holy"), joining the worshippers' praise to that of the angels;
  • the epiclesis, invoking the Holy Spirit upon the gifts and the assembly (especially prominent in Eastern anaphoras);
  • the institution narrative, recounting Christ's words at the Last Supper ("This is my body… This is my blood");
  • the anamnesis, remembering Christ's death, resurrection and promised return;
  • intercessions for the Church, the living and the dead; and
  • a concluding doxology, answered by the people's Great Amen.

Traditions

The Roman Rite historically used a single anaphora, the Roman Canon (now Eucharistic Prayer I); after the Second Vatican Council several additional prayers were introduced into the Roman Missal, some drawing on ancient sources such as the anaphora of the Apostolic Tradition. Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox liturgies employ anaphoras attributed to St John Chrysostom, St Basil the Great and St James, among others. Anglican and Lutheran eucharistic prayers generally follow the same classical structure.
