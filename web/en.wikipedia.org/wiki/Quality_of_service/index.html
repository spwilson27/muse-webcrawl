Quality of service

Quality of service (QoS) is the description or measurement of the overall performance of a service, such as a telephony or computer network, particularly the performance seen by its users. In packet-switched networks, QoS mechanisms control resource allocation so that different traffic types receive differentiated treatment with respect to bandwidth, latency, jitter and packet loss.

Mechanisms

QoS is implemented by traffic classification and marking (e.g. the DSCP field in IP headers), queueing and scheduling disciplines, traffic shaping and policing, and congestion-avoidance algorithms such as random early detection. Two architectural models are standardized by the IETF: Integrated Services (IntServ), with per-flow reservations via RSVP, and Differentiated Services (DiffServ), which aggregates traffic into classes.

Applications

QoS matters most for real-time traffic — VoIP, videoconferencing and streaming media — which is sensitive to delay and jitter, whereas file transfer tolerates delay but needs throughput. In the debate over net neutrality, prioritization of some traffic classes is a recurring point of contention.
