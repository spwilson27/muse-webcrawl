Mechanoreceptor

A mechanoreceptor, also called a mechanoceptor, is a sensory receptor that responds to mechanical pressure or distortion. Mechanoreceptors are innervated by sensory neurons that convert mechanical pressure into electrical signals that, in animals, are sent to the central nervous system.

Mechanoreceptor
SystemSomatosensory system
LocationSkin, muscle, joints, inner ear, blood vessels
StimulusMechanical pressure, stretch, vibration
FunctionTouch, proprioception, hearing, balance, blood-pressure regulation

Types

Cutaneous mechanoreceptors

In glabrous (hairless) and hairy skin, four main types of mechanoreceptor each respond to different stimuli and mediate distinct sensations:

  • Merkel cell–neurite complexes (slowly adapting type 1, SA1) — respond to sustained pressure and texture; important for form and texture perception and Braille reading.
  • Meissner corpuscles (rapidly adapting, RA) — located in dermal papillae; respond to low-frequency flutter and changes in texture.
  • Ruffini endings (slowly adapting type 2, SA2) — respond to sustained skin stretch and contribute to finger position sense.
  • Pacinian corpuscles (rapidly adapting / PC) — deep-pressure and high-frequency vibration receptors with large receptive fields and onion-like lamellae.

Hair follicle receptors and free nerve endings also contribute to mechanosensation.

Proprioceptors

Mechanoreceptors in muscles, tendons and joints — including muscle spindles, Golgi tendon organs and joint receptors — provide proprioceptive information about limb position and movement, and drive reflexes such as the stretch reflex.

Inner ear

Hair cells of the cochlea and vestibular system are mechanoreceptors: deflection of their stereocilia by sound-induced fluid motion or head acceleration opens mechanically gated ion channels, producing hearing and balance senses.

Visceral mechanoreceptors

Baroreceptors in the carotid sinus and aortic arch sense blood-pressure changes via vessel wall stretch; stretch receptors in the lungs, gut and bladder regulate reflexes such as the Hering–Breuer reflex and micturition.

Mechanism

Mechanotransduction occurs when mechanical deformation of the receptor membrane opens mechanosensitive ion channels — in vertebrates prominently the Piezo1 and Piezo2 channels — allowing cation influx that depolarizes the sensory ending and initiates action potentials. The discovery of the Piezo channels was recognized by the 2021 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine awarded to Ardem Patapoutian (jointly with David Julius).
