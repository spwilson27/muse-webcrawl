Overview

Massachusetts is a state in the New England region of the northeastern United States, bordered by the Atlantic Ocean to the east, Connecticut and Rhode Island to the south, New York to the west, and Vermont and New Hampshire to the north. Its capital and largest city is Boston.

The state is named for the Massachusett people who lived around Massachusetts Bay. The Pilgrims founded Plymouth Colony in 1620 and Puritans established the Massachusetts Bay Colony in 1630; the colony's ports and merchants became prosperous, and Massachusetts played a leading role in the movement toward American independence — the Boston Tea Party and the first battles of the Revolutionary War at Lexington and Concord took place there.

Economy and education

Modern Massachusetts is known for higher education, health care, biotechnology and finance. It is home to Harvard University and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in Cambridge, among many other colleges, and the hospital and biotech cluster around Boston and Cambridge is among the largest in the world.

Geographically the state ranges from the Cape Cod peninsula and the islands of Martha's Vineyard and Nantucket, through the Boston metropolitan area where most residents live, to the Berkshire hills in the west.
