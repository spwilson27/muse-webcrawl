The Lockers

Origin: Los Angeles, California
Founded: 1971 (as The Campbellock Dancers)
Style: Locking, street dance
Founder: Don "Campbellock" Campbell

The Lockers were an American street dance group credited with popularizing locking, a funk dance style characterized by freezing in exaggerated poses, wrist rolls, points, and splits. Founded by Don "Campbellock" Campbell in Los Angeles in the early 1970s, the group originally performed as The Campbellock Dancers.

Members and career

Core members included Campbell, Fred "Mr. Penguin" Berry (later known as the actor who played Rerun on television), Greg "Campbellock Jr." Pope, Adolfo "Shabba Doo" Quiñones, Leo "Fluky Luke" Williamson, and Toni Basil, who also acted as the group's manager and choreographer of its television appearances. The Lockers appeared on Soul Train, Saturday Night Live, and numerous variety shows, and opened for acts such as Frank Sinatra.

Legacy

Locking became one of the foundational styles of hip-hop and street dance culture. The Lockers' theatrical, costumed performances — often in knickers, striped socks, and suspenders — helped carry an underground club dance to mainstream audiences and inspired generations of dancers worldwide.
