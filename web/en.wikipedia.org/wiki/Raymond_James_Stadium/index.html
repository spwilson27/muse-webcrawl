Raymond James Stadium is a multi-purpose stadium in Tampa, Florida, United States. Opened in 1998, it is home to the Tampa Bay Buccaneers of the National Football League (NFL) and the South Florida Bulls football team of the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA). The stadium seats 65,618, expandable to around 75,000 for special events.

Features

The stadium is famous for the 103-foot, 43-ton replica pirate ship in the north end zone, which fires cannons whenever the Buccaneers score or win. The ship's cannons also fire confetti and the ship serves as a fan entertainment area. Renovations completed in the late 2010s added enlarged high-definition video boards, a renovated club level and improved concourses.

Events

Raymond James Stadium has hosted three Super Bowls: Super Bowl XXXV (2001), Super Bowl XLIII (2009) and Super Bowl LV (2021), in which the Buccaneers became the first team to play — and win — a Super Bowl in their home stadium. It regularly hosts the Outback Bowl (later the ReliaQuest Bowl), the Gasparilla Bowl, the College Football Playoff National Championship (2017), WrestleMania 37 (2021), and numerous concerts and soccer matches.

History

The stadium replaced Tampa Stadium (the "Big Sombrero") and was funded partly through a community investment tax approved by Hillsborough County voters in 1996. Financial services company Raymond James, headquartered in nearby St. Petersburg, purchased the naming rights.
