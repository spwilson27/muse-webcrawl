Smart casual

Smart casual is an ambiguously defined dress code that blends elements of casual wear with smarter, more polished components. It is commonly requested in modern workplaces, restaurants, and social events, and is generally understood to mean neat, presentable clothing that falls short of formal business attire such as a suit and tie.

Interpretation

For men, smart casual typically includes chinos, tailored trousers, or dark non-distressed jeans; a collared shirt, polo shirt, or fine knitwear; and optionally an unstructured blazer or sports jacket, with loafers, brogues, or clean minimal trainers as footwear. Shorts, sportswear, flip-flops, and heavily ripped or faded clothing are usually excluded. For women, interpretations range across tailored trousers, skirts, blouses, knitwear, dresses of moderate length, and smart flats or heels. Because the code is loosely specified, expectations vary considerably by country, industry, venue, and generation, and ambiguity about what qualifies is a frequent source of confusion.

History

The term arose in the 20th century as workplace dress norms relaxed, and gained prominence from the 1990s onward as technology companies and creative industries adopted informal dress cultures, and as "dress-down Friday" practices spread in corporate offices. Smart casual emerged as a compromise between full business dress and casual clothing, allowing comfort while maintaining a professional appearance for client-facing occasions.

Criticism

Style commentators frequently criticize "smart casual" as one of the least clear dress code instructions, leaving guests and employees uncertain and risking both underdressing and overdressing. Some etiquette guides recommend that hosts specify expectations more concretely, and that in doubt, dressing slightly more formally is the safer choice.
