The Koch snowflake is one of the earliest fractal curves to be described, introduced by the Swedish mathematician Helge von Koch in a 1904 paper. It is built by starting with an equilateral triangle and repeatedly adding smaller triangles to each side.

Construction

Begin with an equilateral triangle. For each straight segment, divide it into three equal parts, replace the middle part with two sides of an outward-pointing equilateral triangle of the same length, and repeat the process indefinitely on every resulting segment. The boundary of the limiting figure is the Koch curve construction applied to all three sides. At each step the number of sides multiplies by four while each side shrinks to one third of its previous length.

Properties

Because each iteration multiplies the perimeter by 4/3, the perimeter grows without bound, yet the enclosed area converges to 8/5 of the area of the original triangle: the snowflake is a finite area bounded by an infinitely long curve. The curve is continuous everywhere but differentiable nowhere, and it is self-similar, with Hausdorff dimension log 4 / log 3 ≈ 1.262 — between a line and a plane figure. Von Koch's original motivation was to give an elementary geometric example of such a pathological curve, independent of the analytic constructions of Weierstrass.
