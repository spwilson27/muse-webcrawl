Bentonville is a city in and the county seat of Benton County, Arkansas, United States, in the Ozarks region of the northwest of the state. It is best known as the home of Walmart, the world's largest retailer by revenue, whose headquarters has driven the city's rapid growth since the late 20th century.

History

Settled in the 1830s and named for Senator Thomas Hart Benton, the town was a centre for apple orchards and tobacco. Sam Walton bought a variety store on the town square in 1950 and built his retail empire from there; his original Walton's 5-10 is now the Walmart Museum. Walmart's vendor ecosystem drew hundreds of supplier offices to the region.

Culture and growth

Alice Walton of the Walton family opened the Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art in 2011, designed by Moshe Safdie and set in a wooded ravine; the Momentary contemporary art space and an extensive trail network have made Bentonville a self-styled mountain-biking capital. The Sam's Club and Walmart home offices anchor an economy that has roughly quintupled the city's population since 1990. Walmart's new multi-billion-dollar headquarters campus opened in phases from 2025.
