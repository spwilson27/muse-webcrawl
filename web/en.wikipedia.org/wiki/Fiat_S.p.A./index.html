Fiat S.p.A.

Fiat S.p.A.
Type: Public (Società per azioni)
Industry: Automotive, industrial
Founded: 1899, Turin, Italy
Defunct: 2014 (merged into Fiat Chrysler Automobiles)
Successor: Fiat Chrysler Automobiles; CNH Industrial

Fiat S.p.A. was an Italian holding company that controlled the Fiat Group, one of the world's largest industrial conglomerates, from its founding until 2014. Unlike the Fiat car brand, the subject of this article is the parent corporation, which at its peak encompassed automobile manufacturing, trucks, agricultural and construction machinery, components, publishing, and finance.

Structure and subsidiaries

The group's automotive arm, Fiat Group Automobiles, included the Fiat, Alfa Romeo, Lancia, Abarth, and (from 2009, progressively) Chrysler brands, together with majority stakes in Ferrari and Maserati. Industrial activities included Iveco (trucks and buses) and Fiat Powertrain. In 2011 the group demerged its agricultural and industrial businesses — Case New Holland and Iveco — into a separate listed company, Fiat Industrial S.p.A., which in 2013 merged with CNH Global to form CNH Industrial.

Chrysler merger and dissolution

Following the 2009 bankruptcy restructuring of Chrysler, Fiat under CEO Sergio Marchionne acquired a growing stake in the American automaker, reaching full ownership in January 2014. In October 2014 Fiat S.p.A. merged into the newly created Dutch-registered Fiat Chrysler Automobiles N.V. (FCA), ending the company's 115-year existence as an independent Italian holding. FCA in turn merged with PSA Group in 2021 to form Stellantis. Ferrari was spun off from FCA in 2016.

The Agnelli family

Throughout its history Fiat S.p.A. was controlled by the Agnelli family through the holding company IFIL/Exor, and its chairmen — Giovanni Agnelli, his grandson Gianni Agnelli, and later John Elkann — were central figures in Italian industrial and political life. The company was long Italy's largest private employer and was sometimes described as a state within a state.
