Cantautor

Cantautor (plural cantautores), roughly "singer-author", is the Spanish and Portuguese term for a singer-songwriter: a musician, typically accompanied by guitar, who writes, composes and sings their own songs, often with poetic or socially conscious lyrics. The equivalent Italian term is cantautore.

Tradition

The cantautor tradition flourished in Spain, Portugal, Italy and Latin America from the 1960s, associated with protest music and resistance to authoritarian regimes. Figures include Joan Manuel Serrat, Luis Eduardo Aute and Paco Ibáñez in Spain; José Afonso in Portugal, whose "Grândola, Vila Morena" signalled the 1974 Carnation Revolution; Fabrizio De André in Italy; and in Latin America Silvio Rodríguez and Pablo Milanés of Cuba's Nueva Trova, Violeta Parra and Víctor Jara of Chile's Nueva Canción, and Mercedes Sosa of Argentina.

Style

Cantautores typically emphasise lyrics, storytelling and social commentary over spectacle, performing solo with acoustic guitar. The movement parallels the French chanson auteur tradition and the Anglo-American singer-songwriter movement of Dylan and his contemporaries.
