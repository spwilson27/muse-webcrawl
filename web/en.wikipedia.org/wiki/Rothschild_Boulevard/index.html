Rothschild Boulevard (Hebrew: שדרות רוטשילד, Sderot Rotshild) is one of the principal streets in the centre of Tel Aviv, Israel, beginning in Neve Tzedek at its southwestern end and running north to Habima Theatre. Named after Baron Edmond James de Rothschild, it is one of the most expensive streets in the city and a major tourist attraction.

History

Laid out in 1910 on the sand dunes of the new garden suburb of Ahuzat Bayit (which became Tel Aviv), the boulevard was originally called Rehov HaAm ("Street of the People") and was renamed at residents' request in honour of the Baron. In 1948, Israel's Declaration of Independence was signed at Independence Hall (the former home of Meir Dizengoff, the city's first mayor) at 16 Rothschild Boulevard.

Architecture and character

The boulevard is a centrepiece of the White City of Tel Aviv, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with many Bauhaus and International Style buildings from the 1930s alongside eclectic and modern towers. A wide, tree-lined central strip with pedestrian and cycle paths, kiosks, cafés and sculptures runs along its length. The street is a hub of Tel Aviv's financial district and start-up scene, and its central promenade hosted the main encampment of the 2011 Israeli social justice protests.
