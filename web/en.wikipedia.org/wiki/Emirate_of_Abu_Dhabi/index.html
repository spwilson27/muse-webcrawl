Emirate of Abu Dhabi

The Emirate of Abu Dhabi (Arabic: إمارة أبو ظبي, romanized: ʾImārat ʾAbū Ẓaby) is one of seven emirates that constitute the United Arab Emirates. It is by far the largest emirate by area (67,340 km2, roughly 87 percent of the federation's land), and the second most populous after Dubai. Its capital, Abu Dhabi, is also the capital of the UAE.

Geography and government

The emirate occupies the southwestern coast of the Persian Gulf and extends inland to the oasis city of Al Ain. It is ruled by the Al Nahyan family; its ruler is by convention the President of the United Arab Emirates. The current ruler is Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan, who became UAE president in 2022.

Economy

Abu Dhabi holds the great majority of the UAE's oil and natural gas reserves, and petroleum income funds sovereign wealth vehicles such as the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority. The emirate has pursued economic diversification into finance, tourism, culture (including the Louvre Abu Dhabi), renewable energy at Masdar City, and technology.

History

The Bani Yas tribal confederation settled Abu Dhabi island in the 18th century, and the Al Nahyan sheikhs signed treaties with Britain in the 19th century, making the territory part of the Trucial States. Oil exports began in 1962, and Abu Dhabi joined the newly independent United Arab Emirates in 1971 under Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan.
