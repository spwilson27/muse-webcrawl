The Hungarians in Romania (Hungarian: romániai magyarok) are the largest ethnic minority in Romania. According to the 2021 census, 1,002,151 people (5.26% of the population with declared ethnicity) identified as ethnic Hungarians, concentrated in Transylvania, which was part of the Kingdom of Hungary until the Treaty of Trianon (1920). The community comprises regional groups including the Székelys of the eastern Carpathians and the Csángós of Moldavia.

History

Hungarian-speaking populations have lived in Transylvania since the medieval Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin. The Székely Land enjoyed historic privileges as border guards of the kingdom. After the First World War, the 1920 Treaty of Trianon assigned Transylvania to Romania, making over 1.5 million Hungarians Romanian citizens overnight. Northern Transylvania was temporarily returned to Hungary by the Second Vienna Award (1940) before being restored to Romania in 1945–47. Under communism, a Hungarian Autonomous Region existed from 1952 to 1968, but Ceaușescu's national-communist policies curtailed minority education and institutions. Ethnic clashes at Târgu Mureș in March 1990, shortly after the revolution, left several dead.

Politics and culture

The Democratic Alliance of Hungarians in Romania (UDMR), founded in 1989, has represented the community in parliament and participated in numerous governing coalitions, advancing language rights, education in Hungarian (including at Babeș-Bolyai University in Cluj-Napoca), and—unsuccessfully—territorial autonomy for the Székely Land. Cultural life is supported by Hungarian-language theatres, media, and churches. Long-running points of tension with Romanian nationalists include bilingual signage, the use of the Székely flag, and disputes over Hungarian-language instruction; relations nonetheless improved markedly after the 1996 Romanian-Hungarian basic treaty.
