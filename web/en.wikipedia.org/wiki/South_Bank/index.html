The South Bank is an entertainment and commercial district of central London, England, on the south bank of the River Thames opposite the City of Westminster. It forms a narrow strip of riverside land stretching roughly a mile (1.6 km) between Westminster Bridge and Blackfriars Bridge, within the London Boroughs of Lambeth and Southwark.

Long a relatively undeveloped area of marshland and later industry, the South Bank was transformed after the Second World War, beginning with the Festival of Britain in 1951, which left the Royal Festival Hall as its principal legacy building. The area is now one of London's most important cultural quarters.

Culture and attractions

The district contains a concentration of nationally significant arts venues, including the Southbank Centre (comprising the Royal Festival Hall, Queen Elizabeth Hall, Purcell Room and the Hayward Gallery), the National Theatre, and the BFI Southbank cinema. Further east, in Bankside, stand Tate Modern and the reconstructed Shakespeare's Globe theatre.

The London Eye, a 135-metre cantilevered observation wheel opened in 2000, stands at the western end of the South Bank near County Hall, the former headquarters of the Greater London Council. The riverside walkway, the Queen's Walk, links these attractions and is lined with book markets, skateboarders (in the famous Undercroft), restaurants and street performers.

History

Before the nineteenth century the south bank was largely marsh and pleasure gardens, including the Vauxhall Gardens. Industrial uses, wharves and the construction of the Albert Embankment in the 1860s reshaped the shoreline. Waterloo station, opened in 1848, anchored the district's transport role. Post-war redevelopment, accelerated by the Festival of Britain and the foundation of the National Theatre company, established the modern cultural identity of the area. In the twenty-first century the South Bank has seen extensive commercial and residential development, including the conversion of the former Bankside power station into Tate Modern (opened 2000) and the completion of The Shard nearby at London Bridge.
