Law & Order: Special Victims Unit (often abbreviated to SVU) is an American police procedural drama television series created by Dick Wolf for NBC. The first spin-off of Law & Order, it premiered on 20 September 1999 and follows the detectives of the Special Victims Unit of the New York City Police Department's fictional 16th Precinct, who investigate sexually based offences and crimes against children and the elderly. Its opening narration declares: "In the criminal justice system, sexually based offenses are considered especially heinous..."

Premise and cast

The series was originally built around partners Elliot Stabler (Christopher Meloni) and Olivia Benson (Mariska Hargitay). After Meloni's departure in 2011, Benson became the central character, rising through the ranks from detective to sergeant, lieutenant and ultimately captain of the unit — Hargitay's tenure made Benson the longest-serving character in a U.S. primetime live-action drama. Long-serving cast members have included Ice-T as Detective/Sergeant Fin Tutuola, Richard Belzer as John Munch, and Dann Florek as Captain Donald Cragen. Episodes typically follow the Law & Order formula of investigation followed by prosecution, though SVU places greater emphasis on the detectives' personal lives and the emotional toll of the work.

Reception and impact

SVU surpassed its parent series and Gunsmoke in 2019 to become the longest-running American live-action primetime television series. Hargitay won an Emmy Award and a Golden Globe for her performance, and the show has been praised for raising public awareness of sexual assault; Hargitay founded the Joyful Heart Foundation to support survivors. Many storylines are "ripped from the headlines", adapting real cases with altered details. Meloni returned to the franchise in 2021, headlining the spin-off Law & Order: Organized Crime with frequent crossovers.
