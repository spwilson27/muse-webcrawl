Chrysler is an American brand of automobiles and a division of FCA US LLC, the United States subsidiary of multinational automaker Stellantis. Founded by Walter Chrysler in 1925 from the remnants of the Maxwell Motor Company, Chrysler was for most of the 20th century one of Detroit's "Big Three" automakers, alongside General Motors and Ford, and the parent of the Plymouth, Dodge, DeSoto, Imperial and Jeep brands.

History

The first Chrysler automobile, the Chrysler Six of 1924, offered advanced features such as a high-compression engine and four-wheel hydraulic brakes at a moderate price. The company expanded rapidly, acquiring Dodge Brothers in 1928 and launching the Plymouth and DeSoto marques the same year. Chrysler became known for engineering innovation — the Airflow streamliners of the 1930s, the Hemi V8 of the 1950s, and the minivan, launched under Lee Iacocca in 1983, which rescued the company after its 1979 federal loan guarantees.

Chrysler merged with Daimler-Benz in 1998 to form DaimlerChrysler, was sold to Cerberus Capital Management in 2007, and entered Chapter 11 bankruptcy in April 2009 during the financial crisis, emerging in alliance with Fiat. Fiat Chrysler Automobiles fully acquired the company in 2014; in 2021 FCA merged with PSA Group to form Stellantis.

The brand today

The Chrysler marque's line-up contracted over the 2010s as FCA prioritized Jeep, Ram and Dodge. Following the discontinuation of the Chrysler 300 sedan in 2023, the brand's range centred on the Pacifica minivan. Stellantis has announced plans for Chrysler to relaunch as an all-electric brand, previewed by the Airflow and Halcyon concepts.
