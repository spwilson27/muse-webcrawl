Emily Dickinson Museum

The Emily Dickinson Museum is a historic house museum in Amherst, Massachusetts, consisting of two houses on Main Street: the Dickinson Homestead, where the poet Emily Dickinson (1830–1886) lived most of her life, and The Evergreens, the home of her brother Austin Dickinson and his wife Susan. The museum was created in 2003 by the merger of the two properties and is owned by Amherst College.

The Homestead

The Homestead was built in 1813 for Samuel Fowler Dickinson, the poet's grandfather, a lawyer and a founder of Amherst College. Facing financial difficulty, the family sold the house in 1833; Edward Dickinson, the poet's father, bought it back in 1855, and Emily Dickinson returned to live there for the rest of her life. It was in her second-floor bedroom that she wrote most of her nearly 1,800 poems.

The Evergreens

The Evergreens, built in 1856 as a wedding present for Austin and Susan Dickinson, stands immediately west of the Homestead. An early example of Italianate domestic architecture, it retained its mid-19th-century furnishings, library, and artwork largely intact when it passed to the Dickinson family heirs, making it an unusually complete record of a Victorian household.

The museum

The two houses were separately administered for many years: the Homestead by Amherst College from 1965 and The Evergreens by the Martha Dickinson Bianchi Trust. They were merged in 2003 to form the Emily Dickinson Museum. The museum offers tours, exhibitions, readings, and educational programs, including the annual Tell It Slant Poetry Festival. In 2015 the museum received wide attention when the Homestead's restoration reproduced the poet's bedroom, including her small writing table and white dress.
