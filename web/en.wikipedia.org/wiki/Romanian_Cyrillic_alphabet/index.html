Romanian Cyrillic alphabet

The Romanian Cyrillic alphabet is the version of the Cyrillic script used to write the Romanian language from its earliest surviving texts until the 1860s, and in some regions well beyond. Romanian, a Romance language, was thus for centuries written in a script associated mainly with Slavic languages, reflecting the influence of Old Church Slavonic liturgy and chancery practice.

Letters

The alphabet had around 43 letters in its classical form, adapted to Romanian phonology with letters for sounds such as /ɨ/ (the letter Ꙟ, "înnă"), and used Slavonic letters and diacritics inherited from the church books. The oldest surviving Romanian text, Neacșu's letter of 1521, is written in this script.

Transition to Latin

From the late 18th century, scholars of the Transylvanian School promoted Romanian's Latin heritage. A transitional alphabet mixing Cyrillic and Latin letters was used in the early 19th century. Wallachia officially adopted the Latin alphabet in 1860 and Moldavia in 1862. Cyrillic returned briefly in the Moldavian SSR (and was used in Transnistria) in a Soviet-modified form, before Moldova adopted the Latin script in 1989.
