Ethnoreligious group
Definition: An ethnic group unified by a common religion
Examples: Jews, Sikhs, Druze, Mennonites, Parsis
Related terms: Ethnic group, Religious community, Ethnocultural group

An ethnoreligious group (or ethnoreligious community) is an ethnic group whose members are also unified by a common religious background or affiliation, such that ethnic and religious identity are intertwined. In such groups, membership is typically inherited, conversion may be rare or complex, and religious practice is bound up with shared ancestry, culture and historical experience.

Characteristics

Ethnoreligious groups usually exhibit endogamy, distinctive customs, and a collective memory in which religious narrative and group history coincide. Unlike purely religious communities, membership is generally understood to be by descent as well as belief; unlike purely ethnic groups, a shared faith supplies much of the group's cultural content.

Examples

Commonly cited examples include Jews, Sikhs, Druze, Samaritans, Parsis (Zoroastrians of India), Mennonites and other Anabaptist groups, Alawites, Yazidis, Mandaeans, and the Copts of Egypt. Some classifications also include groups such as the Armenians and Greeks where national and church identities historically overlap.
