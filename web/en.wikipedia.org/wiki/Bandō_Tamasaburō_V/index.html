Bandō Tamasaburō V (五代目 坂東 玉三郎, born 25 April 1950) is a Japanese kabuki actor and film director, widely regarded as the leading onnagata — a male actor specialising in female roles — of his generation. Not born into a kabuki family, he was adopted into the art and rose to stardom through exceptional artistry, a rare path in the hereditary world of kabuki.

Career

Born Shin'ichi Morita in Tokyo, he was adopted by actor Morita Kan'ya XIV and made his stage debut in 1957. He succeeded to the name Bandō Tamasaburō V in 1969 and quickly became celebrated for roles such as Princess Shiranui, Lady Kuzunoha, and the heroines of the great dance pieces. He has performed with international artists, appeared in Chinese kunqu theatre, and collaborated with cellist Yo-Yo Ma, the film director Andrzej Wajda, and fashion and art photographers. As a director he made the films Gekashitsu (The Operating Room, 1992) and other works, and has directed kabuki productions and the dance troupe Kodo.

Recognition

In 2012 Tamasaburō was designated a Living National Treasure (holder of an Important Intangible Cultural Property) by the Japanese government, one of the highest honours for a traditional artist, and in 2013 he received the Order of Culture. He is also a Chevalier of the French Ordre des Arts et des Lettres.
