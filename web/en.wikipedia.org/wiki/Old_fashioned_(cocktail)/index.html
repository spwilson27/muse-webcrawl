Old fashioned (cocktail)

TypeCocktail
Base spiritWhiskey (bourbon or rye)
ServedOn the rocks
Standard garnishOrange peel and cocktail cherry

The old fashioned is a cocktail made by muddling sugar with bitters and water, adding whiskey, and garnishing with an orange slice or zest and a cocktail cherry. It is traditionally served in a short tumbler called an old fashioned glass, which predates the drink.

History

The drink follows the earliest recorded definition of a cocktail from 1806: spirits, sugar, water, and bitters. By the late 19th century, as bartenders embellished drinks with liqueurs and fruit, purists began asking for drinks made "the old-fashioned way." The Waldorf-Astoria bar popularized the muddled-fruit version in the early 20th century.

Preparation

A sugar cube or simple syrup is combined with two or three dashes of Angostura bitters and a splash of soda or water, then stirred with bourbon or rye whiskey and ice, strained over fresh ice, and expressed with orange peel.
