Death in Venice

Death in Venice (German: Der Tod in Venedig) is a novella by Thomas Mann, first published in 1912.

The story follows Gustav von Aschenbach, an ageing, disciplined writer who travels to Venice seeking rest. There he becomes obsessed with the beauty of Tadzio, a Polish adolescent, while the city is secretly gripped by a cholera epidemic. Aschenbach's self-control collapses and he dies on the beach, still watching the boy.

Themes

The novella explores the tension between Apollonian discipline and Dionysian passion, art and beauty, decay and desire, drawing on Mann's own visit to Venice in 1911.

Adaptations

Luchino Visconti adapted it into a celebrated 1971 film starring Dirk Bogarde, and Benjamin Britten made it into his final opera, premiered in 1973.
