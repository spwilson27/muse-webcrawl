Mitchell Donald Hurwitz (born May 29, 1963) is an American television writer, producer, and actor. He is best known as the creator of the sitcom Arrested Development, as well as a co-creator of The Ellen Show, and a contributor to The John Larroquette Show and The Golden Girls.

Early life and education

Hurwitz was born in Boston, Massachusetts, and grew up in New Canaan, Connecticut. He studied at Georgetown University, where he majored in English and theology and performed with a campus improv group.

Career

Hurwitz began his television career writing for sitcoms in the late 1980s and 1990s, including The Golden Girls and The John Larroquette Show. In 2003 he created Arrested Development for Fox. The series, an intricate comedy about the dysfunctional Bluth family, drew critical acclaim and several Emmy Awards despite modest ratings, and was later revived by Netflix for additional seasons in 2013 and 2018–2019.

He later developed other comedy projects and has made occasional on-screen appearances, including a recurring role in Community. He is frequently cited as an influence on densely plotted, callback-driven television comedy writing.
