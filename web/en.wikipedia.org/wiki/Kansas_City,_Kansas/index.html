Kansas City, Kansas

Kansas City (abbreviated as KCK) is the third-most populous city in the U.S. state of Kansas and the county seat of Wyandotte County. It lies at the confluence of the Missouri and Kansas rivers, directly across the Missouri River from Kansas City, Missouri, with which it forms the heart of the Kansas City metropolitan area. Its population was about 156,000 at the 2020 census.

History

The modern city was formed in 1886 by the consolidation of several towns, including the original Kansas City (founded 1872), Wyandotte, Armourdale and Armstrong. It grew as a meatpacking and railroad centre tied to the stockyards on the Kansas side of the river. Since 1997 the city and Wyandotte County have operated as a consolidated "Unified Government".

Economy and attractions

Major employers include the University of Kansas Hospital and Medical Center and a General Motors assembly plant. The Village West development in the west of the city contains the Kansas Speedway, which hosts NASCAR races, the Sporting Kansas City soccer stadium (Children's Mercy Park), and the Legends Outlets shopping district.
