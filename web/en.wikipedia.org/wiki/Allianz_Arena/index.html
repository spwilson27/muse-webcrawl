The Allianz Arena (known as the Fußball Arena München in UEFA competitions) is a football stadium in Munich, Bavaria, Germany, and the home of FC Bayern Munich. Opened in 2005 ahead of the 2006 FIFA World Cup, it is famous for its facade of inflated ETFE plastic panels, the largest plastic shell in the world, which can be illuminated in different colours — red for Bayern home games, white for the German national team, and formerly blue for TSV 1860 Munich.

Design and construction

Designed by the Swiss architects Herzog & de Meuron, the stadium's exterior consists of 2,874 rhomboidal foil air panels, each individually lit. Construction began in 2002 on a site in Fröttmaning in northern Munich, after a referendum of Munich citizens approved the project. The naming rights were sold to the financial services company Allianz for a 30-year term.

History

The stadium hosted the opening match of the 2006 World Cup and the 2012 UEFA Champions League Final, in which Bayern lost to Chelsea on penalties. Originally co-owned by Bayern and TSV 1860, Bayern bought out their rivals' share in 2006 amid 1860's financial troubles; 1860 left the stadium entirely in 2017. Capacity has been increased several times from the original 66,000 to about 75,000. The arena hosted matches at UEFA Euro 2020 and Euro 2024, and was the venue for the first NFL regular-season game in Germany in 2022.
