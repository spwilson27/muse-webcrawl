William Spencer Vickrey (21 June 1914 – 11 October 1996) was a Canadian-born American economist and professor at Columbia University. He was awarded the 1996 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, jointly with James Mirrlees, for fundamental contributions to the economic theory of incentives under asymmetric information. He died three days after the announcement, before receiving the prize.

Work

Trained at Yale and Columbia, Vickrey worked on tax policy (helping design the income tax systems used in wartime America and advising Japan's postwar tax reform) before producing a series of landmark papers. His 1961 "Counterspeculation, Auctions, and Competitive Sealed Tenders" founded modern auction theory, analysing the second-price sealed-bid auction now called the Vickrey auction, in which bidding one's true value is a dominant strategy.

Congestion pricing and urban economics

Vickrey was a lifelong advocate of Georgist ideas and of pricing road and transit congestion: he proposed time-varying fares for the New York City subway and peak tolls for roads as early as the 1950s, proposals widely implemented decades later. He also contributed to the theory of optimal income taxation and to welfare economics.

Legacy

The Vickrey–Clarke–Groves mechanism generalises his auction to allocations of multiple goods and public decisions, and remains central to mechanism design and internet-advertising markets.
