Overpass

An overpass (called a flyover in British English for road traffic) is a bridge or elevated structure that carries one route — a road, railway or footpath — over another without an at-grade intersection. Overpasses let crossing traffic flows pass without stopping, and are a basic building block of interchanges, grade-separated railways and pedestrian networks.

Uses

Road overpasses carry highways over cross streets, railways and rivers, and form the stacked levels of interchange designs such as the cloverleaf and stack. Rail overpasses eliminate level crossings, improving safety and line capacity. Pedestrian footbridges and wildlife overpasses (ecoducts) are also overpasses, reconnecting habitats and walking routes divided by transport corridors. In some cities, "flyovers" carry through-traffic above congested junctions in dense urban areas.

Design

Overpasses are typically built as beam, girder or box-girder bridges in steel or prestressed concrete, with approach embankments or ramps. Design must accommodate vertical clearance for the route below (typically around 5 metres for roads), drainage, expansion joints and, in seismic regions, earthquake-resistant bearings. Because they are visually prominent and expensive, urban overpasses are sometimes controversial and have occasionally been demolished in favour of surface streets.

Overpass vs. underpass

The distinction is one of viewpoint: the same structure is an overpass for the route it carries and an underpass for the route that passes beneath it.
