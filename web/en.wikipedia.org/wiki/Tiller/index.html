Tiller

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia. For other uses, see Tiller (disambiguation).

A tiller or tiller arm is a lever used to steer a vehicle. In its best-known application, it is attached to the top of the rudder stock of a boat or ship and provides leverage for the helmsman to turn the rudder. Pushing the tiller to one side swings the rudder — and therefore the vessel — the opposite way, so a tiller is moved to port to turn the boat to starboard, a point of frequent confusion for beginners.

On boats

Tillers are standard on small sailing dinghies, keelboats and many outboard-motor boats, where they give the helmsman direct "feel" of the water pressure on the rudder. On sailing boats the tiller is often fitted with a hinged extension (a "tiller extension" or "hiking stick") allowing the helm to steer while sitting out to windward. Larger vessels use a ship's wheel instead, which operates the rudder through cables, chains or hydraulics. Tiller steering survives at large scale in emergency steering gear, and in spirit in the "tiller" orders historically given to helmsmen.

Other uses

The term is also used for the steering lever of some early automobiles and motorcycles, for the control bar of hang gliders in some designs, and for ground-working machinery — the powered rotary cultivator is commonly called a "rototiller" or simply a tiller. In agriculture generally, a tiller may also be a shoot or sucker produced by a grass plant, a process known as tillering.
