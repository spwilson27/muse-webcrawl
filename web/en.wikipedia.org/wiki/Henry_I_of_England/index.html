Henry I, youngest son of William the Conqueror.

Henry I (c. 1068 – 1 December 1135), also known as Henry Beauclerc ("fine scholar"), was King of England from 1100 to his death and also ruled Normandy. The youngest son of William the Conqueror, he seized the English throne on the death of his brother William II in a hunting accident in the New Forest, issuing a Charter of Liberties that promised to redress his brother's abuses — a document later invoked as a precedent for Magna Carta.

Reign

Henry married Matilda of Scotland, uniting the Norman line with the old English royal house. In 1106 he defeated his elder brother Robert Curthose at the Battle of Tinchebray and reunited England and Normandy, imprisoning Robert for the rest of his life. At home Henry strengthened royal administration — developing the Exchequer, itinerant justices, and a professional household government — earning from chroniclers the nickname "Lion of Justice." His reign also saw a long investiture dispute with Archbishop Anselm, settled by compromise in 1107.

Succession disaster

The drowning of his only legitimate son, William Adelin, in the White Ship disaster of 1120 wrecked the succession. Henry compelled his barons to swear oaths to his daughter, the Empress Matilda, but on his death (traditionally after eating a surfeit of lampreys) his nephew Stephen of Blois seized the throne, plunging England into the civil war known as the Anarchy.
