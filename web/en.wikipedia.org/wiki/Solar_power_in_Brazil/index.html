Solar power in Brazil

Solar power in Brazil has grown rapidly since the mid-2010s, from a negligible base to one of the country's largest electricity sources. Brazil has some of the world's best solar resources, with average global horizontal irradiation of roughly 1,500–2,500 kWh/m² per year — higher in the semi-arid northeast. Total installed photovoltaic capacity surpassed 40 GW in the mid-2020s, split between utility-scale plants and distributed rooftop generation.

History

Early solar use in Brazil was dominated by solar water heating. Grid photovoltaics expanded after ANEEL's net-metering Resolution 482/2012 enabled distributed generation. The first energy auction reserving capacity for solar (2014) contracted about 890 MW, and utility projects such as Pirapora in Minas Gerais (321 MW) and the São Gonçalo complex in Piauí followed. Rooftop distributed generation grew fastest of all, especially in Minas Gerais, São Paulo and the south, and Law 14,300/2022 set the long-term compensation framework.

Role in the electricity mix

Brazil's grid is historically dominated by hydropower, whose drought vulnerability in 2014–15 and 2021 encouraged diversification into wind and solar. Solar generation peaks at midday and complements wind (stronger at night in the northeast), reducing reservoir drawdown.

Challenges

Constraints include transmission capacity from the northeast, curtailment, financing costs, and grid-integration of millions of small rooftop systems.
