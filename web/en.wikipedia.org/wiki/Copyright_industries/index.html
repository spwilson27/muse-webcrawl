Copyright industries

The copyright industries are those industries whose activity depends substantially on the creation, production, distribution or exhibition of copyrighted material. The World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) divides them into four categories in its guide on surveying the economic contribution of the copyright industries.

Categories

  • Core copyright industries — wholly engaged in creating copyrighted works: press and literature, music, theatre, film and video, radio and television, photography, software and databases, visual and graphic arts, and advertising services.
  • Interdependent copyright industries — manufacture equipment facilitating the creation and use of works, such as televisions, computers, photocopiers and musical instruments.
  • Partial copyright industries — only part of their activity relates to copyright, such as architecture, apparel, design and household goods.
  • Non-dedicated support industries — general distribution, transport and telecommunications that support the others.

Economic significance

National studies using the WIPO methodology (first developed in the United States by the International Intellectual Property Alliance) typically find the core copyright industries contribute several percent of GDP and employment in developed economies; in the United States, the core industries have been estimated at over 5% of GDP and to grow faster than the economy as a whole.
