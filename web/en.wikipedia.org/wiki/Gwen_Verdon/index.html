Gwyneth Evelyn Verdon (January 13, 1925 – October 18, 2000) was an American actress and dancer. She won four Tony Awards for her musical comedy performances, and she served as an uncredited choreographer's assistant and specialty dance coach to theatre and film. Verdon was a critically acclaimed performer on Broadway from the 1950s to the 1970s and the muse of choreographer-director Bob Fosse, her second husband.

Early life

Verdon was born in Culver City, California, the daughter of British immigrants; her father was an electrician at MGM studios and her mother a former vaudeville dancer and dance teacher. As a toddler she was fitted with orthopaedic boots for rickets-like leg problems and channelled into dance lessons, performing professionally from childhood. She studied with the legendary tap and ballet teachers of Los Angeles and worked as an assistant to choreographer Jack Cole.

Career

After small film roles, Verdon broke through on Broadway in Cole Porter's Can-Can (1953), stealing the show and winning her first Tony. She became a star as the seductive Lola in Damn Yankees (1955) — including the signature number "Whatever Lola Wants" — winning another Tony, and reprised the role in the 1958 film. Her partnership with Bob Fosse produced triumphs in New Girl in Town (1957), Redhead (1959, another Tony), Sweet Charity (1966) and Chicago (1975), in which she originated the role of Roxie Hart opposite Chita Rivera. Later film work included Cocoon (1985) and Marvin's Room (1996).

After Fosse's death in 1987, Verdon helped preserve and reconstruct his choreography, serving as artistic adviser on the revue Fosse (1999). Her life and relationship with Fosse were dramatised in the 2019 television series Fosse/Verdon, in which she was played by Michelle Williams. Verdon died in her sleep of natural causes in Woodstock, Vermont, in 2000; Broadway theatres dimmed their lights in her honour.
