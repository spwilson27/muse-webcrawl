Namco

Namco Limited was a Japanese video game developer and publisher, and a predecessor of Bandai Namco Entertainment. Founded in 1955 by Masaya Nakamura as Nakamura Seisakusho, a maker of coin-operated children's rides, it grew into one of the most influential companies in arcade game history.

History

The company entered video games in the 1970s, acquiring the Japanese division of Atari in 1974 and achieving its first great hits with Galaxian (1979) and Toru Iwatani's Pac-Man (1980), the latter becoming the highest-grossing arcade game of all time and a global cultural phenomenon. Further classics followed, including Galaga, Dig Dug, Xevious, Ridge Racer, Tekken, Time Crisis and the Tales role-playing series.

Namco was also a hardware innovator, producing arcade system boards used across the industry and collaborating with Sony on the architecture that fed into the original PlayStation. In 2005–2006 Namco merged with toy maker Bandai to form Namco Bandai Holdings; the Namco name survived for some years in arcade operations and the amusement-facility business.
