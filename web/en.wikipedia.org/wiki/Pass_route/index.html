Pass route

A pass route, also called a receiving route or pattern, is a predetermined path run by a receiver in American football to get open for a forward pass. Routes are drawn on play diagrams and combined into concepts that attack specific defensive coverages.

Common routes

The standard route tree includes: the flat (short, toward the sideline), the slant (sharp diagonal inside), the curl/hitch (downfield then back toward the quarterback), the out (break toward the sideline), the in/dig (break across the middle), the comeback, the corner, the post (diagonal toward the goalpost) and the go/fly (straight deep). Option routes let a receiver choose his break based on the defender's leverage and coverage.

Use

Quarterbacks read the defence and progress through receivers running complementary routes at different depths. Route-running skill — precise stems, breaks without losing speed, and deception — is a defining trait of elite wide receivers and tight ends.
