Appeal to pity

The appeal to pity (also called argumentum ad misericordiam, the sob story, or the Galileo argument) is a fallacy in which someone tries to win support for an argument or idea by exploiting their opponent's feelings of pity or guilt. It is a specific kind of appeal to emotion.

Type: Informal fallacy
Latin name: Argumentum ad misericordiam
Family: Appeal to emotion

Examples

A classic example occurs in courtroom settings, where a defence lawyer may argue that a defendant should not be found guilty because of the hardship that imprisonment would cause to the defendant's family. Another common example is a student telling a teacher that a poor grade would ruin their life, in an attempt to obtain a better grade rather than addressing the quality of the work itself.

Analysis

While feelings of pity or sympathy may sometimes be relevant to a moral judgement, the appeal to pity is fallacious when the emotional plea is used as a substitute for relevant evidence or logical argument. The fallacy diverts attention from the facts of the issue to the suffering of the person making the claim.
