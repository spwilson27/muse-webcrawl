The Ancient Olympic Games were a series of athletic competitions among representatives of Greek city-states, held in honour of Zeus at the sanctuary of Olympia in Elis. Traditionally dated from 776 BC, when the first victor list was kept, they were celebrated every four years — a period called an Olympiad, which Greeks used to date events — for nearly twelve centuries, and were the most prestigious of the Panhellenic Games.

History

The earliest recorded victor was Coroebus of Elis, a cook who won the stadion footrace — for over a decade the Games' only event. Events were added over time: the diaulos (double-length sprint) in 724 BC, wrestling and the pentathlon in 708 BC, boxing in 688 BC, chariot racing in 680 BC, and the pankration in 648 BC. During the Games a sacred truce (ekecheiria) allowed athletes and spectators to travel safely. The festival grew to five days, combining athletics with religious rites, including the sacrifice of a hundred oxen at the great altar of Zeus, whose colossal gold-and-ivory statue by Phidias inside the temple was one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.

The events and athletes

Competitors were free Greek men who trained for ten months and swore oaths before the statue of Zeus Horkios; married women were barred even as spectators (the priestess of Demeter excepted), though women could win as owners of chariot teams. Victors received only an olive wreath (kotinos) at Olympia but were showered with honours, statues, and civic privileges at home. Famous champions included the wrestler Milo of Croton (six victories), the runner Leonidas of Rhodes (twelve crowns across four Games), and the boxer Theagenes of Thasos.

Decline and revival

Under Roman rule the Games continued but declined in status; the emperor Nero notoriously competed in AD 67. They were suppressed in the late 4th century AD as part of the Christian emperors' campaign against pagan festivals (decrees of Theodosius I, traditionally dated to AD 393). The site was excavated by German archaeologists from 1875, and the Games inspired Pierre de Coubertin to found the modern Olympic Games, first held in Athens in 1896.
