Canvas

Canvas
MaterialCotton, linen, hemp
WeavePlain weave
UsesPainting supports, sails, tents, bags, shoes

Canvas is an extremely durable plain-woven fabric used for making sails, tents, marquees, backpacks, shelters, and other items for which sturdiness is required, as well as in such fashion objects as handbags and shoes. It is also popularly used by artists as a painting surface, typically stretched across a wooden frame.

Materials

Modern canvas is usually made of cotton, linen, or synthetic fibres such as polyester, while historically it was woven from hemp — the word "canvas" derives from the Latin cannabis. Cotton duck canvas is graded by weight and by a numbered duck system. Linen canvas, made from flax, is considered the finest support for oil painting because of its strength and resistance to decay.

Use in painting

Canvas as a painting support came into widespread use in Italy during the Renaissance, displacing wooden panels because it was lighter, cheaper, and could be made in large sizes — Venetian painters adopted it early, partly because of the damp lagoon climate's effect on panels. Artists typically stretch the canvas over a wooden stretcher frame and prime it with gesso before painting. So-called "canvas prints" reproduce images on canvas-like material.

Other uses

Because of its toughness, canvas is used for sails and boat covers, awnings, tarpaulins, workwear, conveyor belts, and the uppers of casual shoes. In boxing and wrestling, "the canvas" is the mat covering the ring floor.
