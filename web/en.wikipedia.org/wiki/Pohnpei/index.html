Pohnpei (formerly spelled Ponape) is the largest island of the Federated States of Micronesia, located among the Senyavin Islands in the eastern Caroline Islands of the western Pacific Ocean. It is the seat of Pohnpei State, one of the country's four states, and hosts the national capital, Palikir, in the island's northwest. The island's largest settlement is Kolonia, the state capital. Pohnpei is the most populous and most developed island of the FSM, and receives some of the highest rainfall on Earth, with annual totals in the mountainous interior exceeding 7,600 mm (300 in).

Geography

Pohnpei is a high volcanic island about 13 km across, encircled by a barrier reef that creates one of the largest lagoons in Micronesia. Its rugged interior is dominated by Mount Nanlaud (772 m), the highest point in the FSM, and Mount Takah, and is covered in dense rainforest fed by frequent rain. The island is divided into municipalities including Madolenihmw, Uh, Nett, Sokehs, and Kitti.

History

Pohnpei was settled by Micronesian voyagers more than two thousand years ago. Off its eastern shore lies Nan Madol, a remarkable ruined city built of massive basalt logs on nearly a hundred artificial islets in the lagoon. Constructed mainly between about 1180 and 1500 CE under the Saudeleur Dynasty, Nan Madol served as the ceremonial and political seat of the island's rulers until the dynasty fell to the semi-legendary conqueror Isokelekel, who founded the chiefly (nahnmwarki) system that endures in modified form today.

Spanish explorers sighted the island in the 16th century, and Spain claimed the Carolines, establishing a colony at Kolonia in 1887. After the Spanish–American War, Germany purchased the islands in 1899. Japan seized them in 1914 and administered them under a League of Nations mandate; the United States took control in 1944–45 and administered Pohnpei as part of the Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands until the Federated States of Micronesia entered into free association with the US in 1986.

Culture

The main indigenous language is Pohnpeian, and traditional social organization centers on clans and the nahnahnmwarki and nahnken chiefly lines. Feasting, sakau (kava) preparation and drinking, and yam cultivation hold central cultural importance. The island is also known internationally for surfing at P-Pass (Palikir Pass), one of the world's noted reef breaks.
