Ultramafic rock

Composition: >90% mafic minerals (olivine, pyroxene)
Silica content: <45% SiO2
Examples: Peridotite, dunite, komatiite, kimberlite

Ultramafic rocks are igneous and meta-igneous rocks composed almost entirely of dark, magnesium- and iron-rich (mafic) minerals, with very low silica content (less than 45% SiO2). They consist chiefly of olivine and pyroxene, with little or no feldspar, and make up most of the Earth's upper mantle.

Types

The most important ultramafic rock is peridotite, the dominant rock of the mantle, whose olivine-rich variety is called dunite. Komatiites are ultramafic lavas that erupted mainly in the Archaean, when the Earth's interior was hotter. Kimberlites are volatile-rich ultramafic rocks famous for carrying diamonds from the deep mantle to the surface.

Occurrence and significance

Ultramafic rocks reach the surface in ophiolite complexes — slices of oceanic lithosphere thrust onto continents — and as xenoliths in volcanic rocks. Their alteration produces serpentine soils, poor in nutrients and rich in heavy metals such as nickel and chromium, which host distinctive plant communities. Ultramafic deposits are sources of nickel, platinum-group elements, chromite and asbestos, and their reaction with carbon dioxide has been studied for carbon sequestration.
