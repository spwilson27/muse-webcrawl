National Housing Act (Canada)

National Housing Act
Citation: R.S.C. 1985, c. N-11
Country: Canada
Original enactment: 1938 (Dominion Housing Act 1935 predecessor)
Administered by: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation

The National Housing Act is a statute of the Parliament of Canada that provides the federal framework for housing finance and mortgage insurance in Canada. First enacted in its modern form in 1944, succeeding the Dominion Housing Act of 1935 and an earlier National Housing Act of 1938, it authorizes the Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC) to insure mortgage loans made by approved lenders.

Provisions

Under the Act, borrowers with down payments below 20 percent are generally required to obtain mortgage default insurance, protecting lenders against loss. The Act also supports a range of federal housing programs, including assistance for social and affordable housing, and underpins the issuance of National Housing Act mortgage-backed securities, which help fund residential mortgage lending.

Role in housing policy

The Act has been amended many times to reflect changing housing priorities, from post-war homebuilding to later programs for rental housing and home ownership. It remains the principal legislation governing CMHC's mortgage insurance and securitization activities.
