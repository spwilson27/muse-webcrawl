This article lists the tallest buildings in London. Since 2012, the tallest building in London — and in the United Kingdom — has been The Shard at London Bridge, at 309.6 metres (1,016 ft). London's high-rises are concentrated in two principal financial clusters, the City of London and Canary Wharf in the Docklands, with growing clusters at the South Bank, Nine Elms, and Croydon. For most of its history London was a low-rise city, its skyline dominated by St Paul's Cathedral; planning rules protecting views of the cathedral and the Palace of Westminster still shape where towers may be built.

History

London's first true skyscraper was Tower 42 (1980), the former NatWest Tower, which held the city height record until One Canada Square topped out in 1990 as the centerpiece of the Canary Wharf redevelopment. The 2000s–2010s brought the distinctive City cluster — the Gherkin, Cheesegrater, Walkie Talkie, and Scalpel — several nicknamed for their shapes, and The Shard, designed by Renzo Piano, which reshaped the skyline south of the river. Residential towers have since spread tall buildings to Nine Elms, Stratford, and Croydon, though height limits and protected viewing corridors remain central to planning debates.
