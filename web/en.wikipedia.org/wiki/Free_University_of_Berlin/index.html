The Free University of Berlin (German: Freie Universität Berlin, abbreviated FU Berlin) is a public research university in Berlin, Germany. It is one of Germany's most prestigious universities and a member of the German Universities Excellence Initiative.

History

The university was founded on December 4, 1948, in the American sector of West Berlin, after students and scholars at the University of Berlin (Humboldt University) in the Soviet sector faced political repression and expulsions. With support from the American military government and funds from the Ford Foundation, the new university was established in the leafy district of Dahlem under the motto Veritas, Iustitia, Libertas — Truth, Justice, Freedom. John F. Kennedy spoke at the university in June 1963 during his famous Berlin visit.

In the late 1960s the FU was a center of the German student movement; figures such as Rudi Dutschke studied there. After reunification the university expanded while absorbing changes in Berlin's academic landscape.

Academics

The FU Berlin enrolls roughly 33,000 students across departments in the humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, law, economics, and medicine (through the Charité, shared with Humboldt University). It is known for its institutes for political science (Otto Suhr Institute), North American studies (John F. Kennedy Institute), and East European studies, and for a large international exchange network. Its campus includes the Philological Library, the "Berlin Brain" designed by Norman Foster.
