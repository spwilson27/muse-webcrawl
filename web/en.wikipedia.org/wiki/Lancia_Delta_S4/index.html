Lancia Delta S4

ManufacturerLancia (Abarth)
Production1985–1986 (200 road cars for homologation)
ClassGroup B rally car
LayoutMid-engine, four-wheel drive
Engine1,759 cc supercharged + turbocharged inline-four
Power output~480 hp (rally trim, est.); 250 hp (Stradale)

The Lancia Delta S4 is a Group B rally car built by Italian manufacturer Lancia in the mid-1980s. Despite its name and general silhouette, it shared almost nothing with the production Delta hatchback: it was a purpose-built mid-engined machine with a tubular space frame and composite bodywork.

Design

The Delta S4's 1,759 cc engine was unique in being both supercharged and turbocharged ("twincharging"): a volumetric supercharger provided low-rpm boost while a large turbocharger took over at high revs, virtually eliminating turbo lag. Official rally output was around 480 hp, and the car could accelerate from 0–100 km/h in about 2.5 seconds on gravel. Two hundred Delta S4 Stradale road cars were built to satisfy Group B homologation.

Competition and legacy

The S4 debuted at the 1985 RAC Rally, winning outright with Henri Toivonen. On the 1986 Tour de Corse, Toivonen and co-driver Sergio Cresto died when their S4 plunged off the road and burned, an accident — following other fatal crashes — that led the FISA to abolish Group B after the 1986 season. The Delta S4 remains an icon of rallying's most spectacular and dangerous era.
