Hexamilion wall

The Hexamilion wall (Greek: Εξαμίλιον) was a defensive wall built across the Isthmus of Corinth in late antiquity to protect the Peloponnese, the only land route into southern Greece. Its name means "six miles", roughly its length.

History

Earlier fortifications on the isthmus date to classical times. The Hexamilion was built under Theodosius II in the early 5th century AD, incorporating towers and a fortress, and was repeatedly repaired — by Justinian, and in the Byzantine and early Ottoman periods. It was breached on several occasions, including by Alaric's Goths before its construction and by Murad II in 1446.

Remains

Substantial stretches survive near the Corinth Canal, excavated by the American School of Classical Studies.
