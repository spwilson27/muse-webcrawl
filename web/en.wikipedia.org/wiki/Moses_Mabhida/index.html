Moses Mabhida (14 October 1923 – 8 March 1986) was a South African politician and anti-apartheid activist. A leading figure in the African National Congress (ANC), the South African Communist Party (SACP) and the trade union movement, he served as general secretary of the SACP from 1978 until his death and was a senior commander of Umkhonto we Sizwe, the ANC's armed wing.

Early life and trade unionism

Born near Pietermaritzburg in Natal, Mabhida was drawn into the labour movement in the 1940s, helping to organise workers in Durban and the Howick rubber factory. He joined the ANC and the Communist Party, and became a full-time organiser for the South African Congress of Trade Unions (SACTU).

Exile and armed struggle

After the banning of the ANC in 1960, Mabhida went into exile, receiving military training abroad and rising to senior positions in Umkhonto we Sizwe. He represented SACTU internationally and sat on the ANC's Revolutionary Council.

Death and honours

Mabhida died in Maputo, Mozambique, in 1986 and was buried there; his remains were repatriated to South Africa in 2006. The Moses Mabhida Stadium in Durban, built for the 2010 FIFA World Cup, is named in his honour.
