Load cell

A load cell is a transducer used to convert a mechanical force, such as tension, compression, pressure or torque, into a measurable electrical signal. The magnitude of the electrical signal is proportional to the force applied. Load cells are used in weighing scales, industrial testing machines, hopper and tank weighing, and structural monitoring.

Types

  • Strain gauge load cells — the most common type. Metal foil strain gauges are bonded to a precisely machined elastic element; deformation under load changes the gauges' electrical resistance, usually measured with a Wheatstone bridge. Common geometries include beam, S-type, canister and pancake cells.
  • Hydraulic load cells — force on a piston compresses a fluid, and the resulting pressure is measured; robust and used in harsh environments.
  • Pneumatic load cells — use air pressure balanced against a diaphragm; intrinsically safe and suited to clean environments.
  • Piezoelectric load cells — generate a charge proportional to force; suited to dynamic measurements.
  • Capacitive load cells — measure changes in capacitance of a sensor element under load.

Strain gauge operation

A typical strain gauge load cell contains four strain gauges in a full Wheatstone bridge, excited by a stable voltage (commonly 5–12 V). Output sensitivity is specified in millivolts per volt (typically 2 mV/V) at rated capacity. Accuracy is affected by nonlinearity, hysteresis, creep under sustained load, and temperature effects, which are compensated in manufacture. Rated capacities range from fractions of a newton in miniature cells to thousands of tonnes in compression canisters.

Applications

Electronic bathroom and kitchen scales, truck and rail weighbridges, aircraft weighing, materials testing machines, crane overload protection, and process control in food, chemical and pharmaceutical production all rely on load cells. Combined with modern digital indicators they form the basis of most legal-for-trade weighing systems.
