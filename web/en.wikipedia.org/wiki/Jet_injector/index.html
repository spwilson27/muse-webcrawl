Jet injector

A jet injector is a type of medical injecting device that uses a high-pressure stream of liquid to penetrate the skin and deliver medication without a needle. Jet injection has been used for mass vaccination campaigns and for insulin delivery.

Mechanism

The device uses a power source — a spring, compressed gas, or an electric motor — to force a fine jet of fluid through a tiny orifice at very high velocity. The jet pierces the epidermis and deposits the dose in subcutaneous or intramuscular tissue.

History and use

Jet injectors were developed in the mid-20th century and widely used by the military and in public-health vaccination drives, including smallpox eradication efforts. Concerns about cross-contamination with reusable multi-use nozzle devices led to disposable-cartridge designs.
