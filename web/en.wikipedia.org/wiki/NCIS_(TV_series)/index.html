NCIS (TV series)

NCIS is an American police procedural television series, revolving around a fictional team of special agents from the Naval Criminal Investigative Service (NCIS), the primary law enforcement agency of the U.S. Department of the Navy. Created by Donald P. Bellisario and Don McGill, it premiered on CBS on 23 September 2003 as a spin-off of JAG, and has become one of the longest-running and most-watched scripted series in television history.

Premise and cast

The series follows the Major Case Response Team based at the Washington Navy Yard, led for its first 19 seasons by Supervisory Special Agent Leroy Jethro Gibbs (Mark Harmon), a former Marine gunnery sergeant known for his rules, head-slaps and taciturn manner. Long-serving characters have included forensic specialist Abby Sciuto (Pauley Perrette), medical examiner Donald "Ducky" Mallard (David McCallum), Special Agent Timothy McGee (Sean Murray), Ziva David (Cote de Pablo), Anthony DiNozzo (Michael Weatherly) and Director Leon Vance (Rocky Carroll). After Harmon's departure in 2021, Gary Cole joined as team leader Alden Parker.

Reception and franchise

NCIS has repeatedly been among the most-watched dramas in the United States and worldwide, at times ranked the most-watched scripted show on American television. It launched a large franchise including NCIS: Los Angeles, NCIS: New Orleans, NCIS: Hawaiʻi, NCIS: Sydney and prequel/origin series. The show is noted for its blend of procedural cases, character humour and military setting.
