The Edwards Plateau is a geographic and ecological region in central Texas, forming part of the southern Great Plains where they meet the hills of the Texas Hill Country. It is bounded by the Balcones Fault and the Blackland Prairies to the east, the Llano Estacado to the north and the Chihuahuan Desert and Rio Grande to the west and south. The plateau takes its name from Haden Edwards, an early land speculator in Mexican Texas.

Geology and ecology

The plateau consists of uplifted Cretaceous limestone — the Edwards Group — dissected by spring-fed rivers whose canyons cut through thin soils. The karst limestone hosts the Edwards Aquifer, one of the most prolific artesian aquifers in the world and the source of major springs at San Antonio, New Braunfels, San Marcos and Austin, as well as extensive cave systems. Natural vegetation is juniper–oak savanna and grassland adapted to recurring fire; overgrazing and fire suppression allowed Ashe juniper and mesquite to spread. The ecoregion is rich in endemic cave invertebrates and provides breeding habitat for the endangered golden-cheeked warbler and black-capped vireo. Ranching (cattle, sheep and Angora goats) historically dominated land use; the Hill Country fringe near Austin and San Antonio is now rapidly urbanising.
