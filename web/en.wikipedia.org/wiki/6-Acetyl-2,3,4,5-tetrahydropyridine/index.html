6-Acetyl-2,3,4,5-tetrahydropyridine

6-Acetyl-2,3,4,5-tetrahydropyridine
Chemical formulaC7H11NO
Molar mass125.17 g/mol
Related compound2-Acetyl-1-pyrroline
OdourFresh-baked bread, popcorn-like
FormationMaillard reaction

6-Acetyl-2,3,4,5-tetrahydropyridine is an aroma compound that, together with its close analogue 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline, is responsible for the characteristic smell of freshly baked bread crust, as well as flavours in foods such as popcorn and cooked rice dishes. It has an extremely low odour threshold, so trace amounts dominate the perceived aroma.

Formation

The compound forms during heating through the Maillard reaction between amino acids (particularly proline and ornithine derivatives) and reducing sugars; in bread it is generated in the crust during baking. Its six-membered nitrogen ring distinguishes it from the five-membered 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline, which predominates in aromatic rice varieties such as basmati and jasmine, and in pandan leaves.

Occurrence and use

Besides bread, the compound and its analogues have been identified in popcorn, tortillas, crackers and roasted products. Flavour chemists regard them as impact odorants — single molecules that capture much of the smell of the whole food — and they have been used as reference standards in aroma research and in attempts to reproduce baked flavours.
