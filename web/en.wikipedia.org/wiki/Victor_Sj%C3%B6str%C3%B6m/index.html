Victor David Sjöström (20 September 1879 – 3 January 1960), sometimes credited in the United States as Victor Seastrom, was a Swedish film director, screenwriter and actor. Together with Mauritz Stiller, he is regarded as one of the pioneers of Swedish cinema and a central figure of the so-called golden age of Swedish silent film.

Early life

Sjöström was born in Silbodal, Värmland County, and grew up in Stockholm. After his mother's death he was sent to live with relatives in Riga, an experience that left a lasting impression on him. He returned to Sweden as a young man and took up acting in travelling theatre companies before joining the film industry in 1912.

Career

At Svenska Biografteatern, Sjöström directed a series of films that helped establish Swedish cinema's international reputation for naturalistic acting and the expressive use of landscape. His notable works include Ingeborg Holm (1913), a landmark of social realism; Terje Vigen (1917), based on Henrik Ibsen's poem; The Outlaw and His Wife (1918); and The Phantom Carriage (1921), celebrated for its innovative use of double exposure and non-linear narrative.

In the 1920s Sjöström worked in Hollywood under the name Victor Seastrom, directing films such as He Who Gets Slapped (1924), starring Lon Chaney, the first film released by Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, and The Wind (1928), starring Lillian Gish. With the coming of sound he returned to Sweden, where he directed fewer films and devoted himself increasingly to acting.

Later years and legacy

Sjöström's final acting role was his widely praised performance as the elderly Professor Isak Borg in Ingmar Bergman's Wild Strawberries (1957). He died in Stockholm in 1960. His work influenced generations of filmmakers, and The Phantom Carriage is regularly ranked among the greatest silent films ever made.
