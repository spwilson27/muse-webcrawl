Demolition

Demolition is the science and engineering of safely and efficiently tearing down buildings and other artificial structures. Demolition contrasts with deconstruction, which involves taking a building apart carefully while preserving valuable elements for reuse.

Methods

Small buildings are usually demolished manually or mechanically with excavators, cranes, bulldozers and wrecking balls. Larger structures, such as tower blocks, chimneys and bridges, may be brought down by implosion: explosives are placed on key structural supports and detonated in sequence so the structure collapses inward onto its own footprint. Before demolition, salvageable materials are removed and hazardous substances such as asbestos and lead are stripped by specialists.

Safety and regulation

Demolition involves risks from falling debris, dust, noise and uncontrolled collapse, and is usually subject to permits, engineered demolition plans and environmental controls. Modern practice emphasises recycling: concrete, steel and other materials from demolition waste make up a large share of construction and demolition waste streams, much of which can be recovered.
