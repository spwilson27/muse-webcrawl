West Frisian Islands


Dune landscapes of the Dutch Wadden Islands.

The West Frisian Islands (Dutch: Waddeneilanden) are a chain of barrier islands in the Wadden Sea off the northern coast of the Netherlands, forming part of the wider Frisian Islands that extend along the Dutch, German, and Danish coasts. They separate the shallow Wadden Sea from the North Sea and are known for their dunes, beaches, tidal flats, and nature reserves.

The islands

The inhabited Dutch islands, from west to east, are Texel, Vlieland, Terschelling, Ameland, and Schiermonnikoog. Texel is the largest and belongs to the province of North Holland; the others are part of Friesland. There are also several smaller uninhabited islands and sandbanks, such as Noorderhaaks, Griend, and Rottumeroog. Much of the archipelago is protected within national parks and the UNESCO World Heritage-listed Wadden Sea, an internationally important habitat for migratory birds and seals.

Economy and access

Tourism is the mainstay of the island economies, and most islands restrict car traffic. Ferries connect them to mainland ports such as Den Helder, Harlingen, Holwerd, and Lauwersoog. A distinctive regional pastime is wadlopen, walking across the tidal flats to the islands at low tide with a guide.
