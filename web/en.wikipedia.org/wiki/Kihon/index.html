Kihon

Kihon (Japanese: 基本, "basics" or "fundamentals") is a term used in Japanese martial arts — especially karate — for the basic techniques and foundational training from which all advanced practice is built. Together with kata (forms) and kumite (sparring), kihon forms one of the three pillars of karate training, sometimes called the "three Ks".

Content

Kihon training drills the elementary building blocks of the art: stances (dachi), punches (tsuki), strikes (uchi), blocks (uke) and kicks (geri), performed first singly, then in combination, stationary and moving, often in lines across the dojo floor. Repetition is intended to ingrain correct posture, hip rotation, breathing, balance and the explosive release of power (kime) until technique becomes instinctive.

Role in training

Instructors emphasise that kata and kumite are only as good as the kihon beneath them: "kihon is everything". Grading examinations in styles such as Shotokan, Goju-ryu and Shito-ryu test set kihon combinations at each belt level. The term is also used in kendo, aikido and other budo for fundamental exercises, and outside martial arts generally means "the basics" of any discipline.
