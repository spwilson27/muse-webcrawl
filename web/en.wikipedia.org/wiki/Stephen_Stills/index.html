Stephen Arthur Stills (born 3 January 1945) is an American singer, songwriter and multi-instrumentalist best known for his work with Buffalo Springfield, Crosby, Stills & Nash, and as a solo artist. He is one of the few musicians inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice on the same night (1997), for his work with both Buffalo Springfield and CSN.

Career

Raised partly in Latin America, Stills absorbed folk, blues, rock and Latin influences. After the Au Go Go Singers and a failed audition for The Monkees, he co-founded Buffalo Springfield in Los Angeles in 1966 with Neil Young and Richie Furay, writing their hit "For What It's Worth". Following Springfield's breakup in 1968 he formed Crosby, Stills & Nash with David Crosby and Graham Nash; their debut album (1969) and the quartet album Déjà Vu (1970, with Young) were landmarks of the era, featuring Stills's "Suite: Judy Blue Eyes", "Helplessly Hoping" and "Carry On".

Stills's self-titled solo debut (1970) included "Love the One You're With" and contributions from Eric Clapton and Jimi Hendrix (whose final studio recording appeared on Stills's 1975 album). With Chris Hillman he formed Manassas (1972), blending rock, country and Latin music. He later collaborated with Judy Collins (Everybody Knows, 2017) and in the blues-rock supergroup The Rides with Kenny Wayne Shepherd and Barry Goldberg. Rolling Stone ranked Stills among the 100 greatest guitarists of all time.
