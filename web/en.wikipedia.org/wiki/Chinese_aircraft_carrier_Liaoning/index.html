Liaoning (16; Chinese: 辽宁舰; pinyin: Liáoníng Jiàn) is the first aircraft carrier commissioned into the People's Liberation Army Navy (PLAN) Surface Force. It was laid down as the unfinished Soviet aircraft-carrying cruiser Varyag in the Soviet Union, purchased by China in 1998 as a hulk, towed from Ukraine in 2001–02, and completed and refitted at Dalian Shipyard before commissioning on 25 September 2012.

The ship is a STOBAR-type carrier — using a ski-jump for Short Take-Off but Arrested Recovery of its J-15 fighter aircraft, lacking catapults. With a displacement of roughly 60,900 tonnes, it is substantially smaller than U.S. Navy supercarriers. Liaoning has served primarily as a training and development platform for the PLAN's carrier aviation programme, making her the foundation of China's carrier capability and the basis for the indigenous Shandong, which commissioned in 2019.

Acquisition and refit

After the Soviet Union's collapse, Varyag remained unfinished in Mykolaiv, Ukraine. A Macau-based company ostensibly intending a floating casino bought the hulk in 1998; the purchase was soon understood to be for the Chinese navy. The tow to China took until early 2002, passing through the Bosporus under tight restrictions. Refit at Dalian lasted from 2005 to 2011, with sea trials beginning in 2011. In November 2012 a J-15 made the first arrested landing on her deck.

Service

Liaoning made her first transit through the Taiwan Strait in 2017 and has since conducted carrier-group exercises in the Western Pacific, including passages through the Miyako Strait and operations east of Taiwan. She was renamed for Liaoning province in Northeast China, where Dalian is located.
