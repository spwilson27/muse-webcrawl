Edwards v. Aguillard

Edwards v. Aguillard, 482 U.S. 578 (1987), was a United States Supreme Court case concerning the teaching of creation science in public schools. The Court held that a Louisiana law requiring that creation science be taught alongside evolution whenever evolution was taught violated the Establishment Clause of the First Amendment, because the law lacked a clear secular purpose.

Background

Louisiana's Balanced Treatment for Creation-Science and Evolution-Science in Public School Instruction Act forbade the teaching of evolution in public schools unless accompanied by instruction in "creation science." The law's stated purpose was to protect academic freedom. Teachers challenged the act, and the case was named for Edwin W. Edwards, the governor of Louisiana, and Don Aguillard, a teacher who was a plaintiff.

Decision

In a 7–2 decision written by Justice William J. Brennan Jr., the Court struck down the act. Applying the Lemon test from Lemon v. Kurtzman, the Court found that the act failed the first prong: it did not have a genuinely secular legislative purpose but was instead intended to advance a particular religious viewpoint, namely the belief that a supernatural being created humankind. The Court noted that the act did not in fact further its stated goal of academic freedom, since it gave teachers no new flexibility beyond what already existed.

Justice Antonin Scalia, joined by Chief Justice William Rehnquist, dissented, arguing that the legislature's stated purpose of academic freedom should be accepted and that creation science could be taught as a scientific theory.

Significance

The decision, together with the earlier ruling in Epperson v. Arkansas (1968), which struck down state bans on teaching evolution, made it unconstitutional for public schools either to ban evolution or to mandate the teaching of creation science as a counterweight. Advocates of alternatives to evolution later promoted intelligent design, which was itself addressed by the courts in Kitzmiller v. Dover Area School District (2005).
