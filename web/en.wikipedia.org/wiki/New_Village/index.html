New Village

This article is about the resettlement settlements in Malaya. For other uses, see New Village (disambiguation).

New Villages (Chinese: 新村; Malay: Kampung Baru) are planned settlements created in the Federation of Malaya during the Malayan Emergency (1948–1960) under the Briggs Plan. Roughly 450 New Villages were established, into which about half a million mostly rural Chinese squatters were resettled.

Background and purpose

Named after General Sir Harold Briggs, Director of Operations, the plan aimed to cut the Malayan Communist Party's guerrillas off from food, intelligence, and recruits supplied by squatters living on the jungle fringe. Villages were enclosed with barbed wire, guarded, and subject to curfews and food controls. Resettlement was coercive and living conditions were initially spartan, though schools, clinics, and utilities were gradually provided.

Legacy

After the Emergency, the villages remained permanent communities. Today there are more than 600 recognized Chinese New Villages in Peninsular Malaysia, many now suburbs of growing towns, retaining distinctive layouts of houses on small plots along straight streets, and they are a recognized part of Malaysia's social and political landscape.
