Frequency allocation (or spectrum allocation) is the part of spectrum management dealing with the designation of electromagnetic spectrum for specific purposes. The radio spectrum is divided into frequency bands, and each band is allocated to one or more radiocommunication services — such as broadcasting, mobile, fixed, satellite, radionavigation or amateur radio — under specified conditions.

International framework

Allocation is coordinated internationally by the International Telecommunication Union through its Radio Regulations, which are revised at World Radiocommunication Conferences. The world is divided into three ITU Regions, and the Table of Frequency Allocations in Article 5 of the Radio Regulations specifies which services may operate in each band in each region. Allocations may be primary or secondary; secondary services must not cause harmful interference to primary services and cannot claim protection from them.

National assignment

Within the international framework, national regulators — such as the Federal Communications Commission in the United States or Ofcom in the United Kingdom — assign specific frequencies or channels to individual users and licensees, often through auctions, comparative hearings or licence-exempt designations. Assignment must conform to the allocation table so that, for example, a band allocated to broadcasting is not assigned to a mobile service unless the table permits it.
