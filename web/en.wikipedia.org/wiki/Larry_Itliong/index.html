Larry Itliong

Larry Dulay Itliong (October 25, 1913 – February 8, 1977) was a Filipino-American labor organizer and civil rights activist. He is best known for organizing the Delano grape strike of 1965 and for helping to bring together Filipino and Mexican farm workers into the alliance that became the United Farm Workers (UFW).

Early life

Itliong was born in San Nicolas, Pangasinan, in the Philippines. He immigrated to the United States in 1929 or 1930, arriving in Seattle with little formal education. He worked in canneries in Alaska and in the fields of California and Washington, experiencing firsthand the harsh conditions faced by Filipino migrant workers, known as manongs.

Labor organizing

By the 1930s Itliong was active in organizing cannery and agricultural workers. After serving in the United States Army during World War II, he continued his union work with the Agricultural Workers Organizing Committee (AWOC), an AFL-CIO affiliate, becoming its assistant director. On September 8, 1965, Itliong led some 1,500 Filipino grape workers in walking out of the vineyards around Delano, California, demanding wages equal to the federal minimum wage. Eight days later, Cesar Chavez and Dolores Huerta's National Farm Workers Association voted to join the strike, merging the two movements.

Later life and legacy

Itliong served as assistant director of the United Farm Workers under Chavez but resigned in 1971 over disagreements with the union's direction. He remained active in the Filipino-American community in Delano until his death in 1977. In 2015, California declared October 25 as Larry Itliong Day, and his role in the farm labor movement has gained wider recognition, including a mural and memorial in Los Angeles's Historic Filipinotown.
