Howardite

Howardites are a group of achondritic stony meteorites belonging to the HED clan — howardites, eucrites and diogenites — which are believed to originate from the crust of the asteroid 4 Vesta. They are named after the English chemist Edward Howard (1774–1816), a pioneer of meteoritics.

Composition

Howardites are polymict breccias: mixtures of rock fragments (clasts) of eucrite and diogenite material, together with impact-melted debris and small amounts of carbonaceous chondrite material delivered by impacts. They represent the regolith — the pulverised surface layer — of Vesta, lithified by subsequent impacts. Their oxygen isotope ratios and mineral chemistry match those of eucrites and diogenites, supporting a common parent body.

NASA's Dawn spacecraft, which orbited Vesta in 2011–2012, confirmed the connection between the HED meteorites and Vesta's surface, including the great southern impact basin Rheasilvia from which most Vesta material is thought to have been excavated.
