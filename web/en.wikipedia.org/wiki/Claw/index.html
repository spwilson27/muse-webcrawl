Claw

Retractable claws of a cat. Claws are curved, pointed appendages made of keratin.

A claw is a curved, pointed appendage found at the end of a toe or finger in many mammals, birds, and some reptiles. It is made of the protein keratin, like nails and hooves, to which it is closely related: a nail is a flattened claw, and a hoof is a claw that has thickened and wrapped around the end of the toe. Claws are typically used for catching and holding prey, digging, climbing, grooming and defence.

Structure and growth

A claw is produced by a growth plate at its base and consists of two layers: a hard, superficial unguis that forms the curved point, and a softer, flaky underlayer, the subunguis. The claw sheathes and protects the tip of the underlying bone (the ungual phalanx). Because claws are worn down by use, they grow continuously. In cats and many other felids the claws are protractile — held off the ground by elastic ligaments at rest, keeping them sharp, and extended by contraction of the deep flexor tendon when needed.

In different groups

  • Mammals — Carnivorans use claws to seize prey and climb; felids (except the cheetah, whose claws are semi-retractable) retract theirs, while canids have blunt, non-retractable claws suited to traction. Sloths and pangolins have enormous digging and climbing claws; primates have largely replaced claws with nails, though some (such as marmosets) retain claw-like tegulae.
  • Birds — Claws on the feet are called talons, especially in birds of prey, where they are the primary weapons for killing. Some birds also bear claws on the wing; hoatzin chicks famously climb with two wing claws.
  • Reptiles and amphibians — Most reptiles have claws on their toes; the African clawed frog sports keratinous claws despite being an amphibian.
  • Arthropods — The pincers of crabs, lobsters and scorpions are called claws or chelae, though they are not homologous with vertebrate claws.

Human interaction

Domestic animals' claws are routinely trimmed, and the surgical removal of cats' claws (onychectomy, "declawing") is controversial and banned in a number of jurisdictions. In falconry and animal husbandry the condition of claws and talons is an important indicator of health.
