Wildlife art

Wildlife art depicts animals in their natural settings.

Wildlife art is artwork that takes wild animals as its subject, typically depicting them in their natural habitats. It spans painting, sculpture, printmaking and photography, and ranges from scientifically exact illustration to looser interpretive work. It differs from animal art featuring domesticated animals, though the boundary is not strict.

History

Depictions of animals are among the oldest art known, from prehistoric cave paintings onward. Albrecht Dürer's studies of animals set an early standard for naturalistic rendering. In the 19th century, John James Audubon's The Birds of America (1827–1838) combined scientific ambition with artistic drama, and Archibald Thorburn became a leading British painter of birds. The 20th century saw wildlife art flourish alongside the conservation movement, with artists such as Robert Bateman bringing field observation and ecological awareness to popular audiences.

Practice

Wildlife artists typically study anatomy, behaviour and habitat in the field, making sketches and photographs before completing studio works. The field supports major exhibitions, conservation fundraising (such as duck stamp programmes), and specialist societies devoted to art of the natural world.
