Welch's method

Welch's method, named after Peter D. Welch, who described it in a 1967 paper, is a technique for estimating the power spectrum of a signal. It improves on Bartlett's method by averaging modified periodograms computed from overlapping windowed segments of the data.

Procedure

The signal is divided into segments that typically overlap by 50%. Each segment is multiplied by a window function — such as a Hann window — to reduce spectral leakage, its periodogram is computed (usually via the FFT), and the resulting periodograms are averaged. Windowing reduces leakage at the cost of frequency resolution, while averaging lowers the variance of the estimate at the cost of bias and resolution determined by the segment length.

Applications

Welch's method is one of the most widely used spectral estimators in engineering and science, implemented in tools such as MATLAB's pwelch and SciPy's welch. It is applied in vibration analysis, acoustics, biomedical signal processing such as EEG analysis, and communications.
