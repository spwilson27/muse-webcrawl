Governor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A governor is an administrative leader and head of a polity or political territory (a state, province, colony or department), ranking under the head of state, and in some cases, such as governors-general, as the head of state's official representative. Governors typically direct the executive administration of their territory, with powers varying from appointed prefect-like roles to powerful elected offices.

Forms

The strongest elected governors are those of US states: chief executives with vetoes (including line-item vetoes in most states), pardon powers, budget authority and command of state National Guards, elected for two- or four-year terms. In federations elsewhere, governors or premiers head states and provinces (Australia, Canada — where premiers are the equivalents and "governor" is vice-regal — India, Nigeria, Brazil, Mexico). Colonial governors historically wielded sweeping authority as the Crown's or metropole's delegates; governors-general continue as the monarch's representatives in Commonwealth realms. The title also serves appointed territorial administrators (Russia's regional governors were appointed between 2005 and 2012), central-bank governors, and heads of institutions from prisons to the Bank of England (whose chief is the Governor).
