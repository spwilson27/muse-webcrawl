MIT OpenCourseWare (MIT OCW) is an initiative of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology to publish course materials from its undergraduate and graduate teaching freely and openly on the web. Announced in 2001, it is widely credited with launching the global open educational resources movement.

History

The project grew out of a 1999 faculty committee charged with studying how MIT should position itself in distance education. Rather than charging for online courses, the committee recommended giving materials away, and the institute committed to publishing materials for essentially all of its roughly 1,800 courses. The first proof-of-concept site opened in 2002 with 50 courses; by 2007, materials for more than 1,800 courses were online.

Course packages typically include syllabi, lecture notes, assignments, and exams; many include full video lectures, with popular series such as Gilbert Strang's linear algebra and Walter Lewin's physics lectures reaching large global audiences. In 2021 the platform was relaunched on a new publishing system as part of MIT Open Learning.

Impact

MIT OCW has reported hundreds of millions of visitors. It inspired the formation of the OpenCourseWare Consortium (later the Open Education Consortium) and similar projects at universities worldwide.
