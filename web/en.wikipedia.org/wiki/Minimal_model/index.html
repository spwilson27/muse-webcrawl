Minimal model

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Minimal model may refer to several related technical concepts in mathematics and physics:

Minimal models arise when a complex object is simplified until no further contraction is possible.

Meanings

Algebraic geometry

In birational geometry, a minimal model of a smooth projective variety is a variety birational to it that is in a suitable sense simplest: for surfaces, a smooth surface containing no (−1)-curves, obtained by repeatedly contracting such curves, as in the classical theory completed by the Italian school and Castelnuovo's criterion. The minimal model program (MMP), initiated by Shigefumi Mori in the 1980s, generalises this to higher dimensions using flips and divisorial contractions, with the aim of classifying algebraic varieties. Mori received the Fields Medal in 1990 for this work, and the programme's threefold case was a landmark achievement completed in part by Caucher Birkar and collaborators for varieties of general type.

Conformal field theory

In theoretical physics, minimal models are a class of exactly solvable two-dimensional conformal field theories introduced by Belavin, Polyakov and Zamolodchikov in 1984. Labelled by a pair of coprime integers (p, q), they have central charge c = 1 − 6(p − q)²/(pq) and only finitely many primary fields. The simplest examples describe critical points of statistical systems: the (3,4) model is the critical Ising model, and the (4,5) model the tricritical Ising model.

Model theory

In mathematical logic, a minimal model may denote a model of a theory with no proper submodel that is also a model of the theory, or — in non-monotonic logic and answer-set programming — a model minimal with respect to set inclusion of true atoms, as in circumscription and the semantics of logic programs.

Other uses

In set theory, the minimal model of ZFC is the smallest transitive model of the axioms, related to Gödel's constructible universe. The term is also used informally in systems biology and climate science for the simplest model capturing a phenomenon of interest.
