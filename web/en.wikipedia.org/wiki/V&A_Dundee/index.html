V&A Dundee is a design museum in Dundee, Scotland, which opened on 15 September 2018. It is the first V&A museum in the world outside London and Scotland's first dedicated design museum, exploring the creativity and innovation of Scottish design alongside international touring exhibitions from the Victoria and Albert Museum. It stands on the city's waterfront at the foot of the River Tay, beside the museum ship RRS Discovery.

Architecture

The building was designed by Japanese architect Kengo Kuma, whose competition-winning design evokes the dramatic cliffs of Scotland's north-east coast and the prow of a ship. Its curving, prow-like form is clad in 2,500 precast concrete panels of varying lengths, and it sits partly over the river on a purpose-built platform. The project was the centrepiece of Dundee's £1 billion waterfront regeneration and drew on the "Bilbao effect" of landmark cultural architecture; final costs rose to about £80 million.

Collections and galleries

The museum's Scottish Design Galleries display some 300 objects spanning centuries: Charles Rennie Mackintosh's Oak Room — a complete 1907 Glasgow tearoom interior, painstakingly restored and reassembled — is the centrepiece, alongside works from Christopher Dresser to contemporary fashion, video games, and Harris Tweed. Dundee's own design heritage, including comics (the city is home to DC Thomson, publisher of The Beano) and the games industry (the birthplace of Grand Theft Auto), features prominently.

Reception

The museum welcomed its millionth visitor ahead of schedule and was credited with boosting Dundee's profile and visitor economy, following the city's designation as a UNESCO City of Design in 2014. A replica of the building's form also appears in the museum's branding of Scotland's Year of Stories and other national programmes.
