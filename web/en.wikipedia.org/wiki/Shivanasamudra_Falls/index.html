Shivanasamudra Falls

Shivanasamudra Falls is a waterfall on the Kaveri River in the Mandya and Chamarajanagar districts of Karnataka, India, near the island town of Shivanasamudra. The river splits into two branches around the island, forming two falls: Gaganachukki on the western branch and Bharachukki on the eastern branch.

Description

The falls are segmented waterfalls about 90 metres (300 feet) high and roughly 305 metres (1,000 feet) wide at Gaganachukki, with a substantial average annual flow, heaviest during the monsoon season from June to September. They are among the best-known waterfalls of Karnataka and a popular excursion from Bengaluru and Mysuru.

Power generation

Shivanasamudra was the site of one of Asia's first hydroelectric power stations, established in 1902 to supply electricity to the Kolar Gold Fields, over 100 kilometres away—then one of the longest transmission lines in the world. The power station was championed by Dewan Sir M. Visvesvaraya and the Maharaja of Mysore, and a second station followed at nearby Shimsha. The falls' name, meaning "Shiva's sea," is associated with local legend.
