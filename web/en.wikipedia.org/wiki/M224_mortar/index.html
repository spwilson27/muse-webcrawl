The M224 is a smoothbore, muzzle-loading, 60 mm lightweight infantry mortar used by the United States Army and Marine Corps. It entered service in 1978, replacing the older M2 and M19 60 mm mortars, and provides close indirect fire support for infantry companies.

Description

The M224 can be fired in a conventional mode mounted on a bipod with a baseplate, weighing about 46.5 pounds (21 kg), or in a handheld mode using a smaller baseplate and a trigger-like handgrip, weighing about 18 pounds (8.2 kg). In handheld mode it is aimed using a simple engraved range scale on the handle. Ammunition types include high explosive, smoke, illumination and practice rounds. A laser rangefinder and the M64 sight unit support conventional employment.

Replacement

The M224 is being supplemented and replaced by the lighter M224A1, which reduced weight by about 20 percent through the use of lighter materials while retaining the same range and lethality. The mortar remains a standard weapon in US infantry, airborne and special operations units, having seen service in conflicts from Grenada and Panama through Iraq and Afghanistan.
