Post-credits scene

Audiences remaining in their seats during the credits in anticipation of an extra scene.

A post-credits scene (also known as a stinger, tag, coda, or button) is a short clip that appears after all or part of the closing credits of a film, television episode or video game have run. Such scenes are typically a joke, an epilogue, or a teaser that sets up a sequel or spin-off.

History

Early examples include The Silencers (1966), which trailed the next Matt Helm film, and The Muppet Movie (1979), in which Animal urged the audience to "go home". Through the 1980s and 1990s the device was used sporadically, often for comedy, as in Airplane! (1980), Ferris Bueller's Day Off (1986) and Lethal Weapon 3 (1992). A scene after the credits of Masters of the Universe (1987) revealed that Skeletor had survived, though no sequel followed.

Popularisation

The practice became widespread in the 2000s, particularly through the films of the Marvel Cinematic Universe. Beginning with Iron Man (2008), in which Nick Fury appears to discuss the "Avengers Initiative", Marvel Studios made mid- and post-credits scenes a signature feature, using them to link otherwise standalone films into a shared narrative. The scenes have trained audiences to remain seated through the credits and have been widely imitated by other franchises in film, television and games.

Functions

Post-credits scenes serve several purposes: they can deliver a final gag, resolve a subplot, provide a twist, or foreshadow future instalments. A related device, the mid-credits scene, appears partway through the credit roll, and some films include both. Filmmakers have also used the format to honour the crew, on the grounds that audiences who stay for a stinger will also read the credits.
