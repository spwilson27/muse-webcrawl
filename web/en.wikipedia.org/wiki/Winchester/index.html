Winchester is a cathedral city in Hampshire, England, lying at the western end of the South Downs National Park on the River Itchen, about 60 miles (97 km) south-west of London. The city district has a population of around 130,000. Winchester developed from the Roman town of Venta Belgarum and became the capital of the Anglo-Saxon kingdom of Wessex and later of England, a status it held until after the Norman Conquest, when London assumed greater prominence.

History

An Iron Age settlement preceded the Roman town, which was established around AD 50 and became the fifth-largest town in Roman Britain. After the Roman withdrawal, Winchester rose to prominence under the kings of Wessex; Alfred the Great made it his capital in the 9th century, and his statue stands in the Broadway. The city's cathedral, begun in 1079, has the longest nave of any Gothic cathedral in Europe and contains the remains of many Anglo-Saxon and Norman kings.

Landmarks

Winchester Cathedral is famed for its associations with Jane Austen, who is buried there, and for the 12th-century Winchester Bible. Nearby stand the ruins of Wolvesey Castle and the medieval Great Hall of Winchester Castle, which houses the legendary Round Table of King Arthur, in fact a 13th-century construction. Winchester College, founded in 1382 by Bishop William of Wykeham, is the oldest public school in England still in its original buildings.

Modern Winchester

Today Winchester is a prosperous market and commuter city, regularly ranked among the most desirable places to live in the United Kingdom. It is served by Winchester railway station on the South Western Main Line and by the M3 motorway. The city hosts the University of Winchester and Hat Fair, one of Britain's longest-running street theatre festivals.
