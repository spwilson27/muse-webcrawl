The Wonder Years is an American coming-of-age comedy-drama television series created by Neal Marlens and Carol Black. It ran on ABC from 1988 to 1993 for six seasons and 115 episodes. The series depicts the suburban childhood of Kevin Arnold (Fred Savage) between 1968 and 1973, with the adult Kevin (voiced by Daniel Stern) narrating in retrospect exactly twenty years later.

Premise and characters

Kevin grows up in a middle-class family with his father Jack (Dan Lauria), mother Norma (Alley Mills), rebellious sister Karen (Olivia d'Abo) and bullying older brother Wayne (Jason Hervey). His best friend is the nerdy Paul Pfeiffer (Josh Saviano), and his on-off love interest is Winnie Cooper (Danica McKellar), whose older brother's death in Vietnam in the pilot episode frames the era's anxieties. The Vietnam War, the civil rights era and changing social mores form the backdrop to ordinary suburban life.

Reception and legacy

The pilot aired immediately after Super Bowl XXII in January 1988. The show won an Emmy Award for Outstanding Comedy Series after only six episodes had aired, and Fred Savage became the youngest actor nominated for Outstanding Lead Actor in a Comedy Series. Period music — including Joe Cocker's version of "With a Little Help from My Friends" as the theme — was integral to the show, though licensing costs delayed its home-video release for many years. A reimagining of the same title, centred on a Black family in Montgomery, Alabama, aired on ABC from 2021 to 2023.
