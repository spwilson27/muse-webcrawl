Serra da Estrela

The Serra da Estrela ("Star Mountain Range") is the highest mountain range in mainland Portugal and one of the largest areas of protected natural landscape in the country. Its highest point, the Torre ("Tower"), reaches 1,993 metres (6,539 ft) above sea level, making it the second-highest peak in Portugal after Mount Pico in the Azores. The range lies in the centro-east of the country, spanning parts of the districts of Guarda and Castelo Branco.

Geography

The massif consists mainly of granite and extends for about 100 km. It is drained by several important rivers, including the Mondego, the longest river entirely within Portugal, which rises near Gouveia, and the Zêzere, which flows through a dramatic U-shaped glacial valley — one of the most prominent glacial valleys in Europe. The range contains glacial lakes, moraines, and high plateaus used historically for transhumant sheep grazing.

Nature park

The Serra da Estrela Natural Park, created in 1976, covers about 1,000 km² and is the largest protected area in Portugal. It shelters species such as the Iberian wolf and is known for the Estrela Mountain Dog, an ancient guard breed, and for Queijo Serra da Estrela, a soft sheep's-milk cheese with protected designation of origin, made from the milk of Bordaleira sheep.

Tourism

The range contains Portugal's only natural ski resort, Vodafone Ski Resort at Torre, and is a popular destination for hiking, climbing (at sites such as Cantaro Magro), and winter sports. Two large hydroelectric schemes and numerous reservoirs are built on its rivers.
