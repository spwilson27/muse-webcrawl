Lorenzo Fertitta

Lorenzo Joseph Fertitta (born January 3, 1969) is an American billionaire businessman and entrepreneur. He is best known as the former chief executive officer of the Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) and as a principal of Station Casinos and Red Rock Resorts. With his older brother Frank Fertitta III, he bought the then-struggling UFC in 2001 and built it into the world's dominant mixed martial arts promotion.

Early life and education

Fertitta was born in Las Vegas, Nevada, into a family involved in the casino business; his father, Frank Fertitta Jr., founded Station Casinos. He graduated from Bishop Gorman High School, where he was a schoolmate of Dana White, and earned a business degree from the University of San Diego (1991) and an MBA from New York University's Stern School of Business (1993).

UFC and Zuffa

In 2001 the Fertitta brothers, through their company Zuffa LLC, bought the UFC for about $2 million and installed White as president. Under Lorenzo's tenure as chairman and CEO the promotion secured regulation in major jurisdictions, struck television deals including the landmark Fox agreement of 2011, launched The Ultimate Fighter reality series, and expanded globally. In 2016 Zuffa sold the UFC to WME-IMG (Endeavor) for approximately $4 billion; Fertitta stepped down as CEO, remaining a minority shareholder for a time.

Other ventures

Fertitta is vice-chairman of Red Rock Resorts and co-founder of Fertitta Capital, a private investment firm, and Fertitta Entertainment. He and his family are prominent philanthropists in Las Vegas, supporting education and military veterans' causes. Forbes has estimated his net worth in the billions of dollars.
