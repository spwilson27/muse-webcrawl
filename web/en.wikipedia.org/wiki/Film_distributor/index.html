Film distributor

A film distributor is responsible for the marketing of a film and for setting its release date. The distributor may be the same company that produced the film, or a separate organisation that acquires distribution rights from the producers. Distribution is the link between production and exhibition: the distributor books films into cinemas, supplies prints or digital files, and designs the advertising campaign.

Methods of distribution

Films are released in "windows": a theatrical release is typically followed by home entertainment (physical media and transactional video on demand), subscription streaming, and television windows. Release strategies include wide releases, platform releases that expand gradually, and day-and-date releases that open simultaneously in cinemas and on streaming.

Terms

Under a typical distribution deal, the exhibitor retains a portion of box office receipts, with the distributor's share ("rentals") declining over the run of the film. Distributors may pay an advance to the producer against future revenues, and recoup their print and advertising ("P&A") costs before the producer earns a share of profits.

Major distributors

The largest distributors are the Hollywood major studios — Walt Disney Studios, Warner Bros. Pictures, Universal Pictures, Paramount Pictures, and Sony Pictures — along with mini-majors and independent distributors such as A24 and Neon, and large distributors in other national industries, notably in India and China.
