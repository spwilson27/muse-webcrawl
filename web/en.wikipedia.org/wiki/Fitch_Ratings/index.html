Fitch Ratings Inc. is an American credit rating agency and, along with Moody's and S&P Global Ratings, one of the "Big Three" agencies recognized by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission as a nationally recognized statistical rating organization (NRSRO). It rates the creditworthiness of sovereign governments, corporations and structured finance instruments worldwide.

History

The firm was founded by John Knowles Fitch in 1914 as the Fitch Publishing Company, which issued financial statistics and guides. Fitch introduced the now-standard AAA-to-D letter rating scale in 1924. Through a series of mergers and ownership changes, including acquisition by the French firm FIMALAC and later majority ownership by Hearst Corporation, it grew into a global agency dual-headquartered in New York and London.

Activities and criticism

Fitch assigns long-term and short-term ratings and publishes research on credit markets. Like its peers, it was criticized for overly favourable ratings of mortgage-related securities before the 2007–2008 financial crisis, prompting regulatory reforms of the issuer-pays model in the United States and Europe.
