Aix-les-Bains

Aix-les-Bains (French pronunciation: [ɛks le bɛ̃]) is a commune in the Savoie department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of south-eastern France. A celebrated spa town since Roman times, it lies on the shore of Lake Bourget, the largest natural lake entirely within France, at the foot of the French Alps. Its population is roughly 30,000.

History

The town's hot sulphurous springs were exploited by the Romans, who founded the settlement of Aquae — hence "Aix", from Latin aquae (waters) — and built baths whose remains, including the arch of Campanus, survive in the town centre. The medieval town belonged to the House of Savoy. In the 19th and early 20th centuries Aix became one of Europe's most fashionable resorts, visited by royalty including Queen Victoria, and it retains grand Belle Époque hotels, a casino (the Grand Cercle, opened 1850) and thermal establishments.

Geography and sights

Aix-les-Bains stretches along the eastern shore of Lake Bourget, opposite the Abbaye d'Hautecombe, the traditional burial place of the House of Savoy. The Mont Revard plateau above the town offers skiing and panoramic views. The poet Alphonse de Lamartine celebrated the lake in his poem "Le Lac" (1820). The annual Musilac music festival is held on the lakeshore esplanade.

Economy

Thermalism remains central to the economy through the national thermal baths, alongside tourism, water sports and light industry. The town is served by Aix-les-Bains–Le Revard station on the line between Chambéry and Annecy.
