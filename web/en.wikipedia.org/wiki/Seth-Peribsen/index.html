Seth-Peribsen
Reignc. 2740 BC (2nd Dynasty)
PredecessorSenedj (uncertain)
SuccessorSekhemib or Khasekhemwy (uncertain)
BurialTomb P, Umm el-Qa'ab, Abydos

Seth-Peribsen (also known simply as Peribsen) was an early Egyptian king (pharaoh) of the 2nd Dynasty, who ruled in the 28th century BC. He is famous in Egyptology for a remarkable break with tradition: whereas every other king placed the falcon of Horus atop his serekh (royal name-frame), Peribsen placed the animal of the god Seth — the "Seth animal" — above his name, and his throne name incorporates Seth rather than Horus.

Interpretations

Older scholarship interpreted the change as evidence of a religious schism or a rebellion, in which Peribsen championed Seth against the Horus cult, and linked it to later conflict resolved under Khasekhemwy, whose serekh bears both Horus and Seth. More recent research tends to see the change as a deliberate theological or political statement — perhaps asserting rule over a divided kingdom or a dualistic conception of kingship — rather than proof of civil war. Peribsen's tomb at Umm el-Qa'ab in Abydos was excavated by Flinders Petrie; a funerary stela of the king of dark granite, bearing the Seth animal, is now in the British Museum.
