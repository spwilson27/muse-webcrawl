Pitch angle

Pitch angle
FieldPlasma physics, aeronautics, engineering
DefinitionAngle between a vector and a reference direction

The term pitch angle is used in several technical fields for the angle between a moving object's direction (or a component of its motion) and a reference direction.

Plasma and space physics

In plasma physics, the pitch angle of a charged particle is the angle between the particle's velocity vector and the local magnetic field direction. A particle with a pitch angle of 0° moves parallel to the field, while one at 90° gyrates perpendicular to it with no parallel motion. The pitch angle governs the particle's helical (spiral) trajectory along field lines, its mirror behaviour in converging field geometry such as a magnetic mirror, and whether it falls into the "loss cone" and precipitates, for example into the upper atmosphere to produce aurorae. Pitch-angle scattering by plasma waves redistributes particles in radiation belts such as the Van Allen belts.

Aeronautics and vehicles

In flight dynamics, pitch is the rotation of an aircraft about its lateral (wing-to-wing) axis; the pitch angle describes the inclination of the aircraft's longitudinal axis relative to the horizon. Related usages include the pitch angle of a propeller or helicopter rotor blade — the angle between the blade chord line and the plane of rotation — and the adjustable "collective pitch" used to control lift.

Other uses

The term also appears in solar engineering (the tilt angle of a collector), in the geometry of spiral galaxies (the angle between a spiral arm's tangent and a circle), in roof and screw-thread geometry, and in wind-turbine blade control, where blades are pitched to regulate power and protect the turbine in high winds.
