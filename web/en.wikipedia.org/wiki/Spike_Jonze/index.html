Spike Jonze (born Adam Spiegel, October 22, 1969) is an American film director, producer, screenwriter, actor and photographer. His work includes music videos, commercials, film and television. Jonze is best known for his critically acclaimed feature films Being John Malkovich (1999), Adaptation (2002), Where the Wild Things Are (2009) and Her (2013), the latter of which he wrote, winning the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay.

Jonze began his career as a teenager photographing BMX riders and skateboarders for Freestylin' Magazine and Transworld Skateboarding. He went on to co-found the influential skateboard magazine Big Brother and the production company Satellite Films. His groundbreaking music videos—including the Beastie Boys' "Sabotage" (1994), Björk's "It's Oh So Quiet" (1995), Fatboy Slim's "Praise You" (1999) and Weezer's "Buddy Holly" (1994)—helped define the visual language of 1990s alternative music television.

Feature films

Jonze's directorial debut, Being John Malkovich, written by Charlie Kaufman, earned him an Academy Award nomination for Best Director. He reunited with Kaufman for Adaptation, a metafictional adaptation of Susan Orlean's The Orchid Thief. His adaptation of Maurice Sendak's children's classic Where the Wild Things Are (2009) took a melancholic, emotionally raw approach to the material. Her (2013), a romantic science-fiction drama about a man who falls in love with an artificial intelligence, was his first solo screenwriting credit and won widespread acclaim.

Other work

Jonze co-created and executive produced the MTV stunt show Jackass with Johnny Knoxville and Jeff Tremaine. As an actor, he has appeared in films including Three Kings (1999) and Moneyball (2011). He co-founded the production company and skateboard brand Girl Skateboards, and has directed acclaimed short films and documentaries, including Beastie Boys Story (2020).
