Renin–angiotensin system

The renin–angiotensin system (RAS), or renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS), is a hormone system that regulates blood pressure, fluid and electrolyte balance, and systemic vascular resistance. It is one of the body's principal long-term mechanisms for controlling arterial pressure.

Mechanism

When renal blood flow is reduced, juxtaglomerular cells in the kidneys release the enzyme renin into the blood. Renin cleaves angiotensinogen, a protein released by the liver, to form angiotensin I. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), found mainly in the lungs, converts angiotensin I to the potent vasoconstrictor angiotensin II. Angiotensin II raises blood pressure directly and stimulates the adrenal cortex to release aldosterone, which increases sodium and water reabsorption in the kidney, and prompts the release of antidiuretic hormone.

Clinical significance

Several major drug classes target this system, including ACE inhibitors, angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs) and direct renin inhibitors, all widely used to treat hypertension and heart failure. Overactivation of the system contributes to cardiovascular and renal disease.
