Itaewon

Itaewon (Korean: 이태원) is a neighbourhood (dong) in Yongsan District, Seoul, South Korea. Long associated with the nearby United States military presence at Yongsan Garrison, it developed into Seoul's most international district, known for foreign restaurants, bars, nightclubs, and shops selling imported goods.

History

The name Itaewon is said to derive from an inn (won) that stood in the area during the Joseon dynasty, with various folk etymologies. During the Japanese occupation and especially after the Korean War, the area served American servicemen stationed nearby; tailors, leather shops, and bars catered to them. From the 1980s, and especially around the 1988 Seoul Olympics, Itaewon broadened into a general nightlife and shopping district for tourists and expatriates. It is also home to the Seoul Central Mosque, opened in 1976, and a growing Muslim community.

2022 crowd crush

On 29 October 2022, during Halloween festivities attended by tens of thousands of people, a crowd crush in a narrow alley off Itaewon-ro killed 159 people and injured nearly 200, one of South Korea's worst peacetime disasters. The event prompted national mourning, investigations into police and official failures in crowd management, and lasting changes to event safety rules.

Today

Since the relocation of US forces from Yongsan, the district has seen redevelopment and gentrification; areas such as Gyeongnidan-gil and Haebangchon have become known for cafés and restaurants, while Itaewon remains a centre of Seoul's LGBTQ nightlife.
