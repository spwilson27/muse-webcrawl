Red Cliffs

Red Cliffs refers most commonly to the Battle of Red Cliffs (Chinese: 赤壁之戰), a decisive naval battle in the winter of AD 208–209 during the end of the Han dynasty of China. It is also the name of a two-part Chinese epic film directed by John Woo.

The battle

The battle pitted the allied forces of the southern warlords Sun Quan and Liu Bei against the numerically superior northern forces of the Chancellor Cao Cao on the Yangtze River. Using a fire attack on Cao Cao's chained-together fleet, advised by the strategist Zhou Yu and aided by the defector Huang Gai's feigned surrender, the allies destroyed the northern fleet and forced Cao Cao into retreat. The victory ensured the survival of Sun Quan and Liu Bei and led directly to the tripartite division of China into the Three Kingdoms of Wei, Shu, and Wu.

The film

Red Cliff (2008–2009), directed by John Woo, dramatizes the battle in two parts and was, at the time of release, the most expensive Asian-financed film ever made. The first part became a major box-office success in China and internationally; an abridged single version was released in Western markets.

In culture

The battle is one of the most famous episodes of the 14th-century historical novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms and inspired the famous "Ode to the Red Cliff" by the Song dynasty poet Su Shi.
