Virgin Rail Group was a British train operating company group, a joint venture between the Virgin Group (51%) and the Stagecoach Group (49%). It was formed in 1996, during the privatisation of British Rail, to bid for and operate passenger rail franchises under the Virgin Trains brand.

History

Virgin Rail Group won its first franchise in 1996, taking over the CrossCountry network in January 1997 under the trading name Virgin CrossCountry. In March 1997 it began operating the InterCity West Coast franchise as Virgin Trains (later commonly called Virgin Trains West Coast), running long-distance services from London Euston to Birmingham, Manchester, Liverpool, Glasgow and Edinburgh.

The group's tenure was marked by the introduction of new rolling stock — the Voyager and Super Voyager diesel trains on CrossCountry routes and the tilting Class 390 Pendolino electric trains on the West Coast Main Line — as well as by performance difficulties during the West Coast Main Line modernisation programme undertaken by Railtrack and later Network Rail.

Loss of franchises

In 2007 the CrossCountry franchise was re-let and passed to Arriva CrossCountry. The West Coast franchise continued with Virgin until 8 December 2019, when it passed to Avanti West Coast, a joint venture of FirstGroup and Trenitalia. The loss of the West Coast franchise ended Virgin's involvement in operating franchised passenger trains in Great Britain after more than two decades.
