Qadr (Islam)

Qadr (Arabic: القدر) is the Islamic concept of divine destiny, predestination and the decree of God (Allah). Belief in qadr is one of the six articles of faith (iman) in Sunni Islam: that God knows, wills and decrees all things that happen, while human beings remain accountable for their choices.

Meaning

The term derives from a root meaning measure, power and decree. Classical theology distinguishes aspects of qadr including God's eternal knowledge of all events, the recording of destinies in the Preserved Tablet (al-Lawh al-Mahfuz), God's will that all that occurs comes to pass, and God's creation of all things and actions. Theological schools differed over how divine decree relates to human free will; the Qadariyya emphasized human responsibility, while other schools stressed God's absolute sovereignty, with Ash'ari doctrine seeking a middle path through the notion of "acquisition" (kasb) of acts.

Laylat al-Qadr

The word also appears in Laylat al-Qadr, the "Night of Decree" (or Night of Power), commemorating the first revelation of the Quran to Muhammad, observed during the last ten nights of Ramadan and described in the Quran's Surah Al-Qadr as "better than a thousand months".
