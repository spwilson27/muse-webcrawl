SimCity 2000

Developer: Maxis
Publisher: Maxis
Designer: Will Wright, Fred Haslam
Released: 1993 (DOS/Mac), later many platforms
Genre: City-building simulation

SimCity 2000 is a city-building simulation video game developed by Maxis and released in 1993. It is the sequel to SimCity (1989) and, like its predecessor, was designed by Will Wright with Fred Haslam. The game greatly expanded on the original: the top-down view was replaced by an isometric (dimetric) view, terrain could be raised and lowered, and players could build underground layers for pipes and subways.

Gameplay

As mayor, the player zones residential, commercial, and industrial land; supplies water and electricity; and provides services such as police, fire, schools, hospitals, and libraries. New features included prisons, hospitals, schools and colleges, museums, marinas, zoos, arcologies, and a much wider choice of power plants, including fusion. The game begins in 1900 (with options to start in 1950, 2000, or 2050 in scenarios) and includes disaster scenarios and pre-built cities. A budget and finance interface, ordinances, and neighbourhood newspapers giving feedback added depth.

Reception and legacy

SimCity 2000 was a major commercial and critical success, selling millions of copies and being ported to numerous platforms including the Amiga, SNES, PlayStation, and Game Boy Advance. An expansion, the Urban Renewal Kit, allowed players to customise building graphics. The game is frequently listed among the greatest video games of all time and cemented the SimCity series as the archetype of the city-building genre. It was followed by SimCity 3000 in 1999.
