István Dobó (c. 1502 – May 1572) was a Hungarian soldier and nobleman, best remembered as the heroic commander who defended the castle of Eger against a vastly larger Ottoman army in 1552. He is one of the most celebrated national heroes of Hungary.

The Siege of Eger

In 1552, during the Ottoman wars in Hungary, a combined Ottoman force of perhaps 35,000–40,000 men under Kara Ahmed Pasha besieged Eger. Dobó commanded a garrison of only about 2,000 soldiers and armed townspeople, including women who famously fought on the walls. After five weeks of assaults and bombardment, the Ottomans withdrew in October 1552, a victory celebrated throughout Christian Europe.

The siege was immortalised in Géza Gárdonyi's novel Eclipse of the Crescent Moon (Egri csillagok, "Stars of Eger"), a classic of Hungarian literature, and Dobó's statue stands in the main square of Eger, which is named after him.

Later life

As a reward Dobó received estates and was appointed Voivode of Transylvania in 1553, though he later fell out with the Habsburg court and was imprisoned for a time on suspicion of treason. He was released and died in 1572; his remains were eventually reburied at Eger Cathedral.
