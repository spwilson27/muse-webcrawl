West Berlin (German: West-Berlin) was the western part of Berlin between 1949 and 1990, formed by the American, British, and French occupation sectors established after the Second World War. Although formally not a constituent state of the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany), it was closely integrated with it politically, economically, and culturally, while remaining under nominal Allied military occupation. It lay entirely within the territory of East Germany (the GDR), which surrounded it — from 1961 physically separated by the Berlin Wall.

History

After the wartime conferences divided Berlin into four sectors, the Soviet blockade of 1948–49 was defeated by the Berlin Airlift. In 1949 the eastern sector became the capital of the GDR, while the western sectors formed West Berlin, governed by a Governing Mayor and Senate. The building of the Berlin Wall on 13 August 1961 sealed the enclave. The Quadripartite Agreement of 1971 regularised access and the city's status. West Berlin developed a distinctive countercultural scene, subsidised by the West German government, and exempted from West German conscription — attracting young draft-averse men and artists.

Reunification

Following the fall of the Berlin Wall on 9 November 1989, West Berlin ceased to exist as a separate entity on 3 October 1990, when East and West Berlin were reunified as the capital of the reunified Germany.
