The Pericope Adulterae is the name traditionally given to the passage in the Gospel of John (John 7:53–8:11) recounting the story of Jesus and the woman taken in adultery. In the passage, scribes and Pharisees bring before Jesus a woman caught in adultery and ask whether she should be stoned as the Law of Moses commands. Jesus bends down and writes on the ground, then replies, "Let him who is without sin among you be the first to throw a stone at her." The accusers depart one by one, and Jesus tells the woman, "Neither do I condemn you; go, and from now on sin no more."

Textual history

The passage is absent from the earliest surviving Greek manuscripts of John, including Papyrus 66, Papyrus 75, Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Vaticanus, and the earliest translations and commentators pass from John 7:52 directly to 8:12. It appears in Codex Bezae (c. 400) and in the majority of later Byzantine manuscripts, though often marked with asterisks or obeli indicating doubt; in some manuscripts it is placed after Luke 21:38 instead. Its vocabulary and style differ notably from the rest of John's Gospel.

The story itself may be older than its insertion: Eusebius reports that Papias (early 2nd century) knew a story of a woman accused of many sins before the Lord, and Didymus the Blind refers to a version "in certain gospels". The passage was known to the Latin West, where Jerome included it in the Vulgate, noting it was found in many Greek and Latin manuscripts.

Scholarly assessment

The great majority of New Testament textual critics conclude that the pericope was not part of the original Gospel of John but is an authentic piece of early Jesus tradition that circulated independently before being inserted. Modern critical editions print it in double brackets, and most modern translations retain it with a footnote explaining its manuscript history. Its teaching on mercy and forgiveness has made it one of the most beloved and frequently depicted episodes of Jesus's ministry.
