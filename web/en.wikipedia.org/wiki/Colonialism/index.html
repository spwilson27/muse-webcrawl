Colonialism

Colonialism
DefinitionPractice of acquiring and maintaining colonies, ruling other peoples and territories
Erac. 1500–1975 (European overseas empires)
[Image: A historical map showing colonial empires in different colours across the world]

Colonialism is the practice of domination that involves the subjugation of one people to another, through acquiring and maintaining colonies. European powers — Spain, Portugal, Britain, France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany, Italy — ruled most of the Americas, Africa, Asia and Oceania between the 16th and 20th centuries.

Legacies

Colonialism moved crops (Potato, Corn), peoples (the slave trade — see Slavery), languages and borders, enriching metropoles and impoverishing colonies by design. Its legacies — arbitrary borders, extractive economies, cultural hierarchies — shape development and memory politics, from restitution claims to curriculum debates.
