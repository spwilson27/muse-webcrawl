Muggle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter series, a Muggle is a person who lacks any sort of magical ability and is not born into the magical world. Muggles also do not have any magical blood. It differs from the terms Squib (a person born to wizard parents but lacking magic) and Muggle-born (a witch or wizard with Muggle parents).

Rowling has said the word derives from "mug", an English term for someone who is easily fooled, softened with the suffix "-le" to make it sound more affectionate and less demeaning. In the books, the term is generally neutral, though some characters use it with contempt, and pure-blood supremacists prefer the slur "Mudblood" for Muggle-born witches and wizards.

Notable Muggles in the series

  • The Dursleys — Vernon, Petunia and Dudley Dursley, Harry Potter's only living relatives, who raise him in Privet Drive and despise all things magical.
  • Jacob Kowalski — an American No-Maj (the American term) in the Fantastic Beasts films.
  • The British Prime Minister — kept minimally informed of the wizarding world by the Minister for Magic.

Use outside Harry Potter

The word entered wider English usage for a person who lacks a particular skill or insider knowledge in some field — for example, in geocaching a "muggle" is a non-geocacher who might stumble upon a hidden cache, and in some subcultures it denotes an outsider generally. It was added to the Oxford English Dictionary in 2003. In the American wizarding world of the Fantastic Beasts films, the equivalent term is No-Maj, short for "no magic".
