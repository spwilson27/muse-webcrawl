Crofters' Holdings (Scotland) Act 1886

The Crofters' Holdings (Scotland) Act 1886 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that created legal definitions of crofting in the Scottish Highlands and Islands, giving crofters security of tenure for the first time.

Background

The Act followed decades of grievance after the Highland Clearances and the agitation of the Highland Land League, including rent strikes and land raids. The Napier Commission of 1883–84 investigated crofters' conditions and recommended reform.

Provisions

The Act granted security of tenure so long as rents were paid, rights to fair rents fixed by the new Crofters Commission, compensation for improvements, and rights of bequest. It applied to holdings in the historic crofting counties including Argyll, Inverness-shire, Ross and Cromarty, Sutherland, Caithness, Orkney and Shetland. It did not, however, restore land lost in the Clearances, which fuelled continued protest.
