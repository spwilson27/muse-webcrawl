Happy End (band)

OriginTokyo, Japan
GenresFolk rock

Happy End (Japanese: はっぴいえんど, Happii Endo) was a Japanese folk rock band formed in Tokyo in 1969 by Haruomi Hosono, Eiichi Ohtaki, Shigeru Suzuki, and Takashi Matsumoto. Despite a career of barely three years, they are regarded as one of the most influential acts in Japanese popular music, pivotal in proving that rock music could be sung convincingly in Japanese.

History

The four members emerged from the band Apryl Fool and from the orbit of the folk scene around the label URC. Their self-titled debut album Happy End (1970) mixed American folk rock and country rock influences with Japanese lyrics written by drummer Matsumoto. Their second album, Kaze Machi Roman ("Wind City Romance", 1971), a nostalgic concept album about Tokyo, is frequently ranked among the greatest Japanese rock albums of all time and includes the classic "Kaze wo Atsumete".

The band recorded a third album, Happy End (1973), in Los Angeles with members of Little Feat and Van Dyke Parks contributing, but broke up amid internal friction before its release; a farewell concert was held in September 1973. Their legacy grew steadily after the split, and the members reunited occasionally for anniversary concerts.

Legacy

Happy End stood at the centre of the "Japanese-language rock controversy", the debate over whether Japanese could carry rock music at all. Their success settled the question for a generation. All four members became central figures in Japanese music: Hosono co-founded Yellow Magic Orchestra, Ohtaki created the beloved solo album A Long Vacation, Suzuki became a leading session guitarist, and Matsumoto became Japan's most famous lyricist for pop idols.
