Artaxerxes (opera)

Title page of the score of Arne's Artaxerxes (1762)

Artaxerxes is an opera in three acts by Thomas Arne, first performed at Covent Garden, London, on 2 February 1762. Set to an English adaptation of Metastasio's famous libretto Artaserse (1730), it is regarded as the most successful English opera seria of the eighteenth century and a landmark in Arne's career.

Background

Metastasio's Artaserse was one of the most frequently set libretti of the Baroque era, with dozens of settings across Europe. Arne's version — the libretto translated and adapted into English, probably by Arne himself — attempted to demonstrate that serious Italian-style opera could succeed in the English language. The title role was created by the castrato Giusto Ferdinando Tenducci, and the part of Mandane by Charlotte Brent, Arne's pupil and muse.

Plot

The action takes place at the Persian court. Artabanus, commander of the royal guard, murders King Xerxes and contrives to blame Darius, the king's son. Artaxerxes, the younger son and successor, is torn between condemning his friend Arbaces — Artabanus's son, falsely implicated — and believing his innocence. Mandane, Artaxerxes' sister and Arbaces' lover, rages against her brother. After conspiracies, a rebellion and an attempted poisoning, Artabanus's guilt is exposed, Arbaces is vindicated, and Artaxerxes rules mercifully.

Music and reception

The opera contains some of Arne's most admired music, including Mandane's bravura arias and the duet "For thee I live". Artaxerxes remained in the repertory well into the nineteenth century and was one of the few English operas of its period known on the Continent. Parts of the score were lost in the Covent Garden fire of 1808; the surviving music has been edited and revived in modern times, including a notable recording and staged performances in the twenty-first century.
