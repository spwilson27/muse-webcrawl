Didius Julianus

Marcus Didius Severus Julianus (29 January 133 – 2 June 193) was Roman emperor from March to June 193, during the Year of the Five Emperors. Julianus had a promising political career, governing several provinces, but he became emperor after purchasing the position from the Praetorian Guard.

Rise

Julianus rose through the cursus honorum, serving as consul and governing Lower Germany, Bithynia, Africa and other provinces. After the murder of the emperor Pertinax, the Praetorian Guard auctioned the empire; Julianus outbid his rival Sulpicianus, reportedly offering 25,000 sesterces per soldier.

Reign and death

The purchase scandalised Rome. Provincial generals — Septimius Severus, Pescennius Niger and Clodius Albinus — were proclaimed emperor by their legions. As Severus marched on Rome, the Senate condemned Julianus, who was deposed and executed after a reign of only 66 days. His fate is a byword for the corruption of the Praetorian auction.
