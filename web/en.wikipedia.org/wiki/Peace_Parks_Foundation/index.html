The Peace Parks Foundation is a non-profit organisation founded in 1997 by South African businessman and conservationist Dr Anton Rupert, together with Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands and President Nelson Mandela as founding patrons. Its mission is to facilitate the establishment of transfrontier conservation areas (TFCAs), informally known as "peace parks," across southern Africa.

Concept

A peace park links protected areas across international borders so that ecosystems, wildlife migrations, and tourism can be managed as a single ecological unit, while also promoting regional peace and cooperation. The idea draws on earlier precedents such as the Waterton-Glacier International Peace Park between Canada and the United States (1932).

Projects

The Foundation supports governments in establishing and managing TFCAs including the Great Limpopo Transfrontier Park (linking Kruger National Park with parks in Mozambique and Zimbabwe, formally established by treaty in 2002), the Kavango–Zambezi (KAZA) TFCA — the largest terrestrial transfrontier conservation area, spanning five countries — the Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park between South Africa and Botswana (opened 2000), the Ai-Ais/Richtersveld Transfrontier Park, and the Greater Mapungubwe TFCA.

Work

Beyond facilitating treaties and management plans, the Foundation invests in ranger training (through the Southern African Wildlife College, which it helped found), anti-poaching operations, community development, removal of fences, and the reintroduction of wildlife. It works with partner governments, NGOs, and donors to secure long-term financing for the parks.
