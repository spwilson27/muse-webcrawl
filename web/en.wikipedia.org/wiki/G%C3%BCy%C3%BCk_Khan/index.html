Güyük Khan

Güyük Khan (c. 19 March 1206 – 20 April 1248) was the third Khagan (Great Khan) of the Mongol Empire, reigning from 1246 to 1248. The eldest son of Ögedei Khan and grandson of Genghis Khan, he succeeded his father after a five-year regency by his mother Töregene Khatun.

Predecessor: Ögedei Khan
Successor: Möngke Khan
Father: Ögedei Khan

Early life

Güyük campaigned in the Mongol invasion of Eastern Europe under his cousin Batu, with whom he quarrelled — a feud that persisted through his reign.

Reign

His enthronement at Karakorum in 1246 was attended by foreign envoys including the Franciscan Giovanni da Pian del Carpine, sent by Pope Innocent IV; Güyük's reply letter, demanding the Pope's submission, survives in the Vatican archives. He reversed many of his mother's appointments and governed as a strict, austere ruler.

Death

Güyük died in 1248 while marching west, possibly to confront Batu; his widow Oghul Qaimish ruled as regent until Möngke's election in 1251.
