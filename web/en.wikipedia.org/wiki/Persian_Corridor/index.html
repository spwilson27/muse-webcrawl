Persian Corridor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Persian Corridor was a supply route through Iran into the Soviet Union during the Second World War, by which the United States and the United Kingdom delivered Lend-Lease supplies to the USSR after the German invasion of 1941. Together with the Arctic convoys and the Pacific route through Vladivostok, it was one of the three main arteries of Allied aid to the Soviet war effort.

Background

In August–September 1941 British and Soviet forces jointly invaded Iran (Operation Countenance) to secure the oilfields, remove German influence, and open the supply route. Reza Shah was forced to abdicate in favour of his son Mohammad Reza Pahlavi. The Trans-Iranian Railway, running from the Persian Gulf port of Bandar Shahpur to Tehran and the Caspian Sea, became the backbone of the corridor.

Operations

From late 1942 the operation was run largely by the United States Army's Persian Gulf Command, which at its peak numbered about 30,000 men. Ports at Khorramshahr, Bandar Shahpur and Bushire were expanded; thousands of trucks were assembled in Iran and driven north; aircraft were assembled at Abadan and flown to the USSR. Over the life of the corridor some 4.16 million tonnes of cargo passed to the Soviet Union — including more than 140,000 trucks, thousands of aircraft, locomotives and rail wagons. Deliveries by this route supplied the Red Army particularly during the campaigns of 1943–45 in the west.

Legacy

The corridor wound down in 1945 as the war ended; American forces withdrew by the start of 1946. The episode remains a notable early episode of American involvement in Iran, preceding the crises of the post-war era.
