Geordie
RegionTyneside, North East England
Refers toPeople, dialect, and accent of the Newcastle area

Geordie is a nickname for a person from the Tyneside area of North East England and the dialect and accent spoken by its inhabitants. The term is most closely associated with Newcastle upon Tyne and the communities along the River Tyne in the counties of Tyne and Wear and Northumberland.

Origins of the term

The origin of the name is uncertain. Theories include a diminutive of the name George, once common among local miners; support for the Hanoverian King George during the Jacobite risings; and George Stephenson's safety lamp, the "Geordie lamp", used in the region's coal mines.

Dialect

The Geordie dialect preserves many features of Old English and was influenced by Norse and Scots. Distinctive words include "canny" (good, pleasant), "bairn" (child), and "gan" (go). The dialect is frequently ranked among the most recognisable and best-liked accents in the United Kingdom.
