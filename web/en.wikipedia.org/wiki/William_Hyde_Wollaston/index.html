William Hyde Wollaston

William Hyde Wollaston (6 August 1766 – 22 December 1828) was an English chemist and physicist who discovered the chemical elements palladium and rhodium.

William Hyde Wollaston FRS (6 August 1766 – 22 December 1828) was an English chemist and physicist who is famous for discovering the chemical elements palladium and rhodium. He also developed a way to process platinum ore into malleable ingots.

Early life

He was born in East Dereham in Norfolk, the son of the Reverend Francis Wollaston (1737–1815), the astronomer. He was educated at Charterhouse School and Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge, where he studied medicine and took his M.D. in 1793.

Chemistry and platinum

Wollaston gave up medical practice and devoted himself to chemistry and physics. In 1800 he left an inheritance and devoted himself to developing a practical method of refining platinum, keeping the details secret during his lifetime while selling malleable platinum commercially — a process that made him wealthy and supplied chemists with platinum crucibles and vessels.

While examining crude platinum ore from South America, he announced the discovery of a new element, palladium, in 1802 (named after the recently discovered asteroid Pallas), and of rhodium in 1804 (named from the Greek rhodon, rose, for the colour of its salts).

Optics and other work

Wollaston invented the camera lucida (patented 1807), an optical device aiding artists in drawing; the reflective goniometer for measuring crystal angles; and he was among the first to observe the dark lines in the solar spectrum later studied systematically by Joseph von Fraunhofer. He also performed early experiments suggesting the equivalence of galvanic and frictional electricity.

Honours and death

He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1793 and served as its president in 1820. The mineral wollastonite and the Wollaston Medal of the Geological Society of London are named after him. He died in London on 22 December 1828.
