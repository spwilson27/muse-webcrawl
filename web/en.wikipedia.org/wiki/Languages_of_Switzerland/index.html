Languages of Switzerland

Switzerland has four national languages: German, French, Italian, and Romansh. German, French, and Italian are official languages of the Swiss Confederation at the federal level, while Romansh is an official language for communication with Romansh speakers. Roughly 62% of the population speaks German as a main language, 23% French, 8% Italian, and 0.5% Romansh; a large share of residents also speak non-national languages, most commonly English and Portuguese.

Geographic distribution

The language areas are geographically stable and largely follow historic boundaries. German is spoken in the central, northern, and eastern parts of the country (17 of the 26 cantons are predominantly German-speaking); French in the west, in the region known as Romandy; Italian in the south, mainly the canton of Ticino and the southern valleys of Graubünden; and Romansh only in parts of the trilingual canton of Graubünden in the southeast. The boundary between the French- and German-speaking areas is popularly called the Röstigraben ("rösti ditch"), and that between German and Italian areas runs through the Alpine passes.

Official status

Under the Federal Constitution, the cantons determine their own official languages, respecting the traditional territorial distribution of languages. Four cantons are officially bilingual (Bern, Fribourg, Valais, and — in part — others), Graubünden is trilingual, and the rest are monolingual. At the federal level, legislation is published in German, French, and Italian. Most cantons require school pupils to learn at least one other national language, plus English.

Swiss German

The German-speaking Swiss overwhelmingly use Swiss German dialects (Schweizerdeutsch) in everyday speech — Alemannic dialects that differ considerably from Standard German and from one another — while Standard German (Schriftdeutsch, "written German") is used for writing, formal speech, and much of broadcasting. This diglossia is a defining feature of life in German-speaking Switzerland.

Romansh

Romansh is a Rhaeto-Romance language descended from the Vulgar Latin of Roman Raetia. It survives in several distinct written idioms in the valleys of Graubünden. A standardised written form, Rumantsch Grischun, was created in 1982 for official use. Despite state support, the number of habitual speakers continues to decline as German spreads in Romansh valleys.
