Levant

The Levant is a historical and geographical term for the eastern Mediterranean region of West Asia, comprising Syria, Lebanon, Israel, Palestine, Jordan and the Turkish province of Hatay, and sometimes Cyprus and parts of Iraq. Its name derives from French for 'rising' (where the sun rises, seen from Europe).

The Levant was a corridor between Africa, Asia and Europe — hominins passed through it out of Africa — and a crossroads of empires from Egypt and Assyria to Rome, Byzantium, the Arab caliphates (whose Umayyad capital was Damascus), the Crusader states, the Ottomans and the French and British mandates. Its cities — Damascus and Aleppo among the oldest continuously inhabited on Earth, Beirut, Jerusalem — are layers of that history. 'Levantine' culture shares cuisine (hummus, falafel, mezze) and Arabic dialects across the region.
