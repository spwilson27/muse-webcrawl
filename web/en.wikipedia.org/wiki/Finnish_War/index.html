Finnish War

Finnish War Date: 21 February 1808 – 17 September 1809
Location: Finland and northern Sweden
Combatants: Russian Empire vs Sweden
Result: Russian victory; Treaty of Fredrikshamn
Territorial change: Finland ceded to Russia, becoming the Grand Duchy of Finland

The Finnish War (Swedish: Finska kriget; Finnish: Suomen sota) was fought between the Kingdom of Sweden and the Russian Empire from February 1808 to September 1809. It resulted in the eastern third of Sweden being established as the autonomous Grand Duchy of Finland within the Russian Empire.

Background

Under the Treaties of Tilsit in 1807, Russia agreed to pressure Sweden to join Napoleon's Continental System against Britain. When Sweden refused, Tsar Alexander I ordered an invasion of Swedish Finland, citing also a pretext of border security. Russian forces under Friedrich Wilhelm von Buxhoevden crossed the border on 21 February 1808 without a formal declaration of war.

Course of the war

The Swedish army under Wilhelm Mauritz Klingspor retreated northward, and the great fortress of Sveaborg surrendered in May 1808, a blow that shocked Sweden. Counteroffensives in the summer, including victories at Revolax and Oravais, failed to reverse the tide; the Battle of Oravais in September 1808 was a decisive Swedish defeat. Fighting continued in Västerbotten in 1809, and a coup in Stockholm deposed King Gustav IV Adolf.

Peace

The Treaty of Fredrikshamn, signed on 17 September 1809, ceded Finland and the Åland Islands to Russia. Finland was organised as an autonomous Grand Duchy with its own Diet and laws, a status it retained until independence in 1917. The war is a central theme of Johan Ludvig Runeberg's epic poem The Tales of Ensign Stål.
