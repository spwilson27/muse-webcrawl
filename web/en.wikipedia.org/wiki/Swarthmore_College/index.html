Swarthmore College is a private liberal arts college in Swarthmore, Pennsylvania, United States. Founded in 1864, with its first classes held in 1869, Swarthmore is one of the earliest coeducational colleges in the United States. It was founded by members of the Religious Society of Friends (Quakers), but by the early 20th century the college had grown independent of religious affiliation.

Swarthmore is a member of the Tri-College Consortium with nearby Bryn Mawr College and Haverford College, and of the Quaker Consortium with the University of Pennsylvania. The college is known for its rigorous academics, its honours programme modelled on tutorial teaching at Oxford, and its campus, which is a nationally recognised arboretum.

History

The college was founded in 1864 by Lucretia Mott and other Hicksite Quakers who wanted a school where men and women could receive an education "under the care of Friends." Classes began in 1869 in Parrish Hall, which still stands as the college's central building. In the 1920s, president Frank Aydelotte introduced the Honours Program, inspired by the tutorial system of the University of Oxford, in which students take seminars and are examined by outside examiners.

Academics

Swarthmore offers more than 600 courses across over 40 programmes of study in the humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, and engineering — it is unusual among liberal arts colleges in offering an accredited engineering programme. The student-faculty ratio is low, and most classes are small seminars. The college is consistently ranked among the top liberal arts colleges in the United States, and its graduates have included five Nobel Prize laureates and numerous Rhodes and Marshall scholars.

Campus

The 425-acre campus is home to the Scott Arboretum, established in 1929, which contains more than 4,000 kinds of ornamental plants. Notable features include the amphitheatre set in a grove of trees, Crum Woods, and the gardens surrounding the historic buildings. The college is located about 11 miles southwest of Philadelphia and is served by its own station on SEPTA's Media/Wawa Line.

Student life and athletics

Nearly all students live on campus for all four years. Swarthmore's athletic teams, the Garnet, compete in NCAA Division III in the Centennial Conference; the mascot is Phineas the Phoenix, adopted in 2006. The college maintains a strong tradition of social activism, reflecting its Quaker heritage of social justice, peace, and equality.

Notable alumni

  • John C. Mather and David Baltimore – Nobel Prize laureates
  • Michael Dukakis – former governor of Massachusetts and 1988 Democratic presidential nominee
  • Alice Paul – suffragist (attended, class of 1905)
  • James A. Michener – novelist
  • Jonathan Franzen – novelist
