Liga MX, known as Liga BBVA MX for sponsorship reasons, is the top professional football division of the Mexican football league system. It is contested by 18 clubs and operates a split-season format with two tournaments per year, the Apertura and the Clausura, each followed by a play-off phase known as the Liguilla.

History

Mexico's first national professional league, the Liga Mayor, began in 1943. The league grew with the country's major industrial clubs — América, Guadalajara (Chivas), Cruz Azul, UNAM's Pumas and Toluca among them — and was rebranded as the Primera División and then, in 2012, as Liga MX with a new visual identity and an associated cup competition, the Copa MX (later discontinued).

Format

Each short tournament consists of a 17-round regular season in which every club plays each other once; the leading teams qualify for the Liguilla play-offs, culminating in a two-legged final. Promotion and relegation with the second tier (Liga de Expansión MX) has been suspended since 2020. Mexican clubs traditionally dominate the CONCACAF Champions Cup.

Popularity

Liga MX is among the most-watched football leagues in the Americas, with high television audiences in both Mexico and the United States and average attendances among the highest in world football.
