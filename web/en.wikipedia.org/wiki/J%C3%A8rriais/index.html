Jèrriais

Native toJersey, Channel Islands
FamilyRomance (Norman)
SpeakersA few thousand (declining)

Jèrriais is the traditional Norman language of the island of Jersey in the Channel Islands. Descended from the Old Norman spoken by settlers from Normandy, it developed in relative isolation and preserves features lost from continental Norman, along with Norse and later English influences.

History

For centuries Jèrriais was the everyday language of the island's rural population, while French served as the language of law and administration and English spread through trade and education. It has a written literature dating from the late eighteenth century, including poetry by writers such as Robert Pipon Marett, who wrote as "Laelius".

Decline and revival

The number of speakers fell sharply during the twentieth century as English became dominant; by the 2001 census only a few percent of islanders spoke Jèrriais fluently, mostly among older generations. Since then, revival efforts have included teaching in schools, a language office (L'Office du Jèrriais), bilingual signage, and official recognition of Jèrriais as a regional language of Jersey.
