Smart meter

A smart meter is an electronic device that records information — such as consumption of electric energy, voltage levels, current, and power factor — and communicates that information to the consumer and to the electricity supplier. Such an advanced metering infrastructure (AMI) differs from automatic meter reading (AMR) in that it enables two-way communication between the meter and the supplier. Smart meters typically record energy near real-time, and report regularly, at short intervals throughout the day.

Operation

Smart meters communicate over wireless mesh networks, cellular connections, power-line communication or broadband links to a central system. Utilities use the interval data for billing (including time-of-use tariffs), outage detection, load forecasting and monitoring power quality; consumers can access their consumption data through an in-home display or web portal. Smart meters can also be installed for gas and water, though electricity meters are by far the most common.

Rollouts

Italy was an early leader, with Enel deploying smart meters to most customers in the early 2000s. The European Union's energy legislation set a target of smart meters for 80% of consumers by 2020 where cost-effective; Sweden, Spain, Finland and Estonia approached full coverage. In North America, smart meter penetration passed half of all customers in the 2010s, and China has deployed hundreds of millions of units. The United Kingdom's rollout, led by energy suppliers, extended through the 2020s.

Benefits and criticism

Claimed benefits include accurate bills without estimated readings, faster outage restoration, support for rooftop solar and electric vehicles, and demand response programmes that shave peak load. Criticisms have included installation costs, privacy concerns over detailed consumption data, health concerns about radiofrequency emissions (generally assessed by regulators as well below safety limits), security vulnerabilities, and reports of faulty early units. Some consumers have also criticised aggressive remote disconnection features.
