The Prusa i3 is a fused filament fabrication (FFF) 3D printer designed by the Czech engineer Josef Průša and produced by his company Prusa Research. First released in 2012 as the third iteration ("i3") of Průša's designs within the open-source RepRap project, it became one of the most widely used and most frequently cloned 3D printer designs in the world.

Design

The i3 uses a Cartesian layout with a distinctive flat frame — in the Original Prusa versions, a single laser-cut aluminium plate — a heated bed moving in Y, and a direct-drive extruder moving in X and Z. Its open-source design, published under the GNU General Public License, allowed countless manufacturers to sell compatible kits, and "i3-style" became a generic description of this printer architecture.

Models

Successive Original Prusa models included the i3 MK2 (2016), which added automatic mesh bed levelling and a print-farm-proven design; the MK2.5 upgrade; the MK3 and MK3S/+ (2017–2020), with a removable spring-steel print sheet, filament sensor and quieter stepper drivers; and the MK4 (2023), with a redesigned Nextruder, load-cell bed probing and input shaping. Prusa Research also runs one of the world's largest 3D-printing farms, using hundreds of its own printers to produce parts for new machines.
