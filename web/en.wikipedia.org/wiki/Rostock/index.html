Rostock

Country: Germany

State: Mecklenburg-Vorpommern

Population: approx. 210,000

First mentioned: 1218

Rostock is the largest city in the German state of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, a historic Hanseatic port on the Warnow River near its mouth on the Baltic Sea. Its seaside district of Warnemünde is a popular resort and cruise-ship port, and the city is home to the University of Rostock, founded in 1419 — one of the oldest universities in the world.

History

The area was originally settled by Polabian Slavs, who called the place Roztoc ("where the river flows apart"). German settlement followed in the twelfth century, and Rostock received Lübeck law city rights in 1218. As a leading member of the Hanseatic League from 1259, it grew wealthy on Baltic trade. The university, founded in 1419, made it an intellectual centre of northern Europe.

Rostock suffered heavily in the Second World War from bombing raids beginning in 1942. In the GDR era it became East Germany's principal overseas port, with major shipbuilding at the Neptun and Warnow yards, and the population swelled with new housing districts. After reunification the shipyards contracted sharply, but the port revived as a ferry and cruise hub, and the city hosts the annual Hanse Sail maritime festival, one of Germany's largest.

Geography and landmarks

The city stretches along both banks of the lower Warnow, about 12 km from the open Baltic. Landmarks include St Mary's Church with its fifteenth-century astronomical clock, St Peter's Church with the city's tallest spire, the gabled merchants' houses of the old town, the Steintor and Kuhtor gates, and the Teepott and lighthouse at Warnemünde. The football club F.C. Hansa Rostock is based at the Ostseestadion.
