Aisha bint Abi Bakr (c. 613/614 – c. 678) was a wife of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and the daughter of Abu Bakr, the first caliph. In Islamic tradition she is honored with the title Umm al-Mu'minin ("Mother of the Believers"). Aisha played an important role in early Islamic history: she was a major transmitter of hadith (reports of Muhammad's words and practices), an authority consulted by senior companions on matters of law and ritual, and a political and military actor during the First Fitna, the first Islamic civil war.

Life

Aisha was born in Mecca to Abu Bakr and Umm Ruman. She married Muhammad in Mecca before the Hijra of 622 and joined his household in Medina. Islamic sources portray her as Muhammad's favorite wife in his later years; he died in her apartment in Medina in 632. Her age at marriage, reported in the hadith literature as six or seven with consummation at nine, has been discussed by classical and modern scholars alike, and remains a subject of historical and ethical debate.

Role in the First Fitna

After the assassination of the third caliph, Uthman, in 656, Aisha joined Talha ibn Ubayd Allah and al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam in demanding punishment of the killers and opposed the fourth caliph, Ali ibn Abi Talib. Their forces met Ali's army at the Battle of the Camel near Basra in 656 — so named because Aisha directed her troops from a howdah on a camel at the center of the battle. Her side was defeated; she was treated with courtesy and escorted back to Medina, where she lived in retirement until her death.

Scholarly legacy

Aisha is credited with narrating more than two thousand hadiths, covering subjects from ritual purity and prayer to medicine, poetry, and genealogy. Companions of Muhammad are reported to have consulted her on difficult questions, and she corrected the transmissions of others. She is one of the most prominent women in Islamic religious scholarship, and her reports form a significant part of the Sunni hadith corpus. Shia tradition, by contrast, views her role in the conflict with Ali critically.
