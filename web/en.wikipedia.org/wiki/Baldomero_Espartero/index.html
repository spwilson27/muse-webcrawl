Born27 February 1793, Granátula de Calatrava, Spain
Died8 January 1879 (aged 85), Logroño, Spain

Joaquín Baldomero Fernández-Espartero y Álvarez de Toro (27 February 1793 – 8 January 1879), known as Baldomero Espartero, was a Spanish general and statesman, one of the dominant figures of 19th-century Spain. Victor of the First Carlist War, he served as prime minister and as regent of Spain from 1841 to 1843 during the minority of Queen Isabella II. For his services he received the titles Duke of la Victoria, Duke of Morella, and Prince of Vergara.

Early career

The son of a carter from a small town in La Mancha, Espartero joined the army as a cadet during the Peninsular War against Napoleon and then spent nearly a decade fighting in the Spanish American wars of independence, rising to the rank of brigadier in Peru before returning to Spain in 1825.

First Carlist War and regency

On the death of Ferdinand VII in 1833, Espartero supported the claims of the infant Isabella II against her uncle Don Carlos. Commanding the Army of the North, he relieved the Carlist siege of Bilbao and won the Battle of Luchana (1836), becoming a popular hero of the Liberal cause. In 1839 he negotiated the Convention of Vergara with the Carlist general Rafael Maroto, which ended the war in the north and earned him his princely title. Falling out with the regent Maria Christina, he forced her resignation and exile in 1840 and was himself elected regent in May 1841.

Fall and later life

Espartero's regency was authoritarian: he ordered the bombardment of Barcelona in 1842 to suppress a revolt, alienating much of his support. A revolt led by Generals Narváez and Concha drove him into exile in England in 1843. He returned after the revolution of 1854 (the Vicalvarada) to head a government with Leopoldo O'Donnell during the Progressive Biennium (1854–1856), but manoeuvring by O'Donnell forced his resignation in 1856. Espartero retired to Logroño, refusing repeated offers of power, and after the revolution of 1868 some supporters even proposed him for the Spanish throne. He died in Logroño in 1879, universally respected as a symbol of humble origins raised by merit — "the people's general".
