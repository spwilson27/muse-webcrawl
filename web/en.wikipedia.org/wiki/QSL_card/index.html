QSL card

A QSL card is a written confirmation of a two-way radio communication between two amateur radio stations, or of the reception of a one-way transmission from an AM, FM, television, or shortwave broadcasting station. "QSL" is the Q-code for "I confirm receipt". Typically postcard-sized, cards are exchanged by mail — directly, or through national QSL bureaus coordinated by member societies of the International Amateur Radio Union — and, increasingly, confirmed electronically through services such as Logbook of The World (LoTW) and eQSL.

Contents and use

A QSL card records the call signs of both stations, the date and time (UTC), frequency or band, mode of transmission, and a signal report, and often features pictures of the operator, the station, or the local area. Collected cards serve as proof of contacts for awards such as DXCC (contacting 100 countries), and for contest verification. Shortwave listeners (SWLs) send reception reports to broadcasters in exchange for QSL cards verifying their reception.

History

The exchange of confirmation cards dates to the earliest days of amateur radio in the 1910s–1920s; the oldest known card dates from 1916. Collecting QSLs became a central tradition of the hobby, and "QSL walls" of cards are a common feature of radio shacks.
