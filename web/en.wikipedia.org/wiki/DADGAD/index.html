DADGAD tuning
TypeOpen / modal guitar tuning
Notes (low to high)D–A–D–G–A–D
Popularised byDavey Graham

DADGAD, or Celtic tuning, is an alternative guitar tuning most associated with Celtic music, though it has also found use in rock, folk, metal and several other genres. The tuning is produced by lowering the first, second and sixth strings of standard tuning by a whole tone (two semitones), giving the open notes D2–A2–D3–G3–A3–D4 from lowest to highest.

Characteristics

Because the open strings form a Dsus4 chord (D, G and A), the tuning is neither major nor minor, which makes it well suited to modal melodies and drone-based accompaniment. The identical notes on the outer strings and the symmetric layout make it easy to produce rich, ringing voicings with simple fingerings, and folk guitarists often exploit the tuning's campanella (harp-like) effects, in which melody notes are played on different strings so that they overlap and sustain.

History and use

The tuning was popularised by British folk guitarist Davey Graham in the 1960s, who adapted it after experimenting with the tuning of the oud during travels in Morocco. It became a staple of the British and Irish folk revival and was used extensively by guitarists such as Bert Jansch, John Renbourn, Martin Carthy and Dick Gaughan, as well as by Irish accompanists accompanying traditional tunes. Jimmy Page of Led Zeppelin used DADGAD on the song "Kashmir", and it has since been adopted by players in genres ranging from fingerstyle to progressive metal.
