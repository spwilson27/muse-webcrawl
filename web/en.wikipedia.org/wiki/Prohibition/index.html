Prohibition

Prohibition is the act or practice of forbidding something by law; more particularly the term refers to the banning of the manufacture, storage, transportation, sale, possession, and consumption of alcoholic beverages. In its most famous application, the period known as Prohibition in the United States lasted from 1920 to 1933, during which a nationwide constitutional law prohibited the production, importation, transportation, and sale of alcoholic beverages.

Origins

The temperance movement, driven by organizations such as the Woman's Christian Temperance Union and the Anti-Saloon League, had campaigned against alcohol throughout the 19th century, blaming it for poverty, domestic violence and political corruption. Their efforts culminated in the ratification of the Eighteenth Amendment in 1919 and the Volstead Act, which defined "intoxicating liquors" and set enforcement rules; national prohibition took effect on 17 January 1920.

Effects

Alcohol consumption fell initially, and rates of cirrhosis and alcohol-related deaths declined. However, enforcement proved difficult and unpopular. Illegal production and sale flourished: bootlegging, speakeasies, and smuggling ("rum-running") supplied a vast black market, while organized crime syndicates — most notoriously that of Al Capone in Chicago — grew rich and violent. Corruption among police and officials was widespread, and adulterated or poisonous liquor caused many deaths.

Repeal

Public support eroded during the Great Depression, as legal alcohol promised jobs and tax revenue. The Twenty-first Amendment, ratified on 5 December 1933, repealed the Eighteenth — the only instance in American history of a constitutional amendment being overturned by another. Regulation of alcohol passed largely to the states, several of which remained "dry" for decades afterward; Mississippi was the last to end statewide prohibition, in 1966.

Other countries

Prohibition has also been enacted, in various forms and durations, in Canada, Iceland, Norway, Finland, and the Russian Empire and Soviet Union, and alcohol remains banned or restricted in a number of countries and regions today, generally on religious grounds.
