Lotus 1-2-3

Lotus 1-2-3

Developer: Lotus Development Corporation

Initial release: 26 January 1983

Type: Spreadsheet

Discontinued: 2014 (support ended)

Lotus 1-2-3 is a discontinued spreadsheet program from Lotus Software (later part of IBM). It was the first "killer application" of the IBM PC, was hugely popular in the 1980s, and significantly contributed to the success of IBM PC-compatibles in the business market.

History

Lotus 1-2-3 was developed by Jonathan Sachs and released by Mitch Kapor's Lotus Development Corporation on 26 January 1983. Its name referred to its three capabilities: spreadsheet, charting/graphing, and database functions. Written in assembly language and bypassing the slow DOS screen output, it was dramatically faster than its rival VisiCalc and could use the full memory of the new IBM PC.

Dominance and decline

Throughout the 1980s 1-2-3 was the standard corporate spreadsheet, but Lotus was slow to transition to Microsoft Windows. Microsoft Excel, launched for the Macintosh in 1985 and Windows in 1987, gradually displaced it; by the mid-1990s Excel dominated. IBM, which acquired Lotus in 1995, finally ended sales of the SmartSuite suite containing 1-2-3 in 2013.

Legacy

Lotus 1-2-3's file formats (.wk1, .wk3, .wk4) were widely used standards, and its menu style and macro language influenced later spreadsheets. The program is credited with popularising the personal computer in large corporations.
