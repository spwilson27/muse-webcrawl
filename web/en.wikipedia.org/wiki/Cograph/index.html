Cograph

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In graph theory, a cograph, or complement-reducible graph, or P4-free graph, is a graph that can be generated from the single-vertex graph K1 by the operations of complementation and disjoint union. Equivalently, cographs are exactly the graphs that do not contain the path graph on four vertices, P4, as an induced subgraph.

Characterization

Cographs can be characterised in several equivalent ways:

  • They are the graphs generated from a single vertex by disjoint union and complementation.
  • They are the graphs with no induced subgraph isomorphic to the four-vertex path P4.
  • Every nontrivial induced subgraph of a cograph has at least two vertices with the same neighbourhood, or is disconnected or has a disconnected complement.
  • Every cograph has diameter at most 2 in each connected component with more than one vertex.

Cotrees

Every cograph has a representation called a cotree: a rooted tree whose leaves are the vertices of the graph, and whose internal nodes are labelled 0 (disjoint union) or 1 (join), alternating by depth. Two vertices are adjacent exactly when their lowest common ancestor in the cotree has label 1. Cotrees can be constructed in linear time and underlie fast algorithms on cographs.

Algorithms and applications

Because of their restricted structure, many problems that are NP-hard in general — such as graph colouring, clique cover, maximum clique, maximum independent set and Hamiltonian path — can be solved in linear or polynomial time on cographs, typically by dynamic programming on the cotree. Cographs have been applied in areas such as scheduling, computational biology (for example in modelling orthology relations between genes), and the analysis of series-parallel networks.

History

Cographs were studied independently by several researchers in the 1970s under different names, including complement-reducible graphs (Corneil, Lerchs and Stewart Burlingham, 1981) and hereditary Dacey graphs. The term "cograph" comes from "complement-reducible graph".
