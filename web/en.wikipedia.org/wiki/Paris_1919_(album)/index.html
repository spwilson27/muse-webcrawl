Paris 1919
Studio album by John Cale
Released: 25 February 1973
Recorded: 1972, Sunwest Studios, Los Angeles
Genre: Baroque pop, art rock
Length: 31:30
Label: Reprise
Producer: John Cale

Paris 1919 is the third solo studio album by Welsh musician John Cale, released on 25 February 1973 by Reprise Records. Produced by Cale himself, it is widely regarded as his most accessible and commercially oriented solo work, combining lush orchestral arrangements (conducted with the UCLA Symphony Orchestra) with literate, historically allusive songwriting. Members of the rock band Little Feat, including Lowell George and Richie Hayward, played on the record, along with bassist Wilton Felder.

Background and content

The album's title refers to the Paris Peace Conference of 1919, held after the First World War. Its songs mix wistful nostalgia with oblique references to European history and culture: "Child's Christmas in Wales" borrows its title from Dylan Thomas, "Macbeth" reworks Shakespeare, and "Graham Greene" pays tribute to the novelist. The closing track, "Antarctica Starts Here", is a portrait of a fading film star, widely interpreted as a reference to Gloria Swanson's character in Sunset Boulevard.

Reception and legacy

Although not a major commercial success on release, Paris 1919 has grown steadily in stature and is frequently cited as Cale's masterpiece. A remastered edition with alternate versions and outtakes was released in 2006. Cale performed the album live in its entirety on several occasions, including a notable series of orchestral concerts beginning in 2009.
