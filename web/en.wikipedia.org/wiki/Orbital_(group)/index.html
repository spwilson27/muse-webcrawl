Orbital (group)

Orbital are an English electronic music duo from Otford, Kent, England, consisting of brothers Phil and Paul Hartnoll. The band's name is taken from Greater London's orbital motorway, the M25, which was central to the early rave scene during the initial years of acid house. Additionally, the cover art on three of their albums showcases stylised atomic orbitals.

Career

Orbital recorded "Chime" in 1989 on a cassette deck in their parents' house; the track became an anthem of the acid house scene and reached the UK top 20 in 1990. Their debut album (the "Green Album", 1991) and its follow-up (the "Brown Album", 1993) are regarded as classics of British techno, the latter featuring "Halcyon + On + On," built around a sample of Kirsty Hawkshaw's voice from Opus III's "It's a Fine Day."

The duo were pioneers of live electronic performance, improvising with banks of sequencers rather than miming to backing tapes, and wearing head-mounted torches that became a visual signature. Their 1994 Glastonbury Festival performance is widely credited with bringing dance music to a mainstream rock audience. Albums such as In Sides (1996), containing "The Box," and The Middle of Nowhere (1999) continued their success; the single "Belfast" and their soundtrack work, including the score for Event Horizon (1997) with Michael Kamen, broadened their profile.

Orbital split in 2004, reformed in 2008, split again in 2014, and reunited in 2017, releasing Monsters Exist (2018) and Optical Delusion (2023).
