The 2017 Atlantic hurricane season was a hyperactive and catastrophic season, among the most destructive ever recorded. It produced 17 named storms, ten hurricanes and six major hurricanes, and featured three devastating storms in quick succession — Harvey, Irma and Maria — that struck the United States and the Caribbean within a five-week span. Total damage approached $295 billion, making it by far the costliest Atlantic hurricane season on record at the time.

Major storms

Hurricane Harvey made landfall in Texas as a Category 4 hurricane on 25 August and stalled over the Houston area, dropping up to 60.58 inches of rain — a US tropical cyclone record — and causing catastrophic flooding and about $125 billion in damage. Hurricane Irma reached peak winds of 180 mph, maintained Category 5 intensity longer than any storm then on record worldwide, and devastated Barbuda, Saint Martin, the Virgin Islands, Cuba and Florida. Hurricane Maria struck Puerto Rico as a high-end Category 4 on 20 September, destroying the island's power grid and causing a prolonged humanitarian crisis; the Puerto Rican government's official death toll was later revised to 2,975.

Other notable storms included Jose, which threatened the Leeward Islands days after Irma; Nate, a fast-moving October hurricane that hit the US Gulf Coast; and Ophelia, the easternmost major hurricane on record in the Atlantic, which later struck Ireland as an extratropical storm.

Retired names

The names Harvey, Irma, Maria and Nate were retired by the World Meteorological Organization and replaced by Harold, Idalia, Margot and Nigel respectively.
