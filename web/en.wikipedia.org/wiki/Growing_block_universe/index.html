Growing block universe

Field: Philosophy of time

Related views: Eternalism, presentism

The growing block universe, or growing block theory of time, is a theory of time according to which the past and present exist, but the future does not yet exist. On this view, reality is like a block that grows as new slices of time come into being; the present is the leading edge of the block.

Overview

The theory was defended by philosophers such as C. D. Broad in the early twentieth century and has been developed in modern form by Michael Tooley and others. Unlike presentism, which holds that only the present exists, the growing block view grants reality to past events; unlike eternalism, it denies existence to future events, preserving an open future.

Criticism

Critics ask how we can know that we are at the present edge of the block rather than at some past slice, and how the view fits with relativistic physics, in which simultaneity is frame-dependent.
