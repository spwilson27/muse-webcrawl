Fez, Morocco

Fez (Arabic: فاس, French: Fès) is a city in northern inland Morocco and the capital of the Fez-Meknes region. It is the second largest city in Morocco, with a population of about 1.11 million (2014). Located northwest of the Atlas Mountains, Fez is linked to several important cities and is known for its ancient walled medina.

History

Founded under Idris I in 789 and built up under Idris II in 808, Fez became a major centre of trade, religion and learning. The University of al-Qarawiyyin, founded in 859 by Fatima al-Fihri, is recognised as one of the oldest existing universities. Under the Marinids in the 13th–15th centuries Fez reached its golden age.

The city consists of Fez el Bali (old Fez), Fez el Jedid (new Fez, 13th century, home to the Mellah Jewish quarter) and the modern Ville Nouvelle built under French rule.

Medina

Fez el Bali is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, believed to be one of the world's largest car-free urban areas. Its narrow alleys contain mosques, madrasas such as Bou Inania and Al-Attarine, souks and tanneries. See Souk and Kasbah.

Geography and culture

Fez lies near the Middle Atlas and the historic routes to Tangier, Algiers and Timbuktu. It hosts the Festival of World Sacred Music. Leather goods from its tanneries and ceramics are famous exports.
