Tulare Lake is a freshwater dry lake in the southern San Joaquin Valley, in Kings County, California. Historically it was the largest freshwater lake west of the Mississippi River and the second-largest freshwater lake entirely within the United States (after Lake Michigan), fed by the Kern, Tule, Kaweah and Kings rivers from the Sierra Nevada and rimmed by tule marshes — from which its name derives. In the late 19th century its inflows were diverted for irrigation and the lake bed was drained and converted to some of the most productive farmland in the world, though the lake re-forms in exceptionally wet years.


Tulare Lake re-formed over its former bed in 2023

History

The lake basin was home to the Yokuts peoples, who fished and hunted waterfowl in its marshes. In wet years such as 1862 the lake rose dramatically, flooding surrounding settlements. Beginning in the 1870s–1880s, settlers built levees and canals and diverted the rivers; by the early 20th century the lake had largely dried, and its bed became farmland controlled by large agricultural enterprises, notably the Boswell and Salyer operations, whose water conflicts became famous in California history. The remaining marshes are protected in fragments, and the lake bed supports towns such as Corcoran, which has struggled with subsidence and flood risk.

Reappearances

The lake has refilled in major flood years including 1969, 1983, 1997 and, most dramatically, 2023, when record Sierra snowpack meltwater inundated well over 100,000 acres of farmland, displacing residents and reviving the name Pa'ashi used by the Tachi Yokut tribe. These episodes have prompted debate about permanent restoration of part of the lake as wetland habitat and flood storage.
