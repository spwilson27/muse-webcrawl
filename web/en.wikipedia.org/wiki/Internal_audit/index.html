Internal audit

Internal auditors independently evaluate an organisation's controls and processes.

Internal auditing is an independent, objective assurance and consulting activity designed to add value and improve an organisation's operations. It helps an organisation accomplish its objectives by bringing a systematic, disciplined approach to evaluate and improve the effectiveness of risk management, control, and governance processes.

Unlike external auditors, who focus mainly on the accuracy of financial statements for outside stakeholders, internal auditors are typically employees of the organisation (or an outsourced provider) and examine a broad range of topics: financial controls, operational efficiency, compliance with laws and policies, information security, and fraud prevention.

Role and independence

The internal audit function usually reports functionally to the board's audit committee and administratively to senior management, preserving independence from the operations it reviews. Work is planned using a risk-based approach, prioritising areas of greatest exposure. The profession is represented globally by the Institute of Internal Auditors (IIA), founded in 1941, which issues the Global Internal Audit Standards and the Certified Internal Auditor (CIA) designation.

Process

Audits follow a cycle of planning, fieldwork (testing and evidence gathering), reporting findings and recommendations, and follow-up on agreed corrective actions. Internal audit is one of the "three lines" of governance, alongside management controls and external assurance providers.
