The 2005 civil unrest in France was a three-week period of rioting beginning on 27 October 2005 in Clichy-sous-Bois, a suburb northeast of Paris, and spreading to suburbs of cities across France. The unrest began after the deaths of two teenagers, Zyed Benna (17) and Bouna Traoré (15), who were electrocuted in an electricity substation while hiding from police.

Rioters, many of them youths from immigrant communities, burned thousands of cars and attacked public buildings, schools, and police. The government of President Jacques Chirac declared a state of emergency on 8 November under a law dating from the Algerian War, authorising curfews, before order was gradually restored by mid-November.

Causes

Commentators linked the unrest to poverty, unemployment, discrimination, and tensions between police and young people in the banlieues, as well as to remarks by Interior Minister Nicolas Sarkozy, who had described delinquents in the suburbs as "scum" shortly before the riots began. The events prompted a national debate about integration and the French republican model.
