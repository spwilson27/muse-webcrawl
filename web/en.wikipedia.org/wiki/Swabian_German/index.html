Swabian (German: Schwäbisch, pronounced [ˈʃvɛːbɪʃ]) is a dialect of Alemannic German spoken in the Swabia region of southwestern Germany, spanning much of Baden-Württemberg — around Stuttgart, the Swabian Alb and Upper Swabia — and the Swabia administrative region of Bavaria around Augsburg. It is distinct from both standard German and the Bavarian dialects spoken to its east, and is a marker of strong regional identity.

Features

Swabian differs from standard German in its vowels (for example, the diphthong in ei words is often monophthongised), in diminutives formed with -le (as in Mädle, "girl", and Spätzle), in verb forms, and in a characteristic lexicon, including the all-purpose greeting and farewell Ade and the particle halt. The dialect varies noticeably between Stuttgart, the Alb, Upper Swabia and the Allgäu fringe, and shades into other Alemannic varieties toward Lake Constance. Swabian famously participated only partially in some High German sound developments, preserving distinctions lost elsewhere.

Status and culture

Like most German dialects, Swabian is under pressure from standard German in media and education, but it remains widely spoken across generations and classes in its heartland, and features prominently in regional theatre, cabaret and dialect literature and music. The stereotyped Swabian virtues of thrift and industry — summed up in the motto Schaffe, schaffe, Häusle baue ("Work, work, build a little house") — are a fixture of German popular humour, as is the figure of the Swabian emigrant: large numbers emigrated in the 18th and 19th centuries to southeastern Europe (the "Danube Swabians"), Russia and the Americas.
