Steve McCurry

Steve McCurry (born February 24, 1950) is an American photographer and photojournalist, a long-time member of Magnum Photos, best known for his photograph Afghan Girl, a portrait of a young Afghan refugee girl with piercing green eyes that appeared on the cover of National Geographic in June 1985 and became one of the most famous magazine images of all time.

Career

McCurry studied cinematography and film at Pennsylvania State University before working for a newspaper and then travelling to India as a freelancer. In 1979 he crossed into Afghanistan disguised in local dress just before the Soviet invasion, smuggling his film out sewn into his clothing; his images were among the first of the conflict and won him the Robert Capa Gold Medal. He went on to cover conflicts and cultures across Asia — Iran, Iraq, Beirut, Cambodia, the Philippines, the Gulf War, and the former Yugoslavia — frequently for National Geographic.

Afghan Girl

McCurry photographed the orphaned Pashtun girl at the Nasir Bagh refugee camp in Pakistan in 1984. The subject's identity remained unknown until 2002, when a National Geographic expedition, assisted by an FBI forensic examiner, located her: Sharbat Gula. McCurry later became one of the last photographers to shoot extensively with Kodachrome film, and was given the final roll produced by Kodak in 2010. In 2016 he faced criticism after viewers noticed digital alterations in some published images, prompting discussion about manipulation in photojournalism.
