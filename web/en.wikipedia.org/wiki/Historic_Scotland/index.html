Historic Scotland

Historic Scotland (Scottish Gaelic: Alba Aosmhor) was an executive agency of the Scottish Government from 1991 to 2015, responsible for safeguarding Scotland's built heritage and promoting its understanding and enjoyment. On 1 October 2015 its functions were merged with those of the Royal Commission on the Ancient and Historical Monuments of Scotland (RCAHMS) to form a new non-departmental public body, Historic Environment Scotland.

Role

The agency managed more than 360 properties in state care, including Edinburgh Castle, Stirling Castle, Skara Brae, Fort George, Urquhart Castle and the prehistoric monuments of Orkney. It advised Scottish Ministers on heritage policy, administered the scheduling of ancient monuments and the listing of historic buildings, distributed grant funding for conservation, and carried out conservation and research work at its sites.

History

Responsibility for ancient monuments in Scotland originally rested with UK government departments, beginning under the Ancient Monuments Protection Act 1882 and passing through the Ministry of Works and the Scottish Development Department. Historic Scotland was established as an agency in 1991. Its commercial activities — admissions, retail, membership and venue hire at major castles — made it substantially self-funding, and its membership scheme enrolled well over one hundred thousand members.

Historic Environment Scotland

The Historic Environment (Scotland) Act 2014 created Historic Environment Scotland, which took over Historic Scotland's properties, archives and regulatory functions alongside the survey and recording work of RCAHMS, forming a single lead body for Scotland's historic environment.
