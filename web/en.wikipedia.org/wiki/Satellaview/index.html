The Satellaview (Japanese: サテラビュー) is a satellite modem peripheral for Nintendo's Super Famicom (Super Nintendo Entertainment System), released only in Japan in 1995. Developed in partnership with the satellite radio company St.GIGA, the system allowed users to receive digital satellite broadcasts containing games, magazines, and other data, which were stored on rewritable flash memory packs.

Overview

The Satellaview hardware consisted of a large modem unit that sat beneath the Super Famicom and the BS-X cartridge, which provided an interactive menu presented as a virtual town. Broadcasts followed a daily schedule: at set times, subscribers could download games and "Soundlink" titles that combined gameplay with streamed voice acting and music transmitted live over the satellite. Because Soundlink content depended on the live broadcast, many of these games were playable only during their scheduled hours.

Games and broadcasts

Notable Satellaview releases included BS Zelda no Densetsu, a reworked broadcast version of The Legend of Zelda; BS Super Mario USA; Radical Dreamers, a text adventure by Squaresoft related to Chrono Trigger; and numerous magazine-style broadcasts. Content also included digital magazines covering news, music, and gaming.

Legacy

Nintendo ended Satellaview support in June 2000. Because much of its content was broadcast ephemerally and rarely preserved in complete form, the Satellaview has become a focus of video game preservation efforts, with enthusiasts working to recover and emulate its games and broadcasts from surviving memory packs and recordings.
