Albert Henry Munsell (6 January 1858 – 28 June 1918) was an American painter, teacher of art and inventor of the Munsell color system, the first widely adopted method for specifying colours numerically according to how they are perceived.

Life

Munsell studied art in Boston and Paris and taught at the Massachusetts Normal Art School. Frustrated by the imprecise and fanciful colour names used by manufacturers and teachers, he set out to build a rational system of colour description based on measured perception.

The Munsell color system

Munsell described his system in A Color Notation (1905) and the Atlas of the Munsell Color System (1915). Every colour is specified by three attributes: hue (the colour family, arranged around a circle of ten principal hues), value (lightness, from black 0 to white 10), and chroma (saturation or colour strength). Colours are written in the form H V/C, such as 5R 6/14 for a strong red. He also invented a photometer to measure value objectively.

Legacy

After his death the Munsell Color Company, founded by his son, continued the work, producing the Munsell Book of Color. The system became a standard in fields from soil science — where soil colours are recorded in Munsell notation — to dentistry, brewing, archaeology and government specification.
