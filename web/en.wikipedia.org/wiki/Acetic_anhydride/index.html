Acetic anhydride

FormulaC4H6O3
Molar mass102.09 g/mol
AppearanceColourless liquid
Boiling point139.8 °C
CAS number108-24-7

Acetic anhydride, or ethanoic anhydride, is the chemical compound with the formula (CH3CO)2O. Commonly abbreviated Ac2O, it is the simplest isolable anhydride of a carboxylic acid and is widely used as a reagent in organic synthesis. It is a colourless liquid that smells strongly of acetic acid, which it forms on contact with moisture.

Production

Acetic anhydride is produced industrially by the carbonylation of methyl acetate, and by routes involving ketene, which is generated by dehydrating acetic acid at high temperature and then reacted with further acetic acid. It hydrolyses readily in water to give two molecules of acetic acid.

Uses

The principal use of acetic anhydride is acetylation. It converts wood cellulose into cellulose acetate, used in films, fibres, and cigarette filters; it acetylates salicylic acid to make aspirin; and it is used in the production of other pharmaceuticals, dyes, and acetylated starch. Because it is also a precursor chemical in the illicit production of heroin (by acetylation of morphine), its sale is regulated in many jurisdictions.

Safety

Acetic anhydride is corrosive, a lachrymator, and flammable. It reacts exothermically with water and alcohols and must be handled with appropriate protective equipment.
