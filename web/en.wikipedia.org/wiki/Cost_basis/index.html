Cost basis

[Image: Cost basis]

Cost basis (or tax basis) is the original value of an asset for tax purposes, usually the purchase price, adjusted for stock splits, dividends, return of capital distributions and depreciation. It is used to determine the capital gain or loss when the asset is sold, which is the difference between the asset's sale price and its cost basis.

Calculation

The initial basis of purchased property is generally its cost, including commissions and fees. Basis is increased by the cost of improvements and decreased by deductions such as depreciation and casualty losses. For inherited property in the United States, the basis is generally "stepped up" to the fair market value at the date of the decedent's death, while gifted property usually retains the donor's basis.

Methods for securities

When shares of stock or mutual funds acquired at different times and prices are sold, taxpayers may identify which shares were sold using specific identification, the first-in first-out (FIFO) method, or, for mutual funds, an average cost method. Since 2011, brokers in the United States have been required to report the cost basis of most covered securities to the Internal Revenue Service.
