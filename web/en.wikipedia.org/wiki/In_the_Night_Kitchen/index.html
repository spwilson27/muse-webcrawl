In the Night Kitchen is a children's picture book written and illustrated by Maurice Sendak, first published in 1970. It tells the dreamlike story of a young boy named Mickey who falls through the dark into the surreal "night kitchen," where three bakers—modeled on Oliver Hardy—nearly bake him into batter before he escapes, fashions an airplane from bread dough, fetches milk for the bakers, and returns to bed.

Style and themes

Sendak drew the book's visual style from 1930s New York City, comic strips such as Winsor McCay's Little Nemo, and his own childhood memories. The book forms an informal trilogy with Where the Wild Things Are (1963) and Outside Over There (1981), each exploring children's emotions and fantasy.

Controversy

The book has frequently appeared on lists of challenged and banned books in the United States, largely because Mickey is depicted nude in several illustrations. Some librarians famously drew diapers on the character with white paint. Despite the controversy, it was named a Caldecott Honor Book and remains a classic of children's literature.
