Great Canadian Flag Debate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great Canadian Flag Debate was a national political controversy in Canada in 1963–64 over the replacement of the Canadian Red Ensign with a distinctive national flag. It culminated in the adoption of the red-and-white maple leaf flag, designed by George Stanley and John Matheson, which was proclaimed by Queen Elizabeth II and first raised on Parliament Hill on 15 February 1965.

Background

Since 1868 Canada had used versions of the Red Ensign — a British ensign defaced with the Canadian arms — as its de facto national flag, though it never had secure statutory status. From the 1920s onward, governments periodically considered a distinct flag, and the issue became urgent as Canada's centennial approached and Prime Minister Lester B. Pearson sought symbols of a bilingual, independent national identity, particularly for French Canadians for whom the British ensign held little appeal.

The debate

Pearson's minority Liberal government introduced a design in May 1964 — the "Pearson Pennant", with three red maple leaves between blue bars — but it was derided by the opposition. Progressive Conservative leader and former prime minister John Diefenbaker fiercely defended the Red Ensign, and the ensuing parliamentary debate became one of the longest and most bitter in Canadian history, with the opposition filibustering through the summer. Pearson referred the question to a 15-member all-party committee, which, after reviewing thousands of submissions, selected Stanley's single-maple-leaf design. The House of Commons approved it on 15 December 1964, after the government invoked closure; the Senate concurred two days later.

Outcome

The new flag was inaugurated on 15 February 1965, a date now marked as National Flag of Canada Day. Although veterans' organisations and many English Canadians initially mourned the Red Ensign, the maple leaf flag was rapidly accepted and is now among the world's most recognisable national symbols. The Royal Union Flag (Union Jack) remains an official flag of Canada for certain purposes, and the Red Ensign retains ceremonial status in some contexts.
