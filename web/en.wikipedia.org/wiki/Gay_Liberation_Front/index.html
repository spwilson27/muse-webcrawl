Gay Liberation Front

Founded1969, New York City
TypeLGBT rights activist movement
Key eventsStonewall uprising (1969), first Pride marches (1970)

The Gay Liberation Front (GLF) was the name of a number of gay liberation groups, the first of which was formed in New York City in 1969, immediately after the Stonewall uprising. Similar groups with the same name later formed in the United Kingdom and Canada, and chapters spread across the United States. The GLF took its name in conscious imitation of liberation movements such as the Algerian and Vietnamese National Liberation Fronts.

History

The New York GLF was founded in the weeks following the Stonewall riots of June 1969 by activists who believed the earlier homophile movement was too cautious. The GLF adopted a radical politics, aligning itself with the New Left, the anti-war movement, and Black liberation, and argued that gay oppression was inseparable from the broader structures of sexism, racism, and capitalism.

In June 1970, GLF members helped organise the Christopher Street Liberation Day march, held on the first anniversary of Stonewall and regarded as the first Gay Pride march. Activists such as Marsha P. Johnson and Sylvia Rivera emerged from this milieu, going on to found Street Transvestite Action Revolutionaries (STAR).

United Kingdom

The London Gay Liberation Front was founded in October 1970 at the London School of Economics. It campaigned against police harassment, psychiatry's treatment of homosexuality, and employment discrimination, and organised the first British Gay Pride march in London in 1972.

Legacy

Although the original GLF organisations were short-lived and soon fragmented into groups such as the Gay Activists Alliance, the Front transformed the politics of homosexuality from quiet respectability to open pride and liberation, and it gave the wider movement its enduring name and symbols.
