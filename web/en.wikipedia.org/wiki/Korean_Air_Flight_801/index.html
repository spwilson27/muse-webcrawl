Korean Air Flight 801

Korean Air Flight 801 (KE801/KAL801) was a scheduled international passenger flight from Seoul–Gimpo, South Korea, to Guam. On 6 August 1997 the Boeing 747-300 operating the flight crashed on approach to Antonio B. Won Pat International Airport, striking Nimitz Hill about 5 km short of the runway in heavy rain. Of the 254 people on board, 229 died, making it one of the deadliest aviation accidents of the decade.

Accident

The flight approached Guam at night in a rainstorm with the airport's instrument landing system glide slope out of service. The crew, whose captain was fatigued and inadequately briefed for the non-precision approach, descended below the minimum altitude; confusion in the cockpit over ground-proximity warnings and over whether the airport was in sight delayed any go-around. The aircraft struck the hill, broke up and burned; rescue efforts were hampered by the remote terrain, a severed fuel pipeline fire and poor weather.

Investigation

The U.S. National Transportation Safety Board concluded that the probable cause was the captain's failure to adequately brief and execute the approach and the flight crew's failure to monitor and cross-check, with Korean Air's inadequate crew training contributing. The accident became a landmark case study in crew resource management and in debates about hierarchical cockpit culture in Korean aviation, prompting reforms at Korean Air under international scrutiny.
