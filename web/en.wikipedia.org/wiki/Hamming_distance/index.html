Hamming distance

In information theory, the Hamming distance between two strings of equal length is the number of positions at which the corresponding symbols are different. Equivalently, it measures the minimum number of substitutions required to change one string into the other, or the number of errors that transformed one string into the other. It is named after Richard Hamming, who introduced the concept in his foundational work on error-detecting and error-correcting codes.

Examples

The Hamming distance between the binary words 1011101 and 1001001 is 2. For decimal digit strings, the distance between 2173896 and 2233796 is 3. For words over an ordinary alphabet, the distance between "karolin" and "kathrin" is 3, while between "toned" and "roses" it is also 3. For binary strings the Hamming distance equals the number of 1 bits (the Hamming weight) in the XOR of the two strings, and it can be computed efficiently with a population-count instruction.

Properties

Hamming distance satisfies the axioms of a metric: it is non-negative, zero only for identical strings, symmetric, and obeys the triangle inequality. For a fixed length n and alphabet size q, the set of all strings forms the Hamming space, often visualised as the vertices of a graph (the Hamming graph) in which adjacent vertices differ in exactly one position.

Applications

The central use is in coding theory: a block code's error-correcting capability is governed by the minimum Hamming distance between its codewords — a code with minimum distance d can detect up to d − 1 errors and correct up to ⌊(d − 1)/2⌋ errors. Hamming distance also appears in computer science for comparing bit strings and fingerprints, in clustering and nearest-neighbour search (where spaces of binary vectors are indexed by distance), in genetics for comparing aligned sequences of equal length, and in linguistics and systematics as a simple measure of dissimilarity. For strings of unequal length, the related Levenshtein (edit) distance, which also permits insertions and deletions, is used instead.
