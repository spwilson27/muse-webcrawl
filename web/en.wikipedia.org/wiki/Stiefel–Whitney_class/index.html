Stiefel–Whitney class

In mathematics, in particular in algebraic topology and differential geometry, the Stiefel–Whitney classes are a set of topological invariants of a real vector bundle that describe the obstructions to constructing everywhere independent sets of sections of the bundle. Stiefel–Whitney classes, named for Eduard Stiefel and Hassler Whitney, are an important example of a characteristic class. They take values in cohomology with coefficients in the field of two elements, Z/2.

Overview

For a vector bundle E over a space B, the total Stiefel–Whitney class is an element w(E) = 1 + w1(E) + w2(E) + ⋯ of the cohomology ring H*(B; Z/2). The individual classes wi(E) live in degree i. The first Stiefel–Whitney class w1 is an obstruction to orientability of the bundle, and the second class w2 is an obstruction to the existence of a spin structure (once the bundle is oriented).

Properties

  • Naturality: the classes are preserved under pullback of the bundle.
  • Whitney product formula: the total class of a direct sum of bundles is the product of the total classes.
  • Normalization: the top class of the tangent bundle of a sphere is nonzero.

Applications

Stiefel–Whitney classes of the tangent bundle of a manifold determine many of its properties: a manifold is orientable if and only if its first Stiefel–Whitney class vanishes, and the Stiefel–Whitney numbers play a central role in cobordism theory, featuring in René Thom's characterization of cobordism classes.
