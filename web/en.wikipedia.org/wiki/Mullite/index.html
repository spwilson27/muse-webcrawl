Mullite

Mullite or porcelainite is a rare silicate mineral of the composition 3Al2O3·2SiO2, an aluminium silicate. It is named after the Isle of Mull in Scotland, where it was first described in 1924, occurring in fused argillaceous xenoliths within Tertiary lavas.

Occurrence and properties

Mullite forms under unusual conditions of very high temperature and relatively low pressure, for example where clay-rich rocks are baked by intruding magma or burned in coal seams. It is orthorhombic and typically forms slender needle-like crystals.

Uses

Synthetic mullite is an important refractory ceramic. Because it retains strength at high temperatures, has low thermal expansion and good thermal shock resistance, it is used in kiln furniture, furnace linings, crucibles and as a component of porcelain and other high-temperature ceramics.
