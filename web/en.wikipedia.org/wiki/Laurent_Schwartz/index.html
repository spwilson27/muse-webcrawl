Laurent Schwartz

Born: 1915, Paris
Died: 2002, Paris
Known for: Theory of distributions
Award: Fields Medal (1950)

Laurent-Moïse Schwartz (1915–2002) was a French mathematician who created the theory of distributions (generalised functions), giving rigorous meaning to objects such as the Dirac delta function, and received the Fields Medal in 1950 — the first French recipient. He was a leading member of the Bourbaki group.

Life and work

Schwartz studied at the École Normale Supérieure. During the Second World War he, being Jewish, lived under a false name while teaching at Clermont-Ferrand. His multi-volume Théorie des distributions (1950–51) revolutionised analysis and partial differential equations. He later worked on functional analysis and probability, taught at the University of Paris and the École Polytechnique, and trained many students, including Alexander Grothendieck among his early associates.

Activism

Schwartz was a prominent left-wing intellectual: a Trotskyist in youth, campaigner against the Algerian War — he was stripped of his professorship after signing the Manifesto of the 121 — and against the Vietnam War and Soviet intervention in Afghanistan. He was also an avid butterfly collector, with species named after him.
