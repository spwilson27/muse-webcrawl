Location: Parkhead, Glasgow, Scotland

Celtic Park is a football stadium in the Parkhead area of Glasgow, and is the home ground of Celtic Football Club. With a capacity of 60,411, it is the largest football stadium in Scotland, and the eighth-largest stadium in the United Kingdom. It is also known as Parkhead or Paradise.

Celtic formed in 1887 and the first Celtic Park was opened in 1888, on a site adjacent to the present stadium. The club moved to the current site in 1892, after the landlord raised the rent on the original ground. The new site was a disused brickworks, and the stadium was built largely by volunteers. Celtic Park hosted its first international match in 1894, when Scotland played Ireland.

Structure

The stadium consists of four stands: the North Stand, the Jock Stein (West) Stand, the Lisbon Lions (East) Stand, and the Main (South) Stand. The North Stand, completed in 1995 as part of Fergus McCann's redevelopment of the ground, is the largest single stand. The redevelopment converted Celtic Park into an all-seater stadium and was carried out in phases between 1994 and 1998. A statue of club founder Brother Walfrid stands outside the main entrance, and statues of Jock Stein, Jimmy Johnstone and Billy McNeill also stand at the stadium.

Other uses

Celtic Park has hosted concerts, and in 2014 it hosted the opening ceremony of the Commonwealth Games. It has also been used for Scotland international matches on occasions when Hampden Park was unavailable.
