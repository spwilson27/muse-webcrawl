Carpool

Carpooling (also car-sharing, ride-sharing, lift-sharing) is the sharing of car journeys so that more than one person travels in a car, reducing each person's travel costs such as fuel, tolls, and driving stress, as well as reducing traffic congestion and air pollution.

History

Carpooling became significant in the United States during World War II as a rationing measure, promoted by government posters urging "When you ride ALONE you ride with Hitler!" It surged again after the 1973 oil crisis and 1979 energy crisis, prompting regulations encouraging ridesharing and the creation of high-occupancy vehicle (HOV) lanes.

Organization

Carpools may be arranged informally among neighbors, coworkers, or parents driving children, or organized through employers, schools, ridematching databases, and smartphone apps. Participants may rotate driving duties or share fuel costs. "Slugging" is a form of casual carpooling popular in cities such as Washington, D.C., where passengers line up at known locations to fill cars so drivers can use HOV lanes.

Benefits and challenges

Benefits include cost savings, fewer vehicles on the road, lower emissions, and access to faster HOV lanes. Challenges include scheduling rigidity, coordination among participants, and safety concerns. Variants include vanpools, real-time ridesharing, and formal ride-sharing programs.
