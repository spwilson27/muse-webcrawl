Arusha Region (Swahili: Mkoa wa Arusha) is one of Tanzania's 31 administrative regions, located in the northeast of the country. Its capital and largest city is Arusha. The region borders Kenya to the north, Kilimanjaro Region to the east, Manyara Region to the south, and Singida and Simiyu regions to the southwest and west respectively.

Geography

The region covers about 37,576 square kilometres and spans dramatic terrain: the volcanic highlands of Mount Meru and the Crater Highlands, the floor of the Great Rift Valley with its alkaline lakes such as Lake Natron, and extensive Maasai steppe savanna. The Ngorongoro Conservation Area—including the famous Ngorongoro Crater, the world's largest intact volcanic caldera—lies in the region's west, as do parts of Lake Manyara and Tarangire ecosystems. The archaeological site of Olduvai Gorge, in the Ngorongoro area, yielded key evidence of early human evolution, including finds by Louis and Mary Leakey.

Districts

Arusha Region is divided into seven districts: Arusha City, Arusha Rural, Meru, Monduli, Karatu, Longido, and Ngorongoro. Longido and Ngorongoro are predominantly Maasai pastoralist districts, while the Meru foothills support dense smallholder farming by the Meru and Arusha peoples.

Demographics

The region's population was 1,694,310 at the 2012 census and about 2.36 million at the 2022 census. Major ethnic groups include the Wa-Arusha, Meru, Maasai, Iraqw, and Sonjo. Swahili is the lingua franca; Maa and other languages are widely spoken in pastoralist areas.

Economy

Tourism dominates the regional economy: Arusha city is the staging point for the northern safari circuit, and Ngorongoro is among Africa's most visited conservation areas. Agriculture includes coffee and cut-flower production in the highlands, maize and beans on smallholdings, and extensive livestock herding. The Mererani area near the regional border produces tanzanite, found nowhere else on Earth. Periodic drought and pressure over grazing land, water, and conservation boundaries are major regional challenges.

History

The modern region was created at Tanzanian independence, and its boundaries were adjusted when Manyara Region was carved from it in 2002. In 2007 a proposal to split off a new region was debated but not implemented. The region's name derives from the Wa-Arusha, a people closely related to the Meru, who settled the western foothills of Mount Meru centuries ago.
