Military history of Canada

Canadian soldiers at the Battle of Vimy Ridge, a defining moment in Canadian military history.

The military history of Canada comprises centuries of conflict on Canadian soil and Canadian participation in wars abroad, from the colonial French and British regimes through the Boer War, the two World Wars, Korea, and Afghanistan.

Colonial era

French and British forces, together with Indigenous allies, contested North America in a series of wars culminating in the Seven Years' War, in which Britain conquered New France after the Battle of the Plains of Abraham (1759). During the American Revolutionary War, American invasions of Quebec were repelled. In the War of 1812, British regulars, Canadian militia, and Indigenous warriors under leaders such as Isaac Brock and Tecumseh defended Canada against American invasion; the war remains a touchstone of Canadian identity.

Confederation to the First World War

After Confederation in 1867, Canada created a permanent force and fielded contingents in the North-West Rebellion (1885) and the Second Boer War (1899–1902). In the First World War, more than 600,000 Canadians served; the Canadian Corps won renown at Ypres, the Somme, Vimy Ridge (1917), and Passchendaele, at a cost of some 61,000 dead.

Second World War and after

In the Second World War over one million Canadians served. Canadian forces fought in the Battle of the Atlantic, the bombing campaign over Europe, the Italian campaign, the Normandy landings, and the liberation of the Netherlands. Canada then fought in the Korean War (1950–1953), became a leading peacekeeping nation during the Cold War, and participated in NATO. In the 21st century, Canada's longest war was in Afghanistan (2001–2014), where 158 Canadian soldiers died.
