National Finals Rodeo

The National Finals Rodeo (NFR) is the premier championship rodeo event in the United States, organized by the Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA). Often called the "Super Bowl of rodeo", it is held each year in early December and crowns the world champions in each of rodeo's events.

History

The first National Finals Rodeo was held in Dallas, Texas, in 1959. The event subsequently moved to Los Angeles and then, from 1965 to 1984, to Oklahoma City, Oklahoma. In 1985 the NFR moved to Las Vegas, Nevada, where it has been held at the Thomas & Mack Center on the campus of the University of Nevada, Las Vegas. In 2020, because of pandemic restrictions in Nevada, the event was temporarily held at Globe Life Field in Arlington, Texas, before returning to Las Vegas.

Format

The top 15 money-winners in each event qualify for the NFR. Over ten consecutive performances ("go-rounds"), contestants accumulate prize money; the competitor with the highest average score or time in each event wins the NFR average title, and the season's total earnings determine the world champions. Events include bareback riding, steer wrestling, team roping (headers and heelers), saddle bronc riding, tie-down roping, barrel racing, breakaway roping, and bull riding.

Impact

The NFR draws hundreds of thousands of fans to Las Vegas each December and anchors ancillary events such as the Cowboy Christmas gift show. Purses have grown substantially over the decades, reaching millions of dollars, making it one of the most lucrative events in Western sports.
