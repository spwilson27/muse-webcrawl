FINA (French: Fédération Internationale de Natation), renamed World Aquatics in January 2023, is the international federation recognised by the International Olympic Committee (IOC) for administering international competitions in water sports. It oversees six aquatic disciplines: swimming, diving, high diving, water polo, artistic swimming, and open water swimming.

History

FINA was founded on 19 July 1908 at the Manchester Hotel in London, during the 1908 Summer Olympics, by representatives of eight national swimming federations: Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Great Britain, Hungary, and Sweden. Its creation aimed to standardise rules for swimming, diving, and water polo, which had varied between countries. Membership grew steadily through the twentieth century to more than 200 national federations organised in five continental associations.

The federation's flagship event, the World Aquatics Championships, was first held in Belgrade in 1973. Long-time president Mustapha Larfaoui led the organisation from 1988 to 2009; he was succeeded by Julio Maglione, and in 2021 by Kuwait's Husain Al-Musallam. In December 2022 the federation announced it would rebrand as World Aquatics, effective January 2023, and it later announced plans to relocate its headquarters from Lausanne to Budapest.

Competitions

World Aquatics organises the World Aquatics Championships (held in odd years and Olympic years on a revised cycle), the World Swimming Championships (25 m) in short-course pools, world cups in several disciplines, the World Junior Championships, and the Masters championships for veteran athletes. It also sets the rules and validates world records in its disciplines, and administers the aquatic events of the Olympic Games together with the IOC.

Governance and controversies

The federation has faced criticism over governance, transparency, and its handling of doping, including disputes over the participation of swimmers linked to state doping programmes and its ban, later contested, of the breakaway International Swimming League. In 2022, following the Russian invasion of Ukraine, Russian and Belarusian athletes were excluded from its events. The same year, its Congress adopted a policy restricting the participation of transgender women in female elite events and proposing an open category, a decision that attracted worldwide attention.
