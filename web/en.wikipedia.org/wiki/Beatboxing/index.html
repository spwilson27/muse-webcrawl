Beatboxing

Beatboxing (also beatbox) is a form of vocal percussion primarily involving the art of mimicking drum machines and other musical instruments using one's mouth, lips, tongue, throat and voice. It is one of the core elements of hip-hop culture, alongside rapping, DJing, breakdancing and graffiti. Modern beatboxing extends far beyond drum imitation to include simultaneous singing, polyphonic textures, electronic-sounding effects and full musical compositions performed solo or in teams.

History

Vocal imitation of instruments has antecedents in scat singing, blues and eefing, but beatboxing emerged as a distinct practice in early-1980s New York, where performers such as Doug E. Fresh — the self-styled "original human beatbox" — and Biz Markie provided rhythm tracks live where drum machines were unavailable or unaffordable. The Fat Boys' Buffy popularised comic, bass-heavy styles on record. In the 1990s and 2000s, Rahzel (of The Roots) demonstrated singing and beatboxing simultaneously, while artists like Killa Kela, Beardyman and Reeps One pushed technical complexity. Dedicated battle leagues and the Swissbeatbox platform in the 2010s globalised the scene, with world championships in solo, tag-team, crew and loopstation categories.

Techniques

Core sounds include the kick drum (a plosive "b"), hi-hat ("ts"), snare (inward "kch" or lip oscillation), and basslines produced by throat bass and vibration techniques. Advanced techniques include inward bass, polyphonic voice-and-beat layering, clicks, whistles, and the use of microphones and loop stations for layered composition. Scientific studies using real-time MRI have documented the unusual vocal-tract configurations beatboxers employ, some shared with sounds in the world's languages.
