Christmas Eve is the evening or entire day before Christmas Day, the festival commemorating the birth of Jesus. Christmas Day is observed around the world, and Christmas Eve is widely observed as a full or partial holiday in anticipation of it. Together, both days are considered one of the most culturally significant celebrations in Christendom and Western society.

Religious observance

Many Christians attend Midnight Mass or evening church services on Christmas Eve, a tradition rooted in the belief that Jesus was born at night; the liturgical day in the Western Church historically began at sunset. Lutheran and Anglican traditions often hold candlelight services with lessons and carols, while Eastern Orthodox churches observe a strict fast ending after the Christmas Eve liturgy, preceded by Royal Hours and the Vesperal Divine Liturgy of St Basil.

Traditions around the world

In much of continental Europe — Germany, Poland, Scandinavia and elsewhere — Christmas Eve (Heiligabend, Wigilia, julafton) is the main occasion for gift-giving and the festive family meal; Polish Wigilia supper traditionally comprises twelve dishes and begins with the first star. In Spain and Latin America Nochebuena is marked by a late family dinner and the Misa de Gallo (Midnight Mass). In Japan, Christmas Eve has become a romantic date night, with fried chicken and strawberry Christmas cake popularised by marketing campaigns. In the United States and Britain, children hang stockings for Santa Claus, and NORAD's Santa-tracking tradition began accidentally in 1955.

In culture

Charles Dickens's A Christmas Carol (1843), set on Christmas Eve, helped define the modern festival; the poem "'Twas the Night Before Christmas" (1823) fixed much of Santa Claus lore. The Christmas truce of 1914 began spontaneously on Christmas Eve in the trenches of the Western Front.
