Small sword

The small sword or smallsword (also called a court sword or dress sword) is a light one-handed sword designed primarily for thrusting, which was popular from the later 17th century through the 18th century. It developed from the rapier and served both as a gentleman's everyday sidearm and as a duelling weapon, and it is the direct ancestor of the modern fencing foil and épée.

Design

A typical small sword has a narrow, stiff blade about 75–90 cm (30–35 in) long, of triangular, hexagonal, or hollow-ground cross-section, optimized for the thrust and generally lacking an effective cutting edge. The hilt consists of a small shell or cup guard, a knuckle bow, quillons, a grip, and a pommel, frequently made of steel, silver, or gold and often richly decorated. The colichemarde blade, wider near the hilt and narrowing abruptly, was a popular variant in the early 18th century.

Use

The small sword was an essential part of a gentleman's attire in Europe and its colonies, worn with civilian dress as a mark of status as much as for defence. Fencing with the small sword was a core accomplishment of polite education, taught by fencing masters whose treatises codified its fast, precise thrust-oriented technique. It was the weapon of formal duels of honour throughout the 18th century, until it was gradually displaced by the pistol and, in everyday wear, fell out of fashion in the early 19th century — though court and ceremonial dress swords survive to this day.

Legacy

The small sword's techniques underlie classical and modern sport fencing. The foil was originally a blunt practice version of the small sword, and the French school of fencing that dominated the 18th century shaped the terminology and pedagogy still used in fencing instruction.
