Werner von Siemens

Ernst Werner Siemens
Born: 13 December 1816, Lenthe, Hanover
Died: 6 December 1892, Berlin
Known for: founding Siemens; dynamo-electric principle

Ernst Werner Siemens (from 1888, von Siemens; 13 December 1816 – 6 December 1892) was a German electrical engineer, inventor and industrialist. He founded the telegraph and electrical engineering company Siemens, originally Siemens & Halske, and is regarded as one of the founding figures of electrical engineering. The SI unit of electrical conductance, the siemens, is named after him.

Career

Trained as an artillery officer in Prussia, Siemens patented an improved electroplating process and, in 1847, founded the Telegraphen-Bauanstalt von Siemens & Halske with mechanic Johann Georg Halske. The company built Germany's first long-distance telegraph line, from Berlin to Frankfurt am Main, in 1848–49, and went on to lay lines across Russia and the Indo-European telegraph line to India.

Inventions

In 1866 Siemens announced the dynamo-electric principle — the self-exciting dynamo — which made large-scale electric power generation practical. He also built one of the first electric elevators (1880), presented an early electric locomotive at the Berlin trade exhibition of 1879, and pioneered the electric street tramway at Berlin-Lichterfelde in 1881.

Legacy

Siemens was ennobled by Emperor Friedrich III in 1888. His company grew into one of the world's largest electrical and electronics concerns, and the Berlin district of Siemensstadt bears the family's name.
