Joan Fontaine (born Joan de Beauvoir de Havilland; October 22, 1917 – December 15, 2013) was a British-American actress best known for her starring roles in Hollywood films of the 1940s. She won the Academy Award for Best Actress for Alfred Hitchcock's Suspicion (1941) and was also nominated for Rebecca (1940) and The Constant Nymph (1943). She and her elder sister, Olivia de Havilland, are the only siblings to have both won Academy Awards in leading acting categories, and their rivalry was one of Hollywood's most famous.

Early life

Fontaine was born in Tokyo to British parents — her father was a patent attorney — and moved with her mother and sister to California as a small child. She made her film debut in 1935 under the name Joan Burfield, later adopting her mother's maiden name, Fontaine, to distinguish herself from her sister.

Career

After several minor roles, Fontaine's breakthrough came when producer David O. Selznick cast her as the unnamed heroine of Hitchcock's Rebecca, opposite Laurence Olivier. The following year she won the Oscar for Suspicion, beating her sister, who was nominated for Hold Back the Dawn. In the late 1940s she starred in Letter from an Unknown Woman (1948), now considered a classic melodrama, and appeared in films such as Jane Eyre (1943), Ivanhoe (1952) and Beyond a Reasonable Doubt (1956). She later worked in theatre and television and published an autobiography, No Bed of Roses (1978).

Personal life and death

Fontaine became a naturalized United States citizen in 1943. She was a licensed pilot, an accomplished equestrienne and a Cordon Bleu–trained cook. Her relationship with her sister was estranged for much of their adult lives. She died of natural causes at her home in Carmel-by-the-Sea in 2013, aged 96.
