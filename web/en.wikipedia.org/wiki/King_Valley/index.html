King Valley

The King Valley is a valley and wine-producing region in north-eastern Victoria, Australia. It follows the course of the King River, which rises in the Victorian Alps and flows north to join the Ovens River near Wangaratta.

Geography

The valley stretches from the alpine foothills around Cheshunt and Whitfield in the south to Moyhu and Oxley in the north. Settlements include Whitfield, Cheshunt, Edi, Moyhu and Oxley. The climate varies with elevation, with cool conditions in the upper valley suited to premium wine production.

Wine region

King Valley is a registered Australian Geographical Indication for wine. The region is notable for its Italian migrant heritage: many growers are descended from Italian families who settled the valley after the Second World War and initially grew tobacco. The valley is especially known for Italian grape varieties such as prosecco (glera), sangiovese, nebbiolo, barbera and pinot grigio. The King Valley Prosecco Road is a popular food and wine tourism trail.

History

The valley was named after the King River, surveyed in the 19th century. Tobacco farming dominated through much of the 20th century before wine grapes became the major crop.
