California Indian Wars

The California Indian Wars were a series of conflicts between the United States Army, the California state militia, and Indigenous peoples of California during the mid-19th century. Following California's admission to the Union in 1850, state and federal forces fought numerous campaigns against Native peoples whose lands were being inundated by settlers during the Gold Rush.

Background

The arrival of tens of thousands of miners after 1848 devastated Indigenous communities through violence, disease, and displacement. Between 1846 and 1873, California's Native population fell from roughly 150,000 to about 30,000. Governor Peter Burnett declared in 1851 that a "war of extermination" would continue until the Indian race became extinct, a statement that reflected official policy toward Native resistance.

Campaigns

Major conflicts included the Mariposa War (1850–1851), campaigns in the Bald Hills and Humboldt County, the Modoc War (1872–1873) in the Lava Beds of northern California — the last major Indian war in the state — and numerous smaller expeditions. State militia campaigns were funded by California and later partly reimbursed by the federal government.

Legacy

In 2019, Governor Gavin Newsom formally apologized to Native Californians for the state's role in violence against Indigenous peoples and established a Truth and Healing Council. Historians have increasingly described the period as a genocide of California Indians.
