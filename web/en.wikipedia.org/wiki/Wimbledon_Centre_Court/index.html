Centre Court is the main tennis court at the All England Lawn Tennis and Croquet Club in Wimbledon, London, and the principal show court of The Championships, Wimbledon. It is widely regarded as the most famous tennis court in the world.

History

The name "Centre Court" derives from the court at the club's original Worple Road site, where the principal court was literally surrounded by the others. When the club moved to its present Church Road grounds in 1922, the name was retained even though the court was no longer central. The stadium was damaged by bombing during World War II and rebuilt after the war.

Retractable roof

After decades of rain delays — the white roof of folklore being simply the sky — a retractable roof was completed in 2009, together with an increased capacity of about 15,000 seats and air conditioning to manage humidity. The roof consists of ten moveable steel trusses covered with a translucent fabric that lets natural light reach the grass. The first full match played under the roof came during the 2009 Championships.

Royal Box

Centre Court contains the Royal Box, used by members of the British royal family and invited guests. Players traditionally bowed or curtsied toward it, a practice made optional in 2003 except when the monarch or the Prince of Wales is present.

Famous matches

The court has hosted many celebrated matches, including the 2008 men's final between Roger Federer and Rafael Nadal, often called the greatest match ever played, and the finals contested under the roof during rain-affected championships.
