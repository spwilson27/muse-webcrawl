Battle of Edson's Ridge

Date12–14 September 1942
LocationGuadalcanal, Solomon Islands
ResultAllied victory
BelligerentsUnited States vs Empire of Japan

The Battle of Edson's Ridge, also known as the Battle of the Bloody Ridge, was fought from 12 to 14 September 1942 during the Guadalcanal campaign of World War II. Japanese forces under Major General Kiyotake Kawaguchi launched repeated night attacks against a ridge south of Henderson Field held by U.S. Marine Raiders and Paramarines under Lieutenant Colonel Merritt A. "Red Mike" Edson.

Background

After the failure of the Ichiki Detachment at the Tenaru in August, Japan sent the Kawaguchi Detachment — about 6,200 men — to retake the airfield. The Marines, warned by coastwatchers and intelligence, positioned Edson's combined battalion of about 830 men on a grassy ridge roughly 1,000 metres long overlooking the field.

Battle

Over two nights the Japanese mounted frontal assaults supported by naval gunfire and air attack. The Marines were pushed back to a final defensive position on the ridge's northern knoll but held, inflicting heavy losses — an estimated 633 Japanese killed, against about 40 Marines killed and 100 wounded.

Aftermath

The defeat convinced Japanese commanders that a much larger force was needed, leading to the build-up for the October Battle for Henderson Field. Edson received the Medal of Honor for his leadership during the battle.
