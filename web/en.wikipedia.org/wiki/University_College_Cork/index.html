University College Cork (UCC) is a constituent university of the National University of Ireland, located in Cork, Ireland. Founded in 1845 as one of three Queen's Colleges, it is one of Ireland's leading research universities.

History

Queen's College, Cork was established under the Colleges (Ireland) Act 1845 alongside sister colleges in Belfast and Galway, and opened to students in 1849. The Main Quadrangle, designed in Gothic Revival style, was built from local limestone. The mathematician George Boole, inventor of Boolean algebra, was its first professor of mathematics. Under the Irish Universities Act 1908 the college became University College Cork within the National University of Ireland.

Academics and campus

UCC is organized into four colleges: Arts, Celtic Studies and Social Sciences; Business and Law; Medicine and Health; and Science, Engineering and Food Science. Landmarks include the Lewis Glucksman Gallery, the Crawford Observatory, and the library's early printed books collection. The university consistently ranks among the top universities in Ireland and is known for sustainability, having been the first university in the world awarded a Green Flag for environmental efforts.

Notable alumni

Alumni include writer Seán Ó Faoláin, actor Cillian Murphy, and numerous Irish politicians and scientists.
