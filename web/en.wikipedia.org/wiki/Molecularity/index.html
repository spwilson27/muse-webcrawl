Molecularity

In chemistry, molecularity is the number of molecules that come together to react in an elementary (single-step) reaction. It is a theoretical concept for elementary reactions only, and is classified as unimolecular (one molecule), bimolecular (two molecules collide), or termolecular (three molecules collide simultaneously).

Relation to reaction order

For an elementary reaction, the molecularity equals the reaction order with respect to the reaction step, because the rate law follows directly from the stoichiometry of the elementary collision or decomposition. However, molecularity must not be confused with the overall order of a complex, multi-step reaction, which is determined experimentally and may be fractional or zero.

Examples

The decomposition of a single molecule (such as the isomerization of cyclopropane) is unimolecular; the collision of two species, as in many gas-phase combination reactions, is bimolecular; termolecular elementary steps are rare because simultaneous three-body collisions are statistically improbable, and steps of higher molecularity are not known.
