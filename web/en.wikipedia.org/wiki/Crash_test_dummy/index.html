Crash test dummy

A crash test dummy, formally an anthropomorphic test device (ATD), is a full-scale anthropomorphic instrument that simulates the dimensions, weight proportions and articulation of the human body during a vehicle crash test, recording data on forces and accelerations that predict injury.

History

The first dummy, "Sierra Sam", was built in 1949 for testing aircraft ejection seats. The Hybrid series developed for General Motors became the industry standard; the Hybrid III, introduced in 1976, remains widely used in frontal-impact regulation tests.

Instrumentation

Modern dummies contain dozens of sensors — accelerometers, load cells and displacement transducers in the head, neck, chest, pelvis and legs. Newer designs such as THOR aim for greater biofidelity, and female-representative dummies have been developed to address the historical reliance on an average-male model.
