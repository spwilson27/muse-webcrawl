Strategos

Strategos (Greek: στρατηγός, plural strategoi; "army leader") was a title used in ancient Greek states for a military general. In classical Athens, the board of ten strategoi, elected annually, became among the most influential magistrates of the democracy. The term also survived into the Byzantine Empire, where strategoi commanded the military provinces known as themes.

Classical Athens

From 501 BC Athens elected ten strategoi, one from each of the ten tribes, with an eleventh added later for the board as a whole. Unlike most Athenian magistracies, the strategos could be re-elected indefinitely, allowing experienced commanders such as Pericles to dominate policy. Strategoi commanded armies and fleets in the field and increasingly exercised political and financial functions, including oversight of military funds and war-related jurisdiction.

Hellenistic and Byzantine use

Hellenistic kingdoms used strategos for both generals and provincial governors. In the Byzantine theme system of the 7th to 11th centuries, the strategos was the military commander and often chief administrator of a theme, before the office declined as themes were subdivided into smaller commands under doukes and katepano.
