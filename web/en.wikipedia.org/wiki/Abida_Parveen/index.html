Abida Parveen

Abida Parveen (Sindhi: عابده پروين; Urdu: عابدہ پروین; born 20 February 1954) is a Pakistani singer of Sufi music, widely regarded as one of the greatest singers of the genre and often called the "Queen of Sufi Music". She sings primarily in Urdu, Sindhi, Punjabi, Saraiki and Persian, performing ghazals, kafis, and the devotional poetry of saints such as Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai, Bulleh Shah, Sachal Sarmast and Rumi.

Life and career

Parveen was born in Larkana, Sindh, into a musical family; her father, Ustad Ghulam Haider, ran a music school and was her first teacher. She later trained under Ustad Salamat Ali Khan of the Sham Chaurasia gharana. She began performing at Sufi shrines and on Radio Pakistan in the early 1970s, gaining national fame through television performances of kalam and kafi in the later 1970s.

Known for her powerful contralto voice and ecstatic performance style, Parveen performs seated with a harmonium, often entering states of wajd (spiritual ecstasy) characteristic of Sufi devotional gatherings. Her celebrated renditions include "Mahi Yaar Di Gharoli", "Yaar Ko Humne Ja Baja Dekha" (by Shah Niaz), "Tere Ishq Nachaya", and the poetry of Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai. She has performed internationally, including at Wembley Arena and for UNESCO, and appeared in Pakistan's Coke Studio with songs such as "Aaqa" and "Mera Piya Ghar Aaya".

Honours

Parveen has received Pakistan's highest civilian honours, including the Pride of Performance (1984), Sitara-e-Imtiaz (2005) and Hilal-e-Imtiaz (2012), and in 2021 she was awarded the Nishan-e-Imtiaz, the country's highest civilian award. UNESCO and numerous critics have ranked her among the world's foremost exponents of Sufi devotional singing.
