Gary Gygax

BornErnest Gary Gygax, 27 July 1938, Chicago, Illinois, U.S.
Died4 March 2008, Lake Geneva, Wisconsin, U.S.

Ernest Gary Gygax (27 July 1938 – 4 March 2008) was an American game designer and author best known for co-creating the pioneering tabletop role-playing game Dungeons & Dragons (D&D) with Dave Arneson in 1974. He co-founded the company TSR, Inc. with Don Kaye in 1973 to publish the game, and is widely regarded as the father of role-playing games.

Early life and wargaming

Gygax grew up in Chicago and Lake Geneva, Wisconsin, where he became an avid player of miniature wargames and board games such as Avalon Hill titles. He co-founded the International Federation of Wargaming and helped organise the Gen Con gaming convention, first held in Lake Geneva in 1968. With Jeff Perren he wrote Chainmail (1971), a medieval miniatures wargame whose fantasy supplement strongly influenced D&D.

Dungeons & Dragons

After playing in Dave Arneson's Blackmoor campaign, Gygax developed the rules that became Dungeons & Dragons, published by TSR in 1974. He went on to write the Advanced Dungeons & Dragons rulebooks — the Monster Manual (1977), Players Handbook (1978) and Dungeon Masters Guide (1979) — and designed the Greyhawk campaign world, the first published D&D setting. His adventure modules, including The Temple of Elemental Evil and Tomb of Horrors, became classics of the genre.

Later career

Gygax lost control of TSR in a 1985 corporate struggle and left the company. He subsequently designed the Dangerous Journeys (1992) and Lejendary Adventure (1999) role-playing systems, wrote fantasy novels including the Gord the Rogue series, and remained an elder statesman of the hobby, writing a column for Dragon magazine and appearing at conventions.

Death and legacy

Gygax died at his home in Lake Geneva on 4 March 2008 after years of ill health. Tributes included a moment of silence in the online game Dungeons & Dragons Online, a memorial at Gen Con, and his induction into the Origins Award Hall of Fame. His birthday, 27 July, is sometimes observed by gamers as Gary Gygax Day.
