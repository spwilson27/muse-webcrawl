Rangerettes

The Kilgore College Rangerettes are a precision dance team from Kilgore College in Kilgore, Texas. Founded in 1940 by Gussie Nell Davis, the Rangerettes were the first women's drill team of their kind and are credited with popularising the American halftime show, the high kick line and elements now standard in dance and drill teams across the United States.

The Rangerettes' signature high kick routine

History

Gussie Nell Davis, a physical education instructor, organised the Rangerettes in 1940 to keep football crowds in their seats at halftime — and, in her words, to give young women an appealing alternative to the rough atmosphere around the games. Dressed in red, white and blue uniforms with cowboy hats and boots, the team combined dance, marching and precision formations, finishing routines with their famous high kick line. The Rangerettes quickly became ambassadors for Texas, performing at bowl games, presidential inaugurations and on international tours in dozens of countries.

Organisation and traditions

The squad numbers about 65–72 members — sophomores ("old members") and freshmen selected through a demanding week-long tryout. Members maintain academic standards and a strict code of conduct and appearance. The team is supported by the Rangerette Forever alumni organisation. The Gussie Nell Davis Dance Studio and the Rangerette Showcase museum on the Kilgore College campus preserve the team's history. Davis directed the Rangerettes for 39 years until 1979; her successors have maintained the team's classic style with minimal change, making the Rangerettes a living symbol of mid-century American pageantry.
