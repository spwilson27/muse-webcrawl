Surveyor program

The Surveyor program was a NASA programme that sent seven robotic spacecraft to the surface of the Moon between 1966 and 1968. Its primary goal was to demonstrate the feasibility of soft landings on the Moon and to characterise the lunar surface in preparation for the crewed Apollo landings.

Missions

Surveyor 1 landed in Oceanus Procellarum on June 2, 1966, just four months after the Soviet Luna 9 probe, and returned thousands of television images. Five of the seven missions succeeded. Surveyor 3's landing site was visited by the crew of Apollo 12 in 1969, who returned parts of the probe to Earth — including its camera — making it the only probe ever visited by humans on another world. Later Surveyors carried a soil mechanics surface sampler and an alpha-scattering instrument to analyse the chemistry of the lunar soil.

Significance

The programme proved that a spacecraft could land safely on the lunar regolith and that the surface could bear the weight of the Apollo Lunar Module, resolving a key engineering uncertainty of the Moon race.
