Illuminance

Common symbolEv
SI unitlux (lx)
Other unitsfoot-candle (fc)
Dimensionlumens per unit area

In photometry, illuminance is the total luminous flux incident on a surface per unit area — a measure of how much visible light "illuminates" that surface as perceived by the human eye. It quantifies the intensity of light hitting a surface, in contrast to luminance, which describes light reflected or emitted from it.

Illuminance is measured with a lux meter held at the surface of interest.

Units

The SI unit of illuminance is the lux (lx), equal to one lumen per square metre. In the United States, the non-SI unit foot-candle (fc) remains common: one foot-candle equals one lumen per square foot, approximately 10.764 lux.

Typical values

Illuminance varies over an enormous range in everyday environments. Direct sunlight reaches roughly 100,000 lux; an overcast day provides about 1,000–10,000 lux. Recommended levels for offices and classrooms are typically 300–500 lux, while detailed tasks such as drafting or inspection may require 750–1,500 lux. A living room in the evening might be lit to only 50–100 lux, and full moonlight delivers roughly 0.1 lux.

Measurement and standards

Illuminance is measured with a photometer or lux meter whose sensor is corrected to match the photopic luminosity function of human vision and to respond accurately to light arriving at oblique angles (cosine correction). Building codes and standards organizations, such as the Illuminating Engineering Society (IES), publish recommended illuminance levels for workplaces, roads, sports facilities, and public spaces, balancing visual performance, energy use, and safety.
