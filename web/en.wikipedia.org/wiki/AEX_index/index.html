The AEX index, derived from Amsterdam Exchange index, is a stock market index composed of Dutch companies that trade on Euronext Amsterdam. Founded in 1983, it is the principal benchmark index of the Netherlands and comprises the largest and most actively traded companies on the exchange, weighted by free-float market capitalisation.

History

The index began on 3 January 1983 with a base level of 100, initially as the EOE index (European Options Exchange). It reached its all-time closing peak of the dot-com era in September 2000 at about 703 points, fell sharply during the subsequent crash and again in the 2008 financial crisis, and surpassed its 2000 record only in 2021. In September 2025 the index was expanded from 25 to 30 constituents.

Composition

Constituents are reviewed quarterly. Major members have included the semiconductor equipment maker ASML — by far the largest weighting — along with companies such as Prosus, Shell (until its move to London), ING Group, Philips, Heineken, Unilever, RELX, Wolters Kluwer, Ahold Delhaize and Adyen. The related AMX index covers the next tier of mid-cap companies.

Investing

The AEX is tracked by exchange-traded funds and serves as the underlying for futures and options traded on Euronext. Because of ASML's size, the index has a pronounced exposure to the semiconductor industry.
