The penny sterling (symbol: p; plural: pence) is a unit of currency and a coin of the pound sterling, the official currency of the United Kingdom and its associated territories. Since decimalisation on 15 February 1971, one penny has been worth one hundredth of a pound. Before decimalisation, the penny (abbreviated d., from Latin denarius) was worth 1/12 of a shilling, or 1/240 of a pound, in the £sd system.

History

The silver penny was introduced in Anglo-Saxon England around the 8th century, modelled on the Carolingian denier, and for centuries was effectively England's only coin. The word "sterling" itself is traditionally linked to the Norman silver pennies ("sterlings"). Copper and then bronze pennies replaced silver ones by the 19th century; the large bronze "bun penny" of Victoria's reign became a familiar emblem of the currency.

Modern coins

Decimal 1p and 2p coins were issued from 1971; since 1992 they have been struck in copper-plated steel rather than bronze. Their low purchasing power periodically prompts debate about withdrawing them, but they remain legal tender and in circulation. The penny's name persists in everyday expressions such as "spend a penny".
