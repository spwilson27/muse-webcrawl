Wallenberg family

The Wallenberg family is a prominent Swedish family renowned as bankers, industrialists, politicians, bureaucrats and diplomats. The family's influence in Swedish business is channelled principally through the investment company Investor AB and the Wallenberg foundations, which together control substantial stakes in companies including ABB, AstraZeneca, Atlas Copco, Electrolux, Ericsson, Saab and SEB.

Stockholms Enskilda Bank, founded in 1856, was the seed of the family's financial empire.

History

The dynasty was founded by André Oscar Wallenberg (1816–1886), who established Stockholms Enskilda Bank in 1856. His son Knut Agathon and grandsons Marcus and Jacob Wallenberg steered the bank through the Kreuger crash of the 1930s and built the family's industrial holdings. Marcus Wallenberg Jr. (1899–1982) was the dominant figure of mid-century Swedish capitalism.

Notable members

Raoul Wallenberg (1912–disappeared 1945), a diplomat in Budapest, is honoured worldwide for issuing protective passports and sheltering tens of thousands of Hungarian Jews during the Holocaust before his arrest by Soviet forces. The fifth generation — Marcus, Jacob and Peter Wallenberg Jr. — continues to lead the family's foundations and Investor AB.
