Kusha (Ramayana)

Kusha (Sanskrit: कुश, IAST: Kuśa), also rendered Kush, and his twin brother Lava are the children of Rama and Sita in Hindu tradition, figuring prominently in the Uttara Kanda, the last book of the epic Ramayana attributed to Valmiki, as well as in later retellings. Kusha is regarded as the founder of the city of Kasur and the progenitor of the Kushwaha/Kachhwaha dynasties in some traditions.

Story

After Rama, bowing to public gossip, exiles his pregnant wife Sita, she takes refuge in the hermitage of the sage Valmiki, where she gives birth to twins, Lava and Kusha. The boys are raised and educated by Valmiki, who teaches them the Ramayana he has composed. Travelling as young minstrels, the twins recite the epic at Ayodhya before Rama himself, who thereby learns that they are his sons.

In Valmiki's account, when Rama performs the Ashvamedha (horse sacrifice), the sacrificial horse wanders into the forest and is seized by Lava and Kusha, who defeat Rama's brothers and the warrior Hanuman in battle before Rama himself arrives and recognises them. Sita, summoned to prove her purity once more, calls upon the earth to receive her and disappears into the ground. Kusha and Lava are then proclaimed Rama's heirs; in later tradition, Kusha is associated with the region of Kosala and the city of Kusinagara (Kushavati), while Lava is linked to Lavapuri (present-day Lahore).

The episode of Lava and Kusha is one of the most beloved in the Ramayana tradition and is retold across South and Southeast Asian literature, drama and festival performance, including India's Ramlila.
