Born Under a Bad Sign (Albert King album)

Born Under a Bad Sign is the second studio album by the American blues musician Albert King, released in August 1967 by Stax Records. Backed by Booker T. & the M.G.'s and the Memphis Horns, the album compiled singles King had recorded for Stax in 1966–67 and became one of the defining records of modern electric blues.

Music and influence

The title track, written by Booker T. Jones and William Bell, along with "Crosscut Saw", "The Hunter", "Laundromat Blues" and King's version of "As the Years Go Passing By", showcased his economical, string-bending guitar style played on a left-handed Flying V strung upside down. The album profoundly influenced a generation of blues-rock guitarists, including Eric Clapton — Cream covered the title song — Jimi Hendrix and Stevie Ray Vaughan.

Recognition

The album was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and the Blues Foundation Hall of Fame, was included in the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry in 2020, and regularly appears on lists of the greatest blues albums ever made.
