Citgo Petroleum Corporation is a United States-based refiner, transporter and marketer of transportation fuels, lubricants, petrochemicals and other industrial products. Headquartered in Houston, Texas, it is owned by PDV Holding, part of Venezuela's state oil company PDVSA. Citgo operates refineries in Lake Charles (Louisiana), Lemont (Illinois) and Corpus Christi (Texas), with a combined capacity of roughly 800,000 barrels per day.

History

Citgo traces its origins to the Cities Service Company, founded in 1910 by Henry L. Doherty to supply gas and electricity to small towns. The Citgo name and its red "trimark" logo were introduced in 1965. The company was acquired by the Southland Corporation in 1982 and sold in stages to PDVSA, which took full ownership in 1990.

Since 2019, control of Citgo has been contested between the governments of Nicolás Maduro and the Venezuelan opposition recognised by the United States, and the company has been drawn into legal proceedings involving Venezuela's creditors. The famous Citgo sign in Boston's Kenmore Square, erected in 1940 and rebuilt in its current form in 1965, is a beloved local landmark.
