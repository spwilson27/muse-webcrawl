Asian Winter Games

First event: 1986, Sapporo, Japan

Occur every: Four years (irregularly)

Organiser: Olympic Council of Asia

The Asian Winter Games (AWG) is an international multi-sport event held every four years for members of the Olympic Council of Asia (OCA) featuring winter sports. The Japanese Olympic Committee first suggested the idea of holding a winter version of the Asian Games in 1982, and the first edition was held in Sapporo in 1986.

History

Participation grew from seven nations at the first Games to 32 in Changchun in 2007, including countries with no natural winter climate whose athletes train abroad. Editions have been hosted by Japan (1986, 1990, 2003, 2017), China (1996, 2007, 2025), South Korea (1999) and Kazakhstan (2011, co-hosted by Almaty and Astana). The 2029 Games were awarded to Trojena in Saudi Arabia, the first edition scheduled for West Asia.

Sports

Core sports include alpine skiing, cross-country skiing, figure skating, ice hockey, short track speed skating, speed skating, biathlon, curling and snowboarding. Japan, China and South Korea have dominated the medal tables, with Kazakhstan also a leading nation.
