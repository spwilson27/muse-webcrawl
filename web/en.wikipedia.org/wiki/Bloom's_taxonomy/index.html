Bloom's taxonomy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bloom's taxonomy is a set of hierarchical models used to classify educational learning objectives into levels of complexity and specificity. It was first published in 1956 in the Taxonomy of Educational Objectives, edited by educational psychologist Benjamin Bloom, and has become one of the most widely used frameworks in teaching and curriculum design.

The cognitive domain

The original taxonomy listed six levels: Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis and Evaluation. A major revision by Anderson and Krathwohl in 2001 restated them as verbs — Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate and Create — placing creation at the top of the hierarchy.

Other domains

Bloom's committee also described an affective domain (receiving through characterisation, covering attitudes and values) and a psychomotor domain (physical skills), though the latter handbook was never completed by Bloom's group.
