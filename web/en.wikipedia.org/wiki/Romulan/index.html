Romulan


Universe: Star Trek
Homeworld: Romulus
Government: Romulan Star Empire
First appearance: "Balance of Terror" (1966)

The Romulans are an extraterrestrial race in the American science fiction franchise Star Trek. Created by Paul Schneider for the 1966 Star Trek: The Original Series episode "Balance of Terror", they are portrayed as a passionate, militaristic people descended from Vulcans who rejected Surak's philosophy of logic and emotion suppression and left Vulcan in ancient times, settling the worlds Romulus and Remus. Their empire, the Romulan Star Empire, is one of the major powers of the Alpha and Beta Quadrants and a recurring adversary — and occasional ally — of the United Federation of Planets.

Characteristics

Romulans closely resemble their Vulcan cousins, with pointed ears, upswept eyebrows, and green-tinted copper-based blood, though most lack Vulcan telepathic discipline and display ridged foreheads in later portrayals. Romulan society is secretive and security-obsessed, dominated by the Senate, the Praetor, and the feared Tal Shiar intelligence agency. Their starships, the great green D'deridex-class warbirds, are powered by artificial quantum singularities rather than matter-antimatter reactors, and Romulan cloaking technology is a signature strategic advantage.

History in the franchise

After a war with Earth in the twenty-second century and a century of isolation behind the Neutral Zone, the Romulans re-emerged in the twenty-fourth century in The Next Generation. They featured prominently in Deep Space Nine's Dominion War arc, entering the war on the Federation's side after the events of "In the Pale Moonlight". The destruction of Romulus by a supernova — the inciting event of the 2009 film Star Trek and the series Star Trek: Picard — shattered the empire and scattered its people, with a long-promised reunification movement with Vulcan finally yielding the joint world Ni'Var centuries later, as shown in Star Trek: Discovery.

Cultural influence

Named for Romulus of Roman myth, the Romulans were conceived during the Cold War as an analogue for a closed, suspicious adversary state. Their language was developed for the films and series by linguists, and Romulan characters such as Commander Tomalak, Senator Vreenak, and the defector Alidar Jarok are among the franchise's most popular antagonists.
