Project Blue Book

Project Blue Book was the code name for the systematic study of unidentified flying objects (UFOs) by the United States Air Force from March 1952 to its termination on December 17, 1969. Headquartered at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio, it was the last of a series of Air Force UFO studies that began with Project Sign (1947) and Project Grudge (1949).

Work and findings

Blue Book collected 12,618 sighting reports and analysed them; 701 remained "unidentified" even after investigation. The project's official conclusions were that no UFO reported, investigated or evaluated by the Air Force had ever given any indication of threat to national security, that there was no evidence the sightings categorised as unidentified represented technological developments beyond modern scientific knowledge, and that there was no evidence unidentified sightings were extraterrestrial vehicles.

Personnel

The project's first head, Captain Edward J. Ruppelt, coined the term "unidentified flying object" to replace the popular "flying saucer". Astronomer J. Allen Hynek served as its scientific consultant for two decades; initially sceptical, Hynek later became a prominent UFO researcher and founded the Center for UFO Studies.

Termination and legacy

Following the University of Colorado's Condon Committee report (1968), which concluded that further extensive study of UFOs could not be justified, the Air Force closed Blue Book in December 1969. Its records were declassified and are held by the National Archives; roughly 130,000 pages were scanned and published online in 2015. The project remains a touchstone in debates over government transparency about aerial phenomena, and it inspired the television series Project U.F.O. and Project Blue Book.
