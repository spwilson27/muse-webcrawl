Anbar campaign (2003–2011)

Date: 2003–2011
Location: Al Anbar Governorate, Iraq
Principal US force: US Marine Corps
Key events: Battles of Fallujah (2004), Ramadi (2006), Anbar Awakening

The Anbar campaign was the series of operations fought in Iraq's Al Anbar Governorate during the Iraq War, from the 2003 invasion until the withdrawal of United States forces in 2011. The vast, overwhelmingly Sunni Arab province west of Baghdad — including the cities of Fallujah, Ramadi, Haditha and Al-Qa'im — became the heartland of the Sunni insurgency and the deadliest region in Iraq for American forces, principally US Marines.

Insurgency

After the fall of Saddam Hussein, Anbar's tribes and former regime elements, joined by Al-Qaeda in Iraq (AQI) under Abu Musab al-Zarqawi, waged a relentless guerrilla war. In 2004 Fallujah became an insurgent stronghold: after the killing of four American contractors there, US forces fought two major battles (April and November–December 2004), the second — Operation Phantom Fury — the fiercest urban combat for US troops since Hue in 1968. Ramadi saw heavy fighting through 2006.

The Awakening

Beginning in 2006, Sunni tribal sheikhs around Ramadi, alienated by AQI's brutality, allied with US forces in the movement known as the Anbar Awakening (Sahwa). Tribal fighters, later organised as the Sons of Iraq, turned on Al-Qaeda, and violence in the province collapsed by 2007–2008, making Anbar the model cited for the broader "surge" strategy. Security was handed to Iraqi forces, and the campaign formally ended with the US withdrawal in December 2011 — though much of Anbar fell to the Islamic State in 2014, reopening the conflict.
