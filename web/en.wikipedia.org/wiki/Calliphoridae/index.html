The Calliphoridae (commonly known as blow flies, blow-flies, carrion flies, bottle flies, or cluster flies) are a family of insects in the order Diptera, with about 1,900 known species in some 150 genera. Many species are metallic blue, green or black in colour, and are among the first insects to arrive at a carcass, where their larvae (maggots) feed on decaying tissue.

Biology

Adult blow flies feed on nectar, carrion and other organic matter, and are important pollinators of some plants. Females lay eggs on carrion, wounds, or decaying material; the larvae pass through three instars before pupating in the soil. Some species, such as the screwworms (Cochliomyia hominivorax and Chrysomya bezziana), are obligate parasites of living mammals and serious livestock pests, while others cause myiasis opportunistically. The sterile insect technique has been used to eradicate the New World screwworm from North and Central America.

Forensic importance

Because the development of blow fly larvae depends predictably on temperature, forensic entomologists use the species present and their developmental stage on a corpse to estimate the post-mortem interval (time since death). Calliphorids are the most important family in forensic entomology.

Medical use

Sterile maggots of the common green bottle Lucilia sericata are used in maggot debridement therapy to clean necrotic wounds, as the larvae consume dead tissue while sparing living tissue.
