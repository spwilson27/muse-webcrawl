Modular form

In mathematics, a modular form is a complex analytic function on the upper half-plane satisfying a transformation law under the modular group SL(2,ℤ) (or a subgroup) and a growth condition at infinity. Modular forms are central objects in number theory, linking analysis, algebra and geometry, and were key to the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem.

Modular form

DomainUpper half-plane ℍ
SymmetryModular group SL(2, ℤ)
ExamplesEisenstein series, Δ (discriminant), j-invariant

Definition

A modular form of weight k for SL(2,ℤ) is a holomorphic function f on ℍ with f((aτ+b)/(cτ+d)) = (cτ+d)k f(τ) for all modular transformations, holomorphic at the cusp; if it also vanishes at infinity it is a cusp form. Examples include the Eisenstein series G4 and G6, and the weight-12 cusp form Δ, whose coefficients give Ramanujan's tau function. Every modular form has a Fourier (q-)expansion in q = e2πiτ.

Theory

Spaces of modular forms are finite-dimensional and spanned by Hecke eigenforms, whose Fourier coefficients encode arithmetic. The modularity theorem — every elliptic curve over ℚ is modular — completed by Wiles and others, implies Fermat's Last Theorem. Related structures include modular functions (weight 0, like the j-invariant), Maass forms, and automorphic forms generalising to other groups in the Langlands programme.

Applications

Modular forms appear in string theory and conformal field theory, monstrous moonshine connecting the j-function to the Monster group, partition identities of Ramanujan, and lattice theory. See mock theta function and elliptic curve.
