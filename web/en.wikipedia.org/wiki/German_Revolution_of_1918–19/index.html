German Revolution of 1918–19

Philipp Scheidemann proclaims the German Republic from the Reichstag, 9 November 1918

The German Revolution of 1918–19 (German: Novemberrevolution) was a civil conflict in the German Empire at the end of the First World War that resulted in the replacement of the German federal constitutional monarchy with a democratic parliamentary republic, the Weimar Republic. The revolutionary period lasted from November 1918 until the formal establishment of the Weimar Republic in August 1919.

Background

By the autumn of 1918 Germany faced military defeat, food shortages and growing war weariness. The naval mutiny at Kiel in early November 1918, triggered by a plan for a final suicidal naval engagement, sparked workers' and soldiers' councils across the country, modelled partly on the soviets of the Russian Revolution.

Course of events

On 9 November 1918, Chancellor Prince Maximilian of Baden announced the abdication of Kaiser Wilhelm II and handed power to Friedrich Ebert of the Social Democratic Party (SPD). Philipp Scheidemann proclaimed a republic from the Reichstag building the same day. The provisional government, the Council of the People's Deputies, ruled until elections for a National Assembly in January 1919.

The radical left, including the Spartacus League under Rosa Luxemburg and Karl Liebknecht, sought a council republic. The January 1919 Spartacist uprising was crushed by government troops and Freikorps units; Luxemburg and Liebknecht were murdered. Further uprisings followed in Bavaria, Bremen and the Ruhr, and a short-lived Bavarian Soviet Republic was proclaimed in April 1919 before being violently suppressed.

Outcome

The Weimar National Assembly adopted a democratic constitution in 1919, establishing the Weimar Republic. The revolution's legacy remained contested: conservatives blamed it and the "stab-in-the-back" myth for Germany's defeat, while the split between the SPD and the radical left weakened the republic in the face of later crises.
