The Recording Academy (formally the National Academy of Recording Arts and Sciences; abbreviated NARAS) is an American learned academy of musicians, producers, recording engineers, and other musical professionals. It is known for its Grammy Awards, which recognize achievements in the music industry.

History

The Academy was established in 1957. It grew out of discussions among recording industry executives in Los Angeles who were involved in selecting honorees for the Hollywood Walk of Fame; concluding that recording artists deserved their own recognition, they founded the academy to honour excellence in recordings. The first Grammy Awards ceremony was held on 4 May 1959, simultaneously in Beverly Hills, California, and New York City.

The Academy later established regional chapters in cities including Chicago, Nashville, New York, San Francisco, Atlanta, and Memphis. In 1997 it launched the Latin Recording Academy, which presents the Latin Grammy Awards.

Activities

Beyond the Grammy Awards, the Academy engages in advocacy on behalf of music creators, music education initiatives, and preservation of recorded music heritage. It operates the Grammy Museum in Los Angeles, which opened in 2008, and supports MusiCares, a charitable foundation established in 1989 that provides assistance to music people in times of need.

The Academy also maintains the Grammy Hall of Fame, honouring recordings of lasting significance, and presents lifetime achievement and trustees awards.

Leadership

Long-time president Neil Portnow served from 2002 to 2019. Deborah Dugan briefly succeeded him, becoming the first woman to lead the organization, before being placed on leave and dismissed in 2020 amid controversy. Harvey Mason Jr., who served as interim president, was appointed president and CEO in 2021.
