Raúl Prebisch

Raúl Prebisch (April 17, 1901 – April 29, 1986) was an Argentine economist known for his contribution to structuralist economics, such as the Prebisch–Singer hypothesis, which formed the basis of the theory of dependency. He became executive director of the Economic Commission for Latin America (ECLA) in 1950. In 1962, he became the founding Secretary-General of the United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD).

Prebisch was born in Tucumán, Argentina, and studied at the University of Buenos Aires. He played a central role in creating Argentina's Central Bank in 1935, serving as its first general manager. After leaving Argentine public service, he led ECLA from Santiago, Chile, where his 1950 manifesto, The Economic Development of Latin America and Its Principal Problems, argued that the terms of trade between the industrial "centre" and the primary-producing "periphery" deteriorated over time, and advocated import-substitution industrialization for Latin America.

The Prebisch–Singer hypothesis

Independently developed with Hans Singer, the hypothesis holds that the price of primary commodities declines relative to the price of manufactured goods over the long term, causing the terms of trade of commodity-based economies to deteriorate. It strongly influenced development economics and the politics of the New International Economic Order in the 1970s.
