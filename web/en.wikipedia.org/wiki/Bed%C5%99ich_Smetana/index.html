Bedřich Smetana (2 March 1824 – 12 May 1884) was a Czech composer who pioneered a musical style closely identified with his country's aspirations for independence. He is regarded in the Czech Republic as the father of Czech music and is best known internationally for the symphonic cycle Má vlast ("My Homeland"), which includes the movement Vltava ("The Moldau"), and for the opera The Bartered Bride.

Life

Smetana was a gifted child pianist who gave his first public performance aged six. After studies in Prague he worked as a music teacher, spent several years in Gothenburg, Sweden, as a teacher and conductor, and returned to Prague in 1861 as Czech cultural nationalism grew. He became conductor at the Provisional Theatre and a central figure in Prague's musical life.

Later years

In 1874 Smetana lost his hearing completely, but he continued to compose, producing Má vlast, the string quartet "From My Life" — whose final movement depicts his deafness — and further operas including The Kiss and The Secret. Declining mental health led to his confinement in a Prague asylum, where he died in 1884. His other operas include Dalibor and Libuše, the latter written for the opening of the National Theatre in 1881.
