Fletcher Christian

Fletcher Christian (25 September 1764 – 20 September 1793) was an English sailor who led the mutiny on HMS Bounty against its captain, William Bligh, on 28 April 1789. Christian and a group of fellow mutineers later settled on remote Pitcairn Island in the South Pacific, where he died within a few years of the settlement's founding.

Died20 September 1793 (aged 28), Pitcairn Island

Early life and the Bounty voyage

Christian was born near Cockermouth in Cumberland to a well-connected family and joined the Royal Navy as a teenager. He sailed twice with Bligh before being appointed master's mate on the Bounty, which set out in 1787 to collect breadfruit plants from Tahiti for transplantation to the West Indies. After a long and idyllic stay in Tahiti, relations between Christian and Bligh deteriorated sharply on the return voyage.

The mutiny and Pitcairn

On 28 April 1789, near Tofua in the Friendly Islands, Christian and his supporters seized the ship and set Bligh and eighteen loyalists adrift in an open launch — who nonetheless survived an epic voyage to Timor. After an unsuccessful attempt to settle on Tubuai, Christian sailed with eight mutineers, Tahitian men and women, to uninhabited Pitcairn Island in January 1790, where the Bounty was burned. Violence between the settlers and the Tahitian men followed; Christian was killed in 1793. His descendants still live on Pitcairn and Norfolk Island, and he and his wife Mauatua (called Isabella) founded a lineage that includes many of the islands' inhabitants.

In popular culture

Christian has been portrayed on film by Clark Gable (1935), Marlon Brando (1962), and Mel Gibson (1984), with the mutiny's story retold in numerous novels and histories that differ widely in their judgment of him and of Bligh.
