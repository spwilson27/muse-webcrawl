Vaikom Satyagraha

Vaikom Satyagraha was a nonviolent protest movement (1924–1925) centred at the town of Vaikom in the princely state of Travancore (present-day Kerala, India). It demanded that the public roads around the Vaikom Mahadeva Temple be opened to all castes, including the so-called "untouchable" communities — Ezhavas, Pulayas and others — who were then forbidden even to use roads near the temple, under rules of ritual pollution enforced by custom.

Course of the movement

The campaign arose from a 1923 resolution of the Indian National Congress at Kakinada supporting temple-road access, and from advocacy by leaders such as T. K. Madhavan, K. Kelappan and George Joseph. On 30 March 1924 satyagrahis began daily marches towards the barricaded temple roads; volunteers were arrested in batches. Mahatma Gandhi visited Vaikom in March 1925 and negotiated with the orthodox opposition and the Travancore government. The social reformer Periyar E. V. Ramasamy came from Tamil Nadu to join the protest, earning the title "Vaikom Veerar" (Hero of Vaikom).

Outcome and significance

In November 1925 the government of Travancore opened most of the roads around the temple to all castes — a compromise that left the eastern approach restricted but marked the first major victory of satyagraha applied to caste discrimination. The agitation is regarded as a precursor of the later Temple Entry Proclamation of 1936, which opened Travancore's temples themselves to all Hindus, and as an early milestone in the Indian movement against untouchability.
