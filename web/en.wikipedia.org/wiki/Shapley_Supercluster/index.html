The Shapley Supercluster or Shapley Concentration is the largest known concentration of galaxies in the nearby universe, forming a gravitationally bound unit that pulls on surrounding matter, including the Local Group. Located roughly 650 million light-years (200 Mpc) away in the direction of the constellation Centaurus, it contains dozens of rich galaxy clusters and many thousands of galaxies.

Discovery

The overdensity was first noticed by Harlow Shapley in the 1930s during his surveys of galaxy distributions, in which he recorded a remarkably dense "cloud" of galaxies in Centaurus. Systematic redshift surveys in the late 1980s, notably by Italian astronomers, established its true extent and mass.

Properties

The supercluster's core includes the rich clusters Abell 3558, Abell 3562 and Abell 3556, embedded in a filamentary web of galaxy groups. Its total mass is estimated at around 1016 solar masses. Together with the Great Attractor region, the Shapley Concentration dominates the gravitational field of the local universe and contributes substantially to the observed motion of the Local Group toward Centaurus, as reflected in the cosmic microwave background dipole.
