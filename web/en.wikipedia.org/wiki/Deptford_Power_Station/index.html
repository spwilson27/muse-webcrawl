Deptford Power Station

Deptford Power Station was a coal-fired power station on the south bank of the River Thames at Deptford, in south-east London. The site played a pioneering role in the history of electricity supply in Britain: it was the location of one of the world's first large central power stations, designed by Sebastian Ziani de Ferranti for the London Electric Supply Corporation (LESCo). In its later years the site was known as Deptford East and Deptford West, and generation continued there until the station finally closed in 1983.

History

In 1887 Sebastian de Ferranti, chief engineer of the London Electric Supply Corporation, proposed building a large generating station at Deptford, well away from central London, from which alternating current at high voltage could be transmitted to substations in the city. Transmission at around 10,000 volts was revolutionary at a time when most electricity supply was low-voltage direct current delivered over short distances. The station, equipped with huge alternators designed by Ferranti, began supplying power in 1891 and demonstrated the practicality of high-voltage AC transmission, a principle that underlies modern electricity grids.

The original station became known as Deptford East. A second station, Deptford West, was built on an adjacent site in the years before the First World War. Both stations burned coal delivered by collier ships to the Thames-side site. Following nationalisation of the electricity supply industry in 1948, the stations passed to the British Electricity Authority and later to the Central Electricity Generating Board (CEGB). Deptford East closed in the 1960s, while Deptford West continued operating until 1983, after which the stations were demolished and the site redeveloped.

Legacy

Ferranti's Deptford scheme is often cited as a landmark in electrical engineering. It proved that electricity could be generated centrally on a large scale and transmitted economically over several miles, paving the way for the interconnected national grid systems of the twentieth century.
