The Yamasee (also spelled Yamassee, Yemassee, and Yamasi) were a multiethnic confederation of Native Americans who lived in the coastal region of present-day South Carolina and Georgia in the late 17th and early 18th centuries. They became important allies and trading partners of the English colony of Carolina before rising against it in the Yamasee War of 1715.

Origins

The Yamasee appear to have formed from the remnants of several groups displaced by Spanish missions and slave raids in the Southeast, including people associated with the former Guale province of Spanish Florida. By the 1680s they had settled in towns near the mouth of the Savannah River and around Port Royal Sound, placing themselves under English rather than Spanish protection. Their language is known only from a short word list and is generally considered unclassified.

Relations with Carolina

For three decades the Yamasee were among Carolina's closest Indigenous allies. They traded deerskins and enslaved captives taken from Spanish-allied peoples, assisted the colony against the Spanish and the Tuscarora, and supplied warriors for colonial expeditions. Over time, however, trade abuses, dishonest weights and prices, demands for payment of debts in slaves, and settler encroachment poisoned the relationship.

Yamasee War

On Good Friday, 15 April 1715, the Yamasee and their allies launched coordinated attacks on traders and settlements across South Carolina, beginning the Yamasee War. The colony came close to destruction in the war's first months, but recovered with Cherokee assistance; the Cherokee decision to side with Carolina in early 1716 was decisive. Defeated Yamasee survivors withdrew south into Florida, where some joined Spanish missions near St. Augustine and others were absorbed among the emerging Seminole.

Later history

Yamasee communities persisted in Florida through the 18th century, raiding northward with Spanish support and gradually merging with neighbouring peoples. The name survives in the South Carolina town of Yemassee and in Yamasee descendants within the Seminole and other tribal communities.
