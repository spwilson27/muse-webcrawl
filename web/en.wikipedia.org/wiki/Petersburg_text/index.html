Petersburg text

The Petersburg text (Russian: Петербургский текст) is a concept in Russian literary studies denoting the coherent body of works of Russian literature — and the shared mythology they construct — about the city of Saint Petersburg. The term was developed by the semiotician Vladimir Toporov and the Tartu-Moscow Semiotic School, notably in Toporov's essay "Petersburg and the Petersburg Text of Russian Literature" (1984), building on earlier work by the scholar Nikolai Antsiferov, who wrote of the "soul of Petersburg."

Characteristics

According to Toporov, the Petersburg text is a single synthetic "super-text" created by many authors in dialogue with one another, unified by a common subject (the city) and a shared set of motifs and semantic oppositions. Central themes include the city's artificial, "intentional" foundation on a swamp by Peter the Great; its spectral, illusory character suspended between being and non-being; the conflict between the imperial "façade" city of parade avenues and the miserable city of slums and clerks; the threat of the flood and of elemental chaos underlying the city's rational grid; and extremes of weather, madness, doubles, and ghosts.

Principal works

The tradition conventionally begins with Alexander Pushkin's narrative poem The Bronze Horseman (1833), in which the animated statue of Peter the Great pursues the poor clerk Evgeny through the flooded city, and Pushkin's story The Queen of Spades. It continues through Nikolai Gogol's "Petersburg tales" (Nevsky Prospekt, The Nose, The Overcoat), Fyodor Dostoevsky's novels Crime and Punishment, The Idiot and the novella The Double, Andrei Bely's modernist novel Petersburg (1913/1916), and the poetry of Alexander Blok, Osip Mandelstam, and Anna Akhmatova, whose Poem Without a Hero reworks the city's myth in the twentieth century.

Legacy

The Petersburg text became a model for studying the "texts" of other cities (the Moscow text, the Perm text, and others) and remains a central framework in studies of Russian literary urbanism and cultural semiotics.
