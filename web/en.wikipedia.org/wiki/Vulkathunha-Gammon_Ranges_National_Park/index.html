Vulkathunha-Gammon Ranges National Park

Vulkathunha-Gammon Ranges National Park is a protected area in the Flinders Ranges of South Australia, located about 600 kilometres north of Adelaide. The park covers roughly 128,000 hectares of rugged semi-arid mountain country, including deep gorges, chasms, and some of the most geologically ancient exposed rock in the ranges.

History

The area is the traditional country of the Adnyamathanha people; "Vulkathunha" is an Adnyamathanha word. The Gammon Ranges were proclaimed a national park in 1970, and the park was later renamed to include the Aboriginal name. The region was explored by Europeans in the nineteenth century and was used for pastoral grazing and some mining before protection.

Landscape and wildlife

The Gammon Ranges are noted for their steep quartzite ridges, the seasonal waterholes of places such as Italowie Gorge and Weetootla Gorge, and the high plateau country around Mount McKinlay and Benbowie. Despite the arid environment, permanent rockholes support wildlife including yellow-footed rock-wallabies, red kangaroos, euros, wedge-tailed eagles, and rare plants such as the Gammon Ranges violet. The park is co-managed with the Adnyamathanha traditional owners and adjoins the Arkaroola Protection Area.
