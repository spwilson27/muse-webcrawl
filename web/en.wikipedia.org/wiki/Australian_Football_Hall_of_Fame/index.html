Australian Football Hall of Fame
Established: 1996
Sport: Australian rules football
Administered by: AFL
Inductees: Players, coaches, umpires, media, administrators

The Australian Football Hall of Fame was established in 1996 to recognise those who have made an outstanding contribution to Australian rules football. It was created as part of the celebrations for the centenary season of the Victorian Football League/Australian Football League.

Induction

An initial intake of 136 inductees was announced in 1996, spanning players, coaches, umpires, media personalities and administrators from all the major leagues, including the VFL/AFL, SANFL and WAFL. Further inductions are made annually. The highest honour within the Hall is elevation to "Legend" status, reserved for a small number of the game's most influential figures; recipients have included John Coleman, Leigh Matthews, Dick Reynolds, Haydn Bunton, Ted Whitten and Ron Barassi, who was the first Legend.
