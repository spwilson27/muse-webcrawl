Battle of Hampton Roads

The Battle of Hampton Roads, fought on March 8–9, 1862, was a naval battle of the American Civil War, famous as the first engagement between ironclad warships: the Union's USS Monitor and the Confederacy's CSS Virginia (built from the raised hull of USS Merrimack). The battle took place in Hampton Roads, Virginia, where the Union blockade squadron lay.

Monitor vs. Virginia

March 8: Virginia attacks

On 8 March 1862 CSS Virginia, commanded by Franklin Buchanan, steamed into Hampton Roads and attacked the wooden Union blockading fleet. She rammed and sank the sloop USS Cumberland, forced the frigate USS Congress to surrender and burn, and drove USS Minnesota aground, before retiring for the night. Union shot had bounced harmlessly off her sloped iron armour — the wooden navy's day was over.

March 9: Monitor arrives

Overnight, the revolutionary USS Monitor — designed by John Ericsson, almost awash, with a single rotating turret mounting two 11-inch Dahlgren guns — arrived from New York. On the morning of 9 March she engaged Virginia in a four-hour duel at close range. Neither ship could seriously damage the other; Monitor withdrew briefly after her commander, Lieutenant John Worden, was temporarily blinded, and Virginia, leaking and short of ammunition, returned to Norfolk, claiming the field.

Significance

The battle was a tactical draw but a strategic Union success: the blockade survived. Worldwide, the action demonstrated that wooden warships were obsolete and that the ironclad — and the gun turret — were the future, accelerating a naval revolution already under way since the French Gloire (1859) and HMS Warrior (1860). Virginia was scuttled by her crew in May 1862; Monitor sank in a storm off Cape Hatteras on 31 December 1862.
