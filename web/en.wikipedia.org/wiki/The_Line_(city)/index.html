The Line (styled THE LINE; Arabic: ذا لاين) is a planned smart linear city under construction in Neom, in Saudi Arabia's Tabuk Province. It is designed as a single mirrored structure 170 kilometres long, 500 metres tall and only 200 metres wide, intended to house nine million residents with no cars, no streets, and no carbon emissions.

Design

Announced by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman in January 2021 as part of Saudi Vision 2030, the city is planned to run on entirely renewable energy, with all daily needs reachable within a five-minute walk and an end-to-end high-speed rail journey of 20 minutes. Two parallel mirrored walls would enclose layered residential, commercial, and leisure spaces, with the exterior mirror facade intended to blend into the desert landscape. The project claims it will preserve 95 percent of Neom's natural environment.

Construction and scaling back

Excavation and foundation work began in the early 2020s along the route from the Red Sea coast toward the mountains near Tabuk. In 2024, reports indicated that the segment expected to be completed by 2030 had been scaled back to about 2.4 km with a projected population under 300,000, amid wider reassessment of Neom's costs and timelines; Saudi officials maintained the overall concept remained the long-term goal.

Criticism

Urban planners and architects have questioned the project's feasibility, cost (initially estimated at hundreds of billions of dollars), environmental impact, and its effect on local wildlife migration, while human rights organisations criticised the displacement of the local Howeitat tribe from the site. Supporters present it as a bold prototype for post-oil, post-car urbanism.
