Story arc

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A story arc is an extended or continuing storyline in episodic storytelling media such as television, comic books, and radio programs. In a series with an arc, each episode or issue advances a larger plot that develops and is resolved over multiple instalments, in contrast to purely episodic formats in which each entry stands alone and the status quo is restored by the end.

Structure and purpose

Story arcs allow writers to develop characters and themes over an extended period, build long-term suspense, and reward sustained audience attention. An arc may occupy a single season (a "season arc"), span an entire series, or form one strand among several intertwining arcs. Character development across episodes is sometimes described as a character arc, in which a protagonist is changed by the events of the story.

History

Serialized storytelling has roots in 19th-century serial novels and soap operas. Television dramas such as Hill Street Blues pioneered multi-episode arcs in the 1980s, and series like Babylon 5 and The X-Files popularized pre-planned, series-long arcs in the 1990s. In comics, multi-issue crossover events and storylines such as "The Dark Phoenix Saga" exemplify the form. The rise of streaming services and binge-watching further normalized heavily serialized storytelling.
