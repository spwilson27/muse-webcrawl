R-process

r-process
Type: Nucleosynthesis process
Full name: Rapid neutron-capture process
Principal sites: Neutron star mergers; some supernovae
Products: About half of the neutron-rich heavy nuclei, incl. gold, platinum, uranium

The rapid neutron-capture process, commonly called the r-process, is a set of nuclear reactions in nuclear astrophysics responsible for the creation of approximately half of the atomic nuclei heavier than iron. In an environment flooded with free neutrons, atomic nuclei rapidly capture many neutrons before they can beta-decay, building extremely neutron-rich nuclei far from stability, which then decay back toward stability to produce heavy elements such as gold, platinum, europium, thorium, and uranium.

Mechanism

The r-process requires neutron densities above roughly 10²⁰ neutrons per cm³ and temperatures above about a billion kelvin, so that neutron captures proceed much faster than beta decays. Starting from seed nuclei such as iron, captures drive the matter to the neutron drip line; the process pauses at "waiting points" where neutron shell closures (magic numbers N = 50, 82, 126) create bottlenecks visible as the characteristic r-process abundance peaks. When the neutron supply is exhausted, the unstable products decay to stable heavy nuclei over seconds to days.

Astrophysical sites

The r-process was proposed in the landmark 1957 B²FH paper on stellar nucleosynthesis. Its astrophysical site was debated for decades between core-collapse supernovae and neutron star mergers. In August 2017 the gravitational-wave event GW170817 — a neutron star merger — was followed by a kilonova whose light curve and spectra showed the production of large quantities of r-process material, confirming mergers as a major, probably dominant, site. R-process-enriched ancient stars in the Milky Way halo and in dwarf galaxies such as Reticulum II preserve the chemical signature of these rare events.
