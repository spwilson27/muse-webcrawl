Strépy-Thieu boat lift

The Strépy-Thieu boat lift, the tallest in the world at its completion

The Strépy-Thieu boat lift (French: L'ascenseur funiculaire de Strépy-Thieu) is a boat lift on the Canal du Centre in the municipality of Le Rœulx, Hainaut, Belgium. With a height difference of 73.15 metres (240 ft) between its upstream and downstream reaches, it was the tallest boat lift in the world when it opened in 2002, a record it held until the lift of China's Three Gorges Dam surpassed it in 2016.

Description

The structure is a funicular lift: two independent water-filled caissons, each about 112 m long and 12 m wide with a usable water depth of 3.35 m, are suspended by cables and counterbalanced so that vessels up to 1,350 tonnes (the European Class IV standard) can be raised or lowered in about seven minutes. The massive concrete structure is about 81 m high and 130 m long, and is powered by electric motors driving winches; because the caissons balance each other, energy consumption per transit is modest.

History

The lift was built to replace a chain of smaller lifts and locks on the Canal du Centre, including the four historic 19th-century hydraulic boat lifts (now a UNESCO World Heritage Site) that could handle only 300-tonne barges. Construction began in 1982 as part of a programme to bring Belgium's main canals up to the 1,350-tonne standard; economic disputes and changing priorities delayed completion by two decades. The lift entered service in September 2002, and the new stretch of canal it serves transformed freight movement between the Meuse and Scheldt basins.
