T-carrier

The T-carrier system, introduced by the Bell System in the United States in the 1960s, was the first successful system of digital telephony. It transmits multiple digitised voice channels over a single physical line using time-division multiplexing, based on the DS0 channel of 64 kbit/s.

A T1 frame consists of 24 time slots of 8 bits each plus one framing bit, giving 193 bits sent 8,000 times per second. Voice is digitised with μ-law companding in North America and Japan. The European counterpart, the E-carrier system, uses a different hierarchy beginning with E1 at 2.048 Mbit/s and 30 channels.

Use

T1 lines became a standard business service for voice trunks and, later, for Internet access, and fractional T1 and bonded T1 services were common before DSL, cable and fibre access displaced them. T-carrier framing and signalling conventions remain embedded in telecom equipment and standards.
