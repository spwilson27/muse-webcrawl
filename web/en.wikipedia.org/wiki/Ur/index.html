Ur

Ur (Sumerian: Urim) was an important Sumerian city-state in ancient Mesopotamia, located at modern Tell el-Muqayyar in southern Iraq. Founded in the Ubaid period (c. 5500 BCE), it grew into one of the largest and wealthiest cities of the ancient world, famous for its Royal Tombs and the Ziggurat of Ur.

Ur
Location: Dhi Qar Governorate, Iraq
Founded: c. 5500 BCE
Major periods: Early Dynastic, Ur III
Patron deity: Nanna (moon god)

History

Ur flourished in the Early Dynastic period (c. 2900–2350 BCE); Leonard Woolley's excavations in the 1920s–30s uncovered the Royal Cemetery, including the tomb of Queen Puabi, the Standard of Ur, and the supposed death pits. Under Ur-Nammu (c. 2112–2095 BCE), founder of the Third Dynasty of Ur, the city ruled most of Mesopotamia and built its great ziggurat. The dynasty fell to Elamites around 2004 BCE, lamented in Sumerian poetry.

Biblical tradition

The Hebrew Bible names 'Ur of the Chaldees' as the birthplace of Abraham, though the identification and its chronology are debated.

Archaeology

Excavations resumed in the 21st century; the site is part of a UNESCO World Heritage listing of southern Iraqi marshland and cities.
