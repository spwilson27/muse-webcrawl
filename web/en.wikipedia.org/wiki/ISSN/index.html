ISSN

An International Standard Serial Number (ISSN) is an eight-digit serial number used to uniquely identify a serial publication, such as a magazine. The ISSN is especially helpful in distinguishing between serials with the same title. ISSNs are used in ordering, cataloging, interlibrary loans, and other processes linked to serial literature.

Format

The system was first drafted as an International Organization for Standardization (ISO) International Standard in 1971 and published as ISO 3297 in 1975. An ISSN consists of eight digits divided by a hyphen into two groups of four; the final digit is a check digit, which may be an "X" representing 10. Unlike the ISBN for books, the ISSN does not encode the country or publisher in its digits.

Assignments

ISSNs are assigned by a network of national ISSN centres, coordinated by the ISSN International Centre in Paris. Separate ISSNs are normally assigned to different media versions of the same serial — print, online, CD-ROM — while a "linking ISSN" (ISSN-L) designates all versions of a publication together. The database of assigned ISSNs covers millions of serial titles worldwide.

Use

The ISSN is printed on or in each issue of a serial and is often encoded in a barcode (EAN-13) on magazine covers. Libraries use it to identify, order, and check in serials and to manage subscriptions and interlibrary loans.
