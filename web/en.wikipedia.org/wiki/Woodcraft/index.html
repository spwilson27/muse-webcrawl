Woodcraft

Woodcraft denotes the skills and knowledge for living and travelling in woodland, including tracking, shelter building, firecraft, foraging, campcraft and nature observation. The term also describes the youth movement founded by Ernest Thompson Seton.

Skills

Traditional woodcraft encompasses plant and animal identification, woodland management, the safe use of axes and knives, navigation, first aid, and low-impact camping. In modern usage it overlaps with bushcraft and survival skills, though woodcraft emphasises long-term harmony with the natural environment rather than short-term survival.

Seton and the Woodcraft Indians

In 1902 Anglo-Canadian naturalist Ernest Thompson Seton founded the Woodcraft Indians in the United States, an outdoor youth programme based on native American lore, nature study and a set of guiding laws. The movement influenced Robert Baden-Powell's Scouting, though the two later diverged.

Woodcraft Folk

In Britain, the Woodcraft Folk was founded in 1925 as a co-operative, egalitarian youth movement and remains active, organising camps, hikes and outdoor education with an emphasis on peace, sustainability and children's participation in decision-making.

Modern practice

Woodcraft courses are offered by outdoor schools and forest school programmes worldwide, teaching carving, green woodworking, coppicing and ecological literacy to children and adults.
