Pacific temperate rainforests

The Pacific temperate rainforests ecoregion is the largest area of temperate zone rainforest on Earth. It runs along the western coast of North America from Kodiak Island in Alaska south through British Columbia and the Pacific Northwest to Northern California. The region is defined by proximity to the Pacific Ocean, the presence of coastal mountains, and high rainfall through much of the year.

Ecology

These forests contain some of the largest and longest-lived trees on the planet, including Sitka spruce, coast redwood, Douglas-fir, western redcedar, and western hemlock. They store immense quantities of carbon in biomass and soils, and support wildlife such as the marbled murrelet, northern spotted owl, Kermode ("spirit") bear, and abundant salmon runs.

Conservation

Intensive industrial logging has removed much of the original old-growth forest outside protected areas, prompting decades of conflict and conservation campaigns, notably in Clayoquot Sound and the Great Bear Rainforest of British Columbia, where ecosystem-based management agreements have protected large areas since 2016.
