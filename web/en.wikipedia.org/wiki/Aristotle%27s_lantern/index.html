Aristotle's lantern

Aristotle's lantern is the name for the complex jaw apparatus of sea urchins, used to graze algae and other food from hard surfaces. The name derives from Aristotle's description of the structure in his History of Animals, in which he compared its shape to a horn lantern.

Structure

The lantern consists of five hard, calcareous teeth arranged radially, each held in a pyramid-shaped ossicle, together with supporting ossicles and muscles — around fifty skeletal elements in all. Muscles protract and retract the lantern and move the teeth, which grow continuously to replace worn tips. Some urchins use the teeth to excavate burrows in rock.

Function and evolution

The apparatus is characteristic of most living sea urchins (class Echinoidea), although it is reduced or absent in irregular urchins such as sand dollars and heart urchins, which feed on sediment instead.
