Neopronoun

Neopronouns are newly coined third-person pronouns used in place of, or alongside, the standard pronouns of a language. In English they include sets such as xe/xem, ze/hir, ey/em (Spivak pronouns), and fae/faer, as well as noun-derived "nounself" pronouns. They are most often used by genderqueer and non-binary people whose identities are not expressed by he, she, or singular they.

History

Proposals for gender-neutral pronouns in English date back centuries; invented sets appeared as early as the 19th century, and "thon" was listed in dictionaries into the 20th century. The Spivak pronouns were created by mathematician Michael Spivak in the 1990s for use in his books. The term "neopronoun" gained currency in online communities in the 2010s.

Use and reception

Neopronouns are used primarily in LGBTQ+ and online spaces. Supporters say they allow precise self-expression; critics question their practicality and necessity. Some style guides and publications now ask for and respect an individual's stated pronouns, including neopronouns.
