Pisanello

Pisanello (c. 1395 – c. 1455), born Antonio di Puccio Pisano, was an Italian painter and medallist of the early Renaissance, among the most distinguished artists of the International Gothic style. He is celebrated for his luminous frescoes, his meticulous drawings of animals and court life, and for reviving the art of the portrait medal, which he effectively created in its Renaissance form.

Life and work

Probably born in Pisa and raised in Verona, Pisanello worked for courts across Italy — the Doge of Venice, the Gonzaga of Mantua, the Este of Ferrara, the Visconti of Milan, and the Pope in Rome. His surviving frescoes include Saint George and the Princess in Sant'Anastasia, Verona. His medal of the Byzantine emperor John VIII Palaiologos (1438), cast during the Council of Ferrara, is the first great Renaissance portrait medal and set the pattern for the genre.

Drawings and reputation

Pisanello left large numbers of studies of animals, birds, costumes, and figures, preserved in collections such as the Codex Vallardi in the Louvre; these drawings are among the finest observational studies of the fifteenth century. Praised by humanist poets in his lifetime, he was one of the most famous Italian artists of his day before tastes shifted toward the classical Renaissance of Florence.
