The Congress of Deputies (Spanish: Congreso de los Diputados) is the lower house of the Cortes Generales, the Spanish parliament. It has 350 members elected by constituencies (matching Spanish provinces) using the D'Hondt method of proportional representation.

The Congress meets in the Palacio de las Cortes in Madrid. Under the Spanish Constitution of 1978, the Congress holds greater power than the upper house, the Senate: it alone can grant or withdraw confidence in the prime minister (President of the Government), and it can override Senate vetoes by an absolute majority.

History

The origins of the chamber lie in the Estatuto Real of 1834, which created a bicameral Cortes with an elected lower house, the Estamento de Procuradores. The modern Congress was established by the Constitution of 1978 following Spain's transition to democracy after the death of Francisco Franco. On 23 February 1981, during the investiture vote for Leopoldo Calvo-Sotelo, Civil Guard officers under Lieutenant Colonel Antonio Tejero stormed the chamber in a failed coup attempt, holding the deputies hostage for 18 hours.

Composition and election

Deputies are elected for four-year terms. Each province forms a constituency returning a minimum of two deputies, with the remaining seats allocated by population; Ceuta and Melilla each elect one. The electoral system, designed to favour large parties and stable majorities, uses provincial constituencies and the D'Hondt formula with a 3% threshold in each province.

Functions

The Congress elects the President of the Government, passes legislation jointly with the Senate (whose amendments and vetoes it can override), approves the national budget, and exercises oversight of the executive through questions, interpellations and committees of inquiry.
