Line level

Line level is the specified strength of an audio signal used to transmit analogue audio between components such as CD and DVD players, television sets, audio amplifiers, and mixing consoles, as distinct from the much weaker microphone level and the stronger speaker level.

Nominal levels

Consumer audio equipment operates at a nominal line level of −10 dBV (0.316 V RMS), while professional equipment uses +4 dBu (1.228 V RMS). The difference of about 12 dB must be accounted for when interconnecting consumer and professional gear, or levels will be too quiet or will distort. Line-level signals are carried over unbalanced RCA cables in consumer equipment and balanced XLR or TRS cables professionally.

Line inputs and outputs

A line input has a relatively high impedance, typically 10 kΩ or more, and expects a line-level signal. Devices such as turntables without a built-in phono preamplifier produce a much lower-level signal requiring RIAA equalisation, and cannot be connected directly to a line input. Headphone outputs and auxiliary outputs are usually line compatible, though their levels vary.
