Art history

Art history is the study of artistic works in their historical development and stylistic contexts — their design, format, function and meaning — encompassing painting, sculpture, architecture, decorative arts, photography and other visual media across all cultures and periods.

Development of the discipline

Giorgio Vasari's Lives of the Artists (1550, 1568) is a founding biographical model. Johann Joachim Winckelmann in the 18th century wrote the first systematic history of ancient art and period styles. In the 19th–20th centuries Heinrich Wölfflin developed formal analysis, Aby Warburg and Erwin Panofsky founded iconology (the study of subject matter and symbolism), and scholars such as Alois Riegl and Ernst Gombrich reshaped the field. Later approaches include social art history (T.J. Clark), feminist critique (Linda Nochlin's 1971 "Why Have There Been No Great Women Artists?"), and global and postcolonial art histories challenging the Western canon.

Periods and movements

Western narratives typically run from prehistoric and ancient art through medieval, Renaissance, Baroque, Neoclassicism and Romanticism to modern movements — Impressionism, Cubism, Surrealism, Abstract Expressionism — and contemporary art, studied alongside African, Asian, Islamic, Indigenous and Oceanic traditions.

Methods

Art historians use archival research, connoisseurship, technical examination (X-ray, infrared, pigment analysis), and theory to attribute, date and interpret works and to establish provenance.
