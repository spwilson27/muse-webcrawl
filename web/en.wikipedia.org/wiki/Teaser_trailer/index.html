Teaser trailer

A teaser trailer (or simply a teaser) is a short trailer and advertisement, and a form of teaser campaign advertising, that focuses on a film or television series. Its purpose is to pique the audience's interest without revealing too much about the production, often released long before the full trailer.

Characteristics

Teaser trailers are usually released well in advance of the product they advertise — sometimes more than a year before release — and run between 30 seconds and two minutes. They often contain little or no footage from the actual film; some are instead shot specifically for the teaser, before principal photography has even begun. Famous examples include the teaser for Terminator 2: Judgment Day, which showed a T-800 endoskeleton being manufactured in a factory, and the announcement teasers for Pixar and Marvel Studios films.

History

Although trailers themselves date to the 1910s, the distinct teaser format emerged in the 1970s with blockbuster promotion campaigns such as those for Jaws (1975) and Star Wars (1977). By the 1990s and 2000s, teaser trailers had become major marketing events in their own right, sometimes attached to other films or debuted online to drive anticipation.

Differences from full trailers

Unlike a theatrical trailer, which typically runs two to two-and-a-half minutes and outlines the plot, characters and tone, a teaser is designed mainly to announce that a production exists and to establish a mood. Teasers for sequels frequently rely on a logo, a release date and a short sequence of iconic imagery or dialogue.
