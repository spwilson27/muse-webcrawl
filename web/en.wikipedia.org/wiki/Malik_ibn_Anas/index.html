Malik ibn Anas ibn Malik ibn Abi Amir al-Asbahi (c. 711–795 CE), commonly known as Imam Malik, was an Arab Muslim jurist, theologian and hadith traditionist from Medina. He is the founder of the Maliki school of jurisprudence (madhhab), one of the four major schools of Sunni Islamic law.

Life

Malik was born in Medina into a family of Yemeni origin. He spent nearly his entire life in the city, studying under scholars such as Nafi, the freedman of Abdullah ibn Umar, and Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri. He became renowned for his teaching circle in the Prophet's Mosque, where students travelled from across the Muslim world to study with him.

Works

His most famous work is Al-Muwatta ("The Well-Trodden Path"), one of the earliest surviving books of Islamic law, combining hadith, the opinions of the Companions, and the practice of the people of Medina into a legal framework. It remains a foundational text of the Maliki school.

Legacy

The Maliki school became dominant in North and West Africa, Upper Egypt, Sudan, and historically in Islamic Spain (al-Andalus). Malik died in Medina in 795 CE and was buried in the al-Baqi cemetery.
