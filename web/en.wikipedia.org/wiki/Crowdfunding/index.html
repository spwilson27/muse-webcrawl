Crowdfunding

Crowdfunding is the practice of funding a project or venture by raising money from a large number of people, typically via the Internet. Modern crowdfunding is generally conducted through online platforms that intermediate between project creators and backers.

Types

Reward-based crowdfunding offers backers a product or perk; donation-based crowdfunding funds charitable or personal causes; equity crowdfunding sells securities in a company; and debt crowdfunding (peer-to-peer lending) repays backers with interest. Kickstarter, launched in 2009, popularised the all-or-nothing reward model.

History

Precursors include subscription publishing and the 1885 campaign that funded the pedestal of the Statue of Liberty through small newspaper donations. Internet crowdfunding took off in the 2000s with ArtistShare (2001), Indiegogo (2008), and GoFundMe (2010), the latter focused on personal and medical fundraising.

Regulation and risks

Equity crowdfunding is regulated — in the United States by the JOBS Act of 2012 and Regulation Crowdfunding (2016). Backers face risks of project failure, delay, and fraud, since reward pledges are generally not guaranteed to deliver.
