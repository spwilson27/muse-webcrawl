The SCMaglev (SuperConducting Magnetic levitation) is a magnetic levitation railway system developed by the Central Japan Railway Company (JR Central) in Japan. Using superconducting magnets on board the train and coils in a U-shaped guideway, it achieves electrodynamic suspension: the train levitates about 10 cm above the guideway at speed, guided and propelled by the interaction of the magnets with ground coils, while running on rubber tyres below roughly 150 km/h.

Development and records

Japanese maglev research began in the 1960s–70s under Japanese National Railways, with tests at Miyazaki and, from 1997, on the 42.8 km Yamanashi test line. The MLX01 test train set a world rail speed record of 581 km/h in 2003; the improved L0 Series reached 603 km/h in April 2015, still the fastest speed attained by a crewed rail vehicle. The system was approved for commercial construction by the Japanese government in 2011.

Chūō Shinkansen

The first commercial application is the Chūō Shinkansen, being built by JR Central between Tokyo (Shinagawa) and Nagoya, with a planned extension to Osaka. The line, mostly in deep tunnel, is designed for a maximum operating speed of 500 km/h, cutting Tokyo–Nagoya travel to about 40 minutes. Construction began in 2014; disputes over water resources in Shizuoka Prefecture delayed tunnelling there, and the opening, originally planned for 2027, has been postponed. JR Central has also promoted the technology abroad, notably for a proposed Northeast Maglev line between Washington, D.C. and Baltimore/New York in the United States.
