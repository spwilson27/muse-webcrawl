John Rackham (c. 1682 – 18 November 1720), commonly known as Calico Jack, was an English pirate captain operating in the Caribbean during the final years of the Golden Age of Piracy. His nickname derived from the calico clothing he was said to wear. Rackham is best known for two things: his Jolly Roger flag design — a white skull above two crossed cutlasses on a black field, one of the most recognisable pirate flags — and for having two female pirates, Anne Bonny and Mary Read, in his crew.

Career

Rackham began as quartermaster aboard the brigantine of the pirate Charles Vane. In November 1718, after Vane refused to fight a French man-of-war, the crew deposed him and elected Rackham captain. Rackham took several prizes in the Caribbean and off the North American coast, accepted a royal pardon at Nassau in 1719, and there met Anne Bonny, the wife of sailor James Bonny, who joined him when he returned to piracy in 1720 by stealing the sloop William from Nassau harbour.

Cruising around Jamaica, Rackham's small crew took fishing boats and small merchantmen. In October 1720 his sloop was attacked near Negril by a pirate-hunting vessel commissioned by the Governor of Jamaica and commanded by Jonathan Barnet; most of the crew, reportedly drunk, were quickly captured, while Bonny and Read were said to have fought on deck. Rackham was tried at Spanish Town, Jamaica, convicted, and hanged at Port Royal on 18 November 1720; his body was afterwards gibbeted on a small cay at the harbour entrance, still known as Rackham's Cay. Bonny and Read escaped execution by “pleading the belly” — both were pregnant; Read died in prison, while Bonny's fate after imprisonment is uncertain.
