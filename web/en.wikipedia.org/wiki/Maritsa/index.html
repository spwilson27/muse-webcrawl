The Maritsa (Bulgarian: Марица; Greek: Έβρος, Evros; Turkish: Meriç) is a river that flows through the Balkans in Southeast Europe, forming much of the land border between Greece and Turkey.

Course

Rising in the Rila Mountains of western Bulgaria, the Maritsa flows east and southeast past Plovdiv, then through Edirne in Turkey, where it turns south and forms the Greek–Turkish frontier before reaching the Aegean Sea near Enez, having formed a large delta.

History

The river valley was a corridor of Thrace in antiquity. The Battle of Maritsa in 1371 saw Ottoman forces defeat a Serbian army, a key step in the Ottoman conquest of the Balkans. In modern times the Evros has been a heavily guarded border and a crossing point in migration crises.

Economy

Its waters irrigate some of the most fertile farmland in Bulgaria and Thrace; hydroelectric dams stand on its Bulgarian tributaries, and the lower river is prone to severe flooding, managed by joint agreements among the three riparian states.
