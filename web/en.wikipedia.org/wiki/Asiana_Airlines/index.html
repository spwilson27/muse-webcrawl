Asiana Airlines (Korean: 아시아나항공) is a South Korean airline headquartered in Seoul. Founded in 1988 as the country's second flag carrier after Korean Air, it grew into a major international airline serving destinations across Asia, Europe, North America and Oceania from its hubs at Incheon International Airport and Gimpo Airport. For many years it was a member of the Star Alliance.

History

The airline was established by the Kumho Asiana Group in 1988, ending Korean Air's monopoly under a government policy of fostering a second national carrier. It launched international services in 1990 and expanded rapidly through the 1990s and 2000s. Financial difficulties in the Kumho group led to the airline being put up for sale in 2019, and after a failed deal with a Hyundai-led consortium, Korean Air agreed in 2020 to acquire it. Following regulatory approvals in multiple jurisdictions, Korean Air completed the acquisition in December 2024, with Asiana operating as a subsidiary pending full integration of the two carriers; its low-cost subsidiary Air Busan and Air Seoul are also part of the group.

Fleet and services

Asiana has operated wide-body types including the Boeing 747-400, 777-200ER, Airbus A330, A350-900 and A380, alongside narrow-body A320-family aircraft. It was known for high service standards and repeatedly won "Airline of the Year" awards from industry publications.

Accidents and incidents

The airline's most notable accident was Flight 214, a Boeing 777-200ER that crashed on landing at San Francisco International Airport on 6 July 2013, killing three passengers; investigators cited the flight crew's mismanagement of the approach. Earlier, Flight 733 crashed into a mountain near Mokpo in 1993, killing 68 people.
