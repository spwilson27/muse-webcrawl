Acta Mathematica is a peer-reviewed scientific journal publishing original research in all fields of mathematics. It was founded in 1882 by the Swedish mathematician Gösta Mittag-Leffler and is published by the Institut Mittag-Leffler, a research institute of the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences. It is widely regarded as one of the most prestigious mathematics journals in the world.

History

Mittag-Leffler founded the journal with the support of King Oscar II of Sweden, intending it as an international journal at a time when most leading journals were national in scope. Early volumes published in French and German carried work by leading mathematicians such as Henri Poincaré, Georg Cantor and David Hilbert. Poincaré's prize-winning memoir on the three-body problem, published in 1890, famously contained an error whose correction led to the discovery of chaotic dynamics. Since 2017 the journal has been open access, published under agreement with Springer.

Scope and standing

The journal appears quarterly and publishes a small number of long, significant papers each year. Because of its selectivity it consistently ranks among the highest-impact mathematics journals.
