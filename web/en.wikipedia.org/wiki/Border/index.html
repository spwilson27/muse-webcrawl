Border

Borders are generally defined as geographical boundaries imposed either by features such as oceans and terrain, or by political entities such as governments, sovereign states, federated states, and other subnational entities. Political borders can be established through warfare, colonisation, or mutual agreements between the political entities that reside in those areas.

Types

Borders may be classified as natural (following rivers, mountain ranges or coasts), geometric (straight lines or arcs, common in Africa and North America as products of colonial partitioning), or cultural (following linguistic or religious divisions). Frontier zones, buffer zones and demilitarised zones describe areas where sovereignty is porous or deliberately suspended.

Border controls

States regulate the movement of people and goods across borders through border controls, ranging from open internal borders (such as within the Schengen Area) to heavily fortified boundaries with walls, fences and surveillance. Border regions often develop distinctive economies and cultures, and cross-border cooperation bodies exist in many parts of Europe and North America.

Maritime and air borders

The United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea defines territorial seas and exclusive economic zones, while airspace sovereignty extends to the airspace above a state's territory under the Chicago Convention.
