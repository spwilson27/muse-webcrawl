Londinium, also known as Roman London, was the capital of Roman Britain during most of the period of Roman rule. It was founded around AD 47–50 on the north bank of the River Thames, at the lowest point where the river could conveniently be bridged, on the site of the present City of London.

History

Londinium grew rapidly as a trading port following the Roman invasion of AD 43. In AD 60/61 it was destroyed by the forces of Boudica during the Iceni revolt, described by Tacitus, but was rebuilt within a decade and replaced Camulodunum (Colchester) as the capital of the province. A large forum and basilica, amphitheatre, temples, baths, and a fort were constructed; a defensive wall (London Wall) was built around AD 200.

At its height in the 2nd century, Londinium had a population estimated at 45,000–60,000. The city declined in the 3rd and 4th centuries and was largely abandoned after the Roman withdrawal around AD 410, though the site later became the Anglo-Saxon trading port of Lundenwic, and the walled city was reoccupied under Alfred the Great.

Archaeology

Major remains include stretches of the London Wall, the Temple of Mithras, the amphitheatre under the Guildhall, and numerous finds displayed at the Museum of London.
