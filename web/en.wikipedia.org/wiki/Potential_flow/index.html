Potential flow

Potential flow
FieldFluid dynamics
AssumptionsIncompressible, inviscid, irrotational
Governing equationLaplace's equation

In fluid dynamics, potential flow is the flow of an incompressible, inviscid fluid with zero vorticity (irrotational flow). Because the velocity field is irrotational, it can be written as the gradient of a scalar velocity potential, and because the fluid is incompressible, that potential satisfies Laplace's equation.

Properties

Potential flows can be constructed by superposing elementary solutions — uniform streams, sources, sinks, doublets, and vortices — which makes the theory analytically tractable. Classic results include flow around a cylinder and d'Alembert's paradox, the surprising prediction of zero drag on a body in steady potential flow.

Applicability

Although real fluids are viscous, potential-flow models give good approximations away from solid boundaries for many flows at large scales, and they remain a foundation of aerodynamics, including the theory of lift via the Kutta–Joukowski theorem.
