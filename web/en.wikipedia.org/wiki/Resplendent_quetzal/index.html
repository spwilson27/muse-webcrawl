Resplendent quetzal

Resplendent quetzal Pharomachrus mocinno
FamilyTrogonidae
RangeSouthern Mexico to western Panama
StatusNear Threatened (IUCN)

The resplendent quetzal (Pharomachrus mocinno) is a bird in the trogon family found in cloud forests from Chiapas, Mexico, to western Panama. It is known for its iridescent green and red plumage; breeding males grow elongated tail coverts up to about 65 cm long.

Description and behaviour

The quetzal feeds mainly on fruit, especially wild avocados, swallowing them whole and regurgitating the pits, and also takes insects and small vertebrates. It nests in holes in dead trees and is a poor flier over long distances, making altitudinal migrations between breeding and non-breeding elevations.

Cultural significance

Sacred to the Aztec and Maya, who associated its feathers with the god Quetzalcoatl, the quetzal is the national bird of Guatemala, appears on its flag and coat of arms, and gives its name to the Guatemalan currency. Habitat loss has made it Near Threatened.
