Leonese language

Leonese (Leonese: llionés) is a Romance language of the Astur-Leonese group, historically spoken in the former Kingdom of León and today surviving principally in the Spanish provinces of León and Zamora (in the autonomous community of Castile and León) and in adjoining areas. Closely related to Asturian to the north and Mirandese across the Portuguese border, it is classified by UNESCO as an endangered language.

History

Leonese descends from the Vulgar Latin of the kingdom of León, and for centuries served as a language of administration and literature; the earliest texts arguably in a Leonese vernacular include the Nodicia de Kesos (c. 980), sometimes cited as the oldest document in an Iberian Romance language. From the 13th century, Castilian progressively displaced Leonese in writing and administration, and the spoken language retreated to rural areas.

Status today

Estimates of speakers vary widely, from a few tens of thousands upward; most are older, and intergenerational transmission is weak. The Statute of Autonomy of Castile and León (reformed 2007) names Leonese as part of the community's linguistic heritage deserving protection, though it is not co-official. Cultural associations, a small publishing scene, some school programs, and annual festivals promote the language, and a distinct Leonese Wikipedia (Wikipedia n'llionés) exists.
