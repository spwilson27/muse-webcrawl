Aleksandr Andronov

Died31 October 1952 (aged 51), Gorky, USSR

Aleksandr Aleksandrovich Andronov (11 April 1901 – 31 October 1952) was a Soviet physicist whose work in nonlinear oscillations founded a major school of dynamical systems theory. He was a member of the Academy of Sciences of the USSR.

Work

Working with Leonid Mandelstam's school in Moscow and later at Gorky (Nizhny Novgorod), Andronov developed the mathematical theory of self-oscillations (auto-oscillations), introducing the concept into science and applying the qualitative theory of differential equations — including limit cycles and bifurcations — to physical and engineering problems. With Lev Pontryagin he formulated a criterion for structural stability of dynamical systems.

Institutions and honours

Andronov founded the school of nonlinear oscillations at Gorky University and helped establish its radiophysics faculty. A lunar crater is named Andronov in his honour.
