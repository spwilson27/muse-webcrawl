Authorship attribution
Field: Stylometry, forensic linguistics, computational linguistics
Task: Infer the author of a text from stylistic evidence
Related: Author profiling, plagiarism detection

Authorship attribution is the task of determining the author of a text whose authorship is unknown or disputed, based on the measurable stylistic properties of the writing. It is a central problem of stylometry and is applied in literary scholarship, history, and forensic linguistics.

Methods

Early statistical work relied on word-length and sentence-length distributions; a landmark study by Mosteller and Wallace (1964) used frequencies of function words in a Bayesian framework to attribute the disputed Federalist Papers to Madison. Modern approaches use features such as character and word n-grams, vocabulary richness and syntactic patterns, classified with machine learning methods. Famous applications include the attribution of the Primary Colors novel to Joe Klein and analyses of disputed Shakespeare texts. The field also includes adversarial stylometry, which studies how authors can disguise their style.
