Heads of terms

A heads of terms document (also called a term sheet, letter of intent or memorandum of understanding in related contexts) is a non-binding document setting out the main commercial terms of a proposed transaction or agreement, agreed in principle between parties before detailed contracts are drafted. The term "heads of terms" is especially common in United Kingdom commercial practice, including property and corporate transactions.

Contents

Heads of terms typically record:

  • the parties and the subject matter (e.g. the property, business or shares involved);
  • the price or rent and how it is to be paid;
  • timetables, conditions (such as due diligence, financing or regulatory approval) and exclusivity periods;
  • confidentiality obligations and allocation of costs;
  • provisions on governing law and the non-binding status of the document.

Legal status

Heads of terms are usually expressly marked "subject to contract" and are not intended to be legally binding as to the main transaction, although some clauses — confidentiality, exclusivity (lock-out), costs and governing law — are commonly agreed to be binding. Courts may nevertheless enforce a document described as heads of terms if, in substance, the parties have agreed all essential terms with the intention to be bound, so careful drafting of the document's status is essential.
