Jewish community

A Jewish community is a group of Jews who live in a particular place and share religious, cultural and social institutions — typically a synagogue, a school, a burial society and charitable organisations. Jewish communities have existed since antiquity, from the ancient communities of the Land of Israel and the diaspora of the Mediterranean world to the large modern communities of Israel, the United States, France and elsewhere.

A synagogue is typically the centre of a Jewish community's religious and social life.

Organisation

Historically, Jewish communities were often self-governing, with a council (kahal) that collected taxes, ran courts and maintained communal institutions. In the modern era, communities are usually organised as voluntary associations, sometimes federated nationally, and they span the full spectrum of Jewish religious life — Orthodox, Conservative, Reform and secular.

Community life

Community institutions include synagogues, Jewish day schools and supplementary schools, kosher food provision, mikvaot (ritual baths), cemeteries, youth movements, and welfare and cultural organisations. Festivals such as Passover, Rosh Hashanah and Hanukkah are celebrated communally, and lifecycle events — circumcision, bar and bat mitzvah, weddings and funerals — are communal occasions.

Diaspora and Israel

About half of the world's roughly 15 million Jews live in Israel and most of the rest in the diaspora, above all in North America. Diaspora communities vary greatly in size, from the millions-strong communities of New York and Los Angeles to small communities of a few families in remote towns, but all share a sense of peoplehood and connection to Jewish history and to Israel.
