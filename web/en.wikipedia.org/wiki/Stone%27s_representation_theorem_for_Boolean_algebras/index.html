Stone's representation theorem for Boolean algebras

In mathematics, Stone's representation theorem for Boolean algebras states that every Boolean algebra is isomorphic to a certain field of sets. The theorem is fundamental to the deeper understanding of Boolean algebra that emerged in the twentieth century. It is named for Marshall H. Stone, who proved it in 1936 in his long paper on the theory of Boolean rings; it was the first of a series of results now called Stone duality.

Statement

Every Boolean algebra B can be represented as a field of sets: there is a set X and an isomorphism from B onto a family of subsets of X containing the empty set and X and closed under union, intersection and complement. In Stone's construction, X is the set of ultrafilters of B, and each element b of B is mapped to the set of ultrafilters containing b.

Stone spaces

Equipping the set of ultrafilters with the topology generated by these sets makes it a compact, totally disconnected Hausdorff space, called a Stone space. The theorem can be strengthened into a duality: the category of Boolean algebras is dually equivalent to the category of Stone spaces, with each Boolean algebra corresponding to the algebra of clopen sets of its Stone space. This duality is the prototype for later dualities in logic and geometry.

Proof idea and consequences

The existence of enough ultrafilters relies on the Boolean prime ideal theorem, a weak form of the axiom of choice. The representation theorem underlies the topological semantics for classical logic and connects Boolean algebras to topology, measure theory, and the theory of Boolean rings, since Boolean algebras and Boolean rings are in natural bijection.
