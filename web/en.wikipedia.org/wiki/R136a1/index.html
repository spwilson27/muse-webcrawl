R136a1

R136a1
Constellation: Dorado
Location: R136 cluster, Tarantula Nebula, Large Magellanic Cloud
Spectral type: WN5h (Wolf–Rayet)
Mass: ~200–260 solar masses (estimates vary)
Luminosity: ~5–6 million solar luminosities

R136a1 is one of the most massive and most luminous stars known. It lies in the dense star cluster R136 at the centre of the Tarantula Nebula (30 Doradus) in the Large Magellanic Cloud, about 163,000 light-years from Earth. Classified as a hydrogen-rich Wolf–Rayet star of spectral type WN5h, R136a1 was for some years listed as the most massive star known, with an estimated initial mass of around 250–320 solar masses; more recent modelling has revised the present-day mass estimates downward to roughly 170–230 solar masses, though it remains at or near the top of the list.

Discovery and identification

R136 was once thought to be a single supermassive star thousands of times the mass of the Sun. Speckle interferometry in the 1980s resolved its core into multiple components, of which R136a1 is the brightest. Studies with the Hubble Space Telescope and the Very Large Telescope, notably a 2010 analysis led by Paul Crowther, established the extreme masses and luminosities of R136a1 and its neighbours R136a2, R136a3, and R136c.

Properties

R136a1 has a surface temperature of roughly 45,000–53,000 K and radiates several million times the Sun's luminosity, mostly in the ultraviolet. It loses mass rapidly through a powerful stellar wind and is only about a million years old. Its existence challenges theoretical limits on stellar mass — it may have formed through the merger of two stars — and it is expected to end its life in a supernova, possibly a pair-instability supernova, leaving no remnant or a black hole depending on its final mass.
