Lake County is a county located in the north-central portion of the U.S. state of California. As of the 2020 census, the population was 68,163. The county seat is Lakeport. The county takes its name from Clear Lake, the dominant geographic feature in the county and the largest natural freshwater lake wholly within the state of California.

Lake County forms the Clearlake, CA micropolitan statistical area. It is largely rural, ringed by the Mayacamas and other Coast Range mountains, and known for its lake recreation, agriculture — especially wine grapes, walnuts and pears — and geothermal energy production at The Geysers, the world's largest geothermal field, which straddles the Lake–Sonoma county line.

History

The area was the homeland of Pomo, Lake Miwok, Wappo and other Indigenous peoples for thousands of years; the Pomo communities around Clear Lake were among the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in California. Bloody Island massacre of 1850, in which US cavalry killed scores of Pomo people, remains a central event in local memory. Lake County was formed on May 20, 1861, from parts of Napa and Mendocino counties. In the late 19th century the shore towns flourished as resorts for San Francisco Bay Area visitors drawn by mineral springs.

Geography

Clear Lake, roughly 19 miles (31 km) long, dominates the county. To its east rises Mount Konocti, a dormant volcano whose slopes hold vineyards. The Cache Creek and Putah Creek watersheds drain the county southward. In the 2010s the county was repeatedly struck by severe wildfires, including the Valley Fire (2015), which destroyed much of Middletown and Cobb, the Clayton Fire (2016) and the Pawnee and River fires (2018).

Communities

The only incorporated cities are Clearlake and Lakeport. Unincorporated communities include Kelseyville, Middletown, Upper Lake, Nice, Lucerne, Cobb, Hidden Valley Lake and Lower Lake.

Economy and wine

The Lake County wine industry, devastated by Prohibition, has revived since the 1990s; the Red Hills Lake County AVA is noted for Cabernet Sauvignon grown in red volcanic soils at elevation. Pears — once the signature crop — and walnuts remain significant.
