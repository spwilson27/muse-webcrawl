Mulhouse

Mulhouse (French pronunciation: [myluz]; Alsatian: Milhüsa; German: Mülhausen) is a city in eastern France, in the Haut-Rhin department of the Grand Est region, close to the Swiss and German borders. It is the largest city in Haut-Rhin and the second largest in Alsace after Strasbourg.

Mulhouse developed as an industrial centre in the 19th century, particularly in textiles, printing and mechanical engineering. It became known as the "French Manchester" because of its cotton industry and printed fabrics.

History

Legend has it that the city was founded around a water mill. Mulhouse became a free imperial city and in 1515 joined the Swiss Confederation as an associate member, remaining closely tied to Switzerland until it voted to join France in 1798. Industrialisation in the 19th century transformed the city, drawing workers and building large textile mills.

Economy

The region's industries included textiles, chemicals and engineering. The Peugeot (now Stellantis) plant at nearby Sochaux-Montbéliard has been a major employer, and Mulhouse retains an important automotive and mechanical engineering sector.

Main sights

The city is home to major museums, including the Cité de l'Automobile, which houses the Schlumpf collection of classic cars, and the Cité du Train, one of the largest railway museums in the world. The historic centre includes the Place de la Réunion and the town hall, noted for its painted façade.
