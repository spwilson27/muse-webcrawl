Darshan (Indian religions)

Darshan (Sanskrit: दर्शन, Darśana; from the root dṛś, "to see") is a term in Indian religions meaning the auspicious sight of a deity, a sacred person or a holy place. It denotes both seeing and being seen by the divine, a reciprocal act in which the worshipper beholds the image of a god or guru and receives their blessing in return.

Practice

In Hindu temple worship, devotees queue to have darshan of the murti (image) of the deity, which is ritually awakened, bathed, dressed and presented to the congregation; the opening of the deity's eyes and the waving of lamps (aarti) are central moments. Darshan is likewise sought from saints and gurus, and pilgrims travel to holy sites such as Varanasi or great temples for the darshan of the presiding deity. The concept also features in Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism.

Philosophical usage

In Indian philosophy, darshana also refers to a "viewpoint" or system of thought: the six orthodox (astika) schools of Hindu philosophy — Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa and Vedanta — are collectively known as the Shad-darshana. The word thus spans both vision of the divine and systematic philosophical insight.
