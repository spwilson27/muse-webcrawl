The Upper Galilee (Hebrew: HaGalil Ha'Elyon) is a mountainous region in northern Israel, the northern and higher part of the Galilee. It is bounded by the Mediterranean coastal plain to the west, the Beit HaKerem valley and Lower Galilee to the south, the Jordan Rift Valley and Hula Valley to the east, and the Litani River in Lebanon to the north.

Geography

The Upper Galilee is a rugged highland of limestone ridges and deep valleys, rising to Mount Meron at 1,208 metres (3,963 ft), the highest point in Israel within the pre-1967 borders. It receives the highest rainfall in the country, supporting Mediterranean woodland, nature reserves and the headwater streams of the Jordan River system around the Hula basin.

History

The region was part of the territory associated in the Hebrew Bible with the tribes of Naphtali and Asher, and Safed later became one of the four holy cities of Judaism and a centre of Kabbalah in the 16th century. Control of the area was contested in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War; Israeli operations Hiram and Yoav brought the Upper Galilee, including Safed and its surrounding Arab villages, under Israeli control, and most Arab inhabitants fled or were expelled. The area has remained near the Lebanese border through subsequent conflicts, including the 2006 Lebanon War.

Communities

The population includes Jewish towns and kibbutzim, Arab towns and villages, Druze communities such as Beit Jann and Hurfeish, and Circassian villages. Attractions include the old city and artists' quarter of Safed, the Mount Meron reserve, the Banias and Dan stream reserves, and the Hula Valley birdwatching wetlands.
