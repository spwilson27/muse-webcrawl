MIM-104 Patriot

TypeMobile surface-to-air missile and anti-ballistic missile system
Place of originUnited States
ManufacturerRaytheon
In service1981–present

The MIM-104 Patriot is a surface-to-air missile (SAM) system, the primary system of its kind in use by the United States Army and several allied nations. Manufactured by Raytheon, its name derives from its AN/MPQ-53/65 radar component: "Phased Array Tracking Radar to Intercept on Target". It replaced the Nike Hercules as the US Army's primary high-to-medium air defense system and the MIM-23 Hawk as its medium tactical air defense system.

Components

A Patriot battery consists of a phased-array radar set, an engagement control station, a power plant, and up to eight launchers, each carrying four PAC-2 missiles or up to sixteen smaller PAC-3 interceptors. The radar performs surveillance, target detection, tracking, and missile guidance simultaneously. PAC-3 uses a hit-to-kill kinetic warhead with its own active radar seeker for ballistic missile defense.

Operational history

The Patriot first saw combat in the 1991 Gulf War, where it was tasked with intercepting Iraqi Scud ballistic missiles fired at Israel and Saudi Arabia; the effectiveness of those intercepts was later the subject of considerable debate. Upgraded systems were used again in the 2003 invasion of Iraq. Patriot batteries have since been exported widely, including to Germany, Japan, Israel, Saudi Arabia, and South Korea, and were supplied to Ukraine from 2023 to defend against Russian missile and drone attacks.
