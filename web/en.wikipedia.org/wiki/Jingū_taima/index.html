Jingū taima

A jingū taima (神宮大麻) is an ofuda — a talisman or amulet — issued by the Ise Grand Shrine (the Jingū) in Mie Prefecture, Japan. It is the most widely distributed ofuda in the country: millions are obtained each year by households and businesses and enshrined in the kamidana (household Shinto altar) as an object of worship of the shrine's principal deity, the sun goddess Amaterasu Ōmikami.

Description and distribution

The taima traditionally consists of a thin strip of cedar wood, nowadays commonly a paper card, inscribed with the name of the Ise shrine and sealed, and supplied in a paper envelope. Until the early Meiji period they were distributed nationwide by hereditary confraternities of Ise proselytisers known as onshi; after the Meiji Restoration the distribution was reorganised, and today the amulets reach households through local parish shrines (ujiko shrines) affiliated with the Association of Shinto Shrines (Jinja Honcho), typically at New Year. The jingū taima is placed in the centre of the kamidana, flanked by ofuda of the local tutelary shrine and of other revered shrines.

Significance

Because Ise is the ancestral shrine of the Imperial House and the cult centre of Amaterasu, the jingū taima has historically been promoted as a universal object of devotion for all Japanese households, transcending local parish ties. In the prewar period its distribution was bound up with State Shinto; after 1945 its promotion passed to the voluntary Jinja Honcho, and the number distributed became a commonly cited index of popular Shinto practice.
