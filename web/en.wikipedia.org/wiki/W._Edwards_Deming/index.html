William Edwards Deming (14 October 1900 – 20 December 1993) was an American business theorist, composer, economist, engineer, lecturer, and management consultant best known for his work in Japan after the Second World War, where his teachings on quality, design and statistical process control profoundly influenced Japanese manufacturing and helped fuel the postwar Japanese economic miracle.

Career

Educated in electrical engineering and mathematical physics (PhD, Yale, 1928), Deming worked for the U.S. Department of Agriculture and Census Bureau, where he applied sampling methods to the 1940 census, and studied under Walter Shewhart, the originator of statistical quality control. Invited to Japan by the occupation authorities, he lectured in 1950 to the Union of Japanese Scientists and Engineers (JUSE); the Deming Prize was founded in 1951 in his honour.

Philosophy

Deming's ideas are summarised in his books Out of the Crisis (1982) and The New Economics (1993): the "14 Points for Management", the Plan–Do–Check–Act (Deming) cycle, and the System of Profound Knowledge. He argued that most quality problems are caused by the system, not workers, and condemned management by numerical targets, slogans and annual ratings. He was largely overlooked in the United States until a 1980 television documentary, "If Japan Can... Why Can't We?", made him famous at home in his eighties.
