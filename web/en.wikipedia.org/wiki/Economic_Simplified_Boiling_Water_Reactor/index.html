The Economic Simplified Boiling Water Reactor (ESBWR) is a Generation III+ nuclear reactor design developed by GE Hitachi Nuclear Energy, derived from the earlier Simplified Boiling Water Reactor (SBWR) and the Advanced Boiling Water Reactor (ABWR). It produces about 1,520 megawatts of electricity and is distinguished by relying entirely on natural circulation for core cooling during normal operation, eliminating the recirculation pumps used in earlier BWRs.

Design and passive safety

The ESBWR's safety philosophy is passive: in an accident, core cooling is provided by gravity-driven water from large pools above the reactor (the gravity-driven cooling system), an isolation condenser system that removes decay heat, and a passive containment cooling system, all operating without AC electrical power or operator action for at least 72 hours. A tall chimney above the core promotes the natural-circulation flow, and the design simplifies or eliminates many active components, which the vendor argued would reduce both construction cost and core-damage frequency (estimated at around 10−8 per year in design analyses).

Licensing history

GE Hitachi applied for U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission design certification in 2005; the NRC issued the final design certification rule in 2014. The design was selected for projects such as North Anna Unit 3 in Virginia and Fermi Unit 3 in Michigan, which received combined licences, but construction did not proceed amid low natural-gas prices and changing utility plans. The ESBWR remains an unbuilt but fully certified design.
