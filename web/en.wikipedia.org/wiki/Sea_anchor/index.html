Sea anchor

A sea anchor (also called a drift anchor, drift sock, parachute anchor or boat brake) is a device that is streamed from a boat in heavy weather. Its purpose is to stabilize the vessel and to limit progress through the water. Rather than tethering the boat to the seabed like a conventional anchor, a sea anchor provides hydrodynamic drag, thereby acting as a brake.

Entirely submerged, a sea anchor provides little trouble and less strain on the boat than alternative storm tactics such as heaving to or running before the storm. It keeps the vessel's bow (or stern) pointed into the waves and slows its drift.

Design

Sea anchors are usually cone- or parachute-shaped fabric devices with an opening at the narrow end, attached by a long rode to the bow. Modern designs include large-diameter parachute anchors for offshore storm use. A drogue is a related but smaller device, typically streamed from the stern to slow a vessel running downwind and prevent broaching or pitchpoling.

Uses

Beyond storm survival, sea anchors are used by fishing vessels to hold station over fishing grounds, by sailors to rest (a form of "parking" at sea), and in drogue form by rowers and kayakers.
