The Kennel Club

The Kennel Club is the official kennel club of the United Kingdom and the oldest recognised kennel club in the world. Founded on 4 April 1873 by Sewallis Shirley and others, it maintains the UK's national register of pedigree dogs, licenses dog shows and trials, and runs Crufts, the world's largest dog show.

History and role

The Club was formed after the first organised dog shows of 1859–1873 exposed the need for consistent rules and a stud book; its first stud book appeared in 1874. It recognises over 200 breeds grouped into seven groups — Hound, Gundog, Terrier, Utility, Working, Pastoral and Toy — and sets breed standards in the UK.

Activities and controversy

Beyond showing, it runs the Good Citizen Dog Scheme, agility, obedience and working trials, the Assured Breeders scheme, and health-testing initiatives. The 2008 BBC documentary Pedigree Dogs Exposed criticised breed standards said to compromise health, prompting reforms, revised standards and the loss of BBC coverage of Crufts.
