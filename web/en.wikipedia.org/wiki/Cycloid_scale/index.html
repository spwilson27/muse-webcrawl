Cycloid scale

A cycloid scale is a type of fish scale with a smooth, rounded posterior edge. Cycloid scales are found on soft-rayed bony fishes such as salmon, trout, carp, and herring.

Structure

The scales are roughly circular, thin, and flexible, overlapping like shingles to protect the fish while allowing movement. They grow by adding concentric rings (circuli) around a central focus; counting these rings, and the wider-spaced annuli formed seasonally, allows biologists to estimate a fish's age.

Comparison

Cycloid scales contrast with ctenoid scales, which bear tiny comb-like spines (ctenii) on the exposed edge and occur on spiny-rayed fishes such as perch and bass. Both are types of elasmoid scales, distinct from the placoid scales of sharks and the ganoid scales of gars.
