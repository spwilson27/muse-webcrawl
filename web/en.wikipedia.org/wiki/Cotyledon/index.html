Cotyledon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A cotyledon (from Greek kotylēdṓn, "cup-shaped cavity") is a significant part of the embryo within the seed of a plant, defined as the embryonic leaf that is the first to appear from a germinating seed. The number of cotyledons present is a key characteristic used to classify flowering plants: monocotyledons have one, dicotyledons have two.

Function

Cotyledons store food reserves in many species (for example in beans and peas) and nourish the seedling until its true leaves can photosynthesise. In epigeal germination the cotyledons are raised above ground and may themselves turn green and photosynthesise; in hypogeal germination they remain below ground within the seed coat.

Other seed plants

Gymnosperms vary widely: conifer seedlings may bear numerous cotyledons, sometimes more than a dozen, a condition called polycotyledony.
