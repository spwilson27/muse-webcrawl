Cosmic Calendar

[Image: Cosmic Calendar]

The Cosmic Calendar is a method to visualize the chronology of the universe, scaling its current age of 13.8 billion years to a single year in order to help intuit it for pedagogical purposes in science education or popular science.

Origin

The concept was popularised by the astronomer Carl Sagan in his 1977 book The Dragons of Eden and in the 1980 television series Cosmos: A Personal Voyage. Neil deGrasse Tyson presented an updated version in the 2014 series Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey.

The scale

On this scale, the Big Bang occurs at midnight on January 1, and the present moment is the end of December 31. Each month represents about 1.15 billion years, each day about 38 million years, and each second about 438 years.

  • The Milky Way forms around May 12.
  • The Solar System forms around September 2.
  • The earliest life on Earth appears around September 21.
  • Multicellular organisms evolve in mid-November.
  • Dinosaurs dominate in late December and go extinct around December 30.
  • All of recorded human history occupies roughly the last 14 seconds of the year.

Purpose

The calendar illustrates the brevity of human existence against cosmic time, a theme Sagan described as giving a " cosmic perspective" on humanity's place in the universe.
