Niagara Gorge

The Niagara Gorge is an 11-kilometre (6.8 mi) gorge carved by the Niagara River along the Canada–United States border, between the cities of Niagara Falls, Ontario and Niagara Falls, New York at its upstream end and Queenston-Lewiston at its downstream end. It was formed as Niagara Falls eroded upstream through the resistant dolostone of the Niagara Escarpment.

Formation

The gorge began forming at the end of the last ice age, about 12,000 years ago, when the falls stood at the escarpment edge near Queenston. The plunge pool under the falls undercut the soft shale beneath the hard Lockport dolostone caprock, causing repeated collapses that have driven the falls upstream at an average historical rate of roughly 1 metre per year — reduced in the 20th century by flow diversions for hydroelectric power. The resulting canyon is up to about 90 metres deep.

Features

The gorge contains the Niagara River's powerful lower rapids, including the Class VI Whirlpool Rapids and the Niagara Whirlpool where the river makes a sharp turn. It is crossed by the Whirlpool Rapids Bridge, the Rainbow Bridge near the falls, and the Lewiston–Queenston Bridge. Trails on the Ontario side form part of the Niagara Glen and Niagara River Recreation Trail; jet-boat tours operate on the river below the rapids.
