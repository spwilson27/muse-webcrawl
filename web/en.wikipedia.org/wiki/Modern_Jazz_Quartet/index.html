The Modern Jazz Quartet (MJQ) was an American jazz group established in 1952. Its classic lineup consisted of John Lewis (piano), Milt Jackson (vibraphone), Percy Heath (double bass) and Connie Kay (drums). The group grew out of the rhythm section of Dizzy Gillespie's big band, in which the four musicians first played together between sets.

The MJQ became famous for its elegant, understated style, blending bebop with classical music forms such as the fugue, and for its members' formal concert dress, which helped present jazz as a concert music. Lewis, the group's musical director, composed many of its best-known pieces, including "Django" (written in memory of guitarist Django Reinhardt), "Vendome" and the score for the film Odds Against Tomorrow.

History

Originally the Milt Jackson Quartet, the group became the Modern Jazz Quartet in 1952, with Kenny Clarke as its first drummer; Connie Kay replaced Clarke in 1955. The quartet recorded extensively for Prestige and later Atlantic Records, releasing landmark albums such as Django (1956), Fontessa (1956), Pyramid (1960) and The Comedy (1962). The group also played a central role in the "third stream" movement, which sought to fuse jazz and classical music, and Lewis co-founded the Lenox School of Jazz.

The MJQ disbanded in 1974, partly because Jackson wished to pursue a looser solo career, but reunited in 1981 and performed, at a reduced pace, until Kay's death in 1994; Albert "Tootie" Heath briefly took over on drums. The group's final performance was in 1997.

Legacy

The quartet is regarded as one of the most influential small groups in jazz history, noted for its longevity with a stable personnel, its chamber-music refinement, and compositions that entered the jazz standard repertoire.
