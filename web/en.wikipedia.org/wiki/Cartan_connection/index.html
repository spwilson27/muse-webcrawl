Cartan connection

In mathematics, a Cartan connection is a flexible generalisation of the notion of an affine connection, introduced by Élie Cartan in the 1920s. It models a manifold on a homogeneous space — a Klein geometry — rather than on affine space, and encodes the geometry in a Lie-algebra-valued one-form on a principal bundle.

Overview

A Cartan geometry consists of a principal bundle with structure group H and a one-form, the Cartan connection, taking values in the Lie algebra of a larger group G containing H, which identifies each tangent space of the manifold with the model tangent space G/H. Affine, projective, conformal and Riemannian geometries can all be expressed in this framework, and the curvature of the connection measures the deviation of the geometry from its flat model.
