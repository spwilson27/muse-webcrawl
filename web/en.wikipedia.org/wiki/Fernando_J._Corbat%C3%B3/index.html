Died12 July 2019, Newburyport, Massachusetts

Fernando José Corbató (July 1, 1926 – July 12, 2019) was an American computer scientist, notable as a pioneer in the development of time-sharing operating systems. At MIT he led the Compatible Time-Sharing System (CTSS), first demonstrated in 1961, and the subsequent Multics project, which influenced Unix and modern operating-system design.

Career

Corbató served in the US Navy during the Second World War, studied physics at Caltech and MIT, and joined MIT's Computation Center in 1950, later becoming a professor of electrical engineering and computer science. His CTSS introduced concepts now taken for granted: multiple users sharing one machine interactively, password-based access — he is credited with inventing the computer password — and files organised in personal directories.

Recognition

He received the ACM Turing Award in 1990 "for his pioneering work in organizing the concepts and leading the development of the general-purpose, large-scale, time-sharing and resource-sharing computer systems." "Corbató's law" is a wry observation attributed to him: the number of lines of code a programmer can write in a fixed time is the same regardless of the language used.
