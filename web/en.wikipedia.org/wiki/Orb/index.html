Orb

An orb is a spherical object, and in ceremonial usage most notably the globus cruciger (Latin for "cross-bearing orb"), a sphere surmounted by a cross that forms part of the regalia of monarchs in Christian Europe. The orb symbolizes the world (the globus) held under the dominion of Christ (the cross) and, by extension, the monarch's God-given authority over the earthly realm. More generally, "orb" can refer to any ball or sphere, to an eye (in poetic usage), or to a celestial body.

History

Globes as symbols of universal rule appeared in Roman iconography, where emperors were shown holding a sphere representing the cosmos or the world. By the fifth century, a cross was added atop the globe on the coinage of Emperor Theodosius II, creating the globus cruciger. Medieval and early modern European monarchs adopted it as a central piece of coronation regalia, paired with the sceptre.

The Sovereign's Orb in the British Crown Jewels, made in 1661 for Charles II, is a hollow gold sphere encircled with jewelled bands and surmounted by a cross; during the coronation the Archbishop of Canterbury places it in the monarch's right hand as a symbol that the monarch's power is derived from God. The Imperial Orb of the Holy Roman Empire, now in the Imperial Treasury in Vienna, dates to around 1200.

Other meanings

In astronomy and astrology, an "orb" is the sphere of influence or allowance within which an aspect between planets is considered effective. In photography, "orbs" are circular artifacts caused by backscatter of a camera flash from dust or moisture near the lens, sometimes claimed in paranormal contexts to be spirits. Poetically, "orb" denotes the eye or a heavenly body such as the sun or moon.
