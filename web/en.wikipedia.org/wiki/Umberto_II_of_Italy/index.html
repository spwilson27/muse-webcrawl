Umberto II (Italian: Umberto Nicola Tommaso Giovanni Maria di Savoia; 15 September 1904 – 18 March 1983) was the last King of Italy, reigning for just 34 days, from 9 May 1946 until the monarchy was abolished following the institutional referendum of 2 June 1946. He is consequently known in Italy as the Re di Maggio, the "King of May". Not to be confused with the Futurist artist Umberto Boccioni.

Umberto II as Lieutenant General of the Realm, 1944.

Early life

The only son of Victor Emmanuel III and Elena of Montenegro, Umberto was born at Racconigi Castle in Piedmont and held the title Prince of Piedmont from birth. He received a military education and in 1930 married Princess Marie-José of Belgium, daughter of King Albert I; they had four children, including Vittorio Emanuele, Prince of Naples.

Lieutenant General and king

After the fall of Mussolini and Italy's armistice with the Allies, Victor Emmanuel III transferred his powers to Umberto as Lieutenant General of the Realm in June 1944, in an attempt to save the monarchy from its association with Fascism. On 9 May 1946 Victor Emmanuel abdicated outright, and Umberto became king.

Referendum and exile

In the referendum of 2 June 1946, 54 per cent of voters chose a republic over the monarchy. Umberto accepted the result — despite monarchist claims of irregularities — in order to avoid civil war, and left Italy on 13 June 1946. The 1948 republican constitution barred male members of the House of Savoy from Italian soil, a provision repealed only in 2002. Umberto lived in exile at Cascais, Portugal, and died in Geneva in 1983; he is buried at Hautecombe Abbey in Savoy, France.
