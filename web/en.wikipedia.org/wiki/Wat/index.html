Wat

A wat (Thai: วัด; Lao: ວັດ; Khmer: វត្ត) is a type of Buddhist temple complex in Thailand, Cambodia, Laos and neighbouring regions of Southeast Asia. The word is derived from the Pali vāṭa or Sanskrit vāṭa, meaning an enclosure.

Components

A typical wat comprises several buildings within a walled compound: a bot (ordination hall), a wihan (assembly or sermon hall housing Buddha images), a chedi or prang (stupa), a library (ho trai) for scriptures, monks' living quarters (kuti), and often a crematorium and a school. Famous examples include Wat Phra Kaew and Wat Pho in Bangkok, Wat Xieng Thong in Luang Prabang, and the temple-mountain of Angkor Wat in Cambodia.

Social role

Wats serve not only as monasteries and places of worship but as schools, community halls, festival grounds and repositories of art and learning. In Thailand there are more than 40,000 wats, and almost every village has one; temporary ordination at a wat remains a common rite of passage for young men.
