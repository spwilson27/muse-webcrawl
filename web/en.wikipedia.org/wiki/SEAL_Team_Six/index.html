The United States Naval Special Warfare Development Group (NSWDG), commonly known as DEVGRU (from "Development Group") and unofficially by its former name SEAL Team Six, is the US Navy component of the Joint Special Operations Command (JSOC). It is one of the United States' principal special mission units, alongside the Army's Delta Force, specialising in counterterrorism, hostage rescue, special reconnaissance and high-value target operations.

History

SEAL Team Six was founded in November 1980 in the aftermath of the failed Operation Eagle Claw rescue of American hostages in Iran. Its first commander, Lieutenant Commander Richard Marcinko, was given six months to make the unit operational; the name "Six" was chosen to confuse Soviet intelligence about how many SEAL teams existed (there were then only two). In 1987 the unit was disbanded under its original name and re-established as the Naval Special Warfare Development Group.

DEVGRU operators participated in operations in Grenada, the Achille Lauro affair, Panama, the Balkans, Afghanistan and Iraq. On 2 May 2011, DEVGRU operators conducted Operation Neptune Spear, the raid on Osama bin Laden's compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan, killing the al-Qaeda leader. In August 2011 a helicopter carrying 15 DEVGRU operators was shot down in Afghanistan (call sign Extortion 17), the deadliest single incident for US forces in that war.

Selection and organisation

Candidates are drawn from existing SEAL teams and Navy explosive ordnance disposal units and undergo an extended assessment and training pipeline at Dam Neck, Virginia, including close-quarters battle and freefall parachuting. The group is organised into colour-coded assault squadrons (reported as Red, Blue, Gold and Silver) plus support and reconnaissance elements. Its budget, size and operations remain classified, though press accounts put its strength at roughly 1,800 personnel including support staff.
