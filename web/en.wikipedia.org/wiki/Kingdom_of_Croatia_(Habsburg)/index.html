Kingdom of Croatia (Habsburg)

Years: 1527 – 1868

Capital: Zagreb (Agram)

Monarch: Habsburg kings of Hungary-Croatia

Succeeded by: Kingdom of Croatia-Slavonia

The Kingdom of Croatia (Croatian: Kraljevina Hrvatska; Latin: Regnum Croatiae) was an administrative division of the Habsburg monarchy from 1527 to 1868. After the death of King Louis II at the Battle of Mohács (1526), the Croatian parliament (Sabor) elected Ferdinand I of Habsburg as king at Cetin in January 1527, in return for protection against the advancing Ottoman Empire.

The Ottoman wars

Over the following century most of Croatian territory was conquered by the Ottomans or organised into the Military Frontier (Vojna Krajina) administered directly from Vienna. The rump kingdom — the "remnants of the remnants" (reliquiae reliquiarum) in Croatian memory — comprised the Zagreb region and the northwest. Croatian forces won a notable victory at the Battle of Sisak in 1593, and Croatian nobles such as Ban Nikola Zrinski were celebrated throughout Europe.

Later Habsburg rule

Following the reconquest of Slavonia in the Great Turkish War (Treaty of Karlowitz, 1699), the kingdom gradually recovered territory, though the Military Frontier remained separate until 1881. In the nineteenth century the Croatian national revival, led by Ljudevit Gaj, asserted Croatian state rights within the monarchy. Under the Croatian–Hungarian Settlement (Nagodba) of 1868, Croatia was united with Slavonia as the autonomous Kingdom of Croatia-Slavonia within the Lands of the Crown of Saint Stephen, ending the Habsburg Kingdom of Croatia as a separate unit.
