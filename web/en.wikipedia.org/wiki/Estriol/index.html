Estriol

IUPAC nameEstra-1,3,5(10)-triene-3,16α,17β-triol
FormulaC18H24O3
Molar mass288.38 g/mol
Other namesE3; 16α-hydroxyestradiol

Estriol (E3), also spelled oestriol, is a steroid, a weak estrogen, and a minor female sex hormone. It is one of three major endogenous estrogens, the other two being estradiol (E2) and estrone (E1). Estriol is produced almost exclusively during pregnancy, when it is made in large quantities by the placenta from 16-hydroxydehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (16-OH DHEA-S), an androgen steroid made in the fetal liver and adrenal glands.

Biological role

In non-pregnant women, estriol levels are very low, comparable to those in men. During pregnancy, urinary and blood estriol levels rise roughly a thousand-fold, and estriol becomes the dominant estrogen produced. Because its synthesis depends on the fetal adrenal glands, liver and placenta acting together as the "fetoplacental unit", measuring maternal estriol was historically used to monitor fetal well-being; unconjugated estriol (uE3) remains one of the markers in second-trimester prenatal screening for chromosomal abnormalities such as Down syndrome.

Pharmacology

Estriol is a weak estrogen compared with estradiol, acting as an agonist of the estrogen receptors ERα and ERβ with a preference for ERβ. It is available as a medication — including oral tablets and vaginal creams and pessaries — used in menopausal hormone therapy, particularly for vaginal atrophy and urinary symptoms. Because of its short duration of action and preferential vaginal effects when given locally, it is widely used in Europe and Asia, though it has never been marketed as a pharmaceutical preparation in the United States.

Chemistry

Estriol, also known as 16α-hydroxyestradiol or estra-1,3,5(10)-triene-3,16α,17β-triol, is a naturally occurring estrane steroid with hydroxyl groups at the C3, C16α and C17β positions. It differs from estradiol by the additional hydroxyl group at C16.
