Norman Robert Foster, Baron Foster of Thames Bank (born 1 June 1935) is an English architect and designer. Closely associated with the development of high-tech architecture, Foster is recognised as a key figure in British modernist architecture. His architectural practice, Foster + Partners, founded in London in 1967, is one of the largest and most influential in the world.

Early life and career

Foster was born in Reddish, Stockport, and raised in Manchester in a working-class family. He studied architecture at the University of Manchester and later at Yale University, where he met Richard Rogers. Returning to Britain, he co-founded Team 4 in 1963 with Rogers and others, before founding Foster Associates (later Foster + Partners) in 1967.

Major works

Foster's landmark buildings include the Willis Building in Ipswich, the Sainsbury Centre for Visual Arts in Norwich, the HSBC Main Building in Hong Kong, the renovated Reichstag in Berlin with its glass dome, the Millau Viaduct in France (with Michel Virlogeux), 30 St Mary Axe ("The Gherkin") in London, Hearst Tower in New York, and the ring-shaped Apple Park campus in Cupertino, California. His practice also designed Beijing Capital International Airport's Terminal 3, then the world's largest airport terminal.

Honours

Foster won the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 1999 and the Stirling Prize twice (for the Imperial War Museum Duxford's American Air Museum and for 30 St Mary Axe, and for the Bloomberg London headquarters). He was knighted in 1990 and made a life peer in 1999 as Baron Foster of Thames Bank. He was the second British architect to win both the Stirling and Pritzker prizes.
