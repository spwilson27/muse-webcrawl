Jan Alojzy Matejko (24 June 1838 – 1 November 1893) was a Polish painter, a leading 19th-century exponent of history painting, known for portraying notable historical, political, and military events. His works include large-scale oil paintings such as Rejtan (1866), Union of Lublin (1869), Astronomer Copernicus, or Conversations with God (1873), and Battle of Grunwald (1878). He is regarded as the most famous Polish painter and the creator of the national school of historical painting.

Born in the Free City of Kraków during the partitions of Poland, Matejko treated painting as a form of patriotic service: his canvases taught Polish history to a nation without a state, and often embedded sharp political commentary on the causes of Poland's downfall.

Life

Matejko was the ninth of eleven children of a Czech-born music teacher. He survived the Kraków uprising of 1846 and the Austrian bombardment of the city in 1848. He studied at the School of Fine Arts in Kraków, then in Munich and Vienna, returning to Kraków in 1860. His melancholy Stańczyk (1862), showing the court jester alone with news of the loss of Smolensk amid a ball, established his reputation while he was still in his twenties.

He became director of the Kraków School of Fine Arts in 1873 and held the post until his death, refusing more lucrative offers abroad. His pupils included many of the next generation's leading Polish artists, among them Stanisław Wyspiański, Jacek Malczewski, and Józef Mehoffer. Matejko also worked to preserve Kraków's monuments and designed the polychrome decoration of St Mary's Basilica.

Major works

  • Skarga's Sermon (1864)
  • Rejtan — The Fall of Poland (1866)

  • Astronomer Copernicus, or Conversations with God (1873)

  • Prussian Homage (1882)
  • Kościuszko at Racławice (1888)
  • The cycle History of Civilization in Poland (1888) and the portrait series of Polish kings, whose images remain the standard popular likenesses of Poland's monarchs

Legacy

Matejko's Kraków house is now the Jan Matejko House museum, a branch of the National Museum in Kraków, and the Academy of Fine Arts in Kraków bears his name. His interpretations — visually researched in costume and artifact, though sometimes deliberately anachronistic for moral effect — fixed the imagery of Polish history in the national imagination. In 2022 his astronomer Copernicus painting was a centrepiece of exhibitions marking the 550th anniversary of Copernicus's birth.
