In mathematics, the rank of an abelian group A is a measure of its "free" part: it is the cardinality of a maximal linearly independent subset of A, or equivalently the dimension of the rational vector space A ⊗ ℚ obtained by tensoring with the rational numbers. For a finitely generated abelian group, the rank is the number of infinite cyclic summands in its decomposition into a free part and a finite torsion part.

Definition and properties

A subset of A is independent if the only integer linear combination of its elements equal to zero is the trivial one. All maximal independent subsets of an abelian group have the same cardinality, so the rank is well defined. The rank of A equals the rank of its maximal free subgroup and equals the rank of the quotient of A by its torsion subgroup. For the group ℤn the rank is n, while any finite abelian group has rank 0.

Generalizations

The notion extends to modules over a principal ideal domain and to the rank of a group more generally. Related invariants include the Prüfer rank and the torsion-free rank used in the theory of infinite abelian groups. In algebraic geometry and number theory, the rank of the Mordell–Weil group of an elliptic curve over a number field — the number of independent rational points of infinite order — is a central and deeply studied invariant tied to the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.
