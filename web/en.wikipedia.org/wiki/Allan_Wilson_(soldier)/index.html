Allan Wilson (1856 – 4 December 1893) was a British soldier who led the Shangani Patrol in the First Matabele War in Rhodesia. His last stand, in which he and his 33 men were killed by Ndebele warriors of King Lobengula, became one of the best-known legends of colonial Rhodesia, often compared to Custer's Last Stand.

Career

Wilson emigrated to southern Africa and served with the Cape Mounted Riflemen and in the Basutoland Gun War before joining the forces of the British South Africa Company. He took part in the invasion of Matabeleland in 1893, commanding the Victoria Column's scouts under Major Patrick Forbes.

The Shangani Patrol

In December 1893 Wilson led a small patrol across the Shangani River in pursuit of Lobengula, who was fleeing north after the fall of Bulawayo. Cut off when the river rose behind them, Wilson's party was overwhelmed on 4 December 1893. According to legend, the wounded men sang "God Save the Queen" as they died, and the Ndebele reportedly admired their bravery. No one in the patrol survived; three American scouts, including Frederick Russell Burnham, had been sent back for reinforcements and escaped.

Legacy

Wilson and his men were reburied at World's View in the Matobo Hills near Cecil Rhodes's grave. The story was retold in books, poems, and the 1968 film Shangani Patrol. Since Zimbabwean independence in 1980 the episode has been reassessed within a broader history of Ndebele resistance to colonisation.
