The Type 10 (10式戦車, Hitomaru-shiki sensha) is a fourth-generation main battle tank manufactured by Mitsubishi Heavy Industries for the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force (JGSDF). It entered service in 2012 as a successor to the Type 74 and a lighter complement to the Type 90, designed under the TK-X programme. Its development emphasised advanced network-centric command and control (C4I), modular armour, and a reduced weight that allows it to use a much larger share of Japan's roads and bridges than the heavier Type 90.

Design

Weighing about 44 tonnes in standard configuration — with modular armour packages allowing weights up to about 48 tonnes — the Type 10 is considerably lighter than Western contemporaries such as the Leopard 2 or M1 Abrams. Sloped modular ceramic/composite armour can be removed for transport and maintenance. Its main armament is a domestically developed 120 mm smoothbore gun with an autoloader in the turret bustle, which reduces the crew to three and fires standard NATO-compatible ammunition as well as a domestic APFSDS round. Secondary armament consists of a coaxial 7.62 mm machine gun and a roof-mounted 12.7 mm machine gun.

Mobility and systems

A 1,200 hp V8 diesel engine with a continuously variable transmission (the first CVT in a production tank) gives strong acceleration and the unusual ability to drive as fast in reverse (about 70 km/h) as forwards. Hydropneumatic active suspension lets the hull tilt fore, aft, and sideways for firing positions. The tank's C4I system links tanks into a network to share target information in real time, and it carries a 360-degree warning and situational-awareness sensor suite.

Service

The Type 10 is built at a slow rate for the JGSDF only — Japan's arms-export rules long precluded foreign sales — with a fleet goal on the order of 300 vehicles, prioritised to units in Honshu and Kyushu as part of Japan's shift toward defending its southwestern islands.
