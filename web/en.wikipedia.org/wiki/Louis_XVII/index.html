Louis XVII

Louis XVII (born Louis Charles, Duke of Normandy; 27 March 1785 – 8 June 1795) was the younger son of King Louis XVI of France and Queen Marie Antoinette. As the heir after the death of his elder brother in 1789, he became Dauphin of France. When his father was executed on 21 January 1793 during the French Revolution, royalists proclaimed the seven-year-old as King Louis XVII, though he was a prisoner of the Revolutionary government and never reigned. He died in the Temple prison in Paris two years later, aged ten.

Early life

Louis Charles was born at Versailles on 27 March 1785, the second son of Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette, and was created Duke of Normandy at birth. His elder brother, the dauphin Louis Joseph, died in June 1789 as the Revolution began, making Louis Charles heir to the throne. After the October Days of 1789 the royal family was brought to Paris and housed in the Tuileries Palace.

Imprisonment in the Temple

After the insurrection of 10 August 1792 overthrew the monarchy, the family was imprisoned in the medieval Temple tower. In January 1793 Louis XVI was guillotined; royalists and foreign courts recognised his son as Louis XVII. In July 1793 the child was separated from his mother and placed in the custody of the cobbler Antoine Simon, who was charged with re-educating him as a revolutionary citizen. Marie Antoinette was executed in October 1793. After Simon's departure in January 1794, Louis Charles was kept in solitary confinement in a cell in the Temple for months, neglected and in deteriorating health.

Death

By 1795 the boy was gravely ill, suffering from scrofula (tuberculous infection of the lymph nodes) and the effects of prolonged neglect. He died in the Temple on 8 June 1795, aged ten. An autopsy was performed, and the physician Philippe-Jean Pelletan secretly removed and preserved the child's heart. The body was buried in an unmarked grave in the Sainte Marguerite cemetery.

Survival legends and the heart

For decades, rumours circulated that the dauphin had escaped and that another child had died in his place. Dozens of pretenders claimed to be Louis XVII, most famously Karl Wilhelm Naundorff. In 2000, DNA testing compared material from the preserved heart with hair samples from Marie Antoinette and her relatives; the results confirmed that the heart belonged to a child of the Habsburg maternal line, strongly supporting that it is the heart of Louis XVII. The heart was interred at the Basilica of Saint-Denis in June 2004.
