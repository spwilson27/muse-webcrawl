Gernikako Arbola

The Tree of Gernika

Gernikako Arbola, "the tree of Gernika" in Basque, is an oak tree in the town of Gernika, Biscay, in the Basque Country of northern Spain. It symbolises the traditional freedoms (fueros) of the Biscayan people and, by extension, of all Basques. The lords of Biscay, and later the kings of Castile and Spain, swore oaths beneath the tree to respect Biscay's liberties, and the assemblies of Biscay met in its shade.

History

A succession of trees has occupied the site since the 14th century; when one dies it is replaced by an acorn-grown successor from the previous tree. The "Old Tree" trunk, dating from 1742, is preserved in a pavilion, while the current tree, planted in 2015, stands before the Casa de Juntas (Assembly House), where the General Assemblies of Biscay still meet. The Lehendakari (Basque president) takes the oath of office at the tree, using a formula invoking God and the old laws.

Song

Gernikako Arbola is also the title of a famous Basque anthem composed by the bard José María Iparraguirre in 1853, celebrating the tree as a blessed symbol of liberty. The town survived into history also for the bombing of 1937, from which the tree itself emerged unharmed.
