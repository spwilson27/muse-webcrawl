Forest Brothers

Active: 1940–1957
Region: Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania
Opponents: Soviet Union (NKVD/MGB)
Strength: Tens of thousands of partisans at peak

The Forest Brothers (Estonian: metsavennad, Latvian: mežabrāļi, Lithuanian: miško broliai) were Baltic partisans who waged guerrilla warfare against Soviet rule in Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania during and after the Second World War. Similar anti-communist groups operated elsewhere in Eastern Europe.

Background

Resistance began with the first Soviet occupation of the Baltic states in 1940–41 and resumed on a large scale when the Red Army returned in 1944, as Balts who faced conscription, arrest or deportation took to the forests. Many partisans were former soldiers, members of pre-war defence leagues and farmers; they hoped that the Western Allies would intervene to restore independence, as suggested by the principles of the Atlantic Charter.

The struggle

At its height in 1945–46, the movement numbered in the tens of thousands, most extensively in Lithuania, where partisans formed military districts and a unified command, the Union of Lithuanian Freedom Fighters (LLKS). Soviet counterinsurgency combined military sweeps, agent infiltration and mass deportations — notably Operation Priboi in March 1949, which deported over 90,000 people from the Baltic states — that severed the partisans from their support base. Western intelligence support (including MI6 operations) was heavily penetrated by Soviet counter-intelligence. Organised resistance was crushed by the mid-1950s; the last Lithuanian partisan, Antanas Kraujelis, died in 1965, and the Estonian August Sabbe survived until 1978.

Legacy

The Forest Brothers are commemorated in the Baltic states as national heroes of resistance to occupation, and their struggle features prominently in the restored republics' historical memory and museums of occupation.
