Trāyastriṃśa

Trāyastriṃśa (Sanskrit: त्रायस्त्रिंश, Pali: Tāvatiṃsa) is an important world of the devas in Hindu and Buddhist cosmology. The Sanskrit word means "the thirty-three (devas)", and the realm is so called because it is governed by thirty-three gods, headed by Śakra (Indra). It is the second of the six heavens of the desire realm (Kāmadhātu) in Buddhist cosmology, located on the summit of Mount Meru (Sumeru).

Description

In Buddhist texts, Trāyastriṃśa lies on the flat peak of Mount Sumeru, at a height of 80,000 yojanas above the sea. At its centre stands Indra's palace, Vejayanta, and the Sudhamma assembly hall, where the gods gather. Also found there is the Pāricchattaka (coral) tree and the Nandana garden. A day and night in Trāyastriṃśa corresponds to one hundred human years, and its inhabitants live for a thousand such years.

In Buddhist tradition

According to tradition, the Buddha ascended to Trāyastriṃśa during his seventh rains retreat to teach the Abhidharma to his mother, Māyādevī, who had been reborn there, and to the assembled gods. The Asura, who in some accounts once lived on the summit of Meru, were expelled from Trāyastriṃśa and dwell at the mountain's foot, waging periodic wars against the devas. The realm's thirty-three gods are commonly identified with figures of the Vedic pantheon, including the eight Vasus, eleven Rudras, twelve Ādityas and the two Aśvins, together with Indra as their king.
