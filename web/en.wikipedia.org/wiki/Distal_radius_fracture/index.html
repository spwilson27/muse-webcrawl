Distal radius fracture

SpecialtyOrthopedics, emergency medicine
SymptomsPain, bruising, and swelling at the wrist
Usual onsetSudden, following a fall
TypesColles', Smith's, Barton's, Chauffeur's
TreatmentCasting, surgery

A distal radius fracture is a common bone fracture of the radius in the forearm. Because of its proximity to the wrist joint, this injury is often called a wrist fracture. Distal radius fractures are among the most common fractures seen in emergency departments, typically resulting from a fall onto an outstretched hand.

Types

The classic form is the Colles' fracture, described by Abraham Colles in 1814, in which the broken fragment is displaced dorsally (backwards), producing the characteristic "silver-fork" or "dinner-fork" deformity. The Smith's fracture is the reverse pattern, with volar (palmar) displacement, usually from a fall onto the back of the hand. Barton's fractures involve the rim of the distal radius with dislocation of the carpus.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is by plain X-ray of the wrist in two planes, which shows the fracture line, the degree of displacement, radial shortening, and any involvement of the joint surface.

Treatment

Undisplaced fractures are treated with a cast for about six weeks. Displaced fractures may require closed reduction and casting, external fixation, or open reduction and internal fixation with a volar plate, the choice depending on stability, patient age, and functional demand. Associated risks include stiffness, malunion, and complex regional pain syndrome.
