GROW model

The GROW model (or process) is a simple method for problem solving or goal setting. It was developed in the United Kingdom in the 1980s and is used extensively in corporate coaching. It was popularised in John Whitmore's influential 1992 book Coaching for Performance, and is credited to Whitmore and colleagues including Graham Alexander and Alan Fine, drawing on the "Inner Game" work of Timothy Gallwey.

The four stages

GROW is an acronym for the four stages of a coaching conversation:

  1. Goal — the endpoint the coachee wants to reach, defined clearly enough that it is obvious when it has been achieved, both for the session itself and for the longer term.
  2. Reality — the current situation: what is happening now, what has been tried, what obstacles exist, and what resources are available.
  3. Options (or Obstacles) — the possible courses of action, generated as freely as possible before judging them.
  4. Will (or Way Forward) — converting the discussion into concrete decisions: what will be done, when, by whom, and how committed the coachee is to doing it.

Use

The model is applied in leadership coaching, sports coaching, life coaching and management. Whitmore emphasised that the coach's role is to raise awareness and responsibility in the coachee through questioning rather than instruction, moving through the stages in order while allowing the conversation to revisit earlier stages as new information emerges.

Criticism and variants

Critics note that GROW can be applied mechanistically, and that its tidy sequence understates the messiness of real organisational problems. Variants include TGROW (which adds an initial Topic stage) and OSKAR, a solution-focused alternative.
