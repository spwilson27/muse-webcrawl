Constitution of Nepal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Constitution of Nepal (Nepali: नेपालको संविधान) is the present governing constitution of Nepal, promulgated on 20 September 2015 by the second Constituent Assembly, replacing the Interim Constitution of 2007. It is Nepal's seventh constitution, and the first to be drawn up by elected representatives of the people. It establishes Nepal as a federal, secular, democratic republic, ending the unitary and formerly Hindu-monarchical structure of the state.

Background

The constitution was the product of a decade-long peace process following the Nepalese Civil War (1996–2006) and the 2006 popular movement that stripped King Gyanendra of power and led to the abolition of the monarchy in 2008. The first Constituent Assembly, elected in 2008, failed to agree on a constitution and was dissolved in 2012; a second assembly, elected in 2013, completed the drafting, with the process accelerated after the April 2015 earthquake.

Main features

The constitution divides Nepal into seven provinces and three tiers of government — federal, provincial, and local — with powers enumerated in its schedules. It creates a bicameral federal parliament (House of Representatives and National Assembly), a president as head of state, and guarantees an extensive list of fundamental rights, including provisions for the representation of women, Dalits, and other marginalised groups, with quotas in elected bodies. Citizenship provisions, the boundaries and names of provinces, and the treatment of Madhesi and Tharu demands proved contentious, provoking protests and a border blockade in 2015–2016; the first amendment, in 2016, addressed some citizenship and representation questions, and a 2020 amendment updated the national map and emblem to include the disputed Kalapani territory. The constitution contains 35 parts, 308 articles, and 9 schedules.
