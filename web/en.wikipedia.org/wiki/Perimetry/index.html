Perimetry

Perimetry, or visual field testing, is the systematic measurement of the visual field — the total area in which objects can be seen in peripheral vision while the eye is focused on a central point. It is an essential part of the eye examination, used to detect blind spots (scotomas) and patterns of vision loss caused by diseases of the eye, optic nerve, or brain.

Methods

In static automated perimetry, the patient sits before a bowl-shaped instrument (such as the Humphrey Field Analyzer) and presses a button whenever a light stimulus of varying brightness appears at different locations; the machine maps the sensitivity of each point. In kinetic perimetry, classically with the Goldmann perimeter, a light target is moved inward from the periphery until it is first seen, tracing isopters of equal sensitivity. Simpler screening methods include confrontation fields and the tangent screen.

Clinical patterns

Characteristic visual field defects localise lesions along the visual pathway: a central scotoma suggests optic nerve disease; the arcuate and nasal-step defects of glaucoma follow the retinal nerve fibre layer; bitemporal hemianopia indicates a lesion at the optic chiasm (classically a pituitary tumour); and homonymous hemianopia points to damage behind the chiasm, in the optic radiations or occipital cortex. Perimetry is also used for driving-licence standards and monitoring vigabatrin-related field loss.
