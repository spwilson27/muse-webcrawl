Plaza de Toros

A plaza de toros is a bullring in Spain. Bullrings are also present in other Spanish-speaking countries where bullfighting takes place, such as Mexico, Colombia, Peru, Venezuela and Ecuador, as well as in southern France and Portugal, where the term used is praça de touros.

Bullrings are typically circular or oval structures with tiered seating surrounding a central arena, known as the ruedo, where bullfights are held. The arena is usually covered in sand or albero, a yellowish earth used in many Andalusian bullrings.

History

Bullfighting developed in Spain over centuries, and purpose-built bullrings began to appear in the 18th century. One of the oldest bullrings in Spain is the Plaza de Toros de Ronda, opened in 1785. Before permanent structures were built, bullfights were held in enclosed town squares (plazas mayores), many of which were adapted for the purpose.

The largest bullring in the world by seating capacity is the Plaza México in Mexico City, which can hold more than 40,000 spectators. The Plaza de Toros de Las Ventas in Madrid, inaugurated in 1931, is considered the home of bullfighting in Spain and hosts the San Isidro festival, the most important bullfighting fair of the year.

Structure

A typical plaza de toros consists of:

  • The ruedo — the circular arena where the bullfight takes place, normally between 45 and 60 metres in diameter.
  • The callejón — the passageway running between the arena and the first rows of seats, where participants and staff move during the event.
  • The tendidos — the tiered general seating areas.
  • The gradas and andanadas — upper covered and uncovered seating tiers.
  • The palcos — boxes, including the presidential box (palco presidencial).
  • The toriles — the pens from which the bulls enter the arena.
  • An infirmary (enfermería) and a chapel, where matadors pray before the fight.

Categories

In Spain, bullrings are classified into three categories according to their importance, tradition and the number of events they host. First-category rings include Las Ventas in Madrid, La Maestranza in Seville, the Plaza de Toros de Valencia, and those of Bilbao, Zaragoza, Córdoba, San Sebastián and Pamplona. Second- and third-category rings are found throughout the country.

Notable bullrings

  • Plaza de Toros de Las Ventas, Madrid
  • Plaza de Toros de la Real Maestranza de Caballería de Sevilla (La Maestranza), Seville
  • Plaza de Toros de Ronda, one of the oldest in Spain
  • Plaza Monumental de Barcelona, closed to bullfighting after the Catalan ban
  • Plaza México, Mexico City, the largest bullring in the world
  • Plaza de Toros Monumental de Pamplona, famous for the Running of the Bulls during San Fermín
