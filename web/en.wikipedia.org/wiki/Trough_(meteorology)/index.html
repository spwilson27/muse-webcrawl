Trough (meteorology)

In meteorology, a trough is an elongated region of relatively low atmospheric pressure, depicted on weather maps as a line extending from a low-pressure centre along which the pressure is lowest. Unlike a frontal system, a trough need not mark a boundary between air masses. Troughs favour rising air, cloud formation and precipitation, and are frequently associated with unsettled weather.

Related featureRidge (opposite: high pressure)
TypesSurface trough, upper-level trough, shortwave trough
WeatherCloud, showers, convergence

Types

A shortwave trough is a smaller-scale kink in the upper-level flow embedded within the broader pattern of longwave troughs and ridges; shortwaves are important triggers for cyclogenesis. In the tropics, the monsoon trough and the near-equatorial trough organise convection and are breeding grounds for tropical cyclones. A lee trough forms downwind of mountain ranges as air descends and stretches vertically.
