Precentor

A precentor is a person who helps facilitate worship — originally the leader of the singing in a church or cathedral choir. The word derives from the Latin praecentor, "one who sings first", corresponding to the office of the primicerius of the early church.

Cathedral office

In medieval cathedral chapters the precentor (or chanter) ranked among the four principal dignitaries, together with the dean, chancellor and treasurer. He was responsible for the organisation of the liturgy and its music: directing the choir, selecting chant, maintaining service books and disciplining the singers and choristers. His stall was traditionally on the opposite side of the choir from the dean's.

Modern roles

In Anglican cathedrals the canon precentor remains a senior post, usually a residentiary canon charged with planning worship and overseeing the music department. In some synagogues, the person who leads the congregation in prayer is called a cantor, an analogous leading-singer role; in other traditions a "precentor" may simply be the person who leads congregational singing, pitching the tunes in churches without instruments.
