Renin–angiotensin system

Renin–angiotensin system
TypeHormone system
Key componentsRenin, angiotensinogen, angiotensin I/II, ACE, aldosterone
FunctionBlood pressure and fluid balance regulation

The renin–angiotensin system (RAS), or renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS), is a hormone system that regulates blood pressure, fluid and electrolyte balance, and systemic vascular resistance.

Mechanism

When blood pressure or sodium delivery falls, the juxtaglomerular cells of the kidney release the enzyme renin. Renin cleaves the liver protein angiotensinogen to angiotensin I, which angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) converts to angiotensin II. Angiotensin II is a potent vasoconstrictor and stimulates aldosterone release from the adrenal glands, increasing sodium and water reabsorption.

Clinical relevance

Overactivity of the system contributes to hypertension and heart failure. Major drug classes target it: ACE inhibitors, angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs), and direct renin inhibitors.
