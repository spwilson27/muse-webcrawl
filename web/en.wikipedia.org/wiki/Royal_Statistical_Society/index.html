The Royal Statistical Society (RSS) is a British learned society and professional body for statisticians and data professionals, founded in 1834 as the Statistical Society of London. It is one of the world's oldest statistical societies and played a prominent role in the development of statistics as a discipline.

History

The society was founded following the 1833 Cambridge meeting of the British Association, with figures including Charles Babbage, Adolphe Quetelet, William Whewell and Thomas Malthus among its early supporters; its first president was the Marquess of Lansdowne. Its emblem, a wheatsheaf with the motto Aliis exterendum, wryly acknowledged that the society would collect facts and leave interpretation to others. Florence Nightingale became its first female member in 1858. The society received a royal charter in 1887.

Activities

The RSS publishes the multi-series Journal of the Royal Statistical Society and, jointly with the American Statistical Association, the magazine Significance. It awards the Guy Medals (gold, silver and bronze) and other honours, charters professional statisticians (CStat), campaigns for statistical literacy through initiatives such as Statistics of the Year, and comments publicly on issues from official statistics to election polling. Its president serves a two-year term; meetings are held at its London headquarters in Errol Street.
