Languages constructed by J. R. R. Tolkien

The English author and philologist J. R. R. Tolkien (1892–1973) created a number of constructed languages, including the Elvish languages that underpin his legendarium of Middle-earth. A trained philologist at the University of Oxford, Tolkien began inventing languages in his teens, and he described his fiction as fundamentally a by-product of his linguistic invention: the stories grew around the languages rather than the other way round.

The Elvish languages

The largest family is the Elvish languages, descended within the fiction from Primitive Quendian. The two best-developed are:

  • Quenya — the "High-Elven" tongue, modelled partly on Finnish, Latin and Greek, preserved in Middle-earth mainly as a language of lore and ceremony. Its most famous text is Galadriel's lament "Namárië".
  • Sindarin — the everyday language of the Grey Elves of Beleriand and later Gondor, inspired in sound by Welsh. Most Elvish names in The Lord of the Rings are Sindarin.

Related languages include Telerin, Nandorin and the Avarin tongues. Tolkien also devised scripts for them, principally the Tengwar of Fëanor and the Cirth runes.

Other languages of Middle-earth

  • Khuzdul — the secret language of the Dwarves, with a triconsonantal structure reminiscent of Semitic languages.
  • Adûnaic — the language of Númenor, ancestor of the Westron (Common Speech) spoken in the Third Age.
  • Black Speech — devised by Sauron for his servants; known chiefly from the Ring inscription.
  • Entish, Valarin and various Mannish and Orcish tongues are sketched more briefly.

Early inventions

Before the legendarium, the teenage Tolkien worked on languages such as Nevbosh ("new nonsense"), Animalic (with his cousins) and Naffarin, and in the 1910s began Qenya and Goldogrin/Gnomish, the direct ancestors of Quenya and Sindarin. His linguistic papers and glossaries continue to be published posthumously in the journals Parma Eldalamberon and Vinyar Tengwar.
