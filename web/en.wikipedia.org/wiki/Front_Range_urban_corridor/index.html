Front Range urban corridor

The Front Range urban corridor is an oblong region of urban population growth along the eastern slope of the Front Range of the Rocky Mountains in the U.S. states of Colorado and Wyoming. It runs roughly 320 km (200 miles) from Pueblo, Colorado, in the south to Cheyenne, Wyoming, in the north, mostly following Interstate 25.

Population

The corridor contains the great majority of Colorado's population — more than four-fifths of the state's roughly 5.8 million residents — concentrated in the Denver–Aurora metropolitan area, Colorado Springs, Boulder, Fort Collins, Greeley and Pueblo. Rapid growth since the mid-20th century has been driven by government installations, universities, technology and outdoor-recreation amenities.

Issues

Growth has brought challenges including water supply from trans-mountain diversions, air quality episodes along the mountain front, housing costs, and wildland–urban interface wildfire risk, seen in events such as the 2021 Marshall Fire.
