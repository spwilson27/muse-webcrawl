Flammability is the ease with which a material or substance ignites, causing fire or combustion. It is a fundamental consideration in fire safety, materials selection, building codes and the transport and storage of chemicals. A flammable material is one that can be easily ignited and burns rapidly, while materials that are difficult to ignite or slow to burn are described as having low flammability, and those that do not burn at all are non-flammable.

Key measurements

The flash point of a volatile material is the lowest temperature at which its vapours will ignite in air when given an ignition source. The autoignition temperature is the lowest temperature at which the material ignites spontaneously without an external spark or flame. Other measures include the rate of flame spread across a surface, the heat released during burning, and the flammable limits — the range of vapour-in-air concentrations that can burn.

Testing and classification

Standardised tests underpin flammability ratings. The UL 94 standard classifies plastics from HB (slow burning) up to V-0 (self-extinguishing within seconds). Building materials are tested for flame spread and smoke development, and fabrics for uses such as children's sleepwear and aircraft interiors must meet strict flammability standards. Under the Globally Harmonized System (GHS), flammable liquids are categorised largely by flash point and boiling point and labelled with the flame pictogram.

Confusion with "inflammable"

The word inflammable means exactly the same as flammable — the prefix is the Latin in- meaning "into" (as in "inflame"), not a negation. Because the word was widely misread as meaning "not flammable", safety authorities have preferred "flammable" on labels since the early twentieth century.

Reducing flammability

Flammability can be reduced with flame retardants — additives or surface treatments that slow ignition or spread — and by choosing inherently resistant materials, such as fibreglass insulation instead of foam. Limiting oxygen, as in some aircraft fuel-tank inerting systems, also reduces fire risk.
