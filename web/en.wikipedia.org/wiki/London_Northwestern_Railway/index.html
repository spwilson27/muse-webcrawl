London and North Western Railway (LNWR)
Years: 1846–1922
Headquarters: Euston station, London
Successor: London, Midland and Scottish Railway
Nickname: "The Premier Line"

The London and North Western Railway (LNWR) was a British railway company between 1846 and 1922. It was created by the merger of the Grand Junction Railway, the London and Birmingham Railway and the Manchester and Birmingham Railway, and at its height was the largest railway company in Britain and arguably the largest joint-stock corporation in the world, with a network centred on the West Coast route between London Euston, Birmingham, Manchester, Liverpool and Holyhead.

Operations

Styled the "Premier Line", the LNWR carried express passenger trains such as the Irish Mail to Holyhead for the Dublin ferry, operated extensive suburban and freight services, and built its locomotives and carriages at Crewe Works, one of the largest railway works of the age. Notable chief mechanical engineers included Francis Webb and George Whale, whose designs included the famous "Jubilee" and "Precursor" classes; the company also ran hotels, steamships to Ireland and a major parcels business.

Grouping

Under the Railways Act 1921, the LNWR was amalgamated with the Lancashire and Yorkshire Railway in 1922 and became the largest constituent of the London, Midland and Scottish Railway (LMS) at the Grouping on 1 January 1923. Its main lines remain in use today as the West Coast Main Line.
