Eurythmics were a British pop duo formed in London in 1980, consisting of vocalist Annie Lennox and musician and producer Dave Stewart. The duo achieved global success with their distinctive blend of synth-pop, new wave, soul, and dance music, and with Lennox's powerful contralto voice and androgynous image. They are best known for the songs "Sweet Dreams (Are Made of This)", "Love Is a Stranger", "Here Comes the Rain Again", "Would I Lie to You?", "There Must Be an Angel (Playing with My Heart)", and "Missionary Man".

History

Lennox and Stewart had previously performed together in the Tourists, which split in 1980. Their first album as Eurythmics, In the Garden (1981), made little commercial impact, but the title track of their second album, Sweet Dreams (Are Made of This) (1983), became a worldwide hit, reaching number one in the United States, aided by its striking music video. The albums Touch (1983), Be Yourself Tonight (1985), Revenge (1986), and Savage (1987) produced a string of international hits.

Later years and legacy

After We Too Are One (1989), the duo quietly disbanded in 1990 as both members pursued solo careers, Lennox achieving major success as a solo artist. They reunited for the album Peace (1999) and have performed together on special occasions since. Eurythmics have sold an estimated 75 million records worldwide. They won the Grammy Award for Best Rock Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocal in 1987, were inducted into the UK Music Hall of Fame in 2005, and were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2022.
