Henry Clay Frick

Born19 December 1849, West Overton, Pennsylvania
Died2 December 1919 (aged 69), New York City
OccupationIndustrialist, art collector

Henry Clay Frick (December 19, 1849 – December 2, 1919) was an American industrialist, financier and art patron. He founded the H. C. Frick & Company coke manufacturing concern, was chairman of the Carnegie Steel Company, and played a central — and notorious — role in the violent Homestead Strike of 1892. His New York mansion and art collection became the Frick Collection museum.

Coke and steel

Born in West Overton, Pennsylvania, of Swiss-German Mennonite stock, Frick borrowed money in 1871 to build coke ovens in the Connellsville coalfield, supplying fuel for steelmaking. By 1880 his company controlled the great majority of Pennsylvania coke production, earning him the title "Coke King". Andrew Carnegie brought him in as partner and chairman of Carnegie Steel in 1889. Frick drove the company's vertical integration and expansion, including the creation of the town and works at Clairton.

Homestead and the assassination attempt

In 1892, as manager of the Homestead steel works during a labour dispute, Frick fortified the plant, cut wages and hired 300 Pinkerton detectives, whose arrival by barge on 6 July led to a gun battle that killed workers and detectives and required the state militia to restore order. The strike collapsed, and the steel industry remained largely non-union for decades. On 23 July 1892 the anarchist Alexander Berkman shot and stabbed Frick in his Pittsburgh office; Frick survived, famously returning to work the same week, and Berkman was imprisoned for attempted murder. Frick also bore responsibility in the public mind for the 1889 Johnstown Flood, as a member of the South Fork Fishing and Hunting Club whose neglected dam had failed, though he was never held legally liable.

Art collecting and death

After a bitter 1900 rupture with Carnegie over the valuation of his shares — settled with a payout of some $30 million — Frick invested in railroads and amassed one of America's great art collections: works by Rembrandt, Vermeer, Turner, Whistler, Goya and Fragonard. His Fifth Avenue mansion in New York, completed in 1914, was left to the public at his death on 2 December 1919, opening as the Frick Collection in 1935.
