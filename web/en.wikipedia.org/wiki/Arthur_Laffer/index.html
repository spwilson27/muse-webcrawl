Arthur Betz Laffer (born August 14, 1940) is an American economist best known for the Laffer curve, a graphical representation suggesting that there exists an optimal tax rate that maximizes government revenue. A prominent advocate of supply-side economics, Laffer advised President Ronald Reagan and influenced the tax cuts enacted during the 1980s.

Early life and education

Laffer was born in Youngstown, Ohio. He received his bachelor's degree in economics from Yale University in 1963, followed by an MBA (1965) and a PhD in economics (1972) from Stanford University.

Career

Laffer taught at the University of Chicago Graduate School of Business and later at the University of Southern California's Marshall School of Business. He served as chief economist at the Office of Management and Budget under George Shultz in the early 1970s—reportedly the office's first chief economist. He later founded Laffer Associates, an economic consulting firm based in Nashville, Tennessee.

The Laffer curve

According to a popular account, in 1974 Laffer sketched the curve on a napkin during a meeting with Dick Cheney and Donald Rumsfeld to argue against President Gerald Ford's proposed tax increase. The curve posits that at a 0% tax rate the government collects no revenue, and at a 100% rate it also collects none, because no one would work or report income; between these extremes lies a revenue-maximizing rate. Laffer argued that U.S. tax rates were beyond that optimum for some taxpayers, so cutting rates could increase incentives to work and invest. The underlying idea had earlier precedents in the writings of thinkers including Ibn Khaldun and John Maynard Keynes, and in the tax policies associated with Andrew Mellon.

Political influence

Laffer was a member of Reagan's Economic Policy Advisory Board and a leading intellectual proponent of the Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981. He later advised other Republican candidates and officeholders, and supported Kansas's 2012 tax-cut experiment under Governor Sam Brownback, which was largely reversed in 2017 after revenue shortfalls. In 2019 President Donald Trump awarded Laffer the Presidential Medal of Freedom.

Criticism

Many economists argue that tax rates in the United States in the 1980s were below the revenue-maximizing rate, so that cuts reduced revenue relative to baseline forecasts. The shape and peak of the curve remain subjects of debate, and Laffer himself has acknowledged the curve is a pedagogical device rather than a precise predictive tool.
