Regional fisheries management organisation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Tuna purse seiners on the high seas, a fishery managed by regional tuna commissions

A regional fisheries management organisation (RFMO; sometimes regional fisheries management organization) is an international organisation dedicated to the sustainable management of fishery resources in a particular region of international waters, or of highly migratory species. RFMOs are a central instrument of modern international fisheries law, established under the framework of the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) and the 1995 UN Fish Stocks Agreement.

Functions

RFMOs typically have authority to establish conservation and management measures for their convention areas, including total allowable catches and quotas, fishing effort limits, closed areas and seasons, gear restrictions, minimum fish sizes, observer programmes, vessel monitoring systems, and measures against illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing such as IUU vessel lists and port state controls. Scientific committees attached to each RFMO assess stock status and recommend catch limits.

Types of RFMOs

RFMOs are commonly divided into general organisations, which manage most fish stocks within an area (such as NAFO in the Northwest Atlantic, NEAFC in the Northeast Atlantic, and SPRFMO in the South Pacific), and tuna RFMOs, which manage tuna and tuna-like species across ocean basins. The five tuna RFMOs are ICCAT (Atlantic), IOTC (Indian Ocean), WCPFC (Western and Central Pacific), IATTC (Eastern Pacific) and CCSBT (Southern bluefin tuna). Salmon fisheries are managed by bodies such as NASCO and the NPAFC, while CCAMLR manages marine living resources in the Southern Ocean under an ecosystem-based approach.

Criticism and reform

RFMOs have been criticised for decisions driven by short-term political interests, consensus rules that allow single members to block conservation measures, over-allocation of quotas relative to scientific advice, and poor compliance. Performance reviews, the Kobe process among tuna RFMOs, and greater transparency of compliance data have been pursued as reforms.
