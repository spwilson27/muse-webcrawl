Brigade

A brigade is a major tactical military formation that typically comprises three to six battalions, plus supporting elements, commanded by a brigadier or brigadier general. In most modern armies a brigade numbers roughly 3,000 to 5,000 soldiers and sits between a regiment or group and a division in the hierarchy of formations.

History

The term, from French brigade, came into general use in the 17th century; Sweden's Gustavus Adolphus organized infantry brigades in the Thirty Years' War, and France formalized the brigade as a formation of two or three regiments. Napoleon used brigades within divisions. In the 20th century many armies reorganized around brigades, and in the early 21st century the United States Army restructured its combat power into modular brigade combat teams (BCTs) — infantry, Stryker, and armored — designed to deploy as largely self-contained formations.

Types

Brigades are usually formed for a specific role: infantry, armoured/armor, mechanized, artillery, aviation, airborne, special forces, and support (sustainment, engineer, signal, and military police) brigades are common patterns. In NATO symbology a brigade is denoted by a single X above its unit icon.
