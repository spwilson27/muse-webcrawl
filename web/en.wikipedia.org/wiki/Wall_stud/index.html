Wall stud

A wall stud is a vertical framing member in a building's wall, typically made of wood or metal, that transfers loads between the wall's horizontal plates and supports the wall covering. Studs are a fundamental element of light-frame construction, the dominant method of building houses in North America and many other regions.

Arrangement

Studs sit between a bottom (sole) plate and a top plate, and in modern platform framing they are commonly spaced 16 inches (about 406 mm) on centre, matching standard sizes of sheathing and plasterboard, or 24 inches in some applications. Load-bearing studs are usually "two-by-four" (nominally 2×4 inch, actually about 38 × 89 mm) or 2×6 lumber. Special members include jack and king studs around openings, and cripple studs above and below windows and doors. Metal studs, formed from light-gauge galvanised steel channel, are common in commercial interiors.

Finding studs

Because nails and screws hold best in solid framing, locating studs matters when hanging heavy objects. Techniques include tapping for a solid sound, using magnetic finders that detect fasteners, or electronic stud finders that sense changes in wall density or capacitance. Loads such as cabinets and televisions are typically anchored directly into studs.
