The McClelland Royal Commission, officially the Royal Commission into British nuclear tests in Australia, was an Australian royal commission established in 1984 by the Hawke government to investigate the British nuclear tests conducted in Australia during the 1950s and early 1960s. It was chaired by Jim (James) McClelland, a former federal minister and judge.

Background

Between 1952 and 1963, the United Kingdom conducted twelve major atmospheric nuclear tests in Australia, at the Montebello Islands off Western Australia and at Emu Field and Maralinga in South Australia, along with hundreds of smaller "minor trials" involving the dispersal of radioactive materials including plutonium. Aboriginal communities were displaced from their lands, and Australian servicemen and Aboriginal people were exposed to fallout. By the early 1980s, public and media pressure had grown for an inquiry into the safety of the tests and the treatment of those affected.

Findings

The commission reported in 1985. It was strongly critical of both the British and Australian governments of the day, finding that the tests had been conducted with inadequate regard for the safety of Aboriginal people and Australian personnel, that the Australian Weapons Tests Safety Committee had failed in its duties, and that the contamination at Maralinga — particularly plutonium contamination from the Vixen B minor trials at Taranaki — was far worse than previously admitted.

The commission recommended that the British government should bear the cost of a full clean-up of the test sites, that compensation should be paid to affected Aboriginal communities, and that the Maralinga lands be returned to their traditional owners.

Aftermath

Following the commission, the Maralinga Tjarutja Land Rights Act returned lands to the Maralinga Tjarutja people. Britain eventually contributed £20 million toward the clean-up of Maralinga, carried out in the late 1990s, and paid compensation to the Maralinga Tjarutja. The commission's report remains a landmark in Australian inquiries into the nuclear test program and its effects on Indigenous communities and veterans.
