Moray eel

FamilyMuraenidae
OrderAnguilliformes
Genera16, about 200 species
HabitatTropical and temperate seas worldwide

Moray eels, or Muraenidae, are a family of eels whose members are found worldwide in tropical and temperate seas. There are approximately 200 species in 16 genera. Morays are long, slender, snake-like fishes that typically inhabit crevices in reefs and rocky areas, from shallow water down to depths of several hundred metres.

Description

Morays lack pectoral and pelvic fins, and their dorsal and anal fins are continuous with the caudal fin. Their skin is thick, scaleless, and often vividly patterned, and they secrete a protective mucus. The largest species, the giant moray (Gymnothorax javanicus), can reach 3 metres in length. Morays possess a second set of jaws in the throat, the pharyngeal jaws, which advance into the mouth to pull prey into the oesophagus.

Behaviour and diet

Morays are primarily nocturnal predators that hunt by smell, feeding on fish, cephalopods, and crustaceans. They often cooperate with roving coral groupers in interspecies hunts. Although they have a fearsome reputation, morays are generally shy and bite humans mainly in self-defence or when hand-fed.
