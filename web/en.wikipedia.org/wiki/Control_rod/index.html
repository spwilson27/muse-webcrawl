Control rods are rods, plates, or tubes containing neutron-absorbing material used to control the fission rate of uranium and plutonium in a nuclear reactor. Making a chain reaction critical relies on their exact position in the core.

Mechanism

Inserting rods absorbs neutrons and reduces the neutron multiplication factor; withdrawing them increases reactivity and power. Operators make small adjustments ("shimming") while rapid full insertion — a SCRAM — shuts the reactor down.

Materials

Boron-10 has a very high neutron-capture cross-section and is used as boron carbide (B₄C). Cadmium, hafnium, and silver-indium-cadmium alloys are alternatives. The absorber is usually clad in stainless steel or Inconel.

Types and designs

PWRs use rod cluster control assemblies entering from the top; BWRs use cruciform blades entering from below. Research reactors may use plates or rotating drums.

Safety

The Chernobyl disaster was worsened by RBMK control rods with graphite tips that briefly increased reactivity on insertion — a positive scram effect that accelerated the power excursion.
