Mount Gilboa (Hebrew: הַר הַגִּלְבֹּעַ, Har haGilboa) is a mountain ridge in northern Israel overlooking the Jezreel Valley to the north and the Jordan Valley to the east. The ridge runs roughly north-west to south-east for about 18 kilometres and rises to about 508 metres at Mount Shaul. It forms part of the border between the territories associated in biblical times with the tribes of Issachar and Manasseh.

Biblical history

Mount Gilboa is best known as the site of the battle in which King Saul and his sons, including Jonathan, were killed by the Philistines (1 Samuel 31). Saul, wounded and fearing capture, fell on his own sword. David's lament for Saul and Jonathan, recorded in 2 Samuel, contains the famous curse: "Ye mountains of Gilboa, let there be no dew, neither let there be rain, upon you" — traditionally blamed for the ridge's historically barren slopes. The story of Gideon's selection of his 300 men by the spring of Harod took place at the foot of the ridge.

Nature

Afforestation by the Jewish National Fund during the 20th century covered much of the range in pine forest. The Gilboa is famed for the purple Gilboa iris (Iris haynei), an endemic wildflower protected in a nature reserve that blooms in March and April and draws large numbers of visitors. Views from the ridge encompass Mount Tabor, the Jezreel Valley, and the hills of Samaria.
