Quadratic function

A quadratic function is a polynomial function of degree two in one or more variables. In a single variable it has the form

f(x) = ax² + bx + c, where a ≠ 0.

Its graph is a parabola: a U-shaped curve that opens upward if a > 0 and downward if a < 0. The highest or lowest point of the parabola is its vertex, located at x = −b/(2a), and the vertical line through the vertex is the axis of symmetry.

Forms

The function can be written in standard form f(x) = ax² + bx + c, in vertex form f(x) = a(x − h)² + k, where (h, k) is the vertex, and, when it has real roots r₁ and r₂, in factored form f(x) = a(x − r₁)(x − r₂).

Applications

Quadratic functions model projectile motion under gravity, the area of shapes, revenue in simple pricing problems, and the shape of parabolic reflectors and antennas.
