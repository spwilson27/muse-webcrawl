Crystallography

Crystallography
Field: Physics, chemistry, biology
Key method: X-ray diffraction
UNESCO: International Year 2014

Crystallography is the experimental science of determining the arrangement of atoms in crystalline solids. It combines geometry, diffraction physics and chemistry, and underpins mineralogy, materials science and structural biology.

History

Johannes Kepler and Robert Hooke speculated on crystal symmetry; Nicolaus Steno noted constant interfacial angles (1669). After Max von Laue's 1912 discovery of X-ray diffraction by crystals, William Henry and William Lawrence Bragg developed X-ray crystallography, later used to solve structures from table salt to DNA and proteins.

Methods

A beam of X-rays, neutrons or electrons diffracts from a periodic lattice; from the diffraction pattern and Bragg's law, electron-density maps and atomic models are computed, solving the phase problem by methods such as molecular replacement. 230 space groups describe possible symmetries.

Applications

Drug design, semiconductors, metallurgy, and the structures recognised by many Nobel Prizes — including those for DNA, ribosomes and quasicrystals — depend on crystallographic data archived in databases such as the Protein Data Bank.
