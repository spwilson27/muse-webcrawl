Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex

The Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, officially designated the Dallas–Fort Worth–Arlington metropolitan statistical area by the U.S. Office of Management and Budget, is the most populous metropolitan area in the U.S. state of Texas and the fourth-largest in the United States, with a population of more than 7.6 million (over 8 million in the wider combined statistical area). Encompassing 11–13 counties in North Texas, it comprises two principal metropolitan divisions centred on the cities of Dallas and Fort Worth, some 30 miles (48 km) apart. The portmanteau "Metroplex", coined in the early 1970s by combining metropolis and complex, was popularised by the North Texas Commission and is now used almost exclusively for this region.

Geography and cities

The metroplex lies on the rolling Blackland Prairie where North, East, and Central Texas meet, drained by the Trinity River. Dallas, with about 1.3 million residents, anchors the east; Fort Worth, with about 1 million, anchors the west, with Arlington between them. Large suburban cities include Plano, Irving, Garland, Frisco, McKinney, Denton, Grand Prairie, Richardson, and Carrollton. The area’s climate is humid subtropical, with hot summers.

Economy

The region grew as a railroad, cotton, and oil centre in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and diversified into banking, defence, telecommunications (the "Telecom Corridor" around Richardson), and technology. It is home to the headquarters of numerous Fortune 500 companies, among them ExxonMobil (Irving, until 2023), AT&T (Dallas), American Airlines (Fort Worth), and Texas Instruments (Dallas), and to Dallas Fort Worth International Airport — one of the world’s busiest airports, opened in 1974 and a principal hub of American Airlines — as well as Dallas Love Field, headquarters of Southwest Airlines. Major-league sports teams include the Dallas Cowboys (NFL), Texas Rangers (MLB), Dallas Mavericks (NBA), Dallas Stars (NHL), and FC Dallas (MLS).
