Fredericksburg is a city in and the county seat of Gillespie County, Texas, United States, in the Texas Hill Country. Founded in 1846 by German immigrants under the auspices of the Adelsverein, it was named after Prince Frederick of Prussia and is known for its well-preserved German heritage, including the Texas German dialect once widely spoken there.

History

Fredericksburg was founded by Baron Otfried Hans von Meusebach, commissioner general of the Adelsverein (the Society for the Protection of German Immigrants in Texas). In 1847 Meusebach negotiated a peace treaty with the Penateka Comanche that, unusually among such treaties, was never broken. The town's distinctive "Sunday houses" — small in-town houses used by farm families on weekends — reflect its early settlement pattern.

The city is the birthplace of Fleet Admiral Chester W. Nimitz, commander of Pacific forces in the Second World War; the National Museum of the Pacific War is located there in the former Nimitz Hotel.

Geography and culture

Fredericksburg lies about 70 miles west of Austin and 65 miles northwest of San Antonio. Nearby Enchanted Rock, a massive pink granite batholith, is a popular state natural area. The surrounding area is a major wine-producing region and is noted for its peach orchards. Tourism centered on the historic Main Street is a mainstay of the local economy.
