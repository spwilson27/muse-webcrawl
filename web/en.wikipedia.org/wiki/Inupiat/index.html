Inupiat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Iñupiat dancers in traditional dress

The Inupiat (singular Inupiaq) are an Alaska Native people whose traditional territory spans from Norton Sound on the Bering Sea to the northernmost coast of Alaska and into the Canadian western Arctic. They speak Inupiaq, an Inuit language of the Eskimo–Aleut family. In their own language the name is written Iñupiat, with iñuk meaning "person" and -piat meaning "real" or "genuine"—"the real people".

Culture

Traditionally the Inupiat lived by hunting marine mammals (whales, seals, walrus), caribou and fish, and fishing; whaling crews (umialik) remain central to social organisation in coastal communities such as Utqiaġvik (formerly Barrow). The Nalukataq whaling festival is a major celebration of a successful spring bowhead whale hunt.

Modern era

The Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act of 1971 conveyed land and capital to regional corporations, including the Arctic Slope Regional Corporation. Inupiat communities today work to maintain subsistence practices and revitalise the Inupiaq language, which is endangered despite immersion and school programmes.
