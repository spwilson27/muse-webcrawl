A duty-free shop (or duty-free store) is a retail outlet whose goods are exempt from the payment of certain local or national taxes and duties, on the requirement that the goods be sold to travelers who will take them out of the country or customs territory. Such shops are found in the international zones of airports and seaports, on board ships and aircraft, and at some land border crossings.

History

The world's first duty-free shop was established at Shannon Airport, Ireland, in 1947 under the Customs Free Airport Act, an innovation credited to Irish entrepreneur Brendan O'Regan; Shannon was then a major refuelling stop on transatlantic routes. The concept spread as international air travel grew, and large operators — among them DFS Group (founded 1960), Dufry, and China Duty Free Group — built global businesses. The abolition of intra-European Union duty-free sales in 1999 reshaped the European market.

Rules and allowances

Because the goods are formally treated as exports, purchasers usually must show a boarding pass or passport, and purchases are subject to the duty-free allowances of the destination country, which cap quantities of alcohol, tobacco and total value. Some jurisdictions run "duty-free" schemes on arrival or downtown for departing tourists, often with goods sealed until departure.

Economics

Duty-free and travel retail is a major source of non-aeronautical revenue for airports, and luxury brands view the channel — sometimes called the "sixth continent" of retail — as a showcase for high-spending international travellers.
