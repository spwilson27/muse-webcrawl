Pallasite

Pallasites are a class of stony–iron meteorites consisting of centimetre-sized olivine crystals embedded in a continuous matrix of iron–nickel metal. They are among the most visually striking meteorites and are named after the German naturalist Peter Simon Pallas, who described the Krasnojarsk mass found in Siberia in the 18th century.

TypeStony-iron meteorite
CompositionOlivine crystals in Fe–Ni metal
Named forPeter Simon Pallas

Origin

Pallasites are thought to sample the core–mantle boundary region of differentiated asteroids: olivine from the rocky mantle mixed with molten metal from the core, later shattered by impacts and reassembled. The main-group pallasites share oxygen isotope signatures with the HED meteorites and the asteroid 4 Vesta has been proposed as a parent body, though iron meteorite compositions point to more than one source.

Classification

Pallasites are divided into the main group (PMG), the Eagle Station grouplet, the pyroxene pallasites, and ungrouped examples. Famous specimens include Krasnojarsk, Esquel, Brenham, Imilac and Fukang, whose translucent olivine crystals glow when thinly sliced and backlit.
