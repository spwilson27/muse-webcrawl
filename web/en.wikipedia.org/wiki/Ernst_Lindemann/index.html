Ernst Lindemann (28 March 1894 – 27 May 1941) was a German naval officer best known as the captain of the battleship Bismarck during her only combat voyage, Operation Rheinübung, in May 1941.

Early career

Lindemann joined the Imperial German Navy in 1913 and served through the First World War, later specialising in naval gunnery, an expertise that led to senior instructional and staff posts. He took part in the Spanish Civil War patrols aboard the pocket battleship Admiral Scheer and at the outbreak of the Second World War served in the Naval High Command.

Command of Bismarck

In August 1940 Lindemann commissioned the new battleship Bismarck, Germany's largest warship. In May 1941 she sailed into the Atlantic with the heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen to raid Allied convoys. At the Battle of the Denmark Strait on 24 May, Bismarck sank the battlecruiser HMS Hood but was herself damaged and hunted down; Lindemann went down with his ship when Bismarck was sunk on 27 May 1941, with the loss of more than 2,000 of her crew.
