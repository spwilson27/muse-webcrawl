Cornish Copper Company

The Cornish Copper Company was an early nineteenth-century British copper-smelting company formed by a group of Cornish investors to challenge the dominance of the Welsh smelters of Swansea. It established works at Hayle, in Cornwall, where it smelted ore close to the mines rather than shipping it to South Wales.

Company

Industry: Copper smelting

Location: Hayle, Cornwall, England

Era: Early 19th century

History

In the eighteenth century Cornish copper ore was shipped to Swansea for smelting because of Wales's cheap coal. The Cornish Copper Company brought smelting back to Cornwall, operating the Copperhouse works at Hayle and building the Copperhouse Dock and associated foundries. The company became a major employer and a rival to the Harvey family interests in Hayle, and the competition between the two firms drove the development of the port and of early steam engineering in the town.

Legacy

Remains of the Copperhouse works, dock and foundry survive in Hayle, and the company's name lives on in local place names such as Copperhouse Pool.
