Integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect

The integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect is the redshift of photons of the cosmic microwave background as they pass through time-varying gravitational potential wells. It is named after Rainer K. Sachs and Arthur M. Wolfe, who described the broader Sachs–Wolfe effect in 1967.

Mechanism

When a photon enters a gravitational potential well it gains energy (blueshift) and loses it again on leaving. If the well deepens or shallows while the photon crosses, the shifts do not cancel, producing a net temperature change in the observed microwave background. In a universe dominated by dark energy, potential wells decay at late times, generating the "late-time" integrated effect.

Observational evidence

The effect is far smaller than the primary microwave background anisotropies, but it can be detected statistically by cross-correlating microwave background maps with catalogues of galaxies and superclusters. Detections since the mid-2000s provide independent evidence for dark energy.
