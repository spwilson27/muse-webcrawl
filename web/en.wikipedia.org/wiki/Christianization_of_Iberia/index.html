Christianization of Iberia

The Christianization of Iberia may refer either to the spread of Christianity in the Iberian Peninsula (Spain and Portugal) in late antiquity, or, in the usage followed here, to the conversion of the ancient Caucasian kingdom of Iberia — roughly modern eastern Georgia — traditionally dated to the 330s AD, making it one of the earliest states to adopt Christianity.

The conversion of Kartli

Georgian and Roman sources credit Saint Nino, a Cappadocian woman, with preaching Christianity in the Iberian capital Mtskheta and healing Queen Nana. King Mirian III then adopted the faith — tradition places his conversion in 326 or 337 — after, it is said, being rescued from supernatural darkness while hunting. Iberia thus became Christian around the time of, or shortly after, neighbouring Armenia's conversion.

Consequences

The new faith tied Iberia to the Byzantine world, spurred church building such as the Svetitskhoveli Cathedral at Mtskheta, and eventually the creation of the Georgian alphabet and a rich Christian literature. Iberia remained contested between Christian Byzantium and Zoroastrian Persia for centuries, and the Georgian Orthodox Church traces its origins to this conversion.
