Grand slam (baseball)

[Image placeholder: Grand slam home run swing]

In baseball, a grand slam is a home run hit with all three bases occupied by baserunners ("bases loaded"), thereby scoring four runs — the most possible in one play. A walk-off grand slam that erases a three-run deficit is sometimes called an "ultimate grand slam." The feat is among the most dramatic offensive plays in the sport.

Records

Alex Rodriguez holds the Major League Baseball career record with 25 grand slams, surpassing Lou Gehrig's long-standing mark of 23. Don Mattingly set the single-season record with six grand slams in 1987, a mark tied by Travis Hafner in 2006. Fernando Tatís famously hit two grand slams in one inning for the St. Louis Cardinals on 23 April 1999, both off pitcher Chan Ho Park of the Los Angeles Dodgers — the only such occurrence in MLB history.

Notable grand slams

Famous grand slams include Tony Lazzeri's in the 1936 World Series, the first in Series history by a player on the winning side's rout of the Giants; Bobby Thomson's pennant-winning 1951 "Shot Heard 'Round the World" was not a grand slam but is often confused with one. In the World Series, grand slams have been hit by players such as Mickey Mantle (1953) and Yogi Berra (1956). Freddie Freeman hit the first walk-off grand slam in World Series history in Game 1 of the 2024 World Series for the Los Angeles Dodgers against the New York Yankees.

Terminology

The term "grand slam" is borrowed from contract bridge, where it denotes winning all thirteen tricks, and is shared with other sports such as tennis and golf for their own supreme achievements. A home run with fewer runners aboard is sometimes informally counted a "slam" only when the bases are loaded; otherwise it is simply a home run.
