The American Quarter Horse Association (AQHA), based in Amarillo, Texas, is an international organization dedicated to the preservation, improvement and record-keeping of the American Quarter Horse. Founded on March 14, 1940, in Fort Worth, Texas, the AQHA maintains the largest equine breed registry in the world, having registered more than five million American Quarter Horses.

History

The association was formed at a meeting of breeders and ranchers during the Fort Worth Fat Stock Show in 1940, when a group of horsemen in the southwestern United States resolved to preserve the type of short-distance sprinting and ranch horse descended from colonial-era stock crossed with western mustangs and later Thoroughbred bloodlines. Wimpy P-1, winner of the stallion class at the 1941 Fort Worth show, was given registration number one. Headquarters moved to Amarillo in 1946.

Activities

The AQHA sanctions horse shows and races, maintains pedigree and performance records, and runs programs including the AQHA World Championship Show, the Bank of America Racing Challenge, youth programs through the American Quarter Horse Youth Association, and the American Quarter Horse Hall of Fame and Museum in Amarillo. It also publishes breed journals and supports equine research and animal welfare initiatives.

Registration controversies

The association's rules have at times been controversial, notably the former exclusion of horses with excessive white markings (the "white rule", relaxed in 2004), the registration of cloned horses (rejected in litigation concluded in 2015), and rules governing genetic diseases such as HYPP inherited from the influential sire Impressive.
