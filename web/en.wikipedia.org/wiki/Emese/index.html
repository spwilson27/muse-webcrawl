Emese

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Dream of Emese, a foundational legend of the Hungarians

Emese (also Emes or Emese, mother of Álmos) is a figure in Hungarian mythology and medieval chronicles, the mother of High Prince Álmos, founder of the Árpád dynasty that ruled the Kingdom of Hungary. She is best known for the legend of her dream, in which the mythical Turul bird appeared to her.

The legend of Emese's dream

According to the Gesta Hungarorum of the anonymous notary of King Béla III (written around 1200), Emese, the wife (or daughter) of the chieftain Ögyek/Eunedubel, became pregnant, and in a dream a divine vision in the form of a Turul — a great falcon-like bird of prey in Hungarian mythology — came to her and told her that from her womb a great river would spring, and that her descendants would be glorious kings in a foreign land. She named her son Álmos, from the Hungarian word álom ("dream").

The legend is interpreted as a dynastic origin myth: Álmos led the Hungarian tribes into the Carpathian Basin at the end of the 9th century, and his son Árpád became the founder of the Hungarian state. Emese's name itself is sometimes explained as deriving from the archaic Hungarian word eme (mother) with a diminutive suffix.
