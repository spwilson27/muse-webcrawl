Ascending chain condition

In mathematics, the ascending chain condition (ACC) is a finiteness property of partially ordered sets and of algebraic structures such as modules and rings. In ring theory

A module satisfies the ACC on submodules if and only if it is a Noetherian module; a commutative ring satisfying the ACC on ideals is a Noetherian ring, named after Emmy Noether. Hilbert's basis theorem shows that polynomial rings over a Noetherian ring are Noetherian, a cornerstone of commutative algebra. The condition is equivalent to the statement that every nonempty set of submodules has a maximal element, and to every submodule being finitely generated.

Related conditions

The dual property is the descending chain condition (DCC), satisfied by Artinian modules and rings. In order theory, ACC together with the absence of infinite antichains is related to well-quasi-orderings.
