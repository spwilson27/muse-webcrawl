TypeMemorial museum

The Memorial of the Victims of Communism and of the Resistance (Romanian: Memorialul Victimelor Comunismului și al Rezistenței) is a memorial and museum in Sighetu Marmației, in the Maramureș region of northern Romania. It occupies the former Sighet prison, where many prominent Romanians — politicians, clergy, academics, and officers — were imprisoned and died under the communist regime after 1948.

History

The Sighet prison was built in 1897, during the Austro-Hungarian period. After Romania came under communist rule, the prison was used from 1948 to hold political detainees, including former prime minister Iuliu Maniu, who died there in 1953, the historian Gheorghe I. Brătianu, and numerous bishops of the Greek Catholic Church. Many prisoners died from malnutrition, cold, disease, and ill-treatment, and were buried in unmarked graves in the nearby "Paupers' Cemetery."

The memorial

In 1993 the Civic Academy Foundation, led by the poet Ana Blandiana and the writer Romulus Rusan, took over the ruined prison and transformed it into a museum. Each cell was converted into an exhibition room documenting aspects of communist repression in Romania and across Eastern Europe: collectivization, political trials, censorship, the Securitate secret police, and the anti-communist resistance in the mountains.

Recognition

In 1998 the Council of Europe recognized the Sighet Memorial as one of the principal memorial sites of the continent, alongside the Auschwitz-Birkenau memorial and the Normandy Peace Memorial. The memorial also includes an International Centre for Studies on Communism and hosts an annual summer school for students.
