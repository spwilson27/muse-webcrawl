The Pittsburgh Penguins are a professional ice hockey team based in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. Founded in 1967 as one of the six expansion teams that doubled the size of the National Hockey League, they compete in the Metropolitan Division of the Eastern Conference and play their home games at PPG Paints Arena.

History

The Penguins struggled through much of their early history but were transformed by drafting Mario Lemieux first overall in 1984. With Lemieux — who later bought the team out of bankruptcy in 1999 — and stars such as Jaromír Jágr, the Penguins won back-to-back Stanley Cups in 1991 and 1992. A second golden era followed the selection of Sidney Crosby in 2005: the team reached the Final in 2008, won the Cup in 2009, and added consecutive championships in 2016 and 2017, becoming the first team in the salary-cap era to repeat.

Notable players

Franchise icons include Lemieux, Crosby, Evgeni Malkin, Kris Letang, Jágr, Ron Francis, and goaltender Marc-André Fleury. Lemieux, Crosby, and Malkin each won the Hart Trophy as league MVP.

Rivalries

The Penguins' fiercest rivals are the Philadelphia Flyers (the "Battle of Pennsylvania") and the Washington Capitals, the latter fuelled by the Crosby–Alexander Ovechkin era.
