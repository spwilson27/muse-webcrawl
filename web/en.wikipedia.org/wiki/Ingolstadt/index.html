Ingolstadt

Ingolstadt (German: [ˈɪŋɡɔlˌʃtat]) is a city in Bavaria, Germany, on the banks of the Danube, in the centre of Bavaria. With a population of around 140,000, it is the fifth-largest city in Bavaria. The city is the headquarters of the automobile manufacturer Audi and of the electronics retailer MediaMarkt, and it played a notable role in German history as a ducal residence, a fortress city, and the home of Bavaria's first university.

History

Ingolstadt was first mentioned in a document of Charlemagne dated 806 as Ingoldes stat, "the site of Ingold." Around 1250 it received town privileges. From 1392 to 1447 it was the capital of the Duchy of Bavaria-Ingolstadt. In 1472 Duke Louis IX founded the University of Ingolstadt, the first university in Bavaria, which became a centre of the Counter-Reformation before being moved to Landshut in 1800 and eventually to Munich, where it survives as Ludwig Maximilian University.

From the sixteenth century onward Ingolstadt was developed into a major Bavarian state fortress. It withstood a siege by Gustavus Adolphus of Sweden in 1632 during the Thirty Years' War; the Swedish king's horse was killed outside the walls, an episode commemorated by the stuffed horse displayed in the city museum, one of the oldest preserved taxidermy animals in the world. In 1776 Adam Weishaupt, a professor at the university, founded the secret society known as the Illuminati in the city.

In literature

Ingolstadt is famous in English literature as the place where Victor Frankenstein creates his monster in Mary Shelley's 1818 novel Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus, in which the young scientist studies at the university there.

Economy

The modern city is dominated by the automotive industry: Audi's largest production plant and corporate headquarters are located in Ingolstadt, employing tens of thousands. Oil refining and the electronics retail groups MediaMarkt and Saturn (founded in the city) are also significant employers.

Sights

Landmarks include the Gothic Liebfrauenmünster church, the medieval Kreuztor gate, the New Castle (Neues Schloss) housing the Bavarian Army Museum, the Old Town Hall, and the Asam Church of Maria de Victoria, with its celebrated ceiling fresco by Cosmas Damian Asam.
