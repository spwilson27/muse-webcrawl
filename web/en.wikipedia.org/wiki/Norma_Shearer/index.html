Norma Shearer

[Image: Norma Shearer publicity photo]

Edith Norma Shearer (August 11, 1902 – June 12, 1983) was a Canadian-American actress who was active in film from 1919 through 1942. One of the most popular actresses of the 1930s, she won the Academy Award for Best Actress for The Divorcee (1930) and was nominated five further times.

Early life and career

Born in Montreal, Shearer moved to New York after her family's fortunes declined and began working as a model and film extra. Signed by Irving Thalberg, she rose at Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer with films such as He Who Gets Slapped (1924) and The Student Prince in Old Heidelberg (1927). In 1927 she married Thalberg, MGM's head of production, becoming known as the "First Lady of MGM".

Sound era stardom

Shearer's sophisticated, independent screen persona flourished in pre-Code dramas such as The Divorcee, A Free Soul (1931), and Smilin' Through (1932). Later prestige roles included The Barretts of Wimpole Street (1934), Romeo and Juliet (1936), Marie Antoinette (1938), and the ensemble comedy The Women (1939). After Thalberg's death in 1936 her career gradually wound down, and she retired following Her Cardboard Lover (1942).

Legacy

Shearer is credited with helping discover actors such as Janet Leigh and Robert Evans in her later years. The American Film Institute ranked her among the great female stars of classic American cinema. She died in 1983 in Woodland Hills, Los Angeles.
