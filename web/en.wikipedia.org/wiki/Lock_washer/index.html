Lock washer

A lock washer is a type of washer designed to prevent a threaded fastener, such as a bolt or nut, from loosening under vibration or torque. Lock washers work by biting into the mating surfaces, by spring tension, or by locking features that resist rotation.

Types

  • Split lock washer — a helical, spring-like ring whose sharp edges dig into the nut and the mounting surface.
  • Tooth lock washer — has internal, external, or combined teeth that bite into the fastener and surface; external-tooth types give greater resistance for larger bolts.
  • Wave washer — a wavy ring that acts as a spring when compressed.
  • Wedge-lock washer — a pair of washers with cam faces and radial teeth, used in demanding bolted joints.
  • Tab washer — tabs are bent up against the flats of a nut to lock it mechanically.

Effectiveness

The effectiveness of common split lock washers has been questioned: studies and fastener-engineering references (including NASA's fastener design manual) report that a split washer flattened under load behaves largely like a plain washer and offers little protection against self-loosening from transverse vibration. Alternatives include prevailing-torque nuts, thread-locking fluid, castellated nuts with cotter pins, and proper joint design and preload.
