Kuiper belt

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Kuiper belt is a circumstellar disc in the outer Solar System, extending from the orbit of Neptune (at 30 AU) to roughly 50 AU from the Sun. It is a region of icy bodies — remnants from the Solar System's formation — including dwarf planets such as Pluto, Haumea and Makemake, and is thought to be the source of short-period comets.

Discovery

The belt was hypothesised by Gerard Kuiper and Kenneth Edgeworth in the mid-20th century but only confirmed from 1992, when David Jewitt and Jane Luu discovered the object Albion (1992 QB1), the first known Kuiper belt object after Pluto and Charon.

Composition and significance

Kuiper belt objects are rich in frozen volatiles such as methane, ammonia and water ice. NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew past Pluto in 2015 and the contact-binary object Arrokoth in 2019, the most distant object visited by a spacecraft. Beyond the belt lies the scattered disc and the hypothesised Oort cloud.
