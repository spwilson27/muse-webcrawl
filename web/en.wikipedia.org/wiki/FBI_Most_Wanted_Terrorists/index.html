FBI Most Wanted Terrorists

The FBI Most Wanted Terrorists is a list maintained by the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation of fugitives indicted for acts of terrorism against Americans. It was created in the aftermath of the September 11 attacks and first announced by President George W. Bush on 10 October 2001.

History

The initial list named 22 terrorists sought in connection with attacks including the 1998 US embassy bombings in East Africa, the 1996 Khobar Towers bombing, the 1993 World Trade Center bombing and the plot against US airliners known as the Bojinka plot. The list is distinct from the older FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list, established in 1950. Many of those originally named have since been captured or killed, including al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden, who headed al-Qaeda and remained at large until his death in 2011.

Selection and rewards

Nominees are proposed by the FBI's Counterterrorism Division and approved by the Director. Rewards for information leading to apprehension are offered through the State Department's Rewards for Justice program, in some cases reaching tens of millions of dollars.
