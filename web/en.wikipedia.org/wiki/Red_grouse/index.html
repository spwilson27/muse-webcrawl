Red grouse

The red grouse (Lagopus lagopus scotica) is a medium-sized game bird in the grouse family. It is a subspecies of the willow ptarmigan and is endemic to the British Isles, found on heather moorland in northern and western Great Britain and Ireland. It differs from the willow ptarmigan in not turning white in winter.

Description

The red grouse is a plump bird roughly 37–42 cm long, with reddish-brown plumage, a short black tail and a distinctive red comb above the eye, which is especially prominent in males. Its call is a distinctive "go-bak, go-bak" sound.

Shooting

Red grouse are among the most sought-after game birds in Britain. Driven grouse shooting, in which beaters flush the birds over a line of waiting guns, is a major rural industry on managed moors. The grouse shooting season opens on 12 August, known as the "Glorious Twelfth", and runs to 10 December. Management of moors for grouse, particularly heather burning and predator control, is a subject of conservation controversy.
