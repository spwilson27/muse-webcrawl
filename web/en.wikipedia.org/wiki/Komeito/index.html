Komeito (公明党, Kōmeitō), formerly New Komeito, is a political party in Japan founded by lay members of the Buddhist organisation Soka Gakkai in 1964. Since 1999 it has been the junior coalition partner of the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) in almost all Japanese governments, providing electoral support and a moderating voice on welfare, social policy and security issues. The party's disciplined support base among Soka Gakkai members makes it one of the most reliable vote-getting organisations in Japanese politics.

History

Komeito ("Clean Government Party") was founded in 1964 after Soka Gakkai's political league had won seats in the House of Councillors. Campaigning on clean politics and welfare, it won 47 seats in its first general election in 1967 and became a major opposition force. In 1970, following a scandal over attempts to suppress a book critical of Soka Gakkai, the party formally separated religion and politics and moderated its platform. In the 1990s realignments it joined the anti-LDP New Frontier Party (1994), and after that party's collapse the party was relaunched in 1998 as New Komeito. In 1999 it entered coalition with the LDP under Keizō Obuchi; except for 2009–2012, when the Democratic Party of Japan governed, the LDP–Komeito coalition has held power continuously since, and the party reverted to the name Komeito in 2014. Long-serving leaders include Takeiri Yoshikatsu, Ōta Akihiro, Yamaguchi Natsuo (chief representative 2009–2024) and Ishii Keiichi, who succeeded Yamaguchi in 2024.

Ideology and policies

Komeito combines social conservatism with an emphasis on welfare — "politics for the people's livelihood" — including support for child allowances, free education measures and disaster relief, shaped by Buddhist humanism derived from Nichiren Buddhism. It has traditionally been dovish on security policy: it pressed to limit the 2015 security legislation enabling collective self-defence, is cautious toward constitutional revision of Article 9, favours nuclear disarmament, and maintains friendly channels with China, often acting as a bridge in LDP diplomacy with Beijing.

Relationship with Soka Gakkai

Although formally independent since 1970, Komeito's voters come overwhelmingly from the roughly eight-million-household Soka Gakkai membership, whose local chapters mobilise votes and candidates. The party's steady bloc of votes in proportional-representation blocs and its candidates' dependence on LDP-endorsed vote trades in single-member districts underpin the coalition arithmetic of Japanese politics.
