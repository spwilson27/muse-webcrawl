Furniture

Furniture refers to movable objects intended to support various human activities such as seating (e.g., stools, chairs, and sofas), eating (tables), storing items, working on an elevated surface and sleeping (e.g., beds and hammocks). Furniture is also used to hold objects at a convenient height for work (as horizontal surfaces above the ground, such as tables and desks), or to store things (e.g., cupboards and shelves).

Furniture can be a product of design and can be considered a form of decorative art. In addition to furniture's functional role, it can serve a symbolic or religious purpose. It can be made from a vast multitude of materials, including metal, plastic, and wood. Furniture can be made using a variety of woodworking joints which often reflect the local culture.

History

People have been using natural objects, such as tree stumps, rocks and moss, as furniture since the beginning of human civilisation. Archaeological evidence suggests that people began to construct and carve their own furniture approximately 30,000 years ago. Wooden furniture from the Neolithic period has been found in Scotland at Skara Brae.

Early furniture from this period is known from artwork such as a Venus figurine found in Russia, depicting the goddess on a throne. The first surviving extant furniture is in the homes of Skara Brae in Scotland, and includes cupboards, dressers and beds all constructed from stone. Complex construction techniques such as joinery began in the early dynastic period of ancient Egypt.

Types

Seating

Seating includes chairs, stools, sofas, benches, ottomans and recliners, designed for sitting.

Surfaces

Tables and desks provide flat elevated surfaces for eating, writing and working.

Storage

Cupboards, wardrobes, drawers, shelves and bookcases store clothing, books and household items.

Sleeping

Beds, futons, hammocks and daybeds are designed for rest and sleep.

Materials and construction

Traditional furniture is made primarily of wood, joined by mortise and tenon, dovetail and other joints, often finished with veneer, lacquer or upholstery. Modern furniture also uses metal, glass, plastic and engineered woods such as plywood and medium-density fibreboard. Mass production from the Industrial Revolution onward made furniture widely affordable, and 20th-century movements such as Bauhaus and Scandinavian design reshaped its forms.
