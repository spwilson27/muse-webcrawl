Heide Museum of Modern Art

The Heide Museum of Modern Art, commonly known as Heide, is an art museum in Bulleen, a suburb of Melbourne, Victoria, Australia. Set in 6.5 hectares of gardens and parkland on the banks of the Yarra River, the museum occupies a site that was central to the development of modern art in Australia. The property was purchased in 1934 by art patrons John and Sunday Reed, who made its farmhouse a gathering place for the circle of artists known as the Heide Circle—including Sidney Nolan, Albert Tucker, Joy Hester, John Perceval and Danila Vassilieff.

History

The Reeds transformed the dairy farm into a centre of modernist patronage; it was at Heide that Sidney Nolan painted his famous Ned Kelly series in 1946–47. In 1964 the Reeds commissioned the modernist residence now known as Heide II from architect David McGlashan; a modern building in limestone and glass, it later became the museum's first gallery. The Reeds gifted the property to the people of Victoria, and the museum opened to the public in November 1981, shortly after their deaths. A third building, Heide III, opened in 1993 with larger exhibition spaces, and a major addition, the Albert and Barbara Tucker Gallery, opened in 2006. Architect Andrew Andersons' Central Galleries completed a further redevelopment in 2023.

Collection and grounds

The museum's collection numbers several thousand works, with strengths in modern and contemporary Australian art and an archive relating to the Heide circle. The grounds contain kitchen gardens, the old farmhouse (Heide I), sculpture park installations, and the Sidney Nolan Kelly cottage.
