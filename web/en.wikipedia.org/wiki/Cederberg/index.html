Cederberg

Weathered sandstone formations are characteristic of the Cederberg.

The Cederberg is a mountain range and nature reserve located near Clanwilliam in the Western Cape province of South Africa, roughly 300 km north of Cape Town. The range is named after the endangered Clanwilliam cedar (Widdringtonia wallichii), a tree endemic to the area that grows in rocky outcrops at higher elevations.

Geology and landscape

The Cederberg is formed of Cape Fold Belt sandstone, eroded into dramatic formations such as the Maltese Cross, the Wolfberg Arch and Cracks, and the Stadsaal Caves. The highest peak is Sneeuberg at 2,027 metres. The vivid orange and red hues of the rock result from iron oxide in the sandstone.

Flora and fauna

The vegetation is principally mountain fynbos, part of the Cape Floristic Region noted for its exceptional plant diversity. The area is famous for rooibos (Aspalathus linearis), cultivated commercially in the surrounding district, and for spring wildflower displays. Animals include leopards, caracals, baboons, dassies (rock hyraxes), and numerous antelope species.

Rock art and history

The Cederberg contains many San (Bushman) rock painting sites, some thousands of years old, including images of elephants and people at Stadsaal and Truitjieskraal. The Cederberg Wilderness Area, covering about 71,000 hectares, is managed by CapeNature and is popular for hiking, rock climbing, and bouldering.
