Haemorrhoids (or hemorrhoids in American English), also known as piles, are vascular structures in the anal canal. In their normal state they are cushions that help with stool control. They become a disease when swollen or inflamed; the unqualified term "haemorrhoid" is often used to refer to the disease. The signs and symptoms of haemorrhoids depend on the type present.

Types and symptoms

Internal haemorrhoids arise above the dentate line and are usually painless, presenting with bright red rectal bleeding during bowel movements; they are graded I–IV by degree of prolapse. External haemorrhoids form below the dentate line and may cause pain, swelling, and itching, particularly when thrombosed — a thrombosed external haemorrhoid appears as a painful, bluish lump at the anal verge.

Causes

The exact cause is not fully understood, but factors that increase pressure in the anal cushions and weaken supporting tissue are implicated: chronic constipation and straining, sitting for long periods on the toilet, low-fibre diet, obesity, pregnancy, and ageing. About 50% of people will experience symptomatic haemorrhoids at some point in their lives, and prevalence peaks between ages 45 and 65.

Treatment

Conservative measures — increased fibre and fluid intake, avoidance of straining, warm sitz baths, and topical preparations — suffice for many cases. Office procedures for persistent internal haemorrhoids include rubber band ligation (the most effective office treatment), sclerotherapy, and infrared coagulation. Surgical options, including excisional haemorrhoidectomy and stapled haemorrhoidopexy, are reserved for high-grade or refractory disease. Rectal bleeding should be investigated to exclude other causes such as colorectal cancer.

History

Haemorrhoids have been described since antiquity: the Edwin Smith Papyrus and the Hippocratic corpus discuss treatments including cautery and ligation, and the condition's sufferers famously included Napoleon, whose severe attack during the Battle of Waterloo is a popular (if debated) anecdote.
