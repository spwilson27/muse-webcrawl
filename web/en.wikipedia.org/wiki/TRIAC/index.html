TRIAC

A TRIAC (triode for alternating current) is a three-terminal electronic component that can conduct current in either direction when triggered. It is effectively a pair of anti-parallel thyristors (SCRs) integrated into a single device with a common gate terminal, making it well suited to controlling alternating-current power.

Operation

The three terminals are usually labelled MT1, MT2 (main terminals) and G (gate). A small gate current pulse triggers conduction between the main terminals; the TRIAC continues to conduct until the current through it falls below the holding current, which naturally happens near each zero crossing of the AC waveform. By varying the point in each half-cycle at which the gate is triggered (phase control), the average power delivered to a load can be adjusted.

Applications

TRIACs are ubiquitous in light dimmers, fan and motor speed controls, and heating controls. They are inexpensive and simple, but phase-control switching generates harmonics and radio-frequency interference, and TRIACs can behave poorly with highly inductive loads unless snubber circuits are used. For very high power or where cleaner waveforms are needed, back-to-back SCRs or other devices may be preferred. Related four-layer devices include the DIAC, often used in TRIAC trigger circuits, and the quadrac, which integrates a DIAC and TRIAC in one package.
