Horse Guards (building)


Horse Guards from Whitehall

Horse Guards is a historic building in the City of Westminster, London, between Whitehall and Horse Guards Parade. It was built in the Palladian style between 1750 and 1759 to a design by William Kent and John Vardy, replacing an earlier guardhouse of the Palace of Whitehall. It is a Grade I listed building and the official entrance to St James's and Buckingham Palaces.

History and use

The building served as the headquarters of the British Army's General Staff until the creation of the Ministry of Defence nearby. Its clock tower is topped by a clock whose face famously has a dark mark on the numeral II, said to commemorate the time of the execution of Charles I in 1649. Through the central archway runs the only entrance at which mounted sentries of the Household Cavalry stand guard, one of London's best-known tourist sights. The changing of the King's Life Guard takes place at Horse Guards daily.

Horse Guards Parade

On the west side of the building lies Horse Guards Parade, a large ceremonial ground originally the tiltyard of Whitehall Palace. It hosts the annual Trooping the Colour ceremony on the sovereign's official birthday and the Beating Retreat, and in 2012 it was temporarily converted into the beach volleyball arena for the London Olympic Games.
