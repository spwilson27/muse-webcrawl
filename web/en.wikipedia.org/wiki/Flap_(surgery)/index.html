Flap (surgery)

Flap surgery
FieldPlastic and reconstructive surgery
PurposeReconstruction of tissue defects

Flap surgery is a technique in plastic and reconstructive surgery where tissue is lifted from a donor site and moved to a recipient site with an intact blood supply. Because the tissue remains connected to its own blood vessels (or is reconnected microsurgically), a flap can bring skin, fat, muscle, or bone into a wound to repair defects caused by trauma, cancer resection, or congenital conditions.

Classification

Flaps are classified by their blood supply (random pattern or axial), their location relative to the defect (local, regional or distant), and their composition (cutaneous, fasciocutaneous, muscle, myocutaneous, osseous, or composite). A free flap is completely detached and its vessels are anastomosed to vessels at the recipient site using microsurgery; a pedicled flap remains attached to the body by its vascular pedicle.

Common examples

Well-known flaps include the TRAM and DIEP flaps used in breast reconstruction, the radial forearm free flap, the latissimus dorsi flap, and the forehead flap used in nasal reconstruction since antiquity. Careful postoperative monitoring of flap perfusion is essential, since vascular compromise can lead to flap failure.
