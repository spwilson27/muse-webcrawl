Animal Farm is an allegorical novella by George Orwell, first published in England on 17 August 1945. Subtitled "A Fairy Story", it tells of a group of farm animals who rebel against their human owner in the hope of creating an equal society, only to find a new tyranny under the pigs.

Plot summary

On Manor Farm, the animals, inspired by the boar Old Major's doctrine of Animalism, overthrow the drunken Mr Jones. The pigs Snowball and Napoleon emerge as leaders. Napoleon expels Snowball with his guard dogs and gradually concentrates power: the Seven Commandments are rewritten one by one, the loyal cart-horse Boxer is worked to death and sold to the knacker, and propaganda by the pig Squealer rewrites history. The commandments are finally reduced to one: "All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others." In the last scene the pigs, walking upright and dressed as humans, become indistinguishable from the men they replaced.

Themes and allegory

Orwell, a democratic socialist, wrote the book as a satire on the corruption of the Russian Revolution and the rise of Stalinism: Napoleon represents Stalin, Snowball Trotsky, and the farm the Soviet Union. Its themes include the corrupting nature of power, the manipulation of language, and the betrayal of revolutions.

Publication and reception

Written during the Second World War, the manuscript was rejected by several publishers — including T. S. Eliot at Faber — because Britain was allied with the USSR; Orwell's preface on censorship, "The Freedom of the Press", was published only decades later. The book made Orwell's reputation and, with Nineteen Eighty-Four, is among the most influential political works of the twentieth century.
