Victory Day (9 May)

Victory Day parade on Red Square, Moscow.

Victory Day is a holiday that commemorates the victory of the Soviet Union over Nazi Germany in 1945. It was inaugurated in the 15 republics of the Soviet Union following the signing of the German Instrument of Surrender late in the evening on 8 May 1945 (9 May Moscow time). The holiday is celebrated on 9 May in Russia and many other post-Soviet states, where it is a major public holiday marked by military parades, fireworks, and the remembrance of the roughly 27 million Soviet citizens who died in the Great Patriotic War.

History

The surrender document was signed in Berlin-Karlshorst shortly before midnight on 8 May; because of the time difference, 9 May was already beginning in Moscow. Victory Day was declared a non-working day in the USSR in 1965 on its twentieth anniversary, and celebrations grew to include a major annual military parade on Red Square. In Western Europe, by contrast, Victory in Europe Day (VE Day) is observed on 8 May.

Observances

Traditions include laying flowers at war memorials and the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, televised addresses by the head of state, a minute of silence, and evening salute firestorms. The Immortal Regiment marches, begun in Tomsk in 2012, in which participants carry photographs of relatives who fought, became a prominent feature of the holiday in the 2010s. Georgia's St. George's ribbon became a widely used symbol.
