PDCA

PDCA (plan–do–check–act or plan–do–check–adjust) is an iterative design and management method used in business for the control and continual improvement of processes and products. It is also known as the Deming circle, Deming cycle, Shewhart cycle, or PDSA cycle (plan–do–study–act).

The four phases

Plan

Establish the objectives and processes necessary to deliver results in accordance with the expected output. By establishing output expectations, the completeness and accuracy of the specification are checked, and where possible the plan is tested on a small scale.

Do

Implement the plan, execute the process, and make the product. Collect data for charting and analysis in the following "check" and "act" steps.

Check

Study the actual results (measured and collected in "do" above) and compare them against the expected results (targets or goals from the "plan") to ascertain any differences. Look for deviations in implementation from the plan and for the appropriateness and completeness of the plan itself.

Act

Take corrective actions on significant differences between actual and planned results. Analyze the differences to determine their root causes, and decide where to apply changes that will include improvement of the process or product.

Origins

The cycle was developed from the work of Walter A. Shewhart, the father of statistical quality control, and was popularized by W. Edwards Deming, who referred to it as the Shewhart cycle. Deming himself advocated the PDSA cycle, arguing that "study" better captures the analytical intent of the third phase. PDCA became a foundation of total quality management (TQM) and is embedded in the ISO 9001 quality management standard and in the structure of many other management system standards.

Applications

PDCA is widely used in manufacturing, healthcare, software development, and lean management. It is closely related to the scientific method and to iterative approaches such as kaizen.
