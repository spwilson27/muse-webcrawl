Billings ovulation method

The Billings ovulation method is a method in which women use their vaginal mucus to determine their fertility. It does not rely on counting days or taking temperature, but on recognising patterns of cervical mucus that reflect ovarian hormone changes.

Billings ovulation method
Type: Fertility awareness
Developed by: John Billings and Evelyn Billings, Melbourne, 1950s–60s
Based on: Cervical mucus observation

Method

Users chart daily observations of sensation and appearance of mucus at the vulva. A basic infertile pattern of dryness or unchanging mucus gives way, as ovulation approaches, to increasingly wet, slippery, stretchy mucus resembling raw egg white; the last day of this fertile-type mucus is the Peak day, closely related to ovulation.

Use and effectiveness

The method is taught for both achieving and avoiding pregnancy and is promoted in many countries, notably by WOOMB International. Effectiveness depends on correct teaching and use; studies report varying perfect- and typical-use rates.

History

Doctors John and Evelyn Billings developed the method in Melbourne, Australia, initially for couples seeking a natural method compatible with Catholic teaching.
