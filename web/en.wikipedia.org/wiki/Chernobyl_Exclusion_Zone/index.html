The Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant Zone of Alienation, commonly known as the Chernobyl Exclusion Zone, is an officially designated exclusion area around the site of the Chernobyl nuclear disaster of 26 April 1986. It covers an area of approximately 2,600 square kilometres in northern Ukraine, with a corresponding Polesie State Radioecological Reserve in Belarus.

History

The zone was established by the Soviet military shortly after the disaster and was initially a 30 km radius area from which some 336,000 people were eventually evacuated, including the entire population of the city of Pripyat and the town of Chernobyl. Access remains restricted and is controlled by checkpoints, although a small number of elderly former residents ("self-settlers") returned to live in their villages.

Environment

With human activity largely absent, the zone has become an inadvertent wildlife sanctuary; populations of wolves, lynx, bears, beavers, and reintroduced European bison and Przewalski's horses have been documented. Radiation levels vary greatly across the zone, remaining highest near the power plant and in areas such as the Red Forest. The destroyed Reactor No. 4 was covered by the New Safe Confinement structure in 2016.

Access and tourism

Before 2022, guided tours brought tens of thousands of visitors annually to Pripyat and the plant vicinity. The zone was briefly occupied during the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine. Ongoing work includes decommissioning of the plant's reactors and long-term management of radioactive waste.
