Ligamentum arteriosum

Ligamentum arteriosum
PrecursorDuctus arteriosus
FromAortic arch
ToPulmonary trunk

The ligamentum arteriosum (arterial ligament) is a small fibrous band in the thorax that connects the underside of the aortic arch to the top of the left pulmonary artery, near the bifurcation of the pulmonary trunk. It is the postnatal remnant of the ductus arteriosus, the fetal vessel that shunted blood away from the fluid-filled lungs by passing it from the pulmonary trunk directly into the aorta.

After birth, with the onset of breathing, the ductus arteriosus constricts — under the influence of rising oxygen tension and falling prostaglandin levels — and normally closes within the first days of life. Over the following weeks it fibroses to become the ligamentum arteriosum. Failure of the ductus to close results in a patent ductus arteriosus, a congenital heart condition.

Clinical significance

The ligament is an important anatomical landmark because the left recurrent laryngeal nerve, a branch of the vagus nerve, hooks underneath the aortic arch just lateral to the ligamentum arteriosum before ascending to the larynx. Enlargement of nearby structures, or surgery in this region, can injure the nerve and cause vocal cord paralysis and hoarseness. In rare cases the ligament itself can calcify, and in traumatic aortic injury the isthmus near the ligament's attachment is a common site of rupture.
