The Pilgrim's Progress

The Pilgrim's Progress from This World, to That Which Is to Come is a Christian allegory written by John Bunyan and published in 1678. It is regarded as one of the most significant works of English religious literature and has been translated more widely than almost any book except the Bible.

AuthorJohn Bunyan
Published18 February 1678
GenreReligious allegory

Plot

Christian, fleeing the City of Destruction with a burden on his back, journeys to the Celestial City. Along the way he passes the Slough of Despond, Vanity Fair, the Delectable Mountains and the River of Death, meeting figures such as Evangelist, Faithful, Hopeful and Giant Despair. A second part, published in 1684, follows his wife Christiana and their children.

Influence

Bunyan wrote the book during imprisonment for unlicensed preaching. Its vivid characters and phrases — "Vanity Fair", "the Slough of Despond" — entered the English language, and the allegory shaped English-speaking Protestant culture for centuries, influencing writers from Charlotte Brontë to C. S. Lewis.
