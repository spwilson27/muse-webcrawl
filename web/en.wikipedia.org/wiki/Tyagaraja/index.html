Tyagaraja

Born4 May 1767, Tiruvarur, Thanjavur Maratha kingdom
Died6 January 1847, Thiruvaiyaru
OccupationComposer, vocalist
Known forCarnatic music compositions (kritis); Pancharatna Kriti
TraditionBhakti; devotee of Rama

Tyagaraja (4 May 1767 – 6 January 1847), also rendered Saint Tyagaraja and Tyagayya, was a composer and vocalist of Carnatic music, the classical music tradition of South India. Along with Muthuswami Dikshitar and Syama Sastri, he forms the "Trinity of Carnatic music". Tyagaraja composed thousands of devotional compositions (kritis), most in Telugu and in praise of Rama, the Hindu god, whom he regarded as his personal deity. Five of his compositions, the Pancharatna Kritis ("five gems"), are sung as a group at the Tyagaraja Aradhana festival each year.

Tyagaraja is revered as a saint-composer whose kritis remain the core repertoire of Carnatic music.

Life

Tyagaraja was born in Tiruvarur into a Telugu Brahmin family; his grandfather Giriraja Kavi had been a poet at the Thanjavur court. He grew up in Thiruvaiyaru, on the banks of the Kaveri river, and learned music under the court musician Sonti Venkataramana. Stories of his life, conveyed through disciples, emphasise his uncompromising devotion: he is said to have refused the patronage and gifts of the Thanjavur king Serfoji II, regarding music offered for wealth as a violation of its sacred purpose, and to have lived simply on alms (unchavritti).

Compositions

Tyagaraja is credited with revolutionising Carnatic music. Tradition attributes some 24,000 compositions to him, of which roughly 700 survive with notation passed down through his disciples. He popularised the kriti form with its three sections (pallavi, anupallavi, charanam), introduced the sangati (systematic melodic variation of a refrain), and composed in both popular ragas and many rare ones. His works range from the grand Pancharatna Kritis in ragas Nata, Gaula, Arabhi, Varali, and Sri, to simple devotional songs and two musical dramas, Prahlada Bhakti Vijayam and Nauka Charitam. His compositions mostly use Telugu, with some in Sanskrit, and dwell on devotion (bhakti), moral conduct, and the spiritual inadequacy of worldly pursuits.

Legacy

Tyagaraja's samadhi (burial shrine) at Thiruvaiyaru is a place of pilgrimage, and the annual Tyagaraja Aradhana held there on Bahula Panchami — the day of his death — draws thousands of musicians, who gather to sing the Pancharatna Kritis in unison. Comparable festivals are held worldwide, including the well-known Cleveland Tyagaraja Festival in the United States. Tyagaraja's melodic and lyrical idiom continues to dominate Carnatic concert repertoire.
