The lingonberry (Vaccinium vitis-idaea), known in North America as the lowbush cranberry and in Britain as the cowberry, is a small evergreen shrub in the heath family Ericaceae bearing edible red berries. It is native to boreal forest and Arctic tundra throughout the Northern Hemisphere, from northern Europe and Asia to North America.

Description

The plant spreads by underground rhizomes to form dense mats. The upright stems reach 10–40 cm in height and carry small, glossy, oval evergreen leaves. Bell-shaped white or pale pink flowers appear in late spring; the round berries turn bright red when ripe in late summer to autumn.

Culinary use

Lingonberries are a staple of Scandinavian cuisine. Though tart, acidic raw — rich in vitamin C and benzoic acid, which acts as a natural preservative — they are most often cooked into lingonberry jam or preserves (Swedish: lingonsylt), traditionally served with meatballs, potato pancakes, and game. In Finland the berries accompany reindeer and liver dishes, and are whipped with semolina into the pink porridge vispipuuro. Finland and Sweden are major exporters of wild-harvested fruit.
