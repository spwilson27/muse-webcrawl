Caoshan Benji (Chinese: 曹山本寂; pinyin: Cáoshān Běnjì; Japanese: Sōzan Honjaku, 840–901) was a Chinese Chan Buddhist monk of the Tang dynasty. He was a dharma heir of Dongshan Liangjie and, with him, is regarded as co-founder of the Caodong school of Chan — the school's name combines the "Cao" of Caoshan with the "Dong" of Dongshan.

Biography

Traditional biographies record that Benji left home and was ordained at about the age of nineteen at Lingyan Temple in Fuzhou. He travelled to study with Dongshan Liangjie, under whom he attained awakening, reportedly on leaving his teacher, when he saw his own reflection crossing a river. After further training, he settled at Mount Cao (Caoshan) in present-day Jiangxi, where he taught a large community until his death in 901.

Teachings

Caoshan elaborated Dongshan's doctrine of the Five Ranks, providing detailed commentaries that expound the relationship between the absolute ("the prince") and the relative ("the minister"). Records of his dialogues are preserved in Chan transmission histories such as the Transmission of the Lamp, and his exchanges with students appear in later koan literature. Although his own lineage did not survive for many generations, the Caodong school continued through other branches of Dongshan's descendants.
