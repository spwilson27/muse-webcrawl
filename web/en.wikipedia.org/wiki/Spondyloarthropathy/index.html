Spondyloarthropathy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Spondyloarthropathy is a term for any disease of the joints of the vertebral column. In clinical practice it is most often used as a synonym for the family of inflammatory conditions known as spondyloarthritis (SpA), also called the seronegative spondyloarthropathies. These disorders characteristically affect the sacroiliac joints and spine, are associated with the HLA-B27 allele, and lack rheumatoid factor.

Seronegative spondyloarthropathies

The group comprises ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, reactive arthritis, enteropathic arthritis (associated with inflammatory bowel disease), and undifferentiated spondyloarthropathy. Common features include inflammatory back pain, enthesitis, dactylitis, uveitis, and skin or gut inflammation. A shared mnemonic lists the features under the acronym PAIR: Psoriasis, Ankylosing spondylitis, Inflammatory bowel disease, and Reactive arthritis.

Other uses of the term

Outside the inflammatory context, "spondyloarthropathy" and the related term "spondylopathy" can denote degenerative or other structural disorders of the spine, such as spondylosis (osteoarthritis of the spine) and deforming spondylosis with osteophyte formation. Medical coding systems (ICD-10) place the inflammatory spondylopathies in the M45–M49 block, which includes ankylosing spondylitis, spinal enthesopathy, spondylitis, and related conditions.
