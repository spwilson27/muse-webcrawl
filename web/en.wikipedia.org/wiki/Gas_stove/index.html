Gas stove

A modern domestic gas stove
TypeKitchen appliance
FuelNatural gas or liquefied petroleum gas
UsesCooking, boiling, frying, baking (combined ranges)

A gas stove is a stove that is fuelled by combustible gas such as natural gas, propane, or butane. Gas stoves use an open flame applied directly to cookware, allowing instant adjustment of heat. Before gas stoves became common, cooking relied on solid-fuel stoves burning wood or coal.

History

The first gas stove was developed in 1802 by the German inventor Zachäus Andreas Winzler, and in 1826 James Sharp patented a gas stove in Northampton, England; his design was marketed from the 1830s. Gas cooking spread as town gas supplies expanded in the 19th century, and by the 1920s gas ranges with ovens and thermostats had become standard household appliances in industrialized countries. Because municipal gas was originally manufactured coal gas, early stoves were later adapted for natural gas.

Design

A typical gas hob has several burners of different sizes, each with a control knob regulating gas flow through a valve. Modern stoves light the gas with an electric spark igniter (replacing the standing pilot light) and include safety devices such as flame failure cut-offs. Gas ranges combine a hob with a gas oven. Professional kitchens often favor gas for its rapid response and compatibility with woks and other cookware.

Health and environmental concerns

Burning gas indoors produces nitrogen dioxide, carbon monoxide, formaldehyde, and fine particulates, and gas stoves leak small amounts of unburned methane even when off. Studies have associated gas stove use with elevated indoor nitrogen dioxide levels and increased risk of childhood asthma symptoms. Concerns about indoor air quality and about greenhouse gas emissions have prompted debates over regulations and, in some cities, moves to restrict gas connections in new buildings in favor of electric and induction cooking.
