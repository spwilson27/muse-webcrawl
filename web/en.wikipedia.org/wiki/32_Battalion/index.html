32 Battalion (sometimes called the Buffalo Battalion) was a light infantry battalion of the South African Army founded in 1975 by Colonel Jan Breytenbach. Unusual among units of the apartheid-era military, it consisted largely of black Angolan soldiers — former fighters of the FNLA liberation movement — led by white South African officers, and became one of the most effective and controversial units of the South African Border War.

History

The battalion grew out of Bravo Group, a formation of ex-FNLA guerrillas recruited during the South African intervention in the Angolan civil war (Operation Savannah, 1975–76). When South Africa withdrew, many of these soldiers and their families relocated to a base in the Caprivi Strip rather than face reprisals. As 32 Battalion they conducted cross-border operations into Angola against SWAPO's PLAN guerrillas throughout the Border War, frequently operating deep inside Angola in support of UNITA. Their reputation for aggression earned them the Portuguese nickname Os Terríveis — "the Terrible Ones" — from their enemies.

Pomfret and disbandment

After Namibian independence the battalion was withdrawn to the remote mining town of Pomfret in the Northern Cape, where its soldiers and their families settled. The unit was disbanded in 1993, ahead of South Africa's transition to democracy. Many veterans later found work as private military contractors; the battalion's legacy remains contested, celebrated in memoirs by its officers but criticised in accounts of atrocities during the war, and its Pomfret community has struggled with neglect since disbandment.
