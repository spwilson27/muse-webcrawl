Boys State

[Image: Boys State delegates at a state capitol]

Boys State is a summer leadership and citizenship program for high school juniors in the United States, sponsored by the American Legion. Participants — called citizens or delegates — are divided into fictional cities and counties within a mock state, form political parties, campaign for office, and operate every level of government from municipal courts to the governorship during the week-long session. The companion program for young women, Girls State, is sponsored by the American Legion Auxiliary.

History

The first Boys State was held in Illinois in 1935, founded by American Legion members Hayes Kennedy and Harold Hurley, partly as a response to the socialist and fascist youth movements of the era, including the Young Pioneer camps and Hitler Youth. The program spread rapidly; by the late 1940s nearly every state hosted a Boys State. Each state program sends two delegates to Boys Nation in Washington, D.C., founded in 1946, where participants simulate the federal Senate and meet national officials.

Program

Delegates are typically selected by their high schools and local Legion posts, usually between their junior and senior years. They are assigned to one of two fictional parties (traditionally the Nationalists and Federalists), draft party platforms, hold elections, and staff legislative, executive, and judicial offices. Sessions are usually hosted at a college or university or, in some states, at the state capitol.

Notable alumni

Alumni include former U.S. President Bill Clinton (whose 1963 Boys Nation meeting with President John F. Kennedy he credited as influential), former Vice President Dick Cheney, astronaut Neil Armstrong, journalist Tom Brokaw, and musician Bruce Springsteen.
