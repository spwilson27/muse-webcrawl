Bishōnen

Bishōnen (美少年, also romanised bishounen) is a Japanese term literally meaning "beautiful boy (or youth)". It describes a male aesthetic ideal found in many East Asian cultures, characterised by grace, beauty, and some androgynous or feminine characteristics. The term refers to a specific aesthetic, particularly in manga, anime, and other popular media, and not to any particular sexuality or gender identity.

Aesthetic

The typical bishōnen is slim and slender with a well-proportioned body, smooth and clear skin, stylish hair, and delicate or refined facial features. This ideal contrasts with the muscular, rugged masculinity more typical of Western media. The aesthetic has roots in classical Japanese literature — the shining Prince Genji of The Tale of Genji is often cited — and in the onnagata (male actors playing female roles) of kabuki theatre.

In media

The bishōnen became a prominent figure in shōjo manga from the 1970s onward through the work of the Year 24 Group, including Moto Hagio and Keiko Takemiya, whose works such as The Heart of Thomas and Toward the Terra featured beautiful young male protagonists. Bishōnen characters are common in boys' love (BL/yaoi) works, otome games (dating games aimed at women), and mainstream shōnen series. Visual design associated with the aesthetic influenced Japanese fashion and idol culture, and similar ideals of youthful male beauty (sometimes called kkonminam, "flower boy") are prominent in South Korean popular culture.

Related terms

  • Bishōjo — "beautiful girl", the female counterpart aesthetic; see bishōjo game.
  • Biseinen — "beautiful young man", for older characters.
  • Ikemen — contemporary slang for a good-looking man.
