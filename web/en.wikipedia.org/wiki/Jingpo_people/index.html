The Jingpo people (also spelled Jingpho, or Singpho in India) are an ethnic group who are the largest subgroup of the Kachin peoples. They live primarily in the Kachin Hills of northern Myanmar's Kachin State and neighbouring areas of Yunnan in China and Arunachal Pradesh in India.

The Jingpo speak the Jingpho language, a Tibeto-Burman language written in a Latin-based script. In China they are officially recognised as one of the 56 ethnic groups, with a population of roughly 150,000 in Yunnan's Dehong Dai-Jingpo Autonomous Prefecture.

Culture

The best-known Jingpo festival is the Manau, a large communal dancing festival traditionally held to mark important occasions and now staged annually in Myitkyina and Ruili. Traditional social organisation was based on chiefs known as duwa, and Jingpo society historically practised a system of feasting and reciprocal obligation.

Religion

Many Jingpo are Christians, the result of missionary activity beginning in the late 19th century, while others follow Buddhism or traditional animist beliefs centred on spirits called nat.
