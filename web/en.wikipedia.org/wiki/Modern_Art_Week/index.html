Modern Art Week

The Theatro Municipal, São Paulo, where the Week was held in February 1922

The Modern Art Week (Portuguese: Semana de Arte Moderna) was an arts festival held at the Theatro Municipal in São Paulo, Brazil, from 13 to 17 February 1922. It is regarded as the founding event of Brazilian modernism, presenting new work in painting, sculpture, literature, poetry and music to a largely scandalised audience.

The event

Organised by a group of young artists and intellectuals — including the writers Oswald de Andrade, Mário de Andrade and Menotti Del Picchia (the "Group of Five" with painters Anita Malfatti and Tarsila do Amaral) and backed by patrons such as Paulo Prado — the Week combined exhibitions with three evening programmes of lectures, poetry readings and concerts. Works by the composer Heitor Villa-Lobos were performed, and the writer Graça Aranha opened proceedings with a lecture on "the aesthetic emotion in modern art."

Audiences booed experimental poems and music, and the press was mixed, though an earlier exhibition by Anita Malfatti in 1917 had already provoked a famous hostile review by Monteiro Lobato that helped galvanise the modernists.

Legacy

The Week launched the modernist movement that reshaped Brazilian culture over the following decades, leading to Oswald de Andrade's Manifesto Pau-Brasil (1924) and the Anthropophagic Manifesto (1928), which proposed that Brazilian art should "devour" foreign influences and make them its own. Its centenary in 2022 prompted nationwide retrospectives and renewed debate about the movement's exclusions and Eurocentric assumptions.
