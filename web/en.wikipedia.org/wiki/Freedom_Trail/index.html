Freedom Trail

The Freedom Trail is a 2.5-mile (4.0 km) walking route through downtown Boston, Massachusetts, marked by a line of red brick and paint, that passes 16 sites significant to the American Revolution. It attracts several million visitors a year and is preserved by the City of Boston and the Freedom Trail Foundation.

History and sites

The Trail was conceived in 1951 by journalist William Schofield and implemented by Mayor John Hynes. Starting at Boston Common, the route includes the Massachusetts State House, Park Street Church, the Granary Burying Ground, King's Chapel, the Old South Meeting House, the Old State House, the site of the Boston Massacre, Faneuil Hall, the Paul Revere House, and the Old North Church, before crossing the Charles River to the Bunker Hill Monument and the USS Constitution in Charlestown.

Management

Most sites are part of Boston National Historical Park. The Freedom Trail Foundation, founded in 1964, works to preserve and market the Trail and offers guided tours by costumed interpreters.
