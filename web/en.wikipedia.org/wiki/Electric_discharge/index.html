Electric discharge

An electric arc, a sustained discharge through air
Electric discharge
TypesCorona, glow, arc, spark, dielectric barrier
MediumGas, vacuum, liquid, solid insulators
Natural exampleLightning

An electric discharge is the release and transmission of electricity through a medium that is normally insulating, such as air or another gas, when the applied electric field exceeds the medium's dielectric strength. The field ionises the material, creating a conductive channel of plasma through which current flows, usually accompanied by the emission of light and heat. The best-known natural example is lightning.

Types of discharge

A corona discharge is a partial discharge around a conductor in a region of high field strength, often seen and heard near high-voltage power lines. A glow discharge, sustained at low pressure in gas, powers neon and fluorescent lamps and is widely used in plasma processing. An electric arc is a high-current, luminous discharge with a very hot plasma column, used in arc welding and arc lamps; breakdown of air requires a field of roughly 3 megavolts per metre. A spark is a brief, transient discharge, as in electrostatic discharge from a fingertip. Dielectric barrier discharges use an insulating layer to limit current and are used in ozone generation and plasma displays. In vacuum, discharges occur by field emission from electrode surfaces; in solids, partial discharges within insulation are a major cause of high-voltage equipment ageing and failure.

Applications

Controlled discharges underpin gas-discharge lamps, plasma televisions, spark plugs in petrol engines, electrostatic precipitators, corona treaters, ozone generators, and many semiconductor fabrication processes. Uncontrolled discharges are a hazard studied in electrical engineering and in ESD (electrostatic discharge) protection for electronics.
