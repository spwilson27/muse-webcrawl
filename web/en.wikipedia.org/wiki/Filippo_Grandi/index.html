Filippo Grandi (born 1957) is an Italian diplomat and United Nations official who has served as the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees since 1 January 2016, succeeding António Guterres. He was elected by the UN General Assembly on the nomination of the Secretary-General and was later re-elected for a further term.

Career

Grandi studied history at the University of Milan and worked with non-governmental organisations in Asia before joining the United Nations in 1988. He spent much of his career with the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR), serving in field operations and at headquarters, including as chief of mission in Afghanistan and as the agency's representative in several countries during the emergencies of the 1990s and 2000s in Africa and Asia. He subsequently served as Deputy Special Representative of the UN Assistance Mission in Afghanistan.

From 2010 to 2014 he was Commissioner-General of the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA). As High Commissioner, he has led UNHCR through a period in which the number of forcibly displaced people worldwide surpassed 100 million, driven by conflicts including those in Syria, Ukraine, Sudan, and Myanmar, and has been a prominent advocate for the Global Compact on Refugees adopted in 2018 and for greater responsibility-sharing by states.
