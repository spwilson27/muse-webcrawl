Kyoto Prefecture (京都府, Kyōto-fu) is a prefecture of Japan located in the Kansai region of Honshu. Its capital is the city of Kyoto, which served as Japan's imperial capital for over a thousand years, from 794 until 1868. The prefecture stretches from the Kyoto basin north to the Sea of Japan coast, where the port of Maizuru and the scenic sandbar of Amanohashidate are located.

Geography and municipalities

The prefecture comprises 15 cities, including Uji — famous for its green tea and the Byōdō-in temple — Kameoka, Nagaokakyō, Fukuchiyama and Ayabe. Kyoto City dominates the prefecture's population and economy. In the south, the prefecture borders Osaka and Nara; the historic Yamashiro tea-growing district around Wazuka produces much of Japan's finest sencha.

History

The modern prefecture was established in 1868 from the old provinces of Yamashiro, Tamba and Tango. Until the Meiji Restoration, the Kyoto imperial court shaped Japanese high culture — tea ceremony, Noh theatre, Zen Buddhism and court cuisine all flourished here. Seventeen sites in the prefecture's south form the UNESCO World Heritage listing "Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto".

Economy

Tourism is a mainstay, but Kyoto is also a technology centre: Nintendo, Kyocera, Murata Manufacturing, Omron and Shimadzu are headquartered in the prefecture, and its universities, led by Kyoto University, anchor a large student population.
