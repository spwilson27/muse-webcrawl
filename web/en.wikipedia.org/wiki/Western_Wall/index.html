The Western Wall (the Kotel) is an ancient limestone wall in the Old City of Jerusalem and one of the holiest sites in Judaism. It is not part of the Temple itself but a retaining wall of Herod the Great's vast expansion of the Temple Mount plaza around 19 BC — the closest place to the site of the destroyed Temples where Jewish prayer has traditionally been permitted, facing the Holy of Holies.

Practice and history

Worshippers pray in a plaza divided by gender according to Orthodox custom, and press written prayers into the wall's cracks (periodically removed and buried on the Mount of Olives). The visible section is about 57 metres long; the wall continues underground and along a tunnel northward. Under the name al-Buraq Wall, the site is also significant in Islam as where Muhammad tethered his steed al-Buraq. Control of the wall was a focus of conflict in 1929, and its capture in 1967 — paratroopers at the Wall — is an iconic moment in Israeli history.
