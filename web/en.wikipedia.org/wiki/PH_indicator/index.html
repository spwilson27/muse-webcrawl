pH indicator

A pH indicator is a halochromic chemical compound added in small amounts to a solution so the pH (acidity or basicity) of the solution can be determined visually or spectroscopically by changes in absorption and/or emission properties. Hence, a pH indicator is a chemical detector for hydronium ions (H3O+) or hydrogen ions (H+) in the Arrhenius model.

Principle

Most indicators are weak acids or bases whose protonated and deprotonated forms have different colours. As the pH changes around the indicator's pKa, the equilibrium between the two forms shifts, and the colour changes over a characteristic transition interval, typically about one pH unit on either side of the pKa.

Applications

pH indicators are used in titrations to detect the endpoint of an acid–base reaction, in universal indicator paper for approximate measurements, in soil testing, aquariums, and swimming-pool water testing. Natural indicators derived from plants—such as litmus from lichens, anthocyanins in red cabbage, and turmeric—have been used for centuries.

For precise measurements, a pH meter with a glass electrode is used instead, but indicators remain valuable where an approximate or visual result suffices.
