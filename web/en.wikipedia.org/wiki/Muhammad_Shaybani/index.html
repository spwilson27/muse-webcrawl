Muhammad Shaybani Khan (1451 – 2 December 1510), also known as Shaybani Khan or Shahi Beg Khan, was a Turco-Mongol military leader and khan of the Uzbeks who conquered the Timurid principalities of Central Asia and founded the Shaybanid dynasty, which ruled Transoxiana (from Bukhara) for most of the sixteenth century. A descendant of Shayban, grandson of Genghis Khan, he ended more than a century of Timurid rule in Samarkand and Bukhara and drove Babur — the future founder of the Mughal Empire — out of Central Asia.

Rise

Shaybani was the grandson of Abu'l-Khayr Khan, who had united the Uzbek tribes of the Dasht-i Qipchaq steppe. After Abu'l-Khayr's death in 1468 the confederation broke up; Shaybani spent years as a soldier of fortune, studying in Bukhara and serving various masters, before rallying his followers in the 1490s as Timurid Central Asia dissolved into rival courts under the descendants of Timur and of Ulugh Beg.

Conquests

In 1500 Shaybani took Bukhara and Samarkand, defeating the young Timurid prince Babur, who briefly recaptured Samarkand with Persian help before losing it again after the siege of 1501. By 1505 Shaybani had taken Khwarazm (Khiva and Urgench), and in 1507 he captured Herat, the great cultural capital of the late Timurids, extinguishing Timurid rule in Khorasan. His coins, struck in the conquered cities, and his patronage of poetry and Sunni scholarship mark him as more than a steppe raider; he composed Turkic verse himself.

Death and legacy

Shaybani's advance brought him into collision with the new Safavid shah of Persia, Ismail I, an ardent champion of Twelver Shi'ism opposed to Shaybani's Sunni zeal. In December 1510, near Merv, Shah Ismail lured Shaybani's army into battle and crushed it; Shaybani was killed — legend holds that the Shah had his skull made into a drinking cup. His nephew Ubayd Allah soon recovered Bukhara, and the Shaybanid khanate endured until 1599, permanently reorienting Central Asia toward the Uzbek khanates and confirming the religious frontier between Sunni Transoxiana and Shi'a Iran.
