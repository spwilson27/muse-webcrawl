Internet radio — also called web radio, net radio or streaming radio — is a digital audio service transmitted via the Internet. Broadcasting on the Internet is usually referred to as webcasting to distinguish it from terrestrial radio broadcasting. Unlike over-the-air radio, an internet stream is not limited by transmitter range, so a station can be heard worldwide; unlike podcasting, it is generally live or scheduled rather than on-demand.

History

Carl Malamud's Internet Talk Radio, launched in 1993, is often cited as the first internet radio station, distributing recorded interviews. In November 1994, WXYC, the student station of the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, became one of the first traditional stations to stream its broadcast online, and the Rolling Stones' concert of November 1994 was an early major webcast. The mid-1990s saw Nullsoft's SHOUTcast (1998) and the Icecast server popularise do-it-yourself streaming with MP3, enabling thousands of small stations. RealAudio had earlier provided the dominant streaming format.

Technology

A station's audio is encoded (historically MP3 or RealAudio; now commonly AAC, Opus or Ogg Vorbis) and distributed by a streaming server. Most streams are unicast — one connection per listener — so bandwidth costs scale with audience size, unlike broadcast radio. Directory services and aggregators (such as TuneIn and radio.net) index stations, and internet radio is now built into smart speakers, phones, cars and dedicated tuner devices. Bit rates of 64–128 kbit/s are typical for music.

Licensing

Webcasters must license the music they play. In the United States, statutory licences administered by SoundExchange cover sound recordings for eligible non-interactive services, with rates set by the Copyright Royalty Board; disputes over royalty rates — including the 2007 rate increases that prompted a "Day of Silence" protest by webcasters — have repeatedly threatened small stations. Terrestrial simulcasts, pure-play webcasters and personalised services (such as Pandora) face differing regimes.
