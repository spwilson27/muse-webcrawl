Pastry chef

A pastry chef or pâtissier (French pronunciation: [patisje]) is a station chef in a professional kitchen, skilled in the making of pastries, desserts, breads, and other baked goods. They are employed in large hotels, bistros, restaurants, bakeries, and some cafés.

Responsibilities

The pastry chef is responsible for the dessert and bread programme of an establishment: creating menus, developing recipes, managing the pastry section's staff and budget, and overseeing production. Typical products include cakes, croissants and other viennoiserie, plated desserts, chocolates and confectionery, ice creams and sorbets, and showpieces in sugar and chocolate.

Training

Training typically combines formal culinary school education with apprenticeships in the brigade de cuisine. Pastry work demands precision — ingredients are usually weighed, and techniques such as tempering chocolate, laminating dough, and pulling sugar require considerable practice. In France, pâtissier is a recognised trade qualification, and accomplished pastry chefs may compete for the title of Meilleur Ouvrier de France (MOF) in pâtisserie.

In the kitchen brigade

In the classical brigade system codified by Auguste Escoffier, the pâtissier oversees subordinate stations such as the boulanger (baker), the glacier (ice cream maker), and the confiseur (confectioner). In smaller establishments, the role may be combined with that of the chef de partie or performed by a single dedicated baker.
