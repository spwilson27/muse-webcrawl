Sextus Julius Frontinus (c. AD 40 – 103) was a Roman civil engineer, author, soldier and senator of the late 1st century AD. He is best known as the author of De aquaeductu ("On the aqueducts [of Rome]"), a technical and administrative survey of the city's water supply, and of the Strategemata, a collection of military stratagems.

Career

Frontinus was praetor in AD 70 and consul three times. From about 73 to 78 he governed Roman Britain, succeeding Quintus Petillius Cerialis; he subdued the Silures of Wales and established the legionary fortress at Caerleon. Later he governed the province of Asia. In 97 the emperor Nerva appointed him curator aquarum, commissioner in charge of Rome's aqueducts, a post in which he investigated and reformed widespread abuses and illegal tapping of the public water supply. He was also an augur, and a friend and correspondent of Pliny the Younger.

Works

De aquaeductu, written in his capacity as water commissioner, describes the history, capacity, quality and administration of the nine aqueducts then supplying Rome, and remains a primary source for Roman hydraulic engineering. The Strategemata collects several hundred examples of military ruses from Greek and Roman history, arranged for the use of commanders. A treatise on military science by Frontinus is lost, and works on land surveying transmitted under his name are partly spurious.
