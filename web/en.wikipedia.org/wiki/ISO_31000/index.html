ISO 31000

The ISO 31000 risk management process: communication, assessment, treatment and monitoring.

ISO 31000 is a family of standards relating to risk management codified by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO). ISO 31000:2018, Risk management — Guidelines, provides principles and generic guidelines on managing risks faced by organisations. It seeks to provide a universally recognised paradigm for practitioners and companies, and is intended to be usable by any organisation regardless of size, activity or sector.

History

The first edition was published in November 2009, drawing heavily on the earlier Australian/New Zealand standard AS/NZS 4360. A revised, streamlined second edition followed in February 2018, emphasising the integration of risk management into governance and decision-making, leadership commitment, and the iterative nature of managing risk.

Framework

The standard is built on three components: principles (such as creating and protecting value, being an integral part of organisational processes, and continual improvement); a framework centred on leadership and commitment, covering integration, design, implementation, evaluation and improvement; and a process of communication and consultation, establishing scope and criteria, risk assessment (identification, analysis, evaluation), risk treatment, recording and reporting, and monitoring and review.

Unlike management-system standards such as ISO 9001, ISO 31000 provides guidelines only and is not intended for certification. Companion publications include ISO/TR 31004 (implementation guidance) and IEC 31010, which catalogues risk assessment techniques.
