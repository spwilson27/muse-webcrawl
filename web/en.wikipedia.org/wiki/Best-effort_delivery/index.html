Best-effort delivery

Best-effort delivery describes a network service in which the network does not provide any guarantee that data is delivered, or that delivery meets a specified quality of service. Packets may be lost, duplicated, delayed or delivered out of order, and the sender receives no notification of failure.

Characteristics

Best-effort delivery is the model used by the Internet Protocol (IP) at the network layer. It keeps the network core simple and places responsibility for reliability — retransmission, ordering and error correction — on higher-layer protocols such as TCP when those guarantees are needed, following the end-to-end principle.

Applications

Many applications, including most web traffic, email and streaming media, run over best-effort networks. Services requiring guaranteed performance can layer reliability protocols on top, or use quality-of-service mechanisms such as DiffServ to prioritise some traffic within an otherwise best-effort network.
