A Coruña (Galician: A Coruña; Spanish: La Coruña) is a city and municipality in Galicia, Spain. It is the second most populated city in the autonomous community and the provincial capital of the province of the same name. Located on the Atlantic coast, it is an important port city with a long maritime history.

History

The city grew around the Roman lighthouse now known as the Tower of Hercules, which has been in continuous use since the 2nd century AD and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — the oldest functioning lighthouse in the world. In 1588 the Spanish Armada sailed from A Coruña for England, and the following year the city withstood a siege by an English expedition led by Francis Drake, a defence in which the heroine María Pita became famous.

Economy and culture

The port handles trade including oil, fish, and bulk goods, and the surrounding metropolitan area hosts major textile and retail industries. The city is home to the football club RC Deportivo de La Coruña, winners of the Spanish league title in 2000.
