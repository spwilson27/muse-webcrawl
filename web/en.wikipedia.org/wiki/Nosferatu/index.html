Nosferatu – Eine Symphonie des Grauens (Nosferatu – A Symphony of Horror) is a 1922 German Expressionist horror film directed by F. W. Murnau and starring Max Schreck as the vampire Count Orlok. An unauthorized adaptation of Bram Stoker's 1897 novel Dracula, the film changed character names and details after the production company, Prana Film, failed to secure the rights from Stoker's estate.

Plot

In 1838, Thomas Hutter, a real estate agent in the fictional German town of Wisborg, travels to Transylvania to sell a house to the sinister Count Orlok. After a harrowing journey, Hutter realizes his client is a vampire. Orlok travels by ship to Wisborg, bringing plague with him, and sets his sights on Hutter's wife Ellen. Ellen sacrifices herself by luring Orlok to her room, keeping him until sunrise, whose rays destroy him.

Legal battle and survival

Stoker's widow, Florence Balcombe, sued for copyright infringement and won; the court ordered all prints destroyed. However, some copies survived, and the film gradually attained classic status. It is widely regarded as a masterpiece of German Expressionist cinema and one of the most influential horror films ever made.

Legacy

The film established many vampire conventions, most famously the idea that sunlight is lethal to vampires. It was remade by Werner Herzog as Nosferatu the Vampyre (1979), and again by Robert Eggers as Nosferatu (2024). The story of its production was fictionalized in Shadow of the Vampire (2000).
