Forward (association football)

Position typeAttacking
Main roleScoring goals
Sub-typesCentre-forward, second striker, winger

Forwards are the players on an association football team who play nearest to the opposing team's goal, and are therefore most responsible for scoring goals. Their advanced position and limited defensive responsibilities mean forwards normally score more goals on behalf of their team than other players.

Types of forward

The traditional centre-forward (or striker) plays centrally and is the team's focal point in attack, expected to hold up the ball and finish chances. A second striker plays slightly deeper, linking midfield and attack, while wingers operate on the flanks, supplying crosses and cutting inside to shoot. The false nine is a centre-forward who drops into midfield to draw defenders out of position.

History

Early formations such as the 2–3–5 fielded five forwards. Over the twentieth century, formations progressively traded forwards for midfielders and defenders, and modern systems commonly use one to three forwards in shapes such as 4–3–3 and 3–5–2.

Numbering

In traditional shirt numbering, the centre-forward wears number 9, inside forwards 8 and 10, and wingers 7 and 11 — numbers still conventionally associated with attacking roles.
