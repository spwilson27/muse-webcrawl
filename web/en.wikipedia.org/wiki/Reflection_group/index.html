Reflection group

Image placeholder: symmetries generated by mirror reflections.

In group theory and geometry, a reflection group is a discrete group generated by reflections of a Euclidean space (or of a sphere or hyperbolic space) across hyperplanes. Reflection groups link geometry, algebra and combinatorics: finite reflection groups are exactly the finite Coxeter groups, and they classify regular polytopes and the symmetry groups of many geometric structures.

Examples

The symmetry group of a regular polygon is a finite reflection group generated by reflections in lines through the centre and in perpendicular bisectors. In three dimensions, the symmetry groups of the Platonic solids are reflection groups. Infinite examples include the symmetry groups of tessellations of the plane by triangles or squares, in which the mirrors form the sides of a fundamental triangle — the basis of kaleidoscope patterns.

Theory

Finite reflection groups were classified by H. S. M. Coxeter using Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams; the irreducible ones include the families An, Bn, Dn and the exceptional types E6, E7, E8, F4, G2 and the non-crystallographic H and I types. The crystallographic ones are the Weyl groups of Lie theory and appear throughout representation theory and algebraic geometry.
