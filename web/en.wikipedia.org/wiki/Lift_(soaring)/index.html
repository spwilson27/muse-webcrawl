Lift (soaring)

Lift in soaring
TypesThermal, ridge, wave, convergence
Used byGliders, hang gliders, paragliders, soaring birds
Measured withVariometer

In soaring flight, lift is rising air that allows an unpowered aircraft — or a bird — to climb or to maintain altitude while descending through the air mass more slowly than the air itself rises. Exploiting lift is the basis of gliding, hang gliding and paragliding cross-country flight.

Types of lift

Thermal lift is formed by bubbles and columns of warm air rising from sun-heated ground, often marked by cumulus clouds. Ridge lift (slope lift) occurs where wind is deflected upward by a hill or escarpment. Wave lift forms downwind of mountain ranges in stable air, and can carry gliders to the stratosphere. Convergence lift arises where air masses meet, for example along sea-breeze fronts.

Use in flight

Pilots circle in thermals to gain height — "thermalling" — then glide toward the next source of lift. The variometer, an instrument sensitive to vertical speed, often with an audio tone, tells the pilot whether the surrounding air is rising. Soaring birds such as vultures and albatrosses use the same phenomena without flapping.
