William Billings

Born: 7 October 1746, Boston, Massachusetts
Died: 26 September 1800, Boston, Massachusetts
Occupation: Composer, singing master, tanner
Known for: "Chester"; The New-England Psalm-Singer

William Billings (October 7, 1746 – September 26, 1800) was an American composer and singing master, widely regarded as the first American choral composer of significance. A Boston tanner by trade, he was largely self-taught in music and became a central figure in the singing-school movement of early New England.

Life

Billings was born in Boston and lost an eye in childhood. He worked as a tanner, street inspector, and sealer of leather while teaching singing schools throughout New England. Despite his popularity as a teacher and composer, he died in poverty and was buried in an unmarked grave.

Works

In 1770 Billings published The New-England Psalm-Singer, the first tunebook consisting entirely of music by an American-born composer, containing over 120 pieces. His later collections included The Singing Master's Assistant (1778), Music in Miniature (1779), The Psalm-Singer's Amusement (1781), The Suffolk Harmony (1786), and The Continental Harmony (1794).

Music and legacy

Billings wrote hymn tunes, anthems, and "fuging tunes" characterised by direct, rugged voice leading. His patriotic tune "Chester" became an unofficial anthem of the American Revolution and remains widely performed. Twentieth-century composers including William Schuman drew on his music, helping to revive interest in his work.
