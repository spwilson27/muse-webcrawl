Greco-Italian War

The Greco-Italian War (28 October 1940 – 23 April 1941) was fought between the Kingdom of Italy and the Kingdom of Greece during the Second World War. Italy, under Benito Mussolini, invaded Greece from Italian-occupied Albania, but Greek forces repelled the attack and counter-invaded Albania — the first major Allied land victory of the war. Germany's intervention in April 1941 (the Battle of Greece) ended Greek resistance.

Background

Mussolini sought a quick triumph parallel to Hitler's victories. After Greece rejected an ultimatum demanding occupation of strategic sites — Prime Minister Ioannis Metaxas's refusal, commemorated as "Ohi Day" on 28 October — Italian forces crossed the border from Albania.

Course of the war

Greek defenders under General Alexandros Papagos stopped the invasion in the Pindus mountains and at the Battle of Elaia–Kalamas, then counterattacked, capturing Korçë, Sarandë, and Gjirokastër deep inside Albania. A renewed Italian spring offensive in March 1941 also failed. The stalemate humiliated Italy and forced Hitler to intervene in the Balkans, invading Greece and Yugoslavia on 6 April 1941; Greece signed an armistice with Germany on 23 April.

Legacy

The Greek resistance was celebrated across the Allied world — contemporary commentators remarked that "Greeks fight like heroes". The war remains a central episode of Greek national memory.
