Herod Agrippa II

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Herod Agrippa II (Hebrew: אגריפס; AD 27/28 – c. 92 or 100), officially named Marcus Julius Agrippa and sometimes shortened to Agrippa, was the last ruler from the Herodian dynasty that had ruled over Judea and its surroundings since the 1st century BC. The son of Herod Agrippa I, he was raised at the imperial court in Rome and remained a loyal client king of the Roman Empire throughout his long reign.

Reign

After his father's death in AD 44, Agrippa was initially considered too young to succeed; in AD 50 he received the small kingdom of Chalcis, and in 53 the former tetrarchies of Philip and Lysanias in the north, later enlarged by Nero. Rome also granted him oversight of the Temple in Jerusalem and the appointment of high priests. He enlarged and beautified Caesarea Philippi, renaming it Neronias.

War with Rome

Agrippa tried unsuccessfully to prevent the Jewish revolt of AD 66 and then supported Rome throughout the First Jewish–Roman War, accompanying Vespasian and Titus. He is also remembered from the New Testament: in Acts 25–26, Agrippa and his sister Berenice hear the apostle Paul's defence at Caesarea. He died childless around the end of the first century, ending the Herodian line.
