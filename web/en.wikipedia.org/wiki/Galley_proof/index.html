Galley proof

Stage ofBook and print publishing
PrecedesPage proofs
Modern analogueAdvance reader copy (uncorrected proof)

In printing and publishing, galley proofs are the preliminary versions of a publication produced for proofreading before the text is made up into final pages. The name comes from the metal trays, called galleys, in which hand-set or machine-set type was held in the letterpress era.

Process

Type set into a galley was inked and a long sheet pulled directly from it. Because the text had not yet been divided into pages, a galley proof appeared as one continuous column, making it the cheapest stage at which to correct errors and make authorial changes; corrections made later, at page-proof stage, were more costly and traditionally charged to the author if extensive.

Modern usage

Although digital typesetting has eliminated physical galleys, the term survives in publishing: "galleys" or "bound galleys" still refers to uncorrected advance copies sent to reviewers and booksellers ahead of publication.
