A calendar era is the period of time elapsed since one epoch of a calendar and, if it exists, before the next one. For example, the Gregorian calendar numbers its years in the Christian era (the Anno Domini era, AD, or the Common Era, CE). The instant, date, or year from which time is marked is called the epoch of the era.

Usage

In antiquity, regnal years, named for a reigning monarch, were used in many places; dating by eponymous officials (such as Roman consuls or Athenian archons) was also common. Religious and cosmological events provided many other epochs: the Islamic calendar counts from the Hijra of Muhammad in 622 CE; the Hebrew calendar from a calculated date of creation; Hindu calendars from epochs such as the Kali Yuga; and the Buddhist calendar from the Buddha's parinirvana.

Examples of eras

  • Anno Domini / Common Era — epoch the traditionally reckoned birth of Jesus, devised by Dionysius Exiguus in 525.
  • Anno Mundi — "in the year of the world", from the creation of the world (e.g. Byzantine and Hebrew calendars).
  • Hijri era — from Muhammad's emigration (Hijra) to Medina, 622 CE.
  • Regnal eras — Japanese nengō, Chinese era names, and years of a monarch's reign.
  • Ab urbe condita — from the legendary founding of Rome, 753 BC.
  • Before Present — used in radiocarbon dating, with "present" defined as 1950.
  • Holocene calendar — adds 10,000 years to the Common Era to encompass all of settled human history.

Conversion

Converting dates between eras requires knowing both the epoch and whether a calendar counts elapsed or current years, and whether it has a year zero — the astronomical year numbering system inserts a year 0, unlike the BC/AD system.
