The Battle of Champion Hill (also known as the Battle of Baker's Creek), fought on 16 May 1863, was the pivotal battle of the Vicksburg Campaign of the American Civil War. Union Major General Ulysses S. Grant's Army of the Tennessee defeated Lieutenant General John C. Pemberton's Confederate army and drove it into the defences of Vicksburg, Mississippi.

Background

After crossing the Mississippi River below Vicksburg at the end of April 1863, Grant marched inland, won at Port Gibson and Raymond, and captured Jackson, the state capital, on 14 May. Turning west, he moved to cut the railroad and trap Pemberton's army between himself and Vicksburg. Pemberton, hampered by contradictory orders from President Jefferson Davis and General Joseph E. Johnston, took a defensive position on Champion Hill, astride the roads to Vicksburg.

The battle

Grant attacked on 16 May with about 29,000 men against Pemberton's roughly 22,000. The fighting centred on the crest of Champion Hill; Union divisions under Hovey, Logan and Crocker, supported by McPherson's corps, gradually drove back the Confederate left after repeated attacks and counterattacks in which the position changed hands several times. Confederate reinforcements arrived too late, and McClernand's corps pressed too slowly to cut the retreat. In the late afternoon Pemberton ordered a withdrawal across Baker's Creek; when the bridge was demolished under fire, part of his army — most of Loring's division — was cut off and forced to detour south to rejoin Johnston.

Union casualties were about 2,460; Confederate losses about 3,850, including some 1,750 captured and 27 guns.

Aftermath

The next day the Union won again at the Big Black River, and Pemberton fell back into Vicksburg, where Grant besieged him. The city surrendered on 4 July 1863, giving the Union control of the Mississippi River and splitting the Confederacy in two.
