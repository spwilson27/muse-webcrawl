Elba is a Mediterranean island in the Tuscan Archipelago, about 10 km off the coast of Tuscany, Italy, known worldwide as the place of Napoleon Bonaparte's first exile. Iron was mined on Elba from Etruscan and Roman times (its ancient name Ilva relates to iron), and the smelting industry around Portoferraio ("iron port") shaped the island for millennia. Today the island, largely within the Tuscan Archipelago National Park, lives on tourism — beaches, clear water and the Napoleonic sites.

Napoleon's exile

By the Treaty of Fontainebleau (April 1814), the defeated Napoleon was granted sovereignty over Elba as a miniature principality, with a personal guard of about 600 men, and arrived at Portoferraio on 4 May 1814. In ten months he reformed the island energetically — roads, vineyards, sanitation, the mines — while following events in France, where the restored Bourbons were unpopular and had stopped his pension. On 26 February 1815 he slipped away with about 1,000 followers and landed in France, beginning the Hundred Days. His two residences, the Villa dei Mulini in Portoferraio and the summer villa at San Martino, are now museums. The famous palindrome "Able was I ere I saw Elba" — apocryphally Napoleon's lament in English, a language he never mastered — dates only from the 19th century.
