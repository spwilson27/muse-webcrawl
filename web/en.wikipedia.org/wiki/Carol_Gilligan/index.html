Carol Gilligan (born 28 November 1936) is an American psychologist, ethicist and feminist writer best known for her 1982 book In a Different Voice, in which she challenged Lawrence Kohlberg's theory of moral development and proposed an ethics of care as a complement to the ethics of justice. The book was described by Harvard University Press as "the little book that started a revolution".

Career

Gilligan studied English literature at Swarthmore College before turning to psychology, earning her doctorate at Harvard, where she later joined the faculty of the Harvard Graduate School of Education and worked alongside Erik Erikson and Kohlberg. In 1997 she became Harvard's first professor of gender studies. She later moved to New York University, where she is a university professor, and co-founded the Listening Project and other research on girls' and boys' development.

In a Different Voice

Gilligan argued that Kohlberg's stages of moral development, derived from studies of boys and men, privileged an abstract morality of rights and rules, and therefore scored women's reasoning as less mature. Based on her own interview studies, she described a different moral orientation centred on relationships, responsibility and care, developing through stages from self-interest, through self-sacrificing goodness, to an ethic of non-violence and care for self and others. The work became foundational for care ethics in philosophy and for feminist psychology, while also drawing criticism from psychologists who found gender differences in moral reasoning smaller than Gilligan suggested.

Later work

Her subsequent books include Meeting at the Crossroads (1992, with Lyn Mikel Brown), on girls' adolescence; The Birth of Pleasure (2002); Kyra (2008), a novel; and Joining the Resistance (2011).
