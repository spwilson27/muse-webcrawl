Over the shoulder shot

The over the shoulder shot (abbreviated OTS, or over shoulder shot) is a camera angle used in film and television, in which the camera is placed behind one character, showing part of their shoulder and head in the foreground while focusing on another character facing them.

Use in dialogue

The technique is a staple of shot/reverse shot editing for conversations. By keeping a fragment of the foreground character in frame, it preserves the spatial relationship between speakers and gives the audience a sense of looking along with that character at the person speaking. When paired with a matching reverse angle, it creates continuous, natural-feeling dialogue coverage.

Variations

A "dirty" over-the-shoulder includes part of the foreground actor's body; a "clean" single excludes the other actor entirely. The amount of shoulder shown, and how far the camera sits from the foreground figure, can convey intimacy, confrontation or detachment. The shot is also common in video games during dialogue and cutscenes.
