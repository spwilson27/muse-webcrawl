Legal system

[Image: World legal systems map]

A legal system is a set of institutions, rules and procedures by which a state or community makes, interprets and enforces laws. Comparative law scholars group the world's national legal systems into families that share characteristic sources of law, methods of reasoning and institutional structures. Most systems in practice are mixed, combining elements from several traditions.

Main traditions

Civil law

The civil law tradition, derived from Roman law as codified in the Corpus Juris Civilis and later in the Napoleonic Code and the German Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch, is the most widespread family. Its hallmark is comprehensive written codes that serve as the primary source of law, with judges applying the code to the facts rather than relying on precedent. Civil law prevails across continental Europe, Latin America, much of Asia and parts of Africa.

Common law

The common law tradition originated in medieval England and spread through the British Empire to the United States, Canada, Australia, India, and other Commonwealth countries. Its central feature is judge-made law: decisions in cases (precedent) bind later courts under the doctrine of stare decisis, alongside statutes enacted by legislatures. Procedure is typically adversarial.

Religious and customary law

Religious legal systems base law on sacred texts and traditions — notably Islamic law (Sharia), Jewish law (Halakha) and canon law of the Catholic Church. Sharia governs in whole or in part in several states of the Middle East and elsewhere. Customary law, arising from the established practices of a community, remains important in many parts of Africa and the Pacific, often alongside civil or common law.

Mixed systems

Many jurisdictions are explicitly mixed: Scotland, South Africa, Louisiana, Quebec, the Philippines and Sri Lanka, for example, combine civil and common law elements, while countries such as China combine a civil-law framework with distinctive socialist characteristics.

Components

A legal system typically includes a legislature that enacts statute law, a judiciary that interprets and applies it, an executive that enforces it, a legal profession, and subsidiary sources such as regulations, legal scholarship and custom. The classification of legal systems is a central concern of comparative law and informs legal harmonisation efforts, including the development of international and transnational law.
