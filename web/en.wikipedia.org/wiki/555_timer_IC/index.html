555 timer IC

DesignerHans Camenzind
Introduced1972
ManufacturerSignetics (originally)
TypeTimer / oscillator integrated circuit

The 555 timer IC is an integrated circuit used in a wide variety of timer, delay, pulse generation, and oscillator applications. Designed by Hans Camenzind and introduced by Signetics in 1972, it is one of the most popular and longest-lived integrated circuits ever made, with billions produced.

Operation

The device contains a voltage divider of three resistors (from which the name "555" is traditionally said to derive), two comparators, a flip-flop, and a discharge transistor. Depending on external resistors and capacitors, it can operate in monostable (one-shot), astable (oscillator), or bistable modes.

Variants and legacy

Dual (556) and quad (558) versions combine multiple timers in one package, and CMOS versions such as the 7555 offer lower power consumption. Despite its age and bipolar power demands, the 555 remains a staple of electronics education and hobbyist design.
