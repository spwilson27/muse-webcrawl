The Gariep Dam is a combined gravity and arch dam on the Orange River in South Africa, situated near the town of Norvalspont on the border between the Free State and Eastern Cape provinces. When it was completed in 1971, it was the largest dam in South Africa, and its reservoir remains the country's largest by volume of water stored.

History

The dam was the centrepiece of the Orange River Project, an ambitious scheme to harness the Orange River for irrigation, water supply and hydroelectric power. It was originally named the Hendrik Verwoerd Dam after the former prime minister; in 1996, after the end of apartheid, it was renamed the Gariep Dam — "Gariep" being the indigenous Khoi name for the Orange River, meaning "big river".

Description

The concrete double-curvature arch-gravity wall is 88 metres high with a crest length of about 914 metres. The reservoir has a surface area of roughly 370 km² at full supply level and stores around 5.34 billion cubic metres of water. A hydroelectric power station at the dam has an installed capacity of 360 MW.

Water is transferred from the Gariep Dam through the Orange–Fish Tunnel, one of the longest water tunnels in the world, to the Great Fish River valley and onward toward the Eastern Cape, supporting irrigation and supplying towns including Gqeberha (via interlinked schemes).
