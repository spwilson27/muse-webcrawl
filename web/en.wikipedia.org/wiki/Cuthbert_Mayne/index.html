Cuthbert Mayne

Cuthbert Mayne (c. 1543–1577), English Catholic priest and martyr.

Cuthbert Mayne (c. 1543 – 29 November 1577) was an English Roman Catholic priest executed under the laws of Elizabeth I. He is considered the "protomartyr" of the English seminary priests trained abroad, the first of the Douai-trained missionaries to be put to death in England. He was canonised by Pope Paul VI in 1970 as one of the Forty Martyrs of England and Wales.

Life

Mayne was born near Barnstaple in Devon and educated at Oxford, where he became a Protestant minister and chaplain of St John's College. Influenced by Edmund Campion and others, he converted to Catholicism and in 1573 went to the English College at Douai, where he was ordained priest in 1575. In 1576 he returned to England as a missionary, working in Cornwall under the protection of the Tregian family at Golden Manor near Probus, where he passed as a steward.

Arrest and execution

In June 1577 Mayne was arrested during a search of the house; a papal bull and an Agnus Dei were found in his possession. He was tried at Launceston for high treason and, refusing to renounce his faith or acknowledge the Queen's religious supremacy, was hanged, drawn and quartered at Launceston on 29 November 1577. His head was set up on the castle gate and his quarters displayed at several towns.

Veneration

Mayne was beatified in 1886 and canonised on 25 October 1970. His feast day is kept on 30 November (or with the Forty Martyrs on other calendars). A church dedicated to St Cuthbert Mayne stands in Launceston, and relics are preserved at several places in the West Country.
