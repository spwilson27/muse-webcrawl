The Rhaetian Railway (German: Rhätische Bahn; Italian: Ferrovia Retica; Romansh: Viafier Retica), abbreviated RhB, is a Swiss transport company that owns the largest network of all private railway operators in Switzerland. Headquartered in Chur, it operates all the railway lines in the Swiss canton of Graubünden, except for the line from Reichenau-Tamins to Disentis/Mustér.

Network

The RhB operates a metre-gauge network of about 384 kilometres, famous for its spectacular mountain engineering: 291 bridges, 91 tunnels and the climb over the Bernina Pass at 2,253 metres above sea level, the highest rail crossing in Europe. Its best-known structures include the Landwasser Viaduct, the Brusio spiral viaduct, and the Albula Tunnel.

History

The company grew from the initiative of the Dutch railway pioneer Willem Jan Holsboer, whose Landquart–Davos line opened in 1889. The Rhaetian Railway proper was formed in 1895, and over the following decades it absorbed or built lines across the canton, including the Albula line (1903–04), the Bernina line (opened in stages 1908–10, merged in 1943), and the Vereina Tunnel, opened in 1999, which provides a year-round link to the Engadine.

World Heritage status

In 2008, the Albula and Bernina lines were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the "Rhaetian Railway in the Albula / Bernina Landscapes," recognised as an outstanding example of railway construction in a high mountain landscape.

Services

The RhB runs regional trains, the car shuttle through the Vereina Tunnel, and famous tourist trains including the Bernina Express (Chur to Tirano in Italy) and, jointly with the Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn, the Glacier Express between St. Moritz and Zermatt.
