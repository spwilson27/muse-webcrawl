Dolphinfish

Dolphinfish are ray-finned fish of the genus Coryphaena, the only genus in the family Coryphaenidae. The name is unrelated to dolphins, which are mammals. Two species are recognised: the common dolphinfish or mahi-mahi (Coryphaena hippurus) and the pompano dolphinfish (Coryphaena equiselis).

Description

Dolphinfish are fast-growing, short-lived pelagic predators of tropical and subtropical waters worldwide. Mahi-mahi are brilliant in colour — iridescent green, blue and gold — though the colours fade rapidly after death. Males develop a high, blunt, bony forehead. They feed on flying fish, squid, crabs and other small fish, often congregating under floating debris and sargassum.

Fishery

Mahi-mahi is a popular game fish and food fish, marketed fresh and frozen; its fast growth makes it a candidate for sustainable fisheries and aquaculture.
