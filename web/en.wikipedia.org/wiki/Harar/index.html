Harar (Amharic: ሐረር; Harari: ሀረር) is a walled city in eastern Ethiopia, capital of the Harari Region. For centuries it was a major commercial and Islamic scholarly centre linking the Ethiopian highlands with the Red Sea and the Indian Ocean, and it is regarded by some Ethiopian Muslims as the fourth holiest city of Islam.

History

Harar was the capital of the Adal Sultanate and, in the 16th century, the base of Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi (Ahmad Gragn), whose campaigns reshaped the Horn of Africa. As Emirate of Harar it remained an independent city-state behind its walls until Egyptian occupation in 1875 and incorporation into the Ethiopian Empire after the Battle of Chelenqo in 1887. The explorer Richard Burton was the first European known to enter the city, in 1855, and the poet Arthur Rimbaud later lived there as a coffee trader.

The old town

Harar Jugol, the walled old town, contains 82 mosques — many dating to the 10th century — and over a hundred shrines, threaded by some 368 narrow alleys within walls pierced by five historic gates. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2006. The city is also famed for its "hyena men", who traditionally feed wild hyenas at the city gates at night, a custom said to date from a famine-era truce.
