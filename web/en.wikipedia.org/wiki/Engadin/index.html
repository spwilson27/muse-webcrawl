Engadin

The Engadin or Engadine (Romansh: Engiadina) is a long, high Alpine valley in the canton of Graubünden, eastern Switzerland, following the course of the river Inn from its source near the Maloja Pass to the Austrian border near Finstermünz. At over 1,000 metres — and in the Upper Engadin over 1,600 metres — it is one of the highest inhabited valleys in Europe.

Geography

The valley divides into the Upper Engadin (Engiadin'Ota), a broad, flat plain dotted with lakes (Sils, Silvaplana, Champfèr, and St. Moritz) beneath the Bernina massif, and the narrower, more rugged Lower Engadin (Engiadina Bassa), deeply cut by the Inn gorge and characterised by picturesque villages such as Scuol, Guarda, and Ardez with their sgraffito-decorated houses.

Culture and tourism

The Engadin is a stronghold of the Romansh language, spoken here in the Putèr (Upper) and Vallader (Lower) idioms. St. Moritz in the Upper Engadin became a cradle of winter tourism after 1864, hosted the Winter Olympics in 1928 and 1948, and remains a byword for luxury skiing; the valley offers some 350 km of pistes, the Engadin Skimarathon cross-country race, and summer hiking beside the Swiss National Park, founded in 1914 at the valley's edge.

The Rhaetian Railway's Albula and Bernina lines, UNESCO World Heritage Sites, connect the valley with the rest of Graubünden and Italy.
