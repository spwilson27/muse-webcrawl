Arsacid dynasty

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Coin of Mithridates II (r. 124–91 BC)

The Arsacid dynasty was a dynasty of Iranian origin that founded and ruled the Parthian Empire, which stretched from northeastern Iran to Mesopotamia from 247 BC to 224 AD. The dynasty was named after Arsaces I, a leader of the Parni tribe, who conquered the region of Parthia in Iran's northeast, then a satrapy (province) under Andragoras, in around 238 BC.

Mithridates I (r. 171–132 BC) greatly expanded the empire by seizing Media and Mesopotamia from the Seleucids. At its height, the Parthian Empire stretched from the northern reaches of the Euphrates, in what is now central-eastern Turkey, to present-day Afghanistan and western Pakistan.

History

The Arsacids were challenged in the west by the Roman Empire, with whom they fought a long series of wars, including the disastrous Roman defeat at the Battle of Carrhae in 53 BC. In 224 AD, after a long period of decline and internal strife, the last Arsacid king, Artabanus IV, was defeated by Ardashir I, founder of the Sasanian Empire.

A branch of the family also ruled the Kingdom of Armenia as the Arsacid dynasty of Armenia from 52 AD until 428 AD.
