Cardiff Airport

Cardiff Airport (Welsh: Maes Awyr Caerdydd; IATA: CWL, ICAO: EGFF) is the principal international airport of Wales, located near Rhoose in the Vale of Glamorgan, about 19 km (12 mi) south-west of Cardiff. It is owned by the Welsh Government, which purchased it in 2013. The airport handles scheduled passenger flights to UK, Irish and European destinations, charter traffic, pilot training, and is an important maintenance base.

History

The site began as RAF Rhoose, a Second World War satellite airfield. In 1952 it became Cardiff's municipal airport, replacing Pengam Moors, and Aer Lingus inaugurated services to Dublin. Through the jet age the airport added a terminal and extended its runway; Cambrian Airways and later British Airways maintenance operations were long a mainstay. A British Airways maintenance hangar (BAMC), opened in 1993, remains one of the largest employers on site, servicing long-haul aircraft. Passenger numbers peaked at over 2 million in 2007, driven by low-cost carrier bmibaby, then fell sharply after that airline's demise; the Welsh Government bought the airport from Abertis in March 2013 for £52 million and has since sought to rebuild traffic, which recovered to about 1.6 million passengers by 2019 before the COVID-19 pandemic.

Facilities and transport

The single terminal offers check-in, security and departures on split levels. Surface access is by road via the A4226 from Barry and the M4; a shuttle bus links the terminal to Rhoose Cardiff International Airport railway station on the Vale of Glamorgan line, with trains to Cardiff Central and Bridgend. Long-running debate surrounds a proposed direct rail link and the airport's competition with Bristol Airport across the Severn.

Operations

Carriers have included TUI Airways (a major charter base), Ryanair, Vueling, KLM (to Amsterdam) and Loganair. The airport also hosts the South Wales Police and coastguard helicopter operations nearby at MOD St Athan, flight training organisations, and occasional military and state traffic. Plans for growth have periodically raised environmental objections concerning emissions and surface-access traffic.
