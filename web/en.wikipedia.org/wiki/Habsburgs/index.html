The Habsburgs were a dynasty of counts, dukes, kings and emperors who shaped European history for more than six centuries. Taking their name from Habsburg Castle in present-day Switzerland, they acquired Austria in the 13th century, the imperial crown in the 15th, and — through a series of dynastic marriages — Spain, the Netherlands, Bohemia, Hungary and vast overseas territories by the early 16th century.

Summary of the dynasty's history

Rudolf I, elected King of the Romans in 1273, defeated Ottokar II of Bohemia and installed his sons in Austria (1282). The family lost the Swiss heartland to the Swiss Confederacy but consolidated the Austrian hereditary lands. Frederick III (reigned 1440–1493) began the near-unbroken line of Habsburg Holy Roman Emperors and coined the device A.E.I.O.U. His son Maximilian I gained the Burgundian lands by marriage; Maximilian's grandson Charles V ruled Spain, the Empire, the Netherlands, southern Italy and the Spanish Americas simultaneously.

After Charles V's abdication (1556) the house split into the Spanish Habsburgs (extinct 1700) and the Austrian Habsburgs (male line extinct 1740). Maria Theresa's marriage to Francis Stephen of Lorraine refounded the dynasty as Habsburg-Lorraine, which ruled the Austrian Empire and then Austria-Hungary until the monarchy's collapse at the end of the First World War in 1918, when the Republic of German-Austria's Habsburg Law (1919) exiled the family and confiscated its property.

Famous members

  • Rudolf I (1218–1291) – founder of Habsburg Austria.
  • Maximilian I (1459–1519) – "the last knight", architect of the marriage strategy.
  • Charles V (1500–1558) – Holy Roman Emperor and King of Spain.
  • Philip II of Spain (1527–1598).
  • Maria Theresa (1717–1780) – ruler of the Habsburg lands for 40 years.
  • Franz Joseph I (1830–1916) – Emperor of Austria for 68 years.
  • Maximilian I of Mexico (1832–1867) – executed emperor of the Second Mexican Empire.
  • Otto von Habsburg (1912–2011) – crown prince, later a member of the European Parliament.
