Cutting (plant)

A plant cutting is a piece of a plant that is used in horticulture for vegetative (asexual) propagation. A piece of the stem or root of the source plant is placed in a suitable medium such as moist soil. If the conditions are suitable, the plant piece will begin to grow as a new plant independent of the parent, a process known as striking. A stem cutting produces new roots, and a root cutting produces new stems. Some plants can be grown from leaf pieces, called leaf cuttings, which produce both stems and roots. The scions used in grafting are also referred to as cuttings.

Technique

Propagating plants from cuttings is an ancient form of cloning. Several advantages of cuttings are: the new plants are genetically identical to the parent plant, so desirable traits such as fruit flavour, flower colour or disease resistance are preserved; many plants can be produced quickly; and cuttings allow propagation of plants that do not produce viable seed, such as many cultivated varieties of banana and fig.

A cutting is usually taken just below a node, where naturally occurring auxins accumulate. The cut surface often forms a callus before adventitious roots emerge. Success depends on the species, the season, the age of the material, temperature, moisture and light. Softwood cuttings are taken in spring from new growth; semi-ripe cuttings in summer; hardwood cuttings in the dormant season, typically from deciduous shrubs and trees.

Types

Stem cuttings

Stem cuttings are the most common method. They include softwood, semi-hardwood and hardwood cuttings, as well as heel cuttings (taken with a small piece of older wood) and nodal cuttings. Many houseplants such as pothos and tradescantia root readily in water or moist compost.

Leaf cuttings

Some plants, notably African violets, begonias and snake plants, can be propagated from a single leaf or leaf section. In bryophyllum, plantlets form naturally along the leaf margins.

Root cuttings

Plants that sucker freely, such as oriental poppies, raspberries and some trees, can be raised from sections of root taken in winter.

Media and environment

Cuttings are usually inserted into a free-draining medium such as a mixture of perlite, vermiculite, sand and compost, or rooted in water. High humidity around the cutting reduces water loss through transpiration before roots form; propagators and plastic covers are used to maintain it. Bottom heat accelerates rooting in many species. Synthetic rooting hormones containing indole-3-butyric acid (IBA) or naphthaleneacetic acid (NAA) are widely used commercially.
