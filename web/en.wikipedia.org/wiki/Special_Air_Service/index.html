Special Air Service

The Special Air Service (SAS) is a special forces unit of the British Army. It was founded as a regiment in 1941 by David Stirling and reconstituted as a corps in 1950.

History

Created in North Africa in 1941, 'L Detachment' raided Axis airfields behind enemy lines, destroying hundreds of aircraft, often with the Long Range Desert Group. It later operated in Italy and north-west Europe. Disbanded in 1945, it was revived for the Malayan Emergency, developing jungle warfare and counter-insurgency roles.

Modern role

The regular 22 SAS Regiment, with reserve regiments 21 and 23 SAS, forms part of United Kingdom Special Forces. Tasks include counter-terrorism — famously ending the 1980 Iranian Embassy siege live on television — reconnaissance, direct action and hostage rescue in the Falklands, Gulf, Iraq and Afghanistan wars.

Selection

Selection, held in the Brecon Beacons and jungle phases, is among the world's most gruelling, with very low pass rates. The SAS model has been copied by special forces worldwide, including Delta Force.
