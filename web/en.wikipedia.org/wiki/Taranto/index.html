Taranto

Taranto (Italian: [ˈtaːranto]; Tarantino dialect: Tarde) is a coastal city and port in Apulia, southern Italy, and the capital of the province of Taranto. It lies on the Gulf of Taranto, part of the Ionian Sea, between two arms of the sea known as the Mar Grande and the Mar Piccolo, and is sometimes called "the city of the two seas." It is a major Italian naval base and commercial port, and home to one of Europe's largest steelworks.

History

Taranto was founded in 706 BC by Spartan settlers as Taras, the only colony of Sparta, traditionally led by the Partheniae under Phalanthos. It became one of the richest cities of Magna Graecia, a centre of philosophy — the Pythagorean Archytas was a leading citizen — famous for its wool, purple dye and ceramics. Taranto's appeal to Pyrrhus of Epirus for help against Rome triggered the Pyrrhic War (280–275 BC); the city fell to Rome in 272 BC. It subsequently passed through Byzantine, Lombard, Saracen, Norman and Spanish hands.

Modern Taranto

In November 1940 the British Fleet Air Arm attacked the Italian fleet in Taranto harbour in the Battle of Taranto, the first all-aircraft naval strike in history, which influenced Japanese planning for Pearl Harbor. Modern Taranto is dominated by the port, the Italian Navy's principal base, and the vast ILVA steel plant, whose pollution has been the subject of long-running public-health and legal controversies. The old town (Città Vecchia) occupies an island between the two seas, connected by the Ponte Girevole swing bridge beside the Aragonese Castle.
