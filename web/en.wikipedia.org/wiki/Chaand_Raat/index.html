Chaand Raat

Chaand Raat (Urdu: چاند رات, Hindi: चाँद रात, Bengali: চাঁদ রাত; literally "Night of the Moon") is a celebration in South Asia marking the night before Eid al-Fitr, when the crescent moon (hilal) of Shawwal is sighted, signalling the end of Ramadan. It is observed with great festivity in Pakistan, India, and Bangladesh, and by South Asian diaspora communities elsewhere.

Traditions

After the announcement that the moon has been sighted — made by bodies such as Pakistan's Central Ruet-e-Hilal Committee — markets and bazaars remain open late into the night. Families shop for new clothes, shoes, sweets, and gifts. A hallmark of the night is women and girls decorating their hands and feet with intricate henna (mehndi) patterns, and buying colourful glass bangles (chooriyan). Stalls and salons do brisk trade well past midnight.

People exchange the greeting "Chaand Raat mubarak" ("Blessed night of the moon") and "Eid mubarak". In Bangladesh, the evening is associated with festive shopping in Dhaka's malls and markets and with special television programming. The night also traditionally marks last-minute preparations of Eid dishes such as sheer khurma and other sweets.

Significance

Chaand Raat functions as the joyful threshold between the fasting month of Ramadan and the festival of Eid al-Fitr. Though rooted in the religious act of moon sighting, it has become a major cultural and commercial occasion in South Asia, comparable in spirit to Christmas Eve in other traditions.
