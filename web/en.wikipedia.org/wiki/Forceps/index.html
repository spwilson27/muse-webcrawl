Forceps

Forceps (plural forceps, or considered a plural noun) are a handheld, hinged instrument used for grasping and holding objects. Forceps are used when fingers are too large to grasp small objects or when many objects need to be held at one time while the hands are used to perform a task. The term "forceps" is used almost exclusively in the fields of biology and medicine. Outside biology and medicine, people usually refer to forceps as tweezers, tongs, pliers, clips, or clamps.

Design

Mechanically, forceps employ the principle of the lever to grasp and apply pressure. Most designs consist of two elongated members joined by a hinge or spring, with tips shaped for the intended task — broad and flat for holding tissue, fine and pointed for extracting splinters, or cupped for handling small samples. Surgical forceps are commonly made of high-grade carbon steel or stainless steel and may be disposable or reusable.

Medical uses

Medical forceps include thumb forceps (tweezers) for handling tissue and dressings, hemostatic forceps (hemostats) for clamping blood vessels, and obstetrical forceps, large paired instruments designed to assist in the delivery of a baby. Dental forceps are used for extracting teeth, and specialized forceps serve in ophthalmic, ENT, and microsurgery.

Laboratory uses

In biology and chemistry laboratories, forceps are used to handle specimens, coverslips, filters, and hot crucibles (in the case of crucible tongs). Fine watchmaker-style forceps are standard tools in dissections and sample preparation.
