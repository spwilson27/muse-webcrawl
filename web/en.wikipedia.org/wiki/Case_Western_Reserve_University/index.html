Case Western Reserve University (CWRU) is a private research university in Cleveland, Ohio. It was formed in 1967 by the federation of Case Institute of Technology (founded 1880 by philanthropist Leonard Case Jr.) and Western Reserve University (founded 1826 in Hudson, Ohio, and later moved to Cleveland). The university is located in the University Circle neighbourhood, near the Cleveland Clinic, University Hospitals and major cultural institutions.

History

Western Reserve College was founded in 1826 in Hudson, in the Connecticut Western Reserve of Ohio. Its medical school became one of the earliest in the region. Case School of Applied Science, later Case Institute of Technology, developed strong engineering programmes. The two neighbouring institutions federated in 1967 as Case Western Reserve University.

Academics

CWRU comprises eight schools, including the College of Arts and Sciences, Case School of Engineering, Weatherhead School of Management, School of Medicine, Frances Payne Bolton School of Nursing, School of Dental Medicine, School of Law and the Mandel School of Applied Social Sciences. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — very high research activity." The Michelson–Morley experiment, a landmark in physics, was performed at the predecessor institutions in 1887.

Notable associations

The university counts numerous Nobel laureates among its alumni and faculty, and its medical school is consistently ranked among the top in the United States for research.
