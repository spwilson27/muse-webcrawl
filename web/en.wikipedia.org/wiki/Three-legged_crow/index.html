Three-legged crow

The three-legged crow is a mythological creature found in the mythologies of several East Asian cultures. In Chinese mythology it is the sanzuwu (三足烏, "three-legged crow"), a crow that lives in the sun; in Japanese mythology it is the Yatagarasu (八咫烏), a giant crow sent by the sun goddess Amaterasu to guide Emperor Jimmu; and in Korean mythology it is the samjok-o (삼족오), a symbol of the sun and of royal power.

China

In early Chinese art, notably on Han dynasty tomb murals including those at Mawangdui, a crow with three legs is depicted inside the solar disc. The Huainanzi and later texts connect the crow with Xihe, mother of the ten suns: originally ten three-legged crows lived in a mulberry tree and took turns crossing the sky as the sun, until their simultaneous appearance scorched the earth and the archer Yi shot down nine of them. The three legs have been interpreted as representing the sun's rising, zenith and setting, or the sun's association with the number three in Chinese cosmology.

Japan

The Yatagarasu of the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki was sent from heaven by Amaterasu to guide Jimmu, the legendary first emperor, through the mountains to Yamato. It remains a symbol of divine guidance: the Japan Football Association uses the Yatagarasu on the national team's crest.

Korea

The samjok-o appears on Goguryeo tomb murals and was regarded as a symbol of the sun, superior even to the dragon and phoenix in some traditions. It features in South Korean popular culture and corporate and governmental emblems.
