Sanford Ballard Dole (23 April 1844 – 9 June 1926) was a Hawaiian lawyer and jurist who became a central figure in the overthrow of the Hawaiian monarchy. He served as President of the Republic of Hawaii from 1894 to 1900 and, after annexation by the United States, as the first territorial governor of Hawaii.

Early life

Dole was born in Honolulu to American Protestant missionary parents, Daniel Dole and Emily Hoyt Ballard. He was educated at Punahou School (founded by his father) and Williams College in Massachusetts, and studied law before returning to Hawaii, where he built a successful legal practice. He served in the legislature of the Hawaiian Kingdom and was appointed a justice of the Supreme Court of Hawaii by King Kalākaua. His cousin James Dole later founded the pineapple company that became Dole Food Company.

Overthrow and Republic of Hawaii

In January 1893, American and European businessmen, backed by the U.S. minister and the landing of U.S. forces, overthrew Queen Liliʻuokalani and formed a provisional government. Dole, initially reluctant, agreed to head it. After U.S. President Grover Cleveland refused annexation and condemned the overthrow, the Republic of Hawaii was proclaimed on 4 July 1894 with Dole as president. His government suppressed a royalist counter-rebellion in 1895, after which the Queen formally abdicated. Following the Spanish–American War, the United States annexed Hawaii in 1898.

Governor and later life

President William McKinley appointed Dole the first governor of the Territory of Hawaii in 1900; he served until 1903, when he became a U.S. district court judge, a post he held until 1915. Dole remained an influential figure in territorial Hawaii until his death in Honolulu in 1926. His legacy is contested: celebrated by annexationists as a nation-builder, he is criticised by Native Hawaiian sovereignty advocates for his role in ending the monarchy.
