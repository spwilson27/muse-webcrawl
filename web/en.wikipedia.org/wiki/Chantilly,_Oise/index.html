Chantilly, Oise

The Château de Chantilly

Chantilly is a commune in the Oise department in northern France, about 50 kilometres north of Paris. The town is best known for the Château de Chantilly, one of the finest princely residences in France, and for Chantilly cream, the sweetened whipped cream named after the town.

The château

The Château de Chantilly houses the Musée Condé, which holds one of France's most important collections of old master paintings and illuminated manuscripts, including the Très Riches Heures du Duc de Berry. The estate was bequeathed to the Institut de France by the Duke of Aumale in the 19th century.

Horse racing

Chantilly is a major centre of horse racing and training. Its racecourse, opened in 1834, hosts the Prix du Jockey Club and the Prix de Diane, and thousands of thoroughbreds are trained in the surrounding forest.
