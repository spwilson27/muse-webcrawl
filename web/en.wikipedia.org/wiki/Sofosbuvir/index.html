Sofosbuvir


Trade names: Sovaldi, others
Class: NS5B polymerase inhibitor
Formula: C22H29FN3O9P
Approved: 2013 (FDA)

Sofosbuvir, sold under the brand name Sovaldi among others, is a medication used to treat hepatitis C. It is a nucleotide analogue inhibitor of the hepatitis C virus (HCV) NS5B RNA polymerase, developed by Pharmasset and acquired by Gilead Sciences. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Sofosbuvir is used in combination with other antivirals, such as ledipasvir (in the fixed-dose combination Harvoni), daclatasvir, velpatasvir (Epclusa, effective against all genotypes), and ribavirin. Combination regimens cure over 90% of treated patients, transforming hepatitis C into a curable disease for most people. Treatment is oral and typically lasts 8 to 12 weeks.

Mechanism of action

Sofosbuvir is a prodrug metabolised to the active uridine triphosphate analogue, which is incorporated into HCV RNA by the NS5B polymerase and acts as a chain terminator.

History and pricing

Approved by the US FDA in December 2013, sofosbuvir was initially priced in the United States at about US$1,000 per pill (roughly $84,000 per course), sparking a major public debate about drug pricing. Generic versions are produced under voluntary licences in many low- and middle-income countries at a small fraction of that cost.
