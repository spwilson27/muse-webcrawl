STS-99

STS-99 was a NASA Space Shuttle mission flown by Endeavour in February 2000, carrying the Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM). During the 11-day flight, the crew used specially modified radar to map the Earth's surface between 60° north and 56° south latitude, producing the most complete high-resolution digital topographic database of Earth of its time.

Crew

Commander Kevin Kregel and pilot Dominic Gorie flew with mission specialists Janet Kavandi, Janice Voss, Mamoru Mohri (of Japan's NASDA), and Gerhard Thiele (of the European Space Agency).

The SRTM payload

SRTM used interferometric synthetic aperture radar: C-band and X-band antennas in the payload bay worked with receiving antennas at the end of a 60-metre (200 ft) mast — the longest rigid structure flown in space at the time. Comparing signals from the two antenna positions yielded elevation data. The mission mapped over 119 million square kilometres, and SRTM elevation data remain a standard reference dataset for geographic information systems worldwide.
