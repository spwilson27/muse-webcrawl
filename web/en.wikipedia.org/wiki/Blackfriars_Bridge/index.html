Blackfriars Bridge is a road and foot traffic bridge over the River Thames in London, between Waterloo Bridge and Blackfriars Railway Bridge, carrying the A201 road. The present bridge, built of wrought iron with five spans, was designed by Joseph Cubitt and opened by Queen Victoria in 1869. Its name derives from the Dominican friary ("black friars") that once stood nearby.

History

The first fixed crossing at the site was a stone arch bridge designed by Robert Mylne, opened in 1769 as a toll bridge and initially named the William Pitt Bridge. By the 1860s it required replacement, and the present iron bridge was constructed between 1864 and 1869. The bridge was widened in the early 20th century and refurbished in the 1970s and 1990s. The red-painted piers visible in the river nearby are remnants of an earlier railway bridge.

Roberto Calvi

In June 1982, the Italian banker Roberto Calvi, chairman of Banco Ambrosiano, was found hanging from scaffolding beneath an arch of Blackfriars Bridge. The death, initially ruled suicide, was later investigated as murder and became central to the Banco Ambrosiano scandal; newspapers linked the location, speculatively, to the "black friars" nickname.
