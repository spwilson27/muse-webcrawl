Quảng Trị province

Quảng Trị (Vietnamese: [kwãːŋ˧˩ ʈi˧ˀ˨]) is a province in the North Central Coast region of Vietnam, north of the former imperial capital of Huế. Its capital is the city of Đông Hà. The province is bordered by Quảng Bình to the north, Thừa Thiên-Huế to the south, Laos to the west, and the South China Sea (East Sea) to the east.

Hiền Lương Bridge over the Bến Hải River

History

Quảng Trị lay just south of the Vietnamese Demilitarized Zone: the Bến Hải River, crossed by the Hiền Lương Bridge, formed the provisional boundary between North and South Vietnam from 1954 to 1976. The province saw some of the heaviest fighting of the Vietnam War, including the Battle of Khe Sanh (1968), fought around a U.S. Marine combat base near the Laotian border, and the fierce battles of the 1972 Easter Offensive, when Quảng Trị city was devastated and briefly captured, then retaken. The ancient citadel of Quảng Trị and the Vĩnh Mốc tunnels, where villagers lived underground to survive bombing, are preserved as war memorials.

The province suffered extensive unexploded ordnance contamination, and demining efforts continue. In 2025, under Vietnam's provincial restructuring, Quảng Trị was merged with Quảng Bình province, retaining the name Quảng Trị.

Geography

The terrain narrows from the Annamite mountain range in the west to a coastal plain along the Thạch Hãn and Bến Hải rivers. The economy rests on agriculture, forestry, fishing, and growing war-history tourism.
