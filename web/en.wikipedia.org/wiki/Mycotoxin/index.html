Mycotoxin

A mycotoxin (from the Greek μύκης mykes, "fungus" and τοξικός toxikos, "poisonous") is a toxic secondary metabolite produced by fungi, especially moulds, that can contaminate food and feed crops. Mycotoxins can cause disease and death in humans and other animals, and illnesses caused by them are called mycotoxicoses.

Major groups

The most important groups of mycotoxins include the aflatoxins, produced by Aspergillus species and among the most potent known carcinogens; ochratoxin A; fumonisins produced by Fusarium species in maize; trichothecenes such as deoxynivalenol (vomitoxin) and T-2 toxin; zearalenone, an oestrogenic toxin; patulin, found in mouldy fruit; and the ergot alkaloids of Claviceps purpurea, historically responsible for ergotism ("St Anthony's fire").

Occurrence and regulation

Mycotoxin contamination occurs in cereals, nuts, spices, dried fruits, coffee and animal feed, both before and after harvest, particularly under warm, humid storage conditions. Because of their health effects, many countries set regulatory limits for mycotoxins in food and feed, and monitoring programmes test commodities for contamination. Preventing fungal growth and proper drying and storage are the principal control measures.
