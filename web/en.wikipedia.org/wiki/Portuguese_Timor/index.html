Portuguese Timor

Portuguese Timor (Timor Português)
Status: Colony of Portugal
Period: 1702–1975
Capital: Dili

Portuguese Timor (Portuguese: Timor Português) was a Portuguese colony that existed between 1702 and 1975 on the eastern half of the island of Timor in Maritime Southeast Asia, along with the exclave of Oecusse and the islands of Atauro and Jaco. It corresponds to the present-day country of East Timor (Timor-Leste).

Portuguese traders and missionaries reached Timor in the early sixteenth century, drawn by trade in sandalwood. The first colonial governor was appointed in 1702, and the capital moved to Dili in 1769. The border with Dutch Timor in the west was settled by treaties in 1859 and 1904, though arbitration was needed to finalise it in 1914.

Second World War and decolonisation

Despite Portugal's neutrality, Australian and Dutch forces occupied the territory in 1941, and Japan invaded in February 1942; the ensuing Battle of Timor involved guerrilla warfare in which many Timorese died. Portuguese rule was restored in 1945.

After Portugal's Carnation Revolution in 1974, decolonisation began abruptly. Timorese parties formed, civil conflict broke out in 1975, and Fretilin declared independence on 28 November 1975. Days later Indonesia invaded, beginning a 24-year occupation. Timor-Leste finally restored its independence in 2002 following a UN-sponsored referendum in 1999.
