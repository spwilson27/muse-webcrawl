Raphael (Hebrew: רְפָאֵל, Rəfāʾēl, "God has healed") is an archangel first mentioned in the Book of Tobit and in the Book of Enoch. He is one of the three archangels named in the traditions of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, alongside Michael and Gabriel. In the Islamic tradition he is identified with Israfil, the angel who will blow the trumpet at the end of time.

In the Book of Tobit

Raphael's principal narrative role is in the deuterocanonical Book of Tobit, where he appears in human form under the name "Azariah" and accompanies Tobit's son Tobias on a journey to Media. Along the way he instructs Tobias to catch a large fish and preserve its heart, liver, and gall. The fish's organs are later used to drive away the demon Asmodeus, who had killed Sarah's seven previous husbands, and to cure Tobit's blindness. Only at the end of the journey does Raphael reveal himself, saying: "I am Raphael, one of the seven holy angels who present the prayers of the saints and enter into the presence of the glory of the Holy One" (Tobit 12:15).

In the Book of Enoch

In the Book of Enoch, Raphael is one of the watchers' overseers and the guide of the souls of the dead. He is entrusted with healing the earth corrupted by the fallen angels and is commanded to bind the fallen angel Azazel hand and foot and cast him into darkness in the desert of Dudael.

Veneration

Raphael is venerated as the patron of travellers, the blind, physicians, and healing. Since 1921 his feast, formerly kept on 24 October, has been celebrated in the Roman calendar on 29 September together with Michael and Gabriel. Numerous churches, hospitals, and the Spanish city of Córdoba — which claims Raphael appeared in the 16th century and ended a plague — are under his patronage.
