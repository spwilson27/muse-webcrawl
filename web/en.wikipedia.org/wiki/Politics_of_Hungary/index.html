Politics of Hungary

Polity type: Unitary parliamentary republic

Constitution: Fundamental Law of Hungary (2012)

Head of state: President of Hungary

Head of government: Prime Minister of Hungary

The politics of Hungary take place in a framework of a parliamentary representative democratic republic. The Prime Minister is the head of government of a pluriform multi-party system, while the President is head of state with largely ceremonial powers.

Constitution

Hungary's current constitution, the Fundamental Law, entered into force on 1 January 2012, replacing the 1949 constitution as amended in 1989–90. Its adoption by the governing Fidesz–KDNP coalition, which held a two-thirds parliamentary supermajority, was controversial domestically and internationally.

Executive branch

The Prime Minister is elected by the National Assembly on the proposal of the President and leads the government. Since 2010 the office has been held by Viktor Orbán of the Fidesz party, whose alliance has won consecutive two-thirds supermajorities in the elections of 2010, 2014, 2018 and 2022. The President is elected by the National Assembly for a five-year term.

Legislature

The National Assembly (Országgyűlés) is unicameral and has 199 members (reduced from 386 in 2014) elected for four years in a mixed system of single-member constituencies and a national party list.

Political parties and debates

Hungarian politics since 2010 has been characterised by debates over judicial independence, media pluralism, electoral rules and relations with the European Union, including rule-of-law proceedings under Article 7 and disputes over EU funding. Opposition parties have at times united behind joint candidates and lists in an effort to overcome the governing party's advantages in the electoral system.
