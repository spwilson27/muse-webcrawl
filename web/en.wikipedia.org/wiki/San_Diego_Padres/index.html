The San Diego Padres are an American professional baseball team based in San Diego. They compete in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member club of the National League West division. The Padres adopted their name from the Pacific Coast League team that arrived in San Diego in 1936; the name (Spanish for "fathers") refers to the Spanish Franciscan friars who founded San Diego in 1769. The team was established as an expansion franchise in 1969.

History

The Padres struggled through their early seasons at San Diego (later Jack Murphy) Stadium. In 1984, led by Tony Gwynn, Steve Garvey, and Goose Gossage, they won their first National League pennant, defeating the Chicago Cubs in the NLCS before losing the World Series to the Detroit Tigers. In 1998, with Gwynn, Ken Caminiti, and Trevor Hoffman, the club won 98 games and again reached the World Series, losing to the New York Yankees. Since 2004 the team has played at Petco Park in downtown San Diego.

Tony Gwynn and identity

The franchise's most celebrated player is Tony Gwynn, "Mr. Padre," who spent his entire 20-year career (1982–2001) in San Diego, winning eight batting titles and 3,141 hits. Hall of Fame closer Trevor Hoffman recorded 601 saves, most of them as a Padre. In 2020 the team returned to its historic brown-and-gold colour scheme, long favoured by fans. San Diego remains the largest American city without a major professional sports championship, and the Padres are one of a small number of MLB franchises never to have won the World Series; their recent star-led squads, featuring players such as Fernando Tatís Jr. and Manny Machado, have kept them in contention in the 2020s.
