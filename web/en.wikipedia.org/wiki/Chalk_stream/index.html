Chalk stream

A chalk stream in southern England

Chalk streams are rivers that rise from springs in landscapes with chalk bedrock. Since chalk is permeable, water percolates through the ground rather than running off the surface, so these streams are fed almost entirely by groundwater held in the chalk aquifer. As a result, chalk streams have stable flows, cool temperatures, and exceptionally clear, mineral-rich water.

There are roughly 200 chalk streams in the world, and about 85 percent of them are found in England, concentrated in the south and east. Well-known examples include the River Test, the River Itchen, and the River Lambourn in Berkshire.

Ecology

The constant temperature (around 10–11 °C year-round) and nutrient-rich water support distinctive wildlife, including Atlantic salmon, brown trout, water voles, otters, and beds of water-crowfoot. Chalk streams are a priority habitat under the UK Biodiversity Action Plan.

Threats

Abstraction of groundwater for public supply, agricultural runoff, and sewage discharges have degraded many chalk streams. Conservation campaigns have sought legal protection for these rare habitats, and in recent years some rivers, such as the River Test, have seen improved management of water abstraction.
