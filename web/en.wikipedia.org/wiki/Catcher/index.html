Catcher

Position number: 2
Tools: Mitt, mask, chest protector, shin guards
Position: Behind home plate

Catcher is a position in baseball and softball. When a batter takes their turn to hit, the catcher crouches behind home plate, in front of the (home) umpire, and receives the ball from the pitcher. In addition to this primary duty, the catcher is also called upon to master many other skills in order to field that position properly. The role of the catcher is similar to that of the wicket-keeper in cricket.

Positioned behind home plate and facing toward the outfield, the catcher can see the whole field, and is thus in the best position to direct and lead the other players in a defensive play. The catcher typically calls for pitches using hand signals. The calls are based on the pitcher's mechanics and strengths, as well as the batter's tendencies and weaknesses.

Duties

The catcher's responsibilities include receiving pitches, blocking balls in the dirt, throwing out base runners attempting to steal, fielding bunts and pop-ups near home plate, and applying tags on plays at the plate. Catchers also confer with pitchers about pitch selection and positioning. Because of the physical demands of crouching for an entire game and the risk of foul tips and collisions, catchers wear extensive protective equipment: a mask or helmet, chest protector, shin guards and a heavily padded mitt.

History

In the earliest days of baseball the catcher stood well behind the batter and caught the ball on the bounce. The position moved progressively closer during the 19th century as equipment — gloves, masks and chest protectors — was introduced, largely through the innovations of players such as James White and Fred Thayer.

Notable catchers

Hall of Fame catchers include Johnny Bench, Yogi Berra, Roy Campanella, Gary Carter, Mickey Cochrane, Carlton Fisk, Josh Gibson, Mike Piazza and Iván Rodríguez.
