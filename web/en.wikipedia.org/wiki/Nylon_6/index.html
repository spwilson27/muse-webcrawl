Nylon 6

Nylon 6, or polycaprolactam, is a semi-crystalline polyamide polymer developed by Paul Schlack at IG Farben in Germany in 1938. Unlike nylon 66, which is formed from two monomers, nylon 6 is synthesised from a single monomer, caprolactam, by ring-opening polymerisation.

Production

Caprolactam is heated to around 260 °C in the presence of water, which opens the ring and allows the molecules to link into long chains. Roughly 10% of the monomer remains in equilibrium with the polymer and is washed out and recycled. Nylon 6 has the same repeating unit composition as nylon 66 but a slightly lower melting point (about 220 °C) and somewhat different dyeing and elasticity characteristics.

Applications

Nylon 6 is spun into fibres for carpets, clothing, tyre cord, and industrial fabrics, and is moulded or extruded into engineering plastics for gears, bearings, and automotive parts. One of its largest uses is in carpet fibre and in fishing nets and ropes, where its toughness and abrasion resistance are valued.
