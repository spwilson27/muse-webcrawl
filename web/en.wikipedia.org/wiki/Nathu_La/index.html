Nathu La

Nathu La
Elevation: 4,310 m (14,140 ft)
Location: Sikkim, India – Tibet, China border
Range: Himalayas

Nathu La (Tibetan: "pass of the listening ears") is a mountain pass in the Dongkya Range of the Himalayas between China's Yadong County in Tibet and the Indian states of Sikkim and West Bengal, at an elevation of about 4,310 metres. It connects the Indian town of Kalimpong and Gangtok with the Chumbi Valley and historically formed a branch of the ancient Silk Road between India and Lhasa.

History

The pass was a major trade route in the late 19th and early 20th centuries following the British expedition to Tibet (1904). Trade ceased after the Sino-Indian War of 1962, and the pass was the site of armed clashes between Indian and Chinese troops in 1967 — the Nathu La and Cho La incidents, in which artillery duels killed several hundred soldiers. Border trade through Nathu La was formally resumed on 6 July 2006, initially for a limited list of goods, and pilgrim traffic to Kailash-Manasarovar via the pass began in 2015.

Tourism and geography

Indian tourists visit the pass, the nearby Tsomgo Lake and the Baba Harbhajan Singh memorial temple, subject to permits. The area is a habitat of the red panda and high-altitude alpine vegetation, and the pass typically closes in winter due to snow.
