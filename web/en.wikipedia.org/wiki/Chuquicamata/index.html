Chuquicamata

The Chuquicamata open pit, one of the largest in the world

Chuquicamata (/tʃuːkiːkəˈmɑːtə/), often called Chuqui, is the largest open-pit copper mine in terms of excavated volume in the world. It is located in northern Chile, in the Atacama Desert near the city of Calama, at about 2,850 m (9,350 ft) above sea level. The mine is owned and operated by Codelco, the Chilean state copper company.

The pit is roughly 4.3 km (2.7 mi) long, 3 km (1.9 mi) wide, and more than 850 m (2,790 ft) deep, making it the second-deepest open-pit mine in the world after Bingham Canyon Mine in Utah.

History

Copper was mined in the district in pre-Hispanic times, and industrial development began in the early twentieth century under the Guggenheim family's Chile Exploration Company, with large-scale production starting in 1915. The mine later passed to the Anaconda Copper Mining Company. It was nationalised under President Salvador Allende in 1971 as part of the Chilean nationalisation of copper.

Underground transition and the town

As open-pit mining became less economic at depth, Codelco developed a massive underground mine beneath the pit, inaugurated in 2019, to extend operations for decades. The adjoining mining town of Chuquicamata, which once housed thousands of workers and their families, was evacuated and buried under mine waste in the 2000s for environmental and expansion reasons; its residents were relocated to Calama.
