Sesame oil

Sesame oil is an edible vegetable oil derived from sesame seeds. It is used as a cooking oil in many cuisines and, because of its distinctive nutty aroma and flavour, also as a flavouring agent, especially in East and Southeast Asian dishes.

Varieties

Light sesame oil, pressed from untoasted seeds, is pale and relatively neutral, with a high smoke point suited to frying; it is a common cooking medium in Indian cuisine. Dark sesame oil, pressed from toasted seeds, is amber to brown, intensely fragrant, and used in small quantities as a finishing oil in Chinese, Japanese and Korean cooking. Cold-pressed sesame oil is also used in Ayurvedic practice, including oil pulling.

Composition and properties

Sesame oil is rich in polyunsaturated and monounsaturated fatty acids, notably linoleic and oleic acids, and contains the lignans sesamin and sesamol, antioxidants that give the oil unusual resistance to rancidity. The oil also finds use in cosmetics, massage and as a solvent in some pharmaceutical preparations.

Production

Seeds are cleaned and pressed mechanically or by expeller; oil may then be filtered or refined. India and Myanmar are among the major producers, alongside China, Sudan and Tanzania.
