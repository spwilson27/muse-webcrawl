Yokozuna

Yokozuna (Japanese: 横綱) is the highest rank in professional sumo wrestling. The word refers to the thick white rope (tsuna) worn around the waist during the ring-entering ceremony, the most distinctive symbol of the rank. As of the modern era, over seventy wrestlers have been promoted to yokozuna since the rank was formalized in the Edo period.

The rank

Unlike every other sumo rank, a yokozuna cannot be demoted. Instead, a yokozuna whose performance falls — or whose conduct is judged to disgrace the rank — is expected to retire. Promotion is decided by the Japan Sumo Association on the recommendation of the Yokozuna Deliberation Council, an external advisory body; the usual sporting criterion is two consecutive tournament championships (or an equivalent record) while ranked ōzeki. Beyond results, candidates are judged on hinkaku — dignity and grace befitting the rank.

Ceremony and regalia

The newly promoted yokozuna performs his first ring-entering ceremony (dohyō-iri) at Meiji Shrine in Tokyo, wearing the tsuna — a heavy rope of white hemp weighing around 15–20 kg, tied with a distinctive knot at the back and hung with zigzag paper streamers (shide) like those at Shinto shrines. Two styles of ceremony exist: Unryū (one arm raised) and Shiranui (both arms extended), traditionally associated with the 10th yokozuna Unryū Kyūkichi and the 11th, Shiranui Kōemon. In tournaments, the yokozuna enters accompanied by a sword-bearing attendant (tachimochi) and a herald (tsuyuharai), and performs the hand-clapping, leg-stomping ritual meant to drive evil spirits from the ring.

Notable yokozuna

The most successful yokozuna of the modern era include Taihō (32 tournament championships), Chiyonofuji (31), and Hakuhō, who retired in 2021 with a record 45 top-division championships. Akebono became the first non-Japanese-born yokozuna in 1993, followed by Musashimaru and a succession of Mongolian-born champions including Asashōryū and Hakuhō.
