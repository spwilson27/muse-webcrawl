Bogeyman

The bogeyman (also spelled boogeyman, bogyman, or boogieman) is a type of mythical creature used by adults to frighten children into socially acceptable behaviour. Bogeymen have no specific appearance, and conceptions vary drastically by household and culture; they are sometimes depicted as a masked, cloven-hoofed, or amorphous figure. Most often, such figures are invoked as a threat to punish disobedient children.

Origins and analogues

The word is probably related to boggart and the Middle English bogge or bugge, meaning a terror or goblin. Bogeyman-like figures appear worldwide: the Spanish and Latin American Cuco or Coco, the Sack Man (Hombre del saco), the German Butzemann, Slavic Baba Yaga, and the French Croque-mitaine. In many traditions the figure carries away naughty children in a bag.

In popular culture

The bogeyman has become a stock figure of horror fiction and film, including the character of Michael Myers in the Halloween franchise and the title figure in Stephen King's short story "The Boogeyman".
