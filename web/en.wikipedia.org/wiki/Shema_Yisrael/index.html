Shema Yisrael

Shema Yisrael (Hebrew: שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל, "Hear, O Israel"), often shortened to the Shema, is a Jewish prayer that serves as a centrepiece of the morning and evening prayer services. Its first verse encapsulates the monotheistic essence of Judaism:

שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ יְהוָה אֶחָֽד

"Hear, O Israel: the LORD is our God, the LORD is One." (Deuteronomy 6:4)

Hero image: a kosher parchment (klaf) bearing the Shema, of the kind placed in a mezuzah and tefillin.

Content

The full Shema in the liturgy consists of three passages from the Torah:

  • Deuteronomy 6:4–9 — the declaration of God's unity and the commandments to love God, teach the words to one's children, bind them as tefillin, and inscribe them on doorposts (mezuzah).
  • Deuteronomy 11:13–21 — the promise of reward for obedience and punishment for disobedience, the "acceptance of the yoke of the commandments".
  • Numbers 15:37–41 — the commandment of tzitzit (ritual fringes) and the remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt.

Immediately after the first verse, worshippers traditionally whisper the response "Blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom for ever and ever." When reciting the first verse, it is customary to cover the eyes with the right hand, concentrating wholly on the declaration.

Observance

Reciting the Shema twice daily is regarded as a biblical commandment (mitzvah) — "when you lie down and when you rise up". It is also recited as the last words before sleep (Keri'at Shema al ha-Mitah), at the beginning of the central Amidah's preceding blessings, and traditionally as a martyr's or dying person's final declaration of faith. The story of Rabbi Akiva, who is said to have recited the Shema while being executed by the Romans, made it a symbol of Jewish martyrdom (Kiddush Hashem). A shortened form is taught to young children as their first prayer.

Significance

The Shema is often called the "watchword of Judaism": a compact affirmation of monotheism, covenant and the duty of transmission to the next generation. Its verses appear on mezuzot on Jewish doorposts and within tefillin worn in prayer. Jesus quotes the Shema's command to love God as the "first and greatest commandment" in the Gospels, paired with the command to love one's neighbour.
