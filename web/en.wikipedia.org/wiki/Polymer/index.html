Polymer

A polymer is a large molecule (macromolecule) built from many repeating small units called monomers, linked by covalent bonds — the word comes from Greek poly (many) and meros (parts). The chain may contain hundreds of thousands of units.

Natural polymers run life itself: proteins (from amino acids), DNA and RNA (from nucleotides), cellulose and starch (from glucose). Synthetic polymers — polyethylene, PVC, nylon, polyester, Teflon — are the plastics, fibres and rubbers of modern industry, produced on a scale of hundreds of millions of tonnes a year.

Making polymers

In addition polymerisation, unsaturated monomers add together with no atoms lost (polyethylene from ethene). In condensation polymerisation, monomers join with loss of a small molecule such as water (nylon, polyester, proteins). Cross-linking between chains turns soft plastics into hard thermosets and vulcanised rubber.

History

Hermann Staudinger proposed the macromolecular hypothesis in the 1920s against strong resistance, winning the 1953 Nobel Prize. Wallace Carothers at DuPont created nylon (1935) and neoprene, founding the modern synthetic polymer industry.
