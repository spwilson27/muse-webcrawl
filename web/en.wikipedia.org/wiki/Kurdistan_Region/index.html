The Kurdistan Region is an autonomous region of federal Iraq, governed by the Kurdistan Regional Government and comprising the governorates of Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, Duhok and Halabja, with other Kurdish-majority territories disputed with Baghdad.

History

Kurdish autonomy was promised in the 1970 March Manifesto but collapsed into war; after the 1991 Gulf War uprising and the no-fly zone, Kurds established de facto self-rule, marred by KDP–PUK civil war (1994–98). Iraq's 2005 constitution recognised the region. Its Peshmerga forces fought the Islamic State from 2014, taking Kirkuk, which Iraqi forces retook after the controversial 2017 independence referendum, in which over 90% voted for independence but which Baghdad and the international community rejected.

Economy and society

Oil exports, agriculture and tourism drive the economy; Erbil's ancient citadel is a UNESCO site. The region hosts large numbers of displaced Iraqis and Syrian refugees.
