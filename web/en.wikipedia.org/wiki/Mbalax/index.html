Mbalax

Mbalax (or mbalakh) is the national popular dance music of Senegal and the Gambia. It fuses traditional Wolof sabar drumming with Western instruments and influences from Afro-Cuban music, jazz, soul, rock and funk. The name derives from the Wolof word for the accompanying rhythms played on the sabar drums.

Development

In the 1970s, Dakar bands such as the Star Band and Orchestra Baobab played mainly Afro-Cuban styles. The group Étoile de Dakar, and later Super Étoile, led by Youssou N'Dour, began systematically incorporating sabar percussion — tama (talking drum), and the layered sabar drum family — into band music, accelerating tempos and adding keyboards and electric guitars. N'Dour's international success from the late 1980s, including the duet "7 Seconds" with Neneh Cherry (1994), carried mbalax to a global audience.

Characteristics

Mbalax is characterised by explosive, complex sabar breaks (bakk), rapid tama lines that imitate Wolof speech tones, call-and-response singing in Wolof, and stop-start arrangements designed for dancing — including the athletic ventilateur dance style. Lyrics often praise patrons, recount social news and address moral themes, continuing the griot tradition in popular form. Other leading artists include Thione Seck, Omar Pène, Baaba Maal (who blends mbalax with traditional pulaar music) and Viviane N'Dour.
