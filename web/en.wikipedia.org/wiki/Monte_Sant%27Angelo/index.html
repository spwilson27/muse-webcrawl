Monte Sant'Angelo is a town and comune of Apulia, southern Italy, in the province of Foggia, on the southern slopes of Monte Gargano. It is famous for the Sanctuary of Saint Michael the Archangel, one of the oldest shrines to St Michael in western Europe.

History

According to tradition, the Archangel Michael appeared in a cave on the mountain in 490 AD and again in subsequent years, asking that the site be consecrated to Christian worship. The sanctuary became a major centre of pilgrimage in the Middle Ages, visited by crusaders, saints including Francis of Assisi, and numerous popes. The town grew up around the shrine, and a Norman castle was built above it.

Sanctuary of Saint Michael

The sanctuary complex includes the cave church (the Grotta), the octagonal Torre Campanaria built by Frederick II, and a museum. Since 2011 it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the serial site "Longobards in Italy: Places of the Power".

Geography

The town stands about 796 metres above sea level on the Gargano promontory, with views over the Adriatic Sea and the plain of Manfredonia below.
