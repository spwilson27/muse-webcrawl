Comparative officer ranks

Comparative officer ranks are systems for aligning the commissioned officer ranks of different armed forces so that equivalent grades can be identified across countries and services. The most widely used framework is the NATO rank code system (STANAG 2116), which assigns codes from OF-1 to OF-10.

History

Modern rank titles derive largely from the organisation of European armies of the 16th–18th centuries: "captain" (head of a company), "lieutenant" (one who takes the place of a superior), "colonel" (commander of a column), and "general" (officer of general duties). Naval ranks developed separately, which is why a naval captain (OF-5) far outranks an army captain (OF-2) — a frequent source of confusion that comparative tables are designed to resolve.
