Minh Mạng (25 May 1791 – 20 January 1841), born Nguyễn Phúc Đảm, was the second emperor of the Nguyễn dynasty of Vietnam, reigning from 1820 until his death. The fourth son of the founding emperor Gia Long, he was chosen as heir over the descendants of the eldest prince, Nguyễn Phúc Cảnh, a decision that shaped the dynasty's direction.

Reign

Minh Mạng was an energetic and strongly Confucian ruler who centralized the imperial administration, reorganizing Vietnam into provinces under close court control. He suppressed major revolts, including the Lê Văn Khôi rebellion in the south (1833–1835), expanded Vietnamese influence over Cambodia, and in 1834 formally annexed Cambodian territory as Trấn Tây province, though the occupation provoked resistance and was abandoned after his death. He renamed the country Đại Nam in 1838.

Attitude toward the West

Distrustful of European influence, Minh Mạng restricted foreign trade, refused diplomatic overtures from France and the United States, and persecuted Catholic missionaries and converts, particularly after missionaries were implicated in the southern rebellion. His policies set Vietnam on a collision course with France later in the century.

Legacy

A prolific poet, Minh Mạng fathered 142 children. His mausoleum, set in landscaped grounds west of Huế, is regarded as one of the most harmonious of the Nguyễn imperial tombs and is part of the UNESCO-listed Complex of Huế Monuments. He is generally judged the most capable of the Nguyễn emperors, though also one of the most conservative.
