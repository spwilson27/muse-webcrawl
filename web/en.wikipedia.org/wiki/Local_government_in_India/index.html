Local government in India

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
A gram sabha, the foundation of rural self-government

Local government in India is the third tier of governance in the Republic of India, below the Union government and the state governments. It consists of rural bodies organised under the Panchayati Raj system and urban bodies known as municipalities. Although local bodies existed earlier — and the idea of village self-rule through panchayats is ancient — they received firm constitutional status only with the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts of 1992, in force from 1993.

Structure

Rural India is governed by a three-tier panchayat structure in most states: the gram panchayat at village level (its general body is the gram sabha of all registered voters), the panchayat samiti (block samiti) at the intermediate or block level, and the zilla panchayat at district level. Urban areas are served by three kinds of municipal bodies: municipal corporations (mahanagar palikas) for large cities, municipal councils for smaller urban areas, and nagar panchayats for areas in transition from rural to urban, plus cantonment boards and notified area committees in special cases. Elections to local bodies are held every five years under state election commissions, and seats are reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in proportion to population, with not less than one-third — and in many states one-half — of seats and chairperson offices reserved for women. The Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules of the Constitution list functions that states may devolve, from drinking water, roads and health to poverty alleviation and primary education. In practice the depth of devolution of "funds, functions and functionaries" varies greatly by state; Kerala's People's Plan Campaign is frequently cited as a model. Local bodies finance themselves from property and profession taxes, state transfers and grants recommended by the Union and state finance commissions, including the Fifteenth Finance Commission's substantial tied grants for water and sanitation.
