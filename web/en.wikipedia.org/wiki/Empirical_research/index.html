Empirical research

Empirical research is research using empirical evidence — knowledge acquired by means of the senses, particularly by observation and experimentation. It is a way of gaining knowledge by direct and indirect observation or experience, in contrast to knowledge derived purely from reasoning, tradition, or authority. Empirical research may be qualitative or quantitative.

Scientific method

Empirical research is the backbone of the scientific method: a researcher formulates a hypothesis, derives testable predictions, gathers data through observation or experiment, and evaluates whether the evidence supports or refutes the hypothesis. Findings are documented so that other researchers can replicate and verify them — a hallmark distinguishing empirical work from anecdote.

Types

Quantitative empirical research assigns numerical values to observations and typically analyses them statistically, as in controlled experiments, surveys, and econometric studies. Qualitative empirical research instead produces descriptive data — interviews, field notes, case studies — analysed for patterns and meanings. Many fields combine both approaches in mixed-methods designs.
