Forma (botany)

In botanical nomenclature, a forma (Latin for "form"; plural formae), abbreviated f., is one of the secondary taxonomic ranks below variety (varietas). It is the lowest principal rank recognised by the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants (ICN). A forma is typically used to denote a sporadic variant distinguished by a minor character — such as flower colour, leaf shape, or habit — that occurs within populations of a species or variety without a clear geographical pattern.

Usage

The name of a forma is written as a trinomial with the rank marker: for example, Quercus robur f. fastigiata for the upright-growing form of the English oak, or white-flowered variants of normally coloured species, often named f. albiflora. Because formae usually reflect single-gene or environmentally minor differences rather than evolutionarily significant lineages, many botanists make little use of the rank today, and numerous formae named in the 19th and early 20th centuries have been abandoned or treated as synonyms without rank. The rank remains useful in horticulture and floristics for recording distinctive, recurring variants.

Position in the hierarchy

The standard sequence of infraspecific ranks under the ICN is:

  1. Subspecies (subsp.)
  2. Variety (var.)
  3. Subvariety (subvar.)
  4. Forma (f.)
  5. Subforma (subf.)

A similar rank exists in zoological nomenclature only informally; the zoological code does not regulate names below subspecies, treating "forms" as infrasubspecific.
