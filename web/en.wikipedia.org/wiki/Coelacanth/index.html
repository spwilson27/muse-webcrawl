Coelacanth

Coelacanths are an ancient order of lobe-finned fishes (Actinistia) once known only from fossils and thought extinct since the Late Cretaceous, until a living specimen was caught off South Africa in 1938. The two living species of the genus Latimeria are classic 'living fossils'.

Rediscovery

On 23 December 1938 trawler captain Hendrik Goosen landed a strange blue fish at East London, South Africa; museum curator Marjorie Courtenay-Latimer preserved it, and ichthyologist J. L. B. Smith named it Latimeria chalumnae. A second population was found in the Comoros, and a second species, L. menadoensis, was identified in Indonesia in 1997–98.

Biology

Coelacanths reach about 2 m, live in deep volcanic caves, bear live young after a very long gestation, and swim with synchronised movements of their lobed fins. They possess a hinged skull joint, a fat-filled vestigial lung and an electrosensory rostral organ. Their genome, sequenced in 2013, evolves unusually slowly.
