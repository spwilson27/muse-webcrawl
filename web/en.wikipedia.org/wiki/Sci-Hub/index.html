Type of siteShadow library / file sharing
Created byAlexandra Elbakyan
Launched16 April 2011

Sci-Hub is a shadow library website that provides free access to millions of research papers and books, bypassing publishers' paywalls by using donated institutional credentials and cached copies. It was founded in 2011 by Alexandra Elbakyan, a researcher from Kazakhstan, who argued that paywalls hinder the spread of knowledge and that the site operates in the public interest.

History

Elbakyan began the project after encountering paywalled articles she needed for her research. The site grew rapidly, automating retrieval through shared university proxy access and later through a large stored corpus keyed by DOI. By the 2020s the collection held tens of millions of papers.

Legal status

Sci-Hub has been sued by major publishers including Elsevier and the American Chemical Society, which won default judgments and injunctions in the United States; courts in several countries have ordered ISPs to block its domains. Elbakyan has not paid the damages, and the site has repeatedly reappeared under new domains. Supporters, including some academics and open-access advocates, view it as civil disobedience against the scholarly publishing business model, while publishers describe it as large-scale copyright infringement.

Usage

Usage studies published in Science showed downloads from nearly every country, including wealthy universities, suggesting convenience as well as cost drives use.
