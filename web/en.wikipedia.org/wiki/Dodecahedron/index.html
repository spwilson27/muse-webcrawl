Dodecahedron

A dodecahedron (from Greek δωδεκάεδρον, "twelve faces") is any polyhedron with twelve flat faces. The most familiar is the regular dodecahedron, one of the five Platonic solids, composed of 12 regular pentagonal faces, with three meeting at each of its 20 vertices; it has 30 edges. Its dual polyhedron is the regular icosahedron.

Properties

The regular dodecahedron's geometry involves the golden ratio: its coordinates can be given using φ = (1 + √5)/2. Plato, in the Timaeus, associated the dodecahedron with the cosmos as a whole, while the other four solids represented the elements. Dodecahedral shapes occur in dice (the d12), in the rhombic dodecahedron structure of garnet crystals and honeycombs, and in Roman bronze artifacts of uncertain purpose.
