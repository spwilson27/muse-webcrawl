Jia Nanfeng (賈南風) (257 – 13 May 300), nicknamed Shi (峕), was the empress consort of Emperor Hui of the Western Jin dynasty of China. She was the daughter of the powerful official Jia Chong. Her domineering rule over her developmentally disabled husband is traditionally blamed for provoking the War of the Eight Princes, the civil wars that shattered the Jin dynasty and opened northern China to invasion.

Marriage and rise

Jia Nanfeng became crown princess in 272 when she married Sima Zhong, the heir of Emperor Wu, despite being older than him and reportedly unattractive and jealous — objections overruled because Emperor Wu favoured her father. When Emperor Hui ascended the throne in 290, the regents Yang Jun and then Sima Liang and Wei Guan tried to sideline her; in 291 she conspired with Sima Wei, Prince of Chu, to have them killed, then had Sima Wei himself executed and seized power in her husband's name.

Rule and fall

For nine years Jia Nanfeng ruled the empire, acting (according to hostile traditional histories) with cruelty and caprice: she executed officials, persecuted rivals and is said to have murdered attendants and lovers. In 299 she deposed, and in 300 murdered, the crown prince Sima Yu, the emperor's son by another consort. This provoked Sima Lun, Prince of Zhao, who staged a coup in April–May 300; Jia Nanfeng was arrested and forced to drink poisoned wine. Her fall did not restore order — the War of the Eight Princes raged on until 306.

Reputation

Traditional historiography made Jia Nanfeng a stock villain of the "calamity of a woman" trope; modern historians treat the sources with more caution, noting that they were written by the scholar-official class she had opposed, while accepting that her coup politics helped destabilise the dynasty.
