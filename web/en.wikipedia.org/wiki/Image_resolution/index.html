Image resolution

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image placeholder: resolution comparison]

Image resolution is the detail an image holds. The term applies to digital images, film images, printers and other types of images; higher resolution means more image detail. Resolution can be measured in several ways, most commonly as the number of pixels in a raster image (width × height) or as pixel density.

Pixel count

For digital cameras and displays, resolution is usually quoted as the total pixel count: a 1920 × 1080 image contains about 2.1 megapixels. Display standards have standardised common sizes, from VGA (640 × 480) through Full HD (1920 × 1080) to 4K UHD (3840 × 2160) and 8K UHD (7680 × 4320). Megapixel counts are used to market cameras, although sensor size and optics matter as much to image quality.

Pixel density

Pixel density — pixels per inch (PPI) on screens, dots per inch (DPI) in printing — describes how finely pixels are packed. Print work commonly targets 300 PPI at final size, while screens range from about 100 PPI for desktop monitors to over 400 PPI for smartphones ("Retina" displays).

Other senses

In optics, resolution is the ability of an imaging system to distinguish detail, limited by diffraction (the Rayleigh criterion). In remote sensing, spatial resolution is the ground size of a pixel. Scanning resolution, film grain and audio-like measures of dynamic range are related but distinct concepts.
