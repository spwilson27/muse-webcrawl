Mummy (film)

Mummy films form a durable subgenre of horror and adventure cinema, centring on an ancient Egyptian priest or prince, cursed or preserved by ritual, who returns to life in the modern world. The archetype was established by Universal Pictures' The Mummy (1932), directed by Karl Freund and starring Boris Karloff as Imhotep, an ancient high priest revived by the Scroll of Thoth who seeks his reincarnated lost love, Ankh-es-en-amon.

History

The 1932 film drew on the wave of Egyptomania following the discovery of Tutankhamun's tomb in 1922 and on public fascination with the supposed "curse of the pharaohs". It was followed by Universal sequels — The Mummy's Hand (1940), The Mummy's Tomb (1942), The Mummy's Ghost and The Mummy's Curse (both 1944) — featuring the bandaged Kharis. Hammer Film Productions revived the monster in The Mummy (1959) with Christopher Lee and Peter Cushing.

Stephen Sommers' The Mummy (1999), starring Brendan Fraser and Rachel Weisz, recast the material as a swashbuckling adventure and launched a successful franchise. A 2017 film of the same title, starring Tom Cruise, attempted to inaugurate Universal's "Dark Universe" of connected monster films but underperformed. The shuffling, bandaged mummy remains one of the classic Universal monsters.
