The Five Boroughs or Five Boroughs of the Danelaw were the five main towns of Danish Mercia in the East Midlands of England: Derby, Leicester, Lincoln, Nottingham and Stamford. These settlements were fortified burhs occupied and governed by the Danish armies that settled in England in the late 9th century.

History

Following the campaigns of the Great Heathen Army, Danish rulers controlled the territory known as the Danelaw. Each of the Five Boroughs was the centre of an area administered by a Danish army and its earl, and each later developed into a county town (Stamford being associated with Lincolnshire). In the early 10th century, Edward the Elder and Æthelflæd, Lady of the Mercians, systematically reconquered the boroughs between 913 and 918, bringing them under English rule, though Scandinavian law, custom and place-names persisted strongly in the region.

Legacy

The boroughs retained distinctive courts and customs, and the term "Five Boroughs" continued to be used in legal documents for centuries. Scandinavian influence remains visible in local place-names ending in -by and -thorpe.
