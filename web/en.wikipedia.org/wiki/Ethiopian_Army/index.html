The Ethiopian Army is the land service branch of the Ethiopian National Defense Force (ENDF). It is one of the largest standing armies in Africa, with roots in the forces of the Ethiopian Empire and a modern history spanning the imperial, Derg and federal periods.

History

The imperial army achieved fame at the Battle of Adwa in 1896, where the forces of Emperor Menelik II decisively defeated Italy, preserving Ethiopian independence. Modernised under Haile Selassie with foreign missions, including a Swedish-staffed military academy and the elite Kagnew Battalions that fought with distinction in the Korean War, the army resisted Mussolini's second invasion in 1935–36 before Ethiopia's liberation in 1941.

After the 1974 revolution, the Derg regime built the army into one of sub-Saharan Africa's largest with Soviet and Cuban support, fighting the Ogaden War (1977–78) against Somalia and long counterinsurgencies in Eritrea and Tigray. The military government fell in 1991, and the army was rebuilt around the victorious EPRDF forces. Ethiopia fought Eritrea in the border war of 1998–2000 and intervened in Somalia from 2006. From November 2020 the army fought the Tigray War against the TPLF alongside Eritrean and regional forces, a conflict marked by mass casualties and atrocity reports, and it continues to face insurgencies in Oromia and Amhara.

Organisation

The army is organised into regional commands (Northern, Western, Central, Eastern and Southern) comprising infantry and mechanised divisions, supported by commando and special forces units. It operates largely Soviet- and Russian-origin equipment, including T-72 tanks, artillery and rocket systems, alongside more recent acquisitions.
