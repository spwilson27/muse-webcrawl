K-beauty

K-beauty (Korean: 케이뷰티; RR: Keibyuti) is an umbrella term for skincare and beauty products derived from South Korea, and for the beauty culture and aesthetic standards associated with them. The industry has become a major global export sector, riding the worldwide popularity of Korean popular culture (the Korean Wave, or Hallyu).

Characteristics

K-beauty is noted for its emphasis on skincare rather than makeup coverage, its relatively affordable prices, and rapid product innovation. Signature product categories popularised by Korean brands include BB creams and cushion compacts, sheet masks, essences, ampoules, and sleeping packs. Formulations frequently feature ingredients marketed as novel or natural, such as snail secretion filtrate (snail mucin), propolis, fermented extracts, centella asiatica ("cica"), and ginseng. The much-publicised "10-step skincare routine" — layering toner, essence, serum, moisturiser and sunscreen, among other steps — became emblematic of K-beauty in Western media, although Korean consumers themselves generally use simpler routines.

History and growth

South Korea's cosmetics industry developed under government export support from the 1960s onwards, with companies such as Amorepacific (founded 1945) and LG Household & Health Care growing into conglomerates. BB cream, reformulated by Korean brands in the 2000s, achieved mass popularity in Asia and then the West. From the 2010s, the spread of K-pop and Korean television drama, social-media beauty influencers, and dedicated retailers (such as Soko Glam in the United States) drove surging demand in North America and Europe. South Korea became one of the world's top cosmetics exporters, with exports reaching several billions of US dollars annually.

Beauty standards and criticism

The K-beauty aesthetic prizes pale, luminous "glass skin", a youthful appearance, and gradient lips. Commentators have criticised the industry for reinforcing narrow beauty standards, colourism, and pressure toward cosmetic surgery — South Korea has one of the highest rates of plastic surgery per capita — while others view its emphasis on sun protection and skin health positively. More recently the market has been shifting toward "skinimalism", gender-neutral products, and clean-beauty claims.
