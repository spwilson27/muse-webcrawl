Güyük Khan

Güyük Khan (Mongolian: Гүюг хаан; c. 1206 – 1248) was the third Khagan-emperor of the Mongol Empire, the eldest son of Ögedei Khan and grandson of Genghis Khan. He reigned from 1246 until his death in 1248.

Life and reign

Güyük participated in the Mongol invasion of Europe under Batu Khan, but relations between the two princes were bitter, and Güyük was recalled by his father. After Ögedei's death in 1241, Güyük's mother Töregene Khatun ruled as regent and engineered her son's election at a kurultai in 1246, attended by foreign envoys including the Franciscan friar Giovanni da Pian del Carpine, sent by Pope Innocent IV; Güyük's reply letter to the Pope, demanding submission, survives in the Vatican archives.

His short reign saw administrative reforms and continued expansion, including campaigns in Korea and the Song dynasty. Güyük died in 1248 while marching west, possibly to confront Batu, and was succeeded after another regency by Möngke Khan in 1251.
