National Action Party (Mexico)

The National Action Party (Spanish: Partido Acción Nacional, PAN) is a conservative political party in Mexico founded in 1939. It is one of the country's three main political parties and has historically represented the principal opposition to the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI). The PAN won the presidency in 2000 with Vicente Fox, ending 71 years of uninterrupted PRI rule, and again in 2006 with Felipe Calderón.

History

Founding and early years

The party was founded in September 1939 by Manuel Gómez Morín, a lawyer and former rector of the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), together with figures such as Efraín González Luna. It emerged in reaction to the leftist policies of President Lázaro Cárdenas, including socialist education and agrarian reform. The PAN drew support from Catholic and business sectors and advocateded private property, free enterprise and political democracy.

Opposition decades

For decades the PAN functioned as a loyal but marginal opposition within Mexico's dominant-party system. It gradually gained ground in northern states such as Baja California, Chihuahua and Guanajuato, winning its first governorship in Baja California in 1989 under Ernesto Ruffo Appel. Its candidate Manuel Clouthier's 1988 presidential campaign alleged electoral fraud, fuelling demands for electoral reform.

Presidency, 2000–2012

In the 2000 election, Vicente Fox, running under the Alliance for Change, defeated the PRI's Francisco Labastida, a watershed in Mexican democratisation. Felipe Calderón narrowly won the disputed 2006 election against Andrés Manuel López Obrador and launched a controversial military-led campaign against drug cartels. The PAN lost the presidency to the PRI's Enrique Peña Nieto in 2012.

Recent period

In 2018 the PAN led the coalition Por México al Frente with Ricardo Anaya as candidate, finishing second to López Obrador. In 2024 its candidate Xóchitl Gálvez, heading the Fuerza y Corazón por México coalition, lost to Claudia Sheinbaum of Morena. The party remains the largest opposition force in several states and in Congress.

Ideology

The PAN describes itself as a party of Christian humanism. Its platform emphasises free-market economics, social conservatism, subsidiarity, and the defence of the family. It is a member of the Centrist Democrat International and an observer of the International Democrat Union networks.
