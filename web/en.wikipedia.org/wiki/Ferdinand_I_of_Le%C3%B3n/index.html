Ferdinand I of León

Reign1037–1065
Bornc. 1015
Died27 December 1065, León
SpouseSancha of León

Ferdinand I (c. 1015 – 27 December 1065), called the Great (el Magno), was Count of Castile from 1029 and King of León from 1037 until his death. A younger son of Sancho III of Navarre, he unified León and Castile and became the first ruler to style himself Emperor of Spain.

Rise to power

Ferdinand inherited Castile as a count under Navarrese overlordship. Through his marriage to Sancha, sister of Bermudo III of León, he acquired a claim to the Leonese crown; after Bermudo was killed at the Battle of Tamarón in 1037, Ferdinand was crowned king in León. He then defeated his brother García Sánchez III of Navarre at the Battle of Atapuerca in 1054.

Reign

Ferdinand campaigned successfully against the Muslim taifa kingdoms, capturing Coimbra in 1064 and exacting tribute (parias) from Toledo, Seville, Badajoz and Zaragoza. He reorganised his kingdom's administration and church, supported Cluniac reform, and began the Romanesque basilica of San Isidoro in León. Following Navarrese custom, he divided his realms among his children — Sancho II received Castile, Alfonso VI received León, and García received Galicia — a partition that triggered civil war after his death.
