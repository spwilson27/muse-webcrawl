Sneferu

Sneferu (also read Snefru or Snofru), well known under his Hellenised name Soris, was an ancient Egyptian pharaoh, the first king of the Fourth Dynasty (Old Kingdom), reigning around 2613–2589 BC. He built more pyramid volume than any other pharaoh.

Pyramids

Sneferu is credited with at least three great pyramids: the Meidum pyramid (begun perhaps under Huni), the Bent Pyramid at Dahshur – whose angle changes mid-construction from about 54° to 43°, an apparent correction – and the Red Pyramid, the first successful true smooth-sided pyramid and model for Khufu's Great Pyramid. Together they document the evolution from step to true pyramid.

Reign

Inscriptions record campaigns in Nubia and Libya, raids for cedar from Lebanon (forty ships), and mining in Sinai. Later Egyptian tradition remembered Sneferu as a wise, approachable king, a central figure in the Westcar Papyrus tales. His son Khufu succeeded him.
