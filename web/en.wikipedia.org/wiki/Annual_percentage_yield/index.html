Annual percentage yield

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The annual percentage yield (APY) is a normalized representation of an interest rate, based on a compounding period of one year. APY figures allow a reasonable, single-point comparison of different offerings with varying compounding schedules. However, it does not account for the possibility of account fees affecting the net gain.

Formula

APY = (1 + r/n)n − 1

where r is the stated annual (nominal) interest rate and n is the number of compounding periods per year. For example, a nominal rate of 5% compounded monthly gives an APY of about 5.116%.

Regulation

In the United States, the calculation and disclosure of APY for deposit accounts is governed by the Truth in Savings Act (Regulation DD), which standardized the formula so consumers can compare accounts across banks. APY is distinct from the annual percentage rate (APR) used for loans, which incorporates certain fees and does not assume compounding within the year.
