Johann Pachelbel

Johann Pachelbel
Baptised: 1 September 1653, Nuremberg
Died: buried 9 March 1706, Nuremberg
Occupation: Composer, organist
Era: Baroque

Johann Pachelbel (baptised 1 September 1653 – buried 9 March 1706) was a German composer, organist, and teacher who brought the south German organ schools to their peak. He composed a large body of sacred and secular music, and his contributions to the development of the chorale prelude and fugue have earned him a place among the most important composers of the middle Baroque era.

Life

Pachelbel was born in Nuremberg and received his early musical education there and in Altdorf and Regensburg. He worked as an organist in Vienna (at St. Stephen's Cathedral), Eisenach — where he became acquainted with members of the Bach family — Erfurt, Stuttgart, and Gotha, before returning in 1695 to Nuremberg as organist of St. Sebald's Church, a post he held until his death.

He was a highly influential teacher; his pupils included Johann Christoph Bach, the eldest brother of Johann Sebastian Bach, and through him Pachelbel's style indirectly influenced the young J. S. Bach.

Music

Pachelbel's output includes organ chorales, fugues, toccatas, chamber music, and sacred vocal works such as Magnificat settings and concerted motets. His style is characterised by clarity, simplicity of counterpoint, and lyrical melody.

His most famous work by far is the Canon and Gigue in D major, commonly known as Pachelbel's Canon. Written for three violins and basso continuo over a repeating two-bar ground bass, the Canon was little known for centuries but became enormously popular from the 1970s onward, becoming a staple at weddings and a recurring presence in popular culture.
