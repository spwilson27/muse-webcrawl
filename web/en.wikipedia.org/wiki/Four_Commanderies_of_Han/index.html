Four Commanderies of Han

The Four Commanderies of Han (漢四郡) were Chinese commanderies established by Emperor Wu of the Han dynasty in 108 BC after his conquest of Wiman Joseon in the northern Korean Peninsula and parts of Manchuria. They were administrative units through which Han China governed the region and conducted trade and cultural exchange with the peoples of the peninsula.

The commanderies

  • Lelang Commandery — centred near modern Pyongyang; the longest-lived and most important, lasting until AD 313.
  • Xuantu Commandery — in the northeast, later relocated westward under pressure from Goguryeo.
  • Lintun Commandery — on the east coast; merged into Lelang in 82 BC.
  • Zhenfan Commandery — in the south of the group; likewise abolished and merged into Lelang in 82 BC.

History

After Lintun and Zhenfan were abolished, Lelang and Xuantu administered the region. Lelang became a flourishing centre of Chinese administration, craft production and commerce for four centuries, leaving rich archaeological remains such as painted lacquerware tombs near Pyongyang. It fell to the expanding Korean kingdom of Goguryeo in AD 313. The commanderies are significant in Korean and Chinese historiography, where their location and legacy have sometimes been politically sensitive topics.
