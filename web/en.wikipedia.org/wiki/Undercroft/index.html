Undercroft

An undercroft is traditionally a cellar or storage room, often brick-lined and vaulted, beneath a traditional Europe church or castle. In modern use, the term has been applied to a ground-level area under the edge of a building that is largely open to a walkway or motorway.

History

Medieval undercrofts were typically built beneath the principal hall or chapel of a house or castle, providing cool, secure storage for food, wine, and valuables. Stone-vaulted undercrofts offered fire protection and were often the most architecturally durable part of a building; many survive where the timber superstructure above has vanished. Examples in England include the undercrofts beneath guildhalls, merchant houses, and monastic buildings, some later converted into taverns or chapels.

Modern usage

In 20th-century architecture the word denotes spaces created where a building is raised on columns or cantilevered, leaving a partly enclosed area at ground floor. The undercroft of London's Southbank Centre became famous from the 1970s as a gathering place for skateboarders, and is regarded as a birthplace of British skate culture.
