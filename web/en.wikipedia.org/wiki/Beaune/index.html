Beaune is a commune in the Côte-d'Or department in eastern France, between Dijon and Chalon-sur-Saône. It is the wine capital of Burgundy, surrounded by some of the world's most famous vineyards of the Côte de Beaune.

History

Beaune was a Gallic and Roman settlement before becoming a residence of the dukes of Burgundy. Its medieval ramparts, moats and old town survive largely intact. In 1443 Nicolas Rolin, chancellor to Duke Philip the Good, and his wife Guigone de Salins founded the Hôtel-Dieu, the famous Hospices de Beaune, a charitable hospital distinguished by its polychrome glazed-tile roof. It remained a working hospital until the 1970s and now houses a museum, including Rogier van der Weyden's polyptych of The Last Judgment.

Wine

Beaune gives its name to the Côte de Beaune wine district, producing both red wines from Pinot noir and whites from Chardonnay; the commune's own premier cru vineyards are renowned. Each November the Hospices hold a charity wine auction of the new vintage, one of the most famous wine events in France, whose prices are watched as a barometer of the Burgundy wine market.

Sights

  • Hospices de Beaune (Hôtel-Dieu)
  • Collegiate church of Notre-Dame, with 15th-century tapestries
  • Medieval ramparts and wine cellars beneath the town
