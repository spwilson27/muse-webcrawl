I Wanna Be the Guy: The Movie: The Game (abbreviated IWBTG) is a freeware platform game created by independent developer Michael "Kayin" O'Reilly, released in 2007. It stars a small character, "The Kid", on a quest to become "The Guy". The game is famous for its extreme, deliberately unfair difficulty: it is built around traps that kill the player without warning — spikes concealed in scenery, fruit that falls (or rises) unexpectedly, shifting gravity, and fake save points — and around pixel-perfect jumps and boss fights assembled from sprites and music borrowed from classic video games.

Gameplay

The Kid can run, double jump, and shoot an unlimited pistol. Almost any hazard kills him instantly, exploding him into a spray of blood, but checkpoints are frequent and restarts are fast, encouraging brute-force trial and error. The game is non-linear: after an opening area, the player can tackle several paths in any order, each ending in a boss — including Mike Tyson, Mecha Birdo, Dracula, and Mother Brain — before a final sequence and a fight against The Guy himself. Difficulty settings reduce the number of save points; the highest, "Impossible", offers none at all.

Development

Kayin built IWBTG in Multimedia Fusion 2 as a tribute to the difficult Japanese platform games and ROM hacks he admired, most notably the Kaizo Mario style of level design and the flash game Jinsei Owata. Nearly all of its graphics and music are taken, in the spirit of a collage, from games such as Mega Man, Castlevania, Metroid, DuckTales and Ghosts 'n Goblins, and from films including Monty Python and the Holy Grail.

Legacy

Spread largely through early Let's Play videos — whose subjects' exaggerated frustration became entertainment in itself — IWBTG became a landmark of indie gaming and spawned an enormous fan-game genre: thousands of "I Wanna" games now exist, with their own conventions (needle platforming, avoidance boss fights, troll traps) and dedicated communities and events. The game is regularly cited as a precursor of the "masocore" genre later popularised by titles such as Super Meat Boy and Celeste's harder stages.
