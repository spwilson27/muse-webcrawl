Tantō

TypeShort blade (knife / dagger)
OriginJapan, Heian period
Blade length15–30 cm (one shaku or less)
Worn withKatana and wakizashi (daishō)

A tantō (Japanese: 短刀, "short blade") is a traditional Japanese short weapon — a knife or dagger with a blade generally between 15 and 30 centimetres long. It was carried primarily by the samurai class of feudal Japan.

History and use

The tantō dates to the Heian period and was designed mainly as a stabbing weapon for close combat, including grappling in armour; some heavier forms, such as the yoroi-dōshi, were intended to pierce armour. Samurai women carried a smaller version, the kaiken, for self-defence. In ceremonial contexts the tantō was the blade used in seppuku.

Construction

Like the katana, traditional tantō are forged from tamahagane steel with differential hardening producing a visible hamon temper line. Mountings range from plain wooden shirasaya to richly decorated pieces signed by famous smiths.
