NBA draft lottery

LeagueNational Basketball Association (NBA)
First held1985
Participants14 non-playoff teams

The NBA draft lottery is an annual event held by the National Basketball Association (NBA) to determine the order of the first picks in the NBA draft. The 14 teams that missed the playoffs take part in a weighted lottery that decides the first four selections; the remaining picks fall into place by inverse order of regular-season record.

System

The lottery uses numbered balls drawn from a machine to produce four-ball combinations assigned to teams in proportion to their odds. Since reforms that took effect with the 2019 draft, the three worst teams each have a 14% chance of winning the first overall pick, and the odds are flattened throughout the table to discourage "tanking" — deliberately losing to improve draft position.

History

The NBA introduced the lottery in 1985, replacing a coin flip between the worst teams of each conference, amid suspicions that teams were losing intentionally to draft stars such as Hakeem Olajuwon. The first lottery was won by the New York Knicks, who selected Patrick Ewing. The system has been adjusted several times (1990, 1994, and 2019) to rebalance the odds between fairness and discouraging tanking.
