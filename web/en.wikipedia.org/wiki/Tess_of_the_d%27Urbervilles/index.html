AuthorThomas Hardy

SubtitleA Pure Woman Faithfully Presented

Tess of the d'Urbervilles: A Pure Woman Faithfully Presented is a novel by Thomas Hardy, first published in serialised form in 1891. Initially censored and bowdlerised because of its challenge to Victorian sexual morals, it is now considered one of the great novels of English literature.

Plot

Tess Durbeyfield, the daughter of a poor Wessex family that claims descent from the noble d'Urbervilles, is sent to "claim kin" with a wealthy family. There she is seduced or raped by Alec d'Urberville and bears a child who dies. Later she falls in love with Angel Clare and confesses her past on their wedding night; he rejects her. Penniless, she becomes Alec's mistress, and when Angel returns she murders Alec. Tess and Angel flee together but she is arrested at Stonehenge and hanged.

Reception

The novel scandalised Victorian readers but was a commercial success. It is notable for its sympathy toward its heroine, its critique of Victorian morality and class, and its pervasive fatalism. It has been adapted many times for stage, film and television, notably Roman Polanski's Tess (1979).
