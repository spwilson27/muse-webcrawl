Bend sinister
Heraldic ordinary: Diagonal band

In heraldry, a bend sinister is a diagonal band running from the viewer's upper right (the shield-bearer's left, or sinister side) to the lower left, the reverse of the ordinary bend dexter.

Heraldic use

The bend is one of the ordinaries. A bend sinister is blazoned by its direction and tincture. A narrower version is the bendlet sinister, and a short couped version in the sinister chief is sometimes called a baton sinister.

Bastardy myth

Popular lore long claimed a bend or baton sinister marked illegitimate birth. In historic heraldic practice marks of cadency and difference varied, and a bend sinister alone was not a universal sign of bastardy, though royal bastards were sometimes granted arms differenced in this way.

Examples

Bends sinister appear in civic, family and institutional coats of arms across Europe and in modern heraldry worldwide.
