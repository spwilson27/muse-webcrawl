City of gold

A city of gold is a legendary or metaphorical place said to be made of, or fabulously rich in, gold. Tales of such cities motivated explorers during the Age of Discovery and remain a recurring motif in literature and popular culture.

Legends

The most famous legend is El Dorado, originally a Muisca ritual in which a king covered in gold dust bathed in Lake Guatavita in present-day Colombia, which later became imagined as a city or empire of gold. In North America, Spanish explorers such as Francisco Vázquez de Coronado searched for the Seven Cities of Gold, or Cíbola, reported by survivors of the Narváez expedition. Other golden-city legends include Paititi in the Andes and Amazon regions, and the lost city of Z sought by Percy Fawcett.

Real "cities of gold"

The epithet has also been applied to real places grown rich from gold: Johannesburg, founded after the Witwatersrand gold rush of 1886, is often called the "City of Gold" (eGoli in Zulu). San Francisco during the California gold rush and Dawson City during the Klondike gold rush acquired similar reputations. In finance and sport the phrase is used metaphorically for places and prizes of great wealth.

In popular culture

Golden cities appear in adventure fiction, films such as The Road to El Dorado (2000), and numerous games, usually as the ultimate goal of a treasure quest.
