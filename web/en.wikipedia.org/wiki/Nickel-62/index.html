Nickel-62

Nickel-62 sits at the peak of the binding-energy curve.

Nickel-62 is a stable isotope of nickel composed of 28 protons and 34 neutrons. It has the highest binding energy per nucleon of any known nuclide, at about 8.7945 MeV per nucleon. It is often stated that iron-56 is the most stable nucleus, but by the precise measure of binding energy per nucleon, nickel-62 exceeds it; iron-56 is instead the nuclide with the lowest mass per nucleon, a difference arising from the masses of the constituent protons and neutrons.

Significance in astrophysics

Because of its exceptional binding energy, nickel-62 is an endpoint of stellar nucleosynthesis chains. In practice, however, the iron-group elements produced in stars and supernovae are dominated by iron-56 and nickel-56: photodisintegration and reaction-rate effects during silicon burning cause the ashes to favour 56Ni, which decays through 56Co to 56Fe. Nickel-62 is therefore not the most abundant heavy nuclide in the universe, despite being the most tightly bound.

Properties

Nickel-62 makes up about 3.63% of natural nickel, behind nickel-58 (about 68%) and nickel-60 (about 26%). It has zero nuclear spin and is stable against all known decay modes.
