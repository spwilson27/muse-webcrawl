Lake Sihwa

LocationGyeonggi Province, South Korea
TypeArtificial lake (tidal flat reclamation)
Created1994 (seawall completed)
Surface areaApprox. 56.5 km²
Nearby citiesSiheung, Ansan, Hwaseong

Lake Sihwa (시화호, Sihwa-ho) is an artificial lake on the west coast of South Korea, in Gyeonggi Province between the cities of Siheung, Ansan and Hwaseong. It was created in 1994 when a 12.7-kilometre seawall was completed across a tidal bay of the Yellow Sea, as part of a land reclamation project intended to provide farmland and fresh water for industry.

Pollution crisis

The project became one of South Korea's most notorious environmental failures. Once cut off from tidal flushing, the enclosed water was rapidly polluted by industrial wastewater and sewage from the rapidly growing cities upstream. Within a few years the lake was so contaminated that in 1996 the government abandoned the freshwater plan, and the scandal — including mass fish deaths and public protests — forced a national reckoning over reclamation policy.

Recovery and tidal power

From 2000 the authorities began releasing seawater through the wall to restore circulation, and water quality steadily recovered, bringing back fish and migratory birds. The seawall now hosts the Sihwa Lake Tidal Power Station, completed in 2011 with an installed capacity of 254 MW — the largest tidal power plant in the world — generating electricity from the very tidal range the project once blocked. The surrounding reclaimed land has been redeveloped with eco-city and tourism projects, including the Sihwa reed wetland park.
