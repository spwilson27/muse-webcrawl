Wannabe (song)

"Wannabe" is the debut single by the English girl group the Spice Girls. Written by the group members along with songwriters Matt Rowe and Richard "Biff" Stannard, it was included on the group's debut album Spice and released on 26 June 1996. The song combines dance-pop with elements of hip hop and rap.

The song was an immediate commercial success, reaching number one in 37 countries including the United Kingdom, where it stayed at the top for seven weeks, and the United States Billboard Hot 100, where it peaked at number one for four weeks. It became the best-selling single by a girl group of all time and is widely regarded as the song that launched "Girl Power" as a cultural phenomenon.

Background and writing

"Wannabe" was written during the group's first professional songwriting session in July 1995 at Strongroom Studios in Shoreditch, London. The group wanted a song that captured their manifesto of female empowerment and friendship over romance. The track was recorded in under an hour, with Melanie C later recalling that the band knew immediately it was special.

Reception and legacy

Critics initially gave the song mixed reviews, but it has since been recognised as an iconic pop song of the 1990s. The famous opening line "Yo, I'll tell you what I want, what I really, really want" and the nonsensical phrase "zig-a-zig-ah" became defining elements of the group's brand. In polls, "Wannabe" has repeatedly been voted one of the most recognisable songs of its era.

Music video

The music video, directed by Johan Camitz and shot at the Midland Grand Hotel in London in April 1996, shows the group gatecrashing a formal party and dancing through the building. Its energetic, one-take style helped establish each member's persona.
