Freeman John Dyson (15 December 1923 – 28 February 2020) was a British-American theoretical physicist and mathematician. In 1949 he proved the equivalence of the three formulations of Quantum electrodynamics — those of Richard Feynman, Julian Schwinger and Sin-Itiro Tomonaga — and introduced the Dyson series and the S-matrix formalism that became quantum field theory's standard machinery, unifying a fractured field in a single legendary paper (and its sequel).

Dyson then ranged across science: nuclear reactor design (the TRIGA), the nuclear-rocket Project Orion, the Dyson sphere concept in the search for extraterrestrial intelligence, the Dyson tree and other space colonisation ideas, arms control (a founding voice against missile defence), and elegant essays (Infinite in All Directions). He spent 57 years at the Institute for Advanced Study without ever taking a PhD — the doctorate's most famous counterexample — and remained a dissenting, luminous presence in physics until his death at 96.

The 1948 road trip

Legendary in the field: after the 1948 Pocono conference failed to reconcile Feynman's and Schwinger's QED, the 24-year-old Dyson crossed America by bus thinking it through, and emerged with the proof that the theories coincide — plus the unified calculational scheme every particle physicist since has used. Feynman called the resulting paper the moment the world could finally understand his diagrams.
