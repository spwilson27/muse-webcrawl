Chavín culture

Chavín culture
Periodc. 900–200 BCE
RegionNorthern highlands and coast of Peru
Key siteChavín de Huántar
TypePre-Incan Andean culture / religious horizon

The Chavín culture was a pre-Incan Andean civilisation that flourished roughly between 900 and 200 BCE. Named after its principal ceremonial centre, Chavín de Huántar in the Peruvian highlands, it represents the first widespread religious and artistic horizon uniting much of the northern Peruvian coast and highlands.

Chavín de Huántar

The site lies at the confluence of the Mosna and Huachecsa rivers at about 3,180 metres elevation. Its temple complex consists of U-shaped platforms and sunken circular plazas threaded by internal galleries. In the heart of the Old Temple stands the Lanzón, a 4.5-metre granite monolith carved with a fanged, clawed deity, still in its original cruciform gallery. Other famous carvings include the Raimondi Stela and tenon heads that once projected from the temple walls.

Art and religion

Chavín art is characterised by complex, interlocking imagery of jaguars, caimans, harpy eagles and snakes, rendered in stone, gold, textiles and ceramics using techniques such as contour rivalry. Scholars believe a religious cult spread peacefully along trade routes linking the highlands, coast and Amazonian regions, accompanied by goods such as spondylus shells, obsidian and the hallucinogenic San Pedro cactus depicted in temple art.

Legacy

Archaeologist Julio C. Tello called Chavín the "mother culture" of Andean civilisation. Though later research shows it built on earlier traditions such as Caral and Kotosh, its influence on subsequent Peruvian cultures in art, religion and temple architecture was profound. Chavín de Huántar is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
