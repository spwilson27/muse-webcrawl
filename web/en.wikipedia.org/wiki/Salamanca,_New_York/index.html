Salamanca, New York

Salamanca is a city in Cattaraugus County, New York, United States, with a population of about 5,900. It is notable as the only incorporated city in the United States that lies almost entirely within an Indian reservation — the Allegany Indian Reservation of the Seneca Nation of Indians — with the city occupying land held under a long-term federal lease authorised in 1890 and renewed in 1990.

History

The area, at the confluence of the Allegheny River and Little Valley Creek, was a Seneca settlement and a crossroads of the region's railroads. Salamanca grew as a rail hub for the Erie Railroad and other lines, and was incorporated first as a village in 1879 and as a city in 1913. The city was named for José de Salamanca y Mayol, a Spanish banker who invested in local railroads. Its unusual tenure stems from the reservation leases: 99-year Congressional leases of 1890 expired in 1991 and were renegotiated, with compensation settled in the Seneca Nation Land Claims Settlement Act of 1990.

Geography and economy

Salamanca sits in the Allegheny foothills near Allegany State Park, New York's largest state park, and serves as a gateway to its hiking, camping, and skiing areas. U.S. Route 219 and Interstate 86 pass through the city. The Seneca Allegany Resort & Casino, opened in 2004, is a major employer, and the city is home to the Seneca-Iroquois National Museum (Onöhsagwë:de' Cultural Center), which presents Seneca history and culture.
