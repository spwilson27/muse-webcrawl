Indigenous inhabitant

An indigenous inhabitant is a member of an Indigenous people — a person belonging to a group with historical continuity to pre-colonial or pre-settler societies in a particular territory, who identify themselves as distinct peoples and are determined to preserve and transmit their ancestral territories, languages, cultures, and identities to future generations.

Definitions

There is no single universally accepted definition of "indigenous peoples" in international law; the working definition associated with UN Special Rapporteur José Martínez Cobo emphasises historical continuity, distinctiveness, non-dominance, and self-identification. The International Labour Organization's Convention No. 169 (1989) and the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (2007) provide the main international frameworks.

Distribution

Indigenous peoples number an estimated 476 million people across some 90 countries, belonging to more than 5,000 distinct groups and speaking the majority of the world's roughly 7,000 languages. Examples include Native Americans, First Nations, Inuit and Métis in North America; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples in Australia; the Māori of New Zealand; the Sámi of northern Europe; and Adivasi peoples in India.
