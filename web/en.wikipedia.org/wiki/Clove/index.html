Clove

Cloves are the aromatic flower buds of the tree Syzygium aromaticum, in the myrtle family Myrtaceae. Native to the Maluku Islands (the "Spice Islands") of Indonesia, they are harvested before opening and sun-dried, and are used as a spice in cuisines worldwide.

History

Cloves were traded to China by the 3rd century BCE and were among the most valuable spices of the medieval and early modern spice trade, reaching many times their weight in gold. The Dutch East India Company enforced a brutal monopoly by restricting cultivation to Ambon and destroying trees elsewhere in the Moluccas; in the 18th century the French smuggled seedlings to Mauritius and Zanzibar, breaking the monopoly. Zanzibar became, and Indonesia remains, a leading producer — much of the Indonesian crop is consumed domestically in kretek clove cigarettes.

Uses

Cloves flavour savoury dishes, mulled wine, spice blends such as garam masala and Chinese five-spice, and baked goods. Clove oil, rich in eugenol, has a long history in dentistry as an antiseptic and analgesic, and eugenol is also used in perfumery and as a fish anaesthetic. In traditional medicine cloves are used for digestive complaints, though clinical evidence is limited.
