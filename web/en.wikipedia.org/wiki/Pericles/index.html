Pericles

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Marble bust of Pericles wearing a Corinthian helmet, Roman copy of a Greek original]

Pericles (c. 495 – 429 BC) was a Greek statesman, orator and general (strategos) of Athens during its golden age — the dominant figure of Athenian politics for over three decades, from about 461 BC until his death in the plague of 429 BC. Under his leadership, expressed through the democratic assembly rather than any formal office, Athens built the Parthenon and the monuments of the Acropolis, extended its maritime empire, and reached the height of its cultural brilliance. Thucydides, an admirer, called Athens under Pericles 'in name a democracy, in fact rule by the first man'.

Leader of democratic Athens

Pericles rose with the democratic faction of Ephialtes, whose reforms (462/461 BC) stripped the aristocratic Areopagus of its powers; after Ephialtes' assassination Pericles led Athens for a generation, introducing pay for jurors so the poor could serve, and opening offices wider. Abroad he managed the Delian League as an Athenian empire, crushing revolts (Euboea, Samos) while the Long Walls bound Athens to its port at Piraeus, making the city siege-proof so long as it ruled the sea.

War, plague and death

When the Peloponnesian War began (431 BC) Pericles' strategy — abandon Attica, refuse pitched battle, strike by sea — tested Athenian nerves as Spartan invasions burned the farms. His Funeral Oration for the first year's dead, as reported by Thucydides, remains the classic eulogy of democracy. In 430 BC plague struck the overcrowded city; Pericles was briefly deposed and fined, then re-elected, but died of the plague in autumn 429 BC. Athens, Thucydides judged, never found his equal, and his successors' recklessness led the city toward disaster.
