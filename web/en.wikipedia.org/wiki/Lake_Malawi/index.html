Lake Malawi

Lake Malawi, also known as Lake Nyasa in Tanzania and Lago Niassa in Mozambique, is an African Great Lake lying between Malawi, Mozambique and Tanzania. It is the southernmost lake in the East African Rift system, about 580 kilometres long, and the ninth-largest lake in the world by area (about 29,600 square kilometres).

Lake Malawi is famous for its extraordinary diversity of cichlid fishes — 500 to 1,000 species, more fish species than any other lake on Earth — which evolved in a celebrated adaptive radiation. Lake Malawi National Park at its southern end was among the first national parks created to protect freshwater fish and is a UNESCO site. The lake is a vital food source for millions of people.
