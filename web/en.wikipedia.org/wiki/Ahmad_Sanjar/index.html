Ahmad Sanjar

Ahmad Sanjar (Persian: احمد سنجر; full name Muizz ad-Dunya wa ad-Din Adud ad-Dawla Abul-Harith Ahmad Sanjar ibn Malik-Shah) (1085 – 8 May 1157) was a Seljuk ruler and the last great sultan of the Seljuk Empire. The son of Malik-Shah I, he first ruled Khurasan as a vassal of his brother Barkiyaruq and then of Muhammad I Tapar, before becoming supreme sultan in 1118. His reign of nearly four decades was the longest of any Seljuk sultan.

Early life and rule in Khurasan

Sanjar was born in 1085 in Sinjar, in northern Iraq, from which his name derives. After the death of Malik-Shah in 1092 the empire fell into civil war among his sons. In 1097 Sanjar, still a boy, was appointed governor of Khurasan, which he would rule for sixty years, first from Balkh and then Merv.

Reign as sultan

On the death of his brother Muhammad I in 1118, Sanjar became head of the Seljuk house, though the empire was by then effectively divided: his nephews ruled in Iraq and western Persia as subordinate sultans. Sanjar intervened repeatedly in the west, installing and deposing sultans, and campaigned against the Ghaznavids, making Bahram Shah his tributary in Ghazna.

In 1141 Sanjar marched into Transoxiana to defend his vassal against the Qara Khitai (Western Liao). His army was crushed at the Battle of Qatwan near Samarkand — one of the great defeats of the medieval Islamic world, which gave rise in Europe to legends connected with Prester John. In 1153 Sanjar was captured by rebellious Oghuz Turks and held prisoner for three years; Khurasan was devastated in his absence. He escaped in 1156 but died the following year at Merv. With his death the Seljuk sultanate in the east effectively ended, and power passed toward the Khwarazmshahs.

Legacy

Sanjar's court at Merv was a centre of Persian culture; the poet Mu'izzi and others were associated with it. His mausoleum in Merv, built during his lifetime, remains one of the masterpieces of Seljuk architecture.
