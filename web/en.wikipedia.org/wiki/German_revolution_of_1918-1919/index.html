German Revolution of 1918–1919

Date29 October 1918 – 11 August 1919
LocationGerman Empire
ResultAbdication of Wilhelm II; Weimar Republic established

The German Revolution of 1918–1919 (German: Novemberrevolution) was the civil conflict that, in the dying weeks of the First World War, brought down the German monarchy and transformed the German Empire into a democratic republic — the state later known as the Weimar Republic.

Origins

By autumn 1918 Germany faced military collapse. When the naval command ordered a final suicidal sortie of the High Seas Fleet, sailors mutinied at Wilhelmshaven and Kiel in late October and early November 1918. Workers' and soldiers' councils (Räte) spread across the country, modelled partly on the Russian soviets.

November 1918

On 9 November 1918 Chancellor Max von Baden announced the abdication of Wilhelm II (who fled to the Netherlands) and handed power to Social Democrat Friedrich Ebert. Philipp Scheidemann proclaimed a republic from the Reichstag, while Karl Liebknecht proclaimed a "free socialist republic" from the Berlin Palace. A Council of the People's Deputies governed Germany pending elections.

January uprising and settlement

In January 1919 the far-left Spartacist uprising in Berlin was crushed by government troops and right-wing Freikorps; Rosa Luxemburg and Karl Liebknecht were murdered on 15 January. Elections on 19 January 1919 — the first in which German women could vote — seated the National Assembly at Weimar, which adopted the Weimar Constitution in August 1919.
