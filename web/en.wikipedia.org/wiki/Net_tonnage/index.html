Net tonnage

Net tonnage (NT, N.T. or nt) is a dimensionless index calculated from the total moulded volume of the ship's cargo spaces by using a mathematical formula. Defined in The International Convention on Tonnage Measurement of Ships that was adopted by the International Maritime Organization in 1969, net tonnage replaced the earlier net register tonnage (NRT), which denoted the volume of the ship's revenue-earning spaces in register tons, units of volume equal to 100 cubic feet (2.83 m³).

Net tonnage is derived from the volume of a vessel's cargo spaces.

Calculation

Net tonnage is calculated from the total volume of all cargo spaces of the ship, multiplied by factors that account for the ship's depth-to-draught ratio and the number of passengers the ship is certified to carry. The calculation is set out in Regulation 4 of Annex I of the 1969 Tonnage Convention, and produces an index with no units; it is not a measure of weight or displacement.

Usage

Port dues, pilotage charges, and canal tolls — including those of the Panama and Suez canals, which use their own tonnage variants — are typically levied on the basis of net tonnage, since it approximates the commercial earning capacity of the vessel. Net tonnage must be shown on a ship's International Tonnage Certificate.
