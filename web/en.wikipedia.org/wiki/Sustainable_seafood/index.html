Sustainable seafood

Certified fish on sale at a market

Sustainable seafood is seafood — fish and shellfish from wild fisheries or aquaculture — that is harvested or farmed in ways that maintain the long-term health of the species and the wider marine ecosystem, and the livelihoods that depend on them. Concern over overfishing, bycatch, habitat destruction, and illegal fishing has driven a global movement of certification schemes, consumer guides, and fishing reforms.

Issues

The FAO estimates that over a third of assessed fish stocks are fished at biologically unsustainable levels. Destructive practices include bottom trawling on vulnerable deep-sea habitats, the bycatch of dolphins, turtles, and seabirds, and "pirate" illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing. In aquaculture, concerns include fishmeal dependence, disease transfer to wild fish, mangrove clearance for shrimp ponds, and pollution.

Certification and guides

The Marine Stewardship Council (MSC), founded in 1997 by Unilever and WWF, certifies wild-capture fisheries against a standard of stock health, ecosystem impact, and management, labelling products with its blue tick. The Aquaculture Stewardship Council (ASC) does the same for farmed seafood. Consumer guides such as the Monterey Bay Aquarium's Seafood Watch rate species by region and gear type into "best choices", "good alternatives", and "avoid".

Approaches

Sustainable management tools include catch shares and individual transferable quotas, marine protected areas, gear modifications such as turtle excluder devices and circle hooks, and traceability systems to keep illegal fish out of supply chains. Eating lower on the food chain — favouring small pelagic fish, bivalves, and seaweed — is often promoted as a lower-impact choice.
