The Dhofar Governorate (Arabic: محافظة ظفار, romanized: Muḥāfaẓat Ẓufār) is the largest of the 11 governorates in the Sultanate of Oman in terms of area. It lies in southern Oman, on the eastern border with Yemen's Al Mahrah Governorate and the northern border with Saudi Arabia. Its capital is Salalah.

Geography and climate

Dhofar is unique on the Arabian Peninsula for its exposure to the south-west monsoon, known locally as the khareef (June–September), which turns the coastal mountains green with mist, seasonal waterfalls and temporary lakes — a stark contrast to the arid interior. Historically the region was the world's chief source of frankincense, and the Land of Frankincense sites (including Khor Rori and Al-Baleed) are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

History

Between 1963 and 1976 the region was the theatre of the Dhofar Rebellion, in which Marxist guerrillas backed by South Yemen fought the Sultan's forces, supported by Britain and Iran; the rebellion ended in defeat for the insurgents and was followed by major development investment. Dhofar's people speak Modern South Arabian languages such as Jibbali and Mehri alongside Arabic.
