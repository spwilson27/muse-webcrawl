Transrapid is a German-developed high-speed monorail train using magnetic levitation (maglev). Planning for the Transrapid system started in 1969, and a test facility, the Emsland test facility in Lower Saxony, was completed in 1987. In 1991 the technology was approved for application, and its only commercial implementation, the Shanghai maglev train, opened in 2004, linking Shanghai Pudong International Airport with the outskirts of central Shanghai.

Technology

Transrapid uses electromagnetic suspension (EMS): electromagnets in the vehicle wrap around the underside of the guideway and are attracted upward to a ferromagnetic stator, levitating the train about 10 mm above the guideway. Propulsion and braking are provided by a long-stator linear synchronous motor built into the guideway, which makes the system independent of wheel-rail adhesion and gives it steep-gradient capability and low wear.

Shanghai maglev

The Shanghai line covers about 30 km in roughly 7 minutes 20 seconds at a maximum commercial operating speed of 430 km/h, making it the fastest commercial electric train service in the world. A Transrapid vehicle reached 501 km/h on the line in November 2003, a record for commercial maglev vehicles.

Cancelled projects and accident

Planned lines in Germany, including a Munich airport link, were cancelled, largely on cost grounds; the Munich project was abandoned in 2008. On 22 September 2006, a Transrapid train on the Emsland test track collided with a maintenance vehicle, killing 23 people — the first fatal accident involving a maglev train. The test facility closed in 2011.
