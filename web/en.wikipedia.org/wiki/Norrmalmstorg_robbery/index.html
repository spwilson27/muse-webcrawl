Norrmalmstorg robbery

[Image: Norrmalmstorg square, Stockholm]

The Norrmalmstorg robbery was a bank robbery and hostage crisis that took place at the Kreditbanken branch at Norrmalmstorg square in Stockholm, Sweden, in August 1973. The event is best known for giving rise to the term Stockholm syndrome, describing the bond that can develop between captives and their captors.

Events

On 23 August 1973, Jan-Erik Olsson, on leave from prison, entered the bank, fired a submachine gun, and took four employees hostage. He demanded money, a getaway car, and the release of his former cellmate Clark Olofsson, whom police brought to the bank. Negotiations drew in Prime Minister Olof Palme, whom one hostage, Kristin Enmark, called to criticise the police response and express trust in the robbers. The standoff lasted six days; police eventually introduced tear gas through a drilled hole on 28 August, and Olsson and Olofsson surrendered. No hostages were physically harmed.

Aftermath

Olsson was sentenced to ten years for the robbery; Olofsson was initially convicted but later acquitted regarding the events in the bank. Criminologist and psychiatrist Nils Bejerot, who advised the police, coined the term "Norrmalmstorg syndrome", later known internationally as Stockholm syndrome, to describe the hostages' positive feelings toward their captors and fear of the police. The incident has been retold in books and films, including the 2018 film Stockholm and the 2022 Netflix series Clark.
