Mezzo-soprano

A mezzo-soprano (Italian for "half-soprano") is a type of classical female singing voice whose vocal range lies between that of a soprano and a contralto, typically from about A3 to A5. The mezzo-soprano voice generally has a heavier, darker timbre than a soprano, and its tessitura sits slightly lower.

Roles

Mezzo-sopranos sing some of opera's most celebrated roles: Bizet's Carmen, Rosina in Rossini's The Barber of Seville, Dalila in Saint-Saëns's Samson et Dalila, Charlotte in Massenet's Werther, and Amneris in Verdi's Aida. Mezzos also specialise in "trouser roles" (en travesti) — young male characters such as Cherubino in Mozart's The Marriage of Figaro, Octavian in Richard Strauss's Der Rosenkavalier, and Hansel in Humperdinck's Hansel and Gretel. Subtypes include the coloratura mezzo, the lyric mezzo and the dramatic mezzo-soprano.

Notable singers

Great mezzo-sopranos of the recording era include Marilyn Horne, Cecilia Bartoli, Teresa Berganza, Christa Ludwig, Frederica von Stade, Agnes Baltsa, Elīna Garanča and Joyce DiDonato. Cecilia Bartoli in particular revived interest in the virtuoso coloratura repertory written for nineteenth-century mezzos.

In popular music

The term is also applied informally to female popular singers whose comfortable range sits below that of a soprano; many well-known popular vocalists are classified as mezzo-sopranos for choir and vocal-pedagogy purposes.
