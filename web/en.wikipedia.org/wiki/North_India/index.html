North India

North India is a loosely defined region consisting of the northern part of India. It generally includes the states and union territories of Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Chandigarh, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, and often Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, and Jharkhand, depending on the definition used. The region is home to well over 500 million people.

Geography

The region is dominated by two great physical features: the Himalayas, which wall off the north, and the Indo-Gangetic Plain, one of the most fertile and densely populated river plains in the world, watered by the Indus, Ganges, and Yamuna river systems. To the southwest lies the Thar Desert in Rajasthan. The climate ranges from alpine in the Himalayas to hot semi-arid and sub-tropical across the plains, with the summer monsoon bringing the bulk of the rainfall.

History

North India was the heartland of several foundational civilizations and empires. The Indus Valley Civilisation flourished in its northwest, and the Vedic culture developed along its rivers. The plains saw the rise of the Maurya and Gupta empires, the Delhi Sultanate, and the Mughal Empire, whose capital cities — Delhi and Agra — remain major urban centers. The region was central to the Indian independence movement and to the events of the Partition of 1947, which divided Punjab and Bengal.

Culture and languages

Hindi, written in Devanagari, is the most widely spoken language, alongside Urdu, Punjabi, and numerous regional languages and dialects such as Haryanvi, Rajasthani, Bhojpuri, and Awadhi. The region is predominantly Hindu, with significant Muslim, Sikh, Jain, and Buddhist populations. North India is known for Hindustani classical music, Kathak dance, Mughlai cuisine, and major pilgrimage sites including Varanasi, Ayodhya, Mathura, and the Char Dham shrines of Uttarakhand.

Economy

Agriculture on the Indo-Gangetic Plain — wheat, rice, sugarcane, and pulses — underpins the economy, while Delhi and its satellite cities (Gurgaon, Noida) have become hubs of services, manufacturing, and information technology. Major cities include Delhi, Lucknow, Jaipur, Kanpur, Chandigarh, Indore, and Patna.
