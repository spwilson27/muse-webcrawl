International Society for Krishna Consciousness

The International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON), known colloquially as the Hare Krishna movement, is a Gaudiya Vaishnava Hindu religious organization. It was founded in 1966 in New York City by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada.

Beliefs and practices

ISKCON's core beliefs are drawn from Hindu scriptures, particularly the Bhagavad Gita and the Bhagavata Purana, and centre on devotion (bhakti) to Krishna as the Supreme Personality of Godhead. The primary practice is chanting the Hare Krishna mantra. Initiated devotees follow four regulative principles: no meat-eating, no intoxication, no gambling, and no illicit sex. The movement operates temples, restaurants, and farming communities worldwide.

History

Bhaktivedanta Swami arrived in the United States in 1965 at age 69 and attracted followers within the 1960s counterculture; George Harrison of the Beatles became a prominent supporter. After the founder's death in 1977, leadership passed to a Governing Body Commission, and the movement endured internal disputes and schisms, including the ritvik movement.

Scale

ISKCON claims several hundred temples and centres in more than 100 countries, and runs the Food for Life food-relief programme, described as the world's largest vegetarian food-relief organization.
