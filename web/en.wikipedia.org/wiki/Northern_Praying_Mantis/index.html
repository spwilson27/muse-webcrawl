Northern Praying Mantis

Northern Praying Mantis (Chinese: 螳螂拳; pinyin: tánglángquán, "mantis fist") is a style of Chinese martial arts originating in Shandong province. Tradition attributes its creation to the martial artist Wang Lang during the late Ming or early Qing dynasty, who is said to have combined techniques from seventeen or eighteen other styles with footwork inspired by the monkey and hand movements imitating the striking forelegs of the praying mantis.

Characteristics

The style is characterised by rapid, precise hand techniques: hooking, grabbing, clawing and striking with the "mantis claw" hand formation, in which the index finger is extended and the remaining fingers gathered. Circular parries redirect attacks while the practitioner simultaneously seizes and strikes. Footwork is agile, drawing on monkey-style stepping.

Branches

Major branches include Seven Star Praying Mantis (Qixing Tanglang), Plum Blossom Praying Mantis (Meihua Tanglang), Six Harmony Praying Mantis (Liuhe Tanglang), Eight Step Praying Mantis (Babutanglang), Tai Chi Praying Mantis, and Wah Lum Tam Tui Northern Mantis, which combines mantis handwork with northern Tam Tui kicking.

Forms

Signature forms include Beng Bu (Crushing Step), Lan Jie, Cha Chui, Bai Yuan Tou Tao (White Ape Steals the Peach) and the various "extract" or essential routines (zhai yao) summarizing the style's core principles.
