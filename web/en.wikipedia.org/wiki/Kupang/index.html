Kupang (Indonesian: Kota Kupang) is the capital of the Indonesian province of East Nusa Tenggara. It is the largest city and port on the island of Timor, located in West Timor on Kupang Bay at the southwestern tip of the island. Kupang is a multicultural city of indigenous Timorese and Rotenese people alongside migrants from across Indonesia, and historically significant numbers of Chinese Indonesians and descendants of other trading communities.

History

Kupang was an important harbour under both Portuguese and Dutch rule as the main port for the sandalwood trade of Timor. The Dutch East India Company (VOC) seized the Portuguese fort Concordia in 1653 and established control over the bay. Captain William Bligh famously reached Kupang in June 1789 after the mutiny on the Bounty, having navigated an open boat some 3,600 nautical miles from the site of the mutiny — one of the greatest feats of open-boat navigation on record; his lieutenant's party and later Matthew Flinders also visited. Kupang was occupied by Japanese forces in 1942 and heavily bombed by Allied aircraft during World War II.

Geography and climate

Kupang has a tropical savanna climate (Köppen Aw) with a pronounced dry season from April to November under the influence of Australian continental winds, making it one of the driest cities in Indonesia. The surrounding landscape of limestone hills and lontar palms contrasts with the wetter eastern end of Timor.

Transport and economy

El Tari International Airport connects Kupang to Indonesian cities and (historically) to Darwin, Australia. The port of Tenau handles regional trade and ferries to the surrounding islands of East Nusa Tenggara. In April 2021, Cyclone Seroja caused widespread flooding and landslides in and around Kupang. The city functions as the administrative, educational (home to Nusa Cendana University) and commercial centre of the province, and as a gateway for travellers to Rote Island and to Timor-Leste.
