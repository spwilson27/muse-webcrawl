Miami Tribe of Oklahoma
Language: Algonquian (Miami–Illinois)
Headquarters: Miami, Oklahoma
Status: Federally recognised tribe

The Miami Tribe of Oklahoma (Miami–Illinois: myaamia) is the federally recognised Native American tribe of the Miami people in the United States. Its tribal headquarters are in Miami, Oklahoma, and it is governed by an elected business committee under a constitution adopted in the Oklahoma Indian Welfare Act era.

History

The Miami people originally lived in the Great Lakes region, in present-day Indiana, Ohio, Michigan, Illinois and Wisconsin. Through a series of 19th-century treaties they ceded most of their homeland; a portion of the tribe was removed westward to Kansas and then to Indian Territory (Oklahoma) in the 1870s, while many Miami remained in Indiana, where the Indiana Miami are not federally recognised as a separate tribe. The western group organised as the Miami Tribe of Oklahoma.

Culture and language

The tribe works to revitalise the Miami–Illinois language (myaamia), which had ceased to be spoken natively by the mid-20th century. In partnership with Miami University in Oxford, Ohio, the Myaamia Project (now the Myaamia Center) reconstructs and teaches the language from historical documentation, and it is now spoken again by tribal citizens. Tribal enterprises include gaming and other businesses in northeastern Oklahoma.
