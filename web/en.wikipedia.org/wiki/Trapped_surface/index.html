Trapped surface

Trapped surface
Field: General relativity
Introduced by: Roger Penrose, 1965
Related: Event horizon, Penrose singularity theorem, apparent horizon

A trapped surface is a concept in general relativity, introduced by Roger Penrose in 1965, describing a closed two-dimensional surface for which both families of future-pointing light rays emanating from it — both 'outgoing' and 'ingoing' — converge, i.e. have negative expansion.

Definition and significance

Normally, a flash of light at a sphere produces an expanding outgoing wavefront and a contracting ingoing one. Inside a sufficiently strong gravitational field, spacetime tilts so much that even the outgoing light front shrinks. A (future) trapped surface is such a surface. Penrose's 1965 singularity theorem showed that a spacetime containing a trapped surface, satisfying energy conditions, must be geodesically incomplete — containing a singularity — without assuming symmetry.

Related concepts

A marginally outer trapped surface, with zero outgoing expansion, defines an apparent horizon, used in numerical relativity to locate black holes locally, whereas the event horizon is a global, teleological concept. Trapped surfaces are central to the Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems and cosmic censorship debates. See Singularity theorems and Black hole.
