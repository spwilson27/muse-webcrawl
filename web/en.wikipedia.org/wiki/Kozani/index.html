Kozani

Kozani (Greek: Κοζάνη) is a city in northern Greece, the capital and largest town of the region of Western Macedonia, with a population of roughly 40,000–45,000. It lies at about 700 metres elevation in a basin between the Vermio and Askio mountains, west of the Aliakmon river and the artificial Lake Polyfytos.

History

The area has been inhabited since antiquity, and the nearby town of Aiani was a capital of the ancient Macedonian kingdom of Elimiotis. Kozani itself developed in the 14th–15th centuries under Ottoman rule and prospered in the 17th and 18th centuries as a trading centre; its merchants maintained links with central Europe, and the town acquired notable mansions, churches, and a rich library (the Koventarios Municipal Library, founded in the 17th century and among the largest in Greece outside Athens). The city was liberated by the Greek army in October 1912 during the First Balkan War.

Economy and culture

The surrounding region has long depended on lignite mining and power generation around Ptolemaida, historically supplying much of Greece's electricity, though the country has committed to phasing out lignite. The Kozani area is also famed for saffron: the village of Krokos produces the PDO-protected spice known as Krokos Kozanis, among the world's most prized.

Kozani is renowned for its carnival (Apokries), one of Greece's most distinctive, featuring neighbourhood fires (fanoi), satirical songs in the local dialect, and brass-band music. The city hosts the University of Western Macedonia and the Archaeological Museum of Kozani, with finds from Aiani displayed at the archaeological museum there.
