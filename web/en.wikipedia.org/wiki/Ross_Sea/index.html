The Ross Sea is a deep bay of the Southern Ocean in Antarctica, between Victoria Land and Marie Byrd Land, bordered by the Ross Ice Shelf — the world's largest ice shelf — to the south. It was discovered by James Clark Ross in 1841 and served as the entry point for many Heroic Age expeditions, including those of Shackleton and Scott, who used huts on Ross Island.

Geography and ecology

The western shore includes Ross Island with the active volcano Mount Erebus and the research stations McMurdo Station (United States) and Scott Base (New Zealand). The nutrient-rich waters support abundant krill, Adélie and emperor penguins, Weddell seals, and orcas; about a third of the world's Adélie penguins live along its shores. Scientists regard the Ross Sea as one of the last relatively intact marine ecosystems on Earth.

Ross Sea region Marine Protected Area

In October 2016, the Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources (CCAMLR) agreed to establish the Ross Sea region Marine Protected Area, which entered into force on 1 December 2017. Covering about 1.55 million km², it was at creation the largest marine protected area in the world, with a general protection zone where commercial fishing is banned for 35 years.
