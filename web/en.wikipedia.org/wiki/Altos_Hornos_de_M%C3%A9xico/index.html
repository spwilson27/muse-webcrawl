Altos Hornos de México

Industry: Steel
Founded: 1942
Headquarters: Monclova, Coahuila, Mexico
Abbreviation: AHMSA

Altos Hornos de México, S.A.B. de C.V. (AHMSA) is one of the largest integrated steel producers in Mexico, headquartered in Monclova, Coahuila. Founded in 1942 by Harold R. Pape, the company grew around its giant integrated steelworks in Monclova, which for decades made it the backbone of Mexican steel production and the economic engine of the surrounding region.

History

AHMSA was established during the Second World War, when Mexico sought domestic steel production to support industrialisation. The Monclova plant began operations in the 1940s and was repeatedly expanded, incorporating blast furnaces, steelmaking, rolling mills and its own iron ore and coal mines, including the MICARE coal operations. The company was privatised in 1991 and acquired by the GAN consortium led by Alonso Ancira.

Operations

The company produces flat and long steel products, including hot- and cold-rolled sheet, tinplate, plate, and structural shapes, supplying automotive, construction, energy and manufacturing industries. Its installed capacity has been in the range of several million tonnes of liquid steel per year.

Financial difficulties

AHMSA suspended payments in 1999 under heavy debts and remained under a long bankruptcy-protection process until 2016. In later years the company faced renewed crises: its name became entangled in corruption investigations surrounding its former chairman, production fell sharply, and in 2023–2024 the Monclova plant halted operations amid insolvency proceedings, leaving thousands of workers unpaid and the regional economy severely affected.
