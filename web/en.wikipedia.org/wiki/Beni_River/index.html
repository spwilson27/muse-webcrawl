Beni River

The Beni River (Spanish: Río Beni) is a major river of northern Bolivia, one of the principal tributaries of the Madeira River and thus of the Amazon. It rises in the Andes near La Paz, flows northeastward through the Bolivian Amazon lowlands past the town of Rurrenabaque and the departmental capital of the Beni, and joins the Mamoré River at the Brazilian border to form the Madeira. The river drains much of the Bolivian Amazon and is central to the ecology and transport of the region.

Course

The Beni's headwaters gather in the Cordillera Real east of La Paz; the river descends through the Yungas cloud-forest valleys before emerging onto the Amazonian plain. Near Rurrenabaque it passes the gateway to Madidi National Park, one of the most biodiverse protected areas on Earth. Its chief tributaries include the Tuichi, Madidi and Madre de Dios rivers. Below Riberalta, the Beni meets the Mamoré at Villa Bella; the combined flow forms the Madeira, interrupted downstream by the rapids near Porto Velho.

History and ecology

The Beni lowlands were home to pre-Columbian societies that built causeways, canals and raised fields across the seasonally flooded Llanos de Moxos savannas. During the Amazon rubber boom of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, the river was a major artery for rubber extraction, with Riberalta as a boomtown. Today the river supports fisheries, river transport and ecotourism, though it faces pressure from deforestation, gold mining (with associated mercury pollution) and proposed hydroelectric dams.
