Hydrochlorothiazide, sold under the trade name Microzide among others, is a diuretic medication used to treat hypertension (high blood pressure) and swelling due to fluid build-up. Other uses include treating diabetes insipidus and renal tubular acidosis and reducing the risk of kidney stones in those with high calcium levels in the urine. It is taken by mouth and is often combined in a single pill with other blood-pressure medications.

Medical uses

Hydrochlorothiazide is a first-line treatment for hypertension, either alone or in combination with ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, calcium channel blockers, or beta blockers. It is also used for edema associated with heart failure, cirrhosis, and kidney disorders. By reducing urinary calcium excretion it helps prevent calcium-containing kidney stones.

Mechanism of action

Hydrochlorothiazide belongs to the thiazide class of diuretics. It acts on the distal convoluted tubule of the nephron, inhibiting the sodium-chloride symporter and thereby reducing reabsorption of sodium and chloride; water follows osmotically, increasing urine output and reducing plasma volume and cardiac output. With long-term use, peripheral vascular resistance also falls, sustaining the antihypertensive effect.

Side effects

Common side effects include low potassium (hypokalemia), low sodium, low magnesium, high calcium, elevated blood sugar and lipids, increased uric acid (which may precipitate gout), dizziness, and photosensitivity. Long-term use has been associated with an increased risk of certain skin cancers, prompting regulatory warnings about sun protection. It is a sulfonamide derivative, and caution is used in people with sulfa allergy.

History and society

Hydrochlorothiazide was patented in 1957 and came into medical use in 1959. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is available as an inexpensive generic medication worldwide; it is one of the most commonly prescribed antihypertensive drugs in the United States.
