AC motor

An AC motor is an electric motor driven by alternating current (AC). An AC motor usually consists of a stationary stator with coils supplied with alternating current to produce a rotating magnetic field, and a rotor inside that turns with the output shaft.

Types

The two main types are the induction motor, in which rotor current is induced by the stator field, and the synchronous motor, whose rotor turns at exactly the supply-related synchronous speed. Induction motors are the most common industrial motors because they are rugged, brushless and cheap to manufacture.

Operating principles

The rotating magnetic field produced by polyphase stator windings drags the rotor around. In an induction motor the rotor always turns slightly slower than the field — the difference is called slip. Variable-frequency drives allow precise electronic speed control.

History

The principles were developed independently by Galileo Ferraris and Nikola Tesla in the 1880s; Tesla's polyphase induction motor patents were licensed by Westinghouse and underpinned the AC power system. See also Induction motor.
