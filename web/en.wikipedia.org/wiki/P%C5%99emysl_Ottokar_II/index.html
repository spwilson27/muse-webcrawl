Přemysl Ottokar II (Czech: Přemysl Otakar II.; c. 1233 – 26 August 1278), known as the "Iron and Golden King", was King of Bohemia from 1253 until his death in 1278. He also held the Austrian duchies of Austria, Styria, Carinthia and Carniola, building a realm that stretched from the Baltic to the Adriatic.

Rise

The second son of Wenceslaus I, Ottokar was elected duke by the Austrian nobility in 1251 and secured Austria and Styria after defeating Béla IV of Hungary at Kressenbrunn (1260). He founded Königsberg during a crusade in Prussia (1255), and founded or chartered many towns, encouraging German settlement and mining wealth, especially the silver of Kutná Hora.

Conflict with Rudolf of Habsburg

Ottokar twice stood for the imperial crown but was passed over; in 1273 the electors chose Rudolf of Habsburg, who demanded the return of the Austrian lands. Defeated in 1276, Ottokar submitted and lost Austria, but renewed the struggle and was killed at the Battle on the Marchfeld (1278), after which the Habsburgs gained Austria.
