Western swing

Western swing, also known as Western swing music, is a subgenre of American country music that originated in the late 1920s in the West and South. It is dance music, often with an up-tempo beat, which combined rural, cowboy, Celtic, folk, Dixieland, and blues influences with jazz improvisation played by a string band augmented by drums, saxophones, pianos, and the steel guitar.

History

The style developed in Texas and Oklahoma dance halls. Milton Brown and His Musical Brownies are credited with founding the genre in the early 1930s, and Bob Wills and His Texas Playboys became its most famous exponents, with hits such as "San Antonio Rose". Wills is often called the "King of Western Swing". Spade Cooley led a rival popular band on the West Coast in the 1940s.

Instrumentation

Typical line-ups featured fiddles, electric and steel guitars, piano, string bass or tuba, drums, and sometimes horns. Singing was often secondary to instrumental solos and novelty vocals.

Legacy

Western swing influenced rockabilly and mainstream country music. Bands such as Asleep at the Wheel revived the style from the 1970s onward, and it remains the official music of Texas.
