Rephaite

Rephaim (Hebrew: רְפָאִים, Rəfāʾīm; singular Rephaite) is a word found in the Hebrew Bible and other ancient Semitic texts. It has two distinct usages: in poetic and prose texts it can refer to the departed spirits or "shades" of the dead dwelling in Sheol; in other passages it denotes a people or individuals of great stature — giants — who inhabited the land of Canaan and Transjordan before the Israelites.

The Rephaim as giants

Deuteronomy describes the Rephaim as tall peoples akin to the Anakim, and identifies groups such as the Emim of Moab and the Zamzummim of Ammon as Rephaim. Their most famous representative is Og of Bashan, "the last of the remnant of the Rephaim", whose enormous iron bedstead is described in Deuteronomy 3. The Valley of Rephaim southwest of Jerusalem preserves the name.

The Rephaim as shades

In passages such as Psalm 88 and Isaiah 14 and 26, the Rephaim are the weak shades of the dead in the underworld; Isaiah 14 depicts them stirring to greet the fallen king of Babylon. Ugaritic texts from Ras Shamra also mention the rpum, royal ancestral spirits invoked in funerary rituals, suggesting a background of ancestor veneration.
