Play (theatre)

A stage production of a play

A play is a form of drama that primarily consists of dialogue between characters, intended for theatrical performance rather than merely reading. Plays are performed at various levels, from London's West End and New York's Broadway to regional, community and school productions, and are usually divided into acts and scenes.

History

The play in the Western tradition descends from the drama of ancient Greece, where tragedies and comedies were performed at religious festivals in Athens in honour of Dionysus; playwrights such as Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides and Aristophanes established enduring forms. Roman drama, medieval mystery and morality plays, and the flowering of Renaissance theatre — above all the works of William Shakespeare — carried the form forward. The rise of realism in the nineteenth century (Ibsen, Chekhov) and the experimental movements of the twentieth century broadened the play's subjects and techniques.

Genres and structure

Plays are classified by genre — tragedy, comedy, history, tragicomedy, melodrama, farce — and by structure, from the classical unities to episodic and absurdist forms. The written text of a play, the script, combines dialogue with stage directions, and its creation and staging involve playwrights, directors, actors, designers and technicians.
