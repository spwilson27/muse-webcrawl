Ahmet Ertegun

Ahmet Ertegun
Born: 31 July 1923, Istanbul, Turkey
Died: 14 December 2006, New York City, US
Known for: Co-founder of Atlantic Records

Ahmet Ertegun (31 July 1923 – 14 December 2006) was a Turkish-American businessman, songwriter, record executive, and philanthropist. He co-founded Atlantic Records in 1947 with Herb Abramson and was instrumental in shaping rhythm and blues, soul, and rock music in the United States.

Atlantic Records

Son of Turkey's ambassador to the United States, Ertegun fell in love with jazz and R&B in Washington, D.C. Atlantic signed and developed artists including Ray Charles, Ruth Brown, Aretha Franklin, Led Zeppelin, the Rolling Stones (distribution), Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young, and many others. Ertegun also wrote songs under the pen name A. Nugetre, including "Mess Around".

Later life

He co-founded the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, whose main exhibition hall bears his name, and was a major benefactor of Turkish-American cultural causes. He died in 2006 after a fall at a Rolling Stones concert benefit, and was buried in Istanbul.
