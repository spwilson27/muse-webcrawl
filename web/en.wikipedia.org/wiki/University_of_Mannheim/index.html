The University of Mannheim (German: Universität Mannheim), abbreviated UMA, is a public research university in Mannheim, Baden-Württemberg, Germany. Founded in 1907 as a municipal commercial college (Handelshochschule), it is particularly known for its programs in business administration, economics and the social sciences, which regularly place it among Germany's most highly regarded institutions in those fields, and for its seat in the Mannheim Palace, one of the largest baroque palaces in Europe.

History

The Handelshochschule Mannheim was incorporated into the University of Heidelberg in 1933 under Nazi pressure and re-established after the Second World War in 1946. In 1955 it moved into the Mannheim Palace. It gained university status and its present name in 1967, adding law, the humanities and additional social sciences to its original commerce programs.

Academics

The university is organized into five schools. Its Mannheim Business School holds the "triple crown" of AACSB, EQUIS and AMBA accreditations, and the university is a core partner of the adjacent ZEW – Leibniz Centre for European Economic Research and of the GESIS Leibniz Institute for the Social Sciences. The Graduate School of Economic and Social Sciences (GESS) runs structured doctoral programs in English. Semesters at Mannheim follow an international calendar, with the autumn semester beginning in September, unusual among German universities.

Campus

The main campus is centered on the Mannheim Palace, a baroque complex completed in the 18th century for the Electors Palatine, with additional buildings across Mannheim's city center grid (the Quadrate).
