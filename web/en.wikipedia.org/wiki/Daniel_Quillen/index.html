Daniel Quillen

Daniel Gray Quillen (22 June 1940 – 30 April 2011) was an American mathematician. He is remembered as the "prime architect" of higher algebraic K-theory, for which he was awarded the Fields Medal in 1978.

Education and career

Quillen was born in Orange, New Jersey. He studied at Harvard University, earning his bachelor's degree in 1961 and his Ph.D. in 1964 under Raoul Bott, with a thesis on partial differential equations. After appointments at MIT and a Sloan fellowship year in Paris, where Alexander Grothendieck influenced him deeply, he developed the model category formalism (1967) that transformed homotopical algebra. In 1984 he became Waynflete Professor of Pure Mathematics at Magdalen College, Oxford, where he remained until retirement.

Work

In 1972 Quillen defined the higher algebraic K-groups of a ring using his "plus construction" and the "Q-construction", solving major open problems including the Adams conjecture (independently proved by Dennis Sullivan) and giving a model for algebraic K-theory that unified the subject. With Graeme Segal's work it laid foundations for decades of research. His other contributions include the Quillen–Suslin theorem (proving Serre's conjecture that projective modules over polynomial rings are free, 1976), rational homotopy theory, the cohomology of groups, and formal group laws in complex cobordism.

Awards

Quillen received the Cole Prize (1975) and the Fields Medal (1978). He died in Florida in 2011, aged 70, after suffering from Alzheimer's disease.
