Katyń (film)

Katyń is a 2007 Polish historical drama film directed by Andrzej Wajda about the Katyn massacre, in which some 22,000 Polish officers and intellectuals were murdered by the Soviet NKVD in 1940. Based in part on Andrzej Mularczyk's novel Post Mortem, the film follows the officers' families — wives, mothers and daughters — as much as the victims, tracing both the crime and the decades-long "Katyn lie" imposed under communist rule. It was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.

Plot

The film opens with the double invasion of Poland in September 1939: on a bridge, refugees fleeing the Germans meet others fleeing the Soviets. Cavalry captain Andrzej is captured by the Red Army and held at Kozelsk camp while his wife Anna waits for news. Interwoven storylines follow his fellow officers, a Kraków university family whose professor father is arrested in the Sonderaktion Krakau, and post-war Poland, where families are forbidden to date the deaths to 1940 because the official line blamed Nazi Germany. The closing sequence depicts the executions in the Katyn forest in unsparing detail.

Production and significance

Wajda's own father, Jakub Wajda, was murdered at Kharkiv in the same operation, making the film a personal work the director said he had waited his whole career to make. Premiered on 17 September 2007 — the anniversary of the Soviet invasion — it was one of the biggest Polish productions of its decade and played a major role in public memory of the massacre in Poland. The film's producer was Michał Kwieciński, with cinematography by Paweł Edelman and music by Krzysztof Penderecki.
