Ivan Konev

Ivan Stepanovich Konev (28 December 1897 – 21 May 1973) was a Soviet general and Marshal of the Soviet Union who led Red Army forces on the Eastern Front during the Second World War, commanding the 1st Ukrainian Front in the capture of Berlin and the liberation of Prague.

Born28 December 1897, Lodeyno, Russian Empire
Died21 May 1973, Moscow, Soviet Union
RankMarshal of the Soviet Union
Commands19th Army, Western Front, 1st Ukrainian Front, Warsaw Pact forces

Second World War

Konev's troops fought at Smolensk and Moscow in 1941, at Kursk in 1943, and in the great offensives of 1944–45: the Korsun pocket, the Lvov–Sandomierz operation, the Vistula–Oder offensive, and the Berlin operation, where his front raced Zhukov's to the capital. His forces liberated Auschwitz in January 1945 and Prague in May 1945.

Post-war

Konev served as commander-in-chief of Warsaw Pact forces and oversaw the suppression of the Hungarian Revolution of 1956. Twice named Hero of the Soviet Union, he was buried in the Kremlin Wall Necropolis.
