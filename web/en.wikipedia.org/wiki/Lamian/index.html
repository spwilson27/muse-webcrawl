Lamian

Alternative namesHand-pulled noodles; lāmiàn (拉麵)
TypeNoodle
Place of originChina
Main ingredientsWheat flour, water, salt (sometimes alkaline agents)

Lamian (simplified Chinese: 拉面; traditional Chinese: 拉麵; pinyin: lāmiàn) is a type of soft wheat-flour noodle from Chinese cuisine made by stretching and folding the dough repeatedly by hand to produce many strands of noodles of varying thickness. The name literally means "pulled noodles."

Technique

A piece of well-rested, gluten-rich dough is stretched, doubled, and stretched again — often with theatrical swinging, twisting, and dusting with flour — until a single piece becomes dozens or hundreds of fine strands. Skilled makers produce grades from thick "belt" noodles down to hair-thin "dragon's beard" strands. Alkaline salts (such as peng hui, traditionally used in Lanzhou) give the noodles their characteristic springiness and yellow tint.

Dishes

The most famous lamian dish is Lanzhou beef noodle soup (兰州牛肉面), associated with the Hui Muslim cooks of Lanzhou, Gansu: clear beef broth, sliced beef, white radish, chilli oil, coriander, and garlic chives over hand-pulled noodles. Lamian is also served stir-fried or in dry mixed preparations across China and in Chinese restaurants worldwide.
