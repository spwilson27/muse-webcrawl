Scale model

A scale model is a physical representation of an object that maintains accurate proportional relationships between all aspects of the model and the original, although absolute values of the original properties need not be identical. Scale models are used in many fields including engineering, architecture, film making, military command, and hobby modelling, and are often used to study how a design will look or behave before full-size construction.

Scales

A scale is expressed as a ratio such as 1:72, meaning one unit on the model equals 72 in reality. Certain scales have become standards within particular hobbies: 1:72 and 1:48 for aircraft kits, 1:35 for military vehicles, 1:24 and 1:43 for model cars, and railway scales such as HO (1:87), N (1:160), and OO (1:76). Die-cast models, plastic injection-moulded kits, resin and photo-etched aftermarket parts, and scratch-built models span levels from snap-together beginner kits to museum-quality "super-detailed" builds.

Uses

Engineers use physical scale models for wind-tunnel and hydrodynamic testing, architects for design presentation and urban planning, and film studios historically used miniatures ("bigatures") for special effects. Tabletop wargaming and role-playing games use miniature figures at scales such as 28 mm. The hobby of scale modelling is organised through societies such as the International Plastic Modellers' Society (IPMS), with competitions judging accuracy, finish, and weathering. Though many physical models fail to reproduce structural strength or fluid behaviour exactly (because physical quantities scale non-linearly), they remain valuable for visualisation and testing.
