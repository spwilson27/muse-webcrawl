Diya (lamp)

A diya, diyo, deya, divaa, deepa or deepam is an oil lamp made from clay or mud with a cotton wick dipped in oil or ghee. These lamps are native to India and are commonly used in religious festivals such as Diwali and other celebrations. Diyas are also lit during daily worship (puja) in homes and temples throughout the Indian subcontinent and the Hindu diaspora.

Description

The traditional diya is a small, shallow bowl, typically hand-shaped from terracotta clay and sun-dried or fired. The lamp is filled with oil — commonly mustard oil, sesame oil, or clarified butter (ghee) — and a rolled cotton wick is placed in the oil with one end resting on the rim of the bowl, where it is lit. Metal diyas, often made of brass, are used for more permanent fixtures, while clay diyas are often regarded as disposable and are purchased in large numbers for festivals.

Religious significance

Light is a central symbol in Hinduism, representing purity, goodness, knowledge, and the triumph of light over darkness and good over evil. Lighting a diya is believed to dispel ignorance and invite prosperity. In Jainism, a lit diya symbolises the light of Mahavira's knowledge; in Sikhism, diyas are lit during Bandi Chhor Divas as well as Diwali.

Festivals

During Diwali, homes, temples, public buildings and even entire streets are decorated with rows of diyas. In the city of Ayodhya, millions of diyas have been lit along the banks of the Sarayu River during Deepotsav celebrations, setting world records. Diyas floating on banana-leaf rafts are also set adrift on rivers during festivals such as Kartik Purnima. In South India, deepams are lit during Karthigai Deepam, when a huge lamp is kindled on the hill of Tiruvannamalai.

Regional varieties

Varieties of the lamp include the Nepali diyo, the Bengali deya, and the Tamil deepam. Decorated and painted diyas, often embellished with colours, mirrors and sequins, are widely sold during the festival season.
