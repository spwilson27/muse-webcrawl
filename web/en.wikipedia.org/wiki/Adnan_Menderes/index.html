Adnan Menderes (1899 – 17 September 1961) was a Turkish politician who served as Prime Minister of Turkey from 1950 to 1960. He was a co-founder of the Democrat Party (DP) in 1946, the first legal opposition party in the country's multi-party era, and led it to three consecutive election victories.

Premiership

Menderes's government liberalized the economy, encouraged private enterprise and foreign investment, mechanized agriculture, and oversaw rapid growth and urbanization. Turkey joined NATO in 1952 under his government. Over time, however, his rule grew increasingly authoritarian, with press restrictions and pressure on the opposition Republican People's Party.

Downfall and death

He was overthrown in the military coup of 27 May 1960, tried by a special court on Yassıada island, and sentenced to death. Menderes was hanged on İmralı island on 17 September 1961, along with two of his ministers. In 1990 the Turkish parliament passed a law restoring his reputation, and his remains were transferred to a mausoleum in Istanbul. İzmir's airport is named Adnan Menderes Airport in his memory.
