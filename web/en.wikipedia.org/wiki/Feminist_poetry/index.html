Feminist poetry

Feminist poetry is poetry written from a feminist perspective, addressing women's experiences, gender inequality and the politics of language. The modern movement is generally traced to the women's liberation movement of the 1960s and 1970s, when women poets sought to reclaim their voices and challenge literary traditions dominated by men.

History

Although earlier poets such as Emily Dickinson, Christina Rossetti and Elizabeth Barrett Browning wrote about women's lives, feminist poetry as a self-conscious movement emerged alongside second-wave feminism. Poets including Adrienne Rich, Sylvia Plath, Audre Lorde, Anne Sexton and Muriel Rukeyser explored themes of motherhood, sexuality, domestic labour, violence and identity, often experimenting with form and voice.

Themes and influence

Feminist poets have used the personal as political, reworking myths and fairy tales from female points of view and challenging the canon through anthologies, small presses and journals devoted to women's writing. The movement expanded to include the perspectives of women of colour, lesbian poets and working-class writers, shaping contemporary poetry worldwide.
