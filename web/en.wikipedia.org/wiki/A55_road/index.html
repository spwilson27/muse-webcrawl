The A55, also known as the North Wales Expressway (Welsh: Gwibffordd Gogledd Cymru), is a major road in Britain running for about 87 miles (140 km) from Chester in England along the North Wales coast to Holyhead on Anglesey. Along with the A483, it is one of the two principal east–west trunk routes in North Wales and forms part of the E22 European route between Holyhead and Leeds (and ultimately Riga and Ishim).

Route

From Chester the road runs west through Flintshire and Denbighshire, passing Queensferry, Mold's outskirts, St Asaph, Abergele, Colwyn Bay, and Conwy—where it passes through the Conwy Tunnel beneath the estuary—then Bangor, across the Britannia Bridge to Anglesey, and on to the ferry port of Holyhead. Most of the route is dual carriageway of near-motorway standard, built or upgraded in stages from the 1960s through the 2001 opening of the Anglesey section.

Features

The route includes notable engineering works such as the Pen-y-Clip and Penmaenbach tunnels around headlands near Conwy and the twin tunnels at Rhuallt Hill. It provides the main road link to the Irish Sea ferry services at Holyhead.
