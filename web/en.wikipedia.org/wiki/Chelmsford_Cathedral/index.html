Chelmsford Cathedral, formally the Cathedral Church of St Mary the Virgin, St Peter and St Cedd, is an Anglican cathedral in Chelmsford, Essex, England. It is the seat of the Bishop of Chelmsford and the mother church of the Diocese of Chelmsford, created in 1914. The building is dedicated to Saint Mary the Virgin, Saint Peter, and Saint Cedd. It is notable both for its long history as the parish church of Chelmsford and for its unusually colourful modern interior decoration.

History

A church dedicated to St Mary has stood on the site since at least the early thirteenth century, when Chelmsford grew as a market town. The present building dates substantially from the fifteenth century; the nave was rebuilt after part of the tower collapsed in 1800. The chancel was extended in the nineteenth century, and in 1914 the church became a cathedral when the new diocese was carved from the Diocese of St Albans.

Interior

The interior is distinguished by a striking colour scheme of blue, gold and white introduced during twentieth-century refurbishments, and by modern artworks including a carved Christ in Glory above the chancel arch. The cathedral contains memorials to Essex regiments and a notable organ. Relics associated with Saint Cedd, the seventh-century missionary bishop of the East Saxons, connect the cathedral to the earliest history of Christianity in Essex.

Music and community

The cathedral maintains choirs of boys, girls and adult singers, an active programme of concerts, and a large education and outreach programme serving the city and county. At roughly 54 metres long, it is among the smallest Anglican cathedrals in England, yet it functions as a major civic gathering place for Essex.
