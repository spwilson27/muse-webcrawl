Yttrium

Yttrium is a chemical element with the symbol Y and atomic number 39. It is a silvery-metallic transition metal chemically similar to the lanthanides and is often classified as a rare-earth element. Yttrium is almost always found combined with lanthanide elements in rare-earth minerals and is never found in nature as a free element.

Properties

Yttrium is soft, silvery and relatively stable in air in bulk form, with a melting point of 1526 °C. Its only stable isotope is 89Y. The element's chemistry is dominated by the +3 oxidation state.

History

In 1787 Carl Axel Arrhenius found an unusual black mineral near the Swedish village of Ytterby; Johan Gadolin analysed the mineral (gadolinite) in 1789 and identified a new "earth", yttria, from which yttrium compounds were later isolated. Friedrich Wöhler obtained impure metallic yttrium in 1828.

Applications

Yttrium compounds are used in phosphors — yttrium oxide and YAG (yttrium aluminium garnet) in LEDs, lasers and television screens — in yttria-stabilized zirconia ceramics, in high-temperature superconductors such as YBCO, in spark plugs and oxygen sensors, and in the radioisotope yttrium-90 used in cancer therapy.
