Virulence factor


Examples of bacterial virulence factors

Virulence factors are cellular structures, molecules and regulatory systems that enable bacteria, viruses, fungi and protozoa to achieve colonisation of a niche in the host, immunoevasion, immunosuppression, entry into and exit from cells, and obtaining nutrition from the host. They are encoded by genes that may be located on the chromosome, on plasmids, or on mobile genetic elements acquired by horizontal gene transfer.

Types

Adherence factors

Many bacteria first adhere to host cells using structures such as pili, fimbriae and surface adhesins, an essential step in establishing infection.

Invasion factors

Invasins and secretion systems, such as the type III secretion system, allow pathogens to enter host cells or inject effector proteins that manipulate host cell biology.

Toxins

Exotoxins are secreted proteins that damage host tissues or disrupt cellular function, while endotoxins such as lipopolysaccharide form part of the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria and provoke strong immune responses.

Evasion factors

Capsules, protein A, and antigenic variation help pathogens resist phagocytosis and the adaptive immune system.

Importance

Virulence factors are targets for vaccines, diagnostics and anti-virulence therapies designed to disarm pathogens without killing them, potentially reducing selection pressure for resistance.
