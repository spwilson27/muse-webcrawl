StageCraft

StageCraft is a virtual production technology developed by Industrial Light & Magic (ILM) for use in film and television production. It centres on large LED video walls — collectively called "the Volume" — which display photorealistic digital environments in real time, allowing actors to be photographed in virtual locations on a sound stage with correct lighting and reflections captured in camera.

An LED volume stage running StageCraft

Development and use

StageCraft was developed for the Disney+ series The Mandalorian (2019), working with Epic Games' Unreal Engine to render environments in real time, tracked to the camera so the background moves with correct parallax. The technology was subsequently used on productions including The Batman, Thor: Love and Thunder, Obi-Wan Kenobi and House of the Dragon-era virtual production stages built by various studios. Permanent LED volumes were constructed at studios in Los Angeles, London and elsewhere.

Advantages and limitations

Advantages cited include in-camera final-pixel backgrounds without green screens, natural interactive lighting on actors and sets, fewer location shoots, and the ability to change time of day or location instantly. Limitations include the high cost of building volumes, the need for environments to be prepared well before shooting, and difficulty with very large or highly reflective scenes.
