Combi oven

A commercial combi oven with racks of trays

A combi oven (or combination oven) is a professional kitchen appliance that combines three cooking functions in one unit: convection heat, steam, and a combination of the two. Combi ovens are widely used in restaurants, hotels, institutions, and catering because a single unit can replace separate steamers, convection ovens, and holding equipment.

Modes of operation

In convection mode the oven circulates hot dry air, typically between 30 °C and 300 °C, for roasting and baking. In steam mode it generates saturated steam at about 100 °C for poaching, blanching, and cooking vegetables and rice. In combination mode, steam is added to the hot air in controlled proportions, which speeds cooking, reduces shrinkage and moisture loss, and allows crust formation to be regulated. Many modern combi ovens include programmable cooking steps, core-temperature probes, automatic cleaning cycles, and low-temperature ("Delta-T") and overnight cooking functions.

History

The combination steamer was developed in Germany in the 1970s; the company Rational introduced its first combi-steamer in 1976 and is generally credited with popularising the category, with other manufacturers such as Convotherm following. Compact countertop and domestic combi steam ovens have since spread to the home market.
