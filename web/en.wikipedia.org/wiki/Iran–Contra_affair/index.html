Iran–Contra affair

The Iran–Contra affair was a major political scandal of the Reagan administration.

The Iran–Contra affair, also referred to as Irangate, was a political scandal in the United States that occurred during the second term of the Ronald Reagan administration. Senior administration officials secretly facilitated the sale of arms to Iran, which was then embroiled in the Iran–Iraq War, in the hope of securing the release of American hostages held in Lebanon. Proceeds from the sales were diverted to fund the Contras, anti-communist rebels fighting Nicaragua's Sandinista government — funding Congress had prohibited under the Boland Amendment.

Exposure

The scheme began to unravel in October 1986 when a transport plane carrying supplies for the Contras was shot down over Nicaragua, and in November 1986 a Lebanese magazine revealed the arms sales to Iran. Attorney General Edwin Meese then disclosed the diversion of funds, which he attributed to National Security Council staff, principally Lieutenant Colonel Oliver North.

Investigations

The Tower Commission, appointed by Reagan, criticised the administration's management but found no evidence Reagan knew of the diversion. Joint congressional hearings were held in 1987, and Independent Counsel Lawrence Walsh's investigation led to indictments and convictions of several officials, some of which were overturned on appeal; President George H. W. Bush pardoned six figures, including former Defense Secretary Caspar Weinberger, in 1992.
