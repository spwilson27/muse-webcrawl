Marina Bay Sands is an integrated resort fronting Marina Bay in Singapore. Developed by Las Vegas Sands, it is one of the world's most expensive standalone casino properties, costing an estimated S$8 billion (about US$5.6 billion) including the cost of the land. The resort opened in phases in 2010 and has become one of Singapore's most recognisable landmarks.

Design and construction

The resort was designed by architect Moshe Safdie. Its most distinctive feature is the trio of 55-storey hotel towers topped by a 1.2-hectare SkyPark, a boat-shaped structure that cantilevers 67 metres beyond the northern tower. The SkyPark houses the world's largest elevated infinity swimming pool, 146 metres long, positioned 200 metres above the ground, along with restaurants, gardens, and an observation deck.

The complex also includes a 120,000-square-metre convention centre, the Shoppes at Marina Bay Sands mall, a museum (the lotus-shaped ArtScience Museum), two large theatres, "floating" crystal pavilions, a skating rink, and one of Singapore's two casinos.

Opening

The casino opened on 27 April 2010, followed by the rest of the property for a preview the next day. The grand opening took place on 23 June 2010, and a two-day celebration in February 2011 marked the completion of the full resort, including the ArtScience Museum and the resident musical The Lion King.

Casino

The casino occupies four levels and is licensed under Singapore's Casino Control Act. Singapore citizens and permanent residents must pay an entry levy to enter. Marina Bay Sands and Resorts World Sentosa were the two integrated resorts licensed as part of Singapore's decision in 2005 to develop casino gaming to boost tourism.

In popular culture

The resort has frequently appeared in films, television, and advertising as a symbol of modern Singapore, and the SkyPark infinity pool is a widely reproduced image of the city skyline.
