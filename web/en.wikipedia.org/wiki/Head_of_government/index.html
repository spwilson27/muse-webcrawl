Head of government

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The head of government is the highest or second-highest official in the executive branch of a state, charged with leading the government and its administration. The office is usually titled prime minister, premier, chancellor or chief minister, and is distinct from the head of state, though in presidential systems the president combines both roles.

In parliamentary systems the head of government is accountable to the legislature and holds office while retaining its confidence; the head of state — a monarch or ceremonial president — appoints the person most likely to command that confidence, usually the leader of the largest party. In semi-presidential systems power is shared between a directly elected president and a prime minister responsible to parliament, producing occasional “cohabitation” when they come from opposing camps.

Powers

Heads of government typically appoint and dismiss ministers, direct policy and the civil service, control the legislative agenda of their majority, and represent the state in many international settings, though summitry is often shared with heads of state. Their power depends on party cohesion, coalition arithmetic and constitutional convention as much as on formal rules: a prime minister with a large disciplined majority may dominate, while a coalition leader must negotiate continuously.

Titles around the world

Germany and Austria use Chancellor; Spain's head of government is formally President of the Government; Ireland's is the Taoiseach; Japan's is the Prime Minister, appointed by the Diet and ceremonially by the Emperor. At subnational level the equivalents include premiers, chief ministers, minister-presidents and governors.
