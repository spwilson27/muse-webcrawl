The Sutta Piṭaka (Pali: Suttapiṭaka, "Basket of Discourses") is the second of the three divisions of the Pali Canon, the scriptural canon of Theravāda Buddhism. It contains the discourses attributed to the Buddha and his close disciples, and is by far the largest of the three baskets, comprising more than ten thousand suttas (discourses).

Structure

The Sutta Piṭaka is organised into five collections called nikāyas:

  • Dīgha Nikāya — the "long discourses", 34 suttas arranged largely by length, including the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta on the Buddha's last days.
  • Majjhima Nikāya — the "middle-length discourses", 152 suttas covering a wide range of doctrine and practice.
  • Saṃyutta Nikāya — the "connected discourses", grouped by subject into 56 saṃyuttas.
  • Aṅguttara Nikāya — the "numerical discourses", arranged by the number of items treated, from ones to elevens.
  • Khuddaka Nikāya — the "minor collection", a miscellaneous group including the Dhammapada, Udāna, Itivuttaka, Sutta Nipāta, Theragāthā and Therīgāthā, Jātaka tales, and others.

Origins

According to tradition, the discourses were recited and communally rehearsed by the monastic community after the Buddha's death, beginning at the First Council at Rājagaha, and were transmitted orally for several centuries before being committed to writing in Sri Lanka in the first century BCE. Parallels to many suttas survive in the Chinese Āgamas and in Sanskrit and Tibetan sources, indicating a shared early heritage.

Content

The suttas record sermons, dialogues, verses, and narratives covering the core teachings of Buddhism: the Four Noble Truths, the Noble Eightfold Path, dependent origination (paṭicca-samuppāda), meditation practice, ethics, and the monastic life. They are the primary source for the Buddha's teaching as preserved in the Theravāda tradition.
