Lieder

Lieder (singular Lied, German for "song") are German-language art songs for voice and piano, typically settings of poetry, that flourished especially in the nineteenth century. The plural form is sometimes used in English for the genre as a whole.

History

Although German song has medieval roots in the Minnesang tradition, the Lied as an art form emerged around 1800 and reached its height with Franz Schubert, who composed more than 600 songs — including the cycles Die schöne Müllerin and Winterreise — elevating the piano accompaniment to an expressive partner of the voice. Robert and Clara Schumann, Johannes Brahms, Hugo Wolf, Gustav Mahler, and Richard Strauss carried the form through the Romantic era and into modernism.

Characteristics

Lieder range from simple strophic settings to through-composed forms following the poem's narrative; song cycles group poems into unified wholes. The genre shaped art song traditions in other languages, such as the French mélodie.
