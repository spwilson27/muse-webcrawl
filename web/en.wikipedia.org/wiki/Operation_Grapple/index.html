Operation Grapple

Operation Grapple was a set of four series of British nuclear weapons tests of early atomic bombs and hydrogen bombs carried out in 1957 and 1958 at Malden Island and Kiritimati (Christmas Island) in the Pacific Ocean as part of the British hydrogen bomb programme. Nine nuclear explosions were initiated during the tests.

Background

During the Second World War, Britain had begun a nuclear weapons project, code-named Tube Alloys, but the 1943 Quebec Agreement merged it into the American Manhattan Project. After the war, Britain developed its own atomic bomb, first tested in Operation Hurricane in 1952. In 1954 the government decided to develop a thermonuclear (hydrogen) bomb to maintain Britain's status as a great power.

The tests

The first Grapple test, Grapple 1 ("Short Granite"), took place on 15 May 1957 but underperformed. Grapple X, on 8 November 1957, achieved a yield of 1.8 megatons, exceeding expectations and demonstrating a true hydrogen bomb capability. Further series — Grapple Y in April 1958 (the largest British test, at about 3 megatons) and Grapple Z in August–September 1958 — refined the designs.

Legacy

The success of Operation Grapple helped lead to the 1958 US–UK Mutual Defence Agreement, restoring nuclear cooperation between the two countries. In later decades, British and Commonwealth servicemen who witnessed the tests campaigned over health effects attributed to radiation exposure.
