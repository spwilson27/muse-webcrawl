Suzhou

Suzhou (Chinese: 苏州; Suzhounese: sou1 tseu1) is a major urban center in Jiangsu province, China. Located in the Yangtze River Delta region, the city sits on the lower reaches of the Yangtze River and on the shores of Lake Tai, about 100 km (62 mi) northwest of Shanghai. Administratively, Suzhou is a prefecture-level city with a population of over 12 million in its administrative area, of whom roughly 5 million live in the urban districts.

Suzhou, the cradle of Wu culture, is one of the oldest towns in the Yangtze Basin, dating back 2,500 years to its founding in 514 BC. In subsequent centuries, the canal and waterway network that criss-crosses the city made it a great hub of trade and commerce; the city was famously described as the "Venice of the East". The city's canals, stone bridges, pagodas, and meticulously designed gardens have made it one of the top tourist attractions in China.

Classical Gardens

The Classical Gardens of Suzhou were added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997 and 2000. The gardens, dating chiefly from the Ming and Qing dynasties, include the Humble Administrator's Garden, the Lingering Garden, the Master of the Nets Garden, and the Lion Grove Garden. They are recognized as the finest embodiment of Chinese "Mountain and Water" garden design, expressing profound harmony with nature within compact urban spaces.

History

Suzhou was founded as the capital of the kingdom of Wu in 514 BC by King Helü, who employed Wu Zixu to design the city. During the Sui dynasty the Grand Canal connected the city to Hangzhou and the north, reinforcing its commercial importance. The Mongol invasion and the Taiping Rebellion both caused severe destruction, but the city recovered each time on the strength of the silk trade. Since the economic reforms of the late 20th century, Suzhou has been among the fastest-growing major cities in the world, with the Suzhou Industrial Park — developed jointly with Singapore — anchoring its modern manufacturing and technology sectors.

Economy and culture

Suzhou's GDP ranks among the highest of any Chinese prefecture-level city. Historically renowned for silk embroidery, the city today produces electronics, textiles, and precision machinery. It is also the home of Kunqu opera, one of the oldest forms of Chinese opera, and of Suzhou pingtan storytelling, both recognized by UNESCO as intangible heritage.
