Salt beef

Salt beef, or salted beef, is beef that has been preserved and cured with salt, traditionally coarse-grained salt or brine. It is closely related to corned beef, from which it differs mainly in terminology and sometimes in the cut and cure used. Salt-cured beef was a staple provisioning food for navies, armies and long sea voyages for centuries because salting draws out moisture, inhibits bacterial growth and preserves the meat without refrigeration.

Preparation

The meat—usually brisket, navel or silverside—is packed in salt or immersed in a strong brine, often with saltpetre (potassium nitrate) or curing salts containing nitrite, which fix the appetising red colour. Curing takes days to weeks. Before serving, the beef is commonly soaked to remove excess salt and then simmered gently until tender.

Culinary uses

Salt beef is famous in London's East End, where Jewish delicatessens and bagel shops—notably in Brick Lane—serve hot salt beef sandwiches and salt beef beigels with mustard and pickles. In Ireland, salted ("corned") beef with cabbage became a traditional dish, and Irish emigrants carried the custom to North America, where corned beef and cabbage became associated with St Patrick's Day and with New York delicatessens. In Newfoundland, salt beef is boiled in the "Jiggs dinner" with pease pudding, cabbage, potatoes and other vegetables. Spiced and pressed variants include pastrami, which is salt-cured, smoked and steamed.
