The AK-47, officially known as the Avtomat Kalashnikova (Russian: Автомат Калашникова; "Kalashnikov's automatic [rifle]"), is a gas-operated assault rifle chambered for the 7.62×39mm cartridge. Developed in the Soviet Union by Mikhail Kalashnikov, it became the standard service rifle of the Soviet Army in 1949 and the archetype of the Kalashnikov family of firearms, of which an estimated 75 million AK-47s and over 100 million Kalashnikov-pattern rifles have been produced — making it the most prevalent firearm in the world.

Design

The AK-47 combines a long-stroke gas piston with a rotating bolt, loose tolerances, and a simple, rugged construction that functions reliably in mud, dust, and extreme cold with minimal maintenance. It fires from a distinctive curved 30-round magazine and offers selective fire (semi-automatic and fully automatic). Early receivers were stamped, then milled; the improved AKM of 1959 returned to a lighter stamped receiver and became the most common variant.

History and spread

Kalashnikov began work on the design during the Second World War, influenced by the German StG 44 and the intermediate 7.62×39mm cartridge. The rifle was adopted in 1949 and spread throughout the Warsaw Pact and to allied and client states; licensed and unlicensed copies were manufactured in China (Type 56), Yugoslavia, Romania, Egypt, and many other countries. It became a symbol of revolutionary movements and appears on the flag of Mozambique and the emblem of Hezbollah.

Legacy

Later derivatives include the AK-74 (5.45×39mm), the AK-100 series, and modernised AK-12. The rifle's reputation rests on its durability, low cost, and ease of use rather than long-range accuracy.
