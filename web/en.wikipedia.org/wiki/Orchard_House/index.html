Orchard House

Orchard House

Location: 399 Lexington Road, Concord, Massachusetts, U.S.

Built: c. 1700–1710s (oldest sections)

Occupants: The Alcott family, 1858–1877

Status: Historic house museum

Orchard House is a historic house museum in Concord, Massachusetts, United States. It is best known as the home of the author Louisa May Alcott and her family from 1858 to 1877, and as the setting of her beloved 1868 novel Little Women. The house takes its name from the apple orchard of some forty trees that once stood on the property.

History

The oldest parts of the house date to the early 18th century. In 1857 the philosopher and educator Amos Bronson Alcott purchased the property — some twelve acres with two houses — for $945, moving the smaller tenant house to adjoin the main structure and renovating the whole with the help of his daughters. The family moved in during the spring of 1858.

It was at Orchard House that Louisa May Alcott wrote Little Women in the spring and summer of 1868, working at a small half-moon desk her father had built for her in her bedroom, writing in bursts of intense activity over roughly ten weeks. The novel's March family home is a thinly veiled version of Orchard House, and many of the Alcotts' furnishings, books, and the sisters' artwork remain in the house today. The youngest sister, May Alcott, an artist, decorated walls and woodwork with drawings and paintings that can still be seen.

Next door, on the same property, Bronson Alcott built the Concord School of Philosophy in 1879, which hosted summer lectures by leading Transcendentalist thinkers including Ralph Waldo Emerson.

Museum

The house was opened to the public as a museum in 1912 by the Louisa May Alcott Memorial Association. Roughly three-quarters of the furnishings on display belonged to the Alcott family. Guided tours present the house as the Alcotts knew it, and the museum runs educational programmes, re-enactments, and holiday events. Orchard House receives tens of thousands of visitors a year, many of them readers of Little Women from around the world.
