Béarn is a former province of south-western France, situated in the foothills of the Pyrenees and forming the eastern part of the modern department of Pyrénées-Atlantiques. Its capital is Pau, and its traditional language is the Béarnese dialect of Gascon (Occitan).

History

The viscounty of Béarn was effectively independent for much of the Middle Ages, passing through the houses of Foix and Albret to Henry of Navarre, who became Henry IV of France in 1589. Béarn remained a separate pays d'état with its own estates and the Fors de Béarn legal code until it was formally incorporated into France in 1620.

Culture

Béarn is associated with béarnaise sauce, Jurançon wine, and the béret. It is the birthplace of Henry IV, celebrated in local tradition as "the good king".
