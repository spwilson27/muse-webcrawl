Wikipedia: Contents

Wikipedia's contents are organised into reference and outline pages that help readers explore the encyclopedia by topic. This page provides a top-level overview of the major subject areas covered by the English Wikipedia, which contains more than six million articles written collaboratively by volunteers around the world.

Articles can be browsed through several complementary systems: the main topic classifications below, outline pages that summarise a subject and its subtopics, portals that act as curated front pages for broad areas, and alphabetical indexes of articles.

Reference

  • General reference — encyclopedias, dictionaries, almanacs and atlases reproduced or linked from articles.
  • Culture and the arts — see also Culture.
  • Geography and places — continents, countries, regions and settlements, from Sri Lanka to Madagascar.
  • Health and fitness — medicine, nutrition and disease, such as coeliac disease.
  • History and events — from the ancient City Dionysia to the Vietnam War.
  • Mathematics and logic
  • Natural and physical sciences — biology, chemistry, astronomy and earth science.
  • People — biographies such as W. E. B. Du Bois and Ulugh Beg.
  • Philosophy and thinking
  • Religion and belief systems — including epic literature such as the Ramayana.
  • Society and social sciences
  • Technology and applied sciences

Ways to browse

Outlines present a subject as a structured tree of topics, useful for seeing how a field is organised. Portals gather featured articles, news and "did you know" entries for a broad area. Lists collect articles by a shared criterion, while categories form a hierarchical tagging system maintained by editors.

Readers unsure where to start can read about Wikipedia, browse current events, or open a random article.
