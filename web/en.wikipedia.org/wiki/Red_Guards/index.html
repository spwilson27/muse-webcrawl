Red Guards

The Red Guards (Chinese: 红卫兵; pinyin: Hóng Wèibīng) were a mass student-led paramilitary social movement mobilized in China by Mao Zedong during the Cultural Revolution. Composed largely of middle school, high school, and university students, the movement was active from 1966 until its suppression in 1968.

Origins

The first Red Guard groups appeared in Beijing in mid-1966, encouraged by Mao and the Cultural Revolution Group to attack "bourgeois" and "revisionist" elements. Mao personally reviewed Red Guard rallies in Tiananmen Square in the summer of 1966, lending the movement immense momentum.

Activities

Red Guards attacked the "Four Olds" — old customs, culture, habits, and ideas — destroying cultural heritage, persecuting teachers, officials, and intellectuals, and engaging in violent factional fighting. The chaos eventually prompted Mao to deploy the People's Liberation Army to restore order and to send millions of young people "down to the countryside".

Legacy

The Red Guard movement is remembered as one of the most destructive episodes of the Cultural Revolution, causing widespread persecution and long-lasting trauma across Chinese society.
