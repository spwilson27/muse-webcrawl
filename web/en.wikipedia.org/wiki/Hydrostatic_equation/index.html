Hydrostatic equation

Hydrostatic equation
FieldFluid mechanics, atmospheric science, astrophysics
DescribesPressure variation in a fluid at rest under gravity

Force balance on a thin fluid layer: the pressure gradient balances gravity.

The hydrostatic equation describes how pressure varies with height in a fluid that is at rest (or in hydrostatic equilibrium) under gravity. It states that the vertical pressure gradient is balanced by the weight of the fluid:

dp/dz = −ρg

where p is pressure, z is height, ρ is the fluid density and g is the acceleration due to gravity. The negative sign indicates that pressure decreases as height increases.

Derivation

Consider a thin horizontal slab of fluid of thickness dz and unit cross-sectional area. The forces acting on it are the pressure force on its bottom face, the pressure force on its top face, and its own weight, ρg dz. For the slab to be in equilibrium these must cancel, which gives dp = −ρg dz and hence the equation. It is a special case of the Navier–Stokes equations with zero velocity and acceleration.

Applications

In meteorology the equation underlies the barometric formula for atmospheric pressure and the hypsometric equation used to compute the thickness between pressure levels from mean temperature. In oceanography it relates sea-surface height to subsurface density structure. In astrophysics, hydrostatic equilibrium — the balance between gravity and the total pressure gradient (gas plus radiation) — determines the internal structure of stars and planets and is a defining condition for a body to be considered a dwarf planet.

Limitations

The equation neglects vertical accelerations, so it becomes inaccurate in vigorous convection, strong storms and other situations where air or water parcels accelerate vertically at a significant fraction of g.
