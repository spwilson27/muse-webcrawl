Tyre (Arabic: صور, Ṣūr; Phoenician: 𐤑𐤓, Ṣūr; Greek: Τύρος, Týros) is a city in southern Lebanon, on the Mediterranean coast about 80 km south of Beirut. One of the oldest continually inhabited cities in the world, it was a great maritime power of the Phoenicians, famed for its trade, its colonies across the Mediterranean — Carthage among them — and its production of the precious purple dye known as Tyrian purple.

Ancient Tyre, originally an offshore island, is now a peninsula joined to the mainland by Alexander's causeway.

History

Legend, repeated by Herodotus, dated Tyre's founding to around 2750 BC. The city originally consisted of a mainland settlement and a fortified island city just offshore with two harbours. Under kings such as Hiram I (10th century BC), an ally and trading partner of Solomon, Tyre rose to become the leading Phoenician city, exporting cedar, purple dye, glass, and metalwork, and founding trading posts and colonies from Cyprus to Spain.

Tyre repeatedly resisted great empires. Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon besieged the island city for thirteen years (586–573 BC) without decisive success. In 332 BC Alexander the Great, refused entry, built a kilometre-long causeway (mole) from rubble and timber to reach the island's walls, and took the city after a seven-month siege; the causeway silted up over time and joined Tyre permanently to the mainland, forming today's peninsula. The city prospered under Hellenistic, Roman, and Byzantine rule — building the great hippodrome whose ruins survive — and was later taken in turn by Arab, Crusader, and Mamluk forces; the Crusaders made it one of the last strongholds of the Kingdom of Jerusalem, falling in 1291.

Tyrian purple

Tyre's most famous product was a dye extracted from the hypobranchial gland of murex sea snails, mainly Bolinus brandaris and Hexaplex trunculus. Tens of thousands of snails were needed for a single garment, and the dye's price made purple the colour of kings and emperors — whence the expression "born in the purple". Huge mounds of crushed murex shells still mark the ancient production sites.

The modern city

Modern Tyre (Sour) is the fourth-largest city of Lebanon, with a population of roughly 160,000 in the wider district. The archaeological sites of Tyre — the Al-Bass necropolis and hippodrome, and the city site with its Roman colonnades and baths — were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1984.
