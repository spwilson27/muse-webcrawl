Dahui Zonggao

Dahui Zonggao (Chinese: 大慧宗杲; pinyin: Dàhuì Zōnggǎo; 1089–1163) was a leading Chinese Chan (Zen) Buddhist master of the Linji school during the Song dynasty. He is best known as the systematiser of kanhua ("observing the phrase") meditation, in which the student concentrates intensely on the critical phrase (huatou) of a koan until doubt and insight break through — a method that became dominant in later Chinese Chan and influential in Korean Seon and Japanese Zen.

Life

Dahui became a monk at eighteen and studied under several teachers before attaining enlightenment under Yuanwu Keqin, the compiler of the Blue Cliff Record. He rose to become abbot of major monasteries and attracted thousands of students, including many lay officials. His outspoken criticism of the war-hawk chancellor Qin Hui's rivals and his association with exiled officials led to his own exile to Hengzhou (in modern Hunan) in 1141, where he nevertheless continued teaching for over a decade before being pardoned late in life.

Teachings

Dahui sharply criticised the "silent illumination" approach associated with the Caodong school and the literary study of koan collections as intellectual pastime. Famously, he is said to have burned the woodblocks of his own teacher's Blue Cliff Record to prevent students from treating it as literature rather than practice. His voluminous letters to lay students (Dahui shu) remain classics of Chan literature, emphasising that awakening must be pursued amid daily life with great doubt, great faith, and great determination.
