Second Life is an online virtual world developed and owned by San Francisco-based Linden Lab and launched on 23 June 2003. It saw rapid growth in the mid-2000s and remains in operation. Its users, called residents, create virtual representations of themselves called avatars and can explore the world (the "grid"), meet other residents, socialize, and create and trade virtual property and services.

Overview

Second Life is not a game in the traditional sense: it has no points, levels, or manufactured conflict. Linden Lab's founding vision, led by Philip Rosedale and inspired in part by the novel Snow Crash, was that the world would be built almost entirely by its residents. Users create content using a built-in 3D modelling tool and the Linden Scripting Language (LSL). In a landmark 2003 policy change, Linden Lab granted residents intellectual-property rights in their creations, giving rise to a functioning virtual economy.

Economy

The in-world currency, the Linden dollar (L$), can be bought and sold for real money on Linden Lab's LindeX exchange at roughly L$250 to US$1. Residents earn income from virtual real estate, clothing, vehicles, entertainment venues, and services; the first Second Life millionaire, Anshe Chung (Ailin Graef), was profiled on the cover of BusinessWeek in 2006. During its peak-media period (roughly 2006–2009), corporations, universities, news agencies, and even national embassies opened presences in the world.

Later years

Although mainstream hype faded and Linden Lab ended resident-last-name choice for a time, Second Life retained a loyal user base, with tens of thousands of concurrent users and a GDP-like in-world economy measured in the hundreds of millions of dollars. An adult-content restructure in 2009 created a separate adult continent, Zindra. Linden Lab's successor project, Sansar, launched in 2017 and was sold in 2020, while Second Life itself continued with graphical upgrades and a mobile companion app. It is often cited as an early precursor of the "metaverse" concept.
