McCormick Place is the largest convention center in North America. It consists of four interconnected buildings and one indoor arena on the shore of Lake Michigan, about 2 mi (3.2 km) south of downtown Chicago, Illinois. McCormick Place hosts numerous trade shows and meetings. The largest regular events held at the center are the Chicago Auto Show, held each February, the International Manufacturing Technology Show, held every other September, and the National Restaurant Association Annual Show.

History

The first McCormick Place opened in January 1960 and was named in honor of Colonel Robert R. McCormick, the longtime editor and publisher of the Chicago Tribune, who had championed the construction of a lakeside convention center. The original building was destroyed by fire in 1967 and was rebuilt, reopening in 1971 as the present-day Lakeside Center (formerly called the East Building). Subsequent expansions added the North Building in 1986, the South Building in 1997, and the West Building in 2007, bringing total exhibit space to about 2.6 million square feet.

Facilities

The complex includes the Lakeside Center, North, South, and West buildings, the 7,200-capacity Arie Crown Theater, and the Wintrust Arena, which opened in 2017. The center is connected by pedestrian skybridges to nearby hotels, including the Hyatt Regency McCormick Place, the Marriott Marquis Chicago, and the Hilton Garden Inn. A dedicated CTA station on the Green Line serves the complex.
