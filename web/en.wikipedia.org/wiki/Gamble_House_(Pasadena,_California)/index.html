Gamble House (Pasadena, California)

Gamble House
Location: 4 Westmoreland Place, Pasadena, California
Built: 1908–1909
Architects: Charles Sumner Greene and Henry Mather Greene
Style: American Craftsman
Designation: National Historic Landmark

The Gamble House, also known as the David B. Gamble House, is an iconic American Craftsman house in Pasadena, California, designed by the architectural firm Greene and Greene. Constructed in 1908–1909 as a winter residence for David B. Gamble, of the Procter & Gamble company, and his wife Mary, it is regarded as a masterpiece of the Arts and Crafts movement in California.


The Gamble House from the street

Architecture

The three-storey house is built largely of wood — teak, maple, oak, cedar and mahogany — with broad overhanging eaves, exposed joinery, and sleeping porches designed for the mild Southern California climate. The interior furniture and fittings were designed by the Greenes as part of a unified artistic whole, and much of the original furniture remains in place.

History

The Gamble family occupied the house until 1966, when it was deeded to the City of Pasadena in a joint arrangement with the University of Southern California School of Architecture, whose students have served as resident caretakers. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1977. The house also appeared as the home of Doc Brown in the film Back to the Future (1985).
