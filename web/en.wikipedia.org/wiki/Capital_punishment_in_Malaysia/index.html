Capital punishment in Malaysia

Capital punishment in Malaysia is a legal penalty, carried out by hanging. Malaysia long imposed a mandatory death penalty for offences including drug trafficking, murder, and certain firearms offences, but reforms in 2023 abolished the mandatory death penalty and imposed a moratorium-era review of death sentences.

Capital punishment in Malaysia
Method: Hanging
Status: Legal; mandatory penalty abolished 2023

History

The death penalty was inherited from British colonial law. The Dangerous Drugs Act 1952 made capital punishment mandatory for trafficking specified quantities of drugs, leading to many executions, including of foreign nationals. A moratorium on executions was in place from 2018 while reform was debated.

2023 reforms

The Abolition of Mandatory Death Penalty Act 2023 gave judges discretion to impose imprisonment (including lengthy terms and whipping) instead of death for previously mandatory offences, and a separate resentencing law allowed over a thousand death-row prisoners to apply for review.
