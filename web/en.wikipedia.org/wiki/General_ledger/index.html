General ledger

A general ledger is a bookkeeping ledger in which accounting data are posted from journals and from subledgers, such as accounts payable, accounts receivable, cash management, fixed assets, purchasing, and projects. A general ledger is used by businesses to create a business's financial statements, including the balance sheet and income statement. It represents the complete record of all financial transactions over the life of a company, organised by accounts.

Structure

The general ledger holds account information needed to prepare financial statements, and includes accounts for assets, liabilities, owners' equity, revenues, and expenses. Each account in the general ledger is divided into two sections: a debit side and a credit side. Periodically, all transactions in a specific journal are summarised and posted, using a double-entry system of accounting, to the corresponding accounts in the general ledger.

Trial balance and financial statements

From the general ledger, a company extracts a trial balance, listing the balances of all accounts, which is used to check that total debits equal total credits before preparing financial statements. The ledger is sometimes divided into subsidiary ledgers for detail, with the general ledger recording summary totals in control accounts.

History

The general ledger is one of the central components of the double-entry bookkeeping system formalised by Luca Pacioli in 1494. In modern practice, general ledgers are maintained within accounting software and enterprise resource planning systems rather than in physical books.
