Bug-out bag
Also called: Go-bag, 72-hour kit, grab bag
Purpose: Emergency evacuation supplies
Intended duration: ~72 hours

A bug-out bag is a portable kit containing items needed to survive for roughly seventy-two hours when evacuating from a disaster. The term is popular among survivalists and preppers, and similar kits are recommended by emergency-management agencies as a basic disaster supplies kit.

Contents

Typical contents include water and a means of purifying more, non-perishable food, a first aid kit, a flashlight and batteries, a battery- or crank-powered radio, warm clothing and rain gear, a multi-tool or knife, cash, copies of important documents, medications, and hygiene supplies. Government guidance, such as that of the U.S. Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), recommends at least a three-day supply of food and water per person.

Terminology

"Bugging out" derives from military slang for a rapid retreat or evacuation. Variants include the "go-bag," "grab-and-go bag," and the "INCH" bag ("I'm never coming home"), intended for indefinite self-reliance.

Criticism and practice

Emergency professionals note that a stocked kit kept at home, in a car, and at work is more practical for most disasters than wilderness-survival gear, and that evacuation plans, meeting points, and communication arrangements matter as much as supplies.
