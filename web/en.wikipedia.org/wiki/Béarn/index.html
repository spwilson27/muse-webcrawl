Béarn

Béarn (French pronunciation: [beaʁn]; Occitan: Bearn) is a historic province in southwestern France, at the foot of the Pyrenees. Its capital was Pau. Today its territory forms the eastern part of the Pyrénées-Atlantiques department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region.

History

Béarn emerged as a viscounty in the 9th century under the Centulle dynasty. Its rulers maintained a large degree of independence between the growing powers of France, Aragon, and Navarre. The province produced Gaston III "Fébus", Count of Foix and Viscount of Béarn (1331–1391), a celebrated patron of letters and author of the Livre de chasse.

In 1517 Béarn's rulers, the Albret family, also became kings of Navarre. Jeanne d'Albret made it a stronghold of Calvinism. Her son, Henry of Navarre — born at the Château de Pau in 1553 — became Henry IV of France in 1589, and in 1620 Louis XIII formally annexed Béarn to the French crown, while preserving its Estates until the Revolution.

Culture

Béarn is known for the Béarnese dialect of Occitan, for Jurançon wine, for the béarnaise sauce named after the province, and for the beret, which originated with Béarnais shepherds. Its sporting emblem is the rugby club Section Paloise, based in Pau.
