Glossary of areas of mathematics

Mathematics is a broad subject that is commonly divided into many areas, each concerned with particular objects, questions or methods. This glossary provides a brief guide to those areas — from the most elementary, such as arithmetic, to modern research fields. Formal classification schemes, such as the Mathematics Subject Classification used by reviewing journals, divide mathematics into dozens of top-level sections and thousands of subfields.

Foundations and algebra

Arithmetic
The study of numbers and basic operations on them.
Algebra
The study of algebraic structures such as groups, rings and fields, and of equations and the rules for manipulating symbols.
Number theory
The study of integers and integer-valued functions, including prime numbers and Diophantine equations; once called "higher arithmetic".
Mathematical logic
The study of formal systems, proof, and the foundations of mathematics.
Set theory
The study of sets, the basic objects of most mathematics.
Category theory
A highly abstract study of mathematical structures and relationships between them.

Analysis and geometry

Calculus and analysis
The study of limits, continuity, differentiation, integration and infinite series; real and complex analysis extend these to functions of real and complex variables.
Geometry
The study of shapes, sizes and spaces, including Euclidean, differential, algebraic and discrete geometry.
Topology
The study of properties preserved under continuous deformation, such as connectedness and compactness.
Differential equations
The study of equations involving derivatives, central to mathematical modelling.

Applied and discrete areas

Probability and statistics
The study of randomness, uncertainty and inference from data.
Combinatorics
The study of counting, arrangements and discrete structures.
Graph theory
The study of graphs — networks of vertices joined by edges.
Mathematical optimisation
The search for best solutions under constraints; includes operations research.
Numerical analysis
The study of algorithms for approximating solutions of continuous problems.
Mathematical physics, biology, finance and computer science
Applications of mathematical methods to other sciences and engineering.
