Bronzite

CategorySilicate mineral (pyroxene group)
Formula(Mg,Fe)SiO3
Crystal systemOrthorhombic
ColourBrown, bronze-like, greenish
Mohs hardness5–6
LustreSub-metallic on cleavage surfaces

Bronzite is a member of the pyroxene group of minerals, belonging with enstatite and hypersthene to the orthorhombic series. It is not a distinct species but an iron-bearing variety of enstatite containing roughly 5–14% iron oxide (FeO), intermediate between enstatite (low iron) and hypersthene (higher iron).

Properties and occurrence

Bronzite takes its name from the characteristic bronze-like sub-metallic lustre on its cleavage faces, caused by minute inclusions and partial alteration. Like other orthopyroxenes it is a rock-forming mineral of mafic and ultramafic igneous rocks such as norite, gabbro, peridotite and pyroxenite, and occurs in some stony meteorites (chondrites). Well-known localities include Styria in Austria, the Bushveld Complex of South Africa, and Stillwater, Montana. Weathered material is sometimes cut and polished as an ornamental stone.
