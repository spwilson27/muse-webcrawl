Chiafalo v. Washington

Chiafalo v. Washington, 591 U.S. 578 (2020)
Argued13 May 2020
Decided6 July 2020
HoldingStates may penalise presidential electors who break their pledge to support the winner of the state's popular vote ("faithless electors").
Opinion byJustice Elena Kagan (unanimous)

Chiafalo v. Washington, 591 U.S. 578 (2020), was a United States Supreme Court case holding that states may punish faithless electors — members of the Electoral College who do not vote for the presidential candidate to whom they are pledged.

Background

In the 2016 presidential election, three Washington state Democratic electors — Peter Bret Chiafalo, Levi Jennet Guerra, and Esther Virginia John — voted for Colin Powell rather than Hillary Clinton, and were each fined $1,000 under Washington law. They challenged the fines, arguing the Constitution gives electors free discretion. The Washington Supreme Court upheld the fines, and the US Supreme Court granted certiorari; a companion Colorado case, Colorado Department of State v. Baca, was decided the same day on related grounds.

Decision

In a unanimous opinion by Justice Elena Kagan, the Court held that a state may constitutionally require electors to pledge to vote for the winner of the state's popular vote and penalise those who break the pledge, grounding the ruling in states' broad power to appoint electors under Article II and the Twelfth Amendment, and in long historical practice.
