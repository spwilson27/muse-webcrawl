Huntsville, Texas

Huntsville, Texas
County: Walker County (county seat)
Population: about 46,000 (2020 census)
Institutions: Sam Houston State University; Texas Department of Criminal Justice headquarters
Named for: Huntsville, Alabama

Huntsville is a city in and the county seat of Walker County, Texas, United States. The population was about 46,000 at the 2020 census. It is the home of Sam Houston State University and the headquarters of the Texas Department of Criminal Justice, and is closely associated with Sam Houston, hero of the Texas Revolution.

History

The town was founded in 1836 when Pleasant and Ephraim Gray opened a trading post, naming it for their former home of Huntsville, Alabama. Sam Houston spent his later years here and died in Huntsville in 1863; his home, the Steamboat House, and his grave are local landmarks, and a 67-foot statue of Houston — among the tallest statues of an American figure — stands beside Interstate 45.

Prisons

Huntsville is known for its association with the Texas prison system. The Texas State Penitentiary at Huntsville ("Walls Unit"), opened in 1849, is the state's oldest prison and houses Texas's execution chamber, where all executions in the state are carried out. Several other prison units in and around the city make corrections one of its largest employers.

Education

Sam Houston State University, founded in 1879 as a teachers' college and named for Houston, enrols more than 20,000 students and is noted for its criminal-justice programmes. The city is also home to the Texas Prison Museum and the Sam Houston Memorial Museum.
