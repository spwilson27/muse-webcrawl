Gregory of Nyssa (Greek: Γρηγόριος Νύσσης; c. 335 – c. 395) was a Christian bishop and theologian, the younger brother of Basil of Caesarea and a close friend of Gregory of Nazianzus; together the three are known as the Cappadocian Fathers. Consecrated bishop of the small town of Nyssa in Cappadocia by Basil in 372, Gregory became one of the most philosophically sophisticated defenders of Nicene Trinitarian doctrine, and is venerated as a saint in Eastern and Western Christianity.

Life

Gregory was educated in rhetoric and appears to have first pursued a secular career as a teacher of rhetoric, marrying before entering church life. As bishop he was briefly deposed by Arian opponents (376) but restored under the emperor Theodosius I, and he played a prominent role at the Council of Constantinople (381), where he was acclaimed a champion of orthodoxy. His sister Macrina, a revered monastic founder, strongly influenced his thought; his dialogue On the Soul and the Resurrection is cast as a conversation with her on her deathbed.

Theology

Gregory's writings include the Life of Moses, a classic of mystical theology in which the spiritual life is an endless progress (epektasis) into the infinite God; homilies on the Song of Songs; the Catechetical Oration; and polemics against Eunomius defending the full divinity of the Son and the Holy Spirit. He is notable for an unusually strong teaching of universal restoration (apokatastasis), for condemning slavery as an offence against the image of God — an almost unique stance in the ancient church — and for a sophisticated account of the infinity of God that shaped later Byzantine and mystical theology.
