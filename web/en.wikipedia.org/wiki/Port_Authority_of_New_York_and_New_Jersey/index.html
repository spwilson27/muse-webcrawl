Port Authority of New York and New Jersey

The Port Authority of New York and New Jersey (PANYNJ) is a joint venture between the U.S. states of New York and New Jersey, established by interstate compact in 1921. It oversees much of the regional transportation infrastructure of the New York–Newark metropolitan area, including airports, seaports, bridges, tunnels, and transit systems.

Facilities

The Authority operates John F. Kennedy, LaGuardia, and Newark Liberty international airports; the George Washington Bridge, Lincoln and Holland tunnels, and Staten Island bridges; the Port Newark–Elizabeth Marine Terminal, the busiest container port on the U.S. East Coast; the Port Authority Bus Terminal; and the PATH rapid transit system linking Manhattan with New Jersey. It also owns the World Trade Center site in Lower Manhattan, redeveloped after the September 11 attacks.

Governance and finance

The Authority is governed by a twelve-member Board of Commissioners, six appointed by each governor. It receives no tax revenue, financing itself through tolls, fees, and bond issues — a self-supporting model that made it the archetype of the modern American public authority. It fields its own police force, the Port Authority Police Department, and its history includes the powerful tenure of executive director Austin Tobin and the 2013 "Bridgegate" scandal.
