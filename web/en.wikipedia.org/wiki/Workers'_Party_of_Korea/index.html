The Workers' Party of Korea (WPK) is the founding and ruling party of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North Korea). Established in 1949 through the merger of the Workers' Party of North Korea and the Workers' Party of South Korea, it is the largest party in the Supreme People's Assembly and governs in a front with two smaller parties. By statute, it is the supreme leading organisation of North Korean society.

History

The party traces its lineage to communist groups of the 1920s and to Kim Il Sung's guerrilla movement. Under Kim, the party consolidated power in the north of the Korean Peninsula after 1945, founded the DPRK in 1948, and led the country through the Korean War (1950–1953). Successive purges eliminated rival factions, and the party's ideology of Juche (self-reliance) was elevated from the 1950s onward.

Leadership

The party has been led by three members of the Kim family: Kim Il Sung (1949–1994), Kim Jong Il (1997–2011, with a party conference formally electing him General Secretary in 1997), and Kim Jong Un (from 2012; initially as First Secretary, then Chairman from 2016, and General Secretary from 2021). Kim Il Sung and Kim Jong Il hold posthumous eternal titles within party and state mythology.

Organisation

The party's supreme body is nominally its Congress; between congresses, power lies with the Central Committee, the Politburo and its Presidium, the Secretariat, and the Central Military Commission. The 8th Party Congress was held in January 2021. Membership is estimated at several million, organised through workplace and residential cells, with affiliated mass organizations for youth, women, workers, and farmers.

Ideology

The party's official guiding ideology is Kimilsungism–Kimjongilism, centred on Juche and, in military affairs, Songun (military-first) in earlier decades. The party emblem combines a hammer, a writing brush, and a Korean sickle, symbolising workers, intellectuals, and farmers.
