The Ronettes were an American girl group from New York City who achieved their greatest popularity in the 1960s. The trio consisted of lead singer Veronica Bennett (later known as Ronnie Spector; 1943–2022), her older sister Estelle Bennett (1941–2009), and their cousin Nedra Talley (born 1946). Known for their beehive hairstyles, heavy eyeliner and the powerful voice of Ronnie Bennett, the group is best remembered for the 1963 hit "Be My Baby," produced by Phil Spector in his signature "Wall of Sound" style, along with "Baby, I Love You," "Walking in the Rain" and "(The Best Part of) Breakin' Up."

Career

The Bennett sisters and Talley grew up in Spanish Harlem and Washington Heights and began singing together as teenagers, performing as the Darling Sisters and then Ronnie and the Ronettes at the Apollo Theater and at the Peppermint Lounge, where their dancing in the twist craze drew attention. After singles for Colpix Records (as Ronnie & the Relatives and then The Ronettes) failed to chart, they signed with Phil Spector's Philles Records in 1963. Spector and songwriters Jeff Barry and Ellie Greenwich shaped "Be My Baby," released in August 1963; it reached No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100 and became one of the defining records of the era, later ranked among the greatest songs of all time and inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame. The Brian Wilson of the Beach Boys famously regarded it as the greatest pop record ever made.

Follow-ups included "Baby, I Love You" (1963), "(The Best Part of) Breakin' Up" and "Do I Love You?" (1964), and "Walking in the Rain" (1964), which won a Grammy nomination for its sound effects. The group's only studio album, Presenting the Fabulous Ronettes Featuring Veronica, appeared in 1964. The Ronettes toured Britain in 1964 (where the Rolling Stones opened for them) and, in the summer of 1966, toured the United States with the Beatles on what proved to be the Beatles' final tour — though Ronnie, by then married to Spector, was replaced on that tour by another cousin, Elaine Mayers, because Spector refused to let her go. As Spector's career waned and his controlling marriage to Ronnie darkened, the group's last singles failed, and the Ronettes disbanded in 1967.

Legacy and later years

Ronnie Spector's memoir Be My Baby (1990) chronicled her career and abusive marriage; she divorced Spector in 1974 and fought a long legal battle over royalties, winning a landmark New York Court of Appeals ruling in 2001 that restored royalty payments. The Ronettes were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2007 and into the Vocal Group Hall of Fame in 2004. Estelle Bennett died in 2009. Ronnie Spector continued performing until her death from cancer on January 12, 2022, aged 78.
