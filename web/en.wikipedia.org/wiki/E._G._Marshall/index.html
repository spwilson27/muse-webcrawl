E. G. Marshall

Born: Everett Eugene Grunz, 18 June 1914, Owatonna, Minnesota, U.S.
Died: 24 August 1998 (aged 84), Bedford, New York, U.S.
Occupation: Actor

E. G. Marshall (born Everett Eugene Grunz; June 18, 1914 – August 24, 1998) was an American actor. One of the first group of actors selected for the Actors Studio, Marshall was best known for his work in film and television, notably as the calm, rational Juror No. 4 in Sidney Lumet's 12 Angry Men (1957) and as defence attorney Lawrence Preston in the television series The Defenders (1961–1965), for which he won two Emmy Awards.

Early life and career

Marshall was born in Owatonna, Minnesota, to parents of German and Scandinavian descent. He attended Carleton College and the University of Minnesota. He began acting on stage and radio in the 1930s, performed with the Orson Welles Mercury Theatre company, and made his Broadway debut in the late 1930s. He served in the U.S. Army during the Second World War. He never publicly explained what "E. G." stood for, and the mystery became a running joke during his lifetime.

Film and television

Marshall's screen career spanned five decades. Notable film roles included Call Northside 777 (1948), The Caine Mutiny (1954), Compulsion (1959), Tora! Tora! Tora! (1970), Superman II (1980), in which he played the President of the United States, Creepshow (1982), National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation (1989), Consenting Adults (1992), and Nixon (1995), in which he played John Mitchell. He recreated a television version of his 12 Angry Men juror in the original 1954 Studio One production before the film version.

On television, besides The Defenders, he hosted The CBS Radio Mystery Theater (1974–1982), starred in The Bold Ones: The Senator and The Lazarus Syndrome, and in his final years played Dr. David Craig in Chicago Hope (1994–1995). He was also a noted narrator of documentary films.

Personal life and politics

Marshall was active in liberal causes, supported civil liberties organisations, and was caught up in the blacklist era investigations, though he was never formally blacklisted. He was married twice and had seven children. He died of lung cancer at his home in Bedford, New York, in 1998.
