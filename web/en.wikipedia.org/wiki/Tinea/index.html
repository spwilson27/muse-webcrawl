Tinea

Tinea is the name of a group of diseases caused by dermatophyte fungi. These superficial infections of the skin, hair and nails are commonly called "ringworm" when they form ring-shaped patches, although no worm is involved. The word tinea is Latin for a gnawing worm or moth.

Classification

Tineas are named for the body site affected: tinea capitis (scalp), tinea corporis (body), tinea cruris (groin, "jock itch"), tinea pedis (foot, "athlete's foot"), tinea unguium (nails) and tinea barbae (beard). The causative genera are Trichophyton, Microsporum and Epidermophyton.

Signs and treatment

Typical lesions are itchy, scaly patches that expand outward with central clearing. Diagnosis is clinical, confirmed by microscopy of skin scrapings or culture. Most cases respond to topical antifungals such as terbinafine or azoles; scalp and nail infections usually require oral therapy.
