The Korea Computer Center (KCC; Korean: 조선콤퓨터중심) is the central state-run information technology research and development centre of North Korea. Founded on 24 October 1990, it operates under the country's Ministry of Post and Telecommunications and oversees much of North Korea's domestic software development and computer networking.

Activities

The KCC is best known internationally as the developer of Red Star OS, North Korea's state-sanctioned Linux-based operating system, and as the operator of technical infrastructure for Kwangmyong, the country's domestic intranet. The centre also administers North Korea's national top-level domain, .kp, and has been involved in developing domestic web portals, office software, Korean-language input systems and educational software.

According to outside analysts, the centre employs roughly a thousand or more engineers and programmers organised into numerous research institutes and branch offices, including a presence in China. Its staff are considered among the best-trained computer specialists in the country.

Controversy

Foreign governments and security researchers have alleged that elements of North Korea's computer establishment, including entities linked to the KCC's overseas operations, have been involved in cyber operations abroad. North Korea has consistently denied such allegations. The centre is subject to international sanctions designations in some jurisdictions because of its role in the state's IT sector.
