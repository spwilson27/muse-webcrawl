Mule (shoe)

A mule is a style of shoe that has no back or constraint around the heel — that is, a backless, typically closed-toe slip-on shoe. Mules can be flat or heeled, casual or dressy, and have been worn by both men and women at various periods. The word derives from the Latin mulleus calceus, a red ceremonial shoe worn by Roman senators, via the French mule.

History

Backless slippers were worn indoors from antiquity, but the mule became a fashionable shoe in its own right in the late 17th and 18th centuries, when elaborately embroidered and heeled mules were worn at the French court and throughout Europe. In the 19th century they retreated to the boudoir, and in the 20th century returned repeatedly to fashion: the feathered, marabou-trimmed mules of 1950s Hollywood, the platform mules of the 1970s, and minimalist slip-on mules in the 1990s and again in the 2010s, when designers revived them in forms ranging from flat pointed loafers to high-heeled sandals.

Styles

Mules differ from clogs (which are generally open- or closed-toe backless shoes on a rigid, often wooden sole) and from slides and slippers, though the categories overlap; fashion usage generally reserves "mule" for a backless shoe with a covered toe and a defined heel or dressier upper. Because the foot is held only by the vamp, mules are easy to slip on and off but unsuited to vigorous activity.
