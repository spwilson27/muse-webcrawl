Mathematical Olympiad

A Mathematical Olympiad is a competition in which students solve challenging mathematical problems under time constraints, typically emphasising creative problem solving and proof rather than routine computation. Competitions exist at regional, national, and international levels and are usually aimed at pre-university students.

History

The oldest such competitions are Hungary's Eötvös Competition, begun in 1894, which helped inspire the modern olympiad movement. The International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO), founded in Romania in 1959 with seven participating countries, is the oldest and most prestigious of the international science olympiads; it now draws teams of up to six students from over 100 countries. Each IMO consists of six problems over two days, each worth seven points, with medals awarded to roughly the top half of contestants.

Olympiad topics and training

Olympiad problems generally cover algebra, combinatorics, geometry, and number theory, and deliberately avoid calculus. National programmes such as the USA Mathematical Olympiad, the British Mathematical Olympiad, and China's national competitions feed into IMO team selection, supported by training camps and problem journals. Related competitions include the European Girls' Mathematical Olympiad (founded 2012), the International Mathematical Olympiad for younger students, and university-level contests such as the William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition in North America.
