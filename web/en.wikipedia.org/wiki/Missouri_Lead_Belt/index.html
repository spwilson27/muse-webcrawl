Missouri Lead Belt

The Missouri Lead Belt is a major lead-mining district in southeastern Missouri, United States, centred on St. Francois County and surrounding counties around the towns of Park Hills, Bonne Terre, Farmington, and Fredericktown. For more than a century it has been one of the world's most productive lead districts, supplying a large share of United States lead production.

History

French colonial miners began digging surface lead near Mine La Motte in the early 18th century, and the district — often called the "Old Lead Belt" around Bonne Terre and Desloge — developed deep mines in the 19th and early 20th centuries under companies such as St. Joe Minerals. From the 1960s, the "New Lead Belt" or Viburnum Trend, further west and south, became the dominant producing area, with mines served by the Doe Run Company and others. Lead ore (galena) occurs in Cambrian dolomites of the Bonneterre Formation, with zinc, copper, and silver as by-products.

Environmental legacy

Centuries of mining left extensive chat piles and lead-contaminated soils, and parts of the district, including the Big River Mine Tailings and areas around Herculaneum (site of a major lead smelter), were designated Superfund sites. The Bonne Terre Mine is now a popular dive site, and Missouri Mines State Historic Site preserves a St. Joe mill complex near Park Hills.
