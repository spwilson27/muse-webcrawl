Tunja

Tunja is a city in central Colombia, the capital of the department of Boyacá. Located on the Eastern Ranges of the Andes at about 2,820 metres above sea level, it has a population of roughly 180,000 and is one of Colombia's oldest Spanish-founded cities.

History

Before the Spanish conquest the site was Hunza, seat of the zaque, one of the principal rulers of the Muisca Confederation. Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada's forces took the city in 1537, and the Spanish city was founded by Gonzalo Suárez Rendón on 6 August 1539. Tunja preserves notable colonial architecture, including the cathedral and the Casa del Fundador.

Importance

The nearby Puente de Boyacá, where Simón Bolívar's army defeated Spanish forces on 7 August 1819, secured the independence of New Granada. Tunja is a university city, home to the Universidad Pedagógica y Tecnológica de Colombia, and hosts the annual Festival Internacional de la Cultura de Boyacá.
