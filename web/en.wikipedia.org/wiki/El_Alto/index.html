El Alto

El Alto (Spanish for "The Heights") is a city in Bolivia, located adjacent to La Paz in the Pedro Domingo Murillo Province on the Altiplano highlands. At an elevation of roughly 4,150 metres (13,620 ft) above sea level, it is among the highest major cities in the world. It is Bolivia's second-largest city, with a population of around 885,000 in the 2012 census.

History

El Alto grew from a suburb of La Paz on the rim of the canyon in which the capital sits. The opening of La Paz's airport there in 1965 and waves of migration from rural areas, especially Aymara-speaking communities, drove rapid growth. It became an independent municipality in 1985 and an officially chartered city in 1988. El Alto played a central role in the Bolivian gas conflict of 2003, whose protests helped bring down President Gonzalo Sánchez de Lozada.

Geography and transport

The city sits on the flat Altiplano plateau above La Paz, to which it is connected by highways and by the Mi Teleférico cable-car system, whose lines climb from the capital to El Alto's districts. El Alto International Airport, one of the highest international airports in the world, serves both cities. The city is known for its vast open-air market, the Feria 16 de Julio, and for the colourful "cholet" architecture associated with architect Freddy Mamani.
