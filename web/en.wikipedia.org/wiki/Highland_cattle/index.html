Highland cattle

The Highland is a Scottish breed of rustic cattle originating in the Scottish Highlands and the Western Islands. It is recognisable by its long horns and long, wavy, woolly coat, and is one of the oldest and most distinctive British cattle breeds. The breed society was founded in 1884 and the first herd book published the following year.

Characteristics

Highlands have a double coat: a downy undercoat and a long, oily outer layer, the longest of any cattle breed, which sheds rain and snow and allows them to thrive in harsh upland conditions where other breeds would need shelter and supplementary feed. The most common colour is a reddish brown, but black, brindle, dun, yellow, and silver also occur. Both sexes carry long horns that curve outwards and upward. Bulls weigh around 800 kg and cows about 500 kg.

Use

The breed is kept primarily for beef, which is lean, well-marbled, and lower in cholesterol than most other beef because the animals' insulation comes from hair rather than a thick layer of fat. Highlands are long-lived, with cows commonly calving into their late teens, and they are valued for grazing rough conservation land. The breed has been exported worldwide, to North America, Australia, and continental Europe.
