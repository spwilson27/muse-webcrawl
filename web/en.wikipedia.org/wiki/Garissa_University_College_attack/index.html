The Garissa University College attack was a mass shooting and hostage siege carried out on 2 April 2015 at Garissa University College in Garissa, Kenya, by gunmen of the Somali Islamist group al-Shabaab. The attackers killed 148 people, mostly students, and injured many more, making it the deadliest terrorist attack in Kenya since the 1998 United States embassy bombings.

Attack

Four gunmen stormed the campus before dawn, killing guards and shooting students in lecture halls and dormitories. Witnesses reported that the attackers separated Muslim from non-Muslim students, sparing the former and executing many of the latter. Kenyan security forces retook the campus after a siege lasting most of the day; all four attackers were killed. Al-Shabaab claimed the attack was retaliation for Kenya's military intervention in Somalia.

Aftermath

The massacre prompted grief and anger over slow security response times, led to funerals and memorials across Kenya, and intensified debate about the safety of the northeast. The university temporarily closed and later reopened with a permanent police post; an annual memorial is held for the victims.
