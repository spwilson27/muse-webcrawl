The AMX-13 is a French light tank produced from 1952 to 1987. Named for its weight class of 13 tonnes, it was one of the most successful and widely exported post-war light tanks, serving with the French Army and the armed forces of more than 25 other countries. It is notable for its unusual oscillating turret and automatic loader.

Design

The AMX-13's most distinctive feature is its two-piece oscillating turret, in which the gun is fixed to the upper half of the turret, which elevates as a unit. This arrangement made it practical to fit a simple autoloader: two six-round revolver-type magazines fed the 75 mm gun, allowing rapid fire, but the magazines had to be replenished from outside the vehicle once exhausted. The three-man crew and compact hull kept the weight low, at the cost of thin armor proof only against small arms and shell fragments.

Variants and service

The chassis spawned a large family of variants, including self-propelled howitzers (the 105 mm Mk 61 and 155 mm F3), anti-aircraft versions, armored personnel carriers (the AMX-VCI family), recovery vehicles, and SS.11 anti-tank missile carriers. French tanks were progressively re-gunned with a 90 mm low-pressure gun. Export users included Israel, which used AMX-13s in the Six-Day War of 1967, India, Indonesia, the Netherlands, Switzerland, and numerous Latin American and Southeast Asian armies. Singapore developed an extensively modernized version, the SM1.

Legacy

Although retired from French service in the 1980s, AMX-13 derivatives remained in use in some countries into the twenty-first century, a testament to the flexibility of the original design.
