Aromatic hydrocarbon

An aromatic hydrocarbon, or arene, is a hydrocarbon with a conjugated planar ring system in which π electrons are delocalised, giving unusual stability. The prototype is benzene (C6H6), whose six π electrons satisfy Hückel's rule of 4n + 2 π electrons.

Types

Arenes include monocyclic compounds such as toluene and the xylenes, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) such as naphthalene, anthracene and benzo[a]pyrene. Aromatic rings undergo electrophilic substitution rather than the addition reactions typical of alkenes.

Occurrence and significance

Aromatic hydrocarbons occur in coal tar and petroleum and are major industrial feedstocks for plastics, dyes and pharmaceuticals. Some PAHs are carcinogenic pollutants formed by incomplete combustion.
