Systems analysis

Systems analysis is the study of a system — its components, their interactions, and the processes by which inputs are transformed into outputs — with the aim of understanding, documenting, and improving it. In the context of information technology, systems analysis is the phase of the systems development life cycle in which the requirements of a proposed system are determined, often through structured interviews, observation, and modelling.

Overview

Systems analysts examine existing procedures and information systems, identify problems and opportunities, and specify solutions that may involve new software, revised business processes, or organisational change. Common tools include data flow diagrams, use case modelling, entity–relationship diagrams, and structured analysis techniques developed in the 1970s by practitioners such as Tom DeMarco and Edward Yourdon.

Approaches

Structured analysis emphasises process and data modelling, while object-oriented analysis and design (OOAD) models a system as interacting objects. Soft systems methodology, developed by Peter Checkland, addresses ill-defined "messy" problem situations in human organisations. Agile methods have shifted analysis toward iterative requirements discovery through close collaboration with users.
