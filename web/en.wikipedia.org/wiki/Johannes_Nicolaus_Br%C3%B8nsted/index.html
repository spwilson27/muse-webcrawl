Johannes Nicolaus Brønsted

Johannes Nicolaus Brønsted
Born: 22 February 1879, Varde, Denmark
Died: 17 December 1947 (aged 68), Copenhagen, Denmark
Known for: Brønsted–Lowry acid–base theory

Johannes Nicolaus Brønsted (22 February 1879 – 17 December 1947) was a Danish physical chemist who, with the English chemist Thomas Martin Lowry, developed the proton theory of acids and bases in 1923, known as the Brønsted–Lowry acid–base theory.

Life and career

Brønsted was born in Varde in Jutland. He studied chemical engineering and chemistry at the University of Copenhagen, earning his doctorate in 1908, and became professor of inorganic and physical chemistry there. His research spanned thermodynamics, solution chemistry, kinetics, and acid–base catalysis; he also founded and directed the university's physico-chemical institute.

Acid–base theory

In the Brønsted–Lowry definition, an acid is a species that donates a proton (hydrogen ion) and a base is a species that accepts a proton. Acid–base reactions are therefore proton-transfer reactions, and every acid has a corresponding conjugate base. This framework, published independently by Brønsted and Lowry in 1923, extended acid–base chemistry beyond aqueous solutions and remains a standard model taught in chemistry. Brønsted also developed a quantitative relationship between acid strength and catalytic activity, known as the Brønsted catalysis law.

Later life

Brønsted was known for strong public engagement; he opposed Nazism during the German occupation of Denmark and was elected to the Danish parliament, the Folketing, in 1947, but died shortly afterwards, in December of that year.
