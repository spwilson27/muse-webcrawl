Muqarnas (Arabic: مقرنص; Persian: مقرنس) is a form of ornamented vaulting in Islamic architecture, sometimes called "stalactite vaulting" or "honeycomb vaulting." It is composed of tiers of small niche-like cells stacked in geometric arrays, mediating between surfaces of different shapes — for instance, from a square chamber to a round dome — and dissolving solid structure into a shimmering, crystalline surface.

Structure and geometry

A muqarnas composition is built up from a two-dimensional geometric plan projected into three tiers of cells. Each cell — variously shaped as small niches, brackets or "stalactites" — supports the tier above. Medieval Islamic mathematicians worked out the geometry rigorously: the Persian mathematician Jamshid al-Kashi (d. 1429) devoted a section of his Key to Arithmetic to measuring muqarnas surfaces, and pattern scrolls such as the Topkapı Scroll preserve workshop designs. Modern scholars note that muqarnas plans anticipate aspects of fractal and tessellation geometry.

History and spread

The form emerged in the 10th–11th centuries, with early examples debated between northeastern Iran/Central Asia and Mesopotamia — fragments survive from Nishapur and the shrine architecture of Iraq. From there muqarnas spread throughout the Islamic world: to Seljuk Anatolia, Fatimid and Mamluk Egypt, Syria, the Maghreb and al-Andalus, where the Alhambra's Hall of the Two Sisters and Hall of the Abencerrajes display domes of thousands of stucco cells. In Iran, glazed-tile muqarnas adorn the portals (pishtaq) and iwan vaults of Timurid and Safavid mosques. The device also passed into Norman Sicily (the painted wooden muqarnas ceiling of the Cappella Palatina in Palermo, executed by Fatimid craftsmen) and later into Armenian and Gothic ornament.

Symbolism

Interpretations vary: muqarnas domes have been read as images of the celestial vault and the atomised, divine light described in the Qur'an's Light Verse, and as representations of the cave — stalactites recalling the cave of the Prophet's first revelation. Practically, muqarnas also marks transitions and acoustically diffuses sound.
