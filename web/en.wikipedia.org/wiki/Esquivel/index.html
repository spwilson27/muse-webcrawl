Esquivel

Esquivel most commonly refers to Juan García Esquivel (1918–2002), the Mexican bandleader, pianist, and composer known for his innovative orchestral arrangements and regarded as a pioneer of "space-age pop" and lounge music. Esquivel is also a Spanish surname borne by a number of other notable people.

Juan García Esquivel

Juan García Esquivel was born in Tampico, Tamaulipas, Mexico, on January 20, 1918. A self-taught pianist, he led a popular orchestra on Mexican radio in the 1940s and became musical director for a long-running comedy programme. In 1958 he moved to the United States, signed with RCA Victor, and recorded a series of albums in Hollywood, including Other Worlds, Other Sounds (1958), Exploring New Sounds in Stereo (1958), and Infinity in Sound (1960), the last of which won a Grammy Award.

Esquivel's arrangements made flamboyant use of stereo separation, wordless vocals (with signature interjections such as "zu-zu" and "pow!"), unusual instrumentation including the ondes Martenot and Chinese bells, and abrupt shifts in tempo and dynamics. His music fell out of fashion in the late 1960s, but he continued working in Mexico, notably composing and performing the theme and music for the children's television show Odisea Burbujas. Esquivel's work was rediscovered during the 1990s lounge revival, with reissue compilations introducing him to a new audience. He died in Jiutepec, Morelos, on January 3, 2002.

Other people

  • Laura Esquivel (born 1950), Mexican novelist, author of Like Water for Chocolate.
  • Adolfo Pérez Esquivel (born 1931), Argentine human rights activist and Nobel Peace Prize laureate.
  • Manuel Esquivel (1940–2022), Belizean politician and two-time prime minister of Belize.
  • Pedro de Esquivel, sixteenth-century Spanish cartographer and mathematician.
