Euro banknotes

Euro banknotes are the banknotes of the euro, the currency of the eurozone. They have been in circulation since 1 January 2002, when they replaced the national currencies of the participating European Union member states. Banknotes are issued by the European Central Bank and the national central banks of the Eurosystem, which together are responsible for their integrity and authenticity.

Denominations

The first series comprised seven denominations: €5, €10, €20, €50, €100, €200 and €500. The Europa series, introduced from 2013 onwards, replaced the denominations one by one with improved security features, but the issuance of the €500 note was discontinued, and it was not replaced in the second series.

Design

The notes were designed by Austrian artist Robert Kalina. The obverse of each note shows windows or gateways, symbolising openness, while the reverse shows bridges, symbolising cooperation and communication. The buildings are generic examples of the architectural styles of successive periods of European history, deliberately not depicting real structures so as not to favour any country. The Europa series features a portrait of Europa, a figure from Greek mythology, in the watermark and hologram.

Security features

Euro banknotes incorporate numerous security features including watermarks, holograms, colour-changing ink, raised print, microprinting, ultraviolet features, and see-through registers. The Europa series added a portrait window in the hologram and an emerald number with a colour-shift effect.
