Mick Jagger

Mick Jagger
Born: 26 July 1943, Dartford, Kent, England
Occupation: Singer, songwriter, actor
Band: The Rolling Stones

Sir Michael Philip Jagger (born 26 July 1943) is an English musician, singer and songwriter who achieved international fame as the lead vocalist and one of the founder members of the rock band the Rolling Stones. His songwriting partnership with Keith Richards is among the most successful in rock history, producing classics such as "(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction", "Jumpin' Jack Flash", "Sympathy for the Devil" and "Brown Sugar".

Career

Jagger formed the Rolling Stones in London in 1962 with Richards and Brian Jones. The band became a central act of the British Invasion and one of the longest-running and most successful groups in popular music, continuing to tour and record into the 2020s; their album Hackney Diamonds appeared in 2023. Jagger's energetic, theatrical stage performances — strutting, dancing and prowling the stage — helped define the role of the rock frontman.

Solo and other work

Jagger released several solo albums, beginning with She's the Boss (1985), and scored a UK number-one duet with David Bowie on "Dancing in the Street" (1985). He also acted, notably in Performance (1970) and Ned Kelly (1970), and co-founded the production company Jagged Films. He was knighted for services to popular music in 2003.
