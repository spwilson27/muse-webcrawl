MMRV vaccine

The MMRV vaccine combines the attenuated viruses MMR (measles, mumps, rubella) plus varicella (chickenpox) vaccine. The MMRV vaccine is typically given to children between 1 and 12 years of age. It is sold under the brand names ProQuad (Merck) and Priorix Tetra (GSK).

Use

Combining the four live attenuated vaccines into a single shot reduces the number of injections a child receives. In the United States, health authorities note that many providers and parents prefer the separate MMR and varicella vaccines for the first dose in younger toddlers, because the combined MMRV carries a slightly increased risk of fever and febrile seizures after the first dose in children 12–23 months old, compared with giving MMR and varicella separately at the same visit. For the second dose, and for first doses in older children, MMRV is generally preferred.

Adverse effects

Common reactions include low-grade fever, a mild measles- or chickenpox-like rash, and temporary swelling of glands. Rare serious events include allergic reactions to vaccine components. As with other live vaccines, MMRV is not recommended for people with severely weakened immune systems or for pregnant women.

History

The MMR vaccine (without varicella) was licensed in 1971, combining components developed from the work of Maurice Hilleman at Merck. The varicella component was added in the MMRV formulation licensed in the United States in 2005.
