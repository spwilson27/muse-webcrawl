Norman Jewison

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Norman Jewison, director of In the Heat of the Night and Fiddler on the Roof.

Norman Frederick Jewison (July 21, 1926 – January 20, 2024) was a Canadian film and television director and producer. Over a career spanning more than five decades, he directed films in a wide range of genres, many of them addressing social and political themes. His films received 46 Academy Award nominations, including three for him as Best Director, and won 12 Oscars. In 1999 he received the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences.

Early life and television

Jewison was born in Toronto, Ontario, and served in the Royal Canadian Navy during the Second World War. After the war he worked in London for the BBC and then joined the CBC in Toronto, directing popular television variety programmes. Moving to New York, he directed acclaimed television specials, including shows for Judy Garland, before transitioning to Hollywood features with light comedies such as 40 Pounds of Trouble (1962) and The Thrill of It All (1963).

Film career

His breakthrough came with The Cincinnati Kid (1965) and the Cold War satire The Russians Are Coming, the Russians Are Coming (1966). In 1967 he directed In the Heat of the Night, a crime drama about a Black Philadelphia detective (Sidney Poitier) investigating a murder in a racist Mississippi town; it won the Academy Award for Best Picture. He followed with the stylish caper The Thomas Crown Affair (1968), the hit musicals Fiddler on the Roof (1971) and Jesus Christ Superstar (1973), and the dystopian Rollerball (1975).

Later notable films include F.I.S.T. (1978), ...And Justice for All (1979), A Soldier's Story (1984), the romantic comedy Moonstruck (1987), which won three Academy Awards including Best Actress for Cher, In Country (1989), Other People's Money (1991), The Hurricane (1999), about boxer Rubin Carter, and The Statement (2003). Jewison founded the Canadian Film Centre in Toronto in 1988 to train new generations of Canadian filmmakers. Despite his surname, he was not Jewish; he remarked that the name led many in Hollywood to assume he was, an experience he credited with sharpening his interest in stories about prejudice and outsiders.

Death and honours

Jewison died at his home in Malibu, California, on January 20, 2024, aged 97. He was an Officer and later Companion of the Order of Canada, and has a star on Canada's Walk of Fame as well as the Hollywood Walk of Fame.
