Hapticity

In coordination chemistry, hapticity is the coordination of a ligand to a metal centre via an uninterrupted and contiguous series of atoms. The hapticity of a ligand is described by the Greek letter eta (η) followed by a superscript denoting the number of contiguous atoms bound to the metal.

For example, ferrocene contains two cyclopentadienyl ligands each bound in an η5 fashion, while in benzene complexes the ring commonly binds η6. Ethylene typically binds through both carbons as an η2 ligand.

History and usage

The η-notation was introduced in the 1950s as organometallic chemistry expanded, providing a compact way to describe bonding in sandwich and half-sandwich compounds without implying a specific bond order to each atom.

Changes in hapticity

Ligands can change hapticity during reactions – for example, a cyclopentadienyl ring may "slip" from η5 to η3 to open a coordination site – an important step in many catalytic cycles.
