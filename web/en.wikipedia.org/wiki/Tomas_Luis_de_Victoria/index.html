Tomás Luis de Victoria
Bornc. 1548, Ávila, Spain
Died27 August 1611, Madrid, Spain
OccupationComposer, organist, priest

Tomás Luis de Victoria (sometimes Italianised as da Vittoria; c. 1548 – 27 August 1611) was the most famous Spanish composer of the Renaissance. He was a Catholic priest and an accomplished organist, and one of the great masters of late Renaissance polyphony, alongside Palestrina and Orlando di Lasso. His output consists entirely of sacred vocal music — masses, motets, hymns, psalms, and magnificats — notable for its mystical intensity and expressive use of text painting.

Life

Victoria was born in Ávila, the seventh of eleven children, and sang as a choirboy at Ávila Cathedral. Around 1565 he went to Rome, where he studied at the Collegio Germanico and possibly with Palestrina, whom he succeeded as choirmaster at the Roman Seminary in 1571. He held posts at Roman churches and a college, was ordained priest in 1575, and published many volumes of his works in Rome. In 1587 he returned to Spain as chaplain to the Dowager Empress María, sister of Philip II, at the Monastery of Las Descalzas Reales in Madrid, where he remained as organist and choirmaster until his death in 1611.

Music

Victoria's best-known works include the six-voice Requiem Mass (Missa pro defunctis, 1603) written for the Empress María's funeral — among the most celebrated requiems of the Renaissance — and the Officium Hebdomadae Sanctae (1585), a complete cycle of music for Holy Week containing the famous Tenebrae Responsories. His motets, such as O magnum mysterium and O vos omnes, remain staples of choral repertoire. Unlike many contemporaries he wrote no secular music, and his roughly 20 masses and 44 motets show an austere, fervent Spanish character that distinguishes him from Palestrina's serenity.
