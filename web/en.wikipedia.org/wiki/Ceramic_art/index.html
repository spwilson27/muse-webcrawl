Ceramic art

Ceramic vessels spanning several traditions and periods.

Ceramic art is art made from ceramic materials, including clay. It may take forms including artistic pottery, tableware, tiles, figurines and other sculpture. As one of the plastic arts, ceramic art is a visual art, and it is one of the oldest art forms, with fired clay figurines such as the Venus of Dolní Věstonice dating back some 28,000 years.

While some ceramics are considered fine art, such as studio pottery and porcelain, most are considered decorative, industrial or applied art objects. Ceramics may also be considered artefacts in archaeology. Ceramic art can be made by one person or by a group of people; in a pottery or ceramic factory, a group designs, manufactures and decorates the art ware.

Main types

The principal categories of ceramic ware are earthenware, stoneware and porcelain, distinguished by the composition of the clay body and the temperature at which it is fired. Surface treatments include slip decoration, glazing, underglaze and overglaze painting, and techniques such as sgraffito, majolica, faience, Delftware, raku and salt glazing.

History

Pottery developed independently in many parts of the world. Early centres include China, where porcelain was perfected; the Islamic world, noted for lustreware and tilework; Japan and Korea, with traditions of stoneware and tea ware; the Mediterranean world of Greek painted pottery; and the Indigenous cultures of the Americas, including Pueblo pottery and Mimbres ware. In the modern era, the studio pottery movement, led by figures such as Bernard Leach and Shōji Hamada, re-established the individual potter-artist.
