The Free Trade Party, officially known as the Australian Free Trade and Liberal Association, was an Australian political party active in New South Wales and, after Federation, in the federal parliament between 1887 and 1909. It advocated the abolition of protective tariffs, in opposition to the Protectionist Party.

History

The party originated in the New South Wales colonial elections of 1887, when candidates supporting free trade, led by Henry Parkes, organised against the protectionists. Parkes became Premier on a free trade platform. After the Federation of Australia in 1901, the tariff question became the central dividing line in the first federal parliaments: the Free Trade Party, led federally by George Reid, opposed the Barton government's protective tariffs.

Free Traders held the balance in the early federal parliaments. Reid served as Prime Minister in 1904–1905, heading a Free Trade government. As the tariff question was settled by legislation and the Labor Party grew, the distinction between Free Traders and Protectionists lost its force. In 1906 the party renamed itself the Anti-Socialist Party to reflect the new anti-Labor alignment, and in 1909 it merged with the Protectionists to form the Commonwealth Liberal Party — the "Fusion" — under Alfred Deakin.
