Nob Hill, San Francisco

Nob Hill is a neighborhood of San Francisco, California, centered on one of the city's original “Seven Hills”. It is one of San Francisco's most affluent districts, historically home to the mansions of railroad barons and today noted for its luxury hotels, Grace Cathedral, and cable-car lines.

History

The hill was settled in the 1860s, when its elevation, views, and relative distance from the rowdier waterfront made it fashionable. During the California Gold Rush era and the building of the transcontinental railroad, the so-called “Big Four” of the Central Pacific Railroad—Leland Stanford, Mark Hopkins, Charles Crocker, and Collis Huntington—built palatial mansions there, giving rise to the nickname “Nob Hill” (from “nabob”, a person of conspicuous wealth).

Nearly all the mansions were destroyed in the fires following the 1906 earthquake. A few stone structures survived, including the Flood Mansion, built of brownstone. In their place rose grand apartment houses and hotels such as the Fairmont, the Mark Hopkins, and the Huntington, clustered around Huntington Park.

Landmarks

Grace Cathedral, an Episcopal cathedral in French Gothic style, stands on the site of the Crocker mansion; construction began in 1928 and was completed in 1964. The Cable Car Museum is nearby in the Washington-Mason powerhouse, and the California and Powell–Hyde cable car lines cross at the hill's summit. The neighborhood remains one of the most expensive residential areas in the United States.
