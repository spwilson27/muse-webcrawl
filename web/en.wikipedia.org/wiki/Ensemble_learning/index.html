Ensemble learning

Ensemble learning is a machine learning paradigm in which multiple models ("weak learners") are trained and their predictions combined to obtain better predictive performance than any single constituent model. The idea rests on diversity: individual learners make different errors, and aggregation — by averaging, voting or a further learned combiner — cancels many of those errors.

Methods

Bagging (bootstrap aggregating) trains learners on bootstrap resamples of the data and averages them, reducing variance; random forests extend bagging by randomising feature selection in decision trees. Boosting trains learners sequentially, reweighting examples that earlier learners got wrong; AdaBoost and gradient boosting machines (such as XGBoost and LightGBM) are prominent examples and reduce bias as well as variance. Stacking learns a meta-model that combines the outputs of diverse base learners.

Theory

The bias–variance decomposition explains why ensembles help: bagging chiefly attacks variance, boosting chiefly bias. Condorcet's jury theorem gives an early intuition — if each voter is slightly better than chance and votes independently, the majority is very likely correct — although real learners' errors are correlated, which limits gains.

Applications

Ensembles dominate tabular-data competitions and are used in ranking, fraud detection, bioinformatics and weather forecasting, where ensemble numerical weather prediction runs a model many times with perturbed initial conditions to estimate forecast uncertainty.
