Seneca Nation of Indians

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Seneca Nation of Indians is a federally recognized Seneca tribe based in western New York. The Seneca are one of the six nations of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy): the Seneca, Cayuga, Oneida, Onondaga, Mohawk, and (from 1722) Tuscarora. In their own language the Seneca are Onöndowa'ga:', "People of the Great Hill"; as the westernmost nation they were traditionally the "Keepers of the Western Door" of the confederacy.

Territory

The Nation holds three territories in western New York: the Allegany Territory along the Allegheny River (including the city of Salamanca, which sits within it on leased land), the Cattaraugus Territory along Lake Erie, and the small Oil Springs Territory. A fourth, the Tonawanda Reservation, is governed separately by the Tonawanda Band of Seneca.

History

Historically allied with the British during the American Revolution, the Seneca lost much of their land after the war through treaties such as Canandaigua (1794) and Big Tree (1797). In the 1960s the construction of the Kinzua Dam flooded about 10,000 acres of the Allegany Territory, displacing hundreds of families despite treaty guarantees — an event memorialised in Johnny Cash's song "As Long as the Grass Shall Grow".

Government

In 1848 the Nation adopted an elected constitutional government, replacing leadership by hereditary chiefs, with a president, treasurer, clerk, and a sixteen-member tribal council. The Nation operates the Seneca Gaming Corporation, running casinos in Niagara Falls, Salamanca, and Buffalo, and has been involved in notable legal disputes over the New York State Thruway easement across the Cattaraugus Territory and over cigarette taxation.
