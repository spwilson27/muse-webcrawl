Runestone

A runestone is a raised stone bearing a runic inscription, typically erected as a memorial. The tradition flourished in Scandinavia during the Viking Age, roughly from the late tenth to the early twelfth century, though runestones were also raised earlier and in regions settled or visited by Norse people.

Distribution

Thousands of runestones survive. Sweden has the greatest number, especially the province of Uppland, followed by Denmark and Norway. Stones also stand in the British Isles, the Isle of Man, and elsewhere in the Norse diaspora. The densest concentration of the earliest stones is in Denmark, including the famous Jelling stones raised by King Harald Bluetooth, one of which proclaims his conversion of the Danes to Christianity.

Inscriptions

Most inscriptions are memorial formulae: "X raised this stone in memory of Y," often with added details about the deceased's travels, death abroad, bridge-building, or Christian prayers. The runes are usually carved within one or more serpent- or dragon-shaped bands, and many stones were originally painted in bright colours. Styles of carving are used by scholars to date the stones.
