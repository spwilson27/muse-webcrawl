Ithaca Hours

The Ithaca Hour is a local currency formerly used in Ithaca, New York, and one of the longest-running local currency systems in the United States. One Hour was valued at ten US dollars, pegged to the average hourly wage in Tompkins County, and was issued in denominations from one-eighth of an Hour to two Hours.

History

The system was launched in November 1991 by community activist Paul Glover. Participants listed goods and services in the system's newspaper, Ithaca Money, and hundreds of local businesses — including the Alternatives Federal Credit Union, which handled accounts in Hours — accepted the notes. At its peak, millions of dollars' worth of Hours had been issued.

Design and decline

The colourful notes featured local scenes such as Ithaca Falls and were printed with anti-counterfeiting serial numbers. Because Hours could not circulate electronically, use declined through the 2000s and 2010s as cashless payment spread; the system is considered a landmark in the modern local-currency and LETS movements.
