Inti Raymi

Inti Raymi, the Festival of the Sun, was the most important religious ceremony of the Inca Empire, held in honour of the sun god Inti at the winter solstice. Today a theatrical re-enactment is held every year on 24 June at the fortress of Sacsayhuamán near Cusco, Peru.

History

According to Inca tradition, the festival was instituted by the emperor Pachacuti in the 15th century. It lasted nine days and involved sacrifices, dances and feasting to thank Inti and ensure his return as the days grew longer. The Spanish colonial authorities banned the ceremony in 1572.

Modern revival

The festival was revived in 1944 by Faustino Espinoza Navarro, based on chronicles of the colonial period. The modern celebration draws thousands of spectators and is one of Peru's largest cultural events.
