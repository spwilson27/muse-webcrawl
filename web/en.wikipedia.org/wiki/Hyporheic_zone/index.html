Hyporheic zone

The hyporheic zone is the region of sediment and porous space beneath and alongside a stream bed, where there is mixing of shallow groundwater and surface water. It is the saturated interstitial zone below the streambed and extending laterally into the banks in which surface water and groundwater exchange (hyporheic exchange). The name derives from the Greek hypo (under) and rheos (flow).

Downwelling and upwelling flows drive exchange between the stream and its bed sediments.

Ecological importance

The hyporheic zone is a biologically active ecotone. It hosts a distinctive invertebrate fauna (the hyporheos) and microbial biofilms that process dissolved nutrients and organic matter, making it important for nutrient spiralling, nitrogen removal by denitrification, and the overall metabolism of the stream. Many fish, including salmonids, bury their eggs (redds) in gravels where hyporheic flow supplies oxygen; invertebrates use the zone as a refuge during floods and droughts.

Hydrology

Exchange is driven by pressure differences over bedforms such as riffles and dunes, by meander bends and obstructions, and by groundwater flow paths. Residence times range from minutes to weeks. Because exchange links stream water quality to groundwater quality, the hyporheic zone is a focus of research in hydrology, biogeochemistry and stream restoration.
