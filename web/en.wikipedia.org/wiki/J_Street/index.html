J Street is an American nonprofit liberal advocacy organisation whose stated aim is to promote American leadership to end the Israeli–Palestinian conflict peacefully and diplomatically. Founded in 2008 by Jeremy Ben-Ami, it describes itself as "the political home for pro-Israel, pro-peace Americans".

History

J Street was launched in April 2008, taking its name from the missing "J" street in Washington, D.C.'s alphabetical street grid and, symbolically, from the Jewish community. It was created as a counterweight in Washington debates over Middle East policy, arguing that support for Israel is compatible with support for a two-state solution and active American diplomacy.

Activities

The organisation lobbies Congress and the executive branch, endorses and raises funds for political candidates through its political action committee, JStreetPAC, and runs educational programmes and campus outreach through J Street U. Its positions have included support for the Iran nuclear agreement of 2015 and opposition to settlement expansion, alongside support for American security assistance to Israel.

Reception

J Street has been welcomed by supporters of the two-state solution and criticised by some pro-Israel organisations and commentators who dispute its claim to the "pro-Israel" label. The debate around it reflects wider arguments within American Jewish and political life about the terms of support for Israel.
