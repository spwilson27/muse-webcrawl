The Wicker Man

Directed by: Robin Hardy
Screenplay by: Anthony Shaffer
Starring: Edward Woodward, Christopher Lee, Britt Ekland
Released: 6 December 1973
Genre: Folk horror

The Wicker Man is a 1973 British folk horror film directed by Robin Hardy and written by Anthony Shaffer, loosely based on David Pinner's 1967 novel Ritual. It stars Edward Woodward as Sergeant Neil Howie, a devout Christian police officer who travels to the remote Scottish island of Summerisle to investigate the disappearance of a young girl, and discovers that the islanders, led by Lord Summerisle (Christopher Lee), practise a form of Celtic paganism.

Plot

Howie's enquiries are frustrated by the islanders' evasions and by their cheerful pagan customs, which offend his strict Presbyterian faith. He becomes convinced that the missing girl, Rowan Morrison, is to be sacrificed at the May Day festival to ensure the return of the island's failing apple crops. In the celebrated climax he discovers that he himself — a virgin, a Christian, and a "king" as a representative of the law — has been lured to the island as the intended sacrifice, and is burned to death inside a giant wicker effigy as the islanders sing the folk song "Sumer Is Icumen In".

Production and reception

The film was shot in Dumfries and Galloway in the autumn of 1972, with a score of folk songs arranged by Paul Giovanni and performed by the band Magnet. Studio interference saw the film cut and released as a supporting feature, and much of the excised footage was lost. Christopher Lee considered it among his finest work and promoted it tirelessly. Restored versions (including the 2013 "Final Cut") helped establish the film's reputation as a classic: it is now routinely cited as one of the greatest British horror films and, with Witchfinder General (1968) and Blood on Satan's Claw (1971), a defining work of folk horror. A poorly received American remake appeared in 2006.
