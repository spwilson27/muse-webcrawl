Bullet catch

A magician performs the bullet catch illusion

The bullet catch is a stage magic illusion in which a magician appears to catch a bullet fired directly at them, or to survive being shot, often holding the marked bullet between their teeth, on a plate, or in a handkerchief. It is considered one of the most dangerous tricks in magic, and a number of performers have been killed or injured attempting it.

History

Versions of the trick date back to the 16th century. Early accounts describe performers appearing to catch bullets in their hands or mouths; the English conjurer Philip Astley's contemporaries and later music-hall acts refined the effect. The contrivance typically relies on a blank cartridge, a bullet that is secretly switched or palmed, a wax or frangible projectile, or a gun modified so the bullet never leaves the barrel — but real firearms and live ammunition make any failure potentially fatal.

Fatalities

The most famous death was that of Chung Ling Soo (the American magician William Ellsworth Robinson) in 1918 at the Wood Green Empire in London: his trick gun had not been properly cleaned, a live round discharged, and he was shot on stage, dying the following day. Other performers killed by the trick include Madame DeLinsky (1820), Arnold Buck (1840), and the Black Wizard of the West (1922).

Modern performers

Harry Houdini owned the rights to the trick but never performed it publicly, reportedly discouraged by its dangers. Penn and Teller made a simultaneous double bullet catch a signature finale of their act, each firing at the other and catching the bullet in the mouth, using rigorously checked procedures. Because of its history, many magicians consider performing the bullet catch an unnecessary risk.
