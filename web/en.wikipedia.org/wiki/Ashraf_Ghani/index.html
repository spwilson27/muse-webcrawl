Mohammad Ashraf Ghani Ahmadzai (born 19 May 1949) is an Afghan politician, academic, and economist who served as the fifth president of Afghanistan from September 2014 until August 2021, when he fled the country as the Taliban captured Kabul, ending the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan.

Early life and career

Born in Logar province into a Pashtun Ahmadzai family, Ghani was educated at Habibia High School in Kabul and the American University of Beirut, and earned a PhD in anthropology from Columbia University. He taught at universities including Aarhus, UC Berkeley, and Johns Hopkins, then worked at the World Bank from 1991, becoming a prominent specialist on state-building and institutional reform. With Clare Lockhart he co-authored Fixing Failed States (2008).

Return to Afghanistan

After the fall of the Taliban in 2001, Ghani returned as a senior adviser to the UN and then as Afghanistan's Minister of Finance (2002–2004), where he introduced currency and budgetary reforms. He served as chancellor of Kabul University and founded the Institute for State Effectiveness. He ran unsuccessfully in the 2009 presidential election.

Presidency

Ghani won the disputed 2014 election and took office under a U.S.-brokered National Unity Government with his rival Abdullah Abdullah as Chief Executive. He pursued anti-corruption measures, regional connectivity, and peace negotiations, and was re-elected in the disputed 2019 election, with a parallel inauguration held by Abdullah in March 2020. Following the February 2020 Doha Agreement between the United States and the Taliban and the withdrawal of U.S. forces in 2021, the Taliban launched a rapid offensive. On 15 August 2021 Ghani left Kabul by air — eventually settling in the United Arab Emirates — as Taliban fighters entered the capital, a departure widely criticized within Afghanistan; he stated he left to prevent bloodshed. He later apologized to Afghans and denied reports that he took large sums of state money.
