Tripoint

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A tripoint, trijunction, triple point, or tri-border area is a geographical point at which the boundaries of three countries or subnational entities meet. There are approximately 176 international tripoints in the world, depending on how disputed areas and partially recognised states are counted. Tripoints are often marked by pillars, obelisks or other monuments and are popular destinations for geography enthusiasts.

Notable tripoints

The tripoint of Austria, Italy and Switzerland near the Piz Lad; the Vaalserberg, where Belgium, Germany and the Netherlands meet and which is the highest point of the European Netherlands; and the "Three Countries Bridge" area near Basel where France, Germany and Switzerland meet at the Dreiländereck. In Africa, the Kazungula area on the Zambezi comes close to being one of the world's only near-quadripoints, with Botswana, Namibia, Zambia and Zimbabwe meeting within a very short stretch of river.

In the Americas, the Triple Frontier of Argentina, Brazil and Paraguay at the confluence of the Iguazu and Paraná rivers is a major tourist area, as is the nearby Iguazu Falls. In Asia, the Tumen River tripoint of China, North Korea and Russia, and the Wakhan Corridor region near the tripoint of Afghanistan, China and Pakistan, are of geopolitical note.

Subnational tripoints

Tripoints also occur within countries between states, provinces or counties. In the United States, the Four Corners is a quadripoint of Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico and Utah. Subnational tripoints are frequently marked by survey markers and are catalogued by confluence and boundary-visiting hobbyists.

Maritime tripoints

Tripoints can also lie in water, where the exclusive economic zones or territorial seas of three states meet; such points are often defined in bilateral treaties even when the third junction is only implied.
