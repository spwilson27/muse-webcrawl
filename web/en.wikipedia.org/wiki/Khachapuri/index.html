Khachapuri

Khachapuri
TypeCheese-filled bread
Place of originGeorgia
Main ingredientsYeast dough, cheese (sulguni, imeruli), egg, butter
VariationsImeruli, megruli, adjaruli, penovani

Khachapuri (Georgian: ხაჭაპური, from khacho, "curd," and puri, "bread") is a traditional Georgian dish of cheese-filled bread, widely regarded as the national dish of Georgia. The bread is leavened and shaped in various forms, filled with cheese, and often topped with butter and egg. In 2019 the tradition of khachapuri was inscribed on Georgia's list of intangible cultural heritage.

Regional varieties

  • Imeruli khachapuri, from Imereti, is a round flat pie with sliced imeruli cheese sealed inside the dough.
  • Megruli khachapuri, from Samegrelo, is similar but topped with extra melted sulguni cheese.
  • Adjaruli (or acharuli) khachapuri, from Adjara, is the famous boat-shaped version: an open-faced dough boat filled with a molten mixture of sulguni and imeruli cheese, finished with a knob of butter and a raw egg yolk in the centre. The diner mixes the yolk and butter into the cheese and tears off pieces of the crust to dip.
  • Penovani khachapuri is made with puff pastry and baked in individual squares.
  • Guruli khachapuri, a half-moon from Guria, adds chopped boiled egg to the filling and is traditionally baked for Christmas.

Cultural significance

Khachapuri is ubiquitous in Georgia, served in restaurants, bakeries, and homes, and has spread throughout the former Soviet Union and beyond. Economists at Tbilisi State University devised the tongue-in-cheek "Khachapuri Index," tracking the cost of the dish's ingredients across Georgian cities as an informal measure of inflation, analogous to the Big Mac index.
