Flare

A flare, also sometimes called a fusee, is a type of pyrotechnic that produces a bright light or intense heat without an explosion. Flares are used for distress signalling, illumination, or as defensive countermeasures in civilian and military applications.

Types and uses

Handheld and aerial flares are standard maritime and aviation distress signals; a red flare is an internationally recognised sign of distress at sea. Signal flares may be launched from a flare gun (such as the Very pistol, invented by Edward Wilson Very) and descend slowly under a small parachute, illuminating a wide area.

Road flares are used by police and motorists to warn of hazards or mark accident scenes. Decoy flares are dispensed by military aircraft and ships to seduce heat-seeking missiles away from their target. Flares are also burned off at industrial plants in gas flaring, where excess flammable gas is ignited at a flare stack.

Composition

A typical flare composition contains an oxidiser such as potassium nitrate or strontium nitrate, a fuel, and a binder. Strontium compounds give the characteristic red colour of distress flares, barium compounds produce green, and sodium compounds yellow. The intense light of illumination flares is often produced by burning magnesium.

Safety

Because they burn at very high temperatures, flares present a fire risk and can cause severe burns. Expired marine flares require careful disposal, as they remain hazardous and are difficult to extinguish.
