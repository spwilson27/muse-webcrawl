Substance

Substance is a key concept in philosophy, particularly in metaphysics and ontology, referring to the fundamental reality that underlies phenomena — that which exists in itself and does not depend on anything else for its existence. The term derives from the Latin substantia, meaning "that which stands under", itself a translation of the Greek ousia and related to Aristotle's notion of the hypokeimenon, the underlying subject of properties.

History

Aristotle developed the earliest systematic theory of substance in the Categories and the Metaphysics. He distinguished primary substance — individual things such as a particular man or horse — from secondary substances, the species and genera to which individuals belong. Primary substances are the subjects of predication: everything else is either said of them or present in them, as accidents inhering in a substance.

In medieval scholasticism, substance was contrasted with accident: the accidents (colour, size, position) change while the substance persists. This framework underpinned the doctrine of transubstantiation in Catholic theology, in which the substance of bread and wine is held to change while their accidents remain.

Early modern theories

René Descartes defined substance as that which requires nothing but itself (and God's concurrence) to exist, and divided created substance into two kinds: res extensa (extended, material substance) and res cogitans (thinking substance). Baruch Spinoza radicalised the definition: since only one thing can truly exist independently, there is only one substance — God or Nature — of which minds and bodies are modes. John Locke treated substance more sceptically, calling it the "supposed, but unknown, support" of qualities, a "something, I know not what". David Hume went further, arguing that we have no impression, and hence no idea, of substance at all.

In chemistry

In chemistry, a substance is a form of matter with a definite, constant composition and distinct properties. Pure substances divide into elements, composed of a single type of atom, and compounds, composed of two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportions. The concept is central to chemical nomenclature and to the classification of mixtures versus pure materials.
