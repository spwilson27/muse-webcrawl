The British Rail Class 801 Super Express is a class of high-speed electric multiple unit built by Hitachi Rail for London North Eastern Railway (LNER) under the government's Intercity Express Programme. The trains, marketed by LNER as Azuma, operate on the East Coast Main Line between London King's Cross, Leeds, York, Newcastle, Edinburgh and beyond. The first unit entered service in September 2019.

Design

Unlike the closely related bi-mode Class 800, the Class 801 is an all-electric train, retaining only a single diesel engine per unit for emergency power and depot movements. The fleet comprises 12 five-car Class 801/1 and 30 nine-car Class 801/2 sets. The trains were built at Hitachi's factories in Kasado, Japan, and Newton Aycliffe, County Durham. They replaced LNER's InterCity 125 and InterCity 225 fleets, offering more seats, faster acceleration and reduced journey times.

Service history

Azuma services were progressively extended, including to Aberdeen and Inverness (served by Class 800 units north of Edinburgh's electrified network where needed). In 2021 the fleet, together with the Class 800s, was temporarily withdrawn after cracks were discovered in yaw damper brackets; inspections and repairs allowed a phased return to service. The trains offer standard and first class accommodation, a café bar and the "Let's Eat" at-seat service.
