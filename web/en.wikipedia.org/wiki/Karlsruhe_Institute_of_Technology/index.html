Karlsruhe Institute of Technology

The Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT; German: Karlsruher Institut für Technologie) is a public research university in Karlsruhe, Baden-Württemberg, Germany, and one of the largest research and education institutions in the country. It was created in 2009 by the merger of the University of Karlsruhe (founded 1825) and the Karlsruhe Research Center, uniting a university with a national large-scale research facility.

History

The university began in 1825 as a polytechnic school modelled on the École Polytechnique in Paris, making it one of the oldest technical universities in Germany. Its professors included Heinrich Hertz, who demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves in Karlsruhe in the 1880s, and Fritz Haber, who developed ammonia synthesis there. In 1956 the Karlsruhe Nuclear Research Center was founded north of the city, growing into a major national laboratory. The 2009 merger joined the two into KIT, a unique institution in the German science system.

Research

KIT is a member of the Helmholtz Association and the TU9 alliance of technical universities. Research centres include energy, mobility systems, climate and environment, particle and astroparticle physics (including the KATRIN neutrino experiment), and information technology. The institute hosts large facilities at Campus North, the former research centre site near Eggenstein-Leopoldshafen, while teaching is centred on Campus South in the city.

Academics

KIT has eleven departments covering engineering, natural sciences, economics, humanities and social sciences. It was successful in Germany's Excellence Initiative and retained the title of University of Excellence in 2019. Notable alumni and researchers include Nobel laureates and founders of companies such as SAP, whose co-founders studied in Karlsruhe.
