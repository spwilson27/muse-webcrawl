The Rolls-Royce Trent is a family of high-bypass turbofan aircraft engines produced by Rolls-Royce. It continues the company's three-spool architecture developed with the RB211 and is named, following Rolls-Royce tradition, after a British river — the River Trent. Trent engines power widebody airliners including the Airbus A330, A340, A350 and A380 and the Boeing 777 and 787 Dreamliner.

Design

Trent engines use three concentric shafts (spools) driving the fan, intermediate-pressure and high-pressure compressors independently, allowing each to run near its optimal speed. Later variants introduced hollow wide-chord fan blades, and the Trent XWB uses advanced aerodynamics and materials for improved efficiency. Thrust ratings range from roughly 53,000 lbf for early models to about 97,000 lbf for the Trent XWB-97.

Variants

  • Trent 500 — Airbus A340-500/600.
  • Trent 700 — the first variant, for the Airbus A330.
  • Trent 800 — Boeing 777 (excluding later long-range models).
  • Trent 900 — Airbus A380.
  • Trent 1000 — Boeing 787 Dreamliner.
  • Trent XWB — Airbus A350, among the most efficient large turbofans in service.
  • Trent 7000 — Airbus A330neo.

Service history

The family has accumulated tens of millions of flight hours. Notable incidents include the uncontained failure of a Trent 900 on Qantas Flight 32 in 2010, after which design and manufacturing changes were made, and premature blade cracking and wear issues on the Trent 1000 from 2016 that required inspection campaigns and design fixes. The Trent also underpins marine and industrial derivatives.
