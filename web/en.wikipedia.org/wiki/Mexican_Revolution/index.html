Mexican Revolution

The Mexican Revolution (Spanish: Revolución mexicana) was an extended sequence of armed regional conflicts in Mexico from 20 November 1910 to 1 December 1920. It has been called "the defining event of modern Mexican history". It saw the overthrow of the long dictatorship of Porfirio Díaz, a decade of civil war among revolutionary factions led by figures such as Francisco I. Madero, Emiliano Zapata, Pancho Villa, Venustiano Carranza and Álvaro Obregón, and the promulgation of the Constitution of 1917, one of the most socially progressive constitutions of its era. Historians estimate the war cost between one and two million lives — perhaps a tenth of the population.

Background: the Porfiriato

Porfirio Díaz ruled Mexico from 1876 to 1911, imposing order and foreign investment while concentrating land in haciendas, suppressing dissent, and enriching a small elite. By 1910 the elderly dictator's remark that Mexico was "ready for democracy" encouraged the wealthy landowner Francisco I. Madero to challenge him under the slogan of "effective suffrage, no re-election". Madero's arrest and the fraudulent 1910 election prompted his Plan de San Luis Potosí, calling for an uprising on 20 November 1910.

Course of the revolution

Madero and Huerta (1910–1914)

Revolutionary forces including Pascual Orozco and Pancho Villa in the north and Emiliano Zapata in Morelos forced Díaz into exile in May 1911. Madero's democratic government alienated both conservatives and radicals; he was overthrown and murdered in the Decena Trágica (Ten Tragic Days) of February 1913 by the counter-revolutionary general Victoriano Huerta, with the connivance of the US ambassador.

Constitutionalists and civil war (1914–1920)

Venustiano Carranza's Constitutionalist Army, with generals Obregón and Villa, drove Huerta from power in 1914, but the victorious coalition split: the Convention of Aguascalientes pitted Carranza against Villa and Zapata. Obregón's victory over Villa at the Battle of Celaya (1915) decided the struggle in the Constitutionalists' favour. A constituent congress at Querétaro produced the Constitution of 1917, with landmark articles on land reform (Article 27), labour rights (Article 123) and restrictions on the Church. Zapata was assassinated in 1919 and Carranza in 1920, after which Obregón became president, marking the end of the main armed phase.

Foreign involvement

The United States occupied Veracruz in 1914, sent the Punitive Expedition after Villa's raid on Columbus, New Mexico, in 1916, and was drawn deeper into the crisis by the Zimmermann Telegram of 1917, in which Germany proposed a Mexican alliance against the US.

Legacy

The revolution institutionalised itself in the party founded in 1929 that, as the PRI, governed Mexico until 2000. Its land reforms created the ejido system; its cultural project produced muralism (Rivera, Orozco, Siqueiros); and its social ideals remained the official ideology of the Mexican state for the rest of the century.
