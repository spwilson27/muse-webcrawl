The 1952 United States presidential election was the 42nd quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 4, 1952. Republican Dwight D. Eisenhower, the former Supreme Commander of Allied forces in Europe, defeated Democrat Adlai Stevenson, the governor of Illinois, in a landslide, ending twenty years of Democratic control of the White House.

Background and nominations

President Harry S. Truman, eligible to run again, withdrew after losing the New Hampshire primary to Senator Estes Kefauver. The Democratic convention in Chicago drafted Governor Adlai Stevenson, who accepted despite professed reluctance. On the Republican side, a hard-fought contest between Senator Robert A. Taft, "Mr. Republican," and General Eisenhower — backed by the party's internationalist eastern wing — ended with Eisenhower's first-ballot nomination; he selected young California senator Richard Nixon as his running mate.

Campaign

Eisenhower campaigned on the slogan "I Like Ike," pledged to end the Korean War ("I shall go to Korea"), and attacked the Truman administration over corruption and communism in the formula "K1C2" — Korea, Communism, and Corruption. The campaign was an early showcase for television advertising, including Eisenhower's short "Eisenhower Answers America" spots. Nixon survived a slush-fund controversy with his televised "Checkers speech," a landmark of political broadcasting. Stevenson was praised for eloquent speeches but could not overcome the desire for change.

Results

Eisenhower won 55.2% of the popular vote and 442 electoral votes to Stevenson's 89, carrying 39 of 48 states including much of the traditionally Democratic South's outer tier (Virginia, Florida, Texas, Tennessee). Republicans also narrowly captured both houses of Congress. The election began a pattern of Republican presidential dominance interrupted only by the Johnson landslide of 1964.
