Ruck

A ruck in rugby union

A ruck is a phase of play in rugby union and rugby league in which one or more players from each team, who are on their feet and in physical contact, close around the ball when it is on the ground between them. The ruck is the principal contest for possession at the tackle area.

Formation in rugby union

A ruck forms after a tackle when the ball carrier is brought to ground and at least one player from each side binds onto each other over the ball. Players joining must bind with at least one arm around a team-mate, enter through the "gate" behind the hindmost foot, and stay on their feet. Once a ruck is formed, players may not handle the ball; they drive the opposition away and heel the ball back with their feet, after which the scrum-half typically passes it away.

Laws and offences

Offences at the ruck include collapsing it, jumping on top of it, using hands (unless the player was the tackler or ball carrier in the initial contest), and being offside ahead of the hindmost foot. Infringements are penalised with a penalty kick or a scrum. In rugby league the term survives in "play-the-ball" contests, but the formal union-style ruck does not exist in the same form.
