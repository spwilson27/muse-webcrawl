The Silence of the Lambs is a 1991 American psychological horror thriller film directed by Jonathan Demme, adapted by Ted Tally from Thomas Harris's 1988 novel. Jodie Foster stars as Clarice Starling, a young FBI trainee who seeks the help of the imprisoned, cannibalistic serial killer Dr. Hannibal Lecter (Anthony Hopkins) to catch another killer, "Buffalo Bill" (Ted Levine), who skins his female victims.

Plot

Starling interviews Lecter at a Baltimore asylum in a tense exchange of "quid pro quo" confidences: in return for insights into Buffalo Bill, Lecter probes Starling's childhood trauma — her futile attempt to save lambs from slaughter on a ranch. Lecter escapes custody in Memphis, but his clues lead Starling to Buffalo Bill's house, where she kills him and rescues his captive. Lecter, now free, telephones her from the Bahamas and departs to pursue his old tormentor, Dr. Chilton: "I'm having an old friend for dinner."

Reception and awards

The film was a critical and commercial success and became only the third film to win Academy Awards in all five top categories — Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor (Hopkins, in a performance of barely sixteen minutes of screen time), Best Actress (Foster) and Best Adapted Screenplay. It is regularly ranked among the greatest films ever made, and in 2011 it was selected for the US National Film Registry. Sequels and prequels include Hannibal (2001), Red Dragon (2002) and Hannibal Rising (2007), and the television series Hannibal (2013–2015) and Clarice (2021).
