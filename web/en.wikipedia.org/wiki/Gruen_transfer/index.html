Gruen transfer

The Gruen transfer (also known as the Gruen effect) is the moment when consumers enter a shopping mall or store and, surrounded by an intentionally confusing, immersive layout, lose track of their original intentions and become more susceptible to impulse purchases.

The phenomenon is named after Victor Gruen, the Austrian-born architect who designed the first enclosed shopping malls in the United States, including Southdale Center in Minnesota (1956). Gruen had envisioned malls as community centres with civic and cultural functions, and he later disavowed the purely commercial, disorienting designs that came to bear his name.

Design techniques associated with the effect include windowless interiors, uniform bright lighting, the concealment of exits, consistent flooring, and the placement of unrelated goods together, all of which slow shoppers down and blur the sense of time and place.
