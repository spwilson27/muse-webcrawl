Robert Adams (photographer)


Born: May 8, 1937, Orange, New Jersey, U.S.
Occupation: Photographer
Known for: The New West (1974)

Robert Adams (born May 8, 1937) is an American photographer who has focused on the changing landscape of the American West. His work was prominent in the New Topographics exhibition of 1975, which redefined landscape photography with a detached, plain-spoken view of human-altered terrain.

Life and work

Adams was born in Orange, New Jersey, and raised in Colorado. He studied English literature, earning a PhD from the University of Southern California, and taught at Colorado College before turning to photography full-time. His books — including The New West (1974), Denver (1977), From the Missouri West (1980), Summer Nights (1985) and Turning Back (2005) — document suburban sprawl, clear-cut forests and the fragile beauty of the western landscape in spare black-and-white images.

Recognition

Adams has received two Guggenheim Fellowships and a MacArthur Fellowship (1994). In 2009 he won the Hasselblad Award. He is also a noted essayist on photography, author of Beauty in Photography (1981) and Why People Photograph (1994).
