Salzkammergut

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Salzkammergut]
Hallstätter See and Hallstatt

The Salzkammergut (Austrian German pronunciation: [ˈsaltskamɐɡuːt]) is a resort area of notable lakes and alpine mountains in Austria, stretching from the city of Salzburg eastwards along the Austrian Bundesländer of Upper Austria and Styria to the peaks of the Dachstein range. The name means "Salt Chamber Estate," deriving from the Imperial Salt Chamber, the authority charged with running the precious salt mines of the Habsburg Monarchy. The Hallstatt–Dachstein/Salzkammergut Cultural Landscape was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997.

Geography

The region contains 76 lakes, the largest being the Attersee, Mondsee, Traunsee, Wolfgangsee, and Hallstätter See, set among the Salzkammergut Mountains of the Northern Limestone Alps. Rivers such as the Traun drain the district. Principal towns include Bad Ischl, the spa town where Emperor Franz Joseph I kept his summer residence, the Kaiservilla; Gmunden on the Traunsee; St. Wolfgang; and Hallstatt, the picturesque lakeside village whose name gives the Hallstatt culture of the early Iron Age and whose salt mine is among the oldest known in the world, worked since prehistoric times.

History and tourism

Salt mining at Hallstatt and Bad Ischl and the salt works of Ebensee underpinned the region's prosperity for millennia, financing the salt trade along the Traun. In the 19th century the Salzkammergut became the fashionable summer retreat (Sommerfrische) of the Viennese court and bourgeoisie, attracting artists and composers. Today it is one of Austria's premier tourist regions, known for hiking, skiing, lake bathing, and the Wolfgangsee setting of the operetta The White Horse Inn. Hallstatt's popularity has been so great that a full-scale replica was built in Guangdong, China, in 2012, and the village has introduced measures to manage overtourism.
