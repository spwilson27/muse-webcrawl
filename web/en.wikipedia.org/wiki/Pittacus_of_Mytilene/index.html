Pittacus of Mytilene (c. 640 – c. 568 BC; Greek: Πιττακὸς ὁ Μυτιληναῖος) was an ancient Mytilenaen military general and one of the Seven Sages of Greece.

Life

Pittacus rose to prominence as a commander in Mytilene's war against Athens over Sigeum in the Troad, where he is said to have defeated the Athenian commander Phrynon in single combat — according to one account, by entangling him in a concealed net. Around 590 BC the citizens of Mytilene elected him aisymnetes, a ruler with dictatorial powers charged with ending the factional strife between the aristocracy and the common people.

He governed for ten years, reformed the laws, and then voluntarily resigned his office. Among his laws was one doubling the penalty for offences committed while drunk, on the reasoning that a drunken offender bore double guilt.

Sayings

Pittacus was famed for terse, pithy maxims in the manner of the other sages. His most famous saying was "Know thine opportunity" (gnōthi kairon). Other sayings attributed to him include "Forgiveness is better than revenge", "Whatever you do, do it well", and "Hard is it to be truly good" — the last famously examined by Plato in the Protagoras. He was a contemporary of the poets Alcaeus, who attacked him in verse, and Sappho, both fellow Lesbians.
