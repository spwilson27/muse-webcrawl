Lafonia

Lafonia is a peninsula in the southern part of East Falkland, the largest of the Falkland Islands. It is separated from the northern part of East Falkland by the Choiseul Sound and connected to it by a narrow isthmus at Darwin and Goose Green. The terrain is low-lying and gently undulating, in contrast to the hillier north of the island, and forms part of the region islanders call "the Camp."

Name and history

The peninsula takes its name from Samuel Fisher Lafone, a Montevideo-based merchant who in 1846 obtained a grant from the British government to develop the wild cattle and resources of the southern part of East Falkland. His venture, later taken over by the Falkland Islands Company, established sheep farming at settlements such as Darwin. Lafonia became the heartland of the Falkland Islands Company's sheep stations, and its principal settlement, Goose Green, grew around the company's farm and shearing sheds.

Geography and settlements

Lafonia's principal settlements are Goose Green, Darwin, North Arm, and Walker Creek, together with scattered farm settlements. The area is characterised by whitegrass plains, diddle-dee heath, and numerous ponds and inlets including Brenton Loch and Bertha's Beach, a Ramsar-listed wetland important for migratory birds. The narrow isthmus carries Bodie Creek Bridge, one of the southernmost suspension bridges in the world, built in 1925 to move sheep across the inlet.

Falklands War

During the Falklands War of 1982, Argentine forces occupied Goose Green and Darwin; the Battle of Goose Green (28–29 May 1982), fought at the isthmus, was the first major land battle of the war and ended with the surrender of the Argentine garrison.
