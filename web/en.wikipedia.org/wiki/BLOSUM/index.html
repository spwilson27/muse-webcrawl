BLOSUM

In bioinformatics, the BLOSUM (BLOcks SUbstitution Matrix) matrix is a substitution matrix used for sequence alignment of proteins. BLOSUM matrices are used to score alignments between evolutionarily divergent protein sequences. They are based on local alignments and were first described by Steven Henikoff and Jorja Henikoff in 1992.

Construction

The matrices were derived from the BLOCKS database of conserved protein sequence motifs. Sequences within each block were clustered at a chosen percentage identity threshold, and the observed frequencies of amino-acid substitutions between clusters were converted into log-odds scores. The number in the matrix name indicates the clustering threshold: BLOSUM62 was built from sequences clustered at 62% identity, while BLOSUM80 and BLOSUM45 use 80% and 45% respectively.

Usage

Higher-numbered BLOSUM matrices are suited to aligning closely related sequences, while lower-numbered matrices perform better for divergent sequences. BLOSUM62 in particular has proved effective across a wide range of alignment problems and is the default scoring matrix used by the BLAST search tools. BLOSUM matrices are generally considered more effective than the older PAM matrices for detecting distant protein relationships.
