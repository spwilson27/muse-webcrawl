Measure problem (cosmology)

In eternal inflation, pocket universes form endlessly, making probability comparisons ill-defined

The measure problem in cosmology concerns how to assign probabilities to different outcomes in theories that predict an infinite universe or multiverse, most prominently eternal inflation. In such theories, every event that can happen occurs infinitely many times, so naive comparisons of "how often" something happens involve ratios of infinities and depend on the procedure used to regulate, or "cut off", the infinities. Different cutoff procedures — different measures — can give wildly different predictions, and there is no consensus on which measure is correct.

Background

In eternal inflation, most of space inflates forever, with "pocket universes" continually budding off in which inflation ends locally. Because the inflating volume grows without bound, the number of pocket universes of every type is infinite. To predict, for example, the expected value of the cosmological constant or the probability that observers find themselves in a universe like ours, one must compare the numbers of events of different kinds. The standard approach applies a cutoff — for instance, counting only events before some time — and then takes the limit as the cutoff is removed. The result depends on how time is measured and how the cutoff is imposed.

Proposed measures

Many measures have been proposed, including proper-time cutoffs, scale-factor cutoffs, light-cone time cutoffs, and causal patch measures. The proper-time cutoff notoriously produces the youngness paradox: because inflating volume grows exponentially with proper time, the measure predicts that observers should overwhelmingly find themselves in universes that thermalised as recently as possible, in conflict with the observed age of our universe. Scale-factor-based measures avoid the youngness paradox but may face other difficulties, such as the Boltzmann brain problem, in which disembodied fluctuating observers would outnumber ordinary observers.

Significance

The measure problem is regarded as one of the central open problems in theoretical cosmology, because without a justified measure, multiverse theories struggle to make testable predictions. Related difficulties include the Boltzmann brain paradox and questions about typicality — whether we should assume we are typical observers.
