Leicester City F.C.

Leicester City Football Club is a professional football club based in Leicester, England. Nicknamed the Foxes, the club was founded in 1884 as Leicester Fosse and adopted its present name in 1919. It plays at the King Power Stadium, its home since 2002.

The 2015–16 title

Leicester achieved worldwide fame in the 2015–16 season, when, having narrowly escaped relegation the year before and rated 5000–1 outsiders by bookmakers, the team managed by Claudio Ranieri won the Premier League title. The triumph, built around Jamie Vardy, Riyad Mahrez and N'Golo Kanté, is widely regarded as one of the greatest sporting upsets of all time.

Other honours

The club won its first FA Cup in 2021, beating Chelsea 1–0 under Brendan Rodgers, and has won the League Cup three times (1964, 1997, 2000). Owned by Thailand's King Power group, the club was chaired by Vichai Srivaddhanaprabha until his death in a helicopter crash outside the stadium in October 2018, after which his son Aiyawatt succeeded him. Leicester were relegated in 2023 but returned to the Premier League as Championship winners in 2024.
