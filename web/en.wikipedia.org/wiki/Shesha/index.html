Shesha

Shesha (Sanskrit: शेष, romanized: Śeṣa), also known by his epithets Sheshanaga (Sanskrit: शेषनाग, romanized: Śeṣanāga) and Adishesha (Sanskrit: आदिशेष, romanized: Ādiśeṣa, lit. "first Shesha"), is a serpent deity (naga) and the Nagaraja (king of all serpents) in Hinduism. He is considered to be a primordial being in the universe, the younger brother of the serpent Vasuki, and is intimately associated with the god Vishnu.

Mythology

Shesha is described as a massive multi-headed serpent who floats coiled in the ocean of space, or the Ocean of Milk (Kshira Sagara), upon which lies Vishnu. He is said to have five, seven, or a thousand hoods, each bearing a jewel that lights up the netherworld. When Shesha uncoils, time moves forward and creation takes place; when he coils back, the universe ceases to exist. He is thus closely identified with time itself, and his name derives from the Sanskrit root meaning "that which remains" — for even when the universe is destroyed at the end of the kalpa, Shesha remains.

According to the Mahabharata, Shesha was born to the sage Kashyapa and his wife Kadru, who mothered a thousand serpents. Disgusted by the cruel behaviour of his brothers toward their half-brother Garuda, Shesha performed severe penance on Mount Gandhamadana and in other holy places until Brahma, pleased with his austerities, granted him the boon of steadiness and control of his mind. At Brahma's request, Shesha descended into a chasm beneath the earth and stabilised it upon his hood, where he continues to support the world.

Association with Vishnu

Shesha serves Vishnu as his bed (shayya), his canopy, and his devoted attendant. In Vaishnavism, he is regarded as an expansion or manifestation of Vishnu himself, and in some traditions as an incarnation of Balarama. When Vishnu descended as Rama, Shesha took birth as his brother Lakshmana; when Vishnu descended as Krishna, Shesha accompanied him as Balarama. The Sri Vaishnava tradition holds that the acharya Ramanuja was himself an incarnation of Shesha.

Iconography

In temple sculpture and painting, Shesha is depicted as a gigantic serpent with many hoods spread like an umbrella over the reclining Vishnu (as in the Anantasayana form). In his anthropomorphic attendant form, he appears as a handsome man with a serpent hood canopy over his head, holding a plough and a conch, standing humbly beside Vishnu with folded hands.
