Miss Julie (Swedish: Fröken Julie) is a naturalistic play written in 1888 by Swedish playwright August Strindberg. It is set on Midsummer's Eve and the following morning, which is Midsummer and St. John's Day. The setting is an estate of a count in Sweden. Miss Julie is drawn to a senior servant, a valet named Jean, who is well-traveled and well-read. The action takes place in the kitchen of Miss Julie's father's manor, where Jean's fiancée, a servant named Christine, cooks and sometimes sleeps while Jean and Miss Julie talk.

Plot

On Midsummer's Eve, the young aristocrat Julie, daughter of a count, joins the servants' dancing in a mood of reckless exaltation. She fixates on Jean, her father's ambitious valet, teasing and commanding him; over the night their flirtation deepens into seduction. Afterwards the class order reasserts itself with a vengeance: Jean reveals his contempt and his plan to use her money to open a hotel, Julie realises she is ruined by the standards of her class, and, unable either to stay or to flee, she is steered by Jean — who hands her his razor — toward suicide as the Count's bell rings for his boots and Jean cringes back into servitude.

The preface

Strindberg's preface to Miss Julie is considered a manifesto of theatrical naturalism. In it he explains his characters' behaviour by a multiplicity of motives — heredity, environment, upbringing, physiology and chance — and calls Julie a "modern character", a half-woman whose fate illustrates the battle of the sexes and of the classes. He advocated single-set staging, continuous action without intervals, and actors playing without makeup toward a more truthful theatre.

Reception and adaptations

The play was banned or censored in several countries for decades but became one of the most performed works in the modern repertoire. It has been adapted as an opera (notably by Ned Rorem, 1965, and William Alwyn), as ballets (Birgit Cullberg's 1950 version), and in numerous films, including Alf Sjöberg's celebrated 1951 Swedish film and Liv Ullmann's 2014 version starring Jessica Chastain and Colin Farrell.
