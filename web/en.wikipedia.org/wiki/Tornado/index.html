A tornado is a violently rotating column of air extending from a thunderstorm to the ground, capable of catastrophic damage. Tornadoes form in severe thunderstorms — especially supercells — when wind shear and instability organise rotation, most famously in the central United States' 'Tornado Alley'.

Measurement and safety

Damage is rated on the Enhanced Fujita scale from EF0 to EF5; the widest recorded tornado (El Reno, 2013) was over 2.6 miles across. Warnings from weather services, storm spotters and radar give minutes to seek shelter in basements or interior rooms. Tornadoes differ from the much larger tropical hurricanes covered in the seed's news sections.
