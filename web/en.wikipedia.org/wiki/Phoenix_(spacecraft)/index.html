Phoenix (spacecraft)


Phoenix was a NASA robotic space probe that landed on Mars on 25 May 2008 to study the history of water and habitability in the Martian Arctic. Launched 4 August 2007; mission declared over November 2008.

Phoenix was a robotic spacecraft on a space exploration mission on Mars under the Mars Scout Program. It was launched on August 4, 2007, and landed in the Vastitas Borealis plains of the northern polar region of Mars on May 25, 2008 — the first successful landing in a Martian polar region.

Mission

The lander's name reflected its "rebirth" from cancelled hardware: it reused the lander built for the cancelled Mars Surveyor 2001 mission. Its two goals were to study the geological history of water on Mars and to evaluate the potential habitability of the ice-rich polar environment for microbes.

Findings

Using its 2.35-metre robotic arm, Phoenix dug trenches in the polygonal-patterned ground and delivered soil samples to onboard instruments, including a wet chemistry laboratory and the Thermal and Evolved Gas Analyzer (TEGA). It directly confirmed the presence of water ice just below the surface — subsurface ice exposed by digging sublimated away over days — and observed falling snow from clouds using its lidar. Analyses found alkaline soil containing magnesium, sodium, potassium and chloride, and detected perchlorate salts. Images also showed droplets on the lander's leg that appeared to grow, interpreted as possible briny liquid water.

The mission was designed to last 90 Martian sols; it operated for more than 150. Contact was lost in November 2008 as the Martian winter closed in and sunlight diminished, and the mission was declared ended on November 10, 2008.
