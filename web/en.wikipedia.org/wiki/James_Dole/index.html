James Drummond Dole (September 27, 1877 – May 20, 1958), known as the "Pineapple King", was an American industrialist who developed the pineapple industry in Hawaii and founded the Hawaiian Pineapple Company, the forerunner of the Dole Food Company.

Early life

Dole was born in Jamaica Plain, Massachusetts, into a family that included his cousin Sanford B. Dole, who became president of the Republic of Hawaii and later its first territorial governor. After graduating from Harvard University in 1899, Dole decided to try his fortune in Hawaii, then newly annexed by the United States.

Pineapple business

In 1900 Dole purchased land at Wahiawa on Oahu and began growing pineapples. He founded the Hawaiian Pineapple Company in 1901 and built a cannery at Wahiawa in 1903, later opening a larger cannery in Honolulu. Working with investors and benefiting from mechanization such as the Ginaca peeling-and-coring machine, the company grew rapidly, and by the 1920s "Dole" had become synonymous with Hawaiian pineapple.

In 1922 Dole purchased most of the island of Lanai and converted it into the largest pineapple plantation in the world. The company town there was named Dole Park. The business struggled during the Great Depression, and in 1932 the company entered a period of reorganization under Castle & Cooke, with Dole gradually losing control of the firm he had founded.

Legacy

Dole died in Honolulu in 1958. His plantation heritage survives in the Dole Plantation tourist attraction at Wahiawa and in the Dole Food Company, one of the world's largest producers and marketers of fruit and fresh vegetables.
