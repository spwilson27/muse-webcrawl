Drive-in

A drive-in is a business or venue designed so that customers can be served while remaining in their cars. The term most commonly refers to the drive-in theater, an outdoor cinema with a large screen, a projection booth and a parking area where patrons watch from their vehicles, with sound provided first by window speakers and later by FM radio transmission.

History

The first patented drive-in theater was opened by Richard Hollingshead in Camden, New Jersey, in June 1933. Drive-ins boomed in postwar America, peaking at roughly 4,000 to 5,000 locations in the late 1950s and 1960s, becoming emblematic of car culture and popular for family outings and B-movies. Rising land values, daylight saving time, indoor multiplexes and home video drove a long decline; only a few hundred remain in the United States, though the format saw a revival during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Other drive-ins

The format extends to drive-in restaurants (served by carhops, as at A&W and Sonic), drive-in banks, and drive-in churches, all products of mid-century American automobile culture.
