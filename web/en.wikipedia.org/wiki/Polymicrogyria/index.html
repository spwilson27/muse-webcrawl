Polymicrogyria

Polymicrogyria (PMG) is a condition involving excessive folding of the surface of the brain, resulting in an abnormally thick cortex. It is a neuronal migration disorder in which developing neurons do not migrate to their proper location in the brain during early foetal development, forming many unusually small gyri (microgyri).

Signs and symptoms

The effects of polymicrogyria depend on how much of the brain is affected, and can range from mild learning difficulties to severe intellectual disability, seizures, cerebral palsy, and problems with speech and swallowing. Bilateral perisylvian polymicrogyria is one of the most common patterns, affecting the regions around the Sylvian fissures on both sides.

Causes

Causes include genetic mutations (such as in the GPR56 and SRPX2 genes), congenital infections such as cytomegalovirus, and disruptions of blood supply to the developing brain. In many cases the cause remains unknown.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain, which shows the characteristic excessive small folds and thickened cortex. There is no cure; treatment is supportive and may include anti-seizure medication, physical therapy, speech therapy, and educational support.
