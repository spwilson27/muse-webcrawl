Chartered accountant is a professional designation for accountants in many Commonwealth and other countries, granted by chartered professional bodies to members who complete prescribed education, examinations, and practical training. Chartered accountants work in audit, taxation, financial management, and insolvency, and are typically the only accountants licensed to sign statutory audit reports in their jurisdictions.

The chartered accountant profession began in Scotland

History

The profession originated in Scotland: the Society of Accountants in Edinburgh received a royal charter in 1854, making its members the first "chartered accountants." Similar bodies followed in England and Wales (ICAEW, 1880) and throughout the British Empire — Canada, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, and India, where the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (1949) is now among the largest accounting bodies in the world.

Qualification

Qualification typically requires a university degree (or equivalent), passing a multi-level professional examination in accounting, auditing, law, and taxation, and completing a period of supervised practical experience with an authorized firm. Members use designatory letters such as CA, ACA (associate), and FCA (fellow, after further experience). Reciprocal recognition agreements allow qualifications to be recognized across many countries. In the United States the analogous designation is the Certified Public Accountant (CPA).

Role

Chartered accountants provide assurance and advisory services: auditing financial statements, preparing tax filings, valuing businesses, acting as liquidators and administrators in insolvency, and serving as finance directors and chief executives. They are bound by codes of ethics enforced by their institutes, with disciplinary sanctions up to expulsion.
