Ven (Danish and older Swedish: Hven) is a small Swedish island in the Öresund strait between Scania (Sweden) and Zealand (Denmark). The island covers about 7.5 square kilometres and has a permanent population of a few hundred, which swells with tourists in summer. It is administered as part of Landskrona Municipality.

History

Ven belonged to Denmark until 1658, when it passed to Sweden under the Treaty of Roskilde; it was confirmed Swedish after the Scanian War by the Treaty of Lund (1679). The island is famous as the domain of the astronomer Tycho Brahe, who received it as a fief from King Frederick II in 1576 and built there his castle-observatory Uraniborg and the partly subterranean observatory Stjerneborg, the finest astronomical facilities in Europe before the telescope. Tycho left the island in 1597 after falling out with Christian IV, and the buildings later fell into ruin.

Geography and sights

The island is a raised plateau of farmland ringed by steep clay cliffs (called backafall), with a highest point of about 45 metres. Attractions include the Tycho Brahe Museum at the Uraniborg site, the medieval church of St Ibb, a whisky distillery, and art galleries. Bicycles are the usual way for visitors to explore the island, which is reached by ferry from Landskrona and, seasonally, from Copenhagen and Helsingborg.
