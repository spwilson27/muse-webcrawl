Stone–Weierstrass theorem

The Stone–Weierstrass theorem is a fundamental result in real analysis, generalising the Weierstrass approximation theorem that polynomials uniformly approximate continuous functions on a closed interval.

Statement

Let X be a compact Hausdorff space and A a subalgebra of the continuous real-valued functions on X that contains a nonzero constant function and separates points of X. Then A is dense in C(X) in the uniform norm: every continuous function on X can be approximated uniformly by elements of A.

History and significance

Karl Weierstrass proved the polynomial case in 1885; Marshall Stone generalised it in 1937 to algebras of functions on compact Hausdorff spaces. A complex version requires the algebra to be closed under conjugation. The theorem is a cornerstone of approximation theory and functional analysis.
