Hardy–Weinberg principle

Hardy–Weinberg principle
Formulated: 1908
By: G. H. Hardy and Wilhelm Weinberg
Equation: p² + 2pq + q² = 1

The Hardy–Weinberg principle states that allele and genotype frequencies in a population remain constant from generation to generation in the absence of evolutionary influences. It is a null model of population genetics, published independently in 1908 by mathematician G. H. Hardy and physician Wilhelm Weinberg.

The model

For a gene with two alleles A (frequency p) and a (frequency q), expected genotype frequencies are AA = p², Aa = 2pq and aa = q², with p + q = 1.

Assumptions

Equilibrium requires no mutation, no natural selection, no gene flow, no genetic drift (infinitely large population), random mating, and no overlap complications. Violations signal that evolution or non-random mating is occurring.

Uses

The principle is used to estimate carrier frequencies of recessive disorders, test for selection or population structure, and as a quality check in genome-wide association studies.
