Essenes

The Essenes were a Jewish sect or movement active from the 2nd century BC to the 1st century AD during the Second Temple period. Ancient writers—Josephus, Philo of Alexandria and Pliny the Elder—describe them as living communally, practising ritual purity and celibacy (in some branches), sharing property, and awaiting an eschatological struggle between the Sons of Light and the Sons of Darkness.

Practices

Josephus reports about 4,000 Essenes, admission after a probationary period, common meals, frequent ritual immersion, strict Sabbath observance, and rejection of slavery and oath-taking. They are generally contrasted with the Pharisees and Sadducees.

Dead Sea Scrolls

Since the 1947 discovery of the Dead Sea Scrolls in caves near Qumran, most scholars have associated the scrolls' community—calling itself the Yahad—with the Essenes, though alternative identifications are debated. The sect disappears from record after the First Jewish–Roman War (66–73 AD) and the destruction of Qumran.
