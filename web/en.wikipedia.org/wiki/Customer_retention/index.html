Customer retention

Retaining existing customers is generally less costly than acquiring new ones.

Customer retention refers to the ability of a company or product to retain its customers over some specified period. High customer retention means customers of the product or business tend to return to, continue to buy or in some other way not defect to another product or business, or to non-use entirely. Selling organizations generally attempt to reduce customer defections.

Customer retention starts with the first contact an organization has with a customer and continues throughout the entire lifetime of a relationship and successful retention efforts are measured by the number of customers retained relative to the number at risk. It is distinct from customer acquisition, which focuses on winning new customers, and from customer satisfaction, though the two are closely related: satisfied customers are far more likely to be retained.

Measurement

The customer retention rate is typically calculated as the percentage of customers retained over a given period, excluding new customers acquired during that period. The complement of the retention rate is the churn rate, widely used in subscription businesses such as telecommunications, software as a service, and streaming media. Companies also track customer lifetime value, which estimates the total revenue a business can expect from a single customer account over the whole relationship.

Strategies

Common retention strategies include loyalty programmes, personalised communication, proactive customer service and support, quality improvements driven by customer feedback, and win-back campaigns aimed at customers who have already defected. Research frequently cited in the field suggests that increasing retention rates by a small margin can have an outsized effect on profitability, since retained customers tend to buy more over time and cost less to serve.
