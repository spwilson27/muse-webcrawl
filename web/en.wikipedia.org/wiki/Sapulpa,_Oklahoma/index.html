Sapulpa is a city in Creek and Tulsa counties in Oklahoma, United States, and the county seat of Creek County. It lies southwest of Tulsa and is part of the Tulsa metropolitan area.

History

The town is named for a Muscogee (Creek) chief, Sapulpa, who settled in the area around 1850. When the St. Louis–San Francisco Railway arrived in 1886 a townsite grew; Sapulpa boomed after the 1905 Glenn Pool oil discovery nearby, becoming a refining and rail centre nicknamed the hub of the "world's richest oil field". Historic Route 66 passes through downtown.

Economy and landmarks

Sapulpa retains oil- and rail-related industry and markets its Route 66 heritage, including the Heart of Route 66 Auto Museum with a very tall vintage gas pump. The Frankoma Pottery works, founded in nearby Sapulpa in the 1930s, is a noted local institution.
