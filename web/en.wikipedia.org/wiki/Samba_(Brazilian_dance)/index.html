Samba (Brazilian dance)

Origin: Afro-Brazilian communities of Rio de Janeiro, early 20th century

Music: Samba, in 2/4 time

Associated events: Rio Carnival, samba school parades

Samba is a Brazilian dance and musical genre originating in the Afro-Brazilian communities of Rio de Janeiro in the early 20th century. With roots in African rhythms brought to Brazil through the transatlantic slave trade and developed in the neighbourhoods and terreiros of Rio, samba became the emblematic dance of the Rio Carnival and a symbol of Brazilian national identity.

Styles

Samba no pé ("samba on the foot") is the solo improvised dance performed by passistas in carnival parades, characterised by rapid footwork, hip movement and a basic three-step pattern. Samba de gafieira is a partner dance performed in ballrooms, while samba de roda, from Bahia, is danced in a circle and was recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage. Other forms include pagode dancing and the stylised ballroom samba of international dancesport.

Music and rhythm

The dance accompanies music played by a bateria of percussion instruments — surdo, caixa, tamborim, agogô and cuíca — in a syncopated 2/4 rhythm. Samba schools parade through the Sambadrome with hundreds of drummers and dancers performing a themed presentation judged during Carnival.

Cultural significance

Once marginalised and even criminalised in the early 1900s, samba was embraced by the Brazilian state in the 1930s and exported worldwide; it remains central to Carnival celebrations and Brazilian cultural life.
