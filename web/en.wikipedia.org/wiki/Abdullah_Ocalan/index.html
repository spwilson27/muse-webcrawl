Abdullah Öcalan (born 4 April 1948 or 1949), also known as Apo, is a Kurdish political activist and the founding leader of the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK), which has waged an insurgency against the Turkish state since 1984.

Early life and PKK

Öcalan was born in Ömerli, a village in Şanlıurfa Province in southeastern Turkey. He studied political science at Ankara University. In 1978 he co-founded the PKK, a Marxist-Leninist organization advocating Kurdish self-determination. In 1984 the PKK launched an armed campaign against Turkey.

Capture and imprisonment

After being expelled from Syria in 1998, Öcalan was captured by Turkish intelligence operatives in Nairobi, Kenya, in February 1999 and flown to Turkey. He was tried and sentenced to death; the sentence was commuted to life imprisonment when Turkey abolished capital punishment. He has been held in solitary confinement on İmralı island in the Sea of Marmara ever since.

Ideology

From prison, Öcalan has developed the ideology of "democratic confederalism", influenced by the social ecologist Murray Bookchin, advocating a non-state, decentralized political system. His writings remain central to Kurdish movements in Turkey and Syria.
