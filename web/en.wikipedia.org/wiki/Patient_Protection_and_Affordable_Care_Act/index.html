The Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (PPACA), commonly called the Affordable Care Act (ACA) or Obamacare, is a landmark United States federal statute enacted by the 111th Congress and signed into law by President Barack Obama on 23 March 2010. It represents the most significant overhaul of the US healthcare system since the creation of Medicare and Medicaid in 1965.

Provisions

The Act requires insurers to accept all applicants and forbids denying coverage or charging more because of pre-existing conditions. It created state and federal health insurance marketplaces where individuals can buy subsidised coverage, expanded Medicaid eligibility to adults with incomes up to 138% of the federal poverty level (in states that adopted the expansion), allowed young adults to remain on a parent's plan until age 26, and required most individuals to carry insurance — the "individual mandate", whose tax penalty was reduced to $0 by the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, effective 2019.

Legal challenges

The law survived repeated challenges in the Supreme Court. In National Federation of Independent Business v. Sebelius (2012) the Court upheld the individual mandate as a tax while making Medicaid expansion optional for states. In King v. Burwell (2015) it upheld subsidies on the federal exchange, and in California v. Texas (2021) it dismissed a further challenge for lack of standing.

Effects

The uninsured rate in the United States fell from around 16% in 2010 to under 9% within several years of full implementation. The Act remains politically contested, with numerous repeal attempts in Congress, none successful, and public opinion gradually shifting in its favour over the 2010s and 2020s.
