Hoegaarden Brewery

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hoegaarden Brewery is a brewery in Hoegaarden, Belgium, famous as the birthplace of Belgian witbier (white beer). The brand is now owned by Anheuser-Busch InBev, and its wheat beer — spiced with coriander and orange peel — is sold worldwide.

History

Brewing in Hoegaarden dates to the Middle Ages; by the 18th and 19th centuries the village supported dozens of breweries making the cloudy, top-fermented wheat beer traditional to the region. The style died out in the 1950s when the last witbier brewery closed. In 1965 local milkman Pierre Celis founded the De Kluis brewery to revive it, launching the modern Hoegaarden witbier. After a fire in 1985, Celis accepted investment from Interbrew (later AB InBev); he later emigrated to Texas and founded Celis Brewery there.

The beer

Hoegaarden Wit is brewed with roughly equal parts barley malt and unmalted wheat, seasoned with Curaçao orange peel and coriander seed, and bottle-conditioned, giving it a hazy pale appearance and a citrusy, spicy aroma. It is traditionally served in a distinctive hexagonal tumbler. Other products include Hoegaarden Rosée (with raspberry) and Grand Cru. In 2005–2006, AB InBev's plan to move production to Jupille provoked protests; brewing returned to Hoegaarden after investment in the site.
