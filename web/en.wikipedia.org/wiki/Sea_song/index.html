Sea song
TypesShanty, forebitter, sea ballad
EraAge of sail

A sea song is a song traditionally sung by sailors, encompassing work songs and songs sung for entertainment aboard ship or ashore. The best-known category is the sea shanty (or chantey), used to coordinate collective labour.

Types

Shanties were timed to tasks: halyard shanties for hoisting sails, capstan shanties for raising anchor, and short-drag shanties for brief pulls, led by a shantyman with crew choruses. Songs sung off watch in the forecastle — forebitters — included ballads, love songs, and songs of home. Sea ballads such as those about press gangs, storms, and famous captains circulated in broadsides.

History

Sea songs flourished in the 19th-century merchant and naval sailing era, drawing on British, Irish, American, African-American, and Caribbean traditions. Collectors such as Stan Hugill preserved the repertoire as steam replaced sail. Examples include "Drunken Sailor", "Wellerman", and "Spanish Ladies".

Revival

Folk revivals and, in 2021, viral social-media "ShantyTok" performances of "Wellerman" brought renewed popularity. See also modern viral song culture for comparison.
