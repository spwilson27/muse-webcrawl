The great white pelican (Pelecanus onocrotalus), also known as the eastern white pelican or rosy pelican, is a bird in the pelican family. It breeds from southeastern Europe through Asia and Africa in swamps and shallow lakes. It is one of the largest flying birds, with a length of 140–180 cm and a wingspan that can exceed 3 metres.

Description

Adults are almost entirely white, with black flight feathers visible in flight and a slight pink tinge in the breeding season. The huge bill is yellow with an orange pouch. Males are larger than females and have longer bills.

Behaviour

Great white pelicans fish cooperatively: flocks herd fish into shallow water by beating their wings, then scoop them up in their pouches. Unlike the brown pelican, they do not dive for food. They are strong fliers that travel long distances between feeding and breeding sites, often soaring in formation.

Distribution

Breeding colonies occur from the Danube Delta to Mongolia, and in sub-Saharan Africa. Northern populations are migratory, wintering in Africa, India and Southeast Asia.
