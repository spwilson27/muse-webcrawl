Robin Goodfellow

Robin Goodfellow is a mischievous fairy or hobgoblin of English folklore, closely associated with the figure of Puck. Known for his "mad pranks and merry jests", he was said to wander at night doing household chores for the tidy, misleading travellers, souring milk, and playing tricks on the lazy and the proud. He is most famous today as a character in William Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream.

Folklore

Robin Goodfellow appears in English popular literature from the early sixteenth century. In ballads and chapbooks he is the son of Oberon, king of the fairies, and a mortal woman; gifted with the power of shapeshifting, he delights in harmless mischief. The anonymous Robin Goodfellow: His Mad Pranks and Merry Jests (1628–29) collected many of his traditional adventures. In some traditions his helpful domestic aspect links him to the brownie and the hob; a bowl of cream left out at night was his customary reward.

In Shakespeare

In A Midsummer Night's Dream (c. 1595), Puck — explicitly called "Robin Goodfellow" — serves Oberon, fetches the love-in-idleness flower, transforms Bottom's head into that of an ass, and delivers the play's epilogue: "If we shadows have offended..."
