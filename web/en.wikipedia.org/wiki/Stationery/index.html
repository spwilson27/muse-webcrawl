Stationery

Stationery encompasses paper goods and writing implements for the office and home.

Stationery refers to commercially manufactured writing materials, including cut paper, envelopes, writing implements such as pens and pencils, and other office supplies. The term derives from "stationer", a bookseller or publisher in medieval England who sold goods from a fixed station, in contrast to itinerant peddlers.

Categories

  • Paper products — notepads, loose-leaf and ruled paper, letterheads, business cards, envelopes, greeting cards, sticky notes.
  • Writing implements — pens (ballpoint, fountain, rollerball), pencils, markers, highlighters.
  • Desk accessories — staplers, hole punches, scissors, paper clips, rulers, erasers, adhesive tape and glue.
  • Filing and organisation — folders, binders, labels, notebooks and diaries/planners.

History and industry

Stationery trades grew with the expansion of literacy, commerce, and postal services in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and stationers' shops became fixtures of high streets. In the twenty-first century, personalised and designer stationery — for weddings, business branding, and crafts such as journaling and calligraphy — has remained popular despite the shift to digital communication. Major manufacturers and retailers have included firms such as Moleskine, Stabilo, Faber-Castell, Mitsubishi Pencil, and WHSmith.
