Nuclear weapons of the United Kingdom

The United Kingdom possesses nuclear weapons and was the third country to develop and test them, after the United States and the Soviet Union. Its first test, Operation Hurricane, took place in 1952. Since 1998 the UK's deterrent has consisted solely of submarine-launched ballistic missiles: four Vanguard-class submarines armed with Trident II D5 missiles, maintaining continuous at-sea deterrence (CASD) with one boat always on patrol.

History

British scientists made key early contributions to nuclear physics, and the wartime Tube Alloys programme was folded into the Manhattan Project under the 1943 Quebec Agreement. After the US McMahon Act of 1946 cut off cooperation, Britain developed its own bomb, testing it off Australia in 1952, and later thermonuclear weapons in the Grapple tests of 1957–58. Delivery systems included the V bomber force, the Blue Streak programme (cancelled), and the US-supplied Polaris missile under the 1962 Nassau Agreement, succeeded by Trident from 1994.

Current arsenal

The UK stockpile was long capped at 180 operationally available warheads, with a declared ceiling lowered to 180 total in 2010; in 2021 the government raised the overall cap to 260 warheads. Warheads are designed and built at the Atomic Weapons Establishment at Aldermaston. The Dreadnought-class submarines are under construction to replace the Vanguard class from the early 2030s.

Policy

The UK assigns its deterrent to NATO but retains independent operational control. Successive governments state the weapons exist to deter the most extreme threats and would be used only in "extreme circumstances of self-defence".
