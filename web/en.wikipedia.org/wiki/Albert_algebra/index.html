Albert algebra

In mathematics, an Albert algebra is an exceptional Jordan algebra — that is, a Jordan algebra which cannot be embedded in an associative algebra of matrices in the usual way. Albert algebras are finite-dimensional Jordan algebras of dimension 27 over a field, constructed from 3×3 Hermitian matrices over an octonion algebra. They are named after the American mathematician Abraham Adrian Albert, who pioneered the study of non-associative algebras.

Definition

Given a field F and an octonion algebra O over F, the Albert algebra is the set of 3×3 Hermitian matrices with entries in O:

X = [[ξ₁, x₃, x̄₂], [x̄₃, ξ₂, x₁], [x₂, x̄₁, ξ₃]]

where ξ₁, ξ₂, ξ₃ ∈ F and x₁, x₂, x₃ ∈ O, equipped with the symmetrised matrix product X ∘ Y = ½(XY + YX). This product is commutative and satisfies the Jordan identity, but the algebra is not special: it cannot be realised inside an associative algebra with the Jordan product.

Properties

Albert algebras are simple, central, and of Jordan degree 3. Over an algebraically closed field there is a unique Albert algebra up to isomorphism. Their automorphism group is a simple algebraic group of type F₄; the full structure group relates to groups of type E₆. Reduced Albert algebras are classified using Galois cohomology, with the mod-2 and mod-3 invariants (the f₃, f₅, and g₃ invariants) playing a key role in modern research.

History

The existence of exceptional Jordan algebras was first demonstrated by Albert in 1934, and the classification of finite-dimensional formally real Jordan algebras by Pascual Jordan, John von Neumann, and Eugene Wigner in the same period showed that the 27-dimensional exceptional algebra is the unique non-special simple formally real Jordan algebra.
