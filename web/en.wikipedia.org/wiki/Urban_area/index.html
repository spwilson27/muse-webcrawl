An urban area, or built-up area, is a human settlement with a high population density and infrastructure of built environment. Urban areas originate through urbanisation and are categorised by urban morphology as cities, towns, conurbations or suburbs. The term contrasts with rural areas such as villages and hamlets, and with uninhabited or agricultural land.

Definitions

Definitions vary by country and are usually set by national statistical offices using criteria such as minimum population, population density, contiguity of built-up land, and share of employment outside agriculture. The United States Census Bureau defines urban areas by densely developed territory with at least 2,000 housing units or 5,000 residents (thresholds revised in 2020). Because administrative city boundaries rarely match the physical extent of settlement, geographers also use metropolitan areas defined by commuting patterns, and continuous built-up areas that ignore municipal lines.

Global urbanisation

In 2007 the world's urban population first exceeded its rural population; the United Nations projects that about two-thirds of humanity will live in urban areas by 2050, with most growth in Africa and Asia. The largest urban agglomerations today include Tokyo (roughly 37 million), Delhi, Shanghai, Dhaka, São Paulo and Cairo. Rapid urbanisation brings economic agglomeration benefits but also challenges in housing, transport, sanitation and exposure to hazards such as urban heat islands and flooding.

Characteristics

Urban areas typically have extensive surface sealing, planned street networks, public transport systems, and land uses specialised into residential, commercial, industrial and civic districts. Their study is the subject of urban geography, urban planning and urban studies.
