Six Nations of the Grand River

LocationBrant and Haldimand Counties, Ontario, Canada
Established1784 (Haldimand Proclamation)
Population~28,000 registered members (largest First Nation in Canada by population)
NationsMohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca, Tuscarora

Six Nations of the Grand River is the largest First Nations reserve in Canada by population, located near Brantford in southwestern Ontario. It is a reserve of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy, comprising members of the Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca, and Tuscarora nations, and includes the smaller Mississaugas of the Credit reserve within its territory.

History

After the American Revolutionary War, in which most of the Six Nations had sided with the British, Mohawk leader Joseph Brant (Thayendanegea) led Loyalist Haudenosaunee to Canada. By the Haldimand Proclamation of 25 October 1784, the Crown granted a tract of land six miles deep on each side of the Grand River from its mouth to its source as compensation for lands lost in New York. Over the following century, much of the original 675,000-plus acre tract was sold, surrendered under disputed circumstances, or lost — giving rise to land claims that continue today — leaving the present reserve of roughly 46,000 acres (about 5% of the original grant). The territory includes the communities of Ohsweken and the city of Brantford's surroundings.

Governance

The community is governed both by the elected Six Nations of the Grand River Elected Council, established under the Indian Act in 1924 when Canada deposed the traditional council by force, and by the Haudenosaunee Confederacy Chiefs Council, which maintains its seat at the Onondaga longhouse and asserts continuing hereditary authority. The Grand River is also home to the oldest surviving church on a reserve in Canada (Her Majesty's Chapel of the Mohawks, 1785) and is notable for its lacrosse tradition, including the Six Nations Arrows.
