A photobioreactor (PBR) is a closed or semi-closed vessel designed for the controlled cultivation of photosynthetic microorganisms such as microalgae and cyanobacteria. Unlike open raceway ponds, photobioreactors enclose the culture in transparent tubes, panels or tanks, allowing control of light, temperature, pH, nutrients and carbon dioxide, and protecting the culture from contamination.

Design

The key engineering challenge is light delivery: cells near the surface shade those deeper in, so designs maximise surface-area-to-volume ratio. Common configurations include horizontal or vertical glass/plastic tubular reactors, flat-panel reactors with thin light paths, vertical bubble columns, and low-cost plastic-bag systems. Mixing is provided by pumping or airlift circulation; cultures may be lit by sunlight, artificial light, or both. Cooling is often required under solar operation because enclosed vessels heat rapidly.

Applications

Photobioreactors are used where purity and productivity justify their high capital and operating costs compared with open ponds: production of high-value compounds such as the pigments astaxanthin and beta-carotene, omega-3 fatty acids, food supplements such as Spirulina and Chlorella, and pharmaceutical research. They are also investigated for algal biofuels, wastewater treatment, carbon capture, and life-support systems for spaceflight, where algae produce oxygen and food while recycling carbon dioxide.
