Eighth Schedule to the Constitution of India

The Eighth Schedule to the Constitution of India lists the official languages of the Republic of India. As of the most recent amendment, the schedule contains 22 languages, often called the "scheduled languages". Inclusion in the schedule carries recognition for the development and official use of the language and entitles it to representation on official bodies, use in examinations for public services, and other benefits.

History

The original Constitution of 1950 listed 14 languages: Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Malayalam, Marathi, Oriya (now Odia), Punjabi, Sanskrit, Tamil, Telugu, and Urdu. The list was expanded by later amendments:

  • The 21st Amendment (1967) added Sindhi.
  • The 71st Amendment (1992) added Konkani, Manipuri, and Nepali.
  • The 92nd Amendment (2003) added Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, and Santhali, bringing the total to 22.

Significance

Articles 344(1) and 351 of the Constitution refer to the Eighth Schedule in connection with the Official Languages Commission and the development of Hindi, which is to draw on the scheduled languages. Although inclusion does not itself make a language an official language of the Union — Hindi and English hold that role — scheduled languages are used in legislatures, courts, and administration in various states. Demands for the inclusion of further languages, such as Tulu, Rajasthani, and Bhojpuri, remain a recurring subject of public debate.
