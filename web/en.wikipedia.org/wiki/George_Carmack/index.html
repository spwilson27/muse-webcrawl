George Washington Carmack
Born: September 24, 1860, Contra Costa County, California, U.S.
Died: June 5, 1922 (aged 61), Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada
Known for: Discovery of gold that started the Klondike Gold Rush
Spouse: Kate Carmack (Shaaw Tláa)

George Washington Carmack (September 24, 1860 – June 5, 1922) was an American prospector, saloon keeper, and rancher. He is credited, together with his brother-in-law Skookum Jim and nephew Dawson Charlie — members of the Tagish First Nation — with the gold discovery at Bonanza Creek in the Klondike in August 1896 that triggered the Klondike Gold Rush, one of the largest gold rushes in history.

Life in the Yukon

Carmack came north in the 1880s after work as a sheep rancher and marine, settling in the Yukon valley where he fished, traded, and prospected along the Yukon River. He married Shaaw Tláa, known as Kate Carmack, of the Tagish people, and lived much of the time in the Tagish way, which led some fellow prospectors to call him "Squaw Man" and doubt his dedication to prospecting.

The discovery

In mid-August 1896, following a tip from prospector Robert Henderson — who, in a much-debated slight, had refused to sell tobacco to Skookum Jim because he was Indigenous — Carmack's party tried their luck on Rabbit Creek, a tributary of the Klondike River. On August 16 or 17, 1896, they found coarse gold in the creek gravels. Carmack staked the discovery claim and renamed the creek Bonanza Creek; he registered the claim at Fortymile on August 17. Exactly who first spotted the gold — Carmack, Skookum Jim, or Dawson Charlie — has been disputed ever since, and much modern scholarship credits Skookum Jim with the find while Carmack took the public credit and the discovery claim.

Later life

News of the strike, carried out when the steamers Excelsior and Portland reached San Francisco and Seattle in July 1897 with tons of gold aboard, set off a stampede of an estimated 100,000 would-be prospectors. Carmack's claims made him wealthy; he sold out, moved to Seattle, then to California, and finally to Vancouver, British Columbia, where he invested in real estate. His marriage to Kate ended amid disputes; he remarried in 1900. He died in Vancouver in 1922.
