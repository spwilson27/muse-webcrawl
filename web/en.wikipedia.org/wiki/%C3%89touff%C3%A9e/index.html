Étouffée

Étouffée (also spelled etouffee) is a dish of Louisiana Creole and Cajun cuisine in which seafood — most classically crawfish, but also shrimp or crab — is smothered in a seasoned roux-based sauce and served over rice. The name comes from the French étouffée, "smothered" or "braised", describing the covered, slow cooking method.

Preparation

Étouffée begins with a roux — in Cajun versions often a lighter, butter-based roux than that used for gumbo — combined with the "holy trinity" of Louisiana cooking: onion, bell pepper, and celery. Stock (usually seafood stock), garlic, thyme, bay, and cayenne or hot sauce season the sauce, and shellfish are simmered just until cooked before the dish is ladled over white rice and finished with scallions and parsley.

Crawfish étouffée

Crawfish étouffée is the most famous version and a signature of Louisiana cooking, especially associated with the Breaux Bridge area, which styles itself the "Crawfish Capital of the World" and hosts an annual Crawfish Festival. Historically a humble bayou dish, it rose to national prominence in the 1980s with the popularity of Cajun cuisine.

Variants

Shrimp étouffée is common in New Orleans restaurants, and crab or mixed-seafood versions are also made. Creole versions may include tomatoes, while rural Cajun versions generally omit them.
