Flags of the world

This is a gallery-style overview of national flags used by countries and territories worldwide. National flags serve as the principal visual symbol of a sovereign state, flown by governments and citizens and used in diplomatic, military and maritime contexts.

The flags of the world display recurring design families. Vertical and horizontal tricolours dominate in Europe, reflecting the influence of the French Tricolore and the Dutch Prince's Flag. Many African flags use the pan-African colours of green, gold and red; many Arab flags use the pan-Arab colours of black, white, green and red. Flags of Commonwealth nations may retain the Union Jack in the canton, while Scandinavian flags share the Nordic cross design originating with Denmark's Dannebrog.

Notable facts

The flag of Nepal is the world's only non-quadrilateral national flag, formed from two single pennons. The flags of Switzerland and Vatican City are the only square national flags. Libya's flag from 1977 to 2011 was a plain green field, the only national flag of a single solid colour. The flag of Denmark, first attested in the 14th century, is generally considered the oldest national flag still in use.
