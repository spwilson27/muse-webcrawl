Ukichiro Nakaya

Born: 4 July 1900, Katayamazu, Ishikawa, Japan
Died: 11 April 1962, Tokyo, Japan
Known for: First artificial snow crystals; Nakaya diagram
Fields: Physics, glaciology

Ukichiro Nakaya (Japanese: 中谷 宇吉郎; 4 July 1900 – 11 April 1962) was a Japanese physicist and science essayist known for his work on snow. Working at Hokkaido University, he created the world's first artificial snow crystals in a laboratory in 1936 and developed the Nakaya diagram, which relates the shape of a snow crystal to the temperature and humidity at which it forms.

Life and career

Nakaya was born in Katayamazu, in present-day Kaga, Ishikawa Prefecture. He studied physics at the University of Tokyo and later worked under Torahiko Terada. In 1930 he became a professor at the newly established Faculty of Science of Hokkaido University in Sapporo, a city whose long, cold winters gave him abundant natural snow to study.

Snow research

Using a microscope and photographic techniques, Nakaya systematically classified natural snow crystals. On 12 March 1936, in a cold laboratory at Hokkaido University, he succeeded in growing the first artificial snow crystal on the tip of a rabbit hair. His studies led to the Nakaya diagram, showing that crystal morphology — plates, columns, needles, dendrites — depends chiefly on temperature and supersaturation. He is often associated with the saying that snow crystals are "letters sent from heaven," encoding the conditions of the upper atmosphere through which they fell.

Later work and legacy

After the Second World War, Nakaya helped found the Institute of Low Temperature Science at Hokkaido University and pursued research in the United States on ice fog and frost prevention. He was also a prolific and celebrated essayist, writing about science for general readers and producing documentary films. His work laid the foundations of modern snow and ice physics.
