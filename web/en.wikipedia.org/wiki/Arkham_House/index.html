Arkham House

Arkham House is an American publishing house specialising in weird fiction, founded in Sauk City, Wisconsin, in 1939 by August Derleth and Donald Wandrei. The company's name derives from the fictional New England city of Arkham, invented by H. P. Lovecraft. Arkham House was founded specifically to publish the writings of Lovecraft, which Derleth and Wandrei feared would otherwise be lost, since Lovecraft's work had appeared mainly in pulp magazines.

History

The firm's first book was Lovecraft's The Outsider and Others (1939), a large omnibus of his stories, printed in an edition of about 1,200 copies which sold slowly at first. Derleth subsidised the early list from his own earnings as a writer. During the 1940s and 1950s Arkham House expanded to publish other Weird Tales authors — Robert E. Howard, Clark Ashton Smith, A. Merritt, William Hope Hodgson, Algernon Blackwood, and Lord Dunsany — as well as new writers such as Ray Bradbury, whose first book, Dark Carnival (1947), was an Arkham House title.

Derleth also organised Lovecraft's posthumous "collaborations" and codified the Cthulhu Mythos, publishing much of the material that kept Lovecraft's reputation alive until scholarly editions appeared. After Derleth's death in 1971, the firm continued under James Turner and later other editors, issuing more than 200 titles before its output slowed in the twenty-first century.

Significance

Arkham House is credited with preserving weird fiction in durable hardcover form at a time when the genre was considered ephemeral, and with launching or sustaining the careers of many major fantasy and horror writers. Its editions are highly collectible.
