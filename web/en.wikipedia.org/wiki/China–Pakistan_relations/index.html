China–Pakistan relations

China–Pakistan relations, also known as Sino–Pakistani relations, describe the bilateral relationship between the People's Republic of China and the Islamic Republic of Pakistan. The two countries established diplomatic relations in 1951 and describe their partnership in official language as an "all-weather strategic cooperative partnership", often characterised as an "iron brotherhood".

History

Pakistan was among the first countries to recognise the People's Republic of China. Relations deepened after the Sino-Indian War of 1962 and a 1963 border agreement in which Pakistan ceded its claim to the Trans-Karakoram Tract to China. China supported Pakistan diplomatically and militarily during its wars with India in 1965 and 1971, and Pakistan served as a conduit for the secret 1971 visit by Henry Kissinger that opened U.S.–China rapprochement. The Karakoram Highway, completed in 1979, physically linked the two countries across the Khunjerab Pass.

Economic cooperation

The centrepiece of modern economic ties is the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC), launched in 2015 as a flagship of China's Belt and Road Initiative. CPEC encompasses tens of billions of dollars of investment in power generation, highways, railways and the development of Gwadar Port on the Arabian Sea, giving China a trade route toward the Indian Ocean that bypasses the Strait of Malacca.

Military and strategic ties

China is Pakistan's largest supplier of military equipment. Joint projects include the JF-17 Thunder fighter aircraft and naval vessels such as Type 054A/P frigates. The two countries conduct regular joint exercises, and China has consistently supported Pakistan's position in international forums, while Pakistan supports China on Taiwan, Xinjiang and the South China Sea.
