Ibirapuera Park (Portuguese: Parque Ibirapuera) is an urban park in São Paulo, Brazil. Opened in 1954 for the city's 400th anniversary celebrations, it is one of the largest and most visited parks in Latin America and a major cultural centre, often compared to Central Park in New York. Its name comes from Tupi words meaning "rotten tree" or "old tree", referring to the swampy ground on which it was built.

Design and buildings

The park's pavilions were designed by architect Oscar Niemeyer and the landscaping by Roberto Burle Marx. Its buildings include the Oca, the Bienal Pavilion (Pavilhão Ciccillo Matarazzo), which hosts the São Paulo Biennial art exhibition, the Museum of Modern Art (MAM), the Afro Brazil Museum, the Ibirapuera Auditorium (completed in 2005 from Niemeyer's original design) and a planetarium. The park also contains the Obelisk of São Paulo and the Monument to the Bandeiras by Victor Brecheret, at its entrance.

Use

Ibirapuera receives millions of visitors a year — around 14 million annually — for running, cycling, skateboarding, concerts and exhibitions. In 2020 its administration was concessioned to a private operator (Urbia) for 35 years, while remaining a public park with free entry.
