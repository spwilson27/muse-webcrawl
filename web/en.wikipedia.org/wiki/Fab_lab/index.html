Fab lab

A typical fab lab workshop

A fab lab (fabrication laboratory) is a small-scale workshop offering digital fabrication tools to the public, typically equipped with a standard set of computer-controlled machines: a laser cutter, a CNC milling machine, a sign cutter, 3D printers, and electronics workbenches for circuit production and microcontrollers.

The fab lab concept was developed at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology's Center for Bits and Atoms under Professor Neil Gershenfeld, growing out of the course "How to Make (Almost) Anything". The first fab lab opened in Boston's South End in 2001–2002, and the network has since grown to well over a thousand labs in more than one hundred countries.

Fab Charter and network

Labs that join the international network subscribe to the Fab Charter, which sets out shared principles: public access, responsibility for safety and cleanliness, open sharing of designs and processes, and participation in the global network of labs. Designs created in one lab can be sent as digital files and fabricated identically in another, enabling distributed manufacturing and collaborative development.

The Fab Foundation, established in 2009, supports the network, and the Fab Academy runs a distributed training programme in digital fabrication taught from MIT-linked nodes.

Relation to maker culture

Fab labs are part of the broader maker movement alongside makerspaces and hackerspaces, but are distinguished by their standardised inventory, charter obligations and formal connection to the global network founded at MIT.
