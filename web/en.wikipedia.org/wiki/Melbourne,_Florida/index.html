Melbourne is a city in Brevard County, Florida, United States. It is located 72 miles (116 km) southeast of Orlando and 175 miles (282 km) northwest of Miami, on Florida's Space Coast. The municipality is the second-largest in the county by both size and population. The population was 84,678 at the 2020 census.

The city was named in honor of its first postmaster, Cornthwaite John Hector, an Englishman who had spent much of his life in Melbourne, Victoria, Australia. According to local tradition, the name was drawn by lot from a hat by Hector's daughter.

History

The area was first settled after the Civil War by formerly enslaved people and their descendants, and a small community known as Crane Creek developed at the mouth of the creek on the Indian River. The city was incorporated in 1888 and merged in 1969 with the neighboring city of Eau Gallie. Growth accelerated with the space program at nearby Kennedy Space Center and Cape Canaveral, which drew aerospace and defense employers.

Economy and education

Melbourne's economy is anchored by defense and technology companies, including L3Harris Technologies, which has major operations and its headquarters in the city. The Florida Institute of Technology, founded in 1958, is located in Melbourne and is known for engineering and aviation programs. Melbourne Orlando International Airport serves the region.

Geography

The city lies along the Indian River Lagoon and extends west toward Lake Washington. Its barrier-island portion, sometimes called Melbourne Beachside, fronts the Atlantic Ocean across the causeways.
