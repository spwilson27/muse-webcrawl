Industrial relations

Industrial relations is a multidisciplinary field of study and practice concerned with the employment relationship — the interactions between workers, employers, their representative organisations (such as trade unions and employers' associations) and the state. It encompasses collective bargaining, labour law, workplace conflict, grievance procedures and mechanisms such as arbitration and industrial action.

Scope

The field emerged alongside industrialisation and the growth of trade unionism in the 19th and 20th centuries. Key institutions include collective agreements, works councils, labour courts and statutory minimum standards. National systems vary widely: from the corporatist bargaining models of Scandinavia and Germany, to decentralised enterprise bargaining in the United States and Australia.

Perspectives

Scholars analyse industrial relations through frames of reference such as unitarism (the workplace as a unified team), pluralism (a legitimate balance of competing interests), and radical or Marxist perspectives emphasising structural conflict between labour and capital. Contemporary research also engages with the gig economy, global supply chains and the decline of union density in many countries.
