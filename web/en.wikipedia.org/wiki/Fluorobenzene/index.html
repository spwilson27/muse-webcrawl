Fluorobenzene is the chemical compound with the formula C6H5F, abbreviated PhF. A colorless liquid, it is the simplest of the fluorobenzenes and the parent member of the organofluorine family of aryl fluorides. It is mainly of interest as a precursor to other fluorinated aromatic compounds and as a laboratory solvent.

Preparation

The laboratory synthesis of fluorobenzene uses the Balz–Schiemann reaction, in which an anilinium tetrafluoroborate salt is pyrolyzed to give fluorobenzene, nitrogen, and boron trifluoride. On larger scales, the related route proceeds by thermal decomposition of benzenediazonium tetrafluoroborate. Direct fluorination of benzene is difficult because the reaction with elemental fluorine is violent and tends to give over-fluorinated products.

Uses

Historically, fluorobenzene was used as an insecticide, but its principal modern role is as an intermediate in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and liquid-crystal materials. It is also employed as a solvent and as a medium for studying fluorine substituent effects in aromatic chemistry, since its 19F NMR spectrum gives it value as a spectroscopic reference.

Related compounds

Related halobenzenes include chlorobenzene, bromobenzene, and iodobenzene. Polyfluorinated derivatives such as 1,2-difluorobenzene and pentafluorobenzene share many of its physical properties but differ in reactivity.
