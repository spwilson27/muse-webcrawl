Rostam

Rostam or Rustam (Persian: رستم) is a legendary hero of Persian mythology, the son of Zāl and Rudaba, and the central figure of the Shahnameh, the national epic of Greater Iran composed by Ferdowsi around the turn of the 11th century. For centuries Rostam serves as the champion of Iran, defending its kings against Turan and against demons (divs) and monsters.

Birth and attributes

Rostam's birth was miraculous: his mother Rudaba was delivered by caesarean section on the instructions of the simurgh, the great mythical bird that had raised his father Zāl. The infant Rostam was so large that he was nursed by ten wet nurses, and he grew into a hero of superhuman strength. His inseparable companion is his stallion Rakhsh, the only horse strong enough to bear him, and he fights wearing the Babr Bayan, an invulnerable garment of tiger (or leopard) skin. His great-grandfather is Sam, and his ancestry goes back to the hero Nariman.

The Seven Labours

Early in his career Rostam undertakes the Seven Labours (Haft Khan) to rescue King Kay Kavus, who has been captured by the demons of Mazandaran. The trials include slaying a lion while Rakhsh fights another, surviving a desert by following a ram to water, killing a dragon, defeating a witch, capturing the demon Olad, and finally killing the White Demon (Div-e Sepid) and using its blood to restore the blinded king's sight.

Tragedies

Rostam's story is marked by two great tragedies. In the tale of Rostam and Sohrab, he unknowingly fights and kills his own son Sohrab, the champion of Turan, recognising him only after dealing the fatal wound — one of the most famous episodes of world literature. Later, as an old man, Rostam is himself compelled to kill Esfandiar, the invincible prince sent against him by a jealous king, shooting him in the eyes with a double-pointed arrow of tamarisk wood, as the simurgh had taught him, and incurring a prophecy of his own doom.

Rostam finally falls through treachery: his envious half-brother Shaghad lures him to Kabul and prepares a hunting pit lined with spears, into which Rostam and Rakhsh fall. Dying, Rostam asks for his bow, strings it, and shoots Shaghad through the tree behind which he hides, killing his murderer with his last strength.

Legacy

Rostam remains the supreme hero of Persian culture, a standard of strength and loyalty ("may you be a Rostam" is a blessing), celebrated in miniature painting, in the traditional naqqali storytelling of coffeehouses, and in modern Iranian and Afghan culture.
