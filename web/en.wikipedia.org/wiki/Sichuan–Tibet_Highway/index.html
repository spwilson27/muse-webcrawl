Sichuan–Tibet Highway

Route: Chengdu, Sichuan → Lhasa, Tibet
Length: c. 2,150 km (southern route, G318)
Opened: 25 December 1954 (as the Kangding–Tibet Highway)
Highest point: c. 5,130 m (Dongda La, southern route)

The Sichuan–Tibet Highway is a highway in western China linking Chengdu, the capital of Sichuan province, with Lhasa, the capital of the Tibet Autonomous Region. Originally called the Kangding–Tibet (Kangzang) Highway, it was built by the People's Liberation Army and more than 100,000 workers between 1950 and 1954, opening to traffic on 25 December 1954 — the same day as the Qinghai–Tibet Highway. Today it comprises the southern route, forming part of China National Highway 318 (G318), and the northern route, which follows G317 through Garzê and Chamdo.

Route and terrain

Leaving the Sichuan Basin at Ya'an, the highway climbs onto the Tibetan Plateau through the Hengduan Mountains, crossing fourteen major mountain passes above 4,000 metres and the great gorges of the Jinsha (upper Yangtze), Lancang (Mekong), and Nu (Salween) rivers. The southern route runs about 2,146 km via Kangding, Litang, Batang, Markam, and Nyingchi; the northern route runs about 2,412 km via Garzê and Dege. Both are notorious for landslides, mudslides, washouts, and glacial-lake outbursts — the 2000 Yigong landslide temporarily dammed a river — and sections still close seasonally.

Significance

The highway was the first modern road into Tibet and remains one of its principal supply arteries, cutting the Chengdu–Lhasa journey from months by pack animal to a few days by vehicle, though the drive still commonly takes a week or more for tourists. The southern route (G318) has become a famed overland travel and cycling itinerary, marketed as one of China's most spectacular road journeys, and progressive upgrading — expressway segments, tunnels such as the Erlangshan (1999) and the 7,079-metre Zheduo and other long tunnels, and the Lhasa–Nyingchi high-grade highway — continues to shorten and stabilize the route.
