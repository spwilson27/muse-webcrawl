Wi-Fi Protected Access 3, commonly called WPA3, is the third iteration of the Wi-Fi Alliance's security certification programme for wireless networks, announced in January 2018 and launched in June 2018 as the successor to WPA2. WPA3 is mandatory for Wi-Fi 6E certification and for devices carrying the Wi-Fi CERTIFIED 6 designation.

Improvements over WPA2

WPA3-Personal replaces the pre-shared key (PSK) exchange with Simultaneous Authentication of Equals (SAE), a variant of the Dragonfly key exchange. SAE resists offline dictionary attacks — a captured handshake can no longer be brute-forced at leisure — and provides forward secrecy. WPA3-Enterprise offers an optional 192-bit security mode aligned with the NSA's CNSA suite, using 384-bit Hashed Message Authentication Mode and GCMP-256 encryption.

Two companion features address other settings: Wi-Fi Easy Connect (Device Provisioning Protocol) simplifies onboarding of devices without displays, typically by QR code, while Wi-Fi Enhanced Open (Opportunistic Wireless Encryption) encrypts traffic on open networks without authentication.

Vulnerabilities

In 2019 researchers Mathy Vanhoef and Eyal Ronen disclosed the "Dragonblood" vulnerabilities — side-channel and downgrade attacks against SAE and Easy Connect — prompting patches and revised guidance from the Wi-Fi Alliance. A WPA2/WPA3 transition mode, intended for compatibility, has also been criticised for permitting downgrade attacks.
