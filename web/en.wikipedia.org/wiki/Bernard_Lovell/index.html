Bernard Lovell

Sir Alfred Charles Bernard Lovell (31 August 1913 – 6 August 2012) was a British physicist and radio astronomer. He founded the Jodrell Bank Observatory in Cheshire and oversaw the construction of the 76-metre (250 ft) radio telescope that now bears his name — for many years the largest steerable dish radio telescope in the world. Lovell pioneered the study of cosmic ray showers and meteors by radar, and Jodrell Bank played a celebrated role in tracking the first satellites and space probes.

Career

Lovell studied physics at the University of Bristol and worked on cosmic rays at Manchester before the Second World War, during which he worked on radar, including the H2S airborne radar system. Returning to Manchester in 1945 with surplus radar equipment, he set up at the university's botanical site at Jodrell Bank to observe cosmic-ray air showers; the echoes he detected proved to come from meteor trails, founding meteor radio astronomy.

The Lovell Telescope

Lovell championed a giant fully steerable telescope, completed in 1957 — just in time to track the carrier rocket of Sputnik 1, the only instrument in the West able to do so. The project ran heavily over budget and Lovell faced personal liability before public donations and a government grant cleared the debt. The telescope, renamed the Lovell Telescope in his honour in 1987, tracked Luna and Pioneer probes and remains a working instrument in the e-MERLIN array. Jodrell Bank became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019.
