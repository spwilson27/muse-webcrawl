Isère (French pronunciation: [izɛʁ]) is a department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of southeastern France. It is named after the river Isère, a tributary of the Rhône, and its prefecture is Grenoble. It is the second most populous department in the region after Rhône.

Geography

Isère spans dramatically varied terrain: the Rhône plain in the northwest, the Chartreuse and Vercors pre-Alpine ranges, and high Alpine massifs including Belledonne, the Grandes Rousses and part of the Écrins, with peaks approaching 4,000 m. The department includes renowned ski resorts such as Alpe d'Huez and Les Deux Alpes. Grenoble, ringed by mountains, is a major centre for research, microelectronics and winter sports, and hosted the 1968 Winter Olympics.

History

The department was created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790 from part of the former province of Dauphiné. Grenoble's museum commemorates the Day of the Tiles (1788), an early revolt against royal authority, and the Estates General meeting at Vizille, in Isère, is often called the cradle of the Revolution. Under the regional reforms of 2016 the department joined the enlarged Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region.
