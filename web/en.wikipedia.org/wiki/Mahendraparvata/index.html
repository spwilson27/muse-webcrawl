Mahendraparvata was an ancient city of the Khmer Empire in present-day Cambodia, situated on the Phnom Kulen plateau about 40 kilometres north-east of Angkor. It is identified as the capital established by Jayavarman II, the founder of the Khmer Empire, who is said to have declared Cambodian independence from "Java" there in 802 AD and proclaimed himself chakravartin (universal ruler).

Discovery

The site's existence was long known from inscriptions, but its extent was revealed by airborne laser scanning (lidar) surveys in 2012 and 2015, which penetrated the jungle canopy to expose a vast, formally planned urban landscape of temples, roads, dykes, reservoirs and neighbourhoods covering some 40–50 square kilometres, predating the better-known capital at Angkor.

Layout

The city was organised around a central temple-pyramid dedicated to Shiva, with a grid of axes, water-management infrastructure and dozens of previously unrecorded temples. Archaeologists interpret Mahendraparvata as the first of the Khmer capitals and a template for the hydraulic city-building later perfected at Angkor.

Decline

The court appears to have moved down to the Angkor plain within a few decades under Jayavarman II's successors, though occupation and religious activity on Phnom Kulen — a sacred mountain with the carvings of the River of a Thousand Lingas — continued.
