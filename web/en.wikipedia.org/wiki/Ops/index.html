Ops

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ops or Opis (Latin: "Plenty") is a fertility deity and earth goddess in Sabine and ancient Roman religion. Her equivalent in Greek religion is Rhea, wife of Cronus, and she was identified as the wife of the god Saturn. Ops was associated with abundance, wealth and the harvest, and her name derives from the Latin word ops, meaning riches, goods or abundance — the root of the English word "opulence".

Cult

Ops had a sanctuary on the Capitoline Hill in Rome and a temple in the Forum said to have been vowed during the early Republic. She was worshipped together with Saturn, and her principal festivals were the Opalia on 19 December, during the Saturnalia season, and the Opiconsivia on 25 August, connected with the storing of the harvest. In her cult, worshippers were required to touch the earth while seated, acknowledging her as a goddess of the ground and its produce.

Mythology and iconography

As Saturn's consort, Ops was the mother of Jupiter, Neptune, Pluto, Juno, Ceres and Vesta. Like Rhea, she was credited with saving the infant Jupiter from being devoured by his father. In art she is typically depicted as a matronly figure holding a sheaf of grain or a cornucopia, sometimes accompanied by a lion or seated beside a globe, signifying her dominion over the earth's bounty. In later periods her figure appeared on Roman coinage as a symbol of plenty and imperial prosperity.
