Running of the bulls

The running of the bulls (Spanish: encierro, from the verb encerrar, "to shut in, to pen") is a practice that involves people running in front of a small group of bulls, typically six, that have been let loose on sectioned-off streets of a town, usually as part of a summertime festival. Particular breeds of cattle may be favoured, such as the toro bravo in Spain, also often used in post-run bullfighting.

Pamplona

The most famous encierro is held in Pamplona during the nine-day festival of Sanfermines in honour of Saint Fermin, held every year from 6 to 14 July. Six fighting bulls and six oxen run a course of about 875 metres from corrals outside the old town to the bullring, where the bulls fight that evening. The event was popularised internationally by Ernest Hemingway's 1926 novel The Sun Also Rises.

Danger

The run is dangerous: since 1910, at least 15 people have died in Pamplona, most from gorings, and hundreds are injured each year. Participants must be 18 or older, must run in the same direction as the bulls, and may not incite the bulls.

Other places

Runs are held in many Spanish towns, including Cuéllar, which claims the oldest encierros, as well as in parts of France, Portugal and Mexico, and in adapted form in other countries.
