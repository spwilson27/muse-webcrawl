Incus

The incus (Latin for "anvil") is a small bone in the middle ear of mammals. It is one of the three ossicles — the malleus, incus and stapes — which transmit sound vibrations from the eardrum to the inner ear. The incus sits between the malleus, to which it is tightly jointed, and the stapes, which it contacts through the lenticular process.

Structure

The incus consists of a body and two limbs: a short crus (process) and a long crus ending in the lenticular process. Like the other ossicles, it is covered by mucous membrane and acts as a lever, helping to amplify the pressure of sound waves as they pass from the air-filled middle ear to the fluid-filled cochlea.

Development and function

The incus develops from the first pharyngeal arch. In non-mammalian vertebrates, its homologue is the quadrate bone of the jaw joint — during mammalian evolution, former jaw bones were repurposed for hearing. Damage or fixation of the ossicles can cause conductive hearing loss, sometimes treated by ossiculoplasty.
