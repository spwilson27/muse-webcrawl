Empress Jitō

Empress Jitō (持統天皇, Jitō-tennō)
Reign: 686–697
Predecessor: Tenmu
Successor: Monmu
Born: 645
Died: 703

Empress Jitō (持統天皇, Jitō-tennō, 645 – 22 December 702) was the 41st monarch of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. Jitō's reign spanned the years from 686 through 697. She was the wife and successor of Emperor Tenmu and the third of eight women to take on the role of empress regnant in Japanese history.

Reign

Jitō took the throne after the death of her husband Tenmu in 686, initially ruling as regent in practice while mourning and succession questions were settled, and formally ascending in 690. She continued Tenmu's programme of centralising reforms: the Taihō Code's groundwork was advanced, a census and land-registration system was implemented, and in 694 she moved the capital to Fujiwara-kyō, the first Japanese capital built on the Chinese grid plan.

Jitō is also remembered as a poet; verses attributed to her appear in the Man'yōshū, including the famous poem on Mount Kagu. In 697 she abdicated in favour of her grandson, Emperor Monmu, becoming the first daijō-tennō (retired sovereign) to wield the title in the form that later became customary. She retained considerable influence until her death in 702.
