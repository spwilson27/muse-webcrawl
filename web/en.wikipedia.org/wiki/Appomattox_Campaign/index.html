Appomattox campaign

The pursuit of Lee's army during the Appomattox campaign, April 1865.

The Appomattox campaign was a series of battles fought in Virginia during March 29 – April 9, 1865, in the American Civil War that concluded with the surrender of Confederate General Robert E. Lee's Army of Northern Virginia to forces of the Union Army under the overall command of Lieutenant General Ulysses S. Grant. The surrender at Appomattox Court House effectively ended the war, although other Confederate forces remained in the field for several more weeks.

Background

After the ten-month siege of Petersburg, Grant's forces broke through the Confederate defenses on April 2, 1865, forcing Lee to abandon both Petersburg and the Confederate capital of Richmond. Lee planned to retreat southwest, resupply at Amelia Court House, and march to Danville and then North Carolina to join General Joseph E. Johnston's army.

The pursuit

Union cavalry under Philip Sheridan and infantry of the Army of the Potomac pursued relentlessly, cutting off Lee's supplies and defeating portions of his army at Namozine Church, Amelia Springs, and Sailor's Creek (April 6), where nearly a quarter of Lee's remaining force was captured. Blocked from reaching supplies at Appomattox Station by Sheridan's troopers, and facing overwhelming numbers, Lee agreed to meet Grant.

Surrender

On April 9, 1865, after a final failed breakout attempt at the Battle of Appomattox Court House, Lee surrendered his army in the home of Wilmer McLean in the village of Appomattox Court House. Grant's generous terms allowed Confederate soldiers to return home on parole, and officers were permitted to keep their sidearms and horses. The surrender is generally regarded as the symbolic end of the Civil War.
