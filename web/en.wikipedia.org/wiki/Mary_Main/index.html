Mary Main (1943 – 6 January 2023) was an American developmental psychologist best known for her work in the field of attachment theory. Together with her colleagues at the University of California, Berkeley, she developed the Adult Attachment Interview (AAI) and identified the "disorganized/disoriented" pattern of infant attachment, a fourth category added to Mary Ainsworth's original three patterns derived from the Strange Situation procedure.

Career

Main studied at the University of Virginia and completed her doctorate at Johns Hopkins University, where she worked in the tradition of Mary Ainsworth and John Bowlby. She spent most of her academic career at the University of California, Berkeley.

Research

In the 1980s, Main and her collaborator Judith Solomon identified disorganized attachment in infants who showed contradictory, fearful, or disoriented behaviour in the Strange Situation. With Carol George and Nancy Kaplan, she developed the Adult Attachment Interview, a semi-structured interview that assesses an adult's state of mind with respect to attachment through the coherence of their narrative about childhood experiences. Main showed that a parent's classification on the AAI strongly predicts their infant's attachment pattern, and that "earned security" — coherent narration despite adverse early experiences — was associated with secure attachment in the next generation. Her work linked unresolved states of mind regarding loss or trauma to disorganized attachment in infants.
