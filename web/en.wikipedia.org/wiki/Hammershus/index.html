Hammershus

Hammershus
Location: Bornholm, Denmark
Type: Medieval castle (ruin)
Built: c. 1250

Hammershus is a medieval fortification on the northwest coast of the Danish island of Bornholm. Its ruins, perched on a cliff 74 metres above the Baltic Sea, are the largest medieval castle ruin in northern Europe.

History

The castle was built around 1250, probably by Jakob Erlandsen, Archbishop of Lund, and was contested for centuries between the archbishops, the Danish crown, and the Hanseatic city of Lübeck, which held Bornholm from 1525 to 1576. It served as a fortress, administrative seat and prison — the captive Princess Leonora Christina was held there in 1660–61. Abandoned in 1743, it was quarried for stone until protected in 1822.

Today

Hammershus is Bornholm's most visited attraction, with a visitor centre opened in 2018 overlooking the ruin.
