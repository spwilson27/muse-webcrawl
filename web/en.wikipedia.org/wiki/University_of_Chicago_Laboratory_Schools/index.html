The University of Chicago Laboratory Schools (often called Lab or UCLS) are a private, coeducational day school system in the Hyde Park neighborhood of Chicago, affiliated with the University of Chicago. They comprise a nursery school, lower school, middle school, and high school (U-High) serving more than 2,000 students.

History

The Laboratory School was founded in 1896 by the philosopher and educational reformer John Dewey as a place to test his theories of progressive education — learning by doing, integrated curricula, and the school as a social community. Dewey directed the school until 1904. His laboratory school became one of the most influential experimental schools in the history of American education.

Academics and alumni

The schools serve many children of University of Chicago faculty as well as other Chicago families. Notable alumni include the daughters of President Barack Obama, who attended Lab while he taught at the university's law school. The Earl Shapiro Hall campus for the youngest students opened in 2013.
