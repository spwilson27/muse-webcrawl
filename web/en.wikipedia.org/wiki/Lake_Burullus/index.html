Lake Burullus


Fishing boats on Lake Burullus

Lake Burullus (Arabic: بحيرة البرلس, Buḥayrat al-Burulus) is a brackish water lake in the Nile Delta of Egypt, lying between the Rosetta and Damietta branches of the Nile along the Mediterranean coast. It covers roughly 420–460 square kilometres, making it one of the largest of the northern delta lakes, and is separated from the sea by a narrow sand barrier pierced by a single outlet near the town of El Burg.

Environment

The lake receives agricultural drainage water from the southern farmlands, which has progressively freshened its western parts and encouraged vast reed beds of Phragmites and Typha. Burullus was designated a protected area in 1998 and is listed as a Ramsar wetland of international importance: it is a major wintering and staging ground for migratory waterbirds, and supports an important fishery for tilapia, mullet and catfish. Pressures on the lake include encroachment by fish farms, pollution from drainage water and land reclamation that has shrunk its open water area.
