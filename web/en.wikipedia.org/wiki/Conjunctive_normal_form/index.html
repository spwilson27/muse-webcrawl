Conjunctive normal form

In Boolean logic, a formula is in conjunctive normal form (CNF) or clausal normal form if it is a conjunction of one or more clauses, where a clause is a disjunction of literals (a variable or its negation); otherwise put, it is a product of sums. For example:

(A ∨ ¬B ∨ C) ∧ (¬D ∨ E ∨ F) ∧ (G)

Conversion

Every propositional formula can be converted into an equivalent formula in CNF. The conversion uses logical equivalences: double negation elimination, De Morgan's laws, and the distributive law. However, naive conversion can cause an exponential blow-up in the size of the formula; the Tseytin transformation produces an equisatisfiable CNF formula of linear size by introducing fresh variables.

Applications

CNF is the standard input format for SAT solvers, typically encoded in the DIMACS file format. A CNF formula in which each clause has at most three literals is the subject of the 3-SAT problem, which is NP-complete. Clauses with at most one positive literal are called Horn clauses, and satisfiability for Horn formulas can be decided in polynomial time.

First-order logic

In first-order logic, conjunctive normal form is used together with Skolemization as a step in automated theorem proving, notably in resolution-based provers: refuting a formula in CNF proceeds by deriving the empty clause.
