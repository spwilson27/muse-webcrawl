Edward R. Murrow (1908–1965) was an American broadcast journalist whose live radio reports from Blitz-era London ('This is London') made him famous, and whose television programme See It Now confronted Senator Joseph McCarthy in 1954 — a landmark in broadcast history.

Career

Murrow built CBS's European news operation, reported from rooftops during the Blitz and on liberation of Buchenwald, then moved to television with See It Now (with producer Fred Friendly) and the interview series Person to Person. His 1958 RTNDA speech warned that television was becoming 'merely wires and lights in a box' used to distract and insulate.

Later life

He directed the US Information Agency under President Kennedy from 1961, resigning due to ill health; he died of lung cancer in 1965.
