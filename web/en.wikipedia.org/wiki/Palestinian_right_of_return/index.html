The Palestinian right of return is the position that Palestinian refugees of the 1948 Arab–Israeli war, and their descendants, have a right to return to the homes and property from which they fled or were expelled in what became the State of Israel. It is one of the most contentious issues in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and a core Palestinian national demand.

Background

During the 1948 war, more than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs — over half the Arab population of Mandatory Palestine — fled or were expelled from their homes; Palestinians commemorate these events as the Nakba ("catastrophe"). Villages were depopulated and in many cases destroyed or repopulated by Jewish immigrants, and refugee camps were established in the West Bank, Gaza Strip, Jordan, Lebanon, and Syria. Israel's Absentees' Property Law (1950) transferred refugee property to state custodianship. The United Nations Relief and Works Agency (UNRWA), founded in 1949, registers the refugees and their descendants, a population now numbering several million.

Resolution 194 and legal arguments

UN General Assembly Resolution 194, adopted on 11 December 1948, resolved in paragraph 11 that "refugees wishing to return to their homes and live at peace with their neighbours should be permitted to do so at the earliest practicable date", and that compensation should be paid for the property of those choosing not to return. The resolution has been reaffirmed by the General Assembly many times. Supporters of the right of return cite it, together with the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (Article 13) and international humanitarian law, as establishing an individual right. Successive Israeli governments have rejected a general return of refugees to Israel, arguing that Resolution 194 is non-binding and conditional, that the issue should be settled by negotiation within a two-state framework, and that implementing a full return would end Israel's character as a Jewish state; Israeli positions have generally offered return to a future Palestinian state, family reunification in limited numbers, and compensation mechanisms.

Negotiations

The refugee issue was designated a "final status" issue in the Oslo Accords. It was addressed at the Camp David Summit (2000), in the Clinton Parameters (2000), which proposed return primarily to a Palestinian state with an international compensation fund, and in the Taba talks (2001) and the unofficial Geneva Initiative. No agreement has been reached. The claim is symbolised for many Palestinians by the keys and deeds of lost homes kept by refugee families across generations.
