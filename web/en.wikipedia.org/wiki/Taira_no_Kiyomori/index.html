Taira no Kiyomori

Taira no Kiyomori (1118–1181)
Clan: Taira
Office: Daijō-daijin (Chancellor)
Era: Late Heian period

Taira no Kiyomori (平 清盛, 1118 – March 20, 1181) was a military leader and courtier of late Heian-period Japan. He established the first samurai-dominated administration in Japanese history, paving the way for the later shogunates.

Rise to power

Son of Taira no Tadamori, Kiyomori distinguished himself in the Hōgen Rebellion (1156) and Heiji Rebellion (1159–1160), defeating the rival Minamoto clan. In 1167 he became Daijō-daijin, the highest court office, the first warrior to hold it. He placed relatives throughout the court, married his daughter Tokuko to Emperor Takakura, and became grandfather of Emperor Antoku.

Rule and wealth

Kiyomori grew rich on trade with Song-dynasty China through the Inland Sea port of Fukuhara (modern Kobe), where he briefly moved the capital in 1180. His Itsukushima Shrine patronage on Miyajima reflected Taira devotion and prestige.

Fall

Taira arrogance provoked the Genpei War (1180–1185). Kiyomori died of fever in 1181, reportedly so feverish that water boiled around his body in legend. Four years later the Taira were destroyed at the Battle of Dan-no-ura. His dramatic life is retold in The Tale of the Heike.
