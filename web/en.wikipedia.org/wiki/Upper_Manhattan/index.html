Upper Manhattan is the northern portion of the New York City borough of Manhattan, generally taken to be the part of the island north of 96th Street (some definitions use 110th Street or 125th Street). Largely residential in character, it contains some of Manhattan's most historically significant and diverse neighbourhoods, as well as its highest natural ground.

Neighbourhoods

Harlem, long the centre of African American culture — celebrated in the Harlem Renaissance of the 1920s — occupies the central part of Upper Manhattan; East Harlem (El Barrio) has historic Italian and Puerto Rican communities. To the northwest, Washington Heights is home to one of the largest Dominican communities outside the Dominican Republic, while Inwood, at the island's tip, retains a semi-suburban feel. Marble Hill, politically part of Manhattan, is attached to the mainland Bronx by landfill. Morningside Heights hosts Columbia University and the Cathedral of St. John the Divine.

Geography and landmarks

The terrain rises to the Manhattan schist ridge along the Hudson, crowned by Fort Tryon Park and The Cloisters museum of medieval art. Other landmarks include the Apollo Theater, the Morris–Jumel Mansion (Manhattan's oldest surviving house), Grant's Tomb, the Little Red Lighthouse beneath the George Washington Bridge, and Inwood Hill Park, Manhattan's last natural forest, with caves used by the Lenape. The area saw major fighting in the Battle of Fort Washington (1776), a British victory in the Revolutionary War.
