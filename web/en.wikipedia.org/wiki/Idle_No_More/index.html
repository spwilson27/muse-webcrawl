Idle No More

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Idle No More is an ongoing protest movement, founded in December 2012 by four women: three Indigenous (Nina Wilson, Sheelah McLean and Sylvia McAdam) and one non-Indigenous ally (Jessica Gordon) in Saskatchewan, Canada. It is a grassroots movement among Indigenous peoples in Canada and their non-Indigenous supporters, set in motion by Indigenous rights and environmental advocates.

Origins

The movement began with teach-ins in Saskatchewan in November 2012 in response to the federal government's omnibus Bill C-45, whose amendments to the Indian Act, the Navigation Protection Act and the Environmental Assessment Act were seen as eroding Indigenous sovereignty and environmental protections for waterways. A National Day of Action on 10 December 2012, and Attawapiskat Chief Theresa Spence's hunger strike near Parliament Hill in Ottawa, drew national attention.

Activities

Idle No More spread through social media (the hashtag #IdleNoMore) and expressed itself in round dances and flash mobs in shopping malls, railway and road blockades, marches and teach-ins across Canada, with solidarity actions internationally. Its stated vision calls for Indigenous sovereignty (nation-to-nation relations), protection of land and water, and the honouring of treaties.

Legacy

The movement is widely credited with reinvigorating Indigenous political activism in Canada and influencing later campaigns over pipelines, missing and murdered Indigenous women, and resource development on Indigenous territories.
