Qahal

Qahal (Hebrew: קָהָל, romanised qāhāl) is a Hebrew term for an assembly or congregation. In the Hebrew Bible it denotes the gathered community of Israel, and it is one of the words translated "church" (ekklesia) in the Septuagint.

The kehilla in Eastern Europe

In the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and later in the Russian Empire, the kehilla (Yiddish: kohol) was the organised, semi-autonomous local Jewish community. Governed by elected elders, it collected taxes, maintained synagogues, schools, ritual baths and cemeteries, ran courts and charities, and represented the community before the authorities. The Council of Four Lands coordinated kehillot at a supra-communal level until its abolition in 1764; the Russian government formally abolished the qahal system in 1844, though communal institutions continued informally.

Modern usage

Today kehillah is used broadly for a Jewish congregation or community.
