Sima Yi

Sima Yi (179 – 7 September 251), courtesy name Zhongda, was a Chinese military general, politician, and regent of the state of Cao Wei during the Three Kingdoms period of China. He is best known for defending Wei against a series of invasions by its rival state Shu Han, commanded by Zhuge Liang, and for seizing control of the Wei government in the Incident at the Gaoping Tombs in 249, laying the foundations for the Jin dynasty founded by his grandson Sima Yan. Posthumously, he was honoured as Emperor Xuan of Jin.

Early life and career

Sima Yi was born in 179 in Wen County, Henei Commandery, into a prominent family of officials. Renowned in his youth for his learning and ability, he initially declined invitations to serve the warlord Cao Cao but eventually entered service in 208. He became a trusted adviser to Cao Cao's son Cao Pi, and after Cao Pi founded the state of Wei in 220, Sima Yi rose through the ranks of the civil administration and the military.

Campaigns

Sima Yi's military reputation was made in a series of campaigns. In 228 he captured and executed the defector Meng Da before he could rebel. From 231 to 234 he commanded the Wei defences against Zhuge Liang's northern expeditions, adopting a cautious strategy of attrition — refusing battle and wearing down the invading Shu army until Zhuge Liang died at the Battle of Wuzhang Plains in 234. In 238 he conquered the Gongsun clan's state of Yan in Liaodong, extinguishing the last semi-independent regime in the north.

Regency and coup

On the death of the emperor Cao Rui in 239, Sima Yi became co-regent for the young Cao Fang alongside Cao Shuang, who progressively marginalised him. Sima Yi feigned senility and illness, then in 249 struck in the Incident at the Gaoping Tombs: while Cao Shuang accompanied the emperor on a visit to the imperial tombs, Sima Yi seized the capital Luoyang and forced Cao Shuang's surrender; Cao Shuang and his associates were executed. Thereafter Sima Yi controlled the Wei court, suppressing a rebellion by Wang Ling in 251 shortly before his own death that year.

Legacy

Sima Yi's descendants completed the usurpation he had begun: his son Sima Zhao dominated Wei and conquered Shu, and his grandson Sima Yan forced the last Wei emperor to abdicate in 266, founding the Jin dynasty and later reunifying China. In the 14th-century novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms and in popular culture, Sima Yi is portrayed as Zhuge Liang's great rival — patient, calculating and ruthless. He was posthumously enshrined as Emperor Xuan, with the temple name Gaozu.
