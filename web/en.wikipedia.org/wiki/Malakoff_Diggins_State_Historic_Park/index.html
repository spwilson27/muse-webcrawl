Malakoff Diggins State Historic Park is a state park in the Sierra Nevada foothills of Nevada County, California, United States. It preserves the site of California's largest hydraulic gold mine and the ghost town of North Bloomfield. The park covers 3,143 acres (1,272 ha) and its centrepiece is the Malakoff Diggins pit, a colourful canyon of eroded cliffs some 600 feet (180 m) deep and about a mile long, created by hydraulic mining in the 19th century.

History

Hydraulic mining at the site was operated by the North Bloomfield Gravel Mining Company from the 1860s. High-pressure water cannons called "monitors" washed entire hillsides through sluices to recover gold, producing vast quantities of debris that choked downstream rivers and farmland in the Sacramento Valley. The resulting conflict between miners and farmers led to the landmark federal court decision in Woodruff v. North Bloomfield Gravel Mining Company (1884), in which Judge Lorenzo Sawyer issued an injunction effectively ending large-scale hydraulic mining in California — one of the earliest environmental rulings in the United States.

The park today

The townsite of North Bloomfield, with its restored buildings including a drugstore, barbershop, general store and the Skidmore house, is interpreted as a museum. The park offers hiking trails around the diggins, a campground at Chute Hill, and tours during the summer season. Humbug Creek flows through the park.
