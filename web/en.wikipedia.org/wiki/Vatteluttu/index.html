Vatteluttu


A Vatteluttu inscription

Vatteluttu (Tamil: வட்டெழுத்து, Vaṭṭeḻuttu, "rounded script") is an abugida writing system that developed in South India from the southern Brahmi tradition. Named for its rounded letterforms—suited to writing with a stylus on palm leaves without tearing them—it was used from roughly the 4th–5th centuries CE to write Tamil, and later Malayalam, in the regions of present-day Tamil Nadu and Kerala.

History

Vatteluttu emerged as the cursive, everyday counterpart of the more angular Tamil-Brahmi and Pallava scripts. It is abundant in inscriptions of the Pandya and Chera kingdoms and in early records from Kerala, where it remained the principal script for centuries. In the Tamil country it was gradually displaced by the modern Tamil script, standardized under the Cholas around the 10th–11th centuries, while in Kerala Vatteluttu evolved further, absorbing Grantha letters for Sanskrit sounds, on the path toward the modern Malayalam script.

Characteristics

Like other Brahmic scripts, Vatteluttu writes consonants with an inherent vowel modified by diacritic signs. Its repertoire initially lacked letters for some Sanskrit consonants, a limitation that prompted the addition of Grantha characters in mixed inscriptions known as Manipravalam. The script's cursive forms can be difficult to distinguish—several letters differ only subtly—making Vatteluttu epigraphy a specialized field.
