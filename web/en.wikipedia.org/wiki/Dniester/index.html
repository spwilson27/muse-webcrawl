Dniester

The Dniester (Romanian: Nistru; Ukrainian: Дністер, romanized: Dnister) is a transboundary river in Eastern Europe. It rises in Ukraine, near the village of Vovche in the Carpathian Mountains, flows through Moldova (including the breakaway region of Transnistria), and empties into the Black Sea via the Dniester Estuary in Ukraine's Odesa Oblast. It is about 1,362 km (846 mi) long.

Geography

The Dniester drains a basin of about 72,100 km². Major tributaries include the Stryi, Zbruch, Seret, Bystrytsia, Răut, and Bîc. Important cities along its course include Khotyn, Mohyliv-Podilskyi, Soroca, Dubăsari, Bender, and Tiraspol. The Dubăsari hydroelectric dam lies on the river in Moldova.

History

Known in antiquity as the Tyras, the river has long formed a frontier: between Dacians and Scythians, later between the Ottoman and Polish-Lithuanian spheres, between the Russian Empire and the Ottomans, and during the interwar period between Romania and the Soviet Union. Its lower course remains politically sensitive because of the Transnistria conflict.
