Geology of Iran

Key facts
Highest peakMount Damavand (5,610 m)
Major rangesZagros, Alborz
SettingArabian–Eurasian collision zone

The geology of Iran reflects the ongoing collision between the Arabian Plate and the Eurasian Plate, which has folded and faulted the region into high mountain belts surrounding a central plateau. Iran lies within the Alpine–Himalayan orogenic belt and experiences frequent damaging earthquakes.

Major structural zones

The Zagros Mountains in the southwest form a great fold-and-thrust belt overlying one of the world's richest petroleum provinces. The Alborz range arcs along the Caspian coast and includes Mount Damavand, a dormant stratovolcano and Iran's highest peak. Between the ranges lie the deserts of the central plateau, including the Dasht-e Kavir salt desert and the Lut Desert. Eastern Iran is cut by major strike-slip fault systems such as those responsible for the 1978 Tabas and 2003 Bam earthquakes.

Resources

Iran holds vast reserves of oil and natural gas, concentrated in the Zagros foreland and the Persian Gulf, along with copper, iron, and other mineral deposits in the central volcanic belts.
