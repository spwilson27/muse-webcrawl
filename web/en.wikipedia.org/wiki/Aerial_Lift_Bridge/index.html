The Aerial Lift Bridge, earlier known as the Aerial Ferry Bridge, is a landmark vertical-lift bridge spanning the Duluth Ship Canal in Duluth, Minnesota, connecting the city with Minnesota Point (Park Point). It is one of the city's most recognizable symbols and a popular vantage point for watching Great Lakes freighters enter and leave the harbour.

History

The bridge opened in 1905 as one of the few transporter (ferry) bridges ever built in the United States: a gondola suspended from a high steel truss carried passengers and vehicles across the canal so as not to obstruct shipping. Growing traffic outstripped the gondola's capacity, and in 1929–1930 the structure was converted into a vertical-lift bridge — the deck was replaced by a lift span that rises between the original towers to let ships pass beneath, with the raised span providing about 135 feet of clearance.

Operation

The bridge lifts on demand for marine traffic, roughly a thousand times per year, halting road traffic for each passage. The bridge and the canal are central to Duluth's identity as the largest inland port in the world by tonnage, handling iron ore, coal, and grain shipments across Lake Superior.
