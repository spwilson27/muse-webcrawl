The Battle of Shusha (Azerbaijani: Şuşa döyüşü) was the final decisive battle of the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War, fought between the armed forces of Azerbaijan and the self-proclaimed Republic of Artsakh, supported by Armenia, for control of the city of Shusha (Shushi). Azerbaijani forces captured the city on 8 November 2020; the loss of the strategically and culturally vital city led Armenia to accept a ceasefire two days later.

Background

Shusha occupies a plateau overlooking the Lachin corridor and the Artsakh capital Stepanakert, only about 11 kilometres away. Azerbaijani forces advancing from the south through Jabrayil, Fuzuli and Hadrut reached the outskirts of Shusha in early November, fighting through mountainous forests in foggy conditions.

The battle

Azerbaijani special forces and infantry infiltrated the city, scaling the cliffs and fighting street-to-street against Armenian defenders, while Armenian forces mounted counterattacks including missile strikes. On 8 November President Ilham Aliyev announced that Azerbaijani forces had taken control of Shusha; Artsakh's leadership initially denied this but acknowledged losing the city the following day.

Aftermath

The fall of Shusha made the defence of Stepanakert untenable. On 9–10 November a Russian-brokered ceasefire statement was signed, ending the war; Azerbaijan retained its captured territories and Armenia agreed to withdraw from surrounding districts. Shusha holds deep cultural significance for both Azerbaijanis and Armenians, and 8 November was declared Victory Day in Azerbaijan.
