The M6 motorway is the longest motorway in the United Kingdom. It runs from the M1 motorway at Catthorpe in Leicestershire, north through the West Midlands and North West England, to the Scottish border near Carlisle, where it continues as the A74(M). Together with the M74, it forms the principal road route between London and Glasgow.

History

The M6 incorporates the Preston Bypass, which opened on 5 December 1958 as the first motorway in the United Kingdom. The main length of the motorway was built in stages during the 1960s and early 1970s, including the Birmingham Northern Relief sections and the busy elevated stretches through Birmingham, including the Gravelly Hill Interchange — popularly known as "Spaghetti Junction" — which opened in 1972. The final section to the Scottish border was completed in 2008 when the Cumberland Gap was closed.

Route

The motorway passes east of Birmingham, where the tolled M6 Toll provides a relief route, then north past Stoke-on-Trent, through Cheshire and Lancashire — including the Thelwall Viaduct over the Manchester Ship Canal — and on through Cumbria, crossing the Lune Gorge near Tebay, one of the most scenic stretches of motorway in England.
