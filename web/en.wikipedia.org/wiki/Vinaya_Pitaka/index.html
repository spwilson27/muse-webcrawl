Vinaya Pitaka

The Vinaya Pitaka (Pali: Vinaya Piṭaka; Sanskrit: विनय पिटक, "Basket of Discipline") is a Buddhist scripture, one of the three parts of the Tripiṭaka (Pali Canon) of Theravada Buddhism. It contains the rules and procedures governing the communal life of the Sangha — the community of Buddhist monks (bhikkhus) and nuns (bhikkhunis).

Contents

The Pali Vinaya is divided into three parts. The Suttavibhaṅga comments on the Pātimokkha, the code of 227 rules for monks and 311 rules for nuns, giving the origin story and analysis of each rule. The Khandhaka (Mahāvagga and Cullavagga) contains rules for communal procedures such as ordination, the rains retreat, and the settling of disputes, along with accounts of the early history of the Sangha. The Parivāra is a later summary and catechism of the whole.

Versions of the Vinaya also survive in the canons of other early Buddhist schools — including the Dharmaguptaka, Mahāsāṃghika, Mahīśāsaka, Sarvāstivāda, and Mūlasarvāstivāda traditions — which differ in the number and arrangement of rules. The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya, used in Tibetan Buddhism, is much larger and includes extensive narrative material.

Function

The Vinaya is recited communally at the fortnightly Uposatha observance and remains the practical legal foundation of monastic life across Buddhist traditions.
