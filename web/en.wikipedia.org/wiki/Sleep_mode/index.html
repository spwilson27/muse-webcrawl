Sleep mode

Sleep mode (sometimes called standby, suspend, or suspend to RAM) is a low-power state that computers and other electronic devices can enter when not in use. In sleep mode, the contents of random-access memory (RAM) are preserved by maintaining power to the memory, while most other components — the processor, storage drives and display — are powered down or placed in their lowest-power states. This allows the device to resume full operation within seconds, far faster than booting from off or restoring from hibernation.

ACPI states

Under the ACPI (Advanced Configuration and Power Interface) standard, sleep corresponds principally to state S3 ("suspend to RAM"). Lighter states (S1, S2) keep progressively more of the system powered, while S4 is hibernation, in which memory contents are written to non-volatile storage and power can be removed entirely. Many modern laptops implement "Modern Standby" (S0 low-power idle), which behaves more like a smartphone's sleep, maintaining network connectivity while the screen is off.

Behaviour and wake events

Devices can be woken from sleep by keyboard or mouse input, opening the lid, pressing the power button, by timers, or remotely over a network (Wake-on-LAN). Because RAM continues to draw power, a sleeping laptop will eventually drain its battery over days, and many operating systems automatically transition to hibernation when the battery runs low or after a set time. Hybrid sleep, common on desktops, writes the memory image to disk as insurance against power loss while still suspending to RAM.

Energy considerations

Sleep mode drastically cuts power consumption compared with leaving a machine idle and running — typically to a few watts — and energy labelling schemes such as Energy Star specify maximum sleep power for certified devices. Nonetheless, regulators and energy agencies note that vast numbers of devices sleeping or in standby still consume a meaningful cumulative amount of electricity, sometimes termed "vampire power" for true standby loads.
