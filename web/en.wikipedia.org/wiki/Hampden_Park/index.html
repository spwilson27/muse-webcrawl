Hampden Park (often referred to simply as Hampden) is a football stadium in the Mount Florida district of Glasgow, Scotland, and the national stadium of Scottish football. It is the home ground of the Scotland national football team and the traditional venue for the finals of the Scottish Cup and Scottish League Cup. The present stadium, opened in 1903, is the third ground to bear the name, the first having hosted the world's first international match at nearby Hamilton Crescent in the Hampden tradition of the original Queen's Park club.

History

Queen's Park, Scotland's oldest football club, built the current Hampden Park, which opened on 31 October 1903 with a match against Celtic. For much of the 20th century Hampden was among the largest stadiums in the world: its record crowd of 149,415 for a Scotland–England match in 1937 remains the European record attendance for an international football match, and 136,505 watched the 1960 European Cup Final in which Real Madrid beat Eintracht Frankfurt 7–3 — often cited as one of the greatest matches ever played. The stadium also hosted the 1976 and 2002 European Cup/Champions League finals and the 2007 UEFA Cup Final.

Redevelopment

Falling into disrepair by the 1980s, Hampden was extensively rebuilt in the 1990s with government and lottery funding, reopening in 1999 as an all-seater stadium with a capacity just under 52,000. For the 2014 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow its pitch was raised on stilts above a temporary running track to host the athletics programme and ceremonies, after which it was restored to a football configuration. The ground also incorporates the Scottish Football Museum and the Scottish Sports Hall of Fame. In 2018 the Scottish Football Association agreed to buy the stadium from Queen's Park, securing its future as the national stadium amid debate about relocating Scotland matches to Murrayfield in Edinburgh. Hampden hosted matches at UEFA Euro 2020 and is among the venues selected for UEFA Euro 2028.

Other uses

Beyond football, Hampden has staged rugby league and American football (including the NFL's Scottish Claymores at the earlier Hampden era and World Bowl games), speedway, and major rock concerts by artists such as U2, Oasis, the Rolling Stones and Beyoncé. Its name — taken from Hampden Terrace, itself named for the English parliamentarian John Hampden — is shared with Hampden Park in Eastbourne and other grounds around the world.
