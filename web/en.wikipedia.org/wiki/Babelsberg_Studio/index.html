Babelsberg Studio

Babelsberg Studio (German: Filmstudio Babelsberg) is a film studio complex in Potsdam-Babelsberg, Germany. Founded in 1912, it is the oldest large-scale film studio in the world and one of the largest in Europe, with a site of about 46 hectares and some 21 sound stages. Classic films produced there include Fritz Lang's Metropolis (1927) and Josef von Sternberg's The Blue Angel (1930), and it remains a major hub for German and international production.

History

Production began in 1912 in a glasshouse studio built on the site. The complex became the centre of the UFA conglomerate in the Weimar era, producing The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari (at nearby facilities), Metropolis, M and Marlene Dietrich's breakthrough The Blue Angel. Under the Nazis it produced propaganda as well as entertainment films. After 1946 it was the home of DEFA, the state studio of East Germany, and after reunification it was privatised and rebuilt as Studio Babelsberg. Recent productions filmed there include Inglourious Basterds, The Grand Budapest Hotel, Bridge of Spies, The Matrix Resurrections and the series Babylon Berlin and Dark.
