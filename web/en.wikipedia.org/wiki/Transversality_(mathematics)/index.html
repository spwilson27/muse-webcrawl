Transversality (mathematics)

In mathematics, transversality is a notion that describes how spaces can intersect. Two submanifolds of a given space are said to intersect transversally if at every point of intersection their separate tangent spaces generate the tangent space of the ambient space. Intuitively, transverse spaces intersect "as expected": they do not merely touch tangentially, and the dimension of the intersection is the sum of their dimensions minus the dimension of the ambient space.

Definition

Given submanifolds A and B of a manifold M, they intersect transversally at a point p if the tangent spaces TpA and TpB together span TpM. The notion extends to maps: a map is transverse to a submanifold if its image meets the submanifold transversally in this sense.

Importance

Transversality is a generic condition: by Thom's transversality theorem, transverse intersections are typical in the sense that maps can usually be slightly perturbed to achieve them. This makes transversality a fundamental tool in differential topology, where it underlies the definitions of intersection numbers, cobordism invariants and the stability of the geometry of intersections under perturbation. Non-transverse intersections, such as a curve tangent to a surface, can change their nature under arbitrarily small perturbations, while transverse intersections are robust.
