Fine dining

Fine dining Characteristics: full table service, tasting menus, high-quality ingredients, formal atmosphere
Guides: Michelin Guide, World's 50 Best Restaurants
Related concepts: haute cuisine, white-tablecloth restaurant

Fine dining is a restaurant experience that is generally considered more sophisticated, elegant, and expensive than casual dining. Fine dining establishments typically offer multi-course meals prepared from high-quality, often seasonal ingredients, served by formally trained staff in carefully designed interiors, with attention to presentation, wine service, and etiquette.

Characteristics

Fine dining restaurants usually provide full table service, reservations, and menus ranging from à la carte selections to fixed-price tasting menus, sometimes with wine pairings. Dishes emphasize technique and plating, and pricing reflects ingredient cost, labour, and ambience. Dress codes, once common, have relaxed in many modern establishments.

History

The tradition descends from French haute cuisine, codified in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries by chefs such as Auguste Escoffier, and from the grand hotel restaurants of Europe. The Michelin Guide, first published in 1900, became the most influential arbiter of fine dining quality, awarding up to three stars. In the late twentieth century, nouvelle cuisine and later movements such as modernist cuisine and the New Nordic movement redefined the genre.

Criticism

Fine dining has been criticised for pretentiousness, cost, small portions, and poor labour practices in some kitchens; contemporary chefs have responded with more casual formats, open kitchens, and a focus on sustainability and local sourcing.
