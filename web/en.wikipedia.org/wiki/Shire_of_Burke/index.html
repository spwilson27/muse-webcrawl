Shire of Burke

The Shire of Burke is a local government area in the far north-west of Queensland, Australia, in the Gulf Country region bordering the Gulf of Carpentaria and the Northern Territory. The shire's administrative centre is the town of Burketown, and the shire is named for the explorer Robert O'Hara Burke, who crossed the area on the Burke and Wills expedition of 1860–61.

Geography

The shire covers an area of roughly 40,000 square kilometres of savanna, river systems, and coastal plains along the southern shore of the Gulf of Carpentaria. Rivers crossing the shire include the Nicholson, Gregory, and Albert rivers. The small settlement of Gregory (formerly Gregory Downs) is the other main population centre besides Burketown.

Economy and attractions

The economy is based mainly on cattle grazing, fishing, and tourism. The region is known for barramundi fishing and for the Morning Glory cloud phenomenon, a rare rolling cloud formation that passes over the southern Gulf in the spring and attracts gliders and spectators to Burketown. The area also includes parts of Boodjamulla (Lawn Hill) National Park nearby, renowned for its creek gorges and fossil fields at Riversleigh.

Population

The shire has a small population of only a few hundred residents, with a significant proportion of Aboriginal people, including the Gangalidda and Garawa peoples, whose traditional lands lie in the region.
