Lake Natron is a salt and soda lake in the Arusha Region of northern Tanzania, close to the Kenyan border, in the eastern branch of the East African Rift. Fed by the Southern Ewaso Ng'iro River and mineral-rich hot springs, it has no outlet, and evaporation leaves water of extreme alkalinity, with pH as high as 10.5.

Ecology

Despite—or because of—its harsh chemistry, the lake is the most important breeding site in the world for the lesser flamingo: up to 2.5 million birds nest on its soda flats in good years. The red and pink colours of the shallows come from haloarchaea and cyanobacteria. The lake is overlooked by Ol Doinyo Lengai, an active volcano and sacred mountain of the Maasai. The lake's name comes from natron (sodium carbonate decahydrate), historically harvested from its shores. Plans for a soda-ash plant and hydroelectric dam on the Ewaso Ng'iro have raised conservation concerns.
