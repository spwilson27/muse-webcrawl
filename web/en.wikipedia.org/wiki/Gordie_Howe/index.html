Gordie Howe

Gordon Howe (March 31, 1928 – June 10, 2016) was a Canadian professional ice hockey player. From 1946 to 1980, he played 26 seasons in the National Hockey League (NHL) and six seasons in the World Hockey Association (WHA). A Detroit Red Wings player for the first 25 years of his NHL career, Howe is often considered one of the greatest hockey players of all time, and is nicknamed "Mr. Hockey."

Early life

Howe was born in a farmhouse in Floral, Saskatchewan, one of nine children of Albert and Katherine Howe. The family moved to Saskatoon when he was an infant. He grew up in poverty during the Great Depression, and was big and strong for his age. He began playing organized hockey as a boy, and at 15 was invited to the New York Rangers' training camp, though he left homesick. The following year he signed with the Detroit Red Wings organization and played junior hockey in Galt, Ontario, before turning professional with the Omaha Knights of the United States Hockey League in 1945.

Playing career

Detroit Red Wings

Howe made his NHL debut on October 16, 1946, scoring a goal against Toronto. He became a fixture on Detroit's famous "Production Line" with Sid Abel and Ted Lindsay. He led the NHL in scoring five consecutive seasons from 1950–51 to 1953–54 and again in 1956–57 and 1962–63, winning six Art Ross Trophies and six Hart Memorial Trophies as league MVP. With Detroit he won the Stanley Cup four times (1950, 1952, 1954, 1955).

In the 1950 playoffs Howe suffered a fractured skull in a collision in the first game, requiring emergency surgery, yet returned the next season to lead the league in scoring. Remarkably durable, he finished in the top five in scoring for 20 consecutive seasons. Famous for his elbows and toughness as much as his scoring, the phrase "Gordie Howe hat trick" — a goal, an assist and a fight in the same game — entered hockey vocabulary, though Howe himself achieved it only twice.

WHA and final years

Howe retired from the Red Wings in 1971 and took a front-office job, but in 1973 he returned to play in the new WHA with the Houston Aeros, alongside his sons Mark and Marty. He won the league's MVP award in 1974 and led Houston to two Avco Cup championships. After the Aeros folded, he joined the New England Whalers; when the WHA merged with the NHL in 1979, the franchise became the Hartford Whalers, and Howe played one final NHL season in 1979–80 at age 51–52, remarkably playing all 80 games. He finished his professional career having played in parts of five different decades.

In 1997, at age 69, Howe signed a one-game contract with the Detroit Vipers of the International Hockey League and took one shift, extending his record to having played professional hockey in six decades.

Records and legacy

When he retired, Howe held NHL records for most goals (801) and most points (1,850), records later surpassed by Wayne Gretzky. Including WHA totals, Howe scored 869 professional goals and 2,358 points. He played 1,767 NHL regular-season games, a record later broken by Patrick Marleau. He was inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame in 1972. The Gordie Howe International Bridge between Detroit and Windsor, Ontario, was named in his honor; the Howe family's fight for the naming became a prominent public campaign before construction began in 2018.

Personal life and death

Howe married Colleen Joffa in 1953; she became a pioneering sports businesswoman and managed his career. She died in 2009. Their sons Mark and Marty both became professional players, and Mark was also inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame. Howe suffered a major stroke in 2014 and died on June 10, 2016, in Toledo, Ohio, at age 88.
