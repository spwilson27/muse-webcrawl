Casual Friday

Casual Friday (also known as dress-down Friday) is a workplace practice, common in North America and other Western countries, in which employees are permitted to dress more casually than the company's usual dress code on Fridays. Typical casual Friday attire falls under business casual: chinos or jeans, polo shirts, and informal shoes replacing suits, ties and dresses.

History

The practice has roots in Hawaii, where "Aloha Friday" began in the 1960s: the Hawaiian garment industry promoted the wearing of aloha shirts on Fridays, and the custom was adopted by Honolulu's city government and businesses. In the mainland United States, casual Friday spread during the late 1970s and 1980s, and expanded rapidly in the 1990s as technology companies with relaxed dress codes influenced wider corporate culture. A widely cited role was played by Levi Strauss & Co., whose Dockers brand distributed a "Guide to Casual Business Wear" to human-resources departments in the early 1990s, helping to define the khaki-centred business casual look.

Purpose and reception

Employers introduced casual Friday as a low-cost morale booster and a small perk ahead of the weekend. Surveys and commentary have been mixed: supporters say it improves morale and comfort, while critics argue it blurs professional standards, creates confusion about what is acceptable, and in some companies the relaxation spread into fully casual dress codes throughout the week. Some organisations pair dress-down days with charitable donations, asking staff to pay a small contribution for the privilege.
