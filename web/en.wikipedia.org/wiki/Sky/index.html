Sky


The daytime sky with scattered clouds

The sky is an unobstructed view upward from the surface of the Earth. It includes the atmosphere and outer space. It may also be considered the apparent dome — the celestial sphere — on which the Sun, stars, planets, and Moon appear to move. In meteorology, the sky is the visible portion of the atmosphere above the horizon.

Why the sky is blue

Daylight sky appears blue because air molecules scatter short (blue) wavelengths of sunlight more strongly than longer wavelengths, a phenomenon called Rayleigh scattering. Near sunrise and sunset the light path through the atmosphere is longer, blue light is scattered away, and the sky takes on yellow, orange, and red hues.

Day and night

During the day, the Sun dominates the sky, along with clouds, and occasionally the Moon. At night, scattered sunlight is absent: the Moon, planets, and stars become visible against a dark sky, increasingly obscured in cities by light pollution. Twilight periods — dawn and dusk — occur when the Sun is below the horizon but its light still illuminates the upper atmosphere.

Sky phenomena

The sky hosts weather phenomena (clouds, rain, rainbows, lightning, auroras) and astronomical ones (eclipses, meteors, comets, and the Milky Way). Historically, observation of the sky underlies astronomy, navigation, calendars, and much mythology and religion, in which the sky was often personified as a deity or realm of the gods.
