Silver Linings Playbook is a 2012 American romantic comedy-drama film written and directed by David O. Russell. It was adapted from Matthew Quick's 2008 novel The Silver Linings Playbook. The film stars Bradley Cooper and Jennifer Lawrence, with Robert De Niro, Jacki Weaver, Anupam Kher, and Chris Tucker in supporting roles.

Cooper plays Patrick "Pat" Solitano Jr., a man with bipolar disorder who is released from a psychiatric hospital and moves back in with his parents (De Niro and Weaver). Determined to win back his estranged wife, Pat meets recently widowed Tiffany Maxwell (Lawrence), who offers to help him if he enters a dance competition with her.

Plot

After eight months of treatment for bipolar disorder, Pat Solitano is released into the care of his parents. Having lost his house, job, and wife Nikki, he is determined to rebuild his life and reconcile with her, despite a restraining order. He meets Tiffany, a young widow with her own mental-health struggles, who offers to deliver a letter to Nikki if Pat partners with her in an upcoming dance competition. As they rehearse, the two grow close, and Pat's father—an obsessive Philadelphia Eagles fan running an illegal bookmaking operation—places a parlay bet tying the Eagles' success to the couple's dance score. The plan ultimately brings Pat to terms with his illness and his feelings for Tiffany.

Production

The Weinstein Company acquired the rights to Quick's novel before publication. Russell, whose own son has bipolar disorder and OCD, was drawn to the story's mixture of family drama, comedy, and romance. Filming took place in Philadelphia and Delaware County, Pennsylvania, in the autumn of 2011.

Reception

The film premiered at the 2012 Toronto International Film Festival, where it won the People's Choice Award. It was a critical and commercial success, grossing over $236 million worldwide against a $21 million budget. Critics praised the direction and the performances of Cooper, Lawrence, and De Niro.

Accolades

The film received eight Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture, Best Director, and acting nominations in all four categories—the first film to do so since Reds (1981). Jennifer Lawrence won the Academy Award for Best Actress. It also received four Golden Globe nominations, with Lawrence winning Best Actress in a Comedy or Musical.
