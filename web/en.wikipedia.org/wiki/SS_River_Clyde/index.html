SS River Clyde was a British collier of 3,913 tons, built in 1905, that became famous as an improvised landing ship during the Gallipoli campaign of the First World War.

Gallipoli landings

For the landing at Cape Helles on 25 April 1915, River Clyde was converted to carry some 2,000 soldiers of the 29th Division, including the Royal Munster Fusiliers, Royal Dublin Fusiliers and Hampshire Regiment. Holes were cut in her steel hull so that troops could emerge onto gangways and a bridge of lighters to the shore at Sedd el Bahr ("V Beach"). The ship was deliberately run aground under heavy Ottoman fire. The troops emerging from the sally ports suffered devastating casualties; the men who held the bridge of boats and rescued the wounded won multiple Victoria Crosses, and the episode is remembered as one of the most costly of the landings. Survivors held a foothold ashore, and the beached ship served afterwards as a breakwater, field dressing station and store.

Later service

After the evacuation of Gallipoli the ship remained aground until refloated in 1919. She was repaired and returned to commercial service under Spanish ownership, passing through a succession of owners and names before finally being scrapped in 1966.
