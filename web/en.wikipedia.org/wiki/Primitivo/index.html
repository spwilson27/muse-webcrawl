Primitivo

Primitivo is a variety of black-skinned wine grape grown predominantly in the Apulia (Puglia) region of southern Italy. DNA profiling has shown that Primitivo is genetically identical to the Croatian grape varieties Crljenak Kaštelanski and Tribidrag, and to the variety known in the United States as Zinfandel.

History

The grape has been grown in Apulia since at least the 18th century; its name derives from the Latin primativus ("first to ripen"), reflecting its early ripening. For decades it was assumed Primitivo had been brought from Italy to California in the 19th century, where it became Zinfandel. Research by ampelographers including Carole Meredith at the University of California, Davis, in collaboration with Croatian scientists, established in the 1990s and early 2000s that both descend from the rare Croatian variety Crljenak Kaštelanski, found near Split.

Wine characteristics

Primitivo wines are typically rich, full-bodied reds with high alcohol, flavours of ripe black fruit, jam, spice, and liquorice. The best-known appellation is Primitivo di Manduria, a DOC in the province of Taranto, including a sweet natural version classified DOCG. Because the variety ripens unevenly, a single bunch may contain both ripe and green berries, adding complexity to harvesting and winemaking.
