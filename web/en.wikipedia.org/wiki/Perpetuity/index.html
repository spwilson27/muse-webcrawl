Perpetuity

A perpetuity is an annuity that has no end, or a stream of cash payments that continues forever. In finance, perpetuity is a concept used in valuation theory: a security or type of annuity that receives regular periodic payments for an indefinite period of time. Because payments never cease, a perpetuity has no maturity date.

Valuation

The present value of a perpetuity that pays a fixed amount C each period at discount rate r is given by the simple formula:

PV = C / r

This follows from summing the infinite geometric series of discounted payments. A growing perpetuity, in which payments grow at a constant rate g, has present value PV = C / (r − g), the formula underlying the Gordon growth model used to value shares of stock.

Examples

Real-world approximations include British government consols — undated bonds issued in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries that paid interest forever until the last were redeemed in 2015 — and some preferred shares. Perpetuities are also used as terminal-value estimates in discounted cash flow analysis, and scholarships or endowments funded so that only the income is spent are sometimes structured as perpetuities.

Legal usage

In law, "perpetuity" also relates to the rule against perpetuities, a common-law doctrine limiting how far into the future a person can control the disposition of property, and to restrictions on perpetual trusts.
