Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons

1954 Statelessness Convention
Signed28 September 1954, New York
Effective6 June 1960
DepositarySecretary-General of the United Nations
Companion treaty1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness

The Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons is a 1954 United Nations multilateral treaty designed to ensure that stateless people enjoy a minimum standard of treatment. It defines a stateless person as "a person who is not considered as a national by any State under the operation of its law", and it establishes the legal framework for the protection of such persons in states party to the convention. It entered into force on 6 June 1960.

Context

The treaty was drafted in parallel with the 1951 Refugee Convention: an early draft protocol on statelessness was detached from the refugee instrument and adopted separately in 1954. Unlike refugees, stateless persons are not necessarily outside their country of residence; many are stateless in the only country they have ever lived in, as with the Rohingya in Myanmar or the Bidoon in Kuwait. Statelessness can arise from conflicts of nationality laws, state succession, discriminatory deprivation of citizenship, or gaps in birth registration.

Key provisions

The convention guarantees stateless persons treatment at least as favourable as that accorded to aliens generally in matters such as access to courts, elementary education, public relief, labour legislation, and social security, together with freedom of movement within the host state, identity papers, and travel documents. It restricts expulsion of stateless persons lawfully in the territory to grounds of national security or public order, with due process. It does not, however, confer a right to acquire the nationality of the host state; the complementary 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness addresses the prevention of statelessness, including safeguards for children born stateless. Together, the two treaties underpin UNHCR's mandate on statelessness and the global campaign to realise the right to a nationality proclaimed in Article 15 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
