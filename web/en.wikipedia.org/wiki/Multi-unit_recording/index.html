Multi-unit recording

Multi-unit recording is a technique in neuroscience in which the combined electrical activity of a population of neurons near an electrode is recorded. Unlike single-unit recording, which isolates the spikes of one neuron, multi-unit activity (MUA) reflects the aggregate firing of many cells in the electrode's vicinity.

Method

Extracellular electrodes — microwires, tetrodes, or silicon probes — are implanted in brain tissue. High-frequency components of the signal (roughly above 300 Hz) are extracted and thresholded so that action potentials from multiple nearby neurons are counted together rather than sorted into individual units.

Uses

Multi-unit recording is used to map sensory and motor representations, track changes in population excitability, and monitor activity during behaviour or sleep. Because it does not require spike sorting, it is more robust to electrode drift, though it sacrifices single-cell resolution. It is often combined with local field potential recordings from the same electrode.
