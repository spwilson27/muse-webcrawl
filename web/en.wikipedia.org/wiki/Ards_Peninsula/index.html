Ards Peninsula

The Irish Sea coast of the Ards Peninsula

The Ards Peninsula (from Irish Ard Uladh, meaning "peninsula of the Ulstermen") is a peninsula in County Down, Northern Ireland, which separates Strangford Lough from the North Channel of the Irish Sea, on Ireland's north-east coast. A number of towns and villages are located on the peninsula, including Donaghadee, Millisle, Portavogie and Portaferry. Newtownards, at the northern end of Strangford Lough, sits at the base of the peninsula.

Geography

The peninsula is roughly 30 kilometres (19 mi) long and generally between 5 and 8 kilometres wide. Its western shore fronts the sheltered waters of Strangford Lough, a large sea lough designated as a marine nature reserve and an Area of Special Scientific Interest. The eastern coast faces the Irish Sea and is more exposed, with sandy bays at places such as Ballywalter and Cloughey. The landscape is low, rolling drumlin country, much of it rich agricultural land.

A seasonal ferry links Portaferry, near the southern tip, with Strangford village on the opposite shore, carrying traffic across the narrow but fast-flowing entrance to the lough.

History

The Ards was one of the first parts of Ireland settled by large numbers of Scots during the Plantation of Ulster in the early seventeenth century, under the Hamilton and Montgomery settlements of 1606, preceding the official Plantation. The peninsula consequently retains strong Ulster Scots cultural traditions. Grey Abbey, a Cistercian abbey founded in 1193 by Affreca, wife of John de Courcy, is among its best-known historic monuments. The area also saw fighting during the Irish Rebellion of 1798.

Settlements

  • Newtownards (at the base)
  • Donaghadee and Millisle on the Irish Sea coast
  • Ballywalter, Ballyhalbert, Portavogie and Cloughey
  • Greyabbey, Kircubbin and Portaferry along the lough shore
