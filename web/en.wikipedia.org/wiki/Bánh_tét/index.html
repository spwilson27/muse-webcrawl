Bánh tét

Bánh tét is a Vietnamese savoury but sometimes sweetened cake made primarily from glutinous rice, which is rolled in a banana leaf into a thick, log-like cylindrical shape, with a mung bean and pork filling, then boiled. It is a traditional cake eaten during Tết, the Vietnamese Lunar New Year, especially in southern Vietnam, where it is the counterpart of the square northern bánh chưng.

Preparation

Glutinous rice is soaked, seasoned—sometimes with coconut milk or coloured with pandan or magenta plant—and spread on banana leaves. A filling of split mung bean paste and seasoned pork belly is placed in the centre, and the leaf is rolled into a tight cylinder and tied with bamboo or plastic string. The cakes are boiled for six to twelve hours depending on size.

Consumption

Before eating, the banana leaf wrapper is removed and the cake is sliced into rounds, often using the tying string. Slices may be eaten as they are, pan-fried until crisp, or served with pickled vegetables and fish sauce. Sweet versions filled with banana or black bean are also common.

Variations

Regional varieties include the smaller bánh tét lá cẩm of Cần Thơ, tinted purple with magenta leaves, and chay (vegetarian) versions made for Buddhist observances.
