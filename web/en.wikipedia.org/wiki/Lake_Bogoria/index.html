Lake Bogoria is a saline, alkaline lake in the Kenyan section of the Great Rift Valley, lying just south of Lake Baringo. It is a Ramsar wetland of international importance and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage "Kenya Lakes System in the Great Rift Valley", inscribed in 2011.

Geology

The shallow lake sits in a volcanic trough fed by the Sandai and Emsos rivers and by numerous hot springs and geysers along its western shore — among the most spectacular geothermal features in Africa, with boiling springs and steam vents discharging into the lake. Its waters have no visible outlet and reach pH values above 10.

Ecology

Despite its harsh chemistry, the lake's cyanobacteria (notably Arthrospira) support vast flocks of lesser flamingos, which at peak seasons have numbered over a million birds, turning the shoreline pink. Greater flamingos, fish eagles, and greater kudu in the surrounding reserve are also notable. Lake Bogoria National Reserve, established in 1973, protects the lake and its catchment, home to the Endorois people, whose ancestral land claims were upheld by the African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights in a landmark 2010 ruling.

Flooding

Like other Rift Valley lakes, Bogoria rose significantly during the 2010s–2020s, inundating shoreline facilities and roads — a regional phenomenon linked to increased rainfall and tectonic and land-use changes.
