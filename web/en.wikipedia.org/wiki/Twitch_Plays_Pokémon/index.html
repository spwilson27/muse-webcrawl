Twitch Plays Pokémon

Twitch Plays Pokémon was a social experiment and channel on the video streaming platform Twitch, launched on 12 February 2014 by an anonymous Australian programmer. Viewers collectively controlled a single game of Pokémon Red by typing commands (up, down, a, b, start, and so on) into the stream's chat, which were parsed and fed into an emulator. Against expectation, the crowd completed the game after 16 days of continuous play.

Gameplay and culture

With tens of thousands of simultaneous inputs, progress was chaotic: the player character spun in circles, released prized Pokémon by accident, and spent hours stuck at obstacles such as a ledge or the Team Rocket hideout's maze. A mechanic added mid-run let viewers vote between "anarchy" (all inputs executed) and "democracy" (commands tallied by vote). The run generated a rich folk culture — memes about the Helix Fossil ("praise Helix"), the released Charmeleon "Abby" and Pidgeot "Bird Jesus" — and attracted over a million participants in total, with more than 55 million views during the first run.

Subsequent runs and legacy

The channel went on to play through other Pokémon generations and many further games, and remains a landmark in crowdsourced gaming and internet culture; Twitch itself commemorated the event, and the phenomenon received a world-record recognition for participation in a massively multiplayer online experience.
