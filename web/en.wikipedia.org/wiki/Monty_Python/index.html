Monty Python

Monty Python were a British comedy troupe formed in 1969, consisting of Graham Chapman, John Cleese, Terry Gilliam, Eric Idle, Terry Jones, and Michael Palin. They created the sketch series Monty Python's Flying Circus, first broadcast on BBC1 on 5 October 1969, which ran for 45 episodes over four series and revolutionised television comedy with its surreal, stream-of-consciousness humour and Gilliam's cut-out animations.

Works

Beyond the series, the group made the films Monty Python and the Holy Grail (1975), Life of Brian (1979) — which provoked blasphemy controversies — and The Meaning of Life (1983), as well as live shows, records, and books. Signature sketches include the Dead Parrot, the Spanish Inquisition, Spam (from which the term for junk email derives), and the Lumberjack Song.

Legacy

Python's influence on comedy is so pervasive that the adjective "Pythonesque" entered dictionaries, and the programming language Python was named after the troupe. The members pursued successful solo careers; Chapman died in 1989 and Jones in 2020. The surviving five reunited for the stage show Monty Python Live (Mostly) at London's O2 Arena in 2014. Idle's musical Spamalot, adapted from Holy Grail, won the Tony Award for Best Musical in 2005.
