GameSpot

GameSpot is an American video gaming website that provides news, reviews, previews, downloads and other information on video games. It was launched on May 1, 1996, by Pete Deemer, Vince Broady and Jon Epstein, and is one of the longest-running and most visited games media sites.

History

GameSpot began as an independent site run from its founders' homes and was acquired in 1997 by ZDNet, which later sold its media properties to CNET Networks. CNET was acquired by CBS Interactive in 2008. The site expanded internationally with UK and Australian editions and produced video programming, including the long-running talk show The HotSpot and review programmes. In 2020 CBS Interactive's entertainment properties were sold to Red Ventures, and in 2022 GameSpot was sold again, to Fandom, Inc.

Content and scoring

GameSpot publishes reviews scored (since a 2017 change of scale) out of 10, with half-point granularity abandoned in favour of whole numbers earlier; its highest-profile reviews historically awarded rare scores such as 10/10 to titles including The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time and Metal Gear Solid 4. The site is also known for its review of The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess (8.8), which provoked strong reader reaction, and for the 2007 dismissal of editorial director Jeff Gerstmann, an event that drew wide attention to conflicts between editorial and advertising and led Gerstmann and colleagues to found Giant Bomb.

Affiliations

GameSpot is a sister site to the review aggregator Metacritic and to Giant Bomb and Comic Vine within Fandom's media portfolio. Its traffic and archives — hundreds of thousands of pages of reviews, cheats, guides and forums — make it one of the most comprehensive English-language games databases.
