Song structure

Song structure is the arrangement of a song's sections, and is a part of the songwriting process. Typical elements include the verse, chorus, bridge, and hook. Sectional forms have evolved over time, from the strophic forms of folk music to the verse–chorus forms that dominate popular music.

Common elements

Intro: an opening section that establishes melody, rhythm, and mood. Verse: sections that advance the lyrics and story, usually with the same music but different words each time. Chorus: the repeated, climactic section containing the song's main message and hook. Bridge: a contrasting section that provides relief and often leads back to the final chorus. Outro: a closing section, sometimes a fade-out.

Verse–chorus form

The verse–chorus form is the dominant structure in rock, pop, and much commercial music since the 1960s. A common pattern is verse–chorus–verse–chorus–bridge–chorus. The related thirty-two-bar form (AABA) was standard in Tin Pan Alley and jazz standards.

Other forms

Other structural types include the strophic form (AAA), the twelve-bar blues form, through-composed songs with no repeated sections, and electronic dance music structures built around builds and drops.
