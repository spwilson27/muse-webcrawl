Diyarbakır Fortress

Diyarbakır Fortress is a historic fortification in Sur, Diyarbakır, Turkey. Built of massive black basalt, its walls form one of the largest and best-preserved ancient fortification systems in the world. Together with the adjoining Hevsel Gardens cultural landscape, the fortress was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2015.

Description

The fortifications consist of an inner fortress (İçkale) and city walls about 5.8 kilometres long, punctuated by dozens of towers (traditionally counted around 82) and four main gates — the Harput, Yenikapı, Mardin, and Urfa gates. The walls, in places 10–12 metres high and 3–5 metres thick, are built from the region's characteristic black basalt and bear inscriptions and reliefs from successive civilisations.

History

The site (ancient Amida) was fortified in Roman times; the Emperor Constantius II strengthened the walls in the fourth century CE, and they were repeatedly repaired and extended under Byzantine, Artuqid, Ayyubid, Akkoyunlu, and Ottoman rule. Each era left inscriptions recording its restorations, making the walls a palimpsest of the region's history. Parts of the old city within the walls (Sur district) were heavily damaged in fighting in 2015–2016, and reconstruction has been controversial.

Hevsel Gardens

Between the fortress and the Tigris River lie the Hevsel Gardens, cultivated for millennia and an essential part of the World Heritage inscription as the city's historic food-producing green belt.
