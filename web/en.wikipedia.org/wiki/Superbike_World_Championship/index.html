The Superbike World Championship (often abbreviated WorldSBK or WSBK) is a world championship series for production-based motorcycle road racing, sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM). Founded in 1988, it is the premier global series for superbikes — heavily modified versions of motorcycles that are also sold for road use.

History

The championship was created by Steve McLaughlin and run in its early years by the Flammini Group. American Fred Merkel won the first two titles aboard a Honda RC30. The 1990s saw the dominance of Ducati, with Carl Fogarty becoming the series' most popular rider, winning four championships between 1994 and 1999. Australian Troy Bayliss, Briton James Toseland, American Ben Spies, Italian Max Biaggi and Spaniard Carlos Checa were among the champions of the 2000s and early 2010s.

Northern Irish rider Jonathan Rea became the most successful rider in the championship's history, winning six consecutive titles with Kawasaki from 2015 to 2020 and amassing over one hundred race victories. From 2013 the series has been promoted by Dorna Sports, which also organises MotoGP.

Format and regulations

A WorldSBK round typically comprises free practice and qualifying sessions, a Superpole session, and three races: two full-length races and a short Superpole Race, whose result sets part of the grid for the second full race and awards reduced points. Motorcycles must be derived from production models — four-cylinder machines are limited to 1000 cc, while twins historically received a capacity allowance, and a system of balancing measures including rev limits is used to equalise performance between manufacturers.

Support classes include the Supersport World Championship (WorldSSP) for mid-capacity production bikes and the Supersport 300 World Championship (WorldSSP300) as an entry-level category.

Motorcycles and manufacturers

Manufacturers to have contested the series include Aprilia, BMW, Ducati, Honda, Kawasaki, MV Agusta, Suzuki and Yamaha. Ducati has been the most successful manufacturer, while Italian machinery's rivalry with the Japanese factories is a defining feature of the championship.
