The Islamic State (IS), also known as the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL), the Islamic State of Iraq and Syria (ISIS), or by its Arabic acronym Daesh, is a Salafi jihadist group and a former unrecognized quasi-state. It is designated as a terrorist organisation by the United Nations and many countries around the world.

The group originated in Iraq under Abu Musab al-Zarqawi and became affiliated with al-Qaeda during the Iraq War. Expanding into Syria during its civil war, it broke with al-Qaeda and in June 2014 proclaimed a worldwide caliphate under Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi.

Territorial rise and fall

At its peak in 2014–2015, the group controlled major cities including Mosul in Iraq and Raqqa in Syria, governing millions of people. It seized oil fields, imposed a harsh interpretation of sharia, and carried out mass atrocities, including the genocide of the Yazidis and widely publicized executions. An international coalition, alongside Iraqi, Kurdish, and Syrian forces, progressively retook its territory; Mosul fell in 2017, Raqqa in 2017, and the last territorial enclave at Baghuz in March 2019. Al-Baghdadi was killed in a US raid in October 2019.

Ideology and affiliates

The group adheres to an extremist Salafi-jihadist doctrine, seeks to expand its claimed caliphate globally, and has directed or inspired terrorist attacks worldwide, including in Paris (2015), Brussels (2016), and Sri Lanka (2019). Affiliates ("provinces") remain active in regions including West Africa, the Sahel, Afghanistan (IS-K), and Southeast Asia, and the group persists as an insurgent network in Iraq and Syria.
