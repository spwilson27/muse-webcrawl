The Second Peace of Thorn of 1466 (Polish: drugi pokój toruński; German: Zweiter Friede von Thorn) was a peace treaty signed in the Hanseatic city of Thorn (Toruń) on 19 October 1466 between the Polish king Casimir IV Jagiellon and the Teutonic Knights, which ended the Thirteen Years' War (1454–1466).

Terms

Under the treaty, the Teutonic Order ceded the western part of its Prussian lands — Pomerelia (Gdańsk Pomerania), the Chełmno Land, Malbork (Marienburg), Elbląg (Elbing) and the bishopric of Warmia (Ermland) — to the Polish Crown. These territories were organised as Royal Prussia, a province of Poland. The Order retained the eastern part of Prussia, but its Grand Master was obliged to swear allegiance to the Polish king and to sit in the Polish Senate; the remaining Order's state thus became a fief of Poland. The treaty was confirmed by Pope Paul II and Emperor Frederick III only later and with difficulty.

Consequences

The settlement shifted the balance of power on the Baltic: Poland regained access to the sea, including the great port of Gdańsk (Danzig), and the Vistula trade. The weakened Order's state in eastern Prussia was secularised in 1525, when Grand Master Albert of Brandenburg-Ansbach converted to Lutheranism and became Duke of Prussia, a Polish vassal. The name distinguishes the treaty from the earlier Peace of Thorn (1411), which had ended the Polish–Lithuanian–Teutonic War after the Battle of Grunwald.
