Carlos Paredes (16 February 1925 – 23 July 2004) was a virtuoso Portuguese guitarist and composer, regarded as the master of the Portuguese guitar (guitarra portuguesa), the twelve-string instrument central to fado and to the Coimbra song tradition.

Life and music

Born in Coimbra into a family of guitarists — his father Artur Paredes was a noted innovator of the instrument — Paredes began playing at the age of four. He composed and performed pieces that drew on the Coimbra fado tradition while extending the guitar's expressive range, producing works such as "Verdes Anos" and "Movimento Perpétuo", the latter the title of a celebrated 1971 album.

A member of the Portuguese Communist Party, Paredes was imprisoned by the PIDE secret police during the Salazar dictatorship. After the Carnation Revolution he performed widely, collaborating with musicians including jazz bassist Charlie Haden, with whom he recorded Dialogues (1990). His music is strongly associated with the films of Manoel de Oliveira.

Legacy

Paredes is credited with elevating the Portuguese guitar from an accompanying instrument to a solo concert instrument, and he remains one of Portugal's most beloved musicians.
