A location-based service (LBS) is a general term denoting software services which use geographic data and information to provide services or information to users. LBS can be used in a variety of contexts, such as health, indoor object search, entertainment, work and personal life. Examples include navigation and turn-by-turn directions, location-based social networking and "check-ins", nearest-point-of-interest search, ride-hailing and delivery tracking, fleet management, emergency-caller location, parental or asset tracking, geofencing-based notifications, and location-based games such as geocaching and Pokémon Go.

How it works

An LBS combines a positioning method — satellite positioning (GPS and other GNSS), cellular network positioning, Wi-Fi positioning systems, Bluetooth Low Energy beacons, or IP-based geolocation — with geographic information system (GIS) data and a communications network. Services are classified as push (triggered by entering an area, e.g., a proximity alert) or pull (requested by the user, e.g., "find coffee near me"), and as reactive or proactive according to whether the user explicitly requests them.

History

Precursors appeared in the 1990s with vehicle telematics and the US E911 mandate requiring carriers to locate emergency callers. Early consumer LBS included friend-finder services and Japan's i-mode location services in the early 2000s; mainstream adoption followed the spread of GPS-equipped smartphones after 2007 and the opening of location APIs on mobile platforms, which made location a standard input to consumer apps.

Privacy

Because location data can reveal home and work addresses, routines, associations and beliefs, LBS raise significant privacy concerns. Location brokers have sold granular movement data, prompting regulatory responses including consent requirements under GDPR and various US state privacy laws, platform-level controls (precise vs. approximate location permissions), and enforcement actions against the sale of sensitive-location data (e.g., visits to clinics or places of worship).
