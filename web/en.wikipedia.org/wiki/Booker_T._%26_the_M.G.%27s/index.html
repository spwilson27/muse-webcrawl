Booker T. & the M.G.'s

Booker T. & the M.G.'s
Origin: Memphis, Tennessee, U.S.
Years active: 1962–1977 and reunions
Genres: R&B, soul, instrumental rock, funk
Label: Stax/Volt, Atlantic
Classic line-up: Booker T. Jones, Steve Cropper, Donald "Duck" Dunn, Al Jackson Jr.
Hit: "Green Onions" (1962)

Booker T. & the M.G.'s were an American instrumental R&B and soul band formed in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1962. The group's name is usually said to stand for the "Memphis Group". As the house band for Stax Records, they played on hundreds of recordings by artists including Otis Redding, Sam & Dave, Wilson Pickett, Bill Withers, and Albert King, shaping the sound of Southern soul.

Career

The band — organist Booker T. Jones, guitarist Steve Cropper, bassist Lewie Steinberg (soon replaced by Donald "Duck" Dunn), and drummer Al Jackson Jr. — scored an immediate hit with the instrumental "Green Onions" in 1962, one of the most popular R&B instrumentals ever recorded. Further hits included "Hip Hug-Her", "Soul Limbo", "Time Is Tight", and "Melting Pot". Their integrated line-up was unusual in the segregated South.

Later years and legacy

The band largely dissolved in the early 1970s, and Jackson was murdered in 1975. Members reunited frequently in later decades, backing Bob Dylan, Neil Young, and others. Booker T. & the M.G.'s were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1992 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2007.
