Agricultural meteorology

Agricultural meteorology, or agrometeorology, is the branch of meteorology concerned with the interactions between weather and climate and agricultural production, including crops, livestock, soils, and forestry. It applies knowledge of the atmosphere, soil moisture, and plant physiology to help farmers plan sowing, irrigation, pest control, and harvesting.

Scope

Agrometeorologists study how temperature, rainfall, humidity, wind, solar radiation, and evapotranspiration affect plant growth and yields, and how extreme events — drought, flood, frost, hail, and heat waves — damage production. The field produces specialised forecasts and advisories, monitors drought, models crop growth and disease risk, and assesses the effects of climate change on food security. The World Meteorological Organization's Commission for Agricultural Meteorology coordinates international work in the field.

Applications

Practical applications include frost warnings for orchardists, irrigation scheduling based on soil-moisture budgets, disease forecasting (for example for potato late blight or wheat rust), and seasonal climate outlooks that inform planting decisions. Remote sensing by weather satellites and automated agrometeorological stations now supply much of the underlying data.
