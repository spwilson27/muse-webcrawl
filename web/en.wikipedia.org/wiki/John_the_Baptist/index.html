Overview

John the Baptist was a Jewish preacher active in the region of the Jordan River in the early 1st century AD, regarded in Christianity as the forerunner who announced the coming of Jesus and baptised him. He is a prophet in Islam (as Yaḥyā ibn Zakariyā), is venerated centrally in Mandaeism, and is considered a prophet in the Baháʼí Faith. The historian Josephus independently describes John, his baptism movement and his execution by Herod Antipas.

Life and death

The Gospels present John as the son of the priest Zechariah and Elizabeth (a kinswoman of Mary), preaching repentance in the wilderness, dressed in camel hair and eating locusts and wild honey — in the pattern of Elijah — and baptising in the Jordan. He baptised Jesus, after which his movement overlapped with Jesus's own. John was imprisoned and beheaded after denouncing Herod Antipas's marriage to Herodias; the Gospel account has Salome request his head at a banquet. His head is claimed by several shrines, including the Umayyad Mosque in Damascus.
