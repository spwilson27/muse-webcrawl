Patriarch of Antioch

Patriarch of Antioch is a title held by the heads of several Christian churches whose origins trace to the ancient see of Antioch, one of the earliest centres of Christianity. Tradition holds that the church at Antioch was founded by the apostles Peter and Paul, and it was at Antioch that the followers of Jesus were first called Christians (Acts 11:26). Today five churches use the title Patriarch of Antioch, a legacy of the schisms that divided Eastern Christianity.

The patriarchate of Antioch ranks among the ancient Pentarchy of patriarchal sees.

History

Antioch on the Orontes (modern Antakya, Turkey) was one of the largest cities of the Roman Empire and its church one of the most prestigious. The Council of Nicaea (325) recognized the special position of its bishop, and the Council of Constantinople (381) confirmed Antioch's jurisdiction over the Diocese of the East. The Christological controversies of the fifth century, above all the Council of Chalcedon (451), split the Antiochene church between Chalcedonian ("Melkite") and non-Chalcedonian (Oriental Orthodox) communities, each maintaining its own line of patriarchs.

The five patriarchs

The churches whose heads bear the title today are:

  • The Greek Orthodox Patriarch of Antioch, head of the Greek Orthodox Church of Antioch, resident in Damascus, Syria, since the 14th century.
  • The Syriac Orthodox Patriarch of Antioch, head of the Syriac Orthodox Church, also resident in Damascus.
  • The Melkite Greek Catholic Patriarch of Antioch, in communion with Rome.
  • The Maronite Patriarch of Antioch, head of the Maronite Church, resident in Bkerké, Lebanon.
  • The Syriac Catholic Patriarch of Antioch, in communion with Rome.

During the Crusades there was also a Latin Patriarch of Antioch, a line maintained as a titular see by the Roman Catholic Church until 1964.
