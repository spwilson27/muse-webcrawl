Secretary of State for War

OfficeSecretary of State for War (United Kingdom)
Existed1794 – 1964 (in its later form)
DepartmentWar Office
Succeeded bySecretary of State for Defence

The Secretary of State for War, commonly called the War Secretary, was the British cabinet minister responsible for the administration of the British Army and head of the War Office. The post was one of the principal military offices of state until defence reorganisation in 1964 folded it into the new Ministry of Defence.

History

A Secretary at War — a more junior, non-cabinet post — existed from the 17th century. The separate office of Secretary of State for War was created in 1794 amid the wars with Revolutionary France, initially covering military affairs broadly, including colonial defence. In 1855, after the administrativefailures exposed by the Crimean War, the older Secretary at War office was merged into it, and the Secretary of State for War became unambiguously responsible for the army. Responsibilities for the colonies were hived off to a separate Colonial Secretary.

The officeholder sat in cabinet and answered to Parliament for army estimates, personnel, and organisation, though operational command rested with the Commander-in-Chief and, after 1904 reforms, the Chief of the Imperial General Staff and the Army Council. Notable holders included Lord Haldane, whose reforms created the British Expeditionary Force and the Territorial Force before the First World War, and Leslie Hore-Belisha in the run-up to the Second World War.

Abolition

On 1 April 1964 the War Office was absorbed into a unified Ministry of Defence, and the post — along with the First Lord of the Admiralty and Secretary of State for Air — was replaced by the single Secretary of State for Defence. The last War Secretary was James Ramsden.
