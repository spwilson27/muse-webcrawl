Dracaena (plant)

Dracaena is a genus of about 200 species of flowering plants in the family Asparagaceae, mostly trees and succulent shrubs native to Africa, southern Asia and northern Australia, with a few in Central America.

Dracaena
Family: Asparagaceae
Species: ~200
Common names: Dragon tree, dragon plant
Notable: D. draco, D. trifasciata (snake plant)

Description

Species range from small shrubs to the umbrella-crowned dragon tree (Dracaena draco) of the Canary Islands, famed for its red resin, "dragon's blood", long used as varnish and dye. Many have sword-shaped leaves, sometimes variegated, borne in rosettes on woody stems that branch after flowering or pruning.

Cultivation

Popular houseplants include D. marginata, D. fragrans (corn plant) and, after taxonomic merger of the genus Sansevieria, the snake plant D. trifasciata. They tolerate low light and infrequent watering but are sensitive to fluoride in tap water and are toxic to cats and dogs if ingested.
