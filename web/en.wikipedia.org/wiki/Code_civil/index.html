Code civil des Français

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Code civil des Français (French Civil Code), commonly called the Napoleonic Code, is the French civil code established by a law of 21 March 1804. It replaced the patchwork of Roman law, royal ordinances and local customs of the old regime with a single, systematic statement of private law: persons, property and the modes of acquiring property. Drafted by a commission of four jurists chaired by Jean-Étienne-Marie Portalis and driven through by Napoleon Bonaparte, who presided over many of the drafting sessions, the Code proclaimed legal equality (for men), security of property, freedom of contract and the abolition of feudal privilege, while reinforcing paternal authority within the family.

Carried by French arms and imitation, the Code became the most influential civil code in the world, the model for codes across Europe, Latin America, Louisiana, Quebec and (in part) Japan and the Ottoman successor states. It remains in force in France, much amended — family and obligations law were substantially reformed in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries (the contract-law reform of 2016) — and is counted among the foundational texts of the civil-law tradition alongside the German BGB.
