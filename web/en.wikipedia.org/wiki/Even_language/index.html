The Even language (Эвэды торэн), formerly known as Lamut, is a Tungusic language spoken by the Evens, an Indigenous people of eastern Siberia, in regions including Magadan Oblast, Kamchatka, Chukotka, and the Sakha Republic. It is closely related to Evenki and is written in the Cyrillic script.

Status

Even is considered endangered: of an ethnic population of roughly 20,000, only a minority — about 5,700 people in the 2010 census — reported speaking the language, and transmission to children is limited outside a few communities. The language has a dozen or more dialects, which differ enough that three broad groupings (eastern, central, and western) are recognised.

Features

Like other Tungusic languages, Even is agglutinative, uses vowel harmony, and normally follows subject–object–verb word order. A literary standard based on the Ola dialect emerged in the Soviet period, when a Cyrillic orthography replaced the Latin-based alphabet introduced in the 1930s; newspapers and schoolbooks have been published in the language.
