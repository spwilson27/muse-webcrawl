University of Wrocław

Established: 1702 (re-established 1945)

Type: Public university

Location: Wrocław, Poland

The University of Wrocław (Polish: Uniwersytet Wrocławski) is a public research university in Wrocław, Poland. It is the largest university in Lower Silesia, with over 26,000 students, and traces its origins to the Jesuit academy founded in 1702 under Emperor Leopold I.

History

The university began as the Leopoldina Academy, founded by the Jesuits in 1702. In 1811 it was merged with the Protestant Viadrina University from Frankfurt an der Oder to form the Silesian Friedrich Wilhelm University in Breslau. After the Second World War, when Breslau became Polish Wrocław, the university was refounded as a Polish institution, with many academics arriving from the former Polish university of Lwów.

Nobel laureates

During its German period the university was associated with nine Nobel Prize winners, including Max Born, Erwin Schrödinger (briefly), Otto Stern and Friedrich Bergius. The main building, on the banks of the Oder River, contains the famous Baroque Aula Leopoldina assembly hall.

Faculties

The university comprises ten faculties covering the humanities, social sciences, law, natural sciences, mathematics and computer science, and biotechnology. Its botanical garden, founded in 1811, is one of the oldest in Poland.
