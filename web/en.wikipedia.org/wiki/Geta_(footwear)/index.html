Geta (footwear)

Geta (下駄) are traditional Japanese wooden footwear, a form of elevated sandal worn with traditional clothing such as kimono and yukata. A pair consists of a flat wooden base (the dai) raised on two transverse wooden teeth (ha) running across the sole, held on the foot by a fabric thong (hanao) that passes between the big toe and second toe. They are worn with the heel slightly overhanging the back of the base.

History and use

Elevated wooden clogs have a long history in East Asia, appearing in China and reaching Japan by the early historic period. The raised teeth keep the wearer's feet and the hem of the kimono above mud, rain, and snow, and they were everyday footwear for townspeople, farmers, and merchants through the Edo period. Distinctive styles include the tall one-toothed tengu geta associated with yamabushi mountain ascetics, and lacquered pokkuri worn by apprentice geisha (maiko).

Modern use

Today geta are worn chiefly with summer yukata at festivals and fireworks displays, and by some workers such as sushi chefs for use in wet environments. The teeth produce a characteristic clacking sound on pavement — in Japanese, karankoron — regarded as a nostalgic sound of summer.
