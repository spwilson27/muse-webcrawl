TypeIsolated neutron star
Period2–12 seconds

An anomalous X-ray pulsar (AXP) is a class of isolated neutron star that emits steady, pulsating X-rays with periods of roughly 2 to 12 seconds. They were named "anomalous" because their X-ray luminosity far exceeds the energy available from their rotational spin-down, unlike ordinary rotation-powered pulsars.

Characteristics

AXPs show slow spin periods clustered in a narrow range, steady spin-down, and occasional bursts and outbursts. Unlike accreting X-ray pulsars in binary systems, they have no detectable companion star to supply infalling matter.

Magnetar model

The leading explanation is the magnetar model, in which AXPs are neutron stars with extremely strong magnetic fields, of order 1014–1015 gauss. The decay of this immense field powers the X-ray emission. AXPs are now generally grouped together with soft gamma repeaters as magnetars.
