Sesshū Tōyō (雪舟等楊; 1420 – 1506) was one of the greatest masters of Japanese ink painting (suiboku-ga) and a Rinzai Zen priest. His powerful landscapes shaped Japanese painting for centuries.

Life and travels

Sesshū trained at Shōkoku-ji in Kyoto under Shūbun. In 1467–1469 he travelled to Ming-dynasty China, where he studied Chinese landscape painting firsthand and was honoured as a leading artist. On returning, he settled in Yamaguchi under the patronage of the Ōuchi clan.

Works

His masterpieces include the Long Scroll of Landscapes (Sansui Chōkan), the splashed-ink Haboku Sansui (1495), and the Landscape of the Four Seasons. Several of his works are designated National Treasures of Japan.
