Monty Python's Life of Brian, also known as Life of Brian, is a 1979 British comedy film written, directed and performed by the comedy group Monty Python. It tells the story of Brian Cohen (Graham Chapman), a young Jewish man born on the same day and next door to Jesus, who is mistaken for the Messiah.

Production

After EMI Films withdrew funding over the script's religious satire, former Beatle George Harrison stepped in, forming Handmade Films and mortgaging his house to finance the production, which was shot in Tunisia on sets built for Jesus of Nazareth. Harrison called the ticket "the most expensive cinema ticket ever bought".

Controversy and reception

The film was denounced as blasphemous by some religious groups and banned in several countries, including Norway and Ireland, and by some British local councils — bans that inadvertently boosted its publicity ("So funny it was banned in Norway!"). It is now widely regarded as one of the greatest comedy films of all time. Its closing song, Eric Idle's "Always Look on the Bright Side of Life", became a popular singalong, including at funerals.
