Polylogarithm

The polylogarithm for several values of the order s

In mathematics, the polylogarithm (also known as Jonquière's function, for Alfred Jonquière) is a special function Lis(z) of order s and argument z. It is defined in the complex plane by the power series

Lis(z) = Σk=1∞ zk / ks = z + z2/2s + z3/3s + …

which converges for |z| < 1 and is extended to other values by analytic continuation. The polylogarithm arises in many areas of mathematics and physics, including number theory, quantum statistics, and the evaluation of Feynman diagrams.

Special cases

For special values of the order, the polylogarithm reduces to elementary functions: Li1(z) = −ln(1 − z) and Li0(z) = z/(1 − z), with negative integer orders giving rational functions. The case s = 2 is the dilogarithm, studied since the time of Leibniz and Euler, and s = 3 gives the trilogarithm. At z = 1, the polylogarithm yields values of the Riemann zeta function, Lis(1) = ζ(s); at z = −1 it gives values related to the Dirichlet eta function. At z = 1/2, notable evaluations include Li2(1/2) = π2/12 − (ln 2)2/2.

Relations and applications

The polylogarithm satisfies numerous identities, including inversion, duplication, and reflection formulas, and the five-term relation (Abel's identity) for the dilogarithm. Successive orders are related by integration: Lis+1(z) = ∫0z Lis(t)/t dt. In physics, integrals of the polylogarithm describe the thermodynamics of ideal Bose and Fermi gases, appearing in the treatment of Bose–Einstein condensation and in the Fermi–Dirac distribution. Closely related functions include the Lerch transcendent, of which the polylogarithm is a special case, and Nielsen's generalised polylogarithms.
