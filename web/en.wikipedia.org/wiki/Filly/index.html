Filly

A filly is a young female horse that is too young to be called a mare. There are several specific definitions in use:

  • In most cases, a filly is a female horse under four years of age.
  • In Thoroughbred horse racing, a filly is defined as a female horse less than five years of age.

In contrast to a filly is a colt, a young male horse. A newborn horse of either sex is a foal, and a young horse still nursing from its mother is sometimes called a suckling; one that has been weaned is a weanling. A female horse aged four or five years and older is called a mare, while an adult male horse is a stallion, or a gelding if castrated.

Usage in racing

In horse racing, the term is important because races are often restricted by age and sex. Many major races are open to fillies, and some prestigious events, such as the Kentucky Oaks in the United States and the Epsom Oaks in the United Kingdom, are restricted to fillies only. A filly that wins a race against male horses is considered to have achieved something notable, as male horses are generally larger and stronger.
