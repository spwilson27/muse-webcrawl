Kunama people

The Kunama are an ethnic group native to the Gash-Barka region of western Eritrea and adjacent areas of Ethiopia's Tigray region. Numbering roughly 200,000–260,000, they are among the smallest of Eritrea's nine officially recognised ethnic groups. The Kunama speak Kunama, a Nilo-Saharan language, and are regarded as some of the earliest inhabitants of the Eritrean lowlands.


The Kunama heartland lies around the town of Barentu in the Gash-Barka region.

Society and culture

Traditional Kunama society is matrilineal — clan membership, inheritance and land rights pass through the mother — with clans (such as the Aimasa, Bisha, Marda and others) playing a central role. Most Kunama are settled agriculturalists growing sorghum, millet and sesame, complemented by cattle keeping. Religions practised include traditional beliefs and Islam, with some Christians.

History

The Kunama historically resisted absorption by neighbouring kingdoms and suffered slave raids from both Ethiopian and Sudanese powers in the 19th century. During the Eritrean War of Independence they were drawn into the conflict, and many fled to Ethiopia during the border war of 1998–2000, where a large refugee community formed around Shimelba camp in Tigray. Displacement continued during the Tigray War from 2020.
