Abuse

TypesPhysical, psychological, sexual, verbal, economic
ContextsChild abuse, domestic abuse, elder abuse, animal abuse

Abuse is the improper usage or treatment of a person or thing, often to unfairly or improperly gain benefit. Abuse can come in many forms, including physical, verbal, emotional, sexual, economic and institutional abuse, and is characterised by an imbalance of power between abuser and victim. It encompasses acts such as assault, coercion, neglect and exploitation.

Types of abuse

Physical abuse involves contact intended to cause pain, injury or suffering. Psychological abuse (emotional abuse) includes humiliation, intimidation, isolation and controlling behaviour. Sexual abuse is any sexual act without consent, including of children. Verbal abuse uses words to attack or demean, while economic abuse controls access to money and resources. Neglect — the failure to provide necessary care — is a form of abuse especially affecting children and the elderly.

Contexts

Recognised categories include child abuse, domestic abuse (intimate partner violence), elder abuse, animal abuse, bullying, and abuse of authority or power, including corruption and human rights violations. Substance abuse is an older term for harmful use of drugs or alcohol, now generally termed substance use disorder.

Effects and response

Abuse is associated with long-term physical and mental health consequences, including post-traumatic stress disorder, depression and anxiety. Responses include criminal law, child protection services, shelters and helplines, and therapeutic support for survivors. Prevention programmes focus on education, early intervention and challenging the social conditions that enable abuse.
