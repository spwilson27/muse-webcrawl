History of Austria

The history of Austria covers the history of the lands of modern Austria and their inhabitants from prehistoric settlement to the present republic.

Early history and the Habsburgs

The region was inhabited by Celts (the kingdom of Noricum) and then incorporated into the Roman Empire. After Germanic and Slavic migrations, Charlemagne established the March of Austria (Marchia Austriae) in the late 8th century; the name Ostarrîchi first appears in a document of 996. The Babenberg dynasty ruled until 1246, after which Rudolf of Habsburg took control in 1278, founding centuries of Habsburg rule. The Austrian Habsburgs accumulated a vast multinational empire — by marriage and inheritance gaining Spain and its colonies, Hungary, Bohemia and the Netherlands — and repeatedly defended south-eastern Europe against the Ottoman Empire, notably at the Siege of Vienna (1683).

Empire to republic

The Napoleonic Wars led to the proclamation of the Austrian Empire in 1804; after 1867 it was the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary. Defeat in the First World War dissolved the empire in 1918, leaving the small Republic of German-Austria. Annexed by Nazi Germany in the Anschluss of 1938, Austria was restored after 1945, regained full sovereignty with the State Treaty of 1955, and adopted permanent neutrality. It joined the European Union in 1995.
