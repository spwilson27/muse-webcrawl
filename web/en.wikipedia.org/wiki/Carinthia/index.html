Carinthia (German: Kärnten; Slovene: Koroška) is the southernmost state (Land) of Austria. It is known for its mountains and lakes, and shares borders with Italy and Slovenia to the south, and the Austrian states of East Tyrol, Salzburg and Styria. The capital and largest city is Klagenfurt am Wörthersee, and another major centre is Villach.

Geography

Carinthia lies mainly within the drainage basin of the Drava (Drau) river, framed by the Hohe Tauern in the northwest and the Karawanks along the Slovenian border. The state contains more than a thousand standing lakes, the largest being Wörthersee, Millstätter See and Ossiacher See, which support a major summer tourism industry; ski resorts such as Nassfeld and Bad Kleinkirchheim draw winter visitors.

History

The region was part of the Celtic kingdom of Noricum and later a Roman province. A Slavic principality, Carantania, arose in the 7th century; the Duchy of Carinthia was formed in 976 within the Holy Roman Empire. After the First World War, a 1920 plebiscite decided that the Klagenfurt basin would remain with Austria rather than join Yugoslavia. A Slovene-speaking minority (Carinthian Slovenes) lives mainly in the south.
