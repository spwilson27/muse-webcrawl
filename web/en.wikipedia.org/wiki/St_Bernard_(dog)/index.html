The St Bernard or Saint Bernard (UK: /ˈbɜːrnərd/, US: /bərˈnɑːrd/) is a breed of very large working dog from the Western Alps in Italy and Switzerland. They were originally bred for rescue work by the hospice workers of the Great St Bernard Pass on the Italian-Swiss border — from which the breed takes its name.

History

The dogs were developed by the Augustinian monks of the Great St Bernard Hospice, founded by Saint Bernard of Menthon around 1050 to aid travellers crossing the dangerous pass. By the 17th century the monks' dogs were being used to locate and warm travellers lost in snowstorms; they worked in pairs and, legend holds, one dog would lie beside the victim while the other returned to the hospice for help. The most famous of the rescue dogs was Barry, credited with saving more than 40 lives between 1800 and 1812. The popular image of the dogs carrying small barrels of brandy derives from an 1820 painting by Edwin Landseer and has no firm historical basis.

Characteristics

St Bernards are giant, muscular dogs with massive heads, drooping ears, and a calm, gentle temperament that makes them good family dogs and "nursemaids" for children. They come in smooth and rough coat varieties. Colours range from red with white markings to mahogany brindle. The breed is prone to rapid growth-related bone disorders, hip dysplasia, and gastric dilatation-volvulus (bloat), and has a relatively short life expectancy of eight to ten years.

In culture

The breed is a national symbol of Switzerland. Famous fictional St Bernards include Buck in The Call of the Wild, Nana in Peter Pan (in the original novel), and the titular dog of the film Beethoven (1992).
