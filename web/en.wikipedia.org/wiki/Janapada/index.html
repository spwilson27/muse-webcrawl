Janapada

Janapada (Sanskrit: जनपद, janapada, from jana, "people, tribe" and pada, "foot") is a term used in ancient Indian texts for the realms, republics and kingdoms of Vedic and early historic India, from the late Vedic period (c. 1000 BCE) to the rise of the empires in the 6th century BCE.

Terminology

The janapadas were territories ruled by tribal polities, some monarchies (rajanya) and some oligarchic republics (gana-sanghas). Sixteen of the largest were recognised as the mahajanapadas ("great janapadas"), listed in texts such as the Buddhist Anguttara Nikaya.

Development

The consolidation of janapadas from Vedic tribal territories reflects the spread of iron technology, settled agriculture, and urbanisation in the Ganges plain — the "second urbanisation" of India. By the time of Mahavira and the Buddha, janapadas such as Magadha, Kosala, Avanti and Gandhara dominated northern India.
