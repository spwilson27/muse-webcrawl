North Pacific Gyre Oscillation

The North Pacific subtropical gyre

The North Pacific Gyre Oscillation (NPGO) is a climate pattern of fluctuations in the ocean circulation and atmosphere of the North Pacific Ocean. It was identified in 2007 by Emanuele Di Lorenzo and colleagues from observations of sea surface height, and it represents the second dominant mode of sea surface height variability in the North Pacific, after the Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO).

Mechanism

The NPGO is linked to variations in the strength of the North Pacific subtropical gyre and the Kuroshio Extension, driven by wind stress curl anomalies associated with the North Pacific Oscillation in the atmosphere. When the NPGO is in its positive phase, the subtropical gyre circulation strengthens, with cooler sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern Pacific along the gyre's eastern flank and warmer anomalies in parts of the Kuroshio region.

Effects

Because it modulates the circulation that transports nutrients, the NPGO is closely tied to fluctuations in salinity, chlorophyll and nutrient concentrations along the west coast of North America, and has been used to explain long-term changes in the marine ecosystems sampled by the California Current System. The NPGO has been proposed to account for some of the decadal variability in Northeast Pacific climate that the PDO index alone does not capture.
