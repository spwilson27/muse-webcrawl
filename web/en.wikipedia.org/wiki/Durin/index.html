Durin

Durin
Created by: J. R. R. Tolkien
Race: Dwarf
Title: King of Khazad-dûm

Durin is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. Called Durin the Deathless, he was the first of the seven Fathers of the Dwarves, created by the Vala Aulë, and the founder of the Longbeards clan and of Khazad-dûm (Moria), the greatest Dwarf kingdom.

Biography

Durin awoke at Mount Gundabad in the First Age and travelled until he came to the Mirrormere in the Dimrill Dale, where he saw a crown of seven stars reflected above his head — a sign by which he chose the site of Khazad-dûm beneath the Misty Mountains. Unusually long-lived, he was said to be reincarnated in his descendants: six later kings bore the name Durin, each resembling his ancestor so closely that he was believed to be Durin returned.

Legacy

Durin's line ruled Khazad-dûm until the awakening of the Balrog known as Durin's Bane in the Third Age. The Doors of Durin guarded the west gate of Moria, and Durin's Day — the rare alignment of sun and moon on the last autumn day of the year — features in The Hobbit. Tolkien took the name from Norse mythology, where Durinn is one of the dwarfs named in the Völuspá.
