Duncan Edwards

Duncan Edwards (1 October 1936 – 21 February 1958) was an English footballer who played for Manchester United and the England national team. One of the "Busby Babes", the young United team built by manager Matt Busby, he is regarded as one of the most talented players of his generation. He died at the age of 21 from injuries sustained in the Munich air disaster.

Career

Born in Dudley, Worcestershire, Edwards signed for Manchester United as a schoolboy and made his first-team debut at 16, then the youngest player in the First Division. A powerful, versatile half-back with superb passing, tackling and shooting with both feet, he helped United win league championships in 1955–56 and 1956–57, and reached the semi-finals of the European Cup. He won 18 caps for England and was expected to become one of the stars of the 1958 World Cup.

Munich air disaster

On 6 February 1958, the aircraft carrying the United team home from a European Cup match in Belgrade crashed on its third attempt to take off from a slush-covered runway at Munich-Riem Airport. Edwards survived the crash but suffered severe injuries, including multiple fractures and kidney damage. He fought for 15 days in hospital, reportedly asking coach Jimmy Murphy "What time is the kick off against Wolves, Jimmy? I mustn't miss that match" — before dying on 21 February 1958.

Legacy

Edwards is remembered in Dudley, where a statue, a stained-glass window in St Francis's Church and the Duncan Edwards Leisure Centre commemorate him, and at Old Trafford, where the Munich memorial honours the eight players who died. Contemporaries such as Bobby Charlton considered him potentially the greatest player of his era.
