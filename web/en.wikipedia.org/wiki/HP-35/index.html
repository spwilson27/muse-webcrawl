HP-35

The HP-35 was Hewlett-Packard's first pocket calculator and the world's first handheld scientific calculator, introduced in 1972 at a price of $395. It could perform trigonometric, logarithmic and exponential functions, effectively making the slide rule obsolete for many engineers.

Design

Named for its 35 keys, the HP-35 used reverse Polish notation (RPN), LED displays and a four-register stack. It was co-developed under Bill Hewlett's challenge to shrink the desktop HP 9100A to shirt-pocket size. More than 300,000 units were sold before it was discontinued in 1975.
