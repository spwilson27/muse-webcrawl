Supermodel

Supermodel
Occupation: Fashion model
Era of prominence: 1980s–1990s, continuing
Known figures: Cindy Crawford, Naomi Campbell, Linda Evangelista, Claudia Schiffer, Kate Moss

A supermodel is a highly paid fashion model who usually has a worldwide reputation and often a background in haute couture and commercial modelling. The term became popular in the 1990s.

History

The invention of the term is disputed. Models such as Lisa Fonssagrives in the 1940s–50s, Twiggy in the 1960s, and Beverly Johnson and Iman in the 1970s achieved celebrity. The 1980s–90s supermodel era was crystallised by Peter Lindbergh's January 1990 British Vogue cover of Naomi Campbell, Cindy Crawford, Tatjana Patitz, Christy Turlington and Linda Evangelista, and by George Michael's "Freedom! '90" video.

Evangelista famously said models "don't wake up for less than $10,000 a day." The original supermodels became household names, launched businesses, and appeared in film and television.

Later eras

Kate Moss and the "heroin chic" look marked a shift in the 1990s. Brazilian models including Gisele Bündchen led a return to glamour in the 2000s. In the social-media era, models such as Kendall Jenner, Gigi and Bella Hadid, and Cara Delevingne built huge online followings, sometimes called "Insta models."

Criticism

The industry has been criticised for promoting unrealistic body standards and for lack of diversity, prompting reforms on age, health and representation on runways.
