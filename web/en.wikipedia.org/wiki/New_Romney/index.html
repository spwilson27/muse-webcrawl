New Romney

Country: England
County: Kent
District: Folkestone and Hythe
Population: about 7,000
Location: Romney Marsh, about 1 mile inland from the English Channel

New Romney is a small town in Kent, England, on the edge of Romney Marsh. It was one of the original Cinque Ports, a confederation of coastal towns that provided ships and men to the Crown in return for privileges. In its medieval heyday New Romney was a significant seaport at the mouth of the River Rother, but the great storm of 1287 changed the river's course and silting left the town about a mile from the sea, ending its career as a port.

History

The settlement grew near the older port of (Old) Romney after shingle accumulation shifted the Rother's harbour. By the 12th and 13th centuries New Romney was a thriving port and Cinque Port, sending ships to royal fleets. The catastrophic storm of February 1287 — part of the same weather that devastated Winchelsea — diverted the Rother to flow into the sea at Rye instead. Deprived of its river, the harbour silted up; the town's sea front is now at Littlestone-on-Sea, developed as a Victorian resort.

New Romney returned Members of Parliament from the Middle Ages until the Reform Act 1832 disfranchised it as a rotten borough. Smuggling was widespread on this coast in the 17th and 18th centuries, and the town later became a quiet market centre for the Marsh.

Landmarks

  • St Nicholas Church — a large Norman church, notable as one of the few surviving Cinque Port churches; the marks of the 1287 flood can still be seen, and the ground around it is lower than the street because of accumulated silt and debris.
  • The Old Stone and historic High Street buildings.
  • Romney, Hythe and Dymchurch Railway — the famous 15-inch gauge miniature railway (opened 1927) has its principal terminus at New Romney, running to Hythe and Dungeness.

Geography

The town lies on slightly higher ground at the edge of the flat alluvial Romney Marsh, historically reclaimed from the sea and used for sheep grazing — the source of the Romney Marsh breed and of wool wealth that built the region's churches. The Royal Military Canal, built during the Napoleonic Wars, passes nearby.
