The Curtiss-Wright Corporation is a globally diversified company that supplies engineered products and services for the aerospace, defense, commercial, power, and general industrial markets. It descends from companies founded by aviation pioneers Glenn Curtiss and the Wright brothers, and was formed in 1929 through the merger of Curtiss Aeroplane and Motor Company and Wright Aeronautical.

History

The merger of July 5, 1929 united Curtiss Aeroplane and Motor Company, Wright Aeronautical, and a number of related firms into one of the largest aviation companies in the United States. During the Second World War, Curtiss-Wright was one of the largest American aircraft manufacturers, producing more than 29,000 aircraft, including the P-40 Warhawk fighter, the SB2C Helldiver dive bomber, and the C-46 Commando transport. The company also manufactured Wright radial aircraft engines in large numbers.

After the war, Curtiss-Wright failed to make a successful transition to jet-powered aircraft; its F-87 Blackhawk interceptor was cancelled and the company's aircraft business withered. It pivoted toward components such as pumps, valves, actuation systems, and electronics, and later grew by acquiring specialist engineering firms. In the 21st century the company has supplied products for nuclear power plants, naval vessels including aircraft carriers and submarines, and commercial aerospace programs.

Operations

Curtiss-Wright organizes its business into segments covering aerospace and ground defense, naval and power markets, and commercial and industrial controls. Its products include flight-control actuators, recoil and turret drive systems for armored vehicles, pumps and valves for nuclear submarines, surface-effect and embedded computing modules, and instrumentation for power generation.
