Anagenesis is the gradual evolution of a species that continues to exist as an interbreeding population. In anagenesis, a single lineage accumulates changes over time until it differs sufficiently from its ancestral form that it may be classified as a new species — the ancestral species is then considered to have been transformed rather than to have split. This contrasts with cladogenesis, in which a lineage splits into two or more distinct lines.

Concepts

When enough evolutionary change has accumulated, taxonomists may divide a continuous anagenetic lineage into successive chronospecies. Because the division is arbitrary — the population remained interbreeding throughout — there is longstanding debate about how readily anagenesis can be identified in the fossil record, and how common it is relative to cladogenesis. Anagenesis does not increase biological diversity, since one species simply replaces another in sequence, whereas cladogenesis does.

The term is also related to the older concept of orthogenesis and to discussions of human evolution, where it has been argued that parts of the hominin fossil record may represent a single evolving lineage rather than many coexisting species.
