The Snake (蛇) is the sixth of the twelve-year cycle of animals which appear in the Chinese zodiac, related to the Chinese calendar. The Year of the Snake is associated with the Earthly Branch symbol 巳. In Chinese tradition the snake is regarded with more ambivalence than in the West: it is linked to wisdom, intuition and beauty — the legendary creator goddess Nüwa and her consort Fuxi are depicted with serpent bodies — and the snake is sometimes called the "little dragon."

Personality traits (traditional astrology)

In popular Chinese astrology, Snake people are said to be intelligent, intuitive, graceful and private, with a tendency toward jealousy and scepticism. They are considered most compatible with the Ox and the Rooster, and least compatible with the Pig. Such descriptions are folkloric and are not supported by scientific evidence — a caveat applying to zodiac personality claims generally.

Cultural notes

The snake's place in the cycle is told in variants of the Great Race legend, in which the Jade Emperor ranked the animals. The Vietnamese zodiac replaces some animals of the Chinese cycle but retains the Snake. The Year of the Snake is celebrated in Chinese New Year imagery worldwide, with red papercut snakes a common motif for 2025.
