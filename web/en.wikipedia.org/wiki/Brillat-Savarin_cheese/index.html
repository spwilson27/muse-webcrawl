Brillat-Savarin cheese

Brillat-Savarin is a soft, white-rinded triple cream cow's milk cheese from France, created in the 1930s by cheesemaker Henri Androuët and named after the 18th–19th-century French gourmet and writer Jean Anthelme Brillat-Savarin, author of The Physiology of Taste. It is produced in Burgundy and Normandy and protected by a PGI (Protected Geographical Indication) since 2017.

Characteristics

Made by adding cream to whole milk, Brillat-Savarin is a triple cream cheese with a fat-in-dry-matter content of at least 72 (and typically 75) percent. It is sold young in small wheels of about 100–600 g. The bloomy rind of Penicillium candidum encloses a dense, slightly chalky centre that ripens to a buttery, mushroomy, mildly salty paste with a gentle lactic tang. It is usually eaten fresh rather than strongly matured.

Serving

It is classically paired with Champagne and light, fruity wines, and is sometimes served with truffles folded into the paste for festive versions. Related enriched cheeses include Explorateur and Délice de Bourgogne.
