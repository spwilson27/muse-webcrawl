Roskomnadzor

The Federal Service for Supervision of Communications, Information Technology and Mass Media, abbreviated Roskomnadzor (Russian: Роскомнадзор), is a Russian federal executive agency responsible for supervision in the sphere of communications, information technology and mass media. Founded in its present form in 2008, it is subordinate to the Ministry of Digital Development, Communications and Mass Media.

Functions

Roskomnadzor licenses broadcasters and telecom operators, registers mass media, supervises personal-data protection, and maintains the federal register of prohibited information — websites that internet service providers in Russia must block. Grounds for inclusion include child pornography, promotion of drugs and suicide, extremist materials, and content designated as prohibited by courts or other agencies.

Internet blocking

The agency has enforced the blocking of opposition media, the messaging service Telegram (attempted, largely unsuccessfully, from 2018 to 2020), LinkedIn, and — following Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine — Facebook, Instagram and Twitter (X), as well as numerous independent news outlets. It also fines or restricts services that fail to store Russian users' data in Russia or to remove banned content. Critics describe Roskomnadzor as the principal instrument of internet censorship in Russia, while the agency presents its work as protecting users and enforcing Russian law.
