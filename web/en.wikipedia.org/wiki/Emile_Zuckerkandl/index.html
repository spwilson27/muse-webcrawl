Emile Zuckerkandl

Emile Zuckerkandl (September 4, 1849, in Győr, Hungary – May 28, 1910, in Vienna) was an Austrian-Hungarian anatomist. He was the brother of urologist Otto Zuckerkandl (1861–1921).

Life and work

Zuckerkandl studied at the University of Vienna, where he was a student of Josef Hyrtl and Karl von Rokitansky, and worked as an assistant to the anatomist Karl Langer. In 1879 he became professor of anatomy at the University of Utrecht, and from 1882 he held the chair of anatomy at the University of Graz; in 1888 he moved to the University of Vienna as professor of descriptive and topographical anatomy.

He carried out extensive research in nearly all branches of anatomy, making contributions in the fields of the normal and pathological anatomy of the nasal cavity, the anatomy of the facial skeleton, blood vessels, the brain, chromaffin tissue, and other areas. He wrote over 200 scientific works.

Eponyms

  • Organ of Zuckerkandl – a chromaffin body derived from neural crest tissue located at the bifurcation of the aorta or at the origin of the inferior mesenteric artery.
  • Zuckerkandl's fascia – the posterior layer of the renal fascia.
  • Zuckerkandl's tubercle – a pyramidal extension of the thyroid gland.
  • Zuckerkandl's convolution – the subcallosal gyrus of the brain.

His wife, Berta Zuckerkandl-Szeps, was a noted writer, journalist and art critic whose salon was a centre of Viennese cultural life.
