Yemeni Civil War (2014–present)

Date16 September 2014 – present
LocationYemen, with spillover into Saudi Arabia
Main partiesHouthi movement (Ansar Allah) and allies; internationally recognised Yemeni government; Saudi-led coalition; Southern Transitional Council; al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula
StatusOngoing; UN-brokered truce in 2022, followed by intermittent hostilities

The Yemeni civil war is an ongoing multilateral civil war that began in late 2014 when the Houthi movement (Ansar Allah), allied with forces loyal to former president Ali Abdullah Saleh, seized the capital Sanaa and forced President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi's government to flee. In March 2015 a coalition led by Saudi Arabia intervened militarily in support of Hadi's government.

Background

Yemen's modern instability followed the 2011 uprising that ended Saleh's rule, a troubled transition under Hadi, long-standing northern grievances of the Houthis (who had fought earlier wars in Saada), southern separatist sentiment, and the presence of al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula.

Course of the war

After taking Sanaa in September 2014, the Houthis advanced south toward Aden in 2015, prompting the Saudi-led intervention (Operation Decisive Storm) with airstrikes, a naval blockade, and Emirati-backed ground forces. The front lines later hardened: the Houthis retained Sanaa and the populous northwest, the UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council took much of the south, and government-aligned forces held areas including Marib and the Hadramawt coast. Saleh broke with the Houthis in December 2017 and was killed by them.

Humanitarian crisis

The war produced what the UN long described as the world's worst humanitarian crisis: hundreds of thousands of deaths from fighting, disease and hunger, widespread displacement, cholera outbreaks, and severe food insecurity. A UN-brokered nationwide truce took effect in April 2022 and largely held militarily even after formally lapsing, while negotiations involving Saudi Arabia, Oman and the Houthis continued.
