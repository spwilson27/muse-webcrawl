Bodhipakkhiyādhammā

[Image: A traditional list of the thirty-seven aids to awakening carved on a temple plaque]

In Buddhism, bodhipakkhiyādhammā (Pali; variant spellings include bodhipakkhiya-dhamma and Sanskrit bodhipakṣadharma) are qualities or "wings" conducive to enlightenment (bodhi). The term applies to seven sets of such qualities regularly enumerated in the Pali Canon and developed in later Abhidharma and commentary literature, comprising 37 individual qualities in total.

The seven sets

The seven sets are:

  • the four foundations of mindfulness (satipaṭṭhāna)
  • the four right exertions (sammappadhāna)
  • the four bases of spiritual power (iddhipāda)
  • the five faculties (indriya)
  • the five powers (bala)
  • the seven factors of enlightenment (bojjhaṅga)
  • the Noble Eightfold Path (ariya-magga)

Significance

The list is an ancient organising framework for the Buddha's teaching on practice: commentators such as Buddhaghosa correlate the sets with stages of meditation and insight, and the 37 qualities are treated as a summary of the path in Theravada Buddhism, with parallels in Mahayana sources, where they appear in texts such as the Abhidharmakośa.
