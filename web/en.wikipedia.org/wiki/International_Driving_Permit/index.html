An International Driving Permit is a multilingual translation of a domestic driving licence.

An International Driving Permit (IDP) is a document that allows the holder to drive a private motor vehicle in another country when accompanied by a valid driving licence issued by their home country. The IDP is essentially an official, multilingual translation of the holder's domestic licence, printed in a standardised booklet format, and it is not valid on its own. It allows foreign authorities and car-rental companies to verify the holder's driving entitlements in their own language.

Legal basis

The IDP derives from two United Nations conventions on road traffic. The Geneva Convention on Road Traffic of 1949 established the model most widely used today, with a maximum validity of one year. The later Vienna Convention on Road Traffic of 1968 provides for a similar permit valid for up to three years, but it has not been ratified by all countries — notably, the United States is a party only to the 1949 Convention — so permits of the 1949 pattern remain the most universally accepted. An IDP issued in one contracting state is generally recognised by other contracting states.

Format and contents

The permit is a grey booklet, slightly larger than a passport, containing translations of the holder's licence details into multiple languages, which may include English, French, Spanish, Russian, Chinese, German, Arabic, Italian and others depending on the issuing country. It bears the holder's photograph, signature, personal details, the vehicle categories for which the domestic licence is valid, and the official stamp of the issuing authority. Vehicle categories follow the convention's classification: motorcycles, passenger cars up to specified weights, vehicles for goods transport, and vehicles for passenger transport with trailer endorsements.

Issuing

IDPs are issued only in the country of the holder's residence, normally by authorised automobile associations or government agencies — for example the AAA and AATA in the United States, the RAC and AA in the United Kingdom, and equivalent bodies elsewhere. Because IDPs cannot be legitimately sold by unauthorised resellers, various fraudulent websites offering "international driving licences" exist; such documents have no legal standing. Fees are typically modest, and applicants usually must be at least 18 years old and hold a full domestic licence.

Use and limitations

Requirements vary: some countries do not require an IDP from foreign drivers whose licences are in the local language or script, while others require one for driving, for renting a car, or both. The IDP does not confer any driving entitlement beyond that of the underlying licence, cannot be used in the holder's country of residence, and ceases to be valid if the domestic licence is suspended or expires.
