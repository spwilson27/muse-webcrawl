Brown Girl Dreaming

Brown Girl Dreaming is a 2014 memoir in verse by American author Jacqueline Woodson. Written for young readers, it recounts Woodson's childhood in the 1960s and 1970s, split between Greenville, South Carolina, and Brooklyn, New York, during the civil rights era.

Content

Through a series of free-verse poems, Woodson describes her family's Great Migration story, her Jehovah's Witness upbringing, the death of her father, summers with her grandparents in the South, and the slow discovery of her gift for storytelling—even as reading came with difficulty at school. Historical figures and events, from Rosa Parks to the march on Washington, thread through the personal narrative.

Reception and awards

The book won the National Book Award for Young People's Literature in 2014 and was named a Newbery Honor book and a Coretta Scott King Award honor book. Critics praised its spare, lyrical language and its portrait of an emerging writer. It is widely taught in American schools.
