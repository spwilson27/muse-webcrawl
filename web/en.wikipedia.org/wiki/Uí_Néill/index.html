Uí Néill

Uí Néill
Country: Ireland
Founder: Niall of the Nine Hostages (legendary)
Branches: Northern Uí Néill, Southern Uí Néill
Period: 5th–12th centuries (dominant)

The Uí Néill (pronounced [iː ˈn̠ʲeːl̪ʲ]; "descendants of Niall") were a group of dynasties in early medieval Ireland claiming descent from Niall of the Nine Hostages (Niall Noígíallach), a legendary High King of Ireland of the 4th or 5th century. From the 6th to the 12th century the Uí Néill dominated Irish politics, monopolising the kingship of Tara — the high kingship of Ireland — for much of that period.

Branches

The dynasty split into two great divisions:

  • The Northern Uí Néill, descended from Niall's sons Eógan and Conall Gulban, who carved kingdoms out of the north-west: Cenél nEógain (ancestors of the O'Neills of Tyrone), Cenél Conaill (Donegal), and later Cenél nEndai and others.
  • The Southern Uí Néill, descended from Niall's other sons, who dominated the Irish midlands, including Clann Cholmáin of Mide (Meath) and the Síl nÁedo Sláine of Brega.

High kingship

By the early 7th century the Uí Néill had established a durable grip on the kingship of Tara. Under kings such as Domnall mac Áedo and later Máel Sechnaill mac Domnaill, the high kingship alternated between the northern and southern branches. Their power rested on control of wealthy monastic churches — Armagh, Iona, Derry and Clonmacnoise among them — and on the subordination of neighbouring provincial kings.

Decline

The rise of Brian Boru of Munster (d. 1014) broke the Uí Néill monopoly, though Uí Néill kings such as Flaithbertach Ua Néill and Domnall Ua Lochlainn remained leading figures into the 12th century. The great families descended from them — O'Neill, O'Donnell, Mac Lochlainn, O'Melaghlin — remained powerful in Ulster and the midlands throughout the medieval period.
