Kidney disease

Kidney disease, or renal disease, is damage to or disease of a kidney. It ranges from acute kidney injury, a sudden loss of function over hours to days, to chronic kidney disease (CKD), a progressive decline over months to years that can lead to kidney failure.

Causes

The leading causes worldwide are diabetes mellitus and hypertension. Other causes include glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease, infections, urinary tract obstruction, autoimmune disease and nephrotoxic drugs.

Diagnosis and staging

Kidney function is assessed mainly by estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) from serum creatinine, and by urine albumin testing. CKD is staged from G1 (normal filtration with kidney damage) to G5 (kidney failure).

Treatment

Management aims to slow progression: blood pressure and blood sugar control, ACE inhibitors or ARBs, dietary measures, and treatment of complications such as anaemia and mineral-bone disorder. End-stage disease requires dialysis or transplantation.
