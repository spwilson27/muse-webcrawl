Transoxiana or Transoxania is an ancient name referring to a region of Central Asia located between the Amu Darya (ancient Oxus) and Syr Darya rivers, roughly corresponding to modern-day eastern Uzbekistan, western Tajikistan, southern Kazakhstan and southwestern Kyrgyzstan. The name means "land beyond the Oxus" from the perspective of the Iranian world to the south.

History

In antiquity the region was inhabited largely by the Sogdians, an Iranian people whose merchant networks dominated the Silk Road, and formed part of the Achaemenid, Hellenistic and Kushan spheres. The Muslim conquest of Transoxiana in the 7th and 8th centuries CE, capped by Qutayba ibn Muslim's campaigns and the Battle of Talas in 751, brought the region into the Islamic world, where Samarkand and Bukhara became great centres of Persianate culture and scholarship under the Samanids.

Mongol and Timurid periods

Devastated by the Mongol invasions of 1219–1221, the region recovered under the Chagatai Khanate and spectacularly under Timur (Tamerlane), who made Samarkand his imperial capital in the late 14th century. Under Timur and his successors, Transoxiana saw a flowering of architecture, astronomy and Persian literature.

Later periods

In the 16th century the region passed to Uzbek khanates centred on Bukhara and Khiva. Russian expansion in the 19th century absorbed Transoxiana into the Governorate of Turkestan, and its lands were divided among Soviet republics in the 1920s national delimitation.
