The University of Colorado (CU) is a public university system in Colorado consisting of four campuses: the University of Colorado Boulder, the University of Colorado Denver, the University of Colorado Anschutz Medical Campus in Aurora, and the University of Colorado Colorado Springs. Founded in 1876, shortly before Colorado became a state, the system enrolls more than 66,000 students and employs tens of thousands of faculty and staff, making it one of the state's largest employers.

Campuses

CU Boulder, the flagship, was founded in 1876 and is known for aerospace engineering, physics and environmental sciences; it is affiliated with national laboratories including NIST and NOAA institutes, and its faculty include Nobel laureates in physics. CU Denver serves a largely urban, commuter population downtown. The Anschutz Medical Campus in Aurora houses the School of Medicine, Children's Hospital Colorado and UCHealth University of Colorado Hospital. UCCS in Colorado Springs, founded in 1965, is the system's fastest-growing campus.

Governance and research

The system is governed by an elected nine-member Board of Regents — Colorado is one of a few states electing university regents — and led by a system president. CU's sponsored research exceeds $1.4 billion annually, and the university is associated with more astronauts than any other public institution, owing to its aerospace programs and proximity to the space industry.

Athletics

The Boulder campus competes as the Colorado Buffaloes ("Buffs"), with Ralphie the buffalo as its live mascot; its football team won a share of the 1990 national championship.
