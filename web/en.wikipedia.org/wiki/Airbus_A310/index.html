Airbus A310

Airbus A310
Type: Wide-body airliner
Manufacturer: Airbus
First flight: 3 April 1982
Introduction: April 1983 with Swissair and Lufthansa
Status: In limited service, mainly cargo and military
Produced: 1983–1998
Number built: 255

The Airbus A310 is a wide-body aircraft, designed and manufactured by Airbus Industrie, then a consortium of European aerospace companies. Airbus had identified a demand for an aircraft smaller than its A300, the first product of the consortium.

On 7 July 1978 the A310 (initially the A300B10) was launched with orders from Swissair and Lufthansa. On 3 April 1982 the first prototype conducted its maiden flight, and the A310 received its type certification on 11 March 1983.

Development

The A310 was a shortened derivative of the A300 with a new wing, smaller tail and a two-crew glass cockpit common to later Airbus types. It kept the same cross-section, giving it a typical capacity of about 220 passengers in two classes, and intercontinental range of roughly 9,540 km (5,150 nmi) for the A310-300.

Variants

  • A310-200 — initial short-to-medium range version.
  • A310-300 — longer-range version with additional fuel capacity and wingtip fences, first flown in 1985.
  • A310 MRTT / MRT — military transport and aerial refuelling tanker conversions and new-builds used by several air forces.
  • A310-300F — freighter version.

Operators and retirement

Major airline operators included Lufthansa, Pan Am, Delta Air Lines, Singapore Airlines, Air India and Pakistan International Airlines. Passenger use declined as newer twinjets such as the Airbus A330 and Boeing 767/777 offered better economics; many A310s were converted to freighters or military use.

Specifications (A310-300)

Length 46.66 m; wingspan 43.9 m; height 15.8 m; typical seating 220; maximum takeoff weight 164,000 kg; cruise Mach 0.80; engines two General Electric CF6-80C2, Pratt & Whitney PW4000 or JT9D turbofans.
