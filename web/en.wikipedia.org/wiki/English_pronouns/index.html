English pronouns

LanguageEnglish
Part of speechPronoun

English pronouns are words such as I, you, he, she, it, we, and they that substitute for nouns and vary by person, number, gender, and case. Personal pronouns distinguish nominative (subject) forms such as she from accusative (object) forms such as her, and possessive forms such as hers and her.

Categories

Besides personal pronouns, English has reflexive pronouns (myself, themselves), demonstratives (this, that), interrogatives (who, what), relative pronouns (who, which, that), possessive determiners, and indefinite pronouns such as someone, anything, and none.

Usage notes

English lost the singular/plural and informal/formal distinctions of thou and you in most dialects. The singular they has been used for centuries for indefinite or unknown-gender reference and is now widely accepted also for specific individuals who use they/them pronouns. The pronoun it serves for inanimate objects, animals of unknown sex, and abstract subjects, and appears in dummy constructions such as "it is raining".
