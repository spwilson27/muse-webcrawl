Fernandina Beach is a city in Nassau County, Florida, United States, and the county seat. Located on Amelia Island in the state's far northeast, it is the northernmost city on Florida's Atlantic coast and is nicknamed the "Isle of 8 Flags" because eight different flags have flown over the island during its history.

History

The area was inhabited by Timucua peoples before European contact. The town, named for Ferdinand VII of Spain, was a Spanish port and smuggling haven, then passed through the hands of patriots, filibusters and the United States, which took permanent possession in 1821. In the late nineteenth century, businessman David Yulee planned the modern "new town" and Florida's first cross-state railroad; the Victorian-era downtown that followed is now a historic district listed on the National Register.

Tourism and economy

The 50-block historic district, anchored by Centre Street, draws visitors to its nineteenth-century buildings, festivals such as the Isle of Eight Flags Shrimp Festival, and nearby Fort Clinch State Park. The Port of Fernandina and two pulp mills are major employers, and the city is a popular stop for recreational boating on the Intracoastal Waterway.
