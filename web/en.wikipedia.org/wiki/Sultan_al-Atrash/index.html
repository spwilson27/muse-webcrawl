Sultan al-Atrash

Sultan al-Atrash (Arabic: سلطان الأطرش; 1891 – March 26, 1982), commonly known as Sultan Pasha al-Atrash, was a Syrian Druze leader, nationalist revolutionary and commander of the Great Syrian Revolt of 1925–1927 against the French Mandate. He remains one of the most celebrated figures of modern Syrian nationalism and of the Druze community.

Early life

Al-Atrash was born in the village of al-Qurayya in the Jabal al-Druze region of southern Syria, into the prominent al-Atrash clan, the traditional leaders of the Druze in the area. As a young man he served in the Ottoman army, and during the First World War he joined the Arab Revolt against Ottoman rule, fighting alongside the forces of Faisal bin Hussein and raising the Arab flag over Damascus in 1918.

Great Syrian Revolt

After France imposed its Mandate over Syria, tensions in the Jabal Druze escalated over French administrative interference and the arrest of Druze notables. In July 1925 al-Atrash led an uprising that began with the destruction of a French column at al-Kafr and the capture of Salkhad. The revolt spread across Syria, reaching Damascus and the Ghouta, and tied down tens of thousands of French troops before being suppressed by 1927. Al-Atrash escaped into exile in Transjordan and later the Arabian desert.

Later life

Following a general amnesty, al-Atrash returned to Syria in 1937 but declined political office, refusing offers including the presidency. He opposed the union with Egypt in the United Arab Republic and remained a revered elder statesman until his death in 1982, when he was given a massive public funeral attended by hundreds of thousands.
