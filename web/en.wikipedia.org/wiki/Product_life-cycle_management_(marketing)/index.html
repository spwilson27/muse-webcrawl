Product life-cycle management (marketing)

The product life cycle: sales over time from introduction to decline.

Product life-cycle management in marketing is the succession of strategies used by business management as a product goes through its life cycle. The product life cycle itself describes the stages a product passes through from its introduction to the market until its withdrawal: introduction, growth, maturity and decline. The goal of managing the life cycle is to prolong the profitable stages and to respond appropriately as sales and profits change.

Stages

  • Introduction — the product is launched; sales grow slowly, costs are high and profits are low or negative. Marketing focuses on building awareness and encouraging trial.
  • Growth — sales and profits rise rapidly as the market accepts the product; competitors enter, and firms focus on building brand preference and expanding distribution.
  • Maturity — sales peak and growth slows; competition is intense, prices and margins come under pressure, and firms defend market share, differentiate and seek new segments or uses.
  • Decline — sales fall as the market becomes saturated or tastes and technology change; firms must decide whether to maintain, harvest or discontinue the product.

Strategies

Life-cycle management tools include modifying the product, the market or the marketing mix — for example, finding new users, increasing usage rates among existing customers, refreshing packaging, repositioning the brand, or extending the range with variants. Extensions and relaunches aim to postpone maturity or decline, while portfolio models such as the BCG growth-share matrix place each product's life-cycle position in the context of the firm's overall product portfolio.

Criticism

Critics note that the life cycle is hard to use predictively: the length of each stage varies enormously between products, the shape of the curve is often known only in retrospect, and acting on the assumption of inevitable decline can become a self-fulfilling prophecy if firms withdraw marketing support prematurely.
