The 2020 Democratic Party presidential primaries and caucuses were a series of electoral contests organised by the Democratic Party to select the delegates to the 2020 Democratic National Convention, who formally chose the party's nominee for president in the 2020 United States presidential election. Former vice president Joe Biden won the nomination, defeating senator Bernie Sanders and a historically large field of candidates.

Candidates

More than two dozen major candidates entered the race, the largest field for a modern presidential primary. Notable candidates included Bernie Sanders, Elizabeth Warren, Michael Bloomberg, Pete Buttigieg, Amy Klobuchar, Kamala Harris, Cory Booker, Beto O'Rourke and Andrew Yang.

Course of the contest

Sanders won the popular vote in Iowa and won New Hampshire and Nevada, establishing an early lead. Biden's campaign was revived by a decisive victory in the South Carolina primary on 29 February, backed by the endorsement of Jim Clyburn. Most centrist candidates withdrew and endorsed Biden before Super Tuesday on 3 March, when Biden won ten of fourteen contests. Sanders suspended his campaign on 8 April 2020, leaving Biden as the presumptive nominee. The later primaries were disrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic, with many states postponing voting or expanding mail-in ballots.

Convention and outcome

Biden was formally nominated at the largely virtual 2020 Democratic National Convention in August, with Kamala Harris as his running mate. The Biden–Harris ticket went on to defeat incumbent president Donald Trump in the general election.
