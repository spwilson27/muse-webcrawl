Cooper River Bridge Run

LocationMount Pleasant to Charleston, South Carolina, United States
Distance10 kilometres (6.2 miles)
Established1978
CourseOver the Arthur Ravenel Jr. Bridge across the Cooper River

The Cooper River Bridge Run is an annual 10-kilometre road running event held in the Charleston, South Carolina area, in the United States. First run in 1978, the race starts in Mount Pleasant, crosses the Cooper River on the Arthur Ravenel Jr. Bridge, and finishes in downtown Charleston. It is one of the largest 10K races in the United States, regularly attracting tens of thousands of participants.

History

The race was founded in 1978 by Dr. Marcus Newberry of the Medical University of South Carolina to promote physical fitness. The first event drew about 1,300 runners across the old Silas N. Pearman Bridge. As the event grew it moved to the Grace Memorial Bridge span and, after the opening of the Arthur Ravenel Jr. Bridge in 2005, onto the new eight-lane cable-stayed bridge, whose pedestrian path and wide deck allowed larger fields.

Course

The point-to-point course begins on Coleman Boulevard in Mount Pleasant, climbs the Ravenel Bridge — the only significant hill on the route — descends into Charleston, and finishes on Meeting Street near Marion Square. A kids' run and a health and wellness expo accompany the main event, held each spring, traditionally on the first Saturday of April.
