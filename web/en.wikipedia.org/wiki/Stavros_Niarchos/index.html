Stavros Spyrou Niarchos (Greek: Σταύρος Σπύρου Νιάρχος; 3 July 1909 – 16 April 1996) was a Greek billionaire shipping tycoon. Starting in 1952, he built one of the world's largest privately owned shipping fleets, pioneering the giant supertankers that transformed the oil trade, and became a celebrated — and sometimes controversial — figure of the post-war jet set. His long rivalry with his brother-in-law Aristotle Onassis was one of the famous business feuds of the twentieth century.

Early life and career

Niarchos was born in Athens to Spyros Niarchos, a naturalised American who had returned to Greece, and Eugenia Coumandaros, from a wealthy shipping family. He studied law at the University of Athens and worked in his family's grain business, where he saw that chartering ships was the costly bottleneck. In the late 1930s he began acquiring vessels of his own. During the Second World War he served as an officer in the Royal Hellenic Navy; the loss of his insured ships to wartime sinking gave him the capital to buy surplus American vessels cheaply after the war.

Shipping empire

In 1952 Niarchos ordered some of the first supertankers, the SS Tina Onassis and SS World Glory, each around 45,000 deadweight tons, then the largest tankers afloat. The closure of the Suez Canal in 1956 caused tanker rates to soar and made his fortune. By the 1970s the Niarchos group controlled more than 80 vessels. He also developed the Hellenic Shipyards at Skaramagas, Greece's largest shipyard, refineries and other industrial holdings.

Personal life and art collection

Niarchos married five times, most famously Eugenia Livanos, daughter of shipping magnate Stavros G. Livanos, and after her death in 1970, her sister Tina — the former wife of his rival Onassis — a union that provoked public scandal. His last marriage was to Charlotte Ford, daughter of Henry Ford II. He was one of the great art collectors of his era, assembling major works by Van Gogh (including the Portrait of Joseph Roulin), Picasso, Rubens and El Greco, and owning the celebrated yacht Atlantis. His name endures in the Stavros Niarchos Foundation, one of the world's largest private philanthropies, which funded the Stavros Niarchos Foundation Cultural Center in Athens, opened in 2016.
