Harmonics

Harmonics are the integer-multiple frequency components of a periodic signal or vibrating system. In physics and music, a harmonic is a wave whose frequency is a whole-number multiple of the frequency of a fundamental wave. The fundamental itself is sometimes called the first harmonic, and the components above it are also known collectively as overtones (though strictly an overtone need not be a harmonic).

In acoustics and music

A vibrating string or column of air resonates at its fundamental frequency and at harmonic frequencies determined by its length and boundary conditions. The relative amplitudes of the harmonics present in a tone determine its timbre — why, for example, a violin and a flute playing the same pitch sound different. String players can produce pure, flute-like tones called harmonics (flageolet tones) by lightly touching a string at a nodal point.

In electrical engineering

In power systems, harmonics are voltages or currents at multiples of the fundamental supply frequency (50 or 60 Hz), produced by non-linear loads such as rectifiers and variable-speed drives. Excessive harmonic distortion can overheat transformers and motors, trip protective devices, and degrade power quality; it is quantified by total harmonic distortion (THD).

In signal processing

Fourier analysis decomposes any periodic waveform into a sum of harmonics. The harmonic spectrum of a signal is central to audio synthesis, telecommunications, and vibration analysis.
