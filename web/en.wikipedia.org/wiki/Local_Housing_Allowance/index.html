Local Housing Allowance

Local Housing Allowance (LHA) is a system for calculating the maximum amount of Housing Benefit (and the housing element of Universal Credit) payable to tenants in the private rented sector in the United Kingdom. Introduced nationally in April 2008 under the Welfare Reform Act 2007, it replaced the previous individual rent-officer determinations with standard rates for each Broad Rental Market Area (BRMA) and household size category.

How it works

Rent officers (the Valuation Office Agency in England, and equivalent bodies in Scotland and Wales) survey local rents and set LHA rates for five categories — a room in a shared property, and one to four bedrooms. Rates were originally set at the median (50th percentile) of local rents; in April 2011 they were reduced to the 30th percentile and subject to national caps. Payment is normally made to the tenant rather than the landlord, intended to give claimants financial responsibility and choice, though direct payment to landlords can be arranged in cases of vulnerability or arrears.

Changes and freezes

From 2013 LHA rates were uprated by CPI rather than actual rents, and from 2016 they were frozen for four years, decoupling them from market rents entirely. A temporary relinking to the 30th percentile occurred in April 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic, followed by another cash freeze; the Autumn Statement of November 2023 announced rates would again be raised to the 30th percentile in April 2024, after which a freeze was again applied. A shared accommodation rate applies to most single claimants under 35, limiting them to the rate for a room in a shared house.

Criticism

Housing charities and local authorities have linked frozen LHA rates to rising rent shortfalls, arrears, and homelessness, noting that in many areas fewer than a small fraction of listings are affordable at the LHA rate. Successive governments have defended freezes as necessary for controlling welfare spending.
