Commedia dell'arte is a form of professional improvised theatre that originated in Italy in the 16th century and spread across Europe. Its name means "comedy of the profession" (that is, of professional actors), distinguishing it from the scripted court comedy of amateurs.

Characteristics

Troupes performed from a brief plot outline (canovaccio) rather than a full script, improvising dialogue around a fixed set of stock characters, many of them masked. Actors specialised in one role for their entire careers and filled the outline with rehearsed comic routines called lazzi, songs, dances and acrobatics.

Stock characters

The best-known characters include Arlecchino (Harlequin), the acrobatic servant in a diamond-patterned costume; Pantalone, the miserly old Venetian merchant; Il Dottore, the pompous, pseudo-learned doctor; Il Capitano, the boastful cowardly soldier; and the unmasked innamorati, the young lovers around whom the plots revolved, attended by clever servants such as Columbina.

Influence

Commedia dell'arte shaped European theatre for two centuries, influencing playwrights such as Shakespeare, Molière and Goldoni, and leaving a legacy visible in pantomime, slapstick cinema and modern improvisational comedy.
