Wilhelm von Tegetthoff

Wilhelm von Tegetthoff (1827–1871), Austria's most celebrated admiral.

Wilhelm von Tegetthoff (23 December 1827 – 7 April 1871) was an Austrian admiral, considered one of the greatest naval commanders of the 19th century. He is best remembered for his victory over a larger Italian fleet at the Battle of Lissa in 1866.

Career

Born in Marburg (Maribor) in Styria, Tegetthoff entered the Austrian naval academy in Venice and rose rapidly. He fought in the blockade of Venice in 1848–49, conducted campaigns against pirates, and distinguished himself at the Battle of Heligoland in 1864 during the Second Schleswig War, where his squadron engaged Danish forces.

Battle of Lissa

During the Third Italian War of Independence, on 20 July 1866, Tegetthoff's fleet of ironclads met the Italian fleet under Admiral Persano near the island of Lissa (Vis) in the Adriatic. Employing aggressive ramming tactics, his flagship Erzherzog Ferdinand Max sank the Italian ironclad Re d'Italia, securing a decisive victory despite numerical and technological inferiority. The battle influenced naval design for decades, encouraging the fitting of rams to warships.

Later life

Promoted to vice admiral and commander of the Austro-Hungarian Navy, Tegetthoff pushed through reforms before dying of pneumonia in Vienna in 1871, aged 43. Monuments in Vienna and Maribor, and several warships, commemorated his name.
