Retail

[Image: shoppers browsing clothing rails in a department store]

Retail is the sale of goods and services directly to consumers for personal use, in relatively small quantities, through shops, markets, mail order and — increasingly — online channels. Retailers buy from wholesalers or manufacturers, break bulk, hold stock, display and provide service, earning the margin between wholesale and retail prices.

Formats and change

Formats include department stores, supermarkets and hypermarkets, convenience stores, discounters, speciality chains, and market stalls; the largest retailers (Walmart, Amazon, Costco, Schwarz Group) are among the world's biggest companies and employers — Walmart alone employs over two million people. Retail is a major employer everywhere and a sensitive barometer of consumer spending. Structural change has been relentless: self-service and supermarkets after 1945, out-of-town malls, big-box discounting, then e-commerce (roughly a fifth of sales in many advanced economies, higher in China and the UK), squeezing high streets and mid-market chains while logistics and data — loyalty schemes, dynamic pricing — become the competitive frontier studied in Marketing.
