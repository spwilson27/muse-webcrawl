Lateen

A lateen (French: voile latine; Italian: vela latina; Portuguese: vela latina) or latin-rig is a triangular sail set on a long yard mounted at an angle on the mast, and running in a fore-and-aft direction. The settee sail is a lateen with the front portion of the yard missing.

The lateen is one of the most consequential rigs in the history of sailing. Because a lateen sail can lie much closer to the wind than a square sail, it allows a vessel to sail at a shallow angle into the wind — to "beat to windward" — which greatly increased a ship's manoeuvrability and opened new routes to regular commerce. The rig became the standard of Mediterranean and Indian Ocean shipping for well over a thousand years.

Description

The lateen consists of a triangular sail whose head is laced to a long, sloping yard (the antenna). The forward, lower end of the yard is tacked down near the bow or the base of the mast, while the after end rises at an angle of roughly 30–45 degrees. The sail's foot is usually loose. To tack, many traditional lateen vessels dip the yard around the mast so the sail stays on the lee side, a laborious manoeuvre on large ships but acceptable on small craft.

History

Triangular fore-and-aft sails appear in the Mediterranean by late antiquity; depictions of lateen-rigged vessels survive from the Roman period, and the rig was widespread by the early medieval period. Arab sailors carried the lateen across the Indian Ocean and along the East African coast, where it remains the characteristic rig of the dhow. In the fifteenth century the lateen was combined with square sails on Portuguese caravels, producing the caravela latina and the hybrid carrack, vessels that undertook the great voyages of exploration.

Variants

The settee is a quadrilateral variant in which the yard is cut short at its lower end so the sail's forward edge drops nearly vertically. The crab claw sail of Oceania is sometimes compared to the lateen, though it evolved independently. Modern small craft such as the Sunfish use a lateen-like rig, and the sail is still widely used on dhows, feluccas, and Mediterranean fishing boats.
