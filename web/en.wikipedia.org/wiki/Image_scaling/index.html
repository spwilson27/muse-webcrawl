Image scaling

In computer graphics and digital imaging, image scaling refers to the resizing of a digital image. In video technology, the magnification of digital material is known as upscaling or resolution enhancement, while reducing size is downscaling.

When scaling a raster (bitmapped) image, a new image with a higher or lower number of pixels must be generated. Because the original samples do not directly provide values at the new sample positions, the values of new pixels are estimated by interpolation or resampling from nearby pixels.

Algorithms

Nearest-neighbour interpolation simply assigns each output pixel the value of the closest input pixel; it is fast but produces blocky enlargements. Bilinear interpolation averages the four nearest pixels, giving smoother results. Bicubic interpolation uses a 4×4 neighbourhood and cubic functions, preserving finer detail with some risk of ringing. More sophisticated resampling filters, such as Lanczos resampling, use a windowed sinc kernel and generally give the sharpest results for photographic images.

Specialised algorithms exist for pixel art (such as hqx and xBR), which preserve crisp edges when enlarging low-resolution game graphics, and content-aware methods such as seam carving, which resize by removing paths of low-importance pixels. Since the 2010s, deep-learning based super-resolution networks have been able to hallucinate plausible high-frequency detail when upscaling.

Applications

Image scaling is built into every web browser, operating system and image editor, and is used in video playback (scaling standard-definition content to HD and 4K displays), thumbnail generation, printing, and texture filtering in 3D graphics (mipmapping).
