International Traffic in Arms Regulations

The International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) is a United States regulatory regime to restrict and control the export of defense and military technologies to safeguard national security and further foreign policy objectives. It implements the Arms Export Control Act of 1976 and is administered by the Department of State's Directorate of Defense Trade Controls.

Scope

ITAR controls items on the United States Munitions List (USML) — 21 categories covering firearms, military electronics, aircraft, satellites, and associated technical data. Exporting a controlled item, or sharing controlled technical data with a foreign person (even within the US — a "deemed export"), requires a licence. USML items are distinct from dual-use items controlled by the Commerce Department's Export Administration Regulations.

Compliance and penalties

Manufacturers and exporters of defence articles must register with the DDTC and obtain export licences or agreements. Violations carry severe civil and criminal penalties, including fines of over $1 million per violation and debarment; major cases have involved satellite exports to China and unauthorized transfers of technical data.

Reform

Beginning in 2013, an Export Control Reform initiative moved many less-sensitive items from the USML to the Commerce Control List. Reform of firearms categories in 2020 was litigated by several states. Critics argue ITAR's treatment of technical data has affected academic research and commercial space activity.
