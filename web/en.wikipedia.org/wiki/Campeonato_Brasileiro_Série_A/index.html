Campeonato Brasileiro Série A

The Campeonato Brasileiro Série A, commonly referred to as the Brasileirão, is a Brazilian professional league for men's football clubs. It is the top level of the Brazilian football league system and the country's premier football competition. It is contested by 20 clubs, operating on a system of promotion and relegation with the Campeonato Brasileiro Série B.

History

Although a national championship in various forms dates to tournaments such as the Taça Brasil (from 1959) and the Torneio Roberto Gomes Pedrosa, the Campeonato Brasileiro was formally created in 1971. For decades the format changed almost yearly, mixing league and knockout phases. In 2003 the league adopted a straightforward double round-robin format (pontos corridos), in which the team with the most points after 38 rounds is champion. The Brazilian Football Confederation (CBF) later unified the historical titles, recognising Taça Brasil and Robertão champions as Brazilian champions.

Champions and clubs

Palmeiras is the most successful club, with more than ten national titles. Other multiple champions include Santos, Flamengo, Corinthians, São Paulo, and Cruzeiro. The Brasileirão is regarded as one of the strongest leagues in the world and a major exporter of playing talent.

Format

Each season, 20 teams play each other twice, home and away. The top clubs qualify for the Copa Libertadores and Copa Sudamericana, and the bottom four are relegated to Série B.
