Astroengineering

Astroengineering refers to engineered constructions or structures on an astronomical scale, such as megastructures. Classic examples of astroengineering in science fiction include the Ringworld of Larry Niven's novel of the same name and the Dyson sphere, a hypothetical structure proposed by physicist Freeman Dyson.

Concepts

Proposed astroengineering projects include Dyson spheres and swarms that capture a large fraction of a star's energy output, star lifting (extracting material from a star to prolong its life or for other uses), Shkadov thrusters that move entire stars, and stellar engines generally. The Kardashev scale classifies civilizations partly by their capacity for such feats: a Type II civilization can harness the energy of its home star, and a Type III civilization that of its galaxy.

Searches

Because astroengineering structures would radiate waste heat in the mid-infrared, astronomers have searched survey data from telescopes such as WISE for anomalous infrared excesses that might indicate them. No confirmed examples have been found; the brief attention given to the unusual dimming of Tabby's Star is now attributed to dust.
