Mary Baker Eddy

Mary Baker Eddy (born Mary Morse Baker; July 16, 1821 – December 3, 1910) was an American religious leader and author, known as the discoverer and founder of Christian Science, a new religious movement. In 1875 she published Science and Health with Key to the Scriptures, the movement's foundational text, and in 1879 she founded the Church of Christ, Scientist.

Life

Born in Bow, New Hampshire, Baker suffered ill health for much of her early life and was widowed young. After a serious fall on ice in Lynn, Massachusetts, in 1866, she reported a remarkable recovery following prayerful reading of the Bible, an experience she regarded as the discovery of Christian Science — the divine laws of healing taught by Jesus.

Church and writings

Eddy taught and practised spiritual healing, chartered her church in Boston, and founded the Massachusetts Metaphysical College to train practitioners. The First Church of Christ, Scientist (the Mother Church) was built in Boston in 1894. She revised Science and Health continually, established the Christian Science Publishing Society, and in 1908, at the age of 87, founded the newspaper The Christian Science Monitor, which went on to win multiple Pulitzer Prizes.

Legacy

Eddy was one of the most prominent American women of her era, listed among the most influential religious figures in American history. The Mary Baker Eddy Library in Boston preserves her papers and home.
