Khardung La

Elevation5,359 m (17,582 ft)
LocationLadakh, India
RangeLadakh Range, Himalayas
ConnectsLeh valley with Shyok and Nubra valleys

Khardung La (Tibetan for "Khardung Pass"; la means pass) is a high mountain pass in the Ladakh Range north of Leh in the Indian union territory of Ladakh. At an elevation of 5,359 metres (17,582 ft), it is the gateway from the Indus valley to the Shyok and Nubra valleys and is maintained by the Border Roads Organisation as a strategic motorable road.

History and significance

The pass lies on the ancient caravan route from Leh to Kashgar in Central Asia, over which caravans of Bactrian camels carried silk, tea, spices and charas. Today its importance is military and touristic: the road supplies the Nubra valley and the Indian Army's positions toward the Siachen Glacier, and it is a famous destination for motorcyclists and mountain bikers riding from Leh.

Elevation claims

Khardung La is frequently promoted locally as the "world's highest motorable pass" at a signposted 18,380 ft (5,602 m). GPS measurements, however, consistently place the summit near 5,359 m (17,582 ft), and several passes in Tibet and the newer Indian pass at Umling La (5,798 m) are higher. The discrepancy is a well-known feature of the pass's folklore.

Travel

The road from Leh climbs about 39 km to the summit, where thin air makes altitude sickness a real risk; travellers are advised to acclimatise in Leh and not to linger at the top. Inner-line permits are required for foreign visitors travelling beyond the pass into Nubra. The pass is generally open from May to October and can close temporarily after snowfall at any time of year.
