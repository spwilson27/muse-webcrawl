Chuchotage

Chuchotage
French for: 'whispering'
Type: Simultaneous interpretation
Equipment: None, or portable tour-guide system
Listeners: Typically 1–2

Chuchotage (from French chuchoter, 'to whisper') is a form of simultaneous interpretation in which the interpreter sits beside one or two listeners and whispers the translation in real time, without electronic equipment — or, in an extended form, using a portable transmitter and headsets for a small group.

Use

Chuchotage is used at meetings, court proceedings, site visits and conferences where only a few participants need a given language and a full booth setup (as used by the United Nations) would be impractical. Its limits are the strain on the interpreter's voice, ambient noise, and disturbance to nearby participants, so interpreters usually work in pairs and swap every 20–30 minutes.

Comparison

Unlike consecutive interpretation, chuchotage adds no time to proceedings; unlike booth simultaneous interpretation, it requires no soundproof booth but serves far fewer listeners.
