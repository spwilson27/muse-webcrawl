Pasiphaë

Pasiphaë
Parents: Helios and Perse
Consort: Minos
Children: Ariadne, Phaedra, Androgeus, the Minotaur, and others

In Greek mythology, Pasiphaë (from Greek Πασιφάη, "wide-shining") was a queen of Crete, the wife of King Minos, and the mother of the Minotaur. She was the daughter of the sun god Helios and the Oceanid Perse, and the sister of Circe and Aeëtes.

Myth

According to the myth, Minos prayed to the sea god Poseidon for a sign of his right to rule, and Poseidon sent a magnificent white bull from the sea, on the condition that it be sacrificed to the god. Minos kept the bull instead, and in punishment Poseidon caused Pasiphaë to fall in love with the animal. She persuaded the craftsman Daedalus to build a hollow wooden cow covered in hide, in which she concealed herself to mate with the bull. From this union she gave birth to the Minotaur, a creature with the head of a bull and the body of a man, which Minos imprisoned in the Labyrinth.

Family and cult

Pasiphaë's other children by Minos included Ariadne, Phaedra, Androgeus, Glaucus, Catreus, Deucalion, and Xenodice. Like her sister Circe, she was associated with witchcraft and potions; in one story she cursed Minos so that his infidelities proved fatal to his lovers, until Procris found a cure. In Sparta she was worshipped as an oracular goddess at a shrine near Thalamae, and some scholars connect her with an older lunar divinity, consistent with her father's solar identity.
