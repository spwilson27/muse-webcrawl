Robotic lawn mower

A robotic lawn mower is an autonomous robot used to cut lawn grass. A typical robotic lawn mower (in particular earlier generation models) requires the user to set up a border wire defining the area of the lawn. The robot uses this wire to locate the boundary of the area to be trimmed and to locate a base station for recharging.

History

The first robotic lawn mower was the MowBot, introduced and patented in 1969 and already showing many features of today's most popular products. In 1995, Husqvarna introduced the Solar Mower, the first fully solar-powered robotic mower, and in 1998 launched the Automower line, which became one of the most widely sold robotic mowers in the world.

Operation

Robotic mowers navigate within a boundary wire laid around the lawn. They typically mow in a random or systematic pattern, mulching the clippings finely so that no collection bag is needed. When the battery runs low, the mower returns to its charging station by following the boundary wire or a guide wire.

Modern developments

Newer models use GPS-assisted navigation, RTK positioning, and camera- or lidar-based obstacle detection, eliminating the need for a boundary wire. Smartphone apps allow owners to schedule mowing, adjust cutting height, and receive theft alerts. Anti-theft features include PIN codes, alarms, and GPS tracking.

Safety

Robotic mowers are fitted with lift and tilt sensors that stop the blades immediately when the unit is picked up or tipped. Their small, pivoting razor blades are recessed beneath the body, making them considerably safer than conventional rotary mowers.
