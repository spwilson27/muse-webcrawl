The Australian Ballet (TAB) is the largest classical ballet company in Australia. It was founded in 1962 by the English-born dancer and administrator Dame Peggy van Praagh, with initial funding from the theatrical firm J. C. Williamson Theatres Ltd and the Australian Elizabethan Theatre Trust. Its first performance was Tchaikovsky's Swan Lake, staged at Her Majesty's Theatre, Sydney, on 2 November 1962.

History

The Australian Ballet emerged from the earlier Borovansky Ballet, the company founded by Edouard Borovansky which had provided Australia with professional ballet from the 1940s until its closure in 1961. Van Praagh built the new company's repertoire on the 19th-century classics alongside works by Australian choreographers and contemporary international figures. Rudolf Nureyev's production of Don Quixote, created for the company in 1970 and filmed in 1973, became one of its signature works.

Subsequent artistic directors have included Sir Robert Helpmann (acting), Marilyn Jones, Maina Gielgud (1983–1996), Ross Stretton (1997–2001), David McAllister (2001–2020), and David Hallberg, the former Bolshoi and American Ballet Theatre principal who took up the post in 2021. Maina Gielgud's 1996 staging of Giselle and Graeme Murphy's distinctly Australian Nutcracker – The Story of Clara (1992) are among its celebrated productions.

Company and school

The company, based in Melbourne, has around 80 dancers and performs around 200 performances a year in Australia's major cities, regional centres, and on international tours. Its activities are closely linked with The Australian Ballet School (founded 1964), the company's official training institution, and Orchestra Victoria, which accompanies its Melbourne seasons. Resident choreographers have included Stephen Baynes and Alice Topp.

Repertoire

The repertoire spans full-length classics (Swan Lake, The Sleeping Beauty, Giselle, Don Quixote), works by Frederick Ashton, Kenneth MacMillan, Jiří Kylián, and John Neumeier, and Australian commissions. The company celebrated its 60th anniversary in 2022.
