Weather Report was an American jazz fusion band active from 1970 to 1986. The band was co-led by keyboardist Joe Zawinul and saxophonist Wayne Shorter, who remained its core throughout its 16-year run. Weather Report was initially a free-form experimental group, but it became one of the most commercially successful jazz groups of the 1970s, pioneering jazz fusion with funk, rock, and world music rhythms.

History

Zawinul and Shorter formed the band in 1970 after working together in Miles Davis's early fusion sessions. Their self-titled debut (1971) and Sweetnighter (1973) explored atmospheric improvisation. With Mysterious Traveller (1974) the music became more groove-oriented. Bassist Jaco Pastorius joined in 1976, and the albums Black Market (1976) and especially Heavy Weather (1977) achieved wide popularity; Heavy Weather contained the hit "Birdland," which became a jazz standard and won a Grammy in a later recording.

Style and legacy

The band was noted for Zawinul's synthesizer orchestration, intricate unison lines between saxophone and keyboards, and a collective improvisation ethic in which every member was treated as a soloist. Its live album 8:30 (1979) won the Grammy for Best Jazz Fusion Performance. Weather Report influenced generations of fusion and contemporary jazz musicians and remains, along with the Mahavishnu Orchestra and Return to Forever, among the defining ensembles of 1970s fusion.
