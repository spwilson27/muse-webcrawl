Barbecue in Kansas City

Kansas City barbecue refers to the regional barbecue style of slowly smoked meats originating in Kansas City, Missouri, and Kansas City, Kansas. It is one of the major regional styles of American barbecue, known for using a wide variety of meats, a thick sweet tomato-and-molasses-based sauce, and burnt ends, a signature dish made from the crusty point of smoked beef brisket.

History

The style traces to Henry Perry, a Tennessee-born pitmaster who, beginning around 1907, sold smoked meats from a trolley in the city's African American district and later opened a restaurant in an old trolley barn. Perry trained many who became legends of Kansas City barbecue, including Charlie Bryant and Arthur Pinkard; Bryant's brother Arthur Bryant took over one restaurant, and George Gates founded Gates Bar-B-Q after working in the Perry tradition. The city's location as a rail and stockyard hub gave pitmasters access to every kind of meat, which is why Kansas City barbecue, unlike more pork-focused regions, includes beef brisket, pork ribs, pulled pork, chicken, turkey, mutton, sausage, and even fish.

Characteristics

Meats are dry-rubbed with spices and slow-smoked over hickory and oak, then served with sauce on the side or applied. Kansas City sauce is thick, sweet, and tangy, based on tomatoes and molasses, and has become the generic template for commercial barbecue sauce in the United States. Burnt ends, once given away free at Arthur Bryant's counter, are now a prized delicacy: the brisket point is cubed, re-seasoned, and smoked further until deeply caramelized.

Culture

The Kansas City metropolitan area has more than one hundred barbecue restaurants and calls itself the "barbecue capital of the world." The American Royal World Series of Barbecue, held annually in the area, is the largest barbecue competition in the world, and the Kansas City Barbeque Society sanctions hundreds of contests internationally.
