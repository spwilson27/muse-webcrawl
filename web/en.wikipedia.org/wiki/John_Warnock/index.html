John Warnock

John Edward Warnock (October 6, 1940 – August 19, 2023) was an American computer scientist and businessman who co-founded Adobe Systems in 1982 with Charles Geschke. He was the chief inventor of PostScript, the page description language that launched desktop publishing, and a driving force behind the Portable Document Format (PDF).

Early life and career

Warnock was born in Salt Lake City, Utah, and studied mathematics and computer science at the University of Utah, earning a PhD in electrical engineering under the pioneering computer graphics programme there. He worked at Evans & Sutherland and then at Xerox PARC, where he and Geschke developed the InterPress page description language that Xerox declined to commercialise — prompting them to leave and found Adobe.

Adobe

Adobe's PostScript interpreter, licensed in the Apple LaserWriter in 1985, ignited the desktop publishing revolution. Warnock later championed the PDF format and the Camelot project from which it grew, and promoted the idea that electronic documents should look the same everywhere. He served as Adobe's chief executive until 2000 and as chairman until 2017.

Honours

Warnock received the National Medal of Technology and Innovation in 2008, shared with Geschke. He died in 2023 at Los Altos, California.
