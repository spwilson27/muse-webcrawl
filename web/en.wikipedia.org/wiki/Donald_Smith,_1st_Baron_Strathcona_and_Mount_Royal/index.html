Donald Smith, 1st Baron Strathcona and Mount Royal

Donald Alexander Smith, 1st Baron Strathcona and Mount Royal, GCMG, GCVO, PC, DL (6 August 1820 – 21 January 1914), known as Sir Donald A. Smith between 1886 and 1897, was a Scottish-born Canadian businessman who became one of the leading figures in the development of Canada. He was a Hudson's Bay Company fur trader and governor, a railway magnate, politician, diplomat, and philanthropist.

Early life and Hudson's Bay Company

Smith was born in Forres, Moray, Scotland. In 1838 he emigrated to Canada to work as a clerk for the Hudson's Bay Company in Labrador, where he spent more than three decades in the fur trade, rising to become chief factor and, later, chief commissioner responsible for the company's trade. He played a key role in the negotiations surrounding the Red River Resistance of 1869–70 as a commissioner of the Canadian government.

Railways and politics

Smith was elected to the Canadian House of Commons in 1871. As an investor, he helped rescue the St. Paul, Minneapolis and Manitoba Railway and was a principal financier of the Canadian Pacific Railway; it was Smith who drove the famous "last spike" at Craigellachie, British Columbia, on 7 November 1885. He also financed and raised Strathcona's Horse for service in the Boer War.

Later career and philanthropy

From 1896 until his death he served as Canadian High Commissioner to London. He was governor of the Hudson's Bay Company from 1889. Created Baron Strathcona and Mount Royal in 1897, Smith endowed, among other benefactions, the Royal Victoria Hospital in Montreal and buildings at McGill University. He died in London in 1914, one of the wealthiest men in the Empire.
