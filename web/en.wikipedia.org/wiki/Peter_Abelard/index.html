Peter Abelard (French: Pierre Abélard, 1079–1142) was a medieval French scholastic philosopher, leading logician, and theologian, celebrated for his brilliance as a teacher and for his tragic love affair with Héloïse d'Argenteuil.

Philosophy

Abelard made his reputation in Paris, where his lectures drew thousands of students. In the debate over universals he defended a form of conceptualism — universals as concepts formed by the mind rather than things or mere words — against the realism of William of Champeaux and the nominalism of Roscellinus. His Sic et Non ("Yes and No") arranged apparently contradictory statements of the Church Fathers side by side, pioneering the scholastic method of reconciling authorities through reason. In ethics, his Scito te ipsum ("Know thyself") located sin in intention and consent rather than the outward act.

Héloïse and later life

Employed to tutor Héloïse, the brilliant niece of Canon Fulbert of Notre Dame, Abelard began an affair with her; they married secretly, and when the affair became known Fulbert had Abelard castrated. Héloïse became a nun and later abbess of the Paraclete; Abelard became a monk. Their surviving letters, together with Abelard's autobiographical Historia Calamitatum ("History of My Calamities"), are among the most famous documents of medieval literature.

Abelard's theological writings were condemned at Soissons (1121) and again at Sens (1140), where Bernard of Clairvaux led the prosecution. He died in 1142 while travelling to Rome to appeal. He and Héloïse are commemorated together at Père Lachaise Cemetery in Paris.
