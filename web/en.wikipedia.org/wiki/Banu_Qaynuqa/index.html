The Banu Qaynuqa (Arabic: بنو قينقاع) were one of the three main Jewish tribes of Yathrib (later Medina), alongside the Banu Nadir and Banu Qurayza. They lived in the south-western part of the city and were renowned as goldsmiths, armourers and traders; unlike the other Jewish tribes they owned little farmland. In the politics of Medina they were allied with the Arab tribe of Khazraj, in particular with the chief Abd Allah ibn Ubayy.

Expulsion

After Muhammad's emigration to Medina in 622, the Qaynuqa were drawn into the Constitution of Medina's framework of alliances, but relations with the growing Muslim community deteriorated amid the escalating conflict with Mecca. Following the Muslim victory at Badr in 624, a market altercation — in the traditional account, a Muslim woman was humiliated at a Qaynuqa goldsmith's shop and a killing followed — led Muhammad to besiege the tribe's strongholds. After about two weeks the Qaynuqa surrendered. Muhammad reportedly intended severe punishment, but at the intercession of Abd Allah ibn Ubayy the tribe was instead expelled from Medina, leaving most of its property behind. The Qaynuqa departed first to Wadi al-Qura and then to southern Syria.

Assessment

The events are known from Muslim sources such as the biography of Ibn Ishaq and the histories of al-Waqidi and al-Tabari. Modern scholars debate the details and causes: some emphasise political and economic conflict and the tribe's refusal to accept Muhammad's authority, others the tension over conversion and alliance politics in a city preparing for war with Quraysh.
