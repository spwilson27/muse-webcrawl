Sense and Sensibility (film)


Theatrical release poster (placeholder image)

Sense and Sensibility is a 1995 period drama film directed by Ang Lee and based on Jane Austen's 1811 novel of the same name. Emma Thompson wrote the screenplay and stars as Elinor Dashwood, alongside Kate Winslet as her younger sister Marianne, with Hugh Grant as Edward Ferrars and Alan Rickman as Colonel Brandon.

Plot

After the death of their father, the Dashwood sisters — Elinor, Marianne and Margaret — are left with reduced means and move with their mother to a cottage on the Barton estate in Devonshire. Elinor suppresses her attachment to Edward Ferrars ("sense"), while the romantic Marianne ("sensibility") falls for the dashing John Willoughby and is courted by the reserved Colonel Brandon. Deception, disappointment and illness ultimately bring both sisters to a balance between reason and feeling.

Production and reception

Producer Lindsay Doran, a longtime admirer of Austen's novel, spent years developing the adaptation; Thompson spent five years on the screenplay, which became the film's most celebrated element. Released by Columbia Pictures, the film was a commercial and critical success, grossing over $135 million worldwide. It won the Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay — making Thompson the only person to win Oscars for both acting and writing — and the Golden Bear at the Berlin International Film Festival, and was nominated for six further Academy Awards including Best Picture.
