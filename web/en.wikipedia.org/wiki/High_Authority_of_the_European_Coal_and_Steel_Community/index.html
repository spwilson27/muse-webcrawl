High Authority of the European Coal and Steel Community

The High Authority was the executive and supranational governing body of the European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC), established by the Treaty of Paris of 1951. It began work on 10 August 1952 in Luxembourg, with Jean Monnet, the chief architect of the Schuman Plan, as its first president.

Powers and composition

The High Authority consisted of nine members — appointed by the six founding states (France, West Germany, Italy, Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg) but sworn to independence from national governments. It could take decisions binding on member states and on coal and steel enterprises: it set prices in certain conditions, levied the first genuinely European tax (a levy on coal and steel production), regulated competition, and managed the common market in coal and steel that opened in 1953.

Merger

Under the Merger Treaty of 1965, in force from 1967, the High Authority was merged with the Commissions of the EEC and Euratom into a single European Commission, ending its separate existence. The High Authority is regarded as the institutional prototype of today's supranational European Commission.
