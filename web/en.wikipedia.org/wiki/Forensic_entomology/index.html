Forensic entomology

Forensic entomology is a field of forensic science involving the study of insects and other arthropods in relation to legal investigations. Its best-known application, medico-legal entomology, uses the insects that colonise decomposing remains — particularly blow flies and their larvae — to estimate the post-mortem interval (PMI), that is, the time elapsed since death. The field also covers urban entomology (pests in legal disputes) and stored-product entomology (infestation of foodstuffs).

Estimating time of death

Within minutes to hours after death, blow flies are attracted to a body and lay eggs in natural openings and wounds. Because the development of fly larvae (maggots) through their instar stages and pupation proceeds at rates that depend predictably on temperature, examination of the oldest specimens present allows an entomologist to calculate a minimum time since death using accumulated degree-hour models. After the first days, investigators also use the predictable succession of insect communities — flies, then beetles such as carrion and skin beetles, and other taxa — to estimate longer intervals.

Other applications

Insect evidence can indicate whether a body was moved after death (if the fauna do not match the discovery site), reveal wounds (where larvae concentrate), and, through toxicological analysis of larvae, detect drugs or poisons in decomposed remains (entomotoxicology). Insects are also used in cases of neglect and abuse, wildlife poaching, and food contamination.

History

An early recorded case comes from 13th-century China, described in the legal medicine text Collected Cases of Injustice Rectified (Xi Yuan Ji Lu), in which a murderer was identified when blow flies were attracted to traces of blood on his sickle. Systematic study began in 19th-century France with Bergeret's use of insect evidence in 1855 and J.-P. Mégnin's work on faunal succession (1894). The discipline was professionalised in the late 20th century through researchers such as Bernard Greenberg and M. Lee Goff, and gained public prominence through popular media.
