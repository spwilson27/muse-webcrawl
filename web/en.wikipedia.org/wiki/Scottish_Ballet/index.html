Scottish Ballet is the national ballet company of Scotland and one of the five national performing arts companies funded directly by the Scottish Government. Founded in 1969 by choreographer Peter Darrell as Scottish Theatre Ballet, it is based at the Tramway arts centre in Glasgow and tours throughout Scotland, the United Kingdom, and internationally.

History

Peter Darrell founded the company as the Western Theatre Ballet in Bristol in 1957; in 1969 it moved to Glasgow at the invitation of the city and was renamed Scottish Theatre Ballet, becoming Scottish Ballet in 1974. Darrell led the company until his death in 1987, building a reputation for dramatic, narrative ballets. After a period of retrenchment in the late 1990s, the company was relaunched in 2003 under artistic director Ashley Page with a modernised, athletic repertoire blending classical technique with contemporary commissions. Christopher Hampson became artistic director and chief executive in 2012.

Repertoire

The company's repertoire includes specially staged full-length classics such as The Nutcracker, Swan Lake, Cinderella, and The Snow Queen, alongside new narrative works such as Cinderella and an acclaimed adaptation of A Streetcar Named Desire (2012). Scottish Ballet has also commissioned work from choreographers including Crystal Pite, David Dawson, and Sophie Laplane, whose Dextera and The Scandal at Mayerling premiered with the company. Its digital seasons, begun in 2017, pioneered short dance films such as Haunted Rooms and The Secret Theatre.

Education and outreach

Scottish Ballet runs extensive education and health programmes, including classes for people living with Parkinson's disease and multiple sclerosis, dementia-friendly performances, and the Royal Academy of Dance-affiliated Junior Associates scheme for young dancers. The company moved into purpose-built headquarters at Tramway in Glasgow's south side in 2009.
