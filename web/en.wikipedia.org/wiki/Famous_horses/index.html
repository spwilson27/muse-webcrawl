Famous horses

Throughout history, individual horses have achieved fame through racing, warfare, exploration and popular culture. This article surveys some of the best-known horses and the reasons for their renown.

Racehorses

Thoroughbred racing has produced many celebrated champions. Secretariat (1970–1989) won the American Triple Crown in 1973, setting records in all three races that still stand, including a 31-length victory in the Belmont Stakes. Man o' War dominated American racing after the First World War. In Australia, Phar Lap became a national icon during the Great Depression, and Winx retired in 2019 with 33 consecutive wins including four Cox Plates. Frankel, unbeaten in 14 starts, is regarded as one of the greatest European racehorses. Earlier, Eclipse (1764–1789) was so superior that the phrase "Eclipse first, and the rest nowhere" entered the language.

War horses

Bucephalus was the beloved mount of Alexander the Great, who tamed him as a boy and rode him through campaigns across Asia. Marengo carried Napoleon at Austerlitz, Jena and Waterloo. Comanche, a cavalry horse, was famously found alive among the dead after the Battle of the Little Bighorn in 1876. Warrior, ridden by General Jack Seely, survived the Western Front of the First World War and was dubbed "the horse the Germans could not kill".

Other famous horses

Trigger was the palomino co-star of Roy Rogers in film and television. Mister Ed was the "talking horse" of the 1960s sitcom of the same name. Seabiscuit, an undersized underdog, became a symbol of hope in Depression-era America. Black Beauty, from Anna Sewell's 1877 novel, raised awareness of animal welfare. The Trojan Horse of Greek legend, though not a real animal, remains the most famous "horse" of mythology.
