Brandenburg (German: [ˈbʁandn̩bʊʁk]; Lower Sorbian: Bramborska) is a state (Land) in north-eastern Germany. It surrounds—but does not include—the national capital and city-state of Berlin, and its own capital and largest city is Potsdam. Brandenburg is one of the federal states formed at German reunification in 1990, drawing on the territory of the historic Margraviate of Brandenburg, the core from which the Kingdom of Prussia grew. With some 29,654 square kilometres it is the largest of the eastern states and the fifth-largest in Germany, though one of the most sparsely populated, a landscape of pine forests, heath, and more than 3,000 lakes.

History

The Margraviate (Mark) of Brandenburg was founded in 1157 when the Ascanian count Albert the Bear conquered the Slavic fortress of Brandenburg an der Havel, from which the territory took its name. Ruled successively by the Ascanians, the Wittelsbach and Luxembourg dynasties, Brandenburg passed in 1415 to the Hohenzollern family, who held it for five centuries. Its electors were among the seven prince-electors of the Holy Roman Empire. In 1618 the Hohenzollerns inherited the Duchy of Prussia, and in 1701 the elector Frederick III crowned himself King in Prussia, making Brandenburg the heartland of the rising Prussian state, with Berlin as its capital and Potsdam as a royal residence city.

The province of Brandenburg was devastated in the Thirty Years' War but recovered through immigration and state-led development. After 1945 the lands east of the Oder–Neisse line were transferred to Poland, and the remainder formed a state in the Soviet occupation zone, dissolved in 1952 into districts of East Germany. The modern Land of Brandenburg was re-established on 3 October 1990, the day of German reunification. A proposed merger with Berlin was rejected by Brandenburg voters in a 1996 referendum.

Geography

Brandenburg is a lowland state of the North European Plain, shaped by the ice ages into gently rolling ground moraine, broad glacial valleys (Urstromtäler), and extensive outwash sands. Major rivers are the Elbe in the west, the Oder forming the Polish border in the east, and the Havel and Spree flowing through the centre. The state contains large protected landscapes, including the Spreewald biosphere reserve with its network of channels, the Schorfheide-Chorin biosphere reserve, the Lower Oder Valley National Park, and the Fläming and Uckermark lake districts. The highest point, the Kutschenberg, reaches only 201 metres. A Lower Sorbian-speaking minority in the south-east (Lusatia) enjoys official recognition.

Economy and transport

Historically agricultural and known as the "sandbox of the Holy Roman Empire" for its poor soils, Brandenburg's modern economy concentrates in the ring around Berlin: logistics, media and film (at the Babelsberg studios in Potsdam), aviation (around Berlin Brandenburg Airport, which lies in Brandenburg), energy, and manufacturing, including steel at Eisenhüttenstadt and chemicals in Lusatia, where lignite mining has long been important. Tourism centres on Potsdam's palaces and parks—UNESCO World Heritage Sites—and on the lakes and waterways. Berlin's suburban rail and motorway rings tie the state closely to the capital.
