Lauds

Lauds is a canonical hour of the Liturgy of the Hours (Divine Office) in the Roman Catholic Church and other Western Christian traditions, prayed at dawn or early morning. The name comes from the Latin laudes, "praises", reflecting the psalms of praise — traditionally Psalms 148–150, the Laudate psalms — that conclude the office.

Structure

Lauds typically includes an opening versicle, a hymn, psalms and a canticle with antiphons, a short reading from Scripture, a responsory, the Benedictus (the canticle of Zechariah from Luke 1) with its antiphon, intercessions, the Lord's Prayer, and a concluding prayer. In the post-Vatican II Liturgy of the Hours, Lauds and Vespers are the two principal ("hinge") hours of the day.

History

Morning prayer at sunrise is among the oldest Christian devotions, described already in early church orders. In the Rule of St Benedict the dawn office took shape as Lauds, distinct from the night office of Matins (Vigils). Before the reforms of Pius X and Paul VI, Lauds was often prayed together with Matins in the early morning. The office is a morning offering of praise and thanksgiving for the new day and for the Resurrection.
