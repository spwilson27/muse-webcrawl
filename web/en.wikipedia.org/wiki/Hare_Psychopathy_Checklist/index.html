The Hare Psychopathy Checklist–Revised (PCL-R) is a psychological assessment tool developed by Canadian psychologist Robert D. Hare to assess the presence and extent of psychopathy in individuals, most often those in criminal-justice settings. It is the most widely used and best validated measure of psychopathy.

Structure

Twenty items — including glibness/superficial charm, grandiose sense of self-worth, pathological lying, manipulativeness, lack of remorse or guilt, shallow affect, callousness/lack of empathy, poor behavioural controls, impulsivity, irresponsibility, failure to accept responsibility, early behaviour problems, juvenile delinquency, revocation of conditional release, and criminal versatility — are each scored 0, 1, or 2 (does not apply, applies somewhat, applies fully) on the basis of a semi-structured interview plus file and collateral information. Scores range from 0 to 40; a score of 30 is the conventional North American cut-off for a diagnosis of psychopathy.

Factors

Factor-analytic work groups the items into two correlated factors — Factor 1 (interpersonal and affective traits) and Factor 2 (impulsive, antisocial lifestyle) — often further divided into four facets: interpersonal, affective, lifestyle, and antisocial.

Use and criticism

PCL-R scores predict recidivism and institutional misconduct and are used in risk assessment, sentencing, and parole contexts in several jurisdictions — a use some critics consider ethically problematic, given the stigma of the label and disputes about how distinct psychopathy is from antisocial personality disorder. Related instruments include the screening version PCL:SV and the youth version PCL:YV.
