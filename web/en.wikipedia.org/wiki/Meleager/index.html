Meleager

In Greek mythology, Meleager (Μελέαγρος) was a hero of Aetolia, prince of Calydon, famed as the host and leader of the Calydonian Boar Hunt and, in some accounts, as an Argonaut. He was the son of King Oeneus of Calydon and Althaea, though some authors made Ares his father. Meleager's story is among the best known of early Greek heroic myth, told already in Book 9 of Homer's Iliad and fully in later sources such as Apollodorus and Ovid's Metamorphoses.

The fateful brand

When Meleager was born, the Fates declared that he would live only as long as a particular log (or brand) on the hearth remained unconsumed by fire. Althaea snatched the brand from the flames and hid it in a chest, preserving her son's life.

The Calydonian Boar Hunt

After Oeneus neglected Artemis in sacrifice, the goddess sent a monstrous boar to ravage Calydon. Meleager assembled the greatest heroes of Greece — including Jason, Theseus, Castor and Pollux, Peleus, Telamon, and the huntress Atalanta. Meleager killed the boar after Atalanta first wounded it, and awarded her the hide, provoking his uncles, the sons of Thestius, who seized the trophy claiming it for their family. In the ensuing fight Meleager killed them.

Death

Althaea, grieving and enraged at the death of her brothers, took out the hidden brand and threw it into the fire. As it burned away, Meleager died. In Homer's version, Meleager instead withdrew from battle against the Curetes over a dispute, like Achilles, and died in the fighting. His sisters, the Meleagrides, wept so bitterly that Artemis transformed them into guinea fowl. The story was a staple of ancient art and drama, and later retold by poets including Swinburne in Atalanta in Calydon (1865).
