Equations of motion

In physics, equations of motion are equations that describe the behaviour of a physical system in terms of its motion as a function of time. More specifically, they describe the relationship between displacement, velocity, acceleration and time for a moving object.

Kinematics: constant acceleration

For motion in a straight line with constant acceleration a, five variables — displacement s, initial velocity u, final velocity v, acceleration a and time t — are related by the "SUVAT" equations:

  • v = u + at
  • s = ut + ½at²
  • s = vt − ½at²
  • v² = u² + 2as
  • s = ½(u + v)t

Newtonian dynamics

Newton's laws of motion provide differential equations of motion for systems of particles: the net force on a particle equals its mass times its acceleration (F = ma). In analytical mechanics the same dynamics are reformulated by the Euler–Lagrange equations, derived from an action principle, and by Hamilton's equations in phase space.

Other systems

Rotational motion has analogous equations with angular displacement, velocity and acceleration. In relativity, motion follows geodesics of spacetime; in quantum mechanics, the Schrödinger equation plays the corresponding role for the wavefunction.
