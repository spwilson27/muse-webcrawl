Corsham Court is an English country house in a park designed by Capability Brown situated in the town of Corsham in Wiltshire, England, a few miles from Chippenham. It is owned by the Methuen family and is noted for its fine art collection.

History

The house was built in 1582 by Thomas Smythe on the site of a royal manor house that had belonged to the queens of several medieval kings. In 1745 the estate was bought by Paul Methuen, a wealthy clothier and diplomat, whose descendants have owned it ever since.

Between 1761 and 1764 the house was remodelled by Lancelot "Capability" Brown, who expanded the parkland and altered the house to accommodate the growing picture collection of Paul Methuen. In the early nineteenth century John Nash further enlarged the house, adding the state rooms to the east and remodelling the stables and entrance.

Art collection

The Methuen collection at Corsham includes works by Van Dyck, Reynolds, Gainsborough and Romney, along with Italian Old Masters. The Picture Gallery, designed by John Nash, remains one of the finest surviving collections of its kind in a private house.

Use in education

During and after the Second World War the house was leased to Bath Academy of Art, whose students studied there until the 1970s. Corsham Court later became associated with Bath Spa University, which used the house as a campus for postgraduate study.
