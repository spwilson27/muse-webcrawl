The BMD-1 is a Soviet airborne amphibious tracked infantry fighting vehicle, introduced in 1969. BMD stands for Boyevaya Mashina Desanta (Боевая Машина Десанта), "combat vehicle of the airborne (troops)". Designed for the Soviet Airborne Forces (VDV), it sacrifices armour for air-transportability: it can be parachute-dropped from aircraft such as the Il-76, giving paratroopers immediate armoured support after landing.

Design

The BMD-1 uses an aluminium-alloy hull to keep its weight to about 7.5 tonnes, and mounts the same turret as the BMP-1, with a 73 mm 2A28 Grom low-pressure smoothbore gun, a coaxial 7.62 mm machine gun, and a launcher rail for 9M14 Malyutka anti-tank guided missiles. Its hydropneumatic suspension allows the ground clearance to be varied, which helps in parachute stowage and cross-country travel. The vehicle is fully amphibious, propelled in water by two water jets, and is powered by a 5D20 diesel engine of 240 hp.

Service history

BMD-1s served extensively with Soviet airborne units in the Soviet–Afghan War (1979–1989), where their light armour proved vulnerable to mines and heavy machine-gun fire. After the dissolution of the Soviet Union they passed to successor states and have appeared in conflicts across the Caucasus and in Ukraine. The improved BMD-2, with a new turret and 30 mm cannon, entered production in the 1980s.
