Desert blues

Desert blues is a musical genre originating among the Tuareg people of the Sahara desert in Mali, Niger, and Algeria, blending traditional Tuareg and Songhai music with Western blues and rock. Characterised by hypnotic, pentatonic electric guitar riffs, call-and-response singing, and handclaps or calabash percussion, the style is often called tishoumaren or assouf ("longing"/"nostalgia" in Tamashek) by its originators, reflecting its roots in exile and rebellion.

History

The genre emerged in the 1980s among Tuareg refugees and exiles in Libya and Algeria, where young men (the ishumar) adopted the electric guitar in military and refugee camps, mixing the traditional tende rhythms with the music of Jimi Hendrix, Dire Straits, and American blues. Tinariwen, formed around 1979–1982, became the style's pioneers; their music, born of the Tuareg rebellions in Mali, circulated on cassettes long before international releases such as The Radio Tisdas Sessions (2001) and the Grammy-winning Tassili (2011) brought them worldwide acclaim.

Artists

Notable desert blues artists include Ali Farka Touré, whose Malian guitar style bridged West African music and American blues and who won Grammys for collaborations with Ry Cooder and Toumani Diabaté; Tinariwen; Bombino (Omara Moctar) of Niger; Mdou Moctar, known for virtuosic, psychedelic playing and the film Rain the Color of Blue with a Little Red in It; Tamikrest; Terakaft; Tartit; and Songhoy Blues. The Festival au Désert, held near Timbuktu from 2001, was a key showcase until conflict in northern Mali forced it into exile.
