Poeciliidae

The Poeciliidae are a family of freshwater and brackish-water fishes in the order Cyprinodontiformes, known as the livebearers. The family contains around 350 species in some 40 genera, native to the Americas from the southern United States through Central America to northern Argentina.

Reproduction

Poeciliids are famous for internal fertilisation and live birth. Males possess a gonopodium, a modified anal fin used to transfer sperm to the female, who stores it and can produce several broods from a single mating. Some species nourish embryos with placenta-like structures, while others provision the egg before fertilisation. The egg-laying African lampeyes and their relatives, once placed in this family, are now usually classified separately.

Familiar species

The family includes some of the world's most popular aquarium fish: guppies, mollies, platies, and swordtails (genera Poecilia and Xiphophorus), as well as the mosquitofish (Gambusia affinis), introduced worldwide for mosquito control and now a widespread invasive species. Because they are hardy, breed readily, and show varied colour and behaviour, poeciliids are also important model organisms in evolutionary biology and genetics.
