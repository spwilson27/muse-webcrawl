Glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) is a protein encoded by the GFAP gene in humans. It is a type III intermediate filament protein expressed predominantly by astrocytes in the central nervous system, and is the classic immunohistochemical marker used to identify astrocytes in tissue sections and cell culture.

Function

GFAP forms part of the astrocyte cytoskeleton and contributes to cell structure, mechanical strength, and astrocyte–neuron signalling. Its expression is strongly upregulated when astrocytes become reactive after brain or spinal cord injury (reactive astrogliosis), and immunostaining for GFAP is a standard tool in neuropathology. GFAP is also released into blood and cerebrospinal fluid after brain injury, and blood GFAP measurement has been developed as a biomarker for traumatic brain injury.

Clinical significance

Gain-of-function mutations in GFAP cause Alexander disease, a rare leukodystrophy characterised by the accumulation of GFAP in protein aggregates called Rosenthal fibres within astrocytes. Autoantibodies against GFAP define autoimmune GFAP astrocytopathy, an inflammatory meningoencephalomyelitis described in 2016.
