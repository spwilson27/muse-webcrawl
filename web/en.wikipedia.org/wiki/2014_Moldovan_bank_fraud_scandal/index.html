2014 Moldovan bank fraud scandal

Discovered: November 2014

Amount: US$1 billion (about 12% of Moldova's GDP)

Institutions: Banca de Economii, Unibank, Banca Socială

Key figures: Ilan Shor, Vlad Filat, Vlad Plahotniuc

The 2014 Moldovan bank fraud scandal was the disappearance of US$1 billion from three Moldovan banks — Banca de Economii, Unibank, and Banca Socială — in November 2014. The amount was equivalent to roughly 12 percent of Moldova's gross domestic product, making it one of the largest banking frauds relative to the size of a country's economy. The affair is often called the "theft of the century" in Moldova.

The fraud

In the days before the November 2014 parliamentary election, the three banks issued a large volume of loans to shell companies controlled by unknown beneficiaries, and the funds were transferred out of the country, largely through accounts in Latvia and offshore jurisdictions. The banks collapsed, and the National Bank of Moldova issued emergency loans, covered by state guarantees, to prevent a wider banking crisis — effectively transferring the loss to the Moldovan public.

Investigation

The government commissioned the corporate investigations firm Kroll to trace the money. Kroll's reports identified businessman Ilan Shor and companies associated with him as central beneficiaries of the scheme. Former prime minister Vlad Filat was arrested in October 2015 and later convicted of corruption in connection with the scandal, and Shor was convicted in absentia after fleeing the country. The affair triggered mass protests in 2015–2016, deepened public distrust of Moldova's pro-European governing parties, and became a defining issue in Moldovan politics. Recovery of the stolen funds has been slow and incomplete.
