Christian Ernest Dior (21 January 1905 – 24 October 1957) was a French fashion designer who founded the house of Dior and revolutionised post-war fashion with the "New Look" of 1947. His house is today the flagship fashion brand of LVMH's empire.

Early life

Born in Granville, Normandy, to a wealthy fertiliser-manufacturing family, Dior studied political science under family pressure, ran an art gallery in Paris, and after military service worked as a fashion illustrator and designer for Robert Piguet and Lucien Lelong.

The New Look

Backed by textile magnate Marcel Boussac, Dior opened his own house in 1946 at 30 Avenue Montaigne, Paris. His first collection, presented on 12 February 1947 — the Corolle and Huit lines — featured rounded shoulders, cinched waists and full, calf-length skirts using extravagant amounts of fabric after wartime rationing. Harper's Bazaar editor Carmel Snow exclaimed, "It's quite a revolution, dear Christian! Your dresses have such a new look!" The silhouette restored Paris as the capital of fashion; the house also launched the perfume Miss Dior in 1947.

Death and succession

Dior died suddenly of a heart attack in Italy in 1957. His assistant Yves Saint Laurent succeeded him at 21, followed over the decades by Marc Bohan, Gianfranco Ferré, John Galliano, Raf Simons and Maria Grazia Chiuri. The company, Christian Dior SE, is the controlling shareholder structure through which the Arnault family controls LVMH.
