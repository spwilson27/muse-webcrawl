Board of trade

Board of trade
Meaning(1) A business association promoting commerce; (2) a commodity exchange; (3) a government department (UK)
Famous exampleChicago Board of Trade (founded 1848)

A board of trade is a body or organisation with one of several related meanings. In North America it commonly denotes a voluntary association of businesses that promotes the commercial interests of a city or region — effectively synonymous with a chamber of commerce. The term also historically names commodity exchanges, most famously the Chicago Board of Trade.

As a business association

Boards of trade, especially in Canada and older American cities, lobby for business interests, promote trade and tourism, and provide networking and services to member firms. The Toronto Board of Trade, founded in 1845, is among the largest such organisations in Canada.

As an exchange

The Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT), founded in 1848, became the world's leading futures exchange for agricultural commodities such as corn and wheat, pioneering standardised futures contracts. It merged with the Chicago Mercantile Exchange in 2007 to form CME Group. Other exchanges, such as the New York Board of Trade (later ICE Futures U.S.), carried the same naming tradition.

In government

In the United Kingdom, the Board of Trade was a committee of the Privy Council originating in the 17th century that evolved into a government department responsible for commerce and industry; its president was a cabinet minister, and its functions now lie with the Department for Business and Trade, though the Board formally still exists.
