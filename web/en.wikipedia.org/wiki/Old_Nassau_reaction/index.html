Old Nassau reaction

The Old Nassau reaction is a chemical clock reaction in which a clear solution suddenly turns orange and then black. It is a variation of iodine clock reactions, in which the timing of the color changes can be adjusted by varying the concentrations of the reactants. The reaction was developed as a chemistry demonstration at Princeton University, whose colors are orange and black and whose main hall is Nassau Hall — hence "Old Nassau."

Description

The demonstration uses three colorless solutions: one containing potassium iodate, one containing sodium metabisulfite with starch, and one containing mercury(II) chloride. When mixed, the bisulfite slowly reduces the iodate to iodide; the iodide reacts with mercury(II) chloride to form orange mercury(II) iodide, producing the first color change. Once the bisulfite is exhausted, excess iodate oxidizes the remaining iodide to iodine, which forms a dark blue-black complex with starch, producing the second, dramatic change.

Chemistry

The sequence is governed by competing reactions between iodate, bisulfite, and iodide. Because the appearance of each color depends on which reagent is consumed first, the intervals between the initial mixing and the orange and black stages can be "tuned" like a clock, making the reaction a popular lecture demonstration of chemical kinetics.
