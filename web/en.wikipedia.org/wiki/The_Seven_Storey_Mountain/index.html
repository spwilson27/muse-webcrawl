The Seven Storey Mountain

Author: Thomas Merton
Published: 1948

The Seven Storey Mountain is the 1948 autobiography of Thomas Merton, an American Trappist monk and writer. It recounts Merton's restless youth, his studies at Cambridge and Columbia University, his conversion to Roman Catholicism in 1938, and his entry in 1941 into the Abbey of Gethsemani in Kentucky, where he spent the rest of his life.

Title and theme

The title alludes to the seven-corniced mountain of Purgatory in Dante's Divine Comedy: Merton frames his life as an ascent from worldly dissatisfaction toward contemplative union with God. The book is notable for the elegance of its prose and for its candid account of a modern, intellectual young man's embrace of strict monastic silence.

Impact

Published shortly after the Second World War, the book became an unexpected bestseller, selling hundreds of thousands of copies in its first years and being translated worldwide. It is credited with a surge of vocations to monasteries and seminaries in the 1950s and remains one of the most influential spiritual autobiographies of the twentieth century, often compared with Augustine's Confessions. An abridged British edition appeared as Elected Silence.
