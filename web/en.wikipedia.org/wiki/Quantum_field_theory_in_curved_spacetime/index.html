Quantum field theory in curved spacetime

Quantum field theory in curved spacetime (QFT in curved spacetime) is the theory of quantum fields propagating in a given classical, curved spacetime, treated as a fixed background. It generalises quantum field theory in flat Minkowski space by quantising matter fields on the geometry described by general relativity, while gravity itself remains unquantised. The framework is regarded as an approximation valid when gravitational fields are too weak, or spacetime curvature too gentle, to require a full theory of quantum gravity.

Overview

In flat spacetime, the Poincaré symmetry of Minkowski space selects a preferred vacuum state and hence a unique notion of "particle". In a general curved spacetime no such symmetry exists, so different observers may disagree about the particle content of the same quantum state — the vacuum of one observer appears populated with particles to another. This observer-dependence underlies the theory's most famous predictions.

Predictions

The framework predicted that black holes emit thermal radiation — Hawking radiation (1974) — with a temperature inversely proportional to the hole's mass, implying that black holes slowly evaporate. A related result, the Unruh effect (1976), says that a uniformly accelerating observer in flat spacetime perceives the inertial vacuum as a thermal bath. Parker showed that the expansion of the universe creates particles, an effect now embedded in inflationary cosmology, where quantum fluctuations in the early universe, stretched by cosmic inflation, are believed to have seeded all structure. The theory also provides the semiclassical Einstein equation, in which spacetime curvature is sourced by the expectation value of the quantum stress–energy tensor.

Approaches

Because the choice of vacuum is ambiguous, modern formulations are algebraic: a quantum field is an algebra of observables, and states (with the Hadamard condition controlling their short-distance behaviour) are chosen afterwards. This locally covariant approach, developed by Brunetti, Fredenhagen, Verch and others, makes the theory applicable to any globally hyperbolic spacetime.
