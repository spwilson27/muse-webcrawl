Sambhaji (14 May 1657 – 11 March 1689), also known as Chhatrapati Sambhaji Maharaj, was the eldest son of Shivaji, the founder of the Maratha Empire, and the second Chhatrapati (ruler) of the empire, reigning from 1681 until his capture and execution by the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb in 1689. A formidable warrior and scholar of Sanskrit and several other languages, he defended the young Maratha state simultaneously against the Mughals, the Siddis of Janjira, the Portuguese in Goa, and Mysore.

Early life

Sambhaji was born at Purandar Fort to Shivaji's first wife Saibai, who died when he was two; he was raised largely by his grandmother Jijabai. At nine he was sent to live as a political hostage with Raja Jai Singh I of Amber under the Treaty of Purandar, and he accompanied his father on the dramatic 1666 visit to Aurangzeb's court at Agra and the celebrated escape. He later rebelled briefly, joining the Mughal commander Diler Khan in 1678, before returning to his father's fold; Shivaji confined him at Panhala fort, from which he escaped after his father's death in April 1680 and defeated the succession claims of his half-brother Rajaram's faction to seize the throne.

Reign

Crowned at Raigad on 16 January 1681, Sambhaji raided Burhanpur, fought the Siddi admiral of Janjira, invaded Portuguese Goa in 1683 (withdrawing only when the Mughal army advanced), campaigned against Chikka Devaraja of Mysore, and gave refuge to Aurangzeb's rebel son Prince Akbar — a provocation that drew the emperor himself into the Deccan with an enormous army. Although outnumbered, Sambhaji's mobile forces resisted for eight years without losing the empire's core territory.

Capture and death

In February 1689 Sambhaji was surprised and captured at Sangameshwar by the Mughal commander Muqarrab Khan. Brought before Aurangzeb at Bahadurgad, he refused offers of clemency in exchange for conversion and surrender of his forts, and after weeks of torture he was executed at Tulapur on 11 March 1689; his body was dismembered and thrown into the Bhima River, the remains recovered and cremated by local villagers. In Maharashtra he is venerated as Dharmaveer, a martyr who died for his faith, and his death galvanised Maratha resistance under Rajaram and later Tarabai for nearly two more decades.
