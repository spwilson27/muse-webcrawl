Papyrus 52 (𝔓52), also known as the Rylands Library Papyrus P52 or the St John's fragment, is a fragment of a papyrus codex containing portions of the Gospel of John in Greek. Held by the John Rylands Library in Manchester, England, it is famous as the earliest known surviving fragment of a New Testament book, and it is routinely cited in discussions of the date of composition of the Fourth Gospel.

Contents

The fragment measures roughly 8.9 by 6 cm (3.5 by 2.4 in) at its widest. The recto (front) preserves parts of John 18:31–33 — Jesus before Pontius Pilate, including the exchange "Do you say this of your own accord?" — and the verso parts of John 18:37–38, including Pilate's question "What is truth?" The text includes the nomen sacrum abbreviation for Jesus (ΙΗ for Ἰησοῦν), characteristic of Christian scribal practice. The fragment comes from a codex rather than a scroll, consistent with early Christian preference for the codex format.

Discovery and dating

The papyrus was among a group acquired in Egypt in 1920 for the Rylands Library by Bernard Grenfell; it lay unnoticed until 1934, when Colin H. Roberts recognised it as a fragment of John, publishing it in 1935. On palaeographic comparison with dated hands, Roberts assigned it to the first half of the 2nd century, and a date around AD 125 became conventional. Later scholarship has cautioned that palaeographic dating carries wide margins: proposed dates range from the late 1st century to as late as the early 3rd century, though a mid-2nd-century date remains common in reference works.

Significance

If the early dating is correct, P52 demonstrates that the Gospel of John — traditionally considered the latest of the four canonical gospels — was composed and had circulated as far as provincial Egypt within a few decades of the end of the 1st century. This constrains theories that dated John's composition to the late 2nd century. Even on later datings, the fragment remains a landmark of biblical archaeology and one of the John Rylands Library's most celebrated holdings.
