Twentieth Century Fox Film Corporation, known as 20th Century Fox, was an American film production and distribution company and one of the "Big Five" major film studios of Hollywood's Golden Age. Formed on 31 May 1935 through the merger of Fox Film Corporation (founded by William Fox in 1915) and Twentieth Century Pictures (founded in 1933 by Joseph Schenck and Darryl F. Zanuck), the company's corporate home was the Fox Studio Lot in Century City, Los Angeles.

History

Under production chief Darryl F. Zanuck the studio became known for prestige dramas, musicals and Shirley Temple films in the 1930s; its stars included Tyrone Power, Henry Fonda, Marilyn Monroe and Elvis Presley. Fox pioneered CinemaScope widescreen with The Robe (1953) to compete with television. The studio nearly collapsed under the escalating costs of Cleopatra (1963) but recovered with The Sound of Music (1965), and later produced such landmark hits as Star Wars (1977), Alien, Die Hard, Home Alone, Avatar (2009) and the X-Men franchise. Rupert Murdoch's News Corporation acquired the studio in the 1980s; Fox's famous fanfare and searchlight logo became among the most recognisable in cinema.

Disney acquisition

On 20 March 2019, the Walt Disney Company completed its $71.3 billion acquisition of 21st Century Fox's entertainment assets, including the studio. In January 2020 Disney renamed it 20th Century Studios, retiring the "Fox" name from the film studio while the Fox Broadcasting Company and Fox News remained with the separate Fox Corporation.
