Law firm

A law firm is a business entity formed by one or more lawyers to engage in the practice of law. The primary service rendered by a law firm is to advise clients (individuals or corporations) about their legal rights and responsibilities, and to represent clients in civil or criminal cases, business transactions, and other matters in which legal advice and other assistance are sought.

Arrangements

Law firms are typically organised as sole proprietorships, general partnerships, limited liability partnerships (LLPs), or professional corporations, depending on the jurisdiction. In a partnership, each partner shares in the profits and management of the firm. Lawyers who are not partners are usually known as associates, and work towards eventually becoming partners under an "up or out" model in many large firms.

Structure and practice areas

Firms range in size from sole practitioners to global organisations employing tens of thousands of lawyers. Large firms often organise their lawyers into practice groups such as litigation, corporate law, real estate, tax, and intellectual property. The largest firms by revenue are sometimes called "Big Law" firms, and in London the leading group of firms is known as the "Magic Circle".

Finances

Law firms traditionally charge clients by the billable hour, although alternative fee arrangements such as fixed fees and contingency fees are also common. Common measures of firm profitability include profits per equity partner (PEP) and revenue per lawyer.
