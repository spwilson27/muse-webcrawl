Anapanasati

Ānāpānasati (Pali; Sanskrit ānāpānasmṛti), meaning "mindfulness of breathing" (sati = mindfulness; ānāpāna = in-and-out breathing), is a foundational meditation practice in Buddhism. It is described in the Ānāpānasati Sutta (MN 118) and is said to have been the practice the Buddha himself used on the night of his enlightenment, as well as one he recommended throughout his teaching career.

Mindfulness of breathing is a core practice across Buddhist traditions.

The sixteen steps

The sutta organises the practice into sixteen steps grouped in four tetrads corresponding to the four foundations of mindfulness: contemplation of the body (experiencing the whole breath, calming bodily formations), of feelings (experiencing rapture and pleasure, calming mental formations), of mind (experiencing, gladdening, concentrating, and liberating the mind), and of dhammas (contemplating impermanence, dispassion, cessation, and relinquishment).

Commentarial development

Buddhaghosa's Visuddhimagga (5th century) gives an elaborate treatment of ānāpānasati as a concentration (samatha) subject capable of leading to all four jhānas, detailing eight methods of attending to the breath including counting, connecting, touching, and fixing. In the Thai Forest Tradition and in modern lay movements, the practice is often taught combined with insight (vipassanā). The Burmese teacher Mahasi Sayadaw's noting method is a related but distinct approach, while Ledi Sayadaw promoted ānāpānasati for laypeople.

Relation to the seven factors of awakening

The Ānāpānasati Sutta states that mindfulness of breathing, when developed, fulfils the four foundations of mindfulness, which in turn fulfil the seven factors of enlightenment, leading to knowledge and liberation.
