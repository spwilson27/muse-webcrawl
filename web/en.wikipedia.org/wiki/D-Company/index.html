D-Company

The syndicate arose from the Mumbai underworld.

D-Company is a name given to an organised crime syndicate founded in Mumbai, India, by Dawood Ibrahim, an Indian crime boss and designated terrorist. The name derives from Ibrahim's first name. The organisation grew out of the Mumbai underworld of the 1970s and 1980s and became one of the largest criminal enterprises in South Asia, with interests in smuggling, drug trafficking, extortion, hawala money transfers and film financing.

History

Dawood Ibrahim built his network in Mumbai in the 1980s, first in smuggling gold and electronics and later in narcotics. He fled India in the mid-1980s, first to Dubai, where the syndicate expanded its operations. Indian authorities accuse D-Company of organising the 1993 Bombay bombings, a series of coordinated explosions that killed 257 people, carried out with the assistance of associates including Tiger Memon; Ibrahim denied involvement and has remained a fugitive. Indian agencies allege that he subsequently relocated to Karachi, Pakistan, a claim Pakistan denies.

Designations and sanctions

Ibrahim has been designated a global terrorist by the United States Treasury and is listed by the United Nations Security Council's al-Qaida and Taliban sanctions committee; Interpol has issued a red notice against him. Indian authorities link the syndicate's network to other fugitives and associates, and the group has frequently featured in Indian investigations of organised crime, terrorism financing and the underworld's connections with Bollywood in the 1990s.
