Monte Viso (Piedmontese: ël Viso; French: Mont Viso) is a mountain in the Cottian Alps in western Piedmont, Italy, close to the French border. At 3,841 metres it is the highest peak of the Cottian Alps, and its isolated pyramidal summit is a landmark visible across the plains of Piedmont — on clear days as far as Milan Cathedral.

Geography

The mountain stands at the head of the Po Valley: the river Po rises on its slopes at Pian del Re, near the village of Crissolo. The surrounding area forms the Parco del Monviso, and the mountain lies within the Monviso Biosphere Reserve designated by UNESCO as part of the transboundary programme with neighbouring France.

History

Monte Viso was known to classical authors; Virgil refers to it in the Aeneid, and it has been suggested as the mountain Petrarch described and as an inspiration for literary and artistic depictions of an archetypal peak. Nephrite (jade) axes from high on the mountain were traded across Neolithic Europe, as the Monte Viso quarries were a major source of this prized stone.

The first recorded ascent was made on 30 August 1861 by the English alpinists William Mathews and Frederick William Jacomb, guided by Michel Croz and his cousin Jean-Baptiste Croz. Beneath the Col de la Traversette on the Franco-Italian ridge nearby runs the Buco di Viso, a short pedestrian tunnel excavated in 1479–1480 under the Marquis of Saluzzo — the oldest tunnel in the Alps.

Climbing

The normal route, from Pian del Re via the Quintino Sella refuge, is a long but non-technical scramble and is among the most popular high ascents in the western Alps. The mountain is also circled by the multi-day Giro di Viso trek.
