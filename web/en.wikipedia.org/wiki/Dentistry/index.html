Dentistry

Dentistry, also known as dental medicine and oral medicine, is the branch of medicine focused on the teeth, gums, and mouth. It consists of the study, diagnosis, prevention, management, and treatment of diseases, disorders, and conditions of the mouth — most commonly dental caries (tooth decay), periodontal disease, and tooth loss, as well as pain and malocclusion.

Dentists work with a team that may include dental hygienists, dental nurses, technicians, and therapists. The profession is among the oldest medical occupations: evidence of dental treatment dates to thousands of years BCE.

History

An Indus Valley molar drilled around 7000 BC is among the earliest evidence of dentistry. Sumerian texts blamed toothache on a "tooth worm", a notion that persisted into the early modern period. Pierre Fauchard's Le Chirurgien Dentiste (1728) founded modern dentistry, and the first dental college opened in Baltimore in 1840. Anesthesia, X-rays, high-speed drills, fluoride, and adhesive materials successively transformed practice in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Treatments

General dentistry includes examinations, radiographs, professional cleaning, restorations (fillings), crowns and bridges, root canal therapy (endodontics), extractions, dentures, and implants. Prevention — oral hygiene instruction, fluoride, sealants, and diet counseling — is central, since caries and gum disease are largely preventable yet remain among the world's most prevalent conditions.

Specialties

Recognized specialties typically include orthodontics, oral and maxillofacial surgery, periodontics, prosthodontics, endodontics, pediatric dentistry, oral pathology, oral radiology, dental public health, and special care dentistry.

Education and regulation

Dental degrees (DDS, DMD, BDS and equivalents) generally require four or more years of study after undergraduate preparation. Dentists are licensed by national or state boards and are usually required to complete continuing education throughout their careers.

Public health

Community water fluoridation, school programs, and access-to-care initiatives are major public-health themes; oral diseases affect an estimated 3.5 billion people worldwide according to the WHO, with untreated caries the single most common health condition.
