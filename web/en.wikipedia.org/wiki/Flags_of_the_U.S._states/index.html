The flags of the U.S. states comprise the flags of the 50 states of the United States of America, exhibited in a fixed order of precedence determined by the date each state entered the Union. Most were adopted between the 1890s and the First World War, after state officials realised at the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago that they needed distinctive banners; the most common design pattern — often criticised by vexillologists — is the state seal or coat of arms centred on a solid, usually dark blue, field, a style derived from regimental colours carried in the Civil War.

Designs and criticism

A minority of state flags follow distinctive heraldic or banner-like designs: Maryland's flag reproduces the Calvert and Crossland arms; California's depicts the grizzly bear of the 1846 Bear Flag revolt; Texas flies the famous Lone Star; Hawaii's flag incorporates the Union Jack with eight stripes; New Mexico uses the Zia sun symbol; and Arizona, Colorado, and Tennessee use bold geometric designs. The North American Vexillological Association's 2001 survey rated New Mexico's flag best and Nebraska's (a seal-on-blue design) worst. Criticism of seal-on-a-field flags — that they are indistinguishable at a distance, complex, and often include lettering — has fuelled a wave of redesigns in the 21st century.

Recent changes

Georgia simplified its flag in 2003. Mississippi adopted a new flag in 2021 — a magnolia blossom design chosen by referendum — after retiring its 1894 flag, the last to include the Confederate battle emblem. Utah adopted a new “Beehive Flag” in 2024, and Minnesota adopted a new abstract design in 2024, replacing seal-centred flags in both cases. Other states, including Illinois and Maine, have held redesign commissions or referendums with mixed outcomes.

Historical flags

State flags evolved from militia and regimental colours of the Revolutionary and Civil Wars, and from unofficial banners carried at expositions. Several states flew sovereignty or independence-era flags, such as the Republic of Texas flags and the California Lone Star of 1836, which continue to influence present designs.
