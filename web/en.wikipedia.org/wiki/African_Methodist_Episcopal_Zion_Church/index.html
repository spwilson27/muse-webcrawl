African Methodist Episcopal Zion Church
Abbreviation: AME Zion
Founded: 1821 (organised), New York City
Tradition: Methodism
Nickname: "The Freedom Church"

The African Methodist Episcopal Zion Church (AME Zion) is a historically African-American Christian denomination based in the United States. It was formally organised in New York City in 1821 by Black members who left the Methodist Episcopal Church over racial discrimination.

History

The denomination grew out of the Zion Church congregation formed in 1800. Known as the "Freedom Church", it was closely tied to the abolitionist movement: Frederick Douglass, Harriet Tubman and Sojourner Truth were among its notable members and licensed preachers. Its first bishop under the AME Zion name was James Varick.

Beliefs and structure

AME Zion shares Wesleyan Methodist doctrine, with an episcopal polity led by bishops elected for life. It reports over a million members, with missions in Africa, the Caribbean and elsewhere.
