Senate of the Republic (Mexico)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Senate of the Republic (Spanish: Senado de la República) — 128 senators, Mexico City. Upper house of the Congress of the Union.

The Senate of the Republic (Spanish: Senado de la República) is the upper house of Mexico's bicameral Congress of the Union, the lower house being the Chamber of Deputies. It consists of 128 senators, elected for six-year terms coinciding with the presidential term; re-election for a second consecutive term became possible following the 2014 political-electoral reform.

Composition

Each of Mexico's 32 federal entities (31 states and Mexico City) elects three senators: two are awarded to the party or coalition winning the largest vote, and one — the primera minoría seat — to the runner-up. The remaining 32 senators are elected nationally by proportional representation from closed party lists. This mixed system is intended to combine territorial representation with pluralism.

Powers

The Senate has exclusive powers in foreign policy: it ratifies treaties, approves the appointment of ambassadors and senior military officers, and authorizes the deployment of Mexican troops abroad and the entry of foreign forces. It also confirms the president's nominees for the Supreme Court (from a shortlist of three), the Attorney General, and heads of certain autonomous bodies, and it may intervene in conflicts between a state government and the federation or declare the disappearance of a state's powers. Alongside the Chamber of Deputies it passes federal legislation and the budget, though revenue bills begin in the lower house.

History

A Mexican senate first sat under the Constitution of 1824, modelled on that of the United States; it was abolished by the liberal Constitution of 1857, which created a unicameral congress, and restored in 1874 under Sebastián Lerdo de Tejada. The present institution derives from the Constitution of 1917.
