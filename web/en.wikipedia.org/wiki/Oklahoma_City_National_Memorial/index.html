Oklahoma City National Memorial & Museum

LocationDowntown Oklahoma City, Oklahoma, USA
CommemoratesVictims, survivors and rescuers of the 19 April 1995 bombing
Dedicated19 April 2000 (outdoor memorial); museum opened 19 February 2001
DesignHans and Torrey Butzer, Sven Berg
Area3.3 acres (outdoor symbolic memorial)

The Oklahoma City National Memorial is a memorial in downtown Oklahoma City, Oklahoma, honouring the victims, survivors, rescue workers and all who were affected by the Oklahoma City bombing of 19 April 1995, in which a truck bomb destroyed the Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building and killed 168 people, including 19 children. It stands on the site of the destroyed building and is administered in partnership by the National Park Service and the Oklahoma City National Memorial & Museum foundation.

Design

The Outdoor Symbolic Memorial, dedicated by President Bill Clinton on the fifth anniversary of the attack, is organised around two monumental bronze Gates of Time at either end of a long reflecting pool: the east gate is inscribed 9:01, representing the last moment of innocence, and the west gate 9:03, the first moment of recovery — framing the 9:02 blast. On a grassed slope, the Field of Empty Chairs holds 168 glass-and-bronze chairs in nine rows (one for each floor of the building), each bearing a victim's name; 19 smaller chairs represent the children. The Survivor Tree, an American elm that withstood the blast, is tended from its own seeds distributed worldwide. A section of the original chain-link fence, covered daily with flowers, toys and messages, remains as a living memorial, and the adjacent museum, opened in 2001, presents the story of the bombing and its aftermath in the former Journal Record Building.
