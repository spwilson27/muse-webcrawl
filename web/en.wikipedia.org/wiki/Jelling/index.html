Jelling is a village in Vejle Municipality, Jutland, Denmark, with a population of a few thousand. It is famous above all as the royal seat of the first kings of Denmark in the Viking Age, and for the Jelling stones — two massive carved runestones standing beside the village church between two enormous burial mounds.

The Jelling monuments

The smaller, older stone was raised by King Gorm the Old in memory of his wife Thyra. The larger stone was raised by their son Harald Bluetooth, who recorded that he "won all of Denmark and Norway and made the Danes Christian"; it bears a figure of Christ and is often called Denmark's "birth certificate". The two great mounds, about 70 metres across, stand north and south of the stone church; excavations found a chamber grave in the northern mound and traces of a huge ship setting and palisade enclosing the complex.

History

Jelling was a royal monument complex of the Jelling dynasty in the tenth century. Archaeological work has revealed a gigantic wooden palisade, one of the largest structures of Viking Age Denmark, enclosing the mounds, stones and a longhouse. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1994 as an outstanding example of the transition from Norse paganism to Christianity in Scandinavia. The modern visitor centre, Kongernes Jelling, opened in 2015.

The village

Today Jelling is a small railway town on the Vejle–Holstebro line, serving tourists visiting the monuments and the annual Jelling Musikfestival. The village church, a Romanesque stone building of around 1100, contains some of Denmark's earliest frescoes.
