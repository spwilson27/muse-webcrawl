Inwood is a neighbourhood in the New York City borough of Manhattan, at the northern tip of Manhattan Island. It is bounded by the Hudson River to the west, the Harlem River and Spuyten Duyvil Creek to the north and east, and Dyckman Street and Fort George Hill to the south. Inwood is sometimes considered part of Upper Manhattan together with neighbouring Washington Heights.

Geography and landmarks

Inwood Hill Park, on the Hudson, contains the last natural forest and salt marsh in Manhattan and caves used by the Lenape people. Legend holds that Peter Minuit purchased Manhattan from the Lenape in 1626 near a tulip tree in the park; a plaque and the Shorakkopoch Stone mark the purported site. The Dyckman House, built around 1785, is Manhattan's oldest remaining farmhouse and a museum. Columbia University's Baker Athletics Complex lies at the island's northern tip, and the neighbourhood is crossed by the Henry Hudson Bridge and Broadway Bridge. The Cloisters museum, technically in Fort Tryon Park just to the south, is closely associated with the area.

Demographics

Inwood has long been a working- and middle-class neighbourhood with a large Dominican population, reflected in its Spanish-language businesses, alongside Irish-American roots from the early and mid-20th century. The area is served by the A and 1 subway lines at Dyckman Street and Inwood–207th Street. A controversial 2018 rezoning of Inwood, partly invalidated and later reinstated in court proceedings, prompted debate about development and displacement.
