English National Opera

English National Opera (ENO) is a British opera company based in London, resident at the London Coliseum in St Martin's Lane. ENO's productions are sung in English, often with surtitles. Along with the Royal Opera, Opera North, Scottish Opera and Welsh National Opera, it is one of the five major opera companies in Great Britain.

History

ENO's origins lie in the opera company Lilian Baylis established at the Old Vic and Sadler's Wells theatres in the 1930s. After the Second World War the Sadler's Wells Opera developed a strong ensemble identity and in 1968 moved to the London Coliseum, the largest theatre in the West End. In 1974 it adopted the name English National Opera. Under leaders including Lord Harewood and, from the 1980s, the "Powerhouse" team of Mark Elder, David Pountney and Peter Jonas, ENO gained an international reputation for bold, director-led productions of works from Monteverdi and Handel to Janáček, world premieres and rarely staged repertoire.

Productions and approach

True to its founding ethos of "opera for everyone", ENO performs all works in English translation, aims to keep ticket prices accessible — including free tickets for under-21s in recent seasons — and works with theatre directors as well as opera specialists. Landmark productions have included Jonathan Miller's staging of Rigoletto transposed to 1950s Mafia New York, and Nicholas Hytner's celebrated Xerxes. The company maintains its own orchestra and chorus and runs extensive learning and community programmes, including ENO Breathe for people recovering from long COVID.

Recent history

In 2022 Arts Council England's funding settlement required ENO to develop a new base outside London while retaining its Coliseum seasons; the company subsequently announced a partnership with Greater Manchester, presenting work there while continuing to perform at the Coliseum. Annilese Miskimmon has served as artistic director since 2020.
