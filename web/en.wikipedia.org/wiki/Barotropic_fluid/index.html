Barotropic fluid

In fluid dynamics, a barotropic fluid is a fluid whose density depends only on pressure, ρ = ρ(p). Equivalently, in a barotropic fluid the surfaces of constant pressure coincide with surfaces of constant density, so the pressure gradient and density gradient are parallel and their cross product vanishes.

Barotropy in meteorology and oceanography

The barotropic assumption is a common simplification in meteorology and oceanography. A barotropic atmosphere is one in which temperature does not vary along surfaces of constant pressure, so contours of geopotential height do not change with height and winds are approximately uniform through the troposphere. By contrast, a baroclinic fluid — in which density depends on both pressure and temperature — supports vertical wind shear and the growth of mid-latitude cyclones through baroclinic instability. The simple barotropic vorticity equation, developed in the late 1940s, was the basis of the first successful numerical weather prediction by Charney, Fjørtoft, and von Neumann in 1950.

Barotropic equations of state

In astrophysics, barotropic equations of state such as the polytrope, p = Kρ1+1/n, are used to model the structure of stars and planets. An incompressible fluid is a special case of a barotropic fluid with constant density.
