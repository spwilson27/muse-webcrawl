Gastón Acurio Jaramillo (born 30 October 1967) is a Peruvian chef and restaurateur widely credited with leading the international recognition of Peruvian cuisine. His flagship restaurant, Astrid y Gastón in Lima, has been ranked among the World's 50 Best Restaurants, and his company operates dozens of restaurants around the world across several brands.

Early life and training

Acurio was born in Lima, the son of Gastón Acurio Velarde, a former senator and minister. He initially studied law in Madrid at his father's wish, but abandoned it to pursue cooking, training at Le Cordon Bleu in Paris. There he met the German-born pastry chef Astrid Gutsche, whom he married.

Career

In 1994 the couple opened Astrid y Gastón in Lima's Miraflores district, initially serving French cuisine. Acurio progressively turned to Peru's native ingredients and traditions — ceviche, anticuchos, Andean potatoes, quinoa, ají peppers — helping spark a national gastronomic movement that redefined Peruvian identity and boosted culinary tourism. In 2014 the restaurant moved to Casa Moreyra, a historic hacienda in San Isidro.

Through his company Acurio International he developed concepts including La Mar (cevichería), Panchita (criollo cuisine), Tanta (casual dining), and Madam Tusan (Peruvian-Chinese chifa), with locations across Latin America, the United States, Europe, and the Middle East. He hosted television programmes in Peru and wrote numerous cookbooks, including Peru: The Cookbook (2015).

Influence and recognition

Acurio is regarded as an ambassador of Peruvian culture; the gastronomic boom he helped lead is credited with revitalizing Peru's restaurant sector, supporting small farmers and fisherfolk, and raising the international profile of dishes such as ceviche. In 2018 he received the Diners Club Lifetime Achievement Award in the World's 50 Best Restaurants programme. He has also been involved in social projects, including the Pachacútec culinary school for disadvantaged youth near Lima.
