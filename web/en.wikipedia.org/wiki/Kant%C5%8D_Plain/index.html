The Kantō Plain (関東平野, Kantō heiya) is the largest plain in Japan, covering roughly 17,000 square kilometres around Japan's capital region. Located in the Kantō region of central Honshu, it is home to Tokyo and the densely populated Greater Tokyo Area, the most populous metropolitan region in the world.

Geography

The plain is bordered by the Japanese Alps and the mountains of northern Kantō to the west and north, the Pacific Ocean to the east, and Tokyo Bay and Sagami Bay to the south. Major rivers draining the plain include the Tone River, the Arakawa, the Tama River, and the Sagami River. Much of the surface consists of diluvial uplands (the Musashino and Shimōsa plateaus) and alluvial lowlands along the rivers and Tokyo Bay.

Formation

The Kantō Plain sits over a deep sedimentary basin. Its surface layers were built up over tens of thousands of years by volcanic ash falls — including thick deposits of red-brown Kantō loam — and by river sediments. The plain overlies the complex junction of the Pacific Plate, the Philippine Sea Plate, and the Eurasian (Okhotsk) Plate, making it prone to major earthquakes, most famously the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake.

Urban development

Most of the plain has been heavily urbanised. Tokyo occupies its southern centre, with Yokohama on Sagami Bay and Chiba on Tokyo Bay. Extensive land reclamation in Tokyo Bay has added industrial and port land, while former farmland has steadily converted to suburban housing for commuters.
