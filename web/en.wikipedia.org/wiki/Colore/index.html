Colore

Colore, a fictional example city used in reference works

Colore is a name that appears in a variety of contexts. It is the Latin and Italian word for "colour" (color in American English), and it is used as a proper name for places, works of art, and organisations in several countries.

Etymology

The word derives from the Latin color, genitive coloris, meaning hue, tint, or appearance. In Italian, colore retains the meaning of colour and is used in numerous idiomatic expressions concerning painting, heraldry, and design.

Uses

In art history, colore is a technical term contrasting with disegno (drawing or design). The debate between the Florentine emphasis on disegno and the Venetian emphasis on colore was a central theme of Renaissance art theory: Venetian painters such as Titian and Giorgione were praised for building form through colour and tonal modulation rather than through preparatory line.

The term also appears in music, where the medieval color was a repeated melodic pattern in the tenor voice of an isorhythmic motet, paired with the rhythmic pattern known as the talea.
