Notre-Dame fire

On 15 April 2019, during the evening of Monday in Holy Week, a structural fire broke out in the roof space of Notre-Dame de Paris, the medieval Catholic cathedral on the Île de la Cité in Paris, France. The fire burned for about fifteen hours, destroying the cathedral's timber roof (the "forest" of oak beams), its 19th-century spire (flèche), and parts of the stone vaulting, before firefighters contained it. The main structure, the two bell towers, the great rose windows, and most of the cathedral's artworks and relics were saved.

Course of the fire

The alarm first sounded during an evening Mass at about 18:20 CEST; after an initial check found no fire (an investigation later suggested the wrong location was inspected), flames were discovered under the roof around 18:43. The fire spread rapidly through the medieval oak roof timbers. At about 19:50 the spire collapsed through the vault. Some 400–600 firefighters fought the blaze, prioritising the north tower and its bells; they also evacuated treasures including the Crown of Thorns and the Blessed Sacrament. The fire was declared extinguished the following morning; no one was killed, though a firefighter and two police officers were injured.

Cause and restoration

Investigators concluded the fire was accidental, most plausibly caused by an electrical short circuit or a cigarette discarded by workers involved in the ongoing renovation of the spire; no evidence of arson was found. President Emmanuel Macron pledged to rebuild within five years. Donations approaching €850 million were pledged within days. A law governed the restoration, carried out under chief architect Philippe Villeneuve; the spire was rebuilt to Eugène Viollet-le-Duc's 19th-century design, with the new golden rooster installed in December 2023. The cathedral reopened with ceremonies on 7–8 December 2024. See also Notre-Dame de Paris and Île de la Cité.
