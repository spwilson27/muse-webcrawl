The Chronic is the debut studio album by American rapper and producer Dr. Dre, released on December 15, 1992, by Death Row Records and Interscope Records. Its title is slang for high-quality cannabis; its cover art pays homage to Zig-Zag rolling papers. The album established the G-funk sound — slow, heavy beats layered with melodic synthesizers and deep bass, drawn heavily from the music of Parliament-Funkadelic — and made West Coast gangsta rap a dominant force in mainstream hip hop through the 1990s.

Background and recording

After leaving N.W.A and Ruthless Records amid a financial dispute, Dre co-founded Death Row Records with Suge Knight. Recorded in Los Angeles in 1992, during and after the Los Angeles riots, The Chronic introduced a young Snoop Dogg (then Snoop Doggy Dogg) as Dre's principal collaborator, alongside Warren G, Nate Dogg, Kurupt, Daz Dillinger, RBX, and The Lady of Rage.

Singles and reception

The singles "Nuthin' but a 'G' Thang", "Fuck wit Dre Day (And Everybody's Celebratin')" (shortened to "Dre Day" for radio), and "Let Me Ride" all became top-40 hits, and the album peaked at number three on the Billboard 200. It has been certified triple platinum in the United States. Critics have ranked it among the greatest hip hop albums of all time, and in 2020 it was selected for preservation in the National Recording Registry by the Library of Congress. The album's legacy — like that of other foundational recordings — parallels debates over how formally statured works such as the nationalist classics shape a genre's identity.
