Margaret Archer

Margaret Scotford Archer (20 January 1943 – 21 May 2023) was a British sociologist, a leading figure in the development of critical realism in social theory. She taught for many years at the University of Warwick and later at the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne.

Work

Archer is known for her "morphogenetic approach" to social theory, set out in Realist Social Theory (1995), which insists on the analytical separation of structure and agency over time as a way of avoiding conflating them. Her later work developed a theory of reflexivity and the "internal conversation" through which people deliberate about their circumstances, in books including Being Human (2000), Structure, Agency and the Internal Conversation (2003), and Making Our Way Through the World (2007). Her early work included Social Origins of Educational Systems (1979).

Archer served as president of the International Sociological Association (1986–1990), the first woman to hold that office, was a founding member of the Pontifical Academy of Social Sciences (1994), and co-founded the journal Journal for the Theory of Social Behaviour. She converted to Catholicism and wrote also on the sociology of religion.
