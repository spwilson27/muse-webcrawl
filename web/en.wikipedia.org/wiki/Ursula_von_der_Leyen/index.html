Ursula Gertrud von der Leyen (born 8 October 1958) is a German politician and physician who has served as President of the European Commission since 2019, the first woman to hold the office. A member of the Christian Democratic Union (CDU), she previously served in the German federal government under Chancellor Angela Merkel, most recently as Minister of Defence from 2013 to 2019.

Early life and education

Von der Leyen was born in Brussels, where her father, Ernst Albrecht, worked for the European Coal and Steel Community and later the European Commission; he went on to become Minister President of Lower Saxony. She studied economics at Göttingen and Münster before switching to medicine at Hannover Medical School, where she qualified as a physician and later earned a doctorate. She spent several years at Stanford University in California while her husband, Heiko von der Leyen, taught there. The couple have seven children.

German ministerial career

Entering politics in Lower Saxony, von der Leyen joined Merkel's first cabinet in 2005 as Minister for Family Affairs, Senior Citizens, Women and Youth, where she promoted expanded childcare and parental-leave benefits (Elterngeld). In 2009 she became Minister of Labour and Social Affairs, and in 2013 the first female Minister of Defence in German history. Her defence tenure combined international deployments and procurement reform with controversies over equipment readiness and the use of external consultants.

European Commission presidency

In July 2019 the European Council nominated von der Leyen as its candidate for Commission President; the European Parliament elected her by a narrow margin. Her first Commission (2019–2024) centred on the European Green Deal and climate-neutrality law, the NextGenerationEU pandemic recovery fund financed by common borrowing, the coordination of COVID-19 vaccine procurement, and the EU's response to Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, including sanctions and support for Ukraine's EU candidacy. Following the 2024 European elections she was re-elected for a second term, with priorities extending to competitiveness, defence and security.
