List of misconceptions about science and technology

This is a list of common misconceptions about science and technology — widely held beliefs that contradict scientific evidence. Each entry states the misconception and the established facts. (This article is part of a wider series on common misconceptions; see the main list for other topics.)

Biology and the human body

  • "Humans use only ten percent of their brains." Brain imaging shows virtually all parts of the brain are active over a day, and damage to almost any area has consequences. The myth may stem from misquotation of William James and early localisation studies.
  • "The tongue has separate taste zones." All taste qualities (sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami) can be sensed on all parts of the tongue; the famous "tongue map" was based on a mistranslated graph of slightly varying sensitivity.
  • "Hair and nails keep growing after death." They do not; the skin dehydrates and retracts, making them appear longer.
  • "Cracking knuckles causes arthritis." Studies have found no such link; the sound is from gas bubbles in synovial fluid.
  • "Humans have exactly five senses." In addition to sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch, humans have proprioception, equilibrioception, thermoception, nociception and interoception, among others.
  • "Evolution is a ladder of progress toward humans." Evolution is branching adaptation, not directed improvement; humans are not the "goal" of evolution.
  • "Individual organisms evolve." Populations evolve over generations; individuals only develop.
  • "Seasons are caused by Earth's varying distance from the Sun." Seasons are caused by the tilt of Earth's axis; Earth is actually near perihelion (closest to the Sun) in early January.
  • "Lightning never strikes the same place twice." Lightning frequently strikes tall structures repeatedly; the Empire State Building is struck many times a year.
  • "The Coriolis effect determines drain swirl direction." At sink scale the effect is negligible compared with basin shape and residual motion; drains swirl both ways in both hemispheres.

Physics and space

  • "There is no gravity in orbit / in space." Gravity in low Earth orbit is about 90% of surface strength; astronauts float because they are in continuous free fall.
  • "Meteors are heated by friction." Infalling meteors are heated mainly by ram-pressure compression of air ahead of them.
  • "The Great Wall of China is visible from space with the naked eye." It is not readily visible from low Earth orbit, and certainly not from the Moon — an oft-repeated claim debunked by astronauts themselves.
  • "A penny dropped from a skyscraper would kill a pedestrian." A coin quickly reaches a modest terminal velocity (~30 mph accounting for fluttering) and would sting but not be lethal.
  • "Goldfish have three-second memories." Goldfish can learn and remember for months.

Technology and inventions

  • "Thomas Edison invented the light bulb." Many inventors (including Joseph Swan, who demonstrated working lamps earlier) developed incandescent lighting; Edison's contribution was a practical, long-lasting lamp plus an entire electrical distribution system.
  • "The Wright brothers' first flight was the first powered flight ever, uncontested." The Wrights are credited with the first sustained, controlled powered flight (1903); earlier experimenters achieved brief or uncontrolled powered hops.
  • "Albert Einstein failed mathematics at school." He excelled at mathematics from an early age and mastered calculus in his teens; the myth arises partly from a reversed Swiss grading scale.
  • "Isaac Newton discovered gravity when an apple hit his head." Newton told of watching an apple fall (not striking him) as an inspiration for wondering whether the same force reached the Moon.
  • "Microwave ovens cook food from the inside out." Microwaves penetrate only a few centimetres; the interior cooks by conduction.
  • "5G / Wi-Fi radiation damages health at normal levels." Radiofrequency emissions from compliant devices are non-ionising and no consistent evidence shows harm at exposure-guideline levels.
  • "Airplane wings produce lift solely because of equal transit time over the curved top." The equal-transit explanation is incorrect; lift is explained by angle of attack, circulation and downward deflection of air (Newton's third law) together with pressure differences.
  • "Glass flows like a liquid over centuries." Old window panes vary in thickness because of crown-glass manufacturing, not because glass flows at room temperature.
