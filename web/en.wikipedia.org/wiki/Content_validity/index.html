Content validity

In psychometrics and research methodology, content validity is the extent to which a measure represents all facets of a given construct. For example, a depression scale that assesses only sleep disturbance but not mood, anhedonia or cognition would lack content validity for the construct "depression". Content validity concerns whether the items of a test adequately sample the relevant content domain, and it is usually evaluated through expert judgement rather than statistics alone.

Assessment

Typical procedures include:

  • Defining the content domain carefully from theory, prior research or job analysis.
  • Having panels of subject-matter experts rate each item's relevance and representativeness.
  • Quantifying agreement, for example with Lawshe's content validity ratio (CVR), calculated from the number of panellists rating an item "essential", or with a content validity index (CVI) averaging relevance ratings.

Relation to other validities

Content validity is one form of validity evidence, alongside criterion validity (agreement with an external outcome) and construct validity (whether scores behave as theory about the construct predicts). Face validity — whether a measure merely looks appropriate — is a weaker, informal cousin. Modern measurement standards (such as the Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing) treat validity as a unitary judgement supported by several sources of evidence, of which test content is one. Unlike criterion validity, content validity cannot be fully expressed by a single correlation coefficient; a test may correlate well with a criterion while still sampling the content domain poorly.

Threats

Content validity is threatened by construct under-representation (failing to include important parts of the domain) and by construct-irrelevant variance (items influenced by factors outside the construct, such as overly difficult vocabulary in a mathematics test).
