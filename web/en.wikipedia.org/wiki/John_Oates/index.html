John William Oates (born 7 April 1948) is an American musician, best known as half of the rock and soul duo Hall & Oates, together with Daryl Hall. He plays guitar and sings backing and occasional lead vocals.

Career

Oates was raised in North Wales, Pennsylvania, and met Daryl Hall at Temple University in Philadelphia in 1967. The duo signed to Atlantic Records in 1972 and moved to RCA in the mid-1970s. Hall & Oates became the best-selling music duo of all time, with hits including "Rich Girl", "Kiss on My List", "Private Eyes", "I Can't Go for That (No Can Do)", "Maneater" and "Out of Touch" — six Billboard Hot 100 number-one singles in total. Oates co-wrote many of the duo's songs and sang lead on tracks such as "Back Together Again" and "Las Vegas Turnaround".

The duo were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2014, and Oates was inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2004.

Solo work

Oates released his first solo album, Phunk Shui, in 2002, and later explored Americana and roots music on albums including Mississippi Mile (2011) and Arkansas (2018). His memoir, Change of Seasons, was published in 2017. He has also been active in historic preservation and conservation causes in Colorado and Tennessee.
