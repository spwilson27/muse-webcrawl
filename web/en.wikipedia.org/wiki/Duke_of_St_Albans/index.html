Duke of St Albans is a title in the Peerage of England, created in 1684 for Charles Beauclerk, the illegitimate son of Charles II and the actress Nell Gwyn. The dukes hold the hereditary office of Grand Falconer of England.

History

According to legend, when Nell Gwyn presented her young son to the king, calling him "Come here, you little bastard, and say hello to your father", Charles objected to the term, and Gwyn replied that the king had given the child no other name to be called by — whereupon the boy was made Earl of Burford. Later holders included Vere Beauclerk, a naval commander, and Osborne Beauclerk, an MP.

The current holder is Murray Beauclerk, 14th Duke of St Albans. The heir apparent is Charles Beauclerk, Earl of Burford.
