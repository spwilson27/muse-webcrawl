John II Casimir Vasa (Polish: Jan II Kazimierz Waza; 22 March 1609 – 16 December 1672) was King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1648 until his abdication in 1668, as well as a claimant to the Swedish throne. He was the last Polish king of the Swedish House of Vasa.

Reign

His reign was marked by a series of catastrophic conflicts, including the Khmelnytsky Uprising of the Cossacks, wars with Russia, and the Swedish invasion known as the Deluge (1655–1660), during which much of the Commonwealth was occupied and devastated. Despite these disasters, Polish forces recovered most of the country, and the Commonwealth survived, though permanently weakened.

Abdication and later life

John Casimir abdicated in 1668, the only Polish king to do so voluntarily. He retired to France, where he became abbot of Saint-Germain-des-Prés in Paris, and died in Nevers in 1672.
