Gun violence in the United States

Gun violence in the United States results in tens of thousands of deaths and injuries annually. In recent years the United States has recorded roughly 40,000 to 48,000 firearm deaths per year, of which a majority are suicides, followed by homicides; a smaller share are accidental deaths, legal interventions and deaths of undetermined intent. The firearm homicide rate in the United States is far higher than in other high-income countries.

Statistics

According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, firearm deaths peaked at about 48,800 in 2021 before declining somewhat in 2022 and 2023. Firearm suicides have risen steadily and account for more than half of all gun deaths and over half of all suicides in the country. Homicides are heavily concentrated geographically and demographically, disproportionately affecting young men in urban areas, while mass shootings, though highly publicised, account for a small fraction of total deaths.

Causes and debate

Researchers and policymakers debate the relative contributions of firearm availability, poverty, drug markets, policing and social conditions. The United States has the highest rate of civilian firearm ownership in the world, with more guns than residents, estimated at over 390 million firearms in civilian hands. Gun politics shapes the policy response, with federal measures limited compared to state-level variation.

Prevention efforts

Approaches include universal background checks, safe-storage laws, extreme risk protection orders ("red flag" laws), community violence intervention programmes, and suicide-prevention measures such as voluntary temporary transfers of firearms. Public health researchers increasingly frame gun violence as a preventable injury problem amenable to data-driven intervention.
