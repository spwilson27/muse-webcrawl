The Artsruni (Armenian: Արծրունի, Artsrunikʿ) were an ancient princely — and later royal — dynasty of Armenia. They ruled the principality of Vaspurakan, the territory southeast of Lake Van, first as princes under the suzerainty of the Bagratuni kings and the Arab caliphate, and from 908 to 1021 as kings in their own right.

Origins

According to the medieval Armenian historian Movses Khorenatsi, the Artsruni claimed descent from Sennacherib, king of Assyria, whose sons were said to have fled to Armenia after murdering their father. Such biblical genealogies were commonly claimed by Armenian noble houses. The family's power was based in the districts around Lake Van, including Rshtunik and Moxoene, and it frequently rivalled the Bagratuni and Mamikonian houses.

Kingdom of Vaspurakan

In 908 the Artsruni prince Gagik I Artsruni was crowned King of Vaspurakan by the Sajid emir of Azerbaijan as a counterweight to the Bagratuni king Smbat I. Gagik proved an able ruler and a great patron of architecture: he founded the palatine Church of the Holy Cross on the island of Akdamar (Aghtamar) in Lake Van, built between 915 and 921 by the architect Manuel and famed for its carved reliefs of biblical scenes.

The kingdom reached its greatest extent under Gagik and his successors, controlling some 40 towns and large territories around Van. Under pressure from Turkic raids and Byzantine expansion, the last king, Senekerim-Hovhannes, ceded Vaspurakan to the Emperor Basil II in 1021 in exchange for estates around Sebasteia (Sivas) in Cappadocia, where the dynasty continued under Byzantine rule.

Legacy

The Artsruni are remembered for the flourishing of Armenian art and architecture in Vaspurakan, above all the Church of the Holy Cross at Akdamar. The historian Thomas Artsruni, a member of the house, wrote a History of the House of the Artsrunikʿ covering events to the early tenth century, a major source for medieval Armenian history.
