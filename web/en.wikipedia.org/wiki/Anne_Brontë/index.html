Anne Brontë (17 January 1820 – 28 May 1849) was an English novelist and poet, the youngest member of the Brontë literary family. Writing under the male pen name Acton Bell, she published the novel Agnes Grey (1847), based on her experiences as a governess, and The Tenant of Wildfell Hall (1848), an unusually frank account of a woman leaving an alcoholic, abusive husband.

Life and reputation

Bringing up the younger Brontës at Haworth Parsonage, Anne spent most of her working life as a governess, including at Thorp Green Hall near York, where her brother Branwell also worked. The Tenant of Wildfell Hall was an immediate success but shocked Victorian critics; after Anne's early death from tuberculosis, Charlotte suppressed its republication, and Anne's reputation long lagged behind her sisters'. Modern critics regard her as a significant early feminist voice in fiction.
