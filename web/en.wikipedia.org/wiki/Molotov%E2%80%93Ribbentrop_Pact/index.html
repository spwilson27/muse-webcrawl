Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact

Signed: 23 August 1939, Moscow
Signatories: Nazi Germany, Soviet Union
Named for: Vyacheslav Molotov, Joachim von Ribbentrop

The Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, officially the Treaty of Non-Aggression between Germany and the Soviet Union, was a neutrality pact signed on 23 August 1939 by Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union. A secret protocol divided Eastern Europe into German and Soviet spheres of influence.

Consequences

Days after signing, Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939, beginning the Second World War; the Soviet Union invaded eastern Poland on 17 September. Under the protocol the USSR annexed the Baltic states and parts of Finland and Romania. The pact ended when Germany invaded the Soviet Union in Operation Barbarossa on 22 June 1941.

Legacy

The Soviet Union long denied the secret protocol's existence, acknowledging it only in 1989. The pact remains a central and controversial episode in the diplomacy leading to the war.
