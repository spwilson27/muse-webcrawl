United States Poet Laureate

The Poet Laureate Consultant in Poetry to the Library of Congress — commonly referred to as the United States Poet Laureate — is the official poet of the United States. During their term, the poet laureate seeks to raise the national consciousness to a greater appreciation of the reading and writing of poetry. The position is appointed by the Librarian of Congress and is administered by the Library's Poetry and Literature Center.

History

The position originated in 1937 as the Consultant in Poetry to the Library of Congress. Early consultants included Robert Frost, who served from 1958 to 1959. In 1985, Congress passed an act changing the title to Poet Laureate Consultant in Poetry, effective in 1986, partly modelled on the British Poet Laureate tradition, though the American post carries a modest stipend and light official duties rather than requirements to produce verse for state occasions.

Duties

The laureate traditionally gives an opening reading at the Library of Congress in the autumn and a closing lecture in the spring, and undertakes a personal project to promote poetry. Notable projects have included Robert Pinsky's Favorite Poem Project, Billy Collins's Poetry 180 for schools, Ted Kooser's newspaper column "American Life in Poetry," Natasha Trethewey's features with PBS NewsHour, and Juan Felipe Herrera's Casa de Colores. Recent laureates have included Joy Harjo (the first Native American laureate, serving three terms), Ada Limón, and Arthur Sze.
