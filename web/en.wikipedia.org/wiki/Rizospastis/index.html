Rizospastis

Rizospastis (Greek: Ριζοσπάστης, "The Radical") is a Greek daily newspaper and the official organ of the Communist Party of Greece (KKE). Founded in 1916, it is one of the oldest continuously associated political newspapers in Greece, though its publication has been interrupted repeatedly by censorship, dictatorship, and civil war.

Origins

The paper first appeared in Thessaloniki in 1916 as the organ of the Socialist Labour movement, initially associated with the Federation and the socialist politician Avraam Benaroya's circles. It became the organ of the Socialist Labour Party of Greece (SEKE), the forerunner of the Communist Party, and after the party's renaming it remained the KKE's official daily.

Interwar and wartime years

During the interwar period Rizospastis was repeatedly banned and its editors prosecuted under the anti-communist laws of the Greek state, notably the Idionymon law of 1929. Under the Metaxas dictatorship (1936–1941) the paper was driven underground. During the Axis occupation of Greece it appeared clandestinely as an organ of the resistance. After liberation and during the Greek Civil War, the paper was outlawed along with the KKE in 1947 and published from exile in Eastern Europe.

Post-1974 era

Following the fall of the military junta and the legalization of the KKE in 1974, Rizospastis resumed legal daily publication in Athens. It continues to present the party's positions on domestic and international affairs, covers labor and protest movements, and maintains a weekend edition. The newspaper's publishing house, Synchroni Epochi ("Contemporary Era"), also publishes the party's theoretical journal and related literature.
