Ligand-gated ion channel

Ligand-gated ion channel
Also known asIonotropic receptor
FunctionTransmembrane ion channel that opens in response to binding of a chemical messenger
ExamplesNicotinic acetylcholine receptor, GABAA receptor, NMDA receptor, AMPA receptor, 5-HT3 receptor

Ligand-gated ion channels (LICs, LGIC), also commonly referred to as ionotropic receptors, are a group of transmembrane ion-channel proteins which open to allow ions such as Na+, K+, Ca2+, and/or Cl− to pass through the membrane in response to the binding of a chemical messenger (i.e. a ligand), such as a neurotransmitter.

When a presynaptic neuron is excited, it releases a neurotransmitter from vesicles into the synaptic cleft. The neurotransmitter then binds to receptors located on the postsynaptic neuron. If these receptors are ligand-gated ion channels, a resulting conformational change opens the ion channels, which leads to a flow of ions across the cell membrane. This, in turn, results in either a depolarization, for an excitatory receptor response, or a hyperpolarization, for an inhibitory receptor response. Ligand-gated ion channels act within milliseconds, giving them a central role in fast synaptic transmission.

Classification

Ligand-gated ion channels are usually classified into three superfamilies according to their structure: the Cys-loop receptors, the ionotropic glutamate receptors, and the ATP-gated (P2X) channels.

Cys-loop receptors

The Cys-loop receptors are pentameric channels named for a characteristic loop formed by a disulfide bond between two cysteine residues. They include the excitatory nicotinic acetylcholine receptors and serotonin 5-HT3 receptors, and the inhibitory GABAA and glycine receptors. They are targets of many drugs, including benzodiazepines, barbiturates and general anaesthetics.

Ionotropic glutamate receptors

These tetrameric receptors bind the neurotransmitter glutamate and comprise the AMPA, kainate and NMDA receptor classes. NMDA receptors are unusual in requiring both glutamate binding and postsynaptic depolarisation to open, and are crucial to synaptic plasticity, learning and memory.

ATP-gated channels

P2X receptors are trimeric channels opened by extracellular ATP, involved in pain signalling, inflammation and smooth-muscle contraction.

Clinical significance

Mutations in ligand-gated ion channels cause channelopathies such as congenital myasthenic syndromes and some epilepsies. Autoantibodies against these channels underlie myasthenia gravis (anti-acetylcholine receptor) and some autoimmune encephalitides (anti-NMDA receptor). Many therapeutic and recreational drugs act on them.
