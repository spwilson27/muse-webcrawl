Yeosu is a coastal city in South Jeolla Province, South Korea, on the southern coast of the Korean Peninsula. It consists of the Yeosu Peninsula and more than 300 islands, and is known for its scenery, fisheries, and one of the country's largest petrochemical industrial complexes.

History

Yeosu has a long maritime history; Admiral Yi Sun-sin used the area as a naval base during the Imjin War (1592–1598), and the reconstructed turtle ship is a local symbol. The modern city was formed by the 1998 merger of Yeosu city, Yeocheon city, and Yeocheon county ("Samryeo integration").

Expo 2012

Yeosu hosted the World Expo in 2012 under the theme "The Living Ocean and Coast", drawing roughly eight million visitors and spurring major infrastructure including the Yi Sun-sin Bridge and expanded rail service.

Economy and tourism

The Yeosu National Industrial Complex hosts major petrochemical plants. Tourism focuses on the harbour, Odongdo island, Hyangiram hermitage, night sea cruises, and the song "Yeosu Night Sea" which popularised the city domestically.
