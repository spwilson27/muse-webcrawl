Fork

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A fork is a utensil, now usually made of metal, whose long handle terminates in a head that branches into several narrow and often slightly curved tines with which one can spear foods either to hold them in place while cutting with a knife or to lift them to the mouth. Used primarily in the West, forks are also common in other parts of the world influenced by Western dining customs.

History

Fork-like implements were used in ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome, mostly as cooking and serving tools. The personal table fork gained popularity in the Byzantine Empire and reached Italy by the eleventh century, where it was at first regarded by some churchmen as an affectation. From Italy the fork spread to France — Catherine de' Medici is often credited with bringing it to the French court in the sixteenth century — and later to England and the Americas. Early table forks had two tines; the four-tined form became standard in the nineteenth century. Thomas Coryat, an English traveller who championed the fork after visiting Italy, was mocked as "Furcifer" (fork-bearer) for his enthusiasm.

Types

Western place settings include specialised forks: the dinner fork, salad fork, fish fork, dessert fork, oyster fork and serving fork, among others. In formal etiquette, forks are generally placed to the left of the plate and used in order from the outside in.

Use and etiquette

Two principal styles of use predominate. In the European (continental) style, the fork remains in the left hand with tines facing down. In the American style, the fork is switched to the right hand after cutting, with tines facing up. Etiquette norms differ considerably around the world: in much of Asia, chopsticks, spoons or the hands are preferred, and the fork is used mainly for serving or for pushing food onto a spoon, as in Thai dining.
