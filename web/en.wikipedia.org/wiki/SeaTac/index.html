SeaTac (pronounced "SEE-tak") is a city in southern King County, Washington, United States. The city is an inner-ring suburb of Seattle and is entirely surrounded by the cities of Burien, Des Moines, Kent, Tukwila, and Renton, together with unincorporated King County. The population was 31,454 at the 2020 census.

Sea–Tac Airport, from which the city takes its name.

History and name

Residents voted to incorporate on March 14, 1989, and the city was incorporated on February 28, 1990. The name is a portmanteau of Seattle and Tacoma, taken from Seattle–Tacoma International Airport (Sea–Tac Airport), which lies within the city and is operated by the Port of Seattle. The airport dominates the local economy, along with associated hotels, parking, rental-car and airline services.

Economy and politics

Alaska Airlines and Horizon Air have their headquarters in SeaTac. In 2013 the city attracted national attention when voters narrowly approved Proposition 1, a local minimum wage of $15 per hour for certain airport and hospitality workers — among the first such measures in the United States — which survived legal challenge for airport workers in modified form. The city is served by Link light rail stations at the airport and Angle Lake.
