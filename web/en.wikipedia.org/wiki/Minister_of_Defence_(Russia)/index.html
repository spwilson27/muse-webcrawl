Minister of Defence (Russia)

The Ministry of Defence building in Moscow

The Minister of Defence of the Russian Federation (Russian: Министр обороны Российской Федерации) is the head of the Russian Ministry of Defence, the government body responsible for the Russian Armed Forces. The minister is a civilian or military official appointed by the President of Russia on the recommendation of the Prime Minister, and is responsible for the political, administrative and operational direction of the armed forces.

History

The position was created in 1992 following the dissolution of the Soviet Union, succeeding the Soviet Minister of Defence. Boris Yeltsin briefly served as acting defence minister before appointing Pavel Grachev in May 1992. Subsequent ministers have included Igor Rodionov, Igor Sergeyev, Sergei Ivanov, Anatoly Serdyukov and Sergei Shoigu, the longest-serving post-Soviet holder, who held the office from 2012 to 2024. In May 2024, economist Andrei Belousov was appointed to the post by President Vladimir Putin.

Duties

The defence minister exercises administrative and operational leadership over the Armed Forces through the General Staff, oversees the defence budget and procurement, and represents the ministry in the government and internationally. The minister is a member of the Security Council of Russia.

List of ministers

Notable holders include Pavel Grachev (1992–1996), Igor Sergeyev (1997–2001, a Marshal of the Russian Federation), Sergei Ivanov (2001–2007), Anatoly Serdyukov (2007–2012), Sergei Shoigu (2012–2024) and Andrei Belousov (2024–present).
