Lake Conemaugh


The clubhouse at Lake Conemaugh

Lake Conemaugh was a man-made reservoir in southwestern Pennsylvania, United States, created by the South Fork Dam across the South Fork of the Little Conemaugh River about 14 miles (23 km) upstream from Johnstown. The lake became infamous when the dam failed on 31 May 1889, unleashing the catastrophic Johnstown Flood.

History

The dam was built between 1838 and 1853 by the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania as part of its canal system, forming what was then called the Western Reservoir. After the canals were superseded by railroads, the reservoir was sold and resold, eventually passing in 1879 to the South Fork Fishing and Hunting Club, an exclusive Pittsburgh club whose members included Andrew Carnegie, Henry Clay Frick and Andrew Mellon. The club lowered the dam to carry a carriage road, removed fish screens and neglected maintenance of the spillway. The lake, about two miles long and a mile wide, held some 20 million tons of water.

The disaster of 1889

After days of exceptionally heavy rain, the earthen dam overtopped and collapsed on the afternoon of 31 May 1889. The drained lake sent a wall of water down the Conemaugh valley that destroyed Johnstown and neighbouring towns, killing 2,209 people in one of the worst disasters in American history. The lake bed has been dry ever since; the site is preserved by the Johnstown Flood National Memorial.
