Logic family

In computer engineering, a logic family is one of a number of monolithic electronic circuit approaches used to implement digital logic in integrated circuits. Members of a logic family share a common circuit design, electrical characteristics and voltage levels, so that chips within a family can be freely interconnected; mixing families usually requires level-shifting or other interfacing.

Characteristics

Families are compared by supply voltage, logic levels, propagation delay, power dissipation, fan-out, and noise margin. TTL operates at 5 V with thresholds around 0.8 V and 2.0 V; CMOS works over a wide voltage range and has thresholds relative to the supply. Modern design is overwhelmingly CMOS, with bipolar families surviving mainly in legacy and special-purpose parts.
