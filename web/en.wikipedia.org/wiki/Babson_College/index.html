Babson College

Babson College is a private business school in Wellesley, Massachusetts, United States. Founded in 1919 by economist and businessman Roger W. Babson as the Babson Institute, it specialises in business and entrepreneurship education and is consistently ranked among the leading schools for entrepreneurship in the United States. Its degree programmes are offered through the undergraduate school and the F. W. Olin Graduate School of Business.

History and campus

Roger Babson, an economist famous for forecasting the 1929 stock market crash, founded the institute to train men (and, later, all students) in practical business administration rather than abstract theory. The campus is known for the Babson Globe, a 25-ton rotating globe built in 1955, and for the Babson World Globe and map collection. The college's business programmes emphasise its "Entrepreneurial Thought & Action" methodology, and its global entrepreneurship research (the Global Entrepreneurship Monitor, co-founded with the London Business School) is widely cited.

Academics

Undergraduates earn a Bachelor of Science in Business Administration with concentrations across finance, marketing, economics and other fields; the Olin School offers MBA and specialised master's degrees. Notable alumni include Terrell Owens's business peers and founders such as Arthur Blank (co-founder of The Home Depot), Akio Toyoda (former president of Toyota) and Deborah DeCotis.
