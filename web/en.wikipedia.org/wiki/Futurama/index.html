Futurama is an American animated science-fiction sitcom created by Matt Groening — creator of The Simpsons — and developed with David X. Cohen. It follows Philip J. Fry, a pizza delivery boy cryogenically frozen on New Year's Eve 1999 and thawed a thousand years later in New New York, where he joins the Planet Express delivery company with his friends: the one-eyed pilot Leela, the hard-drinking robot Bender, Professor Farnsworth, Dr Zoidberg, Amy Wong and Hermes Conrad.

The Planet Express ship above New New York in the year 3000.

History

The series premiered on Fox on 28 March 1999. Despite critical acclaim, it suffered erratic scheduling and was cancelled in 2003 after four production seasons. Strong DVD sales and reruns led to four direct-to-video films (2007–2009), which Comedy Central then aired as a season before ordering new episodes (2010–2013). Episodes such as "Jurassic Bark", "The Luck of the Fryrish" and "The Late Philip J. Fry" are frequently ranked among the finest of animated television, and the show won multiple Emmy Awards. In 2022 Hulu revived the series again, with new seasons beginning in July 2023.

Style and themes

Beneath its gag-driven comedy, Futurama is notable for genuine science-fiction storytelling and surprising emotional depth, for background jokes written by a famously over-qualified writing staff (several held PhDs in mathematics and computer science — the episode "The Prisoner of Benda" contains an original group-theory theorem), and for a dense web of recurring aliens, robots and future-history jokes. Its sign-off gag credits, alien-language alphabets and catchphrases such as Bender's "Bite my shiny metal ass!" have given it a durable cult following.
