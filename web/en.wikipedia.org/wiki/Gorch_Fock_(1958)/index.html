Gorch Fock is a three-masted barque of the German Navy, used as a sail training ship for officer cadets. Launched in 1958 by Blohm & Voss in Hamburg, she is the second German training ship to bear the name, which honours the German writer Johann Wilhelm Kinau, who wrote under the pseudonym "Gorch Fock" and died at the Battle of Jutland in 1916.

History

The first Gorch Fock (1933) passed to the Soviet Union as war reparations and sailed as Tovarishch. The West German navy ordered a replacement to the same basic design, incorporating lessons from the loss of the training ship Pamir in 1957; she was launched on 23 August 1958 and commissioned that December. She is a near sister ship to vessels built for other navies, including Romania's Mircea and Portugal's Sagres.

Service

Based at Kiel and attached to the Naval Academy at Mürwik, the ship has made more than 160 training voyages, sailing some 750,000 nautical miles and visiting ports worldwide as a goodwill ambassador. In 2011 a cadet's fatal fall from the rigging led to reforms in training practices. From 2015 the ship underwent an extensive and costly overhaul, the price of which rose dramatically and drew political scrutiny, before she returned to service in 2021.
