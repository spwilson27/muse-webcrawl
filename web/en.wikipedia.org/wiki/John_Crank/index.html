John Crank (6 February 1916 – 3 October 2006) was a British mathematical physicist best known for his work on the numerical solution of partial differential equations, particularly heat conduction and diffusion problems.

Education and career

Crank was born in Hindley, Lancashire, and studied at the University of Manchester, where he earned his doctorate under William Lawrence Bragg. During the Second World War he worked on applied problems including the heating of rubber and the behaviour of materials. He spent much of his career at the Courtaulds Fundamental Research Laboratory, and in 1957 became the first professor of mathematics at Brunel College of Technology (later Brunel University), where he served as vice-principal.

Crank–Nicolson method

With Phyllis Nicolson, Crank developed the Crank–Nicolson method (1947), a finite difference scheme for solving parabolic partial differential equations that is implicit, second-order accurate, and unconditionally stable. It remains a standard technique in computational physics and engineering.

The Mathematics of Diffusion

Crank's monograph The Mathematics of Diffusion (1956; 2nd ed. 1975) became the standard reference on analytical and numerical methods for diffusion problems, treating free and moving boundaries — terrain later generalised in his Free and Moving Boundary Problems (1984).
