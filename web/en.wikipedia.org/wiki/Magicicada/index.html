Magicicada

Magicicada is a genus of cicadas native to eastern North America, known as the periodical cicadas because entire populations emerge in synchronized mass broods after spending either 13 or 17 years underground as nymphs. The genus comprises seven species, recognizable by their black bodies, red eyes, and orange wing veins.

Life cycle

Nymphs live underground feeding on root fluids for 13 or 17 years — among the longest life spans of any insect — before emerging in spring by the millions when soil temperatures reach about 18 °C. Adults live only a few weeks, during which males sing in deafening choruses to attract mates, females lay eggs in tree twigs, and the newly hatched nymphs drop to the ground to begin the cycle again. The prime-numbered cycle lengths are thought to reduce encounters with predators and with broods of the other cycle length.

Broods

Periodical cicada populations are grouped into broods, designated by Roman numerals, each with its own emergence year and geographic range. Famous emergences include Brood X (the "Great Eastern Brood"), and the rare co-emergence of the 13-year Brood XIX and 17-year Brood XIII, which occurred in 2024 for the first time since 1803. The overwhelming numbers of emerging cicadas satiate predators, ensuring many survive to reproduce.
