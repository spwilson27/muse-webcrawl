Tiki-taka

Tiki-taka (Spanish: tiquitaca) is a style of play in association football characterised by short passing and movement, working the ball through various channels, and maintaining possession. The style is primarily associated with the Spain national team of 2006 to 2013 under Luis Aragonés and Vicente del Bosque, and with FC Barcelona under Pep Guardiola from 2008 to 2012, sides widely regarded among the greatest in the history of the sport.

Characteristics

Tiki-taka is built on short, ground-level passes exchanged at speed between closely spaced players, who constantly move to offer new passing angles and form triangles across the pitch. Possession is both a means of attack and of defence: a team cannot concede while it holds the ball. When possession is lost, an intense collective press (gegenpressing in its Catalan variant) seeks to recover the ball within a few seconds. The system demands high technical ability, patience, and positional discipline, and typically dispenses with a traditional target-man centre-forward; Guardiola's Barcelona famously used Lionel Messi as a "false nine".

Origins and usage of the term

The term is attributed to the late Spanish broadcaster Andrés Montes, who popularised it during television coverage of the 2006 FIFA World Cup. Its roots as a method lie in the positional play (juego de posición) developed at Barcelona by Johan Cruyff in the late 1980s and early 1990s, itself influenced by Dutch Total Football. Spain won UEFA Euro 2008, the 2010 FIFA World Cup and UEFA Euro 2012 playing this style, while Barcelona won fourteen trophies in four seasons under Guardiola, including two UEFA Champions League titles.

Criticism

Critics argue that tiki-taka can become sterile possession without penetration, and that it is vulnerable to disciplined low defensive blocks and fast counter-attacks. Guardiola himself disliked the label, insisting the essence of his system was positioning and pressing rather than passing for its own sake. Nonetheless the term has entered football vocabulary worldwide as shorthand for possession-based, short-passing football.
