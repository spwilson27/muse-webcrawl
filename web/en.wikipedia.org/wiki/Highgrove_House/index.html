Highgrove House

Highgrove House is the family residence of King Charles III and Queen Camilla, near Tetbury in Gloucestershire, England. The late-18th-century Georgian house, built between 1796 and 1798, stands in extensive gardens celebrated for their organic design.

History

The estate was purchased by the Duchy of Cornwall in 1980 for the then Prince of Wales. Charles substantially remodelled the house and, over four decades, created the gardens with designers including Rosemary Verey and Miriam Rothschild, applying organic principles—no chemical pesticides or fertilisers—and features such as a wildflower meadow, a stumpery, and a kitchen garden.

Today

Highgrove's gardens open to the public for tours in the warmer months, with proceeds supporting The King's Foundation. The house remains a private residence; Charles retained it after his accession in 2022 through a lease from the Duchy.
