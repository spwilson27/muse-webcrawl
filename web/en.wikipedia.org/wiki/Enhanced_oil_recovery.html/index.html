Enhanced oil recovery

Enhanced oil recovery (EOR), also called tertiary recovery, is the extraction of crude oil from an oil field that cannot be extracted by primary or secondary recovery methods such as water or gas injection alone. EOR techniques can substantially increase the proportion of original oil in place that is ultimately recovered.

Methods

The principal methods are thermal recovery (such as steam injection and cyclic steam stimulation), gas injection (including carbon dioxide flooding), and chemical injection (polymer and surfactant flooding). Carbon dioxide EOR can also serve as a means of carbon sequestration when the injected CO2 remains stored underground. EOR is more costly than conventional recovery and is typically applied when oil prices or project economics justify it.
