Mushing

Mushing is a sport and method of transport powered by dogs, in which one or more dogs pull a sled, cart or other vehicle. The driver is called a musher. The word is thought to derive from the French marcher, "to walk/go", via the command "marche" used in French Canada.

Dog sledding has ancient roots among Arctic Indigenous peoples and was essential transport in Alaska, Canada, Siberia and Greenland. Famous races include the Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race and the Yukon Quest, each about 1,000 miles.

Equipment and dogs

Teams are hitched in pairs with lead dogs at the front responding to commands such as "gee" (right) and "haw" (left). Alaskan huskies are the most common racing breed, valued for speed and endurance. Dryland mushing with wheeled carts is practised where snow is scarce.
