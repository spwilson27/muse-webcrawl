Malabo is the capital and the second-largest city of Equatorial Guinea, after Bata. Located on the north coast of the island of Bioko (formerly Fernando Po), it has a population of roughly 300,000 inhabitants in its metropolitan area. It is also the capital of the province of Bioko Norte. The planned city of Ciudad de la Paz is being developed on the mainland to succeed Malabo as the seat of government.

History

The city was founded by the British in 1827, who leased Bioko from Spain, and named Port Clarence. It was used as a naval station in efforts to suppress the Atlantic slave trade. Many freed slaves were settled there before Sierra Leone became a Crown colony. The Spanish re-established control later in the 19th century and renamed the city Santa Isabel, a name it retained until 1973, when President Francisco Macías Nguema renamed it Malabo after his home village, as part of his campaign of Africanization of place names.

Geography and economy

Malabo sits on the rim of a sunken volcano on Bioko's northern coast, facing Cameroon across the Bight of Biafra. Its climate is tropical monsoon, with a long rainy season. The discovery of offshore oil reserves in the 1990s transformed the city's economy, attracting foreign energy companies and rapid construction; Malabo International Airport and the deep-water port serve the petroleum industry. Notable landmarks include Malabo Cathedral, built in Spanish colonial Gothic style, and the presidential palace.
