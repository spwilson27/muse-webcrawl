Economy of the United Kingdom

The economy of the United Kingdom is a highly developed social market economy. It is among the world's largest economies by nominal GDP (around $3 trillion in the mid-2020s), with London as a leading global financial centre.

Economy of UK
Currency: Pound sterling (GBP)
Central bank: Bank of England
Major sectors: Services (~80% GDP), finance, manufacturing
Trade partners: EU, US, China

Overview

Services dominate, especially financial and business services centred on the City of London and Canary Wharf. Manufacturing (aerospace, pharmaceuticals, automotive) remains significant, along with North Sea oil and gas, now declining, and growing renewables.

History

Britain pioneered the Industrial Revolution, was the world's leading economy in the 19th century, experienced relative decline, post-war mixed economy, privatisation under Margaret Thatcher in the 1980s, the 2008 financial crisis, austerity, Brexit (EU exit in 2020), and the COVID-19 shock.

Macroeconomics

The Bank of England, independent since 1997, targets 2% inflation. Challenges include low productivity growth since 2008, regional inequality, housing costs, and trade changes after Brexit.
