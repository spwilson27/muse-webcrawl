1953 Iranian coup d'état

The 1953 Iranian coup d'état was the overthrow of the democratically elected government of Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh on 19 August 1953. It was orchestrated by the United States (Operation Ajax) and the United Kingdom (Operation Boot) and consolidated the rule of Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi.

Background

Mosaddegh's government had nationalised the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company in 1951, provoking a British embargo and blockade. Fearing the loss of oil concessions and playing on Cold War anxieties, London and Washington authorised a covert operation, run on the ground by the CIA's Kermit Roosevelt Jr. and relying on Iranian military officers led by General Fazlollah Zahedi.

Events

A first attempt in mid-August failed and the Shah briefly fled, but on 19 August pro-Shah crowds and army units seized control of Tehran; Mosaddegh was arrested, tried, and held under house arrest until his death in 1967.

Consequences

The coup restored Western oil access through a consortium agreement and entrenched the Shah's increasingly authoritarian rule for 26 years. It remains a defining grievance in Iranian–Western relations and a touchstone of the 1979 revolution.
