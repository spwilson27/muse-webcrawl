Vishvakarma


Vishvakarma, the divine architect

Vishvakarma or Vishvakarman is a craftsman deity and the divine architect of the devas (gods) in Hinduism. In the early texts, the term is used for any supreme god, and in later Hinduism he is personified as the creator of many fabulous weapons of the gods and the builder of their cities and chariots.

Mythology

Vishvakarma is credited with constructing the golden city of Lanka, the city of Dwarka, the palaces of Indraprastha, and the weapons of the gods, including Indra's thunderbolt, the Vajra. In the Ramayana, he is the father of Nala, the vanara who designed the Rama Setu bridge.

Worship

Vishvakarma is worshipped especially by artisans, craftsmen, engineers and architects. Vishvakarma Puja is celebrated annually, typically on 17 September, in factories and workshops in India and Nepal, where tools and machinery are honoured.

Iconography

He is usually depicted as an elderly, bearded figure with four arms holding tools such as a water pot, a book, a noose and craftsman's instruments, sometimes accompanied by his vahana, the elephant or the swan.
