Drill team


A high school drill team performing at a football halftime.

A drill team is a group that performs choreographed, precision routines. The term is used for two main traditions: school-based dance teams, especially in the United States, which perform at sports events and competitions, and military exhibition drill units, which perform intricate marching routines, often with rifles or sabres.

Dance drill teams

American dance drill teams originated in Texas in the 1930s, when physical education teacher Gussie Nell Davis founded the Rangerettes at Kilgore College in 1940, popularising the cowboy-hatted, high-kicking line formation. High school drill teams typically perform jazz, kick, pom, lyrical and military-style routines at football halftime shows, pep rallies and regional and national competitions. The Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders and similar professional squads draw heavily on the drill team tradition.

Military drill teams

Military drill teams, such as the United States Marine Corps Silent Drill Platoon and the Air Force and Army drill teams, perform exhibition drill: complex sequences of marching and weapon handling executed in perfect unison without verbal commands. Regulation drill teams are judged on the standard drill manual, while exhibition teams invent their own routines, including rifle tosses and exchanges.

Other forms

Related activities include colour guards, marching bands' auxiliary units, equestrian drill teams performing synchronised riding patterns, and mounted and motorcycle drill teams.
