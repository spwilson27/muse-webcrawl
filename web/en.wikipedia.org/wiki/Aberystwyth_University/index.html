Aberystwyth University (Welsh: Prifysgol Aberystwyth) is a public research university in Aberystwyth, Wales. Founded in 1872 as University College Wales, it was the first university institution in Wales and a founding member of the federal University of Wales.

History

The college opened in the former Castle Hotel on the seafront — now the Old College — with 26 students and three members of staff. It grew through Welsh public subscription, famously including contributions from miners and quarrymen. In 1919 it became home to one of the first university departments of international politics, endowed by David Davies in memory of those who died in the First World War.

Academics

The university is organised into faculties covering arts and social sciences, business and physical sciences, and earth and life sciences. It hosts the Institute of Biological, Environmental and Rural Sciences (IBERS), known for plant breeding and land-use research, and the National Library of Wales stands adjacent to the Penglais campus.

Student life

Aberystwyth is a small coastal town dominated by the university; students make up a large share of the population. The university has consistently ranked highly in UK student satisfaction surveys and is noted for its bilingual Welsh-English provision.
