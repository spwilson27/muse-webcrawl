iPhone 4

The iPhone 4 is a smartphone designed and marketed by Apple Inc. It is the fourth generation of iPhone, announced by Steve Jobs on 7 June 2010 at WWDC and released on 24 June 2010. The iPhone 4 introduced a new industrial design — a stainless steel frame sandwiched between glass panels — the high-resolution "Retina display", Apple's A4 processor, and a front-facing camera enabling FaceTime video calls.

Design and hardware

The phone's 3.5-inch display had a resolution of 960×640 pixels, four times that of its predecessor, which Apple marketed as exceeding the resolving power of the human eye at typical viewing distance. The steel band doubled as the phone's antennas. Inside were the A4 system-on-a-chip, 512 MB of RAM, a 5-megapixel rear camera with LED flash and 720p video recording, and a gyroscope. It shipped with iOS 4 and ultimately received updates through iOS 7.

"Antennagate"

Shortly after launch, users reported that touching the lower-left corner of the phone could bridge its antennas and cause signal loss. The controversy — dubbed "Antennagate" — led Apple to hold a press conference, offer free protective cases, and publish comparative data on other phones; the design of the successor iPhone 4S revised the antenna arrangement. The iPhone 4 was also notable for having been leaked before launch when a prototype left in a California bar was purchased and published by the technology blog Gizmodo.

Legacy

The iPhone 4 remained on sale in various markets for years, serving as Apple's entry-level model, and its design language influenced subsequent generations. It was discontinued in 2014.
