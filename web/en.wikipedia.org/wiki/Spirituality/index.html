Spirituality

The meaning of the term spirituality has developed and expanded over time, and various meanings can be found alongside each other. Traditionally, spirituality referred to a religious process of re-formation aiming to recover the original shape of human beings, oriented at "the image of God" as exemplified by the founders and sacred texts of the world's religions.

Modern meanings

In modern times the term has broadened to include a wider range of experience, encompassing connection with something greater than oneself, a search for meaning in life, and universal human experiences such as love, compassion and awe. Many people describe themselves as "spiritual but not religious", distinguishing personal spiritual experience from organised religion; contemporary usage overlaps with practices such as meditation, mindfulness, yoga and secular ethics.

History

Words relating to spirit appear widely in religious traditions: the Hebrew ruach, the Christian concept of life "in the Spirit", Islamic spirituality centred on purification of the heart and Sufism, Hindu traditions of liberation (moksha), and Buddhist paths to awakening. In the late 19th and 20th centuries, movements such as Theosophy and New Thought, and later the New Age movement, shaped a more individualised, eclectic spirituality in the West.

Research

Psychologists and health researchers study spirituality in relation to well-being and coping, while scholars of religion debate the definition and historical construction of the concept itself.
