The Napoleon of Notting Hill

AuthorG. K. Chesterton
LanguageEnglish
GenreNovel, fantasy, political satire
Published1904

The Napoleon of Notting Hill is a novel written by G. K. Chesterton in 1904, set in a London of 1984 where, though the same technology and fashions persist unchanged, the world has become weary and the monarchy is chosen by lot. It is a comic, philosophical novel that influenced later writers, and is credited with helping to establish the genre of alternate history.

Plot

The new king drawn by lot is Auberon Quin, a tiny, whimsical civil servant who treats his office as a practical joke: he decrees that London's boroughs shall become independent medieval city-states with heraldry, halberdiers, and walls. Most people treat the edict as a joke — except Adam Wayne, the Provost of Notting Hill, who takes it deadly seriously. When a consortium of businessmen and neighboring boroughs schemes to drive a road through Notting Hill, Wayne rallies his suburb in a guerilla war fought with medieval weapons, and his passion transforms Quin's joke into something glorious and terrible.

Themes

Chesterton defends local patriotism, tradition, and the romance of ordinary places against the bland universalism of empire and commerce. The novel's climax contrasts Quin's laughter with Wayne's fanatic love, concluding that the two together make "a good Christian" — humor and devotion both being necessary.

Influence

The novel popularized the idea that a near future might be essentially the present dressed differently. Michael Collins, the Irish leader, admired the book; George Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four is often discussed in relation to it (Orwell criticized it in a 1945 essay on Chesterton but adopted the year 1984, which some see as a nod). It remains Chesterton's best-known novel alongside The Man Who Was Thursday.
