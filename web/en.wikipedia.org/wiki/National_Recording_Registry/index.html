National Recording Registry

National Recording Registry
Country: United States
Established: 2000 (first selections 2002)
Administered by: Library of Congress
Selection criterion: Culturally, historically or aesthetically significant recordings

The National Recording Registry is a list of sound recordings that the Librarian of Congress, advised by the National Recording Preservation Board, selects each year for preservation in the Library of Congress because they are "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".

Background

The registry was created by the National Recording Preservation Act of 2000, and the first 50 recordings were announced in 2002. Each year, 25 recordings at least ten years old are added, chosen from public nominations and board recommendations. The selections span the full range of recorded sound: music of all genres, speeches, radio broadcasts, comedy, field recordings and spoken word.

Selections

Inducted recordings range from Edison-era cylinders and early jazz and blues to landmark albums of rock, hip hop, country and classical music, as well as historic broadcasts such as the Apollo moon landing coverage. The registry parallels the National Film Registry, established earlier for motion pictures.
