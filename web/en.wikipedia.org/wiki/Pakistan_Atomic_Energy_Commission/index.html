Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission

The Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission (PAEC) is a federally funded independent governmental agency of Pakistan concerned with research and development of nuclear power, promotion of nuclear science, and energy policy. It was founded in 1956 and is headquartered in Islamabad.

History

Pakistan's atomic energy programme began in 1955, with the PAEC formally established in 1956 under its first chairman, physicist Nazir Ahmad; physicist Abdus Salam played a central role in its early scientific direction. Early work included research reactors supplied under the United States' Atoms for Peace programme, such as the PARR-1 research reactor at Nilore, and the Karachi Nuclear Power Plant (KANUPP), which entered service in the early 1970s.

Activities

PAEC operates Pakistan's civilian nuclear power plants, including units at Karachi and Chashma built with Chinese assistance, and runs institutes applying nuclear techniques in agriculture, medicine (including a network of nuclear medicine and oncology hospitals), industry and water resources. It also operates educational institutions such as the Pakistan Institute of Engineering and Applied Sciences (PIEAS).

Military-related nuclear work is overseen separately under Pakistan's National Command Authority; PAEC scientists historically contributed to Pakistan's nuclear research in the 1970s. Pakistan's nuclear facilities that are civilian in nature are placed under IAEA safeguards by agreement.
