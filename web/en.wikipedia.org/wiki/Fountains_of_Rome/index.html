Fountains of Rome

The fountains of Rome number more than 2,000, more than any other city in the world, and include both monumental show fountains and hundreds of small public drinking fountains known as nasoni. Fifty of the city's fountains were built in antiquity, fed by Rome's aqueducts; after the aqueducts fell into disrepair in the Middle Ages, the popes rebuilt them from the Renaissance onward and commissioned new fountains as symbols of papal power and urban renewal.

Ancient fountains

Under Augustus, Marcus Agrippa built or restored dozens of fountains fed by the Aqua Virgo and other aqueducts. By the fourth century AD Rome had well over a thousand public fountains, supplied by eleven aqueducts.

Renaissance and Baroque fountains

Pope Sixtus V's restoration of the aqueducts in the 1580s, under engineer Domenico Fontana, revived the city's water supply. The Baroque era produced Rome's most famous fountains: the Trevi Fountain (1732–1762, designed by Nicola Salvi), Gian Lorenzo Bernini's Fountain of the Four Rivers in Piazza Navona (1651) and the Fontana della Barcaccia at the foot of the Spanish Steps (1627–29).

Nasoni

The nasoni ("big noses"), cast-iron drinking fountains with a curved spout first installed in the 1870s, still provide free drinking water across the city and number around 2,500–2,800 including their modern counterparts.
