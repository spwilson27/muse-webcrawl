The Telluride Film Festival is an annual film festival held in the mountain town of Telluride, Colorado, over the Labor Day weekend. Founded in 1974 by James Card, Tom Luddy, and Bill and Stella Pence, it is known for its intimate scale, its secrecy—the programme is not announced in advance—and its influence as an early launchpad in the autumn awards season.

Programme

The festival screens a small selection of new films in converted local venues (including the Sheridan Opera House and the temporary Chuck Jones Cinema), alongside retrospectives and "Tributes" to filmmakers and actors, whose recipients receive the Silver Medallion. Each year also features guest directors who curate sidebar selections.

Reputation

Many films that later won major awards premiered or screened at Telluride early in the season, and the festival is considered one of the "Big Five" fall festivals alongside Venice, Toronto, New York, and the Sundance/Telluride bookends of the festival year. Passes are limited, preserving a casual atmosphere in which prominent filmmakers mingle with ordinary attendees.
