Sister Ping

Cheng Chui Ping (January 9, 1949 – April 24, 2014), also known as Sister Ping (Chinese: 萍姐), was a Chinese "snakehead" who ran a major human-smuggling operation bringing Chinese immigrants, mainly from Fujian province, to New York City between 1984 and 2000. Operating from Manhattan's Chinatown, she charged up to $35,000 per person and was estimated by prosecutors to have smuggled as many as 3,000 people and amassed a fortune of some $40 million.

Life and operation

Cheng was born in the village of Shengmei near Fuzhou, Fujian, and emigrated to the United States in 1981. She built a small business empire in Chinatown, running a variety store and later a bank-like money-transfer business, while developing a reputation in the Fujianese community as a reliable smuggler — "Sister Ping" was regarded by many customers as a benefactor who reunited families and offered flexible payment terms, with migrants paying off their debts through years of restaurant and garment work.

Her operation used container ships, fishing vessels, and air routes via Canada, Central America, and the Caribbean. She employed members of the Fuk Ching gang to enforce collections and guard smuggled migrants.

Golden Venture and arrest

On June 6, 1993, the freighter Golden Venture ran aground off Rockaway Beach, Queens, carrying 286 Chinese migrants linked to her network; ten drowned or died of hypothermia trying to swim ashore. The disaster brought intense law-enforcement attention. Cheng fled the United States in 1994 and was arrested in Hong Kong in 2000, then extradited. In 2005 she was convicted in federal court in New York of alien smuggling, money laundering, and trafficking in ransom proceeds, and sentenced to 35 years in prison.

Death and legacy

Cheng died of pancreatic cancer on April 24, 2014, at the Federal Medical Center in Fort Worth, Texas. Her funeral in New York drew large crowds from the Fujianese community, reflecting her ambiguous legacy as both a convicted trafficker and a folk figure credited with helping thousands emigrate. Her story is recounted in Patrick Radden Keefe's 2009 book The Snakehead.
