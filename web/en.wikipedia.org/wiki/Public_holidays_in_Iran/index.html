Public holidays in Iran

Public holidays in Iran follow the country's official Solar Hijri calendar for national and historical observances and the Islamic lunar calendar for religious observances, so the dates of Islamic holidays move through the solar year. Iran has one of the largest numbers of public holidays in the world — roughly 26 to 28 days a year in total — combining Shia religious commemorations, revolutionary anniversaries and ancient Persian festivals, above all Nowruz, the Persian New Year.

Religious holidays

Islamic holidays observed as public holidays include Tasua and Ashura (9–10 Muharram, commemorating the martyrdom of Imam Hussein at Karbala), Arbaeen, the death and birth anniversaries of the Prophet Muhammad, Eid al-Fitr (marking the end of Ramadan, two days), Eid al-Adha, Eid al-Ghadir, and the birth and martyrdom anniversaries of the Twelve Imams of Shia Islam, including Imam Ali, Fatimah, Imam Reza and the Mahdi (whose birthday on 15 Sha'ban is a major holiday). Because these follow the lunar calendar, their Gregorian dates shift by about eleven days each year.

Observance

The Iranian working week runs from Saturday to Thursday, with Friday as the weekly day of rest. When holidays fall on Fridays or adjacent to weekends they are not generally moved, which can produce long holiday clusters — most notably around Nowruz, when much of the country effectively pauses for two weeks as families travel, visit relatives and set the haft-sin table.
