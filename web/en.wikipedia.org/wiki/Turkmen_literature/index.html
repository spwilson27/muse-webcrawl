Turkmen literature

Turkmen literature comprises the oral and written literatures in the Turkmen language, developed among the Turkmen people of Central Asia. Its roots lie in the oral epic tradition of the Oghuz Turks, including the Book of Dede Korkut, and it flourished in written form in the 18th and 19th centuries with poets such as Magtymguly Pyragy, regarded as the founder of the Turkmen literary language.

Oral tradition

For centuries, Turkmen literature was transmitted orally by bakhshi (bards) who sang epics accompanied by the dutar. The dastans, long narrative poems such as Gorogly, celebrate heroes, love and tribal life, and remain central to Turkmen cultural identity.

Classical period

The 18th century poet Magtymguly Pyragy (c. 1724 – c. 1807) unified the Turkmen tribes culturally through his poetry, writing in a language accessible to ordinary people. Other classical poets include Mollanepes, Kemine, Seydi and Zelili, whose works combined Sufi themes with social commentary.

Soviet and modern periods

In the 20th century Turkmen writing adopted first a Latin and then a Cyrillic alphabet under Soviet rule, and themes of collectivisation and Soviet patriotism predominated. Writers such as Berdi Kerbabayev, author of the novel Decisive Step, shaped Soviet Turkmen prose. After independence in 1991, Turkmen literature returned to the Latin-based alphabet and re-emphasised national heritage and the works of Magtymguly.
