Virtual learning environment

A virtual learning environment (VLE) is a system for delivering learning materials to students via the web. These systems, also called learning management systems (LMS), course management systems, or managed learning environments, support online and blended teaching by giving teachers tools to publish content, set and collect assignments, run quizzes, communicate with students, and track progress, while students use them to access materials and participate in course activities outside the physical classroom.

Components

A typical VLE provides:

  • Course content pages, files, and multimedia organised into modules or weeks;
  • Communication tools — announcements, discussion forums, messaging, and sometimes chat or video conferencing;
  • Assessment tools — assignment submission, online quizzes, peer review, and an electronic gradebook;
  • Administrative functions — enrolment, groups, calendars, and learning analytics on student activity.

Examples and adoption

Widely used platforms include the open-source Moodle, Blackboard Learn, Canvas, D2L Brightspace, Sakai, and Google Classroom. VLEs became standard infrastructure in higher and further education during the 2000s, supporting distance learning as well as supplementing face-to-face teaching in a "blended" model. Their importance grew sharply with the shift to remote teaching during the COVID-19 pandemic. Standards such as SCORM, xAPI, and LTI allow learning content and external tools to interoperate between systems.
