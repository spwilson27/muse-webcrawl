Hoagie

A hoagie is a type of submarine sandwich originating in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. It consists of meats, cheese, vegetables, and condiments served on a long Italian roll, typically dressed with lettuce, tomato, onion, oil and vinegar, salt, pepper, and oregano.

Name and origins

The sandwich's origins are usually traced to Italian-American workers in South Philadelphia in the early 20th century. The name "hoagie" is popularly linked to Hog Island, the shipyard where workers ate sandwiches nicknamed "hoggies," though other etymologies are proposed. Similar sandwiches are called subs, heroes, grinders, wedges, or po' boys in other regions.

Recognition

In 1992 the hoagie was declared the "official sandwich of Philadelphia." Classic variants include the Italian hoagie (with cured meats and provolone), turkey, tuna, and the cheesesteak's roll-mate cousin, the chicken cheesesteak; the sandwich is a fixture of Philadelphia delis and Pennsylvania chains such as Wawa and Primo Hoagies.
