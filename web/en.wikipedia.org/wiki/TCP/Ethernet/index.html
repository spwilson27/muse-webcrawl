Ethernet

Ethernet
Standard: IEEE 802.3
Introduced: 1980; standardised 1983
Inventors: Robert Metcalfe et al., Xerox PARC

Ethernet is a family of wired computer networking technologies for local area networks (LANs), standardised as IEEE 802.3. It defines physical signalling and frame formats at the data link layer.

History

Developed at Xerox PARC by Robert Metcalfe and David Boggs in 1973–74 (inspired by ALOHAnet), Ethernet was commercialised in 1980 by DEC, Intel and Xerox at 10 Mbit/s. Twisted-pair (10BASE-T), switching, and full-duplex operation displaced shared coax and Token Ring.

Modern Ethernet

Speeds progressed through Fast Ethernet (100 Mbit/s), Gigabit, 10 Gigabit and now 100–800 Gbit/s for data centres, over copper and fibre. Frames carry source/destination MAC addresses; Power over Ethernet (PoE) supplies devices such as phones and cameras.
