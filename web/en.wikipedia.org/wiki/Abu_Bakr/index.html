Abu Bakr

Abu Bakr al-Siddiq
Born: c. 573, Mecca, Hejaz
Died: 23 August 634, Medina
Reign: 632–634 (first Rashidun Caliph)
Predecessor: Muhammad (as religious leader)
Successor: Umar ibn al-Khattab
Spouse: Qutaylah, Umm Ruman, Asma bint Umais, Habibah bint Kharijah
Children: including Aisha, Abdullah, Asma

Abu Bakr Abdullah ibn Uthman Abi Quhafa (c. 573 – 23 August 634), known as Abu Bakr al-Siddiq ("the Truthful"), was a senior companion and father-in-law of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and the first caliph of Islam, ruling the Rashidun Caliphate from 632 until his death in 634.

Early life and conversion

Born in Mecca to the Banu Taym clan of the Quraysh tribe, Abu Bakr was a prosperous cloth merchant. Tradition holds he was among the first men to accept Islam, and he used his wealth to free enslaved converts such as Bilal ibn Rabah. His daughter Aisha married Muhammad.

Companion of Muhammad

Abu Bakr accompanied Muhammad on the Hijra to Medina in 622, hiding in the cave of Thawr — an episode referenced in the Quran. He took part in most of the early community's battles and expeditions and led the Hajj pilgrimage in 631.

Caliphate

After Muhammad's death in 632, Abu Bakr was chosen as caliph at Saqifa. He faced the Ridda wars against tribes that renounced allegiance, reunifying Arabia by 633. He then launched the first Muslim conquests into Sasanian Iraq and Byzantine Syria. He is also credited with ordering the first compilation of the Quran under Zayd ibn Thabit after heavy losses among memorisers at the Battle of Yamama.

Death and legacy

Abu Bakr died in Medina in 634 after a short illness and was buried beside Muhammad in the Prophet's Mosque. Before his death he designated Umar as his successor. In Sunni Islam he is revered as the first of the four "Rightly Guided" caliphs; Shia tradition disputes the legitimacy of his accession.
