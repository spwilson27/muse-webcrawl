The International Skating Union (ISU) is the international governing body for competitive ice skating disciplines, including figure skating, synchronized skating, speed skating and short track speed skating. Founded in Scheveningen, Netherlands, in 1892, it is among the oldest international sports federations.

History

The ISU was founded in 1892 to set international standards for speed and figure skating competitions. It organized the first World Speed Skating Championships in 1893 and the first World Figure Skating Championships in 1896. Skating under the ISU was among the original sports of the Winter Olympic Games from 1924, and figure skating had earlier appeared at the Summer Olympics.

Competitions and judging

The ISU organizes World, European, Four Continents, World Junior and Grand Prix events across its disciplines. In figure skating it introduced the ISU Judging System in 2004, replacing the 6.0 system after the judging scandal at the 2002 Winter Olympics, with technical element scores and program component scores replacing ordinal placements.

Organization

The ISU is governed by a Council and a Congress of member federations, which votes on rule changes and elects officials. Its headquarters are in Lausanne, Switzerland, and it is recognized by the International Olympic Committee.
