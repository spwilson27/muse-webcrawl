Salih

Salih (Arabic: صالح, Ṣāliḥ) is a prophet mentioned in the Quran who was sent to the tribe of Thamud in ancient Arabia. He is one of the Arab prophets named in Islamic tradition, alongside Hud, Shuayb, and Muhammad. His story is told in several surahs, including Al-A'raf, Hud, and Ash-Shu'ara.

Salih, prophet to the people of Thamud, and the miraculous she-camel.

Story in the Quran

Salih called the Thamud — a prosperous people who carved dwellings from the mountains at al-Hijr (Mada'in Salih, "the cities of Salih") — to worship God alone and abandon idolatry. As a sign, God provided a miraculous she-camel (naqat Allah, "the she-camel of God"), which Salih commanded them not to harm and to allow to drink from their well on alternate days. Despite the warning, the chiefs of Thamud hamstrung the camel. Salih warned that punishment would come within three days; an earthquake (or a mighty blast) then destroyed the disbelievers, while Salih and his followers were saved.

Significance

The story of Salih and Thamud is cited in the Quran as an example of the fate of peoples who reject their prophets. The archaeological site of Hegra (Mada'in Salih) in present-day Saudi Arabia, with its Nabataean rock tombs, is traditionally associated with the Thamud. Salih's name also means "pious" or "righteous" in Arabic and is a common personal name.
