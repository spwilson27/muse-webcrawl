The Wave-Gotik-Treffen (German: "Wave-Gothic Meeting"; abbreviated WGT) is an annual world festival for "dark" music and culture held in Leipzig, Germany, over the four days of the Pentecost (Whitsun) holiday. It is one of the largest events of the gothic and dark alternative subcultures worldwide, typically drawing around 20,000 visitors and featuring roughly 150–200 performing acts.

History

A first attempt at such a gathering took place in Potsdam in 1987 in the German Democratic Republic, where it was treated as illegal and dispersed. The first official Wave-Gotik-Treffen was held in 1992 at the Eiskeller club in Leipzig, attracting about 2,000 visitors. Since then it has grown steadily, spreading across dozens of venues throughout the city, with the Agra exhibition grounds in the south of Leipzig serving as the main site and campground.

Programme

Musical performances span gothic rock, dark wave, EBM and industrial, metal, neofolk, medieval music, classical concerts, and club nights. Beyond music, the festival includes readings, theatre and film screenings, exhibitions, a medieval market, the Victorian picnic, and the "pagan village" (Heidnisches Dorf). Many visitors attend in elaborate costumes, and the event is known for its fashion as much as its concerts.

Significance

The WGT is regarded, alongside festivals such as M'era Luna, as a fixture of the German gothic scene (the "Schwarze Szene"). A notable tradition is that Leipzig public transport is included in the ticket, allowing costumed festival-goers to travel freely across the city.
