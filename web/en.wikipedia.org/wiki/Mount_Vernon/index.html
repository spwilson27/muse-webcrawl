Mount Vernon


Mount Vernon's east front overlooking the Potomac River.

Mount Vernon is the former plantation home of George Washington, the first president of the United States, and his wife Martha. The estate is on the banks of the Potomac River in Fairfax County, Virginia, near Alexandria.

The Washington family acquired the land in 1734; George Washington expanded the house over several decades into the 21-room mansion seen today and developed the estate into a working plantation of about 8,000 acres, worked by hundreds of enslaved people. Washington directed the design of the house, outbuildings and landscape, including the bowling green, gardens and a ha-ha wall overlooking the river.

Preservation

After Washington's death in 1799 he was buried in a tomb on the estate. The property fell into decline until the Mount Vernon Ladies' Association, founded by Ann Pamela Cunningham in 1853, purchased the mansion and part of the grounds in 1858 — often cited as the beginning of the historic preservation movement in the United States. It remains open to the public as a museum, drawing about a million visitors a year.
