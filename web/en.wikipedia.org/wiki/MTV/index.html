MTV (an initialism of Music Television) is an American cable television channel launched on 1 August 1981. Originally devoted to broadcasting music videos presented by "video jockeys" (VJs), MTV became a defining force in popular music and youth culture in the 1980s and 1990s before shifting toward reality and scripted programming aimed at teenagers and young adults.

History

MTV signed on at 12:01 a.m. with footage of the Apollo 11 moon landing, its flag bearing the MTV logo, followed by The Buggles' "Video Killed the Radio Star" as the first music video. The channel popularised the music video as an art form and marketing tool, boosted the careers of artists such as Michael Jackson, Madonna, and Prince, and launched the annual Video Music Awards in 1984. Programmes such as Yo! MTV Raps helped bring hip hop to a mainstream audience. In 1992, The Real World pioneered modern reality television. From the 2000s onward, music programming declined in favour of reality series including Jersey Shore and Teen Mom; in 2010 the words "Music Television" were removed from the logo. MTV-branded channels operate in dozens of countries around the world.

Cultural impact

MTV's fast-cut visual style, its "I Want My MTV" campaign, and campaigns such as "Rock the Vote" made it a central institution of late twentieth-century youth culture, studied as a driver of globalisation of American pop culture.
