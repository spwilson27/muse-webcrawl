Tanger Med (Arabic: طنجة المتوسط) is a Moroccan cargo port complex on the Strait of Gibraltar, about 40 km east of Tangier near the town of Ksar Seghir. Located at the western entrance to the Mediterranean, roughly 14 km across the strait from Spain, it has grown into the largest port in Africa and the Mediterranean by container capacity.

Development

The project was launched by King Mohammed VI in the early 2000s as the centrepiece of a development strategy for northern Morocco. The first terminals of Tanger Med I entered service in 2007, handling containers, roll-on/roll-off traffic to Europe, and passengers. Tanger Med II, inaugurated in 2019, added major new container terminals (including facilities operated by APM Terminals and a consortium led by CMA CGM interests) and raised total capacity to around 9–10 million TEU annually, surpassing long-established Mediterranean rivals.

Industrial platform

Around the port sits a large special economic zone of free-trade industrial parks hosting more than a thousand companies, prominently the Renault plant at Melloussa — one of the carmaker's largest — and a growing automotive and aeronautics supply chain. The port is linked by motorway and rail to Tangier and, via the Al Boraq high-speed line (opened 2018), to Casablanca.

Traffic

Tanger Med handles millions of passengers and hundreds of thousands of trucks annually on ferries to Algeciras and other Spanish ports, and in the early 2020s it processed on the order of 8–10 million TEU of containers per year, ranking among the world's top 25 container ports.
