Kansyore

The Kansyore ware is a distinctive type of pottery associated with Late Stone Age hunter-gatherer and early fishing communities around Lake Victoria in East Africa. It is generally dated to roughly between 6000 and 1500 years before present, though estimates vary between sites, and it represents some of the earliest pottery in the region.

Characteristics

Kansyore pottery is characterised by carefully executed, often bold decoration, including impressed and stamped motifs, punctates, and geometric patterns covering much of the vessel surface. The vessels are typically small to medium-sized jars and bowls with moderately thin walls, often burnished. The elaborate decoration distinguishes Kansyore ware from later, generally plainer, pastoralist and Iron Age ceramics in the same region.

Distribution and sites

Kansyore sites are concentrated around the shores and islands of Lake Victoria, including localities in present-day Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania, as well as a smaller number of inland sites to the east of the lake. Excavated sites include Kansyore Island and settlements around the Winam Gulf, as well as sites in the Kagera region.

Economy

The Kansyore tradition is strongly associated with aquatic resource exploitation, particularly fishing, supplemented by hunting and gathering of wild plants and animals. Fish bones and harpoons are common at Kansyore sites. Whether the Kansyore people also kept domestic animals or cultivated crops remains debated, but the weight of evidence suggests a largely hunter-fisher-gatherer adaptation, later interacting with incoming pastoralist and farming communities.
