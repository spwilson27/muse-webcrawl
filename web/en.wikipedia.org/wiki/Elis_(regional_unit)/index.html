Elis (Greek: Ηλεία, Ileia) is one of the regional units of Greece. It is part of the region of West Greece and occupies the western part of the Peloponnese peninsula, corresponding closely to the ancient district of Elis. Its capital is the town of Pyrgos. The regional unit is best known internationally for Ancient Olympia, the birthplace of the Olympic Games and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Administration

As part of the 2011 Kallikratis government reform, the regional unit of Elis was created out of the former Elis prefecture, and the municipalities were reorganised. The regional unit is divided into seven municipalities: Ancient Olympia, Andravida-Kyllini, Andritsaina-Krestena, Ilida, Pineios, Pyrgos, and Zacharo.

Geography

Elis is the flattest part of the Peloponnese. The long sandy coastline on the Ionian Sea includes the port of Katakolo and the thermal springs of Kaiafas. The rivers Alpheus — the longest river in the Peloponnese — and Peneus irrigate a fertile plain used for growing currants, olives, citrus fruit, watermelons, and tomatoes. The eastern part of the unit rises into forested hills toward Arcadia, including the Foloi oak forest.

History

The area's history follows that of ancient Elis. In the Byzantine and Frankish periods it formed part of the Principality of Achaea, with Andravida as an early capital and the castle of Chlemoutsi guarding the plain. It later came under Ottoman rule and joined the modern Greek state after the War of Independence. The region suffered severe damage in the wildfires of August 2007, which devastated large parts of the regional unit and threatened Ancient Olympia itself.

Economy and tourism

Agriculture remains the mainstay of the economy, supplemented by tourism centred on Olympia's archaeological site and museum, the long beaches of the Ionian coast, and cruise traffic through Katakolo.
