Pyramid of the Sun

Pyramid of the Sun

LocationTeotihuacan, Mexico
Height71.2 m (234 ft)

MaterialAdobe core, stone, lime plaster

The Pyramid of the Sun is the largest building at Teotihuacan and one of the largest pyramids in the world by volume. Standing about 71 metres high with a base of roughly 223 metres per side, it ranks behind only the Great Pyramid of Cholula and the Great Pyramid of Giza. It was constructed around AD 200, in a single major building phase, over a sacred cave that may have determined the site's religious importance.

Construction and layout

The pyramid stands on the east side of the Avenue of the Dead, aligned so that its front faces the point on the horizon where the sun sets on dates of calendrical significance. It was built of an adobe and rubble core faced with stone and finished with lime plaster, presumably painted. A stairway of 248 steps led to a summit temple, long since destroyed, which the Aztecs — who named the monument centuries after Teotihuacan's fall — associated with the sun god Tonatiuh.

Excavations

The pyramid was excavated and partially reconstructed under Leopoldo Batres during the presidency of Porfirio Díaz in the years before 1910; Batres mistakenly restored it with five terraces rather than four. In 1971 archaeologists discovered that a tunnel beneath the pyramid leads to a four-lobed chamber about 6 metres below its centre, apparently an artificial cave that may have been regarded as the place of human emergence in Teotihuacan cosmology.

Discoveries

Offerings found in and around the structure include arrowheads, obsidian figurines, and — in construction fill and caches — the skeletons of sacrificed children buried at the pyramid's corners, as well as puma remains deposited in chambers within the structure.
