Anhalt is a historical German region in present-day Saxony-Anhalt, centred on the Elbe and Mulde rivers. It was for centuries a principality and later a duchy ruled by the House of Ascania, and from 1918 to 1945 a free state of the Weimar Republic and Nazi Germany.

History

Anhalt emerged in the early thirteenth century from the inheritance of Henry I of Anhalt, son of the Ascanian duke Bernhard of Saxony. It was repeatedly partitioned among branches of the dynasty — including Anhalt-Dessau, Anhalt-Bernburg, Anhalt-Köthen, and Anhalt-Zerbst — before being reunited as a duchy in 1863. Catherine the Great of Russia was born a princess of Anhalt-Zerbst.

Modern era

The duchy joined the German Empire in 1871. After the November Revolution of 1918, the last duke abdicated and Anhalt became a free state. In 1945 it was merged with the Prussian province of Saxony to form Saxony-Anhalt. Its name survives in districts such as Anhalt-Bitterfeld and in the Anhaltisches Theater in Dessau.
