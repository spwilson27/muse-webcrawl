Waraji

Waraji (草鞋) are traditional Japanese sandals made from straw rope. They were the standard footwear of common people in Japan for centuries and were also worn by samurai and foot soldiers (ashigaru), who valued their lightness and grip on the march. Waraji are tied to the feet with straps that wind around the ankles.

Construction

Waraji are woven from rice straw, though other plant fibres such as hemp, palm or wisteria were also used. The sole is a plaited rope lattice; cords at the front pass between the toes, and long ties at the heel are wrapped around the ankle to secure the sandal. Because straw wears quickly, travellers carried spare pairs and replaced them along the way.

Use and symbolism

Monks, pilgrims and travellers traditionally wore waraji, and giant pairs are offered at temples such as Sensō-ji in Tokyo as a symbol of the guardian deities' strength and as a prayer for safe journeys and healthy feet. Today waraji are mainly worn at festivals, in historical re-enactments, and by shrine and temple attendants.
