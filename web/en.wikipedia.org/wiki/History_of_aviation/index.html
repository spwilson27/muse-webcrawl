History of aviation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A montage-style view of an early biplane and a modern jet airliner]
From biplanes to wide-body jets.

The history of aviation extends from ancient kites and the myth of Icarus through ballooning, the Wright brothers\' first powered aeroplane flights in 1903, two world wars that industrialised aircraft production, the jet age, and today\'s global airline industry. Aviation shrank the world, transformed warfare and trade, and led directly to spaceflight.

Early flight

Chinese kites, Leonardo da Vinci\'s designs and the Montgolfier hot-air balloon (1783) preceded heavier-than-air flight. Sir George Cayley identified the forces of flight and built pioneering gliders in the early 1800s; Otto Lilienthal made thousands of documented glider flights in the 1890s.

Pioneers to jets

After 1903, aviation advanced through barnstorming, airmail and the first airlines (KLM founded 1919, still the oldest operating under its original name). The Douglas DC-3 (1935) made airlines profitable; the Second World War brought radar, long-range bombers and the first jet fighters.

The jet age and beyond

The de Havilland Comet (1952), Boeing 707 (1958) and wide-body 747 (1970) created mass air travel. The supersonic Concorde (1976–2003) remains unmatched in scheduled service, while modern concerns centre on efficiency and emissions, with electric and hydrogen aircraft in development.
