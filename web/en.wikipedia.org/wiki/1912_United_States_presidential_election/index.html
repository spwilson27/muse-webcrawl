The 1912 United States presidential election was the 32nd quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 5, 1912. Democratic Governor Woodrow Wilson of New Jersey unseated incumbent Republican President William Howard Taft and defeated former president Theodore Roosevelt, who ran for the new Progressive Party, nicknamed the "Bull Moose Party".

Background

Roosevelt, who had hand-picked Taft as his successor in 1908, broke with him over conservation, tariffs, and trust policy. When the 1912 Republican convention renominated Taft amid contested delegations, Roosevelt's supporters bolted and formed the Progressive Party. The split in the Republican vote made Wilson's victory nearly inevitable, though Wilson also won only a plurality of the popular vote.

Campaign

Wilson campaigned on the "New Freedom" — lower tariffs, banking reform, and strengthened antitrust laws — against Roosevelt's "New Nationalism" of federal regulation and social welfare. Socialist candidate Eugene V. Debs won 6 percent, the party's best-ever showing. On 14 October 1912 Roosevelt survived an assassination attempt in Milwaukee and delivered his speech with the bullet still in his chest.

Results

Wilson carried 40 states and 435 electoral votes, the largest electoral majority at the time since the Civil War era. Roosevelt finished second — the best result for a third-party candidate in US history — while Taft won only Utah and Vermont, the worst result for an incumbent president. The election made Wilson the first Democrat in the White House in 16 years and the only Democrat elected between 1892 and 1932 besides Grover Cleveland's earlier terms.
