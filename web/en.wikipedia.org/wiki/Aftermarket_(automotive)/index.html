Aftermarket (automotive)

The automotive aftermarket is the secondary market of the automotive industry, concerned with the manufacturing, remanufacturing, distribution, retailing, and installation of vehicle parts, equipment, and accessories after the sale of the automobile by the original equipment manufacturer (OEM) to the consumer.

Scope

The aftermarket includes replacement parts for collision repair and mechanical wear (filters, brakes, batteries, exhaust systems), accessories, performance and tuning components, tires, and in-vehicle electronics. Parts may be genuine OEM components, equivalents made by independent suppliers, or remanufactured units. Distribution flows from manufacturers through warehouse distributors and jobbers to repair shops, dealerships, parts retailers, and do-it-yourself motorists.

Size and regulation

The global automotive aftermarket is valued in the hundreds of billions of US dollars annually, and it supports a large share of employment in vehicle service. Because vehicle owners depend on it for affordable repair, the sector is the focus of "right to repair" debates over access to diagnostic data, tools, and parts; in the United States, the Magnuson–Moss Warranty Act protects consumers' ability to use aftermarket parts and independent service without voiding warranties. Trade shows such as SEMA in Las Vegas showcase the performance and accessories segment.
