Crystal habit

In mineralogy, crystal habit is the characteristic external shape of an individual crystal or aggregate of crystals. The habit is influenced by the crystal's internal structure as well as by environmental factors during growth — temperature, pressure, the chemistry of the surrounding fluid, and available space. Habit is a useful diagnostic property in identifying minerals, since the same mineral can display several habits while unrelated minerals may share one.

Common habits

  • Acicular — needle-like (e.g. natrolite, rutile)
  • Bladed — flattened and elongated, like a knife blade (kyanite)
  • Botryoidal — globular masses resembling a bunch of grapes (hematite, malachite)
  • Cubic — cube-shaped (pyrite, halite, galena)
  • Dendritic — branching, tree-like (native copper, manganese oxides)
  • Fibrous — extremely fine, hair-like threads (asbestos minerals)
  • Massive — no discernible crystal form
  • Octahedral — eight triangular faces (diamond, fluorite, magnetite)
  • Prismatic — elongated with well-developed faces (quartz, tourmaline)
  • Tabular — flat, plate-like (feldspar, barite)

Factors affecting habit

Impurities adsorbing onto particular crystal faces can suppress growth on those faces and change the overall shape; for example, sodium chloride grows as cubes from pure water but as octahedra from solutions containing urea. Growth speed, supersaturation and twinning also shape the final habit.
