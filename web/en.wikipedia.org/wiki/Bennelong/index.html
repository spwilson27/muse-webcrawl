Woollarawarre Bennelong (c. 1764 – 3 January 1813) was a senior man of the Wangal clan of the Eora people of the Sydney region. Captured on Governor Arthur Phillip's orders in November 1789 to serve as an intermediary, Bennelong learned English, formed a close (if ambivalent) relationship with Phillip, and became one of the first Aboriginal people to develop sustained relations with the British colonists of New South Wales.

Capture and life at Sydney Cove

After escaping in May 1790, Bennelong later returned to the settlement voluntarily. Phillip had a brick hut built for him in 1790–91 on the point of Sydney Cove now known as Bennelong Point, the site of the Sydney Opera House. In 1792 Bennelong sailed to England with Phillip and another Eora man, Yemmerrawanne, where they were presented at court; Yemmerrawanne died in England, and Bennelong returned to Sydney in 1795 in poor health.

Later life

Bennelong's later years were marked by conflict between his role among his own people and his position with the colony; a letter dictated by him in 1796 is the earliest known surviving text written in English by an Aboriginal Australian. He died at Kissing Point in 1813. His name is commemorated in Bennelong Point, the federal electoral Division of Bennelong, and the opera Bennelong by Stephen Leek, among many other places.
