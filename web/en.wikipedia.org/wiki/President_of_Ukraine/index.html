The president of Ukraine (Ukrainian: Президент України) is the head of state of Ukraine. The president represents the nation in international relations, administers the foreign political activity of the state, and is the commander-in-chief of the Armed Forces of Ukraine.

Election and term

The president is elected by citizens in a direct two-round election for a five-year term, limited to two consecutive terms. Candidates must be at least 35 years old, have the right to vote, and have resided in Ukraine for the previous ten years. Volodymyr Zelenskyy, elected in 2019, is the sixth and incumbent president.

Powers

The president nominates the prime minister (subject to parliamentary consent), appoints ministers of foreign affairs and defence, may veto laws, dissolve parliament under specified conditions, and heads the National Security and Defense Council. Since Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022, the office has operated under martial law.

History

The modern office was established in 1991 as the Soviet Union dissolved; Leonid Kravchuk won the first presidential election held alongside the independence referendum.
