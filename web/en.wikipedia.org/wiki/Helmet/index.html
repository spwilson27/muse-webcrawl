Helmet

A helmet is a form of protective gear worn to protect the head. More specifically, a helmet complements the skull in protecting the human brain. Ceremonial or symbolic helmets (e.g., a policeman's helmet in the United Kingdom) without protective function are sometimes worn, and helmets are also used in sports, construction, mining, and military contexts.

History

Helmets are among the oldest forms of protective equipment and areregistered in history as early as the Bronze Age. Ancient designs included bronze and leather helmets used by Greek hoplites, Roman legionaries, and medieval knights. The development of firearms rendered many metal helmets less effective against bullets, but the First World War revived the steel combat helmet, such as the British Brodie helmet and the German Stahlhelm, primarily to protect against shrapnel.

Types

Common types include motorcycle helmets, bicycle helmets, construction hard hats, sports helmets (for American football, ice hockey, cricket, and skiing), firefighting helmets, and military combat helmets. Modern helmets typically combine a hard outer shell of polycarbonate or composite material with an energy-absorbing inner liner of expanded polystyrene foam.

Standards

Helmet safety standards are set by bodies such as the Snell Memorial Foundation, the U.S. Department of Transportation, and the European ECE regulations. Testing generally involves impact attenuation, penetration resistance, and retention system strength.
