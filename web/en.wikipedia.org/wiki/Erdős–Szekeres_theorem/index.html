Erdős–Szekeres theorem

The Erdős–Szekeres theorem is a pair of foundational results in Ramsey theory proved by Paul Erdős and George Szekeres in a landmark 1935 paper. The first, on monotone subsequences, states that any sequence of distinct real numbers of length (r−1)(s−1)+1 contains a monotonically increasing subsequence of length r or a monotonically decreasing subsequence of length s. The second, the convex polygon theorem, is the result that led Erdős to coin the term "Happy Ending problem".

Monotone subsequence theorem

Assign to each term of the sequence the pair (a, b), where a is the length of the longest increasing subsequence ending there and b the longest decreasing one. All pairs are distinct, so among (r−1)(s−1)+1 terms some pair has a ≥ r or b ≥ s, giving the required subsequence. The bound is sharp: a sequence can be constructed with (r−1)(s−1) terms and neither a long increasing nor a long decreasing subsequence. As a special case, any sequence of n² + 1 distinct numbers has a monotone subsequence of length n + 1.

Convex polygon theorem (Happy Ending problem)

Any set of five points in the plane in general position (no three collinear) contains an empty convex quadrilateral; more generally, for every n there is a smallest number of points, g(n), forcing a convex n-gon. Known values are g(3)=3, g(4)=5, g(5)=10, g(6)=17 — the last proved with computer assistance by Szekeres and Peters (2006). Erdős and Szekeres proved g(n) ≤ C(2n−5, n−2)+1 and conjectured g(n) = 2n−2+1; Andrew Suk (2016) nearly proved the matching upper bound, showing g(n) is at most 2n+o(n).

The "Happy Ending" name came because Szekeres met his future wife, Esther Klein — who posed the original problem — through this work; they married in 1937.

Significance

The Erdős–Szekeres paper is regarded as one of the founding works of Ramsey theory and combinatorial geometry, introducing the idea that sufficiently large structures unavoidably contain highly ordered substructures.
