Regional science

Regional science is a field of the social sciences concerned with analytical approaches to problems that are specifically urban, rural, or regional. Topics in regional science include, but are not limited to, location theory or spatial economics, location modeling, transportation, migration analysis, land use and urban development, interindustry analysis, environmental and ecological analysis, resource management, urban and regional policy analysis, geographical information systems, and spatial data analysis.

Origins

The discipline is generally credited to the American economist Walter Isard, who founded the Department of Regional Science at the University of Pennsylvania in the late 1950s and established the Regional Science Association in 1954. Isard sought to create a rigorous, quantitative science of regions drawing on economics, geography, planning, and other disciplines.

Methods

Regional scientists use tools such as input-output analysis, gravity models, spatial econometrics, and geographic information systems to study how economic activity is distributed in space and how regions grow, decline, and interact.
