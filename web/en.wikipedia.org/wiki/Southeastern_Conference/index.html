Southeastern Conference

The Southeastern Conference (SEC) is an American college athletic conference whose member institutions are located primarily in the South Central and Southeastern United States. Its 16 members include the flagship public universities of ten states and several prominent private institutions. The SEC participates in the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) Division I in sports competitions; for football it is part of the Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS), formerly Division I-A. The conference is widely regarded as one of the most powerful and successful in college athletics, particularly in football.

History

The SEC was founded in 1933 when thirteen members of the Southern Conference departed to form their own conference. Ten of the thirteen founding members have remained in the conference: Alabama, Auburn, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, LSU, Mississippi, Mississippi State, Tennessee, and Vanderbilt. Arkansas and South Carolina joined in 1991, Missouri and Texas A&M in 2012, and Texas and Oklahoma in 2024, bringing membership to sixteen and prompting the abolition of the divisional format in football.

Members

Current members include the University of Alabama (Crimson Tide), Auburn, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, LSU, Ole Miss, Mississippi State, Tennessee, Vanderbilt, Arkansas, South Carolina, Missouri, Texas A&M, Texas, and Oklahoma. Rivalries such as the Iron Bowl between Alabama and Auburn rank among the most intense in American sports.

Football success

SEC programs have won numerous national championships, including a dominant stretch of seven consecutive BCS titles from 2006 to 2012. The conference distributes the largest revenues of any collegiate league to its members, fueled by lucrative television contracts with ESPN and ABC and its own SEC Network, launched in 2014.
