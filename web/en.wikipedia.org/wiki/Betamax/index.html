Betamax (also called Beta) is a consumer-level analog recording and cassette format of magnetic tape for video, commonly known as a video cassette recorder. It was developed by Sony and released in Japan on May 10, 1975, followed by the United States in November 1975.

History

Sony introduced Betamax a year before JVC's rival VHS format. The format war that followed is a classic case study in marketing and technology adoption. Although Betamax was widely considered technically superior in picture quality, VHS offered longer recording times and was licensed more freely to other manufacturers, which gave it a larger share of the rental market. By the late 1980s VHS had decisively won the consumer market.

Technology

Betamax cassettes use half-inch tape housed in a compact shell smaller than the VHS cassette. The format used a helical scan system derived from Sony's professional U-matic format. Later variants included Betamax Hi-Fi for high-quality stereo audio and SuperBeta, which improved horizontal resolution.

Sony Corp. v. Universal City Studios

Betamax was at the centre of the landmark 1984 United States Supreme Court case Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, Inc., which held that making individual copies of television broadcasts for later viewing ("time-shifting") constitutes fair use, and that manufacturers of copying devices are not liable for contributory infringement merely because the device is capable of substantial non-infringing uses.

Professional use and legacy

Although it lost the consumer war, Betamax technology lived on in professional broadcasting through the Betacam family of formats, which became an industry standard for electronic news gathering. Sony announced in November 2015 that it would cease production of Betamax cassettes in March 2016, ending a run of over 40 years.
