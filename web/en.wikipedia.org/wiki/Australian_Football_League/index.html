The Australian Football League (AFL) is the pre-eminent professional competition of Australian rules football. It was originally named the Victorian Football League (VFL) and was founded in 1896 as a breakaway competition from the Victorian Football Association. Through expansions beginning in the 1980s it became a national league and adopted its current name for the 1990 season.

History

Eight clubs broke away from the VFA to form the VFL for the 1897 season. The league remained Melbourne-centred for most of the 20th century, though it drew elite players from all states. In 1982 South Melbourne relocated to Sydney, and the admission of the West Coast Eagles and Brisbane Bears in 1987 began genuine national expansion, followed by Adelaide (1991), Fremantle (1995), Port Adelaide (1997), Gold Coast (2011) and Greater Western Sydney (2012). A Tasmanian club, the Tasmania Devils, has been licensed to join in 2028.

Competition format

The season comprises a home-and-away series of about 23 rounds followed by a four-week finals series among the top eight teams, culminating in the AFL Grand Final, traditionally held at the Melbourne Cricket Ground on the last Saturday in September and watched by crowds of around 100,000. The league is governed by the AFL Commission and operates with a salary cap, national draft, and equalisation policies.

Popularity

The AFL is the most attended and most watched sporting league in Australia, with annual attendances in the millions and record-breaking television audiences. The women's competition, AFL Women's (AFLW), launched in 2017 and expanded to all 18 clubs by 2022.
