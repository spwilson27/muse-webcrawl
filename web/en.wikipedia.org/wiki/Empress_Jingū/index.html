Empress Jingū

Empress Jingū (Japanese: 神功皇后, Jingū-kōgō) was a legendary Japanese empress who is traditionally said to have ruled as regent after the death of her husband, Emperor Chūai, from about 201 to 269. She was the mother of Emperor Ōjin. According to the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, Jingū, possessed by the gods, led an invasion of the Korean peninsula — the conquest of the "promised land" of Silla — while pregnant with Ōjin, whose birth she is said to have delayed by carrying a stone in her clothing.

Historicity

Modern historians regard Jingū's story as largely legendary, though it may preserve memories of 4th- or 5th-century Japanese involvement in Korea, and some scholars have linked her to the shaman-queen Himiko described in Chinese records. She was nevertheless included in the traditional list of emperors for centuries, and was the first woman to appear on Japanese currency, on a government banknote of 1881.
