Modal share

Modal share (also called mode split, mode-share, or modal split) is the percentage of travellers using a particular type of transportation or the number of trips made by that mode. In transport planning, modal share is a key statistic for understanding how people move within a city or region and for assessing policies intended to shift travel from private cars to public transport, walking and cycling.

Measurement

Modal share is typically derived from household travel surveys, census journey-to-work questions, traffic counts and public transport ridership data. Results differ depending on whether shares are calculated by number of trips, by distance travelled, or by time — walking accounts for many short trips but a small share of total distance, while cars dominate distance in most motorised countries. Shares may be reported for all trips or only for commuting, which often shows a higher car share than travel overall.

Comparisons

Modal share varies enormously between cities. In most of the United States, the private automobile accounts for roughly 80–90% of commute trips, while in Amsterdam and Copenhagen cycling reaches 30–50% of trips, and in Hong Kong public transport carries around 90% of motorised trips. Tokyo, London, Paris and New York have public transport shares far above their national averages, reflecting dense development, extensive rail networks and restraints on car use.

Policy use

Many cities set modal-share targets as part of climate, congestion and public-health strategies — for example, goals that a given percentage of trips be made by sustainable modes by a target year. Transport models use mode-choice (logit) models, driven by travel time, cost, reliability and comfort, to forecast how investments and pricing will change the split.
