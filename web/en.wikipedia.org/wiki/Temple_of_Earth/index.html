Temple of Earth

The Temple of Earth (Chinese: 地壇; pinyin: Dìtán) in Beijing is located in the northern part of the city, near Andingmen, in Dongcheng District. It was built in 1530 during the Ming dynasty, and emperors of the Ming and Qing dynasties performed there the annual rites of sacrifice to the Earth at the summer solstice.

Layout and symbolism

The temple grounds embody the traditional Chinese cosmology of "round heaven and square earth": whereas the Temple of Heaven in the south of the city is built to circular plans, the Temple of Earth is square. Its centrepiece is the Fangzetan (方澤壇), a square, two-tiered altar platform faced with glazed tiles, surrounded by a moat-like square water channel and enclosed by walls. Numbers derived from the square and from even, yin numbers govern the altar's dimensions, in deliberate contrast with the odd, circular symbolism of the Temple of Heaven.

History

The Jiajing Emperor built the temple in 1530 as part of a reorganization of imperial ritual that created separate altars for Heaven, Earth, the Sun and the Moon at the four sides of the capital. Emperors travelled to the temple in procession at the summer solstice to perform sacrifices to the Earth deity. The last imperial ceremonies were held before the fall of the Qing dynasty in 1912.

Ditan Park

The temple grounds were opened to the public as a park in the twentieth century and are now known as Ditan Park. The park is a popular site for morning exercise and hosts one of Beijing's best-known temple fairs during the Spring Festival (Chinese New Year), established in the 1980s.
