The Columbus Crew is an American professional soccer club based in Columbus, Ohio. The club competes in Major League Soccer (MLS) as a member of the Eastern Conference. The Crew began play in 1996 as one of the ten charter clubs of the league, and were founded and long owned by Lamar Hunt. The club plays its home games at Lower.com Field, which opened in 2021 in the Arena District.

History

In 1999 the Crew opened Columbus Crew Stadium (later Mapfre Stadium), the first soccer-specific stadium built for an MLS club, a landmark in the league's development. The team won its first major trophy, the U.S. Open Cup, in 2002, and under coach Sigi Schmid won the MLS Cup and Supporters' Shield double in 2008, led by league MVP Guillermo Barros Schelotto.

In 2017 then-owner Anthony Precourt announced plans to relocate the club to Austin, Texas, prompting the "Save the Crew" fan movement and legal action by the city of Columbus and the state of Ohio under a law restricting relocation of publicly subsidised teams. The dispute ended in 2019 with the club's sale to a group led by the Haslam family and the award of an expansion team in Austin, making Columbus one of American soccer's most noted supporter-preservation stories.

Under coach Wilfried Nancy the Crew won a third MLS Cup in 2023, defeating LAFC, having earlier won the 2020 cup under Caleb Porter behind Darlington Nagbe and Lucas Zelarayán, and the 2024 Leagues Cup.

Supporters and rivalry

The Nordecke is the club's main supporters' section. The Crew's fiercest rivalry is the "Hell Is Real" derby with FC Cincinnati, named after a roadside religious sign on the interstate between the two cities.
