The Fighter is a 2010 American biographical sports drama film directed by David O. Russell. It tells the story of professional boxer "Irish" Micky Ward (Mark Wahlberg) of Lowell, Massachusetts, and his older half-brother Dicky Eklund (Christian Bale), a former boxer who once knocked down Sugar Ray Leonard but whose career and life collapsed into crack cocaine addiction — a decline documented by an HBO film crew within the story itself.

Plot

Managed by his formidable mother Alice (Melissa Leo) and trained by the erratic Dicky, Micky's career stalls in mismatches and defeats. Encouraged by bartender Charlene (Amy Adams), he breaks with his family's chaotic management, rebuilds himself, and reunites with a now-clean Dicky as his trainer. The film culminates in 2000 with Micky's victory over Shea Neary in London for the WBU light welterweight title — the stepping stone to his celebrated later trilogy with Arturo Gatti.

Production and reception

Wahlberg trained for years while the project passed through directors including Martin Scorsese (declined) and Darren Aronofsky before Russell took over; Bale lost some 30 pounds for the role and stayed in character as Dicky. Released by Paramount and Relativity Media, the film was a critical success, earning seven Academy Award nominations and winning two: Best Supporting Actor for Bale and Best Supporting Actress for Leo — the first film since 1986 in which both supporting acting Oscars went to the same production. Its gritty working-class realism contrasts in style with the heightened comic-book world of The Dark Knight, another 2008–2010-era awards contender.
