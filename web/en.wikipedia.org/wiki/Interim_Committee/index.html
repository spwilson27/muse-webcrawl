Interim Committee

The Interim Committee was a secret high-level group created in May 1945 by United States Secretary of War Henry L. Stimson at the urging of leaders of the Manhattan Project to advise President Harry S. Truman on matters relating to nuclear energy and the use of the atomic bomb in World War II.

Composition

The committee was chaired by Stimson and included James F. Byrnes (as the President's personal representative), Under Secretary of the Navy Ralph A. Bard, Assistant Secretary of State William L. Clayton, and scientists Vannevar Bush, James B. Conant, and Karl Compton. It was assisted by a scientific panel consisting of J. Robert Oppenheimer, Enrico Fermi, Ernest Lawrence, and Arthur Compton.

Recommendations

On 1 June 1945 the committee recommended that the atomic bomb be used against Japan as soon as possible, on a military target surrounded by workers' housing, and without prior warning. The committee also considered long-term issues, including international control of atomic energy and postwar nuclear policy.
