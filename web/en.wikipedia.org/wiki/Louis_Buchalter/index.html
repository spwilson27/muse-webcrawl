Louis Buchalter, known as Louis Lepke or Lepke Buchalter (February 6, 1897 – March 4, 1944), was an American organized crime figure and head of the Mafia hit squad known as Murder, Inc. during the 1930s. Buchalter was one of the premier labor racketeers in New York City during that era.

He is the only American mob boss ever to receive the death penalty in the United States after being convicted of murder, and was executed at Sing Sing Prison in 1944.

Early life

Buchalter was born on the Lower East Side of Manhattan to a Jewish family. His father died when he was young, and the nickname "Lepke" derived from the Yiddish Lepkeleh ("little Louis"), a term of endearment used by his mother. By his late teens he was involved in street crime and served time in prison for burglary and assault.

Criminal career

During the 1920s Buchalter moved into labor racketeering, using violence and intimidation to control unions in the garment industry and other trades. Working alongside Jacob "Gurrah" Shapiro, he built a lucrative protection and extortion empire. He was closely associated with the National Crime Syndicate and with figures such as Charles "Lucky" Luciano and Meyer Lansky.

Buchalter headed Murder, Inc., the enforcement arm of the Syndicate, which carried out contract killings across the United States. He also became involved in large-scale narcotics trafficking.

Fugitive and arrest

Facing narcotics and extortion charges, Buchalter went into hiding in 1937 and became the subject of an intensive manhunt led by FBI director J. Edgar Hoover. In August 1939 he surrendered, reportedly believing a deal had been arranged, but he was instead handed over to federal and state authorities.

Trials and execution

Buchalter was convicted in federal court of narcotics trafficking, and in 1941 he was tried in Brooklyn for the 1936 murder of candy-store owner Joseph Rosen. Prosecutor Burton Turkus relied on testimony from Murder, Inc. informant Abe "Kid Twist" Reles. Buchalter was sentenced to death and was executed in the electric chair at Sing Sing on March 4, 1944.
