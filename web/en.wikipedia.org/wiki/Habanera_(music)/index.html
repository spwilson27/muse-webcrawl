Habanera (music)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The habanera is the earliest Cuban dance form to achieve international renown.

The habanera (also known as the danza habanera) is a Spanish and Cuban musical genre that originated in Cuba in the early nineteenth century, developing out of the European contradanza that had arrived in the island in the late eighteenth century. It was the first written music to be rhythmically based on a characteristically African rhythmic motif, and the first Latin American dance to gain international popularity, reaching its peak of fame in the 1850s and 1860s.

The habanera is written in a slow, graceful 2/4 time. Its most characteristic feature is the so-called habanera rhythm, an ostinato pattern that may be described as a three-three-two grouping of eighth notes, with a dotted figure in the bass: dotted eighth, sixteenth, and two eighth notes. The dance is performed in a gentle, swaying manner by couples, at a languid tempo contrasting with the more vigorous contradanza.

History

The contradanza was introduced into Cuba from Spain by way of France in the eighteenth century, and after the Haitian Revolution of 1791 refugees from Saint-Domingue brought further influences. In Cuba the contradanza absorbed African rhythmic elements, becoming the contradanza cubana and then, by the early 1800s, the danza habanera — "habanera" meaning simply "of Havana". The earliest named example is "El amor en el baile", from 1841.

In the mid-nineteenth century the danza was the most popular music in Cuba, and the habanera spread abroad: to Spain, where it fed into genres such as the zarzuela; to the rest of Latin America; and to the United States. It is considered one of the foundations on which later Cuban music — danzón, bolero, son — was built, and through the "Spanish tinge" it influenced the early development of jazz, notably in the music of Jelly Roll Morton.

In art music

The best-known habanera in Western art music is the aria "L'amour est un oiseau rebelle" from Georges Bizet's opera Carmen (1875), which Bizet adapted from the song "El Arreglito" by the Spanish composer Sebastián Yradier (1863). Composers including Isaac Albéniz, Emmanuel Chabrier, Claude Debussy and Maurice Ravel also wrote habaneras or used the rhythm, as did the Basque composer Pablo de Sarasate.
