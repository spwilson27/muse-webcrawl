Ralph Breaks the Internet is a 2018 American computer-animated comedy film produced by Walt Disney Animation Studios and distributed by Walt Disney Pictures. A sequel to Wreck-It Ralph (2012) — Disney's 57th animated feature — it follows arcade-game characters Wreck-It Ralph and Vanellope von Schweetz as they travel into the World Wide Web to find a replacement steering wheel for Vanellope's game, Sugar Rush.

Plot

Six years after the first film, a broken steering wheel threatens to shut down Sugar Rush for good. Ralph and Vanellope journey through Litwak's new WiFi connection into a vast internet city of websites, avatars, auctions, and viral videos. Along the way they meet Yesss, the algorithm of the video-sharing site BuzzzTube, and daredevil online racer Shank, whose dangerous game Slaughter Race makes Vanellope question whether she wants to return to the arcade at all. Ralph's insecurity about losing his best friend unleashes a self-replicating virus of his own flaws, forcing the pair to confront their changing friendship.

Reception

The film was a commercial and critical success, grossing about $529 million against a $175 million budget, praised for its animation, humour, and its elaborate internet world — including a celebrated scene gathering nearly all of Disney's princesses. It was nominated for the Academy Award and Golden Globe for Best Animated Feature, losing both to Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse.
