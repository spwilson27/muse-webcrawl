Humidex

The humidex (short for humidity index) is an index number used by Canadian meteorologists to describe how hot weather feels to the average person, by combining the effects of heat and humidity. It was introduced by Environment Canada in 1979 and is similar in purpose to the heat index used in the United States.

Interpretation

A humidex of 20–29 is generally comfortable; 30–39 causes some discomfort; 40–45 great discomfort; and above 45 is considered dangerous, with heat stroke possible. During the 1995 and 2018 heat waves in Canada, humidex values in the high 40s were recorded, and values above 50 have occasionally been observed in the southern Prairies and Ontario.

Formula

The humidex is calculated from air temperature and dew point: it adds to the temperature a term proportional to the vapour pressure of water in the air, reflecting that humid air impedes evaporative cooling of the skin.
