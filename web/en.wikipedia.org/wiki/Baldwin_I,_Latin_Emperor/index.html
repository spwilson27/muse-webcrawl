Baldwin I (July 1172 – c. 1205) was the first Latin Emperor of Constantinople, ruling from 1204 until his capture by the Bulgarians in 1205. As Baldwin IX he was also Count of Flanders and, as Baldwin VI, Count of Hainaut. One of the leaders of the Fourth Crusade, he was elected emperor after the crusaders' sack of Constantinople in April 1204.

Early life

Baldwin was the son of Baldwin V of Hainaut and Margaret of Flanders. He succeeded his mother in Flanders in 1194 and his father in Hainaut in 1195. He fought King Philip II of France over his inheritance, settling by the Treaty of Péronne (1200). He took the cross in 1200 and left for the crusade in 1202, leaving his pregnant wife Marie of Champagne, who died in the Holy Land.

The Fourth Crusade and emperorship

Diverted first to Zara and then to Constantinople, the crusade conquered the Byzantine capital in 1204. By the Partitio Romaniae the crusaders divided the empire; Baldwin was chosen emperor — partly because his rival Boniface of Montferrat was distrusted — and crowned in Hagia Sophia on 16 May 1204.

War with Bulgaria and death

Baldwin alienated the Greek population and quarrelled with Boniface. In 1205 he marched against Adrianople, held by rebellious Greeks allied with Kaloyan of Bulgaria. On 14 April 1205 the crusader army was destroyed at the Battle of Adrianople and Baldwin was captured. He died in captivity at Tarnovo; the exact circumstances were never established, and his brother Henry succeeded him as emperor.
