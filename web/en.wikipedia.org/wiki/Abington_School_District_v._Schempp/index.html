Abington School District v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203 (1963), is a United States Supreme Court case in which the Court held that publicly funded schools may not require students to read from the Bible as part of a daily devotional exercise.

Background

Pennsylvania law required that at least ten verses from the Bible be read without comment at the opening of each public school day. Edward Schempp, a Unitarian, challenged the requirement on behalf of his children, Ellery and Roger, who attended Abington Senior High School. The case was consolidated with Murray v. Curlett, a similar challenge from Baltimore brought by Madalyn Murray O'Hair and her son.

Decision

By an 8–1 vote, the Court struck down the Pennsylvania statute and the Baltimore regulation, holding that state-mandated Bible reading and prayer in public schools violated the Establishment Clause of the First Amendment as applied through the Fourteenth Amendment. The Court articulated the requirement that laws have a secular legislative purpose and a primary effect that neither advances nor inhibits religion.
