George Campbell Scott (October 18, 1927 – September 22, 1999) was an American actor, director, and producer. He had a celebrated career on stage and screen and was known for his commanding presence, gravelly voice, and refusal to accept convention — most famously becoming the first actor to decline the Academy Award for Best Actor, for his performance as General George S. Patton in Patton (1970).

Early life

Scott was born in Wise, Virginia, and grew up largely in Detroit, where his father worked for Buick. After service in the U.S. Marine Corps (1945–49), during which he served as a guard at Arlington National Cemetery, he studied journalism and then acting at the University of Missouri. He built his reputation in New York theatre in the late 1950s, winning acclaim in Shakespeare and in Joseph Heller's We Bombed in New Haven.

Career

Scott's breakout film roles came in Anatomy of a Murder (1959) and The Hustler (1961), both earning Academy Award nominations; he asked that his nomination for The Hustler be withdrawn, an early sign of his distaste for acting competitions. He played General "Buck" Turgidson in Stanley Kubrick's Dr. Strangelove (1964) and starred in Petulia (1968) and The Hospital (1971).

For Patton he won the Academy Award for Best Actor but refused it, calling the Oscars a "meat parade"; producer Frank McCarthy accepted the statuette on his behalf. The Academy formally records Scott as the winner. On television he won an Emmy for The Price (1971) and praise as Ebenezer Scrooge in the 1984 adaptation of A Christmas Carol. His later career mixed stage revivals (including Death of a Salesman and Inherit the Wind) with film work such as The Changeling (1980) and Taps (1981).

Personal life and death

Scott was married five times, including twice to actress Colleen Dewhurst. He struggled with alcoholism throughout his life. He died in 1999 of a ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm and was buried, at his request, in an unmarked grave at Westwood Village Memorial Park in Los Angeles.
