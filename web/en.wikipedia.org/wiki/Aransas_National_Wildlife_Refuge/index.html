The Aransas National Wildlife Refuge is a protected area of about 115,000 acres on the Gulf Coast of Texas, United States, spanning parts of Aransas, Refugio and Calhoun counties. Established in 1937 as the Aransas Migratory Waterfowl Refuge, it is managed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

Wildlife

The refuge is famous as the principal wintering ground of the endangered whooping crane: essentially the entire wild migratory flock winters in its salt marshes from roughly October to April, feeding on blue crabs and wolfberries. When the refuge was established, the world's whooping cranes numbered fewer than twenty; the flock has since recovered to several hundred birds. The refuge also protects coastal marshes, oak mottes and prairie used by alligators, javelinas, white-tailed deer and more than 400 bird species along the Central Flyway.
