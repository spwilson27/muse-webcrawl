Maize (called corn in North America) is a cereal grain first domesticated by Indigenous peoples in southern Mexico about 9,000 years ago from the wild grass teosinte. Through selective breeding it became unrecognisable from its ancestor — its kernels cannot disperse without humans — and spread worldwide after contact with the Americas.

Uses

Maize is eaten directly (sweet corn, tortillas, polenta, popcorn), fed to livestock such as cattle and pigs, and processed into starch, sweeteners and ethanol fuel. Together with rice and wheat it provides a large share of humanity's calories; its dependence on nitrogen fertiliser and water shapes farming debates.
