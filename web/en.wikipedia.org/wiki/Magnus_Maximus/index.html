Magnus Maximus

Magnus Maximus (c. 335 – 28 August 388) was Roman emperor in the western portion of the empire from 383 to 388. A senior general from Hispania who had served with distinction in Britain and Africa under Count Theodosius, he was proclaimed emperor by his troops in Britain in 383, in opposition to the emperor Gratian.

Usurpation and rule

Maximus crossed to Gaul with his army; Gratian was abandoned by his troops and killed near Lyon in August 383. Maximus made his capital at Augusta Treverorum (Trier) and was recognized by the eastern emperor Theodosius I and, reluctantly, by Valentinian II's court in Italy as ruler of Gaul, Hispania, and Britain. He is noted for his strict Nicene orthodoxy: he ordered the execution of the heretic Priscillian and his followers in 385, the first recorded execution of Christians for heresy by a Christian emperor, against the protests of Martin of Tours.

Fall

In 387 Maximus invaded Italy, driving Valentinian II to flee to Theodosius in the east. Theodosius marched west in 388 and defeated Maximus at the battles of Siscia and Poetovio; Maximus surrendered at Aquileia and was executed in August 388, and his son and co-emperor Flavius Victor was killed in Gaul soon after.

In Welsh tradition

As Macsen Wledig, Maximus became a hero of Welsh legend. The tale The Dream of Macsen Wledig in the Mabinogion tells of his marriage to the Welsh princess Elen, and Geoffrey of Monmouth and later genealogies made him the ancestor of Welsh dynasties and the emperor who withdrew Roman troops from Britain, leaving it to defend itself.
