Sanshou

Sanshou (Chinese: 散手, "free hand"), also known as sanda (散打, "free fighting"), is a Chinese full-contact combat sport developed from traditional kung fu and modern fighting techniques. It combines punches, kicks, throws, takedowns, and sweeps in a format resembling kickboxing augmented with wrestling-style throws.

History

Sanshou was developed by the Chinese military in the mid-20th century as a practical hand-to-hand combat system, drawing on traditional wushu, boxing, and wrestling. From the 1980s it was standardised as a competitive sport under the International Wushu Federation, with amateur bouts fought on a raised platform called a leitai. A professional version, sometimes called Sanda, permits additional techniques such as knee and elbow strikes in some formats.

Rules and techniques

Competitors wear gloves, headgear, and protective gear in amateur competition and score points for clean punches and kicks, with throws and takedowns scoring highly; pushing an opponent off the platform also earns points. Bouts typically consist of best-of-three two-minute rounds. Sanshou's emphasis on catching kicks and converting them into throws distinguishes it from other kickboxing styles, and its practitioners have competed successfully in mixed martial arts.
