War of the Fifth Coalition

The War of the Fifth Coalition was a European conflict in 1809 that was part of the Napoleonic Wars and the Coalition Wars. The main combatants were the Austrian Empire and the United Kingdom against Napoleon's French Empire and its allies.

War of the Fifth Coalition
Date10 April – 14 October 1809
LocationCentral Europe, Italy, Netherlands
ResultFrench victory; Treaty of Schönbrunn

Campaign

Austria, encouraged by British subsidies and hoping to exploit France's entanglement in the Peninsular War, invaded Bavaria in April 1809 under Archduke Charles. Napoleon responded rapidly, defeating the Austrians in the Bavarian campaign around Eckmühl and advancing to occupy Vienna in May. At Aspern-Essling (21–22 May) Charles inflicted Napoleon's first significant battlefield defeat, but the French regrouped on the Danube and won the decisive Battle of Wagram on 5–6 July 1809, one of the largest battles of the era.

Treaty of Schönbrunn

The Treaty of Schönbrunn (14 October 1809) stripped Austria of territory and population, imposed a heavy indemnity, and reduced its army. Austria was forced into alliance with France, sealed in 1810 by the marriage of Napoleon to Archduchess Marie Louise.

Other theatres

Britain's Walcheren Campaign in the Netherlands ended in failure, while fighting also occurred in Italy, the Tyrol (where Andreas Hofer led a rebellion), Poland and Dalmatia.
