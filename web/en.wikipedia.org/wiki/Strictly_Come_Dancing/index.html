Strictly Come Dancing

Strictly Come Dancing (informally known as Strictly) is a British television dance-contest programme on BBC One, first broadcast on 15 May 2004. In each series, celebrities are paired with professional ballroom and Latin dancers and compete in weekly live shows, performing choreographed routines scored by a panel of judges and a public vote, with the lowest-scoring couple facing a dance-off and elimination.

Format

The title combines the 1992 film Strictly Ballroom with the BBC's long-running dance programme Come Dancing. The show was originally presented by Sir Bruce Forsyth and Tess Daly; after Forsyth's departure and death, Daly has co-presented with Claudia Winkleman, who also hosts the spin-off Strictly Come Dancing: It Takes Two. Judges have included Len Goodman (head judge until 2016), Craig Revel Horwood, Bruno Tonioli, Arlene Phillips, Darcey Bussell, Shirley Ballas (head judge from 2017), Motsi Mabuse, and Anton Du Beke, himself a former professional on the show.

International versions

The format, sold by BBC Studios as Dancing with the Stars, has been licensed in dozens of countries — the American version premiered in 2005 — making it one of the most successful television formats in the world according to Guinness World Records.

Impact

The programme is a fixture of British autumn television, regularly drawing some of the BBC's largest audiences, and culminates in a final awarding the Glitterball Trophy.
