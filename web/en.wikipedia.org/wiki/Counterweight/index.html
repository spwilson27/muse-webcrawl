Counterweight

A counterweight balances a load in a mechanical system.

A counterweight is a weight that, by applying an opposite force, provides balance and stability to a mechanical system. Its purpose is to make lifting a load faster and more efficient, saving energy and reducing the demands placed on the lifting machine. Counterweights are often used in traction lifts (elevators), cranes, and forklifts.

How it works

By Newton's third law, a suspended load exerts a downward force; a counterweight on the opposite side of a fulcrum, pulley, or pivot exerts a counteracting torque or tension. In a traction elevator, the counterweight typically equals the weight of the car plus about 40–50% of its rated capacity, so the motor need only overcome the difference between the car and counterweight plus friction. In a tower crane, concrete or cast-iron blocks hung at the short end of the jib balance the moment of the load at the long end.

Applications

Counterweights appear in trebuchets (where a heavy falling counterweight powers the throw), drawbridges and bascule bridges, forklift trucks (whose rear body mass balances the load on the forks), crankshafts of piston engines (to balance reciprocating masses), metronomes, and some window sashes and garage doors. In aviation and engineering the broader term "counterbalance" covers equivalent arrangements using springs or pneumatics.

Safety

Improperly secured counterweights are a hazard: crane counterweights must be pinned and retained, and elevator counterweights run in guarded hoistways. Failures have caused serious accidents, leading to strict inspection regimes in most jurisdictions.
