Bormio

Bormio
Comune in Lombardy, Italy
Elevation: about 1,225 m
Known for: skiing, Stelvio Pass, thermal baths

Bormio is a town and comune in the Province of Sondrio, Lombardy region of northern Italy, located in the upper Valtellina valley near the Swiss border. Situated at roughly 1,225 metres above sea level, it is a well-known Alpine resort town, famous for its ski area, its historic thermal baths, and its proximity to the Stelvio Pass, one of the highest paved mountain passes in Europe.

History

The area was settled in antiquity, benefiting from warm thermal springs known already in Roman times. During the Middle Ages Bormio was an important trading post on routes between Italy and the Alpine passes to the north, and it enjoyed a degree of autonomy under its own statutes. It later passed under the rule of the Duchy of Milan and then the Habsburgs, and was contested during the Valtellina campaigns of the Thirty Years' War.

Sport

Bormio is a regular venue on the FIS Alpine Ski World Cup circuit; its Stelvio piste is regarded as one of the most demanding downhill courses in the world, and the town hosted events of the Alpine World Ski Championships in 1985 and 2005. In summer the town is a base for cycling over the Stelvio and Gavia passes.

Thermal baths

The Bagni di Bormio, fed by natural hot springs, have been used since Roman times and remain a major attraction.
