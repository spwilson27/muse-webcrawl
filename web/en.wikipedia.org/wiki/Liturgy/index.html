Liturgy

Liturgy is the customary public ritual of worship performed by a religious group, according to its particular traditions. As a religious phenomenon, liturgy represents a communal response to the sacred through recognisable, repeated forms of activity reflecting praise, thanksgiving, remembrance, supplication or repentance. It forms a basis for establishing a relationship with a divine agency, as well as with other participants in the liturgy.

The word derives from the Greek λειτουργία (leitourgia), "public work" or "service in the name of/on behalf of the people" — in ancient Athens, a public duty performed by wealthy citizens at their own expense. In the Septuagint and the New Testament the word was used of priestly service in the Temple and, by extension, of Christian worship.

Christian liturgy

In Christianity, liturgy most often refers to the prescribed order of public worship, above all the Eucharist. "The Liturgy" in Eastern Christianity — the Orthodox Church, Oriental Orthodoxy, and Eastern Catholic Churches — usually means the Divine Liturgy, the eucharistic service whose principal forms are the Liturgy of St John Chrysostom and the Liturgy of St Basil the Great, together with the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts used in Lent.

In the Latin Church of Roman Catholicism, liturgy encompasses the Mass (in its Roman Rite and other Latin liturgical rites) and the Liturgy of the Hours, the daily cycle of prayer. Liturgical life in the Roman Rite is ordered by the liturgical year, with its seasons of Advent, Christmas, Lent, the Easter Triduum and Ordinary Time. The Second Vatican Council's constitution Sacrosanctum Concilium (1963) initiated a major reform of the Latin liturgy.

Mainline Protestant churches (Lutheran, Anglican, Methodist, Reformed) retain structured liturgies derived from Western tradition, while many evangelical and Pentecostal churches use freer, less formal orders of worship, though scholars still describe these as liturgies in the broader sense — any community has some customary pattern of worship.

Other religions

Comparable patterns of prescribed public worship exist in Judaism (the synagogue service and prayer cycle), Islam (the ritual prayer, ṣalāh, with its prescribed words and movements, though Islam has no priesthood mediating the rite), Buddhism and Hinduism (puja and other temple rituals), and other traditions.

Liturgics

The academic study of liturgy is called liturgics or liturgical studies. Scholars in this field reconstruct the historical development of rites from sources such as early church orders (e.g. the Didache and the Apostolic Tradition), sacramentaries and ordines, and compare the structures of Eastern and Western worship.
