Hyperkinesia

Hyperkinesia refers to an increase in muscular activity that can result in excessive abnormal movements, excessive normal movements, or a combination of both. It is the opposite of hypokinesia and is a core feature of many hyperkinetic movement disorders.

Forms

Hyperkinetic movements include chorea (brief, irregular, dance-like movements), dystonia (sustained or intermittent muscle contractions causing abnormal postures), tremor, myoclonus, tics, athetosis and ballismus. Conditions characterised by hyperkinesia include Huntington's disease, Sydenham's chorea, tardive dyskinesia caused by long-term dopamine-blocking drugs, and Tourette syndrome.

Mechanism

Hyperkinetic disorders are generally attributed to dysfunction of the basal ganglia and their motor circuits, in particular reduced inhibitory output that disinhibits thalamocortical motor pathways. Excess dopaminergic signalling is implicated in several forms, which is why dopamine-receptor blockers can suppress chorea, while dopaminergic treatment of Parkinson's disease can itself provoke hyperkinetic dyskinesias.

Usage of the term

The word has also historically been used in psychiatry: "hyperkinetic disorder" was the ICD term roughly corresponding to attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), in which motor overactivity is a prominent symptom.
