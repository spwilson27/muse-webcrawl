Jāņi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Observed byLatvians, in Latvia and worldwide
Date23–24 June (Līgo Day and Jāņi Day)
TypeMidsummer festival; public holidays
CustomsBonfires, wreaths, singing, cheese and beer, searching for the fern flower

Jāņi is the Latvian midsummer festival, the most important and beloved celebration of the Latvian calendar, held on the night of 23–24 June, the summer solstice. Rooted in pre-Christian celebrations of the sun and fertility, it is marked by bonfires, singing, wreaths of oak leaves and wild flowers, special cheese and beer, and an all-night effort to stay awake until sunrise.

Celebration

The festivities begin on 23 June, Līgo Day, whose name comes from the refrain līgo sung in the festival's folk songs. Families and friends leave the cities for the countryside, where men named Jānis (the most common Latvian male name) are honoured as guests of the day and crowned with oak-leaf wreaths, while women wear wreaths of flowers. Bonfires are lit on hilltops and barrels of pitch raised on poles; a traditional belief holds that the fire's light blesses the fields within its glow. According to custom, celebrants should not sleep before sunrise, and couples slip away into the forest to search for the mythical flowering fern, said to bloom only on this night.

Food and song

The festival table features Jāņi cheese — a round, yellow caraway cheese symbolising the sun — and beer. Thousands of folk songs (dainas) are associated with Jāņi, more than for any other occasion, and singing around the fire continues through the short northern night.

History

Midsummer celebrations in the region are described in chronicles of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries and survived the Soviet period, when the festival was officially discouraged but widely celebrated in private as an expression of national identity. Today 23 and 24 June are public holidays in Latvia, and Jāņi is celebrated by the Latvian diaspora around the world.
