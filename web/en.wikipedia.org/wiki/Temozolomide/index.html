Temozolomide

Temozolomide (TMZ), sold under the brand name Temodar among others, is an oral alkylating agent used to treat certain brain cancers, most notably glioblastoma and anaplastic astrocytoma. It is a prodrug that spontaneously converts in the body to the active compound MTIC, which methylates DNA and thereby kills tumor cells.

Medical use

Temozolomide is the standard chemotherapy for glioblastoma, given concurrently with radiotherapy and then as adjuvant cycles — the regimen established by the 2005 Stupp trial, which improved median survival. It is also used for refractory anaplastic astrocytoma and, off-label, for some other tumors. Benefit is greatest in tumors with a methylated MGMT promoter, since the MGMT enzyme repairs the drug's DNA damage.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include nausea, vomiting, fatigue, constipation, and suppression of blood counts (myelosuppression), particularly low platelets and lymphocytes; prophylaxis against Pneumocystis pneumonia may be given during concurrent chemoradiation.
