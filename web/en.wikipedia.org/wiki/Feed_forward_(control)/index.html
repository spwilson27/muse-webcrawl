Feed forward (control)

A feed forward (sometimes written feedforward) is an element or pathway within a control system that passes a controlling signal from a source in its external environment to a load elsewhere in its external environment. In control engineering, feedforward control acts on a measured disturbance — or on the desired setpoint itself — before the disturbance affects the controlled variable, in contrast to feedback control, which reacts to the resulting output error.

Principle

Because feedforward does not wait for an error to develop, it can in principle cancel the effect of a disturbance entirely if the process model is accurate. A classic example is heating a building: a feedforward controller measuring outside temperature opens the heating valve in advance, while a feedback thermostat corrects residual error. In practice feedforward cannot stand alone — it requires an accurate model, cannot correct for unmeasured disturbances or drift, and cannot make an unstable process stable — so it is usually combined with feedback, as in cascade and ratio control schemes in process industries, and in motion systems where feedforward of the reference trajectory improves tracking.

Other uses of the term

The concept is applied beyond engineering: in physiology, anticipatory responses such as salivation before eating are described as feedforward regulation; in management and learning theory, "feedforward" interviews focus on future behaviour rather than past mistakes; and in computing, feedforward neural networks are those in which information flows only in one direction, without cycles.
