Balochi (بلوچی, Balòči) is a Northwestern Iranian language spoken primarily by the Baloch people in Balochistan, a region spanning southwestern Pakistan (the province of Balochistan), southeastern Iran (Sistan and Baluchestan province), and southern Afghanistan, with diaspora communities in Oman, the Gulf states, Turkmenistan and elsewhere. It belongs to the Western Iranian subgroup of the Iranian branch of the Indo-Iranian languages.

Dialects

Balochi is usually divided into two main dialect groups: Western Balochi (including the Rakhshani and Makrani varieties) and Eastern Balochi (including Sulaimani). Some classifications separate a third, Southern, group. Koroshi, spoken in Iran's Fars province, is a distinct Balochi variety. Mutual intelligibility between the main groups is limited, and the variation has complicated efforts at standardisation.

Writing system

Balochi is written in a modified Perso-Arabic script. A standardised orthography, often called the Balochi Standard Alphabet, was developed in the late twentieth century, notably through the work of the Balochi Academy in Quetta and the linguist Gul Khan Nasir, and was further formalised at a 2012 conference in Uppsala, Sweden. Older literature also exists in other conventions, and some activists promote a Latin-based alphabet.

Phonology and grammar

Balochi distinguishes retroflex consonants under the influence of neighbouring Indo-Aryan languages. The grammar retains a split-ergative alignment in past-tense transitive clauses in many dialects, a feature inherited from Middle Iranian. The language has a rich oral literature of epic poetry, ballads and folk tales; classical poets include figures associated with the Mir Chakar Rind cycle of epics.

Status

In Pakistan, Balochi is taught in some schools and used in regional media and literature, though Urdu and English dominate formal education. In Iran, its public use is more restricted. The language is considered vulnerable in some areas, but it remains the everyday language of millions.
