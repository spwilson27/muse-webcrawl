Golden Circle (Iceland)

The Golden Circle (Icelandic: Gullni hringurinn) is a popular tourist route in southern Iceland, looping from Reykjavík into the southern uplands and back, roughly 300 kilometres (190 mi) in length. Its three principal stops are Þingvellir National Park, the waterfall Gullfoss, and the geothermal area in Haukadalur containing the geysers Geysir and Strokkur.

Main attractions

Þingvellir

Þingvellir is a site of historical, cultural and geological significance and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is where the Althing, the national parliament of Iceland, was founded in 930 AD and met until 1798, making it among the world's oldest parliamentary sites. The park lies in a rift valley on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, where the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates are pulling apart; visitors can walk in the Almannagjá gorge and snorkel or dive in the Silfra fissure.

Geysir and Strokkur

The Haukadalur geothermal area is home to the Great Geysir, the first geyser described in print and the source of the English word "geyser"; it is now largely dormant. Its neighbour Strokkur erupts reliably every 5–10 minutes, throwing boiling water 15–20 metres (sometimes up to 40 metres) into the air.

Gullfoss

Gullfoss ("Golden Waterfall") is a two-tiered waterfall on the Hvítá river, dropping a total of 32 metres into a rugged canyon. In the early twentieth century it was threatened by a hydroelectric scheme; the landowner's daughter, Sigríður Tómasdóttir, campaigned to save it, and the falls are now protected as a nature reserve.

Other stops

Many tours add the volcanic crater lake Kerið, the Faxi waterfall, the greenhouse village of Flúðir and its Secret Lagoon, and the town of Hveragerði. The route is Iceland's most popular day trip from the capital, and is often compared to Norway's "Golden Triangle"-type touring circuits.
