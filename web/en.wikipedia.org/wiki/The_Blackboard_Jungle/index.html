The Blackboard Jungle is a 1954 novel by Evan Hunter about Richard Dadier, an idealistic new teacher at an inner-city vocational high school in New York City who struggles to reach a class of hostile, delinquent students. Hunter drew on his own brief experience teaching in a Bronx vocational school.

Film adaptation

The 1955 film adaptation, titled Blackboard Jungle and directed by Richard Brooks, starred Glenn Ford as Dadier and featured Sidney Poitier in an early major role as the student Gregory Miller. The film was a landmark treatment of juvenile delinquency and is famous for its use of Bill Haley & His Comets' "Rock Around the Clock" over the opening credits, which helped propel rock and roll into the mainstream and made the song a number-one hit.

Themes

The work addresses postwar anxieties about youth crime, race, and the failures of urban education, and it influenced a genre of "troubled classroom" dramas.
