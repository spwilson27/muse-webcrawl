Patrouille de France


Active: 1931; formalised 1953 – present
Country: France
Branch: French Air and Space Force
Role: Aerobatic display team
Base: Salon-de-Provence Air Base
Aircraft: Dassault/Dornier Alpha Jet

The Patrouille de France, officially the Patrouille Acrobatique de France, is the precision aerobatic demonstration team of the French Air and Space Force and the oldest such team still active, with origins in 1931. It is often described, along with the British Red Arrows and the American Blue Angels and Thunderbirds, as one of the world's foremost jet display teams.

History

The first French aerobatic patrol was formed at Étampes in 1931 by instructor pilots. After the Second World War, demonstration teams were formed by several squadrons; in 1953, during a display at Algiers, commentator terminology stabilised the team under the name "Patrouille de France," and it became the Air Force's official display unit. The team has flown the Republic F-84G, the Dassault Ouragan and Mystère IV, the Fouga Magister, and, since 1981, the Alpha Jet.

Displays

The team flies eight Alpha Jets painted in blue, white, and red, with smoke in the colours of the tricolour. Its roughly twenty-minute programme combines tight formation manoeuvres with crossing and solo sequences. The team is a fixture of the Bastille Day flypast over the Champs-Élysées in Paris and performs at airshows throughout France and abroad, serving as an ambassador for the French armed forces.

Organisation

Based at Salon-de-Provence, the unit comprises nine display pilots (eight in formation plus a solo/rhythm pilot), mechanics, and support staff. Pilots are experienced fighter pilots who serve tours of about three years; the leader (known as the leader) and the solo display pilot (Athos call signs) hold numbered positions within the formation.
