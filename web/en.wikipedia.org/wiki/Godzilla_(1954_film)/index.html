Godzilla (Japanese: ゴジラ, Hepburn: Gojira) is a 1954 Japanese kaiju film directed by Ishirō Honda, with special effects by Eiji Tsuburaya. Produced by Toho, it stars Akira Takarada, Momoko Kōchi, Akihiko Hirata, and Takashi Shimura. The film depicts a giant prehistoric monster, awakened and empowered by hydrogen bomb testing, laying waste to Tokyo — a somber metaphor for nuclear weapons and the trauma of a Japan only nine years from Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

Plot

After a series of mysterious ship sinkings, an expedition to Odo Island encounters Godzilla, a legendary sea monster stirred from the depths by American H-bomb tests. Godzilla attacks Tokyo in nightmarish night raids that leave the city a firestorm. Paleontologist Kyohei Yamane urges study rather than destruction, while scientist Daisuke Serizawa secretly possesses a superweapon, the Oxygen Destroyer. Tormented by the arms race his invention could unleash, Serizawa uses it once — burning his research notes and descending with the device himself, dying as Godzilla disintegrates. Yamane warns that if nuclear testing continues, another Godzilla may appear.

Production

Producer Tomoyuki Tanaka conceived the film after a co-production with Indonesia fell through; the March 1954 irradiation of the fishing boat Lucky Dragon No. 5 by the Castle Bravo test gave the project urgency. Tsuburaya's team pioneered "suitmation": actor Haruo Nakajima performed Godzilla in a heavy latex suit amid exquisitely detailed miniatures filmed at high speed. Akira Ifukube's score, built from contrabass strings rubbed with a resin-coated glove, gave the monster its iconic roar's musical counterpart.

Release and legacy

Released on November 3, 1954, the film was a major hit and was re-edited with Raymond Burr into Godzilla, King of the Monsters! (1956) for American audiences. The original Japanese cut was long unseen in the West and is now acclaimed as a classic of world cinema. It launched the Godzilla franchise — the longest-running film series in history — and effectively created the kaiju genre.
