Kill Rock Stars is an independent record label founded in 1991 by Slim Moon in Olympia, Washington. One of the most influential American independent labels of the 1990s, it became closely associated with the riot grrrl movement and the Pacific Northwest underground, releasing early recordings by Bikini Kill, Sleater-Kinney, Elliott Smith, and the Decemberists, alongside influential spoken-word records.

History

Slim Moon founded Kill Rock Stars in Olympia, Washington, in 1991. The label's name was intended as a provocation. Its first release was a split 7-inch single by the Olympia bands Kill Sybil and Steve and the Jerks. Moon, who was also involved with the Olympia fanzine and music scene around Evergreen State College and the label K Records, built the label around a do-it-yourself ethos and an openness to spoken-word performance pieces.

The label quickly became a central document of the riot grrrl movement, releasing records by Bikini Kill, Huggy Bear, and Bratmobile, as well as the compilation albums Kill Rock Stars (1991), Stars Kill Rock, and Rock Stars Kill, which gathered many of the key bands of the Olympia and Portland underground. In 1994 the label released the debut album of Sleater-Kinney and, in 1995, Elliott Smith's self-titled second solo album, followed by Either/Or (1997), which brought the label wider critical attention.

In 1997, Moon turned day-to-day operations over to his wife, Tina Agee, and later Maggie Vail became a co-owner. Moon left the label in 2006 to take an A&R position at Nonesuch Records. The label relocated much of its operation to Portland, Oregon, and continued releasing records by artists including The Decemberists, Deerhoof, Xiu Xiu, and comedian and musician acts on its comedy imprint.

Spoken word releases

Unusual among rock labels, Kill Rock Stars maintained a substantial spoken-word catalogue, releasing albums by performers such as Juliana Luecking, Kathy Acker, and Miranda July, whose The Binet-Simon Test Division appeared on the label. Moon regarded spoken word as continuous with the label's punk ethos.
