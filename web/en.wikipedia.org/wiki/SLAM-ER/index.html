SLAM-ER

AGM-84H/K SLAM-ER
Type: Air-launched cruise missile
Manufacturer: Boeing
Range: ~270 km
Guidance: INS/GPS, imaging infrared, data link
Warhead: ~360 kg blast-fragmentation/penetrator

The AGM-84H/K SLAM-ER (Standoff Land Attack Missile – Expanded Response) is an American precision air-launched cruise missile developed by Boeing for the United States Navy. Derived from the AGM-84 Harpoon anti-ship missile via the earlier SLAM, the SLAM-ER entered service in 2000 and can strike land and sea targets at ranges of about 270 kilometres.

Design

The SLAM-ER adds folding planar wings, a new warhead and improved guidance to its predecessors. It navigates by inertial navigation and GPS, and its imaging infrared seeker, combined with a two-way data link, allows a "man-in-the-loop" terminal phase in which the operator can select or refine the aimpoint — including aiming at a specific part of a target — or abort the attack. An automatic target acquisition mode enables fully autonomous engagements.

Operators

Primary launch aircraft include the F/A-18 Hornet and Super Hornet and the P-3/P-8 patrol aircraft. Export users have included South Korea, Turkey, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates.
