Game jam

A game jam is a contest or hackathon where participants try to make a video game from scratch within a short period, usually from 24 to 72 hours. Participants may work individually or in teams and are often given a theme, revealed at the start of the event, to incorporate into their games. Game jams are intended to promote creativity, experimentation and rapid prototyping, rather than polished commercial products.

History

The concept grew out of demoscene and hackathon culture in the early 2000s. Ludum Dare, founded in 2002, became one of the longest-running online game jams, holding regular 48-hour solo competitions and 72-hour team jams. The Global Game Jam, first held in 2009, organises thousands of participants at physical sites around the world working simultaneously over a single weekend; it has set records as the largest game jam event, with tens of thousands of participants and thousands of games produced each year.

Format

At the start of a jam, a theme is announced — past themes have included single words or phrases such as "waves", "transmission" and "lost and found". Participants brainstorm ideas, form teams (at physical jams, often with strangers), and develop a playable game before the deadline. At the end, games are uploaded, demonstrated and, in competitive jams, judged by peers or a panel, with categories such as innovation, fun, theme interpretation, graphics and audio.

Rules vary: some jams require all assets to be created during the event, while others allow pre-made libraries, code and art. Online jams hosted on platforms such as itch.io run with themes and durations ranging from a few hours to several weeks.

Impact

Game jams serve as training grounds for students and hobbyists and as creative exercises for professionals. Several commercially successful games began as jam prototypes, including Superhot, which originated in a 2013 game jam, and Celeste, whose first version was made in four days for a jam before being expanded into a full release. Jams are also used for educational and social purposes, such as "serious game" jams addressing public health or climate themes.
