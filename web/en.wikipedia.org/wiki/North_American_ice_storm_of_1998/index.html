North American ice storm of 1998

The North American ice storm of 1998 (also known as the Great Ice Storm of 1998) was a massive combination of five successive ice storms in January 1998 that devastated eastern Canada and the northeastern United States. Freezing rain accumulated up to 100 mm in places, collapsing power lines and towers across Ontario, Quebec, New Brunswick, New York, and New England.

Ice storm of 1998
Dates: 4–10 January 1998
Areas: Eastern Canada, northeastern US
Deaths: ~35
Damage: billions of dollars

Impact

More than four million people lost electricity, some for weeks in mid-winter; Montreal came close to a complete blackout when transmission towers feeding the city failed. The Canadian Armed Forces mounted Operation Recuperation, their largest domestic deployment, with over 15,000 personnel assisting recovery. Around 35 people died, many from hypothermia and carbon-monoxide poisoning from improvised heating.

Aftermath

The storm prompted major investment in grid hardening and de-icing technology in Quebec and Ontario, and remains the benchmark natural disaster in eastern Canadian history, with insured losses in the billions of dollars.
