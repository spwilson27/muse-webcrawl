Asana
Sanskrit: आसन (āsana)
Meaning: "seat", posture
Tradition: Yoga

An asana is a body posture, originally and still a general term for a sitting meditation pose, and later extended in hatha yoga and modern yoga as exercise to any type of position, adding reclining, standing, inverted, twisting, and balancing poses. In the Yoga Sutras, Patanjali defines "asana" as "to be seated in a position that is steady but relaxed". Patanjali mentions the ability to sit for extended periods as one of the eight limbs of his system.

History

The earliest reference to asana is found in the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali, dating to around the 2nd century BCE to the 4th century CE. The goraksha sataka and the Hatha Yoga Pradipika (15th century) identify 84 asanas; the Hatha Ratnavali (17th century) provides a different list of 84.

Etymology

Asana is a Sanskrit word meaning "seat", and more broadly "posture". The term derives from the root ās, "to sit or remain".

Cultural significance

Yoga as exercise has spread across the world. Asanas feature prominently in the visual culture of Buddhism and Jainism as well, with the meditating figure seated in lotus position being an iconic image.
