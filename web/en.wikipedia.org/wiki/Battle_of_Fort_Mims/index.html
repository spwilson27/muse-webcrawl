The Battle of Fort Mims, also known as the Fort Mims massacre, occurred on 30 August 1813 during the Creek War, when Red Stick Creek warriors attacked and destroyed Fort Mims in the Mississippi Territory, killing most of its defenders and the settlers sheltering inside.

Background

The Creek Nation was divided by civil war between the traditionalist Red Sticks and those favouring accommodation with the United States. After the Battle of Burnt Corn, settlers fortified the home of Samuel Mims. Major Daniel Beasley commanded the garrison but left the gate open despite warnings of attack.

The attack

At midday on 30 August, some 700–1,000 Red Sticks under William Weatherford rushed the open gate. The fort was overwhelmed and burned; estimates of those killed range from 250 to over 500, including many women and children, Métis settlers and enslaved people.

Aftermath

News of the massacre provoked a major U.S. response. Militia armies from Tennessee, Georgia and Mississippi invaded Creek country, culminating in Andrew Jackson's victory at Horseshoe Bend in March 1814.
