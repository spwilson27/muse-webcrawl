Brazil nut effect

The Brazil nut effect is a phenomenon in which, in a container of mixed nuts or other granular material subjected to shaking, the larger particles tend to rise to the surface. The name comes from the observation that in mixed-nut packages, the large Brazil nuts are typically found at the top. The phenomenon is also known as granular convection, and the reverse effect—in which large particles sink—is called the reverse Brazil nut effect.

Mechanisms

Proposed mechanisms include percolation, in which small particles fall into gaps beneath large ones during each shake; convection, in which grains circulate upward in the center and downward at the walls, carrying large particles up but unable to bring them down; and condensation effects related to particle density and the presence of air. The outcome depends on particle sizes, densities, container shape, and shaking intensity.

Applications

The effect matters in industries handling granular materials, from pharmaceuticals to mining and agriculture, where unwanted segregation of mixtures must be controlled. It has also been observed in asteroid science, with large boulders found on the surfaces of rubble-pile asteroids.
