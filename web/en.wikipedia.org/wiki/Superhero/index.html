Superhero

A superhero or superheroine is a stock character who typically possesses superpowers or abilities beyond those of ordinary people, fits the role of a hero, and usually dedicates themselves to fighting crime and protecting the public. Superhero fiction is the genre centred on such characters, and it has dominated American comic books and, in the 21st century, global cinema.

Common traits

Superheroes commonly display most or all of the following: extraordinary powers or equipment; a strong moral code and willingness to risk their own safety for the greater good; a motivation rooted in a defining event such as the death of a loved one or a sense of responsibility; a distinctive costume, often with a secret identity; a cast of supporting characters and recurring villains; and an independent headquarters or base. Many have a weakness — such as Superman's kryptonite — that balances their powers.

History

The genre drew on pulp-magazine and comic-strip figures such as Zorro, the Shadow, and Doc Savage, and on mythic strongmen like Hercules. The modern superhero era is conventionally dated to the debut of Superman in Action Comics #1 (1938), followed by Batman (1939), Captain Marvel, Wonder Woman, and Captain America during the Golden Age of Comic Books. After a post-war decline, the Silver Age (from 1956) revived the genre with the Flash, Green Lantern, and Marvel Comics' Fantastic Four, Spider-Man, and X-Men, who brought flawed, relatable personalities to the form. Later eras deconstructed the genre in works such as Watchmen (1986–87).

In film and television

Superhero films became the dominant commercial force in world cinema in the 2000s and 2010s, led by the Marvel Cinematic Universe and DC adaptations, alongside long-running television series and animation.

International variants

Superhero traditions exist worldwide, including Japanese manga and anime heroes, the Indian and Filipino comic traditions, and tokusatsu characters such as Ultraman and Kamen Rider.
