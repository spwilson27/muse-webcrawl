Privy Council

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Privy Council, formally His Majesty's Most Honourable Privy Council, is a formal body of advisers to the British sovereign, descended from the council of the medieval kings of England. Its membership — several hundred privy counsellors, styled “The Right Honourable” — consists mainly of senior politicians (cabinet ministers of the present and past, opposition leaders), senior judges, archbishops and Commonwealth figures, appointed for life.

The Council as a whole meets rarely (on a sovereign's accession or royal marriage announcement); its working business is done by committees and by ministers through Orders in Council — executive legislation made by the sovereign on ministerial advice (proclamations, prerogative orders, and statutory instruments where an act so provides). Its Judicial Committee (established 1833) served as the final court of appeal for the British Empire and remains the court of last resort for some Commonwealth realms and territories, sitting mainly in London. Equivalent councils exist or existed across the Commonwealth (the King's Privy Council for Canada, and the defunct Executive Council model in Australasia), and the title “Privy Counsellor” persists as a mark of senior statesmanship in several realms.
