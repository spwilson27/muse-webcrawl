The Basilica of the Sacred Heart of Paris (French: Basilique du Sacré-Cœur de Montmartre), commonly known as Sacré-Cœur, is a Roman Catholic minor basilica dedicated to the Sacred Heart of Jesus, situated at the summit of the butte Montmartre, the highest point in Paris. Its white domes are one of the most recognisable landmarks of the city and the basilica is one of France's most visited religious sites.

History

The basilica was proposed in the aftermath of France's defeat in the Franco-Prussian War and the Paris Commune of 1871, as a national vow of penance and devotion; the National Assembly authorised its construction in 1873. The site on Montmartre — traditionally the place of the martyrdom of Saint Denis and also where the Commune began — gave the project a deliberately symbolic and controversial character. Building work began in 1875 to designs by Paul Abadie in a Romano-Byzantine style. The church was substantially complete by 1914, but consecration was delayed by the First World War until 1919.

Architecture

Sacré-Cœur is built of travertine stone from Château-Landon, which exudes calcite and remains strikingly white even in Parisian pollution. Its great central dome rises 83 metres, flanked by smaller domes and a tall bell tower housing the Savoyarde, one of the heaviest bells in the world. Inside, the apse mosaic of Christ in Majesty — Christ resurrected, clothed in white and with arms outstretched — is among the largest mosaics in the world. Since 1885 the basilica has maintained perpetual adoration of the Blessed Sacrament, day and night. From the parvis and the dome there are panoramic views over Paris.
