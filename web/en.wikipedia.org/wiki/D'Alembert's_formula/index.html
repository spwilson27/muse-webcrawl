D'Alembert's formula, named after the French mathematician Jean le Rond d'Alembert, is the general solution to the one-dimensional homogeneous wave equation with initial conditions. It was among the first explicit solutions obtained for a partial differential equation, published in 1747 in d'Alembert's work on vibrating strings.

Statement

For the wave equation utt − c²uxx = 0 on the whole real line, with initial displacement u(x, 0) = φ(x) and initial velocity ut(x, 0) = ψ(x), the solution is:

u(x, t) = ½[φ(x − ct) + φ(x + ct)] + (1/2c) ∫x−ctx+ct ψ(s) ds

Interpretation

The formula shows that the displacement at (x, t) is the average of the initial displacement at the two points x − ct and x + ct — the ends of the interval of dependence — plus the accumulated effect of the initial velocity over that interval. The initial disturbance splits into two waves, each of half amplitude, travelling left and right at speed c without changing shape.

Derivation

Using the characteristic coordinates ξ = x − ct and η = x + ct, the wave equation factorises and reduces to uξη = 0, whose general solution is u = F(x − ct) + G(x + ct) for arbitrary functions F and G. Matching the initial conditions determines F and G and yields the formula.
