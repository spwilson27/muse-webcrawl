Swing bridge

A swing bridge is a movable bridge that has as its primary structural support a vertical locating pin and support ring, usually at or near its centre of balance, about which the bridge span pivots horizontally to open a navigation channel for river traffic.

Swing bridge
TypeMovable bridge
MotionHorizontal rotation about a vertical axis
AdvantagesWide navigation opening, no counterweights raised

Design

In a centre-bearing design the whole span rests on a circular drum or rollers on a pivot pier. Rim-bearing designs support the span at its ends. When closed, wedges lock the span in line with the fixed roadway; to open, the locks retract and motors rotate the span, often by 90 degrees.

History and use

Swing bridges were common on navigable rivers and canals in the 19th and early 20th centuries, especially for railway crossings, because they need no tall towers or raised counterweights. Drawbacks include the wide pivot pier obstructing the channel and the time required to open and close.
