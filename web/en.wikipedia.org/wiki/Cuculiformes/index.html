Cuculiformes

OrderCuculiformes
FamilyCuculidae
Species~150

Cuculiformes is the bird order containing the cuckoos and their relatives, with a single living family, Cuculidae. The order comprises roughly 150 species in more than 30 genera, distributed on every continent except Antarctica.

Taxonomy

Members of the order share zygodactyl feet and other anatomical features. Some classifications have placed the turacos (Musophagidae) and the hoatzin within or near Cuculiformes, though modern molecular studies generally place turacos in their own order, Musophagiformes. The cuckoo family is divided into subfamilies including the parasitic true cuckoos (Cuculinae), the ground cuckoos and roadrunners (Neomorphinae), coucals (Centropodinae), couas (Couinae) and anis (Crotophaginae).

Biology

The order is notable for the evolution of obligate brood parasitism in many Old World species, contrasting with the cooperative breeding of anis and the self-nesting coucals.
