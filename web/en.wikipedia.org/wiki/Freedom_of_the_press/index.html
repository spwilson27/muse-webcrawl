Freedom of the press

[Image: A printing press of the early newspaper era]

Freedom of the press is the principle that media should be free from government censorship and control, protected in many democracies as part of freedom of expression — in the First Amendment to the US Constitution, Article 10 of the European Convention, and Article 19 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

History

Milton's Areopagitica (1644) argued against licensing; the Zenger trial (1735) established truth as a defence in colonial America; Sweden's 1766 law was the first freedom-of-the-press act. The 20th century added broadcasting regulation and, later, platform governance questions.

Measurement

Reporters Without Borders' World Press Freedom Index and Freedom House rankings track conditions; in much of the world journalists face violence, imprisonment and 'lawfare', and even liberal democracies debate surveillance of reporters and ownership concentration.
