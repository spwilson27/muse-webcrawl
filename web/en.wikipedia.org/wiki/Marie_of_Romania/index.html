Marie of Romania (born Princess Marie of Edinburgh; 29 October 1875 – 18 July 1938) was the last queen consort of Romania, as wife of King Ferdinand I. Born into the British royal family, the daughter of Prince Alfred, Duke of Edinburgh and granddaughter of both Queen Victoria and Tsar Alexander II, she married Crown Prince Ferdinand in 1893.

Reign

On Romania's entry into the First World War in 1916 Marie became a national symbol: she nursed wounded soldiers, toured the front and, after the war, represented Romania at the Paris Peace Conference of 1919, pressing the case for a Greater Romania. In 1922 she and Ferdinand were crowned at Alba Iulia as sovereigns of the enlarged kingdom.

Later life

Widowed in 1927, Marie was sidelined during the minority of her grandson Michael and the return of her son Carol II. A celebrated author and gardener, she built Balchik Palace on the Black Sea coast. She died in 1938 and was buried at Curtea de Argeș.
