Nanomedicine

Nanomedicine is the medical application of nanotechnology, involving devices and materials at the nanometre scale (roughly 1–100 nm) for prevention, diagnosis, monitoring and treatment of disease. It encompasses targeted drug delivery, imaging contrast agents, regenerative scaffolds, nanobiosensors and theranostics.

FieldMedicine, nanotechnology
Scalec. 1–100 nm

Applications

Drug delivery: Lipid nanoparticles deliver mRNA (as in COVID-19 vaccines), and formulations such as liposomal doxorubicin (Doxil) and albumin-bound paclitaxel (Abraxane) reduce toxicity. Targeting ligands direct carriers to tumours or specific cells.
Diagnostics: gold nanoparticles and quantum dots enhance imaging and rapid tests (e.g. lateral flow assays).
Regenerative medicine: nanofibrous scaffolds for tissue engineering.
Theranostics combines therapy and diagnostics in one platform, e.g. radiolabelled nanoparticles.

Challenges

Nanoparticle toxicity, long-term accumulation (especially in liver and spleen), immune activation, manufacturing reproducibility, regulatory pathways and cost are active concerns. Behaviour in the body depends on size, shape, surface chemistry and protein corona formation.
