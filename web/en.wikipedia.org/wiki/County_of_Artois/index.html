County of Artois

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The County of Artois (French: comté d'Artois) was a historic fief in northern France, centred on the city of Arras and roughly corresponding to the modern department of Pas-de-Calais. It belonged for much of its history to the counts of Flanders and their heirs before becoming attached to the French crown and, later, passing into the Burgundian and Habsburg orbits.

History

Artois was separated from Flanders in 1191 when it was given as a dowry to the French crown prince Louis (later Louis VIII), and in 1237 Louis IX granted it as an appanage to his younger brother Robert, founder of the Capetian house of Artois. Through inheritance it passed to the dukes of Burgundy and then to the Spanish Habsburgs. France recovered Artois by the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659), confirmed in 1678, and the title Count of Artois was later famously borne by the future King Charles X.
