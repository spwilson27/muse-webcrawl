Drusus

Drusus was a cognomen borne by several prominent members of the Roman Claudian and Livian families. The most notable bearers include Nero Claudius Drusus, the younger brother of the emperor Tiberius, and Drusus Julius Caesar, Tiberius's son.

Nero Claudius Drusus

Nero Claudius Drusus (38–9 BC), often called Drusus the Elder, was a successful general who campaigned extensively in Germania, reaching the Elbe and earning triumphal honours. He died from injuries sustained during a campaign in 9 BC, and his sons were the emperor Claudius and the general Germanicus.

Drusus Julius Caesar

Drusus Julius Caesar (c. 8 BC – AD 23), Tiberius's only son by Vipsania Agrippina, held the consulship and governed Illyricum. His sudden death in AD 23 — later attributed by some sources to poisoning arranged by Sejanus and his wife Livilla — removed Tiberius's heir and deepened suspicions at court.

Other bearers

The name also appears for Drusus Caesar, son of Germanicus, and in the earlier gens Livia, beginning with Marcus Livius Drusus, a tribune of the plebs in the late Roman Republic.
