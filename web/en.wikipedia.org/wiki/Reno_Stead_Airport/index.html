Reno Stead Airport (IATA: RTS, ICAO: KRTS, FAA LID: RTS) is a large public-use general aviation airport located in the North Valleys area, 10 nautical miles (19 km) northwest of the central business district of Reno, in Washoe County, Nevada, United States. It is owned by the Reno-Tahoe Airport Authority.

The airport originated as an Army airfield during the Second World War era and is named after Croston Stead, a Reno rancher and aviation enthusiast. It serves as a reliever airport for Reno–Tahoe International Airport and hosts flight training, aircraft maintenance, and aerial firefighting operations.

National Championship Air Races

Reno Stead Airport was best known as the long-time home of the National Championship Air Races, held annually in September from 1964 onward. The event drew hundreds of thousands of spectators to watch piston-engine and jet aircraft race around pylon courses. It was the site of a catastrophic accident in 2011, when a modified P-51 Mustang crashed into spectators, killing the pilot and ten people on the ground. Following the 2023 event, organizers announced the races would move from Reno, citing growth and development pressures around the airport.

Facilities

The airport covers a large area in the high desert north of Reno and has two paved runways, the longest of which extends to about 9,000 feet (2,743 m), suitable for a wide range of general aviation and military aircraft.
