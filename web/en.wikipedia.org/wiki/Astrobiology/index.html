Astrobiology is a scientific field within the life and environmental sciences that studies the origins, early evolution, distribution, and future of life in the universe by investigating its deterministic conditions. It combines aspects of astronomy, biology, and geology, and considers the possibility of life on other planets and moons, including the search for biosignatures and the study of extremophiles on Earth as analogues for extraterrestrial environments.

Overview

Astrobiology addresses three fundamental questions: how life begins and evolves, whether life exists elsewhere in the universe, and what the future of life on Earth and beyond will be. The field draws on molecular biology, biochemistry, biophysics, ecology, planetary science, geography, and geology to investigate the possibility of life on other worlds and recognise biospheres that might differ from that on Earth.

The term was first used in print in the 1930s and 1940s, but the field gained institutional form with the founding of the exobiology programme at NASA in 1960 and the Viking programme landers, which performed the first experiments specifically designed to search for life on Mars in 1976.

Life in the Solar System

Planetary bodies considered plausible hosts for life, past or present, include Mars, Jupiter's moon Europa, and Saturn's moons Enceladus and Titan. Subsurface oceans beneath icy crusts, hydrothermal vents, and transient brines are among the environments investigated. The discovery of extreme-environment organisms on Earth — such as those thriving in deep-sea vents, acidic rivers, and Antarctic dry valleys — expanded the known limits of habitability.

Methods

Astrobiologists use telescopes to characterise exoplanet atmospheres and search for spectral biosignatures such as oxygen, methane, and water vapour; spacecraft missions to sample and analyse planetary surfaces; laboratory experiments simulating early-Earth and extraterrestrial conditions; and field studies of analogue environments. The Drake equation frames estimates of the number of communicative extraterrestrial civilisations.
