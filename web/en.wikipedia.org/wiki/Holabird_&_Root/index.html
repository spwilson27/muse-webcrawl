Holabird & Root

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Holabird & Root is a Chicago-based architecture firm founded in 1880 as Holabird & Roche by William Holabird and Martin Roche, becoming Holabird & Root in 1927 when John Wellborn Root Jr. and John Augur Holabird — sons of the original partners — took over. It is one of the oldest continuously operating architecture firms in the United States and a central institution of the Chicago School of architecture.

History and works

As Holabird & Roche, the firm designed early steel-frame skyscrapers including the Tacoma Building (1889) and the Marquette Building (1895), helping to pioneer skeleton-frame construction after the Great Chicago Fire. Renamed Holabird & Root in 1927, the firm produced celebrated Art Deco landmarks such as the Chicago Board of Trade Building (1930, long the city's tallest), the Palmolive Building (1929), the Chicago Daily News Building (1929), and 333 North Michigan Avenue. Its Soldier Field renovation and the North Dakota State Capitol are among its other major works.

Today

The modern firm practices architecture, engineering, and interior design from Chicago, with projects in higher education, science, government, and corporate work across the United States and abroad.
