Lao people

The Lao people are a Tai ethnic group native to Southeast Asia, who speak the Lao language. The majority live in Laos, where they constitute roughly 53.2% of the population (about 3.6 million people), and a large Lao population also lives in northeastern Thailand (Isan), though many there identify as Isan rather than Lao.

Culture and religion

Lao culture is strongly shaped by Theravada Buddhism, which arrived in the region by the 14th–16th centuries; most Lao are practising Buddhists, and the temple (wat) is the centre of village life. Important festivals include Pi Mai (Lao New Year) and Boun Bang Fai (the rocket festival). The traditional Lao skirt, the sinh, and the sticky rice staple are prominent markers of Lao identity.
