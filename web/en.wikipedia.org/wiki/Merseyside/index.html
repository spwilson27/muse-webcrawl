Merseyside is a ceremonial and metropolitan county in North West England. It was created on 1 April 1974 by the Local Government Act 1972 and takes its name from the River Mersey. The county covers 249 square miles (645 km²), making it one of England's smallest counties by area, and it is divided into five metropolitan boroughs.

It borders Lancashire to the north, Greater Manchester to the east, and Cheshire to the south. Its western boundary is the Irish Sea coastline. Liverpool is the county town and largest city. Other major settlements include Birkenhead, Bootle, St Helens, Southport, Wallasey, Huyton, Kirkby and Crosby.

History

The area of Merseyside was historically split between Lancashire (north of the Mersey) and Cheshire (the Wirral peninsula, south of the Mersey). The metropolitan county was formed from parts of both counties in 1974. Merseyside County Council was abolished in 1986 under the Thatcher government, and its functions were devolved to the individual boroughs or to joint boards. The county nonetheless retained its structure as a ceremonial county.

In 2014 the boroughs were brought together as the Liverpool City Region Combined Authority, and in 2017 Steve Rotheram was elected the first Metro Mayor of the Liverpool City Region.

Geography

Merseyside is divided by the Mersey estuary. The eastern parts of the county — Liverpool, Knowsley, St Helens and southern Sefton — are heavily urbanised, while the Wirral and northern Sefton contain more open land. The Mersey tunnels (Queensway and Kingsway) and the Merseyrail network link the two banks. The Silver Jubilee Bridge at Runcorn and Widnes also crosses the river near the county boundary.

Economy

The Port of Liverpool historically dominated the region's economy, and maritime trade, shipping and related industries remain significant. St Helens is known for glass manufacture (Pilkington), while the automotive industry is represented by the Jaguar Land Rover plant at Halewood. Tourism, the visitor economy around Liverpool's musical and maritime heritage, and the creative and digital sectors are growing employers.

Sport

The county is home to two Premier League football clubs, Liverpool F.C. and Everton F.C., whose rivalry forms the Merseyside derby, as well as Tranmere Rovers of the EFL. Aintree Racecourse, just outside the county's traditional boundary area in Sefton, hosts the Grand National. Royal Liverpool Golf Club at Hoylake hosts The Open Championship.
