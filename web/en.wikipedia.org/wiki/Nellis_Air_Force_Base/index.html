Nellis Air Force Base is a United States Air Force installation in southern Nevada, in the northeastern Las Vegas metropolitan area. It is home to the United States Air Force Warfare Center, the largest composite flying wing in the Air Force (the 57th Wing), and the Thunderbirds air demonstration squadron. The base is named for Las Vegas native First Lieutenant William Harrell Nellis, a P-47 pilot killed in action over Luxembourg in December 1944.

Role

Nellis hosts the Red Flag exercises, large-scale realistic combat training exercises held several times a year over the Nevada Test and Training Range (NTTR), the largest contiguous air and ground space for peacetime military operations in the United States. Major units include the 57th Wing, the 99th Air Base Wing, the USAF Weapons School, and aggressor squadrons that simulate adversary tactics; nearby Creech Air Force Base supports remotely piloted aircraft operations.

History

Founded in 1941 as Las Vegas Army Airfield, the base trained gunners during the Second World War, was briefly closed, then reopened in 1949 and renamed for Nellis in 1950. It became the centre of USAF fighter training — the "Home of the Fighter Pilot" — and its ranges were used for training through the Korean, Vietnam, Gulf and later wars.
