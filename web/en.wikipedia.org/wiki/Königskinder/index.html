Königskinder

Königskinder (The King's Children) is a German fairy-tale opera (Märchenoper) in three acts by Engelbert Humperdinck, with a libretto by Ernst Rosmer (the pen name of Elsa Bernstein-Porges). It premiered at the Metropolitan Opera in New York on 28 December 1910, conducted by Alfred Hertz.

Königskinder Composer: Engelbert Humperdinck
Librettist: Ernst Rosmer
Premiere: 28 December 1910, Metropolitan Opera, New York
Language: German

Origins

The work began in 1897 as incidental music with melodrama — spoken dialogue delivered over orchestral accompaniment — for Rosmer's play. Humperdinck later reworked it as a fully sung opera for the Met premiere, which starred Geraldine Farrar as the Goose Girl. The role of the King's Son was created by Hermann Jadlowker, and the production was notable for using real geese on stage.

Plot

The Goose Girl, raised in the forest by a witch, meets the King's Son, who has left his father's court in disguise. When the townspeople of Hellabrunn, guided by a prophecy, seek their king, they reject the true royal children and crown a false king; the Goose Girl and the King's Son die in the winter forest, discovered by the town's children too late.

Music and reception

The opera uses leitmotifs and lush late-Romantic orchestration, and is regarded by admirers as among Humperdinck's finest works, though it has never matched the popularity of his Hansel and Gretel. Modern revivals and recordings have restored some of its reputation.
