Kenneth Ronald French (born March 10, 1954) is an American financial economist. He is the Roth Family Distinguished Professor of Finance at the Tuck School of Business at Dartmouth College. Together with Nobel laureate Eugene Fama, his long-time collaborator, he reshaped empirical asset pricing.

Career

French earned a bachelor's degree in mechanical engineering from Lehigh University in 1975, then an MBA and a PhD in finance from the University of Rochester. He taught at MIT's Sloan School, Yale School of Management and the University of Chicago Booth School of Business before moving to Dartmouth in 2001. He has served as president of the American Finance Association and is a research associate of the National Bureau of Economic Research.

Research

In a series of joint papers, Fama and French showed that the capital asset pricing model's single market factor left much of stock returns unexplained. Their three-factor model (1993) added size (small minus big) and value (high minus low book-to-market) factors to the market factor, and became a standard benchmark in academic finance and investment practice. They later (2015) extended it to a five-factor model, adding profitability and investment factors. French also maintains the widely used online data library of historical factor returns, portfolios and international data, which is one of the most-downloaded resources in financial research.

French is closely associated with Dimensional Fund Advisors, where he has served as a consultant and director, applying the factor research to investment portfolios.
