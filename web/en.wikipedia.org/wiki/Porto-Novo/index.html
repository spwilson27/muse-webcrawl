Porto-Novo

Porto-Novo (also rendered Hogbonu in Gun and Ajashe in Yoruba) is the official capital of Benin, in West Africa, although the seat of government is the larger city of Cotonou. With a population of roughly 260,000–280,000, Porto-Novo is Benin's second-largest city and lies on a lagoon at the southeastern corner of the country, near the border with Nigeria.

History

The city grew around a kingdom founded by the Gun people, traditionally in the 16th or 17th century. The Portuguese gave it the name Porto-Novo ("New Port") in the 18th century, when it became a significant port in the transatlantic slave trade. In the 19th century it came under French protection and later became the capital of the French colony of Dahomey. Many Afro-Brazilian former slaves and traders settled there, leaving a distinctive legacy of Brazilian-style architecture, cuisine and culture.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include the Royal Palace of King Toffa (now the Honmé Museum), the Porto-Novo Museum of Ethnography, and the Great Mosque, whose stuccoed, multicoloured exterior resembles a Brazilian baroque church. The nearby lake town of Ganvié is a notable regional attraction.

Economy

Porto-Novo is an administrative and market centre for an agricultural region producing palm oil, cotton and kapok, and it has a port on the lagoon. Government functions are gradually being developed there as the capital, including the seat of Benin's National Assembly.
