Miracles of Jesus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The miracles of Jesus are the supernatural deeds attributed to Jesus of Nazareth in the canonical Gospels and other Christian texts. They form a major part of his ministry as presented in the New Testament and are traditionally grouped into healings, exorcisms, nature miracles and raisings of the dead.

Categories

Healings include cures of the blind (such as Bartimaeus and the man born blind), lepers, paralytics, a woman with a haemorrhage, and the centurion's servant. Exorcisms include the Gerasene demoniac and the Syrophoenician woman's daughter. Nature miracles include turning water into wine at Cana, the miraculous catch of fish, feeding the multitudes with loaves and fishes, walking on water, calming the storm, and cursing the fig tree. Raisings of the dead include Jairus's daughter, the widow of Nain's son, and Lazarus of Bethany — the last recounted only in the Gospel of John.

In the Gospels

The Synoptic Gospels (Matthew, Mark and Luke) share many miracle stories, while John's Gospel calls them "signs" and gives a distinctive selection, such as the raising of Lazarus. The miracles are presented as evidence of Jesus's authority and of the arrival of the Kingdom of God, frequently accompanied by the response of faith from those healed.

Interpretation

For most Christians the miracles are historical acts of divine power and compassion. Since the Enlightenment, scholars have debated their historicity: source and form critics note parallels with healing traditions in the ancient world, while others argue the accounts are central to the earliest Christian proclamation. In Islam, Jesus (Isa) is also credited with miracles — including healing the blind and lepers and raising the dead — performed by God's permission.
