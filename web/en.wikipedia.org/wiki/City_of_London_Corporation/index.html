City of London Corporation

The City of London Corporation, officially and legally the Mayor and Commonalty and Citizens of the City of London, is the municipal governing body of the City of London, the historic centre and primary financial district of London, England. Led by the Lord Mayor of London, it is based at Guildhall. The Corporation's structure is unique in British local government, retaining features dating to the Middle Ages, including a franchise in which businesses appoint voters alongside resident electors.

Governance

The Corporation is governed by the Court of Common Council, its principal decision-making body, made up of 100 common councillors and 25 aldermen elected from the City's 25 wards. The Court of Aldermen retains certain functions, including vetting candidates for the shrievalty and Lord Mayoralty. The Lord Mayor serves as the Corporation's ceremonial head and acts as an ambassador for the UK financial services industry. Policy is led by the Policy Chairman (styled Leader of the Council in other authorities).

The Corporation runs the City of London Police, the Barbican Centre, the London Metropolitan Archives, and large open spaces beyond the City including Epping Forest, Hampstead Heath and Burnham Beeches, funded substantially through its historic endowment known as City's Cash. It also owns and manages wholesale markets including Smithfield, Billingsgate and New Spitalfields.

History

The City's municipal rights developed from charters of the medieval period; its Liberties were repeatedly confirmed, including in Magna Carta, and the City escaped the Municipal Corporations Act 1835's reforms applied elsewhere, preserving its ancient constitution. Its ceremonial functions include the Lord Mayor's Show and the annual admission of freemen through the livery companies.
