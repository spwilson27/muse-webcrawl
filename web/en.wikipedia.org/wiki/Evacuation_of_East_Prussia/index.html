Evacuation of East Prussia
DatesJanuary – March 1945
People evacuatedOver 2 million
ContextEast Prussian offensive, Second World War

The evacuation of East Prussia was the movement of the German civilian population and military personnel of East Prussia between January and March 1945 as the Red Army advanced in the East Prussian offensive. Over two million people fled westward, many by sea in Operation Hannibal, one of the largest emergency evacuations in history.

Course

Nazi authorities had long forbidden evacuation planning, so the flight began chaotically in bitter winter weather after the Soviet offensive opened on 13 January 1945. Refugees trekked toward Baltic ports such as Pillau and Danzig or crossed the frozen Frisches Haff. The sinking of the refugee ship Wilhelm Gustloff on 30 January 1945 remains the deadliest maritime disaster in history, with around 9,000 deaths.

Aftermath

After the war, northern East Prussia became the Soviet Kaliningrad Oblast and the south passed to Poland; the remaining German population was expelled, completing the flight and expulsion of Germans from Central and Eastern Europe.
