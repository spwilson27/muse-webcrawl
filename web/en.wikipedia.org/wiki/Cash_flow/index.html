Cash flow

Cash flow
DefinitionNet movement of money into or out of a business, project, or financial asset over a period
CategoriesOperating, investing, financing
Reported inStatement of cash flows

Cash flow, in accounting and finance, is the net amount of cash and cash equivalents moving into and out of a business during a defined period. A positive cash flow indicates that liquid assets are increasing, while a negative cash flow means they are decreasing. Cash flow is a central measure of a company's financial health because, unlike accounting profit, it reflects actual money available to pay obligations, invest, and return value to shareholders.

Categories

Under accounting standards (IFRS and US GAAP), cash flows are classified into three activities:

  • Operating activities — cash from core business operations: receipts from customers, payments to suppliers and employees, taxes.
  • Investing activities — purchases and sales of long-term assets such as plant, equipment, and securities.
  • Financing activities — borrowing and repayment of debt, issuing shares, and paying dividends.

Analysis

The statement of cash flows is one of the three principal financial statements. Analysts use measures such as free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capital expenditure), cash flow from operations relative to net income (a check on earnings quality), and discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation, which estimates the value of an investment from its expected future cash flows. A profitable company can still fail through poor cash flow management if receipts lag behind payments.
