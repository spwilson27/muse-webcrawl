Đinh Bộ Lĩnh (924–979), posthumously known as Đinh Tiên Hoàng, was the founding emperor of the Đinh dynasty of Vietnam. He unified the country after the chaotic period of the Twelve Warlords and proclaimed himself emperor in 968, naming the state Đại Cồ Việt.

Early life

Born in Hoa Lư (in present-day Ninh Bình Province), Đinh Bộ Lĩnh was the son of a local official. As a boy he is legendarily said to have led other children in mock battles using reed flags. He rose as a military commander under the Ngô lords before the collapse of central authority.

Unification

After the death of Ngô Quyền and the ensuing Anarchy of the 12 Warlords, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh defeated rival warlords one by one between 966 and 968, reuniting the country. He established his capital at Hoa Lư, built palaces and fortresses, organized an army of ten divisions, and sent tributary missions to Song-dynasty China to secure recognition.

Death and legacy

Đinh Bộ Lĩnh and his eldest son were assassinated in 979 by a palace official. He is remembered as the first ruler to take the imperial title in Vietnam after a millennium of Chinese domination, and temples in his honour stand at Hoa Lư.
