Ivan Asen II (Bulgarian: Иван Асен II) was Emperor (Tsar) of Bulgaria from 1218 to 1241, during the Second Bulgarian Empire. Under his rule Bulgaria reached the peak of its medieval power, extending its territory to the Adriatic, Aegean and Black Seas.

Early life and accession

The son of Ivan Asen I, co-founder of the Second Empire, the young Ivan Asen fled to Galicia after the usurper Boril seized the throne in 1207. Returning with Rus' support in 1218, he deposed Boril, who was blinded, and was crowned at Tarnovo.

Reign

Ivan Asen II skillfully mixed diplomacy and marriage alliances with warfare. His decisive victory over Theodore Komnenos Doukas of Epirus at the Battle of Klokotnitsa on 9 March 1230 destroyed the Epirus empire's ambitions and brought Macedonia, Albania and much of Thrace under Bulgarian rule, making Bulgaria the dominant power in the Balkans. In 1235 the Bulgarian Orthodox Patriarchate was recognised at the Council of Lampsacus in agreement with the Empire of Nicaea and the other eastern patriarchs, and Ivan Asen briefly allied with Nicaea against the Latin Empire of Constantinople before shifting policy.

Domestic achievements

His reign saw flourishing trade (including a trading privilege — an early charter — for the merchants of Dubrovnik), the first coinage of the Second Empire, and major building at Tarnovo, notably the Church of the Forty Martyrs, where an inscription celebrated the Klokotnitsa victory. He died on 24 June 1241; his young son Kaliman succeeded under a regency, and the empire soon declined under Mongol pressure.

Legacy

Ivan Asen II is commemorated in the Bulgarian national anthem's historical imagery and by the Ivan Asen Point in Antarctica.
