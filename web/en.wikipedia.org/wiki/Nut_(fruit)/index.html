Nut (fruit)

A nut is a fruit consisting of a hard or leathery shell around a seed, where the shell does not open to release the seed (indehiscent). In botany, the term has a precise meaning distinct from its everyday culinary usage: most seeds sold as "nuts" in cooking are not true botanical nuts.

Botanical definition

Botanically, a nut is a dry fruit with a single seed (rarely two) whose ovary wall becomes very hard and woody at maturity, and where the seed remains unattached or only loosely attached within the shell. True nuts include acorns, hazelnuts (filberts), and chestnuts. Nuts are an important food source for wildlife, and many are dispersed by animals that cache them, such as squirrels and jays.

Culinary usage

In the kitchen, many hard-shelled seeds and fruits are called nuts even though they are not botanical nuts. These include almonds (the seed of a drupe), peanuts (a legume), cashews, walnuts, pistachios, and Brazil nuts. Culinary nuts are widely used in cooking and baking and are valued for their protein, fats, and flavour, though nut allergies are among the most common food allergies.
