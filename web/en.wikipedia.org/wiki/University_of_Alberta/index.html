The University of Alberta (U of A) is a public research university in Edmonton, Alberta, Canada. Founded in 1908 by premier Alexander Cameron Rutherford and first president Henry Marshall Tory, it is one of Canada's leading universities, with over 40,000 students across five campuses.

Academics and research

The university offers programs through 18 faculties and is noted for research in energy, artificial intelligence (home to the Alberta Machine Intelligence Institute), agriculture, and health sciences. Its alumni include Nobel laureate Richard E. Taylor and prime minister Joe Clark.

Campus

The main North Campus overlooks the North Saskatchewan River valley. The French-language Campus Saint-Jean and the Augustana Campus in Camrose serve distinct communities, while the university also operates research stations and the University of Alberta Hospital.
