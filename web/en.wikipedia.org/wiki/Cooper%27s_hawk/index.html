Cooper's hawk

Cooper's hawk (Accipiter cooperii) is a medium-sized hawk native to the North American continent and found from southern Canada to Mexico. This species is a member of the genus Accipiter, sometimes referred to as true hawks, which are famously agile, relatively small hawks common to wooded habitats around the world.

Description

Adults have steely blue-grey upperparts, warm reddish barring underneath, and red eyes. The large head projects well beyond the wrists in flight, and the long, rounded tail is heavily banded. Females are considerably larger than males, as is typical of bird-eating raptors. Cooper's hawks are very similar to the smaller sharp-shinned hawk, and the two are famously difficult to distinguish.

Behaviour and diet

These hawks hunt birds and small mammals, flying swiftly through woodland cover and taking prey by surprise. They frequent backyard bird feeders in winter, ambushing songbirds. DDT-era declines depressed populations, but numbers recovered strongly after pesticide bans, and the species has adapted readily to suburban and urban nesting.

Taxonomy

The species was described by Charles Lucien Bonaparte in 1828 and named for the naturalist William Cooper. Recent genetic studies have proposed moving North American accipiters to the genus Astur.
