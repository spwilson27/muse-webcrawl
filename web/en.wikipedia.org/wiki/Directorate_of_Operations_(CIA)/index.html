Directorate of Operations (CIA)

The Directorate of Operations (DO), less formally called the Clandestine Service, is a component of the United States Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) responsible for the clandestine collection of foreign intelligence and for covert action. It was known as the Directorate of Plans from 1951 to 1973, and as the National Clandestine Service from 2005 to 2015.

History

The Directorate of Plans was created in 1951, merging the Office of Special Operations and the Office of Policy Coordination, the latter having been responsible for covert action under Frank Wisner. Under leaders such as Allen Dulles and Richard Helms, the directorate ran major covert operations during the Cold War, including the 1953 coup in Iran (Operation Ajax) and the 1954 coup in Guatemala (Operation PBSuccess). It was renamed the Directorate of Operations in 1973.

In 2005, following recommendations made after the September 11 attacks and the Iraq weapons-of-mass-destruction assessments, Director Porter Goss reorganised the directorate as the National Clandestine Service, absorbing the clandestine human intelligence elements of other agencies' coordination under the CIA. In 2015, Director John Brennan restored the name Directorate of Operations as part of a broader modernisation of the agency's structure.

Organisation

The directorate is headed by the Deputy Director of the CIA for Operations. Its officers include case officers, who recruit and handle foreign agents; reports officers; and paramilitary operations officers of the Special Activities Center (formerly the Special Activities Division), which conducts covert paramilitary operations. Overseas, CIA stations — headed by a chief of station — are based in United States embassies and conduct the bulk of the directorate's work.

Functions

The directorate's core mission is human intelligence (HUMINT): recruiting foreign officials, military officers, businesspeople and others with access to information sought by United States policymakers. It also carries out covert action — political, paramilitary and influence operations authorised by presidential findings under United States law — intended to influence conditions abroad while keeping the United States government's role hidden.
