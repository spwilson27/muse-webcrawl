Italo

Italo disco took its name from the "Italo" label used for Italian dance productions.

Italo is a prefix and combining form meaning "Italian" or "relating to Italy," derived from Latin Italia. It appears in compound terms such as Italo-Albanian, Italo-Turkish War, and Italophone. Standing alone, the word is best known as shorthand for Italo disco, a genre of electronic dance music that emerged in Italy in the late 1970s and flourished in the early 1980s.

Italo disco

Italo disco combined American disco and funk rhythms with European electronic instrumentation — synthesisers, drum machines, and vocoders — and often English lyrics sung by Italian performers. Producers such as Giorgio Moroder (whose work with Donna Summer laid the groundwork) and groups and acts including Kano, Klein + M.B.O., and later Den Harrow, Spagna, and Baltimora defined the sound. The name arose in part from the ZYX Records compilation series The Best of Italo Disco. The genre strongly influenced house music, techno, and later electronic styles, and experienced a revival in the 2000s under the "Italo" banner.

Other uses

"Italo" is also a given name in Italian, and the root of terms for cultural and political concepts connecting Italy with other nations, such as Italo-Byzantine art styles and the Italo-Greek Catholic Churches.
