Mary Somerville (née Fairfax; 26 December 1780 – 29 November 1872) was a Scottish scientist, writer, and polymath. She studied mathematics and astronomy and wrote influential works that popularized physical science; her translation and expansion of Laplace's Mécanique céleste (1831) made her famous across Europe.

Scientific work

Somerville's On the Connexion of the Physical Sciences (1834) unified emerging fields and, in a later edition, prompted John Couch Adams's search for Neptune by noting anomalies in Uranus's orbit. She experimented on magnetism and the chemical action of light.

Recognition

She was elected an honorary member of the Royal Astronomical Society in 1835, alongside Caroline Herschel—the first women so honoured. Somerville College, Oxford, and the term 'scientist', coined in a review of her work, commemorate her legacy.
