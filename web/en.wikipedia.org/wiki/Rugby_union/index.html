Rugby union football, commonly known simply as rugby union or rugby, is a contact team sport that originated at Rugby School in England in the first half of the 19th century — legend credits William Webb Ellis with picking up the ball and running with it in 1823. It is played with an oval ball on a rectangular field, with H-shaped goalposts; players score by carrying the ball over the try line (a try, 5 points), kicking conversions and penalties (2 and 3 points) and drop goals (3 points). The ball may be kicked forward but passed only backwards or laterally.

The game split from rugby league in 1895 over payments to players, and remained officially amateur until 1995. It is governed by World Rugby (founded 1886 as the IRFB).

Competitions

The Rugby World Cup, first held in 1987, is rugby's premier event; South Africa (the Springboks) has won a record four titles, most recently in 2023, while New Zealand's All Blacks — famous for the haka they perform before matches — have won three. The annual Six Nations (England, France, Ireland, Italy, Scotland, Wales) and The Rugby Championship (Argentina, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa) are the leading regional tournaments. Jonah Lomu, whose devastating running at the 1995 World Cup made him the sport's first global superstar, is its most celebrated figure.
