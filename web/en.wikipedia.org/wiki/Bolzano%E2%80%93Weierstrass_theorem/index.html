Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem

Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem
Field: Mathematical analysis
Named after: Bernard Bolzano, Karl Weierstrass
Statement: Every bounded sequence in ℝn has a convergent subsequence
Equivalent to: Completeness of the real numbers

In mathematics, specifically in real analysis, the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem, named after Bernard Bolzano and Karl Weierstrass, states that every bounded sequence in finite-dimensional Euclidean space has a convergent subsequence — that is, a subsequence converging to a limit point of the set containing the sequence.

History

Bolzano first proved the theorem in 1817 as a lemma in his proof of the intermediate value theorem, using a bisection method, but the proof remained obscure. Around fifty years later Weierstrass proved the result again, and it became a cornerstone of rigorous analysis. The theorem is often proved together with, or derived from, the least-upper-bound (completeness) property of the real numbers.

Consequences

The theorem implies that a subset of ℝn is compact if and only if it is closed and bounded (the Heine–Borel theorem), and it is used to prove the extreme value theorem: a continuous real-valued function on a closed bounded interval attains a maximum and a minimum. Conversely, in general topological spaces the analogue need not hold: sequences may fail to have convergent subsequences even in compact spaces, where nets or filters are used instead.
