St Mark's Basilica


Location: Piazza San Marco, Venice, Italy
Style: Italo-Byzantine
Consecrated: 1094
Status: Cathedral of the Patriarchate of Venice

St Mark's Basilica (Basilica di San Marco) is the cathedral church of Venice, standing at the eastern end of the Piazza San Marco beside the Doge's Palace. Famous for its Byzantine domes, marble revetment and more than 8,000 square metres of gold-ground mosaics, it is the most celebrated symbol of the Venetian Republic.

History

The church was built to house relics of Saint Mark the Evangelist, brought to Venice from Alexandria in 828. The present building, begun in 1063, was modelled on Constantinople's Church of the Holy Apostles. After the Fourth Crusade its treasury was enriched with objects such as the Horses of Saint Mark and the Pala d'Oro altarpiece.

Architecture

The basilica follows a Greek-cross plan crowned by five domes. Its interior glows with medieval mosaics depicting biblical narratives, while the narthex and facade accumulated Gothic, Islamic and classical elements over centuries of additions.
