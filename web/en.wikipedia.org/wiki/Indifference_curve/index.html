Indifference curve

In microeconomics, an indifference curve connects combinations of two goods that give a consumer the same level of satisfaction or utility, so the consumer is indifferent among them.

Properties

Standard assumptions make curves slope downward, convex to the origin, non-intersecting, and with higher curves preferred. The slope is the marginal rate of substitution (MRS) — the rate at which the consumer trades one good for another while holding utility constant. Diminishing MRS produces convexity.

Consumer optimum

Combined with a budget constraint, the optimum is the tangency where MRS equals the price ratio. Shifts in income or prices trace income and price consumption curves used to derive demand.

History

The concept traces to Francis Ysidro Edgeworth (1881) and was developed by Vilfredo Pareto and later Hicks and Allen, allowing ordinal utility theory without measurable cardinal utility.
