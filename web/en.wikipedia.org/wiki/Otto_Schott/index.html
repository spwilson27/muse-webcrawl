Friedrich Otto Schott (17 December 1851 – 27 August 1935) was a German chemist and glass technologist who founded the Jenaer Glaswerk Schott & Genossen (later Schott AG) in Jena in 1884. He is credited as the inventor of borosilicate glass — sold under names such as Duran — whose low thermal expansion and chemical resistance revolutionized laboratory glassware, optical instruments and household cookware.

Early life and optical glass

Schott was born in Witten into a family of glassmakers, studied chemistry at Aachen and Leipzig, and took his doctorate at Jena. From 1879 he conducted systematic melting experiments in his private laboratory, investigating how chemical composition determines the optical properties of glass. His collaboration with physicist Ernst Abbe and instrument maker Carl Zeiss — the Jena "glass triumvirate" — produced new optical glasses with precisely calculable refractive indices and dispersions, enabling the achromatic microscope and telescope objectives on which the reputation of Zeiss rested.

The Jena glassworks

In 1884 Schott, Abbe, Zeiss and Roderich Zeiss founded the glassworks in Jena. Schott developed lithium- and boron-containing glasses, and in 1893 introduced borosilicate glass for thermometer tubing, laboratory ware and gas-lamp cylinders; later products included heat-resistant household glass (from 1909) and optical glass for virtually every scientific instrument maker. His 1911 catalogue listed hundreds of distinct glass types — a scientific approach to glass composition that turned an empirical craft into a branch of applied chemistry.

Legacy

The firm became part of the Carl Zeiss Foundation structure, and after German division its headquarters moved to Mainz; today Schott AG is a major international specialty-glass manufacturer. Schott's systematic mapping of glass compositions remains a foundation of glass science.
