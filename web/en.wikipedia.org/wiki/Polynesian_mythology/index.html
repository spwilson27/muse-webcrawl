Polynesian mythology

Polynesian mythology comprises the oral traditions of the Polynesian peoples of the central and southern Pacific Ocean, including the cultures of Samoa, Tonga, Tahiti, Hawaii, the Marquesas, and the Māori of New Zealand. Related island groups share many gods and heroes, reflecting their common ancestry.

Major figures

Widely shared deities include Tangaroa (god of the sea), Tāne (god of forests), Tū (god of war), and Rongo (god of agriculture). The demigod Māui is the hero of stories across Polynesia, credited with fishing up islands, snaring the sun, and stealing fire for humankind. Creation traditions often begin with darkness (Po) evolving into light (Ao).

Transmission

Myths were preserved orally through genealogies, chants, and storytelling by specialists, and were first written down in large numbers in the 19th century.
