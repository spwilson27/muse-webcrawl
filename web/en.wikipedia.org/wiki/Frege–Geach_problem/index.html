Frege–Geach problem

The Frege–Geach problem is a problem in metaethics concerning the meaning of moral statements. It is named after Gottlob Frege and Peter Geach. The problem arises for non-cognitivist theories — such as expressivism — which hold that moral sentences do not express propositions but rather attitudes, such as approval or disapproval.

The problem

Consider the argument: "If lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong. Lying is wrong. Therefore, getting your little brother to lie is wrong." For the argument to be valid, the word "wrong" must have the same meaning in the antecedent of the conditional (where it is not asserted, and so, the expressivist says, cannot be expressing an attitude) as it does standing alone. If the meaning of a moral term depends on its being used to express an attitude, then in embedded contexts like the antecedent of a conditional the term appears not to express any attitude at all, threatening equivocation.

Responses

Responses include hybrid theories combining expressive and descriptive elements, and "quasi-realist" programmes such as that of Simon Blackburn, which attempt to earn the right to truth-apt moral discourse from an expressivist base.
