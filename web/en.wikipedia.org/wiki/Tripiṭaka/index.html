Tripiṭaka

The Tripiṭaka (Sanskrit: त्रिपिटक, "Three Baskets"; Pali: Tipiṭaka) is the traditional term for the canonical scriptures of Buddhism, structured in three collections said to have been stored in three baskets. Versions of the Tripiṭaka underpin all major Buddhist traditions, though the Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna canons include additional texts beyond the three baskets.

The three baskets

  • Vinaya Piṭaka — the basket of monastic discipline: rules and procedures governing the life of monks and nuns (saṅgha).
  • Sūtra Piṭaka (Pali: Sutta Piṭaka) — the basket of discourses attributed to the Buddha and his close disciples.
  • Abhidharma Piṭaka (Pali: Abhidhamma Piṭaka) — the basket of systematic philosophy and psychology, analysing the teachings into their constituent phenomena (dharmas).

Traditions

The Pali Canon of Theravāda Buddhism, preserved in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia, is the most complete surviving early canon in an Indic language. The Chinese Buddhist canon (Dàzàngjīng) and the Tibetan Kangyur preserve Mahāyāna Tripiṭakas translated from Sanskrit and Chinese. In Korea, the Tripiṭaka Koreana — over 80,000 woodblocks carved in the 13th century and housed at Haeinsa temple — is regarded as one of the finest and most complete editions.
