Deficient number

In number theory, a deficient number is a positive integer n whose proper divisors sum to less than n - equivalently, sigma(n) < 2n. Most numbers are deficient; all prime numbers and their powers are deficient, as are all odd numbers with one or two distinct prime factors in many cases.

Examples

8 is deficient: 1+2+4=7 < 8. The first deficient numbers are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13... Deficient numbers contrast with perfect numbers (sum equals n) and abundant numbers (sum exceeds n), a classification known since Nicomachus.
