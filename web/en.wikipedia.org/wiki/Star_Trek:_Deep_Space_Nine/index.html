Star Trek: Deep Space Nine

Star Trek: Deep Space Nine (DS9) is an American science fiction television series created by Rick Berman and Michael Piller. It ran in syndication from January 1993 to June 1999, comprising 176 episodes over seven seasons. It is the third series in the Star Trek franchise and the first to be set primarily on a space station rather than a starship — the former Cardassian station Terok Nor, renamed Deep Space Nine, orbiting the planet Bajor.

Premise

After the Cardassian withdrawal from Bajor, Starfleet commander Benjamin Sisko (Avery Brooks) takes command of the station at the request of the Bajoran provisional government. The discovery of a stable wormhole to the distant Gamma Quadrant — whose inhabitants the Bajorans worship as the Prophets, and who name Sisko their Emissary — makes Bajor a strategic and commercial crossroads. The ensemble includes Bajoran liaison Kira Nerys (Nana Visitor), security chief Odo (René Auberjonois), Dr. Julian Bashir (Alexander Siddig), chief engineer Miles O'Brien (Colm Meaney), science officer Jadzia Dax (Terry Farrell), and the Ferengi bartender Quark (Armin Shimerman); Michael Dorn's Worf joined in season four.

Story arcs

DS9 distinguished itself with long serialized arcs, moral ambiguity, and themes of religion, occupation, terrorism, and war — a darker tone than its predecessors. The Dominion, a Gamma Quadrant empire ruled by Odo's people, the Changelings, emerges as the Federation's enemy, and the final seasons depict the Dominion War, the largest conflict arc in Star Trek. Acclaimed episodes include "The Visitor," "Far Beyond the Stars," and "In the Pale Moonlight," in which Sisko narrates his complicity in deception and murder for the war effort.

Reception and legacy

The series won several Emmy Awards, chiefly for makeup and visual effects, and has been reappraised upward over time; its serialized storytelling is now widely regarded as ahead of its time for 1990s television. The 2019 documentary What We Left Behind, produced by showrunner Ira Steven Behr, revisited the series with the cast. Characters and concepts from DS9 have continued to appear in later franchise productions.
