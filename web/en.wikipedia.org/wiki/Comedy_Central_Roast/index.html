The Comedy Central Roasts are a series of celebrity roast television specials broadcast on Comedy Central. In the format, a "roastee" is subjected to good-natured — and frequently brutal — jokes at their expense by a panel ("dais") of comedians and celebrity friends, presided over by a Roast Master; the roastee closes the show with jokes of their own and a moment of thanks or sentiment.

History

Comedy Central began airing roasts in 2003, adapting the format of the New York Friars Club roasts (which the channel had broadcast since 1998) and NBC's Dean Martin Celebrity Roasts of the 1970s. Early roastees included Denis Leary, Jeff Foxworthy, Pamela Anderson and William Shatner. The 2009 roast of Joan Rivers and the 2011 roast of Charlie Sheen became signature editions; the Sheen roast drew the franchise's largest audience, over 6 million viewers.

Comedian Jeff Ross became so associated with the specials that he was nicknamed the "Roastmaster General".
