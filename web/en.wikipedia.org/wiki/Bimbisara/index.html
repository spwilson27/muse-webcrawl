Bimbisara (c. 558 – c. 492 BCE) was a king of the Haryanka dynasty of Magadha in ancient India. He is credited with transforming Magadha from a modest kingdom into the dominant power of the eastern Gangetic plain, laying the foundations for the later Magadhan empires of the Nandas and Mauryas. Buddhist and Jain texts remember him as a contemporary and patron of both the Buddha and Mahavira.

Reign

According to tradition, Bimbisara ascended the throne at the age of fifteen and reigned for about 52 years from his capital at Rajagriha (modern Rajgir, Bihar). He expanded Magadha through conquest — notably annexing the kingdom of Anga to the east, gaining control of the port of Champa and trade along the Ganges — and through marriage alliances with the royal houses of Kosala, Vaishali (the Licchavis), and Madra. He is said to have maintained a large standing administration with village headmen (gramikas) and officials, and to have built the new fortified capital at Rajagriha.

Religion

Buddhist chronicles describe Bimbisara meeting the Buddha shortly after the enlightenment and donating the Veluvana (Bamboo Grove) monastery at Rajagriha, considered the first monastery offered to the Buddhist sangha. Jain texts likewise claim him as a devotee of Mahavira under the name Shrenika.

Death

According to Buddhist tradition, Bimbisara was imprisoned and starved to death by his son Ajatashatru, who had been instigated by the Buddha's treacherous cousin Devadatta and seized the throne. Ajatashatru succeeded him and continued Magadha's expansion.
