Raynaud syndrome

TypeVasospastic disorder
AffectedFingers, toes, sometimes nose and ears
TriggersCold, emotional stress
Named afterMaurice Raynaud

Raynaud syndrome, also known as Raynaud's phenomenon, is a medical condition in which the spasm of small arteries causes episodes of reduced blood flow to end arterioles, typically in the fingers and toes.

Symptoms

During an attack, affected areas turn white due to lack of blood flow, then blue as tissue loses oxygen, and finally red as blood returns, often with pain, numbness, or tingling. Attacks are triggered by cold temperatures or stress.

Types and causes

Primary Raynaud's occurs on its own and is usually mild; secondary Raynaud's accompanies diseases such as systemic sclerosis, lupus, or rheumatoid arthritis, and may follow vibration exposure or certain medications. In severe secondary cases, digital ulcers or gangrene can occur.

Treatment

Management focuses on avoiding cold and smoking; calcium channel blockers such as nifedipine reduce attack frequency, and other vasodilators or surgery are reserved for severe cases.
