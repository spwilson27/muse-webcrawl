Lookism is the discrimination or prejudice against people considered physically unattractive, and favourable treatment of people considered attractive. The term was coined in the 1970s within the fat-acceptance movement in the United States and entered the Washington Post magazine's lexicon in 1978. It denotes stereotyping, bias and unequal treatment based on appearance — in hiring, pay, promotion, criminal sentencing, and everyday social interaction.

Evidence

Empirical research documents a "beauty premium" and "ugliness penalty": economist Daniel Hamermesh's work (Beauty Pays, 2011) estimated that attractive workers earn on the order of 10–15% more than below-average-looking peers over a career. Studies have also found appearance effects in elections, courtroom outcomes and teacher evaluations. Lookism is related to, but distinct from, weight stigma, height discrimination and colourism.

Legal responses

Appearance is generally not a protected characteristic. Exceptions exist: the US city of Santa Cruz and the state of Michigan (which covers height and weight) prohibit some appearance-based discrimination; Victoria, Australia, covers "physical features"; and several jurisdictions have debated bills against discrimination based on hair texture and style (the CROWN Act). In 2023 China's and South Korea's public debates about appearance requirements in job postings also drew attention to the issue.

In popular culture

The concept lent its name to the South Korean webtoon Lookism (2014– ) by Park Tae-joon, adapted into an animated series in 2022, in which a bullied teenager wakes up with two bodies — one handsome, one not — and experiences the differing treatment each attracts.
