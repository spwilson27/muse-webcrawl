The National Museum of Singapore is a public museum dedicated to Singapore art, culture and history. Located in the downtown Civic District, it is the country's oldest museum, with roots in the Raffles Library and Museum founded in 1849.

History

The museum began as part of the Singapore Institution library and moved to its current neo-Palladian building on Stamford Road in 1887. Over the decades it was renamed several times — as the Raffles Museum, then the National Museum — before taking its current name in 1993. A major redevelopment completed in 2006 added a contemporary glass-and-metal extension behind the historic dome.

Galleries

The museum's permanent galleries trace Singapore's story from the 14th century to the present day, covering early history, the colonial era, the Japanese occupation, and the road to independence. Its treasures include one of the Singapore Stone fragments and the will of Munshi Abdullah.

Exhibitions and programmes

Beyond its permanent galleries, the museum hosts travelling exhibitions, film screenings, festivals such as the Singapore Night Festival, and educational programmes for schools and families.
