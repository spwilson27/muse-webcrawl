Große Fuge

The Große Fuge (German for "Grand Fugue" or "Great Fugue"), Op. 133, is a single-movement composition for string quartet by Ludwig van Beethoven, written in 1825 as the original finale of his String Quartet No. 13 in B-flat major, Op. 130. It is among the most demanding and dissonant works of the chamber repertoire, noted for its length (about a quarter of an hour), violence of expression, and dense counterpoint.

Composition and publication

The premiere of Op. 130 in March 1826 left audiences baffled by the fugue finale. At the urging of his publisher Artaria, Beethoven agreed to write a lighter replacement finale — his last completed composition — and the fugue was published separately as Op. 133, dedicated to Archduke Rudolf. A four-hand piano arrangement appeared as Op. 134.

Music

The work opens with an Overtura previewing its themes, then launches into a double fugue of formidable intensity, interspersed with contrasting gentler sections in G-flat and A-flat major. The autograph manuscript, missing for over a century, was rediscovered in 2005 at Palmer Theological Seminary in Pennsylvania and sold at Sotheby's for £1.95 million.

Reception

Long dismissed as the product of Beethoven's deaf isolation, the Große Fuge was championed in the 20th century; Igor Stravinsky called it "an absolutely contemporary piece of music that will be contemporary forever." It is now regarded as a pinnacle of Beethoven's late style.
