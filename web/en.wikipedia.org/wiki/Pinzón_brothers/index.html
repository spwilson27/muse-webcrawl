Pinzón brothers

The Pinzón brothers, from Palos de la Frontera

The Pinzón brothers — Martín Alonso Pinzón (c. 1441–1493), Vicente Yáñez Pinzón (c. 1462–after 1514) and Francisco Martín Pinzón (c. 1445–c. 1505) — were Spanish sailors and shipowners from Palos de la Frontera, in Andalusia. Their support, ships and experience were essential to Christopher Columbus's first voyage of 1492–93.

Role in the first voyage

Columbus struggled to recruit crews in Palos until Martín Alonso, the eldest brother and a respected navigator, endorsed the enterprise. The Pinzóns helped supply and crew the fleet: Martín Alonso captained the Pinta, Vicente Yáñez captained the Niña, and Francisco sailed as master of the Pinta. During the crossing Martín Alonso twice separated from Columbus — including the unauthorised dash that reached Hispaniola ahead of the flagship — and a ship of the Pinta made the first sighting of land debated by historians. Martín Alonso died within days of the fleet's return to Palos in March 1493.

Later voyages

Vicente Yáñez Pinzón went on to an independent career of discovery: in 1499–1500 he reached the coast of Brazil, encountering the Amazon region, and in 1508 he sailed with Juan Díaz de Solís to Central America in search of a passage to the Pacific. The Pinzón family remained prominent in Palos, and witnesses from the family testified in the lawsuits (pleitos colombinos) between Columbus's heirs and the Crown over the Indies.
