Inferior olivary nucleus

The inferior olivary nucleus is a prominent, convoluted mass of grey matter in the medulla oblongata, forming the surface bulge known as the olive. It is the sole source of the climbing fibres that project to the cerebellum, and it plays a central role in motor coordination and motor learning.

The folded "crumpled bag" appearance of the inferior olive.

Anatomy

On cross-section the nucleus resembles a crumpled bag or purse, with its hilum directed medially. Its neurons receive input from many sources, including the spinal cord, red nucleus, cerebral cortex (via the central tegmental tract) and other brainstem structures. Their axons cross the midline and enter the cerebellum through the inferior cerebellar peduncle, terminating as climbing fibres that make powerful synapses on the dendrites of Purkinje cells.

Function

Climbing fibre activity is widely interpreted as an error or teaching signal in cerebellar learning: each Purkinje cell receives input from a single climbing fibre, firing distinctive "complex spikes" that help calibrate movement. The inferior olive is also implicated in the timing of movements; its neurons show subthreshold oscillations and are electrically coupled by gap junctions. Damage to the pathways of the olivary circuit can produce palatal tremor and, in a delayed complication, hypertrophic olivary degeneration.
