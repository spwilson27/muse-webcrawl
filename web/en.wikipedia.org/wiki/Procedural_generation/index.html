Procedural generation

In computing, procedural generation (sometimes shortened to proc-gen) is a method of creating data algorithmically as opposed to manually, typically through a combination of human-generated assets and algorithms coupled with computer-generated randomness and processing power. In computer graphics, it is commonly used to create textures and 3D models. In video games, it is used to automatically create large amounts of content in a game.


Procedural generation can create vast terrains from compact algorithms.

Techniques

Common techniques include fractal terrain generation, Perlin and simplex noise for natural-looking variation, L-systems for plants and branching structures, cellular automata for caves, and grammars for buildings, quests, and items. A seed value for a pseudorandom number generator typically determines the result, so the same seed regenerates the same content.

Applications

Early games such as Elite (1984) used procedural generation to fit a galaxy of thousands of stars into minimal memory. Roguelikes such as NetHack generate dungeons each run. Later titles brought the technique to mass audiences: Minecraft generates its blocky terrain around the player, and No Man's Sky advertises a universe of quintillions of procedurally generated planets. Procedural methods are also used in film effects, music composition, and text generation.
