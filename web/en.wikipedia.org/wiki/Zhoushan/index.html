Zhoushan

Zhoushan (Chinese: 舟山; pinyin: Zhōushān) is an urbanized archipelago with the administrative status of a prefecture-level city in the province of Zhejiang in eastern China. It consists of the Zhoushan Islands at the southern mouth of Hangzhou Bay, off the coast of Ningbo, and is the only prefecture-level city in China administered entirely on islands.

Geography

The archipelago comprises over a thousand islands, the largest being Zhoushan Island itself, the third-largest island under PRC administration after Hainan and Chongming. The population of the prefecture is roughly 1.15 million. Zhoushan Island is connected to the mainland by a series of large sea bridges, completed in 2009.

Economy

Zhoushan is one of China's most important fishing ports and fishery centers, and has major shipbuilding, ship repair and port industries. Together with Ningbo it forms the Ningbo–Zhoushan Port, among the world's busiest ports by cargo tonnage. The area is also known for the Buddhist sacred site of Mount Putuo, one of the four sacred mountains of Chinese Buddhism, dedicated to Guanyin.
