John Bennet Lawes

Sir John Bennet Lawes, 1st Baronet (1814–1900) was an English agricultural scientist who founded the Rothamsted Experimental Station, the world's longest-running agricultural research institution, and pioneered the manufacture of superphosphate fertiliser.

Work

Lawes inherited the Rothamsted estate in Hertfordshire in 1834 and began experiments on manures. In 1842 he patented a process for making superphosphate by treating phosphates with sulphuric acid, founding a fertiliser industry. From 1843 he collaborated with chemist Joseph Henry Gilbert on the famous long-term field experiments — including Broadbalk wheat, grown continuously since 1843 — that founded modern agricultural science.

Legacy

Lawes endowed the Lawes Agricultural Trust in 1889 with £100,000 to secure Rothamsted's future. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society, made a baronet in 1882, and is commemorated by a statue at Rothamsted (now Rothamsted Research) in Harpenden.
