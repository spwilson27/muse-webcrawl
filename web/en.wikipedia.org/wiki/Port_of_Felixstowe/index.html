Port of Felixstowe

The Port of Felixstowe, in Felixstowe, Suffolk, is the United Kingdom's busiest container port, handling nearly half of the country's containerised trade — around 3.5 to 4 million TEU annually — and employing some 3,500 people.

History

The dock basin dates from 1886 and grew as a general cargo and naval port, but its modern success began in the 1960s when it became an early British pioneer of containerisation. It was sold to P&O in 1976 and then to Hutchison Whampoa (now CK Hutchison's Hutchison Ports) in 1991, after which quays and container terminals expanded repeatedly to accommodate ever-larger vessels.

Facilities

The port has two container terminals, Trinity and Berths 8&9 — the latter, opened in stages from 2011, among the few UK berths able to take the largest ultra-large container vessels. Dedicated rail terminals connect Felixstowe to the Midlands and the North, carrying roughly a third of its containers inland by train.
