Nepalese Civil War

The Nepalese Civil War was an armed conflict in Nepal between the Communist Party of Nepal (Maoist) and the government of Nepal, lasting from 1996 to 2006. The Maoists launched their "People's War" on 13 February 1996 with the aim of overthrowing the monarchy and establishing a people's republic.

Course of the war

The insurgency spread from the mid-western hill districts of Rolpa and Rukum across the country. The conflict intensified after 2001, when King Gyanendra ascended the throne following the royal massacre, and the Royal Nepalese Army was mobilized against the rebels. In February 2005 Gyanendra seized direct power, prompting the Maoists to ally with the parliamentary parties in a twelve-point agreement. Mass protests in April 2006 (the Loktantra Andolan) forced the king to restore parliament.

Aftermath

The Comprehensive Peace Accord of November 2006 ended the war, which had killed more than 17,000 people. Maoist combatants were cantoned under United Nations monitoring, an interim constitution was promulgated, and elections to a Constituent Assembly in 2008 led to the abolition of the monarchy and the declaration of Nepal as a federal democratic republic.
