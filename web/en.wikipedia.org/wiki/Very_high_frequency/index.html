Very high frequency

Frequency range30 to 300 MHz
Wavelength range10 to 1 m
Related bandsBand B (NATO); IEEE VHF

Very high frequency (VHF) is the ITU designation for the range of radio frequency electromagnetic waves from 30 to 300 megahertz (MHz), corresponding to wavelengths of ten metres to one metre. Frequencies immediately below VHF are denoted high frequency (HF), and the next higher frequencies are known as ultra high frequency (UHF).

A VHF television antenna, designed for wavelengths of one to ten metres.

Propagation

VHF waves generally travel by line of sight, so they are blocked by hills and mountains, though refraction can extend them slightly beyond the visual horizon. They are less affected by atmospheric noise and interference than lower frequencies, but also penetrate buildings less effectively than UHF. Occasional ionospheric phenomena, such as sporadic E propagation, can reflect VHF signals and allow long-distance reception.

Uses

Common uses of the VHF band include FM radio broadcasting (88–108 MHz in most of the world), television broadcasting (the historic Bands I and III), air traffic control communications and navigation (108–137 MHz), marine VHF radio (around 156–174 MHz), amateur radio (including the popular 2-metre band), and land mobile and emergency services radio. The VHF band was also used for analogue television channels 2–13 in North America before the digital transition moved most television to UHF.
