Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto

Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto, also spelled Tsukiyomi, is the god of the Moon in Japanese mythology and the Shinto religion. His name is commonly interpreted as combining tsuku (moon) and yomi (reading or counting), hence "the one who counts the moons".

Mythology

According to the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, Tsukuyomi was born from the right eye of the creator deity Izanagi as he purified himself after returning from Yomi, the land of the dead. His siblings are Amaterasu, goddess of the Sun, born from Izanagi's left eye, and Susanoo, god of storms, born from his nose.

In one well-known episode, Tsukuyomi attended a banquet hosted by Uke Mochi, goddess of food. Disgusted by the manner in which she produced food from her body, he killed her. Amaterasu, angered by the act, refused to look upon her brother again, which is said to explain why day and night are separated.

Worship

Tsukuyomi is enshrined at several shrines in Japan, including sub-shrines at Ise Grand Shrine and shrines in Kyoto and Nara. Compared with Amaterasu and Susanoo, relatively little narrative material about him survives.
