Bauhaus (band)

Bauhaus were an English rock band formed in Northampton in 1978. The group consisted of Peter Murphy (vocals), Daniel Ash (guitar, saxophone), Kevin Haskins (drums) and David J (bass). Known for their dark, atmospheric sound and Murphy's baritone voice and theatrical presence, Bauhaus are regarded as the founding band of gothic rock, a status cemented by their 1979 debut single "Bela Lugosi's Dead".

History

Taking their name from the German Bauhaus art school, the band recorded "Bela Lugosi's Dead" in a single take in January 1979; the nine-minute dub-influenced song became a club anthem and defined the emerging goth subculture. Signing to 4AD and then Beggars Banquet, they released four albums of increasingly experimental post-punk, scored a UK top-20 hit with a cover of David Bowie's "Ziggy Stardust" (1982), and appeared performing "Bela Lugosi's Dead" in the opening scene of the film The Hunger (1983).

Breakup and reunions

Internal tensions led to a split in 1983 after Burning from the Inside. Ash and the Haskins brothers went on to form Love and Rockets, while Murphy pursued a solo career. Bauhaus reunited for the Resurrection Tour in 1998, again from 2005 (touring and recording Go Away White in 2008 before splitting once more), and re-formed again in 2019 for shows including a headline slot at the Cruel World festival, before cancelling dates in 2022.

Legacy

Bauhaus's fusion of dub, glam, funk and art-rock, and their stark black-and-white aesthetic, profoundly influenced gothic rock and alternative music; bands from The Cure and Siouxsie and the Banshees to Interpol and Nine Inch Nails have cited them as an inspiration.
