BlackBerry (smartphones)

BlackBerry was a brand of smartphones and mobile services originally developed by the Canadian company Research In Motion (RIM), later renamed BlackBerry Limited. First released in 1999 as two-way pagers and from 2002 as smartphones, BlackBerry devices were renowned for their physical QWERTY keyboards, push email through the BlackBerry Enterprise Server, the BlackBerry Messenger (BBM) service, and strong security that made them favourites of business and government users.

Rise and decline

At their peak around 2009–2011, BlackBerry devices accounted for a major share of the world smartphone market, and the devices were so associated with constant email use that they were nicknamed "CrackBerries". The brand declined rapidly after the rise of the iPhone and Android touchscreen phones. BlackBerry's own touchscreen platform, BlackBerry 10, launched in 2013, failed to regain share, and the company exited handset design in 2016, licensing the brand to other manufacturers such as TCL.

End of services

On 4 January 2022, BlackBerry switched off the legacy services supporting devices running BlackBerry OS and BlackBerry 10, ending the era of classic BlackBerry handsets. The company refocused on cybersecurity software and embedded systems.
