The SPAD S.XIII was a French biplane fighter aircraft of the First World War, developed by the Société Pour L'Aviation et ses Dérivés (SPAD) from the earlier SPAD S.VII. Powered by a Hispano-Suiza V-8 engine and armed with twin synchronised Vickers machine guns, it was fast, rugged and an excellent gun platform, and became one of the most important Allied fighters of the final year of the war.

Design and development

The S.XIII grew out of demands for a more powerful successor to the single-gun SPAD S.VII. The new aircraft used the more powerful geared Hispano-Suiza 8B engine of about 220–235 hp, driving a larger propeller, and its wingspan was slightly increased with rounded tips. The most significant improvement was the fitting of two 7.7 mm Vickers guns firing through the propeller arc, doubling the earlier type's firepower. The prototype first flew on 4 April 1917 and production orders followed almost immediately.

Operational history

The S.XIII entered squadron service in the summer of 1917 and equipped most French fighter escadrilles by the Armistice, as well as units of the Royal Flying Corps and the Italian air service. The United States Army Air Service adopted it as its standard pursuit aircraft, and American aces such as Eddie Rickenbacker flew the type. French aces including René Fonck and Georges Guynemer's successors scored many victories in SPADs. In total 8,472 were built, and the type remained in service into the early 1920s.

Specifications

Typical figures for the S.XIII include a maximum speed of about 234 km/h (145 mph) at altitude, a service ceiling of around 6,650 m (21,800 ft), and an endurance of about two hours. Its robust wooden construction and high diving speed made it popular with pilots who favoured energy tactics over close turning dogfights.
