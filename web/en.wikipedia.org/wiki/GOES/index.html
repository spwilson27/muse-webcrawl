Geostationary Operational Environmental Satellite

The Geostationary Operational Environmental Satellite (GOES) is a series of American weather satellites in geostationary orbit operated by the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), with development and launch handled by NASA. GOES satellites provide the continuous imagery and atmospheric soundings that underpin weather forecasting, severe-storm warnings, and environmental monitoring for the Western Hemisphere.

Operations

Two GOES satellites are operational at any time: GOES-East, stationed over the equator at about 75°W, watching the Americas and Atlantic; and GOES-West, at about 137°W, watching the Pacific and western North America. A spare satellite is kept in orbit. The system began with SMS satellites in 1974 and the first GOES satellite in 1975.

GOES-R series

The fourth-generation GOES-R series — GOES-16 (launched 2016, became GOES-East), GOES-17 (2018, GOES-West, later retired early due to a cooling-system fault), GOES-18 (2022, took over GOES-West), and GOES-19 (launched June 2024, became GOES-East in 2025) — introduced the Advanced Baseline Imager, which scans the full disk every 15 minutes (and storm regions every 30–60 seconds) in 16 spectral bands, plus the Geostationary Lightning Mapper, solar and space-weather instruments, and a coronagraph on GOES-19. The data are central to hurricane tracking, wildfire detection, and nowcasting of tornadoes and flash floods.

Successor

NOAA's planned GeoXO constellation is intended to succeed GOES from the 2030s, adding hyperspectral sounding and air-quality instruments.
