Carlos Acosta

Carlos Acosta
Born2 June 1973, Havana, Cuba
OccupationBallet dancer, choreographer, director

Carlos Yunior Acosta Quesada (born 2 June 1973) is a Cuban-British ballet dancer and director. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest male dancers of his generation, celebrated for his powerful technique and dramatic presence. Since 2020 he has been director of Birmingham Royal Ballet, and he founded his own Havana-based company, Acosta Danza, in 2015.

Early life and career

Acosta was born in Havana, the youngest of eleven children, and was sent to ballet school partly to channel his energy and keep him out of trouble. He trained at the Cuban National Ballet School and won the Prix de Lausanne in 1990. He danced with the National Ballet of Cuba under Alicia Alonso, then with English National Ballet, Houston Ballet, and American Ballet Theatre, before joining The Royal Ballet in London in 1998, where he remained a principal (later principal guest artist) until 2015, dancing leads in Swan Lake, Don Quixote, Romeo and Juliet and Manon.

Other work

Acosta has written an autobiography, No Way Home (2007), and the novel Pig's Foot (2013). The 2018 biographical film Yuli dramatises his life. He was appointed CBE in 2014. As director of Birmingham Royal Ballet he has championed new works and revivals such as his production of Don Quixote.
