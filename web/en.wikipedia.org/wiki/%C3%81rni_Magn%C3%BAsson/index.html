Árni Magnússon

Árni Magnússon (13 November 1663 – 7 January 1730) was an Icelandic scholar and collector of manuscripts. He assembled the Arnamagnæan Manuscript Collection, one of the most important surviving collections of medieval Icelandic and Norse manuscripts, which is now divided between the Árni Magnússon Institute for Icelandic Studies in Reykjavík and the Arnamagnæan Institute at the University of Copenhagen.

Life

Árni was born at Kvennabrekka in Dalasýsla in western Iceland. He studied at the University of Copenhagen and later became a professor there, teaching Danish antiquities and philosophy; he was also appointed the university's librarian and served as a royal antiquarian. Between 1702 and 1712 he travelled throughout Iceland on a royal commission, with Páll Vídalín, to register farms, land values and jurisdictional matters, which produced the great land register known as Jarðabók Árna Magnússonar og Páls Vídalíns.

Manuscript collection

While travelling and through agents in Iceland, Árni bought or received huge numbers of vellum and paper manuscripts, sagas, law codes, charters and letters, which he shipped to Copenhagen. Many unique copies of the Icelandic sagas survive only because of his efforts. The great fire of Copenhagen in 1728 destroyed part of the collection and many of his printed books and papers, an event that affected him deeply; he died little more than a year later. After a long dispute, the collection was divided, and between 1971 and 1997 the manuscripts held to be of Icelandic origin were returned to Iceland. In 2009 the Arnamagnæan Manuscript Collection was inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register.
