Slope rating

A golf course evaluated for course and slope ratings

The slope rating of a golf course is a measure of its relative difficulty for a bogey golfer compared to a scratch golfer. Together with the course rating, it forms the basis of the USGA Course Rating System and the World Handicap System, allowing a player's handicap to be portable between courses of different difficulty.

Definition

Slope rating expresses the relative difficulty of a course for bogey golfers. It is calculated as the difference between the bogey rating (the expected score of a bogey golfer, a player with a handicap of about 20 for men) and the course rating (the expected score of a scratch golfer), multiplied by a constant (5.381 for men, 4.24 for women). Slope ratings range from 55 (least difficult) to 155 (most difficult), with 113 representing a course of nominal or "standard" difficulty.

Use in handicapping

Under the World Handicap System, a player's course handicap is computed from their handicap index scaled by the slope rating divided by 113, with an adjustment for the difference between course rating and par. A higher slope means the bogey golfer loses proportionally more strokes to the scratch golfer, so the course handicap increases on difficult courses.

History

The slope system was developed by the United States Golf Association beginning in the late 1970s, drawing on research led by Dean Knuth, and was progressively adopted across the United States in the 1980s and 1990s.
