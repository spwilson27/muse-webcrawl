Lerch zeta function

The Lerch zeta function, sometimes called the Hurwitz–Lerch zeta function, is a special function in analytic number theory that generalises the Hurwitz zeta function and the polylogarithm. It is named after the Czech mathematician Mathias Lerch, who published work on it in 1887.

Definition

The Lerch transcendent Φ(z, s, a) is defined by the series Φ(z, s, a) = Σn=0∞ zn / (n + a)s, convergent for |z| < 1 and by analytic continuation elsewhere. The Lerch zeta function is the special case L(λ, a, s) = Φ(e2πiλ, s, a).

Special cases

Setting z = 1 recovers the Hurwitz zeta function ζ(s, a); a = 1 gives the Riemann zeta function. The polylogarithm Lis(z) equals z·Φ(z, s, 1). Dirichlet L-functions can likewise be expressed in terms of the Lerch zeta function, which makes it a unifying object in the study of L-functions.

Properties

The Lerch zeta function satisfies functional equations relating values at s and 1 − s, known through the work of Lerch and later Apostol. It has integral representations, series expansions (including a Taylor series in a and expansions related to the Bernoulli numbers at rational parameters), and special values at integers connected with Bernoulli polynomials. Its zeros and value distribution remain active topics of research.
