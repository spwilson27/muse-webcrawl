Yoshinkan (養神館, Yōshinkan, roughly "hall for cultivating the spirit") is a style of aikido founded in 1955 by Gozo Shioda, one of the most prominent students of aikido's founder Morihei Ueshiba. Sometimes described as the "hard" style of aikido, Yoshinkan is known for its structured, methodical pedagogy and its emphasis on precise basic movements, and it has been taught to units of the Tokyo Metropolitan Police.

History

Gozo Shioda began training under Ueshiba in 1932 and studied intensively in the pre-war period, when aikido retained close ties to Daitō-ryū aiki-jūjutsu. After the Second World War he demonstrated aikido publicly — notably at an exhibition in 1954 attended by business leaders — and with their support opened the Yoshinkan dojo in Tokyo in 1955. The school grew internationally through students such as Kiyoyuki Terada and foreign instructors who spread the style to North America, Europe, and Australia. After Shioda's death in 1994, leadership passed within the Yoshinkan organizations; some senior instructors later formed independent schools.

Training

Yoshinkan training is built on the kihon dōsa, a set of six basic movements practiced solo and with partners to instill posture, balance, and coordinated body movement, and on a syllabus of some 150 basic techniques tested in standardized gradings. Instruction tends to proceed by deliberate repetition of form before flowing application, in contrast to the emphasis on continuous circular movement in some other aikido styles. The Senshusei course — an intensive eleven-month program originally designed for Tokyo riot police (Kidotai) instructors — became famous in the West through Robert Twigger's book Angry White Pyjamas (1997).
