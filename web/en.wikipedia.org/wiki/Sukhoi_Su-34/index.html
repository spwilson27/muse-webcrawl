The Sukhoi Su-34 (Russian: Сухой Су-34; NATO reporting name: Fullback) is a Soviet/Russian twin-engine, all-weather supersonic medium-range fighter-bomber/strike aircraft developed by Sukhoi. It first flew in 1990 and entered service with the Russian Air Force in 2014, intended to replace the Su-24 tactical bomber.

Design

Derived from the Su-27 'Flanker' family, the Su-34 has a distinctive wide, flattened nose housing a side-by-side two-seat cockpit — a layout chosen to improve crew comfort on long missions, with space for the crew to stand, a small galley and a toilet. The cockpit sits inside a welded titanium armour capsule. The aircraft has canard foreplanes, twelve hardpoints for up to 8 tonnes (later increased) of precision-guided and unguided weapons, a 30 mm GSh-30-1 cannon, and a Leninets V004 passive electronically scanned array radar.

Operational history

The Su-34 has flown combat missions during Russia's intervention in the Syrian civil war from 2015 and has been used extensively in the Russo-Ukrainian War, in which a number have been shot down or destroyed on the ground. Deliveries of the modernised Su-34M variant began in the 2020s.
