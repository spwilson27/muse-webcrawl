Heidelberg University, officially the Ruprecht Karl University of Heidelberg (German: Ruprecht-Karls-Universität Heidelberg), is a public research university in Heidelberg, Baden-Württemberg, Germany. Founded in 1386 on the instruction of Pope Urban VI by Rupert I, Elector Palatine, it is Germany's oldest university and one of the world's oldest surviving universities. It was the third university established in the Holy Roman Empire.

History

The university was modelled on the University of Paris and began with faculties of theology, law, medicine, and philosophy. It became a centre of humanism in the Renaissance and of German Romanticism in the early nineteenth century. In the modern era it has been associated with dozens of Nobel laureates and major figures such as Max Weber, Karl Jaspers, and Hannah Arendt (who studied there). Professors including Robert Bunsen and Gustav Kirchhoff made it a leading centre of the natural sciences; Kirchhoff and Bunsen developed spectral analysis there.

Today

Heidelberg is organised into twelve faculties and is spread across several sites, including the old town, the Neuenheimer Feld science campus, and Bergheim. It is a member of the German Universities Excellence Initiative and consistently ranks among the top universities in Germany and Europe. Its university library, founded in 1388, is one of the oldest in Germany, and the university is closely associated with the German Cancer Research Center (DKFZ) and other institutes in the city.
