Garissa is a city in north-eastern Kenya and the capital of Garissa County. It lies on the western bank of the Tana River, roughly 350 kilometres east of Nairobi, and was the capital of the former North Eastern Province. The population is predominantly ethnic Somali, and the city is a commercial centre for the surrounding pastoralist region.

Geography and climate

Garissa sits in a hot, arid lowland at about 150 metres above sea level. The Tana River, Kenya's longest, provides water for irrigation and domestic use, while the surrounding county is dry rangeland used mainly for livestock herding. The nearby Dadaab refugee complex, one of the largest in the world, lies within Garissa County and has strongly influenced the region's economy and demographics since the 1990s.

Economy

The city's economy is based on livestock trade, cross-border commerce with Somalia, agriculture along the river, and services connected to government, aid agencies and the refugee camps. A bridge across the Tana links Garissa to the road network toward the coast and the Somali border.

Garissa University attack

On 2 April 2015, gunmen from the militant group al-Shabaab attacked Garissa University College, killing 148 people, mostly students, in the deadliest terrorist attack in Kenya since the 1998 US embassy bombing. The university later reopened with enhanced security, and the attack prompted major changes to Kenyan security policy in the north-east.
