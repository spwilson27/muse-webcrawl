Super Mario Bros. is a 1993 American science fantasy adventure film loosely based on Nintendo's Super Mario video game series. Directed by Rocky Morton and Annabel Jankel, it stars Bob Hoskins as Mario and John Leguizamo as Luigi, two Brooklyn plumbers who travel to a parallel dimension — a dystopian city called Dinohattan, populated by descendants of dinosaurs — to rescue Princess Daisy (Samantha Mathis) from the dictator King Koopa (Dennis Hopper). It was the first feature-length live-action film based on a video game.

Production

The production was famously troubled: Nintendo granted the filmmakers broad creative freedom, and the script passed through numerous drafts, shifting from a family-friendly fantasy toward a darker, cyberpunk-influenced tone influenced by Blade Runner and Mad Max. The directors and producers clashed repeatedly, rewrites arrived on set daily, and both lead actors later spoke negatively of the experience. The film reimagines the games' elements in realistic form — Goombas as hulking de-evolved henchmen, "Bob-ombs" as tiny wind-up bombs, and jump boots echoing Mario's jumping.

Reception and legacy

Super Mario Bros. was a critical and commercial failure on release, grossing only $38.9 million against a budget of $42–48 million, and it was long cited as the archetype of the unsuccessful video game adaptation. Nintendo did not license another Mario film for thirty years, until the animated The Super Mario Bros. Movie (2023). In later decades the 1993 film developed a cult following appreciating its production design and eccentricity, with fan restorations and an online sequel comic extending its story.
