City Hall, London (Southwark)

City Hall is a building on the south bank of the River Thames in Southwark, London, near Tower Bridge. Designed by Norman Foster and opened in 2002, it served as the headquarters of the Greater London Authority (GLA), housing the Mayor of London and the London Assembly, until the GLA moved to The Crystal in Newham in 2022. The building was leased rather than owned by the GLA; after the authority's departure it has been marketed for commercial use.

City Hall on the south bank of the Thames, beside the More London development.

Design

The building has a distinctive bulbous, distorted-sphere form intended to reduce the surface area exposed to direct sunlight and thus improve energy efficiency; its shape has been compared to a helmet, a misshapen egg and a glass gonad, among other things. It is about 45 metres tall with ten storeys. Inside, a spiral ramp climbs the full height of the building around the debating chamber, echoing the ramp in Foster's Reichstag dome in Berlin and allowing views into the chamber. The chamber itself is located at the bottom of the building, in line with ideas of transparency and public accessibility.

The building forms part of the More London development on the battlebridge lane site. Its southern facade shades the offices behind it, while the north side facing the river is fully glazed. It was constructed by Arup as structural engineer with steel framing and glass cladding.

History

The GLA was established in 2000 and initially based at Romney House in Westminster. City Hall opened in July 2002 and was inaugurated in the presence of Queen Elizabeth II. In 2020 the Mayor announced the authority would leave City Hall for The Crystal in the Royal Docks, citing lease costs; the move took place in early 2022, after which the building ceased to be London's seat of government.
