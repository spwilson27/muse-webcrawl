Special economic zones of China

The special economic zones (SEZs) of China are designated areas with economic policies and flexible governmental measures distinct from the rest of the country, designed to attract foreign investment and spur rapid growth. They were a cornerstone of Deng Xiaoping's reform and opening-up policy.

History

In 1980, four SEZs were established: Shenzhen, Zhuhai, and Shantou in Guangdong province, and Xiamen in Fujian province. Shenzhen, then a small border town near Hong Kong, grew explosively into a metropolis of over ten million and a global center of electronics manufacturing and technology. In 1988 the whole island of Hainan became the largest SEZ, and later additions included Kashgar in Xinjiang (2010). The success of the zones encouraged broader liberalization, including coastal open cities and development areas across the country.

Characteristics

SEZs offered tax incentives, streamlined approval of foreign investment, greater autonomy in economic decision-making, and special customs arrangements. They served as laboratories in which market-oriented policies — land-use rights sales, labour contracts, price reform — were tested before being adopted nationally.
