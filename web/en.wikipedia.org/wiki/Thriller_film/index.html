Thriller film

Thriller film, also known as suspense film or suspense thriller, is a broad genre of film that revolves around anticipation and suspense. The aim is to stimulate particular moods in the viewer — a high level of anticipation, ultra-heightened expectation, uncertainty, anxiety, terror and surprise. Elements of the genre are commonly combined with others, such as action, crime, horror, spy and psychological drama.

Characteristics

Thrillers typically place an ordinary protagonist against a threat or antagonist whose defeat seems initially unlikely, generating suspense through delays, red herrings, cliffhangers and the withholding or partial revelation of information. The "master of suspense", Alfred Hitchcock, famously distinguished suspense from surprise: suspense arises when the audience knows more than the characters (for example, that a bomb sits under a table), while surprise is a momentary shock. Subgenres include the psychological thriller, the erotic thriller, the conspiracy thriller, the techno-thriller and the legal thriller.

History

Early examples include the silent films of Fritz Lang, notably Dr. Mabuse the Gambler (1922) and Spies (1928), and Hitchcock's British and Hollywood work from The Lodger (1927) through Psycho (1960) and North by Northwest (1959). The genre overlapped with film noir in the 1940s and 1950s, absorbed Cold War espionage themes in the 1960s, and flourished in paranoid conspiracy form in the 1970s (The Conversation, All the President's Men). Later landmarks include The Silence of the Lambs (1991), Seven (1995), The Sixth Sense (1999) and the psychological thrillers of the 21st century such as Gone Girl (2014).
