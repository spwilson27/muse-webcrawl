Yüksek Hızlı Tren

Yüksek Hızlı Tren (abbreviated YHT, English: High Speed Train) is the high-speed railway service of the Turkish State Railways (TCDD) and its successor operator TCDD Transport, operating on Turkey's high-speed rail network at speeds of up to 250–300 km/h.

History

The first YHT service began on 13 March 2009 between Ankara and Eskişehir, the first section of the Ankara–Istanbul high-speed line, making Turkey the sixth country in Europe and eighth in the world to operate high-speed trains. The full Ankara–Istanbul line opened in 2014, and the Ankara–Konya line opened in 2011. Further lines, including Ankara–Sivas (opened 2023), have extended the network.

Services and stock

YHT services connect Ankara with Istanbul, Eskişehir, Konya, Karaman and Sivas, among other destinations. The fleet includes the HT65000 trainsets built by CAF of Spain and the Siemens Velaro TR (HT80000) trainsets, the latter capable of 300 km/h.

Significance

The YHT network has substantially reduced journey times between Turkey's largest cities — Ankara–Istanbul fell to about four and a half hours — and forms the flagship of a broader programme of railway investment and new line construction across the country.
