Najd (Arabic: نَجْد, "highland") is the geographical central region of Saudi Arabia and of the Arabian Peninsula. It is a rocky plateau sloping eastward from the mountains of the Hejaz, bordered by the great sand deserts of al-Nafud to the north and the Rub' al Khali to the south. Its capital and largest city is Riyadh, which is also the national capital.

Geography

The plateau, at 600–900 metres elevation, is cut by the escarpments of Jabal Tuwaiq and by seasonal watercourses (wadis) such as Wadi Hanifa and Wadi al-Rummah, along which oasis towns — including Buraydah, Unaizah, Hail's southern approaches and the historic capital Diriyah — grew around date palm cultivation and wells.

History

Najd is the homeland of the House of Saud and of the Wahhabi movement: the 1744 alliance at Diriyah between Muhammad ibn Saud and Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab launched the Emirate of Diriyah (the first Saudi state), followed by the Emirate of Najd (second Saudi state, 1824–1891) and, after Abdulaziz Ibn Saud captured Riyadh in 1902, the conquests that unified the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia in 1932. The region's older history includes the kingdom of Kinda and the poetic world of pre-Islamic Arabia celebrated in the Mu'allaqat.

Life in the Najd combined settled oasis agriculture with Bedouin pastoralism; its dialects of Arabic and its traditions of poetry and horsemanship remain central to Saudi identity.
