Niche

An architectural niche containing a statue.

Niche may refer to several concepts in architecture, ecology, business and other fields. The word comes from the French niche, from the Latin nidus, "nest".

Architecture

In architecture, a niche is a recess in a wall, often semicircular in plan and capped by a half-dome, used to display a statue, vase or other ornament. Niches were common features of Roman, Gothic, Renaissance and classical architecture, and similar recesses — such as the mihrab indicating the direction of prayer in a mosque — occur in many building traditions.

Ecology

In ecology, an ecological niche is the role and position a species has in its environment: how it obtains food and shelter, how it interacts with other species, and the range of conditions it can tolerate. The competitive exclusion principle, associated with G. F. Gause, holds that two species cannot long occupy the same niche in the same place. Joseph Grinnell defined the niche in terms of habitat, Charles Elton in terms of functional role, and G. Evelyn Hutchinson formalised it as an n-dimensional hypervolume of environmental conditions.

Business and other uses

In marketing, a niche market is a small, specialised segment of a market targeted by a particular product or service. The word is also used for the carved storage alcoves in catacombs and collective graves, and in computing and archaeology in derivative senses of a small specialised place or role.
