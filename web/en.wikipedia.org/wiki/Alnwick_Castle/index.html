Alnwick Castle

Alnwick Castle is a castle and country house in Alnwick, Northumberland, in the north of England. It is the seat of the Duke of Northumberland, head of the Percy family, and after Windsor Castle is the second-largest inhabited castle in England. The castle sits above the River Aln and has been held by the Percy family for over 700 years.

History

A Norman stronghold was established at Alnwick in the late 11th century by Yves de Vescy, Baron of Alnwick. The Percy family acquired the barony in 1309, and Henry Percy, 1st Baron Percy, rebuilt the castle as a powerful border fortress against the Scots, adding towers and a strong gatehouse. The castle figured in the conflicts between England and Scotland and, in the 15th century, changed hands during the Wars of the Roses.

In the 18th century the 1st Duke and Duchess of Northumberland restored the then-ruinous castle, with Gothic interiors by Robert Adam. In the Victorian era the 4th Duke employed Anthony Salvin to remodel the exterior and the Italian architect Luigi Canina's designs informed the opulent Italianate state rooms, which house paintings by Titian, Canaletto, and others, as well as the famous Cucci cabinet from the Gobelins workshops.

Modern use

The castle is open to the public seasonally and is well known as a filming location: it played Hogwarts in the first two Harry Potter films, and appeared in Robin Hood: Prince of Thieves, Downton Abbey, and Transformers: The Last Knight. The adjacent Alnwick Garden, redeveloped from 2001 by the Duchess of Northumberland, is a major visitor attraction with a large water cascade and a Poison Garden. Since the Second World War part of the castle has hosted a teacher-training college and, from 1981, a study centre of St. Cloud State University, Minnesota.
