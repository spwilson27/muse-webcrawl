Media (region)

Media (Old Persian: Māda; Middle Persian: Mād) is a region of north-western Iran, best known for having been the political and cultural base of the Medes, an ancient Iranian people who established an empire in the 7th–6th centuries BC. The region roughly corresponds to the modern Iranian provinces of Azerbaijan, Kurdistan, and parts of Kermanshah and Hamadan, with its historical capital at Ecbatana (modern Hamadan).

History

The Medes appear in Assyrian records from the 9th century BC. Under Cyaxares (r. 625–585 BC), Media joined Babylon in destroying the Neo-Assyrian Empire, capturing Nineveh in 612 BC, and extended its influence from Anatolia to the Iranian plateau. His son Astyages was overthrown in 550 BC by his grandson Cyrus the Great, after which Media became a constituent part of the Achaemenid Persian Empire, retaining a privileged position; later it formed a satrapy and, in the Hellenistic period, was divided between Media proper and Media Atropatene, the latter giving its name to modern Azerbaijan.

Geography and legacy

Media occupies a mountainous zone of the western Zagros, with fertile valleys historically important for horse breeding — the famous Nisaean horses prized by Persian kings. The Median language, though scarcely attested, belonged to the Northwestern Iranian languages.
