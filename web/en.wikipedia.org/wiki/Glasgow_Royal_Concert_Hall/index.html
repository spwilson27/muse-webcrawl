Glasgow Royal Concert Hall

The Glasgow Royal Concert Hall is a major arts venue in Glasgow, Scotland. It is located at the top of Buchanan Street, near Buchanan Street bus station and Buchanan Galleries shopping centre, in the city centre. The hall was built to replace St Andrew's Hall, which had been destroyed by fire in 1962 and was home to the Royal Scottish National Orchestra.

Construction followed Glasgow's designation as European City of Culture for 1990, and the hall was officially opened in October 1990 with a gala concert attended by the Princess Royal. The main auditorium seats around 2,475 people and is renowned for its acoustics. It is the principal home of the Royal Scottish National Orchestra and a regular venue during the annual Celtic Connections festival.

Facilities

In addition to the main auditorium, the building contains the Strathclyde Suite, the Exhibition Hall, the Lomond and Clyde foyers, and the City of Music Studios. A major expansion, opened in 2015, provided a new home and rehearsal facilities for the Royal Scottish National Orchestra, including a 600-seat New Auditorium.
