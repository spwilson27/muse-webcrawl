Symmetric group

In abstract algebra, the symmetric group defined over any set is the group whose elements are all the bijections from the set to itself, and whose group operation is the composition of functions. In particular, the finite symmetric group Sn is defined over a set of n elements and consists of all n! permutations of those elements.

Definition and basic properties

The symmetric group on a finite set of n elements has order n! (the factorial of n). It is non-abelian for n ≥ 3. By Cayley's theorem, every finite group is isomorphic to a subgroup of some symmetric group, which makes symmetric groups fundamental to group theory.

Generators and structure

Every permutation can be written as a product of transpositions (swaps of two elements); the parity of the number of transpositions is well defined, giving the sign of a permutation. The even permutations form the alternating group An, a normal subgroup of index 2. Symmetric groups are generated by the transpositions, and indeed by a single transposition together with an n-cycle.

The symmetric groups Sn are solvable for n ≤ 4 but not for n ≥ 5, a fact at the heart of the proof that there is no general formula for solving quintic equations by radicals (the Abel–Ruffini theorem).

Applications

Symmetric groups arise throughout mathematics — in Galois theory, combinatorics and representation theory — and in physics, where the symmetrisation of wave functions for identical particles underlies the distinction between bosons and fermions.
