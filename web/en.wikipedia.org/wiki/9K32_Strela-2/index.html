The 9K32 Strela-2 (Russian: Стрела, "arrow"; NATO reporting name SA-7 Grail) is a light-weight, shoulder-fired, surface-to-air missile system of Soviet origin. It was one of the first man-portable air-defense systems (MANPADS) and has been exported all over the world.

Design

The system consists of the 9M32 missile in a disposable launch tube, a reusable gripstock, and a thermal battery. The missile uses passive infrared homing with an uncooled lead-sulphide seeker sensitive primarily to hot engine exhausts, which limits it largely to tail-chase engagements against subsonic targets. The improved Strela-2M, introduced in 1970, added modest all-aspect improvements and greater range and ceiling.

Service history

Strela-2s saw extensive combat from the Vietnam War and the 1973 Yom Kippur War onward, in the Iran–Iraq War, the Falklands, Afghanistan, Angola, and many other conflicts. Millions of rounds were produced in the Soviet Union and under licence in countries including China (as the HN-5), Egypt, Pakistan and Romania.

Proliferation concerns

The weapon's wide availability among non-state actors has made it a persistent concern for civil aviation security, despite its limited effectiveness against modern aircraft equipped with countermeasures.
