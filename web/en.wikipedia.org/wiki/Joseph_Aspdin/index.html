Joseph Aspdin

Joseph Aspdin (December 1778 – 20 March 1855) was an English bricklayer, businessman, and inventor who patented Portland cement in 1824, laying the foundation for the modern cement industry.

Life

Aspdin was the eldest son of a Leeds bricklayer and followed his father's trade. On 21 October 1824 he was granted British Patent No. 5022 for "An Improvement in the Method of Producing an Artificial Stone", in which he described heating a mixture of limestone and clay and grinding the result into a powder — a process he called Portland cement because the hardened material resembled the prized building stone quarried on the Isle of Portland in Dorset.

Portland cement

Aspdin's cement was made by calcining finely ground blends of limestone and clay at moderate temperatures. His son William Aspdin later developed the product further, burning the mixture at higher temperatures to produce clinker with superior strength — the basis of true modern Portland cement — and established works at Northfleet in Kent. Joseph Aspdin himself set up a cement works at Kirkgate in Wakefield, where he lived until his death in 1855.

Portland cement became the most important binding material of modern construction and is today the most common type of cement in general use around the world, the basic ingredient of concrete, mortar, and stucco.
