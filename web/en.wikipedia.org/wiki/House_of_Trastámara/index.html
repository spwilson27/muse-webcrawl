House of Trastámara

The House of Trastámara (Spanish: Casa de Trastámara) was a dynasty that ruled the Crown of Castile from 1369 to 1555, the Crown of Aragon from 1412 to 1516, and at times the kingdoms of Navarre and Naples. It was founded by Henry II of Castile, an illegitimate son of Alfonso XI, who took his name from the Galician county of Trastámara.

Castile

Henry II seized the throne from his half-brother Peter I ("the Cruel") after a civil war in which French and English forces intervened during the Hundred Years' War. His descendants — including John I, Henry III, John II and Henry IV — consolidated royal power amid struggles with the high nobility. The dynasty's most famous rulers were Isabella I of Castile and her daughter Joanna.

Aragon and unification

When Martin I of Aragon died without issue in 1410, the Compromise of Caspe (1412) awarded the Aragonese crown to Ferdinand of Antequera, a Castilian Trastámara prince. His grandson Ferdinand II married Isabella of Castile in 1469; their joint rule as the Catholic Monarchs united the two crowns dynastically, completed the Reconquista with the conquest of Granada in 1492, and sponsored Columbus's voyage to the Americas. Their heir Joanna married Philip of Habsburg, and her son Charles I (Emperor Charles V) brought Spain under the House of Habsburg, ending Trastámara rule in the male line.
