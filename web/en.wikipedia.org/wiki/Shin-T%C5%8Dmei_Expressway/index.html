The Shin-Tōmei Expressway (新東名高速道路, Shin Tōmei Kōsokudōro) is a national expressway in Japan running through the Tōkai region of Honshu, roughly parallel to and inland from the older Tōmei Expressway. It connects Ebina in Kanagawa Prefecture with Nagoya in Aichi Prefecture, passing through Shizuoka Prefecture.

The name "Shin-Tōmei" means "New Tōmei"; together with the Shin-Meishin Expressway it forms a second high-capacity corridor between Tokyo and the Kansai region, relieving congestion on the Tōmei and Meishin expressways and providing redundancy in case of a major earthquake along the Nankai Trough.

History

Construction began in the 1990s under the Japan Highway Public Corporation, and after the 2005 privatization of the highway companies the project was continued by NEXCO Central. The first section, between Gotenba Junction and Mikkabi Junction (with a branch to Shimizu), opened on April 14, 2012. Further sections toward Ebina opened in stages through 2016–2022, progressively completing the route from Ebina to Shin-Shiro and Toyota-higashi in Aichi Prefecture.

Design

The expressway was built to high design standards, with gentle gradients (maximum 2%), wide curves, and three lanes in each direction over much of its length, allowing safer operation and higher speeds than the older Tōmei. It features long tunnels and viaducts through the mountainous interior of Shizuoka, large modern service areas such as NEOPASA facilities, and direct connections to the Tōmei Expressway at several junctions. Together the two expressways operate as a "double network" so traffic can be diverted if one route is closed.
