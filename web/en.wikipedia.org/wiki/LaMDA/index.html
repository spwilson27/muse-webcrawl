LaMDA

LaMDA (Language Model for Dialogue Applications) is a family of large language models developed by Google. Announced at Google I/O in May 2021, LaMDA is built on the Transformer neural network architecture and trained specifically on dialogue, with the goal of producing open-ended conversations that are sensible, interesting, and grounded in factual information.

LaMDA was unveiled at Google I/O 2021

Development

LaMDA was pre-trained on a corpus of documents, dialogue, and utterances totalling over a trillion words, then fine-tuned with crowdsourced annotations on measures Google called SSI (sensibleness, specificity, and interestingness) and on grounding using an information retrieval system. LaMDA 2, announced at I/O 2022, added improved reasoning and was demonstrated in Google's AI Test Kitchen app.

Sentience controversy

In June 2022, Google engineer Blake Lemoine publicly claimed that LaMDA had become sentient after conducting extended interviews with the model about its purported self-awareness, emotions, and rights. The claim drew worldwide media coverage and was widely rejected by AI researchers and by Google, which stated that the evidence did not support sentience and placed Lemoine on leave, later dismissing him. The episode became a prominent case study in debates about anthropomorphism and the ethical treatment of advanced language models.

Legacy

LaMDA was never released as a general public product. Its technology fed into Google's Bard chatbot (launched in 2023 and later renamed Gemini), which was initially powered by a lightweight version of LaMDA before moving to the PaLM and Gemini model families.
