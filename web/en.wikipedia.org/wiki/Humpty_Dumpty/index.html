Humpty Dumpty

Humpty Dumpty is a character in an English nursery rhyme, probably originally a riddle, best known in modern depictions as an anthropomorphic egg. The rhyme is among the most famous in the English language:

Humpty Dumpty sat on a wall,
Humpty Dumpty had a great fall.
All the king's horses and all the king's men
Couldn't put Humpty together again.

Origins and interpretations

The rhyme first appeared in print in the late eighteenth century. As a riddle, its answer — an egg — explains why the fall is irreversible. Various historical theories link the figure to a cannon used in the Siege of Colchester (1648) or to King Richard III. Lewis Carroll popularised the egg character in Through the Looking-Glass (1871), where Humpty Dumpty discusses semantics and poetry with Alice, giving his name to the "Humpty Dumpty theory" of meaning.
