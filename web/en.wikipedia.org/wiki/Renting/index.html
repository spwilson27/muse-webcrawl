Renting

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A property offered for rent.

Renting, also known as hiring or letting, is an agreement where a payment is made for the use of a good or property owned by another over a fixed period of time. The owner is commonly called the landlord or lessor and the user the tenant, renter or lessee. Renting differs from leasing mainly in convention and asset type: leases usually concern longer-term arrangements for land, buildings or equipment, whereas rental often refers to shorter-term hire of movable goods, though usage varies by country and industry.

A wide variety of assets may be rented: real estate (houses, apartments, commercial premises), vehicles (car rental), tools and equipment, furniture, electronics, formal clothing, and media such as films. In housing economics, the rented sector — the private rented sector and social housing together — is one of the principal tenures alongside owner-occupation.

Reasons for renting

  • Cost and capital: renting avoids the large upfront cost of purchase and preserves capital for other uses.
  • Flexibility: short-term needs, temporary relocation, or uncertain demand favour renting over owning.
  • Maintenance: the owner often bears repair and servicing costs (especially in property rental, where the landlord has statutory duties in many jurisdictions).
  • Access and trial: renting provides access to expensive or specialised equipment, and a chance to try goods before buying.
  • Tax and accounting: rental payments may be fully deductible business expenses, and rentals keep assets off the balance sheet in ways leasing sometimes does not.

Rental agreements

A rental is typically governed by a written contract — a lease or tenancy agreement — specifying the rent, duration, permitted use, and responsibilities of each party. For housing, most jurisdictions overlay mandatory protections: security of tenure rules, limits on rent increases or deposits, habitability standards, and procedures for eviction. Short-stay rentals of homes through platforms such as Airbnb have generated separate regulatory debates in many cities.

Economics

In economics, "rent" has a technical meaning (payment to a factor of production above what is needed to keep it in its current use) distinct from the everyday contractual sense used here. In property markets, the price-to-rent ratio is used to compare the relative costs of buying and renting housing. Businesses weigh renting against buying through comparisons of the total cost of ownership, depreciation, obsolescence and residual value.
