Agricultural Bank of China

The Agricultural Bank of China (ABC; Chinese: 中国农业银行), also known as AgBank, is one of the "big four" state-owned commercial banks of the People's Republic of China and one of the largest banks in the world by total assets. Founded in 1951, it provides retail and corporate banking with a particular mandate to serve agriculture and rural areas. Its shares are listed in Shanghai and Hong Kong; at its 2010 initial public offering it raised about US$22 billion, then the largest IPO in history.

History

The bank traces its origin to the Agricultural Cooperative Bank established in 1951, and was repeatedly merged into the People's Bank of China and re-established during the planned-economy era, reflecting shifting policy toward rural finance. The modern Agricultural Bank of China was re-founded in 1979 to support agricultural development and rural reform. In the 2000s it underwent restructuring to clear non-performing loans, was converted into a joint-stock company in 2009 with investment from the Ministry of Finance and Central Huijin, and completed its dual listing in Shanghai and Hong Kong in July 2010.

Operations

ABC operates one of the largest branch networks in China, with particular strength in county and rural markets, alongside corporate banking in major cities and growing international operations. Its business segments include personal banking (deposits, cards, wealth management), corporate banking, and treasury operations. The bank reports total assets in excess of RMB 40 trillion in recent years, serving several hundred million personal customers.

Position

ABC is consistently ranked among the world's largest banks by assets and by Tier 1 capital in The Banker's Top 1000 World Banks, and it is designated a global systemically important bank (G-SIB) by the Financial Stability Board. Together with ICBC, China Construction Bank, and Bank of China it forms China's "big four" state-owned commercial banks.
