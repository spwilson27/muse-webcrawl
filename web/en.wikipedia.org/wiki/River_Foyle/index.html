River Foyle

The River Foyle (Irish: An Feabhal) is a river in west Ulster, in the northwest of Ireland, which flows from the confluence of the rivers Finn and Mourne at the towns of Lifford and Strabane. One of the fastest-flowing rivers in Europe for its size, it travels about 32 km (20 miles) to Lough Foyle and the Atlantic.

Course and crossings

The Foyle is formed where the Finn (flowing from County Donegal in the Republic of Ireland) meets the Mourne (from County Tyrone in Northern Ireland), so the river forms part of the border between the two jurisdictions for part of its length. It flows past the city of Derry, where it is crossed by the Craigavon Bridge — one of very few double-decker road bridges in Europe — the Foyle Bridge, among the longest in Ireland, and the Peace Bridge, a cycle and foot bridge opened in 2011 linking the city centre with Ebrington and the Waterside.

Nature and history

The Foyle system is famous for its salmon fishing and supports one of the largest populations of Atlantic salmon in Europe; the mudflats of Lough Foyle are an important wintering site for wildfowl. Historically the river was the gateway to Derry's port, and during the Second World War Lough Foyle hosted a major naval base used in the Battle of the Atlantic.
