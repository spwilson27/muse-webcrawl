Iskut River

Location: British Columbia, Canada; Alaska, United States

Length: approx. 248 km (154 mi)

Mouth: Stikine River

The Iskut River is a major tributary of the Stikine River in northwestern British Columbia, Canada, and the southeastern Alaska Panhandle. At roughly 248 kilometres long, it is the largest tributary of the Stikine, draining a rugged, glacier-covered portion of the Coast Mountains.

Course

The river rises in the Klappan Range area of the Sacred Headwaters region and flows generally southwest and west through deep canyons, past the community of Iskut, and joins the Stikine near the Canada–United States border. Its watershed includes lava flows associated with the Mount Edziza volcanic complex and hot springs such as the Iskut River Hot Springs.

Ecology and use

The Iskut supports salmon runs important to the Tahltan First Nation and commercial fisheries downstream. The watershed has been the focus of mining exploration, hydroelectric development proposals, and conservation debate, particularly around the broader Sacred Headwaters region.
