The Bernina Express is a panoramic tourist train operated by the Rhaetian Railway in Switzerland. It runs from Chur and St. Moritz through the high Alps to Tirano in Italy, traversing the Albula and Bernina railway lines, which together were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008.

Route

The journey crosses some of the most celebrated railway engineering in the world, including the Landwasser Viaduct, a curving limestone viaduct that emerges directly from a tunnel in a cliff face, and the spiral Brusio spiral viaduct. The train climbs without rack assistance to the Ospizio Bernina station at 2,253 metres above sea level, the highest point on the Rhaetian Railway, passing glaciers, the Lago Bianco reservoir and the Palü Glacier before descending through Valposchiavo to Tirano, where palm trees mark the Italian lowlands.

Service

Bernina Express trains feature panoramic coaches with enlarged windows and multilingual commentary. A seat reservation is compulsory in addition to the ticket. The route is also served by ordinary regional trains using the same spectacular line, allowing travellers to stop at intermediate stations. Unlike the Glacier Express, the Bernina Express runs over metre-gauge track for its entire length and changes electrical systems — from alternating current on the Albula line to direct current on the Bernina line — with dual-voltage rolling stock.
