Midyat

Midyat
Country: Turkey
Province: Mardin
Region: Southeastern Anatolia
Population: approx. 120,000 (district)

Midyat (Syriac: ܡܕܝܕ, Mëḏyaḏ; Kurdish: Midyad) is a city and district of Mardin Province in southeastern Turkey. It is one of the principal towns of the Tur Abdin plateau, a historic heartland of the Syriac Orthodox Church, and is noted for its honey-coloured limestone architecture, silversmithing (telkâri filigree) and historic churches and monasteries.

History

The Tur Abdin region ("Mountain of the Servants of God") has been a centre of Syriac Christianity since late antiquity. Midyat grew around its churches and the nearby Mor Gabriel Monastery, founded in 397 AD and one of the oldest functioning monasteries in the world. In 1915 the Assyrian genocide (Sayfo) devastated the Christian population of the area; the town's Syriac community re-established itself, and Midyat became a place of refuge during attacks elsewhere. From the 1980s many Syriac families emigrated to Europe, though some have since returned.

Geography and culture

Midyat lies at about 1,000 metres elevation on a limestone plateau east of Mardin. The old town is distinguished by ornate carved stone houses, bell towers and cave dwellings. The population is mixed, including Arabs, Kurds, and Syriac Christians, with Syriac (Turoyo), Arabic and Kurdish spoken. The town's telkari silver filigree jewellery and its vineyards and wine-making tradition are regionally renowned.
