Operation Epsom, also known as the First Battle of the Odon, was a British offensive in the Second World War between 26 and 30 June 1944, during the Battle of Normandy. The offensive was intended to outflank and seize the German-occupied city of Caen from the west — an important Allied objective in the early stages of the invasion of north-west Europe — by crossing the Odon and Orne rivers, capturing high ground and encircling the city.

The offensive

The attack was made by VIII Corps of the British Second Army, commanded by Lieutenant-General Richard O'Connor, spearheaded by the 15th (Scottish), 43rd (Wessex) and 11th Armoured divisions. Preceded by a powerful artillery barrage and supported by naval gunfire, the infantry advanced through the corn fields west of Caen, forcing crossings of the Odon on 27 June and establishing a bridgehead, later known as the "Scottish Corridor". Armour pushed on towards the Orne and the commanding Hill 112.

German counter-attacks

The offensive drew in the newly arrived II SS Panzer Corps (9th and 10th SS Panzer divisions). Fierce German counter-attacks between 29 June and 1 July failed to eliminate the salient but halted further British advance; the 11th Armoured Division withdrew from Hill 112 to shorten the line. Both sides suffered heavy casualties — the British around 4,000 and the Germans over 3,000, with dozens of tanks lost on each side.

Assessment

Epsom did not capture Caen, which remained in German hands until mid-July, but it created a threatening salient, inflicted serious attrition on the German panzer divisions and prevented them from being used against the American sector or in a coordinated counter-offensive. The fighting around Hill 112 resumed in Operation Jupiter in July.
