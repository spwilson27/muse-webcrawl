Rain chain

Rain chains are functional and decorative alternatives to downspouts for directing rainwater from a roof gutter to the ground or a storage vessel. They originated in Japan, where they are known as kusari-doi (鎖樋, literally "chain gutter"), and have been used for centuries on houses and temples.

Design

A rain chain hangs vertically from the gutter outlet. Water follows the chain downward by surface tension and adhesion. Two main styles exist: link chains, made of interlocking loops of metal wire, and cup chains, in which a series of small open-bottomed cups or funnel shapes catches and slows the water, producing a more controlled flow and a gentle sound in heavy rain. Chains are commonly made of copper, which develops a patina, but also of aluminium, stainless steel or brass.

Use

At the foot of the chain, water is typically delivered into a basin, barrel, drainage grate or a decorative dish (sometimes filled with stones) to prevent splashing and erosion. Rain chains perform best with moderate rainfall; in downpours some water may escape the chain. They are valued in garden and tea-house design for the visual and auditory pleasure of moving water, an aesthetic purpose as much as a drainage one.
