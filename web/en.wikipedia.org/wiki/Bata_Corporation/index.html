Bata Corporation

The Bata Corporation (Czech: Baťa) is a multinational footwear, apparel, and fashion accessories manufacturer and retailer founded in 1894 in Zlín, then in the Austro-Hungarian Empire (now the Czech Republic), by Tomáš Baťa and his siblings Antonín and Anna. It grew into one of the world's largest shoemakers, pioneering mass production, company towns, and global retail networks.

History

Tomáš Baťa revolutionised shoemaking with assembly-line production, standardised pricing (prices famously ending in 9), and vertically integrated factories that included housing, schools, and shops for workers — the model town of Zlín became a landmark of modernist industrial planning. After his death in a 1932 plane crash, his half-brother Jan Antonín Baťa expanded the company abroad before the Second World War and the communist nationalisation of the Czech operations in 1945. The international business was rebuilt from Canada under Thomas J. Bata, Tomáš's son, who fled Czechoslovakia in 1939 and established Batawa, Ontario.

Global presence

Bata operates thousands of stores across Europe, Africa, Asia, and Latin America, and is a household name in countries such as India — where Bata India is a major listed company — Indonesia, and Kenya. The Bata Shoe Museum in Toronto, founded by Sonja Bata, holds one of the world's largest footwear collections. The company remains largely family-influenced and is noted for its slogan "We put shoes on the world."
