Gulab Singh

Gulab Singh (1792–1857)
Maharaja of Jammu and Kashmir
Reign: 1846–1856
Founder of the Dogra dynasty

Gulab Singh (18 October 1792 – 30 June 1857) was the founder of the Dogra dynasty and the first Maharaja of the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir, the second-largest princely state in British India. He was a Dogra Rajput general of the Sikh Empire under Maharaja Ranjit Singh.

Early life and rise

Gulab Singh was born into a Dogra Rajput family in the Jammu region. He joined the army of Ranjit Singh as a young cavalry trooper and rose rapidly through ability and loyalty, distinguishing himself in campaigns in Multan, Kashmir, and the trans-Indus frontier. By the 1820s he and his brothers Dhian Singh and Suchet Singh had become among the most powerful nobles at the Lahore court. Ranjit Singh granted Gulab Singh the jagir of Jammu, and in 1822 he was anointed Raja of Jammu.

Expansion in the hills

From Jammu, Gulab Singh's generals — most famously Zorawar Singh — conquered Ladakh, Baltistan, and vast territories in the western Himalayas during the 1830s and early 1840s, campaigns that brought Dogra arms as far as western Tibet. These conquests laid the territorial foundation of the later state.

Treaty of Amritsar and the state of Jammu and Kashmir

After the First Anglo-Sikh War, Gulab Singh negotiated separately with the British. Under the Treaty of Amritsar of 16 March 1846, the British transferred to him the hill territories between the Ravi and the Indus, including Kashmir, for a payment of 75 lakh rupees. He thus became Maharaja of Jammu and Kashmir, ruling a state that joined together regions of great religious and ethnic diversity.

Death and succession

Gulab Singh abdicated in 1856 in favour of his son Ranbir Singh and died in 1857. His descendants ruled Jammu and Kashmir until the accession to India in 1947.
