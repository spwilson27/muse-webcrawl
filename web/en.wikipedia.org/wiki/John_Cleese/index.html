John Marwood Cleese (born 27 October 1939) is an English actor, comedian, screenwriter, and producer. He achieved success at the Edinburgh Festival Fringe and as a scriptwriter and performer on The Frost Report. In the late 1960s he co-founded Monty Python, the comedy troupe responsible for the sketch show Monty Python's Flying Circus. Along with his Python co-stars, Cleese starred in the films Monty Python and the Holy Grail (1975), Life of Brian (1979) and The Meaning of Life (1983).

Early life and Cambridge

Cleese was born in Weston-super-Mare, Somerset, the only child of Reginald Francis Cleese, an insurance salesman, and Muriel Evelyn Cross. He studied law at Downing College, Cambridge, where he joined the Cambridge Footlights and met future Python collaborator Graham Chapman.

Monty Python

Formed in 1969 with Chapman, Eric Idle, Terry Jones, Michael Palin, and Terry Gilliam, Monty Python revolutionised sketch comedy with surreal, stream-of-consciousness humour. Cleese was especially noted for sketches such as the "Ministry of Silly Walks", the "Dead Parrot sketch" and the furious ranting characters he later developed elsewhere.

Fawlty Towers and film

With his then-wife Connie Booth, Cleese co-wrote and starred in the sitcom Fawlty Towers (1975–1979) as the misanthropic hotelier Basil Fawlty; the series is frequently cited among the greatest British television programmes of all time. He co-wrote and starred in the comedy film A Fish Called Wanda (1988), for which he was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and appeared in two James Bond films as Q.

Other work

Cleese co-founded the production company Video Arts, which made celebrated management training films, and wrote books on psychology with psychiatrist Robin Skynner. He has been a visiting professor at Cornell University and remains a prominent, often controversial, public commentator.
