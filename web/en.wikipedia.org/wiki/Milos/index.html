Milos

Milos
Country: Greece
Archipelago: Cyclades
Area: 160 km²
Known for: Venus de Milo, obsidian, volcanic beaches

Milos (Greek: Μήλος) is a volcanic Greek island in the Aegean Sea, the southwesternmost of the Cyclades group. It is famous as the place where the statue of Aphrodite known as the Venus de Milo was found in 1820.

History

Milos was inhabited from the Neolithic era; its obsidian was traded across the Aegean in prehistory. The Bronze Age settlement of Phylakopi was an important Minoan-Mycenaean centre. In 415 BC Athens attacked the neutral island and, as Thucydides recounts, killed the men and enslaved the women and children after the siege of Melos — a backdrop to the famous Melian Dialogue.

Geography and economy

The horseshoe-shaped island surrounds a flooded caldera bay. Mining of bentonite, perlite and other minerals remains important, alongside tourism drawn to beaches such as Sarakiniko.
