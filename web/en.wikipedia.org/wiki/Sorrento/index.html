Sorrento

Sorrento (Italian: [sorˈrɛnto]; Neapolitan: Surriento) is a resort town in Campania, southern Italy, located on the Sorrentine Peninsula overlooking the Bay of Naples. It is a popular tourist destination, known for its dramatic cliffs, views across to Mount Vesuvius and the island of Capri, and its lemon groves. The town has a population of around 16,000.

Geography

Sorrento sits atop steep tufa cliffs above its small harbours, Marina Grande and Marina Piccola. It is connected to Naples by the Circumvesuviana railway and by ferry services across the bay, and forms the starting point of the Amalfi Coast road to Positano and Amalfi.

History

The site was settled by the Greeks as Surrentum and later became a Roman resort town, favoured for villas by wealthy Romans. In the Middle Ages it passed between Byzantine, Lombard and Norman control, and in 1544 the poet Torquato Tasso was born there. From the nineteenth century Sorrento became a celebrated stop on the Grand Tour and a fashionable resort visited by writers and musicians including Ibsen, Nietzsche and Wagner's circle. The famous Neapolitan song "Torna a Surriento" ("Come Back to Sorrento"), composed in 1902, is named for the town.

Economy and culture

Tourism is the mainstay of the economy. Local products include limoncello liqueur made from the large Sorrento lemons (a protected geographical indication), olive oil, wine, walnuts, and inlaid woodwork (intarsia), a craft practised in the town since the nineteenth century. Landmarks include the fourteenth-century cloister of San Francesco, the cathedral, and the Museo Correale di Terranova.
