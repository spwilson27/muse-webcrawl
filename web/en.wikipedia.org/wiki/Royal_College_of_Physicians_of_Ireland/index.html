Royal College of Physicians of Ireland

Established: 1654
Type: Professional medical body
Headquarters: No. 6 Kildare Street, Dublin, Ireland
President: elected from the fellowship

The Royal College of Physicians of Ireland (RCPI) is an Irish professional body dedicated to improving the practice of medicine, chiefly through the training of doctors. It is one of the six postgraduate training bodies recognised by the Medical Council of Ireland.

History

The college traces its origin to a fraternity of physicians founded in Dublin in 1654 under the leadership of John Stearne. It received royal charters in the 17th century, including a charter from William and Mary in 1692 establishing the King and Queen's College of Physicians in Ireland. Its home since the 1860s has been the landmark building at No. 6 Kildare Street, Dublin.

Activities

The RCPI runs postgraduate training programmes in internal medicine and its subspecialties, occupational medicine, obstetrics, paediatrics (in association), pathology and public health, and admits members and fellows by examination (MRCPI) and election. It also maintains a heritage centre preserving its historic library and archives.
