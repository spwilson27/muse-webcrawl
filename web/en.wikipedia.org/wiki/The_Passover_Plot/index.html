The Passover Plot

AuthorHugh J. Schonfield
SubjectHistorical Jesus
PublisherBernard Geis Associates; Hutchinson
Published1965

The Passover Plot is a controversial 1965 book by British biblical scholar Hugh J. Schonfield, who had worked on the Dead Sea Scrolls. Subtitled "a new interpretation of the life and death of Jesus," it became a bestseller, selling millions of copies and was adapted into a 1976 film of the same name starring Zalman King as Jesus.

Thesis

Schonfield argued that Jesus was a brilliant, deeply observant Jewish teacher who, convinced from extensive study of the prophecies that he was the expected Messiah, deliberately set out to fulfill the scriptural predictions in his own lifetime — including the manner and timing of his death. In Schonfield's reconstruction, Jesus planned his crucifixion for Passover, arranged for a drug administered on a sponge to make him appear dead after a few hours on the cross, and intended to be taken down alive and revive — a "swoon" type theory. The plan went wrong, he suggested, when a Roman soldier pierced Jesus's side with a lance, actually killing him; the resurrection appearances were therefore mistaken identity or visions.

Reception

The book was denounced by many Christians and rejected by most scholars of the historical Jesus, who criticized its speculative chain of conjectures and its treatment of the Gospel texts as a cipher to be decoded. Schonfield, who founded the Commonwealth of World Citizens and served as its president, published a sequel, Those Incredible Christians (1968), and maintained his interpretation was a historical, not theological, argument. The book remains a famous example of popular revisionist writing about Jesus, influencing the genre of conspiracy-flavored bestsellers about early Christianity.
