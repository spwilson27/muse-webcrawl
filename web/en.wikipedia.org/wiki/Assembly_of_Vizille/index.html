The Assembly of Vizille (French: Assemblée de Vizille) was a meeting of deputies from the three estates of the province of Dauphiné held on 21 July 1788 at the Château de Vizille, south of Grenoble. It is regarded as one of the opening acts of the French Revolution, the first occasion on which representatives of clergy, nobility and commoners deliberated together and demanded fundamental reform of the French monarchy.

Background

The meeting grew out of the confrontation between the crown and the parlement of Grenoble, which had resisted royal attempts to impose new taxes and curtail its powers. On 7 June 1788 — the "Day of the Tiles" (Journée des Tuiles) — crowds in Grenoble pelted royal troops with roof tiles to prevent the exile of the magistrates. Prominent lawyers and notables, among them Jean Joseph Mounier and Antoine Barnave, organised an assembly of the province despite a royal ban on such meetings.

The assembly and its demands

Some 540 deputies — nobles, clergy and, in the largest numbers, third-estate commoners — met in the salle du Jeu de Paume of the château, lent by the industrialist Claude Perier. The assembly demanded the restoration of the provincial estates of Dauphiné with freely elected deputies, voting by head rather than by order, doubling of third-estate representation, equality of taxation among all citizens, and the calling of the Estates General of the kingdom. It declared that taxes voted without consent were null.

Significance

Within weeks, the crown capitulated on the essential point: in August 1788 Louis XVI announced the convocation of the Estates General for May 1789. The Vizille programme — representation, fiscal equality, constitutional government — anticipated the demands made across France in the cahiers of 1789. The Château de Vizille now houses the Museum of the French Revolution, the only museum devoted entirely to that period.
