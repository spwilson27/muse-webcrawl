Talyllyn Railway

The Talyllyn Railway (Welsh: Rheilffordd Talyllyn) is a narrow gauge preserved railway in Wales, running for 7.25 miles (11.67 km) from Tywyn on the coast of Cardigan Bay to Nant Gwernol near the village of Abergynolwyn. Opened in 1865 to carry slate from the quarries at Bryn Eglwys to Tywyn, it has a track gauge of 2 ft 3 in (686 mm).

History

The line was built to serve the slate quarry at Bryn Eglwys, owned by the McConnel family. Unlike most Welsh slate railways, it was authorised to carry passengers from the outset. Declining slate traffic and the death of its owner, Sir Henry Haydn Jones, in 1950 put the railway in danger of closure.

Preservation

In 1951 the Talyllyn Railway Preservation Society took over the line, making it the world's first railway to be preserved and operated by volunteers — a landmark that inspired the heritage railway movement worldwide. The rescue was famously championed by author Tom Rolt, and the line's story partly inspired the Rev. W. Awdry's Railway Series books, in which the Talyllyn appears in thinly disguised form as the Skarloey Railway.

Locomotives

The railway's original locomotives, Talyllyn (1864) and Dolgoch (1866), remain in service. The fleet later grew with locomotives from the Corris Railway and other sources, many renamed after characters in Awdry's books.
