Abridgement

Abridgement
An abridged edition of a classic novel
TypeCondensed literary work
PurposeShortening a text while retaining its substance

An abridgement (or abridgment) is a condensing or shortening of a book or other creative work into a shorter form while maintaining the unity of the source. The abridgement keeps the main elements of the work, often summarising or eliminating less important passages.

Types

Abridgements may be prepared for length, for younger or less experienced readers, or for new formats. Abridged audiobooks condense the text to reduce listening time, while "unabridged" editions retain the full text. In law, an abridgment historically referred to a compendium or digest summarising case law, such as the great English abridgments of cases.

Practice

Techniques of abridgement include omitting subplots and digressions, paraphrasing lengthy descriptions, and compressing dialogue. Abridged classics are common in school editions, though purists sometimes criticize them for distorting the author's work.
