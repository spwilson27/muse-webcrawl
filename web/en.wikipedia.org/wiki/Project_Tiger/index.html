Project Tiger

Project Tiger is a wildlife conservation initiative launched by the Government of India on 1 April 1973 to protect the Bengal tiger and its habitat. At the time of its launch, India's tiger population had fallen to roughly 1,800 animals from an estimated 40,000 a century earlier, owing to hunting, poaching, and habitat loss. The project began with nine tiger reserves covering about 18,278 km² and was initially centred on Corbett National Park.

Administration

Project Tiger is administered by the National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA), a statutory body established in 2005 under the Wildlife (Protection) Act. Tiger reserves consist of a core area, kept free of human activity, and a buffer zone with regulated use. By the 2020s the network had grown to more than fifty reserves across India.

Results

India's tiger censuses, conducted every four years, recorded recovery from 1,411 tigers in 2006 to about 3,167 in 2022, making India home to the majority of the world's wild tigers. Challenges remain, including poaching, human–wildlife conflict, and habitat fragmentation.
