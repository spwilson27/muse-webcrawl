Psychoactive drug

A psychoactive drug, psychopharmaceutical, or psychotropic drug is a chemical substance that changes nervous system function and results in alterations in perception, mood, consciousness, cognition or behaviour. These substances may be used medically, recreationally, or ritually.

Classes

Major classes include stimulants (caffeine, nicotine, amphetamine, cocaine), depressants (alcohol, benzodiazepines, opioids), hallucinogens or psychedelics (LSD, psilocybin, mescaline), dissociatives, inhalants, and cannabinoids. Many psychiatric medicines — antidepressants, antipsychotics, anxiolytics and mood stabilisers — are psychoactive.

Mechanisms and effects

Most act by mimicking, blocking or modulating neurotransmitters such as dopamine, serotonin, GABA and glutamate, altering synaptic signalling. Effects depend on dose, set and setting; repeated use can produce tolerance, dependence and withdrawal, central considerations in drug regulation and scheduling systems worldwide.

History

Humans have used plant psychoactives for millennia. Modern psychopharmacology began in the 1950s with chlorpromazine and the first antidepressants, transforming psychiatry, while prohibitions dating to the early 20th century govern many recreational substances.
