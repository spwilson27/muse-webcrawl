Doctor Sleep (novel)

Doctor Sleep is a 2013 horror novel by American writer Stephen King, a sequel to his 1977 novel The Shining. The book reached No. 1 on the New York Times Best Seller list. It follows grown-up Dan Torrance, who uses his psychic "shining" while working in a hospice, and must protect a young girl, Abra Stone, from the True Knot, a cult that feeds on children with the shining.

Plot

Traumatised Dan Torrance descends into alcoholism like his father, then recovers in New Hampshire, joining AA and working at a hospice where he comforts dying patients — earning the nickname "Doctor Sleep". Abra, a girl with an extraordinarily powerful shining, telepathically contacts him. The True Knot, led by Rose the Hat, are near-immortal beings who sustain themselves on "steam" released by tortured shining children. Dan and Abra confront them, culminating at the site of the destroyed Overlook Hotel in Colorado.

Reception and adaptation

The novel won the 2013 Bram Stoker Award for Best Novel. A film adaptation directed by Mike Flanagan, starring Ewan McGregor, was released in 2019, serving as a sequel to both King's novel and Stanley Kubrick's 1980 film.
