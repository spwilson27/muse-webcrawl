Easter, also called Pascha (from Aramaic/Hebrew Pesach, Passover), is a Christian festival and cultural holiday commemorating the resurrection of Jesus from the dead, described in the New Testament as having occurred on the third day of his burial following his crucifixion by the Romans at Calvary c. 30 AD. It is the culmination of the Passion of Jesus, preceded by Lent, a 40-day period of fasting, prayer and penance, and by Holy Week, which includes Maundy Thursday and Good Friday.

Date

Easter is a moveable feast: it is not fixed in relation to the civil calendar. The First Council of Nicaea (325) established that Easter would be held on the first Sunday after the first full moon occurring on or after the spring equinox. In Western Christianity Easter therefore falls between 22 March and 25 April; Eastern Orthodox churches use the Julian calendar for the calculation, so their Easter usually falls later. The difference in dates is a longstanding matter of ecumenical discussion.

Customs

Easter customs vary across the Christian world and include sunrise services, the Easter Vigil, decorating and hunting for Easter eggs (symbols of new life and the empty tomb), parades, and festive foods such as hot cross buns, simnel cake and roast lamb. The Easter Bunny, a folkloric figure who delivers eggs to children, originated among German Lutherans and became popular in the United States. In many cultures, egg rolling, egg tapping and pace egging accompany the season. See also resurrection of Jesus, Good Friday and Lent.
