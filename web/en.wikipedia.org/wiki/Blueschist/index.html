Blueschist, also called glaucophane schist, is a metavolcanic rock formed by the metamorphism of basalt and rocks with similar composition at high pressures and low temperatures (200–500 °C, 6–20 kbar), conditions typical of subduction zones at depths of roughly 15 to 30 kilometres.

Mineralogy

The blue colour comes from the dominant presence of the amphibole glaucophane. Blueschist facies rocks characteristically contain lawsonite, jadeite, aragonite, phengite and garnet, while lacking plagioclase and biotite. The assemblage records pressures too high for the temperature reached, implying a very low geothermal gradient along the subducting slab.

Distribution

Blueschists occur in the Franciscan Complex of California, the Cyclades of Greece (including Syros and Sifnos), the Sanbagawa belt of Japan, New Caledonia, and the Alps. Their presence at the surface requires rapid exhumation, and they are key evidence for ancient subduction.
