History of Mexico

The history of Mexico spans the great pre-Columbian civilisations of Mesoamerica, three centuries of Spanish colonial rule as New Spain, independence in 1821, revolution in 1910, and the modern Mexican republic.

Pre-Columbian era

Mexico was home to successive civilisations: the Olmec (from c. 1200 BCE), often called the mother culture; the Maya of the Yucatán and Chiapas; the Zapotec of Monte Albán; Teotihuacan, one of the ancient world's largest cities; the Toltec; and finally the Mexica (Aztec), whose capital Tenochtitlan, founded in 1325, was among the largest cities on earth when the Spanish arrived.

Conquest and New Spain

Hernán Cortés landed in 1519 and, allied with Tlaxcala and other enemies of the Aztecs, conquered Tenochtitlan in 1521 after the death of Moctezuma II and a devastating smallpox epidemic. As the Viceroyalty of New Spain, Mexico became the centre of Spain's American empire, rich in silver from Zacatecas and Guanajuato, administered from Mexico City and structured around the encomienda and later hacienda systems and the Catholic Church.

Independence and the 19th century

Miguel Hidalgo's Grito de Dolores (1810) launched the independence struggle, consummated in 1821 by Agustín de Iturbide's Plan of Iguala. The early republic saw chronic instability, the loss of Texas (1836), defeat by the United States in the Mexican–American War (1846–1848) — which cost Mexico half its territory — the Reform era of Benito Juárez, and French intervention, ended with the execution of Emperor Maximilian in 1867. The Porfiriato (1876–1911) under Porfirio Díaz brought foreign investment and order at the price of inequality.

Revolution and modern Mexico

The Mexican Revolution (1910–1920) toppled Díaz and produced the Constitution of 1917, but cost perhaps a tenth of the population. The party founded in 1929, eventually the PRI, governed Mexico for 71 years. The 20th century brought land reform, oil nationalisation (1938), industrialisation and the 1968 Tlatelolco massacre. Vicente Fox's victory in 2000 ended one-party rule; since 2006 the state has waged a conflict against drug cartels. Andrés Manuel López Obrador won the presidency in 2018, and Claudia Sheinbaum became Mexico's first woman president in 2024.
