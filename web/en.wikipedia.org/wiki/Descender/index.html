Descender

In typography, a descender is the portion of a letterform that extends below the baseline of a font. In most Latin-alphabet typefaces, the lowercase letters g, j, p, q, and y have descenders, as do some letters in other scripts and certain uppercase forms in older styles. The corresponding part that extends above the x-height is the ascender, and the depth of the descender is a characteristic feature of a typeface's design, affecting line spacing (leading).

Description

The descender occupies the space between the baseline and the descender line. Typefaces with long descenders require more vertical room per line; condensed text faces often use short descenders to fit more lines on a page. In some fonts, descenders take decorative forms, such as the swash tail of an italic Q or the looped tail of a cursive g. The letter j combines a descender with a tittle (dot).

In other contexts

In climbing and caving, a descender is also a friction device (such as a figure-eight descender or a rack) used to control descent on a rope during abseiling (rappelling).
