Paralympic sports

Paralympic sports are the organized competitive sports contested at the Paralympic Games by athletes with physical, visual, and intellectual impairments. Governed under the International Paralympic Committee (IPC) and the respective international federations, the programme comprises more than twenty summer sports and about half a dozen winter sports, with events organized through a system of sport classes based on how an athlete's impairment affects performance.

Summer sports

The Summer Paralympic programme includes athletics (track and field, the largest sport), swimming, wheelchair basketball, wheelchair rugby, sitting volleyball, para cycling, para powerlifting, goalball (a team sport for visually impaired athletes played with a ball containing bells), boccia (for athletes with severe physical impairments), para judo, para table tennis, wheelchair tennis, para archery, para shooting, para canoe, para triathlon, para rowing, para taekwondo, para badminton, football 5-a-side (blind football), and wheelchair fencing, among others.

Winter sports

Winter Paralympic sports include para alpine skiing, para cross-country skiing, biathlon, para snowboard, wheelchair curling, and para ice hockey (formerly ice sledge hockey).

Classification

Because impairments affect sports differently, athletes are grouped into sport classes after evaluation of their eligible impairment — such as impaired muscle power, limb deficiency, vision impairment, or intellectual impairment. Classification aims to minimize the impact of impairment on competitive outcome so that success is determined by skill, fitness, and tactics. Some sports, like goalball and blind football, are specific to particular impairment groups.

History

Organized sport for people with disabilities grew from rehabilitation programmes after the Second World War, notably Dr Ludwig Guttmann's Stoke Mandeville Games (1948) for injured veterans. The first Paralympic Games were held in Rome in 1960, and the programme has expanded steadily in size, professionalism, and global attention since.
