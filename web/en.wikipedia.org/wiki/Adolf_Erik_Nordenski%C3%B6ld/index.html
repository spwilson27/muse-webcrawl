Baron Nils Adolf Erik Nordenskiöld (18 November 1832 – 12 August 1901) was a Finland-Swedish aristocrat, geologist, mineralogist, and Arctic explorer. He is best known for the Vega expedition (1878–1879), in which his ship Vega completed the first successful navigation of the Northeast Passage from the Atlantic to the Pacific along the Siberian coast.

Exploration

Nordenskiöld led a series of expeditions to Spitsbergen and Greenland, conducting geological surveys and collecting natural history specimens. He also undertook expeditions along Siberia's Yenisei River, demonstrating a potential trade route between Europe and Siberia.

The Vega expedition

Departing Sweden in 1878, the Vega rounded Cape Chelyuskin and became icebound near the Bering Strait for ten months before completing the passage in 1879, arriving to great acclaim. Nordenskiöld later became a member of the Swedish Academy and a noted collector of early maps and geographical works; the Nordenskiöld Archipelago in the Kara Sea is named after him.
