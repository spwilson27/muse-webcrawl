Officer (armed forces)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Officers hold a commission from the head of state.

An officer is a person holding a position of authority as a member of an armed force or uniformed service. Broadly, personnel in the armed forces divide into three categories: commissioned officers, who hold a commission from the head of state granting them command authority; warrant officers, senior specialists holding a warrant; and enlisted personnel (other ranks), within whom non-commissioned officers (NCOs) — corporals and sergeants — exercise delegated leadership. Officers typically manage, plan and lead units, while NCOs supervise the execution of tasks by enlisted soldiers.

Commissioned officers

A commission is a formal document, issued in the name of the sovereign or president, appointing an individual as an officer and conferring the legal authority to command. Officers are usually trained at military academies (such as Sandhurst, West Point or Saint-Cyr) or through university-based reserve officer training programmes, and most hold university degrees. Commissioned ranks in most armies ascend from junior officers (lieutenants and captains) through field officers (majors, lieutenant colonels and colonels) to general officers. Naval ranks follow a parallel structure of ensigns, lieutenants, commanders, captains, commodores and admirals.

Roles and responsibilities

Officers bear legal and moral responsibility for the personnel under their command and for the lawful execution of orders. Their duties include planning operations, making tactical and strategic decisions, training and administration, and specialist staff or professional work in branches such as engineering, medicine, law and logistics. The proportion of officers in modern armed forces varies by country and service, typically between roughly 10 and 20 per cent of total strength, with air forces and technical services tending to have higher proportions.

Non-commissioned and warrant officers

Non-commissioned officers are enlisted personnel promoted into leadership grades; they form the backbone of discipline and day-to-day training in most militaries. Warrant officers, found in many but not all armed forces, are technical specialists who rank between NCOs and commissioned officers; in the British system warrant officers are the most senior other ranks, while in the United States they are appointed by warrant and often serve as pilots and technical experts.
