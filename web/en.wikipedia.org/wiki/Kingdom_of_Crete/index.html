The Kingdom of Crete (Venetian: Regno di Candia) was the name of the island of Crete during its period as an overseas colony of the Republic of Venice, from the initial Venetian conquest in 1205–1212 until its fall to the Ottoman Empire during the Cretan War (1645–1669). It was Venice's largest and richest overseas possession.

Conquest and organization

Venice acquired its claim to Crete from Boniface of Montferrat after the Fourth Crusade's sack of Constantinople in 1204, bought the island, and fought off Genoese and local rivals to secure it by about 1212. The island was organised as a feudal colony modelled on Venice itself: governed by a Duke of Candia (modern Heraklion), divided into fiefs granted to Venetian military colonists, and administered from the capital while the Greek population was subordinated under Latin Catholic rule.

Revolts and society

Greek revolts recurred throughout the 13th and 14th centuries, most seriously the Revolt of Saint Titus (1363–1366), in which Venetian Cretan feudatories themselves rebelled over taxation. Over time a mixed Greco-Venetian culture emerged. The later centuries of Venetian rule saw a remarkable cultural flowering—the Cretan Renaissance—producing painters such as El Greco (Domenikos Theotokopoulos, trained as an icon painter in Candia) and literature in the Cretan dialect, including the romance Erotokritos by Vitsentzos Kornaros.

Ottoman conquest

The Ottoman invasion of 1645 opened the Cretan War. The Ottomans overran most of the island within a few years, but Candia itself, behind massive Venetian fortifications, withstood one of the longest sieges in history—21 years—before the Venetian commander Francesco Morosini negotiated its surrender in September 1669. Venice retained only a few island fortresses (Gramvousa, Souda, Spinalonga) until 1715.

Legacy

Venetian rule left Crete a rich architectural heritage of fortresses, harbours, and loggias, and profoundly shaped the island's culture, dialect, and cuisine. The term "Kingdom of Crete" (Regno di Candia) reflected the colony's formal status within the Venetian maritime empire (the Stato da Màr).
