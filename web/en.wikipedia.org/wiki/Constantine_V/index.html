Constantine V (Greek: Κωνσταντῖνος, romanized: Kōnstantînos; 718 – 14 September 775) was Byzantine emperor from 741 to 775. His reign was dominated by his fervent promotion of iconoclasm, the religious policy begun by his father Leo III, and by sustained warfare against the Arabs in the east and the Bulgars in the Balkans. Hostile iconophile sources gave him the scurrilous epithet Kopronymos ("the Dung-named"), alleging that he had defecated in his baptismal font as an infant, but modern historians regard him as one of the most capable soldier-emperors of the eighth century.

Early life and accession

Constantine was born in Constantinople in 718, the son of Leo III and his wife Maria. He was crowned co-emperor in 720 and married Irene, daughter of the Khazar khagan, in 732. When Leo III died on 18 June 741, Constantine succeeded as sole emperor, but within weeks his brother-in-law Artabasdos, count of the Opsikion theme, seized Constantinople. Artabasdos restored the veneration of icons and held the capital for over two years before Constantine defeated him, retook the city in November 743, and had his rival blinded.

Iconoclasm

Constantine was a committed iconoclast who wrote theological treatises defending the policy. In 754 he convened the Council of Hieria, attended by 338 bishops, which condemned the making and veneration of religious images as heretical. After the council he moved against monasticism, which he saw as the backbone of iconophile resistance: monasteries were secularised, monks were publicly humiliated, and Stephen the Younger, an influential monastic adviser, was martyred in 765. The severity of these measures coloured his reputation in later Byzantine chronicles such as that of Theophanes the Confessor.

Wars

Constantine exploited civil war within the Umayyad Caliphate to recover ground in the east, capturing Germanikeia and other frontier fortresses and resettling their populations in Thrace. In the Balkans he campaigned repeatedly against the Bulgar khanate, winning a major victory at Anchialos in 763. His expeditions of the 770s, including the use of large fleets to move cavalry up the Black Sea coast, kept Bulgar power in check, though a Byzantine fleet was destroyed by storm in 766 with heavy loss of life.

Administration and Constantinople

After plague devastated Constantinople in 747, Constantine repopulated the city by transferring inhabitants from Greece and the Aegean islands. He restored the Aqueduct of Valens in 766, employing thousands of masons, plasterers and labourers levied from across the empire, and repaired the Church of Hagia Irene after an earthquake.

Death and succession

Constantine died on 14 September 775 while campaigning against the Bulgars, reportedly of a fever that inflamed ulcers on his legs. He was succeeded by his son by Irene of Khazaria, Leo IV, known as "the Khazar".
