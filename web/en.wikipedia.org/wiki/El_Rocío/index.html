El Rocío

El Rocío is a village in the municipality of Almonte, in the province of Huelva, Andalusia, Spain. It is famous throughout Spain as the destination of the Romería del Rocío, one of the country's most popular pilgrimages, which each Pentecost draws hundreds of thousands of devotees to venerate the Virgin of El Rocío, also known as the "Blanca Paloma" (White Dove).

The village

El Rocío lies at the edge of the marshes (marismas) of the Guadalquivir delta, beside the Doñana National Park. The village has an unusual appearance reminiscent of the American Wild West: wide unpaved sandy streets, low whitewashed houses with verandas, and hitching rails for horses, since many residents and visitors travel on horseback. Most of the houses belong to hermandades (brotherhoods) from towns and cities across Andalusia and beyond, and are occupied chiefly during the pilgrimage.

The pilgrimage

The Romería del Rocío takes place over the weekend of Pentecost. Brotherhoods travel to El Rocío on foot, on horseback or in decorated wagons drawn by oxen or tractors, crossing the Doñana countryside along traditional routes. The climax comes in the early hours of Pentecost Monday, when the image of the Virgin is carried out of the sanctuary and processed through the streets amid crowds of devotees — the moment known as the salto de la reja, when young men of Almonte climb the sanctuary railing to reach the float first. The devotion dates to the 13th century, when, according to tradition, a hunter from Villamanrique found an image of the Virgin in a tree in the area then known as Las Rocinas.

Sanctuary

The hermitage of El Rocío, a large white church built in the 1960s on the site of earlier shrines, houses the small medieval image of the Virgin and Child. Pope John Paul II visited El Rocío in 1993.
