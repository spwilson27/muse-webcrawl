New Zealand Sign Language

New Zealand Sign Language or NZSL is the main language of the deaf community in New Zealand. It became an official language of New Zealand in April 2006 under the New Zealand Sign Language Act 2006, alongside English and Māori. NZSL is related to British Sign Language (BSL) and Auslan, all three descended from British Sign Language of the 19th century; it is usually considered part of the BANZSL family.

History

The first deaf school in New Zealand, at Sumner in Christchurch (now van Asch Deaf Education Centre), was founded in 1880; its first principal, Gerrit van Asch, was a Dutch educator steeped in the oralist tradition, and signing was discouraged in classrooms. Nevertheless, pupils signed among themselves, and the language developed in deaf clubs and communities, absorbing British Sign Language brought by deaf immigrants and later developing distinctive local vocabulary, including signs for Māori concepts.

Features

NZSL uses a two-handed manual alphabet derived from BSL. Its grammar makes extensive use of space, directionality, role shift, classifiers, and non-manual features such as mouth patterns — many borrowed from spoken English — which are used more extensively than in BSL or Auslan. A dictionary of NZSL, first published in 1997 by Victoria University of Wellington, documents several thousand signs.

Status

The New Zealand Sign Language Act 2006 declared NZSL an official language and gave it the right to be used in legal proceedings. NZSL Week is celebrated annually. In 2018 the government established a board to promote and maintain the language.
