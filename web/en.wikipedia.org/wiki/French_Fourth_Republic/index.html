French Fourth Republic

The French Fourth Republic (French: Quatrième République française) was the republican government of France from 1946 to 1958, governed by the fourth republican constitution of 13 October 1946. It was in many ways a revival of the Third Republic, with a powerful parliament, a weak executive, and frequent changes of government — more than twenty cabinets in twelve years.

History

After the Liberation, a Constituent Assembly drafted a new constitution, approved by referendum in October 1946. Early governments rested on "tripartisme" — the Communists (PCF), Socialists (SFIO), and Christian-democratic MRP — until the Communists were expelled from government in May 1947 amid Cold War tensions and strikes.

Achievements

Despite chronic cabinet instability, the Fourth Republic achieved substantial reconstruction and modernization: the Monnet Plan and nationalized industries drove rapid growth (the start of the Trente Glorieuses); the welfare state was consolidated; France became a founding member of NATO (1949), the European Coal and Steel Community (1951), and the European Economic Community under the Treaty of Rome (1957). Pierre Mendès France's government ended the First Indochina War at the Geneva Conference of 1954 after the defeat at Dien Bien Phu.

Fall

The Republic's fatal weakness was the Algerian War (from 1954). Unable to resolve the conflict and facing settler and army rebellion in Algiers in May 1958, President René Coty called on Charles de Gaulle, who was invested as premier on condition of drafting a new constitution. The Fifth Republic, with a strong presidency, was proclaimed on 4 October 1958.
