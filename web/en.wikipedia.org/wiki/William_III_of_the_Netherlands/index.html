William III (Dutch: Willem Alexander Paul Frederik Lodewijk; 19 February 1817 – 23 November 1890) was King of the Netherlands and Grand Duke of Luxembourg from 1849 until his death in 1890. His forty-one-year reign saw the consolidation of parliamentary government, which the liberal constitution of 1848 had established, despite his own frequent clashes with ministers.

Reign

William succeeded his father, William II, in 1849, a year after the constitutional revision that sharply limited royal power. A conservative and often wilful monarch, he repeatedly tested the boundaries of his role, quarrelling with governments over foreign policy and appointments. The Luxembourg Crisis of 1867, in which he attempted to sell the Grand Duchy to France, nearly provoked a Franco-Prussian war and ended with Luxembourg's guaranteed neutrality.

Family and succession

William's first marriage, to Sophie of Württemberg, was unhappy; all three of their sons predeceased him. Widowed in 1877, he married the much younger Emma of Waldeck and Pyrmont in 1879. Their daughter Wilhelmina, born in 1880, succeeded him at the age of ten, with Emma as regent. William was the last male-line descendant of William the Silent to sit on the Dutch throne, and the Netherlands has been reigned by queens for more than a century since.
