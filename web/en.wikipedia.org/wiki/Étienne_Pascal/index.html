Étienne Pascal (2 May 1588 – 24 September 1651) was a French chief tax officer and administrator in Rouen. He was the father of the mathematician, physicist and philosopher Blaise Pascal and of the writer and nun Jacqueline Pascal. A man of scientific interests himself, he gave his name to the limaçon of Pascal, a curve he studied.

Life

Pascal was born in Clermont-Ferrand, the son of Martin Pascal, treasurer of France, and Marguerite Pascal de Mons. He was educated in Paris, where he took a law degree in 1610. Returning to Clermont-Ferrand, he purchased the office of counsellor for Bas-Auvergne and rose in the local administration.

In 1631, five years after the death of his wife Antoinette Begon, Pascal moved with his children to Paris. In 1634 he was appointed deputy commissioner in charge of tax collection at Rouen. When the Nu-pieds revolt against new taxes broke out in Normandy in 1639, Pascal fled to Paris; he was pardoned by Cardinal Richelieu and returned to Rouen in 1640 to continue his work.

Pascal took personal charge of his children's education, and his Paris home became a meeting place for mathematicians such as Gilles de Roberval and Claude Mydorge. He assisted his son Blaise in the design and promotion of the Pascaline, an early mechanical calculator built to ease the burden of tax computations. He died in Paris in September 1651.

Mathematics

The limaçon of Pascal is a roulette curve formed by a point attached to a circle rolling around another circle of equal radius. Étienne Pascal corresponded about the curve with Roberval, and it was named after him by later mathematicians.
