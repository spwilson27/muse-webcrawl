Surface normal

In geometry, a surface normal, or simply normal, to a surface at a point is a vector perpendicular to the tangent plane of the surface at that point. For a plane surface the normal is constant; for curved surfaces it varies from point to point. A normal is usually normalised to unit length (unit normal), and for a closed surface a sign convention distinguishes outward-pointing from inward-pointing normals.

Field: Differential geometry, computer graphics
Definition: Vector perpendicular to a surface's tangent plane at a point

Calculating a surface normal

For a surface given parametrically, the normal at a point is obtained from the cross product of the partial derivatives of the parametrisation, which span the tangent plane. For an implicitly defined surface F(x, y, z) = 0, the normal is given by the gradient of F. For a polygon in a polygonal mesh, the normal of a face follows from the cross product of two of its edges, with the direction determined by the right-hand rule and the vertex winding order.

Applications

Surface normals are fundamental in computer graphics, where they determine how light reflects from a surface in shading models such as Phong shading, and in techniques including normal mapping, which perturbs normals to simulate fine detail. In physics and engineering, normals enter the definitions of pressure, flux, and surface integrals, and in robotics and collision detection they define contact geometry.
