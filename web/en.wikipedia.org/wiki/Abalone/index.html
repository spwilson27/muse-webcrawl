Abalone (from Spanish abulón) are marine snails of the genus Haliotis, the sole genus of the family Haliotidae. Their distinctive flattened, ear-shaped shells have a low, open spiral and a row of holes along the rim through which water and gametes are expelled. The shell's inner layer is nacre — mother of pearl — which is highly iridescent and has been prized for jewellery and inlay for millennia. Abalone are also esteemed as a delicacy, particularly in East and Southeast Asian cuisine.

Biology

Abalone live on rocky shores and reefs in cold coastal waters around every continent except the Atlantic coasts of South America and the Caribbean, and the Arctic and Antarctic. They cling to rock with a powerful muscular foot and graze on algae — kelp and other macroalgae in the wild. Sizes range from about 20 mm in Haliotis pulcherrima to 30 cm in the red abalone (H. rufescens) of the Pacific coast of North America, the largest species. Respiration passes water over the gills and out through the shell's respiratory pores (tremata); as the animal grows, new holes form and old ones seal over, so the count of open holes is a rough marker of species.

Fisheries and farming

Wild abalone fisheries once supported large harvests in California, Mexico, South Africa, Japan, Australia, and New Zealand (where they are known as pāua). Overfishing, poaching — especially of South Africa's H. midae ("perlemoen") — and disease (notably withering syndrome, caused by a rickettsia-like bacterium and worsened by warm water) collapsed many stocks; several species, including the white abalone, are now listed as endangered, and most jurisdictions impose strict size limits, quotas, or outright closures, with California's recreational fishery closed entirely from 2018. Aquaculture has grown rapidly to fill the demand: China is the world's largest producer, farming well over 100,000 tonnes annually, followed by South Korea. Farmed abalone are fed kelp or formulated diets in tanks or sea cages.

As food and material

Abalone meat — the foot muscle — is eaten raw as sashimi, steamed, braised, or dried; dried abalone is one of the most expensive ingredients in Cantonese banquet cooking, its flavour deepening with years of storage. Canned abalone is a major export of Mexico, Australia, and Chile (where the name is also applied to a different mollusc, the loco). The shells are used for buttons, inlay in furniture and musical instruments, and jewellery; pāua shell is an emblematic material of Māori art.
