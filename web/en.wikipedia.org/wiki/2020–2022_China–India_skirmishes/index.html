2020–2022 China–India skirmishes
Date: 5 May 2020 – 11 December 2022
Location: Line of Actual Control (Ladakh, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh)
Result: Disengagement at several friction points; buffer zones created
Casualties: 20 Indian soldiers, at least 4 Chinese soldiers (Galwan)

The 2020–2022 China–India skirmishes were a series of military standoffs and clashes between China and India along the disputed Line of Actual Control (LAC), beginning in May 2020. They marked the most serious escalation between the two countries since the 1962 Sino-Indian War and the first fatalities on the border since 1975.

Galwan Valley clash

On the night of 15–16 June 2020, a violent hand-to-hand clash in the Galwan Valley in Ladakh — fought with clubs and improvised weapons under agreements barring firearms near the LAC — killed 20 Indian soldiers; China later acknowledged four of its soldiers had died. Other standoff points included Pangong Tso, the Hot Springs/Gogra area, Depsang and Demchok, and in January 2021 a minor clash occurred at Naku La in Sikkim. In December 2022 troops clashed at Yangtse in the Tawang sector of Arunachal Pradesh, with injuries on both sides but no deaths.

Disengagement and aftermath

Following many rounds of military and diplomatic talks, both sides disengaged from several friction points, beginning with Pangong Tso in February 2021 and continuing at Gogra (2021) and Hot Springs (2022), creating buffer zones. India responded economically by banning numerous Chinese mobile apps and restricting investment; both countries greatly reinforced their border infrastructure and deployments.

Note: this article covers the period through the December 2022 Tawang clash; the related article on the 2020–2021 phase covers the initial standoff in more detail.
