Henry Poole & Co is a bespoke tailor located at Savile Row in London. Founded in 1806, the firm is widely credited with creating the dinner jacket (tuxedo) and became the archetype of the Savile Row tailor, holding numerous royal warrants and dressing statesmen, royalty and celebrities for two centuries.

History

The business was founded by James Poole in 1806, initially in Brunswick Square, making military uniforms during the Napoleonic Wars. His son Henry Poole took over and in 1846 the firm moved to Savile Row. When the street's premises were rebuilt, Henry Poole's became No. 15 Savile Row, with a famously grand interior; at its Victorian height the company employed some 300 tailors.

The firm dressed Emperor Napoleon III, Queen Victoria's court, and much of European royalty. Its ledger books record orders from figures including Charles Dickens, Benjamin Disraeli, Winston Churchill, Charles de Gaulle and the Prince of Wales, later Edward VII.

The dinner jacket

In the 1860s the Prince of Wales (the future Edward VII) asked Henry Poole & Co to make a short evening coat based on a blue silk smoking jacket, to be worn at informal dinners at Sandringham. In 1886 the American James Potter, a guest of the Prince, had a similar garment made and wore it home to the Tuxedo Park Club in New York — giving the world both the "dinner jacket" and its American name, the tuxedo.

The firm today

Henry Poole & Co remains family-owned and continues to cut fully bespoke garments by hand at Savile Row, holding royal warrants as tailors and livery tailors. It also maintains a presence in China through a partnership in Beijing. The firm preserved its historic ledgers, an unrivalled archive of two centuries of bespoke tailoring.
