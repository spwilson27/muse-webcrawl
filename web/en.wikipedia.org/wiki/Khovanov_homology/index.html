Khovanov homology

Khovanov homology
FieldMathematics (knot theory)
Introduced byMikhail Khovanov (2000)

In mathematics, Khovanov homology is a homology theory for links, introduced by Mikhail Khovanov in 2000, whose graded Euler characteristic is the Jones polynomial. It is an example of a categorification: the homology groups contain more information than the polynomial invariant they refine, and Khovanov homology has been shown to detect the unknot, a property not known for the Jones polynomial itself.

Construction

Given a link diagram, one forms the cube of all smoothings (resolutions) of the crossings. To each resolution one assigns tensor powers of the Frobenius algebra A = Z[x]/(x²), and the edges of the cube are given by multiplication and comultiplication maps, producing a chain complex whose homology is a link invariant. Forgetting the homological grading recovers the (unnormalized) Jones polynomial.

Significance

Khovanov homology is stronger than the Jones polynomial: it distinguishes some pairs of knots the polynomial cannot, and Kronheimer and Mrowka proved in 2011 that it detects the unknot. The theory connects to representation theory, symplectic geometry, and physics — Jacob Rasmussen used a spectral sequence from Khovanov homology to give a combinatorial proof of the Milnor conjecture on the slice genus of torus knots. A generalization, Khovanov–Rozansky homology, categorifies the HOMFLY-PT polynomial, and the theory has been linked to four-dimensional topological quantum field theory and, through work of Khovanov and others, to the physics of supersymmetric gauge theories.
