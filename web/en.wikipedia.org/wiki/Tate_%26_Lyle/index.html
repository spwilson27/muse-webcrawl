Tate & Lyle plc is a British multinational food-ingredients company headquartered in London. It was formed in 1921 by the merger of the sugar-refining businesses of Henry Tate and Abram Lyle, whose firms had been rivals in 19th-century London.

History

Henry Tate, who built a fortune refining sugar in Liverpool and London, also founded the Tate Gallery. Abram Lyle's company created Lyle's Golden Syrup in 1883, whose tin design is among Britain's oldest unchanged brand packagings. The merged company became Britain's dominant sugar refiner.

Modern business

In 2010 Tate & Lyle sold its sugar refining business, including the Lyle's Golden Syrup brand licence rights in Europe were retained under licence. The company now focuses on food and beverage ingredients — sweeteners such as sucralose (Splenda), starches, fibres and stabilisers — supplying manufacturers worldwide.
