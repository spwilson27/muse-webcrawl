Defenestration
DefinitionThe act of throwing a person or object out of a window
EtymologyFrom Latin de ("out of") + fenestra ("window")
Famous instancesDefenestrations of Prague, 1419 and 1618

Defenestration is the act of throwing a person or thing out of a window. The word comes from the Latin de ("out of") and fenestra ("window"). It is most famous as a feature of Bohemian political history: the Defenestrations of Prague in 1419 and 1618 each helped trigger major wars.

Historical instances

  • 1419 — First Defenestration of Prague. Hussite radicals threw the mayor and several councillors of Prague's New Town from the town hall windows, precipitating the Hussite Wars.
  • 1618 — Second Defenestration of Prague. Protestant nobles threw two Catholic imperial governors, Vilém Slavata and Jaroslav Martinic, and their secretary from a window of Prague Castle. All three survived — Catholics credited angels or the Virgin Mary; Protestants, a pile of manure. The event ignited the Bohemian Revolt and thus the Thirty Years' War.
  • 1948 — Jan Masaryk. The Czechoslovak foreign minister was found dead beneath a bathroom window of the Foreign Ministry; whether he jumped, fell, or was pushed remains disputed.

Usage

Beyond its literal sense, the word is used figuratively for forcible removal from office, and humourists have applied it to everything from discarded furniture to computer operating systems. "Self-defenestration" describes jumping from a window oneself.
