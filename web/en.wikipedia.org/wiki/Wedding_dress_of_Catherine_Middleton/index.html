Wedding dress of Catherine Middleton

DesignerSarah Burton for Alexander McQueen
Worn byCatherine Middleton (now Catherine, Princess of Wales)
OccasionWedding of Prince William and Catherine Middleton, 29 April 2011, Westminster Abbey
MaterialsIvory and white satin gazar; hand-made lace appliqué; English and French Chantilly lace
Train270 cm (110 in)

The wedding dress of Catherine Middleton was worn by Catherine Middleton at her wedding to Prince William, Duke of Cambridge, on 29 April 2011 at Westminster Abbey in London. Designed by Sarah Burton, creative director of the fashion house Alexander McQueen, the dress was a closely guarded secret until the bride stepped from her car on the wedding day, and it was immediately praised by fashion commentators as a modern classic that would influence bridal design for years.

Design

The gown combined tradition and modernity, combining a Victorian-inspired corseted bodice, padded at the hips, with a narrow waist. The bodice and skirt were decorated with lace appliqué created by the Royal School of Needlework at Hampton Court Palace, whose workers washed their hands every thirty minutes and replaced needles every three hours to keep the lace pristine. The floral motifs — rose, thistle, daffodil and shamrock, representing the four nations of the United Kingdom — were individually cut from English and French Chantilly lace and hand-appliquéd onto ivory silk tulle. The skirt, of ivory and white satin gazar, was designed to evoke an opening flower, with pleats falling to a train measuring 270 centimetres. The dress fastened with 58 gazar-covered buttons down the back. Middleton wore the Cartier Halo Tiara of 1936, lent by Queen Elizabeth II, holding a veil of silk tulle trimmed with hand-embroidered flowers.

Reception and influence

The dress drew comparisons with the wedding gown worn by Grace Kelly in 1956 and was widely credited with reviving long-sleeved lace bridal gowns as a dominant fashion trend through the following decade. It was exhibited at Buckingham Palace during the 2011 summer opening, where it drew record crowds; Queen Elizabeth II was famously filmed remarking that the headless mannequin display was "horrid". Replicas and "inspired-by" versions at many price points appeared within hours of the ceremony.
