Ip Man is a 2008 Hong Kong biographical martial arts film directed by Wilson Yip and starring Donnie Yen as Ip Man, the Wing Chun grandmaster who taught Bruce Lee. The film is a semi-fictionalised account of Ip's life in Foshan during the Second Sino-Japanese War. It spawned a successful franchise, with sequels Ip Man 2 (2010), Ip Man 3 (2015) and Ip Man 4: The Finale (2019), as well as spin-offs.

Plot

In 1935, Foshan is a flourishing centre of southern Chinese martial arts. Ip Man, a wealthy and modest Wing Chun master, is pressed into friendly challenge matches, defeating the northern master Jin Shanzhao. When the Japanese invade in 1937, Foshan is occupied and impoverished; Ip loses his home and works in a coal mine to feed his family. A Japanese general, Miura, stages arena fights between Chinese martial artists and his karateka soldiers, offering rice as a prize. After witnessing the killing of fellow master Liu, Ip fights and defeats ten Japanese karateka at once, refuses Miura's invitation to teach the Japanese army, and becomes a target of the occupation authorities. He eventually faces Miura in a public duel, defeats him, is shot by a Japanese officer, and escapes with his family to Hong Kong with the help of his friend Chow Ching-chuen.

Production

The film's fight sequences were choreographed by Sammo Hung, emphasising authentic Wing Chun technique — including chain punches, sticky hands and the wooden dummy — rather than wire-assisted fantasy. Ip Man's eldest son, Ip Chun, served as a consultant. Although marketed as a biography, the plot takes considerable liberties: Ip never fought a Japanese general in a public duel, though he did refuse to teach the Japanese military police and left Foshan for Hong Kong after the war.

Reception and awards

Ip Man was a box-office hit in Hong Kong and China and won Best Film at the 28th Hong Kong Film Awards, where it received twelve nominations. It was widely praised for its action and for Yen's restrained performance, and it revived mainstream interest in Ip Man and Wing Chun, leading to numerous rival Ip Man films by other studios.
