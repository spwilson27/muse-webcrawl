Homelessness

Homelessness is the condition of lacking stable, safe and functional housing. It includes people sleeping rough on the streets, in emergency shelters or temporary accommodation, in overcrowded or insecure dwellings, and those displaced by war, disaster or eviction. Definitions vary by country, making global estimates imprecise; the United Nations has estimated that around 150 million people are homeless worldwide, with over a billion living in inadequate housing.

Causes

Homelessness results from combinations of structural and personal factors: shortages of affordable housing, poverty and unemployment, eviction, family breakdown and domestic violence, mental illness and substance use disorders, discharge from institutions (hospitals, prisons, foster care) without housing support, and displacement by conflict or natural disaster. In many cities rising housing costs relative to incomes have been identified as the strongest predictor of homelessness rates.

Effects

People experiencing homelessness face high rates of physical and mental ill health, victimisation, and drastically reduced life expectancy. Children who are homeless experience disrupted schooling and worse health and developmental outcomes. Homelessness also imposes costs on public services through emergency healthcare, policing and crisis accommodation.

Responses

Responses include emergency shelters, soup kitchens and outreach services; transitional housing and supported housing; prevention work such as eviction mediation and rent assistance; and Housing First programmes, pioneered in the United States and adopted influentially in Finland, which provide permanent housing without preconditions, together with voluntary support. Finland is often cited for substantially reducing long-term homelessness through sustained Housing First policy. Debates continue over enforcement approaches such as anti-camping laws, the balance between housing supply and services, and the criminalisation of rough sleeping.
