Intercalation

Guest species inserting between the layers of a host material.

Intercalation is the reversible insertion of a molecule (or ion) into layered materials with layered structures. The concept is used in several fields:

Timekeeping

In calendars, intercalation is the insertion of a leap day, week, or month to keep a calendar aligned with the astronomical year or moon phase. Examples include the leap day of the Gregorian calendar and the embolismic months of lunisolar calendars such as the Hebrew calendar.

Chemistry

In chemistry, intercalation is the reversible inclusion of a guest species between the layers or sheets of a host solid, such as graphite, layered clays, and transition-metal dichalcogenides. The working principle of the lithium-ion battery is the intercalation and de-intercalation of lithium ions into electrode materials. Intercalation compounds often show changed electrical, optical, and magnetic properties.

Biochemistry

In molecular biology, intercalation is the insertion of flat aromatic molecules between adjacent base pairs of DNA. Intercalating agents such as ethidium bromide are used as fluorescent stains; others, including several chemotherapy drugs, interfere with replication and transcription.
