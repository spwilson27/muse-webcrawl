Ranchi

Ranchi (/ˈrɑːntʃi/; Hindi: राँची) is the capital city and largest city of the Indian state of Jharkhand. Situated on the Chota Nagpur Plateau at an elevation of about 651 metres, it is known for its waterfalls, lakes and relatively cool climate, and was historically the summer capital of Bihar. Ranchi is also famous as the hometown of cricketer Mahendra Singh Dhoni.

History

The region around Ranchi formed part of the Nagvanshi kingdom and later came under Mughal and British influence. The area was a centre of tribal resistance to British rule, most notably the uprising led by Birsa Munda at the end of the 19th century. Under British administration Ranchi became the summer capital of Bihar province because of its elevation and climate. When the state of Jharkhand was carved out of Bihar on 15 November 2000, Ranchi became its capital.

Geography and climate

Ranchi lies on the southern part of the Chota Nagpur Plateau, drained by the Subarnarekha and South Koel rivers. Numerous waterfalls — including Hundru, Jonha, Dassam and Hirni falls — surround the city, earning it the nickname "City of Waterfalls". It experiences a humid subtropical climate moderated by altitude, with pleasant winters.

Economy and transport

Ranchi hosts the headquarters and plants of major public-sector enterprises such as Heavy Engineering Corporation, Central Coalfields Limited, MECON and SAIL's research centre. It is a growing education and healthcare hub, home to institutions including IIM Ranchi, NUSRL and the Central Institute of Psychiatry. The city is served by Birsa Munda Airport and rail links to Delhi, Kolkata and Patna, and contains the JSCA International Stadium Complex, a venue for international cricket.
