Systems development life cycle

The systems development life cycle (SDLC), also referred to as the application development life cycle, is a process for planning, creating, testing, and deploying an information system. The SDLC concept applies to a range of hardware and software configurations, and is widely used in systems engineering and software engineering.

Phases

Common formulations divide the life cycle into phases such as preliminary analysis, feasibility study, systems analysis and requirements definition, systems design, development, integration and testing, deployment, and maintenance. The traditional waterfall model completes each phase before the next begins, while iterative and agile models interleave the phases in repeated cycles.

Models

Well-known SDLC models include the waterfall model, the V-model, the spiral model proposed by Barry Boehm, the rapid application development (RAD) model, and agile frameworks such as Scrum and extreme programming. The choice of model depends on project risk, requirements stability, and organisational context.
