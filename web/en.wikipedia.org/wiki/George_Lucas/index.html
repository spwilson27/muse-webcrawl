George Lucas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: George Lucas on the set of Star Wars in 1976, young and bearded, reviewing the droids in the Tunisian desert light]

George Walton Lucas Jr. (born 14 May 1944) is an American film director, producer and screenwriter, creator of the Star Wars and (with Steven Spielberg) Indiana Jones franchises, and founder of Lucasfilm, Industrial Light & Magic and THX. His four films as director of features — THX 1138 (1971), American Graffiti (1973) and the Star Wars films of 1977 and the 1999–2005 prequels — changed the economics and the look of cinema more than any career since Disney's.

Born in Modesto, California, a car-crash survivor who turned from racing to film at USC (where his student film became THX 1138), Lucas co-founded American Zoetrope with Francis Ford Coppola before striking out alone. American Graffiti, made for under a million dollars, grossed over a hundred million and bought him the freedom to make Star Wars.

Empire building

Lucas's enduring achievement may be institutional: ILM (founded 1975 for Star Wars) created modern visual effects; Skywalker Sound set audio standards; THX certified cinemas; LucasArts led games; and his early bets on Pixar (sold to Steve Jobs in 1986) and digital editing and projection seeded the industry's future. His insistence on owning Star Wars's sequel and merchandising rights in 1976 — accepted by Fox in lieu of salary — is business legend and made him a billionaire.

Later career

The prequel trilogy (1999–2005) divided audiences while grossing billions; the Special Edition revisions of the originals fuelled a decades-long fan argument about authorship and preservation. In 2012 he sold Lucasfilm to Disney for $4.05 billion, donating most proceeds to education (his Giving Pledge signature and the Lucas Museum of Narrative Art, planned for Los Angeles, mark his philanthropic turn).
