A barracks is a building or group of buildings designed to house military personnel, usually soldiers. The word derives from the Spanish barraca, originally a temporary hut, and passed into English via French in the seventeenth century. Barracks range from vast permanent complexes of dressed stone housing a whole regiment to temporary field accommodation, and the term is also used for similar institutional dormitories for police, firefighters and other uniformed services.

History

Ancient armies lodged soldiers in tents or in fortified camps, and Roman legionaries were quartered in permanent barrack blocks within fortresses, each contubernium of eight men sharing two rooms. Purpose-built peacetime barracks became common in Europe in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries as standing armies grew; keeping soldiers separate from the civilian population, rather than billeting them in private homes and inns, was both a disciplinary and a political measure. In Britain, a major programme of barracks construction followed the French Revolutionary Wars, and reforms after the Crimean War gradually improved standards of space, sanitation and ventilation, adding married quarters, messes, canteens and hospitals.

Layout and facilities

A traditional barracks centres on a parade ground, around which are arranged barrack blocks containing squad rooms or individual rooms, a guardroom, officers' and sergeants' messes, a cookhouse and dining hall, stores, stables or vehicle sheds, and recreational facilities. In the United States, the modern term "barracks" denotes unaccompanied personnel housing; in British usage a barracks is an entire garrison station, and the accommodation blocks within it are simply part of it.

Temporary barracks

In wartime, armies erect temporary accommodation at scale — from the trench billets of the First World War to the prefabricated Nissen and Quonset huts of the Second World War — and "barracks" has extended to emergency housing for workers, refugees and prisoners, giving rise to the pejorative term "barrack-like" for bleak, regimented housing.
