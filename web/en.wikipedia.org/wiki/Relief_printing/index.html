Relief printing

Relief printing is a family of printing methods in which a printing block, plate or matrix — from which ink is absent in the recessed areas — is brought into contact with paper. Ink is applied to the raised (relief) surfaces, and only those raised parts transfer ink to the sheet. It is one of the oldest printing techniques, historically used for both text and images.

Techniques

Relief processes include woodcut, in which the image is carved into a plank of wood; wood engraving, worked on end-grain blocks; linocut, using linoleum; and letterpress printing, historically the dominant method of printing text from movable type, in which the raised faces of type carry the ink. Flexography is a modern rotary relief process using flexible plates, widely used in packaging.

History

Woodblock printing developed in China by the Tang dynasty and was used in Europe from the late Middle Ages for textiles, playing cards and religious images before the arrival of movable type. Relief printing remained the principal means of reproducing text for roughly five centuries, until offset lithography displaced letterpress in the twentieth century.
