DEPTHX

DEPTHX
Type: Autonomous underwater vehicle
Developer: Stone Aerospace / NASA-funded
Known for: Exploring Zacatón cenote, Mexico

DEPTHX (Deep Phreatic Thermal Explorer) is an autonomous underwater vehicle developed to explore sinkholes (cenotes) in Mexico and to prototype technology for exploring subsurface oceans on icy moons such as Europa.

Missions

In 2007 DEPTHX explored the Zacatón cenote in Tamaulipas, the world's deepest known water-filled sinkhole, mapping its walls with sonar and descending to a depth of about 318 metres — becoming the first autonomous system to map a three-dimensional underwater environment as it navigated.

Technology

The roughly 1.3-tonne spherical vehicle used dozens of sonars, Doppler velocity sensing and SLAM software to navigate without tethers or prior maps, collecting water and microbial samples. Its successor concepts informed the ENDURANCE and ARTEMIS vehicles.
