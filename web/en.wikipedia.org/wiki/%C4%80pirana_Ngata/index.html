Āpirana Ngata

Born3 July 1874, Te Araroa, New Zealand
Died14 July 1950, Waiomatatini, New Zealand
IwiNgāti Porou

Sir Āpirana Turupa Ngata (3 July 1874 – 14 July 1950) was a prominent New Zealand statesman, lawyer, and scholar of Ngāti Porou descent. Often regarded as the most important Māori politician of the first half of the 20th century, he served as a Member of Parliament for nearly four decades and as Minister of Native Affairs, and he led a major revival of Māori culture, language, and land development. His portrait appears on the New Zealand fifty-dollar note.

Early life and education

Ngata was born at Te Araroa on the East Coast of the North Island. Educated at Waiomatatini Native School and Te Aute College, he went on to Canterbury University College and in 1893 became the first Māori to earn a university degree in New Zealand, completing a BA and then an LLB and practising law.

Political career

Ngata was elected to Parliament for the Eastern Māori electorate in 1905 and held the seat until 1943. As a leading figure of the Young Māori Party, he sought Māori advancement within the institutions of the state. Appointed Minister of Native Affairs in 1928, he drove land development schemes that incorporated and farmed Māori land, creating employment during the Depression. He resigned from Cabinet in 1934 after an inquiry into his department's administration, though he remained influential.

Cultural work

Ngata championed a renaissance of Māori arts. He promoted the building of carved meeting houses, supported kapa haka, and collected and annotated Māori songs and poetry in the monumental Ngā Mōteatea. His motto — E tipu e rea ("grow up and thrive") — urged Māori to master Pākehā knowledge while holding fast to the treasures of their ancestors.

Legacy

Ngata died in 1950 at Waiomatatini. He is remembered as a nation-builder who reshaped Crown–Māori relations and secured a lasting place for Māori culture in New Zealand public life.
