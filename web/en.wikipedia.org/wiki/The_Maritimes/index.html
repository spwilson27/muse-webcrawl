The Maritimes, also called the Maritime provinces, is a region of eastern Canada consisting of the provinces of New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, and Prince Edward Island. Together with Newfoundland and Labrador, the Maritimes form the wider region known as Atlantic Canada.

The Maritimes have a population of roughly two million. The region borders the Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf of St. Lawrence, and its largest metropolitan area is Halifax, the capital of Nova Scotia. Fredericton is the capital of New Brunswick and Charlottetown — the site of the 1864 conference that led to Canadian Confederation — is the capital of Prince Edward Island.

History

The region has been inhabited by Indigenous peoples, including the Mi'kmaq, Wolastoqiyik (Maliseet), and Passamaquoddy, for thousands of years. European settlement began in the early 17th century with French Acadia and British settlements; control of the area passed between France and Britain before the British expulsion of the Acadians in 1755. Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, and Prince Edward Island were among the founding provinces of the Dominion of Canada in 1867, with Charlottetown hosting the initial Confederation conference in 1864.

Geography and economy

The Maritime provinces are characterised by rocky Atlantic coastlines, the Bay of Fundy (famous for the world's highest tides), low forested mountains forming the northern end of the Appalachian chain, and fertile agricultural valleys such as the Annapolis Valley. Historically the economy rested on fishing, shipbuilding, timber, and agriculture; today it includes energy, tourism, manufacturing, education, and public services. The region is noted for its seafood, particularly lobster and Atlantic salmon, and for products such as Prince Edward Island potatoes.

Culture

The Maritimes have a strong regional identity shaped by Acadian French culture, Scottish and Irish heritage, and Maritime musical and storytelling traditions. English is the predominant language, with a significant French-speaking Acadian population concentrated in New Brunswick, Canada's only officially bilingual province.
