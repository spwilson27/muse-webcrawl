Lower Silesia (Polish: Dolny Śląsk; German: Niederschlesien) is the northwestern part of the historical region of Silesia, today located mainly in southwestern Poland, roughly corresponding to the Lower Silesian Voivodeship with its capital at Wrocław (German: Breslau).

History

Settled by Slavic tribes and ruled by the Polish Piast dynasty in the early Middle Ages, Silesia fragmented into numerous small duchies. From the 14th century the Silesian duchies passed under the Bohemian crown and thus the Habsburg monarchy. Prussia seized most of Silesia from Austria in the Silesian Wars of the 1740s under Frederick the Great, and Lower Silesia remained German for two centuries.

After the Second World War, the Potsdam Agreement transferred the territory east of the Oder–Neisse line to Poland. The German population was expelled westward, and the region was repopulated by Poles, including many displaced from Poland's former eastern territories annexed by the Soviet Union.

Geography and economy

Lower Silesia stretches along the middle Oder (Odra) river, bounded to the south by the Sudetes mountains, including the Karkonosze range and its highest peak, Śnieżka. The region includes copper-mining districts around Legnica and Lubin, historic towns such as Jelenia Góra and Wałbrzych, and numerous castles and spas.
