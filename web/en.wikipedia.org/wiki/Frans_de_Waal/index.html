Overview

Franciscus Bernardus Maria "Frans" de Waal (1948–2024) was a Dutch-American primatologist and ethologist known for his work on the behaviour and social intelligence of primates. As a young researcher at Burgers' Zoo in Arnhem he documented the power politics of its chimpanzee colony in Chimpanzee Politics (1982), a book later famously recommended to US Congress members for its insights into coalition and rivalry.

Work

De Waal's research — at the Wisconsin primate centre and Emory University's Yerkes centre — demonstrated reconciliation after fights, consolation, empathy, a sense of fairness (the famous capuchin "inequity aversion" cucumber/grape experiment with Sarah Brosnan, 2003) and cultural transmission in apes and monkeys, arguing for evolutionary continuity of morality between humans and other primates (Good Natured, The Bonobo and the Atheist). He named and opposed "anthropodenial" — the a priori rejection of human-like traits in animals. His later books (Are We Smart Enough to Know How Smart Animals Are?, Mama's Last Hug, Different) reached wide audiences.
