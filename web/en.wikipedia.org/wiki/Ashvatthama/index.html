Ashvatthama


Ashvatthama, son of Drona, in the Kurukshetra war.

Ashvatthama (Sanskrit: अश्वत्थामा, Aśvatthāmā), also spelled Ashwatthama, is a prominent warrior in the Hindu epic Mahabharata. The son of the royal preceptor Drona and his wife Kripi, he fought on the side of the Kauravas against the Pandavas in the Kurukshetra War. Along with Kripa and Kritavarma, he is one of the three surviving Kaurava warriors, and is regarded as one of the seven immortal Chiranjivis. His most notorious deed is the night raid on the Pandava camp after the war's end.

Birth and early life

According to the epic, Ashvatthama was born with a divine gem (mani) embedded in his forehead, which protected him from hunger, disease, and harm from gods, demons and beasts. He is considered an incarnation of a portion of Shiva. Trained in the martial arts by his father Drona, he became a mighty archer and master of celestial weapons, though the epic marks his father's favouritism — Drona secretly taught him techniques withheld from others — and an early episode in which the poor boy was fed flour mixed with water in place of milk foreshadows his resentment of his childhood friend-turned-rival, the Pandava prince Arjuna.

Role in the Kurukshetra War

Ashvatthama fought fiercely for the Kauravas and, after Drona's death on the fifteenth day — brought about by the false report that "Ashvatthama is dead" (referring to an elephant) — unleashed the Narayanastra, a divine weapon that could only be calmed by total submission. On the eighteenth day, after the fall of Duryodhana, the dying Kaurava prince appointed Ashvatthama commander-in-chief.

The night raid and curse

That night, inspired by an owl killing sleeping crows, Ashvatthama, Kripa and Kritavarma attacked the sleeping Pandava camp and slaughtered the remaining Panchala warriors, including Dhrishtadyumna and the five Upapandavas, whom he mistook for the Pandavas. The five Pandava brothers, however, had spent the night elsewhere. In his final confrontation with Arjuna, Ashvatthama discharged the Brahmashira weapon and, unable to recall it, redirected it at the womb of Uttara, killing the unborn Parikshit — who was later revived by Krishna. Krishna cursed Ashvatthama to wander the earth for 3,000 years, alone, unhealing and shunned; the gem in his forehead was cut out and surrendered.

Legacy

Ashvatthama embodies the epic's themes of grief, vengeance and the collapse of the warrior code (dharma) by the war's end. Folk traditions in parts of India hold that he still wanders, and several temples are associated with sightings of him.
