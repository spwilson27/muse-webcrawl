Fanzine

Fanzine
Term coined1940, by Russ Chauvenet
FormatAmateur, non-professional publication
Origins1930s science-fiction fandom
SuccessorsWebzines, blogs

A fanzine (a blend of fan and magazine) is a non-professional and non-official publication produced by enthusiasts of a particular cultural phenomenon — such as a literary or musical genre — for the pleasure of other fans. Fanzines were traditionally cheap, photocopied or mimeographed publications traded rather than sold; the term was coined in 1940 by science-fiction fan Russ Chauvenet.

History

The earliest fanzines grew out of 1930s science-fiction fandom, beginning with publications such as The Comet (1930), produced by the Science Correspondence Club. Early fans who published fanzines included later-famous writers and editors such as Ray Bradbury and Julius Schwartz. The Hugo Awards include categories for Best Fanzine and Best Fan Writer, reflecting the format's prestige within science-fiction culture.

Punk and music zines

Fanzines were reinvigorated by punk rock in the 1970s: titles such as Sniffin' Glue (UK, 1976), Search & Destroy and Slash chronicled local scenes with a do-it-yourself ethos, and football fanzines flourished in Britain in the 1980s. The 1990s riot grrrl movement produced influential feminist zines, while photocopied zine culture generally laid groundwork for blogging and social media. Many communities now use the shorter term "zine" for any self-published booklet, fan-made or otherwise.
