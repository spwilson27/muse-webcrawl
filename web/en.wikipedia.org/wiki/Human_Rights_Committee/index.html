The United Nations Human Rights Committee is the body of independent experts that monitors implementation of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) by its states parties. Established under Article 28 of the Covenant, it began work in 1976 and consists of 18 members elected by the states parties who serve in their personal capacity.

Functions

The Committee examines periodic reports submitted by states parties on the measures they have adopted to give effect to the Covenant, and issues concluding observations with concerns and recommendations. Under the First Optional Protocol to the ICCPR, it may receive and consider communications from individuals claiming to be victims of violations by a state party to the Protocol; its final decisions are called "Views". Under Article 41, states may also accept the Committee's competence to hear inter-state complaints, a procedure that has never been used. The Committee also publishes General Comments interpreting Covenant rights — among the most cited being General Comment No. 36 on the right to life (2018) and General Comment No. 34 on freedom of opinion and expression (2011).

Distinction from the Human Rights Council

The Human Rights Committee is frequently confused with the UN Human Rights Council, created in 2006. The Council is a political body of member states; the Committee is an expert treaty body whose authority derives from the ICCPR itself. Some members and commentators refer to the Committee as the "CCPR" committee.

Effect of its work

The Committee's Views under the Optional Protocol are not judgments of a court, but the Committee regards them as authoritative determinations that states parties are obliged in good faith to implement, including by providing an effective remedy. Compliance is followed up through a dedicated reporting procedure.
