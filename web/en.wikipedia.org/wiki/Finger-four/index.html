Finger-four

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The finger-four formation, named for the arrangement of fingertips on a hand.

The finger-four formation (also known as the "four finger formation") is a flight formation used by fighter aircraft. It is based on the positions of the fingertips of a human hand when the fingers are spread apart, which gives it its name. The formation consists of four aircraft: a flight leader, a wingman, and a second element leader with their wingman.

History

The formation was developed independently by several air forces in the 1930s and during the Second World War. The Finnish Air Force is often credited with pioneering its use in 1934–35, and the German Luftwaffe developed it into the Schwarm formation under pilots such as Werner Mölders during the Spanish Civil War. It replaced the rigid three-aircraft "vic" formation used by many air forces at the start of the war.

Description

The four aircraft are arranged in two pairs (elements). The flight leader flies at the front, with their wingman offset to one side and slightly behind; the second element leader flies on the opposite side, roughly level with or behind the leader, with their wingman further out. The arrangement gives each pilot a clear view and allows the flight to maneuver as two mutually supporting pairs while maintaining lookout in all directions.

Modern use

The finger-four remains a standard building block of modern air combat tactics; larger formations are composed of multiple four-aircraft flights.
