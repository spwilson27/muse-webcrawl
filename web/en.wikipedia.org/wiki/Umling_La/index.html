Umling La

Umling La is a mountain pass in Ladakh, India, in the eastern Changthang region near the Line of Actual Control with China. With an elevation commonly cited as 5,798 metres (19,024 feet), the road over Umling La is among the highest motorable roads in the world, surpassing Bolivia's Uturuncu road and the passes of the Indian Himalaya such as Khardung La.

Road

The pass lies on the road built by India's Border Roads Organisation (BRO) under Project Himank, linking the villages of Chisumle and Demchok, a stretch of about 86 kilometres completed in 2017 and subsequently improved and black-topped. The road gives the Indian Army access to the sensitive Demchok sector and connects remote border villages. Guinness World Records recognised the Umling La road as the highest motorable road in the world.

Conditions

At this altitude the air contains roughly half the oxygen of sea level, temperatures fall far below freezing even in summer, and the pass is snowbound for much of the year. Civilian travellers require inner-line permits for the area, and the road's proximity to the disputed border with China means access can be restricted. Altitude sickness is a serious hazard for travellers who ascend quickly from Leh.
