Lycoming O-320

Lycoming O-320
Type: Four-cylinder piston aircraft engine
Manufacturer: Lycoming Engines
First run: 1952
Displacement: 320 cu in (5.24 L)
Power: 150–160 hp

The Lycoming O-320 is a large family of naturally aspirated, air-cooled, horizontally opposed four-cylinder, direct-drive aircraft engines produced by Lycoming Engines. The "320" refers to its approximate piston displacement in cubic inches. It is one of the most widely used aircraft engines in general aviation.

Design and development

Introduced in 1952, the O-320 series grew from the earlier O-290. Depending on variant, models produce 150 hp (112 kW) or 160 hp (119 kW), with compression ratios of 7.00:1 and 8.50:1 respectively. Fuel-injected versions are designated IO-320. The engine family includes over 90 models differing in carburetion, magnetos, mounting, and accessory drives.

Applications

The O-320 powers many popular light aircraft, including variants of the Cessna 172 Skyhawk, Piper PA-28 Cherokee, Piper PA-18 Super Cub (later models), Grumman American AA-5, and numerous homebuilt designs such as the Van's RV series.

Specifications (O-320-A1A)

  • Type: four-cylinder, air-cooled, horizontally opposed piston engine
  • Bore: 5.125 in; Stroke: 3.875 in
  • Displacement: 319.8 cu in (5.24 L)
  • Dry weight: about 244 lb (111 kg)
  • Power output: 150 hp at 2,700 rpm
