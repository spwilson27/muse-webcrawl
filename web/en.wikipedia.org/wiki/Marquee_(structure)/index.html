Marquee (structure)

A marquee is a projecting structure over the entrance of a theatre, hotel, or other public building, designed both to shelter patrons and to display advertising for current attractions. In its most familiar form — the theatre marquee — it is a large, often illuminated sign projecting perpendicular to the building facade, with changeable lettering announcing performers and show titles, surrounded by rows of flashing or chasing incandescent bulbs.

The word came into North American usage from the British "marquee", a large tent, and by extension a canopy. In British English the building sense is more usually called a canopy, while "marquee" retains the tent meaning; the projecting sign sense described here is chiefly American.

History

Projecting canopies existed over theatre entrances in the 19th century, but the electric marquee blossomed with commercial cinema in the early 20th century. As film palaces competed for patrons in the 1910s and 1920s, marquees grew into elaborate steel structures clad in sheet metal, glass, neon, and hundreds of exposed bulbs. Art deco and streamline moderne styling made the marquee the visual centrepiece of the building. The vertical blade sign rising above the roofline, bearing the theatre's name, was frequently paired with the marquee.

After the Second World War, suburbanization and television reduced the palace era, and many marquees were simplified, stripped of neon, or demolished. From the 1970s onward, preservation movements restored notable examples, and LED technology has since replaced incandescent chasing bulbs in many renovated signs while retaining the classic look.

Design

A traditional marquee is a steel frame cantilevered or suspended by tie rods from the facade, with a soffit (underside) studded with light bulbs to illuminate the pavement below. Reader boards on the front and sides carry changeable letters — historically painted or cast letters, later plastic or aluminium letters on tracks, and in modern versions electronic message boards. Building and electrical codes govern projection over sidewalks, structural loading, and sign illumination.
