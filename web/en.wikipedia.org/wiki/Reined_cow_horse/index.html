Reined cow horse

Type: Western equestrian competition
Phases: Herd work, rein work, cow work
Governing body: National Reined Cow Horse Association (NRCHA)
Origin: California vaquero tradition

Reined cow horse is a western equestrian sport, governed by the National Reined Cow Horse Association, in which horse and rider work cattle through a series of tests combining the precision of reining with the practical skills of a working ranch horse. It descends from the Californio vaquero tradition of training bridle horses over several years.

Competition phases

Events typically include herd work (cutting a single cow from a group), rein work (a reining pattern of circles, spins, stops and lead changes), and cow work, in which the horse controls a single cow down the fence, boxes it at the end of the arena and circles it in both directions. In some formats, steer stopping and roping replace or supplement the fence work.

Training tradition

Traditional reined cow horses progress through stages of equipment — hackamore, two-rein, and finally the straight-up bridle — over four or more years, producing a horse that works largely from seat and leg cues with a light rein.
