Expansion of Heathrow Airport

The expansion of Heathrow Airport — principally the proposal for a third runway northwest of the existing airport — has been debated in the United Kingdom since the 1990s. Heathrow, near London, is one of the world's busiest international airports and has operated at close to its runway capacity for decades, prompting repeated proposals to add runway and terminal capacity.

History

Successive governments studied London airport capacity from the Roskill Commission of the 1960s–70s onward. A 2003 aviation white paper supported a third runway at Heathrow; the Labour government approved it in 2009, but the decision was overturned by the coalition government in 2010. The Airports Commission, chaired by Sir Howard Davies, was set up in 2012 and in 2015 unanimously recommended a northwest runway at Heathrow over a second runway at Gatwick.

Parliamentary approval and legal challenges

In June 2018 the House of Commons voted 415–119 in favour of the Airports National Policy Statement supporting the third runway. Opponents, including local councils and environmental groups, challenged the decision; in February 2020 the Court of Appeal ruled the policy unlawful for failing to take the Paris Agreement on climate change into account, but in December 2020 the Supreme Court overturned that ruling, allowing the project to proceed to the planning (Development Consent Order) stage.

Arguments

Supporters argue expansion is needed to maintain the UK's hub connectivity and trade links, creating jobs and adding capacity for tens of millions of passengers a year. Opponents cite increased carbon emissions, noise and air pollution for communities under flight paths, and the demolition of homes in villages such as Harmondsworth and Longford.
