Sanxian

A sanxian
ClassificationChordophone; plucked lute
OriginChina
RelatedShamisen, sanshin

The sanxian (Chinese: 三弦; literally "three strings") is a traditional Chinese lute with a long neck and three strings. Its body is traditionally covered with snakeskin, usually python skin, stretched over a rounded rectangular resonator. It produces a dry, percussive tone similar in character to that of the banjo.

History

The sanxian appeared in China around the Yuan dynasty (13th–14th centuries), probably developing from Central Asian lutes transmitted along the Silk Road. It became a staple accompaniment instrument in narrative singing, opera, and ensemble music across China. In the Qing dynasty it was a central instrument of Jiangnan sizhu ensembles.

Descendants

The sanxian was transmitted to the Ryukyu Islands, where it became the sanshin, and from there to Japan, where it evolved into the shamisen, one of the most recognisable instruments of Japanese traditional music.

Construction and playing

The instrument has a fretless neck, allowing glissando and microtonal inflection. Players pluck the strings with the fingernails or with picks. Sanxian come in several sizes; the smaller northern sanxian is used in genres such as Suzhou pingtan and northern storytelling, while larger instruments are used in southern traditions.
