Nonpartisan League

The Nonpartisan League (NPL) was an American political movement founded in North Dakota in 1915 by former Socialist organizer A. C. Townley. Organizing farmers against out-of-state corporate interests — railroads, banks and grain mills based in Minneapolis and Chicago — the League achieved a remarkable takeover of North Dakota's state government and spread to more than a dozen other states and Canadian provinces.

Program and achievements

Rather than form a third party, the League endorsed candidates in whichever major-party primary it could win. In 1916 its candidate Lynn Frazier became governor, and by 1919 the League controlled the state government, enacting a program of state ownership: the Bank of North Dakota (still the only state-owned general bank in the US), a state mill and elevator, hail insurance, and workmen's compensation. The League declined amid wartime accusations of disloyalty, internal strife and a 1921 recall that removed Frazier.

Legacy

In 1956 the League merged with North Dakota's Democratic Party to form the Democratic–Nonpartisan League Party (D-NPL), which still exists. Its experiment remains a landmark of American agrarian populism and state-level economic intervention.
