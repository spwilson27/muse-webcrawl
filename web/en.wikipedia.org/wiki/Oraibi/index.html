Oraibi

Oraibi, also known as Old Oraibi, is a Hopi village in Navajo County, Arizona, United States, located on Third Mesa on the Hopi Reservation. Founded before A.D. 1100, it is widely regarded as the oldest continuously inhabited settlement in the United States (a distinction it shares in debate with Acoma Pueblo in New Mexico).

History

Oraibi was established by the Hopi people around the late 11th or early 12th century, when its spring made the mesa-top site viable. Spanish missionaries reached the village in the 17th century; a mission was established in 1629 but abandoned following the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, after which the Hopi resisted Spanish re-conquest.

The Oraibi split

In 1906 the village divided in the famous Oraibi Split, a conflict between "Friendly" (Kikmongwi-aligned traditionalists are usually termed Hostiles in the literature) factions over relations with the United States government, schooling, and land allotment. The losing faction left and founded the village of Hotevilla, while others later founded Bacavi. The split sharply reduced Oraibi's population, which had been among the largest of the Hopi villages.

Today

Old Oraibi remains a traditional village; photography and recording are prohibited for visitors out of respect for residents' privacy and religious practice. It lies within the Hopi Reservation, entirely surrounded by the Navajo Nation.
