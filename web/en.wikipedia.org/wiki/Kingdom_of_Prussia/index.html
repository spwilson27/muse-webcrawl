The Kingdom of Prussia (German: Königreich Preußen) was a German kingdom that constituted the state of Prussia between 1701 and 1918. It was the driving force behind the unification of Germany in 1871 and was the leading state of the German Empire until its dissolution.

Founding

The kingdom arose from the personal union of Brandenburg and the Duchy of Prussia under the Hohenzollern electors. In the Treaty of Crown (1700), Frederick III of Brandenburg secured Emperor Leopold I's consent to assume a royal title; because the duchy lay outside the Holy Roman Empire, he crowned himself "King in Prussia" at Königsberg on 18 January 1701 as Frederick I.

Rise to great power

Frederick William I, the "Soldier King", built one of Europe's finest armies and a famously frugal, efficient bureaucracy. His son Frederick II ("the Great") seized Silesia from Austria in the 1740s and survived the Seven Years' War (1756–63), establishing Prussia as a European great power; he also annexed West Prussia in the First Partition of Poland (1772), uniting Brandenburg and Prussia territorially and taking the title "King of Prussia".

Napoleonic era and reform

Crushing defeats at Jena–Auerstedt (1806) triggered the Stein–Hardenberg reforms: abolition of serfdom, municipal self-government, educational reform under Wilhelm von Humboldt, and military reorganisation by Scharnhorst and Gneisenau. Prussian forces contributed decisively to Napoleon's defeat, including at Waterloo (1815), and the Congress of Vienna awarded Prussia the Rhineland and Westphalia, making it a western as well as eastern power.

Unification of Germany

Under Chancellor Otto von Bismarck (from 1862), Prussia defeated Denmark (1864), Austria (1866), and France (1870–71), annexing Hanover, Hesse-Kassel, Frankfurt, and Schleswig-Holstein, and excluding Austria from German affairs. On 18 January 1871 Wilhelm I was proclaimed German Emperor in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles. Within the Empire, Prussia remained dominant—its king was emperor, its minister-president usually imperial chancellor, and it held a blocking minority in the Bundesrat—but its politics kept the restrictive three-class franchise until 1918.

Abolition

Wilhelm II abdicated on 9 November 1918 amid defeat and revolution. The kingdom became the Free State of Prussia within the Weimar Republic, a democratic stronghold until the Preußenschlag of 1932; the state of Prussia itself was formally abolished by the Allied Control Council in 1947.
