Sulfide

A sulfide (or sulphide in British English) is an inorganic anion of sulfur with the chemical formula S2−, or any compound containing one or more S2− ions. Salts of sulfides have strongly basic, corrosive properties, and many important metal ores — such as pyrite, chalcopyrite and galena — are sulfide minerals.

Chemistry

The sulfide ion does not exist free in aqueous solution; sulfide salts hydrolyse strongly, generating hydrosulfide (HS−) and hydroxide ions. Aqueous sulfide solutions absorb oxygen from air and oxidise to polysulfides and thiosulfate. Many metal sulfides are extremely insoluble, a property exploited in classical qualitative inorganic analysis, where precipitation groups are based on sulfide solubility.

Occurrence and uses

Sulfide minerals are the principal ores of many metals, including lead, zinc, copper and mercury. Zinc sulfide is used as a phosphor and pigment, and sulfide chemistry is important in petroleum refining, wastewater treatment and the biogeochemical sulfur cycle.
