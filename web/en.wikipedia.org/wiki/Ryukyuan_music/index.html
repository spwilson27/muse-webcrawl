Ryukyuan music

Ryukyuan music is the traditional and folk music of the Ryukyu Islands of southwestern Japan, principally Okinawa and the Amami Islands. Its defining instrument is the sanshin, a three-stringed lute covered in snakeskin (or, in Amami, played with a distinctive high falsetto vocal style), and its characteristic sound is based on the ryūkyū pentatonic scale (do–mi–fa–sol–ti), which gives Okinawan music its distinctive character.

History and genres

During the Ryukyu Kingdom, a refined court music developed at Shuri, performed for the royal court and for Chinese investiture envoys, alongside ceremonial dances such as those preserved in the classical dance-drama kumi odori (inscribed by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage). Folk music (min'yō) grew in the villages and castle towns, with work songs, festival songs, and love songs sung to the sanshin, often in the ryūka verse form of 8-8-8-6 syllables.

After Okinawa's annexation by Japan and the devastation of 1945, Okinawan music spread to mainland Japan and abroad. Artists such as Rinsho Kadekaru, Seijin Noborikawa, and later Shoukichi Kina with Champloose, BEGIN, Rimi Natsukawa (whose "Nada Sōsō" became a nationwide hit), and the Amami singer Chitose Hajime brought Ryukyuan sounds into Japanese pop. The annual eisa drum-dance festivals remain a central living tradition.

Instruments

Beyond the sanshin, ensembles may include the koto (in court music), jabisen, drums such as the paranku used in eisa, the fu (flute), and finger-whistling (yubi-bue) used to encourage dancers. Amami folk song is distinguished by its falsetto (uragoé) singing and the Amami shamisen, tuned differently from the Okinawan sanshin.
