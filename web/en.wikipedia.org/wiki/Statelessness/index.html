Statelessness

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A stateless person is someone who is "not considered as a national by any state under the operation of its law" (1954 Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons). Stateless people lack the rights citizenship confers — often unable to obtain identity documents, travel, work legally, access education and healthcare, marry or register births — and may be subject to indefinite detention as undeportable non-citizens. UNHCR reports several million stateless people among populations it counts, with the true global total unknown and commonly estimated considerably higher.

Causes and cases

Statelessness arises from conflicting nationality laws (jus soli/jus sanguinis gaps), state succession, discriminatory laws (gender-discriminatory nationality laws in roughly two dozen countries; ethnic exclusion, most notoriously Myanmar's 1982 law against the Rohingya), administrative failure and lack of birth registration, and deprivation of nationality. Post-Soviet "non-citizens" in the Baltic states, the Bidoon of Kuwait, Dominicans of Haitian descent after a 2013 court ruling (partially remedied by law), and Palestinians in various statuses are standard examples. The 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness obliges safeguards for children born stateless in a territory; campaigns (UNHCR's #IBelong, 2014–24) secured reforms and hundreds of thousands of confirmations of nationality, while arbitrary deprivation — including counter-terrorism citizenship-stripping — pushes the other way.
