Terascale computing

Terascale computing refers to computing capable of at least one trillion (1012) operations per second — one teraFLOPS — a milestone first reached by supercomputers in the mid-1990s. The term was used prominently in the United States' Accelerated Strategic Computing Initiative and by Intel's Tera-scale Computing Research Program.

ASCI Red at Sandia National Laboratories

Milestones

The first computer to sustain one teraFLOPS on the LINPACK benchmark was Intel's ASCI Red at Sandia National Laboratories, which achieved 1.06 teraFLOPS in December 1996 using more than 9,000 Pentium Pro processors. It was built under the Department of Energy's Accelerated Strategic Computing Initiative to simulate nuclear weapons performance. Terascale performance subsequently spread: by the late 2000s a single graphics processing unit could reach a teraFLOPS, and the field moved on to petascale (2008) and exascale (2022) computing.

Intel Tera-scale Computing

In the 2000s Intel used "Tera-scale Computing" for a research programme on many-core processors, demonstrating the Teraflops Research Chip (Polaris) in 2007, an 80-core experimental processor achieving over one teraFLOPS. The programme studied how software and hardware should scale to processors with tens or hundreds of cores.
