Ant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ants are social insects of the family Formicidae, evolved from wasp-like ancestors over 100 million years ago and now among the most abundant animals on Earth — an estimated 20 quadrillion individuals. They live in colonies ranging from dozens to millions, with castes of queens, workers and males, and they dominate many terrestrial ecosystems.

Colony life

A colony is effectively a superorganism: one or more queens reproduce while sterile female workers forage, nurse brood, defend the nest and tend fungus gardens (leafcutters) or 'livestock' such as aphids. Communication is largely chemical, through pheromone trails. Ants farm, enslave other ants, sew nests from leaves, and form living bridges and rafts.

Ecological role

Ants turn and aerate soil on a scale rivalling earthworms, disperse seeds, control pests and serve as food for many animals. A few species are serious invaders (fire ants, Argentine ants) or household pests, and ant-keeping and myrmecology are established sciences and hobbies. E. O. Wilson, the leading ant biologist, used ants to develop theories of sociobiology and biodiversity.
