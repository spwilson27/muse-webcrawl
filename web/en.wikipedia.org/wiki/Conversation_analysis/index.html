Conversation analysis (CA) is an approach to the study of social interaction that focuses on the organisation of talk-in-interaction. It examines audio and video recordings of naturally occurring conversations to discover the systematic methods participants use to produce orderly interaction.

Origins

CA grew out of Harvey Sacks's lectures at UCLA in the 1960s, influenced by Harold Garfinkel's ethnomethodology and Erving Goffman's interaction order. Sacks, Emanuel Schegloff, and Gail Jefferson developed its core findings.

Basic structures

  • Turn-taking: speakers alternate with minimal gap and overlap, managed by a turn-constructional system.
  • Adjacency pairs: paired utterances such as question–answer, greeting–greeting, offer–acceptance.
  • Repair: mechanisms for fixing trouble in speaking, hearing, or understanding.
  • Sequence organisation and preference: some responses are structurally preferred (acceptances) over others (refusals).

Transcription

CA uses Jeffersonian notation capturing pauses to tenths of a second, overlaps, intonation, and laughter, because the details of delivery are treated as analytically vital.

Applications

The method is applied to medical consultations, emergency calls, court proceedings, helplines, and human–computer interaction design.
