Cinderella (1950 film)

Cinderella is a 1950 American animated musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney and directed by Wilfred Jackson, Hamilton Luske, and Clyde Geronimi. Based on Charles Perrault's fairy tale "Cinderella", it is the twelfth Disney animated feature film. The film features the voices of Ilene Woods, Eleanor Audley, Verna Felton, and William Phipps.

Plot

Cinderella, a kind young woman, is forced into servitude by her cruel stepmother Lady Tremaine and her jealous stepsisters Anastasia and Drizella after her father's death. With the help of her animal friends — the mice Jaq and Gus and the dog Bruno — and her Fairy Godmother, whose magic transforms a pumpkin into a carriage and Cinderella's rags into a ball gown, she attends the royal ball and falls in love with Prince Charming. Fleeing at midnight, she leaves behind a glass slipper; the Grand Duke's search finds its owner, and Cinderella is freed from her stepmother's household to marry the prince.

Production and reception

The film was Disney's first animated feature since Bambi (1942) and is widely credited with saving the Disney studio from financial ruin after a series of wartime package films. Songs include "Bibbidi-Bobbidi-Boo", "A Dream Is a Wish Your Heart Makes", and "So This Is Love". It was nominated for three Academy Awards. In 2018, the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry. Two direct-to-video sequels and a 2015 live-action remake followed.
