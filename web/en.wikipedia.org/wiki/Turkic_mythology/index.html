Turkic mythology

Turkic mythology comprises the myths, legends and religious beliefs of the Turkic peoples of Central Asia, Siberia and Anatolia. It is closely tied to Tengrism, the shamanic sky-god religion of the early Turks, and shares many features with Mongolic mythology.

Deities and spirits

The supreme god is Tengri, the eternal blue sky. Other important figures include Ülgen, the benevolent creator god; Erlik, ruler of the underworld; Umay, goddess of fertility and protector of children; and Kayra, a primordial creator deity. Nature spirits (iye) inhabit rivers, mountains and trees, and are honoured with offerings.

Cosmology

The world is divided into three layers: the sky, the earth, and the underworld, connected by the World Tree (Ağaç Ana), whose branches reach to heaven and roots to the realm of the dead. Shamans (kam) were believed to travel between these worlds.

Epics and legends

Famous traditions include the Ergenekon legend, in which the Turks escape a hidden valley by melting an iron mountain; the Oghuz Khagan epic; the Manas epic of the Kyrgyz, one of the longest epic poems in the world; and tales of the she-wolf Asena, ancestress of the Göktürks. After the conversion of many Turkic peoples to Islam, these myths blended with Islamic and Persian motifs.
