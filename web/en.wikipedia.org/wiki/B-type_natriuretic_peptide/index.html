B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP), also known as brain natriuretic peptide because it was first identified in porcine brain, is a hormone secreted mainly by the ventricles of the heart in response to stretching of cardiomyocytes caused by increased blood volume and pressure. It is widely used clinically as a biomarker for heart failure.

Physiology

BNP is synthesised as a preprohormone of 134 amino acids, processed to proBNP and then cleaved into active BNP (32 amino acids) and the inactive fragment NT-proBNP. BNP promotes natriuresis (sodium excretion in urine), diuresis, vasodilation and suppression of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system, thereby reducing blood pressure and cardiac workload.

Clinical use

Measurement of BNP or NT-proBNP in blood helps diagnose and assess the severity of heart failure: values are typically markedly raised in acute decompensated heart failure and lower values make the diagnosis unlikely. Levels also carry prognostic information.

History

The peptide was isolated in 1988 by Sudoh and colleagues, following the earlier discovery of atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP).
