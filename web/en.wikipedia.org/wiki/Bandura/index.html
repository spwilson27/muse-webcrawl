Bandura

The bandura (Ukrainian: бандура) is a Ukrainian plucked string folk instrument that combines elements of the zither and the lute. It typically has between 31 and 68 strings and is played by plucking the strings with the fingers of both hands. The bandura is one of the most recognisable symbols of Ukrainian national culture and musical heritage.

History

The bandura developed from earlier lute-like instruments such as the kobza, which was played by itinerant Cossack musicians known as kobzars. By the 18th and 19th centuries the instrument had acquired its characteristic asymmetrical body and a large number of treble strings stretched across the soundboard. In the early 20th century, figures such as Hnat Khotkevych modernised bandura technique and construction, and the instrument became central to Ukrainian folk ensembles.

Construction

Traditional banduras are carved from a single piece of wood, usually willow or maple, while modern instruments are often built from several glued pieces. Short bass strings run along the neck, and numerous treble strings are anchored across the face of the body, allowing a wide pitch range and a harp-like arpeggiated texture.

Playing tradition

Bandura players perform epic historical songs called dumy, religious psalms, and folk melodies. During the Soviet period many kobzars were persecuted, but the instrument survived and experienced a revival after Ukrainian independence in 1991. Today bandura schools and ensembles exist throughout Ukraine and the Ukrainian diaspora.
