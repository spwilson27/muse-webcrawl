Chuuk Lagoon, formerly Truk Lagoon, is a large sheltered body of water in the central Pacific Ocean, in Chuuk State of the Federated States of Micronesia. A protective reef, some 225 kilometres around, encloses a natural harbour of about 2,130 square kilometres studded with volcanic islands, the largest being Weno (Moen), where the state capital is located. The lagoon is famous both as Imperial Japan's principal Pacific naval anchorage in the Second World War and, since 1944, as the world's premier wreck-diving site.

History

The islands of Chuuk were part of Spain's Caroline Islands colony, sold to Germany in 1899, and occupied by Japan in 1914; Japan administered them under a League of Nations mandate after the First World War. The Japanese built Truk into a formidable naval base — sometimes called the "Gibraltar of the Pacific" — serving as the forward anchorage of the Combined Fleet.

Operation Hailstone

On 17–18 February 1944, US Navy carrier aircraft and surface forces attacked Truk in Operation Hailstone, sinking more than 50 Japanese ships (including the Fujikawa Maru, Shinkoku Maru, Rio de Janeiro Maru and Heian Maru) and destroying some 250 aircraft. Rather than invade, the Allies bypassed and isolated the garrison, which remained cut off until Japan's surrender in 1945.

Wreck diving

The sunken fleet — the "Ghost Fleet of Truk Lagoon" — lies largely intact in warm, sheltered water, softened by coral growth and containing aircraft, vehicles, munitions and human remains. The lagoon is a leading destination for technical and recreational wreck divers; the wrecks are protected as a national monument, though leaking fuel oil from corroding hulls is an environmental concern.

Geography and administration

Within the lagoon are eleven major volcanic islands and dozens of smaller islets. Weno hosts the state capital, also called Weno, and Chuuk International Airport. The population of the lagoon islands is around 37,000.
