Częstochowa

Częstochowa
Voivodeship: Silesian, Poland
River: Warta
Known for: Jasna Góra Monastery

Częstochowa is a city in southern Poland on the Warta River, with a population of roughly 215,000. It is internationally known as a major Catholic pilgrimage centre, home to the Jasna Góra Monastery and the revered icon of the Black Madonna of Częstochowa.

History

First mentioned in 1220, Częstochowa developed around the Pauline monastery founded on Jasna Góra in 1382. In 1655, during the Swedish invasion known as the Deluge, the monastery's small garrison famously repelled a much larger Swedish force, an event that became a symbol of Polish national resistance. The city later grew as an industrial centre for iron, textiles, and paper.

Pilgrimage

Millions of pilgrims visit Jasna Góra each year, many walking in organised pilgrimages from across Poland, especially for the feasts of the Assumption (15 August) and the Nativity of Mary (8 September). The Black Madonna icon, crowned "Queen of Poland" in 1717, is credited by the faithful with protecting the nation.
