Gavarnie Falls (French: Grande Cascade de Gavarnie) is a tiered waterfall in France. With an overall drop of 422 metres, it is the tallest waterfall in mainland France. The falls are situated in the Cirque de Gavarnie, within the Pyrenees National Park, close to the village of Gavarnie in the department of Hautes-Pyrénées.

Description

The waterfall is formed by the upper course of the Gave de Pau, which is fed by meltwater from glaciers and snowfields on the Franco-Spanish border, including drainage from the Monte Perdido massif. The water descends the immense glacial amphitheatre of the Cirque de Gavarnie in two main steps: a first cascade of about 281 metres, followed by a shorter, sloping cascade into the valley floor.

Flow is strongly seasonal, peaking in late spring and early summer with snowmelt. In very cold winters the falls can freeze, attracting ice climbers.

Setting and tourism

The Cirque de Gavarnie, with walls up to 1,500 metres high, forms part of the Pyrénées-Mont Perdu UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1997. A footpath from the village of Gavarnie leads across the plateau to the Hôtellerie du Cirque and the base of the falls, and the site is one of the most visited natural attractions in the French Pyrenees.
