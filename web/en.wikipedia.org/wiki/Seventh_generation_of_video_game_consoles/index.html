Seventh generation of video game consoles

The seventh generation of home video game consoles began in November 2005 with the release of Microsoft's Xbox 360 and continued with Sony's PlayStation 3 (2006) and Nintendo's Wii (2006). The generation is noted for high-definition graphics, online services and digital distribution, motion-controlled gaming, and the integration of consoles as home media centres.

Home consoles

  • Xbox 360 (2005) — launched a year ahead of its rivals, building a strong position with the Xbox Live online service. Lifetime sales exceeded 84 million units.
  • PlayStation 3 (2006) — introduced the Cell processor and built-in Blu-ray playback; a high launch price initially slowed adoption, but sales eventually passed 87 million units.
  • Wii (2006) — eschewed raw power for the motion-sensing Wii Remote, greatly expanding the gaming audience; it became the generation's best-selling home console with over 101 million units sold.

Features and innovations

The generation popularised online multiplayer and marketplaces (Xbox Live, PlayStation Network, Wii Shop Channel), downloadable games and add-on content, HD resolutions, wireless controllers as standard, and motion peripherals such as PlayStation Move and Microsoft's camera-based Kinect (2010), which became one of the fastest-selling consumer electronics devices. Handheld gaming in the same era was dominated by the Nintendo DS and PlayStation Portable.

Legacy

All three consoles were succeeded in the eighth generation by the Wii U (2012), PlayStation 4 (2013), and Xbox One (2013), though seventh-generation hardware and software continued to sell for years afterward.
