The Mamirauá Sustainable Development Reserve (Portuguese: Reserva de Desenvolvimento Sustentável Mamirauá) is a protected area in the Brazilian state of Amazonas, near the town of Tefé, at the confluence of the Solimões, Japurá and Auati-Paraná rivers. It protects 11,240 km² of várzea — seasonally flooded Amazonian forest — and was the first sustainable development reserve created in Brazil.

History

The reserve originated in a study area established in the 1980s by primatologist José Márcio Ayres to protect the white uakari monkey (Cacajao calvus calvus), whose range is almost entirely within the reserve. It was created in 1990 as an ecological station and converted in 1996 to the new category of sustainable development reserve, under which resident riverine (ribeirinho) communities participate in managing natural resources.

Biodiversity and research

Mamirauá is home to a rich floodplain fauna including pink river dolphins (boto), tucuxi, arapaima (pirarucu), black caiman, jaguars and many endemic birds and primates. The Mamirauá Sustainable Development Institute conducts research and community-based management programmes — notably the recovery of pirarucu fisheries through community quotas — and supports ecotourism at the floating Uakari Lodge.
