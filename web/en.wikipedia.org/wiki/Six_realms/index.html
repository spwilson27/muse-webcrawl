Six realms

The Six Realms (Sanskrit: ṣaḍgati) are the six modes of existence within saṃsāra in Buddhist cosmology, into which beings are reborn according to their karma. They are conventionally depicted in the bhavacakra, the Wheel of Life, held by Yama, the lord of death. None of the realms is permanent: beings pass through them over countless lives until liberation (nirvana) is attained.

The six realms

  • Deva realm (gods): long, pleasurable lives spent in bliss and privilege; because devas experience little suffering, they neglect the dharma and eventually fall when their karma is exhausted.
  • Asura realm (demigods): powerful beings consumed by jealousy, anger, and endless conflict with the devas. Some schemes merge this realm with the god realm, giving five realms.
  • Human realm: a balance of pleasure and pain; considered the most fortunate rebirth for practice, since humans can hear and follow the dharma.
  • Animal realm: existence dominated by instinct, fear, ignorance, and being preyed upon or exploited.
  • Preta realm (hungry ghosts): beings tormented by insatiable hunger and thirst, often pictured with huge bellies and needle-thin throats.
  • Naraka realm (hells): realms of intense torment — eight hot hells and eight cold hells — where beings suffer for enormous spans of time, though even hell is impermanent.

Interpretation

The realms are understood both as literal destinations of rebirth and, especially in modern and psychological readings, as states of mind that beings move through even within a single human life: anger as the asura realm, craving as the preta realm, complacent pleasure as the deva realm. In East Asian Buddhism the scheme is sometimes given as five realms, with asuras counted among other categories.
