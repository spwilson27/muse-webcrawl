Resampling (audio)

Resampling in audio signal processing is the conversion of a digital audio signal from one sampling rate to another — for example from 48 kHz (common in video) to 44.1 kHz (the CD standard) — while preserving the signal's content.

Process

Sample-rate conversion is ideally performed by interpolating the signal at the new sample times. By the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem, a band-limited signal can be reconstructed exactly using sinc interpolation; practical converters approximate this with long polyphase FIR filters. Down-sampling (decimation) requires an anti-aliasing low-pass filter, while up-sampling (interpolation) requires an anti-imaging filter.

Quality

Cheap or real-time resamplers using linear interpolation introduce aliasing and imaging artefacts. High-quality converters (including asynchronous sample-rate converters in audio interfaces) achieve distortion and noise floors below −120 dB. Rational ratios (e.g. 160:147 for 48↔44.1 kHz) complicate filter design, and jitter in the clocks can add noise.
