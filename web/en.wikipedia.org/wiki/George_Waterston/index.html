George Waterston

George Waterston

Born: 10 April 1911, Leith, Edinburgh, Scotland

Died: 20 September 1980

Occupation: Ornithologist, conservationist

Known for: Fair Isle Bird Observatory; RSPB Scotland

George Waterston OBE (10 April 1911 – 20 September 1980) was a Scottish ornithologist and conservationist. He is best remembered as the founder of the Fair Isle Bird Observatory and as the first director of the Royal Society for the Protection of Birds (RSPB) in Scotland.

Fair Isle

Waterston first visited Fair Isle, between Orkney and Shetland, in the 1930s and became convinced of its importance for studying bird migration. While a prisoner of war in Germany he planned a bird observatory for the island; after the war he purchased Fair Isle in 1948 and established the Bird Observatory Trust. In 1954 he sold the island to the National Trust for Scotland, ensuring its long-term protection.

Conservation work

Waterston helped revive the fortunes of the osprey in Scotland, organising the protection of the Loch Garten nest in the 1950s ("Operation Osprey"), and was instrumental in the re-establishment of the species as a British breeding bird. He served as Scottish director of the RSPB and was a central figure in the post-war Scottish conservation movement.
