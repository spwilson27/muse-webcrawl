The Battle of the Catalaunian Plains, also called the Battle of Châlons, was fought on 20 June 451 AD (date traditional) between a coalition led by the Roman general Flavius Aetius and the Visigothic king Theodoric I, and the Huns under Attila with Ostrogothic and other allies, near modern Châlons-en-Champagne in France.

Background

Attila invaded Gaul in 451, claiming the hand of Honoria, sacking cities including Metz and besieging Orléans. Aetius assembled Roman troops with Visigoths, Alans, Franks and Burgundians to relieve Orléans and pursue Attila eastward.

Battle

On the Catalaunian Plains both sides fought for a ridge; the fighting was exceptionally bloody by ancient accounts. Theodoric was killed, but the Visigoths under his son Thorismund pressed the Huns back to their wagon camp. Attila prepared a funeral pyre of saddles but was not attacked; Aetius let him withdraw across the Rhine, reportedly to preserve the Huns as a counterweight to the Visigoths.

Significance

The battle halted Attila's westward expansion into Gaul; he invaded Italy in 452 instead, meeting Pope Leo I. Casualty figures in sources are exaggerated, but the battle is remembered as one of the decisive clashes of late antiquity in the Migration Period.
