Australian art

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Australian art is the visual arts produced in Australia, or about Australia, since ancient times. It includes Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander art, colonial art, landscape painting of the nineteenth century, and modern and contemporary art.

The Australian landscape, central to the Heidelberg School.

Indigenous art

Australia's Indigenous peoples have produced art for tens of thousands of years, including rock engravings and paintings, bark painting, body decoration and ceremonial objects. In the twentieth century, Aboriginal art — including the dot paintings of the Papunya Tula movement from the 1970s and artists such as Albert Namatjira and Emily Kame Kngwarreye — achieved an international market and acclaim.

Colonial and landscape art

European art in Australia began with natural history illustration and topographical views by convict and visiting artists. In the late nineteenth century the Heidelberg School — Tom Roberts, Arthur Streeton, Frederick McCubbin and Charles Conder — developed an Australian form of impressionism, painting the light and colours of the bush; Roberts' Shearing the Rams (1890) became an iconic national image. Nationalist myth-making continued through the work of Hans Heysen and Russell Drysdale.

Modern and contemporary art

Modernism arrived with artists such as Margaret Preston and Grace Cossington Smith, and was contested by the Angry Penguins circle around Sidney Nolan in the 1940s. Post-war art included abstract painting, the work of Brett Whiteley, and waves of conceptual, feminist and multicultural art. Contemporary Australian art is represented internationally by figures including Tracey Moffatt and Patricia Piccinini, and the country hosts major institutions such as the National Gallery of Australia and the Art Gallery of New South Wales, plus the Biennale of Sydney.
