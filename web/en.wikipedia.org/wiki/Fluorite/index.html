Overview

Fluorite is the mineral form of calcium fluoride, CaF₂, and the principal source of the element fluorine and of hydrofluoric acid. It defines hardness 4 on the Mohs scale, has perfect octahedral cleavage, and occurs in a wide range of colours — purple, green, yellow, blue — caused by colour centres and trace rare earths. The phenomenon of fluorescence is named after fluorite, in which it was studied by George Gabriel Stokes in 1852.

Uses

Fluorite (fluorspar) is graded as acid-grade (for HF and aluminium production), ceramic-grade and metallurgical-grade (a flux in steelmaking, whence its name). Optical fluorite (natural and synthetic CaF₂) is used in apochromatic telescope and camera lenses for its low dispersion. Famous localities include Derbyshire's Blue John variety (a banded purple form unique to two mines) and Illinois's Rosiclare district.
