The Ryugyong Hotel (Korean: 류경호텔), sometimes spelled Ryugyŏng Hotel, is an unfinished 105-storey pyramid-shaped skyscraper in Pyongyang, North Korea. At 330 metres it is the tallest building in the country and one of the tallest unoccupied buildings in the world. Its name means "capital of willows", a historical name for Pyongyang.

History

Construction began in 1987, intended to open for the 1989 World Festival of Youth and Students with 3,000 rooms and revolving restaurants, but stopped in 1992 amid the economic crisis that followed the collapse of the Soviet Union. The bare concrete shell stood windowless over the city for sixteen years, frequently cropped out of official photographs and dubbed the "Hotel of Doom" in foreign media.

Resumption and status

In 2008 the Egyptian Orascom Group began work on the building, installing glass panels and telecommunications equipment; the exterior was completed by 2011 and a partial opening announced for 2013 was cancelled. Plans involving the Kempinski hotel group were suspended, and despite later LED façade displays the interior has never opened to guests, leaving the tower a prominent but empty landmark.
