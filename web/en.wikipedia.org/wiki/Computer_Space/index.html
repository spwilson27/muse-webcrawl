Computer Space

Computer Space is a space combat arcade video game released in 1971 by Nutting Associates. Created by Nolan Bushnell and Ted Dabney, it was the first commercially sold coin-operated video game and the first widely available video game of any kind. It was inspired by Spacewar!, a 1962 game that ran on early minicomputers at research institutions.

Gameplay

The player controls a rocket ship that battles a pair of flying saucers in a field of stars. Using thrust and rotation controls, the player fires missiles at the saucers while avoiding their return fire. A game lasts for a set period of time; if the player scores more hits than the saucers by the end, play continues for another round.

Development

Bushnell, who had played Spacewar! at the University of Utah, wanted to bring the experience to a coin-operated format. Because minicomputers were too expensive, he and Dabney designed dedicated hardware using television components and logic circuits. Nutting Associates manufactured the game in a distinctive fiberglass cabinet with a futuristic curved design.

Reception and legacy

Computer Space was a moderate success, reportedly earning over US$1,000,000, though its controls proved too complex for casual patrons of bars, its intended venues. A two-player version followed in 1973. Bushnell and Dabney went on to found Atari, Inc. in 1972 and released Pong that year, launching the commercial video game industry.
