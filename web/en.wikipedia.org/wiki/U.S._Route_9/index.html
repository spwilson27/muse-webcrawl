U.S. Route 9 (US 9) is a United States Numbered Highway in the northeastern United States, running from Laurel, Delaware, north to Champlain, New York, near the Canada–United States border. It is one of only two U.S. Highways with a ferry connection (the other being US 10): the Cape May–Lewes Ferry carries the route across Delaware Bay between Lewes, Delaware, and North Cape May, New Jersey.

Route description

In Delaware, US 9 runs east–west from Laurel to Lewes. The ferry crossing connects it to New Jersey, where the highway runs north roughly parallel to the Jersey Shore, passing through Atlantic City-area communities and eventually joining the Garden State Parkway corridor. In northern New Jersey it passes through the New York metropolitan area, crossing the Hudson River via the George Washington Bridge alongside US 1 and Interstate 95. In New York, the route follows the east bank of the Hudson River through the Hudson Valley, then continues through the Adirondacks and along the western shore of Lake Champlain to its northern terminus at the Canadian border in Champlain, where it meets Interstate 87.

History

US 9 was designated in 1926 as one of the original U.S. Highways. Over the decades, many segments were bypassed or multiplexed with Interstate highways, and portions of the route follow historic roads including the old King's Highway in New Jersey and the Albany Post Road in New York.
