Leela Zero

Leela Zero is a free and open-source computer Go program released in 2017 by Belgian programmer Gian-Carlo Pascutto, author of the Sjeng chess engine. It is inspired by DeepMind's AlphaGo Zero papers and implements the same Monte Carlo tree search with a deep residual neural network, trained purely by self-play reinforcement learning with no human games as input.

Distributed training

Unlike AlphaGo Zero, which was trained on Google's private hardware, Leela Zero crowdsourced its training: volunteers around the world ran the client software, playing self-play games and uploading them to a central server, which periodically trained and published stronger network weights. Progress was tracked publicly from near-random play to professional-level strength within about a year.

Strength and use

Leela Zero reached a level competitive with top professional players and became a popular analysis tool, often used through graphical interfaces such as Lizzie. Its sister project, Leela Chess Zero, applied the same approach to chess. Development wound down around 2021 as the focus of the community shifted, and its role in analysis was largely taken over by KataGo.
