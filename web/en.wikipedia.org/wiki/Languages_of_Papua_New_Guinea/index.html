Languages of Papua New Guinea

Papua New Guinea is the most linguistically diverse country in the world, with more than 800 living languages — roughly 12% of the world's total — spoken by a population of around ten million. The languages fall into two broad groups: Austronesian languages, concentrated along the coasts and islands, and the many Papuan languages of the interior, a geographic rather than genealogical grouping comprising dozens of families, the largest being the Trans–New Guinea family.

Official and lingua francas

English is an official language and the language of government and secondary education, though spoken fluently by a minority. Two creoles serve as national lingua francas:

  • Tok Pisin — an English-based creole, the most widely understood language in the country, used in parliament and national media.
  • Hiri Motu — based on the Austronesian Motu language, historically used in the former Territory of Papua.

Papua New Guinea Sign Language was recognised as a fourth official language in 2015.

Diversity and endangerment

Many communities speak languages with only a few hundred or thousand speakers, and children in towns increasingly grow up speaking Tok Pisin as a first language. A large number of PNG languages are therefore endangered, though the country's rugged terrain and strong village identities have preserved its diversity far longer than in most regions.
