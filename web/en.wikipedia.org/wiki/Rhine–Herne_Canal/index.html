Rhine–Herne Canal

Location: North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany
Length: 45.6 km
Opened: 1914

The Rhine–Herne Canal (German: Rhein-Herne-Kanal) is a 45.6-kilometre-long transportation canal in the Ruhr area of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. It connects the Rhine at Duisburg with the Dortmund-Ems Canal near Herne, running roughly parallel to the Emscher river.

History

Construction began in 1906 and the canal opened in 1914. It was built to carry coal, steel, and bulk goods from the industrial cities of the Ruhr — including Duisburg, Oberhausen, Essen, Gelsenkirchen, and Herne — to the Rhine and the North Sea ports. A series of locks overcomes the height difference between the Rhine and the eastern canal pound.

Today

The canal remains an important freight waterway and has been progressively widened and deepened for larger vessels. Its banks also feature industrial heritage sites of the Ruhr, including the Gasometer Oberhausen near the canal.
