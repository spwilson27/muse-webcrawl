Tagish First Nation

The Tagish First Nation, formally the Carcross/Tagish First Nation, is a First Nations band government in the Yukon, Canada. Its original population centres are Carcross and Tagish, with membership also residing in Whitehorse and elsewhere. The Tagish people speak a Northern Athabaskan language closely related to Kaska and Tahltan; the last fully fluent native speaker, Angela Sidney (1902–1991), was a celebrated storyteller who worked with linguists to record Tagish language and tales.

History

The Tagish controlled trade routes between the coast and the interior through the White Pass and Chilkoot Pass, acting as middlemen with the coastal Tlingit, from whom they adopted many cultural practices and vocabulary. It was Tagish prospectors — Keish (Skookum Jim), Káa Goox (Dawson Charlie), and Shaaw Tláa (Kate Carmack) — together with George Carmack, who made the gold discovery on Bonanza Creek in 1896 that started the Klondike Gold Rush.

Self-government

The Carcross/Tagish First Nation signed its Final Agreement and Self-Government Agreement with Canada and Yukon in 2005, effective in 2006, under the Umbrella Final Agreement. The nation administers its settlement lands around Carcross, Tagish and the southern lakes, and shares its name officially in legislation as the Carcross/Tagish First Nation.
