Christianization of Armenia

Christianization of Armenia
DateTraditionally AD 301
Key figuresKing Tiridates III; Gregory the Illuminator
OutcomeArmenia becomes the first state to adopt Christianity as its official religion

The Christianization of Armenia took place in the early 4th century, when King Tiridates III adopted Christianity as the state religion — traditionally dated to AD 301 — making the Kingdom of Armenia the first state in history to do so, more than a decade before the Roman Empire granted Christianity toleration. The conversion is credited to Gregory the Illuminator, who became the first head (Catholicos) of the Armenian Apostolic Church.

Tradition

According to the history attributed to Agathangelos, Gregory, a Christian of Parthian royal descent, entered the king's service but was imprisoned in a pit at Khor Virap for some thirteen years after refusing to sacrifice to the goddess Anahit. Tiridates, stricken with an affliction that (in the legend) transformed him into a boar, was restored after his sister Khosrovidukht had a vision that only Gregory could heal him. Gregory was freed, healed the king, and preached throughout the kingdom; Tiridates was baptised, and pagan temples were destroyed or converted.

Consequences

Gregory was consecrated bishop at Caesarea in Cappadocia and built the cathedral of Etchmiadzin ("the Only-Begotten descended") at Vagharshapat. Christianity bound Armenia culturally to the Christian East while its adoption preceded and set it apart from both Rome and Zoroastrian Persia. A century later, Mesrop Mashtots created the Armenian alphabet (c. 405) so the Bible and liturgy could be translated, cementing a distinct national Christian identity.

Historiography

Some modern scholars date the conversion slightly later, around AD 314, following the Edict of Milan, arguing that the traditional date 301 is a later pious tradition; others defend the earlier date. Christianity had in any case been preached in Armenia earlier — tradition names the apostles Thaddeus and Bartholomew as its first evangelists.
