Hyperides, Athenian orator and leader of the anti-Macedonian party.

Hyperides (Greek: Ὑπερείδης, c. 390 – 322 BC) was an Athenian logographer (speechwriter) and politician, counted among the ten Attic orators. A pupil of Isocrates, he became a prominent advocate in the law courts and, with Demosthenes and Lycurgus, a leader of the party that resisted Macedonian domination of Greece.

Life

Hyperides made his career writing speeches for others, most famously defending the courtesan Phryne — legend held that when the case seemed lost he bared her breast to the judges, who acquitted her. In public life he prosecuted corrupt officials and opposed Philip II of Macedon. After the scandal of the Harpalus affair, however, he broke with Demosthenes and successfully prosecuted him (324 BC). Following Alexander the Great's death, Hyperides persuaded Athens to lead the revolt against Macedon known as the Lamian War, and delivered the celebrated funeral oration over the first year's dead. When the revolt was crushed in 322 BC, Antipater demanded the surrender of the anti-Macedonian leaders; Hyperides fled to Aegina, was captured, and was executed — ancient sources say his tongue was cut out before his death.

Works

Antiquity knew some 77 speeches attributed to him (about 52 considered genuine), but all were thought lost until papyrus finds in Egypt in the 19th century recovered substantial texts, including Against Demosthenes, For Lycophron, For Euxenippus, the speech against Athenogenes, and much of the Funeral Oration. A further palimpsest (the Archimedes Palimpsest) yielded additional fragments in the 21st century. Ancient critics praised his charm, wit and subtle irony, pairing him in grace with Lysias among the orators.
