French Wars of Religion

The French Wars of Religion were a series of civil wars between French Catholics and Protestants (Huguenots) from 1562 to 1598. Millions died from violence, famine and disease. The wars weakened the monarchy and were entangled with rivalry between noble houses — Guise, Bourbon and Montmorency — and foreign intervention by Spain and England.


Date: 1562–1598
Parties: Catholics vs Huguenots
Result: Edict of Nantes (1598)

Origins

Calvinism spread in France despite persecution. The Massacre of Vassy in 1562, when the Duke of Guise's men killed Huguenot worshippers, triggered the first war.

Major episodes

The St. Bartholomew's Day massacre of 1572, beginning in Paris after the wedding of Henry of Navarre and Margaret of Valois, killed thousands of Huguenots across France. The War of the Three Henrys (1587–1589) saw the assassinations of the Duke of Guise and King Henry III.

Settlement

Henry of Navarre, as Henry IV, converted to Catholicism — legend attributes to him "Paris is well worth a Mass" — and issued the Edict of Nantes in 1598, granting Huguenots substantial rights. The edict was revoked by Louis XIV in 1685.
