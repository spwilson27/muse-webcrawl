Negative triad

The negative triad, also known as Beck's cognitive triad, is a cognitive-therapeutic view of the three key elements of a person's belief system present in depression. It was proposed by Aaron Beck in 1967 and forms part of his cognitive theory of depression.

Components

The triad consists of negative views about:

  • The self – seeing oneself as inadequate, worthless or deficient
  • The world – interpreting experiences and one's environment as hostile, defeating or demanding
  • The future – expecting that current difficulties will continue indefinitely and that nothing will improve

According to Beck, depressed individuals develop these negative schemas from early experiences, and they bias attention, interpretation and memory, so that information consistent with the negative view is noticed and remembered while contradictory evidence is discounted.

Treatment

Cognitive therapy aims to identify, test and modify the distorted thoughts that make up the triad, using techniques such as behavioural experiments, thought records and activity scheduling.
