Barbara Radziwiłł (Polish: Barbara Radziwiłłówna; 1520/1523 – 8 May 1551) was Queen of Poland and Grand Duchess of Lithuania as the second wife of King Sigismund II Augustus. A member of the powerful Lithuanian Radziwiłł family, she is remembered for the great love affair and controversial marriage that defied the Polish nobility and her mother-in-law, Queen Bona Sforza.

Life

Barbara was the daughter of Jerzy Radziwiłł, Grand Hetman of Lithuania. Her first marriage, to the voivode Stanisław Gasztołd, ended with his death in 1542. She then became the mistress of Sigismund Augustus, the heir to the throne, and secretly married him in 1547. The marriage, revealed after Sigismund became king in 1548, provoked fierce opposition from the Sejm and from Bona Sforza, who feared Radziwiłł influence. Sigismund stood firm, and Barbara was crowned queen in Kraków in December 1550.

Death and legend

Barbara died only five months after her coronation, probably of cervical cancer, though rumours of poisoning by Bona Sforza circulated for centuries. Her early death and the king's devotion made her one of the great romantic figures of Polish and Lithuanian history, the subject of plays, poems, paintings, and later films and television series.
