Dari (دری, Darī, pronounced [dæˈɾiː]) is the variety of the Persian language spoken in Afghanistan, where it is one of the two official languages alongside Pashto. It serves as a lingua franca understood by the majority of Afghans and is the native language of groups including the Tajiks, Hazaras and Aimaks. Dari is mutually intelligible with Iranian Persian (Farsi) and Tajik, the three forming the main standard varieties of Persian, though Dari is often noted for retaining older phonological features and classical vocabulary.

Name and status

The name Dari, historically associated with the language of the royal court (darbār), was officially adopted for Afghan Persian by the 1964 constitution. Under earlier constitutions the language was designated Farsi. The name remains politically sensitive in Afghanistan, where debates over "Dari" versus "Farsi" touch on questions of national identity.

Features

Compared with the Persian of Iran, Dari pronunciation preserves distinctions such as the classical vowels ē and ō (majhul vowels) that have merged in Tehran speech, and Dari vocabulary includes expressions lost in Iran as well as loanwords from Pashto, Turkic languages, English and Russian. Formal written Dari and Iranian Persian are very close.

Use

Dari dominates government administration, higher education, media and commerce in much of Afghanistan, and is the principal language of Afghan literature in the Persian tradition, whose classical figures include Rumi (born in Balkh). Afghan refugees and diaspora communities in Iran, Pakistan and the West maintain Dari-language media and publishing.
