Baidu, Inc. is a Chinese multinational technology company specialising in Internet-related services, products, and artificial intelligence, headquartered in Beijing. Its Baidu search engine is the dominant web search service in China.

History

Baidu was incorporated in January 2000 by Robin Li and Eric Xu. It grew rapidly as Chinese internet use expanded, and its 2005 initial public offering on NASDAQ was the most successful foreign IPO debut on the exchange at the time. The company's name comes from a classical Chinese poem about searching for one's beloved amid chaos and distraction.

Products and services

Beyond search, Baidu operates Baidu Baike (an online encyclopedia), Baidu Maps, the iQIYI video streaming platform, and the Apollo autonomous-driving platform. It has invested heavily in artificial intelligence, including the ERNIE large language model family and its Ernie Bot generative AI service.

Criticism and regulation

Baidu has faced criticism over advertising practices, notably after the 2016 death of student Wei Zexi, who had pursued a treatment found through a paid Baidu search result, prompting tighter regulation of medical advertising in China.
