The Metropolitan Borough of Sefton is a local government district in Merseyside, north-west England. It stretches along the Irish Sea coast north of Liverpool from Bootle in the south to Southport in the north, and includes the towns and suburbs of Bootle, Crosby, Formby, Maghull, Litherland, and Netherton. The borough was created on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, and takes its name from the village and ancient parish of Sefton.

Geography and character

Sefton is a borough of contrasts: the dense, port-related urban fabric of Bootle adjoins Liverpool's docks — the Port of Liverpool lies largely within the borough — while further north lie commuter suburbs, the Victorian resort of Southport with its pier and Lord Street, and extensive protected dune systems. The Sefton Coast, including the pinewoods at Formby famous for red squirrels and the vast sands of Ainsdale, is among England's most important coastal nature areas, much of it designated for its wildlife.

Governance

Sefton Council is the borough's unitary local authority for most services, operating from town halls in Bootle and Southport. The borough forms part of the Liverpool City Region Combined Authority. Its parliamentary representation spans constituencies including Bootle, Sefton Central, and Southport.
