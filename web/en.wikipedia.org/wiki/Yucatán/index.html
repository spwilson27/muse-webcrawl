Yucatán

Yucatán, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Yucatán, is one of the 31 states of Mexico, located on the northern part of the Yucatán Peninsula. Its capital and largest city is Mérida.

Geography

The state is flat limestone lowland with no surface rivers, dotted with cenotes (sinkholes). It borders Quintana Roo, Campeche, and the Gulf of Mexico. The Chicxulub crater, from the asteroid impact that ended the age of dinosaurs 66 million years ago, lies partly offshore near its coast.

History

Yucatán was a heartland of Maya civilisation, home to Chichén Itzá, Uxmal, and other cities. Spanish conquest under the Montejos was completed in the 1540s. In the 19th century Yucatán twice declared independence, and the Caste War (1847–1901) saw Maya rebels hold much of the peninsula. Henequen (sisal) plantations made it one of Mexico's richest states around 1900.

Culture and economy

Yucatecan culture blends Maya and Spanish influences — language (Yucatec Maya is widely spoken), cuisine such as cochinita pibil, and music. Tourism, centred on Mérida and Maya sites, is a major industry.
