New Nordic Cuisine

New Nordic Cuisine (Danish: Ny Nordisk Mad) is a culinary movement launched in the Nordic countries in the early 2000s that emphasizes local, seasonal, and sustainably produced ingredients combined with traditional preservation techniques such as curing, smoking, pickling, and fermenting.

The manifesto

In 2004, Danish chef Claus Meyer and entrepreneur colleagues convened a symposium that produced the "Manifesto for the New Nordic Kitchen," signed by chefs including René Redzepi of Noma. Its principles included purity, freshness, simplicity, and ethics; promoting seasonal produce from the Nordic climate; and combining good taste with health and environmental responsibility. The Nordic Council of Ministers subsequently supported the program.

Influence

Noma in Copenhagen, repeatedly named the world's best restaurant, became the movement's flagship, and New Nordic ideas—such as foraging for wild herbs, using vinegars and fermented products, and celebrating humble ingredients like root vegetables and grains—spread internationally and influenced restaurant cooking worldwide.
