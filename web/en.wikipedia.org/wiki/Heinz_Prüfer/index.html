Heinz Prüfer

Ernst Paul Heinz Prüfer (10 November 1896 – 7 April 1934) was a German mathematician who worked in abelian group theory, algebraic number theory, knot theory and Sturm–Liouville theory. He is remembered for the Prüfer sequence (also called Prüfer code) and for Prüfer domains and Prüfer groups.

Life and work

Born in Wilhelmshaven, Prüfer studied at the University of Berlin under Issai Schur and received his doctorate in 1921. He worked at the universities of Hamburg and Jena and from 1927 at the University of Münster, where he remained until his early death from lung cancer at 37.

Contributions

In 1918 he introduced the Prüfer sequence, a bijection proving Cayley's formula counting labelled trees. His 1923 dissertation classified finitely generated abelian groups' infinite analogues — the Prüfer theorems for p-groups — and he defined the quasicyclic Prüfer p-group. In commutative algebra, Prüfer domains generalise Dedekind domains and remain a standard concept.
