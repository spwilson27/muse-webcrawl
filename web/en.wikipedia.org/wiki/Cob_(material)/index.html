Cob (material)

Cob, also known in some contexts as cobb or, in Devon, as a material for "Devon cob", is a natural building material made from subsoil, water, fibrous organic material (typically straw), and sometimes lime. The contents of subsoil vary, and if it does not contain the right mixture it can be modified with sand or clay. Cob is fireproof, termite proof, resistant to seismic activity, and uses low-cost materials, although it is very labour intensive.

Construction

The mixture is worked—traditionally by treading, giving rise to the term "cob" from an Old English root meaning a lump or rounded mass—and applied in courses called "lifts". Each lift is left to dry before the next is added, gradually building thick, monolithic, load-bearing walls with rounded, sculptural forms. Cob buildings are traditionally protected by lime render or limewash and wide roof overhangs, since prolonged saturation weakens the material.

History

Earth building is ancient worldwide, but cob in the English sense flourished in Devon and Cornwall from the medieval period into the nineteenth century; thousands of cob cottages survive in south-west England. Spectacular multi-storey mudbrick tower houses in Yemen, such as those of Shibam, are built from closely related materials. Cheap fired brick and cement displaced cob in the industrial era.

Revival

From the 1980s and 1990s, cob became a flagship material of the natural building movement, promoted in Oregon by Ianto Evans and Linda Smiley and through the Cob Cottage Company. Modern enthusiasts value its low embodied energy, local availability, and freely sculpted forms, though building-code approval remains a hurdle in many jurisdictions.
