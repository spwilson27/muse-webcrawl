Muhammad ibn Abd al-Karim al-Khattabi (Arabic: محمد بن عبد الكريم الخطابي; 1882 – 6 February 1963), known as Abd el-Krim, was a Moroccan political and military leader of the Rif region. He led a broad resistance movement against the Spanish and French colonial presence in northern Morocco, won the devastating victory over Spain at the Battle of Annual in 1921, and headed the short-lived Republic of the Rif (1923–1926), becoming an early icon of anti-colonial guerrilla warfare.

Early life

Abd el-Krim was born at Ajdir into the Aith Yusuf clan of the Ait Waryaghar tribe. His father was a qadi (judge). Educated at the Qarawiyyin in Fez, he worked as a teacher and journalist in Melilla and served the Spanish administration as a qadi before his imprisonment by the Spanish in 1915–16 soured his relations with them; after his release he returned to the Rif determined to resist Spanish penetration.

The Rif War

In 1921 his tribal forces annihilated a Spanish army advancing from Melilla at Annual, killing some 13,000 Spanish soldiers and capturing huge stocks of rifles and artillery. In 1923 he proclaimed the Republic of the Rif with its capital at Ajdir, fielding a small regular army and a network of tribal levies. In 1925 he attacked the French zone as well, driving toward Fez. Spain and France responded with a joint offensive of several hundred thousand men, an amphibious landing at Alhucemas Bay, aerial bombing (including Spanish use of chemical weapons), and in May 1926 Abd el-Krim surrendered to the French to spare his people further devastation.

Exile and legacy

Exiled to Réunion in the Indian Ocean, he was allowed to leave for France in 1947 but jumped ship at Port Said and took asylum in Cairo, where he championed North African independence until his death in 1963. His methods — mobile guerrilla war by a politically organised movement — were studied by later revolutionaries, and he remains a hero in Morocco, although his remains are still in Cairo, repatriation to the Rif never having been accomplished.
