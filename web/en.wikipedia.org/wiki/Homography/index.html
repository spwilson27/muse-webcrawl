Homography

In projective geometry, a homography is an isomorphism of projective spaces induced by an invertible linear map of the underlying vector spaces. In computer vision the term denotes the projective transformation mapping points on one plane to points on another, represented by a 3×3 matrix defined up to scale.

Properties

Homographies map lines to lines and preserve cross-ratios, but not distances or angles. A planar homography is determined by four point correspondences, no three collinear.

Applications

Estimating homographies is central to image stitching and panoramas, camera calibration, augmented reality, document rectification and sports field registration.

References: Hartley & Zisserman, Multiple View Geometry in Computer Vision.
