Allison V-1710

The Allison V-1710 aircraft engine was the only indigenous United States design for a liquid-cooled V-12 piston aero engine to see service during World War II. Developed by the Allison Engine Company, a subsidiary of General Motors, it powered the P-38 Lightning, P-39 Airacobra, P-40 Warhawk, and early P-51 Mustangs. Over 70,000 examples were built.

Design and development

Allison began the design in 1929 in response to U.S. Navy interest in a modern large-displacement engine. The V-1710 was a 60° V-12 with a single overhead camshaft per bank, four valves per cylinder, and a single-stage, single-speed supercharger in most versions. Unlike the contemporary British Rolls-Royce Merlin, most production V-1710s lacked a two-stage supercharger, limiting their high-altitude performance — a deficiency traced in part to prewar Army doctrine that expected turbosuperchargers to handle high-altitude work.

Service

Fitted with General Electric turbosuperchargers in the twin-engined P-38, the engine performed superbly at altitude. In single-stage form it excelled at low and medium altitudes in the P-39, P-40, and the A-36 and early P-51 Mustangs; the Mustang's transformation into a premier long-range escort came only after re-engining with the Merlin. After the war, V-1710s found long second lives in unlimited hydroplane racing and tractor pulling.
