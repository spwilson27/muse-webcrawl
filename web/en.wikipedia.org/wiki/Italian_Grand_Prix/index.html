Italian Grand Prix

The Italian Grand Prix is one of the longest-running events on the Formula One calendar, first held in 1921 and a part of the World Championship since its inaugural season in 1950. It has almost always been held at the Autodromo Nazionale Monza, near Milan.

History

The race was staged at several venues in the 1920s before Monza became its permanent home in 1950, with one exception at Imola in 1980. Monza's long straights make it the fastest circuit in Formula One, earning it the nickname "Temple of Speed".

Notable races

The 1971 Italian Grand Prix produced the fastest race in F1 history at the time and one of its closest finishes, Peter Gethin winning by 0.01 seconds. Ferrari's victories at Monza, such as Charles Leclerc's in 2019, are celebrated by the passionate tifosi.
