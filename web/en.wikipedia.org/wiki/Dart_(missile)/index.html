Dart (missile)

The SSM-A-23 Dart was an American anti-tank guided missile developed in the 1950s by the Aerophysics Development Corporation for the United States Army. It was one of the earliest attempts to field a man-portable/command-guided anti-tank weapon.

Design and development

The Dart was a wire-guided missile: the operator tracked the target through a sight and steered the missile by commands sent along two thin wires unspooling from the missile as it flew. It carried a shaped-charge (HEAT) warhead intended to defeat contemporary tank armour. The system was large and heavy, requiring a dedicated launcher vehicle or ground mount.

Service history

Development began in the early 1950s and the missile was initially promising, but the programme suffered from cost growth, complexity and reliability problems. The project was cancelled in 1958 before entering operational service. Lessons from Dart informed later American anti-tank missiles such as the ENTAC, the SS.11 (used as the AGM-22) and ultimately the successful TOW and Dragon systems.
