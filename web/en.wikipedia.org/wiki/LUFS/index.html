LUFS

LUFS stands for Loudness Units Full Scale, a unit of measurement for the perceived loudness of audio. LUFS is defined by the International Telecommunication Union in recommendation ITU-R BS.1770 and is identical in scale to LKFS (Loudness, K-weighted, relative to full scale); one LU corresponds to one decibel. The two terms are used interchangeably, with LUFS preferred by the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) and LKFS used in North American standards.

Measurement

LUFS measurements apply K-weighting, a frequency weighting that approximates human hearing sensitivity, together with gating defined in the standard. Measurements are reported as integrated loudness over a whole programme, as short-term and momentary loudness, and alongside the loudness range (LRA). The EBU R 128 standard normalizes broadcast content to −23 LUFS, while the ATSC A/85 practice in the United States targets −24 LKFS. Streaming platforms apply their own normalization targets, commonly around −14 LUFS for music services.
