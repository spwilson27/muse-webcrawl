Commercialization

Bringing a new product to market

Commercialization (or commercialisation) is the process of managing or running something principally for financial gain. In business and innovation contexts it refers specifically to the process of introducing a new product, service, idea, or production method into commerce — making it available on the market. The term often connotes the final stage of product development, following research, invention, prototyping, and testing, in which production is scaled up and marketing, sales, and distribution begin.

Process

Commercialisation of an innovation typically involves building a business case, securing intellectual property (patents, trademarks), raising finance, developing manufacturing or delivery capacity, launching marketing, and achieving market adoption. For research institutions, technology transfer offices manage the commercialisation of academic inventions, often by licensing or by forming spin-off companies. Many inventions fail at this stage — the gap between a proven prototype and a viable product is sometimes called the "valley of death".

Critical usage

The word is also used pejoratively, chiefly in the phrase "the commercialisation of…", to criticise the perceived corruption of cultural, educational, sporting, or public institutions by profit-seeking — for example the commercialisation of Christmas, of higher education, or of public broadcasting. Critics argue that market logic can crowd out other values, while defenders point to wider access and sustainability.
