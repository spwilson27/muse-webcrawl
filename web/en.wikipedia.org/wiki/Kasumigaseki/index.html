Kasumigaseki

Kasumigaseki (霞ヶ関) is a district in Chiyoda Ward, Tokyo, Japan, that houses the headquarters of most of Japan's cabinet ministries and central government agencies. By metonymy, "Kasumigaseki" commonly refers to the Japanese national bureaucracy itself, much as "Whitehall" denotes the British civil service, in contrast to "Nagatachō", the neighbouring district of the Diet and prime minister's residence, which denotes the political world.

History and buildings

Government offices began concentrating in the area in the Meiji era, on land formerly occupied by daimyo residences near Edo Castle. The district contains the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI), Ministry of Justice, the National Police Agency and other agencies, organised around a series of Central Government Buildings constructed from the 1930s onwards.

The Kasumigaseki Building, completed in 1968, was Japan's first true skyscraper at 36 storeys and 147 metres, made possible by changes to building codes after the development of earthquake-resistant design. Kasumigaseki station serves the Marunouchi, Hibiya and Chiyoda subway lines; in 1995 the station was one of the targets of the Aum Shinrikyo sarin gas attack on the Tokyo subway.
