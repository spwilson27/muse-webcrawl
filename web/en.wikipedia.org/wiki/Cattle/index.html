Cattle are large domesticated hoofed mammals (ungulates) widely kept as livestock for meat (beef and veal), milk, leather, and as draft animals. They were domesticated from the wild aurochs in two major events: taurine cattle in the Near East and zebu (indicine) cattle in the Indus valley, roughly 10,500 years ago.

Biology

Cattle are ruminants with a four-chambered stomach (rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum) that ferments cellulose via microbes, enabling them to digest grasses. Adults typically weigh 500–1,000 kg. Terms include bull (intact male), cow (female that has calved), heifer (young female), steer (castrated male), and ox (trained draft animal).

Husbandry and products

India has the world's largest cattle herd; Brazil, China, and the United States follow. Breeds number in the hundreds — from high-yield dairy Holsteins to beef Angus and Hereford, and heat-tolerant zebu types. Husbandry systems range from intensive feedlots to extensive pastoralism.

Impact and culture

Cattle are economically and culturally central — sacred in Hinduism, historically a measure of wealth in many societies — but also a major source of greenhouse-gas emissions (enteric methane), land use, and deforestation, prompting debate over sustainable production.
