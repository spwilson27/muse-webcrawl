Alexander Ernest "Lex" Greensill (born December 1976) is an Australian former financier, best known as the founder of Greensill Capital, a supply-chain finance company whose collapse in March 2021 became one of the largest financial scandals in recent British history and triggered the Greensill lobbying scandal.

Early life and career

Greensill was born in Bundaberg, Queensland, where his family grew sugar cane and melons. After work experience in law and finance, he moved to London, holding roles at Citigroup and Morgan Stanley in supply-chain finance, before founding Greensill Capital in 2011 to provide working-capital financing to businesses.

Greensill Capital

The firm specialised in supply-chain finance — paying suppliers early at a discount against invoices — and expanded rapidly, claiming to finance hundreds of billions of dollars for clients including steel magnate Sanjeev Gupta's GFG Alliance. SoftBank's Vision Fund invested about $1.5 billion, valuing the company at nearly $4 billion. Greensill served as an unpaid adviser to the UK government during David Cameron's premiership, and Cameron later became an adviser to the firm.

Collapse and aftermath

In March 2021, after Credit Suisse suspended investment funds holding Greensill loans and insurers declined to renew cover, Greensill Capital filed for administration. The collapse exposed heavy exposure to GFG Alliance, provoked parliamentary and regulatory inquiries in the UK and Australia, litigation by Credit Suisse to recover investor funds, and a political scandal over Cameron's lobbying of ministers on the firm's behalf. Greensill was appointed CBE in 2017 for services to business.
