Ship classification society

A ship classification society (or classification organisation) is a non-governmental body that establishes and maintains technical standards for the construction and operation of ships and offshore structures, and validates that construction and maintenance comply with those standards.

Functions

Classification societies develop rules covering hull structure, machinery, and safety systems; survey ships during construction and periodically in service; and issue classification certificates. A ship's class is relied upon by insurers, financiers, flag states and charterers as evidence of its condition and seaworthiness. Societies may also act as "recognised organisations" performing statutory surveys on behalf of flag states under conventions such as SOLAS and MARPOL.

Major societies

The largest societies — including Lloyd's Register, Bureau Veritas, DNV, the American Bureau of Shipping, ClassNK and RINA — are members of the International Association of Classification Societies (IACS), founded in 1968, which harmonises many rules and represents the industry at the International Maritime Organization. The oldest society, Lloyd's Register, traces its origins to a London coffee house register of ships in the 1760s.
