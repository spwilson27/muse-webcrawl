Rubis-class submarine

Rubis class Type: Nuclear attack submarine (SSN)
Operator: French Navy
Built: 1976–1993
In commission: 1983–present
Completed: 6
Displacement: 2,385 t surfaced; 2,607 t submerged
Length: 73.6 m

The Rubis class is a class of nuclear-powered attack submarines (SSN) operated by the French Navy. Named after precious stones, the six boats—Rubis, Saphir, Casabianca, Émeraude, Améthyste and Perle—were for decades the smallest nuclear attack submarines in service anywhere in the world. The first, Rubis, was commissioned in 1983.

Design

The class was designed in the 1970s as a compact, economical nuclear attack submarine sharing much of its machinery philosophy with France's ballistic missile submarines. A single pressurised-water reactor driving turbo-electric propulsion gives a submerged speed of about 25 knots. The boats carry a crew of around 70 and are armed with four 533 mm torpedo tubes capable of launching F17 and L5 torpedoes and Exocet SM39 anti-ship missiles, as well as mines.

The last two boats, Améthyste and Perle, were built to a lengthened, quieter AMÉTHYSTE standard with a revised bow shape, and the earlier units were refitted to similar form. All six are based at Toulon and are used for anti-submarine and anti-surface warfare, intelligence gathering and special forces support.

Service and replacement

Rubis-class submarines have deployed worldwide, including operations during the Gulf War, off Libya in 2011 and in support of counter-terrorism missions. In June 2020 the Perle was severely damaged by fire while in dry dock in Toulon; in a remarkable repair, the undamaged forward section of the decommissioned Saphir was grafted onto Perle's stern, and the boat returned to service in 2023. The class is being progressively replaced by the larger Suffren (Barracuda)-class submarines, with retirements proceeding through the 2020s and into the 2030s.
