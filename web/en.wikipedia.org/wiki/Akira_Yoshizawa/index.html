Akira Yoshizawa
Born14 March 1911, Kaminokawa, Tochigi, Japan
Died14 March 2005 (aged 94), Tokyo, Japan
Known forOrigami

Akira Yoshizawa (吉澤 章, 14 March 1911 – 14 March 2005) was a Japanese origamist, widely considered the grandmaster of origami and the founder of modern origami as a creative art form. He created more than 50,000 models, of which only a few hundred designs were published in his books.

Life and work

Yoshizawa learned origami as a child and returned to it while working in a factory in Tokyo, using models to teach geometry. His breakthrough came in the early 1950s, when his work appeared in magazines and he held his first exhibition. Together with Samuel Randlett, he developed the standard system of origami diagramming — the Yoshizawa–Randlett system of lines, arrows and symbols — still used internationally today. He pioneered techniques such as wet-folding, which allows rounded, sculptural forms.

Legacy

Yoshizawa served as a Japanese cultural ambassador, exhibiting worldwide, and founded the International Origami Centre in Tokyo in 1989. He died on his 94th birthday in 2005.
