George William "Bill" James (born October 5, 1949) is an American baseball writer, historian and statistician who pioneered the statistical approach to baseball known as sabermetrics.

Work

Beginning in 1977 James self-published the annual Bill James Baseball Abstract, arguing that conventional baseball statistics mismeasured players and popularising concepts such as runs created. His work underpinned the analytics movement popularised by Moneyball. From 2003 he worked as a senior advisor to the Boston Red Sox, during which the club won four World Series titles.

Other writing

James also wrote about true crime, including Popular Crime (2011), and with Rob Neyer, The Bill James Guide to Baseball Managers.
