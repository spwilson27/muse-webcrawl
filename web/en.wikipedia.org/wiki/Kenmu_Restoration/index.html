The Kenmu Restoration (Japanese: 建武の新政, Kenmu no shinsei) was a brief period of imperial rule in Japanese history between 1333 and 1336, when Emperor Go-Daigo overthrew the Kamakura shogunate and attempted to restore direct political power to the imperial court in Kyoto. It ended with the defection of his general Ashikaga Takauji, who founded the Ashikaga shogunate, and the splitting of the imperial court into rival Northern and Southern courts.

Background

Since 1185 real power in Japan had rested with the shogunate at Kamakura, while the emperors reigned largely as figureheads. The shogunate had been weakened by the cost of repelling the Mongol invasions of 1274 and 1281 and by disputes over rewards to its vassals. Go-Daigo, who ascended the throne in 1318, plotted to restore imperial authority; two early plots were betrayed and he was exiled to the Oki Islands in 1332, but he escaped in 1333.

Fall of the shogunate

In 1333 Go-Daigo's supporters, including Kusunoki Masashige, rose in rebellion. Crucially, the shogunate's commander Ashikaga Takauji defected to the imperial cause and captured Kyoto, while Nitta Yoshisada destroyed the Hōjō regents at Kamakura. The shogunate collapsed and Go-Daigo returned to Kyoto in triumph.

The Kenmu regime

Go-Daigo's new government, named after the Kenmu era (1334–1336), attempted to restore the centralised imperial system of earlier centuries. It re-established court offices, created new organs such as the Records of Miscellaneous Claims to adjudicate land disputes, and issued its own coinage. However, the regime alienated the warrior class: rewards of land went disproportionately to courtiers and temples, while the samurai who had won the war felt neglected, and the flood of land claims overwhelmed the courts.

Collapse

In 1335 Ashikaga Takauji, sent east to crush a Hōjō remnant rising, seized Kamakura and rebelled. Although loyalist forces under Nitta Yoshisada and Kusunoki Masashige briefly drove him from Kyoto, Takauji regrouped in Kyushu and returned, defeating the imperial army at the Battle of Minatogawa in 1336, where Kusunoki Masashige was killed. Takauji occupied Kyoto and installed a rival emperor; Go-Daigo fled to Yoshino, founding the Southern Court and beginning the Nanboku-chō (Northern and Southern Courts) period, which lasted until 1392.

Legacy

Though it lasted only three years, the Kenmu Restoration became a potent symbol of imperial loyalism. Its heroes, above all Kusunoki Masashige, were venerated during the Meiji Restoration of 1868, which consciously echoed Go-Daigo's project of restoring direct imperial rule.
