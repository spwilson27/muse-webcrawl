Queens' College is a constituent college of the University of Cambridge. Founded in 1448 by Margaret of Anjou, wife of Henry VI, and refounded in 1465 by Elizabeth Woodville, wife of Edward IV, it is unique among Cambridge colleges in having been founded by two queens — hence the plural possessive "Queens'", unlike the Oxford college of the same name (The Queen's College, founded by a single queen's patronage).

History and buildings

The college occupies a site on both banks of the River Cam, joined by the famous Mathematical Bridge (originally built in 1749). Its Old Court, dating from 1448–1451, is one of the oldest courts in Cambridge. Other notable buildings include the Walnut Tree Court, the Friars' Building, and the modern Cripps Building across the river. Erasmus was a resident scholar at Queens' in the early 16th century.

People and traditions

Alumni include Erasmus (associated), the naturalist Charles Darwin's brother Erasmus Darwin's circle, actor and writer Stephen Fry, comedian and presenter Sandi Toksvig, and economist and politician Abba Eban. The college is known for its May Week production venue, the Fitzpatrick Hall, and the student-run Bats theatre society.
