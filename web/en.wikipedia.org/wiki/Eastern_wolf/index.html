The eastern wolf (Canis lycaon), also known as the timber wolf, Algonquin wolf or eastern Canadian wolf, is a canine of debated taxonomy native to the Great Lakes region and southeastern Canada. Its core range centres on Algonquin Provincial Park in Ontario, with populations extending into Quebec and around the Great Lakes.

Taxonomy debate

The eastern wolf has variously been classified as a subspecies of the grey wolf (Canis lupus lycaon), as a distinct species (Canis lycaon), or as a hybrid population derived from grey wolves and coyotes. Genetic studies show substantial coyote ancestry in eastern wolf populations, particularly outside protected areas, and the question of its taxonomic status has been the subject of ongoing scientific dispute. In Canada, the Algonquin wolf is assessed as Threatened by COSEWIC and is protected in Ontario.

Description

The eastern wolf is intermediate in size between the grey wolf and the coyote: adults typically weigh 24 to 31 kg, with a grizzled grey-brown coat tinged with black and cinnamon. It has a narrower snout and skull proportions distinct from western grey wolves.

Ecology

Unlike western grey wolves, which specialise on large ungulates such as moose, eastern wolves prey heavily on white-tailed deer and beaver, taking moose less frequently, a difference attributed to their smaller body size and the prey available in the mixed forests of the Great Lakes–St. Lawrence region. Packs are generally smaller than those of grey wolves, averaging around three to five animals.
