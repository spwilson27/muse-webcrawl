Throne

Labelled placeholder: a ceremonial throne of state.

A throne is the seat of state of a potentate or dignitary, especially the chair occupied by a sovereign on state occasions; or the seat occupied by a pope or bishop on ceremonial occasions. "Throne" in an abstract sense can also refer to the monarchy or the Crown itself, an instance of metonymy, and is also used in many expressions describing the monarchy.

History

Ceremonial seats elevated above ordinary chairs are attested from the ancient Near East and Egypt, where thrones of pharaohs and gods embodied divine and royal authority. In classical antiquity and medieval Europe the throne, often placed on a dais under a canopy, became the focal point of court ritual, embodying the office rather than the person of the ruler.

Famous thrones

  • The Peacock Throne of the Mughal emperors, later taken to Persia.
  • The Dragon Throne of the emperors of China in the Forbidden City.
  • The Chrysanthemum Throne of Japan.
  • St Edward's Chair (the Coronation Chair) in Westminster Abbey, containing the Stone of Scone.
  • The papal Cathedra Petri in St. Peter's Basilica.

Religious use

In Christian usage a bishop's ceremonial seat is a cathedra, from which the word "cathedral" derives; enthronement remains the standard term for the formal installation of bishops and monarchs alike.
