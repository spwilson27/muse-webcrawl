Celtic Connections

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Celtic Connections is a Scottish music festival which is held every winter in Glasgow, Scotland. It is the largest annual winter music festival of its kind, and one of the largest Celtic music festivals in the world. The festival was first held in 1994 and is produced by Glasgow Life. It takes place over roughly 18 days in January and February, centred on the Glasgow Royal Concert Hall, with events in venues across the city.

History

The festival was founded in 1994, partly to fill a scheduling gap in the Glasgow Royal Concert Hall's calendar during the post-Christmas period. From an initial audience of around 27,000, it grew steadily; by the 2010s the festival was staging hundreds of events and attracting attendances in excess of 100,000, with thousands of artists participating each year.

Programming

Celtic Connections programmes concerts spanning traditional Scottish and Irish music, folk, roots, Americana, jazz, and world music, and is known for pairing artists in one-off collaborations. Regular strands include the opening concert, the Transatlantic Sessions (which bring together Scottish, Irish and American musicians under the musical direction of Jerry Douglas and Aly Bain), the Danny Kyle Open Stage for emerging performers, schools concerts, workshops, and a late-night festival club. The festival also presents the BBC Radio Scotland Young Traditional Musician award events and has hosted the Scottish Folk Band of the Year competition.

Impact

The festival is a major contributor to Glasgow's winter economy and cultural profile, and is frequently credited with raising the international profile of Scottish traditional music and with encouraging collaborations that continue beyond the festival itself.
