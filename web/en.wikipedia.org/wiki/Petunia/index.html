Petunia

Petunia is a genus of about 20 species of flowering plants in the nightshade family, Solanaceae, native to South America. Petunias are among the most popular garden bedding plants worldwide, grown for their large, trumpet-shaped flowers, which bloom from spring until frost in colours including white, pink, red, purple, blue, yellow, and patterned or bicoloured forms.

Cultivation

Nearly all garden petunias descend from hybrids of Petunia axillaris and Petunia integrifolia, crossed in the 19th century; modern cultivars are classified as Petunia × atkinsiana (also cited as P. × hybrida). Classes include grandifloras with very large blooms, multifloras with many smaller flowers, and trailing "Wave" and "Supertunia" types bred for hanging baskets and ground cover. Petunias want full sun and well-drained soil, tolerate light frost, and benefit from deadheading, though many modern varieties are self-cleaning.

Science

Petunia is also a model organism in plant genetics and biochemistry, notably for the study of flower pigmentation and transposable elements. An unintended experiment with petunias in 1990 — attempts to intensify flower colour by adding a pigment gene instead produced white flowers — was a key early demonstration of co-suppression, a form of RNA interference.
