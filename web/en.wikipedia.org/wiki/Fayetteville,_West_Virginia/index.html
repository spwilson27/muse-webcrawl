Fayetteville is a town in and the county seat of Fayette County, West Virginia, United States. The population was about 2,900 at the 2020 census. The town sits on the rim of the New River Gorge, near the New River Gorge Bridge, one of the longest steel arch bridges in the world, and it serves as a gateway to the New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, designated in 2020.

History

Fayetteville was founded in 1837 and was originally known as Vandalia. It was renamed for the Marquis de Lafayette. During the Civil War the town changed hands several times, and the area later grew with the coal and timber industries served by the Chesapeake and Ohio Railway.

Recreation

The town is a centre for whitewater rafting on the New River and Gauley River, rock climbing in the gorge, and the annual Bridge Day festival, when BASE jumping from the New River Gorge Bridge is permitted.
