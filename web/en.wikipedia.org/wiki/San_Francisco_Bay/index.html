San Francisco Bay

San Francisco Bay
Type: Estuary
Location: California, United States
Connection: Golden Gate strait to the Pacific Ocean
Principal rivers: Sacramento, San Joaquin

San Francisco Bay is a large tidal estuary in the U.S. state of California, and gives its name to the San Francisco Bay Area. It is dominated by the cities of San Francisco, San Jose, and Oakland. It drains water from approximately 40 percent of California, as the state's two largest watersheds, the Sacramento River and the San Joaquin River, meet in the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta and flow through Suisun Bay and the Carquinez Strait into San Pablo Bay and the main bay, exiting to the Pacific Ocean through the Golden Gate strait.

Geography

The drowned river valley that forms the bay was created when sea levels rose at the end of the last ice age, flooding a valley carved by the combined Sacramento and San Joaquin rivers. The bay proper covers roughly 400 to 1,600 square miles depending on which sub-bays and marshes are counted, and is generally shallow — much of it only 10 to 15 feet deep — though shipping channels are dredged. Islands include Alcatraz, Angel Island, Treasure Island, and Alameda.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Ohlone and Miwok, lived around the bay for thousands of years. Europeans famously sailed past its entrance for two centuries without noticing it: the first recorded European discovery was by the expedition of Gaspar de Portolà in 1769, viewing it from land; Juan Manuel de Ayala's ship San Carlos first entered by sea in 1775. The Gold Rush after 1848 transformed San Francisco into the West Coast's great port, and the bay became a hub of shipping, shipbuilding, and industry.

Ecology and development

The bay is the largest estuary on the Pacific coast of the Americas and a critical stop on the Pacific Flyway for migratory birds; much of its fringe is protected as the Don Edwards San Francisco Bay National Wildlife Refuge and state parks. In the 19th and 20th centuries roughly a third of the bay was filled for development and salt ponds, prompting the creation of the San Francisco Bay Conservation and Development Commission in 1965 (spurred by the Save the Bay movement) and ongoing wetland-restoration projects. The bay is crossed by seven major toll bridges, including the Golden Gate Bridge and the San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge.
