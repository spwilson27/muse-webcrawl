Zoot Suit Riots

The Zoot Suit Riots were a series of racial attacks in June 1943 in Los Angeles, California, United States, in which American servicemen, police officers, and civilians attacked Mexican American youths who wore zoot suits — loose, flamboyant suits popular among minority youth.

Background

Tensions had grown following the Sleepy Lagoon murder trial of 1942, in which Mexican American youths were convicted in proceedings widely seen as biased. The press portrayed zoot suiters as delinquents and gang members, and wartime fabric rationing made the suits' excess cloth seem unpatriotic to many.

The riots

Between 3 and 8 June 1943, mobs of sailors and soldiers roamed Los Angeles, beating zoot suiters, stripping them of their suits, and burning the clothes in the street. Police largely arrested the victims rather than the attackers, and local newspapers praised the servicemen. Similar attacks occurred in other cities.

Aftermath

The riots ended after military authorities declared Los Angeles off-limits to servicemen. A citizens' committee concluded the attacks were motivated by racism, while city officials blamed the victims. The episode became a landmark in Mexican American civil rights history.
