The Grenadines are a chain of small islands in the Caribbean Sea stretching roughly 100 kilometres between the islands of Saint Vincent in the north and Grenada in the south. They form part of the Windward Islands of the Lesser Antilles.

Geography

The chain comprises more than 600 islands, islets and cays, most of them very small and many uninhabited. Politically the chain is divided between two nations: the northern two-thirds belong to Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, while the southern portion, including Carriacou and Petite Martinique, belongs to Grenada.

The largest and best-known islands include Bequia, Mustique, Canouan, Mayreau, Union Island, Palm Island, Petit St Vincent and Carriacou. The Tobago Cays, a group of small uninhabited islands protected by a horseshoe-shaped reef, form the centrepiece of a marine park and are a celebrated sailing and snorkelling destination.

History and economy

The islands were originally inhabited by Arawak and later Carib peoples. Control passed between France and Britain during the colonial era before the islands were divided at independence. With few natural resources besides their scenery and reefs, the modern economy depends heavily on tourism, yachting, fishing and small-scale agriculture. The Grenadines are among the most popular sailing grounds in the Caribbean.
