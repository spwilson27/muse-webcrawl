Nazgûl

The Nazgûl (from Black Speech nazg, "ring", and gûl, "wraith, spirit"), also called Ringwraiths, the Nine Riders, or simply the Nine, are fictional characters in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium, appearing principally in The Lord of the Rings. They were once great kings and sorcerers of Men who were corrupted by the nine Rings of Power given to them by the Dark Lord Sauron.

History in the legendarium

During the Second Age, Sauron distributed nine rings to powerful Men — kings, warriors, and sorcerers, three of them great lords of Númenor. The rings prolonged their lives but gradually enslaved them to Sauron's will through the One Ring, until they faded into wraiths, invisible to normal sight and bound utterly to their master. The Nazgûl first appeared around S.A. 2251 and became Sauron's most feared servants. Their chief, the Lord of the Nazgûl, was the Witch-king of Angmar, who destroyed the North-kingdom of Arnor and later commanded Sauron's armies in the War of the Ring.

In The Lord of the Rings

The Nazgûl hunt for the One Ring after Gollum reveals the name "Baggins" and "Shire"; their leader stabs Frodo Baggins with a Morgul-knife on Weathertop. Their horses are drowned at the Ford of Bruinen, after which they return mounted on winged Fell beasts. The Witch-king is slain at the Battle of the Pelennor Fields by Éowyn and Merry, fulfilling Glorfindel's prophecy that he would not fall "by the hand of man". The remaining eight perish when the One Ring is destroyed.

Characteristics

The Nazgûl are invisible except for the black robes Sauron gives them to shape their form; they perceive the wraith-world rather than the physical world and can sense the Ring. Their chief weapons are terror — the Black Breath and their dreadful cries — along with blades and sorcery. They fear fire and, as undead creatures of darkness, are weakened by daylight and water.
