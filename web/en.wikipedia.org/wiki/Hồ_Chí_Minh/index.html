Hồ Chí Minh (19 May 1890 – 2 September 1969), born Nguyễn Sinh Cung, was a Vietnamese revolutionary and statesman. He founded the Việt Minh independence movement, proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in 1945, and served as its president until his death during the Vietnam War. The city of Saigon was renamed Ho Chi Minh City in his honour in 1976.

Early life and travels

Ho was born in Kim Liên village, Nghệ An Province, the son of a Confucian scholar and minor mandarin. In 1911 he left Vietnam, working as a cook and sailor and travelling to France, the United States, and Britain. In Paris after World War I, under the name Nguyễn Ái Quốc ("Nguyễn the Patriot"), he co-founded the French Communist Party in 1920 after being drawn to Lenin's theses on national and colonial questions. He had earlier petitioned the Versailles Peace Conference for Vietnamese self-determination, without success.

Revolutionary organisation

Ho spent time in Moscow and then in Guangzhou, China, where he trained Vietnamese exiles and founded the Revolutionary Youth League. In 1930 he unified Vietnam's communist factions into the Indochinese Communist Party in Hong Kong. Returning to Vietnam in 1941 after thirty years abroad, he founded the Việt Minh front and established a base at Pác Bó, adopting the name Hồ Chí Minh ("Ho, the Bringer of Light").

August Revolution and the First Indochina War

Taking advantage of Japan's surrender in August 1945, the Việt Minh seized power in the August Revolution. On 2 September 1945, Ho read the Vietnamese Declaration of Independence in Ba Đình Square, Hanoi. France attempted to restore colonial rule, and full-scale war broke out in December 1946. The Việt Minh victory at Điện Biên Phủ in 1954 ended the First Indochina War; the Geneva Accords divided Vietnam at the 17th parallel, with Ho's government in the North.

North Vietnam and the Vietnam War

As president of North Vietnam, Ho presided over land reform and the building of a socialist state, while reunification became the central goal. As conflict with South Vietnam and the United States escalated in the 1960s, real power increasingly rested with party first secretary Lê Duẩn, but Ho remained the movement's revered symbol, known affectionately as "Uncle Ho" (Bác Hồ). He died in Hanoi on 2 September 1969, six years before the war ended with the fall of Saigon in 1975.

Legacy

Ho's embalmed body lies in a mausoleum in Hanoi. His thought, codified as "Ho Chi Minh Thought," is an official ideology of the Communist Party of Vietnam, and his portrait appears on Vietnamese currency and in classrooms and public offices throughout the country.
