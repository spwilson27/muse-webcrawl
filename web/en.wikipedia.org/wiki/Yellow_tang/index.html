Yellow tang

The yellow tang (Zebrasoma flavescens) is a reef fish of the surgeonfish family Acanthuridae, native to the Pacific Ocean, particularly around Hawaii and Japan. It is bright lemon-yellow, laterally compressed and oval in shape, reaching about 20 cm in length.

Biology

Yellow tangs are herbivores grazing on benthic algae, and juveniles are solitary while adults may form loose groups. Like other surgeonfish, they carry a sharp, scalpel-like spine on each side of the caudal peduncle.

Aquarium trade

The species is among the most popular marine aquarium fish, historically collected chiefly in Hawaii; restrictions on the Hawaii aquarium fishery have reduced that trade. Yellow tangs were first captive-bred successfully in 2015 by the Oceanic Institute in Hawaii.
