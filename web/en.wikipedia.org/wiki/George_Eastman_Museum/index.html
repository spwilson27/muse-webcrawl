TypePhotography and film museum

The George Eastman Museum, the world's oldest museum dedicated to photography and one of the world's oldest film archives, opened to the public in 1949 in Rochester, New York. It is located on the estate of George Eastman, founder of the Eastman Kodak Company, whose Colonial Revival mansion and gardens form the centrepiece of the site.

Collections

The museum's photography collection includes more than 400,000 photographs and negatives dating from the invention of photography to the present day, by some 14,000 photographers, together with photographic equipment including the first Kodak camera. Its moving image collection comprises more than 28,000 film titles, together with millions of film stills, posters and papers relating to cinema. The museum also holds the Technicolor corporate archive and major collections of literature on photography and film.

The mansion and gardens

Eastman's house, built between 1902 and 1905, is a National Historic Landmark. Its restored interiors, pipe organ and landscaped gardens are open to visitors. The museum also operates the Dryden Theatre, a 500-seat cinema that screens classic and archival films.

Preservation and education

The museum is a leading centre for film preservation and runs the L. Jeffrey Selznick School of Film Preservation, the first school of its kind. It presents the annual George Eastman Award for distinguished contribution to the art of film.
