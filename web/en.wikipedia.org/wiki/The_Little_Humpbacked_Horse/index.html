The Little Humpbacked Horse

The Little Humpbacked Horse (Russian: Конёк-Горбунок, Konyok-Gorbunok) is a Russian literary fairy tale in verse by Pyotr Yershov, first published in 1834. Written when Yershov was only nineteen, it became a beloved classic of Russian children's literature.

Story

The tale follows Ivan, the youngest and seemingly foolish son of a peasant, who captures a magical mare and receives from her two splendid horses and a small humpbacked horse. With the humpbacked horse's help, Ivan enters the Tsar's service and accomplishes a series of impossible tasks: catching the Firebird, bringing the beautiful Tsar-Maiden from across the ocean, and retrieving a ring from the sea floor. Ordered to bathe in boiling milk, Ivan survives through the horse's magic and emerges handsome, while the Tsar, attempting the same feat, dies; Ivan marries the Tsar-Maiden and becomes Tsar.

Adaptations

The tale has inspired a ballet by Cesare Pugni (1864) and Rodion Shchedrin (1955), animated films (notably the 1947 Soviet film by Ivan Ivanov-Vano), and numerous illustrated editions.
