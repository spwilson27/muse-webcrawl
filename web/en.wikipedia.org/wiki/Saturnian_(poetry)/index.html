Saturnian (poetry)

The Saturnian metre is the earliest known form of Latin verse, used in early Roman poetry and inscriptions before the adoption of Greek metres such as the hexameter. Its structure remains debated: it may be accentual, syllabic, or quantitative, and is traditionally divided into two cola.

Saturnian
Language: Old Latin
Used by: Livius Andronicus, Naevius
Superseded by: Greek-derived metres

Examples

Livius Andronicus's translation of the Odyssey (Odusia) and Naevius's epic on the First Punic War (Bellum Poenicum) were written in Saturnians, as were early epitaphs such as those of the Scipios — including inscriptions associated with the family of Scipio Aemilianus's ancestors.

Debate

Because only fragments survive and Latin stress and quantity interact, scholars disagree on the underlying principle; Horace dismissed the metre as crude ("horridus Saturnius").
