Sukiyaki (すき焼き) is a Japanese dish prepared and served in the nabemono (hot pot) style. It consists of thinly sliced beef and vegetables slowly cooked or simmered at the table in a shallow iron pot in a mixture of soy sauce, sugar, and mirin. The cooked ingredients are traditionally dipped in a small bowl of beaten raw egg before being eaten.

History

Sukiyaki became popular in the Meiji era (1868–1912), as beef eating — long discouraged in Japan — was promoted during the country's modernisation. One theory holds that the name derives from suki, a spade, on which farmers grilled food; another links it to the verb suku, "to slice thinly".

Regional styles

In the Kantō style, a pre-mixed simmering sauce (warishita) is used; in the Kansai style, the beef is grilled first with fat and sugar, and soy sauce and sake are added directly to the pot. Common additions include tofu, negi (long onions), shungiku (chrysanthemum greens), shiitake mushrooms, and shirataki noodles.
