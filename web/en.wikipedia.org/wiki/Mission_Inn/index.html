Mission Inn

The Mission Inn, now known as the Mission Inn Hotel & Spa, is a historic landmark hotel in downtown Riverside, California. It occupies an entire city block and is renowned for its eclectic Mission Revival architecture, assembled over decades into a labyrinthine complex of arcades, towers, domes, flying buttresses, courtyards, and a rotunda wing.

History

The inn traces its origins to a boarding house purchased by Christopher Columbus Miller in the 1870s. His son, Frank Augustus Miller, opened the first wing of the Mission Inn in 1903, designed by architect G. Stanley Wilson, and continued expanding it for decades. Miller filled the hotel with art and artefacts collected from around the world, including bells, crosses, stained glass, and a catacomb-like network of tunnels and chapels.

Notable guests and features

The inn has hosted numerous United States presidents and celebrities. Richard and Pat Nixon married there in 1940, and Ronald and Nancy Reagan honeymooned there in 1952. The hotel includes the St. Francis Chapel, with a gold-leaf altar and Louis Comfort Tiffany stained-glass windows, and the Rotunda Wing, completed in 1912. The inn is also famous for its annual Festival of Lights during the holiday season, one of the largest public light displays in the United States.

Preservation

After decades of decline and closure in the 1980s, the inn was restored and reopened in 1992 under the ownership of Duane Roberts. It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and designated a National Historic Landmark.
