Brown pelican

Brown pelican
Scientific name: Pelecanus occidentalis
Family: Pelecanidae
Range: coasts of the Americas

The brown pelican (Pelecanus occidentalis) is a North and South American species of pelican known for plunge-diving into the sea to catch fish. It is one of the best-known birds of the Gulf Coast and the Caribbean, and the state bird of Louisiana.

Description and behaviour

Adults have a white head and neck (with a yellow wash in breeding season), a dark brown body, and a very long bill with an expandable throat pouch. Unlike most pelicans, which scoop fish at the surface, brown pelicans spot prey from the air, fold their wings, and dive headlong into the water, filling the pouch with fish and water.

Conservation

Populations crashed in the mid-twentieth century because the pesticide DDT caused eggshell thinning. The species was listed as endangered in the United States; after DDT was banned, numbers recovered, and the brown pelican was removed from the endangered species list in 2009—a notable conservation success. It remains vulnerable to oil spills and disturbance at nesting colonies.
