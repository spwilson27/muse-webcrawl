Maida Vale is an affluent residential district in West London, England, north of Paddington and south of Kilburn. It is part of the City of Westminster and is known for its wide tree-lined avenues, large Victorian and Edwardian mansion blocks, and the canalside area known as Little Venice.

History

The name derives from the Battle of Maida in Calabria, Italy, in 1806, in which a British force under Sir John Stuart defeated the French. A public house called The Hero of Maida stood on the Edgware Road near the junction with the present Maida Avenue, and the district took its name from the pub sign. Development began in the early 19th century, and by the latter half of the century the area had become a prosperous middle-class suburb.

Little Venice

At the southern end of Maida Vale lies Little Venice, where the Regent's Canal meets the Paddington Arm of the Grand Union Canal. The area is characterised by houseboats, waterside cafés, and white stucco terraces, and is a popular location for canal boat trips.

BBC Maida Vale Studios

The district is home to the BBC Maida Vale Studios on Delaware Road, a complex of seven radio studios converted from a Victorian roller-skating rink in the 1930s. The studios have hosted countless radio sessions and concerts, including many of the famous Peel Sessions recorded for BBC Radio 1.

Transport

Maida Vale is served by Maida Vale and Warwick Avenue stations on the Bakerloo line of the London Underground. Numerous bus routes run along the Edgware Road and Maida Vale road itself.

Notable residents

Past and present residents have included the pioneering computer scientist Alan Turing, who was born in Warrington Crescent in 1912, the actress Joan Collins, and the musicians Björk and Noel Gallagher.
