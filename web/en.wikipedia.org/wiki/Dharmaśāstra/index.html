Dharmaśāstra

Dharmaśāstra (Sanskrit: धर्मशास्त्र) is a genre of Sanskrit theological and legal texts in ancient India, and one of the bodies of literature (smriti) dealing with dharma — religious duty, law, conduct and morality. The Dharmaśāstras systematise rules for the four varnas and the stages of life (ashramas), kingship, civil and criminal law, penance and purification.

Principal texts

  • The early prose Dharmasutras of Apastamba, Gautama, Baudhayana and Vasishtha (c. 600–200 BCE).
  • The Manusmriti (Laws of Manu, c. 200 BCE–200 CE), the most famous and influential.
  • The Yajnavalkya Smriti, noted for its more developed legal procedure, and later the Narada and Brihaspati smritis, dealing mainly with law.
  • Medieval commentaries and digests (nibandha), such as Vijnaneshvara's Mitakshara, which became the basis of one school of Hindu law.

Contents

Classical treatments divide dharma into the conduct of the castes and life-stages (varnashrama-dharma), the law of the king (rajadharma) including courts and punishments, and expiation (prayashcitta) for transgressions.

Influence

Under British rule, translations of the Manusmriti and digests were used to codify "Hindu law" in Anglo-Indian courts. Modern India's personal law retains a complex relationship with the tradition, and scholars debate how far the texts described actual practice versus Brahmanical ideals.
