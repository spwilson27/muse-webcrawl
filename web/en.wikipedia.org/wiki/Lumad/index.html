Lumad

The Lumad are the Indigenous peoples of the southern Philippine island of Mindanao. "Lumad" is a Cebuano word meaning "native" or "indigenous", adopted collectively in 1986 by delegates of fifteen ethnolinguistic groups, who formed the Lumad Mindanaw Peoples Federation to assert their rights to self-determination and ancestral land; the count of groups has since grown to eighteen.

The peoples

The Lumad groups include the Manobo, Bagobo, B'laan, T'boli, Tiruray (Teduray), Subanen, Mandaya, Mansaka, Higaonon, Banwaon, Bukidnon, Dibabawon, Mamanwa, Talaandig, Tagakaolo and Ubo, among others. Unlike the Moro peoples of Mindanao, the Lumad are neither Muslim nor (historically) Christian, though many have since converted to Christianity; they are distinct from the Moro and from migrant settler populations under the Philippines' "tri-people" framing of Mindanao society.

Issues

Lumad communities have long struggled to retain their ancestral domains against logging, mining, plantations and militarisation. Disputes intensified during counter-insurgency campaigns, when Lumad schools and villages were accused of links to communist rebels and leaders were killed or displaced — controversies that drew national and international attention during the martial-law period in Mindanao (2017–2019) and after.

Culture

Each group maintains its own language, epics, music and ritual life, with shared features such as the authority of the datu, the role of the babaylan (ritual specialist), and swidden agriculture in forest territories.
