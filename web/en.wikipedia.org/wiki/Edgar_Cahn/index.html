Edgar Cahn

BornEdgar S. Cahn, March 23, 1935, New York City, U.S.
DiedJanuary 23, 2022 (aged 86), Bethesda, Maryland, U.S.
OccupationLawyer, social innovator
Known forCreator of Time Banking and Time Dollars; co-founder of the National Legal Services Program

Edgar S. Cahn (March 23, 1935 – January 23, 2022) was an American law professor and social innovator best known as the creator of Time Banking, a system in which services are exchanged using time credits rather than money. He was also a key figure in the American legal services movement.

Career

A graduate of Swarthmore College and Yale Law School, Cahn worked in the Kennedy and Johnson administrations. With his wife Jean Camper Cahn, he authored the influential 1964 Yale Law Journal article "The War on Poverty: A Civilian Perspective," which helped lay the groundwork for the federal Legal Services Program, and the couple co-founded the Antioch School of Law in Washington, D.C., notable for its clinical legal education.

Time Banking

In the 1980s Cahn developed the concept of Time Dollars: an hour of service given earns an hour of service in return, regardless of the market value of the work. The idea aimed to value unpaid community work — caring for children and the elderly, mentoring, neighbourhood help — and to build social capital in low-income communities. Time banks spread internationally, and Cahn promoted the model through the organisation TimeBanks USA and books including No More Throw-Away People (2000).

Later life

Cahn taught at the University of the District of Columbia David A. Clarke School of Law. He died in 2022.
