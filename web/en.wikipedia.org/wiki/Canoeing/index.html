Canoeing

Canoeing is an activity which involves paddling a canoe with a single-bladed paddle. It is practised for recreation, transport and sport, on lakes, rivers and the sea. In some parts of the world the term "canoeing" is used broadly to include kayaking, in which a double-bladed paddle is used.

History

Canoes are among the oldest forms of watercraft; dugout canoes have been used for thousands of years by peoples around the world, from the birchbark canoes of North America's Indigenous peoples to the voyaging canoes of the Pacific. Modern recreational canoeing took shape in the 19th century, popularised by writers such as John MacGregor, whose Rob Roy canoe journeys helped found organised paddling in Britain.

Competitive canoeing

Canoeing is an Olympic sport governed by the International Canoe Federation. It comprises two main disciplines: canoe sprint, flatwater races over distances up to 1,000 metres, contested at the Olympics since 1936; and canoe slalom, in which competitors navigate a whitewater course of gates. Other disciplines include marathon, wildwater, freestyle, canoe polo and dragon boat racing.

Recreational canoeing

Recreational canoeing ranges from short outings on calm water to multi-week wilderness expeditions, often combined with camping and portaging between lakes. Canoe tripping is especially associated with Canada, where the canoe is a national symbol.
