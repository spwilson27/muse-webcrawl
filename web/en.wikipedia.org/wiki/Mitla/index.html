Mitla

Mitla is the second-most important archaeological site in the Mexican state of Oaxaca. The Zapotec name is Lyobaa, "place of rest"; the Nahuatl name Mictlán, "place of the dead", gave Mitla its name. The site was a major religious centre, associated with the Zapotec god of death.

Description

Mitla is famous for its intricate geometric stone mosaics (grecas), made of precisely cut stones fitted without mortar, decorating tombs, panels, and friezes — unique in Mesoamerica. The main structures include the Group of the Columns, with the Hall of the Columns and subterranean tombs, and the Church Group, over which the colonial church of San Pablo was built.

History

Occupied from about 900 BC, Mitla rose as nearby Monte Albán declined, flourishing until the Spanish conquest.
