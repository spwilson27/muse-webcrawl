NAAFI

The Navy, Army and Air Force Institutes (NAAFI, pronounced "naffy") is an organisation created by the British government in 1920 to run recreational establishments for the British Armed Forces and to supply goods and services to service personnel and their families. It operates shops, canteens, bars, clubs and cafés on military bases in the United Kingdom and overseas.

History

NAAFI was established in the aftermath of the First World War, when the government decided that the patchwork of canteen arrangements for soldiers should be unified and run on proper lines. During the Second World War it expanded enormously, following British forces to every theatre and employing tens of thousands of staff; its mobile canteens and Expeditionary Force Institutes served troops near the front lines. The comedian Eric Sykes and many other entertainers performed through ENSA in NAAFI venues.

Role and operations

NAAFI is a not-for-profit organisation: surpluses are returned to the services for welfare and recreational purposes. It has operated in Germany, Cyprus, the Falkland Islands, Afghanistan, Iraq and aboard Royal Navy ships, among many other locations. In recent decades its monopoly has diminished, and some contracts overseas have passed to other providers, but it remains a familiar institution of British military life. Its name is sometimes humorously said to stand for "No Ambitions And Fuck-all Interest" among servicemen.
