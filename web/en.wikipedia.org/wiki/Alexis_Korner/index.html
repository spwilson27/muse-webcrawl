Alexis Andrew Nicholas Koerner (19 April 1928 – 1 January 1984), known professionally as Alexis Korner, was a British blues musician and broadcaster, widely regarded as the founding father of British rhythm and blues. His band Blues Incorporated provided a training ground for many of the musicians who went on to define British rock in the 1960s.

Early life

Korner was born in Paris to an Austrian Jewish father and a Greek mother, and grew up in London from 1940. Enthused by American blues records, he began playing guitar and piano and performed in skiffle and jazz groups in the 1950s, notably with Cyril Davies.

Blues Incorporated and the British blues boom

In 1961 Korner and harmonica player Cyril Davies founded Blues Incorporated, which played at the Ealing Club and released the landmark album R&B from the Marquee (1962). Musicians who passed through the band included Charlie Watts, Jack Bruce, Ginger Baker, Long John Baldry and Graham Bond, while Mick Jagger and others sat in — a circle from which the Rolling Stones, the Animals and much of the British blues boom emerged.

Later career

After Davies's death in 1964, Korner continued with various bands, scored a hit with the big band CCS ("Whole Lotta Love", 1970), and worked with artists such as Peter Thorup and the supergroup Snape. He was also an influential BBC radio broadcaster championing blues and world music. He died of lung cancer in 1984.
