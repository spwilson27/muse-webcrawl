Hevsel Gardens

The Hevsel Gardens (Turkish: Hevsel Bahçeleri) are a fertile stretch of land, about 700 hectares in extent, between the fortress of Diyarbakır and the Tigris River in southeastern Turkey. Cultivated for millennia, the gardens have supplied food to the city from antiquity through the present day and form part of the landscape recognized by UNESCO.

History

The gardens lie in the floodplain below the basalt walls of Diyarbakır, watered by the Tigris and by springs seeping from the plateau. References to intensive cultivation in this valley date back to Assyrian times, and the gardens provided the besieged city with sustenance through centuries of sieges and wars. Traditional plots are divided by poplar windbreaks and irrigation channels, and are farmed for vegetables, fruit, and mulberry trees.

UNESCO status

In 2015 the gardens were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List together with the Diyarbakır Fortress under the listing "Diyarbakır Fortress and Hevsel Gardens Cultural Landscape". The inscription recognizes the unbroken relationship between the fortified city and its agricultural hinterland.

Ecology

The gardens and the adjacent Tigris valley form an important green corridor and bird habitat, supporting numerous species including migratory birds, and the area is associated with endemic wildlife of the upper Tigris basin. In recent years the gardens have faced pressure from urban development projects and dam-related changes to the river regime, prompting conservation campaigns by local groups.
