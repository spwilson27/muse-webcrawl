Entr'acte

Literal meaning"Between the acts" (French)
ContextTheatre, opera, ballet

An entr'acte (French for "between the acts") is a piece of music performed between the acts of a theatre production, or more generally the interval itself during which such music may be played. In earlier theatrical practice, entr'actes could include songs, dances or short instrumental pieces designed to entertain the audience while scenery was changed.

In opera and theatre

Composers have written notable entr'actes for operas and plays; examples include the entr'acte music in Bizet's Carmen and the incidental pieces in productions of Shakespeare. In the modern theatre the term is largely synonymous with the intermission, during which audiences leave their seats.

Entr'acte (film)

Entr'acte is also the title of a 1924 French short film directed by René Clair, with a score by Erik Satie, shown during the interval of the ballet Relâche. It is a landmark of Dada and surrealist cinema.
