WorldCom was an American telecommunications company that, at its peak around 1999, was the second-largest long-distance carrier in the United States. Its collapse in 2002, following the disclosure of a massive accounting fraud, produced what was then the largest bankruptcy in US history and became a defining scandal of the early-2000s corporate crime wave.

History

The company began in 1983 as Long Distance Discount Services (LDDS) in Mississippi, reselling long-distance capacity after the breakup of AT&T. Under CEO Bernie Ebbers it grew through more than 60 acquisitions, including MFS Communications and internet backbone operator UUNET (1996), and in 1998 it completed a $37 billion merger with MCI Communications, briefly making it a rival to AT&T. A planned merger with Sprint was blocked by regulators in 2000.

Accounting scandal

Under pressure from falling telecom prices and the company's deteriorating finances, executives capitalised ordinary operating expenses — chiefly "line costs" paid to other carriers — as assets, inflating profits. In June 2002, internal auditors led by Cynthia Cooper uncovered $3.8 billion of improper entries; the total was eventually estimated at about $11 billion. WorldCom filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on 21 July 2002, listing $107 billion in assets.

Aftermath

Ebbers was convicted of fraud and conspiracy in 2005 and sentenced to 25 years in prison; CFO Scott Sullivan pleaded guilty and served five years. The scandal contributed to passage of the Sarbanes–Oxley Act of 2002. The company emerged from bankruptcy in 2004 renamed MCI, Inc., and was acquired by Verizon Communications in 2006.
