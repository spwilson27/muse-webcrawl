The Arizona Cardinals are a professional American football team based in the Phoenix metropolitan area. The Cardinals compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the National Football Conference (NFC) West division. The team plays its home games at State Farm Stadium in Glendale, Arizona.

Founded in Racine, Wisconsin, in 1898 as the Morgan Athletic Club, the team joined the NFL as a charter member on September 17, 1920, as the Racine Cardinals. The franchise is the oldest continuously run professional football team in the United States. It moved to Chicago in 1922 (becoming the Chicago Cardinals), then to St. Louis in 1960, and to Arizona in 1988, playing first as the Phoenix Cardinals before adopting the Arizona Cardinals name in 1994.

History

The Cardinals won NFL championships in 1925 and 1947, the latter as the Chicago Cardinals. The club endured the longest active championship drought in North American professional sports for many years. In the 2008 season, led by quarterback Kurt Warner and wide receiver Larry Fitzgerald, the team reached Super Bowl XLIII, losing narrowly to the Pittsburgh Steelers, 27–23.

Franchise records and players

Notable players in franchise history include Larry Fitzgerald, Kurt Warner, Aeneas Williams, Dan Dierdorf, Roger Wehrli, Charley Trippi, and Ollie Matson. The team retired several numbers, including Pat Tillman's No. 40.
