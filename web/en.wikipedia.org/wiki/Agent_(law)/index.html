Agent (law)

In law, an agent is a person who has the legal authority to act on behalf of another person or entity, called the principal. The area of law governing this relationship is called agency law. An agent who acts within the scope of authority conferred by the principal binds the principal in dealings with third parties.

Creation of agency

An agency relationship may arise by express agreement, by implication from conduct, by ratification — where a principal later adopts an act done on their behalf — or by operation of law, for example in cases of necessity. An agent's authority may be actual (express or implied from the principal's words and conduct) or apparent (ostensible), where a third party reasonably relies on the principal's manifestations that the agent is authorised.

Duties

An agent owes the principal fiduciary duties of loyalty, obedience to lawful instructions, reasonable care and skill, and a duty to account for money and property received. In return, the principal generally owes the agent remuneration (if agreed), reimbursement of expenses, and indemnification for liabilities properly incurred. A principal may be liable for the agent's contracts and, in many circumstances, for the agent's torts committed within the scope of the agency or employment (respondeat superior).
