The Norwegian krone (Norwegian: krone, plural kroner; code NOK) is the currency of Norway and its overseas territories. The name means "crown", as in the Danish krone and Swedish krona. One krone is divided into 100 øre, though øre coins ceased to be legal tender in 2012 and prices are rounded in cash transactions.

History

Norway introduced the krone in 1875 when it joined the Scandinavian Monetary Union with Sweden and Denmark, replacing the speciedaler at a rate of 4 kroner per daler. The union dissolved during the First World War. The krone was pegged successively to gold, the pound sterling, the US dollar, and later to currency baskets including the ECU; since 1992 it has floated, with Norges Bank targeting low, stable inflation (2 per cent since 2018, previously 2.5 per cent).

Coins and banknotes

Circulating coins are 1, 5, 10 and 20 kroner; the 1-krone and 10-krone coins have a perforated hole, a distinctive feature. The eighth banknote series, issued from 2017, has a striking pixelated maritime design by the architecture firm Snøhetta, with each denomination depicting a different aspect of Norway's relationship with the sea — a lighthouse on the 50, a cod on the 200, and a rescue vessel on the 1000.

Exchange rate character

The krone is a heavily traded minor currency whose value is influenced by oil and gas prices, reflecting petroleum's role in the Norwegian economy, and by interest-rate differentials. Norway has frequently debated — and repeatedly rejected, in referendums on EU membership — replacing the krone with the euro.
