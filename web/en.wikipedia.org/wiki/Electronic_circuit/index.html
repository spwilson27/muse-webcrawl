Electronic circuit

An electronic circuit is composed of individual electronic components, such as resistors, transistors, capacitors, inductors and diodes, connected by conductive wires or traces through which electric current can flow. It is a type of electrical circuit, and to be referred to as electronic, it must contain at least one active device. The combination of components and wires allows various simple and complex operations to be performed: signals can be amplified, computations can be performed, and data can be moved from one place to another.

Classification

Circuits can be constructed of discrete components connected by individual pieces of wire, but it is far more common today to create interconnections by photolithographic techniques on a laminated substrate (a printed circuit board) and solder the components to these interconnections. In an integrated circuit, the components and interconnections are formed on the same substrate, typically a semiconductor such as doped silicon.

Analog and digital circuits

Analog circuits process signals that vary continuously over a range of voltages, using components whose behaviour is described by continuous functions; examples include amplifiers, oscillators, and filters. Digital circuits, by contrast, represent information with discrete levels—conventionally two, interpreted as binary 0 and 1—and form the basis of logic gates and computing. Mixed-signal circuits combine both domains, for instance in analog-to-digital converters.

Design and analysis

Circuit behaviour is analysed with Kirchhoff's circuit laws together with the constitutive relations of the components. Analog design uses techniques such as nodal analysis and small-signal modelling; digital design is abstracted into logic, register-transfer, and physical levels. Computer-aided design tools—circuit simulators such as SPICE—are used universally for verification before manufacture.
