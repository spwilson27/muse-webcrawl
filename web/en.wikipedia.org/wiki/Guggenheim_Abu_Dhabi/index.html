The Guggenheim Abu Dhabi is a planned museum of modern and contemporary art in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates, being developed by the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation in partnership with Abu Dhabi's Department of Culture and Tourism. Located in the Saadiyat Island Cultural District alongside the Louvre Abu Dhabi, the Zayed National Museum and the Abrahamic Family House, the museum is designed by architect Frank Gehry and, at roughly 30,000 square metres of galleries, is set to be the largest of the Guggenheim museums.

Design

Gehry's design arranges galleries of varied shapes and scales around a central atrium, punctuated by large cone-shaped structures that echo the region's traditional wind towers (barjeel) and serve to shade and ventilate the courtyards. The building mixes conventional white-cube galleries with very large industrial-scale spaces intended for oversized contemporary installations.

Collection and programme

The museum's collection, assembled since 2009, focuses on global art from the 1960s to the present, with particular attention to West Asian, North African and South Asian (WANASA) art placed in dialogue with Western movements. Pre-opening exhibitions in Abu Dhabi, including Seeing Through Light: Selections from the Guggenheim Abu Dhabi Collection (2014), previewed the holdings.

History and controversy

The project was announced in 2006 as part of Abu Dhabi's cultural development strategy. Its schedule slipped repeatedly; construction of the main building began in 2019, with completion announced for the mid-2020s. The project, like others on Saadiyat Island, drew sustained criticism from artists and human-rights organisations over the treatment of migrant construction workers in the UAE; the artist-led group Gulf Labor organised protests and boycotts, and the Guggenheim Foundation and Abu Dhabi authorities responded with commitments on worker welfare monitoring.
