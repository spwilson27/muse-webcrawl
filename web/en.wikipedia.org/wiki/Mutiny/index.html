Mutiny

Mutiny
TypeMilitary offence
ExamplesSepoy Mutiny (1857), Bounty mutiny (1789)

Mutiny is a revolt among a group of people, typically military personnel or sailors, to oppose, change, or overthrow their superiors or orders. Mutiny has historically been among the most serious military offences, often punishable by death.

Notable mutinies

Famous examples include the 1789 mutiny on HMS Bounty, the Indian Rebellion of 1857 (often called the Sepoy Mutiny), the 1905 mutiny aboard the battleship Potemkin, and the French Army mutinies of 1917 during the First World War.

Law

Under the United States Uniform Code of Military Justice, mutiny — creating violence or disturbance with intent to usurp lawful military authority — is a capital offence in wartime. Modern definitions distinguish mutiny from lawful refusal of illegal orders.
