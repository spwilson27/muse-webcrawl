The MG Midget is a small two-seater sports car produced by MG from 1961 to 1979. It revived the "Midget" name first used on MG cars of the late 1920s and 1930s. The car was essentially a badge-engineered version of the Austin-Healey Sprite, and the two models — known jointly as "Spridgets" — were built at MG's factory in Abingdon.

Development

The first Midget (1961) was closely based on the Mk II Sprite, with a 948 cc BMC A-series engine. Successive versions — Mk II (1964) with a 1,098 cc engine and improved trim, and Mk III (1966) with a detuned 1,275 cc unit related to the Mini Cooper S engine — refined the formula. From 1974, US-market cars used the 1,493 cc Standard SC engine from the Triumph Spitfire and gained raised ride height and large black rubber bumpers to meet American regulations.

Production

Nearly 225,000 Midgets were built across all versions, alongside their Sprite siblings. Affordable, simple and fun to drive, the Midget remains a popular entry point into classic British sports car ownership.
