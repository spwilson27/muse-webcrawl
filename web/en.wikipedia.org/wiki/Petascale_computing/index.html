Petascale computing

Petascale computing refers to computing systems capable of performing at least one petaFLOPS — 1015 floating-point operations per second. Such systems marked a milestone in supercomputing first crossed in 2008.

History

IBM's Roadrunner at Los Alamos National Laboratory was the first system to sustain one petaFLOPS on the LINPACK benchmark, in June 2008. Cray's Jaguar followed, and petascale machines proliferated through the 2010s for weather forecasting, nuclear stockpile stewardship, molecular dynamics, and astrophysics. The petascale era was succeeded by exascale computing, reached in 2022 by Frontier.

Challenges

At petascale, power consumption, memory bandwidth, interconnect performance, and fault tolerance — with failures expected somewhere in millions of components — become central design concerns, driving heterogeneous architectures combining CPUs with GPU accelerators.
