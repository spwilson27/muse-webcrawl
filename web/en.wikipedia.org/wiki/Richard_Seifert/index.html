Richard Seifert

Richard Seifert (25 November 1910 – 26 October 2001) was a Swiss-British architect best known for designing many prominent London office buildings during the post-war development boom, including Centre Point and Tower 42, originally the National Westminster Tower.

Life

Born in Zurich to a family that moved to London, Seifert was naturalised British and served in the Royal Engineers during the Second World War. He founded R. Seifert and Partners, which became one of Britain's most prolific commercial practices.

Works

His best-known buildings include Centre Point (1966) at St Giles Circus, the NatWest Tower (1980), then London's tallest building, Tolworth Tower, the Metropole Hotel in Brighton, and numerous hotels and office blocks for developers such as Harry Hyams. His firm was responsible for more London buildings than any other practice of the period.

Reception

Seifert's work was often criticised by architectural critics for its commercial character, though public and later reassessments have recognised buildings such as Centre Point, now Grade II listed, as icons of 1960s London.
