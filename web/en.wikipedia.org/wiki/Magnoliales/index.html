Magnoliales

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Plantae
Clade:Angiosperms
Clade:Magnoliids
Order:Magnoliales

The Magnoliales are an order of flowering plants. They belong to the magnoliids, a basal group of angiosperms, and are among the more ancient lineages of flowering plants, retaining a number of features considered primitive within the angiosperms, such as numerous spirally arranged floral parts and undifferentiated perianth segments (tepals).

Classification

Under the APG IV system (2016), the order includes six families:

  • Annonaceae — the custard apple family, including pawpaw, soursop and cherimoya; the largest family in the order, with over 2,000 species.
  • Degeneriaceae — a small family from Fiji.
  • Eupomatiaceae — a small family from Australia and New Guinea.
  • Himantandraceae — a small family from Australia and the Maluku Islands.
  • Magnoliaceae — the magnolia family, including Magnolia and the tulip tree (Liriodendron).
  • Myristicaceae — the nutmeg family.

Characteristics

Members of the Magnoliales are mostly woody plants — trees, shrubs and lianas — found mainly in tropical and subtropical regions. Many have aromatic bark and leaves containing ethereal oils. The flowers are often large and showy, with numerous stamens and carpels arranged spirally on an elongated receptacle. The seeds of some members, such as nutmeg (Myristica fragrans), are economically important spices.

Economic importance

The order includes commercially significant plants: nutmeg and mace from Myristicaceae; edible fruits such as soursop, custard apple and cherimoya from Annonaceae; and ornamental trees and timber from Magnoliaceae.
