Cedilla

A cedilla (from Spanish cedilla, "small ceda", i.e. small "z"), is a hook or tail ( ¸ ) added under certain letters as a diacritical mark to modify their pronunciation. In Catalan, French, and Portuguese it is used only under the letter c (forming ç), where it changes a hard /k/ sound to a soft /s/ before back vowels. The cedilla is also used in Latvian, Marshallese, and historically in Romanian, and in romanisations of other scripts.

History

The cedilla originated in medieval Spanish manuscripts as a small Visigothic z written beneath the letter c to indicate a voiceless alveolar affricate or fricative sound where plain c before a, o, or u would otherwise be read as /k/. From Spain the mark spread to French, Portuguese, and Catalan scribal practice. Although modern Spanish no longer uses the cedilla, the letter ç remains essential in the orthographies of French (français), Portuguese (açúcar), and Catalan (força).

Usage by language

  • French: ç is pronounced /s/ before a, o, u: façade, garçon, reçu.
  • Portuguese: ç is always /s/: coração, praça.
  • Catalan: ç is /s/, word-finally often written -ç: feliç.
  • Turkish: ç represents /tʃ/ ("ch"), and ş represents /ʃ/: çay, şehir.
  • Latvian: cedillas appear under g, k, l, n to indicate palatalisation: ģ, ķ, ļ, ņ.

Encoding

In Unicode, the combining cedilla is U+0327, and the spacing modifier cedilla is U+00B8. Precomposed characters such as Ç (U+00C7) and ç (U+00E7) are widely used. In Romanian, the correct mark under s and t is a comma below rather than a cedilla, a distinction long handled inconsistently in fonts and encodings.
