War in Donbas

The war in Donbas, also known as the Donbas war, was a phase of the Russo-Ukrainian War in the Donbas region of eastern Ukraine. From 2014 until the Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the war was fought between the Ukrainian government and Russian-supported separatist forces in the Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts.

Background

Following the Euromaidan protests and the Revolution of Dignity in early 2014, pro-Russian unrest spread across eastern and southern Ukraine. In April 2014, armed groups seized government buildings in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts and proclaimed the Donetsk People's Republic (DPR) and the Luhansk People's Republic (LPR).

Course of the war

Ukraine launched an "Anti-Terrorist Operation" to retake the seized territory. Fighting intensified over the summer of 2014; Malaysia Airlines Flight 17 was shot down over the conflict zone on 17 July 2014, killing all 298 people aboard. Regular Russian forces intervened at key moments, notably at Ilovaisk in August 2014 and Debaltseve in early 2015. The Minsk Protocol (September 2014) and Minsk II agreement (February 2015) established ceasefires that were never fully implemented, and the front line largely froze into a war of attrition along a line of contact.

Humanitarian impact

By early 2022 the conflict had killed more than 14,000 people, including several thousand civilians, and displaced well over a million residents. In February 2022 Russia recognised the DPR and LPR and launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, subsuming the Donbas war into a much larger conflict.
