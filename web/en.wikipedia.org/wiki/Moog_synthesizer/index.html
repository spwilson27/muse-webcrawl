Moog synthesizer

Moog synthesizer may refer to any of several analog synthesizers designed by American engineer Robert Moog and manufactured by his company from the 1960s onward. The Moog was the first commercial synthesizer, debuting in 1964 as a modular system of voltage-controlled oscillators, filters, amplifiers, and envelope generators connected by patch cords and played with a keyboard.

History

Composers such as Wendy Carlos brought the instrument to fame with the bestselling album Switched-On Bach (1968), and the Moog featured in recordings by the Beatles, the Doors, Kraftwerk, and Keith Emerson. In 1970 Moog introduced the Minimoog, a compact, preset-patched portable instrument that became the archetypal analog synthesizer and shaped progressive rock, funk, disco, and electronic music. Its ladder filter and three-oscillator sound remain widely imitated.

Later years

The original company lost ground to digital instruments and went bankrupt in the 1980s; Robert Moog regained the rights to his name and founded Moog Music again in 2002, producing instruments such as the Minimoog Voyager, the Sub 37, and reissues of the classic modular systems and Model D. Moog synthesizers are foundational to the design vocabulary of nearly all later synthesizers.
