Galway (Irish: Gaillimh) is a city in County Galway on the west coast of Ireland, in the province of Connacht. It lies on the River Corrib between Lough Corrib and Galway Bay. With a population of 85,910 at the 2022 census, it is the sixth most populous city in Ireland and the only city in Connacht. Galway is a centre of Irish culture, renowned for festivals, traditional music and its association with the Irish language; it was European Capital of Culture in 2020 (events extended into 2021).

History

The Galway settlement began in 1124 as Dún Bhun na Gaillimhe, a fort at the mouth of the Corrib built by the King of Connacht. Anglo-Norman de Burghs founded the walled town in the 1230s. From the 15th to 17th centuries, Galway was dominated by fourteen wealthy merchant families — the "Tribes of Galway" — and traded extensively with Spain and France in wine. Besieged during the Cromwellian and Williamite wars, the city declined in the 18th century and suffered in the Great Famine, before revival as a university (Queen's College 1845, now University of Galway), tourism and technology centre.

Culture

Galway hosts the Galway International Arts Festival, the Galway Races at Ballybrit, the Galway Film Fleadh and the Macnas parades. Landmarks include Eyre Square, the Spanish Arch, Galway Cathedral, St Nicholas' Collegiate Church (1320), Lynch's Castle and the Claddagh — the fishing village across the river that gave its name to the Claddagh ring. The Latin Quarter's pubs and buskers are famous; Galway is a UNESCO City of Film.

Economy and education

Galway is a hub for medical-device and ICT companies (including Medtronic and Boston Scientific plants) and a gateway for tourism to Connemara and the Aran Islands. The University of Galway and the Atlantic Technological University serve over 20,000 students.
