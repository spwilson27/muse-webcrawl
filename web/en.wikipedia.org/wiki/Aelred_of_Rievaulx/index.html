Aelred of Rievaulx (1110 – 12 January 1167), also written Ailred or Ælred, was an English Cistercian monk and writer who served as abbot of Rievaulx Abbey in Yorkshire from 1147 until his death. He is regarded as one of the most influential spiritual writers of the 12th century, sometimes called the "Bernard of the North" after Bernard of Clairvaux.

Life

Aelred was born at Hexham into a family of hereditary priests, and was educated at the court of King David I of Scotland, where he became steward of the royal household. In 1134, travelling on the king's business, he visited the recently founded Cistercian abbey of Rievaulx and decided to join it. He became novice master, then abbot of the new foundation of Revesby in Lincolnshire (1143), and in 1147 was elected abbot of Rievaulx itself, which under his leadership grew to a community of some 140 choir monks and 500 lay brothers and servants. Despite chronic ill health in later life, he travelled widely on Cistercian visitations and acted as a mediator in secular and ecclesiastical disputes.

Writings

Aelred's best-known work is On Spiritual Friendship (De spirituali amicitia), a dialogue modelled on Cicero's De amicitia that presents friendship as a school of charity and a path to God — an unusual theme treated with warmth and frankness. His other writings include The Mirror of Charity, lives of saints (including a Life of Edward the Confessor written for the saint's translation in 1163), sermons, a rule for recluses written for his sister (De institutione inclusarum), and historical works connected with Hexham and the Scottish wars.

Veneration

Aelred was venerated locally soon after his death and is commemorated on 12 January in the Roman Catholic Church and by the Church of England. His writings, long overshadowed, regained wide readership in the 20th century through new translations.
