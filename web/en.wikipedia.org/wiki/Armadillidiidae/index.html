Armadillidiidae is a family of woodlice, terrestrial isopod crustaceans commonly known as pill woodlice, pillbugs, or roly-polies. The family includes the well-known genus Armadillidium.

Characteristics

Members of the family are able to roll into a near-perfect sphere (conglobation) as a defence against predators and desiccation, a trait that distinguishes them from pill millipedes, which they superficially resemble. The uropods are short and do not project beyond the body outline, unlike many other woodlice.

Distribution and ecology

The family is centred on the Mediterranean region, with many species endemic to small areas; some, such as Armadillidium vulgare, have been introduced worldwide. They feed on decaying plant matter and are important decomposers in soil ecosystems.
