Goal (association football)

In association football, a goal is scored when the whole of the ball passes over the goal line, between the goalposts and under the crossbar, provided no offence has been committed by the scoring team. A goal is the only method of scoring in the game and is worth one point; the team scoring more goals wins the match.

The goal structure

Under Law 1 of the Laws of the Game, the goal consists of two posts 7.32 metres (8 yards) apart joined by a crossbar 2.44 metres (8 feet) above the ground, with a net to catch the ball. The goal area and penalty area are marked in front of it.

Methods and terminology

Goals may be scored with any part of the body except the hands and arms (unless by a goalkeeper in their own area). Common terms include the open-play goal, penalty, free kick, header, own goal, and the "golden goal" once used to decide drawn knockout matches. Goal-line technology and VAR are now used to verify disputed goals.
