Game Developers Conference

The Game Developers Conference (GDC) is an annual conference for video game developers. The event entails presentations on topics such as programming, design, production, business and management, audio, and visual arts, given by leading industry professionals. Held each spring in San Francisco, California, it is the largest annual gathering of professional video game developers, attracting tens of thousands of attendees from around the world.

History

The conference was founded by game designer Chris Crawford in 1988 as the Computer Game Developers Conference. Its first edition drew only 27 attendees, meeting in Crawford's living room in San Jose, California. The event grew rapidly with the industry, moving to larger venues in Santa Clara and later to the Moscone Center in San Francisco, and was renamed the Game Developers Conference in 1999.

By the 2010s GDC regularly attracted more than 25,000 attendees, with a record attendance of about 29,000 in 2019. The 2020 conference was postponed and eventually held virtually because of the COVID-19 pandemic; subsequent editions have combined in-person and virtual formats.

Features

GDC includes lectures, panels, tutorials and roundtable discussions across tracks for programming, game design, audio, visual arts, production, and business. The GDC Expo showcases development tools, platforms and services. Notable recurring features include the Experimental Gameplay Workshop and technical sessions such as postmortems of classic and contemporary games.

Awards ceremonies

Two major awards ceremonies take place during GDC. The Game Developers Choice Awards honour the developers of the best games of the previous year, as voted by members of the International Game Developers Association and industry peers. The Independent Games Festival (IGF), established in 1998, celebrates independent games, with awards including the Seumas McNally Grand Prize; its pavilion showcases finalist games on the expo floor.
