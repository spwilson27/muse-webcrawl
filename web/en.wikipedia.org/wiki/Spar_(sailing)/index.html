Spar (sailing)

A spar is a pole of wood, metal or lightweight material such as carbon fibre used in the rigging of a sailing vessel to carry or support its sails. The best-known spars are the mast, which rises vertically from the hull, and the many horizontal and diagonal poles attached to it. Collectively, the spars, sails and cordage constitute the vessel's rig.

Principal spars

  • Mast — the upright spar supporting the sails; large ships may have fore, main and mizzen masts, built in sections (lower, top and topgallant masts).
  • Boom — the spar along the foot of a fore-and-aft sail, hinged or pivoted at the mast.
  • Yard — a horizontal spar hung from a mast, from which a square sail is bent (set).
  • Bowsprit — the spar projecting forward from the bow, to which headsails and their stays are attached.
  • Gaff — the spar along the head of a four-sided fore-and-aft (gaff-rigged) sail.
  • Spinnaker pole and whisker pole — light spars used to hold out downwind sails.
  • Boomkin, sprits and stuns'ls booms — auxiliary spars for particular sails and rigs.

Materials

Traditional spars were made of straight-grained timber — pine, fir and spruce being favoured for lightness and resilience — sometimes in several pieces "fished" together for great lengths. From the later nineteenth century, hollow steel and then aluminium spars became common; modern racing yachts use carbon-fibre spars, which can be both lighter and stiffer and may take the form of wing masts.

In nautical usage the word also survives in "spar buoy", a floating navigation mark shaped like a pole, and in names of vessels' parts such as the spar deck.
