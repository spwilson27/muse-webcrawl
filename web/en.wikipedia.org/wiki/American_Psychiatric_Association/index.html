American Psychiatric Association

The American Psychiatric Association (APA) is the main professional organization of psychiatrists and trainee psychiatrists in the United States, and the largest psychiatric organization in the world, with more than 38,000 members. It is distinct from the American Psychological Association, which shares the same abbreviation.

History

Founded in Philadelphia in 1844 as the Association of Medical Superintendents of American Institutions for the Insane by thirteen asylum superintendents, including Thomas Story Kirkbride, it adopted its current name in 1921. Its seal features Benjamin Rush, regarded as the father of American psychiatry.

Publications

The APA publishes the American Journal of Psychiatry and the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM), whose fifth edition, DSM-5, appeared in 2013 and was revised as DSM-5-TR in 2022. The DSM is the standard classification of mental disorders used by clinicians and researchers in the United States, and has been both influential and controversial.

Activities

The association holds an annual meeting, issues position statements and practice guidelines, and advocates on mental-health policy, insurance coverage and research funding.
