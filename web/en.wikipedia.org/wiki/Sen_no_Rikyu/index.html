Sen no Rikyū (千利休, 1522 – April 21, 1591), also known simply as Rikyū, is considered the historical figure with the most profound influence on chanoyu, the Japanese "Way of Tea", particularly the tradition of wabi-cha.

Born in the merchant city of Sakai, Rikyū studied tea under Takeno Jōō and served as tea master first to the warlord Oda Nobunaga and then to his successor, Toyotomi Hideyoshi, becoming the most trusted and celebrated tea master of his age. He refined the rustic, restrained aesthetic of wabi — simplicity, imperfection, and quietness — designing small thatched tearooms such as the two-tatami Tai-an, promoting rough Raku tea bowls, and codifying the aesthetic and spiritual principles that still govern the tea ceremony.

Death

Despite his unrivalled position, Rikyū fell out of favour with Hideyoshi for reasons that remain debated — pride, political friction, and disputes over a wooden statue of Rikyū placed at a temple gate are among the explanations given. In 1591 Hideyoshi ordered him to commit seppuku, which he performed after a final tea ceremony. His death made him a martyr of the Way of Tea; the three great tea schools of today (Omotesenke, Urasenke, and Mushakōjisenke) all descend from his lineage through his grandson Sen no Sōtan.
