Vincenzo Florio (18 March 1883 – 6 January 1959) was an Italian entrepreneur, heir of the rich Florio economic dynasty of Sicily, and is remembered principally as the founder of the Targa Florio, the celebrated open-road endurance automobile race first held in Sicily in 1906.

Biography

Florio was born in Palermo, the youngest son of Ignazio Florio Jr. and Baroness Giovanna d'Ondes Trigona. The Florio family had built one of Italy's great fortunes in the 19th century through shipping (the Navigazione Generale Italiana), tuna fisheries, wine (Marsala), sulphur, banking, and metal foundries. Vincenzo grew up in the family's Palermo residences amid the splendor of the Sicilian Belle Époque, and developed a passion for the new sport of motor racing.

Florio himself raced competitively in the early 1900s, competing in events in Italy and France. In 1906, drawing on the success of races such as the Coppa Florio (which he had established in 1905 and named after his family), he created the Targa Florio: a grueling race over public mountain roads in the Madonie near Palermo. The first running, on 6 May 1906, covered three laps of a 148-kilometre circuit; it was won by Alessandro Cagno in an Itala. The prize, a plaque—the targa—designed by the sculptor Ettore Ximenes, gave the race its name.

The Targa Florio race

Under Vincenzo Florio's direction, the Targa Florio became one of the most prestigious events in international motor sport, attracting manufacturers including Alfa Romeo, Maserati, Bugatti, Mercedes-Benz, Ferrari, and Porsche. The circuit changed over the years, most famously settling on the 72-kilometre Piccolo Circuito delle Madonie. The race was held, with interruptions for the World Wars, until 1977, when it was discontinued as a speed event on safety grounds and continued in rally form. Florio remained its patron and organizer for most of his life.

Later life

The Florio family fortune declined sharply in the interwar period, and the family's companies were progressively absorbed or liquidated. Vincenzo Florio married twice: first to Princess Annina Alliata di Montereale, who died young, and later to the French journalist Lucie Henry. He retained his love of motoring and of Sicily until his death. Florio died in Épernay, France, on 6 January 1959.

Legacy

The Targa Florio is widely regarded as one of the founding events of motor racing, and the Florio name survives in the race's historic re-enactments, the Targa Florio Classica, and in Palermo's architecture from the family's era—including the opera singer's villas and the Art Nouveau buildings of the city's golden age. A modern ferry company, Tirrenia, operated ships named for the family, and the Vincenzo Florio airport at Trapani-Birgi bears his name.
