Parallel (geometry)

Parallel lines marked with matching arrowheads, cut by a transversal.

In geometry, parallel lines are coplanar infinite straight lines that do not intersect at any point. Parallel planes are planes in the same three-dimensional space that never meet. Parallel curves are curves that do not touch each other or intersect and keep a fixed minimum distance. In three-dimensional Euclidean space, a line and a plane that do not intersect are also said to be parallel. However, two noncoplanar lines are called skew lines.

Parallel lines are the subject of Euclid's parallel postulate. Parallelism is primarily a property of affine geometries and Euclidean geometry is a special instance of this type of geometry. In some other geometries, such as hyperbolic geometry, lines can have analogous properties that are referred to as parallelism.

Symbol

The parallel symbol is ∥. For example, AB ∥ CD indicates that line segment AB is parallel to line segment CD. In the Unicode character set, the "parallel" and "not parallel" signs have codepoints U+2225 (∥) and U+2226 (∦), respectively. In addition, U+22D5 (⋕) represents the relation "equal and parallel to".

Euclidean parallelism

Given the lines l and m, the following conditions are equivalent: the lines are parallel; the lines have the same direction; every line through a point on one perpendicular to it is also perpendicular to the other. As a consequence of Euclid's parallel postulate, if two lines are parallel, the distance between them is constant. When parallel lines are cut by a transversal, corresponding angles are equal, alternate interior angles are equal, and consecutive interior angles are supplementary.

Two lines in the same three-dimensional space that do not intersect need not be parallel: only if they are in a common plane are they called parallel; otherwise they are called skew lines. Two planes in three-dimensional space that do not meet are called parallel, and a line is said to be parallel to a plane if the line's direction vector lies within, or is parallel to, the plane.

In non-Euclidean geometries

In spherical geometry, all straight lines (great circles) intersect, so there are no parallel lines. In hyperbolic geometry, through a point not on a given line there pass infinitely many lines that do not intersect the given line; the limiting such lines are called limiting parallels, and parallels in hyperbolic geometry need not maintain a constant distance.
