Christ Pantocrator is one of the oldest and most famous surviving religious icons, held at Saint Catherine's Monastery at Mount Sinai, Egypt. Dating to the 6th century, it is an encaustic (hot wax) painting on wood depicting Christ as "Pantocrator" — ruler of all — holding a jewelled Gospel book in his left hand and raising his right hand in blessing.

Description

The icon is renowned for its deliberate duality: the two sides of Christ's face are painted differently — the right side serene and compassionate, the left side stern and judging — interpreted as representing Christ's dual nature, human and divine, and his roles of mercy and judgement. The painting's naturalism reflects the last flowering of late-antique portraiture before Byzantine stylisation hardened.

History

The monastery, founded by the emperor Justinian in the 6th century, survived the Byzantine iconoclastic controversy (726–843) because of its remote desert location, preserving some of the earliest icons in existence. The Pantocrator icon became the prototype for countless later images of Christ, including the Christ in Majesty tradition, and remains among the most reproduced icons in Christianity.
