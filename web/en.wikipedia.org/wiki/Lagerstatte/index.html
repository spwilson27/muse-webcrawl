Lagerstatte

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lagerstatte (the umlaut-free rendering of German Lagerstätte, plural Lagerstatten or Lagerstätten) is a term used in palaeontology for a sedimentary rock unit that preserves fossils of extraordinary quality or quantity. The spelling without the umlaut is common in English-language technical writing and in contexts where the ä cannot be displayed.

Definition

Two main kinds are distinguished. A Konservat-Lagerstätte preserves soft tissues and complete soft-bodied organisms — famous examples include the Burgess Shale, Chengjiang, Solnhofen and Messel deposits — usually because bottom waters were anoxic and burial was rapid. A Konzentrat-Lagerstätte is a dense concentration of fossil material such as a bone bed or a mass-mortality fish layer.

Importance

Although Lagerstatten form only a tiny fraction of the sedimentary record, they dominate knowledge of the history of soft-bodied animals and of complete fossil ecosystems. Their uneven distribution through geological time is a major source of bias in estimates of ancient diversity, and their discovery — as with the Cambrian Chengjiang biota found in 1984 — can transform understanding of evolutionary history.
