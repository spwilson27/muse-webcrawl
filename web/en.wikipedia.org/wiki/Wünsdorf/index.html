Wünsdorf

Wünsdorf is a district of the town of Zossen, in the Teltow-Fläming district of Brandenburg, Germany, about 40 kilometres south of Berlin. It is chiefly known for its enormous former military complex, which served successively as the largest barracks of the Imperial German Army, the headquarters of the Wehrmacht's high command of the army (OKH) during the Second World War, and the headquarters of the Soviet Group of Soviet Forces in Germany during the Cold War.

History

Military use of the area began before the First World War, when the site was developed as a training camp and barracks near the existing infantry school and shooting ranges at Kummersdorf. During the First World War, Wünsdorf hosted the Halbmondlager ("Half Moon Camp"), a prisoner-of-war camp built specially for Muslim prisoners, which contained one of the first mosques built in Germany, intended to encourage the prisoners to join the Ottoman-German cause.

Between the wars, Wünsdorf became a center of German motorized warfare development, including the motor sports school and early tank training. During the Second World War, the underground Maybach I and Maybach II bunker complexes housed the OKH and the OKW communications center (Zeppelin), linked directly to Berlin.

After 1945, the Red Army took over the site, which became the headquarters of Soviet forces in East Germany and a closed, self-contained garrison town housing up to 75,000 Soviet soldiers and family members — sometimes called "Little Moscow". Russian troops withdrew in 1994, four years after German reunification, leaving extensive derelict barracks, now partly converted to housing, archives, and a museum, and partly abandoned as the "forbidden city" (Verbotene Stadt) of ruins.
