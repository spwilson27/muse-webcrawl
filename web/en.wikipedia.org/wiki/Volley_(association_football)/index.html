Volley (association football)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A player volleying the ball before it touches the ground.

A volley is an air-borne strike in association football, where a player's foot meets and directs the ball in an angled direction before it has time to reach the ground. A volley can be extremely hard to aim at, and requires good technique, timing and balance to execute well.

Technique

The player must judge the flight of the ball, position the body, and strike through the ball with the foot while it is still in the air, usually from a cross, a headed clearance or a pass. Because the ball is moving and has not bounced, volleys are among the most difficult shots in football, but a well-struck volley can generate great power and give the goalkeeper little reaction time.

Variations

A half volley is struck just after the ball bounces. A bicycle kick (or overhead kick) is an acrobatic volley in which the player kicks the ball backwards over their own head while airborne. A volley attempted while diving forward is sometimes called a diving volley.

Famous volleys

Celebrated volleys include Marco van Basten's zero-angle volley for the Netherlands in the Euro 1988 final and Zinedine Zidane's left-footed volley for Real Madrid in the 2002 UEFA Champions League Final, widely considered one of the greatest goals in the competition's history.
