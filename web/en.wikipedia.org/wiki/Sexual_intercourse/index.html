Sexual intercourse

Sexual intercourse (also coitus or copulation) is a sexual activity typically involving the insertion and thrusting of the penis into the vagina for sexual pleasure, reproduction, or both. It is also known as vaginal intercourse or vaginal sex. Other forms of penetrative sexual intercourse include anal sex, oral sex (sometimes not classified as "intercourse"), fingering and penetration with a sex toy.

Reproduction

In human reproduction, vaginal intercourse may result in fertilisation when sperm deposited in the vagina reach an egg cell. It is the natural means of conception, and the fertile window around ovulation determines the probability of pregnancy. Contraception — barrier methods such as condoms, hormonal methods, intrauterine devices and others — is used to reduce that probability.

Health

Sexual intercourse can transmit sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV, chlamydia, gonorrhoea, syphilis and HPV; condom use substantially reduces transmission risk. Regular sexual activity has been associated in research with various physical and psychological effects, while pain during intercourse (dyspareunia) and other dysfunctions may have medical or psychological causes.

Social and legal aspects

Attitudes and laws governing sexual intercourse vary widely: legal ages of consent, the treatment of sex within and outside marriage, and offences such as rape — defined by absence of consent — are central legal issues. Marital rape was criminalised in most Western jurisdictions only in the late 20th century.

Other animals

Copulation is the usual means of reproduction in most sexually reproducing animals; its duration and form vary enormously, from seconds in many insects to prolonged intromission in some mammals.
