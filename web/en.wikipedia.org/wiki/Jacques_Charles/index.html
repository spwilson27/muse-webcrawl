Life

Jacques Alexandre César Charles (12 November 1746 – 7 April 1823) was a French inventor, scientist, and mathematician, a member of the Académie des Sciences and professor of physics at the Conservatoire des Arts et Métiers. He first earned his living as a clerk in the Ministry of Finance; turned out of office, he devoted himself to experimental physics, becoming a celebrated public lecturer in Paris.

The hydrogen balloon

Prompted by the Montgolfier brothers' hot-air balloon, Charles — working with the Robert brothers, instrument makers — built the first balloon inflated with hydrogen, a gas then newly isolated. The unmanned balloon La Charlière rose from Paris on 27 August 1783 before a vast crowd (Benjamin Franklin among them) and landed at Gonesse, where terrified peasants attacked the "monster" with pitchforks. On 1 December 1783, Charles and Nicolas-Louis Robert made the first manned hydrogen balloon flight, ascending from the Tuileries and flying about 36 km to Nesles-la-Vallée; Charles then rose alone to roughly 3,000 m — the first solo balloon flight. His designs introduced the valve line, the appendix, and sand ballast still used in ballooning.

Charles's law

Around 1787 Charles discovered, but never published, that the volume of a gas at constant pressure increases proportionally with its absolute temperature — Charles's law (V/T = constant). Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac published the result in 1802, crediting Charles; the law, combined with Boyle's law, yields the ideal gas law.
