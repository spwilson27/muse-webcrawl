The black-winged stilt (Himantopus himantopus) is a widely distributed, very long-legged wader in the avocet and stilt family Recurvirostridae. Its exceptionally long pink legs—the longest relative to body length of any bird after flamingos—allow it to feed in deeper water than most shorebirds. It breeds in wetlands across warm temperate and tropical zones worldwide.

Description

Adults are 33–36 cm long, with black upperparts and wings contrasting with white underparts and head (the head pattern varies; males usually show more black). The bill is long, thin, and straight, and the legs are pinkish-red. Juveniles are browner above. The taxonomy of the stilt complex has been debated: forms in the Americas, Australia, and elsewhere have variously been treated as subspecies of H. himantopus or as separate species such as the black-necked stilt.

Behaviour

Black-winged stilts feed by picking insects, crustaceans, molluscs, and small fish from the water's surface and mud while wading. They are noisy and aggressive in defence of nests, which are scrapes or low mounds near shallow water; both parents incubate the typically four eggs, and the downy young can swim soon after hatching.

Distribution

The species occurs around the Mediterranean, sub-Saharan Africa, central and southern Asia, Southeast Asia, and parts of Oceania, with northern populations migrating south for the winter. It has expanded northward in Europe in recent decades and now breeds irregularly in Britain.
