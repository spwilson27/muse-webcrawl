Norte Chico civilisation

Pyramid structures at Caral, one of the principal Norte Chico sites.

The Norte Chico civilisation (also Caral or Caral-Supe civilisation) was a complex society of Late Archaic period (c. 3500–1800 BCE) settlements spanning the north-central coast of Peru. It is considered one of the oldest known civilisations in the Americas, roughly contemporaneous with the earliest civilisations of Egypt and Mesopotamia, and one of six recognised "cradles of civilisation" worldwide.

Sites

The civilisation is named for the Norte Chico region, where researchers have documented some 30 major population centres in the valleys of the Fortaleza, Pativilca, Supe, Huaura and Chillón rivers. The best-known site is Caral, in the Supe Valley, a large urban centre with monumental architecture including sunken circular plazas and platform mounds. Other major sites include Áspero, Miraya, Lurihuasi and Bandurria.

Society

Remarkably, the Norte Chico sites show little evidence of warfare, fortifications, ceramics or even art in their early phases. Subsistence relied on a maritime economy of anchovies and sardines traded inland for cotton, squash and other agricultural products. Evidence of a knotted-cord recording device (a proto-quipu) and a gourd fragment bearing a deity figure suggests early complexity in communication and religion.
