Ad hoc network

An ad hoc network is a decentralised type of computer network in which each node participates in routing by forwarding data for other nodes, so the network does not rely on a pre-existing infrastructure such as routers, base stations or access points. The term ad hoc (Latin for "for this") reflects that such networks can be formed spontaneously, wherever and whenever devices come into range of one another.

TypeComputer network
TopologyDecentralised, peer-to-peer / mesh
ExamplesMANETs, VANETs, wireless sensor networks

Types

  • Mobile ad hoc networks (MANETs) — networks of mobile devices connected by wireless links, used in military communications, disaster recovery and conferences.
  • Vehicular ad hoc networks (VANETs) — networks among moving vehicles and roadside units for safety and traffic applications.
  • Wireless sensor networks — networks of spatially distributed sensors monitoring environmental conditions.
  • Mesh networks — a related topology where nodes relay traffic cooperatively; many home and community "mesh Wi-Fi" systems operate in a partially ad hoc fashion.

Routing

Because nodes move and links appear and disappear, ad hoc routing protocols must continually rediscover routes. Proactive protocols such as OLSR maintain routes to all destinations in advance, reactive protocols such as AODV and DSR discover routes on demand, and hybrid protocols combine both approaches. The classic IEEE 802.11 "ad hoc mode" (IBSS) allows Wi-Fi devices to form a network without an access point.

Challenges

Ad hoc networks face difficulties including limited battery power, variable link quality, security (no central authority to authenticate nodes), and scalability of routing overhead as the number of nodes grows.
