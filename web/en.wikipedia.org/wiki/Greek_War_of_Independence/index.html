Greek War of Independence

Date: 1821–1829
Location: Greece, Aegean
Result: Greek independence recognised (1830 London Protocol)
Key battle: Navarino (1827)

The Greek War of Independence (1821–1829) was a successful war waged by Greek revolutionaries against the Ottoman Empire, resulting in the establishment of the modern Greek state — the first independent state created from Ottoman territory in the Balkans.

Outbreak and course

Prepared by the secret Filiki Eteria society, revolt broke out in 1821 in the Danubian Principalities under Alexandros Ypsilantis and in the Peloponnese, where Bishop Germanos is said to have raised the flag at Agia Lavra. Greeks captured Tripolitsa but suffered civil wars, while Ottoman and Egyptian forces under Ibrahim Pasha nearly crushed the revolt by 1827. Philhellenes such as Lord Byron, volunteer fighters and funds, supported the cause abroad.

Great Power intervention

Britain, France and Russia destroyed the Ottoman-Egyptian fleet at Navarino (October 1827); a French expedition expelled Ibrahim from the Morea and Russia defeated the Ottomans in 1829. The London Protocol of 1830 recognised Greek independence, and in 1832 Otto of Bavaria became king. See Ottoman Greece.
