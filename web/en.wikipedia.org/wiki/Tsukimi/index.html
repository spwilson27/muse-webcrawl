Tsukimi (Japanese: 月見, "moon-viewing"), also known as Jugoya, refers to Japanese festivals honouring the autumn moon, a version of the Mid-Autumn Festival. The celebration of the full moon typically takes place on the 15th day of the eighth month of the traditional Japanese calendar; the waxing moon is celebrated on the 13th day of the ninth month.

Customs

Tsukimi traditions include displaying decorations made from Japanese pampas grass (susuki) and eating rice dumplings called tsukimi dango in order to celebrate the beauty of the moon. Seasonal produce such as chestnuts, taro and edamame are also displayed as offerings. The custom of moon-viewing parties became popular among the aristocracy in the Heian period, influenced by the Chinese Mid-Autumn Festival, and spread to commoners in the Edo period, when it also became a harvest thanksgiving.

In popular culture

Japanese folklore associates the moon with a rabbit pounding mochi — the pattern of dark patches on the moon is seen as a rabbit rather than, as in the West, a man. Seasonal "tsukimi" dishes appear on the menus of Japanese fast-food chains each autumn, typically featuring a fried egg representing the moon.
