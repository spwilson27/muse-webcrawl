THORP, the Thermal Oxide Reprocessing Plant at Sellafield in Cumbria, England, was a facility for reprocessing irradiated oxide nuclear fuel from thermal (water-cooled) reactors, both British Advanced Gas-cooled Reactors and overseas light-water reactors. Operated by British Nuclear Fuels Ltd (BNFL) and later Sellafield Ltd, it separated uranium and plutonium from spent fuel using the PUREX solvent-extraction process.

History

Authorised after a celebrated public inquiry in 1977 (the Windscale Inquiry under Mr Justice Parker) and constructed through the 1980s, THORP began active operations in 1994. Over its lifetime it reprocessed more than 9,000 tonnes of fuel for customers in the UK, Japan, Germany and other countries, returning separated uranium and plutonium (the latter in part as MOX fuel) and vitrified high-level waste. The plant attracted sustained environmental controversy over discharges to the Irish Sea and the civil plutonium stockpile it created. A serious leak of radioactive liquor inside the plant in April 2005—undetected for months—led to a prosecution and fine of BNFL.

Closure

Reprocessing operations ended on 14 November 2018 after the completion of existing contracts; the plant then moved into a post-operational clean-out and decommissioning phase, with its receipt and storage facilities continuing to support spent-fuel management at Sellafield.
