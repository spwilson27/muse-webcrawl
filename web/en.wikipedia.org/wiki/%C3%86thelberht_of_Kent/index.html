Æthelberht of Kent

Æthelberht (c. 550 – 24 February 616) was King of Kent from about 589 until his death. The eighth-century monk Bede, in his Ecclesiastical History of the English People, lists him as the third king to hold imperium over other Anglo-Saxon kingdoms. He was the first English king to convert to Christianity.

Reign

Æthelberht's wife Bertha, a Frankish princess, was already a Christian when she arrived in Kent. In 597 he welcomed the mission of Augustine, sent by Pope Gregory the Great, and was baptised — traditionally the first Anglo-Saxon king to accept the new faith — founding Canterbury as the centre of English Christianity.

Law code

Around 602–603 he issued the earliest surviving law code written in Old English, a set of some ninety laws covering compensation and the protection of the Church. He died in 616 and was later venerated as a saint.
