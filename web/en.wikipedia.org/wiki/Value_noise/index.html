Value noise

In computer graphics, value noise is a type of noise commonly used as a procedural texture primitive. It is generated by assigning random values to points on a regular lattice (grid) and smoothly interpolating between them, producing a cloudy, blob-like random function.

Method

For a given point, the values at the surrounding lattice points are interpolated — typically with bilinear or bicubic interpolation, often smoothed with a curve such as smoothstep — to give a continuous result. Summing several "octaves" of value noise at increasing frequency and decreasing amplitude produces fractal noise resembling terrain or cloud patterns.

Comparison

Value noise is simple and fast to implement, and was used for many early procedural textures. It tends to show grid-aligned, blocky artefacts, which is why gradient noise (such as Perlin noise) and simplex noise are often preferred when higher visual quality is needed.
