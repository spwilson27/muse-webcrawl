Laser run

Laser run is a multi-sport competition combining running and laser pistol shooting, governed by World Pentathlon (formerly the UIPM). It was created as the standalone version of the combined running-and-shooting finale of the modern pentathlon, and is contested as an individual sport in its own right, including at world championships, as well as by city-level and school competitions around the world.

Format

Races combine cross-country-style running with shooting stops at a range, where athletes fire a laser pistol at electronic targets from 10 metres, needing five successful hits (or exhausting a time limit of about 50 seconds) before continuing. Adult senior races are typically run over five laps of 600 metres — a total of 3,000 metres — interrupted by four shooting series. Younger age groups race over shorter distances and fewer laps, and team and mixed relay formats exist. The laser pistols emit an eye-safe infrared beam, so events can be staged safely in city centres, parks, and stadiums.

History

When the modern pentathlon replaced its separate running and shooting phases with a combined event using laser pistols (adopted around 2009–2011), the UIPM developed the format as a separate discipline — initially called "laser-run" within its family of non-Olympic multi-sports — to widen participation. The first UIPM Laser Run World Championships were held in 2015 in Perpignan, France, and the event has since been staged in numerous cities, including as part of urban sports festivals. Within the Olympic modern pentathlon, the laser run remains the dramatic concluding event that decides the medals, with athletes starting on a handicap based on their scores in the preceding disciplines.
