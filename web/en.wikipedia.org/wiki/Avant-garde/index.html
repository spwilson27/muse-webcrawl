Avant-garde

OriginFrench military term, “advance guard”

The avant-garde (from French, “advance guard”) refers to people or works that are experimental, radical or unorthodox with respect to art, culture or society — pushing accepted boundaries and norms. The term was applied to art from the late 19th century.

Movements

Avant-garde movements include Dada, Surrealism, Futurism, Cubism, Expressionism, Constructivism, Fluxus and conceptual art, as well as avant-garde music (atonality, noise), theatre and film. Many movements issued manifestos and sought to merge art with social or political transformation.

Theory

Theorists such as Peter Bürger (Theory of the Avant-Garde, 1974) and Clement Greenberg analysed how the avant-garde challenges institutions of art, while successful innovations are often absorbed into mainstream culture and commercialised, prompting new avant-gardes.
