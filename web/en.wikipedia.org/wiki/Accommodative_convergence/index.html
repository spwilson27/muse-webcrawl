Accommodative convergence
FieldOphthalmology, optometry
Measured byAC/A ratio (prism dioptres per dioptre)
Normal AC/AApproximately 3–5 : 1

Accommodative convergence is the convergence of the eyes that occurs automatically as part of the near reflex when the crystalline lens accommodates to focus on a near object. Accommodation, convergence and constriction of the pupil together constitute the near triad, coordinated so that the near target is focused on both foveae simultaneously.

AC/A ratio

The relationship between accommodative convergence and accommodation is expressed as the AC/A ratio — the amount of convergence (in prism dioptres) induced per dioptre of accommodation. A typical value is about 4:1. The ratio can be measured by gradient methods (comparing phoria through lenses) or calculated from near and distance phorias and interpupillary distance.

Clinical significance

An abnormally high AC/A ratio causes accommodative esotropia, in which uncorrected hyperopic children over-converge when focusing, turning an eye inward; it is treated with bifocal or progressive glasses, or surgery. A low AC/A ratio is associated with convergence insufficiency and some forms of intermittent exotropia. Assessment of the AC/A ratio guides the choice of spectacle correction and strabismus surgery.
