Fort Robinson

Fort Robinson is a former United States Army fort in the Pine Ridge region of northwestern Nebraska, near the town of Crawford. Established in 1874, it played a central role in the Sioux Wars and later became a major cavalry remount station. It is now Fort Robinson State Park.

History

The post began in March 1874 as Camp Robinson, a tent camp guarding the Red Cloud Agency, and was named for Lieutenant Levi H. Robinson, killed by Lakota warriors earlier that year. It was designated a permanent fort in 1878. The Cheyenne Outbreak of 1878–79 ended here when Northern Cheyenne under Dull Knife, imprisoned in a barracks after escaping their Oklahoma reservation, broke out in January 1879; many were killed in the surrounding hills. Crazy Horse surrendered at the fort in 1877 and was killed there while resisting imprisonment in September of that year.

In the 20th century Fort Robinson became the Army's largest remount station, processing horses and mules, and trained K-9 dogs during World War II. Buffalo Soldiers of the 9th and 10th Cavalry were stationed here. The fort closed in 1948 and the site became a state park in 1962; it is a National Historic Landmark District.
