The Explorers program is a NASA exploration program that provides flight opportunities for physics, geophysics, heliophysics, and astrophysics investigations from space. Launched in 1958, it is the longest-running US spaceflight program: Explorer 1 was the first satellite launched by the United States, and its instruments discovered the Van Allen radiation belt. Over more than 90 missions, the program has included landmark observatories such as Uhuru (X-ray astronomy), the Cosmic Background Explorer (COBE), and the Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP).

History

The program began under the US Army in response to Sputnik; after NASA's creation in 1958 it became the agency's principal series of small and medium science satellites. In the 1990s NASA restructured Explorers into cost-capped competed classes — Medium-class Explorers (MIDEX), Small Explorers (SMEX), University-class Explorers (UNEX) and Missions of Opportunity — intended to fly more frequently at lower cost.

Notable missions

  • Explorer 1 (1958) — first US satellite; discovered the Van Allen belts.
  • Explorer 49 / RAE-2 (1973) — lunar-orbiting radio astronomy observatory.
  • COBE (1989) — measured the cosmic microwave background; led to a Nobel Prize.
  • SWIFT (2004) — gamma-ray burst observatory.
  • WISE / NEOWISE (2009) — infrared survey telescope.
  • TESS (2018) — exoplanet transit survey, a Medium Explorer.
