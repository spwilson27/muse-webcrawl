The Reichsrat (German: [ˈʁaɪçsˌʁaːt], "Imperial Council") was the legislature of the Austrian Empire (from 1867 the Cisleithanian part of Austria-Hungary) from 1861 to 1918. It was a bicameral body: the upper house was the House of Lords (Herrenhaus) and the lower house was the House of Deputies (Abgeordnetenhaus). The Reichsrat met in the Austrian Parliament Building on the Ringstraße in Vienna.

History

The Reichsrat was created by the February Patent of 1861. Following the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 it legislated only for Cisleithania, the Austrian half of the monarchy, while Hungary had its own Diet. The House of Deputies was initially elected by the provincial diets; direct elections were introduced in 1873, and universal male suffrage in 1907.

Parliamentary life was frequently paralysed by conflict between the monarchy's nationalities, and ministers often governed by emergency decree under Article 14. The Reichsrat was dissolved as the Habsburg monarchy collapsed at the end of the First World War in November 1918; the Provisional National Assembly of German-Austria succeeded it.
