Overview

Pol Pot (born Saloth Sâr; 1925–1998) was a Cambodian revolutionary and dictator who led the Khmer Rouge from 1963 until his death in 1998. As leader of Democratic Kampuchea (1975–79) he implemented a radical agrarian-communist programme that caused the Cambodian genocide, in which an estimated 1.5–2 million people died.

Rise, rule and fall

Educated in Phnom Penh and Paris (where he joined the French Communist Party), Saloth Sâr built the Communist Party of Kampuchea in the jungle after 1963, fighting first the Sihanouk monarchy and then Lon Nol's US-backed republic amid the spillover of the Vietnam War and US bombing. After victory in 1975 his regime ("Angkar", the Organisation) emptied cities, collectivised agriculture and purged perceived enemies relentlessly. Driven out by Vietnam in 1979, Pol Pot led guerrilla resistance from the Thai border, was sidelined by his own movement in 1997 in a show trial, and died in 1998, never facing international justice.
