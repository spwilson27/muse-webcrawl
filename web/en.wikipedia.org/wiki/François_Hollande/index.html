François Gérard Georges Nicolas Hollande (born 12 August 1954) is a French politician who served as President of France from 2012 to 2017. Previously First Secretary of the Socialist Party from 1997 to 2008, mayor of Tulle and president of the departmental council of Corrèze, he defeated incumbent Nicolas Sarkozy in the 2012 presidential election with about 51.6% of the vote in the second round.

Presidency

Hollande's presidency combined social reforms — notably the 2013 law legalising same-sex marriage — with an initially tax-raising, later supply-side economic policy (the "responsibility pact"), amid persistently high unemployment. In foreign policy he ordered the 2013 military intervention in Mali (Operation Serval) and French participation in operations against the Islamic State after the November 2015 Paris attacks, which prompted a state of emergency. His approval ratings fell to record lows for a Fifth Republic president — at one point around 4% — and in December 2016 he announced he would not seek re-election, the first incumbent of the Fifth Republic to step aside; he was succeeded by his former economy minister, Emmanuel Macron.

Personal life

Hollande's long-term partner was fellow Socialist politician Ségolène Royal, with whom he has four children; his relationship with journalist Valérie Trierweiler, and its public end in 2014, drew intense media attention. He later married actress Julie Gayet in 2022, and published bestselling memoirs of his presidency.
