Rajanya

Rajanya (Sanskrit: राजन्य, rājanya) was the name given in early Vedic literature to the royal and warrior class of ancient Indian society. The term is closely associated with the origin of the Kshatriya varna in the four-fold varna system.

The rajanya class formed the martial and ruling stratum of Vedic society.

Etymology and origin

The word rajanya is derived from the root rāj, "to rule", and is connected with rājan, "king". The term appears prominently in the Purusha Sukta hymn of the Rigveda (10.90), which describes the cosmic being Purusha being divided into the four varnas: the Brahmins from his mouth, the Rajanya (Kshatriyas) from his arms, the Vaishyas from his thighs and the Shudras from his feet.

Role in society

The rajanyas constituted the ruling aristocracy of the Vedic tribes. Their duties included warfare, protection of the community, governance and the patronage of sacrifices (yajna) performed by Brahmin priests. Over time, in later Vedic and post-Vedic texts, the term Kshatriya came to be used more commonly for this class, and rajanya survived largely as a poetic or archaic synonym.

In some later texts and inscriptions, "Rajanya" also appears as a designation for members of royal families or as a clan name, and it has been linked by some historians with later communities claiming Kshatriya descent.
