The Russian Airborne Forces (Russian: Воздушно-десантные войска России, Vozdushno-desantnye voyska, abbreviated VDV) are the airborne forces of the Russian Armed Forces. Formed from the Soviet Airborne Forces after the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991–92, they constitute a separate branch of service, functioning as the strategic reserve of the Supreme Commander-in-Chief, rather than forming part of the Ground Forces.

History

The Soviet Union was a pioneer of airborne warfare, conducting its first parachute drop experiments in 1930 and fighting large airborne operations in the Second World War. Under General Vasily Margelov (commander 1954–1979), revered by paratroopers as "Uncle Vasya", the VDV became a large, mechanised elite force equipped with air-droppable armoured vehicles such as the BMD series. Soviet airborne troops fought in Afghanistan (1979–1989).

Russian VDV units served in the Chechen wars, the 2008 Russo-Georgian War, the intervention in Syria, and peacekeeping in the Balkans — including the famous 1999 dash to Pristina airport. Paratroopers' Day, 2 August, is celebrated across Russia, traditionally with veterans gathering — and often bathing — in city fountains. The VDV played a leading role in the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, including the assault on Hostomel Airport, and suffered heavy casualties.

Organisation

The VDV has comprised airborne and air assault divisions (including the 7th Guards Mountain Air Assault, 76th Guards, 98th Guards, 104th Guards and 106th Guards formations), independent brigades, and the Ryazan Guards Higher Airborne Command School, its principal training institution.
