Dromomania

Dromomania was a historical psychiatric diagnosis describing an uncontrollable impulse or desire to wander or travel. The term comes from the Greek dromos (running, a course) and mania. The condition was described mainly in France in the late 19th and early 20th centuries and is related to the diagnosis of dissociative fugue.

SpecialtyPsychiatry (historical)
SymptomsCompulsive wandering or travelling
Related conditionsDissociative fugue, poriomania

History

Interest in pathological wandering grew after the case of Jean-Albert Dadas, a Bordeaux gas-fitter treated in the 1880s who repeatedly travelled long distances in altered states of consciousness, sometimes waking far from home with no memory of the journey. His case, written up by the medical student Philippe Tissié in 1887, became famous in French psychiatry.

Physicians debated whether the wanderers were epileptics, hysterics or degenerates. The philosopher of science Ian Hacking later used dromomania — "mad travellers" — as a case study of how transient mental illnesses appear in particular cultural settings, in his 1998 book Mad Travelers.

Modern view

Dromomania is no longer a recognised separate diagnosis. Similar behaviour is now considered under dissociative fugue (a specifier of dissociative amnesia in modern manuals), in which a person travels away from home with amnesia for their past, or under other psychiatric and neurological conditions.
