Climate change in Europe

Climate change in Europe has resulted in an increase in temperature of around 2.3 °C above pre-industrial levels, making Europe the fastest-warming continent in the world. Warming has been accompanied by more frequent and intense heatwaves, changing precipitation patterns, retreating glaciers, and sea level rise along the continent's coasts.

[Image: Temperature anomaly map of Europe during a summer heatwave]

Temperature trends

According to the European Environment Agency and the Copernicus Climate Change Service, Europe has warmed at roughly twice the global average rate since the 1980s. The Arctic portions of Europe are warming fastest of all. Recent years — including 2022, 2023 and 2024 — rank among the hottest recorded on the continent.

Impacts

Heatwaves such as those of 2003, 2019 and 2022 caused tens of thousands of excess deaths. Southern Europe faces intensifying drought and wildfire risk, while northern and central Europe have seen increased heavy precipitation and river flooding, as in the July 2021 floods in Germany and Belgium. Alpine glaciers have lost substantial volume, and Mediterranean sea level rise threatens low-lying cities and deltas. Agriculture, forestry, fisheries, and winter tourism are all affected.

Mitigation and policy

The European Union operates the Emissions Trading System (EU ETS), the world's largest carbon market, and in 2019 launched the European Green Deal, aiming for climate neutrality by 2050. The European Climate Law of 2021 made the target binding and set an intermediate goal of cutting net greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% by 2030 relative to 1990. The "Fit for 55" package and the subsequent 2040 target debate form the core of EU climate policy.
