Grógaldr

Grógaldr ("Gróa's spell" or "Gróa's chant") is a poem in Old Norse preserved in later paper manuscripts and printed editions of the Poetic Edda. Together with the following poem Fjölsvinnsmál, it forms the composite work known as Svipdagsmál.

Content

In the poem, the hero Svipdagr wakes his dead mother Gróa from her grave mound and asks for her help on the quest imposed by his stepmother: to find and woo the maiden Menglöð. Gróa responds by chanting nine spells (galdrar) to protect him on his journey — against rivers, storms, enemies, chains, cold and other dangers.

Transmission

Unlike most Eddic poems, Grógaldr is not found in the medieval Codex Regius but survives in numerous post-medieval Icelandic manuscripts. It was first printed in the seventeenth century. Scholars date its composition to the twelfth century or later, though it preserves older mythological motifs, including necromancy and protective magic.
