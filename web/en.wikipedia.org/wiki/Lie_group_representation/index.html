Representation of a Lie group

In mathematics and theoretical physics, a representation of a Lie group is a linear action of a Lie group on a vector space. Equivalently, it is a smooth homomorphism from the group into the group of invertible operators on the vector space. Representations play an important role in the study of continuous symmetry in physics: particles correspond to representations of the Poincaré group, and internal symmetries such as spin and isospin are described by representations of groups such as SU(2) and SU(3).

Definitions

A representation of a Lie group G on a vector space V is a smooth group homomorphism Φ : G → GL(V) into the general linear group of invertible linear transformations of V. A representation is unitary if V is a Hilbert space and the operators are unitary; it is irreducible if it has no nontrivial invariant subspaces.

Relation to Lie algebra representations

Differentiating a Lie group representation at the identity yields a representation of the corresponding Lie algebra. For simply connected groups this gives a one-to-one correspondence between representations of the group and of its algebra, so classification problems can often be transferred to the algebra, where they are more tractable. This underlies, for example, the classification of the finite-dimensional representations of semisimple Lie groups by highest weights (the theorem of the highest weight).

Examples

  • The circle group U(1) has one-dimensional representations indexed by integers
  • SU(2) has an irreducible representation in every dimension; the two-dimensional one models spin-½ particles
  • The Lorentz group and Poincaré group representations, classified by Wigner, correspond to particles of definite mass and spin
  • The adjoint representation, in which the group acts on its own Lie algebra by conjugation

Harmonic analysis

The study of infinite-dimensional unitary representations of non-compact groups is central to harmonic analysis. For compact groups, the Peter–Weyl theorem guarantees that every unitary representation decomposes into finite-dimensional irreducibles, generalising Fourier series.
