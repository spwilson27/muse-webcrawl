Langues d'oc

The langues d'oc are the Romance languages and dialects traditionally spoken in southern France, named after their word for "yes" — oc (from Latin hoc) — in contrast to the northern langues d'oïl, ancestral to modern French, whose word for yes was oïl (modern oui). Dante popularised the oc/oïl distinction.

The principal langue d'oc is Occitan, with dialects including Provençal, Languedocien, Gascon, Limousin, Auvergnat and Vivaro-Alpine. Occitan was the language of the medieval troubadours and a major literary language of Europe.

Use of the langues d'oc declined sharply under the centralising French state from the Ordinance of Villers-Cotterêts (1539) onward; today Occitan is classified as endangered, though revival and teaching efforts continue.
