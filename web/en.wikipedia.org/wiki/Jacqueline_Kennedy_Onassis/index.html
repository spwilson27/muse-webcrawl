Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis

Jacqueline Lee Kennedy Onassis (née Bouvier; July 28, 1929 – May 19, 1994) was an American writer, book editor, and socialite who served as First Lady of the United States from 1961 to 1963, as the wife of President John F. Kennedy. A popular first lady, she was known for her style, elegance, and devotion to preserving the White House and American cultural heritage.

Early life

Jacqueline Bouvier was born in Southampton, New York, to a wealthy family. She studied at Vassar College and George Washington University, spending a junior year in Paris, and worked as an "Inquiring Photographer" for the Washington Times-Herald. She married Senator John F. Kennedy in 1953; they had two surviving children, Caroline and John Jr.

First Lady

As first lady, she undertook a famous restoration of the White House, guided by a televised tour in 1962 that won her an Emmy, promoted historic preservation, and hosted elegant cultural events. She was beside her husband when he was assassinated in Dallas on November 22, 1963, and her poise in its aftermath — including planning the state funeral modeled on Abraham Lincoln's — drew worldwide admiration. In a post-assassination interview she framed his presidency as "Camelot," shaping its legend.

Later life

In 1968 she married Greek shipping magnate Aristotle Onassis. After his death in 1975 she returned to New York and built a career as a book editor at Viking Press and Doubleday, and campaigned to save Grand Central Terminal. She died of non-Hodgkin lymphoma in 1994 and was buried beside President Kennedy at Arlington National Cemetery.
