Dolomitization

Dolomitization is a geological process by which the calcite in limestone or other carbonate rock is converted, wholly or partly, into the mineral dolomite, CaMg(CO3)2.

Dolomitization
Type: Diagenetic process
Rock: Carbonate sedimentary rock
Product: Dolomite / dolostone
Key ion: Magnesium (Mg2+)

Process

The classic reaction replaces two moles of calcite with one mole of dolomite through the introduction of magnesium-rich fluids: 2 CaCO3 + Mg2+ → CaMg(CO3)2 + Ca2+. Because dolomite has a smaller molar volume than the calcite it replaces, secondary porosity is often created, making dolomitized rocks important hydrocarbon reservoirs.

Models

Geologists have proposed several models: the seepage-reflux model (hypersaline brines sink through lagoonal carbonates), the mixing-zone or Dorag model (fresh and seawater mix), burial dolomitization by compacting basinal fluids, and hydrothermal dolomitization along faults and fractures. Microbial mediation at low temperatures has also been demonstrated in modern environments such as Lagoa Vermelha, Brazil.

Significance

Dolomitized carbonates host major oil and gas reservoirs in the Middle East, West Texas and western Canada, and lead-zinc ores of Mississippi Valley type. The origin of massive ancient dolomites — the "dolomite problem" — remained debated for over a century because dolomite rarely precipitates directly at Earth-surface temperatures in the laboratory.
