Architecture of metropolitan Detroit
LocationDetroit, Michigan, United States
StylesArt Deco, Gothic Revival, Beaux-Arts, Modernist, International Style
Notable buildingsGuardian Building, Fisher Building, Penobscot Building, Renaissance Center, Fox Theatre
Peak period1920s (automotive boom)

The architecture of metropolitan Detroit reflects the city's rise as a center of the American automobile industry. During the economic boom of the 1920s, Detroit became one of the largest cities in the United States, and its downtown skyline filled with ornate skyscrapers in the Art Deco and Gothic Revival styles. Many of these buildings were designed by prominent architects such as Albert Kahn, Wirt C. Rowland, and Louis Kamper.

The Detroit skyline viewed from the Detroit River

Downtown skyscrapers

The Guardian Building (1928–1929), designed by Wirt C. Rowland for the Union Trust Company, is a celebrated example of Art Deco architecture with colorful Pewabic tile work and Native American and Aztec-inspired ornament. It has been called a "cathedral of finance." The Fisher Building (1928), designed by Albert Kahn and funded by the Fisher brothers of Fisher Body, features a gold-leafed tower and an arcade lined with marble and mosaics, and is often described as Detroit's largest art object. The Penobscot Building (1928) was for decades the tallest building in the city.

Theaters and landmarks

The Fox Theatre (1928), designed by C. Howard Crane, is one of the largest surviving movie palaces of the 1920s. Other notable venues include the Detroit Opera House and the Masonic Temple, the largest Masonic temple in the world. The Book Tower (1926), designed by Louis Kamper, is an Italian Renaissance-style skyscraper that underwent a major restoration in the 2010s and 2020s.

Modern era

The Renaissance Center (1977), designed by John Portman, is a group of seven interconnected skyscrapers on the riverfront and serves as the world headquarters of General Motors. The city also contains significant works by Minoru Yamasaki, including the McGregor Memorial Conference Center and buildings at Wayne State University, and mid-century modern designs at the Lafayette Park neighborhood, designed by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe.

Residential architecture

Historic neighborhoods such as Boston-Edison, Indian Village, and Palmer Woods contain large early-20th-century mansions built for automotive executives. The suburbs of Grosse Pointe, Birmingham, and Bloomfield Hills feature estates by architects including Albert Kahn, whose works include the Edsel and Eleanor Ford House in Grosse Pointe Shores.
