Egyptian–Ottoman War (1831–1833)

The Egyptian–Ottoman War of 1831–1833, also known as the First Egyptian–Ottoman War, was a military conflict between the Ottoman Empire and Egypt under Muhammad Ali Pasha. Although Egypt was nominally an Ottoman province, Muhammad Ali had built a powerful modern state and army, and sought control of Syria as a reward for his assistance to the Sultan during the Greek War of Independence.

Course of the war

In late 1831 Egyptian forces under Muhammad Ali's son Ibrahim Pasha invaded Syria, capturing Acre after a siege and defeating Ottoman armies at Homs and Beilan. Advancing into Anatolia, Ibrahim crushed the main Ottoman army at the Battle of Konya in December 1832, leaving the road to Constantinople open.

Settlement

Alarmed, Sultan Mahmud II turned to Russia for help, and Russian troops landed on the Bosphorus. The intervention of the European powers produced the Convention of Kütahya in May 1833, which granted Muhammad Ali control of Syria and the district of Adana in return for withdrawal from Anatolia. The settlement left Egypt in control of a large empire but did not resolve the underlying rivalry, which erupted again in the Second Egyptian–Ottoman War of 1839–1841.
