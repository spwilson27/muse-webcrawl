Manaku of Guler (c. 1700 – c. 1760) was an Indian painter of the Pahari school of miniature painting, active in the hill state of Guler in the western Himalayas. He was the elder son of the painter Pandit Seu and the elder brother of Nainsukh, together forming one of the most celebrated families of Indian painters.

Works

Manaku's known masterpiece is the series of illustrations to the Gita Govinda of Jayadeva, dated 1730, which display a visionary, lyrical style with saturated colour and densely patterned landscapes. He also painted an important early series illustrating the Ramayana (the "Siege of Lanka" series, c. 1725), a Rasikapriya series, and illustrations of the Bhagavata Purana. His mature work marks the transition of Guler painting toward the refined naturalism later perfected at Kangra.

Style and legacy

Compared with his younger brother Nainsukh — famous for intimate, sparse court scenes of his patron Balwant Singh of Jasrota — Manaku's art is more traditional in format yet intensely poetic, favouring dramatic night skies, stylized foliage, and emotional intensity in devotional subjects. Art historians such as B. N. Goswamy have reconstructed the family's output, establishing the father-and-sons workshop of Seu, Manaku, and Nainsukh as central to the flowering of Pahari painting in the 18th century. Works attributed to Manaku are held by major collections including the Los Angeles County Museum of Art and museums in India and Europe.
