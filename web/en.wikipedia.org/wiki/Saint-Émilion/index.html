Saint-Émilion (Gascon: Sent Milion) is a commune in the Gironde department in Nouvelle-Aquitaine in southwestern France. It is a medieval town perched on a limestone plateau above the Dordogne valley, world-famous as the centre of one of Bordeaux's great red-wine appellations. In 1999 the Jurisdiction of Saint-Émilion was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as a cultural landscape — the first wine-growing area so honoured.

History

The town takes its name from Émilion, an eighth-century Breton monk and confessor who settled in a hermitage carved into the rock here; a monastic community grew around his cell. The town flourished on the pilgrimage road to Santiago de Compostela and on the wine trade with England, to which Aquitaine belonged for three centuries — Saint-Émilion's wines were shipped down the Dordogne from Libourne and prized at the English court.

Sights

The town's most striking monument is its monolithic church, hewn entirely from the limestone rock between the 9th and 12th centuries, with a 68-metre bell tower rising above the market square. Other sights include the collegiate church, the Cordeliers cloister, the King's Tower keep, the great medieval walls, and the hermitage and catacombs beneath the monolithic church.

Wine

The Saint-Émilion appellation, on the "right bank" of the Dordogne, is dominated by Merlot and Cabernet Franc grown on limestone, clay and gravel soils. Its official classification, revised roughly every ten years, ranks estates as Premiers grands crus classés A and B and Grands crus classés; Château Cheval Blanc, Château Ausone, Château Angélus and Château Pavie are among its most celebrated properties. Macarons and the local sparkling wine are other noted products.
