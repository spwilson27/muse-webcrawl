Brennus

Brennus, leader of the Senones, at the sack of Rome in 390 BC.

Brennus (or Brennos) is the name given in classical sources to two celebrated Gallic chieftains. The earlier Brennus led the Senones, a Gallic tribe, into Italy and defeated the Romans at the Battle of the Allia (traditionally dated 18 July 390 BC), then occupied and plundered the city of Rome — the only time before AD 410 that the city fell to a foreign enemy. According to legend, when the Romans complained about the false weights used to measure their gold ransom, Brennus threw his sword onto the scales and uttered the famous words Vae victis ("woe to the vanquished").

The second Brennus

A later Brennus led a great coalition of Celts in the invasion of the Balkans in 279 BC, defeating the Macedonians before his army was destroyed at Delphi in Greece; some of his surviving followers crossed into Asia Minor and founded the kingdom of Galatia. Brennus died of his wounds during the retreat from Delphi.

Legacy

Because the name recurs, some historians have suggested that "Brennus" may have been a title rather than a personal name. The sack of Rome became a defining trauma in Roman memory and was celebrated in later French national tradition as a feat of the Gauls, regarded as ancestors of the French.
