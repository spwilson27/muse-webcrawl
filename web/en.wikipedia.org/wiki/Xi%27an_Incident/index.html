Xi'an Incident

Xi'an Incident
Date: 12–26 December 1936
Location: Xi'an, Shaanxi, China
Outcome: Second United Front against Japan

The Xi'an Incident was a political crisis in December 1936 in which the Nationalist leader Chiang Kai-shek was detained in Xi'an by his own generals Zhang Xueliang and Yang Hucheng, who demanded an end to the civil war against the Chinese Communists and united resistance against Japanese expansion.

Course of events

Chiang had arrived in Xi'an to press the campaign against the Communists. On 12 December Zhang's troops seized him at the Huaqing hot springs. Negotiations involving Zhou Enlai and the Communists, and Soong Mei-ling on Chiang's side, led to his release on 26 December after an understanding to form what became the Second United Front.

Consequences

Zhang Xueliang accompanied Chiang back to Nanjing and was placed under house arrest for decades. The incident is regarded as a turning point that redirected China from civil war toward full-scale resistance after the Marco Polo Bridge Incident of 1937. See also Second United Front.
