George Müller

George Müller
Born: 27 September 1805, Kroppenstedt, Prussia
Died: 10 March 1898 (aged 92), Bristol, England
Occupation: Evangelist, orphanage director
Known for: Founding the Ashley Down orphan houses in Bristol; the Scriptural Knowledge Institution

George Müller (born Johann Georg Ferdinand Müller; 27 September 1805 – 10 March 1898) was a Christian evangelist and the director of the Ashley Down orphanage in Bristol, England. During his lifetime he cared for more than 10,000 orphans, establishing five large orphan houses on Ashley Down. He was famous for his faith principle: he never solicited funds or went into debt, relying instead on unsolicited donations received, as he believed, in answer to prayer, and publishing annual reports of the provision he described as God's faithfulness.

Early life and conversion

Müller was born in Kroppenstedt, in the Kingdom of Prussia, and led a dissolute youth marked by theft, lying, and a spell in prison at sixteen. Studying divinity at Halle, he underwent a conversion in 1825 at a Moravian-influenced prayer meeting. He moved to England in 1829, intending missionary work among Jews, then settled as pastor of a Baptist chapel in Teignmouth, Devon, where he renounced a regular salary. In 1832 he moved to Bristol and, with Henry Craik, began preaching at Bethesda Chapel; the two became leading figures in the early Plymouth Brethren movement.

The orphan work

Disturbed by the plight of street orphans — a reality Charles Dickens also dramatised in Oliver Twist — Müller began taking orphans into his home in 1836. The work outgrew rented houses on Wilson Street, and in 1849 he opened the first purpose-built orphan house on Ashley Down; four more followed, eventually housing over 2,000 children at a time. The institutions provided food, clothing, and education, with girls trained for service or teaching and boys apprenticed. In 1834 he had also founded the Scriptural Knowledge Institution for Home and Abroad, which supported missionaries, distributed Bibles and tracts, and ran day schools.

Later years and legacy

From 1875 to 1892, Müller undertook seventeen preaching tours, travelling to the United States, India, Australia, and elsewhere and preaching to millions. He married twice; after the death of his first wife Mary (née Groves) in 1870, he married Susannah Sangar. He died in Bristol in 1898; thousands lined the streets for his funeral. The George Müller Charitable Trust continues Christian work in Bristol, and the museum at one of the former orphan buildings preserves his record. His published Narratives of "the Lord's dealings" remain classics of devotional literature.
