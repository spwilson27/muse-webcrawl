Anaïs Nin

Angela Anaïs Juana Antolina Rosa Edelmira Nin y Culmell (21 February 1903 – 14 January 1977), known as Anaïs Nin, was a French-born American diarist, essayist, novelist, and writer of short stories and erotica. Born in Neuilly-sur-Seine, France, to Cuban parents, she spent her childhood in Europe and moved to New York as a teenager.

Nin is best known for her journals, which spanned more than sixty years, beginning at age eleven, and were published in edited form from 1966. They record her relationships with many notable figures, including the writer Henry Miller and the psychoanalyst Otto Rank, and explore her inner life, psychoanalysis, and artistic development. Her fiction, including House of Incest (1936) and the collection Under a Glass Bell, is written in a surrealist, poetic prose style.

Erotica

In the 1940s Nin wrote erotic short stories for a private collector, later published posthumously as Delta of Venus (1977) and Little Birds (1979). These works contributed to her reputation as a pioneering woman writer of erotica.

Personal life

Nin married banker Hugh Parker Guiler in 1923 and, from 1947, led a bicoastal life with a second husband, Rupert Pole, in California — her marriages overlapped for years. She died of cancer in Los Angeles in 1977.
