Leon Lai

Leon Lai Ming (born 11 December 1966) is a Hong Kong actor, singer, film director, and businessman. He is one of the "Four Heavenly Kings" of Cantopop, alongside Jacky Cheung, Andy Lau, and Aaron Kwok, who dominated Hong Kong popular music in the 1990s.

Early life and career

Born in Beijing to a Hakka family, Lai moved to Hong Kong at age four and was educated partly in England. He entered the entertainment industry through TVB's New Talent Singing Awards in 1986, then acted in television dramas before launching his recording career in 1990. His early albums brought rapid stardom in Hong Kong, Taiwan, and South Korea, where he was among the first Hong Kong stars to build a major following.

Music and film

Lai was known for pioneering electronic dance music in Cantopop in the late 1990s. As an actor he appeared in acclaimed films including Fallen Angels (1995) for Wong Kar-wai, Comrades: Almost a Love Story (1996), City of Glass (1998), Infernal Affairs III (2003), and Bodyguards and Assassins (2009). He won the Golden Horse Award for Best Leading Actor for Three: Going Home (2002). In 2004 he founded the record label Amusic, and he later directed the film Wine War (2017).

Honours and charity

Lai has long supported UNICEF and polio-eradication fundraising in China and Hong Kong; his fans famously raised large sums through campaigns he led. He was awarded the Medal of Honour and later the Bronze Bauhinia Star by the Hong Kong government. In 2019 he held a record run of concerts and remains a prominent figure in Hong Kong entertainment.
