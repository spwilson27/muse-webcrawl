Pleasure pier

A pleasure pier is a pier built primarily for recreation and entertainment rather than for docking ships or cargo. Pleasure piers became a defining feature of seaside resorts in Britain during the Victorian era, and were also built in North America, continental Europe, and Australia.

History

Early piers were simple landing stages; as seaside holidays grew with the coming of the railways, piers were lengthened and elaborated into promenades over the water offering sea air without a boat trip. Eugenius Birch became the most noted designer of the type, engineering fourteen piers including those at Margate and Eastbourne using iron screw piles. Later piers added pavilions, theatres, bandstands, kiosks, and amusement rides; Blackpool's three piers and Brighton's Palace Pier are among the best-known survivors.

Decline and preservation

Fire, storm damage, wartime sectioning, and changing holiday habits destroyed many piers in the 20th century; of more than 100 built around the British coast, roughly half survive, many maintained by trusts or listed as historic structures. Comparable American examples include the Santa Monica Pier and the Galveston Island Historic Pleasure Pier.
