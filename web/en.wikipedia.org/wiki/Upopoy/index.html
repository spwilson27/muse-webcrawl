Upopoy (Ainu: ウポポイ, "singing together in a large group"), officially the Upopoy National Ainu Museum and Park, is a national centre dedicated to the history and culture of the Ainu, the indigenous people of northern Japan. It is located in the town of Shiraoi on the island of Hokkaido, on the shore of Lake Poroto, and opened on 12 July 2020 after delays caused by the COVID-19 pandemic.

Facilities

The complex comprises three main areas. The National Ainu Museum, the first national museum in Japan devoted to an indigenous people, presents Ainu history, language, and lifeways through exhibits organized around themes such as language, the universe (beliefs), lives, history, work, and community. The National Ethnic Harmony Park is an open-air space for experiencing Ainu culture, including a reconstructed kotan (village) with traditional cise houses, craft workshops, and performances of traditional dance and oral literature. A memorial facility on elevated ground provides a place for contemplation and houses remains of Ainu ancestors repatriated from universities and museums.

Background

Upopoy was conceived as a "symbolic space for ethnic harmony" under Japan's Ainu policy. Its creation followed the 2008 Diet resolution recognizing the Ainu as an indigenous people and preceded the Ainu Policy Promotion Act of 2019, the first Japanese law to explicitly recognize the Ainu as indigenous. The centre aims to promote understanding of Ainu culture, support its revitalization and transmission, and draw visitors to the region.
