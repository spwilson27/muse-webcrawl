Tribeč

Tribeč is a mountain range in western Slovakia, part of the Fatra-Tatra Area of the Inner Western Carpathians. It stretches in a broad arc between the cities of Nitra and Partizánske and the Tribeč and Hron regions, and is known for its forested ridges of granite, quartzite, and limestone, as well as for a number of castle ruins.

Geography

The range is usually divided into several subgroups, including the Zlatno, Rokoš, and High Tribeč sections. Its highest peak is Veľký Tribeč at 829 metres above sea level. Much of the range is covered by deciduous forest, principally beech and oak, and parts are protected within the Ponitrie Protected Landscape Area.

Landmarks

Ruined medieval castles such as Gýmeš Castle and the fortress at Hrušov overlook the range's valleys, and the arboretum and hunting lodge at Topoľčianky lie at its northern foot. In modern Slovak popular culture the Tribeč gained fame as the setting of reported mysterious disappearances, popularized by Jozef Karika's horror novel Trhlina ("The Crack", 2016) and its film adaptation.
