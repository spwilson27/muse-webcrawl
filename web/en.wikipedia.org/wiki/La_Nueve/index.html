La Nueve

La Nueve ("The Ninth", in Spanish) was the nickname of the 9th Company of the Régiment de marche du Tchad, part of General Philippe Leclerc's French 2nd Armoured Division during the Second World War. The company was composed almost entirely of Spanish Republican veterans of the Spanish Civil War who had fled to France and North Africa after 1939. La Nueve is famous for being the first Allied unit to enter Paris on the evening of 24 August 1944 during the Liberation of Paris.

Formation

After the fall of the Spanish Republic, tens of thousands of Republican soldiers were interned in French camps or enrolled in foreign labour companies and the French Foreign Legion. Following the Allied landings in North Africa, many joined the Free French Corps d'Afrique and then Leclerc's division. By 1943 the 9th Company numbered 146 men, of whom 144 were Spanish, commanded by the French captain Raymond Dronne. The soldiers named their American-built M3 half-tracks after battles of the Spanish Civil War — Guadalajara, Teruel, Ebro, Madrid, Brunete — and flew Republican colours alongside the division's insignia, expecting that after France they would march on to liberate Spain.

Liberation of Paris

La Nueve landed in Normandy on 1 August 1944 and fought at Écouché and in the Falaise pocket. On 24 August Leclerc ordered Dronne to take a detachment ahead into Paris in support of the Resistance uprising. Entering through the Porte d'Italie at about 21:22, three tanks and La Nueve's half-tracks, led by the lieutenant Amado Granell, reached the Hôtel de Ville, where Granell was photographed with Resistance leaders — the first Allied troops in the capital. The company went on to fight in the Vosges, Alsace and Germany, ending the war at Berchtesgaden. Its Spanish members were largely written out of French official memory for decades, and most never saw Franco's fall; public recognition came notably with Paris commemorations in 2004 and the naming of the Jardin des combattants de la Nueve in 2017.
