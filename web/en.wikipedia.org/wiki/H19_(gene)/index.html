H19 (gene)

H19 is a gene for a long noncoding RNA found in humans and other mammals. It is located on chromosome 11 (11p15.5) in humans, in a cluster with the IGF2 gene, and is one of the best-studied examples of genomic imprinting: H19 is expressed exclusively from the maternally inherited allele, while the neighbouring IGF2 gene is expressed from the paternal allele.

Function

The H19 transcript is a 2.3-kilobase noncoding RNA that is highly expressed during embryonic development and largely silenced after birth, except in skeletal muscle and some other tissues. It acts as a molecular sponge (competing endogenous RNA) for microRNAs of the let-7 family and is the precursor of miR-675, a microRNA involved in regulating placental growth.

Imprinting mechanism

Expression of H19 and IGF2 is controlled by an imprinting control region (ICR) located between them. On the maternal chromosome the unmethylated ICR binds the insulator protein CTCF, blocking downstream enhancers from activating IGF2 and allowing H19 expression. On the paternal chromosome, methylation of the ICR prevents CTCF binding, so the enhancers activate IGF2 and H19 is silenced.

Clinical significance

Loss of imprinting at the H19/IGF2 locus is associated with the overgrowth disorder Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome and with Silver–Russell syndrome, and biallelic IGF2 expression (with loss of H19) is found in Wilms tumor and several other cancers. H19 overexpression has been reported in many tumour types, where it can promote proliferation and metastasis.
