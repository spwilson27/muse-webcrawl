Lot number

Lot number

Purpose: Identification of a production batch

Used in: Manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, food safety, logistics

A lot number or batch number is an identification number assigned to a particular quantity or lot of material from a single manufacturer. Lot numbers can be found on bulk purchases of metal, pills, chemicals and many consumer products, and are essential for traceability and quality control.

Purpose

Lot numbers allow manufacturers and regulators to trace a specific group of products back through production, inspection and distribution. If a defect or contamination is discovered, a lot number lets the manufacturer identify exactly which units are affected, so that a product recall can be narrow and targeted rather than covering an entire product line.

Use in pharmaceuticals and food

Drug and food regulations in many countries require lot numbers on packaging, together with expiry dates. Vaccine vials, for instance, carry lot numbers that are recorded when doses are administered, enabling authorities to investigate adverse events by batch. Lot identification is a key element of Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP).

Format

There is no universal standard format; manufacturers design their own coding schemes, which may encode the plant, date, production line and shift. Increasingly the lot number is carried in a barcode or 2D data matrix alongside serialisation codes.
