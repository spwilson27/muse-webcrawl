Whipsnade Zoo

Whipsnade Zoo, formerly Whipsnade Wild Animal Park, is a zoo and safari park in Bedfordshire, England, near the village of Whipsnade on the Chiltern Hills. Opened in 1931, it is one of the largest zoos in the United Kingdom, covering about 600 acres, and is operated by the Zoological Society of London (ZSL), which also runs London Zoo.

History

The zoo was founded as an open-air animal park where animals could roam in large enclosures rather than cages. Its chalk hillside is marked by the Whipsnade White Lion, a large hill figure cut into the chalk in 1933. During the Second World War the zoo remained open and housed animals evacuated from London Zoo.

Animals and conservation

Whipsnade keeps more than 3,500 animals, including Asian elephants, white rhinos, Amur tigers, giraffes, lions, brown bears, and a large herd of deer that roams the downs. The zoo is active in captive breeding programmes for endangered species, including the scimitar-horned oryx and the European bison, and in field conservation work through ZSL.
