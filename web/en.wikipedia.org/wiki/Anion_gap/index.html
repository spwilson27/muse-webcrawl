Anion gap

Reference range8–16 mEq/L (without potassium)
Measured fromSerum electrolytes
Used inDifferential diagnosis of metabolic acidosis

The anion gap (AG or AGap) is a value calculated from the results of an electrolyte blood panel that is used to help identify the cause of metabolic acidosis. It is the difference between the measured cations and the measured anions in serum, plasma or urine, and reflects the concentration of unmeasured anions.

Calculation

The anion gap is conventionally calculated as:

AG = ([Na+] + [K+]) − ([Cl−] + [HCO3−])

Because potassium concentrations are relatively small and stable, they are often omitted, giving AG = [Na+] − ([Cl−] + [HCO3−]). A typical reference range is roughly 8 to 16 mEq/L, though it varies with the laboratory method.

Clinical significance

Metabolic acidosis is commonly divided into high-anion-gap and normal-anion-gap (hyperchloremic) types. A high anion gap suggests accumulation of unmeasured anions, as in diabetic ketoacidosis, lactic acidosis, renal failure, or poisonings such as methanol, ethylene glycol and salicylates — causes recalled by mnemonics such as MUDPILES and GOLD MARK. The gap should be corrected for the serum albumin level, since albumin is a major contributor to the normal gap.
