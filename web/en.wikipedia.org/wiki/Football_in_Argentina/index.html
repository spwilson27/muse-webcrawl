Football in Argentina

Governing body: Argentine Football Association (AFA)
National team: Argentina
Top division: Primera División
FIFA World Cup titles: 3 (1978, 1986, 2022)

Football in Argentina is the most popular sport in the country. Introduced by British immigrants in the 19th century, the game took deep root among the local population and became a central part of Argentine culture and identity.

History

The first football match in Argentina was played in 1867 in Buenos Aires, organised by British railway workers. The Argentine Football Association was founded in 1893, making it one of the oldest national associations in the world. The league championship began the same year, and Argentina's Primera División is among the oldest football leagues still in existence.

Club football

The two most successful and popular clubs are Boca Juniors and River Plate, both based in Buenos Aires; their rivalry, the Superclásico, is one of the most intense in world football. Other historically important clubs include Independiente, Racing Club and San Lorenzo — together with Boca and River, the "big five". Argentine clubs have won the Copa Libertadores more times than clubs from any other country, with Independiente holding the record of seven titles.

National team

The Argentina national football team has won the FIFA World Cup three times, in 1978, 1986 and 2022, and has produced some of the greatest players in history, including Diego Maradona and Lionel Messi. Argentina has also won the Copa América a record number of times.

Culture

Football permeates Argentine daily life; neighbourhood clubs, street football (potrero) and the passionate terrace culture of the hinchadas are integral to the sport's character. The country's distinctive style of play, emphasising skill and improvisation, is known as criollo football.
