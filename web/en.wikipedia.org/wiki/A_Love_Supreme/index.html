A Love Supreme

A Love Supreme is an album by the American jazz saxophonist John Coltrane. Recorded on 9 December 1964 at Van Gelder Studio in Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey, and released in January 1965 by Impulse! Records, it is a four-part suite — "Acknowledgement", "Resolution", "Pursuance" and "Psalm" — conceived as a spiritual offering of gratitude to God following Coltrane's recovery from drug addiction in 1957.

Background

Coltrane composed the suite at his home in Dix Hills, Long Island, after a period of seclusion. The recording features his "classic quartet": McCoy Tyner on piano, Jimmy Garrison on bass and Elvin Jones on drums. The final movement, "Psalm", is a musical recitation of a poem Coltrane wrote and printed in the liner notes, phrased syllable by syllable on the saxophone. The recurring four-note bass motif of "Acknowledgement" corresponds to the phrase "a love supreme", which the quartet chants near the end of the track.

Reception and legacy

The album sold unusually well for a jazz record and is widely regarded as one of the greatest albums ever made. It was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and selected for the National Recording Registry. Rolling Stone placed it among the top albums of all time, and it has influenced musicians across genres. The suite's spirituality, modal improvisation and intensity marked a turning point toward the freer, more exploratory direction of Coltrane's final years. In 2021, A Love Supreme: Live in Seattle, a rare live recording of the suite from 1965, was released to wide acclaim.
