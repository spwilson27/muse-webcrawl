Ceremony

[Image: A graduation ceremony, a secular rite of passage]

A ceremony (UK: /ˈsɛrɪməni/, US: /ˈsɛrəˌmoʊni/) is a unified ritualistic event with a purpose, usually consisting of a number of artistic components, performed on a special occasion. Ceremonies mark significant transitions, celebrations, or commemorations, and may be religious or secular. The word derives from Latin caerimonia, denoting religious awe or ritual observance.

Types

Ceremonies accompany the major milestones of life — so-called rites of passage — including births and naming, coming of age, marriages, and funerals. Religious ceremonies include worship services, sacraments, festivals and initiations. Civic and state ceremonies include inaugurations, coronations, state openings of parliament, remembrance services and naturalisation ceremonies. Institutions hold graduation ceremonies, award ceremonies and opening ceremonies, such as those of the Olympic Games. Ceremonies may also be celebratory, such as those marking anniversaries, harvests, and the turning of the year.

Elements and function

Typical components include processions, formal dress or vestments, music, speeches, oaths and pledges, symbolic objects and gestures, and prescribed ritual language. Anthropologists note that ceremonies create social cohesion, publicly affirm shared values, legitimise authority, and mark changes of status for individuals and communities. Ceremonial roles are often filled by designated figures such as clergy, officials, or a master of ceremonies.
