Perpetual calendar

A perpetual calendar is a calendar valid for many years, usually designed to allow the day of the week for any given date to be determined. In horology, the term denotes a watch complication that automatically displays the correct day, date, and month, accounting for months of different lengths and for leap years.

Calendar tables

Printed perpetual calendars use the fact that the Gregorian calendar repeats every 400 years, and that within a century the pattern of weekdays repeats in a 28-year cycle except across non-leap century years. Tables of dominical letters and year codes let a reader find any date's weekday without a fresh calendar.

Watches

A mechanical perpetual calendar watch, such as those made by Patek Philippe and other high-end makers, uses a programmed gear train — a mechanical memory of the calendar cycle — that needs no correction until the next skipped leap year, in 2100. Simpler annual calendars require adjustment once a year, at the end of February.
