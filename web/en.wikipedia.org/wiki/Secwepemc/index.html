The Secwépemc (pronounced roughly "shuh-WHEP-muh"), historically known in English as the Shuswap, are an Indigenous people of the Interior Plateau of south-central British Columbia, Canada. Their traditional territory, Secwepemcúl̓ecw, extends across the basins of the Fraser, Thompson, and Columbia rivers — one of the largest Indigenous territories in the province. The Secwepemc Nation is made up of 17 communities organised in regional tribal councils, including the Shuswap Nation Tribal Council and the Northern Shuswap Tribal Council.

Culture and language

Secwepemc society was traditionally organised around extended family groups that moved seasonally to fish (especially for sockeye salmon), hunt, and gather roots and berries, wintering in semi-subterranean pit houses (c7ístkten). Their language, Secwepemctsín, belongs to the Interior Salish branch of the Salishan family and has eastern and western dialects; like many Indigenous languages it is endangered, and community programmes, immersion nests, and university courses support its revitalisation.

History

Fur traders arrived in the early 19th century, followed by the Fraser Canyon Gold Rush of 1858 and settler colonisation, which brought disease, reserve allotments, and the residential school system — including the Kamloops Indian Residential School, on whose grounds the Tk'emlúps te Secwépemc announced in 2021 the detection of potential unmarked graves, an announcement that drew worldwide attention. The Secwepemc never ceded their territory by treaty and continue to assert Aboriginal title, pursuing land claims and self-government through courts, negotiation, and direct action, including opposition to pipeline expansion through their lands.
