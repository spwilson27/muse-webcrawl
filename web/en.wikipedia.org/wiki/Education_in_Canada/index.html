Education in Canada

Education in Canada is the responsibility of the provinces and territories; there is no federal department of education and no national curriculum. Each province maintains its own system of public elementary and secondary schools, generally free and compulsory from about age six until 16 to 18 depending on the jurisdiction. Canada consistently ranks among the highest-performing countries in international assessments such as PISA.

Structure

Schooling is typically divided into elementary (primary), middle or junior high, and secondary (high school) stages, ending with a high school diploma. Public education is delivered in English and French, and most provinces fund separate public and Catholic school boards; private and independent schools educate a minority of students. School boards administer schools at the local level under provincial ministries.

Post-secondary education

Canada has a large network of publicly funded universities and colleges, including internationally prominent institutions such as the University of Toronto, McGill University and the University of British Columbia. Community colleges and polytechnics offer diplomas and applied degrees, while CEGEPs in Quebec bridge secondary and university study. Canada is also a major destination for international students.
