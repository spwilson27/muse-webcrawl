José María Arizmendiarrieta Madariaga (22 April 1915 – 29 November 1976) was a Spanish Catholic priest and the founder of the Mondragon Corporation, the world's largest federation of worker cooperatives, based in the Basque Country of Spain.

Early life

Arizmendiarrieta was born in Markina-Xemein in Biscay. He lost an eye in childhood. As a seminarian he supported the Basque nationalist cause during the Spanish Civil War and was briefly imprisoned; he was ordained a priest in 1940 and in 1941 was assigned as assistant parish priest to the town of Mondragón (Arrasate) in Gipuzkoa.

Mondragon cooperatives

Finding a town scarred by unemployment and division after the civil war, Arizmendiarrieta began by organizing youth, cultural and sporting initiatives, then in 1943 founded a technical school (now Mondragon University) to train workers in skills and cooperative values. In 1956, five of his former students founded ULGOR, a small workshop making paraffin stoves — the first industrial cooperative of what became the Mondragon group. A cooperative bank, the Caja Laboral (1959), followed, providing finance and advice to new cooperatives.

Arizmendiarrieta framed the enterprise in Catholic social teaching and Basque traditions of communal labor: workers were members and owners, managers were elected, and wage differentials were kept narrow. He declined executive roles himself, acting as teacher and moral guide. By the time of his death in 1976 the Mondragon cooperatives employed thousands; the federation has since grown into a major industrial and retail group while retaining its cooperative structure.

Legacy

Arizmendiarrieta is widely studied as a pioneer of the social economy and worker ownership. The Roman Catholic Church has recognized his heroic virtues in the process toward possible beatification.
