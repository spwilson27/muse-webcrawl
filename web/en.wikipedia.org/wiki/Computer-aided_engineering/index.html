Computer-aided engineering

Computer-aided engineering (CAE) is the general usage of technology in engineering — the discipline of using computer software to assist in engineering analysis, simulation, and design tasks. It is used in many fields, including automotive, aerospace, shipbuilding, and electronics. CAE is a complement to computer-aided design (CAD) and computer-aided manufacturing (CAM) in the overall product lifecycle management (PLM) process.

Areas of CAE

CAE areas include:

  • Stress and structural analysis on components and assemblies using finite element analysis (FEA).
  • Thermal and fluid analysis using computational fluid dynamics (CFD).
  • Multibody dynamics (MBD) and kinematics of mechanisms.
  • Analysis tools for process simulation in manufacturing operations such as casting, moulding, and die pressing.
  • Optimization of products and processes, increasingly including topology optimization and design exploration.

The CAE workflow

A typical CAE task follows three stages: pre-processing (building a model of the geometry, meshing the domain, and specifying material properties, loads, and boundary conditions), solving (the numerical computation itself), and post-processing (visualization and interpretation of results). Major commercial CAE suites include ANSYS, Abaqus (SIMULIA), Nastran, Altair HyperWorks, and COMSOL Multiphysics; open-source tools include OpenFOAM for CFD and CalculiX for FEA.

Role in product development

By moving testing and evaluation into simulation, CAE reduces the number of physical prototypes, shortens development cycles, and lets engineers explore more design variants. CAE is a component of the broader trend toward digital prototyping and digital twins, in which a virtual model mirrors a physical asset through its lifecycle. Engineers use CAE simulations to predict performance, improve product reliability, and investigate failures without the expense of building and destroying hardware.
