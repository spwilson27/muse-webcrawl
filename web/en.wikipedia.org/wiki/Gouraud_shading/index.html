Gouraud shading

[Image: Gouraud shading]

Gouraud shading, named after Henri Gouraud, is an interpolation method used in computer graphics to produce continuous shading of surfaces represented by polygon meshes. Gouraud first published the technique in 1971.

Method

A surface normal is computed at each vertex of a polygonal model, and a lighting calculation gives the colour at each vertex. Colours are then interpolated across the interior of each polygon, producing a smooth appearance on low-polygon models.

Comparison

Gouraud shading is cheaper than per-pixel (Phong) shading but can miss specular highlights that fall inside a polygon, and Mach bands can appear at polygon boundaries. It was widely used in early 3D games and hardware, including the original PlayStation, before per-pixel lighting became standard.
