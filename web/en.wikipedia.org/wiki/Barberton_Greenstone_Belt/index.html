Barberton Greenstone Belt

The Barberton Greenstone Belt, also known as the Makhonjwa Mountains, is a range of hills in Mpumalanga province, South Africa, near the town of Barberton and the border with Eswatini. Its rocks, part of the Kaapvaal Craton, are between about 3.2 and 3.6 billion years old, making the belt one of the best-preserved successions of early Archaean crust on Earth and a key natural laboratory for studying the young planet.

Geology

The belt consists of volcanic and sedimentary sequences — the Onverwacht, Fig Tree and Moodies groups — including komatiites (ultramafic lavas named after the Komati River here), pillow basalts recording eruption under water, cherts and some of the oldest known surface sedimentary rocks. The rocks preserve evidence of early life, including microfossils and stromatolite-like structures, traces of meteorite impacts in the form of spherule beds, and the earliest evidence for plate-tectonic-style processes. Gold was discovered in the belt in the 1880s, sparking a gold rush and the founding of Barberton; mines such as Sheba and Fairview have operated for more than a century.

Conservation

In 2018 the Barberton Makhonjwa Mountains were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, covering some 113,000 hectares and recognised for containing the best-preserved record of volcanic and sedimentary rocks dating back 3.25 to 3.6 billion years.
