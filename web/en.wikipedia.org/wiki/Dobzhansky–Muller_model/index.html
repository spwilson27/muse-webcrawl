Dobzhansky–Muller model

Dobzhansky–Muller model
FieldEvolutionary biology, speciation genetics
Proposed byTheodosius Dobzhansky (1937); Hermann Joseph Muller (1942)

The Dobzhansky–Muller model, also known as the Bateson–Dobzhansky–Muller model, explains how reproductive isolation can evolve between two populations without either population passing through a low-fitness intermediate genotype. It is a foundational model of genetic incompatibilities in speciation.

The model

An ancestral population with genotype aabb splits into two isolated populations. In the first, a new allele A arises and spreads, giving AAbb; in the second, a new allele B spreads, giving aaBB. Both populations remain fit because each new allele has only ever been tested by natural selection on its own population's genetic background. However, when the populations meet and hybridise, the hybrid genotype AaBb combines A and B for the first time. If these alleles interact negatively (negative epistasis), hybrids suffer reduced viability or fertility — a Dobzhansky–Muller incompatibility — creating a postzygotic barrier to gene flow.

Empirical support

Incompatibilities matching the model have been identified in Drosophila (for example the genes Hmr, Lhr, OdsH and Nup96), in plants, yeasts and other taxa. Studies show incompatibilities accumulate faster than linearly with divergence time — the "snowball effect" — because the number of possible pairwise incompatible combinations grows with the square of the number of substitutions.
