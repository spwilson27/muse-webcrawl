Rohan is a fictional kingdom in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. It is the realm of the Rohirrim, a people of Northmen famed as horsemen, lying on the great grassland plains between the Misty Mountains, Fangorn Forest and the White Mountains, north of its ally Gondor. Rohan plays a central role in The Two Towers and The Return of the King.

Description

Rohan — called the Riddermark ("the Mark") by its people — is a land of wide grass plains suited to horse-breeding. Its capital is Edoras, built on a hill below the White Mountains and dominated by Meduseld, the golden-roofed hall of the kings. Other strongholds include the fortress of Helm's Deep in the Hornburg valley and Aldburg, the original capital. The Rohirrim live in a society Tolkien modelled on the Anglo-Saxons of his academic studies: their names, language (represented in the books as Old English) and heroic poetry — laments, boasts and battle-verses — all echo works such as Beowulf.

History

The Rohirrim descend from the Éothéod, Northmen of the vales of Anduin. In the Third Age 2510 their lord Eorl the Young rode south to save Gondor at the Battle of the Field of Celebrant, and was rewarded by Steward Cirion with the province of Calenardhon — the Oath of Eorl sealing the perpetual alliance of Rohan and Gondor. Kings of Rohan include Helm Hammerhand, who gave his name to Helm's Deep, and Théoden, who, freed from Saruman's influence by Gandalf, led his riders to victory at the Hornburg and fell at the Battle of the Pelennor Fields, where his niece Éowyn slew the Witch-king. He was succeeded by his nephew Éomer.

In adaptations

In Peter Jackson's film trilogy, Rohan's scenes were filmed mainly around Mount Sunday in New Zealand, and the charge of the Rohirrim at the Pelennor Fields is among the films' most celebrated sequences. The animated film The Lord of the Rings: The War of the Rohirrim (2024) tells the story of Helm Hammerhand.
