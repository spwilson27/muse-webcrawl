History of McDonald's

The history of McDonald's began in 1940 with a restaurant opened by Richard and Maurice McDonald in San Bernardino, California. Their introduction of the "Speedee Service System" in 1948 established the principles of the modern fast-food restaurant. Ray Kroc joined the company as a franchise agent in 1955, bought out the McDonald brothers in 1961, and drove its worldwide expansion.

Early years

The McDonald brothers streamlined their restaurant around a limited menu of hamburgers, fries, and drinks, prepared on an assembly-line model. Kroc, a Multimixer milkshake-machine salesman, was impressed by the volume of business and opened his own restaurant in Des Plaines, Illinois, in 1955.

Expansion

Under Kroc, McDonald's grew through franchising, standardisation, and real-estate control. It went public in 1965, opened its first international restaurants in Canada and Puerto Rico in 1967, and introduced signature items such as the Big Mac (1968), Egg McMuffin (1972), and Happy Meal (1979). By the late twentieth century it had become the world's largest restaurant chain, operating in more than 100 countries; later decades brought menu diversification, redesigns, and responses to criticism over health, labour, and the environment.
