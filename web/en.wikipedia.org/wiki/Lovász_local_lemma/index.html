Lovász local lemma

Lovász local lemma

Field: Probability theory, combinatorics

Proved by: Paul Erdős and László Lovász (1975)

Use: Showing existence of objects avoiding "bad events"

In probability theory, if a large number of events are all independent of one another and each has probability less than 1, then there is a probability greater than 0 that none of the events will occur. The Lovász local lemma allows a slight relaxation of the independence condition: events may be dependent, but only on a bounded number of others, and still show that there exists a probability greater than 0 that none of the events occur.

Statement

Let A1, …, An be "bad" events, each with probability at most p, and suppose each event is independent of all but at most d of the others. If e·p·(d + 1) ≤ 1 (in the symmetric version), then the probability that none of the events occurs is positive. The lemma was proved by Paul Erdős and László Lovász in 1975.

Applications

The local lemma is one of the principal tools of the probabilistic method in combinatorics. It has been used to prove the existence of graph colourings, satisfiable Boolean formulas (such as k-SAT instances where each clause shares variables with few others), Latin transversals and packings, where the union bound alone is too weak.

Algorithmic versions

For decades the lemma was purely existential. In 2009–2010 Robin Moser, and Moser with Gábor Tardos, gave a simple resampling algorithm that finds the desired outcome efficiently under the local lemma's conditions — the Moser–Tardos algorithm — making the lemma constructive in many settings.
