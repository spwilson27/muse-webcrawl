Cuyahoga County, Ohio

Downtown Cleveland, the county seat of Cuyahoga County.

Cuyahoga County is a large urban county in the northeastern part of the U.S. state of Ohio. It is the second-most populous county in the state, with a population of roughly 1.26 million. The county seat and largest city is Cleveland, on the southern shore of Lake Erie. The county takes its name from the Cuyahoga River, whose name derives from a Native American word often interpreted as "crooked river".

Geography

Cuyahoga County covers about 1,246 square miles, of which roughly 457 square miles are land and the rest Lake Erie water. The Cuyahoga River divides Cleveland into east and west sides and flows through the Cuyahoga Valley, part of which is protected as Cuyahoga Valley National Park in the county's southeast. The county is entirely urbanised or suburban, comprising Cleveland and 58 other municipalities.

History

The county was created in 1807 from Geauga County and organised in 1810, when Cleveland — then a small village — became the seat. The completion of the Ohio and Erie Canal and later the railroads made Cleveland a major manufacturing and shipping centre, and the county grew rapidly with steel, oil refining (John D. Rockefeller's Standard Oil began in Cleveland), and automotive industries. Population peaked at over 1.7 million in 1970 before suburbanisation and industrial decline reduced it.

In 2011, following a corruption scandal, the county replaced its three-commissioner government with a charter government headed by an elected county executive and an eleven-member county council.

Economy and culture

Major institutions include the Cleveland Clinic, University Hospitals, Case Western Reserve University, the Cleveland Orchestra, the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, and three major-league sports teams — the Guardians (baseball), Browns (football), and Cavaliers (basketball). The county is served by Cleveland Hopkins International Airport and the Greater Cleveland Regional Transit Authority.
