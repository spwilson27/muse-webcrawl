Fancy Bear is a Russian cyber espionage group, believed to be associated with the Russian military intelligence agency, the GRU. The cybersecurity firm CrowdStrike, which coined the name "Fancy Bear" (following its convention of naming Russian state actors after bears), identified it as GRU Unit 26165. The group is also designated APT28, and is known as the Sofacy Group, Pawn Storm, Sednit, and by Microsoft's names STRONTIUM and later Forest Blizzard.

Activities

Fancy Bear has operated since at least the mid-2000s, targeting governments, militaries, defence contractors, media organisations, and political parties in the United States, Europe, Ukraine, and elsewhere. Its methods include spear-phishing, credential harvesting with look-alike domains, watering-hole sites, and custom malware such as the X-Agent implant, Sofacy backdoor, and Zebrocy. The group has exploited zero-day vulnerabilities in Windows, Flash, and Java.

Notable operations

The group gained worldwide attention for the 2015–2016 breaches of the Democratic National Committee and the Clinton campaign during the US presidential election, after which stolen emails were leaked through outlets including "Guccifer 2.0" and DCLeaks; in July 2018 a US grand jury indicted 12 GRU officers in connection with the operation. Fancy Bear was also linked to the 2015 hack of the German Bundestag, attacks on the World Anti-Doping Agency and the International Olympic Committee following doping sanctions against Russia, the 2016 breach of the Dutch Safety Board, and extensive operations against Ukraine, including during the full-scale invasion launched in 2022.
