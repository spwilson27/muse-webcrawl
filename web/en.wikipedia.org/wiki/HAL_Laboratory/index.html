HAL Laboratory

HAL Laboratory, Inc., formerly also known as HALKEN, is a Japanese video game developer based in Tokyo. It was founded on 21 February 1980 by Mitsuhiro Ikeda. The company is closely affiliated with Nintendo and is best known for creating the Kirby and Mother (EarthBound) series, as well as the first two Super Smash Bros. games. Its name is said to derive from the HAL 9000 computer in 2001: A Space Odyssey, chosen, in the words often attributed to the founders, because each letter is one step ahead of IBM.

History

HAL Laboratory began by producing games for early Japanese home computers such as the MSX, VIC-20 and Commodore MAX Machine. In the early 1990s the company faced bankruptcy after costly development of a building and unsuccessful projects. Nintendo rescued it with an investment in exchange for developing games exclusively for Nintendo platforms; Satoru Iwata, a HAL programmer since the company's early days, became president in 1993 and later president of Nintendo itself in 2002.

Notable games

The company's breakthrough came with Kirby's Dream Land (1992) for the Game Boy, created by the young designer Masahiro Sakurai and introducing the pink, inflatable hero Kirby. The Mother series, written by Shigesato Itoi, produced EarthBound (1994) and Mother 3 (2006). HAL also developed Super Smash Bros. (1999) and Super Smash Bros. Melee (2001), the Pokémon Snap and Pokémon Ranger spin-offs, and the box-boy puzzle series BoxBoy!. Its logo depicts the mythical dog Inukshuk — a creature brooding spotted eggs, symbolising creativity.
