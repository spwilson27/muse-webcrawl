Cherokee Phoenix

The Cherokee Phoenix (Cherokee: ᏣᎳᎩ ᏧᎦᏔᏅᏍᏗ, Tsalagi Tsulehisanvhi) was the first newspaper published by a Native American tribe and the first published in a Native American language. It was first printed on 21 February 1828 at New Echota, capital of the Cherokee Nation (in present-day Georgia), using the Cherokee syllabary devised by Sequoyah.

History

The Cherokee National Council authorised the paper in 1827 and bought a press and type cast with Sequoyah's syllabary characters. The first editor, Elias Boudinot, published the paper bilingually, in Cherokee and English, covering Cherokee laws and customs, national affairs, and arguments against removal. The Phoenix became a central voice opposing Georgia's campaign to seize Cherokee land, but publication faltered as the removal crisis deepened; the Georgia Guard seized the press in 1835, and Cherokee removal on the Trail of Tears followed in 1838–39.

Revival

The Cherokee Nation revived the title in the 20th century; today the Cherokee Phoenix is published in Tahlequah, Oklahoma, as the official news outlet of the Cherokee Nation, in print and online.
