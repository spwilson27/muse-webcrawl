UNGG reactor

The UNGG (Uranium Naturel Graphite Gaz) reactor is a type of gas-cooled nuclear reactor developed in France during the 1950s and 1960s. It used natural uranium metal fuel, a graphite moderator, and carbon dioxide coolant, a combination that allowed France to build reactors without uranium enrichment facilities.

Design

In a UNGG reactor, fuel elements of natural uranium metal clad in a magnesium alloy (Magnox-type cladding) sit in channels bored through a graphite moderator core. Carbon dioxide gas, pressurized in later designs, carries heat to steam generators. Refuelling could be performed on-load in some designs. The dual-purpose Marcoule reactors G1, G2 and G3 also produced plutonium.

Reactors built

UNGG units operated at Marcoule, Chinon, Saint-Laurent and Bugey in France, and one, Vandellòs-I, in Spain. The type was superseded in France by pressurized water reactors licensed from Westinghouse, and the last French UNGG, Bugey-1, shut down in 1994. Vandellòs-I closed in 1989 after a turbine fire.
