Najran (Arabic: نجران‎) is a city in southwestern Saudi Arabia near the border with Yemen. It is the capital of Najran Province. The city lies in the Wadi Najran valley at the edge of the Empty Quarter (Rub' al Khali) and has a history reaching back more than 4,000 years as an oasis on the incense trade routes of South Arabia. Historically it was an important centre of Christianity in pre-Islamic Arabia; today its population is predominantly Muslim, including a significant Ismaili Shia community, and its older quarters were built in distinctive mud-brick tower-house style.

History

Ancient and pre-Islamic period

Najran was a flourishing oasis under the South Arabian kingdoms and lay on the frankincense road from Yemen to the Mediterranean. In late antiquity it became the principal Christian centre of Arabia, associated with the Church of the East and Miaphysite missions. In about 523 CE the Himyarite king Dhu Nuwas (Yusuf As'ar Yath'ar), a convert to Judaism, besieged the city and massacred its Christians who refused to renounce their faith — an event recorded in Syriac sources such as the Book of the Himyarites and the letters of Simeon of Beth Arsham, commemorated in the Qur'an's reference to the "People of the Ditch" (Aṣḥāb al-Ukhdūd, Sura 85), and a trigger for the Ethiopian invasion of Yemen under Kaleb of Axum.

Islamic era

In 631 CE a delegation of Najran's Christians negotiated a pact with the Prophet Muhammad in Medina, under which the community kept its religion and property in return for tribute — an episode famous in Islamic tradition as the occasion of the proposed mubāhala (Qur'an 3:61). Under the caliph Umar many of Najran's Christians were relocated to Iraq, where they founded al-Najrāniyya near Kufa. The city remained a regional trading and agricultural centre, and for centuries was contested between Yemen and the emerging Saudi state; it was incorporated into Saudi Arabia following the 1934 Saudi–Yemeni war and the Treaty of Taif.

Modern period

Modern Najran is an administrative and agricultural centre known for citrus fruit and the Wadi Najran Dam, one of the kingdom's largest. Its Ismaili community, the largest in Saudi Arabia, has been the subject of tensions with the state, including clashes in 2000 and longstanding complaints of discrimination. Since 2015, the Yemeni civil war has periodically spilled across the border, with shelling and missile fire reaching the city.

Geography and climate

Najran sits at about 1,290 m elevation in a valley enclosed by mountains, with a hot desert climate moderated by altitude: summer days are very hot, nights cool, and winters mild. The wadi's seasonal floods historically supported terraced agriculture, and the surrounding region contains archaeological sites including the ruined city of al-Ukhdud, just south of the modern city, and rock inscriptions along ancient caravan routes.

Demographics

The population is overwhelmingly Arab and Muslim. Najran Province is home to the Yam tribe and to the majority of Saudi Arabia's Ismaili Shia citizens, concentrated in the city; there is also a Sunni population that has grown with migration from other regions. The ancient Christian community disappeared in the early Islamic centuries.
