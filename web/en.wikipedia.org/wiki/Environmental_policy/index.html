Environmental policy

Environmental policy is the commitment of an organisation or government to laws, regulations and other policy mechanisms concerning environmental issues such as air and water pollution, waste management, ecosystem protection, biodiversity and climate change. Such policies aim to correct market failures — resources and harms that markets alone do not price — and to reconcile economic activity with ecological limits.

Instruments

Policy tools fall broadly into three groups. Command-and-control regulation sets standards or bans (for example emission limits or protected species lists). Market-based instruments use prices and trading: pollution taxes, carbon pricing and cap-and-trade systems such as the EU Emissions Trading System, subsidies for clean technology, and payments for ecosystem services. Information and voluntary approaches include environmental impact assessment, eco-labelling, disclosure requirements and negotiated agreements with industry.

Levels of governance

Environmental problems cross borders, so policy operates at every scale: local planning rules, national statutes such as the U.S. Clean Air Act, regional regimes such as European Union directives, and international treaties including the Montreal Protocol, the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement on climate change.

Principles

Widely invoked principles include the precautionary principle, the polluter pays principle, prevention at source, sustainable development, and public participation — the last reflected in instruments such as the Aarhus Convention. Policy design must also weigh effectiveness, cost, enforcement capacity and distributive fairness, including the concept of a just transition for affected workers and communities.
