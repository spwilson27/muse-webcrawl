Ancient Egyptian deities

Ancient Egyptian deities are the gods and goddesses worshipped in ancient Egypt. The beliefs and rituals surrounding these gods formed the core of ancient Egyptian religion, which emerged alongside the civilisation itself and endured for more than three thousand years. The Egyptians worshipped a vast and fluid pantheon — more than 1,500 deities are known by name — many of them tied to particular places, natural phenomena, or cosmic functions.

Characteristics

Egyptian gods were not fixed personalities in the manner of Greek mythology; a deity could merge with others in syncretic combinations such as Amun-Ra, acquire new attributes over time, or be worshipped under quite different forms in different cities. They were commonly depicted with human bodies and animal heads — Horus with a falcon's head, Anubis with a jackal's, Bastet with a cat's — or as humans wearing distinctive crowns and regalia. The gods were believed to inhabit their cult statues in temples, where priests performed daily rituals of feeding, washing, and clothing them.

Major deities

  • Ra — the sun god, creator, and king of the gods, who travelled across the sky in his solar barque
  • Amun — "the hidden one", chief god of Thebes, later fused with Ra as Amun-Ra
  • Osiris — god of the afterlife, death, and resurrection, murdered and dismembered by his brother Set
  • Isis — goddess of magic and motherhood, wife of Osiris and mother of Horus
  • Horus — falcon-headed sky god, identified with the living pharaoh
  • Set — god of chaos, storms, and the desert
  • Ptah — creator god of Memphis, patron of craftsmen
  • Hathor — goddess of love, music, and joy, often shown with cow's horns
  • Thoth — ibis-headed god of wisdom and writing
  • Anubis — jackal-headed god of mummification
  • Ma'at — goddess personifying truth, justice, and cosmic order

Cosmologies

Different priesthoods developed competing creation stories. At Heliopolis the Ennead of Atum and his descendants prevailed; at Memphis, Ptah created by speaking the world into being; at Hermopolis the Ogdoad of eight primordial deities embodied the chaos before creation; and at Thebes Amun was the hidden creator. These systems coexisted and were combined rather than treated as exclusive.
