Reproductive health

Reproductive health, as defined by the World Health Organization and the 1994 International Conference on Population and Development (ICPD), is "a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being ... in all matters relating to the reproductive system and to its functions and processes", rather than merely the absence of disease. It includes sexual health, family planning, maternal and newborn care, and the prevention and treatment of sexually transmitted infections and reproductive cancers.

Components

  • Family planning and contraception: access to information and methods to decide the number and spacing of children.
  • Maternal health: antenatal care, skilled attendance at birth, emergency obstetric care and postnatal care; about 287,000 women died from pregnancy-related causes in 2020, mostly from preventable causes in low-income settings.
  • Sexually transmitted infections: prevention, diagnosis and treatment, including HIV.
  • Safe abortion and post-abortion care, where legal.
  • Prevention of harmful practices, such as female genital mutilation and child marriage, and screening for reproductive cancers.

Reproductive rights

The ICPD Programme of Action recognised reproductive rights — including the right to decide freely the number and spacing of children and to attain the highest standard of sexual and reproductive health — as part of established human rights. Access remains uneven globally: unmet need for contraception affects well over 150 million women, and legal restrictions vary widely by country.
