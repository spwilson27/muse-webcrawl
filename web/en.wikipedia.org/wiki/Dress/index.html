Dress

A dress (also known as a frock or a gown) is a one-piece garment worn traditionally by women and girls, consisting of a skirt with an attached bodice that covers the torso and legs. Dresses have been worn from ancient times and vary widely in length, fabric, and style according to fashion, occasion, and culture.

History

Early dresses were draped garments; fitted bodices and skirts developed in Europe during the Middle Ages and Renaissance. The 19th century saw silhouettes shaped by crinolines and bustles, followed by simpler 20th-century styles from flapper dresses to the New Look. Trousers largely replaced dresses as everyday wear for many women in the later 20th century, but dresses remain common for formal occasions, and garments such as wedding dresses and evening gowns retain a central place in fashion.

Types

Common types include the little black dress, cocktail dress, ball gown, sundress, maxi dress, sheath dress, and wrap dress.
