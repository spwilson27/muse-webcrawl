Elections in Ukraine

Elections in Ukraine
President: elected every 5 years
Parliament: Verkhovna Rada, 450 seats
Voting age: 18

Elections in Ukraine are held to choose the President, the members of the Verkhovna Rada (parliament), and local government officials. Ukraine is a unitary semi-presidential republic, and its electoral system combines elements of proportional representation and single-member constituencies.

Presidential elections

The President of Ukraine is elected by popular vote for a five-year term using a two-round system: if no candidate wins an absolute majority in the first round, a runoff is held between the two leading candidates. Volodymyr Zelenskyy won the 2019 election in a landslide second round against incumbent Petro Poroshenko.

Parliamentary elections

The Verkhovna Rada has 450 deputies. Under the system used since 1998, half the seats are filled by proportional representation from party lists in a single nationwide district, and half from single-member districts, although reforms have repeatedly proposed moving to a fully proportional open-list system. Regular elections are held every five years.

Local elections

Local elections choose mayors, city councils, and regional (oblast) councils. Following decentralisation reforms after 2014, amalgamated territorial communities (hromadas) gained greater powers and their own elected councils.

History

The first competitive elections in independent Ukraine took place in the early 1990s after the dissolution of the Soviet Union. The 2004 presidential election was marred by fraud, sparking the Orange Revolution and a court-ordered repeat of the runoff. Elections were suspended under martial law following Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022.
