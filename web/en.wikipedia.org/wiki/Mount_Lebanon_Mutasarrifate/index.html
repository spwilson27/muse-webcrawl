The Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate was an autonomous administrative division (mutasarrifate) of the Ottoman Empire, established in 1861 following the 1860 civil conflict in Mount Lebanon and Damascus between Maronite Christians and Druze, and the ensuing French military intervention. Guaranteed by the European powers under the Règlement Organique, it provided the mountain district with a distinct status that endured until the First World War and formed an institutional precursor of modern Lebanon.

Establishment and government

Under the settlement, Mount Lebanon was removed from the authority of the Ottoman governors of Syria and placed under a mutasarrif — a non-Lebanese Ottoman Christian governor appointed by the Sultan with the approval of the signatory powers (Britain, France, Austria, Prussia, and Russia). The first governor was Garabet Artin Davoudian ("Daoud Pasha"). The mutasarrif was assisted by an elected Administrative Council of twelve members apportioned among the religious communities: Maronites, Druze, Greek Orthodox, Greek Catholics, Sunni and Shia Muslims, and others. The district was exempt from Ottoman military conscription and paid reduced taxes, and Ottoman troops could not enter without the governor's consent.

Society and economy

The mutasarrifate excluded Beirut, Tripoli, Sidon, and the fertile Beqaa valley, leaving it economically dependent on sericulture — raw silk exported through Beirut to Lyon — and on remittances from a growing stream of emigrants to the Americas and Egypt. Nevertheless, the decades of the mutasarrifate were relatively peaceful and prosperous, marked by administrative building, schools, and press activity that fed the Arab cultural renaissance (Nahda).

End

In 1915, during the First World War, the Ottoman commander Jamal Pasha abolished the autonomous regime, appointed an Ottoman Muslim governor, and imposed direct rule; blockade and requisitioning produced a devastating famine in Mount Lebanon. After the war, the French Mandate proclaimed the State of Greater Lebanon in 1920, joining the former mutasarrifate to the coastal cities and the Beqaa.
