Ichabod Crane is a fictional character and the protagonist of Washington Irving's short story "The Legend of Sleepy Hollow", first published in 1820 as part of The Sketch Book of Geoffrey Crayon, Gent. Crane is a lanky, superstitious schoolmaster from Connecticut who comes to the Dutch village of Sleepy Hollow in the Hudson Valley and meets his fate at the hands of the ghostly Headless Horseman.

Character

Irving describes Crane as tall and exceedingly lank, with narrow shoulders, long arms and legs, and a small head — "he resembled a scarecrow eloped from a cornfield." He is a strict but much-loved schoolmaster, a psalm singer, and a great consumer of local ghost stories and of the Dutch housewives' cooking. He hopes to marry Katrina Van Tassel, the daughter and heiress of a wealthy farmer, but is rivalled by the burly Brom Bones.

The legend

After a party at the Van Tassel farm, Crane rides home through the dark hollow and is pursued by the Headless Horseman, said to be the ghost of a Hessian soldier decapitated by a cannonball during the American Revolutionary War. Crane vanishes; the next morning only his horse, his hat, and a smashed pumpkin are found near the bridge by the old Dutch church. Irving leaves his fate ambiguous — Brom Bones' knowing laughter suggests a prank, while the old Dutch wives believe the Horseman carried him off.

In popular culture

Crane has been portrayed in many adaptations, including Disney's 1949 animated The Adventures of Ichabod and Mr. Toad and Tim Burton's 1999 film Sleepy Hollow, where he is reimagined as a police constable played by Johnny Depp. He is a central character in the 2013 television series Sleepy Hollow.
