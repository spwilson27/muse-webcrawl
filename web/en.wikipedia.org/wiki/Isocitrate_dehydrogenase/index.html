Isocitrate dehydrogenase

Isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH)
EC number: 1.1.1.42 (NADP+-dependent); 1.1.1.41 (NAD+-dependent)
Reaction: isocitrate → α-ketoglutarate + CO2
Location: mitochondria (IDH2, IDH3); cytosol and peroxisomes (IDH1)

Isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) is an enzyme that catalyses the oxidative decarboxylation of isocitrate, producing alpha-ketoglutarate (α-ketoglutarate) and carbon dioxide. It is a key enzyme of the citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle), and the reaction it catalyses is one of the cycle's major regulatory steps and a source of NADH or NADPH.

Isoforms

Humans have three isozymes: IDH1, a NADP+-dependent enzyme in the cytosol and peroxisomes; IDH2, a NADP+-dependent mitochondrial enzyme; and IDH3, a NAD+-dependent mitochondrial enzyme and the main form active in the citric acid cycle. IDH1 and IDH2 also play roles in redox balance and lipid metabolism, and IDH2 is involved in the reductive carboxylation pathway used by some cancer cells.

Clinical significance

Somatic mutations in IDH1 and IDH2 are among the most common genetic alterations in certain cancers, notably gliomas, acute myeloid leukaemia, chondrosarcomas and cholangiocarcinomas. Mutant IDH gains a neomorphic activity, converting α-ketoglutarate to the oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate, which interferes with enzymes that demethylate DNA and histones, producing a hypermethylated epigenetic state. IDH mutation status is a key diagnostic and prognostic marker in glioma classification, and IDH inhibitors such as ivosidenib and enasidenib are approved for treatment of IDH-mutant leukaemia.
