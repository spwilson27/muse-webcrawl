Hilbert class field

In algebraic number theory, the Hilbert class field E of a number field K is the maximal abelian unramified extension of K. Its degree over K equals the class number of K, and its Galois group over K is canonically isomorphic to the ideal class group of K.

Properties

The Hilbert class field has several characterising properties: every ideal of the ring of integers of K becomes principal in E (the principal ideal theorem, proved by Philipp Furtwängler); no prime of K ramifies in E; and the Artin reciprocity map identifies the class group with the Galois group, so that a prime ideal of K splits completely in E if and only if it is principal.

History and generalisations

David Hilbert conjectured the existence of such a field in his Zahlbericht (1897); its existence was proved by Furtwängler in 1907. The Hilbert class field is the prototype for class field theory, which generalises the correspondence between abelian extensions and ideal class groups. For imaginary quadratic fields, the Hilbert class field is generated by singular moduli (values of the j-invariant), a fact at the heart of the theory of complex multiplication and Kronecker's Jugendtraum.
