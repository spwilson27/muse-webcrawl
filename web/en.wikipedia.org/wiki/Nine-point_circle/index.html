Nine-point circle

In geometry, the nine-point circle is a circle that can be constructed for any given triangle. It is so named because it passes through nine significant concyclic points defined from the triangle. These nine points are:

  • The midpoint of each side of the triangle
  • The foot of each altitude
  • The midpoint of the line segment from each vertex of the triangle to the orthocenter (where the three altitudes meet)

The nine-point circle is also known as Feuerbach's circle (after Karl Wilhelm Feuerbach), Euler's circle, Terquem's circle, the six-points circle, the twelve-points circle, the n-point circle, the medioscribed circle, the mid circle or the circum-midcircle. Its center is the nine-point center of the triangle.

Significant nine points

The diagram above shows the nine points of the nine-point circle. Points D, E, and F are the midpoints of the three sides of the triangle. Points G, H, and I are the feet of the altitudes of the triangle. Points J, K, and L are the midpoints of the line segments between each vertex of the triangle (A, B, C) and the orthocenter (point S).

For an acute triangle, six of the points (the midpoints and altitude feet) lie on the triangle itself; for an obtuse triangle two of the altitudes have feet outside the triangle, but these feet still lie on the nine-point circle.

History

Although credited to him, Karl Wilhelm Feuerbach did not entirely discover the nine-point circle, but rather the six-point circle, making the slightly different claim that the midpoints of the three sides of the triangle and the feet of the three altitudes of the triangle all lie on the same circle. (See Fig. 1, points D, E, F, G, H, and I.) He published this discovery in 1822. However, a very short time before, Charles Brianchon and Jean-Victor Poncelet had stated and proven the same theorem in 1820. Soon after that, Olry Terquem also had proved the existence of the nine-point circle, and was the first to recognize the added property that the midpoints of line segments between the vertices and orthocenter also lie on the circle in 1842.

Properties

Center and radius

The nine-point center N is the midpoint of the line segment joining the orthocenter H and the circumcenter O: that is, ON = NH. The radius of the nine-point circle is half the radius of the triangle's circumcircle: R = 2r. Hence the circumcircle is the image of the nine-point circle under a homothety with center H and factor 2, mapping the nine points to the corresponding vertices and the circumcenter.

Other properties

  • The nine-point circle is tangent internally to the incircle and externally to each of the three excircles of the triangle (Feuerbach's theorem).
  • The nine-point center lies on the Euler line, midway between the orthocenter and circumcenter.
  • All triangles inscribed in a given circle with the same orthocenter share the same nine-point circle.
  • The image of the nine-point circle under a homothety centered at the centroid with factor −1/2 is the medial circle, and conversely.
  • The reflection of any point on the circumcircle over any side of the triangle lies on the nine-point circle's associated line through the orthocenter.
