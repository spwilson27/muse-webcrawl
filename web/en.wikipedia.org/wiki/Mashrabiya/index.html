Mashrabiya

A mashrabiya (Arabic: مشربية) is an architectural element characteristic of traditional Islamic architecture: a projecting oriel window enclosed by carved wooden latticework, often lined with stained glass, found on upper floors overlooking streets or courtyards.

Function

The lattice provides privacy — especially for women in the harem — while allowing air to circulate, light to be filtered, and occupants to see out without being seen. The porous water jars once kept in such bays gave the element its name and cooled drinking water by evaporation.

Distribution and history

Mashrabiyas are found across the Middle East and North Africa, notably in Cairo, Jeddah (where they are called roshan), and old Sanaa. Elaborate examples flourished in the Mamluk and Ottoman periods. Contemporary architects have revived the principle in responsive facades such as the Institut du Monde Arabe in Paris.
