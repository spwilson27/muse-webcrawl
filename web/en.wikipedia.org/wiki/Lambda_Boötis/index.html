Lambda Boötis

Lambda Boötis (λ Boötis, abbreviated Lambda Boo or λ Boo) is a star in the northern constellation of Boötes. With an apparent visual magnitude of about 4.18, it is visible to the naked eye from dark skies. Based on parallax measurements, it lies approximately 97–99 light-years from the Sun. The star is the prototype of the Lambda Boötis stars, a class of chemically peculiar stars with unusually low abundances of iron-peak elements in their surface layers.

Lambda Boötis is a white main-sequence star of spectral type A0. Its spectrum is peculiar: absorption lines of iron and other refractory metals are weak, suggesting surface metal abundances as low as one-hundredth of the Sun's, while lighter elements such as carbon, nitrogen, oxygen and sulphur are near-solar. The leading explanation is that the star has accreted metal-depleted gas from a circumstellar or interstellar cloud, after radiation pressure separated dust grains (which carry the metals) from the gas.

The star rotates rapidly, with a projected rotational velocity of around 130 km/s, and shows evidence of a dusty debris environment. It was historically given the name Aulad al Nathlat in some star catalogues, though this name is not in common use.
