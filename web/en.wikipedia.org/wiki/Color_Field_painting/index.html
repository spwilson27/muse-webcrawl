Color Field painting

Color Field painting is a style of abstract painting that emerged in New York City during the 1940s and 1950s within Abstract Expressionism. It was inspired by European modernism and is closely related to Abstract Expressionism, in that many of its leading practitioners were among the movement's founding figures. Color Field is characterised by large areas, or fields, of flat, solid colour spread across or stained into the canvas, creating an unbroken surface and a sense of immersion.

Characteristics

Color Field paintings emphasise the overall consistency of form and process, with colour treated as the subject itself rather than as a means of depicting something else. Many works are very large in scale, intended to envelop the viewer. Unlike the gestural brushwork of "action painting", Color Field painters sought an impersonal, contemplative effect, often by thinning paint so it soaked into unprimed canvas.

Artists

Leading figures include Mark Rothko, with his soft-edged floating rectangles; Barnett Newman, known for fields divided by vertical "zips"; Clyfford Still; and the second-generation painters Helen Frankenthaler, whose soak-stain technique influenced Morris Louis and Kenneth Noland, as well as Jules Olitski and Sam Gilliam. Art critic Clement Greenberg was the style's most influential champion, organising the 1964 exhibition Post-Painterly Abstraction.

Legacy

Color Field painting helped shift attention from gesture and drawing toward colour, opticality and the flatness of the picture plane, and influenced later movements including Minimalism and Lyrical Abstraction.
