Soldering

Soldering is a process in which two or more metal items are joined by melting and flowing a filler metal, called solder, into the joint. The solder has a lower melting point than the adjoining metals, so the workpieces themselves do not melt — distinguishing soldering from welding. Brazing is a closely related process using filler metals that melt above 450 °C; "soft" soldering occurs below that threshold. Soldering is used extensively in electronics, plumbing, jewelry, and metalwork, and evidence of the technique dates back some 5,000 years to Mesopotamia.

Process

The joint surfaces are cleaned, a flux is applied to remove oxides and promote wetting, the parts are heated (with a soldering iron, torch, hot plate, or in a reflow oven), and molten solder is fed into the joint, where capillary action draws it in. On cooling, the solder solidifies, forming a mechanically sound and, for electronics, electrically conductive bond. Good wetting — the solder spreading and alloying with the surface — is the mark of a quality joint; a dull, grainy "cold joint" results from insufficient heat or movement during cooling.

Solders and fluxes

Traditional electronics solder is a tin–lead alloy, classically 60/40 or the eutectic 63/37 (melting at 183 °C). Health and environmental concerns, and regulations such as the EU's RoHS directive (2006), have driven widespread adoption of lead-free solders, commonly tin–silver–copper (SAC) alloys melting around 217 °C. Plumbers' solders for potable water are likewise now lead-free. Fluxes range from rosin (for electronics) to aggressive acid fluxes (for plumbing and sheet metal, never used on electronics).

Methods

  • Hand soldering with a temperature-controlled iron for assembly and repair.
  • Wave soldering — circuit boards pass over a standing wave of molten solder, soldering all through-hole joints at once.
  • Reflow soldering — solder paste printed on pads melts in an oven, the standard method for surface-mount components.
  • Desoldering with braid, vacuum tools, or hot air for rework.
