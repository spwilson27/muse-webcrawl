East Sussex

East Sussex is a ceremonial county in South East England. It is bordered by Kent to the north-east, West Sussex to the west, Surrey to the north-west, and the English Channel to the south. The county town is Lewes. The city of Brighton and Hove forms a separate unitary authority within the ceremonial county.

Geography and settlements

Much of the county lies within the South Downs National Park, with chalk downland running to the sea at the cliffs of Beachy Head and the Seven Sisters. Other major towns include Eastbourne, Hastings, Bexhill-on-Sea, and Uckfield. Hastings is famous as the landing site of William the Conqueror; the Battle of Hastings in 1066 was actually fought near the town of Battle. The county has a population of roughly 560,000 (over 800,000 including Brighton and Hove).

History

East Sussex and West Sussex were administered as a single county of Sussex until 1974, when they were formally separated. The historic county had its origins in the Kingdom of Sussex, one of the Anglo-Saxon kingdoms.
