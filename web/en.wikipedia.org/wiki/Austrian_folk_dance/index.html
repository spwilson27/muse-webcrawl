Austrian folk dance

Austrian folk dances are the traditional dances of Austria and its regions, most of them associated with the Alpine culture of the countryside and frequently performed in traditional costume (Tracht). The best-known forms are the Ländler, the waltz, the polka, the Zwiefacher, and the spectacular Schuhplattler of Bavaria and Tyrol.

Forms

  • Ländler — a couple dance in 3/4 time with hopping and stamping steps, widely seen as a forerunner of the waltz; accompanied by yodelling melodies in some regions.
  • Waltz — the Viennese waltz became world-famous from the ballrooms of Vienna, but its roots lie in rural turning dances such as the Ländler.
  • Schuhplattler — a lively men's dance from Upper Bavaria and Tyrol in which dancers slap their thighs, knees and shoe soles in rhythm, accompanied by accordion and yodelling.
  • Polka — a fast 2/4 couple dance of Bohemian origin that became a staple of Austrian folk and ballroom repertoires.
  • Zwiefacher — a dance alternating between 3/4 and 2/4 bars in irregular patterns.
  • Regional figure dances — named dances tied to particular valleys and festivals, such as ribbon, sword and miners' dances in old mining districts.

Culture and preservation

Folk dance is maintained by local Trachtenvereine (costume associations) and performed at festivals, harvest celebrations and church fairs (Kirtag). Dancers typically wear dirndls and lederhosen, and music is provided by brass bands (Blasmusik), zithers, accordions and violins. Austrian folk dance societies coordinate through national umbrella organisations, and folk dance festivals draw groups from across the Alpine region.
