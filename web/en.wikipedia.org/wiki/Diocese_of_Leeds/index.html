Diocese of Leeds

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The name Diocese of Leeds is shared by two dioceses in West Yorkshire, England:

  • The Anglican Diocese of Leeds, a Church of England diocese in the Province of York, created on 20 April 2014 by the merger of the historic dioceses of Bradford, Ripon and Leeds, and Wakefield. It is unusual in having three cathedrals — at Bradford, Ripon, and Wakefield — and is divided into five episcopal areas (Bradford, Huddersfield, Leeds, Ripon, and Wakefield), each with its own area bishop. The diocesan Bishop of Leeds is the senior bishop; the first holder was Nick Baines, translated from Bradford in 2014.
  • The Roman Catholic Diocese of Leeds, a Latin Church diocese erected in 1878 when the Diocese of Beverley was divided into the dioceses of Leeds and Middlesbrough. Its cathedral is St Anne's Cathedral, Leeds, and it covers West Yorkshire together with parts of neighbouring counties. The diocese is in the ecclesiastical province of Liverpool; Marcus Stock became its tenth bishop in 2014.

Anglican diocese

The Anglican diocese covers most of West Yorkshire, parts of North and South Yorkshire, and small parts of several neighbouring counties. Its creation followed the recommendations of the Dioceses Commission and was intended to provide a single diocese for the Leeds city region and the western part of Yorkshire. The diocesan office is in Leeds.

Roman Catholic diocese

The Catholic Diocese of Leeds comprises over 80 parishes and is a suffragan of the Archdiocese of Liverpool. Among its notable churches are St Anne's Cathedral and the church of St Austin's in Wakefield; the diocese also runs the Leeds Trinity and Leeds Cathedral choirs, notable for their schools singing programme.
