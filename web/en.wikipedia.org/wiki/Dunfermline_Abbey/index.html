Dunfermline Abbey

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Abbey Church, Dunfermline, burial place of Scottish kings

Dunfermline Abbey is a Church of Scotland parish church in Dunfermline, Fife, Scotland. The church occupies the site of the ancient chancel and transepts of a large medieval Benedictine abbey, founded in the 11th century by Queen Margaret of Scotland and refounded by her son David I in 1128. The monastic buildings were sacked during the Scottish Reformation in 1560, but the nave survived in use as a parish church until a new church was built within the eastern end in the 19th century.

History

Dunfermline became the royal mausoleum of Scotland: among those buried here were Saint Margaret herself, Malcolm III, David I, and Robert the Bruce, whose tomb — rediscovered during building work in 1818 — lies beneath the pulpit of the present Abbey Church. The new church, built 1818–1821 to designs by William Burn, bears around the top of its central tower the carved words "KING ROBERT THE BRUCE".

The ruined nave, a fine example of Romanesque architecture modelled on Durham Cathedral, and the surviving gatehouse, refectory wall and palace ruins are cared for by Historic Environment Scotland; the adjacent Dunfermline Palace was a favoured residence of Scottish monarchs and the birthplace of Charles I in 1600.
