Japan Post Holdings Co., Ltd. (日本郵政株式会社, Nippon Yūsei Kabushiki-gaisha) is a Japanese state-influenced conglomerate operating postal services, banking, and life insurance through the Japan Post Group. At the time of its stock-market listing in 2015 it was one of the largest companies in Japan by revenue, and its banking and insurance arms rank among the largest financial institutions in the world by assets and policies.

History

Japan Post Holdings was created under the postal privatisation programme of Prime Minister Junichiro Koizumi, whose government made splitting and privatising Japan Post the centrepiece of its reform agenda and won a landslide in the 2005 "postal election". On October 1, 2007, the public corporation Japan Post was dissolved and replaced by the holding company and its operating subsidiaries.

A revised law in 2012 softened the original privatisation timetable but required the government to sell down its stake over time. In November 2015 Japan Post Holdings, Japan Post Bank, and Japan Post Insurance were listed simultaneously on the Tokyo Stock Exchange in one of Japan's largest-ever share offerings.

Group companies

  • Japan Post Co. — mail delivery and the post-office network.
  • Japan Post Bank — one of Japan's largest deposit-taking institutions, with savings gathered through the post-office network.
  • Japan Post Insurance ("Kampo") — a major life insurer selling simple products through post offices.

Issues

The group has faced criticism over aggressive and improper sales practices at Japan Post Insurance, which led in 2019 to revelations that many elderly customers had been sold disadvantageous policies, prompting executive resignations, regulatory action, and a suspension of insurance sales. Declining mail volumes and low interest rates have pressed the group to expand logistics and real-estate activities.
