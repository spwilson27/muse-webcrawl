The Tupolev Tu-154 (NATO reporting name: "Careless") is a three-engined, medium-range, narrow-body jet airliner designed by the Soviet Tupolev design bureau. First flown in 1968, it became the workhorse of Aeroflot and the airlines of the Soviet Union and its allies, and for decades the most common airliner on domestic routes across the USSR and Russia.

Design

The Tu-154 follows the rear-engined trijet layout similar to the Boeing 727 and Hawker Siddeley Trident: three Kuznetsov NK-8 (later Soloviev D-30 on the Tu-154M) turbofans are mounted at the rear fuselage, two in side pods and one in the tail fed by an S-shaped duct. The design emphasised the ability to operate from relatively rough, unpaved or poorly equipped airfields in Siberia and the Arctic: it has a high-flotation, six-wheel-bogie main landing gear and powerful high-lift devices, giving good short-field performance at the cost of high fuel consumption and noise. Typical seating is 164–180 passengers, with a range of around 4,000–6,600 km depending on variant.

Service history

Production ran from 1970 to about 2013, totalling roughly 1,026 aircraft. The type served with dozens of airlines across Eastern Europe, Asia and the Middle East. It was involved in a number of high-profile accidents, including the 2010 Smolensk crash that killed Polish President Lech Kaczyński and 95 others. Most airlines retired the Tu-154 from scheduled passenger service during the 2010s; a small number remained in government, military and VIP service, notably with the Russian and North Korean fleets.
