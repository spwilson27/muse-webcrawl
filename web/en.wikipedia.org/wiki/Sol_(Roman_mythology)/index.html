Sol (Roman mythology)

Sol is the Roman god of the Sun and the personification of the sun itself, corresponding broadly to the Greek Helios. Sol was worshipped in Rome from an early period, with a temple on the Quirinal Hill and rites shared with Luna, goddess of the Moon.

Sol Indiges and Sol Invictus

The older cult of Sol Indiges was an indigenous Roman worship, with a festival on August 9. In the later Empire, Sol rose to exceptional prominence as Sol Invictus, "the Unconquered Sun". The emperor Aurelian elevated Sol Invictus in 274 AD, building a magnificent temple in Rome and establishing games in his honour; the festival of the Dies Natalis Solis Invicti on December 25 became one of the most popular in the Roman calendar. Successors such as Constantine continued to use solar imagery before and after their turn toward Christianity.

Iconography

Sol is depicted as a radiant figure, often wearing a crown of sunbeams and driving a four-horse chariot (quadriga) across the sky. He appears frequently on imperial coinage of the 3rd and early 4th centuries AD.
