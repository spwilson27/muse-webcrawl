The areoid is the planetary geoid of Mars — an equipotential surface of the planet's gravity field that serves as the reference surface from which topographic heights on Mars are measured. The name combines Ares, the Greek god of war (the counterpart of the Roman Mars), with the suffix -oid, by analogy with Earth's geoid.

A topographic map of Mars, with elevations measured relative to the areoid

Definition and use

Because Mars has no global ocean, "sea level" cannot be defined directly as it is on Earth. Instead, elevations are measured relative to the areoid, the surface of constant gravitational potential that best fits the planet's shape. Data from the Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter (MOLA) aboard Mars Global Surveyor provided the precise topography used to construct modern areoid models.

Historically, elevation on Mars was referenced to a surface defined by an atmospheric pressure of 6.1 millibars, the triple point of water, before the gravity-based areoid became standard.

Applications

The areoid is used in mapping Mars, in defining the landing ellipses and surface operations of landers and rovers, and in comparing regional topography, such as the great elevation difference between the northern lowlands and the southern highlands (the Martian dichotomy). Heights of features such as Olympus Mons — about 21.9 km above the reference datum — are expressed relative to the areoid.
