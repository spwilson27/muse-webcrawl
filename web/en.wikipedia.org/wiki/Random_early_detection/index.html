Random early detection

TypeActive queue management algorithm
Introduced1993, by Sally Floyd and Van Jacobson
PurposeCongestion avoidance in packet-switched networks
Also known asRandom early drop (RED)

Random early detection (RED), also known as random early drop, is a queueing discipline for a network scheduler suited for congestion avoidance. Proposed by Sally Floyd and Van Jacobson in 1993, it is an active queue management (AQM) algorithm used in routers.

Operation

RED monitors the average queue size. When the average is below a minimum threshold, all packets are admitted. When it exceeds a maximum threshold, all incoming packets are dropped. In between, packets are dropped (or marked, when used with Explicit Congestion Notification) with a probability that increases with the average queue size. By dropping packets probabilistically before the queue fills, RED signals senders to slow down early and avoids the global synchronisation and tail-drop problems of simple FIFO queues.

Variants

Variants include Weighted RED (WRED), which applies different thresholds and drop probabilities to different classes of traffic, and Adaptive RED, which tunes parameters automatically.
