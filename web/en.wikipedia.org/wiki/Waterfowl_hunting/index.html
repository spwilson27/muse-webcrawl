Waterfowl hunting

Waterfowl hunting is the practice of hunting ducks, geese and other waterfowl for food and sport. In North America it is among the most highly regulated forms of hunting, organised around migratory flyways.

Waterfowl hunting
GameDucks, geese, swans (where permitted), coots
MethodsBlinds, decoys, calls, retrieving dogs
Regulation (US)Migratory Bird Treaty Act; federal Duck Stamp

Practices

Hunters typically conceal themselves in blinds near wetlands, deploy spreads of decoys, and use calls to draw birds within shotgun range; trained retrievers, such as Labrador retrievers, recover downed birds from water and marsh. Non-toxic shot (steel or other approved materials) is required in many countries because spent lead shot poisons scavenging birds and waterfowl that ingest it.

Conservation

Market hunting in the late nineteenth century devastated North American waterfowl, prompting the Migratory Bird Treaty (1916) and subsequent regulation. Licence and Duck Stamp revenues, together with organisations such as Ducks Unlimited, have funded extensive wetland conservation, making waterfowl hunting a major source of habitat funding.
