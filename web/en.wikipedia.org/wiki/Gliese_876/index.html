Gliese 876 is a red dwarf star 15.3 light-years from Earth in the constellation Aquarius. It is notable as one of the nearest stars known to host a multi-planet system, and its planets form one of the first discovered examples of a Laplace resonance chain outside the Solar System.

Planetary system

The first planet, Gliese 876 b, a gas giant about twice Jupiter's mass, was announced in 1998. A second gas giant, Gliese 876 c, followed in 2001; the two, together with the outer planet Gliese 876 e (announced 2010), are locked in a 1:2:4 orbital resonance similar to that of Jupiter's moons Io, Europa and Ganymede. In 2005 astronomers announced Gliese 876 d, a super-Earth of roughly six to eight Earth masses orbiting in under two days — at the time the smallest known exoplanet around a normal star.

Characteristics

Gliese 876 is a small, cool M-type main-sequence star with about a third of the Sun's mass and only about 1% of its luminosity. It is a BY Draconis variable, designated IL Aquarii. Its low activity and proximity make its system a benchmark for studies of planet formation around red dwarfs.
