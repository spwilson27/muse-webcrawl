Superclásico

The Superclásico at La Bombonera is famous for its intense atmosphere.

The Superclásico (Spanish: Superderby) is the name given to the football match in Argentina between Buenos Aires rivals Boca Juniors and River Plate. It derives from the Spanish usage of "clásico" for a derby match, with the prefix "super" marking it as the most important in Argentine football. The fixture is known worldwide for the passion of both clubs' supporters, the choreographed displays (tifos), and an atmosphere frequently listed among the greatest in sport.

Both clubs originated in the working-class La Boca neighbourhood of Buenos Aires; River Plate later moved to the more affluent Núñez district, which sharpened the class dimension of the rivalry — River acquired the nickname Los Millonarios ("the Millionaires"), while Boca retained its identity as the people's club. The first official meeting took place in 1913, and the fixture has been played more than 250 times in official competition.

2018 Copa Libertadores final

The rivalry reached an unprecedented stage in 2018, when Boca and River met in the final of the Copa Libertadores, South America's premier club competition — the first time the two had contested a continental final. After a 2–2 draw at La Bombonera, the second leg was postponed following an attack on the Boca team bus outside the Monumental, and was controversially relocated to the Santiago Bernabéu Stadium in Madrid, where River won 3–1 after extra time to take the trophy 5–3 on aggregate.

Fan culture and incidents

The Superclásico has also been marred by violence. In 1968, 71 fans died in a crush at the Monumental in the worst stadium disaster in Argentine history. Away supporters have been banned from Argentine league matches since 2013 in an effort to reduce violence between rival groups.
