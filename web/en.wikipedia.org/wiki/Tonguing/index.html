Tonguing

Articulation in wind playing
TypeArticulation technique
Used inWoodwind and brass instruments
Basic syllable"tah" / "dah"

Tonguing is a technique used with wind instruments to enunciate notes. It is performed by interrupting the airflow with the tongue, producing a defined attack at the start of a note. Rather than the tongue literally stopping the air column for long, a quick touch of the tongue to the teeth, gums, reed or lips briefly blocks the air, and its release allows the note to speak crisply.

Basic technique

In single tonguing, the player articulates each note with a syllable such as "tah" or "dah". On single-reed and double-reed instruments the tip of the tongue lightly touches the tip of the reed; on flute and brass instruments it touches behind the upper teeth or the alveolar ridge. The choice of syllable varies with register and desired sharpness of attack: harder consonants such as "t" give a marcato effect, while "d" produces a softer legato articulation.

Double and triple tonguing

For rapid passages, players use double tonguing, alternating syllables such as "ta-ka" (or "du-gu"), and triple tonguing, using groupings such as "ta-ta-ka" to articulate triplet figures evenly. These techniques are standard on brass instruments and the flute, and are common on reed instruments at fast tempi.

Other forms

  • Legato tonguing – a very light touch used to separate notes without interrupting the musical line.
  • Staccato tonguing – a firmer touch that shortens the note.
  • Flutter tonguing – rolling the tip of the tongue (like a rolled "r") or using a uvular trill while playing, producing a growling effect used in contemporary and jazz music.
  • Slap tonguing – on saxophone and clarinet, creating a vacuum with the tongue on the reed so its release makes a percussive "pop".
