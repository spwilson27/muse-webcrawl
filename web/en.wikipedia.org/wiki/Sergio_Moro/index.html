Sergio Moro

Sergio Moro

Born: 1 August 1972, Maringá, Paraná, Brazil
Occupation: Jurist and politician
Offices: Federal judge (1996–2018); Minister of Justice and Public Security (2019–2020); Senator for Paraná (2023– )

Sergio Fernando Moro (born 1 August 1972) is a Brazilian jurist and politician who became nationally prominent as the federal judge presiding over Operation Car Wash (Lava Jato), the vast corruption investigation centered on the state oil company Petrobras. His conviction of former president Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva was later annulled, after leaked messages and rulings questioned his impartiality, and he went on to serve as Minister of Justice under President Jair Bolsonaro before entering electoral politics himself, winning a Senate seat in 2022.

Judicial career

Moro studied law at the State University of Maringá and earned a doctorate from the Federal University of Paraná, later visiting Harvard Law School. He became a federal judge in 1996 and specialized in money laundering and organized crime cases, assisting at the Supreme Federal Court in the Mensalão trial. From 2014, sitting in Curitiba, he presided over the first-instance cases of Operation Car Wash, which exposed a kickback scheme involving Petrobras contracts, construction conglomerates such as Odebrecht, and politicians across the spectrum. The investigation produced hundreds of convictions, recovered billions of reais, and reshaped Latin American politics; Moro's methods — extensive use of plea bargains, preventive detention, and the release of wiretap recordings — drew both praise for confronting elite impunity and criticism for alleged violations of due process.

In July 2017, Moro convicted Lula of corruption and money laundering in the "triplex" case, sentencing him to nine and a half years (later increased on appeal); the conviction barred Lula, then leading the polls, from the 2018 presidential election under Brazil's clean-record law. Days after that election, Bolsonaro — who won it — announced Moro as his Minister of Justice, a move critics said suggested prior political coordination.

Minister and the Vaza Jato controversy

As minister (January 2019 – April 2020), Moro pushed an anti-crime package through Congress in modified form. In June 2019, the news site The Intercept Brasil began publishing leaked Telegram messages ("Vaza Jato") between Moro and Car Wash prosecutors that appeared to show improper collaboration and political motivation. Moro disputed the leaks' authenticity and denied wrongdoing. He resigned in April 2020, accusing Bolsonaro of interfering with the Federal Police; Bolsonaro denied the charge.

In March 2021, the Supreme Federal Court annulled Lula's convictions on jurisdictional grounds and, in a separate ruling, declared Moro biased (suspeito) in Lula's case, voiding his acts — one of the most consequential judicial reversals in Brazilian history, which freed Lula to run again in 2022.

Political career

After a period in private consulting in the United States, Moro joined the Podemos party in 2021 and initially sought a presidential run, later switching parties and states before winning election as senator for Paraná in October 2022 with about 1.98 million votes. In 2024 his mandate survived an electoral-court challenge over campaign financing, when the Superior Electoral Court rejected cassation. In the Senate he has focused on public security and anti-corruption legislation.
