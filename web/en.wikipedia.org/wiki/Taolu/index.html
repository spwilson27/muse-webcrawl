Taolu (wushu)

Taolu (Chinese: 套路; pinyin: tàolù) refers to the set of connected, predetermined movement exercises—commonly called "forms"—in the Chinese martial art of wushu. In modern competitive wushu, taolu is one of the two main disciplines, alongside sanda (sparring). Athletes perform choreographed routines, either empty-handed or with weapons, and are scored by judges on the quality of movement, difficulty, and overall performance.

Events

Modern taolu events are divided by style and apparatus. Barehand events include changquan (long fist), nanquan (southern fist), and taijiquan (tai chi). Weapon events include daoshu (broadsword), jianshu (straight sword), gunshu (staff), qiangshu (spear), and their southern and tai chi counterparts, as well as taijijian. There are also group routines (jiti) and choreographed sparring sets (duilian). Since the early 2000s, routines at elite level have incorporated "difficulty movements" (nandu)—high-difficulty jumps, spins and balances awarded additional points.

Judging

Under International Wushu Federation rules, routines are scored out of ten points, combining separate panels for quality of movements (technique), overall performance (choreography, speed, power, coordination), and degree of difficulty. Deductions are made for errors such as loss of balance, extra steps, or dropped weapons, and routines must fall within prescribed time limits, usually between one minute twenty seconds and two minutes for most events.

History

Forms have been part of Chinese martial arts training for centuries as a means of recording techniques and training the body. The modern sport of wushu was standardized in the People's Republic of China during the 1950s, when traditional styles were codified into standardized competition routines. Taolu made a tournament appearance at the 2008 Beijing Olympics (the Beijing 2008 Wushu Tournament) alongside the Olympic Games, though wushu has not been added to the official Olympic programme.
