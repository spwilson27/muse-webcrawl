The United Kingdom possesses nuclear weapons and is one of the five nuclear-weapon states recognized under the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT). It formerly operated chemical and biological weapons programmes, which it abandoned in the 1950s. Britain was the third country to develop and test nuclear weapons, after the United States and the Soviet Union.

Nuclear weapons

British scientists contributed to the Manhattan Project through the Tube Alloys programme and the Quebec Agreement. After the United States ended cooperation in 1946, Britain developed its own bomb; the first test, Operation Hurricane, took place off Western Australia in October 1952. A thermonuclear device was tested in the Operation Grapple series at Christmas Island in 1957–58, making Britain the third thermonuclear power.

Delivery systems evolved from V-bomber aircraft carrying free-fall bombs and the Blue Streak missile project, to the American-supplied Polaris submarine-launched missiles acquired under the 1962 Nassau Agreement, and then to Trident. Since 1998, when the WE.177 free-fall bomb was retired, Britain's deterrent has been exclusively submarine-based: four Vanguard-class ballistic missile submarines operating from HMNB Clyde maintain continuous at-sea deterrence, to be replaced by the Dreadnought class. In 2021 the government announced an increase of the warhead stockpile ceiling from 180 to 260, reversing earlier reductions.

Chemical and biological weapons

Britain used chemical weapons in the First World War and maintained stockpiles thereafter. During the Second World War it developed anthrax weapons, including the "cattle cakes" tested on Gruinard Island, which remained contaminated until a cleanup completed in 1990. The offensive chemical and biological programmes were abandoned in the 1950s; defensive research continues at Porton Down. The UK ratified the Geneva Protocol in 1930, the Biological Weapons Convention in 1975, and the Chemical Weapons Convention in 1993, destroying its remaining declared chemical stocks under OPCW supervision.
