Gakuran

The gakuran (学ラン) and tsume-eri (詰襟) are uniforms for middle school and high school boys in Japan. The gakuran is modeled on the Prussian Waffenrock-style military uniforms of the 19th century and on the uniforms of Imperial Japanese military cadets. It consists of a dark jacket with a standing (mandarin) collar buttoned all the way to the throat, worn with straight-leg trousers and a belt.

Appearance

The jacket is typically black for high school students and navy blue for middle school students, with metal buttons often bearing the school emblem. The standing collar is traditionally fitted with a stiff plastic or celluloid insert. A white or light-coloured shirt may be worn underneath. School uniforms of this type have been standard since the Meiji era, when Japan westernised its military and educational dress.

Cultural significance

The gakuran is a familiar symbol of Japanese school life and appears extensively in manga, anime, and film, where modified or exaggerated versions (such as the long chōran coats associated with delinquent yankī and banchō characters) signal rebellion. A popular romantic custom, associated with graduation, is for a boy to give the second button of his gakuran — the one nearest the heart — to a girl he likes. In recent decades many schools have replaced the gakuran with blazers, though it remains in use.
