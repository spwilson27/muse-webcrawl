Country: Trinidad and Tobago
Island: Tobago
Population: approx. 17,000

Scarborough, capital of Tobago.

Scarborough is the capital and largest town of Tobago, one of the two main islands of the Republic of Trinidad and Tobago. Located on the southwestern coast of the island on Rockly Bay, it serves as Tobago's administrative, commercial and port centre, and is the seat of the Tobago House of Assembly.

History

Tobago changed hands repeatedly among European powers — Dutch, French, Courlanders and British — and Scarborough, founded by the Dutch as Lampsinsburg in the 17th century, became the island's capital under British rule in the later 18th century. Fort King George, built by the British above the town in the 1770s and 1780s, now houses the Tobago Museum and offers views over the town and bay.

Geography and economy

The town is built on steep hills rising from the harbour, where ferries from Port of Spain in Trinidad and cruise ships dock. Scarborough's economy rests on government services, commerce for the island, fishing, and tourism linked to Tobago's beaches and reefs.
