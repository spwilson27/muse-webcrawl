Theater District, Manhattan

Theater District

Borough: Manhattan, New York City

Approximate bounds: West 40th to West 54th Streets, Sixth to Eighth Avenues

Key venues: 41 Broadway theatres

Centrepiece: Times Square

The Theater District is a neighbourhood in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, where most Broadway theatres are located, as are many other theatres, movie theatres, restaurants, hotels and other places of entertainment. It is roughly bounded by West 40th Street to the south, West 54th Street to the north, Sixth Avenue to the east and Eighth Avenue to the west, and includes Times Square at its heart.

History

New York's theatres migrated uptown along Broadway through the nineteenth century, from the Bowery and Union Square to Herald Square and then Longacre Square — renamed Times Square in 1904 when The New York Times moved there. Major venues such as the Metropolitan Opera House (1883, at 39th Street) and the New Amsterdam Theatre (1903) anchored the district. The zoning and preservation battles of the 1960s–80s, including the demolition of the Morosco and Helen Hayes theatres in 1982, led to the designation of most Broadway houses as landmarks and to the creation of the Theater Subdistrict, which allows theatres to transfer their air rights.

Today

The district contains 41 Broadway theatres, each with 500 or more seats, as well as Off-Broadway venues such as New World Stages. Broadway productions in the district draw around 14–15 million attendees in a typical pre-pandemic season, with grosses exceeding a billion dollars, making the Theater District one of New York's principal tourist attractions. The TKTS discount booth at Duffy Square, opened in 1973, is a district landmark.
