Alice Pleasance Hargreaves, née Liddell (4 May 1852 – 16 November 1934), was an English woman who, as a child, was acquainted with Lewis Carroll (Charles Lutwidge Dodgson) and is widely identified as the inspiration for the character of Alice in his books Alice's Adventures in Wonderland (1865) and Through the Looking-Glass (1871).

Early life

Alice was the fourth child of Henry George Liddell, Dean of Christ Church, Oxford, and Lorina Hanna Liddell. Dodgson, a mathematics lecturer at Christ Church, befriended the family and often took Alice and her sisters Lorina and Edith on boating trips. On the famous excursion of 4 July 1862, he improvised the story that became Alice's Adventures in Wonderland; Alice asked him to write it down, and he presented her with a hand-written, illustrated manuscript, Alice's Adventures Under Ground, in November 1864.

Later life

Alice married Reginald Hargreaves, a cricketer and former pupil of Dodgson, in 1880 at Westminster Abbey. They had three sons, two of whom were killed in the First World War. Widowed and facing the upkeep of their house, Cuffnells in Hampshire, she sold Dodgson's manuscript at Sotheby's in 1928; it was later presented to the British people and is now in the British Library. In 1932 she travelled to New York to receive an honorary degree from Columbia University during the Carroll centenary celebrations.

Death and commemoration

Alice Hargreaves died in 1934 and is buried at Lyndhurst, Hampshire. A memorial plaque stands in Christ Church, Oxford, and the connection between the real Alice and the fictional one continues to draw scholarly and popular interest.
