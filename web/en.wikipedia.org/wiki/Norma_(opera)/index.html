Norma (opera)

Norma is a tragedia lirica in two acts by Vincenzo Bellini with an Italian libretto by Felice Romani, after Alexandre Soumet's play. It premiered at La Scala, Milan, on 26 December 1831, and is regarded as a supreme example of the bel canto tradition.

Norma
Composer: Vincenzo Bellini
Librettist: Felice Romani
Premiere: 26 December 1831, La Scala, Milan
Language: Italian

Synopsis

In Roman-occupied Gaul, the druid high priestess Norma has secretly broken her vows and borne two children to the Roman proconsul Pollione. When Pollione abandons her for the young priestess Adalgisa, Norma is torn between vengeance and love; in the finale she confesses her guilt and mounts the sacrificial pyre, and Pollione, moved, joins her in death.

Music

The opera's most famous number is Norma's Act 1 prayer "Casta diva," one of the most demanding and celebrated soprano arias in the repertoire. The title role — sung in the 20th century most famously by Maria Callas — requires both coloratura agility and dramatic weight, making the opera a rarity despite its fame.
