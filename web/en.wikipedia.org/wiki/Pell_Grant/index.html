Pell Grant

A Pell Grant is a subsidy provided by the United States federal government to students who demonstrate exceptional financial need, to help pay for post-secondary education. Unlike a loan, a Pell Grant generally does not have to be repaid. It is the largest federal grant program for undergraduates.

History

The program was created by the Higher Education Act amendments of 1972 as the Basic Educational Opportunity Grant and renamed in 1980 in honor of Senator Claiborne Pell of Rhode Island, its principal sponsor. Eligibility is determined largely through the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA), which computes a Student Aid Index; awards are capped annually by Congress — the maximum grant in recent years has been in the range of $7,000–$7,400.

Scope

Several million students receive Pell Grants each year, a large share of them at community colleges and public universities. The program is frequently debated in discussions of college affordability, as the maximum grant covers a smaller share of average college costs than it did when created.
