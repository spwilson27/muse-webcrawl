Hurley (stick)

A hurley or hurl (Irish: camán) is a wooden stick used in the Irish Gaelic games of hurling and camogie. It is used to strike the ball, called a sliotar, either along the ground or through the air, and to carry and balance the ball while running.

Description

The hurley is traditionally made from the butt and lower trunk of the ash tree, whose grain and natural bend shape the curved head, or bas — the flat, broad end of the stick. Senior hurleys measure between 76 and 91 cm (30–36 inches), sized to the player. Since 2019 the Gaelic Athletic Association has permitted synthetic composite hurleys, long used by some players, though ash remains the traditional material. The bas may be banded with metal for protection, and goalkeepers use a hurley with a wider bas. In camogie the stick is slightly shorter.

Use

Players strike the sliotar with the bas, dribble it balanced on the flat end while running, and use the hurley to block opponents' shots ("hooking") and to flick the opponent's stick as they strike ("blocking"). Because hurleys break frequently in play, traditional makers (hurlers or hurley-makers) produce them by hand from Irish ash, though ash dieback disease in Europe has raised concerns about future supply and increased interest in bamboo and synthetic alternatives.
