A seborrheic keratosis on the skin

A seborrheic keratosis is a common non-cancerous (benign) skin tumour that grows from skin cells called keratinocytes. It is one of the most common skin growths in older adults, sometimes informally called "barnacles of ageing". Despite the name, the condition is unrelated to sebaceous glands or sebum and is not caused by a virus or by poor hygiene.

Signs and symptoms

Seborrheic keratoses appear as slightly raised, round or oval growths with a waxy, scaly or wart-like surface, as though "stuck on" to the skin. They range in colour from light tan to brown or black, and in size from a few millimetres to several centimetres. They occur most often on the chest, back, face, scalp and neck, and usually multiply with age. The lesions are generally painless, though they may itch or become irritated by clothing; they do not turn into skin cancer.

Causes

The exact cause is not fully understood, but genetic factors play a clear role — the tendency to develop many lesions runs in families — and cumulative sun exposure may contribute. Somatic mutations in growth-signalling genes such as FGFR3 and PIK3CA are commonly found in the lesions. The sudden appearance of very many itchy seborrheic keratoses (the sign of Leser-Trélat) has been reported in association with internal malignancy, though the strength of that association is debated.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical, supported by dermoscopy, which shows characteristic features such as comedo-like openings and milia-like cysts. Because seborrheic keratoses can resemble melanoma, any changing or atypical lesion should be assessed by a clinician and, if uncertain, biopsied.

No treatment is required for typical lesions. Removal is performed for cosmetic reasons, for irritation, or to exclude malignancy, using cryotherapy with liquid nitrogen, curettage, shave excision, electrocautery or laser ablation. A topical hydrogen peroxide solution was approved in the United States for the treatment of raised seborrheic keratoses.
