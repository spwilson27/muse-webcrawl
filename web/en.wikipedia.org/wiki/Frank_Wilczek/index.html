Frank Wilczek

Frank Anthony Wilczek (born 15 May 1951) is an American theoretical physicist, mathematician, and Nobel laureate. As a graduate student working with David Gross at Princeton, he discovered asymptotic freedom in the theory of the strong interaction in 1973 — work that, together with David Politzer's independent result, earned the three the 2004 Nobel Prize in Physics. Wilczek has also proposed the axion as a dark-matter candidate, coined the term "anyon" for particles with fractional statistics, and written widely read books on fundamental physics.

Biography

Wilczek was born in Mineola, New York, to parents of Polish and Italian descent, and grew up in Queens. He received his bachelor's degree in mathematics from the University of Chicago in 1970 and his PhD in physics from Princeton in 1974. After faculty positions at Princeton and the Institute for Theoretical Physics in Santa Barbara, he became Herman Feshbach Professor of Physics at MIT in 2000. He later held additional posts at Arizona State University and Stockholm University.

Research

The 1973 asymptotic freedom papers by Gross and Wilczek established that in non-Abelian gauge theories such as quantum chromodynamics, the interaction strength decreases at short distances — resolving the paradox of quarks appearing free in high-energy experiments yet never being isolated. Wilczek went on to work on axions (hypothetical particles named after a laundry detergent, proposed to solve the strong CP problem and now leading dark-matter candidates), fractional statistics and anyons in two-dimensional systems, color superconductivity in dense quark matter, and the concept of time crystals, proposed in 2012 and realised experimentally several years later. His popular books include Longing for the Harmonies (with Betsy Devine), The Lightness of Being, A Beautiful Question, and Fundamentals: Ten Keys to Reality. He received the Templeton Prize in 2022.
