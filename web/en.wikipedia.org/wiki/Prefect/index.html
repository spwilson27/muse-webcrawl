Prefect

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Prefect (from the Latin praefectus, "put in front" or "in charge") is a magisterial title of varying definition, but essentially refers to the leader of an administrative area. The term has been used in many states and organizations across history.

Ancient Rome

In the Roman Republic and Empire, prefects held a wide range of military and civil offices: the praefectus urbi (urban prefect) governed Rome in the emperor's absence; the praetorian prefect commanded the Praetorian Guard and became one of the empire's most powerful officials; other prefects governed Egypt, commanded fleets and the vigiles, or administered the grain supply (praefectus annonae).

Modern usage

France

In France, a prefect (préfet) is the state's representative in a department or region, appointed by the central government, responsible for public order, oversight of local authorities, and implementation of national policy. The system was created by Napoleon in 1800 and remains a cornerstone of French administration.

Other countries

Japan's prefectures are headed by elected governors rather than prefects in the French sense, though the word is used in English translation. Prefects also appear in the administration of Italy (prefetti), several Latin American and African states, the Vatican City (Prefects of the Papal Household and of dicasteries), and in schools in the British tradition, where senior pupils given disciplinary and leadership responsibilities are called prefects.
