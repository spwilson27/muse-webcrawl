Gaius Asinius Pollio (76 BC – AD 4) was a Roman soldier, politician, orator, historian and literary patron who survived the fall of the Republic to become a grand old man of Augustan letters. He fought for Caesar, governed for Antony, yet kept his independence under Augustus, and founded the first public library in Rome.

Pollio founded Rome's first public library in the Atrium Libertatis.

Career

Pollio, of Marrucinian stock from Teate in the Abruzzi, began his public career prosecuting in the courts and attached himself to Caesar in the civil war, fighting at Pharsalus and in Africa and Spain. After Caesar's death he governed Further Spain, then joined Mark Antony, brokered the Treaty of Brundisium in 40 BC, and as consul that year campaigned in Illyricum, triumphing over the Parthini in 39 BC. During the final conflict between Antony and Octavian he pointedly retired to private life, declaring that he would be "the prize of the victor". His celebrated independence survived into old age: Augustus tolerated his barbed speeches and histories.

Literature and patronage

Pollio rebuilt the Atrium Libertatis with the spoils of his triumph and installed there Rome's first public library, with separate Greek and Latin sections and portraits of famous authors — only Varro, he noted, was honoured while still alive. He is also credited with introducing the recitatio, the public reading of new works. He patronised Virgil, whose Fourth Eclogue celebrates the birth of a child — often identified as Pollio's son — during his consulship, and Horace, who addresses Odes 2.1 to him. As a critic Pollio was tart: he faulted Cicero's style, accused Sallust of affectation, and charged Livy with "Patavinity".

Works

His Histories, in seventeen books, covered the civil wars from 60 BC onward and were used by Appian and Plutarch; they are lost apart from fragments. His speeches and tragedies are likewise lost. Horace's ode preserves his reputation as a fearless historian writing "a work full of dangerous hazard" on events within living memory.
