Symbols of Ontario

[Image placeholder: symbols of Ontario]

The Canadian province of Ontario has a number of official emblems. Its armorial bearings date to 1868, when arms were granted by Queen Victoria shortly after Confederation, and its flag — a Red Ensign bearing the Ontario shield in the fly — was adopted in 1965 following the adoption of the national Maple Leaf flag.

Coat of arms

The shield bears the cross of St. George above three golden maple leaves on green, signifying Canada. Arms augmented in 1909 include a crest of a black bear standing on a crown, supporters — a moose and a deer — and the motto Ut incepit fidelis sic permanet ("Loyal she began, loyal she remains"), referring to the United Empire Loyalists who settled the province.

Provincial symbols

Ontario's floral emblem is the white trillium (Trillium grandiflorum), adopted in 1937; picking it was long popularly believed to be illegal. The provincial bird is the common loon (1994), the tree is the eastern white pine (1984), and the mineral emblem is amethyst (1975). The Ontario tartan, adopted in 2000, incorporates red, green, and other colours representing the province's people and landscapes.
