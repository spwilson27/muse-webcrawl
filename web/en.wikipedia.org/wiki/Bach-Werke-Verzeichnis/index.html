Bach-Werke-Verzeichnis

The Bach-Werke-Verzeichnis (German for "Bach Works Catalogue"; abbreviated BWV) is a catalogue of the works of Johann Sebastian Bach. It was compiled by Wolfgang Schmieder and first published in 1950 under the title Thematisch-systematisches Verzeichnis der musikalischen Werke von Johann Sebastian Bach. The numbers assigned to works in the catalogue are universally used by scholars and musicians to identify Bach's compositions — for example, BWV 846 is the Prelude and Fugue in C major that opens The Well-Tempered Clavier.

Organisation

Unlike catalogues organised chronologically, the BWV groups works by genre: BWV 1–224 are cantatas, 225–231 motets, 232–243 masses and related liturgical works, 244–249 the Passions and oratorios, and so on through the organ works, keyboard works, chamber music and orchestral works. An appendix, the BWV Anhang (Anh.), lists lost, doubtful and spurious works.

Editions

A second, revised edition appeared in 1990, and a thoroughly revised third edition, BWV3, was published in 2022, reflecting decades of new research that reassigned dates and authenticity for numerous works. Because renumbering would create chaos, Schmieder kept the original numbers largely stable, a convention later editions have maintained.
