Unsaturated fat

An unsaturated fat is a fat or fatty acid in which there is at least one double bond within the fatty acid chain. A fatty acid with one double bond is monounsaturated; with more than one, polyunsaturated. These double bonds can be in cis or trans configuration.

Unsaturated fat
Examples: Oleic acid, linoleic acid
Sources: Olive oil, nuts, seeds, oily fish
State at room temp: Usually liquid

Chemistry

Cis double bonds introduce kinks that prevent tight packing, lowering melting point, so unsaturated fats such as olive oil are liquid at room temperature. Trans fats, mostly created by partial hydrogenation, pack more tightly and behave more like saturated fats; industrial trans fats are now heavily restricted due to cardiovascular risk.

Health

Replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fat is generally associated with improved blood lipids and reduced cardiovascular disease risk. Polyunsaturated fats include essential omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids that humans cannot synthesize.
