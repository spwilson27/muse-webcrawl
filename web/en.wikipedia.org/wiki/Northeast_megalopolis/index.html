Northeast megalopolis

The BosWash corridor at night

The Northeast megalopolis, also known as the Boston–Washington corridor or BosWash, is the most populous megalopolis in the United States, stretching from the Boston metropolitan area in the north-east to the Washington, D.C. metropolitan area in the south-west. It runs along a roughly 750-kilometre axis following Interstate 95 and the Northeast Corridor rail line.

Extent and population

The corridor passes through the metropolitan areas of Boston, Providence, Hartford, New York City, Philadelphia, Baltimore and Washington, D.C., together with many smaller cities. Home to roughly 50 million people — about 17 percent of the United States population on less than 2 percent of its land area — it produces a disproportionate share of national GDP and is the seat of the federal government, much of the finance industry and many leading universities.

History of the concept

The French geographer Jean Gottmann coined the term "megalopolis" for the region in his 1961 study Megalopolis: The Urbanized Northeastern Seaboard of the United States, describing an almost continuous urban and suburban complex. The term "BosWash" was popularised by futurist Herman Kahn in the 1960s. The region is sometimes treated as part of a still larger "Northeast megaregion" in federal planning documents.

Transport

The corridor is served by Amtrak's Northeast Corridor, the busiest passenger rail line in the United States, including the Acela high-speed service, and by a dense network of commuter railways, interstate highways and major airports.
