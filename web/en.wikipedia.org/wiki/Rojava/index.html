Rojava

Rojava (Kurdish: رۆژئاڤا, literally "the West") is the Kurdish name for the de facto autonomous region in northeastern and northern Syria administered since 2012 by the Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria (AANES). The region emerged during the Syrian civil war as Kurdish-majority areas came under the control of the Democratic Union Party (PYD) and allied groups, and its political system is based on the ideology of democratic confederalism developed by Abdullah Öcalan.

History

In 2012, Syrian government forces withdrew from much of the Kurdish-majority north, and local councils affiliated with the PYD took over administration. In 2014 the region was organised into three self-proclaimed cantons — Afrin, Jazira and Kobanî — and its military forces, the People's Protection Units (YPG) and Women's Protection Units (YPJ), gained international prominence during the Siege of Kobanî (2014–2015), when they broke the Islamic State's assault with US-led coalition air support. Backed by the United States, the multi-ethnic Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF), formed in 2015, captured large territories from the Islamic State including Raqqa (2017) and the last territorial stronghold at Baghuz (2019). Turkey, which regards the YPG as an extension of the PKK, launched cross-border operations — Euphrates Shield (2016), Olive Branch (2018, seizing Afrin), and Peace Spring (2019) — displacing the administration from parts of the north.

Politics and society

The administration proclaims a decentralised, multi-ethnic and feminist system: communes and councils with mandatory co-chairs (one man, one woman) and ethnic quotas; guaranteed representation for Arabs, Assyrians/Syriacs and other groups; and Kurdish-, Arabic- and Syriac-language education. Its social contract (charter) was revised in 2016 and again in 2023. Critics, including human rights organisations, have accused the authorities of arbitrary arrests, conscription including of minors, and suppression of rival Kurdish parties, while supporters praise its gender equality and pluralism relative to regional norms.

Geography

The region covers much of Syria north and east of the Euphrates, including the cities of Qamishli (its administrative centre), Al-Hasakah, Kobanî, Manbij, Tabqa and (until 2018–2019 losses in the northwest) Afrin. It contains most of Syria's oil fields, wheat-growing land and the Tabqa Dam.
