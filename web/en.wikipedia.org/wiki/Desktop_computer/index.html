Desktop computer

A typical desktop computer setup

A desktop computer is a personal computer designed for regular use at a stationary location on or near a desk, as opposed to a portable computer such as a laptop. The most common configuration has a case housing the power supply, motherboard, disk storage and expansion cards, together with an external monitor, keyboard and mouse.

History

Before the widespread use of microprocessors, a computer small enough to fit on a desk was considered remarkable; most computers filled rooms. The first desktop-sized systems appeared in the late 1960s and 1970s, and by the early 1980s the form factor had become the standard shape of the personal computer, exemplified by the IBM PC and its compatibles. Through the 1990s and 2000s desktops dominated home and office computing.

Form factors

Desktop cases range from large full-tower enclosures, through mid-towers and small-form-factor cases, to all-in-one designs that integrate the computer into the monitor housing, such as the iMac line. Compared with laptops, desktops generally offer more processing power, storage and upgradeability for a given price, at the cost of portability.

Market

Since the 2010s, desktop sales have declined as laptops, tablets and smartphones have taken over general-purpose computing. Desktops remain common in offices, education, and performance-oriented niches such as gaming, engineering workstations and content creation, where their larger displays, ergonomics and expandability are valued.
