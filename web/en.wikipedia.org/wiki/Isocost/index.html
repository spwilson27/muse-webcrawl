Isocost

[Image: Isocost lines tangent to isoquants]
Isocost lines (straight) tangent to isoquant curves; the tangency points give least-cost input combinations.

In economics, an isocost line shows all combinations of two inputs, such as labour and capital, that a firm can purchase for a given total cost. The term combines the Greek iso ("equal") with "cost". Isocost lines are used together with isoquants in the theory of the firm to determine cost-minimising production.

Definition

If a firm spends total cost C on labour L at wage w and capital K at rental rate r, the isocost equation is:

C = wL + rK

Rearranged, K = C/r − (w/r)L, so the line has intercept C/r on the capital axis and slope −w/r, reflecting the relative price of the two inputs.

Cost minimisation

A firm minimises the cost of producing a given output where an isocost line is tangent to the corresponding isoquant. At the optimum, the marginal rate of technical substitution equals the ratio of input prices. A rise in total cost shifts the isocost line outward in parallel; a change in relative input prices changes its slope.
