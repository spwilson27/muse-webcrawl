The Ballon d'Or (French pronunciation: [balɔ̃ dɔʁ]; lit. 'Golden Ball') is an annual football award presented by French news magazine France Football since 1956. It is widely regarded as the most prestigious individual award in association football, honouring the male player deemed to have performed the best over the previous season (previously the calendar year).

The Ballon d'Or trophy.

History

The award was conceived by France Football journalists Gabriel Hanot and Jacques Ferran. From 1956 to 2006 it was voted on by football journalists and was originally restricted to European players, which is why greats such as Pelé and Diego Maradona were ineligible. Eligibility was extended to all players at European clubs in 1995 — the year Liberia's George Weah became the first non-European winner — and to players worldwide in 2007. Between 2010 and 2015 the award merged with FIFA's World Player of the Year as the FIFA Ballon d'Or, before the partnership ended and the two honours separated again.

Ballon d'Or Féminin

A women's award, the Ballon d'Or Féminin, was introduced in 2018. Its first winner was Norway's Ada Hegerberg; Spain's Alexia Putellas and Aitana Bonmatí have since won it multiple times. The ceremony also includes the Kopa Trophy (best young player) and the Yashin Trophy (best goalkeeper).
