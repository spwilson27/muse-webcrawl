The Hillsborough disaster was a fatal crowd crush at Hillsborough Stadium in Sheffield, South Yorkshire, England, on 15 April 1989. It occurred during an FA Cup semi-final football match between Liverpool and Nottingham Forest. Ninety-seven Liverpool supporters died as a result of overcrowding in the Leppings Lane end of the stadium, making it the deadliest disaster in British sporting history.

Events

Shortly before kick-off, a build-up of Liverpool fans outside the Leppings Lane turnstiles led police to open an exit gate to relieve pressure outside. Thousands of fans streamed through a tunnel into already-full central pens of the standing terrace. The resulting crush against the perimeter fencing caused fatal compressive asphyxia. The match was stopped after six minutes. Many victims died at the scene; the death toll eventually reached 97, including Andrew Devine, who died in 2021 from injuries sustained in the disaster and was ruled by a coroner to have been unlawfully killed.

Aftermath and inquiries

The Taylor Report (1990) attributed the disaster primarily to failures of police control and led to the elimination of standing terraces at major stadia in England and the removal of perimeter fences. Early official accounts and some media coverage controversially blamed Liverpool supporters, a narrative the families of the victims campaigned against for decades.

The Hillsborough Independent Panel reported in 2012, finding that no Liverpool fans were responsible and that failures by police and emergency services contributed to the deaths. Fresh inquests concluded in 2016 with a verdict that the 96 victims then recognised had been unlawfully killed and that the fans bore no responsibility. Campaigners' long fight for accountability became a landmark in British public life; "Justice for the 97" remains the campaign slogan.
