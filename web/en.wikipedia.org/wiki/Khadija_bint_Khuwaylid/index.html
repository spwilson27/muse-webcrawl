Khadija bint Khuwaylid

Khadija bint Khuwaylid (Arabic: خديجة بنت خويلد, c. 554 – 619 CE) was the first wife of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and the first person to convert to Islam. A successful and respected merchant of the Quraysh tribe in Mecca, she employed Muhammad to manage her trading caravans and later proposed marriage to him.

Life

Khadija was a wealthy widow who ran her own trade business. Impressed by Muhammad's honesty — he was known as al-Amin, "the trustworthy" — she married him around 595 CE; by most accounts he was 25 and she was older, often given as 40, though some sources suggest she was younger. Their marriage was monogamous and lasted about 25 years until her death. They had several children, including Fatima.

Role in early Islam

When Muhammad received his first revelation in 610, Khadija was the first to believe him, offering crucial emotional and financial support during the difficult early years of the new faith. She died in 619, in the "Year of Sorrow", shortly before Muhammad's migration to Medina. Muslims honour her as one of the four greatest women and as "Mother of the Believers".
