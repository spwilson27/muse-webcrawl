Tiffany &amp; Co. (colloquially known as Tiffany's) is an American luxury jewellery and specialty design house headquartered in New York City. Founded in 1837 by Charles Lewis Tiffany and John B. Young as a stationery and fancy-goods store in Lower Manhattan, it became renowned for jewellery, particularly diamond and sterling silver pieces, and for its signature robin's-egg blue colour (Tiffany Blue).

History

Under Charles Lewis Tiffany — dubbed the "King of Diamonds" — the company bought celebrated jewels including stones from the French Crown Jewels and the 128.54-carat Tiffany Yellow Diamond. It introduced the Tiffany Setting engagement ring in 1886 and set standards such as the .925 sterling silver standard adopted by the United States. The flagship store at Fifth Avenue and 57th Street (opened 1940) was immortalised by the 1961 film Breakfast at Tiffany's.

Recent ownership

After a contested takeover process, the French luxury group LVMH acquired Tiffany &amp; Co. in January 2021 for about US$15.8 billion, its largest acquisition at the time. The company operates roughly 300 stores worldwide and designs jewellery, watches, fragrances and accessories, with notable design collaborations including Elsa Peretti, Paloma Picasso and Jean Schlumberger.
