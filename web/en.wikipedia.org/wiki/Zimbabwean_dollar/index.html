The Zimbabwean dollar was the name given to four distinct currencies in Zimbabwe between 1980 and 2009. Introduced at independence to replace the Rhodesian dollar at par, the first dollar was initially worth more than the US dollar, but a long economic decline culminated in one of the worst episodes of hyperinflation ever recorded. The currency was effectively abandoned in 2009 in favour of a multi-currency system, formally demonetised in 2015, and the name revived in weakened forms afterwards before the introduction of the ZiG in 2024.

First dollar (1980–2006)

The first Zimbabwean dollar exchanged at roughly US$1.47 to Z$1 on introduction in 1980. Coins and notes featured the Zimbabwe Bird and, on higher denominations, balancing rocks and Victoria Falls imagery. Through the 1990s and early 2000s, fiscal deficits, land reform disruption and falling export earnings drove accelerating inflation, met from 2003 by bearer cheques as cash substitutes.

Redenominations

In August 2006 the Reserve Bank struck three zeros (Operation Sunrise), creating the second dollar. Inflation nevertheless climbed into the millions of percent; in August 2008 ten more zeros were removed for the third dollar. In February 2009, with official annual inflation estimated at 231 million percent (and independent estimates far higher), twelve further zeros were struck for the fourth dollar. Notes of the era — culminating in the famous $100 trillion note — became collectors' items.

Abandonment and aftermath

In early 2009 the government authorised transactions in foreign currencies, and in April 2009 the Zimbabwean dollar was suspended indefinitely. Demonetisation in 2015 exchanged old balances at US$1 per 35 quadrillion fourth-dollar units. A surrogate "bond note" system and the 2019 RTGS dollar (later formally renamed the Zimbabwean dollar) failed to restore confidence, and in April 2024 Zimbabwe introduced the gold-backed ZiG as its new currency.
