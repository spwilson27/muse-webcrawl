WorkChoices was the name given by the Australian Government to amendments made to the Workplace Relations Act 1996 by the Workplace Relations Amendment (Work Choices) Act 2005, which came into effect on 27 March 2006. The changes were the centrepiece of the industrial relations policy of the Howard Government and substantially restructured Australia's labour law, shifting the system toward individual agreements and reducing the role of industrial awards and unions.

Background

Following the Coalition's victory in the 2004 federal election, the Government gained control of both houses of Parliament for the first time in decades, allowing it to pass industrial relations reforms that had previously been blocked in the Senate. The legislation was introduced in late 2005 and passed amid intense public debate and protest.

Main features

The reforms introduced Australian Workplace Agreements (AWAs) as a favoured form of employment contract, limited the matters that could be included in awards, restricted the circumstances in which industrial action was lawful, exempted businesses with up to 100 employees from unfair dismissal laws, and established the Australian Fair Pay Commission to set the federal minimum wage. A "fairness test" was added in 2007 after sustained criticism that the original scheme disadvantaged low-paid workers.

Opposition and repeal

WorkChoices was fiercely opposed by the Australian Council of Trade Unions, whose "Your Rights at Work" campaign is credited with contributing to the Coalition's defeat at the 2007 federal election. The incoming Rudd Labor Government abolished AWAs and replaced the framework with the Fair Work Act 2009, under which WorkChoices ceased to operate.
