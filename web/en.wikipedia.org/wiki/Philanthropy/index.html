Philanthropy

Philanthropy is a form of altruism that consists of private initiatives for the public good, focusing on quality of life. The word derives from the Greek philanthrōpia — "love of humanity". In its modern sense, philanthropy usually means the donation of money, property, time or expertise by individuals, foundations and corporations to charitable causes, in contrast to government-funded welfare or business initiatives aimed at profit.

History

Charitable giving is ancient — religious traditions including Christian charity, Islamic zakat and waqf endowments, and Jewish tzedakah all institutionalised aid to the poor. Modern organised philanthropy emerged in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, when industrial fortunes funded large foundations and public goods: Andrew Carnegie, who argued in "The Gospel of Wealth" (1889) that the rich were trustees of their surplus wealth, funded thousands of public libraries; John D. Rockefeller created the Rockefeller Foundation (1913), which helped eradicate hookworm and yellow fever and funded the Green Revolution.

Modern philanthropy

Today the largest philanthropic institutions include the Gates Foundation, the Wellcome Trust and the Ford Foundation, funding global health, education and poverty programmes. In 2010 Warren Buffett and Bill and Melinda Gates launched the Giving Pledge, encouraging billionaires to commit the majority of their wealth to philanthropy. Individual charitable giving in the United States alone exceeds $500 billion annually, most of it from individuals rather than foundations or corporations.

Approaches and criticism

Approaches range from traditional grant-making to venture philanthropy, impact investing and "effective altruism", which advocates using evidence and cost-effectiveness analysis to maximise the good done per dollar. Critics question the democratic legitimacy of large tax-advantaged foundations, their influence on public policy, and cases where philanthropy burnishes the reputations of donors whose wealth came from harmful practices.
