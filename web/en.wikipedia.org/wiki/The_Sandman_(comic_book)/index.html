The Sandman is a comic book series written by Neil Gaiman and published by DC Comics, initially under its Vertigo imprint. It ran for 75 issues from 1989 to 1996 and is widely regarded as one of the greatest graphic novel series of all time.

Premise and story

The series follows Dream, also called Morpheus, the personification of dreams and one of the seven Endless — siblings who embody fundamental aspects of existence: Destiny, Death, Dream, Destruction, Desire, Despair, and Delirium. In 1916 Dream is captured by an occult ritual intended to imprison Death, and is held for seventy years. Escaping in 1988, he must recover his tools of office — his helm, his pouch of sand, and his dreamstone — and rebuild his realm, the Dreaming, while reckoning with how his long absence and his own rigid character have harmed others. Later arcs, including A Doll's House, Dream Country, Season of Mists, A Game of You, Brief Lives, Worlds' End, and The Kindly Ones, weave horror, fantasy, history, and mythology, drawing on Shakespeare, classical myth, and DC Comics lore.

Reception and influence

The Sandman was one of Vertigo's flagship titles and one of the first graphic novels to reach mainstream bestseller lists and a readership beyond traditional comics fans. Its story "A Midsummer Night's Dream" won the World Fantasy Award for Best Short Fiction in 1991, prompting a rule change so that comics would no longer be eligible in that category. The series won numerous Eisner Awards. A television adaptation premiered on Netflix in 2022, starring Tom Sturridge as Dream.
