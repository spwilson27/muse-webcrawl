Optical microscope

The optical microscope, also called a light microscope, uses visible light and a system of lenses to magnify images of small objects. Compound microscopes, developed around the end of the sixteenth and beginning of the seventeenth century and improved by Antonie van Leeuwenhoek and others, employ an objective lens and an eyepiece to achieve magnifications commonly up to about 1,000–2,000 times.

Resolution is limited by the wavelength of light to roughly 200 nanometres, so structures smaller than this cannot be distinguished with conventional techniques. Variants include bright-field, dark-field, phase-contrast and fluorescence microscopy, each suited to different specimens.

Optical microscopes remain fundamental tools in biology, medicine, materials science and education, and modern techniques such as confocal and super-resolution microscopy extend their capabilities beyond the classical diffraction limit.
