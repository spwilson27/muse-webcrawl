Systematic desensitization

Systematic desensitization, also known as graduated exposure therapy, is a type of behaviour therapy developed by the South African psychiatrist Joseph Wolpe and introduced in his 1958 book Psychotherapy by Reciprocal Inhibition. It is used to overcome phobias and other anxiety disorders.

Procedure

The therapy proceeds in three stages. First, the patient learns a relaxation technique such as deep muscle relaxation or controlled breathing. Second, therapist and patient construct an anxiety hierarchy: a ranked list of feared situations from least to most distressing. Third, while relaxed, the patient is exposed — first in imagination, and later often in real life — to progressively more frightening items on the hierarchy. The relaxation response, being incompatible with anxiety, is thought to countercondition the fear response; this mechanism is called reciprocal inhibition.

Evidence

Systematic desensitization and modern exposure therapies are among the most effective psychological treatments for specific phobias, and exposure-based methods are also used for panic disorder, social anxiety, and PTSD.
