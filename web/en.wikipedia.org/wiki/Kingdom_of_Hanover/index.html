Kingdom of Hanover

Years: 1814 – 1866

Capital: Hanover

First king: George III

Last king: George V

The Kingdom of Hanover (German: Königreich Hannover) was a state in northwestern Germany established at the Congress of Vienna in 1814 from the former Electorate of Hanover. Until 1837 its kings were simultaneously kings of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland, in the personal union that had begun in 1714.

History

The restored electorate was elevated to a kingdom and enlarged with East Frisia, Osnabrück and other territories. Because Hanover followed Salic law, the personal union ended on the death of William IV in 1837: Victoria inherited the British throne, while Hanover passed to Ernest Augustus I, who promptly annulled the liberal constitution of 1833, prompting the protest of the Göttingen Seven.

Austro-Prussian War and annexation

Hanover's last king, the blind George V, sided with Austria in the Austro-Prussian War of 1866. Hanoverian forces won a tactical success at Langensalza but were compelled to surrender days later, and Prussia annexed the kingdom outright, converting it into the Province of Hanover. George V went into exile in Austria and never renounced his claim; the Hanoverian Guelph Party continued to agitate for restoration for decades within the German Empire.
