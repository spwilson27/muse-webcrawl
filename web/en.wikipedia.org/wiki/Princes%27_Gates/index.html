Princes' Gates

Princes' Gates
Location: Exhibition Place, Toronto, Canada
Opened: 1927
Architects: Chapman and Oxley
Style: Beaux-Arts

The Princes' Gates is a monumental Beaux-Arts triumphal arch at the eastern entrance to Exhibition Place in Toronto, Ontario, Canada, and the traditional ceremonial entrance to the Canadian National Exhibition (CNE).

History

The gates were designed by the Toronto firm Chapman and Oxley and opened on 30 August 1927 by Edward, Prince of Wales (later Edward VIII) and Prince George, Duke of Kent, for whom they are named. Their opening marked the CNE's jubilee year.

Architecture

The structure consists of a central arch flanked by colonnades stretching some 300 feet, topped by a winged figure representing progress. It is built of concrete finished to resemble stone and has been restored several times, remaining a Toronto landmark at the foot of Strachan Avenue.
