Paper clip

A paper clip (or paperclip) is a device used to hold sheets of paper together, usually made of steel wire bent into a looped shape. The familiar "Gem" design, with two parallel loops, dates from the 1890s.

History

Early patents for bent-wire clips were granted in the United States in 1867 and later decades; the Gem clip became standard in Britain and America. A Norwegian patent by Johan Vaaler (1899–1901) is often popularly cited, though his design differed from the common clip; the paper clip nevertheless became a Norwegian national symbol, notably in resistance displays during World War II.

Other uses

Paper clips are used as improvised tools — ejecting SIM trays, resetting electronics, and picking simple locks — and famously in the thought experiment about a "paperclip maximizer" artificial intelligence.
