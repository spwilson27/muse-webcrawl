Video game localization

Video game localization is the process of preparing a video game for release in a region outside its original market, encompassing translation of text and dialogue, voice re-recording, cultural adaptation, and compliance with local ratings and law.

Levels

Industry models range from no localization, through box-and-manual translation, partial localization (subtitles), to full localization with dubbed voice acting. "Sim-ship" — simultaneous shipment in all languages — became standard for major releases in the 2010s.

Challenges

Games present unique difficulties: non-linear and variable-driven text, character limits inherited from hardware, humour and idiom, and lip-sync. Early notorious translations ("All your base are belong to us") gave way to professional localisation teams and dedicated QA. Culturalisation goes beyond language, adjusting imagery, gestures and content to regional sensibilities.

Industry

Major localization vendors and in-house studios handle dozens of languages; fan translations continue to fill gaps for unreleased titles.
