Royal Opera House

The Bow Street facade of the Royal Opera House

The Royal Opera House is an opera house and performing arts venue in Covent Garden, central London. It is home to The Royal Opera, The Royal Ballet and the Orchestra of the Royal Opera House, and is often referred to simply as "Covent Garden".

History

The first theatre on the site opened in 1732 under actor-manager John Rich. The present building, the third on the site, was designed by Edward Middleton Barry and opened in 1858 after its predecessor burned down. The portico, foyer and auditorium survive from this building; almost everything else was rebuilt during a major redevelopment in the 1990s, which added the Floral Hall (now the Paul Hamlyn Hall) and new backstage facilities.

Companies

The Royal Opera is one of the world's leading opera companies, performing a repertoire from Handel and Mozart to new commissions. The Royal Ballet, founded by Ninette de Valois and resident at the house since 1946, is the largest of Britain's five major ballet companies.

The building

The main auditorium seats 2,256 people across four tiers of boxes and balconies. A smaller space, the Linbury Theatre, hosts experimental opera, ballet and theatre.
