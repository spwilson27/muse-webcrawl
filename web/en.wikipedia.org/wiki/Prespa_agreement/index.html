Prespa agreement

The Prespa agreement, also known as the Prespes agreement, Prespa accord or Treaty of Prespa, is an agreement reached in 2018 between Greece and the then Republic of Macedonia, under the auspices of the United Nations, resolving the long-standing Macedonia naming dispute. It was signed on 17 June 2018 on the shore of Lake Prespa, near the village of Psarades, and entered into force on 12 February 2019.

Terms

Under the agreement, the Republic of Macedonia changed its name to the Republic of North Macedonia, for all uses, domestic and international (erga omnes). Greece recognised the Macedonian language and nationality (as "Macedonian/citizen of the Republic of North Macedonia") while the agreement affirmed that these had no relation to the ancient Macedonian heritage of Greek Macedonia, and Skopje undertook to review monuments and school textbooks for irredentist content. In return, Greece withdrew its veto on North Macedonia's NATO and EU accession.

Ratification

A referendum in Macedonia in September 2018 showed over 90% support among voters but turnout below the 50% threshold; the Macedonian parliament proceeded to pass the constitutional amendments in January 2019, and the Greek parliament ratified the agreement on 25 January 2019 despite large protests. North Macedonia joined NATO in March 2020.

Negotiators

The deal was negotiated by prime ministers Alexis Tsipras and Zoran Zaev and foreign ministers Nikos Kotzias and Nikola Dimitrov, with UN mediator Matthew Nimetz. Tsipras and Zaev were widely praised internationally and nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize, though both faced domestic backlash.
