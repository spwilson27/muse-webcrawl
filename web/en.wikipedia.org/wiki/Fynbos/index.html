Fynbos

Fynbos (Afrikaans pronunciation: [ˈfɛinbɔs], "fine bush") is a small belt of natural shrubland or heathland vegetation located in the Western Cape and Eastern Cape provinces of South Africa. It grows in the Cape Floristic Region, the smallest but richest of the world's six floral kingdoms, famous for exceptionally high plant diversity and endemism.

Ecology

Fynbos vegetation is dominated by three plant families: Proteaceae (such as proteas, the national flower of South Africa being the king protea), Ericaceae (heaths), and Restionaceae (Cape reeds). The soils are nutrient-poor, and the vegetation is adapted to a Mediterranean climate and to recurring fire, which many species require for seed germination. Of the region's roughly 9,000 plant species, around two-thirds occur nowhere else.

Conservation

Fynbos is threatened by agriculture, urban expansion around Cape Town, and invasive alien plants such as Australian acacias and pines. Large areas are protected within the Cape Floral Region Protected Areas, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Fynbos plants of economic value include rooibos and honeybush teas and a major wildflower export industry.
