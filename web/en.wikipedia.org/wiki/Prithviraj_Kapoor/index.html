Prithviraj Kapoor (3 November 1906 – 29 May 1972) was an Indian actor and pioneer of Indian theatre and cinema, and the patriarch of the Kapoor family. He founded Prithvi Theatre in 1944 and starred in India's first talkie, Alam Ara (1931).

Career

Born in Samundri and educated in Peshawar, Kapoor began acting in silent films in Bombay in 1929 and appeared in Alam Ara, the first Indian sound film. He joined the Indian People's Theatre Association and in 1944 founded his own travelling company, Prithvi Theatres, whose socially conscious plays — such as Deewar, Pathan and Paisa — toured India for 16 years and launched many careers. His major film roles include Alexander in Sikandar (1941) and the emperor Akbar in Mughal-e-Azam (1960).

Legacy

Kapoor was nominated to the Rajya Sabha and received the Padma Bhushan in 1969 and the Dadasaheb Phalke Award in 1971. His sons Raj, Shammi and Shashi Kapoor became leading film actors; the family remains central to Hindi cinema. Bombay's Prithvi Theatre, built in his memory in Juhu by Shashi Kapoor and Jennifer Kendal, opened in 1978 and is a landmark of Indian theatre.
