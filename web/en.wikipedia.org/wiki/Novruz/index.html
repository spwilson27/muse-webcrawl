Novruz

Novruz (also spelled Nowruz, Navruz, Nooruz, Nevruz and Nauryz, from Persian نوروز, "new day") is the traditional New Year festival marking the spring equinox, usually celebrated on 20 or 21 March. Originating in the Zoroastrian tradition of ancient Persia, it is celebrated by an estimated 300 million people across Iran, Central Asia, the Caucasus, Turkey, Afghanistan, parts of the Balkans and the Middle East, and by diaspora communities worldwide.

Customs

Preparations include spring cleaning (khane tekani), growing sprouted wheat or lentils (sabzeh), and setting the haft-sin table with seven items beginning with the Persian letter "sin", symbolising rebirth, health, prosperity and other hopes for the year. Festivities commonly include visiting family, exchanging gifts, egg painting and decorating, special foods such as sumalak in Central Asia and fish with herbed rice in Iran, and in some regions jumping over bonfires on the last Wednesday eve of the old year (Chaharshanbe Suri).

Recognition

In 2009 Novruz was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, and the United Nations proclaimed 21 March the International Day of Nowruz. The festival is a public holiday lasting several days in many countries, including Iran, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Tajikistan.
