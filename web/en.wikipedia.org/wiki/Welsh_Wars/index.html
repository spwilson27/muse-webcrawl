Welsh Wars

The Welsh Wars were a series of conflicts between the Welsh principalities and first the Anglo-Norman Marcher lords and later the English Crown, fought intermittently from the Norman invasion of Wales in the late 11th century until the final conquest of Wales by Edward I in 1282–83, with later revolts including the rising of Owain Glyndŵr in the early 15th century.

Norman incursions

After 1066, Norman barons granted lands along the Welsh border (the Marches) gradually conquered the lowlands of south and east Wales, building motte-and-bailey castles, while Welsh rulers retained the mountainous north and west. Princes such as Gruffudd ap Llywelyn briefly reunified much of Wales before his defeat in 1063. In the 13th century the princes of Gwynedd — Llywelyn the Great and his grandson Llywelyn ap Gruffudd — extended Welsh authority, and Llywelyn ap Gruffudd was recognised as Prince of Wales by Henry III in the Treaty of Montgomery (1267).

Edwardian conquest

Edward I invaded Wales in 1277, forcing Llywelyn to submit under the Treaty of Aberconwy, and again in 1282 after a Welsh revolt led by Llywelyn's brother Dafydd. Llywelyn was killed at the Battle of Orewin Bridge in December 1282 and Dafydd was captured and executed in 1283. The Statute of Rhuddlan (1284) annexed the principality to the English Crown, and Edward consolidated control with a ring of great castles — Conwy, Caernarfon, Harlech and Beaumaris among them — and new English boroughs.

Later revolts

Major later uprisings included those of Madog ap Llywelyn (1294–95) and Owain Glyndŵr (1400–c. 1415), the last great Welsh revolt, which briefly established Welsh parliaments and alliances with France and Scotland before being suppressed.
