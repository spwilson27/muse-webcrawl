How to Read a Book

The classic guide to intelligent reading, first published in 1940.

How to Read a Book is a book about reading comprehension and literary criticism by the philosopher Mortimer J. Adler. First published in 1940, it was extensively revised in 1972 in a new edition co-authored by Charles Van Doren, which remains the best-known version. The book presents reading not as a passive pastime but as an active skill that can be practised at progressively deeper levels.

Levels of reading

Adler distinguishes four cumulative levels of reading:

  • Elementary reading — basic literacy: learning to decode words and sentences.
  • Inspectional reading — systematic skimming: examining a book's structure, preface, table of contents and index in a limited time to decide what it offers.
  • Analytical reading — thorough, careful reading aimed at full understanding: classifying the book, stating its problem and argument, finding its key terms and propositions, and judging it fairly.
  • Syntopical reading — reading many books on the same subject comparatively, placing them in relation to one another and to a question the reader brings to them.

Argument and influence

The book argues that the difference between good and poor readers is chiefly activity: the good reader asks questions — What is the book about as a whole? What is being said in detail, and how? Is it true, in whole or part? What of it? — and answers them from the text. The 1972 edition expanded the treatment to different kinds of reading matter, including practical books, imaginative literature, history, science and philosophy, and appended a list of recommended "Great Books". The work has remained in print for decades and is widely cited in discussions of liberal education and the Great Books movement, with which Adler was closely associated.
