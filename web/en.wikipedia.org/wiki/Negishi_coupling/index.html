Negishi coupling

The Negishi coupling is a widely employed transition-metal-catalysed cross-coupling reaction in organic chemistry. The reaction couples an organozinc compound with an organic halide (or pseudohalide) in the presence of a nickel or palladium catalyst to form a new carbon–carbon bond.

The reaction is named after Ei-ichi Negishi of Purdue University, who reported it in 1977 and who shared the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Richard Heck and Akira Suzuki "for palladium-catalyzed cross couplings in organic synthesis".

Mechanism

The catalytic cycle consists of three steps: oxidative addition of the organic halide to the low-valent metal centre; transmetalation, in which the organic group on zinc is transferred to the metal; and reductive elimination, which releases the coupled product and regenerates the catalyst.

Scope

Organozinc reagents are more reactive than the boronic acids used in Suzuki coupling but remain compatible with many functional groups, including ketones, esters, amines and nitriles. Both sp2–sp2, sp2–sp3 and sp–sp2 couplings can be achieved. The reaction has been used in the total synthesis of numerous complex natural products.
