Osmanya script

The Osmanya script (Somali: Farta Cismaanya), also known as Far Soomaali, is a writing system created to transcribe the Somali language. It was invented between 1920 and 1922 by Osman Yusuf Kenadid, of the Majeerteen Sultanate, a nephew of Sultan Yusuf Ali Kenadid of the Sultanate of Hobyo.

History

While Osmanya gained reasonably wide acceptance in Somalia and was rapidly used to produce a great deal of literature, it proved difficult to spread among the general population, partly due to stiff competition from the long-established Arabic script as well as the emerging Somali Latin alphabet developed by the linguist Shire Jama Ahmed. As nationalist sentiment grew after the Second World War, a search began for a universally recognized national script; after years of discussion, the Latin-based alphabet designed by Ahmed was adopted in 1972 as the official writing script in the Somali Democratic Republic.

Description

The direction of reading and writing in Osmanya is from left to right. Letter names are based on the names of letters in Arabic, and the vowels are represented by diacritic-like letters. The alphabet has 22 consonants and 5 vowels. The script was encoded in the Unicode Standard in version 4.0, in the Osmanya block (U+10480–U+104AF).
