Masan

Masan is an administrative region of Changwon, South Gyeongsang Province, South Korea. Formerly an independent city, Masan merged with Changwon and Jinhae in 2010 to form the integrated city of Changwon. It comprises the two districts of Masanhappo-gu and Masanhoewon-gu.

History

Masan grew as a trading port from the late Joseon period and opened to foreign trade in 1899. It developed into an important industrial and export-processing centre in the 1970s, when the Masan Free Export Zone (established 1970) attracted textile and electronics manufacturing. The city played a role in South Korea's democratisation movement: in 1979 the Bu-Ma Democratic Protests, centred on Busan and Masan, helped trigger the crisis that ended the Park Chung-hee regime.

Geography and culture

Masan faces Masan Bay on the Korea Strait and is ringed by mountains including Muhaksan. It is known for its fish market — one of Korea's largest — and for agu-jjim, a spicy braised monkfish dish invented locally. Major employers historically included textile, machinery and shipbuilding-related suppliers; the waterfront has been redeveloped with the Marine New Town project.

Transport

Masan station serves the Gyeongjeon Line, and the port handles coastal and international shipping, including ferries to Japan in the past.
