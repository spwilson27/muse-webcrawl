Christian Reformed Church in North America


Placeholder image: a Christian Reformed congregation

The Christian Reformed Church in North America (CRCNA; Dutch: Christelijke Gereformeerde Kerken in Noord-Amerika) is a Protestant Christian denomination in the United States and Canada, with roots in the Dutch Reformed tradition. It has roughly a thousand congregations and about 200,000–250,000 members. Its administrative offices are in Grand Rapids, Michigan, and Burlington, Ontario.

History

The denomination was founded in 1857, when four congregations of Dutch immigrants in western Michigan seceded from the Reformed Church in America over concerns including hymn singing, open communion and insufficient confessional strictness. Under the influence of the Dutch Afscheiding (Secession of 1834) and later of theologian Abraham Kuyper's neo-Calvinism, the church grew with successive waves of Dutch immigration, especially after the Second World War.

Beliefs and practices

The CRCNA subscribes to the ecumenical creeds and the Three Forms of Unity: the Belgic Confession, the Heidelberg Catechism and the Canons of Dort. Its contemporary testimony is Our World Belongs to God. The church maintains a strong tradition of Christian education, supporting hundreds of Christian day schools and Calvin University and Calvin Theological Seminary in Grand Rapids. Synod voted in 1995 to permit women to serve as elders, ministers and evangelists subject to classis approval, a decision that prompted conservative departures, including the formation of the United Reformed Churches in North America.

Agencies

Denominational ministries include Resonate Global Mission, World Renew (development and disaster response), Back to God Ministries International (the media ministry behind the Back to God Hour), and Faith Alive Christian Resources.
