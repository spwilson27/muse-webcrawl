Spider

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Spiders are air-breathing arthropods of the order Araneae, with two body segments, eight legs, fangs (chelicerae) that usually inject venom, and spinnerets that produce silk. With more than 50,000 described species, they are found on every continent except Antarctica and in nearly every habitat, and are among the world's most important predators of insects.

Biology

Spider silk — produced in several types for webs, draglines, egg sacs and wrapping prey — is extraordinarily strong and elastic for its weight. Hunting strategies range from orb webs and trapdoors to active hunters such as jumping spiders, which have excellent vision. Most spiders are harmless to humans; medically significant bites come from a few groups such as widow and recluse spiders and the Australian funnel-webs, and antivenoms exist.

Ecology and culture

By eating vast numbers of insects, spiders provide major pest control in farms and forests. They inspire both arachnophobia — one of the commonest specific phobias — and admiration in myth and popular culture. Spider silk and venom components are studied for materials and medicines.
