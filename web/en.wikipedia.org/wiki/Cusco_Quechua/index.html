Cusco Quechua (Quechua: Qusqu qhichwa simi) is a dialect of Southern Quechua spoken by some 1.5 million people in the Cusco Region of Peru. It is the variety historically associated with the Inca Empire, whose capital was Cusco, and it carries considerable prestige among Quechua varieties.

Characteristics

Like other Southern Quechua varieties, Cusco Quechua is agglutinative, building words by stacking suffixes onto roots, and it distinguishes inclusive and exclusive first-person plural pronouns. A feature shared with the neighbouring Ayacucho variety is the retention of a contrast in the stop consonants: Cusco Quechua has a three-way distinction among plain, aspirated, and ejective stops at bilabial, alveolar, velar, and uvular places of articulation — a feature attributed to influence from Aymara.

The dialect has been influenced by Spanish over five centuries of contact, borrowing vocabulary and some phonological patterns, but it has in turn influenced the local Spanish of the Cusco region.

Writing

Debate over the orthography of Cusco Quechua has been vigorous. The Academia Mayor de la Lengua Quechua, based in Cusco, promotes a five-vowel alphabet reflecting the Spanish-influenced pronunciation of vowels near uvular consonants, while the Peruvian Ministry of Education uses a three-vowel phonemic alphabet in which those variants are written with the same letters as their non-uvular counterparts. Cusco Quechua has a written tradition going back to the colonial period, including religious texts and the famous drama Ollantay, and today it is broadcast on radio and taught in intercultural bilingual education programs.
