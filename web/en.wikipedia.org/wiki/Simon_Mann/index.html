Simon Mann

Born26 June 1952, Aldershot, England
ServiceBritish Army; Scots Guards; SAS
Known forExecutive Outcomes, Sandline; 2004 Equatorial Guinea coup attempt

Simon Francis Mann (born 26 June 1952) is a British former army officer and mercenary. Educated at Eton and Sandhurst, he was commissioned into the Scots Guards and later served in the Special Air Service (SAS). After leaving the army he worked in computer security and in the oil business.

Private military career

In the 1990s Mann co-founded the private military companies Executive Outcomes and Sandline International, which supplied military training, combat support and security services to governments in Angola, Sierra Leone and elsewhere. Executive Outcomes' operations in Angola (against UNITA) and Sierra Leone (against the RUF rebels) made it the best-known of the post-Cold War private military firms. Mann was also involved in the controversial 1998 "Arms to Africa" affair connected with Sandline's shipment of weapons to Sierra Leone.

The Wonga coup

In March 2004 Mann was arrested at Harare airport in Zimbabwe when the Boeing 727 carrying him and some 70 men stopped to pick up weapons; he was the operational leader of a planned coup d'état in oil-rich Equatorial Guinea, intended to overthrow President Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo and install the exiled politician Severo Moto. The plot — nicknamed the "Wonga coup" — became an international scandal, drawing in figures including Sir Mark Thatcher, who pleaded guilty in South Africa to negligently financing a helicopter connected with the plot.

Mann served four years in Zimbabwe's Chikurubi prison before extradition to Equatorial Guinea in 2008, where he was sentenced to 34 years' imprisonment. In November 2009 he received a presidential pardon on humanitarian grounds and was released, returning to the United Kingdom. He later wrote a memoir, Cry Havoc (2011), about his experiences.
