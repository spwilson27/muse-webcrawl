Judah ibn Tibbon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: medieval Hebrew manuscript translation]

Judah ben Saul ibn Tibbon (c. 1120 – c. 1190) was a Jewish physician and translator in southern France. Born in Granada, Spain, he settled in Lunel, Provence, where he became known as the "father of translators" for his pioneering Hebrew translations of major Judeo-Arabic philosophical and scientific works.

Translations

Ibn Tibbon translated into Hebrew such foundational works as Judah Halevi's Kuzari, Bahya ibn Paquda's Duties of the Heart, Saadia Gaon's Book of Beliefs and Opinions, Ibn Gabirol's Improvement of the Moral Qualities, and Jonah ibn Janah's Arabic grammar. His translations created much of the Hebrew philosophical vocabulary used for centuries afterward.

Legacy

He founded a dynasty of translators: his son Samuel ibn Tibbon famously translated Maimonides' Guide for the Perplexed. Judah's ethical will, addressed to Samuel, is a noted work of medieval Jewish literature.
