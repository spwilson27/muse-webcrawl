The Medina of Marrakech is the old walled city (medina quarter) of Marrakech, Morocco. Founded with the city itself in 1070 by the Almoravid emir Abu Bakr ibn Umar, it contains the historic core of the city, including its principal monuments, souks (markets), palaces, mosques and the vast square of Jemaa el-Fnaa. In 1985 the medina was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Marrakech was founded around 1070 as the capital of the Almoravid Empire. The first walls of the medina, built of the region's characteristic red rammed earth (giving Marrakech its nickname, the "Red City" or "Ochre City"), were erected under Ali ibn Yusuf in 1126–1127. Under the Almohads, who took the city in 1147, major monuments such as the Koutoubia Mosque were constructed. Later dynasties, including the Saadians in the 16th century, added palaces and tombs, including the Saadian Tombs and El Badi Palace.

Landmarks

Key sites within the medina include:

  • Jemaa el-Fnaa — the great central square and marketplace, a focus of storytellers, musicians, food stalls and performers; the cultural space of the square was proclaimed by UNESCO a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.
  • Koutoubia Mosque — the largest mosque in Marrakech, with a 77-metre Almohad minaret that dominates the skyline.
  • Bahia Palace — a lavish 19th-century vizier's palace.
  • Saadian Tombs — the royal necropolis of the Saadian dynasty, rediscovered in 1917.
  • Ben Youssef Madrasa — a celebrated Islamic college founded in the Marinid/Almoravid era and rebuilt under the Saadians.
  • The souks — a labyrinth of specialised market streets selling leather goods, carpets, metalwork, spices and textiles.

City walls and gates

The medina is enclosed by some 19 kilometres of ramparts pierced by historic gates (bab), including Bab Agnaou, Bab Doukkala and Bab el-Khemis. The walls are built of reddish clay and chalk, renewed repeatedly over the centuries.
