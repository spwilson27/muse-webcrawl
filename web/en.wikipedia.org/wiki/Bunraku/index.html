Bunraku

Bunraku (文楽), also known as ningyō jōruri, is a form of traditional Japanese puppet theatre founded in Osaka in the beginning of the 17th century. Three kinds of performers combine: puppeteers (ningyōtsukai), chanters (tayū), and shamisen musicians. In 2008 UNESCO inscribed bunraku on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Performance

Each main puppet — roughly one-half to two-thirds life-size — is operated by three puppeteers dressed in black, the chief manipulating head and right arm with face uncovered. A single chanter voices all characters while a shamisen player accompanies. Plays divide into historical dramas (jidaimono) and domestic plays (sewamono), often about love suicides.

History

The art takes its name from Uemura Bunrakuken, an early-19th-century puppeteer. Its golden age featured the playwright Chikamatsu Monzaemon, often called Japan's Shakespeare. The National Bunraku Theatre in Osaka is its principal home today.
