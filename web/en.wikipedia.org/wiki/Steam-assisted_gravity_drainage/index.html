Steam-assisted gravity drainage

Steam-assisted gravity drainage (SAGD) is an enhanced oil recovery technique used to extract heavy crude oil and bitumen, most extensively in the Athabasca oil sands of Alberta, Canada. It was developed in the 1970s and 1980s, notably by Roger Butler at Imperial Oil.

Process

Two horizontal wells are drilled one above the other, typically about five metres apart. Steam is injected into the upper well, heating the surrounding bitumen until its viscosity falls enough for it to drain by gravity into the lower production well, from which it is pumped to the surface. The heated zone, called the steam chamber, grows upward and sideways over time.

Advantages and concerns

SAGD can recover a high proportion of oil in place without surface mining, but it is energy-intensive: large volumes of natural gas are burned to raise steam, giving the method a significant carbon footprint and substantial water requirements. Variants such as solvent-assisted SAGD aim to reduce steam use.
