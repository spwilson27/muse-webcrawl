Glass is an amorphous (non-crystalline) solid, most commonly a silicate: soda-lime glass made from silica sand, soda ash and limestone, melted and cooled so fast that crystals cannot form. Transparent, hard but brittle, glass is used for windows, bottles, lenses and mirrors, tableware, fibres (fibreglass, optical fibre) and laboratory ware (borosilicate, e.g. Pyrex).

Humans made glass beads in Mesopotamia and Egypt by about 1500 BC; glassblowing (1st century BC, Roman Syria) democratised vessels; medieval stained glass, Venetian cristallo, and the float-glass process (Pilkington, 1950s — molten glass floated on tin) made today's flat, flawless panes. Glass is infinitely recyclable without quality loss, though collection economics vary. Modern special glasses include Gorilla Glass for phones, telescope mirrors, and the fused-silica optics of lasers and fibre networks that carry the internet.
