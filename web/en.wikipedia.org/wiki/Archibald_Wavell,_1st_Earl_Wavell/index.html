Archibald Wavell, 1st Earl Wavell

Archibald Wavell

Born: 5 May 1883, Colchester

Died: 24 May 1950, London

Rank: Field Marshal, British Army

Offices: Viceroy of India (1943–1947)

Archibald Percival Wavell, 1st Earl Wavell (1883–1950) was a senior officer of the British Army who commanded Allied forces in the Middle East and South East Asia during the Second World War, and later served as the penultimate Viceroy of India. He is remembered both for his brilliant early desert victories and for a famously literary turn of mind — he edited the anthology Other Men's Flowers (1944).

Early career

Educated at Winchester and Sandhurst, Wavell served in the Boer War and on the North-West Frontier of India, losing an eye at the Second Battle of Ypres in 1915. He served under Allenby in Palestine, whose biography he later wrote, and between the wars held senior staff and command appointments, advocating mechanisation and combined-arms warfare.

Second World War

As Commander-in-Chief Middle East from 1939, Wavell directed Operation Compass (December 1940 – February 1941), in which his outnumbered Western Desert Force destroyed the Italian Tenth Army in Libya and took some 130,000 prisoners. Subsequent campaigns in Greece, Crete, Iraq, Syria and East Africa stretched his resources; after the failed Operation Battleaxe against Rommel, Churchill replaced him with Auchinleck in mid-1941, swapping him to the India command. Appointed the first head of the short-lived ABDACOM in early 1942, he could not halt the Japanese advance through Malaya, Singapore and Burma.

Viceroy of India

In 1943 Wavell succeeded Linlithgow as Viceroy. He confronted the aftermath of the Bengal famine, released Gandhi and the Congress leadership, and sought a constitutional settlement through the Simla Conference of 1945, which failed over communal representation. His insistence on practical planning for transfer of power, combined with tensions with London, led to his replacement by Mountbatten in 1947. He was created Earl Wavell and died in 1950.
