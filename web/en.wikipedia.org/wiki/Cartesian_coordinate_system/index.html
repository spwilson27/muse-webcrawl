Overview

A Cartesian coordinate system specifies each point by its signed distances from two (in a plane) or three (in space) mutually perpendicular axes meeting at an origin. It is named after René Descartes, whose La Géométrie of 1637 showed how geometry could be done with algebra — curves became equations, and analytic geometry was born. Pierre de Fermat developed similar ideas independently at about the same time.

Every other common system converts to and from Cartesian coordinates: polar and spherical coordinates in geometry, and projected map coordinates in cartography, where the geographic angles of latitude and longitude are transformed by a map projection into flat x–y grid values measured in metres.
