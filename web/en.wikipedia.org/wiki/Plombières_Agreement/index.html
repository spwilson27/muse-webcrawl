Plombières Agreement

Plombières-les-Bains, site of the secret meeting of July 1858

The Plombières Agreement (Italian: Accordo di Plombières) was a secret understanding reached on 21 July 1858 at Plombières-les-Bains in the Vosges between the French emperor Napoleon III and Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour, prime minister of the Kingdom of Sardinia (Piedmont-Sardinia).

Terms

Cavour sought French military support for a war against the Austrian Empire, the dominant power in northern Italy. Under the agreement, France would join Sardinia in war against Austria if Austria could be made to appear the aggressor. A victorious settlement would reorganise Italy into four states: an enlarged Kingdom of Upper Italy under the House of Savoy (absorbing Lombardy, Venetia, Parma and Modena), a Kingdom of Central Italy, the Papal States reduced to Rome and its surroundings, and the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies left intact. In return, Sardinia would cede Nice and Savoy to France.

Consequences

The agreement was formalised in a treaty of alliance in January 1859, and Cavour successfully provoked Austria into declaring war in April 1859, triggering the Second Italian War of Independence. French and Piedmontese victories at Magenta and Solferino followed, though Napoleon III's separate armistice at Villafranca disappointed Cavour. Nevertheless the agreement set in motion the chain of events that produced the proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy in 1861, a decisive step in Italian unification.
