Pratt & Whitney JT8D

The Pratt & Whitney JT8D is a low-bypass (0.96 to 1) turbofan jet engine introduced by Pratt & Whitney in February 1963 with the inaugural flight of the Boeing 727. It was a modification of the J52 turbojet engine, which powered the A-6 Intruder and other US Navy aircraft. The JT8D powered the Boeing 727, 737-100/-200, and the McDonnell Douglas DC-9 and MD-80 families, becoming one of the best-selling jet engines in history, with more than 14,000 built.

Design and development

The JT8D is a twin-spool, axial-flow turbofan with a single-stage fan, six-stage low-pressure and seven-stage high-pressure compressor, a can-annular combustor, and single-stage high-pressure and three-stage low-pressure turbines. Thrust in the original series ranged from about 12,250 to 17,400 pounds. The later JT8D-200 series, developed for the MD-80, raised bypass ratio and thrust to up to about 21,700 pounds and was quieter and more efficient.

Applications

  • Boeing 727 and Boeing 737-100/-200
  • McDonnell Douglas DC-9, MD-80 series and MD-90 (JT8D-200 series)
  • Dassault Mercure and Sud Aviation Caravelle (later models)
  • Saab 37 Viggen (as the licence-built Volvo RM8 derivative)
