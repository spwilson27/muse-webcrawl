Iron oxide

Iron oxides are chemical compounds composed of iron and oxygen. There are more than sixteen known iron oxides and oxyhydroxides, the best known of which is rust, a form of iron(III) oxide. Iron oxides and oxyhydroxides are widespread in nature and play an important role in many geological and biological processes.

Oxides

Principal oxides include iron(II) oxide (wüstite, FeO), iron(III) oxide (hematite, Fe2O3) and iron(II,III) oxide (magnetite, Fe3O4), which is strongly magnetic. Oxyhydroxides include goethite and lepidocrocite, the main components of rust and of many iron ores.

Uses

Iron oxides are used as iron ores for steel production, as pigments (ochres, siennas and umbers), as catalysts, in thermite, and as the magnetic material in recording media and in magnetite-based applications. Iron oxide pigments have been used since prehistoric cave art.

Biological role

Iron oxides feature in biology in the iron-storage proteins ferritin and in magnetotactic bacteria that biomineralise magnetite crystals.
