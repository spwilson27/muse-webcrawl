Market square

A market square (or marketplace) is an open public square in the centre of a town or city, traditionally used for markets and often surrounded by shops, a town hall, and a church. Market squares were the commercial and civic heart of European towns from the Middle Ages onward.

History and examples

Many towns were founded around a market square laid out under a market charter. Notable examples include the Rynek Główny in Kraków, the largest medieval town square in Europe; the Grand-Place in Brussels; and market squares across Britain such as those in Norwich and Ripon, many marked by a market cross. Modern uses include farmers' markets, fairs, and public gatherings.
