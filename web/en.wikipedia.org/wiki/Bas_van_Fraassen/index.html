Bas van Fraassen

Bastiaan Cornelis van Fraassen (born 5 April 1941) is a Dutch-American philosopher noted for his contributions to philosophy of science and logic. Born in Goes, the Netherlands, he emigrated with his family to Edmonton, Canada, and studied at the University of Alberta and the University of Pittsburgh, where he received his PhD in 1966. He taught at Yale, the University of Toronto, the University of Southern California and Princeton University (1982–2008), and later at San Francisco State University.

Constructive empiricism

Van Fraassen is best known for constructive empiricism, set out in The Scientific Image (1980), one of the most influential books in modern philosophy of science. On this view, the aim of science is not truth about unobservable entities but empirical adequacy: accepting a theory involves believing only that it is accurate about what is observable. The book defended antirealism against scientific realism while taking the semantics and structure of theories fully seriously, and introduced the distinction between acceptance and belief that framed decades of realism debates.

His other works include Laws and Symmetry (1989), which criticises accounts of laws of nature as relations among universals; Quantum Mechanics: An Empiricist View (1991); and The Empirical Stance (2002), which develops empiricism as a stance or attitude rather than a doctrine. Van Fraassen also made major contributions to free logic, supervaluation semantics and the philosophy of probability. He received the Lakatos Award in 1986.
