Ei-ichi Negishi

Ei-ichi Negishi (根岸 英一, Negishi Eiichi, 14 July 1935 – 6 June 2021) was a Japanese chemist best known for his discovery of the Negishi coupling, a palladium- or nickel-catalysed cross-coupling reaction of organozinc compounds. He shared the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Richard F. Heck and Akira Suzuki "for palladium-catalyzed cross couplings in organic synthesis."

Life and career

Negishi was born in Hsinking (now Changchun), in Japanese-occupied Manchuria, and grew up in Japan. He studied at the University of Tokyo, graduating in 1958, and worked at the chemical company Teijin before receiving a Fulbright scholarship to the University of Pennsylvania, where he earned his PhD in 1963. He worked as a postdoctoral researcher with Herbert C. Brown at Purdue University, joined the faculty at Syracuse University in 1972, and returned to Purdue in 1979, where he became the Herbert C. Brown Distinguished Professor of Chemistry.

Research

Negishi's research centred on organometallic chemistry and its application to organic synthesis. In 1977 he reported the cross-coupling of organozinc reagents with organic halides catalysed by palladium or nickel, the reaction that now bears his name. The Negishi coupling is widely used in pharmaceutical and materials chemistry for forming carbon–carbon bonds. He also developed methods involving organozirconium compounds, including zirconium-catalysed asymmetric carboalumination.

Later life

Negishi died on 6 June 2021 in Indianapolis, Indiana, aged 85. In 2018 he and his wife had been found by police after becoming lost while driving in Illinois; his wife died in the incident. His awards included the Nobel Prize, the Order of Culture of Japan, and the American Chemical Society Award for Creative Work in Synthetic Organic Chemistry.
