Anne Teresa De Keersmaeker (born 11 June 1960) is a Belgian choreographer and one of the leading figures of contemporary dance in Europe. In 1983 she founded the dance company Rosas in Brussels.

Early works

De Keersmaeker studied at Maurice Béjart's Mudra school in Brussels and at New York University's Tisch School of the Arts. Her breakthrough came with Fase, Four Movements to the Music of Steve Reich (1982), in which her choreography of spinning, pendulum-like duets mirrored Reich's phase-shifting scores. Rosas danst Rosas (1983), performed by four women to music by Thierry De Mey and Peter Vermeersch, established her international reputation with its fierce, repetitive everyday gestures.

Rosas and later work

From 1992 to 2007 Rosas was company-in-residence at La Monnaie in Brussels, where De Keersmaeker also directed an opera production of Bartók's Bluebeard's Castle. In 1995 she founded the dance school P.A.R.T.S. (Performing Arts Research and Training Studios) with De Munt/La Monnaie. Later major works include Rain (2001), set to Reich's Music for 18 Musicians, Drumming, Cesena (2011) and Mitten wir im Leben sind/Bach6Cellosuiten (2017).

Recognition

De Keersmaeker has received numerous honours, including Bessie Awards, the Praemium Imperiale for Theatre/Film in 2019, and a Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement at the Venice Biennale in 2015. Beyoncé's 2011 "Countdown" video, which borrowed choreography from Rosas danst Rosas and Achterland, brought her work to wide public attention.
