Tanabata

Tanabata (Japanese: たなばた or 七夕, "Evening of the Seventh"), also known as the Star Festival, is a Japanese festival originating from the Chinese Qixi Festival. It celebrates the meeting of the deities Orihime and Hikoboshi — the stars Vega and Altair — who, according to legend, are separated by the Milky Way and allowed to meet only once a year.

Date and customs

Tanabata is traditionally held on the seventh day of the seventh lunar month; in modern Japan it is most often celebrated on 7 July, though some regions, notably Sendai, hold it in August by the old calendar. People write wishes on small strips of coloured paper called tanzaku and hang them on bamboo branches, often with other paper decorations.

The legend

Orihime, a weaver princess, and Hikoboshi, a cowherd, fell so deeply in love that they neglected their work. The angered Sky King separated them across the Heavenly River (the Milky Way), permitting a single annual reunion — if rain falls, magpies are said to form a bridge with their wings.

Festivals

The Sendai Tanabata Matsuri is Japan's most famous celebration, drawing millions with elaborate streamers, while Shonan Hiratsuka and many local festivals mark the occasion across the country.
