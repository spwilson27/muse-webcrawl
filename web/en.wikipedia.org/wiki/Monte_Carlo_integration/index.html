Monte Carlo integration

In mathematics, Monte Carlo integration is a technique for numerical integration using random numbers. Where deterministic quadrature rules (such as the trapezoidal or Simpson's rule) evaluate the integrand at points on a regular grid, Monte Carlo methods sample points at random and use the average value of the function at those points, scaled by the volume of the domain, as an estimate of the integral.

The method's great advantage appears in high dimensions: the error of grid-based rules grows catastrophically with dimension (the curse of dimensionality), whereas the Monte Carlo error decreases as 1/√N with the number of samples N, independent of dimension. This makes it the standard tool for integrals in statistical mechanics, Bayesian inference, finance, and computer graphics. Accuracy can be improved by variance reduction techniques, including importance sampling, stratified sampling, and control variates; quasi-Monte Carlo methods replace random points with low-discrepancy sequences to converge faster.
