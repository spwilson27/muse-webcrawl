Walters Art Museum

The Walters Art Museum is a public art museum located in the Mount Vernon neighbourhood of Baltimore, Maryland. Founded in 1934, it holds collections established during the mid-19th century by father and son William Thompson Walters and Henry Walters, who amassed a substantial collection of art and artefacts.

Collections

The museum's holdings span seven millennia, from pre-dynastic Egypt to 20th-century Europe. Strengths include Egyptian, Greek, Roman, Etruscan, and Byzantine art; medieval ivories, manuscripts, and armour; Renaissance and Baroque painting; and one of the finest Asian art collections in the United States, including Chinese porcelain and Japanese arms and armour. Notable works include the Walters' illuminated manuscripts—among the most important in North America—and the Rubens, Monet, and Delacroix paintings collected by the founders.

History

William Walters, a railroad magnate, began collecting European painting in the 1860s; his son Henry greatly expanded the collection, famously purchasing the contents of the Palazzo Acciaiuoli in Rome in 1902. Henry Walters bequeathed the collection and its buildings to the City of Baltimore "for the benefit of the public." The museum, known as the Walters Art Gallery until 2000, made admission free in 2006 and has placed tens of thousands of images of its works into the public domain.
