Bochum

Bochum is a city in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. With a population of about 365,000, it is one of the largest cities in the Ruhr metropolitan region. The city is known for its former coal mining and steel industries, the Bochum Total theatre festival, and the musical Starlight Express, which has played there since 1988. Bochum is home to the Ruhr University Bochum, one of Germany's largest universities.

History

Bochum was first mentioned in documents in the 9th century and received city rights in 1321. Its transformation came with industrialisation: the first coal mine shafts were sunk in the mid-19th century, and the Bochumer Verein steelworks made it a centre of German heavy industry. The city was heavily bombed during the Second World War. The last coal mine closed in 1973, and the city successfully shifted towards services, education and technology.

Economy and education

The Ruhr University Bochum, founded in 1962, was the first new university in the Federal Republic of Germany and now enrols around 43,000 students. Major employers include the university, hospitals, and technology firms. The car manufacturer Opel operated a large plant in Bochum from 1962 until its closure in 2014.

Culture

The Schauspielhaus Bochum is one of Germany's most respected theatres. The German Mining Museum (Deutsches Bergbau-Museum) is the world's largest museum of its kind. The musical Starlight Express has been running in a purpose-built theatre since 1988, making it one of the longest-running musicals in the world. The football club VfL Bochum plays at the Ruhrstadion.

Twin towns

Bochum is twinned with Sheffield in England, Oviedo in Spain, Donetsk in Ukraine, Nordhausen in Germany and Xuzhou in China.
