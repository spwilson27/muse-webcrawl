International Mathematical Olympiad

IMO
First held: 1959, Romania
Frequency: Annual
Participants: 100+ countries

The International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) is a mathematical olympiad for pre-university students, and is the oldest of the International Science Olympiads. The first IMO was held in Romania in 1959 with seven countries participating; it has since grown to include over 100 countries from five continents.

Format

Each country sends a team of up to six students. The contest consists of two papers on consecutive days, each with three problems to be solved in four and a half hours. Problems come from geometry, number theory, algebra and combinatorics, and require no knowledge beyond secondary school mathematics. Each problem is scored out of seven points. Medals are awarded to roughly the top half of participants, with gold, silver and bronze cut-offs set so that about one twelfth receive gold.

History and records

The competition was dominated in its early decades by Eastern European countries. China has been the most successful nation since joining in 1985, with the United States and South Korea also frequent winners. Several Fields medallists, including Grigori Perelman and Terence Tao — who competed at age ten — are IMO alumni.
