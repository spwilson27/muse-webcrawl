Slide Mountain (Ulster County, New York)

Slide Mountain is the highest peak in the Catskill Mountains of New York, with an elevation of 4,180 feet (1,274 m) above sea level. It lies in the town of Shandaken in Ulster County, within the Slide Mountain Wilderness Area of the Catskill Park. The mountain takes its name from a landslide on its steep north face in the early 19th century.

Geography

Slide Mountain rises between the valleys of the West Branch of the Neversink River and Woodland Creek. Its summit is covered in a dense boreal forest of balsam fir and red spruce, a relict of the post-glacial forest found only on the highest Catskill summits. It is the highest point in New York State outside the Adirondack High Peaks region, and one of the most prominent peaks in the northeastern United States.

History

The mountain's height was disputed with nearby Hunter Mountain until surveys in the 1880s confirmed Slide as the higher. The naturalist and essayist John Burroughs, who lived nearby, climbed it frequently and wrote about it in essays such as "The Heart of the Southern Catskills"; a plaque near the summit commemorates him. The Catskill Mountain Club and later the state conservation department built trails and, for a time, a fire tower (long since removed) served the summit. A spring near the summit is the source of the West Branch of the Neversink.

Hiking

The most popular ascent follows the Wittenberg–Cornell–Slide Trail (the Burroughs Range Trail) from the Slide Mountain trailhead on County Route 47, climbing about 1,760 feet over some 2.7 miles. The trail crosses the summit and continues east across Cornell and Wittenberg mountains. The Curtis–Ormsbee Trail provides an alternative, more scenic approach from the south, joining the main trail below the summit. In winter the climb is a popular snowshoe and mountaineering route. The summit's limited views — mostly from ledges just below the top — have been slowly opening as trees die back.
