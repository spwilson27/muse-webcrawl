Coco Chanel

Gabrielle Bonheur "Coco" Chanel (19 August 1883 – 10 January 1971) was a French fashion designer and businesswoman. The founder and namesake of the Chanel brand, she was credited in the post–World War I era with popularizing a sporty, casual chic as the feminine standard of style. She is the only fashion designer listed on Time magazine's list of the 100 most influential people of the 20th century.

Early life

Chanel was born in a poorhouse in Saumur to a laundress and a market stallholder. After her mother's death, her father placed her in a convent orphanage at Aubazine, where she learned to sew. A brief career as a cabaret singer in Moulins and Vichy earned her the nickname "Coco," possibly from songs she performed or from cocotte. She moved in wealthy circles through relationships with Étienne Balsan and the English industrialist Arthur "Boy" Capel, who financed her first shops.

Career

Chanel began as a milliner, opening a hat shop in Paris in 1910 and a boutique in Deauville in 1913. She liberated women from corseted silhouettes, designing loose garments in jersey, a fabric previously used for men's underwear. In the 1920s she launched the perfume Chanel No. 5 (1921), the "little black dress" (1926), the Chanel suit with collarless jacket and fitted skirt, costume jewellery, and the interlocking-CC monogram. Her style drew on menswear, sailor clothing, and the tweeds of her life in Scotland with the Duke of Westminster.

Wartime and comeback

Chanel closed her couture house in 1939 and lived at the Ritz Paris during the German occupation. Her wartime relationship with a German officer, Hans Günther von Dincklage, and her dealings with both German and British intelligence made her a controversial figure; she moved to Switzerland after the war. She returned to Paris fashion in 1954, aged 70, and her comeback collection—initially panned in France but embraced in America—re-established the house with the classic Chanel suit, quilted handbag with chain strap (the 2.55), and two-tone shoes.

Legacy

Chanel died at the Hôtel Ritz in 1971 while still working on a new collection. The house continued under Karl Lagerfeld from 1983, who expanded the brand into a global luxury empire. Her aphorisms—"Fashion passes, style remains" among them—remain widely quoted.
