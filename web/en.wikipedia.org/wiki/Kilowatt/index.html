Kilowatt

Kilowatt
Symbol: kW
Unit of: Power
In SI base units: 1000 kg·m²·s⁻³
Equal to: 1,000 watts ≈ 1.34 horsepower

The kilowatt (symbol: kW) is a unit of power in the International System of Units (SI) equal to one thousand watts. It is widely used to express the output power of engines and motors and the power consumption of electrical appliances, heaters, and broadcasting transmitters.

Definition and use

One kilowatt equals 1,000 joules per second, or approximately 1.34 horsepower. Typical examples include:

  • A domestic electric kettle: about 2–3 kW
  • The peak power consumption of a typical household: several kW
  • A small car engine: 50–150 kW
  • The output of a large wind turbine: 1,000 kW (1 MW) or more

Kilowatt-hour

The kilowatt should not be confused with the kilowatt-hour (kWh), a unit of energy equal to the work done by one kilowatt acting for one hour (3.6 megajoules). Electricity is billed to consumers in kilowatt-hours. A kilowatt-hour is sometimes called a "unit" of electricity in the United Kingdom and India.

Multiples

Larger powers are expressed with SI prefixes: the megawatt (MW, one million watts) for power stations, and the gigawatt (GW, one billion watts) for national generating capacity. For very small powers the milliwatt (mW) is used.
