Xizang (Chinese: 西藏, "Western Treasury") is the Chinese name for Tibet, and specifically for the Tibet Autonomous Region of the People's Republic of China. The autonomous region covers most of the Tibetan Plateau, the highest region on Earth, with an average elevation above 4,000 metres.

Geography

The region is bordered by Xinjiang, Qinghai, Sichuan, and Yunnan within China, and by Nepal, India, Bhutan, and Myanmar internationally. Its capital is Lhasa, home to the Potala Palace and Jokhang Temple. Major rivers including the Yarlung Tsangpo (Brahmaputra), the upper Yangtze, Mekong, and Salween rise on the plateau.

Administration and demographics

The Tibet Autonomous Region was established in 1965. The population is predominantly ethnic Tibetan, most of whom practise Tibetan Buddhism, with Han Chinese and other groups concentrated in urban areas. The region includes the prefecture-level cities of Lhasa, Shigatse, Chamdo, Nyingchi, Shannan, and Nagqu, and Ngari Prefecture.
