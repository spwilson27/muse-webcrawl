Lucius Vibullius Hipparchus Tiberius Claudius Atticus Herodes (c. AD 101 – c. 177), commonly known as Herodes Atticus, was a Greek rhetorician and Roman senator, the most celebrated orator of the Second Sophistic and one of the richest men of his age.

Life

Born at Marathon to a fabulously wealthy family, Herodes inherited a fortune enlarged by treasure his father had discovered. He served as consul in AD 143 and tutored the future emperors Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus. His marriage to the Roman heiress Annia Regilla ended in scandal when she died in childbirth and he was accused (but acquitted) of her murder; his extravagant public mourning produced famous monuments.

Benefactions

Herodes spent lavishly on public works across Greece and Italy: the Odeon on the slope of the Acropolis at Athens, the Panathenaic Stadium rebuilt in marble, aqueducts at Olympia and Canusium, and the Triopion sanctuary on the Appian Way. His speeches, only fragments of which survive, made him the emblematic figure of Greek eloquence under Rome.
