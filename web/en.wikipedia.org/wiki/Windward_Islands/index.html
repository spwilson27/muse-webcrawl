Windward Islands
Location: Caribbean Sea
Archipelago: Lesser Antilles
Major islands: Martinique, Saint Lucia, Barbados, Saint Vincent, Grenada, Dominica
Highest point: Morne Diablotins (Dominica)

The Windward Islands are the southern, generally larger islands of the Lesser Antilles in the Caribbean, forming the eastern boundary of the Caribbean Sea with the Atlantic Ocean. They lie south of the Leeward Islands and stretch from Dominica in the north to Grenada in the south, including Martinique, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, and Barbados to the east. The group takes its name from the prevailing trade winds, which blow from the northeast: sailing ships bound for the New World traditionally reached the Windwards first after the Atlantic crossing.

Terminology

Usage differs between languages. In English, the Windward Islands are the southern chain and the Leeward Islands the northern chain of the Lesser Antilles. In French, Spanish and Dutch usage, the dividing line is drawn differently, and islands such as Guadeloupe and the Virgin Islands may be classed differently, which can cause confusion.

British Windward Islands

From 1833 the British administered Barbados, Grenada, Saint Vincent, Tobago, Saint Lucia and (from 1940) Dominica as the colony of the Windward Islands, with its capital first at Bridgetown and later at St. George's, Grenada. The colony was dissolved in 1960 after the islands joined and then left the West Indies Federation.

Cricket and culture

The islands jointly field the Windward Islands cricket team in West Indian domestic competitions. The Windward Islands are also grouped in regional institutions such as the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States, and share the Eastern Caribbean dollar.
