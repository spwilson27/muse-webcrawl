Carnivora (kar-NIV-ər-ə) is an order of placental mammals that have specialised in eating flesh. Its members are formally referred to as carnivorans. The order, which comprises over 280 species, is the fifth largest order of mammals and one of the most successful members of the class, spreading to every continent and a wide range of habitats. Carnivorans range in size from the tiny least weasel (~25 g) to the polar bear and southern elephant seal (several thousand kg). Nearly all have characteristic shearing cheek teeth called carnassials, though diet varies widely: some, like the giant panda, are almost entirely herbivorous, and others are omnivores or insectivores.

Evolution

Carnivorans evolved in North America and Europe during the Eocene, around 52–42 million years ago, from small miacid-grade ancestors. The two main lineages, Feliformia (cat-like) and Caniformia (dog-like), diverged early in the order's history. Carnivorans largely replaced the earlier creodonts as the dominant mammalian predators by the Miocene, and the group underwent major radiations as grasslands spread.

Classification

The order is divided into two suborders:

  • Feliformia — cats (Felidae), hyenas (Hyaenidae), mongooses (Herpestidae), civets and genets (Viverridae), the fossa and relatives (Eupleridae), and the African palm civet (Nandiniidae).
  • Caniformia — dogs (Canidae), bears (Ursidae), raccoons (Procyonidae), weasels, otters and badgers (Mustelidae), skunks (Mephitidae), the red panda (Ailuridae), and the pinnipeds — seals, sea lions and the walrus (Phocoidea).

Anatomy and diet

Carnivorans typically possess strong jaws, prominent canine teeth, forward-facing eyes, and claws suited to seizing prey. The carnassial pair (upper fourth premolar and lower first molar) acts as a shear for slicing flesh and tendon. Despite the name, only a minority of species are obligate meat-eaters ("hypercarnivores"); many are omnivorous, and several have secondarily adopted specialised diets such as bamboo (giant panda), ants and termites (aardwolf, in part) or seals and krill.

Ecology and humans

As apex and meso-predators, carnivorans shape ecosystems through predation and scavenging. Humans have domesticated two carnivorans — the dog and the cat, among the world's most popular pets — while others are hunted for fur, persecuted as livestock predators, or threatened by habitat loss; several large species are of conservation concern.
