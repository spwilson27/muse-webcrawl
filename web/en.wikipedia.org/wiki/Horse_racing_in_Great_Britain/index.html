Horse racing in Great Britain

Horse racing in Great Britain is the second-largest spectator sport in the country and one of the longest established, with a history dating back many centuries. It is governed by the British Horseracing Authority (BHA), and takes two principal forms: flat racing, run on turf in spring and summer and on all-weather tracks year-round, and National Hunt racing over hurdles and fences, centred on the winter season.

History

Organised racing developed in the 17th and 18th centuries around Newmarket, which remains the sport's headquarters. The Jockey Club, founded about 1750, codified the rules and supervised the General Stud Book, establishing the Thoroughbred breed. Royal patronage — from Charles II, a keen owner and rider, through Queen Elizabeth II — gave racing its epithet "the sport of kings".

Major races and festivals

The five British Classic Races for three-year-olds are the 2000 Guineas, 1000 Guineas, the Derby, the Oaks, and the St Leger. Royal Ascot, the Cheltenham Festival (culminating in the Cheltenham Gold Cup), the Grand National at Aintree — first run in 1839 and among the most-watched races in the world — Glorious Goodwood, and the Ebor Festival at York are the sport's other landmarks. There are 59 licensed racecourses in Great Britain, including Ascot, Epsom Downs, Newmarket, Doncaster, Aintree, and Cheltenham.

Industry and betting

Racing supports a large rural economy of training yards, stud farms, and racecourse employment, and is closely linked with the betting industry through the Horserace Betting Levy. Attendances run to several million spectators per year.
