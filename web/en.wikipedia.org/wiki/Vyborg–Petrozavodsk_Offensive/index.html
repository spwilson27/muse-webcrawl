The Vyborg–Petrozavodsk Offensive (Russian: Выборгско-Петрозаводская наступательная операция) was a strategic offensive by the Soviet Leningrad and Karelian Fronts against Finland during the Continuation War, conducted from 10 June to 9 August 1944. Coordinated with the Allied Normandy landings and Operation Bagration, it was the last and largest Soviet offensive of the war against Finland, designed to knock Finland out of the war.

Course of the offensive

The offensive opened on the Karelian Isthmus on 10 June, one day after a massive artillery and air preparation. The Leningrad Front under Leonid Govorov broke through the Finnish defences on the Mannerheim Line's successor positions, capturing Vyborg (Viipuri) on 20 June. Finnish forces under Karl Lennart Oesch stabilised the front at the VKT-line in early July, repelling Soviet attempts to break through at Tali-Ihantala (25 June – 9 July), the largest battle in Nordic military history, as well as at Vuosalmi and Nietjärvi.

Meanwhile the Karelian Front under Kirill Meretskov attacked across the Svir River on 21 June, liberating Petrozavodsk on 28 June and driving Finnish forces back to roughly the 1940 border in East Karelia before halting at the U-line.

Outcome

Although the Red Army failed to break into southern Finland proper, the offensive's strategic effect was decisive: it demonstrated Finland's untenable position, especially as German aid could not be guaranteed. Finland sought a separate peace, and the Moscow Armistice of 19 September 1944 ended the Continuation War. Finland ceded Petsamo, leased Porkkala to the USSR, paid reparations, and undertook to expel German forces from its territory — precipitating the Lapland War.
