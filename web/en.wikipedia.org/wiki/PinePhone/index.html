The PinePhone is a smartphone developed by Pine64, a community-driven hardware company, designed to run mainline Linux rather than Android. Announced in 2019 and first shipped in early 2020, it became a reference device for mobile Linux development.

Hardware

The phone uses an Allwinner A64 quad-core ARM processor, 2 or 3 GB of RAM, and 16 or 32 GB of storage expandable by microSD — from which it can also boot. It features hardware kill switches for the modem, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth, microphone, and cameras, a removable battery, and a USB-C port supporting video output. A later model, the PinePhone Pro, uses a Rockchip RK3399S processor.

Software

Because the device ships without a fixed operating system, users install community builds of systems such as postmarketOS, Mobian, Manjaro ARM, and Ubuntu Touch, with interfaces including Phosh, Plasma Mobile, and Lomiri. Early "community edition" phones were sold bundled with different distributions.

Reception

The PinePhone was aimed at developers and enthusiasts rather than mainstream users; reviews praised its openness, repairability, and low price (originally about US$149) while noting modest performance and immature software.
