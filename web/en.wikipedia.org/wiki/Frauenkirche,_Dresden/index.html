Frauenkirche, Dresden

LocationNeumarkt, Dresden, Germany
DenominationEvangelical Lutheran
Built1726–1743; rebuilt 1994–2005
ArchitectGeorge Bähr (original)

The Frauenkirche (Church of Our Lady) is a Lutheran church in Dresden, Germany, standing on the Neumarkt square. Built in the 18th century, it is considered an outstanding example of Protestant Baroque architecture and has one of the largest stone church domes in Europe.

History

The original church was designed by architect George Bähr and constructed between 1726 and 1743. Its great bell-shaped sandstone dome, the "Steinerne Glocke" (stone bell), was an engineering marvel of the age. The church became a symbol of the city and of Protestant civic pride.

Destruction

During the bombing of Dresden in February 1945, the church withstood two days of attacks before the heat of the firestorm caused the dome to collapse on the morning of February 15, 1945. Under the East German government the blackened ruin was left standing as a war memorial.

Reconstruction

After German reunification, a citizens' initiative led to the church's reconstruction, begun in 1994. Thousands of original stones recovered from the rubble were catalogued and reintegrated into the new structure, where they remain visible as dark stones in the lighter new sandstone. A new orb and cross, crafted partly by a British silversmith whose father had participated in the bombing, crown the dome — a gift from the British people. The church was reconsecrated on October 30, 2005, and has become an international symbol of reconciliation.
