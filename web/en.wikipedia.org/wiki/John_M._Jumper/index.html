John Michael Jumper (born 1985) is an American computational biologist and director at Google DeepMind. He led the development of AlphaFold, the artificial intelligence system that predicts protein structures from amino-acid sequences with near-experimental accuracy. For this work he shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Demis Hassabis, with the other half of the prize going to David Baker for computational protein design.

Education and early career

Jumper earned a B.S. in physics and mathematics from Vanderbilt University, then a Marshall Scholarship took him to the University of Cambridge, where he gained an MPhil in theoretical condensed matter physics. He completed a PhD in theoretical chemistry at the University of Chicago, working on methods for simulating protein dynamics, and did postdoctoral research on coarse-grained models of biomolecules. He also worked at the quantitative trading firm D. E. Shaw Research on molecular dynamics simulation.

AlphaFold

Jumper joined DeepMind in 2017. He led the team that built AlphaFold 2, which at the CASP14 competition in 2020 effectively solved the 50-year-old "protein folding problem" for single-chain proteins, predicting structures with accuracy comparable to experimental methods. The subsequent release of the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database made more than 200 million predicted structures freely available to researchers. AlphaFold 3, released in 2024, extended the approach to interactions between proteins, nucleic acids, ligands and other molecules.

Honours

Jumper was named to Nature's 10 in 2021 and received the Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences (2023, shared with Hassabis and others) and the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2024.
