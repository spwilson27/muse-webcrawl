Gustav I of Sweden

Gustav I
Reign: 6 June 1523 – 29 September 1560
Born: 12 May 1496
Died: 29 September 1560, Stockholm
House: Vasa
Successor: Eric XIV

Gustav I, born Gustav Eriksson (12 May 1496 – 29 September 1560), was King of Sweden from 1523 until his death. He founded the House of Vasa and is regarded as the founder of modern Sweden, having led the Swedish War of Liberation against Denmark and established a strong centralised monarchy.

Early life and liberation war

Gustav was a Swedish nobleman whose father was executed in the Stockholm Bloodbath of 1520 by Christian II of Denmark. Gustav fled, rallied support in Dalarna, and led a rebellion that liberated Stockholm in 1523, ending the Kalmar Union.

Reign

Elected king on 6 June 1523, Gustav centralised power, promoted the Protestant Reformation in Sweden, confiscated church lands to strengthen the crown's finances, and built a hereditary monarchy. He reorganised the military and administration, laying foundations for Sweden's later great-power era.

Legacy

Gustav is celebrated as a national hero in Sweden; 6 June is Sweden's National Day, commemorating his election. He was succeeded by his son Eric XIV.
