The 21 Club, often simply '21', was a traditional American cuisine restaurant and former Prohibition-era speakeasy located at 21 West 52nd Street in New York City. Founded in 1922 by cousins Jack Kriendler and Charlie Berns, it became one of the city's most famous dining institutions, known for its celebrity clientele, its prohibition-era wine cellar, and the cast-iron lawn jockey statues that lined its front steps.

History

The club began as a small speakeasy in Greenwich Village in 1922 and moved several times as federal agents raided it repeatedly during Prohibition. At its final location, opened on New Year's Eve 1929, the owners installed elaborate anti-raid engineering: a secret wine cellar accessible through a hidden door in an adjacent brownstone, chutes to sweep liquor bottles into the sewers, and an alarm system connected to neighbouring bars. The cellar, built into the foundations, at times held the private collections of presidents and celebrities; it survived intact when Prohibition was repealed in 1933.

The jockeys

The club's front railing and stairs were decorated with painted cast-iron lawn jockeys, the first donated in the 1930s by horse-owning patrons. The jockeys were painted in the racing colours of prominent stables, and by the club's later years about 37 stood outside and inside the premises; they were removed for safekeeping during periods of closure and became a symbol widely reproduced in New York iconography.

Closure

'21' suspended operations in March 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic. In December 2020 its owner, Belmond, announced that the restaurant would not reopen in its previous form, ending a 90-year run, though the company explored new concepts for the site and later indicated the restaurant would return in reimagined form under LVMH ownership.

In culture

The club appears in numerous films and novels set in New York and is credited in popular lore with the invention of the Bloody Mary in the United States by its bartender Henry Zbikiewicz in the 1930s, a claim also made for the King Cole Bar at the St. Regis.
