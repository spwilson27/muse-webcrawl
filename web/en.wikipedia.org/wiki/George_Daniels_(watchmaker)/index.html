George Daniels (watchmaker)

George Daniels CBE
Born: August 19, 1926, Sunderland, England
Died: October 21, 2011, Isle of Man
Occupation: Horologist
Known for: Co-axial escapement

George Daniels CBE (19 August 1926 – 21 October 2011) was an English horologist widely regarded as one of the greatest watchmakers of the 20th century. Working almost entirely by hand on the Isle of Man, he built a small number of complete watches — including every component — and invented the co-axial escapement, one of the few genuinely new escapements since the 18th century, later adopted industrially by Omega.

Life and work

Daniels grew up in poverty in London, left school at 14, and after wartime army service trained in watch repair at evening classes, qualifying as a Fellow of the British Horological Institute. Specialising in restoring Breguet watches, he began making his own pocket chronometers in 1969. His watches incorporated tourbillons, independent dead seconds, perpetual calendars and his own escapements, and were made using traditional hand methods he described in his book Watchmaking (1981), the standard text on hand watchmaking.

Co-axial escapement

Patented in the 1970s, the co-axial escapement transmits impulse tangentially through two coaxial escape wheels, greatly reducing sliding friction and the need for lubrication, permitting longer service intervals than the Swiss lever escapement. After years of rejection, Daniels licensed it to Omega, which introduced it industrially in 1999; his protégé Roger W. Smith continues independent watchmaking on the Isle of Man.

Honours

Daniels received the Gold Medal of the British Horological Institute and numerous other awards, was appointed MBE and later CBE, and held an honorary doctorate. The George Daniels Educational Trust supports students in horology and engineering. His personal collection of watches and vintage cars was sold at auction after his death, with his "Space Traveller's" watch achieving record prices.
