Triple product

In vector calculus, the term triple product refers to two operations on three vectors in three-dimensional Euclidean space: the scalar triple product and the vector triple product.

Scalar triple product

The scalar triple product of vectors a, b, c is a · (b × c). Its absolute value equals the volume of the parallelepiped spanned by the three vectors, and its sign indicates their orientation (right- or left-handed). It equals the determinant of the matrix whose rows (or columns) are the vectors' components, and it is unchanged by cyclic permutation of the vectors but changes sign under any swap. It is zero exactly when the vectors are coplanar.

Vector triple product

The vector triple product a × (b × c) is a vector lying in the plane of b and c, given by the expansion formula (sometimes called Lagrange's formula or the "BAC–CAB" rule):

a × (b × c) = b(a · c) − c(a · b).

Unlike the scalar product, the cross product is not associative, so the parentheses matter. Identities involving repeated cross products appear in rigid-body mechanics, electromagnetism, and the theory of the Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector.
