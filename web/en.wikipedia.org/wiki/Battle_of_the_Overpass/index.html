Battle of the Overpass

The Battle of the Overpass was an incident that occurred on 26 May 1937, in which Walter Reuther and members of the United Auto Workers (UAW) clashed with Ford Motor Company security guards at the River Rouge Plant in Dearborn, Michigan, United States. Photographs of the beating of union organisers — published in newspapers across the country — badly damaged Ford's public reputation and became a landmark in American labor history.

The overpass at the River Rouge Plant where UAW organisers were attacked in 1937.

Background

Henry Ford was the most determinedly anti-union of the Detroit automakers. His Ford Service Department, run by Harry Bennett, employed thousands of security men and informants to suppress organising. After General Motors and Chrysler recognised the UAW following the sit-down strikes of 1937, the union turned to Ford. On 26 May 1937 the UAW obtained a permit for a leafleting rally at the Miller Road overpass outside the River Rouge plant's Gate 4.

The incident

As Reuther and fellow organisers including Richard Frankensteen posed for press photographers on the overpass, Ford security men attacked them without warning, beating them and throwing some down the overpass steps. Women organisers distributing leaflets were also assaulted. Photographer Scotty Fitzpatrick of the Detroit News hid his photographic plates from the guards, who seized other journalists' film; his images of the beatings were published nationwide and won a Pulitzer Prize the following year for the newspaper's photography.

Aftermath

The National Labor Relations Board charged Ford with labor law violations, and the publicity from the Overpass photos turned public opinion further against the company. Ford nonetheless held out until 1941, when — after a strike shut the Rouge — Henry Ford finally signed a contract with the UAW, the last of the major Detroit automakers to do so.
