The River Aire is a major river in Yorkshire, England, 92 miles (148 km) in length. Part of the river below Leeds is canalised, and is known as the Aire and Calder Navigation.

The Aire rises at Malham Tarn in the Yorkshire Dales, flowing underground at Malham Cove before re-emerging and passing through Gargrave, Skipton, Keighley, Bingley, Shipley, and Leeds. East of Leeds it flows through Castleford, where it is joined by the River Calder, and continues past Ferrybridge and Knottingley before joining the River Ouse at Airmyn, near Goole.

Course

The river's source is Malham Tarn, the highest marl lake in England. The outflow sinks into the limestone and reappears at Aire Head, south of Malham. From there the Aire flows south-east through the Aire Gap, a low pass across the Pennines used by road, rail, and the Leeds and Liverpool Canal. In its upper reaches the river is a fast-flowing limestone stream popular with anglers for its brown trout and grayling.

Through the industrial towns of the Aire Valley — Skipton, Keighley, Bingley, and Shipley — the river powered textile mills during the Industrial Revolution. At Leeds, once heavily polluted, the river has been substantially cleaned since the late twentieth century, and otters and salmon have returned to stretches of the river.

Navigation

The Aire and Calder Navigation, authorised by Act of Parliament in 1699, made the river navigable from Leeds and Wakefield to the Ouse and the Humber Estuary. Improvements by engineers including John Smeaton enabled coal from the Yorkshire coalfield to be carried to Hull and beyond. The navigation remains in use for commercial and leisure traffic.

Flooding

The Aire has flooded significantly on several occasions, notably on Boxing Day 2015, when record levels in Leeds city centre led to the construction of the Leeds Flood Alleviation Scheme, one of the largest flood defence projects in the United Kingdom, incorporating movable weirs.
