Dullahan

The Dullahan (Irish: dúlachán, "dark man") is a headless rider in Irish folklore, usually mounted on a black horse, who carries his own head under one arm. He is regarded as a harbinger of death: where the Dullahan stops riding, a person dies. The figure belongs to the wider tradition of the unseelee or unseelie court of malevolent fairies.

Description

The Dullahan's head is said to have the colour and texture of mouldy cheese or stale dough, with small black eyes that dart about and a huge grin. He uses a whip made from a human spine. In some accounts he drives a black coach, the cóiste bodhar ("deaf coach" or "silent coach"), drawn by headless horses; in others the wheels are made of thigh bones and the spokes of the carriage rattle as it passes.

Behaviour

The Dullahan cannot be barred by any gate or lock, which spring open before him. He does not speak except to call out the name of the person about to die. He dislikes being watched, and may throw a basin of blood at onlookers or strike out their eyes with his whip. Gold is said to frighten him away; even a gold pin can drive off the rider.

In popular culture

The Dullahan influenced Washington Irving's Headless Horseman in "The Legend of Sleepy Hollow" through Irish and German folklore traditions. In modern Japanese popular culture, the dullahan appears frequently in anime, manga, and light novels — for example the character Celty Sturluson in Durarara!! — where it is often depicted as a female rider searching for her lost head.
