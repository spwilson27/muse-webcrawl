Bone marrow

Bone marrow
SystemHematopoietic; immune
TypesRed marrow; yellow marrow
FunctionProduction of blood cells (hematopoiesis)
LatinMedulla ossium

Bone marrow is the soft, spongy tissue inside bones. It produces the cellular components of blood: in an adult human, the marrow generates roughly 200 billion new red blood cells, 10 billion white blood cells, and 400 billion platelets each day. Red marrow is the site of hematopoiesis, the formation of blood cells from hematopoietic stem cells; yellow marrow, found in the long bones of adults, consists mainly of fat and can convert back to red marrow in emergencies such as severe blood loss.

Structure

Red marrow is concentrated in the flat bones—pelvis, sternum, skull, ribs, vertebrae—and the ends of long bones. It is organized into cords of developing blood cells interspersed with vascular sinusoids, supported by stromal cells, adipocytes, and macrophages that nurture stem cells in specialized "niches." B lymphocytes mature in the marrow, while T-cell precursors migrate to the thymus.

Clinical significance

Marrow disorders include aplastic anemia (failure of cell production), leukemias and lymphomas (cancers of white-cell lines), myeloma, and myelodysplastic syndromes. Diagnosis often requires bone marrow aspiration and biopsy, commonly of the posterior iliac crest. Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation—popularly "bone marrow transplant"—uses donor stem cells harvested from marrow or peripheral blood to treat leukemias, lymphomas, and immune disorders; matching is coordinated through international donor registries.
