Bluff, New Zealand

Bluff (Māori: Motupōhue)
Region: Southland
Population: about 1,800

Bluff (Māori: Motupōhue), previously known as Campbelltown and often referred to as "the Bluff", is a town and seaport in the Southland region of New Zealand's South Island. It is the southern terminus of the country's main trunk railway and the gateway for ferries to Stewart Island / Rakiura. Bluff is dominated by Bluff Hill / Motupōhue, a conical hill rising 265 m above the harbour.

History

Bluff is one of the earliest European settlements in New Zealand. A whaling station was established in the area in the 1820s and 1830s, and the town was surveyed in 1856. The port, originally known as Port Bluff, developed as the shipping point for Southland's agricultural produce, and a rail link to Invercargill opened in 1867.

Port and industry

South Port, the modern port facility, is built on a reclaimed island in Bluff Harbour and handles cargo including aluminium from the Tiwai Point smelter, petroleum, fertiliser, and exports of meat, wool, and timber. The town is famous for its oyster fishery: Bluff oysters, harvested from Foveaux Strait between March and August, are regarded as a delicacy throughout New Zealand.

Landmarks

At Stirling Point, a signpost shows distances to the South Pole, the Equator, and other locations, and marks the beginning of the State Highway 1 route north and the southern end of the Te Araroa Trail. The Bluff Maritime Museum displays the town's seafaring history.
