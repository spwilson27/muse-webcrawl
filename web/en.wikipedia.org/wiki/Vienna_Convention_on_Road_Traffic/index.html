Overview

The Convention on Road Traffic, concluded in Vienna on 8 November 1968, is an international treaty designed to facilitate international road traffic and to increase road safety by standardising traffic rules and signs among the contracting parties. It succeeded and largely superseded the 1949 Geneva Convention (to which the United States, a notable non-party to Vienna, still adheres).

Provisions

The Convention standardises road sign shapes and meanings (the European-style system used across most of the world), rules of the road, vehicle equipment, and the International Driving Permit, which lets a holder drive in other contracting states. Its companion is the 1968 Convention on Road Signs and Signals. Amendments in force from 2022 explicitly address automated driving systems, clarifying that the "driver" requirement can be met by an automated system in defined conditions — the legal groundwork for cross-border self-driving.
