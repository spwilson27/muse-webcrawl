Low-discrepancy sequence

In mathematics, a low-discrepancy sequence is a sequence with the property that for all values of N, its subsequence x1, …, xN has a low discrepancy — informally, the points of the sequence fill their space as if they were generated by a true random number generator, but with the advantage that the coverage is more even than genuinely random points would provide. Low-discrepancy sequences are also called quasirandom sequences, due to their common use as a replacement of uniformly distributed random numbers in Monte Carlo methods.

Definition

The discrepancy of a set of N points in the s-dimensional unit cube measures the maximum difference, over all axis-aligned boxes anchored at the origin, between the proportion of points falling in the box and the box's volume. A sequence is a low-discrepancy sequence if its discrepancy DN satisfies

DN ≤ c (log N)s / N

for some constant c depending only on the dimension s. This is dramatically better than the typical O(1/√N) behaviour of random sequences, and is essentially optimal.

Examples

  • The van der Corput sequence in one dimension, formed by reversing the base-b digits of the integers.
  • The Halton sequence, generalizing van der Corput to higher dimensions using a different prime base in each coordinate.
  • The Sobol sequence, widely used in practice, based on binary digit manipulation with direction numbers.
  • The Faure sequence and Niederreiter sequences, powerful general constructions in finite fields.

Applications

The Koksma–Hlawka inequality bounds the error of numerical integration by the product of the integrand's variation and the discrepancy of the sample points. Using low-discrepancy sequences therefore yields the quasi-Monte Carlo method, whose convergence rate of roughly O((log N)s/N) beats plain Monte Carlo's O(1/√N) for moderate dimensions. Quasi-Monte Carlo with low-discrepancy sequences is used extensively in computational finance (pricing derivatives, risk estimation), computer graphics (rendering integrals), and experimental design.
