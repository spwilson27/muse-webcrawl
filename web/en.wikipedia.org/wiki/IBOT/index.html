IBOT

The iBOT is a powered wheelchair developed by Dean Kamen's DEKA Research and Development, originally marketed by Johnson & Johnson's Independence Technology division from 2003. It is notable for its ability to balance on two wheels, raising the user to standing eye level, and to climb stairs.

Design

The iBOT uses a system of gyroscopes, sensors and computers derived from the same self-balancing technology as the Segway PT to control clusters of wheels. In its standard function it drives on four wheels; in balance function it rises onto two wheels, elevating the seat; in stair function the wheel clusters rotate over each other to climb or descend stairs while the user holds a rail; a four-wheel-drive mode handles sand, gravel and rough terrain.

History

Unveiled publicly in 1999 and approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2003, the original iBOT was discontinued in 2009 after reimbursement difficulties — US Medicare classified it as a standard power wheelchair despite its roughly US$26,000 price. In 2016 DEKA partnered with Toyota to develop a new generation, and a revived iBOT, the iBOT PMD, was later marketed by Mobius Mobility.
