Boardwalk Empire is an American period crime drama television series created by Terence Winter and broadcast on HBO from 2010 to 2014. Set in Atlantic City, New Jersey, during Prohibition, it stars Steve Buscemi as Enoch "Nucky" Thompson, a political fixer and treasurer who controls the city and builds a bootlegging empire in alliance and conflict with gangsters including Arnold Rothstein, Lucky Luciano, Meyer Lansky and Al Capone. The pilot episode was directed by Martin Scorsese, who served as an executive producer; it was reported to be among the most expensive television pilots ever made.

Plot and themes

Beginning on the eve of Prohibition in 1920, the series follows Nucky's rise and moral decline across the decade, weaving fictional characters with historical figures and events, including the early organisation of the Mafia and the corruption of the Harding administration. Later seasons move to the early 1930s, with flashbacks to Nucky's childhood. The fifth and final season concluded in 1931 with Nucky's death.

Reception

The series was critically acclaimed, winning 20 Primetime Emmy Awards from 57 nominations, and the Golden Globe for Best Television Series – Drama in its first season; Buscemi won the Golden Globe for Best Actor. Its production design and recreation of 1920s Atlantic City — including a large boardwalk set built in Brooklyn — were widely praised.
