Bruschetta

Bruschetta
CourseAntipasto (appetizer)
Place of originItaly
Main ingredientsBread, garlic, olive oil; commonly tomato and basil

Bruschetta (Italian pronunciation roughly "broo-SKET-ta") is an antipasto (starter dish) from Italy consisting of grilled bread rubbed with garlic and topped with olive oil and salt. Variations may include toppings of tomato, vegetables, beans, cured meat, or cheese. The noun bruschetta derives from the Roman dialect verb bruscare, "to roast over coals"; the dish originated as a way for olive growers to sample freshly pressed oil and as a use for stale bread.

Preparation

Thick slices of rustic bread are grilled or toasted until crisp and golden, rubbed while hot with a cut clove of garlic, drizzled with extra-virgin olive oil and sprinkled with salt. The best-known topping, bruschetta al pomodoro, mixes chopped ripe tomatoes with basil, garlic and oil; in Tuscany a plain version with oil is called fettunta ("oiled slice"). Other regions top it with cannellini beans, sautéed greens, mushrooms, or liver pâté (closer to crostini). In English-speaking countries "bruschetta" often refers to the tomato topping itself rather than the prepared bread.
