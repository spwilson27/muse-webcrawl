The Sukhoi Su-35 (Russian: Сухой Су-35; NATO reporting name: Flanker-E) is the designation for two improved derivatives of the Su-27 air superiority fighter. They are single-seat, twin-engine, supermanoeuvrable air superiority fighters designed by the Sukhoi Design Bureau and built by Sukhoi. The type is regarded as a "generation 4++" fighter, bridging the gap to fifth-generation aircraft.

Development

The first Su-35 design, developed in the 1980s from the Su-27M programme, served mainly as a technology demonstrator. The current version, marketed as the Su-35S, made its first flight in 2008. It features a reinforced airframe with increased internal fuel, Saturn AL-41F1S engines with thrust-vectoring nozzles, the Irbis-E passive electronically scanned array radar — able to track many targets at long range — an OLS-50 infrared search and track system, and a modernised glass cockpit, while dispensing with the canards of earlier Flanker variants.

Service

The Russian Aerospace Forces are the largest operator, having deployed the type to Syria from 2016 and used it extensively in the Russo-Ukrainian War. China purchased 24 Su-35s, the type's first export sale; a planned Indonesian purchase was cancelled. Egypt's order was also abandoned under pressure of US sanctions.
