Tumulus culture

The Tumulus culture (German: Hügelgräberkultur) was a Bronze Age culture that dominated Central Europe during the Middle Bronze Age, roughly 1600 to 1300 BCE. It developed from the preceding Únětice culture and was succeeded by the Urnfield culture.

Characteristics

The culture is named for its practice of burying the dead in inhumation graves covered by earthen barrows (tumuli), often with rich bronze grave goods — swords, axes, pins, spiral ornaments and jewellery — indicating a warrior elite. Settlements included fortified hilltop sites as well as open villages, and the economy rested on farming and cattle raising, together with copper and tin mining and bronze working.

Distribution

Its territory stretched from the Rhine and France eastward to the Carpathians, and from the Baltic south to the Alps, encompassing regional groups such as the Lüneburg and Fulda-Werra groups. The shift from inhumation under barrows to cremation in urnfields marks the transition to the Late Bronze Age.
