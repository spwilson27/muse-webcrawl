The Kingdom of Poland (Polish: Królestwo Polskie) was the Polish state from the coronation of Bolesław I the Brave in 1025 until the Union of Lublin of 1569 merged it with the Grand Duchy of Lithuania into the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. For much of the Middle Ages it was one of the major powers of east-central Europe.

History

The Piast duchy of Mieszko I had accepted Christianity in 966; his son Bolesław I crowned himself king in 1025, weeks before his death. Royal authority then lapsed and was intermittently restored (1076, 1295) amid fragmentation into duchies after 1138. Władysław I the Elbow-high reunited the core lands and was crowned at Kraków in 1320, and his son Casimir III the Great (r. 1333–1370) codified law, founded the University of Kraków (1364), and doubled the kingdom's territory by annexing Red Ruthenia.

From 1386 the kingdom was joined in personal union with Lithuania under the Jagiellonian dynasty, following the marriage of Queen Jadwiga to Jogaila (Władysław II Jagiełło). Their victory over the Teutonic Order at Grunwald in 1410, sealed by the Peace of Thorn, and the recovery of Royal Prussia in 1466 made Poland-Lithuania the largest state in Europe. Under Sigismund I and Sigismund II Augustus the kingdom became a noble republic in all but name, dominated by the szlachta and the Sejm.

Legacy

The name "Kingdom of Poland" was revived for later entities: the Russian-ruled Congress Kingdom (1815–1915) and the German-sponsored Regency Kingdom (1916–1918).
