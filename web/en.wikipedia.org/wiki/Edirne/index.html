Edirne (Turkish pronunciation: [eˈdiɾne]), historically known as Adrianople, is a city in northwestern Turkey, in the region of Eastern Thrace, close to the borders with Greece and Bulgaria. Founded by the Roman emperor Hadrian on the site of an earlier Thracian settlement, it was the third capital of the Ottoman Empire, from the 1360s until the conquest of Constantinople in 1453.

History

Hadrian refounded the city as Hadrianopolis; it was the site of several major battles, most famously the Battle of Adrianople in 378, in which the emperor Valens was killed by the Goths. Captured by the Ottomans under Murad I in the 1360s, Edirne became the empire's capital and a major palace and trading centre. It was occupied in turn during the Russo-Turkish wars, the Balkan Wars, and after the First World War, and was returned to Turkey by the Treaty of Lausanne in 1923.

Landmarks

The city's crown is the Selimiye Mosque (1575), designed by Mimar Sinan, who considered it his masterpiece; its huge dome and four slender minarets form a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Other sights include the earlier Eski (Old) Mosque and Üç Şerefeli Mosque, the Grand Synagogue of Edirne (restored 2015), historic bridges over the Tunca and Maritsa rivers, and the former palace complex. Edirne is also famous for the Kırkpınar oil wrestling tournament, held annually since the 14th century and recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.
