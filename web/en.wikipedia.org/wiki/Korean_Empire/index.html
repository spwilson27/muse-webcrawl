The Korean Empire, officially the Empire of the Great Han (Daehan Jeguk), was the Korean state proclaimed in October 1897 by King Gojong, who took the title of emperor to assert Korea's independence and equal status amid the rivalry of China, Japan, and Russia. It succeeded the Joseon kingdom and lasted until Japan's annexation of Korea in 1910.

Gwangmu Reform

The empire undertook the Gwangmu Reform, a programme of modernisation "old foundations, new tools" that promoted industry and commerce, introduced electric lighting, trams, telephones and modern hospitals in the capital, reformed land surveys and taxation, and sent students abroad. The reforms were limited by fiscal weakness, court factionalism, and growing Japanese interference.

Loss of sovereignty

After victory in the Russo-Japanese War, Japan imposed the Eulsa Treaty of 1905, making Korea a protectorate and stripping its diplomatic rights; Gojong's secret envoys to the 1907 Hague Peace Conference were refused a hearing, and Japan forced his abdication in favour of his son Sunjong. The army was disbanded, sparking "righteous army" guerrilla resistance. In August 1910 Korea was formally annexed to the Empire of Japan, ending the Korean Empire after thirteen years.
