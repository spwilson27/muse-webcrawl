Off the record (journalism)

"Off the record" describes information given to a journalist that may not be published or attributed.

In journalism, information is described as off the record when a source provides it to a journalist under the condition that it will not be published, quoted, or used to reveal the source's connection to it. The term forms part of a spectrum of attribution agreements between reporters and sources.

Levels of attribution

Conventions vary by country and newsroom, but the commonly cited scale runs: on the record (everything may be quoted and attributed to the named source); on background (the information may be used but attributed only generally, e.g. "a senior official"); on deep background (the information may be used but not attributed to any source at all); and off the record (the information is for the journalist's understanding only and should not be published). Because sources and reporters sometimes understand these terms differently, the terms ideally are agreed explicitly before the information is given.

Ethics and disputes

Professional codes hold that an off-the-record agreement, once accepted, binds the journalist; publishing anyway can end a reporter's access and credibility. Disputes arise when a source attempts to declare information off the record only after revealing it, or when a journalist believes a source is using anonymity to spread falsehoods. Anonymous and off-record sourcing is especially common in political and diplomatic reporting, where officials discuss sensitive matters.
