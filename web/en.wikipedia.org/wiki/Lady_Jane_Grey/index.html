Lady Jane Grey

Lady Jane Grey (c. 1537 – 12 February 1554), also known as Lady Jane Dudley after her marriage and as the "Nine Days' Queen", was an English noblewoman who claimed the throne of England and Ireland from 10 July until 19 July 1553.

Early life and education

Jane was the eldest daughter of Henry Grey, 1st Duke of Suffolk, and Frances Brandon, daughter of Mary Tudor, younger sister of Henry VIII. Through her mother she was a great-granddaughter of Henry VII. Renowned for her learning—she was tutored by John Aylmer and corresponded with continental scholars such as Heinrich Bullinger—she was fluent in Latin and Greek and read Hebrew, and was a devout Protestant.

Accession and fall

Under Henry VIII's will, Edward VI's half-sisters Mary and Elizabeth were in the succession after Edward, but Edward's "Devise for the Succession" of 1553, engineered with John Dudley, 1st Duke of Northumberland—whose son Guildford Dudley Jane had married in May 1553—settled the crown on Jane instead, bypassing Mary as illegitimate and Catholic. On Edward's death (6 July), Jane was proclaimed queen on 10 July and reluctantly took up residence in the Tower of London. Support drained away as Mary rallied the country; the Privy Council proclaimed Mary queen on 19 July, and Jane was imprisoned.

Execution and legacy

Convicted of high treason in November 1553, Jane was initially spared, but her father's involvement in Wyatt's rebellion in early 1554 sealed her fate. She and Guildford were beheaded on 12 February 1554 at Tower Green; Jane was about sixteen or seventeen. Her youth, learning, and steadfast Protestant faith made her an enduring subject of literature and drama, and a Protestant martyr in popular memory.
