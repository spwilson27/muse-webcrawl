Regina (ship)

History
NameRegina
Launched1904
FateWrecked in the Great Lakes Storm of 1913

Regina was a steel-hulled package freighter that sank during the Great Lakes Storm of 1913, one of the most destructive natural disasters ever to strike the Great Lakes region. The vessel was lost with all hands in Lake Huron.

Built in 1904, Regina was approximately 250 feet (76 m) long and served the package freight trade between ports on the upper Great Lakes. During the storm of November 1913 — often called the "White Hurricane" — the ship was caught in extreme winds and waves while making her way across Lake Huron.

Loss

During the night of the storm, Regina foundered and sank. Bodies of crew members, some wearing the ship's life jackets, came ashore near Port Franks, Ontario, in the days that followed. A lifeboat from Regina was found overturned on the beach, and a bottle containing a message from a crewman was reportedly recovered, though its authenticity has been debated.

Wreck discovery

The wreck of Regina was discovered in 1986 in deep water off the Michigan coast, resting upside down on the lake bed in roughly 270 feet (82 m) of water. The discovery solved a 73-year-old mystery about the ship's final resting place. Divers and researchers have documented the wreck, which remains protected as an underwater historical site.
