Stonewall riots
Date28 June – 3 July 1969
LocationStonewall Inn, Greenwich Village, New York City
CausePolice raid on a gay bar; resistance by patrons
OutcomeCatalyst for the modern LGBTQ rights movement; first Pride marches in 1970

The Stonewall riots (also known as the Stonewall uprising) were a series of spontaneous demonstrations by members of the gay community in response to a police raid that began in the early morning hours of 28 June 1969, at the Stonewall Inn in the Greenwich Village neighbourhood of New York City. Patrons of the Stonewall and other gay and lesbian bars, along with neighbourhood street people, fought back against routine police harassment.

Background

In the 1960s, gay bars in New York operated under constant threat of raids; the State Liquor Authority refused licences to bars serving homosexuals, and patrons could be arrested for "masquerading" as the opposite gender. The Stonewall Inn, a Mafia-run bar on Christopher Street, was raided frequently, but on this night the patrons resisted. Transgender women of colour, including Marsha P. Johnson and Sylvia Rivera, are remembered among those at the forefront of the resistance.

The uprising

The confrontation drew crowds of hundreds into the streets over several nights of protest and clashes with police. Within weeks, activists had founded the Gay Liberation Front, and within months new gay rights organisations and newspapers sprang up across the United States and beyond.

Legacy

Stonewall is widely regarded as the watershed moment of the modern LGBTQ rights movement. The first Pride marches were held on 28 June 1970 in New York, Chicago, Los Angeles, and San Francisco to commemorate the anniversary. The Stonewall Inn and surrounding streets were designated a US National Monument in 2016, the first dedicated to LGBTQ rights.
