Tarn (lake)

A tarn (or corrie loch) is a small mountain lake or pond, often formed in a cirque excavated by a glacier. The word derives from Old Norse tjörn, "a small steep-banked mountain lake without a visible inflow".

Formation and distribution

As a cirque glacier retreats, meltwater collects in the rock hollow it has scoured, sometimes dammed by a moraine or rock lip. Tarns are characteristic of glaciated uplands: the Lake District of England (where "tarn" is in everyday use, as in Tarn Hows and Red Tarn), the Scottish Highlands, Scandinavia, the Alps, and the Sierra Nevada, where examples include the lakes of Yosemite's high country.

Ecology

Tarns are typically cold, clear, and nutrient-poor, supporting specialised communities of alpine plants, invertebrates, and sometimes fish such as Arctic char. Because of their small catchments they are sensitive indicators of environmental change, including acid deposition and climate warming.
