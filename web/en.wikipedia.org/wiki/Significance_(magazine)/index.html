Significance is a bimonthly magazine about statistics and data science published since 2004 by Wiley on behalf of the Royal Statistical Society (RSS). Since 2010 it has been produced jointly with the American Statistical Association (ASA), and members of both societies receive it as a benefit of membership. The magazine presents statistical ideas and their applications to a general audience, avoiding heavy technical detail.

Content

Articles apply statistics to topics in the news and everyday life—elections, sport, medicine, crime, lotteries, the environment and data visualisation—and the magazine has carried well-known pieces on subjects such as the statistics of chocolate consumption and Nobel prizes, statistical detective work on fraud, and the communication of uncertainty. It runs an annual writing competition for early-career statisticians and a long-running series celebrating statistical "unsung heroes". Regular features include book reviews, career profiles and a back-page column. The editorial aim, reflected in its strapline, is "statistics making sense of the world".

History

The RSS launched Significance in March 2004, succeeding earlier member magazines. In 2010 the ASA joined as co-publisher, broadening the contributor base and circulation across the Atlantic. The magazine's website (significancemagazine.com) publishes additional web-only articles, podcasts and material from the print issues, much of it open access after an embargo.
