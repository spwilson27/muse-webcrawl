Microcephaly

Microcephaly (from Neo-Latin microcephalia, from Ancient Greek μικρός mikrós "small" and κεφαλή kephalé "head") is a medical condition involving a smaller-than-normal head. Microcephaly may be present at birth or develop in the first few years of life. Because the size of the skull reflects the growth of the brain, microcephaly usually indicates that the brain has not developed properly or has stopped growing. It is defined as a head circumference more than two standard deviations below the mean for age and sex (severe microcephaly, more than three standard deviations below).

Causes

Microcephaly is a sign rather than a single disease. Primary (congenital) microcephaly results most often from genetic causes, including autosomal recessive primary microcephaly (MCPH), caused by mutations in genes such as ASPM, WDR62 and MCPH1 (microcephalin), which regulate the proliferation of neural progenitor cells; it also occurs in chromosomal conditions such as Down syndrome and in syndromes such as Seckel syndrome and Smith–Lemli–Opitz syndrome. Acquired causes include intrauterine infections (TORCH infections, and notably the Zika virus), exposure to alcohol (fetal alcohol spectrum disorder), certain drugs and radiation, severe maternal malnutrition, and hypoxic or infectious injury after birth. The 2015–16 Zika epidemic in the Americas drew worldwide attention when infection during pregnancy caused a sharp rise in babies born with severe microcephaly and other brain abnormalities, termed congenital Zika syndrome.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is by measuring head circumference against standard growth charts, supplemented by prenatal ultrasound, MRI imaging and genetic testing. There is no treatment that restores normal brain growth; care focuses on managing associated problems — seizures, developmental delay, feeding and movement difficulties — with early intervention, therapy and educational support. Prognosis varies widely with cause and severity; some individuals have normal intelligence, while severe congenital microcephaly is associated with significant intellectual disability.

History

People with microcephaly were exhibited in 19th- and early 20th-century sideshows as "pinheads", a history now viewed as exploitative. Evolutionary studies of microcephaly genes such as ASPM and MCPH1 have informed research on human brain-size evolution, and microcephaly was central to (discredited) claims about the small-brained hominin Homo floresiensis.
