Politics of Malaysia

The politics of Malaysia takes place in the framework of a federal representative democratic constitutional monarchy, in which the Yang di-Pertuan Agong is head of state and the Prime Minister of Malaysia is the head of government. Executive power is exercised by the federal government and the 13 state governments. Legislative power is vested in the federal parliament and the 13 state assemblies. The judiciary is independent of the executive and the legislature, though the executive maintains a certain level of influence in the appointment of judges.

The constitution of Malaysia was drafted by the Reid Commission in 1957, prior to independence from the British. Malaysia is a multi-party system; however, in practice, one coalition, Barisan Nasional, governed the country from independence until 2018, when the Pakatan Harapan coalition won the general election, resulting in the first change of government in the country's history.

Monarchy

Malaysia's head of state, the Yang di-Pertuan Agong ("King"), is elected for a five-year term by and from among the nine hereditary rulers of the Malay states. This elective monarchy is unique in the world.

Political parties and coalitions

Major political formations include the United Malays National Organisation (UMNO), the Malaysian Chinese Association (MCA), the Democratic Action Party (DAP), the People's Justice Party (PKR), the Malaysian Islamic Party (PAS), and Bersatu. Coalition politics — Barisan Nasional, Pakatan Harapan, Perikatan Nasional — dominate the political landscape.
