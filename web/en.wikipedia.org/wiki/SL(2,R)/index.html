SL(2,R)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the special linear group SL(2, R) or SL2(R) is the group of 2 × 2 real matrices with determinant one:

SL(2, R) = { (a b; c d) : a, b, c, d ∈ R and ad − bc = 1 }.

It is a connected, non-compact, simple real Lie group of dimension three, and a central object in geometry, representation theory and mathematical physics.

Geometry

SL(2, R) acts on the complex upper half-plane by fractional linear (Möbius) transformations, giving the model of two-dimensional hyperbolic geometry; the quotient by the stabiliser SO(2) is the hyperbolic plane. It is also isomorphic (up to covering) to the group of orientation-preserving isometries of the hyperbolic plane and to the Lorentz group SO+(2,1) of 2+1-dimensional special relativity. Its discrete subgroup SL(2, Z) is the modular group, fundamental in the theory of modular forms.

Structure and representations

The Iwasawa decomposition expresses every element uniquely as a product of rotation, scaling and shear matrices. SL(2, R) has trivial centre ±I, and its quotient PSL(2, R) is simple. The representation theory of SL(2, R), including the principal series, discrete series and complementary series of unitary representations, was worked out by Valentine Bargmann and is a foundational example for non-compact semisimple Lie groups. Its universal covering group plays a role in the theory of the metaplectic representation.
