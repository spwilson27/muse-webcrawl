Mumbai Harbour

Mumbai Harbour is a natural deep-water harbour in the southern portion of the Ulhas River estuary on the west coast of India. The harbour opens to the Arabian Sea to the south and separates the city of Mumbai, on Salsette Island to the west, from the mainland at Navi Mumbai to the east.

Geography

The harbour is roughly 40 kilometres long and contains several islands, including Elephanta Island, known for its rock-cut cave temples, and Butcher Island, used for oil storage. Thane Creek forms its northern continuation, while the Panvel Creek joins it from the east.

Ports

The harbour hosts the historic Mumbai Port on its western shore and the modern Jawaharlal Nehru Port (Nhava Sheva), one of India's busiest container ports, on the eastern shore. The Bandra–Worli and Mumbai Trans Harbour Link (Atal Setu) bridges connect the city with the mainland across the region.
