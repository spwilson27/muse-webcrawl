Bal Gangadhar Tilak (23 July 1856 – 1 August 1920), born Keshav Gangadhar Tilak, was an Indian nationalist, teacher, and independence activist. He was one-third of the Lal Bal Pal triumvirate of assertive nationalists and is honoured with the title Lokmanya. British authorities called him "the father of Indian unrest"; Mahatma Gandhi later called him "the maker of modern India". Tilak famously declared: "Swaraj is my birthright, and I shall have it."

Early life and education

Tilak was born in Ratnagiri, Maharashtra, into a Marathi Chitpavan Brahmin family. He earned a degree in mathematics from Deccan College, Pune, and a law degree from Government Law College, Bombay. With Gopal Ganesh Agarkar and others he founded the Deccan Education Society (1884) and Fergusson College (1885) to promote education in Indian languages and culture.

Journalism and festivals

Tilak ran two newspapers: the Marathi weekly Kesari and the English The Mahratta, both sharply critical of British rule. To build nationalist sentiment he transformed the private Ganapati festival into a large public celebration from 1893 and revived the Shivaji festival honouring the Maratha king Shivaji, linking cultural pride with political mobilization.

Nationalist leadership

Tilak led the radical (Garam Dal) wing of the Indian National Congress, advocating swadeshi, boycott, and self-rule (swaraj), which led to the Surat split of 1907 with the moderates led by Gopal Krishna Gokhale. He was imprisoned several times for sedition, most notably from 1908 to 1914 in Mandalay, Burma, where he wrote Gita Rahasya, his commentary on the Bhagavad Gita. On release he rejoined Congress, helped found the Home Rule League in 1916, and brokered the Lucknow Pact between Congress and the Muslim League.

Death and legacy

Tilak died in Bombay on 1 August 1920. His funeral drew enormous crowds. His emphasis on mass mobilization, cultural nationalism, and swaraj laid groundwork for the later Gandhian movement, and he remains one of the most revered figures of India's freedom struggle.
