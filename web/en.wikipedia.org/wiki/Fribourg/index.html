Fribourg

Fribourg (French: Fribourg; German: Freiburg im Üechtland) is the capital of the Swiss canton of Fribourg and of the district of Sarine. Situated on both sides of the river Saane/Sarine on the Swiss Plateau, it is a bilingual city (French- and German-speaking) known for its exceptionally well-preserved medieval old town.

History

Fribourg was founded in 1157 by Duke Berthold IV of Zähringen on a rocky promontory above the Sarine river. It passed to the Habsburgs in 1277 and, after allying with Bern, joined the Swiss Confederation in 1481 as its first French-speaking member. As a Catholic stronghold in the Reformation era, it hosted Jesuit institutions including the Collège Saint-Michel. The University of Fribourg, founded in 1889, is Switzerland's only bilingual university.

Geography and landmarks

The old town (Vieille-Ville) preserves over 200 Gothic house façades, medieval fountains, and stretches of the town walls. Landmarks include the Gothic St Nicholas Cathedral (begun 1283) with Art Nouveau stained glass by Józef Mehoffer, the Hôtel de Ville, the Espace Jean Tinguely–Niki de Saint Phalle, and a network of funiculars — including the Neuveville–Saint-Pierre funicular powered by wastewater. The Poya Bridge (opened 2014), the longest cable-stayed bridge in Switzerland, carries traffic north of the old town.

Culture

Fribourg is home to the ice hockey club HC Fribourg-Gottéron, whose local derby rivalry is among Switzerland's fiercest, and to festivals including the International Film Festival Fribourg (FIFF) and the Belluard Bollwerk International arts festival. Its bilingual character makes it a symbol of the Swiss "Röstigraben" language boundary.
