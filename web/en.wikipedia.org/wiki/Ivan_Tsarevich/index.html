Ivan Tsarevich

Ivan Tsarevich (Russian: Ива́н Царе́вич, "Ivan, Prince") is a stock hero of Russian folklore, the protagonist of many fairy tales collected in the 19th century by Alexander Afanasyev and others. He is typically the youngest of three sons of a tsar, akin to the "third son" figures of European folktales.

Principal tales

The most famous tale, "Ivan Tsarevich and the Grey Wolf", has the hero aided by a magical wolf in stealing the Firebird, a marvellous horse, and the princess Yelena the Beautiful from a succession of tsars. In "The Frog Princess" Ivan shoots an arrow that is retrieved by a frog, who proves to be the enchanted princess Vasilisa the Wise. Other tales pair him against the deathless sorcerer Koschei, whom he defeats by finding the needle that holds Koschei's death.

Character

Unlike the fool figure Ivan the Fool, Ivan Tsarevich is a noble hero whose success comes through courtesy, persistence, and magical helpers. He has inspired celebrated artworks, including Viktor Vasnetsov's painting of the hero on the Grey Wolf (1889) and designs by Ivan Bilibin that became emblematic of the Russian fairy-tale book.
