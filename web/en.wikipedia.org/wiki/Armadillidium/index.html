Armadillidium is a genus of terrestrial isopod crustaceans in the family Armadillidiidae, commonly known as pill woodlice or roly-polies. Its members can roll into a near-perfect sphere (conglobation) when disturbed — a defence they share, convergently, with pill millipedes and armadillos.

Distribution and species

The genus is native to Europe and the Mediterranean region, with the greatest diversity around the Mediterranean basin; the widespread Armadillidium vulgare has been introduced to many parts of the world. Around 190 species are recognised.

Ecology

Pill woodlice feed mainly on decaying plant matter and are important decomposers. Unlike insects, they are crustaceans that breathe through gill-like pleopods and must live in humid microhabitats to avoid desiccation.
