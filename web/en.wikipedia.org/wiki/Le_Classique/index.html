Le Classique

Le Classique (also le Classico or le Derby de France) is the name given to the football rivalry between French clubs Paris Saint-Germain (PSG) and Olympique de Marseille (OM). It is the biggest club fixture in French football, pitting the capital against the country's second city and its most fervently supported provincial club. The teams first met in 1971, and the rivalry intensified dramatically in the late 1980s and 1990s.

History

The fixture was unremarkable until the late 1980s, when the clubs' presidents — Francis Borelli at PSG and Bernard Tapie at Marseille — built star-studded teams and deliberately stoked a north-versus-south, capital-versus-province antagonism to sell the Canal+ television era of French football. Marseille won five consecutive league titles (1989–1993, the last later stripped) and the 1993 European Cup, while fierce on-pitch clashes, controversial refereeing episodes, and fan violence made the fixture notorious. A low point of the feud came with the 1993 Valenciennes bribery scandal under Tapie.

Marseille's relegation in 1994 cooled the contest for a time, but it re-ignited in the 2000s. Since the Qatari takeover of PSG in 2011, the balance of power has tilted heavily toward Paris, which has dominated Ligue 1; even so, meetings remain the most-watched domestic matches in France and flashpoints for supporter culture, including tifo displays and recurrent incidents that have led to away-fan bans at both grounds.

Character of the rivalry

Le Classique is often framed in cultural and political terms — Parisian wealth and centralisation against Marseille's Mediterranean, working-class, and immigrant identity — though both clubs now draw support nationally. Marseille remains the only French club to have won the European Cup/Champions League until PSG's victory in 2025, a fact long central to OM fans' claims of superiority.
