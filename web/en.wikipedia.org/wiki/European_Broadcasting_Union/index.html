European Broadcasting Union

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Eurovision Song Contest stage ablaze with light during a final
The Eurovision Song Contest stage ablaze with light during a final

The European Broadcasting Union (EBU), founded on 12 February 1950 and headquartered in Geneva, is the world's largest alliance of public service media organisations, with over 100 member broadcasters in more than 50 countries. It operates the Eurovision and Euroradio networks for exchanging news, sport and programmes, sets technical standards, and organises the Eurovision Song Contest (since 1956) — the world's longest-running annual televised music competition.

Functions

The EBU's daily work is exchange: the Eurovision News Exchange circulates members' footage, sports-rights pools bring Olympics and World Cups to public screens, and co-productions and format trades bind Europe's public broadcasters. It defends public media's interests in spectrum, copyright and platform regulation, and admits associate members worldwide (including US and Australian broadcasters).

The Song Contest

Created by Marcel Bezençon to test the young network, the Contest grew into a cultural phenomenon of 160+ million viewers, launching ABBA and Céline Dion and serving as a soft-power seismograph (Ukraine's 2022 victory amid invasion; recurring political rows over participation). The EBU's insistence on the Contest's non-political nature is tested annually and defended — sometimes by expulsion (Belarus 2021, Russia 2022).
