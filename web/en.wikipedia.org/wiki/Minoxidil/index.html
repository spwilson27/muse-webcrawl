Minoxidil

Trade namesRogaine, others
TypeVasodilator; potassium channel opener
RoutesBy mouth; topical
Legal statusOver-the-counter (topical); prescription (oral)

Minoxidil is a medication used for the treatment of high blood pressure and hair loss. It is an antihypertensive and a vasodilator. It is available as a generic medication by mouth and as an over-the-counter topical liquid or foam, sold under brand names including Rogaine.

Medical uses

High blood pressure

Oral minoxidil was developed in the 1960s and approved for severe hypertension resistant to other treatments. Because of side effects such as fluid retention and reflex tachycardia, it is generally reserved for difficult cases and combined with a beta blocker and a diuretic.

Hair loss

Patients taking oral minoxidil frequently developed increased hair growth (hypertrichosis), which led to the development of a topical formulation for androgenetic alopecia (pattern hair loss). Topical minoxidil is applied to the scalp, typically as a 2% or 5% solution or foam, and is one of the most widely used hair-loss treatments worldwide.

Mechanism of action

Minoxidil is a potassium channel opener that relaxes vascular smooth muscle, producing vasodilation. In hair follicles it is converted by the enzyme sulfotransferase to minoxidil sulfate, the active form, which is thought to prolong the anagen (growth) phase of the hair cycle and increase follicle size.

Side effects

Topical use may cause scalp irritation, itching and, rarely, unwanted facial hair growth. Oral use can cause fluid retention, rapid heartbeat, and in rare cases pericardial effusion.
