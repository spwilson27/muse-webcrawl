Quintus Naevius Cordus Sutorius Macro (21 BC – AD 38), usually known simply as Macro, was a prefect of the Praetorian Guard under the Roman emperors Tiberius and Caligula. He succeeded the infamous Sejanus in AD 31 and played a decisive role in the succession of Caligula, only to be forced to suicide by him the following year.

Rise under Tiberius

Macro was born at Alba Fucens and first comes to notice as prefect of the vigiles (the night watch and fire brigade). When Tiberius, from Capri, resolved to destroy his over-mighty prefect Sejanus in October AD 31, Macro was secretly appointed to succeed him: he slipped into Rome, presented Tiberius's letter to the Senate, and supervised Sejanus's arrest and execution, along with the purge of his family and supporters. As prefect, Macro became Tiberius's chief agent in Rome and was implicated in the treason trials of the reign; the historian Tacitus also credits him with hastening Tiberius's end — some sources say he smothered the old emperor in AD 37 after he unexpectedly revived from a coma.

Caligula and fall

Macro had cultivated Tiberius's heir Gaius (Caligula), even, according to Suetonius and Philo, encouraging — or at least tolerating — Gaius's relations with his own wife Ennia Thrasylla to secure favour. He delivered the Praetorians' support for Caligula's accession and was initially among the new emperor's most powerful men, receiving high honours and the governorship of Egypt for his future. But Caligula soon turned on him: in AD 38 Macro was stripped of office, arrested on charges relating to alleged procuring, and compelled to kill himself; Ennia also died by suicide. His fate is a classic case of the dangers facing a kingmaker in the early Principate.

In popular culture

Macro appears as a major character in Robert Graves's novel I, Claudius and its BBC adaptation, and features in historical dramas about Caligula as the archetypal ruthless guards commander.
