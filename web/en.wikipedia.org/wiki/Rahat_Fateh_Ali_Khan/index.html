Rahat Fateh Ali Khan

Rahat Fateh Ali Khan performing qawwali.

Rahat Fateh Ali Khan (born 9 December 1974) is a Pakistani singer, primarily of qawwali, the devotional music of Sufism. The nephew and successor of Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, he is one of the best-known qawwali performers in the world and a prolific playback singer for Pakistani and Indian films.

Early life and training

Rahat was born in Faisalabad, Punjab, into a family of qawwals stretching back generations. He was trained from childhood by his uncle Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan and his father, Farrukh Fateh Ali Khan, and performed publicly from a young age, touring the United Kingdom with his uncle's party as a teenager.

Career

After Nusrat's death in 1997, Rahat became the leader of the family qawwali party. He achieved international recognition with songs for Bollywood films such as "Mann Ki Lagan" from Paap (2003), "Jiya Dhadak Dhadak" and "Tere Mast Mast Do Nain", and performed on Pakistani television's Coke Studio. In 2010 he became the first Pakistani singer to perform at the Nobel Peace Prize concert. He has also contributed to Hollywood soundtracks, including Apocalypto.

Recognition

Rahat has received numerous awards, including Filmfare and Lux Style Awards, and holds an honorary doctorate from the University of Oxford (2019) for his contribution to music. See also qawwali and Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan.
