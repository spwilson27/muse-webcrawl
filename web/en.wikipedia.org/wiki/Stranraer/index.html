Stranraer (Scottish Gaelic: An t-Sròn Reamhar) is a town in Dumfries and Galloway, south-western Scotland. It lies on the shores of Loch Ryan, a sea loch opening into the North Channel, at the neck of the Rhins of Galloway peninsula. Stranraer is Dumfries and Galloway's second-largest town and was for centuries a major port for ferries to Northern Ireland.

History

The town developed around the Castle of St John, a four-storey tower house built about 1500 by the Adairs of Kilhilt. Stranraer became a burgh of barony in 1596 and a royal burgh in 1617. Its sheltered harbour on Loch Ryan made it a natural departure point for Ireland; in the 19th century steamers ran to Larne, and the railway reached the town in 1861, with Stranraer Harbour station serving boat trains. During the Second World War the area around Loch Ryan was an important base for flying boats and anti-submarine operations guarding the western approaches.

Ferry port

For over 150 years ferries sailed from Stranraer (later from the harbour station pier) to Larne in County Antrim. In November 2011, Stena Line moved its operations from Stranraer to a new port at Old House Point near Cairnryan, several miles north, where P&O Ferries also operates services to Larne; the move shortened the sea crossing to Belfast. The old harbour area has since been the subject of regeneration plans.

Transport and amenities

The A75 road links Stranraer to Dumfries and the motorway network, while the A77 runs north along the coast toward Ayr and Glasgow. Stranraer railway station is the southern terminus of the Glasgow South Western Line. The town's football club, Stranraer F.C., founded in 1870, is among the oldest in Scotland and plays at Stair Park. Local attractions include the Stranraer Museum, housed in the old town hall, and nearby Castle Kennedy Gardens.

Climate and geography

Stranraer has a mild maritime climate influenced by the North Atlantic Drift; the nearby gardens of the Rhins are known for tender plants that survive outdoors thanks to the sheltered, nearly frost-free conditions.
