European Union energy label

The European Union energy label is a standardised label, required on many household appliances and other energy-related products sold in the European Union, that displays the product's energy efficiency class and key consumption figures. It uses a scale of coloured bars running from green (most efficient) to red (least efficient), accompanied by letter classes.

History

Energy labelling was introduced by Directive 92/75/EEC in 1992, with classes from A (best) to G (worst). As technology improved, most products clustered in the top classes, so additional grades A+, A++, and A+++ were added from 2010 onwards. The proliferation of "plus" classes was widely judged confusing, and a 2017 framework regulation reintroduced a simple A-to-G scale. Rescaled labels for refrigerators, washing machines, dishwashers, televisions, and lighting took effect in 2021, with the most efficient products initially placed in class B or C to leave headroom at the top.

Content

Besides the efficiency class, labels show annual energy consumption and product-specific data such as water consumption and noise emissions, and carry a QR code linking to the EPREL product database. The label is mandatory for a defined list of product groups and must be displayed at the point of sale, including in online shops.
