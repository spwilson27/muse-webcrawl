Azrael is an alias used by several fictional characters appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. The character was created by Denny O'Neil and Joe Quesada and first appeared in Batman: Sword of Azrael #1 (October 1992). The name derives from Azrael, the traditional name of the angel of death. The most prominent Azrael is Jean-Paul Valley, a genetically engineered test-tube baby raised by the secretive Order of St. Dumas to be its assassin and avenging angel.

Publication history

Azrael was introduced in the 1992 miniseries Batman: Sword of Azrael. Valley became a supporting character in the Batman titles and gained wide prominence during the Knightfall storyline (1993–1994), in which Bruce Wayne, his back broken by Bane, chose Valley as his temporary replacement as Batman. Outfitted with an armoured, high-tech batsuit, Valley's crusade grew increasingly violent and unstable under the influence of his conditioning — "the System" — culminating in him letting a villain fall to his death. Wayne returned, defeated Valley, and reclaimed the mantle. Valley subsequently starred in his own ongoing series, Azrael (1995–2003), seeking redemption as an agent of good rather than vengeance.

Other versions and in other media

A second Azrael, former Gotham police officer Michael Washington Lane, took up the mantle in the late 2000s, wearing the Suit of Sorrows and wielding the Sword of Sin. The character has appeared in other media, including the television series Gotham, where Theo Galavan becomes Azrael, and in video games such as the Batman: Arkham series, in which an Azrael based on Lane features.
