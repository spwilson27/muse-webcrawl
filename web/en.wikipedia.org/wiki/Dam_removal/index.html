Dam removal

Dam removal is the process of demolishing a dam and restoring a river's natural flow, undertaken for ecological restoration, safety, economic, and cultural reasons.


Purpose: River restoration, fish passage, safety
Examples: Elwha River, Klamath River

Reasons

Dams block migratory fish, trap sediment, alter temperature, and create safety liabilities as they age. Indigenous nations have led many removal campaigns to restore fisheries.

Effects

Sediment management is the central challenge. Elwha removals (2011–2014) showed rapid salmon recolonisation; Klamath removals (2023–2024) became the largest ever.

Debate

Supporters cite ecosystem recovery; opponents cite loss of hydropower, storage, and lake recreation.
