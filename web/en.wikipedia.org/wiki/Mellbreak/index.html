Mellbreak

Mellbreak is a hill in the Western part of the English Lake District, Cumbria. Despite being surrounded on three sides by a "moat" of deep valleys (the Lorton Vale, Buttermere valley, and the valley of the River Cocker/Loweswater), Mellbreak is not isolated, being connected to the higher fell of Hen Comb by a marshy col. It stands 512 m (1,680 ft) high and is a Wainwright fell, described in Alfred Wainwright's Pictorial Guide volume on the Western Fells.

Topography

Mellbreak's distinctive shape — a long, steep-sided ridge falling sharply to Crummock Water to the east — makes it one of the most recognisable profiles in the north-western Lakes, commonly admired across the lake from Rannerdale. It has two summits about half a mile apart, the northern top being the higher. A prominent notch ("the Nick") crosses the ridge. The fell's name is probably of Brittonic origin, from elements meaning "bare hill".

Geology and ascent

The hill is formed of the mudstones and sandstones of the Kirkstile Formation of the Skiddaw Group. Popular ascents start from Loweswater village or the Kirkstile Inn, climbing beside Mosedale Beck and through the plantation before crossing the Nick to the summit cairn; Wainwright called its traverse above Crummock one of the finest low-level fell walks in the district.
