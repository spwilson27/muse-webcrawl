A multimeter is a measuring instrument that combines several measurement functions in one unit. A typical multimeter measures voltage, current, and resistance; many also test continuity, capacitance, frequency, temperature, and transistors.

Types

Analog multimeters use a moving-pointer galvanometer and draw a small current from the circuit under test. The archetype was the British Avometer, designed by Donald Macadie and introduced in 1923. Digital multimeters (DMMs), which became dominant from the 1970s, convert the measured quantity with an analog-to-digital converter and show the value on a numeric display; they offer high input impedance (typically 10 MΩ) and features such as auto-ranging and true-RMS AC measurement.

Operation and safety

Voltage is measured in parallel with the circuit, current in series, and resistance on a de-energised component. Because multimeters may be used on mains and industrial circuits, modern instruments carry safety category ratings (CAT I–CAT IV) indicating the transient voltages they can withstand; using an under-rated meter on high-energy circuits is a recognised hazard.
