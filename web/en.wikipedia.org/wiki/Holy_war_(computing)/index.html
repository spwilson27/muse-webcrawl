Holy war (computing)

A holy war in computing is an intense, often interminable debate over a matter of opinion or preference — such as the choice of text editor, operating system, or programming style — in which participants defend their position with near-religious fervour and the underlying issues are largely matters of taste rather than measurable fact. The term is documented in hacker culture and the Jargon File.

Classic holy wars

  • Editor wars: Emacs versus vi (and their descendants), famously parodied by the "Church of Emacs" and Saint IGNUcius.
  • Operating systems: Unix versus VMS historically; later Windows versus Macintosh versus Linux.
  • Indentation style: tabs versus spaces, and brace placement (K&R versus Allman) in programming.
  • Endianness: big-endian versus little-endian — the very terms were borrowed from the religious war in Swift's Gulliver's Travels, which also inspired the phrase itself.
  • Programming languages and paradigms: for example, C versus Pascal, or static versus dynamic typing.

Character

Holy wars are conducted through flame wars on mailing lists and forums, manifestos, and parody. While usually good-natured within the community, the concept recognises that such arguments are rarely settled by evidence, because the criteria are subjective — ergonomics, familiarity, and aesthetics.
