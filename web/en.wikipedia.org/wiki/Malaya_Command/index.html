Malaya Command

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Malaya Command was a formation of the British Army formed in the early 1920s for the defence of British Malaya — comprising the Straits Settlements, the Federated Malay States and the Unfederated Malay States — together with Singapore. Its headquarters were in Singapore.

Second World War

During the Malayan campaign of December 1941 – February 1942, Malaya Command was commanded by Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival. The command included III Indian Corps, the 8th Australian Division, and other British, Indian and Malayan troops. Despite outnumbering the invading Japanese Twenty-Fifth Army, the command's forces were defeated in a rapid campaign culminating in the fall of Singapore on 15 February 1942, the largest surrender of British-led forces in history.

Post-war

After the Japanese surrender in 1945, Malaya Command was re-established to administer the British Military Administration of Malaya. It later became part of the Far East Land Forces structure and was involved in operations during the Malayan Emergency (1948–1960), before the command was wound up as British forces withdrew from east of Suez.
