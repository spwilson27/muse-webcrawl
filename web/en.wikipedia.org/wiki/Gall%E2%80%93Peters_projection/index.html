Gall–Peters projection
Type: Cylindrical equal-area map projection
Described by: James Gall (1855); promoted by Arno Peters (1973)
Property: Preserves relative areas

The Gall–Peters projection is a rectangular, equal-area map projection. Like all equal-area projections, it distorts most shapes, stretching regions near the equator vertically. It was first described in 1855 by the Scottish clergyman James Gall and was independently presented in 1973 by German historian Arno Peters as a purportedly new invention, sparking a long controversy in cartography.

Debate

Peters argued that the popular Mercator projection unfairly exaggerated the size of Europe and North America relative to Africa and South America, and promoted his map as a corrective for education and development work. Cartographers countered that the projection was not new, that Mercator was designed for navigation rather than area comparison, and that the Peters map's distortions were themselves severe. Nonetheless, the map was adopted by some schools and organisations, and the debate raised lasting awareness that all world maps make trade-offs.
