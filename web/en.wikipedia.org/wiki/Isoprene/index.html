Isoprene

Isoprene, or 2-methyl-1,3-butadiene, is a common volatile organic compound with the formula CH2=C(CH3)−CH=CH2. In its pure form it is a colourless volatile liquid with a boiling point of about 34.1 °C. Isoprene is produced by many plants and animals, and its polymers are the main component of natural rubber.

Natural sources

Isoprene is produced and emitted by many species of trees, particularly oaks, poplars, eucalypts, and some legumes. Annual global emissions of isoprene are estimated at 500–750 million tonnes, making it the most abundant single biogenic volatile organic compound released into the atmosphere. In the human body, isoprene is a by-product of cholesterol synthesis and is present in exhaled breath.

Biological role

The isoprene unit is the building block of the terpenes and terpenoids (isoprenoids), a vast class of natural products that includes carotenoids, steroids, and the side chain of chlorophyll. In plants, isoprene emission appears to protect leaves against heat stress and reactive oxygen species.

Industrial use

Industrially, isoprene is polymerised to give polyisoprene, a synthetic substitute for natural rubber, and is copolymerised with isobutylene to make butyl rubber.
