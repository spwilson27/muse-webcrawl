Root-raised-cosine filter

The root-raised-cosine (RRC) filter is a filter frequently used as the transmit and receive filter in a digital communication system to perform matched filtering. Its name stems from the fact that its frequency response is the square root of the frequency response of the raised-cosine filter.

Rationale

A raised-cosine channel response satisfies the Nyquist criterion for zero intersymbol interference (ISI). If the overall response is split equally between the transmitter and the receiver, each side implements the square root of the desired response — the root-raised-cosine filter — and the cascade of the two is a raised-cosine filter. In addition, using identical filters at both ends makes the receive filter a matched filter for the transmitted pulse shape, maximising the signal-to-noise ratio at the sampling instants.

Parameters

The filter is characterised by the symbol period and the roll-off factor β (0 ≤ β ≤ 1), which determines the excess bandwidth beyond the minimum Nyquist bandwidth. Small values of β give a sharper spectral cut-off but a longer impulse response with larger sidelobes, making practical implementations more sensitive to timing errors.

Use

Root-raised-cosine filtering is specified in many digital radio standards, including satellite links and the physical layers of several cellular and broadcast systems, typically implemented as a finite impulse response (FIR) filter spanning many symbol periods.
