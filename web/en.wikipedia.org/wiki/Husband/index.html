Husband

A husband is a male in a marital relationship, commonly also referred to as a spouse. The rights, obligations and social status assigned to a husband vary considerably across cultures and legal systems, and have changed over time. The female counterpart is a wife.

Etymology

The English word derives from Old Norse húsbóndi, meaning "master of a house", from hús (house) and bóndi (dweller, holder). Related ideas survive in the term "husbandry" — the careful management of household or farm resources.

Historical and legal roles

In many historical societies the husband was recognised as head of the household, with legal authority over his wife and children and responsibility for the family's economic support and protection. Under English common law, the doctrine of coverture merged a wife's legal identity into her husband's. Reforms from the nineteenth century onwards — married women's property acts, no-fault divorce, and the criminalisation of marital rape in most Western jurisdictions by the late twentieth century — moved marriage toward a partnership of legal equals. In exchange for a bride, some cultures historically involved bride price or dowry arrangements tied to the husband's household.

Related terms

A man during his engagement is a fiancé; after his wife's death he is a widower. Terms for husbands in same-sex marriages follow the same usage in jurisdictions recognising such unions. In informal usage, "hubby" is a common diminutive.
