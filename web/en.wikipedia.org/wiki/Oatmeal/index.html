Oatmeal

Oatmeal is a porridge made from oats that have been husked, steamed, and flattened (rolled oats), or ground into meal or flour. Cooked with water or milk, it is eaten as a breakfast dish in many countries — traditionally in Scotland, where porridge is made with steel-cut "pinhead" oats, water, and salt. Oatmeal is valued for its soluble fibre, particularly beta-glucan, which is associated with reduced blood cholesterol.

Types

Oat products range from whole oat groats through steel-cut oats (groats chopped into pieces), traditional rolled oats, quick oats (rolled thinner), and instant oatmeal (pre-cooked, dried, and often flavoured). The finer the processing, the shorter the cooking time and the higher the glycaemic response. Oatmeal is also used in baking — oatcakes, flapjacks, and oatmeal cookies — and as a thickener and in cosmetics and skincare.

Nutrition

A typical serving of plain oatmeal provides complex carbohydrates, about 4–5 grams of fibre and 5 grams of protein per cooked cup, plus manganese, phosphorus, magnesium, and B vitamins. Health authorities in several countries permit a heart-health claim for oat beta-glucan when consumed in sufficient quantities as part of a diet low in saturated fat.
