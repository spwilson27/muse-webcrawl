Lomography is a photographic movement and commercial enterprise centred on low-fidelity, analogue snapshot photography. It takes its name from the Russian manufacturer LOMO (Leningrad Optical Mechanical Association) and its LC-A compact camera, whose lens produced pictures with strong vignetting, saturated colours and distortion. The movement was founded in 1992 in Vienna, Austria, when a group of students discovered the LC-A in Prague and began exhibiting the spontaneous photographs it produced.

Principles

Lomography is codified in "Ten Golden Rules", which include "take your camera everywhere you go", "use it any time – day and night", "lomography is not an interference in your life, but part of it", and the concluding "don't think, just shoot". The aesthetic values spontaneity, shooting from the hip, and embracing technical imperfection over the precision associated with conventional photography.

Company and products

The Lomographische AG company secured distribution rights for the LC-A and later commissioned its production under the name Lomo LC-A+. It has since produced a wide range of film cameras — including the Diana, Holga-derived models, the Sprocket Rocket, the Lomo'Instant instant cameras and the Petzval-art lenses — as well as its own colour negative, black-and-white and special-effect films in 35 mm, 120 and 110 formats. Lomography operates "embassy" stores and gallery spaces in cities worldwide and maintains an online community magazine and competitions.

Influence

Lomography is credited with reviving interest in film photography during the digital era and with influencing the lo-fi filters of early smartphone photo apps. The word "lomography" is sometimes used generically for the snapshot aesthetic it popularised.
