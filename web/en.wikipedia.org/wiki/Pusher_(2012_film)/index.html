Pusher is a 2012 British crime thriller film directed by Luis Prieto. It is an English-language remake of the 1996 Danish film Pusher by Nicolas Winding Refn, who served as an executive producer on the remake. The film relocates the story from Copenhagen to London and stars Richard Coyle as a drug dealer whose life disintegrates over the course of one desperate week. Zlatko Burić, who played the Serbian drug lord Milo in the original Danish trilogy, reprises the role in the remake.

Plot

Frank is a London drug dealer with a comfortable life: money, a nightclub lifestyle, and a girlfriend, Flo, a former dancer. Persuaded by his old friend Tommy to join him in a big one-off deal, Frank borrows a consignment of cocaine from the crime boss Milo. The deal goes wrong when the police raid the meeting and Frank is forced to dump the drugs in a lake. Released without charge but having lost both the drugs and the money, Frank is given a short deadline to repay Milo, with interest and penalties mounting daily. Over the following days Frank descends into frantic, increasingly violent attempts to collect old debts and raise the money, alienating friends, brutalising debtors and destroying his relationship with Flo, while Milo's patience and Frank's options run out.

Production and reception

The remake was produced by Vertigo Films and premiered at film festivals in 2012, including a screening at the Toronto International Film Festival, before a UK theatrical release in October 2012. Its electronic soundtrack was provided by Orbital. Reviews were mixed: critics praised Coyle's intense performance and the film's propulsive style, but many judged the remake unnecessary next to Refn's original, one of the landmarks of modern Danish cinema. It performed modestly at the box office and is generally remembered as a stylish but inessential reworking.
