Counties of Norway

[Image placeholder: map of Norwegian counties]

Norway is divided into 15 counties (fylker, singular fylke), which form the first-level administrative divisions of the country; the counties are further divided into municipalities. Each county has an elected county council and a county mayor, responsible for areas such as upper-secondary education, public transport and regional planning, alongside a state-appointed county governor (Statsforvalteren) who supervises municipalities and represents central government.

The number of counties has changed substantially in recent years. A regional reform that took effect on 1 January 2020 merged the traditional 18 counties (excluding Oslo's dual status) into 11 larger regions, including the controversial mergers of Troms and Finnmark and of Viken around Oslo. Following political opposition, several mergers were reversed on 1 January 2024, restoring Troms, Finnmark, Akershus, Buskerud, Østfold and others, bringing the total to 15.
