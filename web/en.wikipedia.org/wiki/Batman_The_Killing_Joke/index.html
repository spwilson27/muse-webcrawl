Batman: The Killing Joke

Batman: The Killing Joke is a 1988 DC Comics graphic novel written by Alan Moore, illustrated by Brian Bolland and coloured by John Higgins. One of the most influential Batman stories ever published, it presents a possible origin of the Joker and centres on the villain's attempt to prove that any person can be driven insane by "one bad day" — a thesis he tests by attacking Commissioner Gordon and his daughter Barbara.

The Joker photographed Barbara Gordon in one of the book's most notorious scenes.

Plot

Intercut with Batman's search for the escaped Joker is a flashback narrative: a failed stand-up comedian, destitute and expecting a child, agrees to help criminals rob the chemical plant where he once worked, donning the Red Hood disguise. The heist goes wrong; his wife dies in a freak accident the same day, and fleeing Batman he falls into a vat of chemicals that bleaches his skin and drives him mad. In the present, the Joker shoots and paralyses Barbara Gordon (Batgirl), photographs her, and subjects Commissioner Gordon to a grotesque funhouse ordeal to break his sanity. Gordon endures, Batman captures the Joker, and the book ends ambiguously as Batman reaches out to his enemy and the Joker tells a joke about two lunatics — both dissolve into laughter as the rain begins.

Reception and legacy

The story won the Eisner Award for Best Graphic Album and has remained continuously in print. Barbara Gordon's paralysis became lasting continuity: she was reinvented as the wheelchair-using information broker Oracle, a prominent representation of a disabled hero, until DC restored her as Batgirl in the 2011 New 52 reboot. The book's dark tone and its treatment of Barbara have also drawn enduring criticism. Moore himself later distanced himself from aspects of the work. An animated film adaptation was released in 2016, reuniting Kevin Conroy and Mark Hamill.
