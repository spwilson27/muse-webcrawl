Muscarinic antagonist

A muscarinic antagonist (also known as a muscarinic receptor antagonist or antimuscarinic agent) is a type of anticholinergic drug that blocks the activity of the muscarinic acetylcholine receptors. Blocking these receptors inhibits the parasympathetic nervous system effects mediated by acetylcholine at muscarinic sites.

Effects and uses

Antimuscarinic effects include reduced secretions, relaxation of smooth muscle, pupil dilation (mydriasis), increased heart rate and decreased gastrointestinal motility. Drugs in this class are used for many purposes: atropine in anaesthesia and ophthalmology and as an antidote to organophosphate poisoning; scopolamine for motion sickness; ipratropium and tiotropium as inhaled bronchodilators in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; and oxybutynin for overactive bladder.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, urinary retention, tachycardia and confusion, particularly in older patients. They are often summarised as effects on the eyes, glands, bladder, gut and heart.
