Helmut Rahn


Helmut Rahn
Born: 16 August 1929, Essen
Died: 14 August 2003, Essen
Position: Outside right / forward

Helmut Rahn (16 August 1929 – 14 August 2003), known as Der Boss ("The Boss"), was a German footballer who played as a forward. He is best remembered as the scorer of the winning goal for West Germany in the final of the 1954 FIFA World Cup against Hungary — the "Miracle of Bern" — one of the most famous moments in German sporting history.

Career

Rahn began his career with SC Oelde and Sportfreunde Katernberg before joining Rot-Weiss Essen in 1951, where he won the DFB-Pokal in 1953 and the German championship in 1955. He later played for 1. FC Köln, SC Enschede in the Netherlands, and Meidericher SV, with whom he finished Bundesliga runner-up in 1963–64. He was among the first players to score 100 Bundesliga-era goals in the preceding Oberliga system and ended with 40 caps and 21 goals for West Germany between 1951 and 1960.

1954 World Cup

In the 1954 World Cup final in Bern, West Germany trailed favourites Hungary 2–0 before recovering to 2–2. Rahn scored the equaliser and, in the 84th minute, the winning goal from the edge of the penalty area: "Rahn schießt — Tor!" ("Rahn shoots — goal!") cried radio commentator Herbert Zimmermann, in a broadcast that became legendary. Rahn also scored in the 1958 World Cup, becoming one of few players to score at least four goals in two different World Cups.

Later life

After retiring, Rahn worked as a car salesman in Essen and struggled at times with alcohol. He died in 2003, two days before his 74th birthday. A statue of him stands in Essen, and he remains an iconic figure of the post-war "Wirtschaftswunder" era.
