Fisherman's Bastion

The Fisherman's Bastion (Hungarian: Halászbástya) is one of the best known monuments in Budapest, Hungary. It is located on the Buda bank of the Danube, on Castle Hill, around Matthias Church. It was built between 1895 and 1902 in a Neo-Romanesque style, on the base of a stretch of the Buda Castle walls, and was designed by architect Frigyes Schulek.

History

The Bastion's name recalls the Guild of Fishermen who defended this stretch of the city walls in the Middle Ages. Its seven towers represent the seven Magyar tribes that founded Hungary in 895. The terrace offers panoramic views over the Danube, Margaret Island, and Pest, including the Hungarian Parliament Building directly across the river.

Between the Bastion and Matthias Church stands an equestrian statue of Stephen I of Hungary, erected in 1906. The bastion was badly damaged in the Second World War and was restored afterwards; a major restoration was completed in the 1990s and 2000s. Since 1987 it has formed part of Budapest's UNESCO World Heritage listing.
