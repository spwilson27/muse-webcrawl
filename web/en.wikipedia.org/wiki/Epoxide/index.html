Epoxide

An epoxide is a cyclic ether with a three-membered ring: an oxygen atom bonded to two adjacent carbon atoms. The simplest epoxide is ethylene oxide (oxirane). The ring is highly strained, which makes epoxides far more reactive than ordinary ethers and valuable intermediates in organic synthesis.

Synthesis

Epoxides are commonly prepared by epoxidation of alkenes with peroxy acids such as mCPBA (the Prilezhaev reaction), or industrially by reacting alkenes with hypochlorous acid followed by base (chlorohydrin route). Ethylene oxide is produced on a huge scale by silver-catalysed direct oxidation of ethylene with air or oxygen. Asymmetric epoxidation of allylic alcohols is achieved by the Sharpless epoxidation.

Reactions

The ring opens readily with nucleophiles under acidic or basic conditions, giving trans-1,2-disubstituted products such as diols, amino alcohols, and ethers. Epoxides are the key functional group in epoxy resins, formed by curing epoxide monomers (typically based on bisphenol A diglycidyl ether) with hardeners to produce adhesives, coatings, and composites. Some epoxides, such as the arene oxides formed in metabolism, are reactive intermediates implicated in toxicity and carcinogenesis.
