Kees Schouhamer Immink

Placeholder image: Kees Schouhamer Immink.

Kornelis Antonie "Kees" Schouhamer Immink (born 18 December 1946) is a Dutch engineer, inventor and entrepreneur best known for his pioneering work on digital recording systems. At Philips, he was a principal designer of the coding technology behind the Compact Disc, and he also contributed to the DVD and Blu-ray Disc formats.

Career at Philips

Immink joined Philips Research Laboratories in Eindhoven in 1967. Working with Toshitada Doi and others, he developed the Eight-to-Fourteen Modulation (EFM) code used in the Compact Disc, which converts 8-bit symbols into 14-bit channel patterns satisfying the disc's run-length constraints, greatly improving robustness and playing time. He later designed EFMPlus for DVD.

Later career and honours

In 1994 Immink founded the Institute for Experimental Mathematics at the University of Duisburg-Essen and later Turing Machines, a research consultancy in Rotterdam. He has received numerous honours, including an Emmy Award, the IEEE Edison Medal (2017), and election to national academies of engineering and sciences. He holds hundreds of patents in coding and recording technology.
