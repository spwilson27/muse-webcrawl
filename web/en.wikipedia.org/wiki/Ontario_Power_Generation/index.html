Ontario Power Generation

Ontario Power Generation Inc. (OPG) is a Crown corporation wholly owned by the Government of Ontario. It is responsible for roughly half of the electricity generation in the province of Ontario, Canada, producing power primarily from nuclear and hydroelectric sources. Its headquarters are in Toronto.

History

OPG was created in 1999 as one of the successor companies when the provincial utility Ontario Hydro was broken up under Ontario's Energy Competition Act. OPG inherited Ontario Hydro's generating assets, including its nuclear, hydroelectric, and fossil-fuel stations, while transmission was assigned to Hydro One.

Generation fleet

OPG operates the Pickering and Darlington nuclear generating stations in the Greater Toronto Area; the Bruce nuclear station is leased to Bruce Power. Its hydroelectric fleet includes large stations on the St. Lawrence River and at Niagara, such as the Sir Adam Beck stations, along with many smaller facilities across the province. The company formerly operated coal-fired plants, including the Nanticoke and Lambton stations, but Ontario completed a phase-out of coal-fired generation in 2014; some former coal sites were converted to biomass or solar generation.

Projects

Major undertakings have included the refurbishment of the Darlington station to extend its operating life, the Niagara Tunnel project to divert additional water to the Sir Adam Beck complex, and plans for small modular reactors at Darlington. OPG is also responsible for the long-term management of nuclear waste from its stations through Canada's Nuclear Waste Management Organization framework.
