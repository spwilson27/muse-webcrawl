Boris Karloff (born William Henry Pratt; 23 November 1887 – 2 February 1969) was an English actor who became one of the great stars of horror cinema, best known for his portrayal of Frankenstein's monster in Frankenstein (1931), Bride of Frankenstein (1935) and Son of Frankenstein (1939). For his early horror roles he was sometimes billed simply as "Karloff" or by a question mark in the credits.

[ Image: Boris Karloff in Jack Pierce's make-up as Frankenstein's monster, 1931 ]

Career

Pratt emigrated to Canada in 1909 and spent a decade in travelling theatre before reaching Hollywood, where he laboured for years in silent films and bit parts. His breakthrough came at 44, when director James Whale cast him as the Monster in Universal's Frankenstein (1931), wearing Jack Pierce's celebrated make-up. He followed it with The Old Dark House (1932), The Mummy (1932), The Black Cat (1934) with Bela Lugosi, and the Val Lewton productions The Body Snatcher (1945) and Bedlam (1946) for RKO.

Karloff worked extensively in theatre, radio and television — he hosted and starred in the anthology series Thriller (1960–62) — and narrated the animated special How the Grinch Stole Christmas! (1966), also voicing the Grinch. His final major film performance, in Peter Bogdanovich's Targets (1968), was a valedictory portrait of an ageing horror star.

Personal life and legacy

A founding member of the Screen Actors Guild, Karloff was renowned for his gentleness off screen, in contrast to his menacing roles. He has two stars on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, for film and television.
