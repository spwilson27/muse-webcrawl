Child mortality

Child mortality is the death of children under the age of five, measured by the under-five mortality rate (U5MR): the probability, per 1,000 live births, that a newborn will die before reaching age five. It is a key indicator of child health, nutrition and overall development, tracked by UNICEF and targeted by the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 3.2 aims for under-five mortality of at most 25 per 1,000 live births by 2030).

Causes

Nearly half of under-five deaths occur in the first month of life (the neonatal period), mainly from preterm birth complications, intrapartum events (birth asphyxia) and infections such as sepsis. Beyond the neonatal period, the leading killers are pneumonia, diarrhoeal diseases, malaria and congenital anomalies, with undernutrition underlying an estimated 45 per cent of all child deaths.

Trends

Global child mortality has fallen dramatically: the under-five mortality rate declined by about 60 per cent between 1990 and 2022, and annual deaths dropped from 12.8 million to under 5 million, thanks to immunisation, oral rehydration therapy, insecticide-treated bed nets, antibiotics, improved water and sanitation, and better maternal education and nutrition. Progress has been slower in sub-Saharan Africa and fragile states, and the first month of life now accounts for a growing share of deaths. Related measures include the infant mortality rate (deaths under age one) and neonatal mortality rate (deaths in the first 28 days).
