Rauma, Finland

Rauma
Country: Finland
Region: Satakunta
Founded: 1442

The wooden houses of Old Rauma.

Rauma (Swedish: Raumo) is a town and municipality on the west coast of Finland, in the Satakunta region, located about 92 km north of Turku and 50 km south of Pori. Granted town privileges in 1442, it is one of Finland's oldest harbours.

Old Rauma

The wooden old town, Old Rauma (Vanha Rauma), is one of the best-preserved Nordic wooden town centres and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1991. It contains some 600 wooden buildings, most dating from the 18th and 19th centuries, and remains a living residential and commercial district.

Economy and culture

Rauma is a significant port and industrial centre, with paper and pulp mills, shipbuilding, and the nearby Olkiluoto Nuclear Power Plant in neighbouring Eurajoki. The town is also famous for its lace-making tradition, celebrated in the annual Lace Week (Pitsiviikko), and for its distinctive local dialect influenced by centuries of seafaring contact.
