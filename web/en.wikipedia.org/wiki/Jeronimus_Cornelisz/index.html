Jeronimus Cornelisz (c. 1598 – 2 October 1629) was a Dutch apothecary and an officer of the Dutch East India Company (VOC). He is best known as the leader of a mutiny and bloody massacre after the wreck of the VOC ship Batavia on Morning Reef in the Houtman Abrolhos islands off the coast of Western Australia in 1629.

Background

Cornelisz was born in Leeuwarden in Friesland and trained as an apothecary. After his business failed and he fled Haarlem amid allegations of heretical Anabaptist sympathies, he joined the VOC and sailed on the Batavia in October 1628 as under-merchant, second in rank to the ship's commander, Francisco Pelsaert. During the voyage Cornelisz and the skipper Ariaen Jacobsz plotted to seize the ship and its treasure.

The wreck and the reign of terror

On 4 June 1629 the Batavia struck a reef. Most of the roughly 322 people aboard reached the barren islands, and Pelsaert departed in the ship's longboat for Batavia (Jakarta) to fetch help. In his absence Cornelisz, the senior officer remaining, established a reign of terror: he and his followers systematically murdered more than 110 castaways — men, women and children — to reduce competition for food and to consolidate power, drowning, stabbing or strangling groups on the surrounding islands. A band of soldiers under Wiebbe Hayes resisted from West Wallabi Island, defeating two attacks by the mutineers.

Capture and execution

Pelsaert returned aboard the Sardam on 17 September 1629, just as the final assault on Hayes's men was underway. Cornelisz and his accomplices were captured, tried on the islands, and on 2 October 1629 Cornelisz had both hands cut off before being hanged, along with six of his men, on Seal Island. Of the original complement, only about 116 people survived the entire episode.

Legacy

The Batavia massacre is one of the most notorious episodes of maritime history and has been retold in numerous books, including Mike Dash's Batavia's Graveyard (2002). The wreck site and artefacts are preserved by the Western Australian Museum.
