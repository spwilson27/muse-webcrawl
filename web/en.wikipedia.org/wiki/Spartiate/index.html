Spartiate

A Spartiate in full panoply

A Spartiate (Greek: Σπαρτιάτης, Spartiátēs, plural Σπαρτιᾶται, "Spartiatai") was a citizen of the ancient Greek city-state of Sparta. Spartiates — who styled themselves homoioi (ὅμοιοι), "those who are alike" or "peers" — formed the ruling class of Spartan society and its professional military caste. Full Spartiate status required Spartan parentage, completion of the rigorous agoge upbringing, election to a common mess (syssition), and continued payment of mess contributions from the produce of one's allotted land (kleros), which was worked by helots.

Life and status

Spartiates were forbidden from trade and manual labour and lived as full-time soldiers. From age seven they were raised in the communal agoge; at twenty they joined their syssitia and could marry, though they lived in barracks until thirty. Only then did a Spartiate become a full citizen entitled to vote in the assembly (apella) and to hold office. Failure to keep up mess dues cost a man his citizenship, demoting him to the ranks of the "inferiors" (hypomeiones). Spartiate women, by Greek standards, enjoyed unusual education, physical training, and property rights, since they managed estates while men soldiered.

Decline in numbers

Spartiate numbers dwindled from perhaps 8,000–9,000 adult males around 480 BC to fewer than 1,000 by the time of Aristotle, who blamed the concentration of land — much of it inherited by women — and the resulting inability of poorer families to pay mess contributions. Losses at Leuctra (371 BC), where 400 Spartiates died, could not be replaced. Reforming kings Agis IV and Cleomenes III in the third century BC attempted to restore the citizen body by cancelling debts and redistributing land, but the reforms were reversed after Cleomenes' defeat at Sellasia (222 BC).

Distinctions

Spartiates must be distinguished from the perioikoi, free but non-citizen inhabitants of surrounding Lacedaemonian towns who supplied troops, craftsmen, and traders; from the helots, the enslaved agricultural population; and from the Spartoi of Thebes, mythical warriors with no connection to Sparta. Within the Spartiate class, an elite of 300 hippeis ("knights") formed the kings' bodyguard.
