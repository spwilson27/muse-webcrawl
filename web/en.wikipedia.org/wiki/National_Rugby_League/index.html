The National Rugby League (NRL) is the top-level professional rugby league competition in Australasia. Run by the Australian Rugby League Commission, it features 17 clubs — 16 based in Australia and one in New Zealand — and is regarded as the world's premier club rugby league competition. Its season runs from March to October, culminating in the NRL Grand Final.

History

Rugby league began in Australia in 1908 with the New South Wales Rugby League premiership, founded in a dispute over player compensation. The competition expanded nationally in the 1980s and 1990s, but the "Super League war" of 1995–97 — a battle between the ARL and Rupert Murdoch's rival Super League over pay-television rights — split the sport. The NRL was formed as a compromise for the 1998 season as a partnership between the ARL and News Limited, with the ARL Commission assuming full control in 2012.

Clubs and competition

Foundation clubs include the Sydney Roosters and South Sydney Rabbitohs; other major clubs include the Melbourne Storm, Brisbane Broncos, Penrith Panthers, and the New Zealand Warriors. Teams play a round-robin season followed by a finals series. The premiership trophy is the Provan-Summons Trophy, named for Norm Provan and Arthur Summons, depicted in the famous "Gladiators" photograph. The league also runs the NRL Women's Premiership (NRLW), launched in 2018. Rugby league's most watched event overall, however, is the interstate State of Origin series between New South Wales and Queensland, contested by NRL players.

Audience

The NRL draws some of the largest television audiences in Australian sport, with the Grand Final and State of Origin regularly among the year's most-watched broadcasts, and it has expanded into the United States with season-opening matches in Las Vegas beginning in 2024.
