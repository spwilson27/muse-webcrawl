Gastropub

A gastropub (sometimes gastro pub) is a pub that serves high-quality food of restaurant standard alongside its drinks, combining the relaxed atmosphere of a traditional public house with ambitious cooking. The term is a portmanteau of gastronomy and public house.

History

The concept is usually dated to 1991, when David Eyre and Mike Belben took over The Eagle in Clerkenwell, London, and began serving Mediterranean-influenced dishes in a working pub setting. The idea spread rapidly through Britain in the 1990s and 2000s as pubs, squeezed by changing drinking habits and supermarket alcohol prices, turned to food-led trade. The movement reached its symbolic peak when The Hand & Flowers in Marlow, under chef Tom Kerridge, became the first pub to hold two Michelin stars.

Characteristics

Gastropubs typically retain a bar serving cask ales, craft beers and wines, with a dining room or restaurant area offering seasonal menus built on British and European classics — roasts, pies, fish dishes and puddings — often using locally sourced produce. Many occupy refurbished village inns and have contributed to the revival of rural pubs, though critics sometimes lament the loss of the traditional "wet-led" local and the gentrification of pub culture.

International spread

The term and format were exported to the United States, Australia and elsewhere from the 2000s, where "gastropub" came to denote an informal bar-restaurant with an emphasis on craft beer and elevated comfort food such as gourmet burgers.
