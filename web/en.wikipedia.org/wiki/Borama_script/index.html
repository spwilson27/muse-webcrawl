The Borama script, also called the Gadabuursi script, is a writing system for the Somali language. It was invented around 1933 by Sheikh Abdurahman Sheikh Nuur, a religious teacher and judge (qadi) of the Gadabuursi clan in the Borama district of present-day Somaliland, who devised it for teaching the Quran and literacy in his locality.

Description

The script is an alphabet of signs derived largely from Arabic letterforms, adapted to represent Somali consonants and vowels. It was used in Borama and surrounding areas for religious instruction, correspondence, and record-keeping, but it never displaced the older Osmanya script or the Arabic script beyond its home region, and it saw far more limited use than either.

Context

In the early 20th century several scripts competed to write Somali, including the indigenous Osmanya (invented c. 1920 by Osman Yusuf Kenadid) and Kaddare scripts, modified Arabic orthographies (Wadaad's writing), and Latin-based proposals. When Somalia adopted an official orthography in 1972, the government of Siad Barre chose the Latin alphabet (based on the Shire Jama Ahmed proposal); the Borama script thereafter fell out of active use and is now of mainly historical and scholarly interest.
