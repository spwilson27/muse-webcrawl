The September 2022 United Kingdom mini-budget, officially known as The Growth Plan, was a statement presented to the House of Commons by Chancellor of the Exchequer Kwasi Kwarteng on 23 September 2022, under the government of Prime Minister Liz Truss. It was not accompanied by the usual independent forecast from the Office for Budget Responsibility (OBR), and was termed a "mini-budget" because it was not a formal budget statement.

The statement announced a package of tax cuts and spending measures worth some £45 billion, including the abolition of the 45% additional rate of income tax, a reversal of the planned rise in corporation tax, a cut in stamp duty, and a permanent reduction in the basic rate of income tax to 19% from April 2023. The package was to be funded largely by increased government borrowing.

Market reaction

The announcement triggered an immediate and severe reaction in financial markets. The pound sterling fell to a record low against the US dollar, reaching close to $1.03 on 26 September, while yields on UK government bonds (gilts) rose sharply. The turmoil threatened pension funds using liability-driven investment (LDI) strategies, prompting the Bank of England to intervene on 28 September with a temporary programme of gilt purchases to restore market functioning.

Aftermath

On 3 October 2022 the government abandoned the plan to abolish the 45% rate, and on 14 October Kwarteng was dismissed as Chancellor and replaced by Jeremy Hunt. Hunt reversed nearly all of the remaining measures on 17 October. Truss resigned as Prime Minister on 20 October, after 49 days in office, making her the shortest-serving prime minister in British history. The episode became a byword for policy-driven market instability.
