The Original Dixieland Jass Band, the first band to record jazz commercially.

The Original Dixieland Jass Band (ODJB) was a Dixieland jazz band formed in New Orleans in 1916. The group made the first jazz phonograph records in early 1917, and their "Livery Stable Blues" became the first jazz record ever issued. The group composed and recorded many jazz standards, including "Tiger Rag", which have been covered by many others. The band, consisting of five white musicians from New Orleans, played in Chicago and New York City, where their Victor records helped launch the jazz craze of the 1910s and 1920s.

History

The band's classic lineup comprised cornetist Nick LaRocca, trombonist Eddie Edwards, clarinetist Larry Shields, pianist Henry Ragas, and drummer Tony Sbarbaro (also spelled Tony Spargo). After early success at Schiller's Cafe in Chicago, the band moved to New York in January 1917 to play at Reisenweber's restaurant. Columbia Records recorded them first but delayed release; Victor Talking Machine Company then recorded "Livery Stable Blues" and "Dixie Jass Band One-Step" on 26 February 1917, and the record, issued in March, was a runaway hit, selling over a million copies by some estimates.

When the records proved successful, the spelling of "Jass" in the band's name was changed to "Jazz," and the group billed itself as "The Creators of Jazz" — a claim championed by LaRocca for the rest of his life and disputed ever since, since jazz had been created overwhelmingly by African-American musicians in New Orleans who had fewer opportunities to record. The band toured England in 1919–20, popularising jazz in Europe, and made further records before personnel changes and shifting tastes led to its breakup in 1925. LaRocca later re-formed a band under the name in the 1930s.

Legacy

Whatever the disputes over origins, the ODJB's recordings are landmarks: they fixed the model of the small jazz combo, introduced standards such as "Tiger Rag", "Margie", "At the Jazz Band Ball", and "Fidgety Feet," and made jazz a national and international phenomenon. Their "Tiger Rag" was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame, and "Darktown Strutters' Ball," recorded by the band in 1917, entered the repertoire of countless later groups.
