The Fellowship of the Ring is the first of three volumes of the epic novel The Lord of the Rings by the English author J. R. R. Tolkien. It was published on 29 July 1954 in the United Kingdom. The volume consists of a foreword, a prologue, and two books, Book I and Book II.

Plot summary

In the Shire, the hobbit Frodo Baggins inherits from his uncle Bilbo a magic ring that the wizard Gandalf identifies as the One Ring forged by the Dark Lord Sauron. Frodo leaves the Shire with Sam, Merry, and Pippin, pursued by the Nazgûl; with the help of the ranger Aragorn they reach Rivendell, where Frodo recovers from a wound. At the Council of Elrond it is decided the Ring must be destroyed in Mount Doom in Mordor, and a Fellowship of nine is formed: Frodo, Sam, Merry, Pippin, Gandalf, Aragorn, Boromir of Gondor, Legolas the Elf, and Gimli the Dwarf. Passing through the Mines of Moria, they lose Gandalf to a Balrog. In Lothlórien, Galadriel gives them gifts. At Amon Hen, Boromir tries to seize the Ring; Frodo decides to go on alone, but Sam insists on accompanying him, and the Fellowship is broken.

Composition

Tolkien began the work in 1937 as a sequel to The Hobbit. The title refers to the company of nine companions. The volume introduces much of the legendarium's poetry and song, including "The Road Goes Ever On" and the lament for Gandalf.
