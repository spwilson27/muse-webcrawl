Levi Stubbs (born Levi Stubbles; June 6, 1936 – October 17, 2008) was an American baritone singer, best known as the lead vocalist of the R&B group the Four Tops, who released a variety of Motown hit records during the 1960s and 1970s. He has been ranked by Rolling Stone magazine among the greatest singers of all time, praised for the emotional intensity he brought to the group's Holland–Dozier–Holland productions.

Career

Stubbs formed a singing group with friends Abdul "Duke" Fakir, Renaldo "Obie" Benson and Lawrence Payton while at Pershing High School in Detroit in 1954. After years of touring and near-misses, the Four Tops signed with Berry Gordy's Motown label in 1963 — unusually, with the original line-up intact, which would remain unchanged for more than four decades until Payton's death in 1997.

Stubbs's urgent, pleading baritone defined the group's classics, including "Baby I Need Your Loving" (1964), "I Can't Help Myself (Sugar Pie Honey Bunch)" (1965), "Reach Out I'll Be There" (1966) — a US and UK number one — "Standing in the Shadows of Love", and "Bernadette" (1967). After leaving Motown in 1972 the group continued to score hits such as "Ain't No Woman (Like the One I've Got)".

Acting and later life

Stubbs provided the voice of Audrey II, the carnivorous plant, in the 1986 film Little Shop of Horrors, and voiced Mother Brain in the animated series Captain N: The Game Master. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the Four Tops in 1990. Ill health forced him to stop touring in 2000, and he died at home in Detroit in 2008.
