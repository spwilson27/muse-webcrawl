OO gauge

Scale4 mm to the foot (1:76)
Track gauge16.5 mm
Prototype gaugeStandard gauge, 4 ft 8½ in (1,435 mm)
RegionUnited Kingdom

OO gauge (also written 00 gauge) is the most popular model railway scale in the United Kingdom. Models are built to a scale of 4 mm to the foot (1:76) but run on track with a gauge of 16.5 mm — the same track as the international HO scale (1:87). Because of this compromise, OO models are oversized relative to their track: a true 4 mm-scale representation of standard gauge would require 18.83 mm track gauge.

Origins

The anomaly arose in the 1920s and 1930s, when British manufacturers such as Hornby adopted the HO track gauge but found that the small British prototypes, constrained by the tight British loading gauge, left too little room for the electric motors of the period at 3.5 mm scale. They therefore enlarged the bodies to 4 mm scale while keeping 16.5 mm track. The compromise became entrenched and remains the British standard, served by manufacturers including Hornby, Bachmann Branchline, Dapol, Heljan and Accurascale.

Finescale alternatives

Modellers seeking correct gauge use EM gauge (18.2 mm) or P4/Scalefour (18.83 mm), both at the same 4 mm scale but with accurately gauged track and finer standards. Despite these alternatives, OO remains dominant commercially because of the enormous range of ready-to-run models available.
