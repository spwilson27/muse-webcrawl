The 1880 United States presidential election was the 24th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 2, 1880. Republican Representative James A. Garfield of Ohio defeated Democratic General Winfield Scott Hancock of Pennsylvania by the smallest popular-vote margin in American history — fewer than 2,000 votes separated them out of more than nine million cast — although the Electoral College margin was more comfortable, 214 to 155.

Background

Incumbent President Rutherford B. Hayes honored his pledge not to seek a second term. The Republican convention deadlocked between supporters of former President Ulysses S. Grant ("Stalwarts") and Senator James G. Blaine ("Half-Breeds"), eventually nominating Garfield as a compromise "dark horse" on the 36th ballot, with Chester A. Arthur as his running mate. The Democrats nominated Hancock, a Union general and hero of Gettysburg, with William Hayden English as his running mate.

Campaign

The campaign centered on the protective tariff, civil service reform, Chinese immigration, and memories of the Civil War. A forged letter purporting to show Garfield supporting Chinese immigration (the "Morey letter") caused a late scandal but was exposed as a forgery. Turnout was exceptionally high, at about 79 percent of eligible voters.

Aftermath

Garfield was inaugurated on March 4, 1881, but was shot by Charles J. Guiteau, a disgruntled office-seeker, on July 2, 1881, and died on September 19. Chester A. Arthur succeeded him. The assassination spurred passage of the Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act in 1883.
