Corps de ballet

[Image placeholder: corps de ballet in performance]

In ballet, the corps de ballet (from French, meaning "body of the ballet") is the group of dancers who dance together as an ensemble, as opposed to the soloists and principal dancers. The corps de ballet performs synchronised, coordinated movements that form the visual backdrop of a production — the swans in Swan Lake, the shades in La Bayadère, and the wilis in Giselle are among the most famous corps roles.

Members of the corps de ballet are typically the most junior rank in a ballet company's hierarchy, below soloists (or demisoloists) and principals. Despite this, corps work demands great precision: lines must be exactly matched in timing, spacing and port de bras, and a corps performance is often judged by its uniformity. Many dancers spend their entire careers in the corps, while others use it as the first professional step after graduating from a company school.

In the hierarchy of the Paris Opera Ballet, the corps is subdivided into quadrilles, coryphées and sujets before the ranks of premier danseur and étoile. Other major companies, such as The Royal Ballet and American Ballet Theatre, use structures of artists/corps, soloists and principals.
