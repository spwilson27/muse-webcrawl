Synthetic diamond

A synthetic diamond (also called a laboratory-grown, laboratory-created, or cultured diamond) is a diamond produced by a controlled technological process, as opposed to a natural diamond formed by geological processes. Synthetic diamonds are composed of carbon in the same crystal structure as natural diamonds and share essentially identical physical and chemical properties.

Production methods

The two dominant methods are high-pressure high-temperature (HPHT), which mimics the conditions of natural diamond formation using large presses, and chemical vapour deposition (CVD), in which diamond grows atom by atom from a hydrocarbon gas on a seed crystal. The Swedish company ASEA and General Electric achieved reproducible synthesis in 1954–55.

Uses

Most synthetic diamond production is industrial: abrasives, cutting tools, heat sinks, and high-pressure anvils. Since the 2010s, gem-quality lab-grown diamonds have taken a growing share of the jewellery market at prices well below those of mined stones; detection and certification distinguish them for trade purposes.
