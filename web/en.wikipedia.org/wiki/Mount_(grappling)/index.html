Mount (grappling)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The full mount, one of the most dominant positions in grappling.

The mount, or full mount, is a dominant grappling position in which one combatant sits astride the torso of the other, facing the opponent's head, with knees pinning the ground on either side. It is a staple position in Brazilian jiu-jitsu, judo (where it is called tate-shiho-gatame), and mixed martial arts.

Advantages

From the mount, the top grappler's full body weight presses on the opponent while the bottom player's hips are pinned, making the position highly stable and exhausting to escape. The top player can strike with punches and elbows (in MMA, the basis of "ground and pound"), or attack with submissions such as the armbar, the americana, and the cross-collar choke. In Brazilian jiu-jitsu competition, achieving the mount scores four points, tied with back control as the most valuable position.

Variations and escapes

Variations include the high mount (sitting up on the chest), low mount, technical mount (S-mount), and knee-on-belly as a transitional position. Common escapes are the elbow-escape (shrimping to recover guard), the bridge-and-roll (upa), and the trap-and-roll. Defence focuses on keeping the elbows tight and frames against the opponent's hips before the position settles.
