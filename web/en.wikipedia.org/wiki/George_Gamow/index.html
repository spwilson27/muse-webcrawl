Gamow in the 1950s.

George Gamow (born Georgiy Antonovich Gamov; 4 March 1904 – 19 August 1968) was a Soviet and American physicist, cosmologist and science populariser. He provided the first quantum-mechanical explanation of alpha decay (1928, via tunnelling), co-authored the Alpher–Bethe–Gamow (αβγ) paper on Big Bang nucleosynthesis (1948), and championed the hot Big Bang model — predicting (with Alpher and Herman) the residual heat radiation, the Cosmic microwave background, at about 5 K.

Gamow defected from the Soviet Union in 1933 (after failed attempts, including kayaking toward Turkey and Norway), settled at George Washington University and later Colorado, and wrote the beloved Mr Tompkins books and One Two Three... Infinity, which formed generations of physicists. His humour was institutional: he added Hans Bethe's name to the αβγ paper for the pun, against Bethe's mild objection; Bethe sportingly allowed it.

Cosmology

With Ralph Alpher and Robert Herman, Gamow's group computed how the hot early universe cooked hydrogen into helium (Big Bang nucleosynthesis) and predicted the microwave background's existence and temperature — the prediction forgotten until Dicke, Peebles, Roll and Wilkinson re-derived it in 1965 as Penzias and Wilson stumbled on the signal itself. The Gamow programme — the universe as a physics problem in its first minutes — is modern cosmology's founding charter.
