Enclave and exclave

An enclave is a territory (or part of one) that is entirely surrounded by the territory of one other country. An exclave is a portion of a state geographically separated from the main territory and surrounded by other states. Many exclaves are also enclaves, but not all: an exclave surrounded by several states, or bordering the sea, is not an enclave.

True enclaves

Only three sovereign states are entirely enclaved by a single other country: Lesotho (surrounded by South Africa), San Marino, and Vatican City (both surrounded by Italy). Exclaves at the international level include Nakhchivan (Azerbaijan, separated by Armenia), Kaliningrad Oblast (Russia, on the Baltic), and Ceuta and Melilla (Spain, on the Moroccan coast).

Subnational and complex cases

Within countries, enclaves and exclaves arise from historical boundaries: the Belgian municipality of Baarle-Hertog consists of fragments inside the Netherlands, some containing Dutch counter-enclaves. The India–Bangladesh enclaves along the Cooch Behar border once formed one of the world's most complex enclave systems, with third-order enclaves; most were exchanged under a 2015 land boundary agreement. Pene-enclaves and practical enclaves are territories not strictly surrounded but accessible only through another country, such as Spain's Llívia in France (a true enclave) or parts of valleys cut off by terrain.
