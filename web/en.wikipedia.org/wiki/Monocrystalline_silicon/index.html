Monocrystalline silicon

MaterialSilicon (single crystal)
Crystal structureDiamond cubic
Typical efficiency20–26% (solar cells)
ProductionCzochralski process

Monocrystalline silicon, also called single-crystal silicon or abbreviated mono c-Si, is the base material for silicon-based discrete components and integrated circuits used in virtually all modern electronic equipment. It is also used as a light-absorbing material in the production of solar cells.

It consists of silicon in which the crystal lattice of the entire solid is continuous, unbroken to its edges, and free of any grain boundaries. Mono-Si can be prepared as an intrinsic semiconductor that consists only of exceedingly pure silicon, or it can be doped with other elements such as boron or phosphorus to make it p-type or n-type silicon suitable for electronic devices.

Production

Most monocrystalline silicon is grown by the Czochralski process, in which a seed crystal is dipped into molten silicon and slowly withdrawn while rotating, pulling a large cylindrical single-crystal ingot. Ingots for the electronics industry can exceed 300 mm in diameter. The ingots are then sliced into thin wafers with diamond saws.

An alternative method is float-zone crystal growth, which produces material of even higher purity, used mainly for power semiconductors and detectors.

Applications

In microelectronics, monocrystalline silicon wafers are the substrate on which transistors and other devices are fabricated by photolithography, doping, and etching. In photovoltaics, mono-Si cells are cut from cylindrical ingots; because the ingot is round, the cells often have cropped corners. Monocrystalline solar panels typically achieve higher efficiencies than polycrystalline silicon panels, though at higher cost. The PERC (passivated emitter and rear cell) architecture and half-cut cell designs are widely used to raise module efficiency.

Appearance

Monocrystalline solar cells usually have a uniform dark colour, often black or deep blue, distinguishing them visually from the speckled blue appearance of polycrystalline cells.
