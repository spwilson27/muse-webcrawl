Vote Leave was a campaigning organisation that supported a Leave vote in the 2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum. On 13 April 2016 it was designated by the Electoral Commission as the official lead campaign for Leave, in preference to the rival Leave.EU, entitling it to a public grant, a free mailshot, and a higher spending limit.

Campaign

Vote Leave was a cross-party campaign involving Conservatives such as Michael Gove and Boris Johnson and Labour MP Gisela Stuart as chair; its campaign director was Dominic Cummings and its chief executive Matthew Elliott. Its slogan was "Take back control," and its central message argued that the UK sent £350 million per week to the EU that could instead fund the National Health Service — a figure emblazoned on its campaign bus and disputed by the UK Statistics Authority and fact-checkers, which noted it ignored the UK's rebate and EU spending in Britain. The campaign also emphasized immigration control, sovereignty, and warnings against further EU integration, including Turkish accession.

Result and aftermath

In the referendum of 23 June 2016, Leave won with 51.9% of the vote, leading to David Cameron's resignation and the start of the Brexit process. In 2018 the Electoral Commission found Vote Leave guilty of breaking electoral spending law by exceeding its limit through coordinated spending with the youth group BeLeave, fining it £61,000 and referring matters to the police; the findings were upheld after legal challenges, though the Metropolitan Police later declined prosecutions. Vote Leave personnel went on to prominent roles in the Johnson government, including Cummings as chief adviser.
