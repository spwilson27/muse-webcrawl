Yeghegnadzor (Armenian: Եղեգնաձոր, romanized: Eġegnajōr) is a town in southern Armenia and the administrative capital of Vayots Dzor Province. It lies on the right bank of the Arpa River along the highway linking Yerevan with Goris and the Iranian border, about 123 km south of the capital. The name means "valley of canes" or "gorge of reeds" in Armenian, referring to the reeds once abundant along the river.

History

The settlement is first mentioned in the 5th century under the name Pondzatagh and later known as Keshishkend. In the Middle Ages the area belonged to the Siunia (Syunik) princes; nearby sites include the Selim caravanserai (built 1332) on the mountain pass to Lake Sevan, the fortress of Smbataberd, and the monasteries of Tsakhats Kar and, a short distance east, the celebrated Noravank complex in its red-rock gorge. The town received its present name in 1935 and town status in Soviet times, developing light industry including wine production and stone processing.

Geography and economy

Set at nearly 1,200 m elevation amid the arid, rocky hills of the Arpa valley, Yeghegnadzor is a centre for the surrounding wine-growing region — the village of Areni nearby is famous for its Areni noir grape and for the 6,100-year-old winery discovered in the Areni-1 cave complex. Fruit growing, dairy processing, and tourism serving Noravank and the Jermuk spa route are the mainstays of the local economy. The town has a regional museum and a branch campus of an Armenian university.
