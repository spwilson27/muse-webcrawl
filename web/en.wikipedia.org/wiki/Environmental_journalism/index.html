Environmental journalism

Environmental journalism is the collection, verification, production, distribution and exhibition of information regarding current events, trends, issues and people that are associated with the non-human world. It is a genre of journalism closely related to science journalism, and it requires the journalist to understand scientific language and practice, the history of environmental events, and the ability to assess environmental policy decisions and environmental organizations' work accurately.

Scope and history

Environmental reporting has roots in nature writing and conservation advocacy of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, but it developed as a distinct news beat after the environmental movement of the 1960s and 1970s. Rachel Carson's Silent Spring (1962), serialized in The New Yorker, is frequently cited as a catalyst for public environmental reporting. The first Earth Day in 1970, the creation of environmental protection agencies, and disasters such as Love Canal, Bhopal, Chernobyl and the Exxon Valdez oil spill expanded dedicated environment desks at newspapers and broadcasters.

The field today encompasses climate change, biodiversity loss, pollution, resource extraction, environmental justice, and the politics of environmental regulation. Professional organizations such as the Society of Environmental Journalists (founded 1990 in the United States) support training, networking and press freedom for journalists on the beat.

Debates

Environmental journalism sits at the center of debates about objectivity and advocacy: critics of mainstream coverage have argued that "balance" norms produced false equivalence on settled questions such as anthropogenic climate change, while others argue that environment reporters should avoid taking positions. Scholars classify coverage along spectrums from anthropocentric to biocentric framing, and study how economic interests, source access and the complexity of environmental science shape reporting. Journalists covering environmental conflicts in many countries face harassment and violence, particularly when reporting on illegal logging, mining and land disputes.
