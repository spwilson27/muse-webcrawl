Vasily Filippovich Margelov (Russian: Василий Филиппович Маргелов; 27 December 1908 – 4 March 1990) was a Soviet general who commanded the Soviet Airborne Forces (VDV) for more than two decades and is regarded as the principal architect of the modern Soviet and Russian airborne troops. Russian paratroopers are often called "Uncle Vasya's troops" in his honour, and the VDV's motto is associated with his leadership.

Early life and Second World War

Born in Yekaterinoslav (now Dnipro) into a working-class family of Belarusian origin, Margelov joined the Red Army in 1928 and was commissioned after graduating from the United Belarusian Military School in Minsk. During the Second World War he commanded a guards rifle regiment and later a division, fighting in the defence of Leningrad, the Battle of Stalingrad, the liberation of Ukraine and Romania, and operations in Austria and Czechoslovakia. He received the title Hero of the Soviet Union in 1944 for his role in the crossing of the Dnieper and the liberation of Kherson.

Commander of the Airborne Forces

Margelov was appointed commander of the VDV in 1954, holding the post until 1959 and again from 1961 until 1979. Under his command the airborne forces were transformed into a strategic elite arm equipped with dedicated air-droppable armoured vehicles such as the BMD-1 airborne infantry fighting vehicle, heavy-lift transport aviation, and new parachute systems that allowed crews to descend inside their vehicles. He insisted that paratroopers be able to fight immediately upon landing, anywhere and in any conditions.

Legacy

Margelov's name is borne by the Ryazan Guards Higher Airborne Command School, the principal training institution of the Russian airborne forces. Monuments to him stand in many Russian cities, and his image — in the blue beret and striped telnyashka shirt he made emblematic of the VDV — remains a symbol of the airborne troops.
