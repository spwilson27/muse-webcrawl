Poughkeepsie

The Mid-Hudson Bridge and the Walkway over the Hudson at Poughkeepsie.

Poughkeepsie (pronounced pə-KIP-see) refers to both a city and a surrounding town in Dutchess County, New York, United States, on the east bank of the Hudson River, roughly midway between New York City and Albany. The city of Poughkeepsie is the county seat of Dutchess County and had a population of about 31,500 at the 2020 census; the town, which surrounds the city, had about 45,000 residents. The name derives from a Native American (Wappinger) word, U-puku-ipi-sing, meaning "the reed-covered lodge by the little water place".

History

The area was settled by the Dutch in the late seventeenth century and by English settlers after 1683; the settlement grew around a mill and ferry landing. Poughkeepsie served briefly as New York's second state capital, and in 1788 the state's ratifying convention met there and voted to adopt the United States Constitution. In the nineteenth century the city was a manufacturing and whaling-outfitting centre and a base of the brewer Matthew Vassar, founder of Vassar College (opened 1861). IBM established a major plant in the town in the 1940s, long the area's largest employer, producing mainframe computers.

Geography and landmarks

The city and town lie in the Hudson Valley, served by the Poughkeepsie station of Metro-North Railroad's Hudson Line and Amtrak. Two bridges cross the Hudson at Poughkeepsie: the Mid-Hudson Bridge (1930), carrying US 44/NY 55, and the former Poughkeepsie Railroad Bridge, reopened in 2009 as the Walkway over the Hudson, a pedestrian park and the world's longest pedestrian footbridge at 1.28 miles. Other institutions include Marist College and the Culinary Institute of America in nearby Hyde Park.
