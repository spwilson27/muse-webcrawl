Thanksgiving (French: Action de grâce), or Thanksgiving Day (Jour de l'Action de grâce), is an annual Canadian holiday held on the second Monday in October. Apart from the churches, a thanksgiving observance in Canada has historically taken the form of a celebration of the harvest and other blessings made during the year, in gratitude for a safe voyage that ended in the landing of explorers and for general good fortune.

History

The origins of Thanksgiving in Canada are sometimes traced to the explorers of the 16th century. In 1578, the English explorer Martin Frobisher held a ceremony in present-day Nunavut to give thanks for surviving the long voyage from England. French settlers under Samuel de Champlain held feasts of thanksgiving in the early 17th century, forming the Order of Good Cheer in 1606.

The first Thanksgiving celebration after Canadian Confederation was held on April 15, 1872, to celebrate the recovery of the Prince of Wales from a serious illness. Thanksgiving became an annual holiday on November 6, 1879, and in 1957 Parliament fixed the date as the second Monday in October.

Celebration

Thanksgiving in Canada is typically celebrated with family gatherings and meals featuring roast turkey, mashed potatoes, gravy, stuffing, cranberry sauce and pumpkin pie. The weekend also features the Canadian Football League's Thanksgiving Day Classic and community parades, including the Kitchener–Waterloo Oktoberfest Thanksgiving Day Parade. Because Canada's harvest season ends earlier than that of the United States, the Canadian holiday falls about six weeks before American Thanksgiving.
