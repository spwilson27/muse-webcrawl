TypeCoal combustion residual
CompositionSilicon dioxide, aluminium oxide, iron oxide, calcium oxide
ClassesClass F and Class C (ASTM C618)
Main useSupplementary cementitious material in concrete

Fly ash, also known as pulverised fuel ash in the United Kingdom, is a coal combustion product composed of the fine particulates of burned fuel that are carried out of coal-fired boilers together with the flue gases. It is collected by electrostatic precipitators or bag filters before the gases reach the chimneys. Fly ash is generally spherical, glassy, and pozzolanic.

Composition and classification

Fly ash consists mainly of silicon dioxide (SiO2), aluminium oxide (Al2O3), iron oxide (Fe2O3), and calcium oxide (CaO). Under ASTM C618, Class F fly ash, from anthracite and bituminous coals, is low in calcium and purely pozzolanic, whereas Class C fly ash, from lignite and sub-bituminous coals, contains more calcium and is also self-cementing.

Uses

The principal use of fly ash is as a supplementary cementitious material replacing a portion of Portland cement in concrete. In the presence of water and calcium hydroxide, its pozzolanic reaction forms additional cementitious compounds, improving the workability, long-term strength, and durability of concrete while reducing cost and embodied carbon. Fly ash is also used in road base, flowable fill, bricks, and geopolymer synthesis.

Environmental concerns

Fly ash contains trace concentrations of heavy metals and other contaminants, and ash stored in surface impoundments can pollute groundwater or cause disasters if impoundments fail, as in the 2008 Kingston Fossil Plant spill. In the United States it is regulated as a coal combustion residual under rules issued by the Environmental Protection Agency. Recycling into concrete is widely considered a beneficial use that keeps ash out of disposal sites.
