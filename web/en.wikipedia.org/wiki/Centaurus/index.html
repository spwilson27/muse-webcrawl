Centaurus is a bright constellation in the southern sky, one of the 48 constellations listed by the second-century astronomer Ptolemy and among the 88 modern constellations. It depicts a centaur and contains Alpha Centauri, the nearest star system to the Sun, and Omega Centauri, the largest globular cluster in the Milky Way. The constellation spans about 1,060 square degrees, making it the ninth largest.

Stars

Alpha Centauri is a triple system: the close binary Alpha Centauri A and B, and the red dwarf Proxima Centauri, at about 4.25 light-years the closest known star to the Sun and host of the Earth-sized exoplanet Proxima Centauri b in its habitable zone. Beta Centauri (Hadar) is a blue giant that, with Alpha, forms the "Pointers" used to locate the Southern Cross. Other named stars include Menkent (Theta Centauri) and Toliman (Alpha Centauri B's name, formally applied to the star).

Deep-sky objects

Omega Centauri, visible to the naked eye, contains several million stars and may be the stripped core of a dwarf galaxy. The active galaxy Centaurus A (NGC 5128), about 12 million light-years away, is one of the brightest radio sources in the sky and a favourite amateur target. The constellation also contains the Blue Planetary Nebula (NGC 3918) and the open cluster NGC 3766, the "Pearl Cluster".

Mythology

The constellation represents a centaur — in Greek tradition often identified with Chiron, the wise healer, though Chiron is also associated with Sagittarius. The centaur is depicted holding a lance directed at the neighbouring constellation Lupus, the wolf.
