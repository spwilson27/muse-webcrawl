PiHKAL: A Chemical Love Story is a book by Alexander Shulgin and Ann Shulgin, published in 1991. The title is an acronym that stands for Phenethylamines I Have Known and Loved. The book is divided into two parts. The first is a fictionalised autobiographical narrative of the authors (under the names "Shura" and "Alice"), describing their relationship and experiences with psychedelic substances. The second part systematically details 179 phenethylamine compounds, most of which Alexander Shulgin synthesised and tested on himself and associates.

For each compound, the second part gives synthesis procedures, dosage, duration, and qualitative ratings of effects, recorded in trip reports using Shulgin's rating scale. The best-known compound documented in the book is the empathogen MDMA, which Shulgin resynthesised in 1976 and introduced to psychotherapists; the book also covers mescaline analogues in the 2C family and many other substituted phenethylamines.

Publication and reception

PiHKAL was published by Transform Press, a small Berkeley publisher founded for the project. It became a reference work in underground chemistry and psychedelic culture and was followed in 1997 by TiHKAL (Tryptamines I Have Known and Loved). The second part of PiHKAL has been made freely available online by the authors, and the Erowid vaults host much of its content.

The book also attracted regulatory attention: in 1994, two years after the publication of PiHKAL, the U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration raided Shulgin's laboratory and revoked his DEA licence to work with scheduled compounds, citing the book as a source of synthesis procedures for controlled substances.

Contents of Part Two

The 179 entries progress from mescaline through its analogues, the 2C-x series, the DOx compounds, and various other substituted phenethylamines, each with physical data, synthesis, and qualitative commentary. An index cross-references the compounds with the narrative chapters of part one.
