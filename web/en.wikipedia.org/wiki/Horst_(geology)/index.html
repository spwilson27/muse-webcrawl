Horst (geology)

Horst
FieldStructural geology
Formed byNormal faulting under extension

ExamplesVosges Mountains, Harz, Black Forest

In physical geography and geology, a horst is a raised block of the Earth's crust that has lifted, or has remained stationary, while the land on either side has subsided. Horsts form under tensional (extensional) stress and are bounded by normal faults; the complementary sunken block is called a graben.

Formation

As the crust is stretched, blocks bounded by normal faults move vertically relative to one another. A block left relatively high—either raised or simply less subsided than its neighbors—forms a horst, often expressed in the landscape as a range of hills or a mountain block with steep fault scarps. Horsts may range from a few kilometres to over 100 km wide.

Examples

The Vosges Mountains in France and the Black Forest in Germany are horsts flanking the Rhine Graben. The Harz mountains of Germany are a classic uplifted fault block, and horst-and-graben terrain characterizes much of the Basin and Range Province in the western United States.
