Ipperwash Crisis

The crisis took place at Ipperwash Provincial Park on Lake Huron, Ontario.

The Ipperwash Crisis was a dispute over Indigenous land in Ontario, Canada, that culminated in the death of Dudley George, an unarmed Ojibwe protester, who was shot by an Ontario Provincial Police (OPP) officer on September 6, 1995.

Background

Members of the Stony Point First Nation had long claimed land that included Ipperwash Provincial Park, which contained a burial ground. During the Second World War the federal government appropriated the Stony Point reserve under the War Measures Act for a military base, Camp Ipperwash, promising to return it after the war; the return was delayed for decades.

Occupation and shooting

In 1993 protesters occupied Camp Ipperwash, and on September 4, 1995, a group occupied Ipperwash Provincial Park after it closed for the season. On the night of September 6, police moved against protesters outside the park; in the confrontation, Acting Sergeant Kenneth Deane shot Dudley George, who died of his wounds. Deane was later convicted of criminal negligence causing death.

Inquiry and aftermath

The Ipperwash Inquiry, led by Commissioner Sidney Linden and reporting in 2007, examined the events, criticised the conduct of the provincial government of Premier Mike Harris and the police, and made wide-ranging recommendations on policing of Indigenous protests and land-claim resolution. The park land was eventually transferred to the Chippewas of Kettle and Stony Point First Nation, and the former Camp Ipperwash lands were also returned under a settlement.
