Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub

Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub is a biome defined by the World Wildlife Fund. Named for the lands around the Mediterranean Sea, the biome is generally characterized by dry summers and rainy winters, although in some areas rainfall may be uniform. It occurs in five widely separated regions: the Mediterranean Basin, California (chaparral), central Chile (matorral), the Cape region of South Africa (fynbos), and southwestern and southern Australia (mallee).

Ecology

The biome's plant communities — evergreen sclerophyllous forests, oak woodlands, maquis and garrigue shrublands, and grasslands — are adapted to drought and frequent fire, and they support exceptionally high levels of plant diversity and endemism. Characteristic trees and shrubs include holm oak, cork oak, stone pine, olive, and carob around the Mediterranean, and analogous lineages elsewhere. Fire regimes, grazing, and land conversion are major ecological pressures.

Ecoregions

The WWF divides the biome into dozens of ecoregions, including the Iberian conifer forests, Mediterranean acacia-argania dry woodlands, California coastal sage and chaparral, Chilean matorral, and the Southwest Australia woodlands. Many are classified as critical or endangered owing to habitat loss.
