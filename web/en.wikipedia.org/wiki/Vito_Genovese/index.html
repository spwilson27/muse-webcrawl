Vito Genovese

Vito Genovese (1897–1969) was an Italian-born American mobster who rose to power within the American Mafia during Prohibition and later became boss of the crime family that bears his name, the Genovese crime family.

Career

Born in Risigliano, near Naples, Genovese emigrated to New York in 1913. He became a close associate of Lucky Luciano, served as underboss, fled to Italy in 1937 to avoid a murder charge, and returned after World War II. In 1957 he manoeuvred to take control of the Luciano family, ordering the shooting of boss Frank Costello and the murder of Albert Anastasia, and convened the ill-fated Apalachin meeting.

Downfall

Convicted of narcotics conspiracy in 1959 — in a case many historians believe was engineered by rivals — Genovese died in federal prison in Springfield, Missouri, in 1969. The family he built became regarded as the most powerful and disciplined of New York's Five Families.
