Terra preta

[Image: Terra preta: the dark, charcoal-rich anthropogenic soil of Amazonia]

Overview

Terra preta (Portuguese for "black soil" or "black earth") is a type of very dark, fertile anthropogenic soil found in the Amazon Basin, created by pre-Columbian Indigenous peoples from roughly 500 BC to AD 1500 by incorporating charcoal (biochar), bone, manure and ceramic sherds into the region's naturally poor, weathered soils.

Significance

Terra preta sites — often associated with large, long-settled villages — can hold many times the nutrients of surrounding oxisols and retain their fertility for centuries because the charcoal resists decomposition and microbial oxidation. Their existence helped overturn the old view that Amazonia could support only sparse populations (the "counterfeit paradise" debate), implying dense pre-1492 settlement now supported by lidar surveys of lost settlements. Modern "biochar" soil science and carbon sequestration proposals explicitly imitate terra preta.
