Acetone

IUPAC namePropanone
FormulaC3H6O
Molar mass58.08 g/mol
AppearanceColourless liquid
Boiling point56.1 °C

Acetone (systematically named propanone) is an organic compound with the formula (CH3)2CO. It is the simplest and smallest ketone, a colourless, highly volatile and flammable liquid with a characteristic pungent odour. Acetone is miscible with water and serves as an important organic solvent in its own right, in industry, home, and laboratory.

Production

About 7 million tonnes are produced annually worldwide, mainly by the cumene process, in which benzene is alkylated with propylene and the resulting cumene is oxidised to give acetone and phenol as co-products. Acetone is also produced in small quantities in the human body as a ketone body.

Uses

Roughly a third of acetone output is used as a solvent — famously as the active ingredient in nail polish remover and paint thinner. It is also a building block for producing methyl methacrylate and bisphenol A, and is the storage form shipped worldwide for acetylene gas.

Safety

Acetone has low acute toxicity but is highly flammable; its vapours can form explosive mixtures with air.
