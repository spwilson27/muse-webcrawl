Hoist (device)

An electric chain hoist

A hoist is a device used for lifting or lowering a load by means of a drum or lift-wheel around which rope or chain wraps. It may be manually operated, electrically or pneumatically driven, and may use chain, fibre rope or wire rope as its lifting medium. The load is attached to the hoist by means of a lifting hook. Hoists are widely used in construction, manufacturing, mining, warehousing and on ships.

Types

The most familiar form for general use is the chain hoist (also called a chain block or chain fall), in which the lifting medium is a metal chain pulled through a set of gears or pulleys to gain mechanical advantage. Wire rope hoists use a grooved drum and are common on overhead cranes for heavier industrial loads. Hoists may also be classified by power source: manual, electric, pneumatic (air) and hydraulic. A builder's hoist carries personnel and materials on construction sites, while a mine hoist (or winder) raises and lowers conveyances in a mine shaft.

Operation and safety

Every hoist has a rated capacity or working load limit that must not be exceeded. Modern hoists incorporate brakes that hold the load when power is removed, limit switches to prevent over-travel, and overload protection. Regular inspection of chain, rope, hooks and brakes is required by occupational safety regulations in most jurisdictions, since failure of a hoist under load can be catastrophic.

History

Simple hoists based on ropes and pulleys were known in antiquity and were used in building and in theatre machinery. The geared chain hoist was developed in the 19th century, and electric hoists became common in factories in the early 20th century, improving both capacity and control.
