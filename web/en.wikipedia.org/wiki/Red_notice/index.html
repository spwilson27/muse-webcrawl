Red notice

An Interpol Red Notice is a request to law enforcement worldwide to locate and provisionally arrest a person pending extradition, surrender, or similar legal action. It is issued by Interpol at the request of a member country or an international tribunal, based on a valid national arrest warrant. A Red Notice is not itself an international arrest warrant: each member country decides what legal weight to give it, though many treat it as a sufficient basis for provisional arrest.

System of notices

Red Notices are the best known of Interpol's colour-coded notices. Others include Blue Notices (to locate or identify a person of interest), Green Notices (warnings about persons who have committed offences and are likely to reoffend elsewhere), Yellow Notices (missing persons), Black Notices (unidentified bodies), Purple Notices (criminal methods and modus operandi), and Orange Notices (imminent threats, such as disguised weapons or parcel bombs). There is also an Interpol–United Nations Security Council Special Notice for persons subject to UN sanctions.

Numbers and process

Interpol publishes public extracts of some Red Notices on its website; the great majority are restricted to law enforcement. Tens of thousands of Red Notices are in circulation at any time, with roughly 10,000–13,000 new ones issued annually in recent years. Requests are reviewed by Interpol's General Secretariat for compliance with its constitution, which forbids interventions of a political, military, religious or racial character. Individuals may seek deletion of a notice through the Commission for the Control of Interpol's Files (CCF).

Criticism

Human-rights organisations and investigative journalists have documented cases in which authoritarian governments obtained Red Notices against dissidents, journalists and business rivals, prompting reforms to Interpol's review process. High-profile disputes over politically motivated requests continue to test the system.
