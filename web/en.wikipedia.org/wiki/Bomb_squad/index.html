Bomb squad

A bomb squad is a specialised unit of a police force, military organisation, or other public-safety agency trained and equipped to respond to incidents involving explosive devices, suspicious packages, and hazardous explosive materials. In the United States, public safety bomb squads are typically part of municipal or county police departments and sheriff's offices, and they operate under standards accredited by the Federal Bureau of Investigation.

Responsibilities

Bomb squads respond to reports of suspect packages and vehicles, recover and dispose of abandoned or illegal fireworks and military ordnance found by the public, support searches ahead of major public events and dignitary visits, and conduct post-blast investigations in cooperation with agencies such as the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF). Many squads also maintain explosive-detection canine teams and provide training to patrol officers and the public.

Training and accreditation

In the United States, bomb technicians attend the FBI's Hazardous Devices School at Redstone Arsenal, Alabama — the only facility of its kind for civilian technicians — followed by regular recertification and refresher courses. A typical accredited squad fields a minimum number of certified technicians and maintains prescribed equipment, including bomb suits, portable X-ray systems, remotely operated robots, and total containment vessels (TCVs) that allow a suspect device to be transported while containing the effects of an accidental detonation.

Equipment

Modern squads rely heavily on remote technology: tracked robots with manipulator arms and cameras allow a technician to inspect and disrupt a device from a safe distance, while disruptors use a water charge to break devices apart. Bomb suits of aramid fibre protect technicians during unavoidable manual approaches. Squads also employ explosive vapour detectors, hook-and-line kits, and specialised vehicles.
