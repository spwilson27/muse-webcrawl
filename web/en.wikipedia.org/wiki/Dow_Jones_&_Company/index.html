Dow Jones & Company, Inc. is an American publishing company owned by News Corp since 2007. It was founded in 1882 by reporters Charles Dow, Edward Jones and Charles Bergstresser, initially to deliver financial news bulletins to Wall Street firms.


Dow Jones publishes The Wall Street Journal.

Publications and services

The company publishes The Wall Street Journal (founded 1889), one of the largest newspapers in the United States, as well as Barron's, MarketWatch, Investor's Business Daily and the Factiva database, and operates Dow Jones Newswires. It also created the Dow Jones Industrial Average, first published in 1896 — although the index business was later transferred to S&P Dow Jones Indices, a separate joint venture.

Ownership

The Bancroft family controlled the company for over a century before accepting a $5 billion takeover bid from Rupert Murdoch's News Corporation in 2007. Following the 2013 split of News Corp, Dow Jones remained with the publishing side of the business.
