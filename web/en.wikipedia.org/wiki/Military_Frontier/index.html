Military Frontier

The Military Frontier (German: Militärgrenze; Croatian: Vojna krajina) was a borderland of the Habsburg monarchy and later the Austrian Empire and Austria-Hungary, established as a defensive cordon against the Ottoman Empire. At its greatest extent it stretched from the Adriatic coast through present-day Croatia, Serbia and Romania to the Carpathians.

History

The frontier originated in the sixteenth century, when the Habsburgs, after the Ottoman conquest of much of Hungary, organised the remaining border districts of Croatia and Slavonia into military captaincies. Lands were settled by soldier-peasants, many of them refugees from Ottoman territory — Croats, Serbs, Vlachs, Germans and others — who received land and tax privileges in exchange for hereditary military service.

Over the following centuries the system was expanded eastward through the Banat and Transylvania. The frontier was administered directly from Vienna by the Hofkriegsrat, bypassing the civilian governments of Croatia and Hungary, a source of lasting political friction. The frontiersmen, known as Grenzer, formed light infantry regiments that served across Europe in Habsburg armies.

Organisation

The frontier was divided into districts such as the Croatian, Slavonian, Banat and Transylvanian frontiers, each subdivided into regimental areas. Society was organised around the extended family household (zadruga), and every able-bodied male owed military service. Frontier communities were religiously mixed, including Catholics, Orthodox Christians and others, with privileges granted by imperial statutes such as the Statuta Valachorum of 1630.

Demilitarisation

As the Ottoman threat receded in the nineteenth century, the frontier was progressively demilitarised. The process was completed in 1881, when the remaining frontier districts were incorporated into the Kingdom of Croatia-Slavonia within Austria-Hungary.
