The Filmfare Awards are annual awards honouring artistic and technical excellence in the Hindi-language film industry of India. Presented by Filmfare magazine of The Times Group, the ceremony is one of the oldest and most prominent film events in India, first held in 1954.

History

The awards were first presented on 21 March 1954 at the Metro Cinema in Bombay, honouring films of 1953; originally only five awards were given, and Do Bigha Zamin won the first Best Film award while Dilip Kumar won Best Actor for Daag. The trophy, a statuette of a woman in a dance pose known as the "Black Lady", was designed by N. G. Pansare under the supervision of art director Walter Langhammer.

Categories and voting

Awards are presented in popular categories (such as Best Film, Best Director, Best Actor and Best Actress), critics' categories, and technical categories. Winners of the popular awards are determined by votes from the public and a committee of experts. Regional editions were later introduced, including Filmfare Awards South (for the Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, and Kannada industries, since 1954), Filmfare Awards East, Marathi, Punjabi, and Bangla.

Records

Dilip Kumar and Shah Rukh Khan share the record for most Best Actor awards, with eight each. Black (2005) holds the record for most awards won by a single film, with eleven.
