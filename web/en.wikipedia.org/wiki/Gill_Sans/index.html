Gill Sans

Gill Sans, the classic British humanist sans-serif

Gill Sans is a humanist sans-serif typeface designed by the British sculptor, letter-cutter and type designer Eric Gill, and released by the British branch of the Monotype Corporation in 1928. It is one of the best-known British typefaces and has been widely used in British design, by the railways, the Church of England, Penguin Books, and British government bodies.

History

Gill Sans grew out of lettering Gill painted for a shop sign in Bristol in 1926, and of the alphabet he designed for the London and North Eastern Railway. Monotype's Stanley Morison championed its commercial release in 1928. Its letterforms are based on classical proportions — Gill had studied under the calligrapher Edward Johnston, whose Underground typeface for the London Underground influenced its design — but with a greater range of weights than Johnston's design.

Use

The typeface became the visual identity of the LNER's posters, of Penguin Books' paperback covers under Jan Tschichold's standardisation, and after 1965 of British Rail. The Church of England adopted it for the Common Worship service books, and it was for many years the BBC's corporate typeface. A digital revival, Gill Sans Nova, was released by Monotype in 2015.
