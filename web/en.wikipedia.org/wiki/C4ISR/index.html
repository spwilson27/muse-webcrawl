C4ISR is an acronym used by the United States Department of Defense and other militaries standing for Command, Control, Communications, Computers, Intelligence, Surveillance and Reconnaissance. It denotes the systems, procedures and technologies that allow commanders to collect and distribute information, maintain situational awareness and direct forces — in effect the nervous system of modern network-centric warfare.

Concept

The term evolved from earlier formulations: C2 (command and control), C3 (adding communications), C3I and C4I (adding computers and intelligence). The ISR component was appended to emphasise the integration of sensors and intelligence collection — satellites, reconnaissance aircraft, unmanned aerial vehicles, radar and signals intelligence — with the communications and computing networks that fuse their data into a common operational picture.

Variants

Related acronyms include C4ISTAR (adding "target acquisition", used in the United Kingdom), C5ISR (adding "combat systems" or "cyber"), and C6ISR (further adding cyber and combat systems). The C4ISR concept underlies doctrines such as network-centric warfare and joint all-domain command and control (JADC2), which aim to link sensors and shooters across services and domains.
