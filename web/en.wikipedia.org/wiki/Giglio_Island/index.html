Giglio Island (Italian: Isola del Giglio) is an Italian island and comune in the Tyrrhenian Sea, off the coast of Tuscany, and part of the Province of Grosseto. It is one of seven islands in the Tuscan Archipelago. The island is known for its granite geology, clear waters, and the medieval hill town of Giglio Castello.

Geography

The island is separated from the mainland promontory of Monte Argentario by about 16 km of sea. Its territory is mostly mountainous, culminating in the Poggio della Pagana (496 m). The main settlements are Giglio Porto, the ferry port on the east coast; Giglio Castello, the fortified medieval centre; and Campese on the north-western bay.

History

Giglio was settled in antiquity — the Etruscans and Romans exploited its granite, which supplied columns for buildings in Rome. In 1544 the island was attacked by the corsair Hayreddin Barbarossa, who deported much of the population; it was later repopulated by families from Liguria. On 13 January 2012 the cruise ship Costa Concordia struck a reef off the island and partially sank, killing 32 people; thousands were evacuated to the island, whose residents were widely praised for sheltering them. The wreck was raised in 2013 and refloated and towed to Genoa in July 2014 in one of the largest maritime salvage operations ever undertaken.
