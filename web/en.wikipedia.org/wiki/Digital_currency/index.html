Digital currency

Digital currency (digital money, electronic money, or electronic currency) is any currency, money, or money-like asset that is stored and exchanged primarily on digital computer systems, especially over the internet. Types include cryptocurrencies, virtual currencies, and central bank digital currencies (CBDCs).

Types

Cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin (launched 2009) use decentralised networks and cryptography to record transfers without a central issuer. Virtual currencies are used within specific online communities. CBDCs, by contrast, are issued directly by central banks; several countries have launched them and many more are piloting them.

Regulation and impact

Digital currencies raise questions about monetary policy, financial stability, privacy, and illicit finance. Most modern money already exists in digital form as bank deposits; the term usually refers to newer systems of issuance and transfer built on digital ledgers.
