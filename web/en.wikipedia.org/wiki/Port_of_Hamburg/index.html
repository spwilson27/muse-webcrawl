Port of Hamburg

Port of Hamburg (Hamburger Hafen)
Location: Hamburg, Germany
Operated by: Hamburg Port Authority
Type: River and sea port

The Port of Hamburg (German: Hamburger Hafen) is a seaport on the river Elbe in Hamburg, Germany, 110 kilometres from the mouth of the Elbe into the North Sea. Known as Germany's "Gateway to the World" (Tor zur Welt), it is the country's largest seaport and one of Europe's largest container ports, after Rotterdam and Antwerp.

The port was founded in the twelfth century and flourished as a member of the Hanseatic League. In the late nineteenth century it became one of the world's principal ports of emigration and trade, and Hamburg's free port status, granted in 1888, underpinned its growth. The historic Speicherstadt warehouse district, built in this period, is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Terminals

Major container terminals include Altenwerder, Burchardkai, Tollerort, and Eurogate. The port also handles bulk goods, cruise ships at terminals in HafenCity and Steinwerder, and extensive rail freight; Hamburg is one of Europe's largest rail ports.
