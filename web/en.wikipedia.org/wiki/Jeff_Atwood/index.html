Jeff Atwood (born 1970) is an American software developer, entrepreneur, and writer. He is best known as a co-founder of Stack Overflow and the Stack Exchange network of question-and-answer websites, and for his programming blog Coding Horror.

Coding Horror

Atwood began writing the blog Coding Horror in 2004, covering software development practices, human–computer interaction, and the craft of programming. The blog gained a large following among programmers and helped establish Atwood's reputation as a commentator on software engineering culture. He is associated with "Atwood's Law", the observation that any application that can be written in JavaScript eventually will be written in JavaScript.

Stack Overflow and Stack Exchange

In 2008, Atwood co-founded Stack Overflow with Joel Spolsky, launching a question-and-answer site for programmers that combined wiki-style editing, reputation systems, and gamification elements such as badges and voting. The model was generalized into the Stack Exchange network of topic-specific Q&A communities. Atwood left Stack Exchange in 2012.

Discourse

In 2013, Atwood co-founded the Civilized Discourse Construction Kit, Inc., the company behind Discourse, an open-source discussion platform intended to modernize internet forums. He announced the project with the goal of raising the standard of online discussion software.
