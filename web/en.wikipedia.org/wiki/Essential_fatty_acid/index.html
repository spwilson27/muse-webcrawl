Essential fatty acid

Essential fatty acids (EFAs) are fatty acids that humans and other animals must ingest because the body cannot synthesize them. Strictly, only two qualify: the omega-3 alpha-linolenic acid (ALA) and the omega-6 linoleic acid (LA). Other fatty acids are "conditionally essential", needed when synthesis from precursors is insufficient.

Functions

From ALA the body can produce EPA and DHA (inefficiently in humans), important for cell membranes, the brain and retina; from LA it produces arachidonic acid, a precursor of eicosanoids regulating inflammation. EFAs also maintain skin barrier function.

Sources and deficiency

ALA occurs in flaxseed, chia, walnuts and canola oil; LA in most seed oils. Deficiency, now rare except in malabsorption or artificial feeding without fat, causes scaly skin, hair loss and impaired wound healing. George and Mildred Burr demonstrated essentiality in rats in 1929.
