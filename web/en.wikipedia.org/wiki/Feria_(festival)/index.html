Feria (festival)

A feria (Spanish for "fair" or "festival") is a traditional local festival celebrated in towns and cities across Spain and parts of Latin America and southern France. Ferias typically last several days and combine religious observances honouring a patron saint with music, dancing, processions, bullfighting and fairground attractions.

Andalusian ferias

The best-known ferias are those of Andalusia, above all the Feria de Abril (April Fair) in Seville, held two weeks after Easter. A dedicated fairground, the Real, is filled with hundreds of canvas pavilions called casetas, where families and associations socialise, drink sherry and the mixed drink rebujito, and dance sevillanas late into the night. Women traditionally wear flamenco dresses (trajes de flamenca) and horsemen parade in traditional dress during the day. Málaga's August fair and the Feria del Caballo in Jerez de la Frontera are other celebrated examples.

Elsewhere

In France, ferias in cities such as Nîmes, Arles and Béziers centre on bullfighting seasons and street parties. In Latin America, town fairs share the name and much of the festive character. Originally many ferias were livestock and trade fairs timed to religious feasts, and some still feature agricultural exhibitions.
