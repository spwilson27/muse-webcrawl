Solid-phase extraction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Solid-phase extraction (SPE) is a sample preparation technique used in analytical chemistry to separate, concentrate, and purify analytes from a liquid sample before analysis, typically by chromatography or mass spectrometry. The sample is passed through a cartridge, disk, or well packed with a solid sorbent; compounds of interest are retained on the sorbent while interferences pass through, or vice versa, and the retained analytes are then recovered in a small volume of solvent.

Procedure

A typical SPE procedure has four steps:

  1. Conditioning — the sorbent is wetted with a solvent (often methanol) followed by water or buffer, to prepare the stationary phase.
  2. Loading — the sample is applied; analytes bind to the sorbent under chosen conditions of pH and polarity.
  3. Washing — a weak solvent removes interferences while leaving analytes bound.
  4. Elution — a stronger solvent disrupts the analyte–sorbent interaction and collects the analytes in a concentrated fraction.

Flow is driven by vacuum, positive pressure, centrifugation, or gravity, and formats range from single cartridges to automated 96-well plates used in high-throughput laboratories.

Sorbents and mechanisms

The most common sorbents are silica-based materials bonded with functional groups: C18 and C8 alkyl chains for reversed-phase extraction, bare silica and alumina for normal-phase work, and ion-exchange and mixed-mode phases for charged analytes. Polymeric sorbents, such as hydrophilic–lipophilic balanced copolymers, tolerate a wider pH range and are widely used for extracting drugs and environmental contaminants from water and biological fluids. Specialized phases include molecularly imprinted polymers and immunoaffinity sorbents.

Applications

SPE is used in environmental testing (pesticides and pharmaceuticals in water), clinical and forensic toxicology, food safety, and pharmaceutical bioanalysis, where it replaced much older liquid–liquid extraction because it uses less solvent, is easily automated, and gives cleaner extracts.
