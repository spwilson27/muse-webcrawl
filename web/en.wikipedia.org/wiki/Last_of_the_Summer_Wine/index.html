Last of the Summer Wine

Genre: Sitcom
Created by: Roy Clarke
Country: United Kingdom
Original run: 1973–2010
Series/Episodes: 31 series, 295 episodes

Last of the Summer Wine is a British sitcom created and written by Roy Clarke that aired on BBC One from 1973 to 2010. Set in the Yorkshire town of Holmfirth, it follows a group of elderly men — a shifting central trio — as they wander the countryside, engage in childlike schemes, and gently observe village life. With 31 series and 295 episodes, it is the longest-running TV comedy in Britain and the world's longest-running sitcom by years on air.

Premise and characters

The original trio consisted of the scruffy, work-shy Compo Simmonite (Bill Owen), the mild-mannered Norman Clegg (Peter Sallis), and the snobbish Cyril Blamire (Michael Bates). Over the decades the third member changed several times — including Foggy Dewhurst (Brian Wilde), Seymour Utterthwaite (Michael Aldridge), and Herbert "Truly" Truelove (Frank Thornton) — while a large ensemble of villagers accumulated, notably Nora Batty (Kathy Staff), the formidable object of Compo's affections, café owners Sid and Ivy, and Howard and Marina. After Owen's death in 1999 the show continued with Compo's son Tom among the newer characters; Peter Sallis (Clegg) and Frank Thornton appeared through to the end.

Production

The series began as a Comedy Playhouse pilot, "The Last of the Summer Wine", broadcast in January 1973, and the first full series followed that November. Nearly all exterior filming took place in and around Holmfirth, West Yorkshire, which became a place of pilgrimage for fans, with exhibitions, tours, and Sid's Café preserved as a real café. Roy Clarke wrote every episode. The gentle tone, Ronnie Hazlehurst's pastoral theme music, and the absence of studio laughter tracks in later location-heavy series gave it a distinctive character.

End and legacy

The BBC announced the show's end in 2010; the final episode, "How Not to Cry at Weddings", aired on 29 August 2010. Clarke and several cast members later reunited for the prequel series First of the Summer Wine (1988–1989) and the spin-off-era retrospective specials. The show remains a staple of British television repeats and of nostalgia broadcasting internationally.
