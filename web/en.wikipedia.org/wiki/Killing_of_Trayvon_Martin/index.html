Killing of Trayvon Martin

On the evening of 26 February 2012, Trayvon Martin, an unarmed 17-year-old African American high school student, was shot and killed in Sanford, Florida, by George Zimmerman, a 28-year-old neighbourhood watch coordinator. Martin was visiting his father's fiancée in the gated community of Twin Lakes and was walking back from a convenience store when Zimmerman, who had reported him as suspicious to police, confronted him; in the ensuing struggle Zimmerman shot Martin once in the chest. Zimmerman claimed self-defence and was initially not charged, prompting nationwide protests. He was later tried for second-degree murder and acquitted in July 2013.

Background and shooting

Zimmerman, a volunteer neighbourhood watch coordinator, called the Sanford police non-emergency line to report a "suspicious" person. Despite the dispatcher saying "we don't need you to do that", Zimmerman left his vehicle; an altercation followed in which Zimmerman said Martin attacked and beat him, and he fired one shot from his handgun. Martin, who had been talking on a mobile phone with a friend, died at the scene. Police took Zimmerman into custody, treated his head injuries, questioned him and released him, citing Florida's self-defence laws — including its "stand your ground" statute — as preventing arrest absent evidence contradicting his account.

Public reaction

The delay in charging Zimmerman provoked weeks of protests, petitions signed by millions, and commentary — including by President Barack Obama, who said, "If I had a son, he'd look like Trayvon." The case became a national debate over racial profiling, gun laws and self-defence statutes. Florida's governor appointed a special prosecutor, who charged Zimmerman with second-degree murder in April 2012.

Trial and aftermath

At trial in June–July 2013, the defence argued Zimmerman acted in self-defence; a six-woman jury acquitted him on 13 July 2013. The US Department of Justice later closed its civil-rights investigation without charges. The case was a catalyst in the emergence of the Black Lives Matter movement, founded in 2013 in response to the acquittal, and Martin's name became a symbol in debates about race and policing in the United States. His parents established the Trayvon Martin Foundation and remained prominent advocates.
