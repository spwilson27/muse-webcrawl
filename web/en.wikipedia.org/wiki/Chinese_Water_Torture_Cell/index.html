Chinese Water Torture Cell

The Chinese Water Torture Cell is a predicament escape made famous by the Hungarian-American magician Harry Houdini, who first performed it publicly in Scotland in 1913. Despite its name, the escape has no connection to the legendary Chinese water torture; Houdini renamed his earlier "water cell" concept with a more sensational billing.

The escape

In the escape, Houdini's feet were locked into stocks on the top of a glass-and-steel tank filled with water. He was lowered upside down into the tank, which was then sealed and curtained off. A reserve of air — possible because his head was lowered into an air pocket created by the displacement of his body — allowed him to survive while he worked free of the stocks and swapped places with the frame, emerging behind the curtain after more than three minutes in some performances.

History

Houdini performed the escape from 1913 until his death in 1926, and guarded its method so jealously that he patented aspects of the tank — unusual for a magician, since patents are public documents. The original cell was reconstructed after being damaged and passed to the collection of magician and collector Sidney Radner, later appearing in museums. Illusionists including Doug Henning and David Copperfield have performed versions of the effect, which remains one of the most famous escapes in magic history.
