Corporate sustainability

Corporate sustainability is an approach to business that seeks long-term value by taking into account how a company operates in its ecological, social, and economic environments. It extends the older concept of corporate social responsibility (CSR) by embedding sustainability into strategy, operations, and reporting rather than treating it as philanthropy.

Origins

The concept builds on the Brundtland Commission's 1987 definition of sustainable development — development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. John Elkington popularized the triple bottom line (“people, planet, profit”) in the 1990s, arguing that companies should account for social and environmental performance alongside financial results.

ESG and reporting

Investors increasingly evaluate companies using environmental, social, and governance (ESG) criteria. Voluntary reporting frameworks include the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) standards, the Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB) standards, and the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD). In the European Union, the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) has made detailed sustainability disclosure mandatory for large companies.

Practices

Corporate sustainability programs commonly address greenhouse gas emissions and science-based reduction targets, energy and water efficiency, waste and circular-economy initiatives, sustainable supply chains and procurement, product life-cycle assessment, and commitments such as net-zero carbon goals. Companies also join initiatives such as the UN Global Compact and set targets aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

Criticism

Critics warn of greenwashing — marketing that exaggerates environmental credentials — and question the consistency of ESG ratings, which can differ widely between providers. Others argue that sustainability obligations belong to governments rather than firms, echoing Milton Friedman's view that the social responsibility of business is to increase its profits.
