Reflex

In biology, a reflex, or reflex action, is an involuntary, unplanned sequence of movements or a nearly instantaneous response to a stimulus. Reflexes are mediated by neural pathways called reflex arcs, which can act without the involvement of the brain, typically passing only through the spinal cord or brainstem.

Types

Familiar examples include the patellar (knee-jerk) reflex, the pupillary light reflex, the gag reflex and the withdrawal reflex from a painful stimulus. Reflexes may be monosynaptic, with a single synapse between sensory and motor neurons, or polysynaptic, involving interneurons. Autonomic reflexes regulate internal functions such as digestion and blood pressure.

Clinical significance

Testing reflexes is a standard part of the neurological examination: abnormally brisk, diminished or absent reflexes can help localise lesions of the nervous system, distinguishing upper from lower motor neuron damage. Newborn infants display primitive reflexes, such as the grasp and rooting reflexes, which disappear as the nervous system matures.
