The American Legion


The American Legion's national headquarters, Indianapolis

The American Legion, commonly known as the Legion, is a non-profit organization of U.S. war veterans headquartered in Indianapolis, Indiana. It is made up of state, territorial and overseas departments, which in turn consist of local posts. The Legion was chartered by Congress in 1919 as a patriotic veterans organization, and it played a leading role in the drafting and passage of the Servicemen's Readjustment Act of 1944, commonly known as the G.I. Bill.

History

The American Legion was founded in March 1919 in Paris, France, by members of the American Expeditionary Forces, and was chartered by the United States Congress on 16 September 1919. Among its founders was Theodore Roosevelt Jr., a son of the former president. The organization quickly grew to become the largest veterans service organization in the United States, with membership peaking at over 3 million in the years after the Second World War.

Purposes and programmes

The Legion's mission centers on veterans' advocacy, youth programmes, Americanism, and national security. It sponsors American Legion Baseball, the Boys State and Boys Nation civic education programmes, oratorical contests, and Scouting units. Its four pillars are Veterans Affairs & Rehabilitation, National Security, Americanism, and Children & Youth.

The Legion is known for its role in establishing the Department of Veterans Affairs, supporting the PACT Act of 2022 expanding benefits for veterans exposed to toxic substances, and promoting the display and proper handling of the United States flag.

Organization

Membership is open to anyone who has served at least one day of active military duty since 7 December 1941 and was honorably discharged, or is still serving, following the LEGION Act of 2019. The Legion's motto is "Veterans Strengthening America." Its auxiliary organizations include the American Legion Auxiliary and the Sons of The American Legion.
