In physics, power is the amount of energy transferred or converted per unit time. In the International System of Units, the unit of power is the watt, equal to one joule per second. Power is a scalar quantity.

Definition

Power is the rate at which work is done: P = W/t, where W is work and t is time. For a force F acting on a body moving with velocity v, the instantaneous power is P = F · v. In electrical circuits, power equals the product of voltage and current: P = V·I.

Units

Besides the watt, commonly used units include the horsepower (about 746 W), historically devised by James Watt to compare the output of steam engines with the power of draft horses. Energy bills are commonly measured in kilowatt-hours, the energy delivered by one kilowatt of power sustained for one hour.

Average and peak power

Engineers distinguish average power over an interval from instantaneous and peak power. Machines are often rated by the maximum continuous power they can deliver, while pulsed systems such as radar may have peak powers far exceeding their average output.
