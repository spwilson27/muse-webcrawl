Neutrophil elastase

Neutrophil elastase
Gene: ELANE
Class: Serine protease
Found in: Azurophilic granules of neutrophils
Natural inhibitor: Alpha-1 antitrypsin

Neutrophil elastase (EC 3.4.21.37) is a serine protease stored in the azurophilic (primary) granules of neutrophils and encoded in humans by the ELANE gene. It is released during inflammation and phagocytosis, where it degrades proteins of invading bacteria as well as extracellular matrix components such as elastin, collagen and proteoglycans.

Function

Alongside cathepsin G and proteinase 3, neutrophil elastase helps kill engulfed microorganisms inside the phagolysosome and is a component of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs). In the extracellular space its activity is normally held in check by antiproteases, chiefly alpha-1 antitrypsin.

Clinical significance

An imbalance between elastase and its inhibitors damages lung tissue: inherited alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency allows unopposed elastase activity and leads to early-onset emphysema. Mutations in ELANE cause cyclic neutropenia and severe congenital neutropenia. Excess elastase activity is also implicated in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and cystic fibrosis lung disease.
