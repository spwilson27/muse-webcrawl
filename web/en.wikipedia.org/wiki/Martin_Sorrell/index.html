Martin Sorrell

Sir Martin Stuart Sorrell
Born: February 14, 1945, London, England
Education: Christ's College, Cambridge; Harvard Business School (MBA)
Occupation: Businessman
Known for: Founder of WPP and S4 Capital

Sir Martin Stuart Sorrell (born February 14, 1945) is a British businessman best known as the founder of WPP plc, which he built over three decades into the world's largest advertising and public relations company, and subsequently of S4 Capital.

Early life and career

Sorrell was born in London and educated at Haberdashers' Aske's Boys' School, Christ's College, Cambridge, where he read economics, and Harvard Business School. He worked at Saatchi & Saatchi from 1975 to 1984, rising to group finance director and earning a reputation for financial deal-making in the advertising industry.

WPP

In 1985 Sorrell bought a controlling stake in Wire and Plastic Products, a maker of wire shopping baskets, and used it as a vehicle for an extraordinary acquisition programme, acquiring major agencies including J. Walter Thompson (1987) and the Ogilvy Group (1989), followed by Young & Rubicam and Grey Global. As chief executive for 33 years he was one of the FTSE 100's longest-serving and highest-paid bosses. He resigned from WPP in April 2018 following a board investigation into allegations of personal misconduct, which he denied.

S4 Capital

Weeks after leaving WPP, Sorrell launched S4 Capital, a digital advertising and marketing services group built around MediaMonks and MightyHive, championing a purely digital, data-driven model.

Honours

Sorrell was knighted in 2000 for services to the media and communications industries.
