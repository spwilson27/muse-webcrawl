Gunnhildr Gormsdóttir, known as Gunnhild, Mother of Kings (Old Norse: Gunnhildr konungamóðir), was a figure of the Viking Age known from the Icelandic sagas. She was said to be the wife of Eric Bloodaxe, king of Norway, and the mother of several kings, including Harald Greycloak.

Gunnhild as depicted in later tradition

Saga tradition

According to sources such as Heimskringla, Egils saga and Historia Norwegiæ, Gunnhild was the daughter of Gorm the Old, king of Denmark, though other traditions give her a humbler origin in Hålogaland and credit her with training in magic by Finnic (Sámi) sorcerers. The sagas portray her as beautiful, cunning and ruthless: she is blamed for intrigues at the Norwegian court, for the deaths of rivals, and for the feud that destroyed the family of Egill Skallagrímsson, whose curse against Eric and Gunnhild is a famous episode of Egils saga.

After Eric's death in England, Gunnhild raised her sons at the Danish court of Harald Bluetooth and engineered their return to power in Norway, where they killed King Haakon the Good's successor and ruled harshly until they were themselves overthrown. Modern historians treat the saga portrait with caution, noting that Gunnhild became a stock villainess of medieval Scandinavian literature and that many of the deeds attributed to her are literary construction.
