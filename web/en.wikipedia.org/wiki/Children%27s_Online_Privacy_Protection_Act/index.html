Children's Online Privacy Protection Act

Short titleCOPPA
Enacted1998; effective April 21, 2000
Administered byFederal Trade Commission (FTC)
Applies toOnline services collecting data from children under 13

The Children's Online Privacy Protection Act of 1998 (COPPA) is a United States federal law that imposes requirements on operators of websites and online services directed at children under 13 years of age, and on operators that knowingly collect personal information from children under 13.

Requirements

COPPA requires covered operators to post a clear privacy policy, obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information from children, allow parents to review and delete their child's data, and maintain reasonable data security.

Enforcement and revisions

The FTC enforces COPPA and revised its implementing rule in 2013 to cover persistent identifiers, geolocation, photos and voice recordings. Enforcement actions have included major settlements with video platforms and toy makers.
