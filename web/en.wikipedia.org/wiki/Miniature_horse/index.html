Miniature horse

A miniature horse is a small horse bred to retain the proportions and conformation of a full-sized horse at greatly reduced height. Depending on the registry, miniature horses must stand no more than 34–38 inches (86–97 cm) at the withers. They are not ponies: registries require horse-like proportions rather than the heavier build typical of pony breeds.

History

Miniature horses descend from small horses bred in Europe from the 17th century for royal collections, and from undersized Shetland and other stock later used in coal mines. Organized breeding began in the United States in the 20th century, drawing on Shetland, Welsh, and Hackney bloodlines refined toward a scaled-down Arabian or Thoroughbred appearance. The American Miniature Horse Association (founded 1978) and the American Miniature Horse Registry maintain studbooks.

Uses and care

Miniature horses are kept as companion animals, shown in hand and in driving classes, and trained as guide animals for the blind and as therapy animals. They cannot be ridden except by very small children. Their small size brings special health concerns: selective breeding for dwarfism traits can cause skeletal and dental problems, and the breed is prone to obesity and laminitis if overfed. Despite their size, they require the same veterinary care, hoof trimming, and exercise as full-sized horses.
