Test preparation

Test preparation (or test prep) is the process of studying and practicing specifically to improve performance on standardized tests and other examinations. It ranges from self-study with published materials to commercial courses, tutoring, and online programs offered for admissions tests such as the SAT, ACT, GRE, GMAT, LSAT, and MCAT, as well as professional licensing exams.

Methods

Test preparation typically includes review of the subject matter tested, instruction in test-taking strategies (such as time management and process of elimination), and practice with simulated or previously administered questions under timed conditions. Providers include large companies such as Kaplan and The Princeton Review, as well as nonprofit and free resources such as Khan Academy, which became the official SAT preparation partner of the College Board.

Debate and research

Coaching effects are debated: research studies generally find modest average score gains from commercial preparation, with larger gains associated with extended practice and familiarity with test format. Critics argue that the cost of preparation advantages affluent students and exacerbates inequality in admissions, while proponents note that free and low-cost resources have narrowed the access gap.
