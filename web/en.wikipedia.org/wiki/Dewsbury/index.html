Dewsbury is a market town in the Metropolitan Borough of Kirklees, West Yorkshire, England. It lies on the River Calder, 8 miles (13 km) south of Leeds and west of Wakefield, within the Heavy Woollen District of towns that grew up around the textile trade.

History

Dewsbury's name is of Old English origin, and the town has an early ecclesiastical history: Saint Paulinus is said to have preached here in the 7th century, and Dewsbury Minster stands on a site of great antiquity. In the 19th century the town boomed as a centre of the shoddy and mungo trade — recycling woollen cloth — and of blanket and carpet manufacture. The grand Victorian town hall opened in 1889.

Today

Textile manufacturing has declined, but Dewsbury retains a busy market, first chartered in the 14th century. The town has a significant South Asian community, largely descended from migrants who arrived to work in the mills. Dewsbury railway station provides services to Leeds, Huddersfield and Manchester, and the town is the home of the Dewsbury Rams rugby league club.
