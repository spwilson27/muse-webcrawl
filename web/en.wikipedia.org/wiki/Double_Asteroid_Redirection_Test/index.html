Double Asteroid Redirection Test

The Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART) was a NASA space mission aimed at testing a method of planetary defense against near-Earth objects. It was designed to assess how much a spacecraft impact deflects an asteroid through momentum transfer. Launched on 24 November 2021, the DART spacecraft deliberately crashed into Dimorphos, the smaller moonlet of the binary asteroid 65803 Didymos, on 26 September 2022.

Mission

DART, a box-shaped spacecraft of about 610 kg with a single camera (DRACO) and autonomous navigation (SMART Nav), was launched aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Vandenberg Space Force Base. It was accompanied by LICIACube, a cubesat provided by the Italian Space Agency, which separated before impact to photograph the collision and the resulting ejecta plume. Because Didymos is a binary system, astronomers could measure the deflection precisely from Earth by timing how Dimorphos's roughly 11-hour-55-minute orbital period around Didymos changed.

Impact and results

DART struck Dimorphos at about 6.6 km/s (roughly 23,760 km/h). On 11 October 2022 NASA confirmed the impact had shortened Dimorphos's orbital period by about 32 minutes — far exceeding the mission's success threshold of 73 seconds and the roughly 10 minutes expected. The ejecta blasted from the surface contributed additional momentum, giving a momentum enhancement factor (beta) of about 3.6. Telescopes including Hubble and Webb observed a long comet-like tail of debris extending thousands of kilometres. Neither asteroid posed or poses any threat to Earth; the target was chosen purely because the change in its orbit could be measured.

Follow-up

The European Space Agency's Hera mission, launched in October 2024, is travelling to the Didymos system to survey the impact crater and measure Dimorphos's mass and internal structure, completing the assessment of the deflection experiment.
