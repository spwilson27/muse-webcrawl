The Boeing F/A-18E and F/A-18F Super Hornet are a series of American supersonic twin-engine, carrier-capable, multirole fighter aircraft derived from the McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet. The single-seat F/A-18E and tandem-seat F/A-18F variants are larger and more advanced versions of the F/A-18C and D Hornet.

The Super Hornet has a new, larger airframe with larger leading-edge extensions, bigger rectangular air intakes, more powerful General Electric F414 engines, and capacity for additional weapons stations and internal fuel. It entered service with the United States Navy in 1999, replacing the Grumman F-14 Tomcat, and serves alongside the original Hornet's replacement in many strike fighter squadrons.

Design

The Super Hornet is roughly 20 percent larger than the original Hornet and can carry significantly more fuel internally, extending mission range by up to 41 percent. It has eleven weapons stations and can return to the carrier with a heavier load of unexpended ordnance. Later Block II and Block III aircraft incorporate the APG-79 active electronically scanned array (AESA) radar, advanced crew stations, and reduced radar signature features, while the EA-18G Growler electronic warfare variant is derived from the F/A-18F airframe.

Operational history

The Super Hornet first saw combat during Operation Southern Watch and the opening of Operation Iraqi Freedom in 2002–2003. It has since served in Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria and other theatres, performing air superiority, strike, close air support, reconnaissance and buddy refuelling missions. The Royal Australian Air Force operates 24 F/A-18Fs, and Kuwait ordered Super Hornets as well. The type starred in the 2022 film Top Gun: Maverick.
