Atlanta University Center

Atlanta University Center Consortium
Type: Private consortium of HBCUs
Established: 1929
Location: Atlanta, Georgia, US
Members: Clark Atlanta University, Morehouse College, Morehouse School of Medicine, Spelman College

The Atlanta University Center Consortium (AUC Consortium) is a consortium of historically black colleges and universities (HBCUs) in Atlanta, Georgia, United States. It is the oldest and largest contiguous grouping of African-American higher education institutions in the United States.

Members

The consortium's member institutions are Clark Atlanta University, Morehouse College, Morehouse School of Medicine, and Spelman College. Morris Brown College was formerly a member, and Clark College and Atlanta University consolidated in 1988 to form Clark Atlanta University. The Interdenominational Theological Center is an affiliate member. Together the institutions enrol over 12,000 students.

History

The consortium traces its origins to 1929, when Atlanta University agreed to coordinate programs with Morehouse College and Spelman College, sharing resources and administration under president John Hope. Cooperation deepened through the 20th century, and the Atlanta University Center was formally established in 1964. The Robert W. Woodruff Library, opened in 1982, serves as the shared library for the member institutions.

Significance

The AUC has produced a disproportionate share of African-American leaders in politics, business, science, and the arts. Alumni of its member institutions include Martin Luther King Jr. (Morehouse), W. E. B. Du Bois (Atlanta University faculty and alumnus of its era), and numerous members of Congress, judges, and university presidents. The campuses, just west of downtown Atlanta, include districts listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
