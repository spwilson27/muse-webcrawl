Loch Leven

Loch Leven is a freshwater loch in Perth and Kinross, Scotland, near the town of Kinross. Roughly triangular and about 16 square kilometres (6 square miles) in area, it is a shallow lowland loch celebrated for its birdlife, its trout fishery, and the island castle where Mary, Queen of Scots was imprisoned.

Islands and history

The loch contains several islands, the largest being St Serf's Island, long a monastic site. On Lochleven Castle island stand the ruins of Loch Leven Castle, held by the Douglas family. Mary, Queen of Scots was imprisoned in the castle in 1567 after her forced abdication; in May 1568 she escaped by boat with the help of the young Willie Douglas and supporters, an event commemorated by the Queen's Ferry and local tradition. The castle had earlier hosted Robert the Bruce and other Scottish monarchs.

Nature

Loch Leven is designated as a National Nature Reserve and a Ramsar site. Its shallow, nutrient-rich waters support large numbers of breeding and wintering wildfowl, including pink-footed geese, ducks, and swans. In the nineteenth century the loch's level was lowered by drainage works on the River Leven, its outflow to the Firth of Forth, which enlarged several islands and increased the exposed shore. The loch's brown trout fishery was once world famous, and Loch Leven trout were exported to stock waters across the British Empire and beyond.
