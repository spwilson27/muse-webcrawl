Enola Gay

Unit509th Composite Group, USAAF

The Enola Gay is a Boeing B-29 Superfortress bomber, named after Enola Gay Tibbets, the mother of its pilot, Colonel Paul Tibbets. On 6 August 1945, piloted by Tibbets and flown by the 509th Composite Group, it dropped the "Little Boy" atomic bomb on the Japanese city of Hiroshima, becoming the first aircraft to drop an atomic bomb in warfare.

The Hiroshima mission

The aircraft took off from North Field, Tinian, in the early hours of 6 August 1945, accompanied by two observation B-29s. The bomb was released at 08:15 local time over Hiroshima and detonated at an altitude of about 580 metres. The Enola Gay returned safely to Tinian. Three days later a second B-29, Bockscar, bombed Nagasaki.

Post-war history

After the war the Enola Gay participated in the Operation Crossroads nuclear tests in 1946 and was transferred to the Smithsonian Institution in 1949. It was stored, partly outdoors, for decades before a long restoration. Its display at the National Air and Space Museum in 1995, for the fiftieth anniversary of the bombing, provoked intense controversy over the planned exhibition text, which critics said emphasised Japanese casualties over the context of the war; the exhibit was substantially reduced and the museum's director resigned. The fully restored aircraft has been on display at the Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center in Virginia since 2003.
