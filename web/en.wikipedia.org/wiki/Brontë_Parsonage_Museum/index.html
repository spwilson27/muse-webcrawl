The Brontë Parsonage Museum is a museum and writer's house in Haworth, West Yorkshire, England, maintained by the Brontë Society in honour of the Brontë sisters — Charlotte, Emily and Anne. The museum is in the former home of the family, Haworth Parsonage, where the sisters spent most of their lives and wrote their famous novels.

History

The Parsonage was built in 1778–79. In 1820 the Reverend Patrick Brontë moved in as perpetual curate of Haworth with his wife Maria and their six children. Here Charlotte wrote Jane Eyre, Emily wrote Wuthering Heights and Anne wrote The Tenant of Wildfell Hall. After Patrick's death in 1861 the contents were auctioned, but from the 1890s the Brontë Society began acquiring surviving furniture and manuscripts. The Parsonage was purchased for the nation by Sir James Roberts and opened as a museum on 4 August 1928.

Collections

The museum houses the world's largest collection of Brontë manuscripts, early editions, furniture and personal possessions, including the sisters' writing desks, Charlotte's wedding dress, and the tiny hand-written juvenile books the siblings produced in childhood. The rooms are arranged as they would have appeared in the family's time, including Mr Brontë's study and the dining room where the sisters wrote.
