Mamba

Mambas are fast-moving, highly venomous snakes of the genus Dendroaspis in the family Elapidae, native to parts of Africa. The genus comprises four species, most famously the black mamba (Dendroaspis polylepis) and the green mambas.

Species

The four recognised species are the eastern green mamba (D. angusticeps), western green mamba (D. viridis), Jameson's mamba (D. jamesoni), and black mamba (D. polylepis). Mambas are slender, agile, primarily diurnal snakes; the black mamba is largely terrestrial while green mambas are arboreal.

Venom and behaviour

Mamba venom is predominantly neurotoxic and cardiotoxic, and bites can be rapidly fatal without antivenom. Despite their fearsome reputation, mambas are generally shy and flee from humans when given an escape route, biting repeatedly only when cornered. The black mamba's name refers to the inky-black interior of its mouth, not its body colour, which is usually grey to khaki.

Distribution

Mambas occur across sub-Saharan Africa in savanna, rocky hillsides, and coastal forest. The black mamba is among the longest venomous snakes, commonly 2 to 3 metres and reportedly up to 4.5 metres.
