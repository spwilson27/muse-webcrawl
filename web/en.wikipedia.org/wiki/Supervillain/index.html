Supervillain

A supervillain is a type of fictional villain character, commonly found in comic books, films and other superhero fiction, who possesses extraordinary abilities, resources or intellect and uses them for evil, criminal or destructive ends. The supervillain serves as the antagonist and counterpart of the superhero, just as the hero's powers and weaknesses are typically mirrored by those of the villain.

Supervillains are often as visually distinctive as the heroes they oppose

Characteristics

Supervillains are typically distinguished by flamboyant costumes and code names, grandiose schemes (often involving world domination), superhuman powers or advanced technology, and a personal connection or thematic opposition to the hero. Many possess a tragic or sympathetic backstory, and the most enduring — such as the Joker, Lex Luthor, Doctor Doom and Magneto — have become cultural icons in their own right. Recurring features include the secret lair, henchmen, and the tendency to explain their plans to a captured hero.

History

The archetype developed alongside the superhero in the Golden Age of Comic Books of the late 1930s and 1940s, with figures such as Lex Luthor (1940) and the Joker (1940), and expanded during the Silver Age with the Marvel Comics villains created by Stan Lee, Jack Kirby and Steve Ditko. Precursors exist in earlier literature, including Professor Moriarty in the Sherlock Holmes stories and the mad scientists of pulp fiction.

Related concepts

The term is sometimes applied loosely to real-world criminals or to antagonists in other genres. The anti-villain is a variant with heroic qualities or sympathetic goals pursued by villainous means.
