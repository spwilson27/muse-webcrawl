Cairngorms

Cairngorms
Location: Scottish Highlands
Highest point: Ben Macdui (1,309 m)
Named for: Cairn Gorm

The Cairngorms are a mountain range in the eastern Highlands of Scotland, closely associated with the mountain Cairn Gorm. They form the core of the Cairngorms National Park, the largest national park in the United Kingdom.

Geography

The range is a great granite plateau, deeply dissected by glacial valleys, and contains five of the six highest mountains in Scotland, including Ben Macdui (1,309 m), Braeriach, Cairn Toul, Sgor an Lochain Uaine and Cairn Gorm itself (1,245 m). The high plateau is the most extensive area of arctic-alpine ground in Britain, holding snow patches that sometimes persist all summer.

Climate and nature

The Cairngorms are famous for severe weather and record British wind speeds. The ancient Caledonian pinewoods of the foothills — as at Rothiemurchus and Abernethy — shelter red squirrels, Scottish crossbills, capercaillie and wildcats, while the plateau supports ptarmigan, snow buntings and mountain hares.

Recreation

The Cairngorm Mountain ski area above Aviemore was Scotland's first major snowsports centre, served by the Cairngorm Mountain Railway funicular. The range is also popular with hillwalkers, climbers and cross-country skiers; the Lairig Ghru pass cuts through its heart.
