The Type 22 frigate, also known as the Broadsword class, was a class of fourteen frigates built for the Royal Navy in three batches during the 1970s and 1980s. Designed primarily as anti-submarine warfare platforms for the North Atlantic, they were large and comfortable ships — sometimes nicknamed "the Broadsword class" or wryly called "frigates" despite approaching destroyer size — and became a familiar part of the Royal Navy's fleet from their entry into service in 1979 until the last was withdrawn in 2011.

Design and batches

The Type 22 was designed around the Sea Wolf point-defence missile system and towed-array sonar, carrying a Lynx helicopter and modern command systems, but originally lacked a medium-calibre gun — an economy decision reversed after the Falklands War. Batch 1 (four ships, headed by HMS Broadsword) displaced about 4,500 tonnes. Batch 2 (six ships, headed by HMS Boxer) was lengthened, with improved sensors. Batch 3 (four ships, headed by HMS Cornwall) added a 114 mm (4.5-inch) gun, Goalkeeper CIWS, and Harpoon-class anti-ship capability (Exocet in RN service), making them general-purpose combatants of about 5,300 tonnes.

Service

Type 22s served prominently in the Falklands War of 1982: HMS Broadsword and HMS Brilliant pioneered Sea Wolf in combat, the famous "Type 64" pairing with Type 42 destroyers. Later ships of the class performed worldwide patrol, embargo enforcement, and counter-narcotics duties. Under defence cuts the class was retired early, the last four Batch 3 ships leaving service in 2011 under the 2010 Strategic Defence and Security Review. Surplus ships were sold abroad: Batch 1 and 2 vessels went to Brazil (as the Greenhalgh class), Romania (as the Regele Ferdinand class), and Chile (as the Almirante Williams).
