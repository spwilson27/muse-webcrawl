District Collector

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A District Collector, also known as District Magistrate or Deputy Commissioner, is the chief administrative and revenue officer of a district in India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh. The office, which dates to British rule, remains the linchpin of district administration: the collector heads the revenue administration, exercises magisterial powers for the maintenance of law and order, and coordinates the work of the development departments in the district.

History

The office was created by Warren Hastings in 1772 in Bengal, initially to collect land revenue and administer justice. Over the colonial period the revenue and magisterial functions were repeatedly combined and separated, but the collector emerged as the head of the district. The title "Deputy Commissioner" is used in some states and in Pakistan and Bangladesh, while "Collector" and "District Magistrate" emphasise the revenue and judicial roles respectively; in most of India the three titles refer to the same officer.

Functions

  • Revenue: assessment and collection of land revenue, maintenance of land records, and relief during calamities.
  • Magisterial: preventive powers under criminal procedure law, licensing, and overall responsibility for law and order alongside the district police chief.
  • Development: coordination and supervision of government welfare and development programmes in the district.
  • Elections: conduct of parliamentary and assembly elections in the district as district election officer.
  • Disaster management: chairing the district disaster management authority and directing relief operations.

The post is normally held by an officer of the Indian Administrative Service (IAS) in India, or the corresponding administrative services in Pakistan and Bangladesh.
