Improvisation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A jazz quartet improvising in a club, the saxophonist mid-phrase
A jazz quartet improvising in a club, the saxophonist mid-phrase

Improvisation is the activity of making or doing something not planned beforehand, using whatever resources are available — in the performing arts, the creation of music, theatre or dance in the moment of performance. Found in traditions worldwide (jazz, Indian raga, Baroque ornamentation, commedia dell'arte, freestyle rap), improvisation balances freedom with deep internalised structure: scales, tala, status games and 'yes, and' rules.

Music

Much of the world's music is improvised within frameworks: the jazz solo over changes, the alap of a raga, maqam taqsim, blues and gospel practice; Western classical music once prized it (cadenzas, figured bass, organ improvisation — Bach and Handel were famous improvisers) before the score-bound nineteenth century demoted it. Modern neuroscience studies of jazz improvisers show characteristic brain states — reduced self-monitoring with heightened self-expression circuits.

Theatre and beyond

Commedia dell'arte scenarios, Viola Spolin's theatre games, Keith Johnstone's Impro, and the Chicago tradition (Second City, long-form improv) built an art of spontaneous narrative now taught to executives and therapists. Improvisation theory — preparedness meeting indeterminacy — informs fields from emergency medicine to software ('bricolage'), and Turkle's and Schön's accounts of reflective practice.
