E major (or the key of E) is a major scale based on E, consisting of the pitches E, F♯, G♯, A, B, C♯ and D♯. Its key signature has four sharps. Its relative minor is C-sharp minor and its parallel minor is E minor.

Characteristics and usage

E major is a particularly resonant key for the guitar, since in standard tuning both the lowest and highest open strings sound E, and the other open strings (A, B) also belong to the scale; it is therefore one of the most common keys in rock and blues guitar music. For string orchestras it is less convenient than sharp-side keys such as D and A major, but it appears frequently in Romantic music. Well-known works in E major include Mendelssohn's overture to A Midsummer Night's Dream, the Spring movement of Vivaldi's The Four Seasons, and the slow movements of several of Beethoven's and Schubert's works. Composers have often described the key as bright, clear or triumphant.
