Buridan's ass

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Buridan's ass is an illustration of a paradox in philosophy in the conception of free will. It refers to a hypothetical situation wherein an ass (donkey) that is equally hungry and thirsty is placed precisely midway between a stack of hay and a pail of water. Since the paradox assumes the donkey will always go to whichever is closer, it dies of both hunger and thirst since it cannot make any rational decision between the hay and water.

The paradox is named after the 14th-century French philosopher Jean Buridan, whose philosophy of moral determinism it satirises. Although the illustration is named after Buridan, philosophers have discussed the concept before him, notably Aristotle in On the Heavens and the Persian philosopher al-Ghazali.

History

A version of the paradox appears in Aristotle's De Caelo, where he asks how a dog faced with two equally tempting meals could rationally choose between them. Buridan's writings discuss the question of a will suspended between two equal goods; subsequent writers popularised the donkey version, sometimes attributing it to Buridan himself, though it does not appear in his extant works.

Discussion

The paradox highlights the difficulty faced by a purely deterministic account of choice: without some asymmetry or random tie-breaker, an agent with exactly balanced motivations would be paralysed. Some commentators argue that the example shows the necessity of free will, while others hold that rationality itself provides grounds for choosing arbitrarily between equals without a reason to prefer one.
