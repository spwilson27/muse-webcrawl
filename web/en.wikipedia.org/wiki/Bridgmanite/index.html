Bridgmanite is a magnesium-rich silicate mineral with the perovskite crystal structure and formula (Mg,Fe)SiO3. Although it cannot be observed directly at Earth's surface under stable conditions, it is believed to be the most abundant mineral on Earth, making up roughly 38% of the planet's volume, concentrated in the lower mantle between about 660 and 2,700 km depth.

History

The high-pressure phase was first synthesised in laboratories in the 1970s and long known informally as "magnesium silicate perovskite". Because mineral names require a naturally occurring specimen, it was only formally named in 2014, when researchers characterised grains in the shocked Tenham meteorite — where impact pressures had created and preserved the phase — and named it for Percy Williams Bridgman, the Nobel laureate who pioneered high-pressure physics.

Properties and significance

Bridgmanite forms when olivine-derived minerals transform under the extreme pressures (about 24 GPa and above) at the top of the lower mantle, accompanied by ferropericlase. Its density, elasticity and ability to host iron, aluminium and even water-related defects govern how seismic waves travel through the deep Earth, how the mantle convects, and how heat escapes the core. Near the base of the mantle it may transform further into "post-perovskite".
