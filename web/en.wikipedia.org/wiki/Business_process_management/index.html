Business process management

A business process modelled in BPMN, the standard notation used in BPM

Business process management (BPM) is the discipline in which people use various methods to discover, model, analyze, measure, improve, optimize, and automate business processes. Any combination of methods used to manage a company's business processes is BPM. Processes can be structured and repeatable or unstructured and variable. Although not required, enabling technologies are often used with BPM.

The BPM lifecycle

BPM is typically described as a continuous lifecycle of activities: design, in which existing processes are identified and represented; modelling, in which the process is captured in a notation such as BPMN and analysed, often with simulation; execution, in which the process is enacted manually or through a process engine; monitoring, in which performance is tracked against indicators; and optimization, in which improvements are identified and fed back into the design.

History and approaches

The discipline's roots reach back to Frederick Taylor's scientific management, statistical quality control, and the business process re-engineering movement of the early 1990s associated with Michael Hammer and James Champy. Later waves included Six Sigma, lean management, and workflow automation. Modern practice distinguishes operational BPM, centred on documented processes, from approaches such as case management and robotic process automation that handle less structured work.

Technology

Business process management systems (BPMS) provide process modelling tools, execution engines, rules engines, and monitoring dashboards. Process mining tools can automatically discover process models from event logs in enterprise systems, and are increasingly combined with task mining and automation platforms.
