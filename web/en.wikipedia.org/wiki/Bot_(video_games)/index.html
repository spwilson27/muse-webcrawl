Bot (video games)

TypeComputer-controlled game character
PurposeSimulate human opponents or teammates
Common genresFirst-person shooters, real-time strategy, MOBAs

In video games, a bot is a type of artificial intelligence–based expert system software that plays a video game in the place of a human. Bots fill out matches when human players are unavailable, provide practice opponents, and can play supportive roles in cooperative and multiplayer games.

Usage

Bots are most prominent in multiplayer first-person shooters such as Counter-Strike and the Unreal Tournament series, where they populate servers and allow solo practice. In strategy games and MOBAs, bots serve as tutorial and low-difficulty opponents. Massively multiplayer games have grappled with unauthorized third-party bots used to farm resources and gold, which developers treat as cheating.

Design

Game bots typically rely on navigation meshes to move around maps, finite-state machines or behavior trees for decision-making, and parameterized aim and reaction skills to simulate human performance. Because a bot with unrestricted access to game state could aim perfectly, designers deliberately restrict its perception and add human-like error so play feels fair.

Distinction from NPCs

Whereas non-player characters (NPCs) are designed as part of a game's world and rules, bots specifically emulate the role of a human player within the game's multiplayer framework, joining matches, choosing equipment, and following team objectives.
