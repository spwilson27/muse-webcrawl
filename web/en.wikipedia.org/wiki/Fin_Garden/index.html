Fin Garden (Persian: باغ فین, Bagh-e Fin) is a historical Persian garden located in Kashan, Iran. It is considered one of the finest and oldest surviving examples of the classical Persian garden, embodying the chahar bagh layout of four-part water channels symbolising the elements and Zoroastrian sky, water, earth and plants.

History

The garden's origins may reach back to the 7th century, fed by the Soleymaniyeh spring; its surviving design dates mainly to the Safavid period under Shah Abbas I, with extensive rebuilding in the Qajar era under Fath-Ali Shah, who added pavilions and painted ceilings. The garden is famous as the site of the Fin Bath (Hammam-e Fin), where in 1852 the reformist Qajar chancellor Amir Kabir was murdered on the orders of Naser al-Din Shah — an event commemorated in Iran to this day.

Layout

Water from the spring is distributed by gravity through turquoise-tiled channels, pools and fountains throughout the 2.3-hectare walled garden, with cypress and fruit trees providing shade. Buildings include the central Kushak pavilion, the Qajar-era Shotor Galu pavilion, a museum, and the two bathhouses. In 2011 UNESCO inscribed Fin Garden on the World Heritage List as one of nine representative Persian gardens.
