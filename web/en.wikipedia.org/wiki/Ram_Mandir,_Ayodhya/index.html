Ram Mandir, Ayodhya

The Ram Mandir (lit. "Rama Temple") is a Hindu temple complex in Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh, India. Many Hindus believe it is located at the site of Ram Janmabhoomi, the legendary birthplace of Rama, a principal deity of Hinduism. The temple was inaugurated on 22 January 2024 after a pran pratishtha (consecration) ceremony led by Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

Background

The site was occupied from 1528 by the Babri Masjid, a mosque built under the Mughal emperor Babur. Hindu groups claimed the mosque stood on the ruins of an earlier temple marking Rama's birthplace. The dispute became one of India's most contentious religious and political issues, culminating in the demolition of the mosque by Hindu activists (kar sevaks) on 6 December 1992, an event followed by widespread rioting. Excavations by the Archaeological Survey of India reported evidence of a large non-Islamic structure beneath the mosque, a finding debated by scholars.

Supreme Court verdict and construction

On 9 November 2019, a five-judge bench of the Supreme Court of India awarded the disputed land for the construction of a temple, while directing that five acres elsewhere in Ayodhya be allotted for a mosque. The Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Teerth Kshetra trust was formed to oversee construction. A ground-breaking ceremony was held on 5 August 2020. The temple was designed by the Sompura family of temple architects in the Nagara style, built principally of pink sandstone without iron or steel, using traditional techniques. Larsen & Toubro oversaw construction, and the idol of Ram Lalla (the five-year-old Rama) was sculpted by Arun Yogiraj from black stone.

Architecture

The main temple measures about 110 metres long, 72 metres wide and 49 metres high, with three storeys, five mandapas (halls) and a main shikhara over the sanctum. The complex, planned over roughly 28 hectares (70 acres), includes subsidiary shrines, a museum and facilities for the millions of pilgrims expected annually.
