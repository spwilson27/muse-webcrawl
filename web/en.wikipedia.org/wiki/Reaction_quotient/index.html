Reaction quotient

In chemical thermodynamics, the reaction quotient (Q) is a quantity that expresses the relative amounts of products and reactants present in a reacting mixture at a particular point in time. Comparing Q with the equilibrium constant K indicates the direction in which a reaction will proceed to reach equilibrium.

Definition

For a general reaction aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, the reaction quotient is:

Q = ([C]c [D]d) / ([A]a [B]b)

where the terms are activities (often approximated by concentrations or partial pressures) raised to their stoichiometric coefficients. Unlike K, which is defined only at equilibrium, Q can be evaluated for any composition of the mixture.

Relation to equilibrium

If Q < K, the forward reaction predominates and products form; if Q > K, the reverse reaction predominates; if Q = K, the system is at equilibrium. The connection appears in the equation for Gibbs free energy: ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q, so the sign of ΔG follows from the comparison of Q and K. The reaction quotient is widely used in equilibrium calculations, biochemistry, and electrochemistry (via the Nernst equation).
