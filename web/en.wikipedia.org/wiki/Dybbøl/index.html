Dybbøl

CountryDenmark
RegionSouth Denmark
MunicipalitySønderborg

Dybbøl (German: Düppel) is a small town in the municipality of Sønderborg in Region of Southern Denmark, on the Jutland peninsula in southern Denmark. It is located on a hill west of Sønderborg, overlooking the Als Sound. The town gives its name to the lines of fortifications that played a central part in both Schleswig Wars.

Battle of Dybbøl

Dybbøl is best known for the Battle of Dybbøl on 18 April 1864, during the Second Schleswig War. After a long siege and a massive artillery bombardment, Prussian forces stormed the Danish entrenchments and inflicted a decisive defeat; Denmark subsequently lost the duchies of Schleswig, Holstein and Lauenburg. The defeat remains a national trauma in Danish history, and the white Dybbøl Mill, repeatedly destroyed and rebuilt, has become a Danish national symbol.

An earlier engagement, the Battle of Dybbøl in 1848 during the First Schleswig War, had ended in Danish victory.

Present day

The battlefield is preserved as a historic landscape, with reconstructed trenches and redoubts, the Historiecenter Dybbøl Banke museum, and an annual commemoration on 18 April. The area formed part of Germany between 1864 and 1920, when the Schleswig plebiscites returned North Schleswig, including Dybbøl, to Denmark.
