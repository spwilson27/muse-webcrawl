Photofinishing

Photofinishing refers to the processing of photographic films and the making of prints from negatives or digital files, whether carried out commercially by laboratories or by consumers themselves. In the film era, photofinishing was a major consumer industry: customers handed in exposed film at shops, pharmacies, or by mail, and received back negatives and a set of prints days later — a timescale later compressed by "one-hour photo" services.

Process

For colour negative film, the standard process is C-41 development, followed by printing onto colour photographic paper (RA-4 process) using an automated printer that analyses each negative for density and colour balance. Black-and-white and slide (E-6) films use their own chemistries. From the 1980s, compact "minilabs" brought full processing into retail shops, enabling one-hour turnaround. Modern labs often work from digital files, exposing photographic paper with lasers or printing by inkjet, and also produce enlargements, photo books, canvas prints, and gifts.

Industry history

The separation of picture-taking from processing was popularised by Kodak's 1888 slogan "You press the button, we do the rest", which made photography accessible to amateurs. Through the twentieth century, photofinishing ranged from central wholesale labs serving whole regions to independent local labs. The shift to digital photography in the 2000s drastically reduced film volumes, but photofinishing businesses adapted to printing from digital media and personalised photo products, while film processing survives as a specialist and enthusiast service.
