Vienna Literary Agreement

The Vienna Literary Agreement (Serbo-Croatian: Bečki književni dogovor) was a document signed in March 1850 in Vienna by prominent Serbian and Croatian writers and linguists, establishing the foundations of a common literary language for Serbs and Croats. The agreement is considered a landmark in the history of the Serbo-Croatian language.

Signatories and provisions

The principal figures were the Serbian language reformer Vuk Stefanović Karadžić, his close associate Đuro Daničić, and the Croatian Slovene linguist Franc Miklošič, along with Croatian writers including Ivan Mažuranić, Dimitrija Demeter, and others. The signatories agreed that a single literary language should be based on the Shtokavian dialect — chosen because it was the most widely spoken — and that writers should use the speech of the "southern" (Herzegovinian) variety as a model. They also settled rules of orthography, urging that letters be written as they are pronounced and that the letter h be written where it belongs etymologically.

Significance

The agreement aligned Croatian literary practice with Karadžić's earlier reforms of Serbian and laid the groundwork for the later Novi Sad Agreement of 1954 and the unified Serbo-Croatian standard used in Yugoslavia. After the breakup of Yugoslavia in the 1990s, the successor states developed separate Serbian, Croatian, Bosnian, and Montenegrin standards, though the common Shtokavian base remains recognized by linguists.
