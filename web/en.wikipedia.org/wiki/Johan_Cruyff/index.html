Hendrik Johannes "Johan" Cruyff (April 25, 1947 – March 24, 2016) was a Dutch professional football player and manager. Regarded as one of the greatest players of all time, he won the Ballon d'Or three times, in 1971, 1973, and 1974. Cruyff was the most famous exponent of the football philosophy known as Total Football, explored by Rinus Michels, and is widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in modern football.

Playing career

Cruyff came through the Ajax academy and led the club to three consecutive European Cups (1971–1973) and six Eredivisie titles in his first spell. In 1973 he transferred to Barcelona for a world-record fee, winning La Liga in his first season. He later played in the North American Soccer League and in Spain for Levante before returning to Ajax and finishing his career at Feyenoord, where he won a league and cup double in 1984. For the Netherlands he earned 48 caps and captained the side that reached the 1974 World Cup final; his name is attached to the "Cruyff turn," a famous feint he popularized at that tournament.

Managerial career and legacy

As a manager, Cruyff led Ajax to the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1987 and then, at Barcelona (1988–1996), built the famed "Dream Team," winning four consecutive La Liga titles and the club's first European Cup in 1992. His insistence on attacking, possession-based football and on youth development at La Masia profoundly shaped Barcelona and modern football coaching. The Johan Cruyff Institute, the Cruyff Foundation, and Ajax's renamed Johan Cruijff ArenA perpetuate his legacy.
