Regularization (mathematics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, statistics and machine learning, regularization is the process of adding information or a penalty term to a problem in order to prevent overfitting or to solve an ill-posed problem. A model is trained not only to fit the data but also to keep its parameters small or smooth, trading a little bias for much lower variance and better generalisation.

Common forms

  • Tikhonov regularization / ridge regression (L2): penalises the sum of squares of the coefficients, shrinking them towards zero.
  • Lasso (L1): penalises the sum of absolute values, driving many coefficients exactly to zero and so performing feature selection.
  • Elastic net: combines L1 and L2 penalties.

The strength of the penalty is set by a hyperparameter (often written λ), typically chosen by cross-validation. In Bayesian terms a regularizer corresponds to a prior over parameters — an L2 penalty, for example, is a Gaussian prior. Related techniques in deep learning include weight decay, dropout and early stopping.

Ill-posed problems

The idea grew out of work by Andrey Tikhonov on solving integral equations whose naive solutions are unstable: adding a stabilising functional makes the problem well-posed and the solution robust to noise.
