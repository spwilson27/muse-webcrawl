Waterloo Medal

The Waterloo Medal was a British campaign medal awarded to soldiers who participated in the Battle of Waterloo on 18 June 1815, as well as the preceding engagements at Ligny and Quatre Bras. It was the first campaign medal issued by the British government and the first to be awarded to all ranks present at a battle, including the next of kin of those killed.

History

The medal was instituted by order of the Prince Regent in 1816. Unlike earlier honours such as the Military General Service Medal, which came decades later, the Waterloo Medal was awarded promptly to the roughly 37,000 men of Wellington's army. Each medal was impressed with the recipient's name, rank and regiment around the edge — a notable innovation at the time.

Description

The medal is struck in silver and measures about 37 millimetres in diameter. The obverse bears the laureate head of the Prince Regent with the inscription "GEORGE P. REGENT." The reverse, designed by Benedetto Pistrucci, depicts the winged figure of Victory above a plaque inscribed "WATERLOO" with the date "JUNE 18 1815" below. It is suspended from a crimson ribbon with dark blue edges, matching the ribbon of the Military General Service Medal.

Significance

Because it was awarded to every participant regardless of rank, the Waterloo Medal is often regarded as the first truly universal British campaign medal and set the pattern for later issues. Named examples to famous regiments or casualties remain highly collectible.
