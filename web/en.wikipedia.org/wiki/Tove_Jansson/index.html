Tove Marika Jansson (9 August 1914 – 27 June 2001) was a Swedish-speaking Finnish author, novelist, painter, illustrator, and comic strip author. She is best known as the creator of the Moomins, the gentle troll family of Moominvalley, whose books, picture books, and comic strips have been translated into more than fifty languages and adapted for television, film, and stage around the world.

Life and career

Jansson grew up in an artistic family in Helsinki — her father Viktor was a sculptor and her mother Signe Hammarsten-Jansson a graphic designer and illustrator — and studied art in Stockholm, Helsinki, and Paris. She began publishing the Moomin stories during and after the Second World War: the first book, The Moomins and the Great Flood, appeared in 1945, followed by Comet in Moominland (1946) and Finn Family Moomintroll (1948), which brought international success. A daily Moomin comic strip ran in the London Evening News from 1954, later continued by her brother Lars Jansson.

Adult fiction and later life

From the 1970s Jansson turned largely to writing for adults, producing the much-loved novel The Summer Book (1972), about a grandmother and granddaughter spending a summer on a small island in the Gulf of Finland, as well as Sun City, The True Deceiver, and collections such as Letters from Klara. She spent summers with her life partner, the graphic artist Tuulikki Pietilä, on the tiny island of Klovharun in the Pellinki archipelago — the model for the island world of her fiction. Jansson received the Hans Christian Andersen Award in 1966 and remains one of Finland's most celebrated cultural figures.
