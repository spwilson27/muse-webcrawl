First Battle of Panipat

First Battle of Panipat
Date: 21 April 1526
Location: Panipat (modern Haryana, India)
Combatants: Babur's Timurid forces vs Delhi Sultanate (Ibrahim Lodi)
Result: Decisive Mughal victory; foundation of the Mughal Empire
[Image: Babur's army with field artillery and carts drawn up at Panipat, Mughal miniature style]

The First Battle of Panipat, fought on 21 April 1526, was a decisive battle between the invading forces of Babur, the Timurid ruler of Kabul, and Ibrahim Lodi, Sultan of Delhi. Babur's victory destroyed the Delhi Sultanate and laid the foundation of the Mughal Empire in India; it is notable as one of the earliest battles in India involving gunpowder firearms and field artillery.

Background

Babur, a descendant of Timur and Genghis Khan who had lost his ancestral Ferghana and Samarkand, had established himself at Kabul and long coveted Hindustan. Invited by disaffected Lodi nobles, he invaded with a small, disciplined army — perhaps 12,000–25,000 men — against Lodi's far larger host, which chroniclers put at 40,000–100,000 with numerous elephants.

The battle

At Panipat, about 90 km north of Delhi, Babur lashed hundreds of carts together as a defensive laager, set matchlockmen and some twenty field guns under Ustad Ali Quli in the gaps, and placed cavalry on the flanks to execute the tulughma envelopment — wheeling around the enemy's wings. Lodi's army advanced on 21 April and was compressed against the cart line under artillery and musket fire; Babur's horsemen closed the trap. By midday the Sultan's army was annihilated; Ibrahim Lodi and some 15,000–20,000 men lay dead on the field.

Aftermath

Babur occupied Delhi and Agra and was proclaimed emperor, founding a dynasty that would rule much of India for three centuries. Panipat's plain saw two further epochal battles — in 1556, when Akbar's forces secured Mughal rule, and 1761, when Ahmad Shah Durrani shattered the Marathas.
