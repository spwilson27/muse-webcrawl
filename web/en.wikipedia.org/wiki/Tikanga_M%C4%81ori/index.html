Tikanga Māori

Tikanga Māori is a Māori concept incorporating practices and values, customs, and protocol within Māori culture. It is often translated as "the Māori way" or "correct procedure", and is derived from the word tika, meaning right or correct. Tikanga encompasses moral judgements about appropriate ways of behaving, informed by concepts such as tapu (sacred restriction), mana (prestige and authority), noa (ordinary, unrestricted), and whakapapa (genealogy).

Practice

Tikanga varies between iwi (tribes) and hapū (subtribes), and is passed down through generations. It governs occasions such as pōwhiri (welcomes), tangihanga (funerals), weddings, and the conduct of visitors on a marae, including who may speak, where people sit, and the roles of men and women in ceremony.

In New Zealand law

Tikanga has increasing recognition in New Zealand's legal system. Statutes such as the Resource Management Act 1991 required decision-makers to take tikanga into account, and landmark court decisions, including the Supreme Court's 2022 judgment in the Takamore v Clarke litigation and the Peter Hugh McGregor Ellis case, have treated tikanga as part of the values of the common law of New Zealand.
