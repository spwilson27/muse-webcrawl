Tehri Garhwal is a district in the Indian state of Uttarakhand, in the Garhwal Himalaya. Its administrative headquarters is New Tehri, a planned town built to replace the old town of Tehri, which was submerged by the reservoir of the Tehri Dam. The district takes its name from "Trihari", signifying a place that washes away the three types of sins — of thought, word and deed (Mansa, Vacha and Karmana).

History

The area formed the heart of the princely state of Tehri Garhwal, ruled by the Panwar (Shah) dynasty of the Garhwal Kingdom from the town of Tehri after the kingdom's capital moved in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. After the Anglo-Nepalese War of 1814–16, the western part of Garhwal was restored to Sudarshan Shah as the Tehri state. The last Maharaja, Manabendra Shah, acceded to India in 1949, and the state became a district of Uttar Pradesh before Uttarakhand was formed in 2000.

Geography

The district spans the valleys of the Bhagirathi and Bhilangna rivers, which join at Tehri, and rises to high Himalayan ridges in the north bordering Uttarkashi. The Tehri Dam, one of the tallest rock-fill dams in the world at 260.5 metres, impounds a large reservoir used for hydroelectricity, irrigation and drinking water supply to Delhi. Important towns include Narendra Nagar, Devprayag (where the Bhagirathi meets the Alaknanda to form the Ganges), Chamba and Ghansali.

Economy and culture

Agriculture on terraced hillsides, horticulture, tourism and remittances sustain the largely rural population; the district is predominantly Garhwali-speaking and Hindu, with temples such as those at Devprayag drawing pilgrims on the Char Dham routes.
