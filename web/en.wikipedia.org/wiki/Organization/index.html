Organization

Organization

Studied in: Organization theory, management, sociology

Examples: Companies, governments, NGOs, armies, schools

An organization (or organisation in British English) is an entity — such as a company, an institution, a government agency, or an association — comprising one or more people and having a particular purpose. Organizations are social systems: structured groups in which tasks are divided, coordinated and controlled to achieve collective goals that individuals could not achieve alone. The word derives from the Greek organon, meaning a tool or instrument.

Characteristics

Definitions vary, but organizations typically display common features: a purpose or set of goals; a membership with defined roles; a division of labour; rules and procedures coordinating members' activities; and continuity beyond the participation of any single individual. Organizations may be formal, with written constitutions, legal personality and bureaucracy, or informal, resting on networks of relationships and shared norms.

Structures

Organizational structures describe how authority and work are allocated. Common forms include:

  • Hierarchy (bureaucracy) — a chain of command with specialised offices, classically analysed by Max Weber.
  • Functional structure — grouping by specialism such as marketing, finance and production.
  • Divisional structure — semi-autonomous units organised by product, market or geography.
  • Matrix structure — staff report to both functional and product managers.
  • Flat and network structures — minimal hierarchy, with coordination through teams and projects.

Study of organizations

Organization theory, drawing on sociology, psychology, economics and management science, examines how organizations are designed, how they behave, and how they interact with their environments. Early 20th-century approaches such as Frederick Taylor's scientific management and Henri Fayol's administrative theory emphasised rational design; later schools — human relations, contingency theory, institutional theory, and organizational ecology — stressed people, context and adaptation. Leadership, culture, incentives and decision-making remain central topics.
