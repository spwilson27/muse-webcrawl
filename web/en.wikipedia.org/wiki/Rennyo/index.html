Rennyo

Rennyo (蓮如)
Born1415, Kyoto, Japan
Died1499 (aged 84)
SchoolJōdo Shinshū
Title8th Monshu (head priest) of Hongan-ji

Rennyo (蓮如, 1415–1499) was the eighth Monshu (head priest) of the Hongan-ji temple of the Jōdo Shinshū sect of Japanese Buddhism, and the descendant credited with reviving the sect and making it one of the largest and most influential in medieval Japan. A descendant of the founder Shinran, Rennyo took over a small and declining temple and, through preaching tours, letters to followers (the Ofumi), and organisational reforms, built a mass movement of peasant and provincial followers (monto).

Life

Rennyo became head of Hongan-ji in 1457. Persecuted by the Tendai monks of Enryaku-ji, who burned Hongan-ji in 1465, he fled and rebuilt the temple at Yamashina in Kyoto, then later established a new centre at Ishiyama in Osaka — the fortress-temple Ishiyama Hongan-ji — and retired to it in his later years.

Legacy

Rennyo's followers, organised in congregations, periodically rose in the Ikkō-ikki revolts, which came to control Kaga Province — the only province in feudal Japan ruled by peasants. After his death, his many sons divided the leadership, leading eventually to the split of Hongan-ji into eastern (Higashi) and western (Nishi) branches. Rennyo is also credited with composing the Shōshinge liturgy and popularising the phrase "Namu Amida Butsu" in daily devotion.
