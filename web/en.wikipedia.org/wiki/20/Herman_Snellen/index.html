Herman Snellen (February 19, 1834 – January 18, 1908) was a Dutch ophthalmologist who in 1862 introduced the Snellen chart, the standard eye chart used worldwide to measure visual acuity. He also defined visual acuity as a fraction — the ratio between the distance at which a patient reads a line of letters and the distance at which a person with standard vision can read the same line.

Life and career

Snellen studied medicine at Utrecht University and became a pupil of the great Dutch physiologist and ophthalmologist Franciscus Cornelis Donders. In 1862 he published his optotype chart, calibrated so that each letter subtends five minutes of arc with stroke widths of one minute of arc, allowing comparable measurements across clinics. Snellen succeeded Donders as professor of ophthalmology at Utrecht and directed the Netherlands Hospital for Eye Patients.

Legacy

The Snellen fraction — expressed as 20/20 in the United States or 6/6 in metric countries — remains the commonest clinical expression of distance visual acuity, and Snellen's design principles underlie modern charts such as the logMAR and ETDRS charts. Snellen also worked on astigmatism, glaucoma surgery, and the correction of refractive errors.
