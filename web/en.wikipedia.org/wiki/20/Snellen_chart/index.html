Snellen chart

A Snellen chart is an eye chart used by eye care professionals to measure visual acuity. It is named after the Dutch ophthalmologist Herman Snellen, who developed it in 1862. The chart displays rows of letters, called optotypes, in decreasing size, which the patient reads from a standard distance, typically 20 feet (6 metres).

Snellen fractions

Results are expressed as a Snellen fraction such as 20/20 (6/6 in metric notation). The first number is the test distance; the second is the distance at which a person with normal vision can read the same line. Thus 20/20 vision is considered normal, while 20/40 means the patient reads at 20 feet what a normal eye reads at 40 feet. The letters on the chart are designed so that, at the appropriate distance, each letter subtends an angle of five minutes of arc.

Variations

Variations include the tumbling E chart for patients who cannot read, picture charts for children, and the LogMAR chart, which uses equal logarithmic size steps and is favoured in research. The Snellen chart nonetheless remains the most widely recognised symbol of vision testing.
