Visual acuity

Visual acuity (VA) is the sharpness or clarity of vision, measured by the ability to distinguish shapes and details of objects at a given distance. It depends on the optical properties of the eye, the health and density of photoreceptors in the retina, and the processing of the visual pathways in the brain.

Measurement

Visual acuity is commonly measured with an eye chart such as the Snellen chart and expressed as a fraction (for example 20/20) or in decimal and LogMAR notation. It quantifies the smallest visual angle at which details can be resolved; normal acuity corresponds to resolving features of about one minute of arc. Acuity is usually tested one eye at a time, with and without corrective lenses.

Factors and disorders

Refractive errors such as myopia, hyperopia, and astigmatism are the most common causes of reduced acuity and are corrected with glasses, contact lenses, or surgery. Other causes include cataracts, macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, and neurological disorders. "20/20 vision" is a term for normal acuity at a distance, but acuity is only one component of overall visual function, which also includes contrast sensitivity, colour vision, and peripheral vision.
