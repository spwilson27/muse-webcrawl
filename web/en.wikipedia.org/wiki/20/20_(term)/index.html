The term 20/20 refers to a measure of visual acuity, the sharpness or clarity of vision at a distance. A person is said to have 20/20 vision if they can clearly see at 20 feet what a person with normal vision can see at 20 feet. In countries using the metric system the equivalent expression is 6/6 (six metres).

The Snellen fraction

The notation derives from the Snellen chart developed by Dutch ophthalmologist Herman Snellen in 1862. The fraction compares the test distance (numerator) with the distance at which a "normal" eye can read the same line of letters (denominator). Thus 20/40 vision means the patient must stand at 20 feet to read letters that a normal eye can read at 40 feet — worse than average acuity — while 20/15 is sharper than the standard.

"Perfect" vision

Contrary to popular belief, 20/20 is not perfect vision; it is simply the reference standard for normal acuity. Many young adults can achieve 20/15 or better, and the theoretical limit of the human eye is around 20/8. Visual acuity also says nothing about other aspects of sight such as peripheral vision, depth perception, colour vision or focusing at near distances (presbyopia).

Hindsight idiom

Because 20/20 denotes clear sight, English uses "20/20 hindsight" idiomatically to describe the clarity with which past events seem obvious after the fact — the basis of the psychological phenomenon known as hindsight bias.
