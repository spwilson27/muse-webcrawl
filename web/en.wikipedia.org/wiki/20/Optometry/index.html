Optometry

FocusVision care, refraction, eyeglasses and contact lenses, detection of eye disease
PractitionerOptometrist (OD)
Key toolsPhoropter, retinoscope, autorefractor, visual field analyser

Optometry is a health-care profession concerned with the eyes and vision. Optometrists are primary eye-care practitioners who examine eyes for refractive errors such as myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism and presbyopia; prescribe and dispense corrective lenses; and detect signs of eye disease and systemic conditions, referring patients to ophthalmologists or other physicians when medical or surgical treatment is needed.

Practice

A typical eye examination measures visual acuity, determines the refractive correction (refraction, often with a phoropter or retinoscope), assesses binocular vision and eye health, and may include screening for glaucoma, cataracts and retinal disease. In many jurisdictions optometrists can prescribe certain medications, and their scope of practice has steadily expanded. Optometrists also fit contact lenses, provide low-vision rehabilitation, and manage some paediatric and sports-vision care.

History and training

The profession grew out of the work of spectacle makers and the applied-optics movement of the late 19th and early 20th centuries; the term "optometrist" was proposed in 1886, and the first licensing laws were enacted in the United States in 1901 (Minnesota). In the US and Canada, optometrists earn a Doctor of Optometry (OD) degree after a four-year professional programme. In the United Kingdom and many other countries, optometrists (formerly ophthalmic opticians) complete an accredited degree plus pre-registration training, and are regulated distinctly from dispensing opticians.
