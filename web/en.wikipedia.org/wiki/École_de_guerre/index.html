The École de guerre ("School of War") is the senior staff college of the French Armed Forces, located at the École militaire in Paris. It provides advanced joint education to selected senior officers of the French Army, Navy, Air and Space Force, Gendarmerie, and allied nations, preparing them for high command and staff appointments.

History

The school descends from the École supérieure de guerre founded in 1876, after the Franco-Prussian War exposed weaknesses in French staff training. Its interwar teaching was associated with the doctrine of methodical battle. In 1993 the separate service war colleges were merged into the Collège interarmées de défense (CID); the institution took the name École de guerre in 2011 (officially from 23 January 2011, per decree of 2010), reviving the historic title.

Programme

The one-year course admits officers by competitive examination, typically majors and lieutenant colonels with extensive operational experience. Teaching covers strategy, operational planning, joint and combined operations, defence policy, and international relations, with lectures by senior officials, academics and foreign officers. Graduates receive the brevet d'études militaires supérieures. The school works under the Centre des hautes études militaires (CHEM), alongside the Institut des hautes études de défense nationale (IHEDN).

Notable alumni

Alumni of the predecessor schools include marshals Foch, Joffre and Leclerc, and Charles de Gaulle, as well as generations of French chiefs of staff and many foreign senior officers.
