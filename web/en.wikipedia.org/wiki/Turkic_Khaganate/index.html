The Turkic Khaganate, also known as the Göktürk Khaganate, was a khaganate established by the Ashina clan of the Göktürks in 552. It was the first empire to use the name "Turk" as a political identity, and at its height it ruled the Eurasian steppe from Manchuria in the east to the Black Sea in the west, dominating the Silk Road trade between China, Persia, and Byzantium.

Foundation and expansion

The Ashina Türks had been metalworkers — reputedly blacksmiths — under the Rouran Khaganate. In 552 Bumin, leader of the Türks, destroyed the Rouran with aid from Western Wei and founded his own khaganate, taking the title of qaghan. His brother Istämi governed the west as yabghu, expanding Turkic power through Central Asia in alliance with Sasanian Persia against the Hephthalites, and then opening direct diplomatic and commercial relations with the Byzantine Empire — famously in the embassy of Zemarchus in 568 — aimed against Persia.

Division and fall

A civil war in the 580s, aggravated by Sui-dynasty intrigue, split the khaganate into eastern and western halves. The Eastern Khaganate was conquered by Tang China in 630, and the Western Khaganate fell to the Tang in 657; Turkic peoples then lived under Tang rule for half a century. In 682 Ilterish Qaghan restored the empire as the Second Turkic Khaganate, which flourished under Bilge Qaghan and his brother Kül Tigin before collapsing to the Uyghurs in 744.

Legacy

The khaganate left the earliest substantial texts in a Turkic language: the 8th-century Orkhon inscriptions, carved in Old Turkic runiform script for Kül Tigin and Bilge Qaghan, which recount the empire's rise and warn future generations against Chinese soft power. The name "Turk," spread by the khaganate, came to designate the whole family of Turkic peoples and languages across Eurasia.
