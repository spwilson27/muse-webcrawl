Database design

Database design is the organization of data according to a database model. The designer determines what data must be stored and how the data elements interrelate; the design process proceeds from requirements analysis through conceptual and logical modelling to physical implementation, with the goal of producing a database that is consistent, efficient, and free of harmful redundancy.

Process

Design typically begins with a conceptual data model — often an entity–relationship diagram identifying entities, attributes, and relationships — followed by a logical model expressed in the structures of the chosen database system (relations, documents, or graphs), and finally a physical design covering indexes, storage, and partitioning. For relational databases, the logical design is refined by normalization, decomposing tables to eliminate update, insert, and delete anomalies, while deliberate denormalization may be applied for read performance.

Considerations

Good design balances integrity constraints (keys, foreign keys, and check constraints), query performance, security, scalability, and ease of evolution. Poor design leads to redundant data, inconsistency, and difficult maintenance. Design methods and notations include ER modelling, UML class diagrams, and tools ranging from pencil sketches to dedicated CASE software.
