Pauline privilege

The Pauline privilege (Latin: privilegium Paulinum) is a doctrine in the canon law of the Catholic Church permitting the dissolution of a marriage contracted between two unbaptised persons when one of them is later baptised and the other refuses to live peacefully with the convert. It derives from the teaching of Paul the Apostle in the First Epistle to the Corinthians and is distinct from a declaration of nullity (annulment): the Pauline privilege dissolves a valid, though non-sacramental, marriage bond in favour of the faith (in favorem fidei).

Biblical foundation

The doctrine rests on 1 Corinthians 7:12–15, where Paul counsels converts married to unbelievers:

"If any brother has a wife who is an unbeliever, and she consents to live with him, he should not divorce her... But if the unbelieving partner separates, let it be so. In such cases the brother or sister is not enslaved. God has called you to peace."

The concluding words — that the believer "is not bound" (or "not enslaved") — were interpreted by the Church as releasing the baptised party from the natural marriage bond when the unbaptised spouse departs or refuses cohabitation without offence to the faith.

Conditions and procedure

Under the 1983 Code of Canon Law (canons 1143–1147), the privilege applies when: (1) the marriage was contracted by two persons, neither of whom was baptised; (2) one party subsequently receives baptism; and (3) the unbaptised party refuses to cohabit, or to cohabit peacefully without "offence to the Creator" — for example by obstructing the convert's practice of the faith. Before the dissolution takes effect, the unbaptised spouse is normally formally questioned ("interpellated") whether they wish to be baptised or at least to live peacefully with the convert; if the answer is negative or none is given, the local bishop may permit the baptised party to remarry. The first marriage is dissolved at the moment the baptised party validly contracts a new marriage.

Distinctions

The Pauline privilege must be distinguished from the Petrine privilege (or "privilege of the faith" in its broader sense), in which the Pope dissolves certain marriages involving one baptised party in favour of the faith — a power not governed by the strict conditions of 1 Corinthians 7 — and from annulment, which declares that no valid marriage ever existed. The Orthodox churches apply analogous reasoning through the principle of oikonomia, and many Protestant traditions that accept divorce on grounds of desertion by an unbeliever trace the practice to the same Corinthian text.
