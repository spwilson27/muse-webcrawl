Circuit training

Circuit training is a form of body conditioning that involves endurance training, resistance training, high-intensity aerobics, and exercises performed in a circuit, similar to high-intensity interval training. It targets strength building and muscular endurance. An exercise "circuit" is one completion of all set exercises in the program. When one circuit is complete, one begins the first exercise again for the next circuit. Traditionally, the time between exercises in circuit training is short, often with rapid movement to the next exercise.

History

The program was developed by R. E. Morgan and G. T. Anderson in 1953 at the University of Leeds in England. A systematic and progressive circuit training programme was outlined, developed and promoted in the 1973 book Circuit Training by Morgan and Anderson. Circuit training is now widely used in gymnasiums, schools, and military fitness training.

Structure

A typical circuit consists of 6 to 12 exercise stations performed in sequence, each targeting different muscle groups. Exercises commonly include squats, push-ups, lunges, jumping jacks, abdominal exercises, and resistance work with dumbbells or elastic bands. Complete circuits may be repeated several times, with rest intervals kept brief to maintain elevated heart rate and produce both aerobic and anaerobic benefits.
