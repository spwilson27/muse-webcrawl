Pyrocumulonimbus

Pyrocumulonimbus (pyroCb) is a type of cumulonimbus cloud generated by the intense heat of a wildfire, volcanic eruption, or other large fire. Such clouds can produce lightning, strong downdrafts and erratic winds that worsen the fire that created them, and they can inject smoke and aerosols into the upper troposphere and stratosphere.

Formation

When a fire heats air strongly enough, the rising plume may reach the level of free convection and develop into a full thunderstorm. Moisture released by burning vegetation and drawn in from the surrounding atmosphere condenses as the plume rises. PyroCb events are distinguished from the smaller pyrocumulus clouds by reaching thunderstorm intensity and producing lightning.

Effects

Pyrocumulonimbus storms can ignite new fires through lightning strikes and ember transport, generate fire-generated tornadic vortices, and spread smoke over continental distances. The Australian Black Summer fires of 2019–20 produced dozens of pyroCb events that injected smoke into the stratosphere, where it circled the Southern Hemisphere.
