Neutron magnetic moment

The neutron magnetic moment is the intrinsic magnetic dipole moment of the neutron. Although the neutron has no net electric charge, it possesses a magnetic moment of approximately −1.913 nuclear magnetons, a fact that provides strong evidence that the neutron is a composite particle made of charged quarks.

History

The neutron's magnetic moment was measured indirectly in the 1930s and directly by Luis Alvarez and Felix Bloch in 1940. Its nonzero, negative value and its deviation from simple predictions were early clues to subatomic structure, later explained by the quark model, in which the neutron consists of one up quark and two down quarks.

Applications

Because the neutron interacts with magnetic fields and atomic magnetic moments, neutron scattering is a powerful probe of magnetic structure in materials.
