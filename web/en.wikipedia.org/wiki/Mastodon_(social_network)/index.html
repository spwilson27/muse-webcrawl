Mastodon is a free and open-source software platform for running self-hosted social networking services, and the decentralised social network formed by those interconnected servers. Developed by Eugen Rochko beginning in 2016, it offers microblogging features similar to those of Twitter (now X), but instead of a single company-operated service, it consists of thousands of independently run servers ("instances") that interoperate as a federation, collectively known as part of the Fediverse.

Structure and features

Each instance sets its own rules and moderation policies and can interconnect with others using the ActivityPub protocol. Users post short messages (historically called "toots", with a default limit of 500 characters on most instances), follow accounts across instances, and view home, local, and federated timelines. There is no central algorithm ranking content; timelines are chronological.

Growth

Mastodon grew steadily after its launch and saw large surges of new users during controversies at Twitter, most notably after Elon Musk's acquisition of Twitter in late 2022, when monthly active users rose into the millions. Development is coordinated by Mastodon gGmbH, a German non-profit, and funded largely by donations and grants.
