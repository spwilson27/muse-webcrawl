HMAS Perth (D 38)

HMAS Perth (D 38) was the lead ship of the Perth-class guided missile destroyers of the Royal Australian Navy (RAN). Derived from the United States Navy's Charles F. Adams class, she was built by the Defoe Shipbuilding Company at Bay City, Michigan, launched in 1963 and commissioned into the RAN in 1965. She was named after the city of Perth, Western Australia.

Service

Perth was the first Australian warship to come under fire in the Vietnam War era: she made three deployments to Vietnam between 1967 and 1971, serving on the gunline providing naval gunfire support, and was damaged by North Vietnamese shore batteries in October 1967. During her career she was modernised several times, receiving upgrades to her Tartar/Standard missile system, guns and sensors, and took part in exercises and deployments throughout the Asia-Pacific and Indian Ocean.

After 34 years of service, Perth was decommissioned in October 1999. In November 2001 she was scuttled as a dive wreck off Albany, Western Australia, near the resting place of her Second World War predecessor, the light cruiser HMAS Perth (D29) lost in 1942.
