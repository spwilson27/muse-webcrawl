Passage des Panoramas
Location2nd arrondissement, Paris, France
Opened1800
StatusOldest surviving covered passage in Paris; listed historic monument

The Passage des Panoramas is a covered shopping arcade in the 2nd arrondissement of Paris, running between the Boulevard Montmartre and the Rue Saint-Marc. Opened in 1800, it is the oldest of the covered passages of Paris to survive and is considered the first example of the type — an ancestor of the modern shopping mall, offering gas-lit, weather-protected shopping decades before the department store.

History

The passage was built on the site of the Hôtel de Montmorency-Luxembourg and took its name from the two great rotundas at its Boulevard Montmartre entrance, where the American inventor Robert Fulton exhibited panoramic paintings of Paris, Toulon, and other cities. The rotundas were demolished in 1831, but the passage prospered; it was among the first places in Paris lit by gas (1817) and later housed the engraver and stamp dealer quarters for which it remains known. An extension, including several galleries and the entrance to the Théâtre des Variétés, was built in the 1830s.

Today

The passage now contains restaurants, wine bars, and shops dealing in stamps, coins, postcards, and autographs, retaining much of its nineteenth-century decoration of mirrors, woodwork, and glazed roofing. It was designated a French historic monument in the 1970s, as were many of the surviving passages couverts, which enjoyed a revival after decades of neglect.
