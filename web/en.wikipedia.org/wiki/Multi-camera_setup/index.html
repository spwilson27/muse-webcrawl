Multi-camera setup

The multi-camera setup is a method of filmmaking and television production in which several cameras record or broadcast a scene simultaneously, allowing the action to be captured in a single continuous performance from multiple angles.

Television

In television, multi-camera production is standard for sitcoms, soap operas, talk shows, game shows, news, and sports. A director in a control room (gallery) selects shots in real time using a vision mixer, calling cues to camera operators; the programme may be broadcast live or recorded "as live". The setup typically uses three or four cameras: wide shots, mid shots, and close-ups of individual performers.

The classic American sitcom format — pioneered in the 1950s by Desi Arnaz and Karl Freund for I Love Lucy, filmed before a live studio audience with three 35mm cameras — contrasts with the single-camera setup, in which scenes are shot out of sequence with one camera, as in most film production.

Advantages and drawbacks

Multi-camera shooting is fast and economical once sets are lit, preserves actors' performance continuity, and suits audience-based comedy. Its drawbacks include lighting that must flatter all angles at once, constraints on set design (the "fourth wall" is open), and a visual style often considered less cinematic.
