Fishmeal

Fishmeal is a commercial product made from whole wild-caught fish, bycatch, and fish-processing offcuts, cooked, pressed, dried and ground into a protein-rich powder or pellet. Historically used as a fertiliser, fishmeal is now predominantly used as a high-protein feed ingredient for farmed animals — especially farmed fish and shrimp, but also pigs and poultry — because its amino acid profile (rich in lysine and methionine), digestibility, and content of long-chain omega-3 fatty acids make it difficult to replace entirely.

Production

In a typical reduction plant, raw fish is steam-cooked, pressed to separate solids from liquids, and the liquid is centrifuged to recover fish oil; the press cake and concentrated stickwater are dried (often steam-dried) and milled, with antioxidants added to stabilise the product. Roughly 4–5 tonnes of whole fish yield about 1 tonne of meal plus oil. About a third of world fishmeal comes from processing trimmings and by-products rather than whole fish, a share that has grown as reduction fisheries have been capped. World production is around 5 million tonnes per year; Peru (anchoveta) and Chile are the leading producers, followed by countries such as Thailand, Denmark, and Iceland.

Composition and quality

Good fishmeal contains 60–72% crude protein, 5–12% fat, and minerals including calcium and phosphorus from bone; quality is judged by protein content, freshness of the raw material (measured by volatile nitrogen), salt and sand content, and digestibility. "LT" (low-temperature) steam-dried meal made from fresh fish commands a premium for use in aquaculture and early-weaning pig diets.

Sustainability

The fishmeal industry has been criticised for diverting small pelagic "forage fish" — anchovies, sardines, menhaden, sand eels — that are food for wild fish, seabirds and marine mammals, and potentially edible by people, into animal feed. Stock collapses, notably of the Peruvian anchoveta during strong El Niño events, have caused price spikes. In response, the aquaculture industry has steadily reduced fishmeal inclusion rates, substituting plant proteins, insect meal, single-cell proteins and algal oils, and an increasing share of fishmeal is certified as responsibly sourced.
