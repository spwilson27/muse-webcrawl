The IndyCar Series, officially the NTT IndyCar Series for sponsorship reasons, is the premier level of open-wheel racing in North America. Sanctioned by IndyCar, LLC, the series is best known for its flagship event, the Indianapolis 500, held each May at Indianapolis Motor Speedway.

History

The series traces its origins to the Indy Racing League, founded in 1994 by Indianapolis Motor Speedway owner Tony George and launched in 1996 as an oval-focused rival to the established CART (Championship Auto Racing Teams) championship. The resulting "split" in American open-wheel racing lasted until 2008, when the rival Champ Car series folded and its teams joined the IndyCar Series, reunifying the sport. In 2020 Roger Penske's Penske Entertainment purchased both the series and the Speedway.

Cars and racing

All teams use a common Dallara chassis (the DW12, introduced in 2012 and named for the late Dan Wheldon) powered by twin-turbocharged V6 engines of about 2.2 litres supplied by Chevrolet or Honda, producing roughly 700 horsepower depending on the circuit. The calendar mixes superspeedways and short ovals with road courses and temporary street circuits. An aeroscreen cockpit protection device was added in 2020.

Champions

Multiple champions include Scott Dixon, whose six titles make him the most successful driver of the modern era, alongside champions such as Dario Franchitti, Will Power, Josef Newgarden, and Álex Palou. The Indianapolis 500 remains one of the largest single-day sporting events in the world.
