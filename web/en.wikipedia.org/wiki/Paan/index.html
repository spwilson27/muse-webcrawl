Paan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Paan (from Sanskrit parṇa, "leaf") is a preparation combining betel leaf with areca nut, sometimes also with tobacco, that is widely consumed throughout South and Southeast Asia. It is chewed for its stimulant and psychoactive effects; after chewing, it is either spat out or swallowed.

Preparation

A typical paan is assembled by spreading slaked lime paste on a betel leaf, adding sliced areca nut and flavourings such as cardamom, clove, catechu, coconut or sweeteners, then folding the leaf into a parcel. Sweet versions (meetha paan) omit tobacco and are eaten as a mouth freshener and digestive after meals; vendors (paan wallahs) prepare them to order from street stalls.

Health effects

Betel quid chewing is classified as carcinogenic to humans by the International Agency for Research on Cancer, being associated with oral and oesophageal cancers, and the red-stained saliva produced is a familiar sight — and a public-cleanliness issue — where the habit is common. The practice has ancient roots and cultural significance in Indian, Bangladeshi, Pakistani, Burmese and other regional traditions, including religious offerings and hospitality.
