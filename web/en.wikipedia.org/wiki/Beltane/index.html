Beltane
TypeGaelic seasonal festival
Date1 May (or eve of 1 May)
SignificanceBeginning of summer; pastoral season

Beltane (Irish: Bealtaine) is the Gaelic May Day festival, marking the beginning of summer. Historically it was widely observed in Ireland, Scotland and the Isle of Man on 1 May, or about halfway between the spring equinox and summer solstice.

History

Early Irish literature attests Beltane as one of the four Gaelic seasonal festivals, alongside Samhain, Imbolc and Lughnasadh. Cattle were driven between two bonfires to protect them before being taken to summer pastures, and people and livestock jumped flames or passed between fires for blessing and protection.

Customs

Household fires were traditionally doused and relit from the communal Beltane fire. Yellow flowers such as primrose and gorse decorated doors and windows. Offerings and rituals sought protection from harmful spirits and the fairies, thought to be especially active at Beltane.

Modern observance

Beltane fell into decline but was revived in folk and Neopagan traditions, notably Wicca, where it is a sabbat celebrating fertility. Edinburgh's Beltane Fire Festival on Calton Hill, founded in 1988, draws large crowds.
