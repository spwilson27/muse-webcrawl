Food allergy

Food allergy

DefinitionAdverse immune reaction to food protein — usually IgE-mediated
Major allergensMilk, egg, peanut, tree nuts, fish, shellfish, soy, wheat, sesame (the "big nine" in US law)

Overview

A food allergy is an abnormal immune response to a food protein. In the most common (IgE-mediated) form, the immune system produces IgE antibodies to a food; on re-exposure, the food triggers mast-cell mediator release causing symptoms from hives and swelling to vomiting, wheeze and anaphylaxis. This differs from food intolerance (such as lactose intolerance), which is non-immune.

Patterns and management

Milk and egg allergies of childhood usually resolve; peanut, tree-nut and seafood allergies tend to persist. Prevalence has risen in Western countries — hypotheses include the "dual allergen exposure" theory (sensitisation through inflamed skin vs tolerance through early gut exposure), supported by the LEAP trial, which showed early peanut introduction prevents most peanut allergy. Diagnosis relies on history plus skin-prick or specific-IgE tests and, where uncertain, supervised food challenges. Management is avoidance, label literacy (major allergens must be declared in the US, EU and many jurisdictions) and adrenaline auto-injectors for those at risk of anaphylaxis; oral immunotherapy (e.g. Palforzia for peanut) can raise the reaction threshold.
