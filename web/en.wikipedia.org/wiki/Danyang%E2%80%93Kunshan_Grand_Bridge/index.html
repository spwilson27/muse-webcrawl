Danyang–Kunshan Grand Bridge

The Danyang–Kunshan Grand Bridge is a 164.8-kilometre (102.4 mi) viaduct on the Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway in China. Opened in 2011, it is the world's longest bridge, a record recognised by Guinness World Records. It runs between Shanghai and Nanjing in Jiangsu province, roughly parallel to the Yangtze River.

Construction

Construction began in 2006 and took about four years, employing some 10,000 workers and costing roughly 8.5 billion US dollars. The bridge is a viaduct carried on thousands of piers set into the soft delta soils of the Yangtze lowland; near Suzhou it includes a 9-kilometre section over open water across Yangcheng Lake. The elevated design conserves farmland, avoids the region's many canals and waterways, and keeps the high-speed line straight and level.

Significance

Part of one of China's busiest high-speed rail corridors, the bridge cut the travel time between Beijing and Shanghai to under five hours. It exemplifies the viaduct-based approach used across much of China's high-speed network, where elevated structures make up a large share of total route length.
