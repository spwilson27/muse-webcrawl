Võ Nguyên Giáp

Võ Nguyên Giáp (25 August 1911 – 4 October 2013) was a Vietnamese general and statesman, commander of the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) and a leading figure in the wars against France and the United States. One of the foremost military strategists of the 20th century, he masterminded the victory at Điện Biên Phủ (1954) and oversaw the war effort that ended with the fall of Saigon in 1975.

Early life

Born into a scholarly family in Quảng Bình Province, Giáp trained as a teacher and studied law and history. Radicalised by colonial rule, he joined the Indochinese Communist Party and in 1940 fled to China, where he met Hồ Chí Minh. In 1944 he founded the Armed Propaganda Brigade — the nucleus of the PAVN — in the Việt Bắc jungles.

First Indochina War

As commander-in-chief during the First Indochina War, Giáp built a regular army from guerrilla bands, survived early defeats along the Chinese border in 1950 (turned into victory at the Border Campaign), and in 1954 destroyed the French fortress at Điện Biên Phủ after secretly hauling artillery through mountains — a triumph that ended French rule in Indochina.

Vietnam War and later life

As Minister of Defence he directed strategy during the war against South Vietnam and the United States, combining guerrilla warfare, protracted attrition and conventional offensives; he was closely involved in the 1968 Tết Offensive and the 1972 Easter Offensive, and remained a national symbol through reunification in 1975. Eased from power in the 1980s, he lived to 102, revered in Vietnam as a national hero.
