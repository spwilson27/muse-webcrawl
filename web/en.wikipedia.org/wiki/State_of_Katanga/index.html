The State of Katanga was a breakaway state of the Republic of the Congo that proclaimed its independence on 11 July 1960, eleven days after the Congo gained independence from Belgium. Led by Moïse Tshombe and his CONAKAT party, and backed by Belgian mining interests — above all the Union Minière du Haut-Katanga, which coveted the province's copper and cobalt wealth — Katanga's secession precipitated the Congo Crisis.

History

Katanga was never recognized diplomatically by any country, though it received covert support from Belgium and sympathizers. The United Nations Operation in the Congo (ONUC) was mandated to restore Congolese territorial integrity; Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld died in a plane crash in September 1961 while travelling to negotiate a ceasefire. Fighting between Katangese gendarmes, foreign mercenaries, and UN forces culminated in Operation Grandslam in December 1962–January 1963, after which Katanga was forcibly reintegrated into the Congo and Tshombe fled into exile. He later returned as prime minister of the whole Congo in 1964.
