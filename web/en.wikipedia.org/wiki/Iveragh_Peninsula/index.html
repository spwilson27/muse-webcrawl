The Iveragh Peninsula (Irish: Uíbh Ráthach) is a peninsula in County Kerry in south-western Ireland, projecting into the Atlantic Ocean between Dingle Bay to the north and Kenmare Bay to the south. It is the largest of the great peninsulas of Kerry and contains some of Ireland's most celebrated scenery.

Geography

The peninsula is dominated by MacGillycuddy's Reeks, the highest mountain range in Ireland, whose summit Carrauntoohil rises to 1,038.6 metres. Glaciated valleys such as the Gap of Dunloe and the Black Valley cut through the mountains, and the Lakes of Killarney lie at the peninsula's eastern edge. Off its western tip lie the Skellig Islands, including Skellig Michael, a UNESCO World Heritage Site famed for its early Christian monastery and seabird colonies, and Valentia Island, linked to the mainland by bridge at Portmagee.

The Ring of Kerry

The Ring of Kerry is a 179-kilometre circular tourist route around the peninsula, following the N70 and N71 roads through Killorglin, Cahersiveen, Waterville, Sneem and Kenmare. It is one of Ireland's most popular scenic drives. The Kerry Way, one of Ireland's longest waymarked walking trails, follows much of the same circuit inland.

History and heritage

The peninsula is rich in prehistoric remains, including stone forts such as Staigue and Cahergal, ogham stones and beehive huts. Daniel O'Connell, the nineteenth-century political leader known as "the Liberator", was born near Cahersiveen, and his home at Derrynane is now a national monument. The transatlantic cable station at Valentia made the area a hub of early global communications from the 1860s.
