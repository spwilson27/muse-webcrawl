The Burgtheater (German for "castle theatre"), originally known as the K.K. Theater an der Burg and later as the K.K. Hofburgtheater, is the national theatre of Austria and one of the most important German-language theatres in the world. Its ensemble of actors is among the most renowned in Europe, and membership in it has traditionally been considered a pinnacle of a stage career in the German-speaking world.

History

The theatre was founded on 14 March 1741 by Empress Maria Theresa of Austria, who had the former imperial tennis court next to the Hofburg palace converted into a theatre. In 1776 Emperor Joseph II declared it the German National Theatre and required that plays be performed in German rather than French or Italian.

On 14 October 1888 the company moved to a grand new building on the Ringstrasse, designed by Gottfried Semper and Karl von Hasenauer in an Italian High Renaissance style, with ceiling paintings by Gustav Klimt and his brother Ernst among others. The building was heavily damaged by bombing and fire in 1945 and was restored and reopened in 1955.

The company

The Burgtheater's acting ensemble includes some of the most celebrated names in Austrian theatre history; actors who perform there for ten years may be granted the title of Kammerschauspieler(in). The theatre's directors have included Adolf Wilbrandt, Max Burckhard, and in more recent times Claus Peymann (1986–1999), whose tenure brought many premieres of works by Thomas Bernhard, Elfriede Jelinek, and Peter Handke. Other venues of the house include the Akademietheater and the Kasino.
