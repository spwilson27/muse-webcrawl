Rogers Centre

Rogers Centre
Former name: SkyDome (1989–2005)
Location: Toronto, Ontario, Canada
Opened: June 3, 1989
Tenants: Toronto Blue Jays (MLB)

Rogers Centre (originally named SkyDome) is a retractable roof stadium in downtown Toronto, Ontario, Canada, situated on the shore of Lake Ontario just southwest of the CN Tower. Opened in 1989, it was the first stadium with a fully retractable motorised roof, whose four panels can open or close in about 20 minutes. It is the home of the Toronto Blue Jays of Major League Baseball and has hosted concerts, conventions and other sports.

History

The stadium was conceived after Toronto's experience hosting the 1983 Grey Cup in the open-air Exhibition Stadium in poor weather, and the Blue Jays' World Series ambitions. Designed by architect Rod Robbie and engineer Michael Allen, it cost about C$570 million, financed by a mix of public funds and corporate contributions. The Blue Jays won the 1992 and 1993 World Series at the stadium. The Toronto Argonauts of the CFL played there until 2015, and the Toronto Raptors played there from 1995 to 1999 before moving to the Air Canada Centre. In 2005, Rogers Communications bought the stadium and renamed it Rogers Centre.

Features

The roof is 86 metres (282 ft) above the field at its highest point. The building incorporates a hotel (the Toronto Marriott City Centre) with rooms overlooking the field, restaurants and one of the world's largest video boards at its opening. Capacity is roughly 41,500 for baseball after renovations, and over 50,000 for concerts. Major renovations in 2022–2024 converted the stadium toward a baseball-first configuration with new seating, social spaces and a raised bullpen design, funded by the Blue Jays.

Notable events

Beyond baseball, the Rogers Centre hosted the 1991 MLB All-Star Game, WrestleMania VI (1990) and WrestleMania X8 (2002), the 2015 Pan American Games ceremonies, and Buffalo Bills regular-season "Bills Toronto Series" games (2008–2013).
