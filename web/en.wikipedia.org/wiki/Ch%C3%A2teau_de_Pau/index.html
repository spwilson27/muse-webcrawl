Château de Pau

The Château de Pau is a castle in the centre of Pau, the capital of Pyrénées-Atlantiques in southwestern France. It dominates the city from a rocky outcrop above the Gave de Pau and is famous as the birthplace of Henry IV of France, who was born there on 13 December 1553.

History

Founded in the Middle Ages by the viscounts of Béarn, the castle was enlarged by Gaston Fébus in the 14th century and converted into a Renaissance residence by Marguerite d'Angoulême and Henri II of Navarre. According to legend, the infant Henry IV was cradled in the shell of a giant tortoise, which is still displayed in the castle. Restored under Louis-Philippe and Napoleon III, the château became a national museum.

Museum

The Musée national du Château de Pau displays tapestries from the Gobelins and Beauvais manufactures, period furniture, and objects relating to Henry IV, the only king of France born in the Pyrenean city. Its keep, the Tour Fébus, offers views toward the Pyrenees.
