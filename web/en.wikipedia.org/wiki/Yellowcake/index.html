Yellowcake

Yellowcake (also called urania) is a type of uranium concentrate powder produced in the milling of uranium ore. Despite the name, modern yellowcake is usually brown or black rather than yellow; the colour and texture depend on the extraction process and drying temperature. It consists largely of triuranium octoxide (U3O8) and related uranium oxides, typically making up 70–90 percent of the product by weight.

Production and use

Mined ore is crushed and leached — usually with acid or alkali — to dissolve the uranium, which is then precipitated, filtered, and dried into yellowcake. It is the form in which uranium is traded and transported to conversion facilities, where it is refined and converted to uranium hexafluoride (UF6) for enrichment, or to uranium dioxide for use in reactors (such as CANDU designs) that run on natural uranium. Yellowcake itself is only mildly radioactive, mainly an alpha emitter, and is handled as a low-level hazard, though its chemical toxicity and dust require care.

The Niger affair

"Yellowcake" entered public vocabulary during the run-up to the 2003 invasion of Iraq, when the claim that Iraq had sought yellowcake from Niger — based on documents later shown by the International Atomic Energy Agency to be forgeries — featured in the case for war, including the "sixteen words" in the 2003 US State of the Union address. The ensuing controversy, involving diplomat Joseph Wilson's public rebuttal and the exposure of his wife Valerie Plame as a CIA officer, became known as the Plame affair.
