Cushendall

Cushendall
Irish: Bun Abhann Dalla
County: Antrim
Country: Northern Ireland

Cushendall (from Irish Bun Abhann Dalla, meaning "foot of the Dall River") is a coastal village and townland in County Antrim, Northern Ireland. It sits on the A2 Coast Road where three of the Glens of Antrim meet: Glenariff, Glenballyeamon and Glencorp.

History

The village is known for its distinctive architecture, including the Curfew Tower, built in 1817 by Francis Turnley as a place of confinement for "idlers and rioters." The tower later housed artist Bill Drummond. Cushendall's harbour and beach make it a popular stop on the Antrim Coast and Glens scenic route.

Features

Landmarks include the ruins of Layd Church, the Red Bay Castle site, and views toward the Mull of Kintyre in Scotland on clear days. The village hosts the Heart of the Glens Festival each August. The surrounding landscape of the Glens of Antrim is designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.
