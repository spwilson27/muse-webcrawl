Texas Instruments Incorporated (TI) is an American multinational semiconductor company headquartered in Dallas, Texas. It is one of the top 10 semiconductor companies worldwide, based largely on its dominant position in analog chips and embedded processors, which account for the bulk of its revenue. TI also produces digital light processing (DLP) technology and the graphing calculators widely used in education.

History

The company began in 1930 as Geophysical Service Incorporated (GSI), providing seismic exploration services to the oil industry. In 1951 the parent company was renamed Texas Instruments. In 1954 TI produced the first commercial silicon transistor, and in 1958 TI engineer Jack Kilby built one of the first working integrated circuits — work for which he later shared the Nobel Prize in Physics (2000). TI introduced the first handheld electronic calculator (the Cal Tech prototype, 1967), the single-chip microcontroller, and the Speak & Spell educational toy, an early consumer application of digital signal processing.

Products

Today TI focuses on analog semiconductors (power management, signal chain) and embedded processing (microcontrollers and processors). Its TI-84 Plus series of graphing calculators remains a standard in secondary-school mathematics in the United States. Its DLP Cinema chips are used in digital projectors worldwide.

Corporate affairs

Texas Instruments is a component of the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 indices and is known for substantial capital returns to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases.
