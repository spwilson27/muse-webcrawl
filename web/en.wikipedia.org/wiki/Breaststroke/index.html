Breaststroke

Breaststroke is a swimming style in which the swimmer lies on the chest and the torso does not rotate. The arms move in a simultaneous, symmetrical in-sweep under the water while the legs perform a whip kick resembling the motion of a frog. It is the most popular recreational style, since the head can remain out of the water much of the time, but the slowest of the four competitive strokes — front crawl, butterfly, backstroke, and breaststroke — because of the large resistance created during the recovery of the limbs underwater.

Technique

The stroke cycle begins with the body streamlined, arms extended. In the outsweep and insweep the hands sweep outward and then inward and upward beneath the chest, generating propulsion, while the head lifts to breathe. The arms then shoot forward during recovery as the heels are drawn up toward the buttocks with feet flexed and turned out; the whip kick snaps the legs backward and together in a circular motion, providing most of the stroke's power. Elite swimmers undulate the body in a wave-like rhythm, timing the kick to drive the body forward over the water. Rules require simultaneous, symmetrical movements of the arms and legs on the same horizontal plane, the head to break the surface each cycle, and both hands to touch the wall together at turns and the finish.

History

Breaststroke is the oldest known swimming style, depicted in ancient Egyptian art and described in early swimming manuals such as Melchisédech Thévenot's The Art of Swimming (1696). It was the dominant racing stroke of the 19th century: Captain Matthew Webb swam the English Channel in 1875 using breaststroke. It entered the Olympics in 1904; the butterfly stroke originated in the 1930s as a variant of breaststroke before being recognised as a separate style in 1952. Controversies over underwater dolphin kicks after the start — highlighted at the 2004 Olympics — led World Aquatics to permit a single dolphin kick during the underwater pullout.

Competitive breaststroke

Olympic and world championship races are held over 100 m and 200 m, plus breaststroke legs in medley events. Renowned breaststrokers include Kōsuke Kitajima of Japan, Adam Peaty of Great Britain — who transformed the 100 m event with a high-stroke-rate, powerful technique and held the world record under 57 seconds — and Lilly King of the United States.
