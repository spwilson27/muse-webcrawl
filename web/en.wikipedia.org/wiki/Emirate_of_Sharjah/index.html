The Emirate of Sharjah (Arabic: إمارة الشارقة) is one of the seven emirates of the United Arab Emirates. Its capital and largest city is Sharjah, which forms part of the Dubai–Sharjah–Ajman metropolitan area. The emirate is ruled by the Al Qasimi dynasty; Sheikh Sultan bin Muhammad Al-Qasimi has been ruler since 1972.

Geography

Sharjah is the third-largest emirate by area. Uniquely, it has territory on both the Persian Gulf coast and the Gulf of Oman coast, including the enclaves of Kalba, Khor Fakkan and Dibba Al-Hisn on the east coast, as well as islands such as Sir Bu Nu'ayr and Abu Musa (the latter disputed with Iran).

Culture

Sharjah is regarded as the cultural capital of the UAE and was named UNESCO Cultural Capital of the Arab World in 1998 and World Book Capital in 2019. It hosts the Sharjah International Book Fair, the Sharjah Biennial, numerous museums in the Heart of Sharjah heritage district, and the University of Sharjah. Alcohol is prohibited throughout the emirate.

History

Settlement in the area dates back over 6,000 years. The Al Qasimi family's maritime power brought it into conflict with Britain in the early 19th century, leading to treaties that created the Trucial States. Sharjah was the site of the first airport in the region, opened in 1932 as a stopover for Imperial Airways flights to India.
