Committee

A committee or commission is a body of one or more persons subordinate to a deliberative assembly or other organisation. A committee is not itself considered to be the main decision-making body; rather, it is usually tasked with specific functions — investigating a matter, drafting proposals, overseeing an activity, or making recommendations — and reports to the parent body, which retains final authority.

Functions

Committees are used because a smaller group can study an issue in more depth than a large assembly, can meet more flexibly, and can develop specialist expertise. In legislatures, bills are typically referred to committees for detailed scrutiny, hearings, and amendment before returning to the floor. Committees may be permanent (standing committees), temporary (select or ad hoc committees), or charged with continuing oversight of an executive agency or programme.

Types

  • Standing committee — a permanent committee with jurisdiction over a defined subject area, such as finance, foreign affairs, or the judiciary.
  • Select committee — created for a specific inquiry or task and dissolved when it reports.
  • Committee of the whole — the entire membership of an assembly sitting as a committee, usually under relaxed procedural rules.
  • Subcommittee — a smaller group formed from a committee's membership to handle part of its work.
  • Conference committee — in bicameral legislatures, a joint body that reconciles differing versions of a bill passed by each chamber.
  • Executive committee — a small group empowered to act on behalf of a board or organisation between meetings.

Procedure

Committees generally have a chair, fixed membership reflecting the composition of the parent body, quorum rules, and the power to call witnesses and request documents. Their influence varies widely: in the United States Congress, committees and their chairs exercise substantial gatekeeping power over legislation, while in many parliamentary systems committees serve mainly a scrutinising role. The word committee derives from the idea of a body to whom a matter is "committed" for consideration.
