Cell (telecommunications)

Cells are often drawn as hexagons for planning purposes.

In mobile telecommunications, a cell is the geographical area covered by a single base station transmitter in a cellular network. The concept—dividing a service area into many small cells, each reusing radio frequencies also used in non-adjacent cells—underlies all modern mobile telephony and gives "cellular" its name.

Cell types

Cells vary greatly in size. Macrocells cover several kilometres in rural areas; microcells serve dense urban districts over a few hundred metres; picocells cover a building floor or station; and femtocells cover a home or small office. Umbrella cells may overlay smaller cells to serve fast-moving users and reduce handover frequency.

Frequency reuse and handover

Because each cell uses only a subset of the available channels, the same frequencies can be reused in cells separated by enough distance to limit co-channel interference. As a subscriber moves, the network performs a handover, transferring the connection to the neighbouring cell without interrupting service.

Cell site

The physical installation serving a cell is the cell site, comprising antennas (often in three sectors of 120°), transceivers, and backhaul connections. Site sharing and densification with small cells are central strategies for increasing network capacity.
