Gulf of Genoa

Gulf of Genoa (Italian: Golfo di Genova)
Part of: Ligurian Sea
Location: Northwest Italy
Major port: Genoa

The Gulf of Genoa (Italian: Golfo di Genova) is the northernmost part of the Ligurian Sea. Its width is about 145 kilometres, measured from La Spezia in the east to Cap Ferrat in France in the west, though the name is often applied more narrowly to the waters off the Italian region of Liguria. The gulf's shoreline forms the Italian Riviera.

Geography

The gulf is bounded by the Maritime Alps and the Ligurian Apennines, which descend steeply into the sea, leaving a narrow coastal strip. The city of Genoa, one of the Mediterranean's great historic ports, sits near the centre of the gulf's northern shore. Other important towns include Savona, Imperia, Sanremo, La Spezia, Rapallo, and Portofino. The smaller Gulf of La Spezia lies at the eastern end.

Climate and oceanography

The gulf is a notable centre of cyclogenesis: low-pressure systems frequently form or deepen over its waters, especially in autumn and winter, bringing heavy rainfall to Liguria and the Po valley. Cold-air outbreaks funnelled down the mountain valleys produce the strong northerly tramontana wind. Sea temperatures support a mild coastal climate that made the Riviera an early winter resort.

History

Control of the gulf was the foundation of the maritime power of the Republic of Genoa, whose fleets contested the western Mediterranean with Pisa and Venice. The port of Genoa remains among Italy's busiest, and the gulf's waters are crossed by ferry routes to Corsica, Sardinia, and Sicily.
