Susquehanna River

The Susquehanna River is a major river in the Mid-Atlantic region of the United States, about 444 miles (715 km) long. It rises at Otsego Lake near Cooperstown, New York, flows through Pennsylvania — passing Binghamton (just across the state line), Wilkes-Barre, and Harrisburg — and empties into the Chesapeake Bay at Havre de Grace, Maryland. It is the longest river on the U.S. East Coast and the largest contributor of fresh water to Chesapeake Bay.

Course and branches

The river has two main branches: the North Branch, considered the main stem, and the West Branch, which joins it near Northumberland, Pennsylvania. Major tributaries include the Chenango, Lackawanna, Juniata and Conodoguinet. The river is famously wide and shallow, with rocky ledges that make most of it unnavigable for large vessels.

History and environment

The name derives from the Susquehannock, an Iroquoian-speaking people who lived along its lower reaches. The river figured in the 1979 Three Mile Island accident, which occurred on an island near Harrisburg. Floods, notably from Hurricane Agnes in 1972, have caused extensive damage in its basin, and the river carries significant sediment and nutrient loads into the Chesapeake.
