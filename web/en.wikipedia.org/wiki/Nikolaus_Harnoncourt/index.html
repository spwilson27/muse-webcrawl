Nikolaus Harnoncourt

Nikolaus Harnoncourt (born Johann Nikolaus Graf de la Fontaine und d'Harnoncourt-Unverzagt; 6 December 1929 – 5 March 2016) was an Austrian conductor, cellist, viol player and founder of the period-instrument ensemble Concentus Musicus Wien. He was one of the pioneers of the historically informed performance movement in early music.

Nikolaus Harnoncourt

Born: 6 December 1929, Berlin, Germany

Died: 5 March 2016, St. Georgen im Attergau, Austria

Occupation: Conductor, cellist

Ensemble: Concentus Musicus Wien (founded 1953)

Career

Harnoncourt studied cello in Vienna and played with the Vienna Symphony Orchestra from 1952 to 1969. With his wife, violinist Alice Hoffelner, he founded Concentus Musicus Wien in 1953 to perform Renaissance and Baroque music on period instruments. Beginning in the 1970s, he and Gustav Leonhardt undertook the landmark project of recording all of J.S. Bach's sacred cantatas.

Later work

From the 1980s Harnoncourt increasingly conducted modern orchestras, including the Vienna Philharmonic, the Berlin Philharmonic and the Royal Concertgebouw Orchestra, applying his rhetorical, historically informed insights to repertory from Mozart and Beethoven through Schubert, Brahms and even Gershwin. He also conducted opera in Vienna, Zurich and at the Salzburg Festival. Widely honoured, he retired from performing in December 2015 for health reasons and died three months later.
