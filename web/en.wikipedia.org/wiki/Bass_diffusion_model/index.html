Bass diffusion model

The Bass diffusion model, developed by Frank M. Bass and published in 1969, is a widely used mathematical model describing how new products and technologies are adopted by a population of consumers. It has become one of the most famous empirical generalisations in marketing science and management.

The model

Bass proposed that adopters fall into two groups: innovators, who adopt independently of others, and imitators, whose adoption is driven by contact with previous adopters (word of mouth). If p is the coefficient of innovation, q the coefficient of imitation and m the total market potential, the rate of adoption follows a differential equation whose solution gives the characteristic S-shaped cumulative adoption curve, with sales peaking as the market saturates.

Applications

The model is used to forecast sales of genuinely new products — consumer durables, telecommunications services, entertainment releases — before or shortly after launch, by estimating p, q and m from analogous products or early sales data. Extensions incorporate price, advertising and successive product generations. Typical values found across many categories are p ≈ 0.03 and q ≈ 0.38.
