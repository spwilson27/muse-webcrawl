East Prussia

East Prussia (German: Ostpreußen) was a province of the Kingdom of Prussia and later of the German Empire and Weimar/Nazi Germany, from 1772 to 1945. Its capital was Königsberg (now Kaliningrad). The region lay on the southeastern coast of the Baltic Sea and was separated from the rest of Germany after World War I by the Polish Corridor, making it an exclave.

History

The territory was originally inhabited by the Old Prussians, a Baltic people conquered by the Teutonic Order in the 13th century. It formed the eastern part of the Duchy of Prussia and then of Brandenburg-Prussia. After the partitions of Poland, it was organized as the province of East Prussia in 1773.

East Prussia saw heavy fighting in both world wars, including the battles of Tannenberg (1914) and the Soviet East Prussian Offensive (1945). After World War II, the Potsdam Agreement divided the province: the northern part, including Königsberg, went to the Soviet Union (now Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast), and the southern part to Poland (now the Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship). The German population fled or was expelled, and the region was repopulated by Poles and Russians.
