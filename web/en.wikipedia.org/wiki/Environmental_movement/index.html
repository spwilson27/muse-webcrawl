Environmental movement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The environmental movement, sometimes referred to as the ecology movement, is a diverse philosophical, social, and political movement for addressing environmental issues. Environmentalists advocate the just and sustainable management of resources and stewardship of the environment through changes in public policy and individual behavior.

History

Early roots lie in 19th-century conservation, with figures such as John Muir and the founding of the Sierra Club (1892), and preservation of wilderness areas and national parks. The modern movement is often dated to Rachel Carson's book Silent Spring (1962), which documented the dangers of pesticides such as DDT. The first Earth Day in 1970 mobilized some 20 million Americans and was followed by landmark legislation including the Clean Air Act, Clean Water Act, and the creation of the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency.

Scope and organizations

The movement encompasses concerns from pollution, waste, and resource depletion to biodiversity loss and climate change. Major organizations include Greenpeace, the World Wide Fund for Nature (WWF), Friends of the Earth, and 350.org, alongside green political parties in many countries. More recent strands include environmental justice, which focuses on the unequal distribution of environmental burdens, and youth-led climate activism such as Fridays for Future.
