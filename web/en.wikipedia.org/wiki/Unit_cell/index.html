Unit cell
A unit cell defined by lengths a, b, c and angles α, β, γ
FieldCrystallography
ParametersThree edge lengths, three interaxial angles
RelatedBravais lattices; Wigner–Seitz cell

In geometry, biology, mineralogy and solid state physics, a unit cell is a repeating unit, the building block of a crystal structure. It is defined by its lattice points: the smallest arrangement of atoms, ions or molecules that, when repeated in space by translation, reconstructs the whole crystal.

Definition

A unit cell is typically a parallelepiped described by three edge lengths (a, b, c) and three angles between them (α, β, γ), collectively called the lattice parameters. The choice of cell is not unique: a primitive cell contains exactly one lattice point, while conventional cells are often larger, chosen to display the symmetry of the lattice clearly — as in the face-centred cubic cell of metals such as copper and aluminium, which contains four lattice points.

Crystal systems

The possible shapes of unit cells underlie the seven crystal systems (triclinic, monoclinic, orthorhombic, tetragonal, trigonal, hexagonal and cubic) and, combined with centring, the fourteen Bravais lattices. The positions of atoms within the cell are given in fractional coordinates, and planes and directions in the crystal are described using Miller indices referenced to the cell edges.

An alternative primitive construction, the Wigner–Seitz cell, is formed by the region of space closer to one lattice point than to any other, and is central to solid-state physics, where its reciprocal-space counterpart is the first Brillouin zone.
