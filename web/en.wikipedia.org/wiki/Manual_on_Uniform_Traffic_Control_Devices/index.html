The Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices (MUTCD) is the national standard in the United States governing traffic signs, road surface markings, and traffic signals. It is published by the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) of the U.S. Department of Transportation. Federal regulation requires that all traffic control devices on any street, highway, or bicycle trail open to public travel conform substantially to the standards in the MUTCD or in a state manual approved as being in substantial conformance with it.

History

The first MUTCD was published in 1935, consolidating the competing sign standards of rural and urban highway associations. Before standardisation, each jurisdiction designed its own signs; early manuals standardised shapes with distinct meanings — octagons for stop, diamonds for warnings, crosses for railroad crossings. The manual has been revised many times; the widely used 2009 edition was followed by the 11th edition in December 2023, which among other changes added fuller provisions for bicycle facilities and automated vehicles.

Contents

The MUTCD specifies the design, placement, operation, and maintenance of:

  • Signs — regulatory, warning, and guide signs, with standard shapes, colours, legends, and retroreflectivity requirements.
  • Pavement markings — centre lines, lane lines, crosswalks, and their colours and patterns.
  • Signals — traffic lights, pedestrian signals, and flashing beacons.
  • Special situations — temporary traffic control in work zones, school zones, railroad grade crossings, and bicycle facilities.

Compliance and state supplements

States must adopt the national MUTCD, a state manual in substantial conformance, or the national manual with a state supplement, within two years of each new edition taking effect. Some states, such as California, publish their own full manuals in conformance with the federal standard.

Criticism

Transportation reformers have criticised the MUTCD for prioritising motor-vehicle throughput and for procedures, such as the 85th-percentile rule for setting speed limits, that can lock in high speeds on urban streets. The 2023 edition was intended in part to respond to such concerns.
