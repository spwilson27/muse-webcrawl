Milford Sound

LocationFiordland, New Zealand
Length~15 km
Rainfall~6–7 m per year
[Image: Mitre Peak in Milford Sound]
Mitre Peak in Milford Sound

Milford Sound (Piopiotahi) is a fiord in the south-west of New Zealand's South Island, within Fiordland National Park — the country's most famous natural attraction, often called the "eighth wonder of the world" after Kipling.

Features

Carved by glaciers, the fiord runs 15 km from the Tasman Sea between cliffs rising over 1,000 m; Mitre Peak (1,692 m) rises directly from the water. Milford is one of the wettest inhabited places on Earth, with around 6–7 m of rain a year, which feeds hundreds of temporary waterfalls after storms and stains the surface water tea-dark with tannins over the salt water beneath. Cruises, kayaking and the end of the Milford Track at Sandfly Point bring visitors to the small settlement, reached by the Homer Tunnel road from Te Anau.
