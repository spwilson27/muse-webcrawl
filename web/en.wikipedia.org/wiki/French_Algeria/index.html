French Algeria (French: Algérie française) was the period of French colonial rule in Algeria from the invasion of Algiers in 1830 until Algerian independence in 1962. Uniquely among French colonies, northern Algeria was legally incorporated into France itself as three departments, and over 132 years it attracted about a million European settlers — the pieds-noirs — who dominated the economy and politics while the Muslim majority remained subjects under the discriminatory code de l'indigénat.

Algiers, capital of French Algeria, in the nineteenth century.

Conquest

France invaded in 1830, ostensibly over the "fly-whisk incident" in which the Dey of Algiers struck the French consul. The conquest of the interior, resisted by leaders such as Emir Abdelkader, took decades and was marked by brutal warfare, land expropriation and devastating losses among the Algerian population. From 1848 the north was declared French territory, and settler colonisation — by French, Spanish, Italian and Maltese immigrants — steadily expanded onto confiscated communal land.

Colonial society

European settlers, though never more than about a tenth of the population, controlled the best farmland, the wine industry and municipal government, and sent deputies to the French parliament. Muslim Algerians were French subjects but not citizens unless they renounced Islamic personal law; most lived under the indigénat, a system of summary penalties. Algerian soldiers nevertheless served in both world wars, and demands for equality grew after 1945 — the year of the Sétif massacres, in which French repression of nationalist demonstrations killed thousands.

The Algerian War and independence

On 1 November 1954 the National Liberation Front (FLN) launched the Algerian War. The conflict — including the Battle of Algiers (1956–57), the use of torture by French forces, and the collapse of the Fourth Republic that brought Charles de Gaulle to power in 1958 — ended with the Évian Accords of March 1962. Independence followed on 5 July 1962, and nearly a million pieds-noirs fled to France within months.
