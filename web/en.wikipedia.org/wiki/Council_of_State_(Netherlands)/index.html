Council of State (Netherlands)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Council of State (Dutch: Raad van State) is a constitutionally established advisory body of the government of the Netherlands, and also serves as the country's highest general administrative court through its Administrative Jurisdiction Division.

History and role

Founded in 1531 by Emperor Charles V, the council is one of the oldest Dutch state institutions still in existence. The monarch is its formal president; in practice it is led by the vice-president, often described as the second most influential official in the country. The government is obliged to consult the council on all proposals for legislation and on many administrative decisions, and its advice, while not binding, carries great weight.

Administrative jurisdiction

Since 1976 the council's Administrative Jurisdiction Division has acted as the highest court of appeal in administrative law matters, hearing cases concerning planning, environmental law, immigration, and other disputes between citizens and the state.
