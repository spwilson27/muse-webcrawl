Venustiano Carranza de la Garza (29 December 1859 – 21 May 1920) was a Mexican landowner and politician who served as President of Mexico from 1917 to 1920. As "First Chief" of the Constitutionalist Army he led the forces that defeated the government of Victoriano Huerta during the Mexican Revolution, and presided over the drafting of the Constitution of 1917.

Revolution

A wealthy rancher and former senator from Coahuila, Carranza supported Francisco Madero's revolution against Porfirio Díaz and became governor of Coahuila. After Huerta's coup and Madero's murder in February 1913, Carranza issued the Plan of Guadalupe, repudiating Huerta and raising the Constitutionalist Army, whose generals included Álvaro Obregón and Pancho Villa. Constitutionalist victory in 1914 was followed by a split with Villa and Emiliano Zapata, whom Carranza's forces eventually defeated.

Presidency

Carranza convened the Constituent Congress at Querétaro, which produced the Constitution of 1917 — a landmark document incorporating land reform, labour rights and restrictions on the Church and foreign ownership of resources. He was elected president in 1917. His government kept Mexico neutral in the First World War despite provocations such as the Zimmermann Telegram.

Death

In 1920 Carranza attempted to impose a civilian successor, Ignacio Bonillas, over the ambitions of Obregón. Obregón's supporters issued the Plan of Agua Prieta, and Carranza fled Mexico City by train toward Veracruz. He was killed on 21 May 1920 at Tlaxcalantongo, Puebla, in an ambush by forces of Rodolfo Herrero, under circumstances that remain debated.
