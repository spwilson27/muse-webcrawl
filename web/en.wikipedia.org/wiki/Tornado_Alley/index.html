Tornado Alley

RegionCentral United States
Core statesTexas, Oklahoma, Kansas, Nebraska
PhenomenonFrequent tornadoes

Tornado Alley is a loosely defined region of the central United States where tornadoes occur most frequently. Although no official boundaries exist, the core of the region is generally taken to include northern Texas, Oklahoma, Kansas and Nebraska, with activity extending into parts of Colorado, the Dakotas, Iowa, Missouri and beyond.

Causes

The region's geography favours severe thunderstorms: warm, moist air from the Gulf of Mexico collides with cold, dry air from the Rockies and Canada, often along a boundary called the dry line, while strong upper-level winds provide rotation. Tornado frequency peaks in spring and early summer, shifting northward as the season progresses. A related southeastern region with frequent, often nocturnal tornadoes is sometimes called "Dixie Alley".

Impact

Tornado Alley communities are served by the National Weather Service's Storm Prediction Center in Norman, Oklahoma, and have extensive warning systems, sirens, storm shelters and storm-spotting networks. Notable outbreaks in the region include the 1999 Oklahoma City/Moore tornado and the May 2013 Moore tornado.
