Playing field

A marked grass pitch

A playing field is a field used for outdoor sports and games. Depending on the sport, the surface and dimensions vary: a playing field may be called a pitch (association football, field hockey, cricket in part), a field (American football, baseball, lacrosse), a court (when enclosed or hard-surfaced) or a ground when referring to the whole sporting venue.

Surfaces and markings

Traditional playing fields are natural grass, valued for ball behaviour and player comfort but requiring mowing, irrigation and rest periods. Since the 1960s, artificial turf has been used where climate, heavy use or maintenance costs make grass impractical, and modern third-generation synthetic surfaces incorporate rubber infill to mimic soil. Fields are marked with lines defining boundaries, goals, creases and other zones specified by each sport's laws; dimensions are standardised, for example a senior association football pitch is recommended at 105 × 68 metres.

In schools and communities

In Britain and Commonwealth countries, schools and villages traditionally maintain playing fields for physical education and local sport. Their protection and sale has been a recurring public-policy issue, and charitable bodies such as Fields in Trust work to safeguard recreational land in perpetuity.
