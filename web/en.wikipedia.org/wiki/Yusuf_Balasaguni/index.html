Yusuf Balasaguni

Yusuf Khass Hajib Balasaguni (c. 1019 – c. 1085) was an 11th-century Karakhanid poet, scholar and statesman from Central Asia, best known as the author of the Kutadgu Bilig ("Wisdom of Royal Glory" or "Knowledge Which Brings Happiness"), the first major work of Turkic Islamic literature and a classic of the "mirror for princes" genre. He wrote in the Karakhanid Turkic language, and his poem of some 6,600 couplets remains a foundational text for Turkic peoples, especially in Kyrgyzstan, where he is a national figure.

Life

Yusuf was born at Balasagun, near modern Tokmok in Kyrgyzstan, then a capital of the Karakhanid Khanate. Little is certain about his biography beyond what he states in his poem: he completed the Kutadgu Bilig in Kashgar in 1070 and presented it to the Karakhanid ruler Tabgach Bughra Khan, who rewarded him with the court title Khass Hajib (privy chamberlain). Tradition holds that he died in Kashgar around 1085; a mausoleum there is venerated as his tomb.

Kutadgu Bilig

The poem is written in the mutaqarib metre of Arabic prosody and structured as dialogues among four allegorical figures: King Küntogdi (justice), the vizier Aytoldi (fortune), his son Ögdülmish (intellect) and the ascetic Odgurmish (contentment). It instructs rulers and subjects alike in ethics, statecraft, etiquette and the pursuit of knowledge, blending Turkic custom, Islamic teaching and Persianate court culture. Three medieval manuscripts survive (the Vienna, Fergana and Cairo copies), and the work has been translated into many languages.

Legacy

Balasaguni appears on the Kyrgyz 1000-som banknote, and the Kyrgyz-Turkish Manas University's predecessor institution and streets across Central Asia bear his name. The year 2016 was marked by Turkic cultural organisations as the Year of Yusuf Khass Hajib.
