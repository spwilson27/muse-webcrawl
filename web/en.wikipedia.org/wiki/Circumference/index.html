Circumference

In geometry, the circumference is the perimeter of a circle or ellipse — that is, the arc length of the curve traced by its boundary. The term may also refer to the circle itself, the locus of points at a fixed distance from the centre.

Circles

The circumference C of a circle is related to its radius r and diameter d by

C = 2πr = πd,

where π (pi) is the mathematical constant defined as this very ratio. The ratio C/d is the same for all circles, approximately 3.14159. Archimedes, approximating the circle with inscribed and circumscribed 96-sided polygons, bounded π between 223/71 and 22/7. If a circle has diameter 1, its circumference equals π, a fact illustrated by "unrolling" the circle into a line segment.

Ellipses

For an ellipse with semi-major axis a and semi-minor axis b, the circumference has no elementary closed form; it is expressed exactly using the complete elliptic integral of the second kind, C = 4aE(e), where e is the eccentricity. Srinivasa Ramanujan gave celebrated approximations, including

C ≈ π [ 3(a + b) − √((3a + b)(a + 3b)) ],

which is accurate to within a tiny fraction for all but the most elongated ellipses.

Graph theory and other uses

In graph theory, the circumference of a graph is the length of its longest cycle. More generally in everyday usage, circumference denotes the measured distance around any approximately round object — for example, the circumference of the Earth (about 40,075 km at the Equator) or a person's waist or head circumference, used in health and clothing.
