School shooting

A school shooting is an armed attack at an educational institution, such as a primary school, secondary school, high school or university, involving the use of a firearm. Many school shootings are also categorised as mass shootings due to multiple casualties. The phenomenon is most widely reported in the United States, which has the highest number of school-related shootings, though such attacks have occurred in many other countries.

Characteristics

School shootings are most often perpetrated by current or former students of the targeted school, and research profiles commonly note grievances, social rejection, mental health problems, and imitation of earlier attacks amplified by media coverage (the "contagion effect"). Attackers frequently obtain firearms from their own homes or from family members. Most incidents are planned in advance, and in many cases the attacker communicated intentions or threats beforehand, a pattern known as "leakage".

History

Shootings at schools date to the 19th century in the United States, but the modern era of mass school shootings is often dated from the 1966 University of Texas tower shooting and, more influentially, the 1999 Columbine High School massacre in Colorado, which killed 13 people and reshaped public perception and school security practices. Subsequent major attacks include the Virginia Tech shooting (2007), the Sandy Hook Elementary School shooting (2012), the Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School shooting in Parkland, Florida (2018), and the Robb Elementary School shooting in Uvalde, Texas (2022). Outside the US, notable attacks include the Dunblane massacre in Scotland (1996), the Erfurt school massacre in Germany (2002), and the Winnenden school shooting (2009).

Responses and prevention

Responses include "zero tolerance" disciplinary policies, lockdown and active-shooter drills, threat assessment teams, school resource officers, hardened infrastructure, and anonymous reporting systems such as Safe2Tell. Gun control debates intensify after major incidents; the Parkland shooting spurred the student-led March for Our Lives movement. Researchers emphasise behavioural threat assessment and intervention over purely punitive or architectural measures.

Effects

Beyond the immediate casualties, school shootings produce lasting psychological trauma for survivors, families and communities, and affect the broader student population's sense of safety. Exposure to local gun violence has been associated in studies with declines in student enrolment, attendance and academic performance.
