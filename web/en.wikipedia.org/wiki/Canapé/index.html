Canapé

A canapé (French: [kanape]) is a type of hors d'œuvre, a small, prepared, and often decorative finger food consisting of a base of bread, puff pastry, or a cracker topped with some savoury food, held in the fingers and often eaten in one bite. The name comes from the French word for a sofa or couch, upon which the topping "sits" as a person sits on a couch.

Composition

Traditionally, a canapé is built on stale white bread, though other breads, blinis, or pâte feuilletée may be used. The bread is cut into shapes — rounds, squares, triangles, or diamonds — toasted or fried, spread with butter or a flavoured spread, and topped with meat, fish, cheese, caviar, foie gras, purées, or relishes, finished with a garnish. Common examples include smoked salmon on rye with dill, roquefort with walnuts, and devilled egg canapés. A savoury butter, mayonnaise, or cream cheese typically acts both as flavouring and as "glue" holding the topping in place.

Service

Canapés are generally served on a canapé salver at cocktail parties, receptions, and banquets, often before a dinner, and are meant to be eaten standing. In French terminology the technically related amuse-bouche and hors d'œuvre differ in that canapés specifically have a bread or pastry base. Variations with a vegetable or cucumber base are sometimes called canapés as well in modern catering.
