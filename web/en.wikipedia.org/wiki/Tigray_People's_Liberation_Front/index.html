The Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF; Tigrinya: ሕዝባዊ ወያነ ሓርነት ትግራይ) is an Ethiopian political party and former guerrilla movement established in 1975 in the Tigray region. Having led the coalition that overthrew the Derg military regime in 1991, the TPLF dominated Ethiopian politics for nearly three decades as the core of the ruling Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front (EPRDF), before losing power in 2018 and becoming the principal belligerent against the federal government in the Tigray War of 2020–2022.

Guerrilla war (1975–1991)

The TPLF was founded in Dedebit in February 1975 by a small group of Tigrayan nationalists inspired by the Ethiopian student movement. It built a disciplined army and mass organisations in rural Tigray, survived the Derg's offensives, and — after breaking with and defeating rival movements — formed the EPRDF coalition in 1989. In May 1991 EPRDF forces captured Addis Ababa, ending the civil war.

In government (1991–2018)

Under Meles Zenawi, first as president of the transitional government and then as prime minister (1995–2012), the TPLF-led government rebuilt Ethiopia as an ethnic federation, achieved rapid economic growth, and fought a border war with Eritrea (1998–2000). Critics accused it of authoritarianism and human-rights abuses. After Meles's death in 2012, Hailemariam Desalegn succeeded him, but mass protests from 2015 brought reformist Abiy Ahmed to power in 2018; the EPRDF was dissolved into the Prosperity Party in 2019, which the TPLF refused to join.

Tigray War and aftermath

Relations between Mekelle and Addis Ababa collapsed, and in November 2020 war erupted between Tigrayan forces and the Ethiopian National Defense Force, allied with Eritrean troops. The conflict caused mass casualties, displacement and famine conditions before a cessation of hostilities agreement was signed in Pretoria in November 2022, after which the TPLF recognised an interim regional administration and began disarmament, while its legal status remained contested.
