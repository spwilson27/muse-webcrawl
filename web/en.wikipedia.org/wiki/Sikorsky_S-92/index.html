The Sikorsky S-92 is an American twin-engine medium-lift helicopter built by Sikorsky Aircraft for the civil and military helicopter markets. The S-92 was developed from the Sikorsky S-70 helicopter and has similar parts such as flight control and rotor systems.

Development

After the oil crisis of 1973 and the subsequent interest in offshore oil transport, Sikorsky began work on an enlarged civil helicopter based on the Black Hawk. The S-92 first flew on 23 December 1998 and received FAA certification in 2002, entering service in 2004. It seats up to 19 passengers in an airline-style cabin.

Variants and use

The H-92 Superhawk is the military variant, from which the Canadian CH-148 Cyclone maritime helicopter is derived. The S-92 is widely used for offshore oil and gas transport in the North Sea and Gulf of Mexico, search and rescue (notably by the Irish Coast Guard and UK operators), and head-of-state transport. A modified S-92 serves as the basis for the VH-92A, the U.S. Marine Corps' presidential helicopter replacement.
