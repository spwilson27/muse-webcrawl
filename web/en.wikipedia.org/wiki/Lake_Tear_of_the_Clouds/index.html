Lake Tear of the Clouds is a small mountain tarn in the Adirondack High Peaks of New York State, on the southwest slopes of Mount Marcy, the state's highest mountain. At about 4,292 feet above sea level it is often cited as the highest lake in New York and is regarded as the highest source of the Hudson River.

Geography and history

The lake drains through Feldspar Brook into the Opalescent River, a tributary of the infant Hudson. It was named by surveyor Verplanck Colvin, who described the lake during his Adirondack surveys in the 1870s, drawing on a poetic rendering of its sky-reflecting appearance among the peaks.

The lake figures in a famous episode of American history: in September 1901, Vice President Theodore Roosevelt was hiking on Mount Marcy when a messenger reached him at Lake Tear of the Clouds with news that President William McKinley, shot by an assassin in Buffalo, was dying. Roosevelt descended from the mountain and travelled through the night by wagon and train to Buffalo, where he took the presidential oath of office.
