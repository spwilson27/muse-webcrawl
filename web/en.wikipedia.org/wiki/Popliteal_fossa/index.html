Popliteal fossa

Popliteal fossa
Part of: Lower limb
System: Musculoskeletal

The popliteal fossa (sometimes referred to as the kneepit in analogy with the armpit) is a shallow depression located at the back of the knee joint. It is a diamond-shaped space that serves as an important passageway for structures passing between the thigh and the leg.

Boundaries

The fossa is bounded superolaterally by the biceps femoris, superomedially by the semimembranosus and semitendinosus muscles, and inferiorly by the two heads of the gastrocnemius. Its floor is formed by the popliteal surface of the femur, the posterior capsule of the knee joint, and the popliteus muscle; its roof consists of skin and fascia.

Contents

The popliteal fossa contains the popliteal vessels (the popliteal artery and vein), the tibial and common fibular (peroneal) nerves, the small saphenous vein, the posterior femoral cutaneous nerve, popliteal lymph nodes, and fat.

Clinical significance

Swellings in the fossa may arise from a Baker's cyst, popliteal aneurysm, or enlarged lymph nodes. The popliteal pulse is palpated deep within the fossa with the knee flexed.
