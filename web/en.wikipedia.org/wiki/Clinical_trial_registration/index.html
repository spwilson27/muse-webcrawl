Clinical trial registration

Clinical trial registration is the practice of publishing information about a clinical trial in a publicly accessible registry before or shortly after the trial begins. Registration is intended to prevent publication bias and selective outcome reporting, to help patients and researchers find ongoing studies, and to make the existence of every trial a matter of public record.

[Image: A registry entry for a clinical trial showing its identifier and study details]

History

Calls for trial registration date to the 1980s, but the decisive shift came in 2004–2005, when the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) announced that its journals would publish only trials registered prospectively in a public registry. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration Amendments Act of 2007 expanded the legal requirement to register trials and report results to ClinicalTrials.gov, maintained by the National Library of Medicine. The World Health Organization's International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (ICTRP) links primary registries worldwide and defines a minimum 24-item trial registration data set.

Requirements

Registries typically require the trial's sponsor, interventions, eligibility criteria, primary and secondary outcomes, target sample size, and study design before enrolment begins. Many jurisdictions additionally require results reporting within a set period after completion. Each registered trial receives a unique identifier, such as an NCT number, which must be cited in publications.

Compliance and criticism

Audits, including the FDAAA TrialsTracker and EU Trials Tracker projects, have repeatedly found that many sponsors fail to report results on time, and enforcement penalties have historically been rare. Advocates argue registration is necessary but not sufficient: prespecified analysis plans and full results disclosure are needed to fully counter reporting bias.
