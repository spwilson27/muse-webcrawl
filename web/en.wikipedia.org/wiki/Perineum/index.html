Perineum

In human anatomy, the perineum is the space between the anus and the scrotum in males, or between the anus and the vulva in females. The perineal region is diamond-shaped, bounded by the pubic symphysis, ischial tuberosities and coccyx, and is divided by the transverse perineal muscles into an anterior urogenital triangle and a posterior anal triangle.

Structure

The perineum contains the perineal body, a central fibromuscular mass, and is traversed by structures of the urinary, genital and digestive systems. Its muscles include the superficial and deep transverse perineal muscles, bulbospongiosus and ischiocavernosus, supplied by the pudendal nerve and internal pudendal vessels.

Clinical significance

The perineum may tear during childbirth; obstetric perineal tears are graded from first to fourth degree, and an episiotomy is a surgical incision sometimes made to enlarge the vaginal opening. Perineal trauma, abscesses and pudendal nerve entrapment are also clinically relevant.
