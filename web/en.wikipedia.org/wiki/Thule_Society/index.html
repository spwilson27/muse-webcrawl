The Thule Society (German: Thule-Gesellschaft) was a German occultist and völkisch group founded in Munich in 1918 by Rudolf von Sebottendorff. Named after Thule, a mythical northern land in Greek legend, the society combined Germanic mythology, Ariosophy, and extreme nationalism and antisemitism.

Activities and influence

The society bought the newspaper that became the Münchener Beobachter and later the Nazi Party's Völkischer Beobachter. It sponsored the German Workers' Party (DAP), the precursor of the Nazi Party, and several early figures in Adolf Hitler's circle moved in its milieu, though Hitler himself was never a member and later dismissed occultism in politics.

Decline

The Thule Society lost influence after 1919, when the Nazi Party eclipsed it, and its members were caught up in the counter-revolutionary violence of post-war Munich, including the murder of seven members by the Red Army during the Bavarian Soviet Republic. Post-war occult literature greatly exaggerated the society's role in the rise of Nazism, a mythology historians treat with caution.
