AN/AWG-9

The AN/AWG-9 is an American pulse-Doppler radar and weapons control system developed by Hughes Aircraft for the Grumman F-14 Tomcat fighter aircraft. The designation follows the Joint Electronics Type Designation System: AN/ for Army-Navy, A for piloted aircraft, W for armament, G for fire control.

Capabilities

The AWG-9 was revolutionary for its track-while-scan ability: it could track up to 24 airborne targets and guide up to six AIM-54 Phoenix long-range missiles against them simultaneously. Its large antenna and powerful transmitter gave it detection ranges exceeding 100 nautical miles (about 190 km) against bomber-sized targets. It also provided look-down/shoot-down capability against low-flying targets, and could guide Sparrow and Sidewinder missiles.

History

The system grew out of Hughes' work on the cancelled XF-108 and F-111B programs. It served on US Navy F-14s from the 1970s until the Tomcat's 2006 retirement, and on Iranian F-14s delivered before 1979. The later AN/APG-71 upgrade, fitted to the F-14D, incorporated a fully digital architecture derived from the F-15's APG-70.
