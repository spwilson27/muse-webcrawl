Infrared homing

Infrared homing is a passive weapon guidance system which uses the infrared (IR) light emission from a target to track and follow it seamlessly. Missiles using infrared homing are often called "heat-seeking" missiles, since infrared radiation is strongly emitted by hot bodies such as jet and rocket exhausts.

How it works

The missile's seeker head contains an infrared sensor, in early designs a lead sulfide detector spinning behind a reticle, in modern designs a focal-plane imaging array. The seeker measures the direction to the heat source and generates steering commands that keep the target centred. Seeker cooling, with compressed gas or cryogenic systems, increases sensitivity.

Use and countermeasures

Infrared-guided missiles such as the AIM-9 Sidewinder, R-73 and man-portable systems like Stinger are mainly short-range weapons. Targets defend themselves with flares, which produce competing heat signatures, and with directional infrared countermeasures; modern imaging seekers can discriminate flares from real targets.
