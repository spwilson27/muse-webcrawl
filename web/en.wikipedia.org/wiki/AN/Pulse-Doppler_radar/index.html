Pulse-Doppler radar

Pulse-Doppler radar is a radar system that combines the features of pulse radar and continuous-wave radar, using the Doppler effect to determine the velocity of targets and to reject clutter. This page is filed under the AN/ designation path (AN/Pulse-Doppler radar).

Principle

By measuring the frequency shift of successive returned pulses, pulse-Doppler processing separates moving targets from stationary ground clutter that would overwhelm a simple pulse radar. This gives airborne radars a "look-down/shoot-down" capability: detecting low-flying aircraft against the ground below.

Characteristics

Performance depends on the pulse repetition frequency: high-PRF modes give unambiguous velocity but ambiguous range, medium-PRF modes balance both, and blind speeds and zones are managed by PRF staggering and digital signal processing. Fighter radars such as the AN/APG-63 and AN/APG-68 are classic pulse-Doppler designs. See also Doppler effect and Radar.
