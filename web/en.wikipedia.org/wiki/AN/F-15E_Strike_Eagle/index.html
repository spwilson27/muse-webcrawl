McDonnell Douglas F-15E Strike Eagle

F-15E Strike Eagle
Type: Multirole strike fighter
Manufacturer: McDonnell Douglas / Boeing
First flight: 11 December 1986
Crew: 2 (pilot and weapon systems officer)

The McDonnell Douglas F-15E Strike Eagle is an American all-weather multirole strike fighter derived from the McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle. It was designed in the 1980s for long-range, high-speed interdiction without requiring escort or electronic-warfare aircraft.

Design

The F-15E adds a second seat for a weapon systems officer, conformal fuel tanks along the fuselage, and strengthened structure for heavier payloads. Its APG-70 (later APG-82) radar and LANTIRN targeting pods allow precision attacks at night and in poor weather with laser- and GPS-guided weapons, while it retains the air-to-air capability of the F-15.

Service

The Strike Eagle entered service in 1989 and saw extensive combat in the 1991 Gulf War, the Balkans, Afghanistan, Iraq, Libya and Syria. Derivatives such as the F-15I, F-15K, F-15SG, F-15SA and F-15QA serve with allied air forces, and the related F-15EX Eagle II was adopted by the USAF in the 2020s.
