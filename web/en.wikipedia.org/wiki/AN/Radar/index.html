Radar

Radar is a radiolocation system that uses radio waves to determine the distance (ranging), direction (azimuth and elevation), and radial velocity of objects relative to the site. The word is an acronym of "radio detection and ranging". A radar system consists of a transmitter producing electromagnetic waves, a transmitting antenna, a receiving antenna, and a receiver and processor to determine properties of the objects.

Principles

The transmitter emits pulses or continuous waves; a fraction of the energy scattered by a target returns to the receiver. Range follows from the round-trip travel time of the signal, and the Doppler shift of the echo gives the target's radial velocity. Modern radars use phased-array antennas, pulse compression and digital signal processing to track many targets.

Applications

Radar was developed in secrecy by several nations before and during the Second World War and proved decisive in air defence. Today it is used for air traffic control, weather forecasting, marine navigation, speed enforcement, astronomy, and military surveillance and weapon guidance.
