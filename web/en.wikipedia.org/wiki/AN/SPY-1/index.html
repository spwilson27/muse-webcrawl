The AN/SPY-1 is a United States naval radar system manufactured by Lockheed Martin. It is a passive electronically scanned array (PESA) operating in the S band and forms the primary air-search and fire-control sensor of the Aegis Combat System fitted to Ticonderoga-class cruisers and Arleigh Burke-class destroyers, as well as to allied warships in Japan, Spain, Norway, South Korea, and Australia.

Description

The system uses four fixed, octagonal antenna faces mounted on the ship's superstructure, each containing roughly 4,000 radiating elements, to provide continuous 360-degree coverage. SPY-1 can search for, detect, and simultaneously track well over a hundred targets — aircraft, cruise missiles, and ballistic missiles — while guiding the ship's Standard missiles. Successive variants (SPY-1A through SPY-1D(V)) improved processing, resistance to clutter and jamming, and performance in littoral environments and against ballistic missile targets, in support of Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense.

Successor

The SPY-1 is being supplemented and succeeded by the AN/SPY-6, an active electronically scanned array (AESA) built from scalable radar module assemblies, which equips new Flight III Arleigh Burke-class destroyers and offers substantially greater sensitivity.
