AIM-120 AMRAAM

AIM-120 AMRAAM
Type: Beyond-visual-range air-to-air missile
Manufacturer: Raytheon
In service: 1991–present
Guidance: Inertial, midcourse updates, active radar terminal

The AIM-120 Advanced Medium-Range Air-to-Air Missile (AMRAAM) is an American beyond-visual-range air-to-air missile capable of all-weather day-and-night operations. It is a "fire-and-forget" weapon: its active radar seeker allows the launching aircraft to turn away after firing.

Development and design

The AMRAAM was developed from the late 1970s to replace the AIM-7 Sparrow, entering service in 1991. The missile is guided in its midcourse phase by inertial navigation aided by datalink updates from the launch aircraft, then switches to its own monopulse radar for the terminal phase. Successive variants, including the AIM-120C and the longer-ranged AIM-120D, improved the seeker, range and resistance to countermeasures.

Use

The AMRAAM is carried by most Western fighters, including the F-15, F-16, F/A-18, F-22, F-35, Eurofighter Typhoon and Gripen, and recorded its first combat victory in December 1992. A surface-launched version forms the basis of the NASAMS air defence system.
