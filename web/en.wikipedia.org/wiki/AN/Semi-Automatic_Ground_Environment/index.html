Semi-Automatic Ground Environment

SAGE
Type: Continental air-defence command and control system
Operator: United States Air Force / NORAD
Operational: 1958–1983
Computer: AN/FSQ-7

The Semi-Automatic Ground Environment (SAGE) was a system of large computers and associated networking equipment that coordinated data from many radar sites and processed it to produce a single unified image of the airspace over North America. It was operated by NORAD for air defence against Soviet bombers during the Cold War.

System

Radar stations sent digitised track data over telephone lines to Direction Centers, where AN/FSQ-7 computers — the largest computers ever built, developed from MIT's Whirlwind project and manufactured by IBM — maintained the air picture and computed interception courses for fighters and Bomarc missiles. Operators worked at consoles with light guns, an early form of interactive computing.

Legacy

Beginning operation in 1958, SAGE pioneered real-time computing, magnetic core memory, computer networking, and dual-redundant fault-tolerant design, and drove major advances at IBM and in the young computer industry. It was gradually replaced in the early 1980s.
