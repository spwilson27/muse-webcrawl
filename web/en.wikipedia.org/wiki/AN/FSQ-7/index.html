AN/FSQ-7 Combat Direction Central

AN/FSQ-7
Type: Vacuum-tube military computer
Manufacturer: IBM
Introduced: 1958
Used in: SAGE air defence system

The AN/FSQ-7 Combat Direction Central, informally the Q7, was a vacuum-tube computer developed for the United States Air Force's SAGE (Semi-Automatic Ground Environment) air defence system. Built by IBM, it was the largest computer system ever built.

Description

Each of the 24 SAGE Direction Centers housed a duplex pair of Q7s, each weighing about 275 tons, containing some 49,000 vacuum tubes and occupying much of a four-storey blockhouse, while using about 3 megawatts of power. Operators at light-gun consoles tracked incoming aircraft on radar displays fed from distant radar stations over telephone lines.

Legacy

Operational from 1958 into the early 1980s, the AN/FSQ-7 pioneered real-time computing, magnetic-core memory at scale, interactive displays and modem data links, and its development earned IBM the experience that fed into its commercial mainframe business. See also SAGE.
