AIM-7 Sparrow

AIM-7 Sparrow
Designation (Joint Electronics Type Designation System): AN/AIM-7
Type: Medium-range air-to-air missile
Manufacturer: Raytheon
In service: 1958–present
Guidance: Semi-active radar homing

The AIM-7 Sparrow (designated AN/AIM-7 under the Joint Electronics Type Designation System) is an American medium-range, semi-active radar homing air-to-air missile operated by the United States Air Force, Navy and Marine Corps, as well as many allied air forces.

Development

The Sparrow emerged from a Navy programme begun in the late 1940s; the first successful intercept came in 1952 and the missile entered service in 1958. Early beam-riding versions performed poorly in Vietnam, prompting the much-improved AIM-7E and later the AIM-7F and AIM-7M with monopulse seekers and solid-state electronics.

Use and derivatives

The Sparrow scored numerous victories in the 1991 Gulf War and formed the basis of the RIM-7 Sea Sparrow shipborne point-defence missile and the later Evolved Sea Sparrow Missile. It has been progressively replaced in the beyond-visual-range role by the active-radar AIM-120 AMRAAM. See also AIM-120 AMRAAM.
