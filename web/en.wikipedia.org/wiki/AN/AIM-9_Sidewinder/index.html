AIM-9 Sidewinder

AIM-9 Sidewinder
Designation (Joint Electronics Type Designation System): AN/AIM-9
Type: Short-range air-to-air missile
Manufacturer: Raytheon and others
In service: 1956–present
Guidance: Infrared homing

The AIM-9 Sidewinder (designated AN/AIM-9 under the Joint Electronics Type Designation System) is a short-range air-to-air missile developed by the United States Navy at China Lake and one of the oldest, cheapest and most successful air-to-air missiles, with more than 110,000 produced for 27 nations.

Design

The Sidewinder homes on the infrared signature of a target's engines. Its famously simple design used rollerons on the tail fins for gyroscopic stabilisation. Successive variants improved the seeker from rear-aspect-only to all-aspect capability; the current AIM-9X adds an imaging infrared focal-plane array, thrust vectoring and helmet-mounted cueing for high off-boresight shots.

Combat history

First used in combat over the Taiwan Strait in 1958, the Sidewinder has been credited with hundreds of aerial victories in Vietnam, the Middle East, the Falklands and the Gulf War. See also Infrared homing.
