Gertrude Lawrence (4 July 1898 – 6 September 1952) was an English stage actress, singer and musical comedy performer who was one of the great stars of the theatre in Britain and the United States between the wars and in the 1940s. Remembered for her wit, elegance and luminous stage presence, she was the lifelong friend and favourite leading lady of Noël Coward and created the role of Anna Leonowens in Rodgers and Hammerstein's The King and I.

Career

Born Gertrud Alexandra Dagmar Lawrence Klasen in London, the daughter of a Danish-born builder and a singer, she went on stage in childhood and trained at Italia Conti's school. She understudied Beatrice Lillie in André Charlot's revues and rose to stardom in Charlot's Revue of 1924, which transferred to Broadway and made her famous in America as well as London. Coward wrote for her the comedy Private Lives (1930), in which they starred together in London and New York, and the cycle of one-act plays Tonight at 8.30 (1936).

In the 1930s and 1940s Lawrence headlined in musicals and plays on both sides of the Atlantic, including Coward's Nymph Errant, the Moss Hart–Kurt Weill–Ira Gershwin musical Lady in the Dark (1941), in which she introduced "The Saga of Jenny", and a celebrated run as Eliza Doolittle in Pygmalion. Her film appearances were comparatively few but included the screen version of The Glass Menagerie (1950).

The King and I and death

It was Lawrence who brought Margaret Landon's novel Anna and the King of Siam to Richard Rodgers and Oscar Hammerstein II, and she opened as Anna in The King and I on Broadway in March 1951, opposite Yul Brynner, winning the Tony Award for Best Actress. During the run her health failed; she died suddenly in New York on 6 September 1952 of what was found to be liver cancer, and was buried in the gold dress from the "Shall We Dance?" number. Julie Andrews portrayed her in the 1968 biographical film Star!
