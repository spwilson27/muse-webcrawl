Clutter family murders

The Clutter family murders took place in Holcomb, Kansas, on November 15, 1959, when four members of the Clutter family — Herbert Clutter, his wife Bonnie, and their teenage children Nancy and Kenyon — were murdered in their home by ex-convicts Perry Smith and Richard ("Dick") Hickock. The killings and their aftermath became famous through Truman Capote's 1966 non-fiction novel In Cold Blood.

The murders

Hickock had learned from a former cellmate, Floyd Wells, who had once worked for Herbert Clutter, that Clutter supposedly kept a safe with large amounts of cash at his farmhouse. Shortly after midnight on November 15, Smith and Hickock entered the unlocked house, bound and gagged the family, and — finding no safe and only about $50 — killed all four victims, cutting Herbert Clutter's throat and shooting each of the others with a shotgun.

Investigation and trial

The Kansas Bureau of Investigation, led by agent Alvin Dewey, pursued the case; Wells came forward after hearing news reports, and Smith and Hickock were arrested in Las Vegas on December 30, 1959. Both men confessed — Smith later said he alone killed all four victims. They were tried in Garden City, Kansas, convicted of capital murder in March 1960, and, after years of appeals, hanged at the Kansas State Penitentiary in Lansing on April 14, 1965.

In Cold Blood

Capote, accompanied by Harper Lee, spent years researching the case in Kansas, interviewing residents, investigators, and the killers. In Cold Blood, serialised in The New Yorker in 1965 and published in book form in 1966, was a landmark of the "nonfiction novel" and New Journalism, and has been adapted several times for film and television.
