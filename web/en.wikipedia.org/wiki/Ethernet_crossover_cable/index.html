Ethernet crossover cable

Ethernet crossover cable
Type: Networking cable
Connector: 8P8C (RJ45)
Wiring: T568A on one end, T568B on the other
Purpose: Connect two similar devices directly

An Ethernet crossover cable is a type of twisted-pair Ethernet cable used to connect two computing devices of the same type directly to each other, such as two computers or two switches, without an intermediate hub or switch. It does this by crossing over the transmit and receive pairs so that the transmitter of one device feeds the receiver of the other.

Wiring

In a standard straight-through patch cable both ends follow the same wiring scheme (either T568A or T568B). A crossover cable uses T568A on one end and T568B on the other, which swaps the orange and green pairs—the transmit and receive pairs for 10BASE-T and 100BASE-TX. Gigabit Ethernet uses all four pairs bidirectionally, so a different form of crossover applies, though it is rarely needed in practice.

Decline in use

Since the widespread adoption of Auto MDI-X, in which network ports automatically detect and adapt to the cable type, crossover cables have become largely unnecessary. Most modern Ethernet equipment, and all gigabit equipment (where Auto MDI-X is mandatory), will link correctly with an ordinary straight-through cable.
