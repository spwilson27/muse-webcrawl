Mining accident

A mining accident is an accident that occurs during the process of mining minerals or metals. Thousands of miners die from mining accidents each year, especially in underground coal mining, though accidents also occur in hard rock and surface mining. Common causes include explosions of firedamp and coal dust, roof collapses, flooding, suffocation by toxic gases, and failures of hoisting and transport machinery.

Causes

Underground coal mines are especially hazardous because of methane (firedamp) and airborne coal dust, both explosive; ignition can produce devastating explosions. Other major hazards include falls of roof and rib, inundation from old workings, carbon monoxide poisoning ("afterdamp"), outbursts of gas and rock, and seismic or "bump" events. Surface mines present risks from slope failures, vehicle collisions and blasting.

Notable disasters

Among the deadliest mining disasters in history are the Benxihu colliery explosion in China in 1942 (more than 1,500 deaths), the Courrières disaster in France in 1906 (1,099 deaths), and the Monongah disaster in the United States in 1907. More recent events, such as the Sago Mine disaster (2006), the Upper Big Branch explosion (2010) and the 2010 Copiapó accident in Chile — in which 33 miners were rescued alive after 69 days underground — drove reforms in mine safety law and rescue practice.

Prevention and rescue

Safety measures include ventilation, gas monitoring, rock dusting, safety lamps and later electronic detectors, refuge chambers, self-rescue breathing apparatus and organised mine rescue teams. Many countries maintain specialised inspectorates and legislation, such as the US Mine Safety and Health Administration.
