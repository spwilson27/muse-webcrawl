Galen John Strawson (born 1952) is a British analytic philosopher and literary critic who works primarily on philosophy of mind, metaphysics (including freedom, determinism, and the philosophy of self), and the philosophy of John Locke. He is the son of the philosopher P. F. Strawson. Until 2016 he held the Chair in Metaphysics at the University of Reading, and he has also taught at the University of Oxford, the City University of New York Graduate Center, and the University of Texas at Austin.

Philosophical work

Strawson is known for his defence of panpsychism, the view that consciousness is a fundamental and ubiquitous feature of the physical world. In the paper "Realistic Monism: Why Physicalism Entails Panpsychism" (2006) and in his book Mental Reality (1994), he argues that a genuinely physicalist account of the world must take conscious experience — which we know directly in our own case — to be a real physical phenomenon, and that since we cannot explain how experience emerges from wholly non-experiential matter, experience must be present at the fundamental level of reality.

He is also a prominent sceptic about free will. His "basic argument" holds that true moral responsibility for one's actions would require being responsible for the way one is — one's character and motives — which would require an impossible infinite regress of self-creation. Strawson's position is that free will of the kind needed for ultimate moral responsibility is impossible whether determinism is true or false.

In his widely discussed 2004 paper "Against Narrativity", Strawson distinguished between diachronic and episodic forms of self-experience. Against the popular thesis that human beings naturally experience their lives as narratives, he argued that some people — episodic types like himself — do not think of their past or future selves as continuous with their present self, and that this is neither a defect nor an obstacle to a good life.
