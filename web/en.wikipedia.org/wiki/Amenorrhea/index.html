Amenorrhea (also spelled amenorrhoea) is the absence of a menstrual period in a female of reproductive age. Physiological states of amenorrhea are seen during pregnancy and lactation (breastfeeding), the latter also forming the basis of a form of contraception known as the lactational amenorrhea method. Outside the reproductive years, there is an absence of menstruation before puberty and after menopause.

Classification

Amenorrhea is classified as primary when menstruation has never occurred by age 15 (or by age 13 in the absence of secondary sexual development), and secondary when menstruation ceases for three months in a person with previously regular cycles, or six months in a person with irregular cycles.

Causes

The most common cause of secondary amenorrhea is pregnancy. Other causes include polycystic ovary syndrome, functional hypothalamic amenorrhea (from stress, weight loss, or excessive exercise), hyperprolactinaemia, thyroid disorders, primary ovarian insufficiency, and hormonal contraception. Primary amenorrhea may result from Müllerian agenesis, Turner syndrome, or androgen insensitivity syndrome.

Diagnosis and treatment

Evaluation typically includes a pregnancy test and measurement of hormones such as TSH, prolactin, FSH, and LH, along with a clinical history and pelvic examination or imaging where indicated. Treatment is directed at the underlying cause and may include lifestyle modification, hormonal therapy, or surgery. Prolonged hypoestrogenic amenorrhea increases the risk of osteoporosis.
