Alberic of Cîteaux

Alberic, second abbot of Cîteaux, in a medieval miniature

Alberic of Cîteaux (died 26 January 1109), sometimes called Aubrey of Cîteaux, was a French monk and one of the founders of the Cistercian Order. A member of the community Robert of Molesme led out of Molesme Abbey in 1098 to found the New Monastery of Cîteaux in Burgundy, Alberic succeeded Robert as abbot in 1099 and guided the fledgling house through its precarious first decade.

Abbacy

When Robert was ordered by the pope to return to Molesme in 1099, Alberic was elected abbot in his place. He obtained papal protection for the community from Pope Paschal II in 1100 and is traditionally credited with adopting the white habit for Cîteaux's monks — made from undyed wool — distinguishing them from the black-habited Benedictines and giving the Cistercians their nickname of "White Monks". Under Alberic the community adopted stricter observance of the Rule of St Benedict, manual labour and a simple diet; vocations nevertheless remained few, and the abbey's survival was uncertain until new recruits, including Stephen Harding and, later, Bernard of Clairvaux, arrived.

Veneration

Alberic is venerated as a saint together with Robert of Molesme and Stephen Harding, the three founders of the Cistercian Order, with a shared feast day on 26 January.
