The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation Limited, commonly known as HSBC, is a Hong Kong-based multinational bank and financial services company. It was the founding member of the HSBC Group and remains the group's largest subsidiary in the Asia-Pacific region. The bank was established in 1865 in Hong Kong and Shanghai to finance the growing trade between China and Europe.

History

The bank was founded by Scotsman Sir Thomas Sutherland, then superintendent of the Peninsular and Oriental Steam Navigation Company, who saw the need for local banking facilities in Hong Kong and on the China coast. The bank opened in Hong Kong on 3 March 1865 and in Shanghai a month later. It was the first bank to be established under local, rather than British, company law.

HSBC played a pioneering role in the development of modern banking in Asia. It financed trade, issued banknotes, and made loans to governments for infrastructure projects such as railways. The bank survived the Second World War, during which its headquarters and records were caught up in the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong, and re-emerged as a central institution in Hong Kong's post-war economic growth.

In 1990 the group established HSBC Holdings plc in London as a parent company to facilitate the acquisition of Midland Bank in the United Kingdom; the Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation became a wholly owned subsidiary. The bank remains one of the three note-issuing banks in Hong Kong, alongside the Bank of China (Hong Kong) and Standard Chartered.

Operations

The bank provides a full range of personal, commercial, corporate, and private banking services in Hong Kong and across the Asia-Pacific region. Its headquarters, the HSBC Main Building at 1 Queen's Road Central in Hong Kong's Central district, completed in 1985 to a design by Norman Foster, is regarded as a landmark of high-tech architecture. The building is guarded by a pair of bronze lions, nicknamed Stephen and Stitt, which have become symbols of the bank.

Hang Seng Bank

In 1965, the bank acquired a controlling stake in Hang Seng Bank, which it had rescued during a banking crisis; Hang Seng remains a listed subsidiary of the group and operates under its own brand in Hong Kong.
