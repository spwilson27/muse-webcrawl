Pulitzer Prize

[Image: The Pulitzer gold medal for Public Service]

The Pulitzer Prizes are annual American awards for journalism, literature and music, established by the will of publisher Joseph Pulitzer and first awarded in 1917, administered by Columbia University. The journalism categories — including Public Service, Breaking News, Investigative and Explanatory Reporting — are the most prestigious honours in US news.

Mechanics and landmarks

Entries are judged by juries and a board; the Public Service gold medal goes to the news organisation rather than an individual. Landmark winners range from Watergate (the Washington Post, 1973) to the first online winner (ProPublica, 2010) and collaborative cross-border investigations.
