Mambo

Mambo is a Latin dance of Cuban origin that corresponds to the music of the same name, developed in Havana in the 1940s. It grew out of the Cuban danzón tradition and became an international dance craze in the 1950s, particularly in the United States.

Music

Mambo music combines elements of Cuban son and danzón with the big band jazz of the United States. The style was crystallised by bandleader Dámaso Pérez Prado, often called the "King of Mambo", whose recordings such as "Mambo No. 5" and "Cherry Pink (and Apple Blossom White)" became worldwide hits. Other major figures included Tito Puente and Machito. The music is characterised by driving brass riffs, syncopated rhythms and short repeated melodic patterns called guajeos.

Dance

The mambo dance is a fast, energetic partner dance closely related to salsa, from which it differs in its breaking pattern: mambo dancers step forward and back on the second beat of the measure (dancing "on 2"), while basic salsa is typically danced "on 1". The dance features sharp hip movements, turns, and improvisational shines in which partners separate and dance solo steps.

Legacy

Although the mambo craze faded in the early 1960s, the music and dance heavily influenced the development of salsa in New York, and mambo remains a competitive ballroom dance style and a staple of Latin dance clubs.
