Gethsemane

Gethsemane (Greek: Γεθσημανή, Gethsēmanē; from Aramaic Gaṯ Šəmānē, "oil press") is a garden at the foot of the Mount of Olives in Jerusalem, across the Kidron Valley from the Old City. According to the four Gospels of the New Testament, Jesus prayed there with his disciples on the night before his crucifixion, in the episode known as the Agony in the Garden, and it was there that he was arrested after being identified by Judas Iscariot.

New Testament account

After the Last Supper, Jesus went with his disciples to Gethsemane, where he asked Peter, James and John to keep watch while he prayed in anguish: "My soul is overwhelmed with sorrow to the point of death" (Mark 14:34). Luke's Gospel describes his sweat becoming "like drops of blood falling to the ground". The disciples repeatedly fell asleep; then Judas arrived with an armed crowd, identified Jesus with a kiss, and the arrest followed, during which a servant of the high priest had his ear cut off.

The garden today

The traditional site, cared for by the Franciscans since the seventeenth century, contains a grove of eight very ancient olive trees — radiocarbon studies of their roots suggest some may be around 900 years old, among the oldest known broad-leaved trees. Beside the garden stands the Church of All Nations (Basilica of the Agony), built in 1919–1924 over the remains of earlier Byzantine and Crusader churches, in front of whose altar lies the exposed rock traditionally identified as the place of Jesus's prayer. Nearby are the Grotto of Gethsemane and the Church of the Tomb of the Virgin Mary. An alternative site north of the Old City, known as the Garden Tomb area's Gethsemane, was favoured by some nineteenth-century Protestant visitors, but the traditional site at the foot of the Mount of Olives is supported by the earliest pilgrim accounts.

In culture

The name Gethsemane has become a byword for a place or occasion of deep suffering and prayer. It appears in hymns (such as "Go to Dark Gethsemane"), in the music of Andrew Lloyd Webber's Jesus Christ Superstar, and as the namesake of gardens, churches and cemeteries around the world.
