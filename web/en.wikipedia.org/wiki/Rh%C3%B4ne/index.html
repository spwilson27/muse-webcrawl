The Rhône is a major river of Europe, 813 kilometres (505 miles) long, rising in the Swiss Alps and flowing into the Mediterranean Sea. It is one of the great rivers of France and the largest river flowing to the Mediterranean from the European mainland by discharge.

Course

The river begins at the Rhône Glacier in the canton of Valais, flows through Lake Geneva, and enters France, where it is joined at Lyon by its chief tributary, the Saône. Turning south, it passes Valence, Avignon, and Arles before dividing into the Grand Rhône and Petit Rhône, which enclose the marshy Camargue delta.

Use and development

Since the mid-20th century a series of hydroelectric dams and locks operated by the Compagnie Nationale du Rhône has made the river navigable to Lyon and a major source of power and irrigation. The Rhône valley is also a celebrated wine region, home to appellations such as Côte-Rôtie, Hermitage, and Châteauneuf-du-Pape, and the fierce mistral wind funnels down its lower course.
