Al-Masīḥ ad-Dajjāl (Arabic: المسيح الدجّال, "the Deceitful Messiah"), usually shortened to ad-Dajjal, is an evil figure in Islamic eschatology, comparable to the Antichrist in Christian tradition. According to the hadith, he will appear before the Day of Judgement, claiming to be the Messiah (and, in some reports, God), performing deceptive miracles, and gathering followers before being defeated by the returning Jesus (Isa) and the Mahdi.

Descriptions in the hadith

The Dajjal is not mentioned in the Quran but is described extensively in hadith collections. He is said to be blind in one eye — often depicted as one-eyed, with the word kāfir ("disbeliever") visible on his forehead, legible to every believer. He will emerge from the east, traditionally from the direction of Khurasan or Isfahan, travel the earth rapidly, bring rain and famine, and produce a false paradise and a false hell. Traditions warn that his greatest trial (fitna) will deceive many, and the Prophet Muhammad is reported to have taught prayers seeking refuge from his trial; recitation of the opening verses of Surah Al-Kahf is recommended as protection.

Defeat

In the standard eschatological sequence, the Mahdi will lead the Muslims, Isa will descend at a white minaret in Damascus, pray behind the Mahdi, and kill the Dajjal at the gate of Lud (Lod, in modern Israel). The Dajjal's appearance is counted among the major signs of the Hour.

Interpretations

Classical and modern scholars have debated whether the Dajjal is an individual, a system, or a symbol of deception and materialism; some modernist readings treat him allegorically, while tradition holds him to be a literal person. The figure has been invoked throughout Islamic history during periods of crisis and in contemporary popular culture.
