The Konkan, also called the Konkan Coast, is a rugged stretch of the west coast of India, running from around Raigad in the north to Mangalore in the south. It lies between the Western Ghats mountain range and the Arabian Sea, covering coastal districts of Maharashtra, Goa and Karnataka.

Geography

The region is a narrow strip of lowland, generally no more than 50 km wide, crossed by many short rivers flowing down from the Ghats. It receives heavy monsoon rainfall and is known for laterite plateaus, coconut palms and estuaries.

Culture

The Konkan is the homeland of the Konkani language, though Marathi and Kannada are also widely spoken. The culture blends Hindu, Catholic and Muslim influences, with cuisine centred on rice, coconut and seafood. Major cities include Mumbai, Panaji, Ratnagiri and Mangalore.

Transport

The Konkan Railway, completed in 1998, runs along the coast through difficult terrain of tunnels and viaducts, linking Mumbai with Mangalore.
