William Steig (14 November 1907 – 3 October 2003) was an American cartoonist, illustrator and writer of children's books. In 1969 he won the Caldecott Medal for Sylvester and the Magic Pebble, and his 1990 picture book Shrek! became the basis of the DreamWorks animated film franchise.

Cartooning career

Steig, the son of Polish-Jewish immigrant parents in Brooklyn, began contributing cartoons to The New Yorker in 1930 and became one of its most prolific artists, producing more than 1,600 drawings and over 100 covers across seven decades. His books of drawings for adults, such as The Lonely Ones (1942), popularised expressive "symbol" drawings of emotional states.

Children's books

Encouraged by editor Ursula Nordstrom, Steig began writing picture books at the age of 61. His spare stories and rich vocabulary won wide acclaim: Sylvester and the Magic Pebble (Caldecott Medal), Amos & Boris, Abel's Island (a Newbery Honor novel), Doctor De Soto and Shrek!, the tale of an ogre who finds love, which he published at 82. Steig continued writing into his nineties, with When Everybody Wore a Hat appearing in 2003.

Legacy

Steig is remembered for treating child readers as intelligent equals and for a body of work that twice — in cartoons and in picture books — reshaped an American art form.
