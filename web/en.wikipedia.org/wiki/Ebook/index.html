Ebook

An ebook (short for electronic book) is a book publication made available in digital form, consisting of text, images or both, readable on computers and other electronic devices such as dedicated e-readers, tablets and smartphones. Commercially produced ebooks are usually distributed over the Internet or on physical media and may be protected by digital rights management (DRM).

History

The concept predates the modern Internet: Project Gutenberg, founded by Michael S. Hart in 1971, began digitising texts such as the United States Declaration of Independence. Early dedicated readers appeared in the late 1990s, including the Rocket eBook and SoftBook. Sony's readers and, decisively, Amazon's Kindle (2007) — with its E Ink display and built-in bookstore — brought ebooks into the mainstream. Ebook sales grew rapidly in the early 2010s before levelling off as a substantial minority of the trade book market.

Formats

Common formats include EPUB, an open standard maintained by the World Wide Web Consortium; Amazon's Kindle formats (AZW, KFX); PDF, which preserves fixed layout; and plain text and HTML. E-readers typically use electrophoretic ("electronic paper") displays that are readable in sunlight and consume very little power.

Advantages and issues

Ebooks allow a whole library to be carried in one lightweight device, adjustable type size, built-in dictionaries and instant purchase and delivery. Issues include DRM restrictions on lending and resale, incompatibility between proprietary ecosystems, library licensing disputes, and questions about long-term preservation compared with print.
