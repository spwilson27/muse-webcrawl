Lunar dynasty

Lunar dynasty (Chandravamsha)
OriginChandra, the Moon god
TraditionHindu — Mahabharata, Puranas
Famous housesKuru, Yadava, Puru
CounterpartSolar dynasty (Suryavamsha)

In Hindu tradition, the Lunar dynasty (Sanskrit: Chandravamsha) is one of the principal lineages of legendary kings, descended from Chandra, the Moon. Together with the Solar dynasty (Suryavamsha), it forms the framework of royal genealogy in the Mahabharata, the Ramayana, and the Puranas.

Genealogy

The line is usually traced from the god Brahma through the sage Atri to Chandra, whose son Budha (the planet Mercury) married Ila, daughter of the solar king Manu. Their son Pururavas founded the dynasty's royal line. Later descendants include the Paurava kings and, through Yayati's sons, the branching houses of Puru and Yadu.

Major houses

The Puru line produced Bharata, after whom India is traditionally named Bharat, and the Kuru clan of Hastinapura, whose feud between the Pandavas and Kauravas forms the plot of the Mahabharata. The Yadu (Yadava) line produced Krishna, the divine hero of the Mahabharata and Bhagavata Purana. Many historical Rajput and other Indian royal families later claimed Lunar-dynasty descent to legitimise their rule.

Sources

Genealogies differ in detail among the Mahabharata, Harivamsha, Vishnu Purana, and Bhagavata Purana, reflecting centuries of bardic transmission. Modern historians treat the lists as legendary, though they may preserve distant echoes of early Indo-Aryan tribal history.
