Branch: Theoretical physics, conformal field theory

Central charge

In theoretical physics, a central charge is an operator Z that commutes with all the other symmetry operators. The adjective "central" refers to the center of the symmetry group — the subalgebra of all operators that commute with every other symmetry operator. In many cases the central charge is a c-number quantity appearing in the algebra of symmetry generators.

In conformal field theory

The best-known example is the central charge c of the Virasoro algebra, the central extension of the algebra of conformal transformations in two dimensions. The Virasoro commutation relations contain a term proportional to c:

[Lm, Ln] = (m − n)Lm+n + (c/12)(m³ − m)δm+n,0

The central charge measures the conformal anomaly and the effective number of degrees of freedom of the theory: a free real boson has c = 1, a free real fermion has c = 1/2, and the minimal models are rational conformal field theories with c < 1.

In supersymmetry

In extended supersymmetric theories, central charges appear in the superalgebra and set the BPS bound: states whose mass equals the magnitude of a central charge are BPS states, protected from quantum corrections. Central charges also occur in generalisations such as affine Lie algebras (where the level plays the analogous role) and in the theory of anyons and topological order.
