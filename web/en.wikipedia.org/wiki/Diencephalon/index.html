The diencephalon sits between the cerebral hemispheres and the midbrain, surrounding the third ventricle.

In the human brain, the diencephalon (or 'tween brain) is a division of the forebrain (embryonic prosencephalon). It is situated between the telencephalon and the midbrain (embryonic mesencephalon). The diencephalon has also historically been known as the 'tweenbrain in older literature. It consists of structures that are on either side of the third ventricle, including the thalamus, the hypothalamus, the epithalamus and the subthalamus.

Components

The thalamus, the largest part, relays sensory and motor signals to the cerebral cortex and regulates consciousness and sleep. Below it, the hypothalamus links the nervous system to the endocrine system via the pituitary gland and controls hunger, thirst, body temperature and circadian rhythms. The epithalamus includes the pineal gland, which secretes melatonin, while the subthalamus contains the subthalamic nucleus, part of the basal ganglia circuitry. The optic nerves and optic chiasm are also developmentally part of the diencephalon.

Function

Together these structures form the brain's great relay and homeostatic centre: nearly all sensory information passes through the thalamus on its way to the cortex, while the hypothalamus maintains the body's internal environment and coordinates autonomic and hormonal responses.
