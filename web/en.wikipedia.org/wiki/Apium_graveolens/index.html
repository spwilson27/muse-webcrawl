Apium graveolens

Kingdom: Plantae
Family: Apiaceae
Genus: Apium
Species: A. graveolens
Binomial name: Apium graveolens L.

Apium graveolens is a species of flowering plant in the family Apiaceae, native to coastal regions of Europe, North Africa, and western Asia. It has been cultivated for thousands of years and is the wild ancestor of the varieties grown as vegetables and flavourings.

Varieties

Three main cultivated forms are recognised. Apium graveolens var. dulce is celery, grown for its thick, crisp leaf stalks (petioles). Var. rapaceum is celeriac, grown for its large, swollen, edible hypocotyl or root. Var. secalinum, leaf celery or smallage, is grown for its leaves and for its seeds, which are used as a spice (celery seed) and in celery salt.

History and use

The wild plant, sometimes called marsh celery, grows in salt marshes and damp places. Celery leaves were found in the tomb of Tutankhamun, and the plant was known to the ancient Greeks and Romans, who valued it more as a medicine and flavouring than as a vegetable; the familiar blanched stalk celery was developed by Italian growers in the seventeenth century. Celery is a component of the French mirepoix and the Cajun "holy trinity" of cooking, and its stalks are a classic garnish for a Bloody Mary. Some people have a severe allergy to celery, which is one of the fourteen allergens required to be labelled in European Union food law.
