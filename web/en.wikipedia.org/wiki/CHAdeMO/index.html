CHAdeMO

CHAdeMO is a fast-charging system for battery electric vehicles (BEVs), developed in Japan beginning in 2010 by a consortium of Japanese automakers and energy companies, including TEPCO, Nissan, Mitsubishi, and Subaru. The name is an abbreviation of "CHArge de MOve", equivalent to "move using charge", and also a pun on the Japanese phrase o cha demo ikaga desuka ("how about a cup of tea?"), suggesting charging takes about as long as a tea break.

History

The CHAdeMO Association was formed in March 2010. The first commercial vehicles equipped with the connector, such as the Nissan Leaf and Mitsubishi i-MiEV, appeared from 2010–2011. CHAdeMO was adopted as an IEC standard (IEC 62196 Type 4) in 2014.

Version 1.0 supported charging up to 62.5 kW (125 A at 500 V). CHAdeMO 1.2 raised this to 200 kW, and CHAdeMO 2.0, published in 2018, supports up to 400 kW (400 A at 1 kV) with a liquid-cooled cable. CHAdeMO 3.0, co-developed with the China Electricity Council and known in China as ChaoJi, targets charging power beyond 500 kW and aims to harmonize Japanese and Chinese fast-charging standards.

Technology

CHAdeMO uses a dedicated DC charging connector separate from the AC inlet on Japanese vehicles. Communication between the vehicle and charger uses the CAN bus, with the vehicle acting as the master controlling the charger's output — a design intended to allow smart charging and vehicle-to-grid (V2G) operation. CHAdeMO was the first widely deployed standard to support bidirectional charging, enabling vehicle-to-home and vehicle-to-grid services.

Deployment

CHAdeMO chargers were installed worldwide, with tens of thousands of stations. From the late 2010s, however, the rival Combined Charging System (CCS) became dominant in Europe and North America, and Nissan's switch to CCS for vehicles such as the Ariya in those markets reduced CHAdeMO's share outside Japan, where it remains the mainstream standard.
