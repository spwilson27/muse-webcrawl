The Canada convoy protest, known as the Freedom Convoy (French: Convoi de la liberté), was a series of protests and blockades in Canada in January and February 2022. It began as a protest by truck drivers against a federal requirement, effective 15 January 2022, that cross-border truckers be vaccinated against COVID-19, and grew into a wider movement against pandemic measures generally. Convoys from across the country converged on Ottawa, occupying downtown streets around Parliament Hill for three weeks, while affiliated blockades shut border crossings at Coutts, Alberta; Emerson, Manitoba; and the Ambassador Bridge at Windsor, Ontario.

Background

Canada and the United States both imposed vaccine requirements for cross-border commercial drivers in January 2022. Organisers — including Tamara Lich, Benjamin Dichter, and Pat King — promoted a "memorandum of understanding" calling for the end of all mandates. Fundraising through GoFundMe raised more than C$10 million before the platform froze and then cancelled the campaign amid police reports; a subsequent campaign on GiveSendGo raised further millions.

Occupation of Ottawa and blockades

From 28–29 January, hundreds of trucks and thousands of demonstrators occupied Wellington Street and surrounding areas. Residents complained of continuous horn noise (later restricted by court injunction), harassment, and disruption; many downtown businesses, including the Rideau Centre mall, closed. Blockades of the Ambassador Bridge — North America's busiest commercial border crossing — disrupted an estimated hundreds of millions of dollars of trade per day, drawing pressure from the United States government.

Emergencies Act and clearing

On 14 February 2022, Prime Minister Justin Trudeau announced the first invocation of the Emergencies Act in its history, granting authorities powers to compel tow trucks, freeze protesters' financial accounts, and prohibit assemblies in designated areas. The Ambassador Bridge was cleared on 13–14 February, Coutts on 15 February (where police also seized weapons and laid conspiracy charges), and the Ottawa occupation was dismantled in a large police operation on 18–20 February with hundreds of arrests. Parliament approved the emergency declaration on 21 February; the government revoked it on 23 February.

Aftermath and inquiries

The Public Order Emergency Commission (Rouleau Commission), reporting in February 2023, concluded — "with reluctance" — that the threshold for invoking the Emergencies Act had been met. In January 2024, however, the Federal Court ruled the invocation unreasonable and a violation of the Charter, a decision the government appealed. Organisers Tamara Lich and Chris Barber were tried on mischief and other charges and were convicted in 2025. Copycat convoys and protests appeared internationally, including in France, New Zealand, and at the United States border.
