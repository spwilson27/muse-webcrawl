Professional degree

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

LevelGraduate (first professional) or undergraduate, varies by country
PurposePreparation for licensure in a specific profession

A professional degree, formerly known in the US as a "first professional degree", is a degree that prepares someone to work in a particular profession or gain an license, often meeting the academic training requirements for a license in the field. Professional degrees may be either graduate or undergraduate entry, depending on the profession concerned and the country.

Unlike research degrees, which culminate in a dissertation advancing original knowledge, professional degrees emphasise applied competence, supervised clinical or practical training, and the standards of a licensing body. Examples of professional degrees include:

  • Doctor of Medicine (M.D.)
  • Juris Doctor (J.D.) for law
  • Doctor of Dental Surgery / Doctor of Dental Medicine (D.D.S./D.M.D.)
  • Doctor of Pharmacy (Pharm.D.)
  • Doctor of Veterinary Medicine (D.V.M.)
  • Doctor of Optometry (O.D.)
  • Master of Architecture (M.Arch) and Bachelor of Architecture (B.Arch)
  • Master of Divinity (M.Div.)
  • Professional master's degrees such as the M.B.A. and M.P.H.

By country

In the United States, first professional degrees typically require prior undergraduate study and are administered by professional schools. In the United Kingdom and Commonwealth countries, degrees such as the Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS) and Bachelor of Laws (LL.B.) are undergraduate-entry professional qualifications.
