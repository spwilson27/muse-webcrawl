Azaleas are flowering shrubs in the genus Rhododendron, principally in the subgenera Tsutsusi (evergreen azaleas) and Pentanthera (deciduous azaleas). They are widely cultivated as ornamental plants for their showy spring flowers, which range in colour from white and pink to red, orange and purple.

Cultivation

Azaleas prefer acidic, well-drained soils and partial shade. They are popular in gardens throughout temperate regions and are a traditional feature of Japanese gardens. Thousands of cultivars have been developed, including the Kurume, Satsuki and Southern Indica hybrids.

Cultural significance

The azalea is the national flower of Nepal (where the related rhododendron holds that status) and is celebrated in festivals in Japan, Korea and the southern United States. In Chinese culture the azalea is known as the "thinking of home bush".

Toxicity

All parts of the plant contain grayanotoxins and are poisonous if ingested; honey made from azalea and rhododendron nectar ("mad honey") has intoxicating properties.
