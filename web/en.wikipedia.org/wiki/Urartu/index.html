Urartu

Urartu was an Iron Age kingdom centred around Lake Van in the Armenian Highlands, flourishing from the 9th to 6th centuries BCE. Known in the Bible as Ararat, it was a powerful rival of Assyria, famed for its fortresses, metalwork, and hydraulic engineering.

Urartu
Capital: Tushpa (Van)
Existed: c. 860–590 BCE
Language: Urartian (Hurro-Urartian)
Chief god: Haldi

History

Assyrian inscriptions first mention Urartian tribes in the 13th century BCE; the kingdom unified under Arame and Sarduri I, who built Tushpa. Kings Menua, Argishti I, and Rusa I expanded the state, built canals (the Menua Canal still carries water) and fortresses such as Erebuni (modern Yerevan, founded 782 BCE).

Decline

Weakened by Cimmerian and Scythian invasions and Assyrian campaigns, Urartu fell in the late 7th–early 6th century BCE, succeeded in the highlands by Armenian polities; the name survives in 'Ararat'.

Culture

Urartians wrote in cuneiform adapted from Assyrian, cast fine bronze cauldrons and helmets, and built cyclopean stone fortresses.
