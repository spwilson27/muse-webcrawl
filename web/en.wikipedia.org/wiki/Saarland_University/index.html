Saarland University (German: Universität des Saarlandes) is a public research university in Saarbrücken, the capital of the German state of Saarland, with a medical campus in Homburg. Founded in 1948 during the French administration of the Saar Protectorate, it began as a bilingual institution affiliated with the University of Nancy.

The Saarbrücken campus, set in woodland east of the city.

History

The university was founded in 1948, initially offering courses in Homburg in medicine and in Saarbrücken in law and the humanities, in cooperation with French universities. As the Saar's status changed, the institution developed into a full German university while retaining strong ties with France; it remains a founding member of the cross-border University of the Greater Region network.

Research profile

Saarland University is particularly known for computer science. Its campus hosts the Max Planck Institute for Informatics, the Max Planck Institute for Software Systems, the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (DFKI), and the CISPA Helmholtz Center for Information Security. Other strengths include medicine, pharmacy, materials science, and European law, the latter served by the Europa-Institut founded in 1951.

Faculties

The university is organised into faculties covering medicine; mathematics and computer science; natural sciences and technology; human and business sciences; law; and philosophy (humanities). It teaches around 17,000 students, with a notably high proportion of international students reflecting its border location between Germany, France and Luxembourg.
