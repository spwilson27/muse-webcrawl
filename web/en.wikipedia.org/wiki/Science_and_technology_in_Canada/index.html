Science and technology in Canada

Science and technology in Canada encompasses a long record of research and innovation supported by federal institutions such as the National Research Council (founded 1916), granting councils (NSERC, CIHR, SSHRC), and universities including Toronto, McGill, UBC, and Waterloo. Canada spends around 2% of GDP on research and development, below the OECD average but with notable strengths in particular fields.

Achievements

Canadian contributions include the discovery of insulin by Frederick Banting and Charles Best (1921), the Canadarm space robotics program, the Sudbury Neutrino Observatory's solution of the solar neutrino problem (Arthur McDonald, Nobel Prize 2015), and foundational work in artificial intelligence — Geoffrey Hinton and colleagues in Toronto helped pioneer deep learning, contributing to a major tech sector. Canada has produced numerous Nobel laureates, including John Polanyi (chemistry) and Donna Strickland (physics, 2018).

Institutions

Key bodies include the Canadian Space Agency, Atomic Energy of Canada, TRIUMF, Perimeter Institute, and the Vector, Mila, and Amii AI institutes. Federal science policy debates recurrently concern funding levels, basic-versus-applied balance, and retaining researchers.
