Symbiodinium

Symbiodinium
Domain: Eukaryota
Phylum: Myzozoa
Class: Dinophyceae
Order: Suessiales
Family: Symbiodiniaceae
Genus: Symbiodinium

Symbiodinium is a genus of dinoflagellates that encompasses the largest and most prevalent group of endosymbiotic dinoflagellates known. These unicellular algae, commonly called zooxanthellae, live inside the tissues of reef-building corals, sea anemones, jellyfish, giant clams and other marine invertebrates, as well as occurring free-living in the water column.

Ecology

The symbiosis is mutualistic: the algae photosynthesise and transfer sugars, lipids and oxygen to their host, supplying much of the host's energy needs, while the host provides the algae with shelter and access to carbon dioxide and nutrients such as nitrogen and phosphorus. This partnership underpins the productivity and calcification of tropical coral reefs.

When corals are stressed — most often by elevated sea temperatures — they expel their symbionts, a phenomenon known as coral bleaching, which can lead to starvation and death of the coral if conditions do not improve.

Diversity and taxonomy

What was once treated as a single species, Symbiodinium microadriaticum, is now recognised as a diverse family, the Symbiodiniaceae, comprising multiple genera (including Breviolum, Cladocopium and Durusdinium) formerly grouped as lettered "clades". Different members differ in thermal tolerance and other traits, so the particular symbionts a coral hosts influence its resilience to warming.
