Location: Inner ear — scala tympani and scala vestibuli of the cochlea; around the membranous labyrinth
Composition: Extracellular fluid, high in Na+, low in K+
Related fluid: Endolymph

Perilymph

Perilymph is an extracellular fluid located within the inner ear. It fills the space between the bony labyrinth and the membranous labyrinth, bathing the outside of the membranous ducts. Within the cochlea it occupies the scala vestibuli and scala tympani, on either side of the cochlear duct (scala media), which instead contains endolymph.

Composition and circulation

Perilymph resembles cerebrospinal fluid and plasma in composition, with a high sodium and low potassium concentration — the opposite of potassium-rich endolymph. It communicates with cerebrospinal fluid via the cochlear aqueduct, and its motion, set up by the stapes at the oval window, transmits pressure waves through the cochlea: sound-driven movement of the perilymph displaces the basilar membrane, whose travelling wave pattern encodes frequency.

Clinical significance

An abnormal connection between the perilymph spaces and the middle ear — a perilymph fistula, which may follow trauma, barotrauma, or stapes surgery — can leak perilymph and cause hearing loss, tinnitus, and vertigo. Third-window lesions such as superior semicircular canal dehiscence also disturb perilymph mechanics and produce sound- or pressure-induced vertigo (the Tullio phenomenon).
