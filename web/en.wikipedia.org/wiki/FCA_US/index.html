FCA US

FCA US LLC, doing business as Stellantis North America, is the American subsidiary of the multinational automotive manufacturer Stellantis, and one of the traditional "Big Three" US automakers. Founded by Walter Chrysler in 1925 as the Chrysler Corporation, the company has operated under successive names including DaimlerChrysler, Chrysler LLC and Chrysler Group LLC. It took the FCA US name in December 2014 after its full acquisition by Italy's Fiat, forming Fiat Chrysler Automobiles (FCA).

History

Chrysler survived a government-backed restructuring in 1979, a merger with Daimler-Benz in 1998, and a sale to Cerberus Capital Management in 2007. After bankruptcy in 2009, Fiat progressively acquired the company, completing the takeover in January 2014. In January 2021, FCA merged with France's Groupe PSA to form Stellantis; FCA US continues as the North American operating arm, selling the Chrysler, Dodge, Jeep and Ram brands, the last two of which account for most of its volume and profit through pickup trucks and SUVs.

Operations

The company is headquartered at the Chrysler Technology Center in Auburn Hills, Michigan, and operates assembly plants across the United States, Canada and Mexico. Mopar is its parts and service division.
