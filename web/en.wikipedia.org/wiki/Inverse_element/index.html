Inverse element

In mathematics, the concept of an inverse element generalises the notions of negation (in relation to addition) and reciprocal (in relation to multiplication). Given an element a in a set equipped with a binary operation and an identity element e, an inverse of a is an element b such that a · b = e and b · a = e.

Definitions and examples

For integers under addition, the inverse of 5 is −5; for non-zero real numbers under multiplication, the inverse of 5 is 1/5. In a group, every element has exactly one two-sided inverse, and inversion reverses the order of products: (ab)−1 = b−1a−1. In a monoid or semigroup, an element may have no inverse or several one-sided inverses; a matrix has a two-sided inverse only if it is square with non-zero determinant.

Terminology

For addition, the inverse is usually called the additive inverse or opposite; for multiplication, the multiplicative inverse or reciprocal. Functions under composition have inverses only when bijective. The idea underlies the axioms of groups, rings and fields, and of category-theoretic notions such as isomorphism.
