Batman is an American live-action television series based on the DC Comics character, broadcast on ABC from 1966 to 1968. Starring Adam West as Bruce Wayne / Batman and Burt Ward as Dick Grayson / Robin, the show is famous for its campy, colourful style, its on-screen comic-book sound effects ("Pow!", "Bam!") during fight scenes, and its deliberately humorous tone.

Format

Episodes were typically two-part stories airing on consecutive nights, ending with Batman and Robin in a deathtrap cliffhanger resolved the following evening. The narration by producer William Dozier urged viewers to tune in "same Bat-time, same Bat-channel". Recurring villains included Cesar Romero as the Joker, Burgess Meredith as the Penguin, Frank Gorshin as the Riddler and Julie Newmar and Eartha Kitt as Catwoman.

Popularity and decline

The series was a ratings phenomenon in its first season, sparking a wave of "Batmania", and a theatrical film was released in 1966. By the third season ratings had fallen; Batgirl (Yvonne Craig) was added, but ABC cancelled the show in 1968.

Legacy

For decades the series defined Batman for the general public, and its reputation has been reappraised as a witty pop-art satire. West and Ward reprised their roles in animated films in 2016 and 2017.
