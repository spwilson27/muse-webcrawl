Headword

A headword (also lemma) is the word under which a set of related dictionary or encyclopedia entries is entered.

A headword, also called a lemma or catchword, is a word or phrase under which a set of related entries is entered in a dictionary, encyclopedia, glossary, or index. Headwords appear in alphabetical order and are usually printed in bold type or a contrasting style so that readers can scan a page quickly.

Form of headwords

Dictionaries conventionally list words in a canonical form: nouns in the singular, verbs in the infinitive (in English, the bare stem), and adjectives in the positive degree. Irregular forms are usually cross-referenced to the headword. Multiword expressions may be entered under their first word, under the most significant word, or as independent headwords of their own. In digital dictionaries and corpora, lemmatisation is the computational process of grouping inflected forms under a single headword, a basic step in natural language processing.

Guide words

Traditional printed dictionaries print guide words — the first and last headwords on a page — at the top of each page to help users locate entries. Encyclopedias likewise use headwords as article titles.
