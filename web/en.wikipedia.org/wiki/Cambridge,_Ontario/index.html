Cambridge is a city in the Regional Municipality of Waterloo, Ontario, Canada, located at the confluence of the Grand and Speed rivers. As of the 2021 census, its population was 138,479. The modern city was formed on January 1, 1973, by the amalgamation of the city of Galt, the towns of Preston and Hespeler, and parts of surrounding townships — communities whose distinct identities residents still maintain.

History

Galt, the largest predecessor community, was founded in 1816 by William Dickson and named for the Scottish novelist and Canada Company commissioner John Galt. Its limestone architecture and mills along the Grand River made it an important manufacturing centre. Preston grew around its mineral springs and mills, and Hespeler around textile production. The 1973 amalgamation was controversial; the name "Cambridge" was chosen as a compromise among the rival communities.

Economy

Manufacturing remains central to Cambridge's economy. Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada operates a major plant in the city producing vehicles including the Lexus RX, and other employers include ATS Corporation, Loblaw distribution operations, and a growing technology sector linked to the wider Waterloo Region innovation corridor.

Culture and attractions

The city's historic stone architecture — especially in Galt — has made it a popular filming location, standing in for period settings in productions such as The Handmaid's Tale and Murdoch Mysteries. Attractions include the Cambridge Butterfly Conservatory, the historic Preston Towne Centre area, and trails along the Grand River. The University of Waterloo's School of Architecture is located in the former Riverside Silk Mill in Galt.
