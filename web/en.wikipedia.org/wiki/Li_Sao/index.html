Li Sao

The Li Sao (Chinese: 離騷; "Encountering Sorrow") is a Chinese poem from the anthology Chu Ci, traditionally attributed to Qu Yuan of the Warring States period (c. 340–278 BCE). It is the longest and most famous poem in the anthology and one of the masterpieces of classical Chinese literature.

Content

The poem describes the poet's laments over his slander and exile by a corrupt court, his protestations of purity and loyalty, and a series of fantastic shamanistic journeys through heaven and earth in search of a worthy lord and an ideal companion. It is rich in fragrant-plant imagery, in which orchids and other aromatics symbolise virtue.

Influence

The Li Sao established the sao style and profoundly influenced later Chinese poetry. Qu Yuan's loyalty and his death by drowning in the Miluo River are commemorated in the Dragon Boat Festival.
