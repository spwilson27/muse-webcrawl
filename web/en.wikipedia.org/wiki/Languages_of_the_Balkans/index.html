Languages of the Balkans

The Balkan peninsula in south-eastern Europe is home to languages of several branches of the Indo-European family, together with Turkish. Major groups include:

  • South Slavic — Bulgarian, Macedonian, Serbian, Croatian, Bosnian, Montenegrin and Slovene.
  • Balkan Romance — Romanian, Aromanian, Megleno-Romanian and Istro-Romanian.
  • Albanian — its own branch, in Geg and Tosk varieties.
  • Greek — the modern continuation of ancient Greek, including Pontic and other varieties.
  • Balkan Romani and Turkish, plus minority languages such as Hungarian in Vojvodina.

The Balkan sprachbund

The Balkans are the classic example of a sprachbund: languages from different families have converged structurally through centuries of multilingual contact. Shared features of the core Balkan languages include a postposed definite article (in Romanian, Bulgarian, Macedonian and Albanian), loss or reduction of the infinitive, a future formed with "want", and merger of genitive and dative cases. These similarities across unrelated or distantly related languages are attributed to the multilingual societies of the late Byzantine and Ottoman worlds.

Politics of language

Language in the Balkans is tightly bound to nation-building: the status of Macedonian, the mutually intelligible standards that succeeded Serbo-Croatian, and the classification of Moldovan versus Romanian have all been politically contested.
