Supporters' group

A supporters' group is an organised association of fans supporting a sports team, most commonly an association football club. Supporters' groups organise travel to away matches, produce banners and tifo displays, lead chanting, and fundraise, and they often maintain a distinct identity, symbols and sections within a stadium. The most committed groups in many countries are known as ultras, while similar organised fandom exists in North American leagues through independent supporters' associations.

By region

Europe

In Italy, organised ultras groups emerged in the late 1960s, occupying the curve (curved ends) of stadiums. In England, supporters' trusts — democratic, not-for-profit bodies — grew from the 1990s onward, sometimes taking ownership stakes in clubs, while traditional firms were associated with hooliganism. German fan culture is shaped by the 50+1 rule and strong club-member associations.

South America

In Argentina and elsewhere, barras bravas are powerful organised groups closely intertwined with club politics, while in Brazil the torcidas organizadas perform similar roles with elaborate carnival-style displays.

North America

Major League Soccer clubs have supporters' groups modelled partly on European practice, with designated sections, capo-led chanting and tifo; examples include Seattle's Emerald City Supporters and Portland's Timbers Army. Similar groups exist in ice hockey, basketball and college sports.

Relationship with clubs

Relations between groups and club management range from official recognition and cooperation to conflict over ticket prices, kick-off times, standing accommodation and commercialisation — grievances that supporters' groups frequently campaign against.
