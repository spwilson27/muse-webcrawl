Put option

A put option is a financial contract that gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to sell an underlying asset at a specified strike price on or before a specified date, in exchange for a premium paid to the seller (writer).

Payoff

The buyer profits when the underlying price falls below the strike price minus the premium. The maximum loss for the buyer is the premium; the writer's loss can be large if the asset price falls sharply. Puts are used for speculation on declines and for hedging portfolios, functioning like insurance.

Valuation

Put values are modelled by Black–Scholes and related models, depending on underlying price, strike, time to expiry, volatility, interest rates, and dividends. Put–call parity links put and call prices for European options.
