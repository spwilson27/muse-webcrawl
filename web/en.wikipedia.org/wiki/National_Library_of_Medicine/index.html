National Library of Medicine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The United States National Library of Medicine (NLM), operated by the United States federal government, is the world's largest medical library. Located in Bethesda, Maryland, on the campus of the National Institutes of Health, the NLM collects materials in all areas of biomedicine and health care, as well as works on biomedical history, and provides information and research services in all their forms through resources in the library or via remote access on the Internet.

Since 1879, the NLM has published the Index Medicus, a monthly guide to medical articles in thousands of journals. The last issue of Index Medicus was printed in December 2004, but this information is offered in the freely accessible PubMed search engine, among the more than fifteen million MEDLINE journal article references.

History

The library traces its origins to 1836, when it was founded as the Library of the Surgeon-General's Office of the United States Army. Under John Shaw Billings, who directed it from 1865 to 1895, it became the principal medical library of the United States and began publishing Index Medicus and the Index-Catalogue. It became the Armed Forces Medical Library in 1952, and was transferred to the Public Health Service and renamed the National Library of Medicine by an act of Congress in 1956, moving to Bethesda in 1962.

Services

  • PubMed and MEDLINE — bibliographic access to the biomedical literature, using the MeSH (Medical Subject Headings) vocabulary.
  • PubMed Central — a free archive of full-text biomedical journal articles.
  • MedlinePlus — consumer health information for patients and the public.
  • National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) — established in 1988 as a division of the NLM, maintaining GenBank, BLAST and other molecular databases.
  • National Network of Libraries of Medicine — promotes equal access to biomedical information across the United States.
  • History of Medicine Division — one of the world's richest collections of historical medical texts, images and manuscripts.

Toxicology and environmental health

Through its Toxicology and Environmental Health Information Program, the NLM maintains databases such as TOXNET, the Hazardous Substances Data Bank, and resources supporting the National Library of Medicine's disaster information management research.
