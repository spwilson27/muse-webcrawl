High Middle Ages

The High Middle Ages, or High Medieval Period, was the period of European history that lasted from around AD 1000 to 1300. It followed the Early Middle Ages and preceded the Late Middle Ages, and was marked by rapid population growth, agricultural expansion, urbanization, and the flourishing of medieval culture.

Society and economy

Europe's population roughly doubled or tripled between 1000 and 1300, aided by the Medieval Warm Period, new farmland cleared from forest, and technologies such as the heavy plough, horse collar, and three-field rotation. Towns grew as centers of trade; guilds organized crafts, and long-distance commerce — the Champagne fairs, the Hanseatic League, Italian maritime trade — revived monetized economies.

Politics and religion

Feudal monarchies consolidated into more centralized kingdoms in France, England, and Spain. The period saw the Gregorian Reform, the Investiture Controversy, the rise of the mendicant orders, and the Crusades (beginning 1095), which reshaped relations between Latin Christendom, Byzantium, and the Islamic world.

Culture

The era produced Gothic architecture, the first universities (Bologna, Paris, Oxford), scholastic philosophy, courtly literature in the vernaculars, and Romanesque then Gothic art. It ended amid the crises of the early 14th century: famine, plague, and war that define the Late Middle Ages.
