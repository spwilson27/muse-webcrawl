The Iowa State Fair is an annual state fair held in Des Moines, Iowa, over eleven days each August. One of the largest and best-known state fairs in the United States, it regularly draws more than a million visitors and is famous for its livestock competitions, agricultural exhibits, grandstand concerts, midway and — above all — its food, particularly foods served on a stick. Rodgers and Hammerstein's musical State Fair was inspired by the Iowa fair, as was Phil Strong's 1932 novel of the same name.

History

The first Iowa State Fair was held in Fairfield in 1854, with a budget of a few hundred dollars; it moved between several Iowa cities before settling permanently in Des Moines in 1886. The fairgrounds cover about 450 acres, including a large campground, and many of its buildings date from the early 20th century. The fair was cancelled in 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic, one of very few interruptions in its history (others include wartime years when the grounds were used for military purposes).

Attractions

  • The Butter Cow — a life-sized cow sculpted from several hundred pounds of butter, displayed in the Agriculture Building's refrigerated case since 1911. Norma "Duffy" Lyon sculpted it for nearly five decades; annual butter sculptures have included figures from Elvis to characters from Star Trek.
  • Livestock and the Big Boar — competitions for cattle, swine, sheep, horses and poultry, including the Super Bull and Big Boar contests; the record Big Boar weighed over 1,300 pounds.
  • Food — hundreds of food stands, dozens offering items on a stick, from corn dogs (popularised at fairs like this one) to deep-fried butter and salad on a stick. New foods are introduced each year to much publicity.
  • Grandstand entertainment — major concert acts, harness racing and auto racing traditions.
  • The Sky Glider and midway rides and games.

Political significance

Because Iowa traditionally holds the first-in-the-nation caucuses, the fair is a required stop for presidential candidates in the year before an election. Candidates give speeches at the Des Moines Register Political Soapbox, tour the grounds, eat pork chops on a stick and are photographed with the Butter Cow; the fair thus functions as an informal rite of passage in American presidential politics.

Cultural references

Phil Strong's novel State Fair (1932) was adapted into a 1933 film and into Rodgers and Hammerstein's 1945 movie musical (remade in 1962) and 1996 stage musical; its song "It Might as Well Be Spring" won an Academy Award. The fair is frequently listed among the top state fairs and summer events in America.
