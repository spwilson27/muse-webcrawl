Aquarius (constellation)

AbbreviationAqr
GenitiveAquarii
SymbolismThe Water Bearer (or Water Carrier)
Area980 sq. deg. (10th largest)
Brightest starSadalsuud (β Aqr), mag. 2.87
ZodiacSign between Capricornus and Pisces

Aquarius is an equatorial constellation of the zodiac, between Capricornus and Pisces. Its name is Latin for "water-carrier" or "cup-carrier", and its old astronomical symbol is ♒︎, representing water. Aquarius is one of the oldest of the recognised constellations along the zodiac, listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy, and remains one of the 88 modern constellations.

Stars

Despite its size, Aquarius contains no very bright stars. Its brightest, Sadalsuud (Beta Aquarii, "luck of lucks"), shines at magnitude 2.87. Sadalmelik (Alpha Aquarii) is a yellow supergiant whose name means "luck of the king". The constellation also contains Gliese 876 and TRAPPIST-1, red dwarfs famous for their systems of exoplanets; TRAPPIST-1 hosts seven Earth-sized planets, several in the habitable zone.

Deep-sky objects

Aquarius contains three Messier objects: the globular clusters M2 and M72 and the open cluster M73 (an asterism). It is also home to two well-known planetary nebulae: the Helix Nebula (NGC 7293), one of the closest planetary nebulae to Earth, and the Saturn Nebula (NGC 7009).

Meteor showers

Several meteor showers radiate from Aquarius, including the Eta Aquariids in early May — debris of Halley's Comet — and the Delta Aquariids in late July.

Mythology

In Greek myth Aquarius is often identified with Ganymede, the beautiful youth carried to Olympus to serve as cup-bearer to Zeus. Babylonian astronomers knew it as GU.LA, "the Great One", associated with the god Ea, who was depicted holding an overflowing vase. In astrology, Aquarius is an air sign ruled traditionally by Saturn and in modern practice by Uranus; the Sun appears in the constellation from mid-February to mid-March.
