The Histadrut, or the General Federation of Labour in Israel (Hebrew: ההסתדרות הכללית של העובדים בארץ ישראל), is Israel's national trade union centre, founded in December 1920 during the British Mandate period. Far more than a conventional labour federation, the Histadrut historically functioned as one of the central institutions of the pre-state Jewish community (the Yishuv) and of the early State of Israel.

The Histadrut's headquarters in Tel Aviv.

History

The Histadrut was established in Haifa in 1920 to organise Jewish workers, secure employment, and build cooperative economic institutions. Under leaders such as David Ben-Gurion, its first general secretary, it became closely associated with the labour Zionist movement and the Mapai party. It founded or controlled major enterprises including the construction company Solel Boneh, Bank Hapoalim, the Kupat Holim health fund, and the agricultural marketing cooperative Tnuva.

Through its economic arm, Hevrat HaOvdim, the Histadrut was at its peak one of Israel's largest employers, and it played a central role in absorbing immigrants and developing industry and agriculture. Its newspaper, Davar, was published from 1925 to 1996.

Decline and reform

From the 1970s, economic liberalisation and political change weakened the organisation. Reforms in the 1990s, including the National Health Insurance Law of 1994, severed the link between Histadrut membership and health coverage, causing membership to fall sharply. The federation sold off most of its business holdings and refocused on collective bargaining and labour representation. It remains Israel's largest union body, organising strikes and negotiating national wage agreements.
