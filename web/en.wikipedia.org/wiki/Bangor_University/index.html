Bangor University (Welsh: Prifysgol Bangor) is a public research university in Bangor, Gwynedd, Wales. Founded in 1884 as the University College of North Wales with funds raised by public subscription — including contributions from local quarrymen — it was one of the founding constituent colleges of the University of Wales (1893) and became an independent degree-awarding university in 2007.

History

The college opened in 1884 in a former coaching inn with 58 students, led by its first principal, Harry Reichel. In 1911 it moved to the purpose-built Main Arts Building on College Road, designed by Henry Hare in a neo-Baroque style and now a city landmark topped by a distinctive tower. The university developed strengths in ocean sciences (with its research ship based at Menai Bridge), forestry, psychology, Welsh studies, and electronic engineering.

Organisation and research

Bangor is organised into colleges covering arts, humanities and business; environmental sciences and engineering; and human sciences. It operates the Menai Science Park (M-SParc) on Anglesey, teaches substantially through the medium of Welsh as well as English, and has a partnership campus presence in China (Bangor College China). Its School of Ocean Sciences is one of the largest university marine science departments in Europe.
