The King's Cross fire was a fire on 18 November 1987 at King's Cross St Pancras Underground station in London, which killed 31 people and injured about 100.

The fire

At about 19:30 a passenger reported fire beneath a wooden escalator serving the Piccadilly line. Debris, grease and a probable discarded match ignited the escalator's undercroft. At 19:45 a flashover — driven by the then-unrecognised "trench effect," in which flames lay along the inclined escalator trench and superheated the ticket hall above — engulfed the ticket hall in fire and toxic smoke.

Investigation and legacy

The Fennell Inquiry (1988) criticised London Underground's safety culture. Smoking was banned throughout the Underground, wooden escalators were removed, and staffing, training and fire regulations were overhauled. The trench effect was demonstrated by computer modelling and experiments, advancing fire science.
