Lake Okeechobee, also known as Florida's Inland Sea, is the largest freshwater lake in the U.S. state of Florida and the tenth-largest natural freshwater lake in the United States. Its name comes from the Seminole oki (water) and chubi (big). Despite its great surface area — about 1,900 km² — it is exceptionally shallow, averaging only about 2.7 m deep.

Hydrology and environment

The lake is the headwaters of the Everglades: water historically sheet-flowed south from its rim into the "River of Grass." The Kissimmee River supplies most of its inflow. Nutrient runoff from agriculture has caused eutrophication and harmful algal blooms, and regulated discharges to the Caloosahatchee and St. Lucie estuaries are a perennial controversy in Everglades restoration.

Hurricanes and the dike

The 1928 Okeechobee hurricane drove a storm surge over the muck dike at the lake's south shore, killing at least 2,500 people — one of the deadliest hurricanes in U.S. history. In response the federal government built the Herbert Hoover Dike, a 230 km (143 mi) levee system encircling the lake, now maintained by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. Towns on the southern shore include Clewiston, Belle Glade, and Pahokee; the lake is also renowned for largemouth bass fishing.
