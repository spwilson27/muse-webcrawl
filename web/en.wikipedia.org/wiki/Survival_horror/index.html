Survival horror

Survival horror is a subgenre of video games inspired by horror fiction that focuses on the survival of the player character as a central gameplay element. The player is armed with fewer resources than in typical action games — limited ammunition, health, and inventory space — which emphasises evasion, puzzle-solving, and resource management over direct combat.

Characteristics

Survival horror games tend to de-emphasise combat in favour of challenges such as running from enemies and solving puzzles. Common features include a vulnerable protagonist, scarce supplies, claustrophobic environments, fixed or constrained camera angles, and an atmosphere of dread built through lighting and sound design. Inventory management is often a deliberate constraint: the player must choose carefully what to carry.

History

The term was popularised by Capcom's Resident Evil (1996), which itself drew heavily on Alone in the Dark (1992) and earlier titles such as Capcom's Sweet Home (1989). Konami's Silent Hill series (beginning 1999) shifted the genre toward psychological horror. In the 2000s, entries like Resident Evil 4 (2005) moved toward action, prompting debate about the genre's identity; independent titles such as Amnesia: The Dark Descent (2010) later revived the emphasis on helplessness, and the late 2010s and 2020s saw both indie successes and high-profile remakes return to classic survival horror design.
