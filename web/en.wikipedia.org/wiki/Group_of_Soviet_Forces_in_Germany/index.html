Group of Soviet Forces in Germany

Group of Soviet Forces in Germany (GSFG)
Active: 1945–1994
Country: Soviet Union / Russia
Headquarters: Wünsdorf
Known from 1988 as the Western Group of Forces.

The Group of Soviet Forces in Germany (GSFG) was the grouping of Soviet (and, after 1991, Russian) troops stationed in the Soviet occupation zone of Germany and later in the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) from 1945 until their complete withdrawal in 1994. It was the largest and most heavily armed formation of Soviet forces stationed outside the USSR during the Cold War.

History

The formation originated in 1945 as the Group of Soviet Occupation Forces in Germany, built around front-line armies that had fought in the Battle of Berlin. It was renamed the Group of Soviet Forces in Germany in 1954. Its headquarters was at Wünsdorf, south of Berlin, in a heavily fortified complex.

Role during the Cold War

The GSFG was the forward-deployed first echelon of the Warsaw Pact in Central Europe, intended to spearhead any offensive against NATO. At its height it numbered several hundred thousand troops with thousands of tanks and, at times, tactical nuclear weapons. Soviet forces suppressed the East German uprising of June 1953 and were placed on alert during the Berlin crises and the Prague Spring of 1968.

Withdrawal

Following German reunification in 1990, the Soviet Union agreed to withdraw all troops by the end of 1994. Renamed the Western Group of Forces in 1988, the formation completed its withdrawal in August 1994, closing nearly half a century of Soviet military presence in Germany.
