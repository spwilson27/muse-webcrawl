Royal Shakespeare Theatre

The Royal Shakespeare Theatre (RST) is a theatre in Stratford-upon-Avon, Warwickshire, England, on the banks of the River Avon. It is the main home of the Royal Shakespeare Company (RSC) and one of the most famous theatres in the English-speaking world. The present building, which seats just over 1,000 people around a thrust stage, opened in 2010 within the shell of the earlier 1932 theatre.

History

The first Shakespeare Memorial Theatre opened in 1879, founded through the efforts of Stratford brewer Charles Edward Flower, who promoted a theatre in Shakespeare's birthplace. That Victorian Gothic building burned down in 1926. Its replacement, designed by Elisabeth Scott — one of the first important British buildings designed by a woman architect — opened in 1932 as the Shakespeare Memorial Theatre, with a large proscenium-arch auditorium seating about 1,500.

In 1961, under Peter Hall, the company became the Royal Shakespeare Company and the theatre took its present name. The main house was criticised for decades for its distant, fan-shaped auditorium in which actors felt remote from the audience. A smaller studio theatre, The Other Place (1973), and the courtyard-style Swan Theatre (1986, built within the surviving shell of the 1879 theatre) offered more intimate spaces and influenced the redesign of the main house.

The 2010 redevelopment

Between 2007 and 2010 the RST was transformed in a £112.8 million project designed by Bennetts Associates. Scott's Art Deco brick shell and foyer were retained, but a new auditorium was inserted: a one-room space with a thrust stage surrounded on three sides by steeply raked seating, reducing the maximum distance from stage to seat from 27 metres to about 15 metres, with a capacity of 1,040. The project added a rooftop restaurant, a viewing tower of 32 metres overlooking the town and river, new public spaces, and improved access. The theatre reopened in November 2010, with the first full productions in the new auditorium staged in 2011.

The theatres today

The RSC complex in Stratford comprises the Royal Shakespeare Theatre, the Swan Theatre (about 450 seats), and The Other Place studio theatre, hosting productions of Shakespeare, his contemporaries, and new writing that frequently transfer to London and tour internationally.
