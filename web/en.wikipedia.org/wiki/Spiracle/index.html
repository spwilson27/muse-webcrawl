Spiracle

A spiracle is an external respiratory opening found in a wide range of animals. The word is used for the openings of the tracheal system on the body surface of many arthropods (insects, spiders and some other groups), and also for a modified gill slit found in some fishes, including sharks, rays and sturgeons, located just behind the eye.

In arthropods

In insects, spiracles are the entrances to the tracheal network that delivers oxygen directly to tissues. Most insects have paired spiracles on the thorax and abdomen, often protected by hairs, valves or closable shutters that reduce water loss. Some aquatic insect larvae breathe through spiracles held at the surface film or through spiracular gills.

In fishes

In cartilaginous fishes and some primitive bony fishes, the spiracle is the reduced first gill slit. Bottom-dwelling rays draw clean water in through the large spiracles on top of the head and pass it over the gills, avoiding sediment taken in through the mouth on the underside. In tetrapod evolution, the spiracle is homologous to structures associated with the ear region.
