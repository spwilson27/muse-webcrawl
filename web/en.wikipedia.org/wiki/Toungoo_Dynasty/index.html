Toungoo dynasty

The Toungoo dynasty (also spelled Taungoo) ruled Burma (Myanmar) from the early 16th century to 1752. Founded by Mingyi Nyo in 1510 at Toungoo, a city south of Ava, the dynasty reunified the country after the fragmentation that followed the fall of the Pagan Empire.

First Toungoo Empire

Mingyi Nyo's son Tabinshwehti (r. 1530–1550) conquered the Hanthawaddy kingdom and much of Lower Burma, and his successor Bayinnaung (r. 1551–1581) built the largest empire in the history of Southeast Asia, encompassing Manipur, the Shan states, Siam, and Lan Xang (Laos). Bayinnaung's empire overextended itself and fragmented rapidly after his death; his son Nanda Bayin lost Ayutthaya and the throne itself.

Restored dynasty

The dynasty was restored by Nyaungyan Min (r. 1599–1605) and his son Anaukbaklun, who reunified Upper and Lower Burma by 1613 and repelled Portuguese adventurers at Syriam. The capital later moved to Ava (Inwa). The later dynasty is often called the Nyaungyan or Restored Toungoo dynasty.

Decline and fall

From the late 17th century the kingdom weakened under pressure from Manipuri raids, internal revolts, and the rising power of the Mon of Lower Burma. In 1752 Hanthawaddy forces captured Ava and took the last Toungoo king, Mahadhammaraza Dipadi, ending the dynasty; Alaungpaya's Konbaung dynasty soon replaced it.
