Bernd Becher (20 August 1931 – 22 June 2007) and Hilla Becher (2 September 1934 – 10 October 2015) were German conceptual artists and photographers working collaboratively as a married couple. They are best known for their extensive series of photographic images of industrial buildings and structures, organised in grids and termed "typologies."

Work

The Bechers began photographing together in 1959, documenting the vanishing industrial architecture of the Ruhr district and beyond: water towers, blast furnaces, cooling towers, grain elevators, gas tanks, and coal bunkers. They photographed their subjects frontally, in flat, overcast light, centred in the frame and devoid of people, so that differences of form between buildings of the same functional type became visible when the images were arranged in grids.

Their strict, documentary approach treated industrial structures as "anonymous sculptures" and had a decisive influence on minimalist and conceptual art. In 1976 Bernd Becher became the first professor of photography at the Kunstakademie Düsseldorf, where their students — including Andreas Gursky, Candida Höfer, Thomas Ruff, and Thomas Struth — formed the influential Düsseldorf school of photography.

Recognition

The Bechers represented Germany at the Venice Biennale in 1990, where they won the Golden Lion for sculpture. Their books, beginning with Anonymous Sculptures (1970), remain landmarks of photographic publishing, and their work is held in major museum collections worldwide.
