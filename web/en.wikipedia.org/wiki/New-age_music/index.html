New-age music

New-age is a genre of music intended to create artistic inspiration, relaxation, and optimism. It is used by listeners for yoga, massage, meditation, and reading as a method of stress management to bring about a state of ecstasy rather than trance, or to create a peaceful atmosphere in homes or other environments, and is associated with environmentalism and New Age spirituality.

Characteristics

New-age music includes both acoustic forms, featuring instruments such as flutes, piano, acoustic guitar and a wide variety of non-Western acoustic instruments, and electronic forms, frequently relying on sustained synth pads or long sequencer-based runs. Nature soundscapes are often used as backgrounds. Melodies are generally simple and repetitive, with slow tempos and an emphasis on consonant harmony.

History

The genre developed in the 1960s and 1970s from the work of artists in ambient, progressive rock and jazz circles, including Brian Eno, whose Ambient 1: Music for Airports (1978) was foundational for ambient music. The Greek composer Vangelis, the Japanese musician Kitarō and the Irish singer Enya — whose 1988 single "Orinoco Flow" topped charts internationally — brought the sound to a mass audience. The record label Windham Hill, founded in 1976, became closely associated with the style, and a Grammy Award for Best New Age Album was introduced in 1987.

Criticism

Critics have sometimes dismissed new-age music as bland background music, while others argue this misunderstands its functional, contemplative purpose.
