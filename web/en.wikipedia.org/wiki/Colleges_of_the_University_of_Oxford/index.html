Colleges of the University of Oxford

The University of Oxford is made up of 39 self-governing colleges and a small number of permanent private halls. The colleges are independent, financially autonomous institutions within the federal university: each admits its own students, employs its own fellows and staff, and manages its own endowment and buildings, while the central university is responsible for examinations, degrees, libraries, laboratories and museums. Every student and most academics at Oxford belong to a college or hall, and much undergraduate teaching takes place in colleges through the tutorial system.

The historic colleges cluster in the centre of Oxford, around the university's central buildings.

History

The earliest colleges grew out of the medieval halls in which students lived under a master. University College claims a founding date of 1249, followed by Balliol (c. 1263) and Merton (1264), whose statutes became a model for later foundations. Colleges were originally small societies of graduate fellows; over the centuries they became the centre of undergraduate teaching. New foundations continued into the modern era, largely for groups previously excluded: women's colleges such as Lady Margaret Hall and Somerville were founded in 1878–79, and all colleges now admit both men and women. The newest college, Reuben College, admitted its first graduate students in 2021.

Permanent private halls

Alongside the colleges, Oxford has permanent private halls, founded by religious denominations and similar in size and function to small colleges. They include Blackfriars, Campion Hall, Regent's Park College, St Benet's Hall (which closed in 2022), St Stephen's House and Wycliffe Hall.

Rivalries and traditions

Colleges compete in sport — notably rowing in the Torpids and Summer Eights bumps races — and in published (and contested) measures of academic performance such as the unofficial Norrington Table. Long-standing rivalries, such as that between Christ Church and Keble, or Brasenose and Lincoln, colour college life, and each college maintains its own arms, colours, grace and traditions.
