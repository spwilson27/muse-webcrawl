Provinces of Sweden

The provinces of Sweden (Swedish: landskap) are historical, geographical and cultural regions. Sweden has 25 provinces; they have no administrative function today — modern administration is carried out by counties (län) — but remain important for regional identity, dialect, tourism and heraldry.

History

The provinces grew from older folklands and "lagsaga" jurisdictions of the Viking Age and early Middle Ages, each originally having its own law and assembly (thing). Some, such as Scania (Skåne), Blekinge and Halland, were Danish until ceded to Sweden by the Treaty of Roskilde in 1658; others, such as Jämtland and Härjedalen, were Norwegian. Lapland, the largest province, was long only loosely integrated into the Swedish state.

Organisation

Provinces are traditionally grouped within three lands (landsdelar): Götaland in the south, Svealand in the centre, and Norrland in the north. Every province has a coat of arms dating from the 16th century, and ducal crowns associated with them are still used for royal titles.

List

The 25 provinces are: Ångermanland, Blekinge, Bohuslän, Dalarna, Dalsland, Gotland, Gästrikland, Halland, Hälsingland, Härjedalen, Jämtland, Lapland, Medelpad, Närke, Norrbotten, Öland, Östergötland, Scania (Skåne), Småland, Södermanland, Uppland, Värmland, Västerbotten, Västergötland and Västmanland.
