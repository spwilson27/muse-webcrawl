Harmonic number

In mathematics, the n-th harmonic number is the sum of the reciprocals of the first n natural numbers:

Hn = 1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + ... + 1/n = Σk=1..n 1/k.

Harmonic numbers are related to the harmonic mean and appear throughout number theory, analysis, and the analysis of algorithms (for example in the average-case analysis of quicksort and the coupon collector's problem).

Properties

The harmonic numbers grow without bound, but very slowly: Hn increases roughly like the natural logarithm of n. More precisely,

Hn = ln(n) + γ + 1/(2n) − ...

where γ ≈ 0.57721 is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. For n > 1, Hn is never an integer, since among the denominators there is a unique highest power of two.

Generating function and identities

The generating function is Σ Hn xn = −ln(1 − x)/(1 − x). Harmonic numbers satisfy many identities, including connections with the digamma function: Hn = ψ(n + 1) + γ.

Generalizations

The generalized harmonic numbers of order m are the sums Hn(m) = Σ 1/km; for m ≥ 2 these converge as n → ∞ to values of the Riemann zeta function.
