Dolomite (rock)

Dolomite
Type: Sedimentary carbonate rock
Composition: CaMg(CO3)2
Named for: Déodat de Dolomieu

Dolomite is a sedimentary carbonate rock composed primarily of the mineral dolomite, calcium magnesium carbonate CaMg(CO3)2. The term is also used for the rock type once called dolostone.

Formation

Most dolomite forms by dolomitisation, the post-depositional replacement of limestone (calcite) by magnesium-rich fluids. The 'dolomite problem'—why modern dolomite formation is rare despite abundant ancient deposits—remains a classic question in geology, with microbial mediation proposed as a factor.

Properties and uses

Dolomite is slightly harder than limestone and reacts only weakly with cold dilute hydrochloric acid unless powdered. It is used as aggregate, a source of magnesium, flux in steelmaking, and dimension stone.

Occurrences

The Dolomites mountain range in northern Italy, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, takes its name from the rock described there by French geologist Déodat Gratet de Dolomieu in 1791.
