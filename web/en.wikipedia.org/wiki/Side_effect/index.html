Side effect

In medicine, a side effect is an effect, whether therapeutic or adverse, that is unintended and secondary to the one for which a drug or therapy is administered. The term is most commonly used to describe adverse effects, but it can also apply to beneficial, unintended consequences of a treatment.

Terminology

Side effects are distinguished from adverse drug reactions, which are harmful and unintended responses occurring at normal doses. Side effects may be dose-related, allergic, or idiosyncratic. Many side effects are mild and transient, such as drowsiness from antihistamines or nausea from antibiotics, but some can be severe or life-threatening.

Management and reporting

Clinicians weigh the benefits of a therapy against its side-effect profile (risk–benefit analysis). Regulatory agencies require manufacturers to document known side effects in product labelling, and pharmacovigilance systems collect post-marketing reports — for example, the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System in the United States and the Yellow Card scheme in the United Kingdom.

Beneficial side effects

Some drugs were repurposed because of useful side effects: minoxidil, developed for hypertension, was found to stimulate hair growth, and sildenafil, investigated for angina, became a treatment for erectile dysfunction.
