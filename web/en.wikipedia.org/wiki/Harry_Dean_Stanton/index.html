Harry Dean Stanton

Harry Dean Stanton (July 14, 1926 – September 15, 2017) was an American actor. In a career that spanned more than six decades, Stanton played supporting roles in films such as Cool Hand Luke (1967), Kelly's Heroes (1970), The Godfather Part II (1974), Alien (1979), Escape from New York (1981), and Pretty in Pink (1986). His main starring roles were in Paris, Texas (1984) and Lucky (2017).

Born: July 14, 1926, West Irvine, Kentucky, U.S.

Died: September 15, 2017 (aged 91), Los Angeles, California, U.S.

Occupation: Actor, musician, singer

Years active: 1954–2017

Early life

Stanton was born in Kentucky, the son of a tobacco farmer and barber and a cook. He served in the United States Navy during the Second World War as a cook aboard a landing ship during the Battle of Okinawa. After the war he attended the University of Kentucky and studied at the Pasadena Playhouse, where his classmates included Gene Hackman and Dustin Hoffman's contemporaries in the Los Angeles theatre scene.

Career

Stanton worked steadily in television westerns and small film roles through the 1950s and 1960s, becoming a quintessential character actor of New Hollywood. His gaunt face and laconic delivery made him a favourite of directors including Sam Peckinpah, John Huston, Francis Ford Coppola, Ridley Scott, Martin Scorsese, and David Lynch, with whom he collaborated repeatedly, including in Wild at Heart, Twin Peaks: Fire Walk with Me, The Straight Story, and the 2017 revival of Twin Peaks.

His breakthrough lead role came in Wim Wenders's Paris, Texas (1984), written by Sam Shepard, in which his near-silent performance as Travis Henderson, a drifter who walks out of the desert, won wide acclaim; the film won the Palme d'Or at Cannes. That same year he starred in Alex Cox's cult film Repo Man. On television, he played polygamist patriarch Roman Grant in HBO's Big Love (2006–2011). His final starring role, in Lucky (2017), was written for him and released shortly before his death at 91.

Music

Stanton was also a singer and guitarist, performing folk, country, and mariachi music with the Harry Dean Stanton Band; his performances are documented in the film Harry Dean Stanton: Partly Fiction (2012).
