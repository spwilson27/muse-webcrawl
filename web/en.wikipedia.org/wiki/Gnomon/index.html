Gnomon

A gnomon is the part of a sundial that casts a shadow, typically a triangular blade whose hypotenuse — the style — is aligned with Earth's axis, pointing to the celestial pole.

Gnomon
Type: Sundial component
Greek: γνώμων, "one that knows"
Uses: Timekeeping, astronomy, geometry

History

Shadow-casting sticks were used in ancient Egypt, Babylon, China and Greece; Anaximander is credited with introducing the gnomon to Greece. Large gnomons served as astronomical instruments, such as the 18th-century Samrat Yantra at Jaipur.

Other meanings

In geometry, a gnomon is the figure left when a parallelogram is removed from a corner of a larger similar parallelogram — a concept used by Euclid and in figurate numbers. In computing graphics, a gnomon widget shows 3D axes orientation.
