Glioblastoma

Glioblastoma, previously known as glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), is the most aggressive and most common type of cancer that originates in the brain, and has a very poor prognosis for survival. Initial signs and symptoms of glioblastoma are nonspecific and may include headaches, personality changes, nausea, and symptoms similar to those of a stroke. Its cause is generally not known. Risk factors are rare and include genetic disorders such as neurofibromatosis and Li–Fraumeni syndrome, and previous radiation therapy.

Glioblastoma accounts for a substantial share of all primary brain tumors. It is classified as a grade IV astrocytoma by the World Health Organization. Under the WHO 2021 classification, glioblastoma is defined as an IDH-wildtype diffuse astrocytic tumor with characteristic molecular features.

Treatment

Treatment usually involves surgery, after which chemotherapy and radiation therapy are used; the standard regimen combines radiotherapy with the alkylating agent temozolomide (the Stupp protocol). Even with treatment, prognosis remains poor: median survival is around 12–15 months, and only a small fraction of patients survive five years. Glucocorticoids, particularly dexamethasone, are often used to reduce symptoms from cerebral edema.
