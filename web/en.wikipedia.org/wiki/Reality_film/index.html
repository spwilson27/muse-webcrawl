Reality film

Reality film is a film genre or style that presents material in a purportedly realistic fashion, blurring the boundary between documentary and fiction. The term has been applied to nonfiction films that emphasise unscripted, slice-of-life footage, and, in the era of reality television, to feature films structured like reality shows — including compilation films assembled from reality series, staged "found footage" productions, and low-budget features shot with non-professional actors in real settings in the manner of cinéma vérité and Italian neorealism.

Reality films favour handheld camerawork, natural light, and unscripted or seemingly unscripted action.

Characteristics

Reality films typically favour location shooting, natural lighting, handheld or observational camerawork, improvised dialogue, and non-actors or unknown performers. The aesthetic descends from documentary movements such as Direct Cinema and cinéma vérité, and from narrative movements like Italian neorealism, whose postwar features (for example Bicycle Thieves, 1948) cast non-professionals in real locations to heighten social realism.

Development

The success of reality television from the late 1990s onward encouraged theatrical analogues: films compiled from reality series footage, celebrity and concert documentaries marketed on their "real" content, and scripted features borrowing the confessional interviews, surveillance footage, and competition formats of reality TV. The found-footage horror cycle begun by The Blair Witch Project (1999) and Paranormal Activity (2007) is a prominent fictional offshoot, presenting scripted events as authentic recordings.

Scholars debate how far such films differ from conventional documentary: where observational documentaries minimise intervention, reality-format productions typically shape events through casting, editing, and producer prompting, a hybrid often compared with the "reality" constructed by television formats. The genre overlaps with the mockumentary, which uses documentary form for deliberate fiction or satire.
