Metronidazole, sold under the brand name Flagyl among others, is an antibiotic and antiprotozoal medication in the nitroimidazole class. It is used, alone or with other antibiotics, to treat infections caused by anaerobic bacteria and certain parasites, including pelvic inflammatory disease, endocarditis, bacterial vaginosis, giardiasis, trichomoniasis, and infections with Clostridioides difficile. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Mechanism of action

Metronidazole is a prodrug that diffuses into anaerobic organisms, where its nitro group is reduced by microbial electron-transport proteins. The resulting reactive intermediates damage DNA, inhibiting nucleic acid synthesis and causing cell death. Aerobic human cells lack the strongly reducing environment needed for this activation, which gives the drug its selectivity.

Medical uses

  • Anaerobic bacterial infections, including intra-abdominal and dental infections
  • Bacterial vaginosis and trichomoniasis
  • Giardiasis and amoebiasis
  • C. difficile colitis (now generally reserved, with oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin preferred)
  • Part of combination therapy for Helicobacter pylori eradication
  • Topical treatment of rosacea

Side effects and interactions

Common side effects include nausea, a metallic taste, and loss of appetite. Patients are traditionally advised to avoid alcohol during treatment because of a possible disulfiram-like reaction, though the evidence for the interaction has been questioned. Prolonged use can cause peripheral neuropathy; rare serious effects include seizures and encephalopathy. Metronidazole can potentiate warfarin.

History

The drug was developed in the 1950s at Rhône-Poulenc from the natural product azomycin and introduced into clinical practice around 1960, initially for trichomoniasis; its broad anaerobic antibacterial activity was recognized shortly afterward.
