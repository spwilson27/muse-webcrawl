Waterloo campaign

The Waterloo campaign (15 June – 8 July 1815) was the final campaign of the Napoleonic Wars, fought in Belgium between Napoleon Bonaparte's French Army of the North and two Seventh Coalition armies: the Anglo-Allied army under the Duke of Wellington and the Prussian army under Field Marshal Blücher. It ended the Hundred Days and Napoleon's rule.

The campaign

Napoleon advanced into Belgium hoping to defeat the Allied and Prussian armies separately before they could unite. On 16 June the French won the Battle of Ligny against the Prussians, while Wellington fought Marshal Ney to a draw at Quatre Bras. The Prussians retreated northward to Wavre, remaining in supporting distance of Wellington — a decision that proved decisive.

Waterloo and aftermath

On 18 June 1815 Napoleon attacked Wellington's position south of Waterloo. The Anglo-Allied army held through the day, and the arrival of Prussian forces on the French right flank in the late afternoon turned the battle into a rout of the French army. Napoleon abdicated on 22 June, Allied troops entered Paris on 7 July, and Louis XVIII was restored. Napoleon was exiled to Saint Helena, where he died in 1821.
