Săpânța (Hungarian: Szaplonca) is a commune in Maramureș County, Romania, near the town of Sighetu Marmației and the Ukrainian border. It is world-famous for its Merry Cemetery (Cimitirul Vesel), where graves are marked by brightly painted wooden crosses decorated with naive paintings and humorous, often rhyming, epitaphs describing the life — and sometimes the faults and manner of death — of the deceased.

Merry Cemetery

The cemetery's tradition began in the 1930s with local folk artist Stan Ioan Pătraș, who carved and painted the first crosses in a distinctive vivid blue ("Săpânța blue"). After his death in 1977, his apprentice Dumitru Pop Tincu continued the work; the cemetery now contains over 800 painted crosses and is one of Romania's most visited folk-art sites.

Săpânța-Peri Monastery

The commune is also home to the Săpânța-Peri Monastery, whose wooden church, at about 78 metres tall, is among the tallest wooden churches in the world. The monastery stands on the site of a historic medieval monastery that was an important centre of Romanian culture in Maramureș before its destruction in the 18th century.
