The California State University (CSU) is a public university system in California, and the largest public university system in the United States by enrolment. With around 450,000 students and tens of thousands of faculty and staff, it is one of the largest employers in the state.

History

The system's origins lie in the Minns Evening Normal School of 1857, which became San José State University, the oldest CSU campus. The modern system was created by the Donahoe Higher Education Act following the California Master Plan for Higher Education of 1960, which assigned the CSU the primary mission of undergraduate and master's-level education, especially teacher training, in contrast to the research-oriented University of California.

Campuses

Campuses include San Diego State, San Francisco State, San José State, Cal State Long Beach, Cal State Fullerton, Sacramento State, Cal Poly San Luis Obispo and Cal Poly Pomona, among others. In 2025 the California State University Maritime Academy was integrated into Cal Poly San Luis Obispo, reducing the count of separately administered campuses.

Governance and mission

The system is governed by a 25-member Board of Trustees; a chancellor based at the system office in Long Beach is its chief executive. The CSU awards the majority of bachelor's degrees in California and a large share of its master's degrees, and since 2020 has been authorised to offer a limited number of applied doctorates.
