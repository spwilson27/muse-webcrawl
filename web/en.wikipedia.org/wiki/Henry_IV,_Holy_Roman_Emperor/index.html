Henry IV (German: Heinrich IV; 11 November 1050 – 7 August 1106) was Holy Roman Emperor from 1084 to 1105, king of Germany from 1054 to 1105, king of Italy and Burgundy from 1056 to 1105, and duke of Bavaria from 1052 to 1054. He was the son of Henry III, Holy Roman Emperor — the second monarch of the Salian dynasty — and Agnes of Poitou.

Early reign

Henry succeeded his father as a child of six. His minority was marked by struggles among regents, including the Coup of Kaiserswerth (1062), in which Archbishop Anno II of Cologne abducted the young king. Reaching his majority in 1065, Henry faced revolts in Saxony and sought to restore royal authority.

Investiture Controversy

Henry's reign is dominated by the Investiture Controversy with the papacy over who should appoint bishops. In 1075 Pope Gregory VII asserted papal claims in the Dictatus papae; Henry responded by declaring Gregory deposed, and Gregory excommunicated and deposed Henry in 1076. Facing rebellion, Henry made his famous penitential journey to Canossa in January 1077, standing in the snow to beg absolution from the pope. Civil war followed against the anti-king Rudolf of Rheinfelden, and Henry later marched on Rome, installed the antipope Clement III, and was crowned emperor by him in 1084.

Later years and death

Henry's later reign saw further wars in Italy and conflict with his own sons: his elder son Conrad rebelled, and in 1105 his younger son Henry forced him to abdicate. Henry IV died at Liège in 1106, still excommunicated; his body was eventually buried in Speyer Cathedral in 1111.
