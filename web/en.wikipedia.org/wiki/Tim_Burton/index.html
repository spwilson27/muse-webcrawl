Tim Burton

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Tim Burton on a film set in the 1990s, shock-haired and in black, reviewing a stop-motion puppet between takes]

Timothy Walter Burton (born 25 August 1958) is an American film director, producer, artist and animator, the laureate of the lovable macabre. His gothic-fairytale style — German Expressionism filtered through suburban California and Rankin/Bass Christmas specials — produced Beetlejuice (1988), Batman (1989), Edward Scissorhands (1990), Ed Wood (1994), Sleepy Hollow (1999), Big Fish (2003), Corpse Bride (2005), Sweeney Todd (2007) and Alice in Wonderland (2010).

Born in Burbank and animating at Disney as a young man (miserably, on The Fox and the Hound), Burton was fired toward freedom: his live-action short Frankenweenie (1984, remade as a feature in 2012) got him hired for Pee-wee's Big Adventure (1985).

Style and collaborators

Burton's world — crooked houses, spiral hills, pale outsiders with scissor hands or striped suits — is drawn before it is written; his books of drawings and the MoMA retrospective (2009) treat him as a visual artist first. Recurring collaborators (Johnny Depp in eight films, Helena Bonham Carter, composer Danny Elfman, whose scores hum Burton's themes for him) made his sets a repertory company of the weird.

Later career

The billion-dollar Alice made him a corporate auteur on his own terms; later works (Miss Peregrine's Home for Peculiar Children, Dumbo, the Netflix series Wednesday, 2022 — a phenomenon among young audiences — and Beetlejuice Beetlejuice, 2024) sustain the brand into a fifth decade. His influence on a generation of animators and illustrators is generational and obvious.
