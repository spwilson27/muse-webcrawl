Plasticizer

Plasticizers make rigid polymers such as PVC flexible enough for products like this tubing.

A plasticizer (or plasticiser) is a substance that is added to a material to make it softer and more flexible, to increase its plasticity, to decrease its viscosity, or to decrease friction during its handling in manufacture. Plasticizers are commonly added to plastics such as polyvinyl chloride (PVC), which otherwise is hard and brittle, to produce a flexible product such as clothing, inflatable products, shower curtains, hoses, and medical tubing. They are also added to concrete (as superplasticizers) and to gypsum wallboard.

Mechanism

Plasticizer molecules insert themselves between polymer chains, increasing the spacing and free volume and reducing intermolecular forces (lubricity theory and gel theory describe this in different terms). The result is a lower glass transition temperature and a softer, more workable material. Because most plasticizers are not chemically bonded to the polymer, they can migrate out over time, which causes products to stiffen with age and releases the plasticizer into the environment.

Common plasticizers

Phthalate esters historically dominated: di(2-ethylhexyl) phthalate (DEHP), diisononyl phthalate (DINP) and dibutyl phthalate (DBP) were the most widely used. Others include adipates, citrates such as acetyl tributyl citrate, epoxidised soybean oil, terephthalates (DEHT), and DINCH (a hydrogenated phthalate). Global production runs to millions of tonnes per year.

Health and environmental concerns

Some phthalates are endocrine disruptors and have been restricted in toys, childcare articles and food-contact materials in the European Union, the United States and elsewhere, driving substitution toward alternative plasticizers. Plasticizer migration also contributes to indoor dust contamination and the "new car smell" of fogged windscreens. In concrete, polycarboxylate superplasticizers allow large reductions in water content while maintaining workability, improving strength and durability.
