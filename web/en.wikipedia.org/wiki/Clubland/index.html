Clubland

Clubland is a term used to describe the area of London, particularly around St James's and Pall Mall in the West End, where many of London's historic gentlemen's clubs are concentrated. The term is also used more generally to refer to the world and culture of private members' clubs.

History

Gentlemen's clubs proliferated in London from the late 17th century, growing out of coffee houses and chocolate houses. By the 19th century, the streets around St James's Palace, St James's Street and Pall Mall were lined with grand clubhouses — many designed by leading architects — housing institutions such as White's, Boodle's, Brooks's, the Athenaeum, the Reform Club, the Travellers Club and the Army and Navy Club. The district became known simply as "Clubland".

Character

Clubs traditionally provided their members with dining rooms, libraries, reading and smoking rooms, bedrooms, and a discreet setting for social, political and business life; membership was by election, often with a ballot in which a single black ball could exclude a candidate. Although the number of clubs has declined since the mid-20th century, and most now admit women as members, Clubland remains a distinctive part of London's social geography.

In culture

The world of Clubland is a recurring setting in British literature and film, from the novels of P. G. Wodehouse — whose fictional Drones Club parodies it — to works referencing real clubs such as the Reform Club in Jules Verne's Around the World in Eighty Days.
