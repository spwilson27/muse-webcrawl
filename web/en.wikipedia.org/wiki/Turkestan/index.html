Turkestan

Turkestan, also spelled Turkistan (from Persian Turkistān, "land of the Turks"), is a historical region of Central Asia lying between Siberia to the north, Persia and Afghanistan to the south, the Caspian Sea to the west and the Gobi Desert and Mongolia to the east. It corresponds broadly to the lands historically inhabited by Turkic peoples, crossed by the Silk Road and watered by the Amu Darya (Oxus) and Syr Darya (Jaxartes) rivers. The name is regional and cultural rather than that of any single modern state.

Divisions

Turkestan was traditionally divided into Western (Russian/Soviet) Turkestan — the steppes and oasis khanates of Khiva, Bukhara and Kokand, conquered by the Russian Empire in the 19th century and organised as the Turkestan Governor-Generalship, roughly modern Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Turkmenistan — and Eastern (Chinese) Turkestan, the Tarim Basin of modern Xinjiang, centred on Kashgar and the Taklamakan Desert oases. A southern strip is sometimes called Afghan Turkestan.

History

The region was the arena of empires from the Göktürks and the Arab conquests through the Samanids, Karakhanids, Mongols and Timur (Tamerlane), whose capital Samarkand became a byword for Silk Road splendour. In the 19th century it formed the chessboard of the "Great Game" between Britain and Russia. After the Russian Revolution, the Bolsheviks dissolved the Turkestan ASSR and, in the national delimitation of 1924–1936, partitioned the region into the Soviet republics that became today's independent Central Asian states; the idea of a single Turkestani identity nevertheless persisted in exile politics and cultural memory.

A city also bears the name: Turkestan (now officially Turkistan) in southern Kazakhstan, site of the great mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi.
