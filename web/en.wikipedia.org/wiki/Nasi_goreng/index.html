Nasi goreng

Nasi goreng served with a fried egg, krupuk (crackers), and fresh vegetables.

Place of originIndonesia
RegionSoutheast Asia
Main ingredientsRice, kecap manis (sweet soy sauce), shallot, garlic, chilli, egg, chicken or prawns

Nasi goreng is a Southeast Asian rice dish with pieces of meat and vegetables added, which can refer simply to fried pre-cooked rice, a meal including stir-fried rice in a small amount of cooking oil or margarine, typically spiced with kecap manis (sweet soy sauce), shallot, garlic, ground shrimp paste, tamarind and chilli and accompanied by other ingredients, particularly egg, chicken and prawns. It is widely considered the national dish of Indonesia, and variants are found across Malaysia, Singapore, Brunei, and southern Thailand, as well as in the Netherlands through its colonial ties with Indonesia.

History

Fried rice is believed to have originated in China and spread through Southeast Asia with Chinese migration and trade. In Indonesia, the dish developed a distinctive character through the use of kecap manis — a thick, sweet soy sauce sweetened with palm sugar — which gives nasi goreng its caramelised flavour and dark brown colour. It was traditionally a way to use leftover rice from the previous day, fried for breakfast to avoid waste and the growth of bacteria in cooked rice kept at tropical room temperature.

Preparation

Nasi goreng is usually made with day-old cooked rice, which is drier and fries better than freshly cooked rice. A spice paste of shallots, garlic, chillies, and sometimes terasi (shrimp paste) is pounded or blended, then fried in oil before the rice and kecap manis are added and tossed over high heat. Common additions include eggs (scrambled in, or fried and served on top), chicken, prawns, lamb, salted fish, and vegetables such as cabbage and spring onions. It is typically served with krupuk (deep-fried crackers), emping, pickled vegetables (acar), and sliced cucumber and tomato.

Variants

  • Nasi goreng kampung — "village-style" fried rice with anchovies, kangkung (water spinach), and no kecap manis.
  • Nasi goreng pattaya — popular in Malaysia, the rice is wrapped in a thin omelette.
  • Nasi goreng jawa — Javanese style, often cooked over a wood fire with a mortar-ground spice paste.
  • Nasi goreng mawut — fried rice mixed with fried noodles.

Cultural significance

In 2011, an online poll by CNN International named nasi goreng the second most delicious food in the world, behind rendang. In the Netherlands, nasi goreng became a staple of Dutch-Chinese-Indonesian restaurant cuisine and gave rise to the snack nasischijf, a deep-fried disc of nasi goreng in breadcrumbs. The dish is sold everywhere in Indonesia, from street-side warung and travelling night vendors to high-end restaurants.
