Tiger reserves of India

The tiger reserves of India are protected areas established under Project Tiger, a conservation programme launched by the Government of India in 1973 to protect the Bengal tiger. Administered by the National Tiger Conservation Authority, the reserves consist of a core zone managed for wildlife conservation and a buffer zone of mixed use. India is home to roughly 75 percent of the world's wild tigers.

History

Project Tiger began in 1973 with nine reserves, following concern that India's tiger population had fallen to under 2,000. The first reserves included Corbett, Ranthambore, Kanha, Manas, and the Sundarbans. The network has since grown to more than fifty reserves spread across the country.

Population trends

National tiger estimation exercises, conducted every four years using camera traps and genetic analysis, recorded a recovery: the 2018 census estimated about 2,967 tigers, and the 2022 estimate rose to more than 3,100. Notable reserves by tiger numbers include Corbett in Uttarakhand and Bandipur and Nagarahole in Karnataka.

Management

Reserves are assessed periodically under the Management Effectiveness Evaluation framework, and conservation is coordinated internationally through the Global Tiger Initiative.
