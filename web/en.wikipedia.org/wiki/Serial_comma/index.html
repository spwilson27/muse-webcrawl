Serial comma


The serial comma in a list (placeholder image)

The serial comma (also known as the Oxford comma or Harvard comma) is the comma placed immediately before the coordinating conjunction (usually "and" or "or") in a series of three or more items: for example, "red, white, and blue" uses it, while "red, white and blue" omits it.

Usage and style guides

Practice varies by style guide and region. The Chicago Manual of Style, the MLA Handbook and Oxford University Press style recommend or require the serial comma; the Associated Press Stylebook, used by most American newspapers, generally omits it except where needed for clarity. British usage outside Oxford style tends to omit it more often. Advocates argue the serial comma prevents ambiguity — as in the humorous dedication "to my parents, Ayn Rand and God" — while opponents find it redundant where no ambiguity arises.

Legal significance

The presence or absence of a serial comma has decided real disputes. In O'Connor v. Oakhurst Dairy (2017), the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit found a Maine overtime statute ambiguous precisely because it lacked a serial comma in a list of exempt activities; the case settled for about $5 million in favour of delivery drivers. Maine's legislature subsequently rewrote the provision using semicolons.
