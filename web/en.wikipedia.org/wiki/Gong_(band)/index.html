Gong is a progressive rock band founded in Paris in 1967 by the Australian musician Daevid Allen and the Welsh vocalist Gilli Smyth. The band blended psychedelic rock, jazz, space rock and whimsical mythology, and became associated with the Canterbury scene. Its best-known work is the Radio Gnome Invisible trilogy (1973–74).

History

Allen, a former member of Soft Machine, was denied re-entry to Britain after a tour and settled in Paris, where Gong formed around his surreal "Planet Gong" mythology — tales of pixies, flying teapots and the "Octave Doctors" beamed to Earth by the Radio Gnome pirate station. Early albums such as Magick Brother (1970) and Camembert Electrique (1971) established the style; the arrival of guitarist Steve Hillage, saxophonist Didier Malherbe ("Bloomdido Bad de Grasse"), bassist Mike Howlett and drummer Pierre Moerlen powered the classic trilogy: Flying Teapot (1973), Angel's Egg (1973) and You (1974).

Allen and Smyth left in 1975, and under Moerlen the band evolved into the jazz-fusion outfit Pierre Moerlen's Gong (Gazeuse!, Expresso II). Numerous spin-offs followed — Mother Gong, Gongmaison, Planet Gong, and the acoustic and New York variants — before Allen reconvened the "classic" Gong in the 1990s. Allen died in 2015, having asked the band to continue; Gong has since recorded and toured with guitarist Kavus Torabi fronting the group, releasing albums such as Rejoice! I'm Dead! (2016) and The Universe Also Collapses (2019).

Style and influence

Gong's music mixes Allen's glissando guitar, space-whisper vocals by Smyth, jazz improvisation and surreal humour. The group influenced space rock, progressive rock and later psychedelic and ambient acts, and its shifting lineups seeded a web of related bands including Here & Now, Gong Global Family and Steve Hillage's solo career.
