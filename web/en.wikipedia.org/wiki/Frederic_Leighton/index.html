Frederic Leighton, 1st Baron Leighton (3 December 1830 – 25 January 1896), known as Sir Frederic Leighton between 1878 and 1896, was a British Victorian painter and sculptor. His works, frequently depicting historical, biblical, and classical subjects, made him the leading figure of Victorian academic art, and he served as President of the Royal Academy for eighteen years.

Life

Leighton was born in Scarborough and educated largely on the Continent, studying in Frankfurt, Paris, and Rome, where his Cimabue's Celebrated Madonna Carried in Procession through the Streets of Florence (1855) won him European fame and was bought by Queen Victoria. Settling in London, he built the lavish Leighton House in Holland Park, now a museum, complete with an Arab Hall decorated with Islamic tiles.

Work

Among his most celebrated paintings are Flaming June (1895), now in the Museo de Arte de Ponce, Puerto Rico; The Bath of Psyche (1890); Captive Andromache; and Cymon and Iphigenia. As a sculptor he produced An Athlete Wrestling with a Python (1877), a landmark of the New Sculpture movement.

Honours and death

Leighton was knighted in 1878, made a baronet in 1886, and on 24 January 1896 became the first artist raised to the peerage, as Baron Leighton of Stretton. He died the following day; because he left no heir, his barony became extinct after the shortest tenure in peerage history. He is buried in St Paul's Cathedral.
