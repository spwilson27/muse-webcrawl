Robert J. Lang (born 4 May 1961) is an American physicist and origami artist, recognised as one of the world's leading theorists and practitioners of modern origami. Trained as a laser physicist, he has applied mathematics and computer algorithms to the design of origami models of extraordinary complexity, and to engineering problems in folding technology.

Physics career

Lang studied electrical engineering and applied physics at the California Institute of Technology, earning his doctorate in 1986 for work on semiconductor lasers. He worked at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory and at JDS Uniphase, publishing extensively on lasers and optoelectronics and holding numerous patents.

Origami

Lang took up origami as a child and went on to design hundreds of original models, including insects, animals, and figures of great technical difficulty. His book Origami Design Secrets (2003; 2nd ed. 2011) set out the mathematical design methods he pioneered. He developed software such as TreeMaker, which computes crease patterns for complex bases, and ReferenceFinder. His work bridges art and engineering: he has consulted on folding deployable structures such as space telescope components, solar arrays, automotive airbags, and medical devices, and has lectured widely, including a TED talk on the mathematics of origami.
