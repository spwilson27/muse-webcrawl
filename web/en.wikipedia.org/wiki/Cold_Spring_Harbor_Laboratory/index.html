Overview

Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory (CSHL) is a private, non-profit research institution on Long Island, New York, founded in 1890 as a marine biological station. It became a cradle of molecular genetics: under director Milislav Demerec and later James Watson (director 1968–1993, who made it a powerhouse of cancer and neuroscience research).

Science and history

At CSHL, Salvador Luria and Max Delbrück ran the phage course that founded bacterial genetics; Barbara McClintock discovered transposons ("jumping genes") in maize here, work for which she received the Nobel Prize in 1983. The annual Cold Spring Harbor Symposia on Quantitative Biology (since 1933) are landmark meetings; the 1953 symposium season saw Watson present the double helix. CSHL also figures in the history of eugenics — Charles Davenport's Eugenics Record Office operated here from 1910 to 1939, a legacy the laboratory has publicly examined and repudiated.
