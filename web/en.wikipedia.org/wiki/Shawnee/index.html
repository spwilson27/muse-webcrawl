Shawnee

The Shawnee are a Native American people whose historic homeland centred on the Ohio River Valley, in present-day Ohio, Kentucky, Indiana, and West Virginia. They speak an Algonquian language and were historically a loose confederacy of autonomous divisions and towns, known for wide-ranging migration and trade across eastern North America.

History

During the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries Shawnee communities moved repeatedly under pressure from the Iroquois wars, colonial expansion, and shifting alliances, with towns at various times along the Savannah, Cumberland, Susquehanna, and Ohio rivers. In the early nineteenth century the Shawnee brothers Tecumseh and Tenskwatawa (the Prophet) built a pan-Indian confederacy to resist United States expansion; Tecumseh's death at the Battle of the Thames in 1813 ended the movement. Under the Indian Removal policy most Shawnee were forced west to Kansas and then to Indian Territory (Oklahoma).

Contemporary tribes

Today there are three federally recognized Shawnee tribes, all headquartered in Oklahoma: the Shawnee Tribe, the Eastern Shawnee Tribe of Oklahoma, and the Absentee-Shawnee Tribe of Indians of Oklahoma. The Shawnee language (Sawanwa) is endangered, with revitalization efforts under way in all three nations.
