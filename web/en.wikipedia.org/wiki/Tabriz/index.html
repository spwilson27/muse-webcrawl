Tabriz (Persian: تبریز; Azerbaijani: تبریز) is a city in northwestern Iran, the capital of East Azerbaijan Province. With a population of about 1.6 million, it is the largest city in Iranian Azerbaijan and a major economic and cultural centre. Located in a valley north of the Sahand volcanic massif at an elevation of about 1,350 metres, Tabriz has been a strategic gateway between the Iranian plateau, Anatolia and the Caucasus for centuries, and a key station on the Silk Road.

History

The city's origins are ancient but poorly documented; it was repeatedly destroyed by earthquakes, most devastatingly in 1721. Tabriz rose to prominence under the Mongol Ilkhanate, when it briefly served as the empire's capital in the late 13th century; Marco Polo passed through and described it as a flourishing commercial city. Under the Safavids it was at times the capital of Persia before the court moved to Qazvin and Isfahan. Because of its frontier position it was occupied several times by Ottoman and Russian forces between the 16th and 20th centuries.

Tabriz played an outsized role in modern Iranian history. During the Constitutional Revolution (1906–1911), its citizens, led by figures such as Sattar Khan and Baqer Khan, defended the constitution against royalist forces, earning Tabriz the honorific "City of the Firsts" for its pioneering role in Iranian modernity. In 1945–46 it was the capital of the Soviet-backed Azerbaijan People's Government before being retaken by Tehran.

Landmarks and culture

The Grand Bazaar of Tabriz, one of the oldest and largest covered bazaars in the world (roughly 7 km of vaulted brick passages), is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Other landmarks include the Blue Mosque (1465), the Arg-e Tabriz citadel, the Azerbaijan Museum, and the El Gölü (Shah Gölü) octagonal pool. The city is famous for its hand-knotted carpets, silverwork and leather goods, and for the Constitutional House museum. The majority of residents are ethnic Azerbaijanis speaking Azerbaijani Turkic, with Persian used in government and education.

Economy and transport

Tabriz is a heavy-industry centre (petrochemicals, machinery, tractors, oil refining) and a hub for road and rail links to Turkey and the Caucasus. It is served by Tabriz International Airport, a metro line, and a major railway station on the Tehran–Turkey route.
