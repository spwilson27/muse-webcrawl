Salvation in Christianity

In Christianity, salvation (also called deliverance or redemption) is the saving of human beings from sin and its consequences—which include death and separation from God—by Christ's death and resurrection, and the justification entailed by that salvation. The study of salvation is called soteriology. Christian beliefs about salvation vary among denominations and are shaped by doctrines of sin, grace, faith and the atonement, but all centre on the person and work of Jesus Christ.

Biblical basis

The New Testament presents Jesus's death as a sacrifice for sins and his resurrection as victory over death; passages such as John 3:16 and Romans 10:9–10 link eternal life to faith in him. Salvation is described as a gift of God's grace (Ephesians 2:8–9) and is associated with new birth, forgiveness, adoption into God's family, and the hope of resurrection and the life of the world to come.

Theological positions

The Catholic Church teaches that salvation comes through Christ and his Church, ordinarily initiated in baptism and sustained by grace, faith and works within a process of justification and sanctification. Eastern Orthodoxy emphasises theosis, humanity's participation in the divine life through synergistic cooperation with grace. Most Protestant traditions, following the Reformation, teach justification by faith alone (sola fide): the sinner is declared righteous through faith in Christ, with good works as the fruit rather than the root of salvation; within Protestantism, Calvinist and Arminian traditions differ over predestination, perseverance and whether grace can be resisted or salvation lost. The Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification (1999) expressed a broad Lutheran–Catholic consensus on the basics of justification.

Related concepts

Soteriology interacts with doctrines of original sin, atonement theories (such as Christus Victor, satisfaction and penal substitution), sanctification, glorification and eschatology.
