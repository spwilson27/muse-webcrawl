Dragones de Cuera

The Dragones de Cuera, or leather dragoons, were mounted frontier soldiers of Spain's northern borderlands in North America from the late 17th to early 19th centuries, named for the cuera, a heavy multi-layered leather coat that resisted arrows.

Role

Manning presidios from Texas to California, they patrolled vast distances, escorted missions and supply trains, and fought Apache, Comanche and other Indigenous opponents, as regulated by the Reglamento of 1772.

Equipment

Besides the cuera they carried a lance, shield (adarga), musket, pistols and sword, and were famed horsemen adapted to desert warfare.
