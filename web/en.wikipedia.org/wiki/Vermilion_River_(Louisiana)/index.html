The Vermilion River (French: Rivière Vermilion) is a river of about 70 miles (113 km) in southern Louisiana, United States. It flows southward through Acadiana — the Cajun heartland — passing through the city of Lafayette, and empties into Vermilion Bay, an arm of the Gulf of Mexico near Intracoastal City.

Course

The river is formed by the confluence of small bayous in St. Landry Parish and flows generally south through Lafayette Parish and Vermilion Parish. Through Lafayette it is a slow, bayou-like stream lined with live oaks and cypress. South of Abbeville it is a tidal, navigable waterway connecting to the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway, and serves commercial fishing and oil-and-gas traffic.

History and name

The name derives from the reddish ("vermilion") colour of its banks and waters after rains. The river was historically a transportation artery for the Acadian settlers of the region and for the Attakapas and Opelousas peoples before them. Lafayette grew up at a crossing of the river on the Old Spanish Trail.

Environment and issues

The Vermilion has periodically been listed among Louisiana's impaired waterways because of low dissolved oxygen and bacterial contamination from urban and agricultural runoff. Local organisations, such as the Bayou Vermilion Preservation Association, sponsor clean-ups and paddling events — including an annual "Bayou Vermilion Festival" and boat parades — to promote the river. The river flooded severely during the Louisiana floods of August 2016, when reverse flow from Vermilion Bay pushed water northward into Lafayette.
