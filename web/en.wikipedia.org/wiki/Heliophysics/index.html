Heliophysics

Heliophysics (from Greek hḗlios, "sun", and the related field of physics) is the physics of the Sun and its connection to the Solar System. The NASA Science Mission Directorate has defined heliophysics as the "comprehensive new term for the science of the Sun–Solar System connection", encompassing the study of the Sun, the heliosphere, and planetary environments — including Earth's magnetosphere, ionosphere, thermosphere and mesosphere — as elements of a single interconnected system.

Heliophysics treats the Sun and planetary space environments as one coupled system.

Scope

Heliophysics unites disciplines traditionally separated: solar physics, solar wind and heliospheric physics, magnetospheric physics, and aeronomy. Its central questions concern how the Sun's magnetic activity and outflows — the solar wind, solar flares and coronal mass ejections — drive space weather that affects spacecraft, astronauts, communications, navigation and power grids, and how planetary atmospheres and magnetic fields respond.

Missions and observatories

Key heliophysics missions include the Parker Solar Probe, which flies through the Sun's corona; Solar Orbiter (ESA/NASA), which images the solar poles; SOHO and the Solar Dynamics Observatory, which monitor the Sun continuously; ACE and DSCOVR, which provide upstream solar wind measurements at the L1 point; the Van Allen Probes and THEMIS/ARTEMIS, which studied Earth's radiation belts and magnetosphere; and the Voyagers, which crossed the heliopause into interstellar space. The emerging field is coordinated as "Heliophysics" within NASA's science divisions and by national decadal surveys.

History of the term

The word has older roots — a Berlin institute for "Heliophysik" existed around 1900 — but its modern sense was popularised in the 2000s by NASA associate administrator Richard Fisher and others, and institutionalised in NASA's Heliophysics Division. Debates over the term's scope versus older labels such as "solar-terrestrial physics" and "space physics" continue in the community.
