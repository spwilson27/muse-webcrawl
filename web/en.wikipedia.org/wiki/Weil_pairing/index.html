Weil pairing

The Weil pairing is a bilinear pairing defined on the torsion points of an elliptic curve (and, more generally, of an abelian variety), named after André Weil, who introduced the general construction for curves in his work on the arithmetic of algebraic curves. It is a fundamental tool in the arithmetic geometry of elliptic curves and has important applications in cryptography.

Definition

Let E be an elliptic curve over a field K, and let n be an integer prime to the characteristic of K. The Weil pairing is a map en : E[n] × E[n] → μn from pairs of n-torsion points to the group of n-th roots of unity. It is bilinear, alternating (so en(P, P) = 1), non-degenerate, and Galois invariant. It can be defined explicitly using rational functions on the curve whose divisors encode the torsion points, and computed in practice by Miller's algorithm.

Applications

Within number theory, the Weil pairing underlies the proof of properties of the Tate module and class field theoretic constructions, and it generalises to the Tate pairing and to pairings on Jacobians and abelian varieties. In cryptography, pairings on elliptic curves enabled identity-based encryption (proposed by Dan Boneh and Matthew Franklin in 2001, using Weil and Tate pairings), short signatures such as the BLS signature scheme, and tripartite key agreement. The existence of a pairing also gives rise to attacks: the MOV attack uses the Weil pairing to reduce the discrete logarithm problem on certain elliptic curves to a finite-field problem.
