Navayana (Devanagari: नवयान, meaning "new vehicle"), also known as Neo-Buddhism or Ambedkarite Buddhism, is a modern reinterpretation of Buddhism founded by the Indian jurist, economist, and social reformer B. R. Ambedkar. On 14 October 1956, Ambedkar publicly converted to Buddhism at Nagpur together with several hundred thousand followers — most of them Dalits, members of communities oppressed under the Hindu caste system — in the largest religious conversion of the modern era. Navayana rejects elements of traditional Buddhism that Ambedkar regarded as later accretions and presents the Buddha's teaching as a rational, socially engaged path to liberty, equality, and fraternity.

Doctrine

In his posthumously published work The Buddha and His Dhamma (1957), Ambedkar set out his interpretation. Navayana discards or reinterprets doctrines such as karma and rebirth understood as supernatural mechanisms, the idea that the Buddha's renunciation arose from personal sorrow, and monastic otherworldliness. Instead, it emphasises:

  • Dhamma as morality — the Buddha's teaching as a social gospel concerned with justice and human relations.
  • The Four Noble Truths read as an account of suffering rooted in social conflict and exploitation.
  • Rejection of caste — conversion as liberation from the Hindu varna order; converts take Ambedkar's Twenty-two Vows, which renounce Hindu gods and rituals.
  • Rationalism — doctrines are to be tested against reason and experience rather than accepted on authority.

History and community

Ambedkar died on 6 December 1956, weeks after the conversion, but the movement continued to grow, particularly in Maharashtra. The 2011 Census of India recorded about 8.4 million Buddhists in India, the great majority of them Navayana adherents in Maharashtra. Deekshabhoomi, with its great stupa, and Chaitya Bhoomi in Mumbai, where Ambedkar was cremated, are major pilgrimage sites. Navayana has influenced Dalit politics and literature and is studied alongside other forms of socially engaged Buddhism worldwide.
