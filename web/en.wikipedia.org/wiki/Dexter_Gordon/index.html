Dexter Gordon (February 27, 1923 – April 25, 1990) was an American jazz tenor saxophonist, composer, and bandleader. He was among the most influential early bebop musicians, translating the language of Charlie Parker to the tenor saxophone. His image is also widely recognised from the poster for the 1986 film 'Round Midnight, in which he starred and for which he was nominated for an Academy Award for Best Actor.

Gordon, "Long Tall Dexter", with his tenor saxophone.

Career

Gordon played clarinet from age 13 and switched to tenor saxophone at 17, joining Lionel Hampton's band in 1940, then working with Louis Armstrong and Billy Eckstine. In the mid-1940s he became a leading figure in bebop, recording classic duels with Wardell Gray ("The Chase", 1947). Hampered in the 1950s by drug addiction and imprisonment, he re-emerged with a celebrated series of Blue Note albums including Doin' Allright, Dexter Calling... and Go! (1962).

European years and return

From 1962 Gordon lived in Europe, mainly Copenhagen, recording extensively and enjoying personal stability. His triumphant return to the United States in 1976 — documented on the album Homecoming — helped spark the mainstream jazz revival. In 1986 he starred as the fictional saxophonist Dale Turner in Bertrand Tavernier's 'Round Midnight, earning an Oscar nomination and introducing his playing to a new generation.

Style and legacy

Gordon's sound was big, warm and behind-the-beat, with a wry, quoting wit. Standing 6 ft 6 in, he was nicknamed "Long Tall Dexter". He was named an NEA Jazz Master and is regularly ranked among the greatest tenor saxophonists in jazz history, a key bridge between swing and bebop whose approach influenced John Coltrane and Sonny Rollins.
