Scam baiting

Scam baiting (also spelled scambaiting) is the practice of engaging with online fraudsters — typically advance-fee ("419") scammers — under a false persona, wasting their time and resources, amusingly manipulating them into performing humiliating or absurd tasks, and sometimes gathering information for law enforcement.

Practices

Baiters reply to scam emails pretending to be willing victims, stringing the scammer along with delays, misunderstandings, and elaborate fictional obstacles. Achievements prized by the community include getting scammers to pose for trophy photographs holding signs, sculpt or build objects, travel to meet nonexistent victims, or fill out lengthy fake forms. Communities such as 419 Eater publish the resulting exchanges for entertainment and education.

Motives and ethics

Baiters cite wasting scammer time (and thus protecting real victims), entertainment, and raising awareness. Critics raise questions about vigilante harassment and racial undertones in some baiting, and warn that baiting can be unsafe if a scammer obtains the baiter's real identity; experienced baiters therefore use entirely fictitious details and secure email accounts.

Related phenomena

Similar tactics are applied to tech-support scammers and romance scammers, sometimes through videos and live streams by popular creators.
