Ten Tigers of Canton

The Ten Tigers of Canton or Ten Tigers of Guangdong (Chinese: 廣東十虎) were a group of ten of the top Chinese martial artists in Guangdong (Canton) towards the end of the Qing dynasty (1644–1912). Revered in southern Chinese martial arts tradition, they are celebrated in folklore and in numerous novels, television series and films of the wuxia and kung fu genres.

The Ten Tigers of Canton

The ten tigers

Lists vary slightly, but the ten are generally given as:

  • Wong Tai (王泰)
  • Wong Ching-ho (黃澄可)
  • Sou Hin-dal (蘇顯達, "Iron Fingers")
  • Wong Yin-lam (黃隱林)
  • Lai Fuk-sun (黎福順)
  • Sou Hak-fu (蘇黑虎, "Black Tiger Sou")
  • Chau Tai (周泰)
  • Tam Chai-kwan (譚濟筠)
  • Wu Kam-ching (胡金清)
  • Lai Yeung (黎勇)

Some accounts instead include Leung Kwan (梁坤), nicknamed "Iron Bridge Three" for his mastery of the iron wire fist, and the herbalist and Hung Ga master Wong Fei-hung is often associated with the group through his father Wong Kei-ying, who appears in some lists.

Legacy

The Ten Tigers became staple heroes of Cantonese popular culture. Shaw Brothers' 1979 film Ten Tigers from Kwangtung, directed by Chang Cheh, and numerous Hong Kong television dramas retell their exploits, often mixing history with legend. Their stories are intertwined with those of the Shaolin Temple, the anti-Qing resistance and the development of southern styles such as Hung Ga.
