2017 Pacific hurricane season

Season summary
Season boundaries15 May 2017 (Eastern Pacific) – 30 November 2017
Named storms18 (Eastern Pacific basin)
Strongest stormFernanda — Category 4, 145 mph (230 km/h)

The 2017 Pacific hurricane season was a moderately active season in the Eastern and Central Pacific basins. It officially ran from 15 May to 30 November 2017 in the Eastern Pacific and from 1 June in the Central Pacific, and began unusually early when Tropical Storm Adrian formed on 9 May, the earliest named storm on record in the basin at that time.

Seasonal activity

The Eastern Pacific produced 18 named storms, nine hurricanes and four major hurricanes. The strongest was Hurricane Fernanda, which reached Category 4 intensity in July but remained over open water. Hurricane Dora brought swells to the Mexican coast in June, while Hurricane Norma threatened Baja California in September before weakening.

Central Pacific

Activity in the Central Pacific was below average, with few systems forming within or entering the basin and no direct impacts on Hawaii.

Season effects

Damage from the season was generally limited, as most strong systems stayed offshore. Tropical Storm Lidia, which crossed Baja California and mainland Mexico in early September, was among the season's most damaging storms, causing flooding and several deaths.
