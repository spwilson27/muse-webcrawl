Telangana movement

The Telangana movement was the campaign for the creation of a separate Indian state of Telangana from the northwestern part of Andhra Pradesh. It culminated on 2 June 2014, when Telangana became the 29th state of India, with Hyderabad as its capital.

Background

The Telugu-speaking Telangana region had been part of the princely state of Hyderabad until 1948. In 1956 it was merged with Andhra State to form Andhra Pradesh, despite concerns recorded by the States Reorganisation Commission and safeguards promised in a "Gentlemen's Agreement". Grievances over water allocation, government jobs and educational resources fuelled demands for separation.

Early agitation

The 1969 Telangana agitation saw widespread strikes and protests in which several hundred people died. A counter-movement, the Jai Andhra agitation of 1972–73, demanded a separate Andhra state. Political arrangements in the 1970s suppressed both demands for a generation.

Final phase, 2001–2014

The movement revived with the founding of the Telangana Rashtra Samithi (TRS) by K. Chandrashekar Rao in 2001. Mass mobilisations, a hunger strike by Rao in late 2009 and sustained protests led the Union government to announce the start of statehood in December 2009, then pause after backlash in coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema. The Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act was passed in February 2014, and Telangana formally came into being that June, with Rao as its first chief minister.
