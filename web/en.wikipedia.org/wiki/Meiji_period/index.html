The Meiji period is the era of Japanese history spanning the 44-year reign of Emperor Meiji (1868–1912), during which Japan transformed itself from an isolated feudal society under the Tokugawa shogunate into a centralised, industrialised nation-state and empire. In barely a generation Japan abolished the samurai order, built a conscript army and modern navy, adopted a constitution, created national systems of education, banking, railways and law, and laid the foundations of the Empire of Japan.

The Restoration

The arrival of Commodore Matthew Perry's "black ships" in 1853 exposed the shogunate's weakness; the "unequal treaties" that followed fuelled the sonnō jōi ("revere the emperor, expel the barbarians") movement led by the domains of Satsuma and Chōshū. In 1868 reformist samurai proclaimed the restoration of imperial rule; the Tokugawa were defeated in the brief Boshin War, and the young emperor took the era name Meiji ("enlightened rule").

Reform and modernisation

The new government moved the capital to Tokyo, abolished the feudal domains in 1871 and replaced them with prefectures, dismantled the hereditary class system, and ended the samurai's right to wear swords; the last great samurai revolt, Saigō Takamori's Satsuma Rebellion, was crushed by the conscript army in 1877. The Iwakura Mission (1871–73) toured America and Europe studying Western institutions. Land-tax reform funded state-led industrialisation; the first railway opened in 1872, followed by telegraphs, lighthouses, mills and shipyards; a national education system (1872) and universities followed. After years of debate and a growing popular-rights movement, the Meiji Constitution of 1889 created a constitutional monarchy with an elected (if narrowly franchised) Diet — while reserving sovereignty to the emperor.

Empire

Victory over Qing China in 1894–95 won Taiwan and ended the "unequal treaties" with the Western powers; the Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902) recognised Japan as a peer power; victory over Russia in 1904–05 — the first modern defeat of a European great power by an Asian state — brought southern Sakhalin and primacy in Korea, annexed outright in 1910. Emperor Meiji died in 1912, succeeded by the Taishō Emperor.
