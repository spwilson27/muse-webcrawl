Aspheric lens

Aspheric lens
TypeOptical lens
Surface profileNon-spherical; often a conic section
AdvantageCorrects spherical aberration with fewer, thinner elements
ApplicationsCameras, eyeglasses, telescopes, intraocular lenses

An aspheric lens or asphere is a lens whose surface profiles are not portions of a sphere or cylinder, but are shaped — often as conic sections or as a sphere modified by polynomial terms — so that the radius of curvature changes with distance from the optical axis. Aspheric lenses are used to correct spherical aberration and other aberrations using fewer elements than conventional spherical designs.

Benefits

In a spherical lens, rays passing through the edge of the lens are focused at a different point from rays near the axis (spherical aberration). A properly designed asphere brings all rays to a common focus, allowing camera lenses that are faster, lighter and sharper with fewer elements. In eyeglasses, aspheric designs produce thinner, flatter lenses with less distortion at the periphery, especially at high prescriptions.

Manufacture

Aspheres are harder to make than spherical optics: they are produced by precision grinding, glass moulding, plastic injection moulding (common in phone cameras and laser-diode collimators), or diamond turning, and are measured with interferometry or profilometry. Small moulded aspheres are ubiquitous in consumer electronics, while large aspheres are central to telescope mirrors and high-end photographic lenses.
