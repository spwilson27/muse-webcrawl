The Korean People's Army (KPA) is the armed forces of North Korea (the Democratic People's Republic of Korea). With roughly 1.28 million active personnel and several million more in reserves and paramilitary organisations, it is one of the largest standing military forces in the world relative to population, and stands at the centre of the state's songun ("military-first") ideology.

History

The KPA was formally established on 8 February 1948 from Korean guerrilla and volunteer units that had fought alongside Soviet and Chinese communist forces. In June 1950 it invaded South Korea, beginning the Korean War; driven back after the Inchon landing, it was restored by Chinese intervention and the war ended in armistice in 1953. Since then the KPA has maintained a massive forward deployment near the Demilitarised Zone.

Structure and capabilities

The army comprises a large ground force with thousands of tanks and artillery pieces, a navy centred on coastal and submarine forces, an air force of mostly ageing aircraft, a Strategic Force operating ballistic missiles, and special operations forces among the largest in the world. Since the 2000s its missile and nuclear programmes — conducted under KPA auspices — have become its most prominent strategic element, including intercontinental ballistic missiles.
