Japanese architecture

Japanese architecture (日本建築) has traditionally been characterised by extensive use of wood, raised structures, sliding shoji partitions, deep eaves, and an intimate relationship with landscape and seasonality. Modern Japanese architects such as Tadao Ando and Kenzo Tange have become internationally influential.

Prehistoric and early periods

Earthquake-prone Japan built in wood from early times; raised granaries became the model for Shinto shrines such as Ise Grand Shrine, periodically rebuilt in the shikinen sengū tradition. Buddhist architecture arrived from China and Korea in the 6th century, producing pagodas and halls such as Hōryū-ji, the world's oldest wooden buildings.

Feudal and Edo periods

Zen influence brought refined temple gardens; the tea house aesthetic gave rise to the shoin style and the detached palace of Katsura. Monumental castles such as Himeji crowned the late feudal era.

Modern architecture

Since Meiji, Japan has blended Western construction with Japanese spatial ideas. The Metabolism movement, postwar masters, and contemporary minimalists have made Japan one of the world's most decorated architectural nations, with multiple Pritzker Prize laureates.
