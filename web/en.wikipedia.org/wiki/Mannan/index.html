Mannan

A mannan is a plant polysaccharide that is a linear polymer of the sugar mannose. Plant mannans occur as storage polysaccharides in seeds (notably the ivory nut and coffee beans) and, most abundantly, as hemicelluloses in the cell walls of softwoods, where galactoglucomannans are the principal hemicellulosic component. The repeating backbone consists of β-1,4-linked mannose residues, sometimes with glucose interspersed (glucomannan) or galactose side chains (galactomannan).

Types and sources

  • Glucomannan — found in konjac (Amorphophallus konjac) tubers; a highly water-absorbent fibre used in foods and dietary supplements.
  • Galactomannans — guar gum, locust bean gum, and tara gum, widely used as thickeners and stabilizers in the food industry.
  • Galactoglucomannans — the dominant hemicellulose of coniferous wood.
  • Yeast mannans — mannose-rich polysaccharides in the cell walls of yeasts such as Saccharomyces cerevisiae, important in immunology as antigens and in brewing and animal feed.

Degradation and uses

Mannans are hydrolysed by enzymes called mannanases, produced by many microbes and used industrially in detergents, pulp bleaching, and food processing. In humans, undigested food mannans act as dietary fibre. Mannose-binding lectin, an innate immune protein, recognizes mannan patterns on microbial surfaces and can activate the lectin pathway of complement.
