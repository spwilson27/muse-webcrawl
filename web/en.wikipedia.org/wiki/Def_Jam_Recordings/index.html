Def Jam Recordings (also simply Def Jam) is an American multinational record label owned by Universal Music Group. Founded in 1984 by Rick Rubin in his New York University dorm room and soon partnered with Russell Simmons, it became the most important early label in hip hop, bringing the music from New York's streets to a national audience.

History

Def Jam's first releases included T La Rock & Jazzy Jay's "It's Yours" and LL Cool J's "I Need a Beat" (1984). A distribution deal with Columbia/CBS in 1985 powered hits by LL Cool J (Radio), the Beastie Boys (Licensed to Ill, 1986 — the first rap album to top the Billboard 200), and Public Enemy, whose It Takes a Nation of Millions to Hold Us Back (1988) is a landmark of the genre. Rubin left in 1988 to found Def American; Simmons and Lyor Cohen built Def Jam into a broader entertainment company, launching Def Comedy Jam and the Rush Communications empire.

Later years

Absorbed fully into Universal's Island Def Jam Music Group in the late 1990s, the label remained a hip-hop powerhouse through Jay-Z's presidency of the label (2004–2007) and releases by Kanye West, Rihanna, DMX, Ludacris, and Nas. In 2014 Def Jam again became a standalone frontline label within UMG; Eminem's manager Paul Rosenberg served as CEO from 2018 to 2020, and Tunji Balogun took over in 2022.

Legacy

Def Jam is credited with defining hip hop's commercial era and its fusion of rap with rock and pop marketing. Its catalogue and artist roster have made it a byword for the genre, celebrated at its 30th anniversary concert in 2014 and in museum exhibitions on hip-hop history.
