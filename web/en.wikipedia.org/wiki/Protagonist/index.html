Protagonist

Placeholder image — the protagonist in Greek drama

A protagonist (from Ancient Greek πρωταγωνιστής, prōtagōnistḗs, "one who plays the first part, chief actor") is the main character of a story. The protagonist makes key decisions that affect the plot, primarily influencing the story and propelling it forward, and is often the character who faces the most significant obstacles and choices. If a story contains a subplot, or is a narrative made up of several stories, then each subplot may have its own protagonist.

The protagonist is the character whose fate is most closely followed by the reader or audience, and who is opposed by the antagonist. The antagonist provides obstacles and complications and creates conflicts that test the protagonist, revealing the strengths and weaknesses of the protagonist's character and having the protagonist develop as a result.

Etymology

The term protagonist comes from Ancient Greek, combining prōtos ("first") and agōnistḗs ("actor, competitor"), itself from agṓn ("contest"). In ancient Greek drama, the protagonist was the leading actor, distinguished from the deuteragonist (second actor) and tritagonist (third actor) as additional performers were introduced, traditionally credited to Aeschylus and Sophocles respectively.

Characteristics

The protagonist is usually, though not always, the hero of the work. A protagonist with predominantly negative or evil traits is sometimes called an antihero. The audience is intended to identify with the protagonist's goals and experiences; the character typically undergoes change — an arc — over the course of the narrative, responding to the central conflict.

False protagonist

A false protagonist is a technique where a character is introduced as though they will be the main character, only to be removed from the story — often killed — with another character taking over the central role. A famous example is Marion Crane in Alfred Hitchcock's Psycho (1960).
