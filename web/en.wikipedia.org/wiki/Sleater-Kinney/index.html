Sleater-Kinney is an American rock band formed in Olympia, Washington, in 1994 by Corin Tucker and Carrie Brownstein, emerging from the riot grrrl movement of the Pacific Northwest. Longtime drummer Janet Weiss joined in 1996 and remained until 2019. The band is celebrated for its interlocking dual-guitar arrangements (both guitars tuned down, with no bassist), Tucker's powerful vibrato, and politically charged lyrics.

History

Tucker (formerly of Heavens to Betsy) and Brownstein (of Excuse 17) formed the band, named after Sleater Road and Kinney Road near Olympia. Albums Call the Doctor (1996) and Dig Me Out (1997) drew wide critical acclaim; The Hot Rock (1999), All Hands on the Bad One (2000), and One Beat (2002) followed, the latter openly responding to the September 11 attacks and the Bush administration. The Woods (2005), produced by Dave Fridmann, was louder and more expansive; the band then went on indefinite hiatus in 2006.

Reunion

Sleater-Kinney reunited in 2014, releasing No Cities to Love (2015). The Center Won't Hold (2019), produced by St. Vincent, preceded Weiss's departure. As a duo, Tucker and Brownstein released Path of Wellness (2021) and Little Rope (2024).

Legacy

Critics have repeatedly ranked Sleater-Kinney among the essential rock bands of their era; Greil Marcus called them "America's best rock band," and their work is a standard reference point for feminist punk and indie rock.
