Trinity College (in full, The College of the Holy and Undivided Trinity in the University of Oxford) is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England. Founded in 1555 by Sir Thomas Pope, the college stands on Broad Street, on the site formerly occupied by Durham College, a monastic college dissolved during the Reformation.

Despite its large physical grounds, Trinity is one of the smaller Oxford colleges by student numbers, with roughly 300 undergraduates and a similar number of graduate students. It is known for its extensive gardens and lawns, and for buildings designed in part by Sir Christopher Wren.

History

Durham College was founded in the late 13th century as a house of study for Benedictine monks of Durham Cathedral. After its suppression under Henry VIII, the site was purchased by Sir Thomas Pope, a Catholic lawyer and statesman, who refounded it in 1555 as Trinity College. Pope's statutes provided for a president, twelve fellows and twelve scholars, and unusually for the time, the college was intended to be a place of prayer and study in perpetuity.

The college grew steadily over the centuries. The 17th century saw the construction of the Chapel (completed in 1694), notable for its Grinling Gibbons wood carvings, and new buildings by Wren. Further expansion took place in the 19th and 20th centuries, including buildings by the architect Thomas Graham Jackson. Women were first admitted in 1979.

Buildings and grounds

Trinity's main entrance is on Broad Street, between Balliol College and Blackwell's bookshop. The college comprises several quadrangles, including the Front Quad, the Garden Quad and the Jackson Building. The gardens, covering several acres, are unusual among Oxford colleges for their openness, being bounded by an iron grille rather than a wall along Parks Road.

Student life

The college has a reputation for being friendly and relatively informal. Notable alumni include the theologian John Henry Newman (who later converted to Roman Catholicism), the Prime Minister Lord Salisbury, and the explorer and writer Richard Burton. Trinity's rivalry with neighbouring Balliol College is long-standing and celebrated in songs and practical jokes.
