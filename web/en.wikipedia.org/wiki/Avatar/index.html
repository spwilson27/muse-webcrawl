Avatar

Avatar (Sanskrit: अवतार, avatāra, "descents") is a concept within Hinduism that in Sanskrit literally means "descent". It signifies the material appearance or incarnation of a powerful deity, goddess or spirit on Earth. The relative verb avatarana refers to the descent of the divine into material realms. Theologically, the term is most often associated with the Hindu god Vishnu, though it has also come to be associated with other deities.

Vishnu avatars

Vishnu is said to descend in the form of an avatar to restore cosmic order (dharma). The best-known list is the Dashavatara, the ten principal avatars: Matsya (the fish), Kurma (the tortoise), Varaha (the boar), Narasimha (the man-lion), Vamana (the dwarf), Parashurama (the warrior with the axe), Rama (the prince of Ayodhya), Krishna (the cowherd-prince), the Buddha in many lists, and Kalki (the future warrior yet to come). The epic and Puranic literature narrate the deeds of Rama and Krishna at length in the Ramayana and Mahabharata/Bhagavata Purana.

Other uses of the term

In the modern era the word has been borrowed widely: in computing, an avatar is a graphical representation of a user or their character in a virtual environment; in film and literature it labels works such as James Cameron's Avatar (2009), in which a human remotely operates a biological body.
