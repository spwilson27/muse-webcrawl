Claudia Livia, usually known by her nickname Livilla ("little Livia", after her grandmother Livia Drusilla), was a niece and daughter-in-law of the Roman emperor Tiberius and a central figure in the intrigues of his reign. She was the daughter of Nero Claudius Drusus and Antonia Minor, sister of the emperor Claudius and of Germanicus, and is remembered chiefly for her liaison with the praetorian prefect Sejanus and her alleged part in the murder of her husband Drusus.

Marriages

Livilla was first married, around 2 BC, to Gaius Caesar, the grandson and heir of Augustus; Gaius died in AD 4. She was then married to Drusus Julius Caesar (Drusus the Younger), the son of Tiberius, by whom she had a daughter, Julia Livia, and twin sons, Tiberius Gemellus and Germanicus Gemellus (who died young).

Sejanus and the death of Drusus

According to Tacitus, Cassius Dio and Suetonius, Livilla became the lover of Lucius Aelius Sejanus, the ambitious commander of the Praetorian Guard, who sought a place in the imperial family. In AD 23 Drusus died suddenly; years later it was alleged that Livilla and Sejanus had poisoned him, with the help of Drusus's cupbearer Lygdus and Livilla's physician Eudemus. After Sejanus's fall and execution in AD 31, his former wife Apicata revealed the plot in a letter before killing herself. Livilla was condemned; Tacitus reports that her own mother, Antonia Minor, starved her to death — or, in another version, she was executed. Her memory was officially damned (damnatio memoriae) and her statues destroyed.

Sources and reputation

Modern historians remain divided on Livilla's guilt, since the evidence rests on hostile senatorial sources and a confession extracted after Sejanus's fall. She appears as a character in Robert Graves's novel I, Claudius and its BBC television adaptation, portrayed as a scheming murderess.
