Variable (mathematics)

In mathematics, a variable is a symbol, typically a letter, that stands for a value that may vary or that is not yet specified. Variables are fundamental to algebra and are used to express general relationships, formulate equations, and define functions. The term originally denoted a quantity that "varies" over the course of an argument or calculation, as in the variables of a function.

History

The systematic use of letters for unknown and arbitrary quantities developed in the 16th and 17th centuries. François Viète introduced the use of letters for parameters and unknowns in the late 16th century, and René Descartes established the convention of using letters from the end of the alphabet (x, y, z) for unknowns and from the beginning (a, b, c) for known parameters — a convention still widely followed.

Kinds of variables

  • Independent variables are inputs of a function, whose values can be chosen freely within a domain; dependent variables take values determined by the function.
  • Free and bound variables: in logic and calculus, a variable is bound when it is quantified or defined within an expression (such as the variable of integration or a summation index, sometimes called a dummy variable), and free otherwise.
  • Random variables in probability and statistics map outcomes of random phenomena to numerical values.
  • Unknowns are variables in an equation whose values are to be solved for.

Notation and conventions

Variables are commonly denoted by single letters, often italicized. Specific letters have conventional meanings in different branches: n often denotes an integer, x a real number, z a complex number, θ an angle, and i, j, k indices. In applied mathematics and statistics, variables may carry subscripts or descriptive names.

Variables and constants

A variable contrasts with a constant, a value that is fixed throughout a given discussion. A symbol whose value is fixed but unspecified for the duration of a problem — such as a parameter — lies between the two notions.
