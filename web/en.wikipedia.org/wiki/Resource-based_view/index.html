Resource-based view

The VRIN framework for sustainable competitive advantage

The resource-based view (RBV) is a managerial framework used to determine the strategic resources a firm can exploit to achieve sustainable competitive advantage. It proposes that firms are heterogeneous bundles of resources and capabilities, and that differences in performance arise from resources controlled by the firm rather than from the structure of the industry alone.

Origins

The view is commonly traced to Edith Penrose's The Theory of the Growth of the Firm (1959), which treated the firm as a collection of productive resources. The approach was formalized in the 1980s and 1990s by scholars including Birger Wernerfelt, who coined the term "resource-based view" in 1984, and Jay Barney, whose 1991 article "Firm Resources and Sustained Competitive Advantage" became the framework's most influential statement.

The VRIN framework

Barney argued that resources generate sustained competitive advantage when they are valuable (they exploit opportunities or neutralize threats), rare (not widely possessed by competitors), imperfectly imitable (costly for rivals to duplicate, owing to unique historical conditions, causal ambiguity, or social complexity), and non-substitutable (without strategically equivalent alternatives). The framework was later extended to VRIO, adding the question of whether the firm is organized to capture value from its resources.

Criticism and development

Critics have charged the RBV with tautology — defining valuable resources in terms of the advantage they produce — and with neglecting the dynamic processes by which resources are built. The dynamic capabilities perspective, associated with David Teece and colleagues, was developed partly in response, emphasizing firms' capacity to reconfigure resources in changing environments.
