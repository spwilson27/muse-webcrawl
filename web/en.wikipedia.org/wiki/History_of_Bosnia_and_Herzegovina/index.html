History of Bosnia and Herzegovina
Key periods: Medieval kingdom; Ottoman (1463–1878); Austro-Hungarian (1878–1918); Yugoslavia; independence 1992

The history of Bosnia and Herzegovina spans from prehistoric settlement and the Illyrian tribes of antiquity, through Roman, Slavic, medieval Bosnian, Ottoman and Habsburg rule, to the Yugoslav period and independence in the 1990s.

Medieval and Ottoman periods

A Bosnian kingdom flourished in the 14th century under Tvrtko I. The Ottomans conquered Bosnia in 1463 and Herzegovina by 1482; over four centuries much of the population converted to Islam, and Sarajevo and Mostar became important Ottoman cities.

Modern era

Austria-Hungary occupied the country in 1878 and annexed it in 1908. The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo in 1914 precipitated the First World War. After 1945 Bosnia and Herzegovina was a republic within socialist Yugoslavia. It declared independence in 1992, followed by the Bosnian War (1992–1995), ended by the Dayton Agreement, which created the present federation of two entities: the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and Republika Srpska.
