County of Edessa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The County of Edessa was one of the Crusader states established during the First Crusade, and the first of them to be founded. It existed from 1098 to 1150 in the region of Upper Mesopotamia around the city of Edessa (modern Şanlıurfa, Turkey), lying largely east of the Euphrates.

History

Baldwin of Boulogne, invited by the Armenian ruler Thoros of Edessa, succeeded him in March 1098 and became the first count. Under Baldwin I and Baldwin II (later kings of Jerusalem) and Joscelin I, the county expanded to become the largest of the Crusader states, though much of it was sparsely held. Its territory east of the Euphrates was lost in stages; in 1144 the city of Edessa fell to Imad al-Din Zengi after a siege — the shock that prompted the Second Crusade. The remnant of the county west of the river was sold to the Byzantines in 1150 and soon overrun, and the county ceased to exist.
