Sir Michael Elias Balcon (19 May 1896 – 17 October 1977) was an English film producer, noted for his leadership of Ealing Studios from 1938 to 1959, where he oversaw the celebrated cycle of Ealing comedies. He is regarded as one of the founding figures of the British film industry.

Career

Balcon entered films in the 1920s, co-founding Gainsborough Pictures, where he gave Alfred Hitchcock his first directing assignments (The Lodger, 1927). He later ran production at Gaumont-British and briefly at MGM-British before taking charge of Ealing Studios in 1938.

At Ealing he built a collaborative "team" system of directors and writers — including Alexander Mackendrick, Charles Crichton, Robert Hamer and T. E. B. Clarke — producing wartime films such as Went the Day Well? and, after the war, the famous comedies Hue and Cry (1947), Passport to Pimlico (1949), Kind Hearts and Coronets (1949), The Lavender Hill Mob (1951), The Man in the White Suit (1951) and The Ladykillers (1955), as well as the Scott of the Antarctic epic (1948).

Legacy

Balcon was knighted in 1948. After Ealing he worked with Bryanston Films and served as chairman of the British Film Institute production board, encouraging young filmmakers. The BAFTA award for Outstanding British Contribution to Cinema is named in his honour. A blue plaque marks Ealing Studios' famous gatehouse, bearing his name.
