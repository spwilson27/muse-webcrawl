Arrakis (also known as Dune) is a fictional desert planet featured in the Dune franchise created by American author Frank Herbert. Introduced in the 1965 novel Dune, Arrakis is the third planet of the Canopus system and the only known source of the spice melange, the most valuable substance in the universe. The planet later becomes the seat of the empire of Paul Atreides and, over the course of the series, undergoes dramatic ecological transformation.

Description

Arrakis is covered almost entirely by a hostile desert of sand and rock, with a thin oxygen atmosphere and almost no surface water. Its native life includes the colossal sandworms (called Shai-Hulud by the Fremen), apex creatures whose life cycle produces the spice. Settlements cluster near the polar regions and behind the Shield Wall mountain range, which protects the capital, Arrakeen, and other northern cities from storms and worms.

Importance of the spice

Melange extends life, expands human consciousness, and makes interstellar travel possible by enabling the folded-space navigation of the Spacing Guild's Navigators. Because all spice production depends on Arrakis, control of the planet is the central political prize of the Imperium: as the novels repeat, "He who controls the spice controls the universe."

Society

The planet's human population includes the Imperial administrators and the off-world workforce of whatever noble House holds the fief, as well as the indigenous Fremen, desert-adapted tribes descended from Zensunni wanderers. The Fremen, led by figures such as Liet-Kynes and Stilgar, pursue a long-term plan to terraform Arrakis into a wetter world — a project that Paul Atreides's rise to power accelerates and complicates.

Later history in the series

Following Paul's jihad and the reign of his son, the God Emperor Leto II, Arrakis (later called Rakis) is gradually terraformed into a green world, retaining only a remnant desert. In Heretics of Dune and Chapterhouse: Dune, Rakis again becomes a focus of conflict among the Bene Gesserit, the Honored Matres and the last sandworms.
