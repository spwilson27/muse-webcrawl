Marty is a 1955 American romantic drama film directed by Delbert Mann in his directorial debut. The screenplay was written by Paddy Chayefsky, expanding his 1953 teleplay of the same name for The Philco Television Playhouse. The film stars Ernest Borgnine as Marty Piletti, a shy, kind-hearted Italian-American butcher in the Bronx who, under pressure from his mother and customers to marry, reluctantly attends a dance and meets Clara (Betsy Blair), a plain schoolteacher who has been stood up by her blind date. The two lonely people form a genuine connection, though Marty must overcome his friends' ridicule and his mother's jealousy to pursue the relationship.

Reception and awards

Made on a small budget by Burt Lancaster and Harold Hecht's production company, Marty became a critical and commercial success and a landmark of naturalistic American filmmaking. It won the Palme d'Or at the 1955 Cannes Film Festival — the first American film to do so — and at the 28th Academy Awards it won Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor (Borgnine), and Best Adapted Screenplay. It remains one of only a handful of films to win both the Palme d'Or and the Academy Award for Best Picture. Its success demonstrated that low-budget, character-driven dramas adapted from television could succeed on the big screen, and it helped establish Borgnine as a dramatic leading man.

Legacy

In 1994 Marty was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant. The story was later adapted as a stage musical in 2002.
