Darkroom

A darkroom is a room that can be made completely dark to allow the processing of light-sensitive photographic materials, including film and photographic paper. Darkrooms have been used since the early days of photography in the 19th century.

Equipment and process

A typical darkroom contains an enlarger for projecting a negative image onto photographic paper, trays of chemicals (developer, stop bath and fixer), running water for washing prints, and a light-tight work area. Film must be loaded and developed in total darkness, but once film negatives are processed, prints can be made under dim red or amber "safelights", to which black-and-white photographic paper is relatively insensitive.

Uses

Besides traditional silver-gelatin printing, darkrooms are used for developing colour film and prints (requiring stricter temperature control and darkness), for alternative processes, and in science and medicine for handling light-sensitive materials. Although digital photography has greatly reduced their use, darkrooms remain in use by fine-art photographers, schools and enthusiasts.
