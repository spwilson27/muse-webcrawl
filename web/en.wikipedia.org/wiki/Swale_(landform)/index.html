Swale (landform)

A swale is a low-lying or depressed stretch of land, typically a shallow channel with gently sloping sides. In contemporary landscape architecture and stormwater management, a swale — often called a bioswale or vegetated swale — is a designed channel, planted with vegetation, that conveys, slows, and filters stormwater runoff, promoting infiltration and removing silt and pollutants.

Natural swales

Natural swales occur between dune ridges on barrier islands and beaches, where the lower troughs may hold freshwater wetlands, and more generally in any gently undulating terrain where shallow depressions collect water. Such inter-dune swales support distinctive plant communities adapted to alternating wet and dry conditions.

Bioswales in stormwater design

Engineered bioswales are a form of green infrastructure and low-impact development. Water flows slowly through dense vegetation over a bed of engineered soil, allowing sediment to settle, nutrients and metals to be taken up or adsorbed, and some water to infiltrate, reducing the volume and speed of runoff reaching drains and streams. They are commonly built along roads, car parks, and housing developments, and also feature in permaculture, where contour swales are dug on slopes to harvest rainwater and recharge soil moisture.
