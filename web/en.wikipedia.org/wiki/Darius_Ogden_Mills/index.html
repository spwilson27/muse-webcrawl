Darius Ogden Mills


Image placeholder: Darius Ogden Mills

Darius Ogden Mills (September 5, 1825 – January 3, 1910) was an American banker and philanthropist who made his fortune in California during the Gold Rush era and became one of the wealthiest men in the United States.

Career

Born in North Salem, New York, Mills travelled to California in 1849 and prospered as a merchant and banker in Sacramento, co-founding a bank that dealt in gold. He was president of the Bank of California, which he helped reorganise after its 1875 collapse, and invested in railroads, mining (including the Virginia City mines), and real estate. He later moved to New York City, where the Mills Building and Mills hotels bear his name.

Philanthropy

Mills endowed educational and charitable institutions and built a chain of "Mills hotels" providing inexpensive lodgings for working men. His estate, Mills Mansion at Staatsburgh, New York, is now a state historic site. His daughter Elisabeth married Whitelaw Reid, editor of the New-York Tribune.
