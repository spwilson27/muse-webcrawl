Karen people

The Karen (S'gaw Karen: ကညီ) are a group of related ethnic peoples speaking Karenic languages, a branch of the Sino-Tibetan family. They live primarily in the hills and delta regions of south-eastern Myanmar — especially Kayin (Karen) State — and across the border in western Thailand. Population estimates vary considerably; the Karen are among Myanmar's largest ethnic minority groups.

Groups and culture

The main Karen subgroups include the S'gaw and Pwo, as well as related peoples such as the Kayah (Karenni) and Pa'O. Traditionally many Karen were hill rice farmers practising swidden agriculture; in the delta, Karen communities were introduced under British rule as agricultural labour. American Baptist missionaries worked among the Karen from the 19th century, creating written scripts for Karen languages and converting a substantial minority to Christianity; most Karen today are Buddhist, often blending Buddhism with animist practice.

Conflict and diaspora

After Burma's independence in 1948, the Karen National Union (KNU) launched an armed struggle for an independent or autonomous Karen state, Kawthoolei — one of the world's longest-running insurgencies, begun in 1949. Fighting and military campaigns displaced hundreds of thousands of Karen, many of whom fled to refugee camps along the Thai border, such as Mae La, and later resettled in countries including the United States, Australia and Canada. Ceasefires were signed in 2012 and 2015, though conflict resumed in parts of Karen State following Myanmar's 2021 military coup.
