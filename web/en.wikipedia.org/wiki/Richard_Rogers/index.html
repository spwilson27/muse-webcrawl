Richard George Rogers, Baron Rogers of Riverside, (23 July 1933 – 18 December 2021) was a British-Italian architect noted for his modernist and constructivist designs in high-tech architecture. He is perhaps best known for co-designing the Centre Pompidou in Paris with Renzo Piano, and for the Lloyd's building and the Millennium Dome in London.

Early life and education

Rogers was born in Florence to an English father and an Italian mother, and the family moved to England in 1939. He studied at the Architectural Association School of Architecture in London and then at the Yale School of Architecture in the United States, where he met Norman Foster. On returning to Britain they formed Team 4 with their wives, designing the Reliance Controls factory, an early landmark of high-tech industrial architecture.

Career

In 1971 Rogers and Renzo Piano won the international competition for the Centre Pompidou in Paris, a radical "inside-out" building with its structure and services exposed and colour-coded on the exterior. Opened in 1977, it became one of the most visited cultural buildings in the world and made both architects famous. The Lloyd's building in the City of London (1978–1986) applied the same inside-out logic to an office tower, with lifts, ducts, and lavatories in external towers.

Later works include the European Court of Human Rights in Strasbourg, the Millennium Dome in Greenwich (1999), Madrid Barajas Airport Terminal 4 (Stirling Prize 2006), the Welsh Senedd building, the Leadenhall Building ("the Cheesegrater") in London, and One Hyde Park. His practice, founded as the Richard Rogers Partnership, became Rogers Stirk Harbour + Partners in 2007.

Urbanism and public life

Rogers was a passionate advocate of dense, sustainable, mixed-use cities. He chaired the British government's Urban Task Force, whose 1999 report Towards an Urban Renaissance influenced national planning policy, and served as chief advisor on architecture and urbanism to the Mayor of London. He was knighted in 1991 and made a life peer in 1996. He received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2007.

Death

Rogers died in London on 18 December 2021, aged 88.
