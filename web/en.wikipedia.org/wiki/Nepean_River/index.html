Nepean River

The Nepean River is a perennial river in New South Wales, Australia, forming part of the Hawkesbury–Nepean river system that wraps around the western and northern edges of Sydney. The river rises near Robertson in the Southern Highlands and flows generally north for about 178 kilometres before joining the Grose River near Agnes Banks to form the Hawkesbury River.

Course

From its headwaters the Nepean flows through a deep sandstone gorge and is dammed at several points to supply Sydney's drinking water, most importantly at Nepean Dam. Downstream it passes the towns of Camden and Penrith, where the Nepean is a centre for recreation and rowing, before joining the Hawkesbury. Its principal tributary is the Warragamba River, impounded by Warragamba Dam, Sydney's main water supply.

History

The river's valley was the home of the Dharug and Gundungurra peoples for tens of thousands of years. Recorded by Europeans in 1789, the river was named by Governor Arthur Phillip for Evan Nepean, Under-Secretary of the Home Office. The fertile floodplains around Camden and Richmond became some of the earliest farming districts of the colony.

Flooding

The Hawkesbury–Nepean valley has one of the most serious flood risks in Australia; major floods occurred in 1867 and again in 2021 and 2022, inundating low-lying suburbs and farmland.
