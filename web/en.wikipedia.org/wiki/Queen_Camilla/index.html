Camilla (born Camilla Rosemary Shand, later Parker Bowles; born 17 July 1947) is Queen of the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth realms as the wife of King Charles III. She became queen consort on Charles's accession on 8 September 2022 and was crowned alongside him at Westminster Abbey on 6 May 2023.

Early life and first marriage

Camilla Shand was born at King's College Hospital, London, the daughter of Major Bruce Shand, a British Army officer and wine merchant, and Rosalind Cubitt, daughter of the 3rd Baron Ashcombe. She grew up in East Sussex and South Kensington, was educated at Queen's Gate School and finished in Switzerland and Paris, and worked as a secretary and receptionist in London. In 1973 she married cavalry officer Andrew Parker Bowles; they had two children, Tom and Laura, and divorced in 1995.

Marriage to Charles

Camilla first met Charles, then Prince of Wales, in the early 1970s; their relationship, renewed in the 1980s, became a focus of tabloid scandal during the breakdown of Charles's marriage to Diana, Princess of Wales. After Diana's death in 1997 and Charles's divorce, the couple gradually appeared in public together, and they married in a civil ceremony at Windsor Guildhall on 9 April 2005. As Charles's wife she used the title Duchess of Cornwall (and in Scotland, Duchess of Rothesay), deliberately not styling herself Princess of Wales. In February 2022, Queen Elizabeth II expressed her "sincere wish" that Camilla be known as Queen Consort when Charles became king; on his accession she was styled Queen Consort, and from the coronation simply "Queen Camilla".

Work and patronages

As a senior royal, Camilla supports literacy (including her online Reading Room), campaigns against domestic and sexual violence, raises awareness of osteoporosis — a cause taken up after her mother and grandmother died of the disease — and champions animal welfare, the arts and British food and farming. She is patron or president of more than 100 charities and organisations.
