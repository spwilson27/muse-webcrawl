Kingdome

Kingdome Full name: King County Multipurpose Domed Stadium
Location: Seattle, Washington, United States
Opened: 27 March 1976
Closed: 9 January 2000
Demolished: 26 March 2000 (implosion)
Tenants: Seattle Seahawks, Seattle Mariners, Seattle SuperSonics

The Kingdome (officially the King County Multipurpose Domed Stadium) was a multi-purpose stadium in the Industrial District of Seattle, Washington. Opened in 1976, it was the home of the Seattle Seahawks of the NFL, the Seattle Mariners of Major League Baseball, and for a time the Seattle SuperSonics of the NBA. It was demolished by implosion in 2000, an event that set a record as the largest structure ever demolished by implosion at the time.

History

The stadium arose from a 1968 bond issue approved by King County voters to build a domed stadium that would attract professional football and baseball franchises to Seattle. Construction began in 1972, and the building's thin concrete dome — 660 feet (200 m) across — was a notable feat of engineering.

The Seahawks played their first season in the Kingdome in 1976, and the Mariners joined in their inaugural 1977 season. The building hosted the 1977 NFL Pro Bowl, the 1979 MLB All-Star Game, and the 1987 NBA All-Star Game, as well as concerts, trade shows and other events.

Decline and demolition

By the 1990s the stadium was widely regarded as outdated, and an incident in July 1994 in which ceiling tiles fell into the seating area before a Mariners game hastened plans for separate new venues. The Mariners moved to Safeco Field (now T-Mobile Park) in 1999, and the Seahawks played their final Kingdome game in January 2000. The stadium was imploded on 26 March 2000; its replacement, now Lumen Field, was built on the adjoining site. Remarkably, construction debt on the Kingdome was not fully paid off until 2015, fifteen years after its demolition.
