Jazz standard

Jazz standards are musical compositions widely known, performed and recorded by jazz musicians as part of the genre's shared repertoire. Standards come from Broadway and Hollywood show tunes, the Great American Songbook, traditional songs, and original compositions by jazz musicians themselves.

Origins

Early standards derived from Tin Pan Alley and musical theatre songs of the 1920s–1940s by composers such as George Gershwin, Cole Porter and Irving Berlin. Their durable melodies and rich harmonies — notably the "rhythm changes" of Gershwin's "I Got Rhythm" — made them ideal vehicles for improvisation.

Notable standards

Frequently played standards include "All the Things You Are", "Autumn Leaves", "Body and Soul", "Summertime", "My Funny Valentine", "Blue Bossa", Duke Ellington's "Take the 'A' Train", and Dave Brubeck's "Take Five". Lead sheets are collected in fake books such as The Real Book. Bebop-era originals like Charlie Parker's contrafacts added a further layer of standards.
