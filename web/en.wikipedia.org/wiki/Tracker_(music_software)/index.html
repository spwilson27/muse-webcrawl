Tracker (music software)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A music tracker (or simply tracker) is a type of music sequencer software for creating music. Music is represented as discrete musical notes positioned in several channels at discrete chronological positions on a vertical timeline. A tracker's musical interface is traditionally numeric; notes and effects are entered via the keyboard into a grid of fixed time slots as a spreadsheet-like list of characters.

History

The first tracker, the Ultimate Soundtracker, was written by Karsten Obarski and released in 1987 for the Commodore Amiga. Successors such as NoiseTracker, Protracker, Scream Tracker, FastTracker 2, and Impulse Tracker spread with the demoscene of the late 1980s and 1990s, in which programmers and musicians produced size-limited demonstrations and "mods" — module files bundling samples, patterns, and playback instructions into a single compact file.

Interface

In the tracker interface, a song is divided into patterns of typically 64 rows. Each channel shows, for each row, a note pitch, an instrument/sample number, a volume, and an effect command (such as vibrato, portamento, or arpeggio). Playback scrolls vertically through the pattern — a paradigm reproduced in later software such as Renoise, OpenMPT, and the hardware Polyend Tracker, and adapted in vertically scrolling formats by LSDj for chiptune on the Game Boy.

Legacy

Trackers were central to demoscene and early video game music, and remain in use for chiptune and retro styles because their column-based editing and sample triggers suit rapid, keyboard-driven composition.
