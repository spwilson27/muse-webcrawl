Öre

The öre (Swedish: [ˈøːrɛ]; plural öre or ören) is the centesimal subdivision of the Swedish krona, equal to one hundredth of a krona. Cognate units have been used historically in Norway and Denmark (the øre) and descend from medieval Scandinavian coinage. Although the öre survives as a unit of account in Sweden, physical öre coins were withdrawn from circulation, the last — the 50 öre coin — being demonetised in 2010.

History

The öre derives from medieval weights and coins: in the Viking Age and early Middle Ages an eyrir (Old Norse) was a unit of silver weight, and öre coins were struck in Sweden from the 16th century. When Sweden adopted the krona in 1873 under the Scandinavian Monetary Union, the öre became its hundredth part, with coins of 1, 2, and 5 öre issued.

Withdrawals

Inflation progressively eliminated the small denominations: the 1 and 2 öre coins disappeared in the early 1970s, the 5 and 25 öre in the mid-1980s, the 10 öre in 1992, and the 50 öre coin — the last öre coin — ceased to be legal tender on 30 September 2010. Cash payments in Sweden are now rounded to the nearest krona, though prices may still be quoted in öre and electronic payments settle exactly.
