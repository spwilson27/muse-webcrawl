Brahmin

A Brahmin (Sanskrit: brāhmaṇa) is a member of the priestly and scholarly varna in Hinduism, traditionally responsible for teaching, learning, performing rituals and preserving sacred texts such as the Vedas.

Role and history

In the varna system Brahmins rank first among the four varnas, ideally devoted to study, priesthood and advising rulers. Actual occupations have always varied widely, including farming, cooking, administration and scholarship. Major traditions include Smarta, Shaiva and Vaishnava lineages, with regional communities such as Iyer and Iyengar (Tamil), Namboodiri (Kerala), and Saraswat Brahmins.

Modern period

Brahmins played prominent roles in Indian intellectual, reform and independence movements, and also faced criticism and anti-Brahmin movements challenging caste privilege, notably in Tamil Nadu. Caste remains socially and politically significant in modern India.
