Imperial Universities


Yasuda Auditorium, University of Tokyo

The Imperial Universities (Japanese: 帝國大學, Teikoku Daigaku) were a group of nine universities established by the Empire of Japan between 1886 and 1939. Modeled on Western research universities and founded under the Imperial University Ordinance, they formed the apex of the pre-war Japanese higher education system and trained the empire's civil servants, scientists, and academics.

History

The first Imperial University was established in Tokyo in 1886 (later the University of Tokyo), followed by Kyoto (1897), Tōhoku in Sendai (1907), Kyūshū in Fukuoka (1911), Hokkaido in Sapporo (1918), Keijō in Seoul (1924), Taihoku in Taipei (1928), Osaka (1931), and Nagoya (1939). The two colonial universities, Keijō and Taihoku, were founded in Korea and Taiwan respectively.

After 1945

Following the Second World War, the imperial designation was dropped and the seven universities in Japan proper became known as the former Imperial Universities, continuing as leading national research universities. Keijō Imperial University was dissolved and its assets formed part of the basis of Seoul National University, while Taihoku Imperial University became National Taiwan University.
