Battle of Worcester

The Battle of Worcester on 3 September 1651 was the final battle of the English Civil Wars. Oliver Cromwell's Parliamentarian army destroyed the predominantly Scottish Royalist army of Charles II at Worcester, ending the Wars of the Three Kingdoms. Cromwell called it his "crowning mercy".

Background

Crowned by the Scots at Scone, Charles II invaded England in August 1651 hoping to rally English Royalists, who largely failed to rise. Cromwell, after leaving Lambert to shadow the invaders, concentrated a much larger army around Worcester, where Charles took position between the Severn and the Teme.

Battle

Cromwell attacked on the anniversary of Dunbar: Fleetwood forced crossings of the Teme, while Cromwell crossed the Severn by a bridge of boats to reinforce the assault. A Royalist counterattack from the city's St John's side briefly threatened the Parliamentarian right before Cromwell recrossed and crushed it. Fighting ended in the streets; Fort Royal was stormed. Charles escaped, beginning his famous flight — including hiding in the Boscobel Oak — eventually reaching France. Thousands of Scots prisoners were transported to the Americas and elsewhere.

Aftermath

With no Royalist army left in the field, the Commonwealth controlled all three kingdoms; Scotland was incorporated into the Commonwealth. Worcester's loyalty to the Stuarts earned it the title "the Faithful City".
