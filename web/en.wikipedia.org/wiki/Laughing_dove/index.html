Laughing dove

Laughing dove
Spilopelia senegalensis
Family: Columbidae
Conservation status: Least Concern

The laughing dove (Spilopelia senegalensis) is a small pigeon that is a resident breeder in Africa, the Middle East, South Asia, and Western Australia, where it was introduced around Perth in 1898. Its name comes from its call, a low bubbling coo that resembles laughter.

The laughing dove, showing its speckled breast band.

Description

The laughing dove is a slim pigeon, typically 25 cm (9.8 in) in length. The upper parts are brownish, with a bluish-grey band along the wing; the head and underparts are pinkish, and the neck is chequered with dark spots forming a distinctive speckled band. The tail is long and graduated, with white tips visible in flight.

Behaviour

It is a common and tame species of dry scrub and open country, often found around human habitation, feeding on seeds and small insects on the ground. The nest is a flimsy platform of twigs in a low bush or tree, where two white eggs are laid.
