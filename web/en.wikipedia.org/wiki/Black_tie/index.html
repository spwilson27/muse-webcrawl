Black tie

Black tie is a dress code for formal evening events, worn in place of the more formal white tie. For men it consists principally of a black or midnight-blue dinner jacket (in the United States, a tuxedo) worn with matching trousers, a white dress shirt, a black bow tie, and formal black shoes. For women it ordinarily means a formal evening gown. Black tie events include formal dinners, balls, award ceremonies, weddings, and opening nights at the opera or theatre.

History

The dinner jacket emerged in Britain in the 1860s as a tailless alternative to the tailcoat for informal evening dress; Henry Poole & Co of Savile Row is traditionally credited with making a short smoking jacket for the Prince of Wales (later Edward VII) around 1865. In the United States the style was famously adopted at the Tuxedo Park country club in New York in the 1880s, giving the garment its American name, "tuxedo." After the First World War, black tie largely displaced white tie as the standard for formal evenings, and by the mid-20th century it was the dominant formal dress code.

Components for men

  • Jacket: single- or double-breasted dinner jacket in black or midnight blue, with silk-satin or grosgrain facings on peaked lapels or a shawl collar, and no vents preferred traditionally.
  • Trousers: matching trousers with a single silk braid stripe down the outer seam, worn with braces rather than a belt.
  • Shirt: white dress shirt with a pleated or piqué front, turndown (or historically wing) collar, and cufflinks.
  • Bow tie: black silk, self-tied, matching the lapel facings.
  • Waist covering: a low-cut evening waistcoat or a cummerbund.
  • Footwear: black patent-leather Oxford shoes or court pumps; socks are black silk.
  • Accessories: shirt studs, cufflinks, and optionally a white pocket square and a boutonnière.

Women's attire

The black tie code for women traditionally calls for a floor-length evening gown, though in modern practice shorter formal or cocktail dresses are often accepted depending on the occasion. Evening accessories such as a wrap, clutch bag, and formal jewellery complete the ensemble.

Variations

"Black tie optional" permits men to wear a dark lounge suit with a necktie instead. "Black tie preferred" signals that black tie is encouraged but suits are acceptable. "Creative black tie" invitations allow coloured bow ties, shirts or other departures from the classic ensemble, though traditionalists regard these as non-standard.
