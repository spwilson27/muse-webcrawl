Frida is a 2002 American biographical drama film directed by Julie Taymor which depicts the professional and private life of the surrealist Mexican artist Frida Kahlo. It stars Salma Hayek in her Academy Award–nominated portrayal as Kahlo and Alfred Molina as her husband, Diego Rivera. The film was adapted by Clancy Sigal, Diane Lake, Gregory Nava, and Anna Thomas from the 1983 book Frida: A Biography of Frida Kahlo by Hayden Herrera.

Plot

In 1922 Mexico City, a teenage Frida Kahlo is seriously injured when a wooden streetcar she is riding in crashes into a bus; an iron handrail impales her through the pelvis. Confined to a plaster body cast, she begins painting in bed. After recovering, she approaches muralist Diego Rivera to ask his opinion of her work, beginning a lifelong, tumultuous relationship. The film follows their marriage, affairs, political involvement with communism, Rivera's work in the United States, Kahlo's affair with Leon Trotsky, and her growing artistic recognition, ending with her first solo exhibition in Mexico in 1953, which she attends in her bed, and her death a year later.

Reception

The film received generally positive reviews, with particular praise for Hayek's performance and the film's visual style, which incorporates Kahlo's paintings into live-action sequences. At the 75th Academy Awards, Frida won Best Makeup and Best Original Score (for Elliot Goldenthal), from six nominations. It grossed $56.3 million worldwide against a $12 million budget.
