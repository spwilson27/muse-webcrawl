Dramaturgy (sociology)

Dramaturgy is a sociological perspective commonly used in micro-sociological accounts of social interaction in everyday life, developed chiefly by Erving Goffman in The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life (1956/1959).

Dramaturgy
Field: Sociology, symbolic interactionism
Founder: Erving Goffman
Key concepts: Front stage, back stage, impression management

The theatrical metaphor

Goffman treats social life as theatre: people are actors performing roles for audiences, managing impressions through setting, appearance and manner. The front stage is where the performance is given; the back stage is where performers relax, prepare and drop character. Performances are often given by teams who collude to sustain a definition of the situation.

Concepts and influence

Related ideas include face-work, embarrassment, role distance and frames. Dramaturgy underpins research on workplaces, total institutions, stigma and — in the social-media era — online self-presentation. Critics argue it overstates calculation and cynicism in everyday conduct.
