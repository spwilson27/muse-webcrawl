Christiaan Neethling Barnard (8 November 1922 – 2 September 2001) was a South African cardiac surgeon who performed the world's first human-to-human heart transplant, at Groote Schuur Hospital in Cape Town on 3 December 1967.

The first transplant

The recipient, 53-year-old Louis Washkansky, received the heart of Denise Darvall, a young woman fatally injured in a road accident. Washkansky survived 18 days before dying of pneumonia brought on by immunosuppression. Barnard's second patient, Philip Blaiberg, lived for over 19 months. The operation made Barnard an international celebrity overnight.

Career

Barnard trained at the University of Cape Town and the University of Minnesota, where he worked under Owen Wangensteen. He later pioneered other procedures, including a double ("piggyback") heart transplant in 1974, before retiring in 1983 because of rheumatoid arthritis. He died of an asthma attack while on holiday in Cyprus in 2001.
