Outcome-based education

Outcome-based education (OBE) is an approach to curriculum design and teaching in which decisions about what is taught and how are driven by the outcomes students are expected to demonstrate at the end of a course or programme — what learners should know, understand and be able to do — rather than by the content covered or the time spent in class.

Principles

The model is closely associated with educator William Spady, who promoted it from the 1980s. Its characteristic principles include: clarity of focus on significant, demonstrable outcomes; "designing down" from those outcomes to assessments and instruction; high expectations for all students; and expanded opportunity, allowing varied time and methods for learners to reach the standard. Assessment is criterion-referenced — students are judged against the stated outcomes rather than against each other.

Implementation and debate

OBE was adopted in various forms in school systems in countries including Australia, South Africa (where "Curriculum 2005" proved controversial and was later revised), the Philippines and India, and it underlies accreditation frameworks for professional degrees such as engineering (for example the Washington Accord) and medical education. Supporters argue it makes expectations transparent and aligns teaching with assessment; critics contend it can reduce education to checklists, impose heavy administrative burdens, and neglect knowledge not easily expressed as measurable performance.
