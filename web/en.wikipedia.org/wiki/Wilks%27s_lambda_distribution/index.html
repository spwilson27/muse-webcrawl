Wilks's lambda distribution

In statistics, Wilks's lambda distribution (named for Samuel S. Wilks) is a probability distribution used in multivariate hypothesis testing, especially in multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA) and likelihood-ratio tests involving covariance matrices.

Definition

Wilks's lambda arises as the ratio of determinants |E| / |E + H|, where E is the error (within-groups) sum-of-squares-and-cross-products matrix and H is the hypothesis (between-groups) matrix. Under the null hypothesis, lambda follows Wilks's lambda distribution with parameters given by the dimension of the data, the error degrees of freedom, and the hypothesis degrees of freedom. It can be expressed as a product of independent beta-distributed random variables.

Approximations

Because the exact distribution is cumbersome, the statistic is usually transformed to an approximate chi-squared or F distribution using approximations due to Bartlett and Rao. Values of lambda near 1 indicate that group means are alike, while values near 0 indicate large differences.
