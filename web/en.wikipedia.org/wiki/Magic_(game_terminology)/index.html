Magic (game terminology)

In role-playing games and video games, magic is a common game mechanic that allows characters to perform supernatural actions — casting spells, healing wounds, summoning creatures or manipulating the elements — subject to resource limits and rules. Characters who use magic are often called mages, wizards, sorcerers or magic users, and typically trade physical strength for powerful but limited supernatural abilities.

Magic systems

Game magic systems vary widely. Many draw on the "Vancian" model popularised by Dungeons & Dragons, in which casters memorise a limited number of spells that are forgotten when cast. Others use a pool of magic points, commonly called mana, which is spent per spell and replenishes over time. Some systems organise spells into schools or elements — such as fire, water, healing (white magic) and destructive (black magic) traditions — with strengths and weaknesses against one another.

Terminology

Common terms include spell, mana, casting time, cooldown, area of effect (AoE), buff and debuff for beneficial and harmful status effects, and magic resistance for a defence statistic. Magic is frequently contrasted with technology in games that mix fantasy and science fiction settings.
