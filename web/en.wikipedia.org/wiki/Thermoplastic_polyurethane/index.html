Thermoplastic polyurethane

[Hero image placeholder: clear TPU pellets and a flexible phone case moulded from TPU.]

Thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) is a class of polyurethane plastics with many properties including elasticity, transparency, and resistance to oil, grease, and abrasion. Technically, TPUs are thermoplastic elastomers consisting of linear segmented block copolymers composed of hard and soft segments.

Structure and properties

TPU is formed by the reaction of diisocyanates with short-chain diols (chain extenders, giving hard segments) and long-chain diols (giving soft segments). The alternating hard and soft segments phase-separate: the hard segments act as physical cross-links, making the material behave like rubber at room temperature while allowing it to be melt-processed like a thermoplastic. By varying the ratio and chemistry of the segments, grades range from soft and rubbery to hard and rigid.

Uses

Applications include phone cases and screen protectors, footwear soles, industrial belts and hoses, cable jacketing, automotive instrument panels, medical devices, sports equipment, and filaments for fused-deposition 3D printing (where TPU provides flexible prints).
