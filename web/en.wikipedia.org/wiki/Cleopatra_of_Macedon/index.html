Cleopatra of Macedon (Greek: Κλεοπάτρα; c. 356 – 308 BC) was the only full sister of Alexander the Great and a key dynastic prize in the Wars of the Diadochi.

Life

Daughter of Philip II and Olympias, Cleopatra married her uncle Alexander I of Epirus in 336 BC — the wedding festivities at Aegae during which Philip was assassinated. Widowed in 331, she ruled Epirus as regent and arbitrated grain relief for the Greek cities, building prestige across Greece.

The Diadochi contests

After Alexander's death, her hand was sought by Leonnatus, Perdiccas, Cassander, Lysimachus, Antigonus, and Ptolemy — marriage to the king's sister promised legitimacy. Held under guard by Antigonus at Sardis, Cleopatra was murdered in 308 BC, allegedly on Antigonus's orders, as she prepared to depart for Ptolemy's court in Egypt.

Significance

Her career exemplifies how Argead royal women functioned as sovereign actors and legitimacy-conferring assets in the succession wars.
