Physiologist

A physiologist is a scientist who studies physiology, the branch of biology concerned with the normal functions of living organisms and their parts. Physiologists investigate how organs, organ systems, tissues, cells, and biomolecules carry out the chemical and physical functions of life.

Work

Physiologists work in universities, hospitals, pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, and research institutes. Subfields include exercise physiology, neurophysiology, cardiovascular physiology, plant physiology, and comparative physiology. Clinical physiologists perform diagnostic tests of organ function, such as cardiac and respiratory investigations, within healthcare settings.

History

The discipline descends from the work of figures such as William Harvey, who described the circulation of the blood in 1628, and Claude Bernard, who developed the concept of the internal environment (milieu intérieur) in the nineteenth century. The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, first awarded in 1901, recognises major discoveries in the field.

Professional bodies

Major societies include The Physiological Society (founded 1876, United Kingdom) and the American Physiological Society (founded 1887).
