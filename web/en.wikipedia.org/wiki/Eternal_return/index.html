Eternal return

Eternal return (or eternal recurrence) is a philosophical concept that the universe and all existence and energy has been recurring, and will continue to recur, in a self-similar form an infinite number of times across infinite time or space.

Ancient thought

The concept of cyclical time is found in ancient Egyptian, Indian, Chinese and Greek thought. In classical Greece the Stoics taught that the cosmos is periodically destroyed by fire (ekpyrosis) and reborn identically, while Pythagorean tradition also held a doctrine of recurrence. In Indian religions, the wheel of time (kalachakra) and the cycle of rebirth (samsara) reflect related ideas of endless cosmic cycles.

Nietzsche

The concept is most closely associated with Friedrich Nietzsche, who presented it in The Gay Science (1882) as a thought experiment: a demon proposes that this life, exactly as lived, will recur innumerable times, and the question is whether one would greet this with despair or with joy. In Thus Spoke Zarathustra the idea becomes a test of affirmation of life, linked to the attitude of amor fati—loving one's fate. Whether Nietzsche intended it also as a literal cosmological claim remains debated among scholars.

Modern discussion

In physics, Poincaré's recurrence theorem shows that certain mechanical systems return arbitrarily close to their initial states, and some cyclic models of cosmology revive the idea of an eternally recurring universe. The concept has also been treated extensively in literature, for example by Jorge Luis Borges and in Mircea Eliade's study of myth, The Myth of the Eternal Return, which contrasts archaic cyclical time with linear historical time.
