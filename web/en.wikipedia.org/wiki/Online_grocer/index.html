Online grocer

Online grocery orders are picked in stores or automated warehouses

An online grocer is a grocery store or service that allows customers to order food and household goods over the internet — through a website or mobile app — for home delivery or in-store pickup ("click and collect"). Online grocers include supermarket chains operating their own services, pure-play internet-only retailers, and marketplace platforms that shop from partner stores on the customer's behalf.

History

The first wave of online grocers appeared during the dot-com boom of the late 1990s. Webvan, founded in 1996, raised over a billion dollars to build automated warehouses across the United States but collapsed spectacularly in 2001, becoming a symbol of the bubble. Peapod, founded in 1989 as an early dial-up service, survived and became part of Ahold Delhaize. In the United Kingdom, Tesco built a profitable store-pick model, while Ocado, founded in 2000, pioneered automated warehouse fulfilment and later licensed its technology internationally.

The sector grew steadily through the 2010s with the entry of Amazon (AmazonFresh, and its 2017 acquisition of Whole Foods Market), Walmart, and app-based intermediaries such as Instacart (founded 2012). The COVID-19 pandemic of 2020 caused an abrupt surge in adoption, pushing online grocery's share of total grocery sales from low single digits to around a tenth or more in markets such as the US and UK within months.

Business models

  • Store-pick — staff pick orders from existing supermarkets; cheap to start but labour-intensive.
  • Centralised fulfilment — dedicated warehouses or automated customer fulfilment centres, as used by Ocado.
  • Marketplace/gig platforms — independent shoppers pick and deliver from partner stores (Instacart, Deliveroo, Uber Eats).
  • Quick commerce — "dark stores" promising delivery in 10–30 minutes, a model that boomed and then consolidated sharply after 2021.

Challenges

Online grocery is notoriously low-margin: picking and last-mile delivery add costs that in-store shoppers absorb themselves, fresh and frozen goods require an unbroken cold chain, and customers dislike substitutions for out-of-stock items. Profitability has generally depended on delivery fees, minimum baskets, advertising revenue, and automation.
