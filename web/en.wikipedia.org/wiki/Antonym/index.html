Antonym

An antonym is a word or phrase that means the opposite of another word or phrase. The word antonym and the phenomenon of antonymy were introduced by Charles John Smith in 1867. Antonymy is a relation between words (lexemes) rather than between concepts in general: for example, hot and cold are antonyms, as are buy and sell.

Types of antonyms

Linguists commonly distinguish three kinds. Gradable antonyms, such as hot/cold or big/small, denote points on a scale and allow degrees and comparison; something can be neither hot nor cold. Complementary antonyms (binary pairs), such as dead/alive or true/false, divide a domain exhaustively: negating one implies the other. Converse or relational antonyms, such as buy/sell, parent/child, and above/below, describe the same relationship or event from opposite perspectives.

Auto-antonyms

Some words are their own antonyms — so-called auto-antonyms or contronyms. For example, cleave can mean both "split apart" and "adhere to", and sanction can mean both "approve" and "penalise". Such words must be disambiguated by context.

Markedness

In many gradable pairs one member is "unmarked": it is used in neutral measurement questions and nouns of extent. We ask "how long is it?" rather than "how short is it?" of an object of any length, so long is the unmarked member of long/short.
