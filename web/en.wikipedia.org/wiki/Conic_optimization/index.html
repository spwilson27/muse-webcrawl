Conic optimization

Conic optimization is a subfield of convex optimization that studies problems in which a linear function is minimised over the intersection of an affine subspace with a convex cone. In standard form, a conic program minimises cTx subject to Ax = b and x belonging to a cone K, where K is typically a product of simple cones. Because the objective and the affine constraints are linear and the cone is convex, every conic program is a convex optimisation problem and enjoys strong duality under mild conditions.

Special cases

Several classical problem classes are conic programs:

  • Linear programming — the cone is the non-negative orthant.
  • Second-order cone programming (SOCP) — the cone is one or more Lorentz (second-order) cones; it captures least squares, robust linear programs, and many engineering problems.
  • Semidefinite programming (SDP) — the cone is the set of positive semidefinite matrices; used in control theory, combinatorial relaxations, and quantum information.
  • Problems over exponential and power cones, which model entropy, geometric programming, and logistic-type objectives.

Algorithms and applications

Interior-point methods, developed for linear programming and extended by Nesterov and Nemirovski's theory of self-concordant barriers in the 1990s, solve conic programs efficiently and are implemented in solvers such as MOSEK, SeDuMi, SDPT3, and ECOS, and in modelling frameworks like CVX and CVXPY. Applications include portfolio optimisation, structural design, signal processing, compressed sensing, machine learning, and robust control.
