RET proto-oncogene

The RET proto-oncogene encodes a receptor tyrosine kinase for members of the glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) family of extracellular signalling molecules. In humans the gene is located on chromosome 10 (10q11.2). "RET" is an acronym for "rearranged during transfection", because the gene's DNA sequence was discovered to be rearranged within a 3T3 fibroblast cell line after transfection with human lymphoma DNA.

Function

RET protein is a cell-surface receptor that, together with GFRα co-receptors, binds GDNF-family ligands. Signalling through RET is essential for the development of the enteric nervous system and the kidneys, and for spermatogenesis.

Clinical significance

Loss-of-function mutations in RET cause congenital aganglionic megacolon (Hirschsprung's disease), because enteric neurons fail to develop. Gain-of-function mutations cause the hereditary cancer syndromes multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A and 2B (MEN2A, MEN2B) and familial medullary thyroid carcinoma. Somatic RET rearrangements (fusions) occur in papillary thyroid carcinoma and in a subset of non-small-cell lung cancers, and targeted RET inhibitors such as selpercatinib and pralsetinib are used to treat RET-driven cancers.
