The Book of Joshua (Hebrew: סֵפֶר יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, Sefer Yehoshua) is the sixth book of the Hebrew Bible and the Christian Old Testament, and the first book of the Former Prophets. It narrates the Israelites' conquest of Canaan under Joshua, Moses' successor, the division of the land among the twelve tribes, and Joshua's farewell addresses.


The battle of Jericho

Content

After Moses' death, Joshua leads Israel across the Jordan (chapters 1–5, including the spies and Rahab at Jericho). Chapters 6–12 describe the campaigns: the fall of Jericho, the defeat at and then victory over Ai, the treaty with the Gibeonites, the southern campaign — in which the sun is said to stand still at Gibeon — and the northern campaign against Hazor, concluding with a list of defeated kings. Chapters 13–21 allot territory to the tribes, appoint cities of refuge and Levitical towns. The book closes with the dismissal of the eastern tribes (ch. 22), Joshua's farewell sermon, and the covenant renewal at Shechem (ch. 24), ending with the deaths of Joshua and Eleazar.

Composition and historicity

Most critical scholars treat Joshua as part of the Deuteronomistic history, composed and edited mainly in the late 7th–6th centuries BC from older traditions, rather than as an eyewitness account. Archaeology has complicated a literal reading: several cities said to be destroyed (notably Jericho and Ai) show no matching destruction layers in the Late Bronze Age, and scholars debate whether Israel emerged largely from within Canaanite society. The book nevertheless preserves old local traditions and remains central to biblical theology of land and covenant.
