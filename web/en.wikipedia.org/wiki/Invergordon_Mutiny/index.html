Invergordon Mutiny


Ships of the Atlantic Fleet at Invergordon, September 1931.

The Invergordon Mutiny was an industrial action by around a thousand sailors in the British Atlantic Fleet that took place on 15–16 September 1931 at Invergordon in Scotland. For two days, sailors on several warships refused to go to sea in protest against drastic pay cuts imposed by the National Government in response to the Great Depression.

Causes

The government's economy measures cut naval pay by up to 25 per cent for men who had joined before 1925, reverting them to the lower 1919 pay scale while newer entrants were already on lower rates; the disparity, and the way the cuts emerged in the press before being properly explained to the fleet, provoked outrage. On 13 September crews began organising at the canteen ashore, and when orders were given to sail on exercises on 15 September the crews of several ships refused duty.

Course and settlement

The action was largely disciplined and non-violent; sailors carried out essential duties and maintained order aboard while declining to put to sea. The Cabinet, fearing wider unrest and facing a run on the pound, quickly conceded that no man's pay would fall by more than 10 per cent, and promised no victimisation. The fleet sailed on 16 September, although a number of men identified as ringleaders were later discharged.

Aftermath

The mutiny shocked the establishment and financial markets, contributing to Britain's abandonment of the gold standard on 21 September 1931. The Atlantic Fleet was renamed the Home Fleet to remove the association with the incident.
