Sámi parliament

A Sámi parliament is a representative body for the Sámi people, the Indigenous people of Sápmi (northern Norway, Sweden, Finland and Russia's Kola Peninsula). Elected Sámi parliaments exist in Norway, Sweden and Finland; they act mainly as consultative and administrative bodies on matters affecting Sámi culture, language and livelihoods.

Norway

The Sámi Parliament of Norway (Sámediggi) was established in 1989 under the Sámi Act of 1987 and sits in Karasjok (Kárášjohka), in a distinctive building whose plenary chamber evokes a traditional lavvu. Its 39 representatives are elected every four years by voters on the Sámi electoral roll.

Sweden and Finland

Sweden's Sámi Parliament (Sámediggi) was inaugurated in 1993 and is based in Kiruna. Finland's Sámi Parliament (Saamelaiskäräjät) was established in 1996, succeeding the earlier Sámi Delegation of 1973, and is based in Inari. The three parliaments cooperate through the Sámi Parliamentary Council.
