The Airbus A321XLR (Extra Long Range) is a long-range variant of the Airbus A321neo narrow-body airliner, launched at the Paris Air Show in June 2019. With a range of up to 4,700 nautical miles (8,700 km) — the longest of any single-aisle airliner — it enables airlines to operate thinner transatlantic and other long-haul routes economically with a narrow-body aircraft.

Development

The XLR extends the earlier A321LR chiefly by adding a permanent Rear Centre Tank in the aft cargo hold, increasing fuel capacity, together with a higher maximum take-off weight of 101 tonnes and strengthened landing gear and wing structure. Engine options are the CFM International LEAP-1A and Pratt & Whitney PW1100G geared turbofan.

Service entry

The type first flew on 15 June 2022 from Hamburg. Certification, delayed by regulatory requirements for additional fire protection around the rear centre tank, was achieved in 2024; Iberia became the launch operator, introducing the type on services including Madrid–Boston in November 2024. Large customers include IndiGo, American Airlines, United Airlines, Qantas and Aer Lingus.

Market significance

The A321XLR is widely seen as Airbus's answer to the market once served by the Boeing 757 and the proposed Boeing New Midsize Airplane, opening "long and thin" routes — for example between secondary cities across the Atlantic — that cannot support a wide-body aircraft.
