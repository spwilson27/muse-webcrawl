Staff (military)

A military staff is a group of officers and enlisted personnel that provides a flow of information and support to a commanding officer and the units under their command. The staff plans operations, gathers and analyses intelligence, manages logistics and personnel, and translates the commander's decisions into orders — but, with rare exceptions, exercises no command authority of its own.

History

Informal staffs existed for centuries in the form of aides and secretaries, but the modern professional general staff was created in Prussia in the early nineteenth century, under reformers such as Gerhard von Scharnhorst, as a response to the Napoleonic Wars. The Prussian (later German) General Staff became famous for rigorous selection, staff colleges, and systematic war planning, and its model was widely copied. Other armies developed their own forms: France's état-major and, eventually, the joint staff systems of the twentieth century.

Staff system

Many NATO and Commonwealth armies organise staff work using the "continental" or "G/J" staff system, in which functional areas are numbered: 1 — personnel, 2 — intelligence, 3 — operations, 4 — logistics, 5 — plans, 6 — signals/communications, 7 — training, 8 — finance, and 9 — civil–military cooperation. A prefix indicates the level: G for a ground-force headquarters, J for a joint headquarters, A for air, N for naval, and S at battalion or brigade level.

The staff is headed by a chief of staff (or executive officer) who coordinates its branches and acts as the commander's principal assistant. Principal staff officers advise the commander in their functional area, while personal staff (such as aides-de-camp and legal or public affairs advisers) report directly to the commander.
