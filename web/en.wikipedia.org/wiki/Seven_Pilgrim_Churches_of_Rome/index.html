Seven Pilgrim Churches of Rome

The Seven Pilgrim Churches of Rome are seven ancient and major churches traditionally visited by pilgrims walking a roughly 25-kilometre itinerary through the city, a devotion formalised by Saint Philip Neri in the 16th century.

The churches

The seven are the four major papal basilicas — St Peter's Basilica, the Archbasilica of Saint John Lateran, Santa Maria Maggiore, and Saint Paul Outside the Walls — together with San Lorenzo fuori le Mura, Santa Croce in Gerusalemme, and San Sebastiano fuori le Mura.

The pilgrimage

The walk, traditionally completed in a single day, became especially popular during Holy Years (Jubilees), when visiting the basilicas was associated with indulgences. The tradition continues today, with many pilgrims undertaking the route during jubilee years.
