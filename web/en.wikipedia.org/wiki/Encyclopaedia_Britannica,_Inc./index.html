Encyclopaedia Britannica, Inc. is the company that publishes the Encyclopædia Britannica, the world's oldest continuously published encyclopaedia. Founded in Edinburgh, Scotland, in 1768 to issue the first edition of the encyclopaedia, the company has been based in the United States since the early twentieth century and is headquartered in Chicago, Illinois. It also owns the dictionary publisher Merriam-Webster.

History

The company began as a partnership between printer Colin Macfarquhar and engraver Andrew Bell, whose first edition appeared in weekly instalments from 1768 to 1771. The Britannica grew through successive Edinburgh editions before American rights were acquired at the turn of the 20th century by Horace Everett Hooper and Walter Montgomery Jackson; Sears, Roebuck and Co. held the company for a period, after which it passed in 1943 to William Benton, who bequeathed it to the Benton Foundation. In 1996 the company was bought by Swiss financier Jacqui (Jacob) Safra.

Digital transition

Britannica was an early mover into electronic publishing, issuing a CD-ROM edition in the 1990s and launching Britannica Online in 1994. After 244 years of print publication, the company announced in March 2012 that it would no longer print the encyclopaedia, focusing on its digital products for schools, libraries, and consumers, including the Britannica website and educational software.
