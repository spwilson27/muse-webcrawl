Neapolitan pizza

Neapolitan pizza (Italian: pizza napoletana) is a style of pizza originating in Naples, Italy, made with a soft, thin-centred dough and a puffy, airy rim (cornicione), baked very briefly in an extremely hot wood-fired oven. The art of the Neapolitan pizzaiuolo (pizza maker) was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2017.

History

Flatbreads with toppings were sold to Naples' working poor in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Antica Pizzeria Port'Alba, founded as a stand for travellers in 1738 and converted to a pizzeria in 1830, is often cited as the world's first pizzeria. Tradition holds that the pizza Margherita — topped with tomato, mozzarella, and basil in the red, white, and green of the Italian flag — was created in 1889 by Raffaele Esposito of Pizzeria Brandi for Queen Margherita of Savoy, though historians debate the letter of thanks displayed at the restaurant.

Standards

The Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana (AVPN), founded in 1984, codifies the method: dough of type 0 or 00 wheat flour, water, salt, and yeast, kneaded and proofed, hand-stretched without a rolling pin, topped sparingly, and baked for 60–90 seconds on the stone floor of a wood-fired oven at about 430–480 °C. Only two variants are officially recognised as traditional: the marinara (tomato, garlic, oregano, olive oil — the older, sailor's style) and the Margherita (tomato, mozzarella — traditionally fior di latte or buffalo mozzarella from Campania — basil, oil). In the European Union, "Pizza Napoletana" holds Traditional Speciality Guaranteed (STG/TSG) status.
