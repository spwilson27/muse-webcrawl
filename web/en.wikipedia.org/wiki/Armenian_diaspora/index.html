Armenian diaspora

The Armenian diaspora refers to the communities of Armenians living outside Armenia and the Republic of Artsakh (Nagorno-Karabakh). Armenians have lived in exile communities since antiquity, but the modern diaspora was decisively shaped by the Armenian genocide of 1915–1917 in the Ottoman Empire, whose survivors scattered across the Middle East, Europe, and the Americas. Estimates of the worldwide Armenian population commonly exceed 10 million, of whom roughly 3 million live in Armenia itself; the largest diaspora communities are in Russia, the United States, France, Georgia, Iran, Lebanon, and Argentina.

History

Armenian merchant and religious communities existed from the medieval period in Persia, India, Poland, and the Ottoman cities, and the Armenian quarter of New Julfa in Isfahan dates to a deportation by Shah Abbas I in 1604. Following the genocide and the collapse of the First Republic of Armenia in 1920, refugee communities formed across the Levant (notably Beirut and Aleppo), France, and the United States (notably California). After the Second World War, Soviet Armenia encouraged some repatriation, and after the 1988 Spitak earthquake and the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, new emigration waves went to Russia, the United States, and Europe. The 2020 war and the 2023 exodus of Armenians from Nagorno-Karabakh further enlarged the diaspora.

Culture and institutions

The diaspora maintains a dense network of institutions: the Armenian Apostolic Church (under the catholicosates of Etchmiadzin and Cilicia at Antelias), Armenian schools, political parties (Dashnaktsutyun, Ramgavar, Hnchakian), and charitable and cultural organisations such as the Armenian General Benevolent Union (AGBU). Western Armenian, the diaspora's traditional literary language, is listed by UNESCO as endangered, while most diaspora communities are increasingly bilingual or assimilated into host-country languages.

Recognition campaigns

Diaspora organisations have been central to the international campaign for recognition of the Armenian genocide, commemorated annually on 24 April, and to humanitarian and political support for Armenia and Artsakh.
