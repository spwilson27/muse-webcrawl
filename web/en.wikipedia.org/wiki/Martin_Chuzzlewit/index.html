Author: Charles Dickens
Language: English
Genre: Novel
Published: 1843–1844 (serial); 1844 (book)
Full title: The Life and Adventures of Martin Chuzzlewit

The Life and Adventures of Martin Chuzzlewit is a novel by Charles Dickens, published serially in 1843–1844. Considered the last of Dickens's picaresque novels, it has selfishness as its declared theme and is noted for its vivid satire of the United States, drawn from Dickens's own American tour of 1842.

Plot summary

The wealthy old Martin Chuzzlewit quarrels with his grandson and namesake, young Martin, who has fallen in love with his grandfather's ward, Mary Graham. Disinherited, young Martin travels to America with the cheerful Mark Tapley, where he is swindled in a land scheme in the malarial settlement of Eden and nearly dies of fever. Meanwhile in England the hypocritical architect Seth Pecksniff schemes to ingratiate himself with old Martin, and the villain Jonas Chuzzlewit murders his own father and then blackmailer Tigg Montague. Old Martin, who has feigned submission to Pecksniff to test his relatives, finally exposes the hypocrite, reconciles with his grandson, and blesses his marriage to Mary.

Characters

Major figures include Seth Pecksniff, the epitome of unctuous hypocrisy; the nurse Sairey Gamp, famous for her imaginary friend "Mrs Harris" and one of Dickens's great comic creations; and the murderer Jonas Chuzzlewit.

Reception

Early sales were disappointing by Dickens's standards, but the novel's reputation has grown, and its American chapters remain a landmark of literary satire.
