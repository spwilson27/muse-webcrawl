The Bishop of Peterborough is the ordinary of the Church of England Diocese of Peterborough in the Province of Canterbury. The diocese covers the counties of Northamptonshire, Rutland, and the Soke of Peterborough in Cambridgeshire. The bishop's seat (cathedra) is located at Peterborough Cathedral, and the bishop's residence is Bishop's Lodging at The Palace, Peterborough.

History

The Diocese of Peterborough was founded during the English Reformation, created by Henry VIII in 1541 out of the Diocese of Lincoln, following the dissolution of Peterborough Abbey. The abbey church — burial place of Catherine of Aragon — survived as the new cathedral, reputedly spared from destruction because of her tomb. John Chambers, the last abbot of Peterborough, became the first bishop.

Until the creation of the Diocese of Leicester in 1927, the Peterborough diocese also included Leicestershire. The bishop is assisted by a suffragan, the Bishop of Brixworth (a title created in 1988, taken from the Northamptonshire village famous for its Anglo-Saxon church).

Role

Like other diocesan bishops of the Church of England, the Bishop of Peterborough ordains clergy, oversees the ministry and mission of the diocese, and has a seat in the House of Lords as one of the Lords Spiritual (by seniority of consecration, subject to the statutory arrangements). The bishop works with the Bishop's Council, the diocesan synod, and archdeacons of Northampton and Oakham.

Recent bishops

Donald Allister served as Bishop of Peterborough from 2010 until his retirement in 2023. Debbie Sellin, previously Bishop of Southampton, was confirmed as the 39th Bishop of Peterborough and installed in 2024, becoming the diocese's first female bishop.
