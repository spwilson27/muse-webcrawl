The sacred mountains of China are divided into several groups in Chinese religion. The best known are the Five Great Mountains (五嶽, Wǔyuè), associated with Taoism and the imperial cult, and the Four Sacred Mountains of Buddhism (四大佛教名山), each associated with a particular bodhisattva.

The stairway to the summit of Mount Tai, chief of the Five Great Mountains.

The Five Great Mountains

Since antiquity the five peaks were arranged by compass direction and offered a focus for imperial sacrifices: Mount Tai in Shandong (east), sacred above all and climbed by emperors for over two thousand years; Mount Hua in Shaanxi (west), famed for its precipitous paths; Mount Heng in Hunan (south); Mount Heng in Shanxi (north); and Mount Song in Henan (centre), near the Shaolin Temple.

The Four Buddhist Mountains

Mount Wutai (Shanxi) is the abode of Manjushri, bodhisattva of wisdom; Mount Putuo (Zhejiang), an island mountain, of Guanyin, bodhisattva of compassion; Mount Emei (Sichuan) of Samantabhadra; and Mount Jiuhua (Anhui) of Ksitigarbha. Each draws large numbers of pilgrims and is crowned with temples, monasteries and pilgrim infrastructure.

Cultural significance

The sacred mountains combine natural beauty — celebrated in centuries of Chinese painting and poetry — with dense religious heritage, and several are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Pilgrimage to them remains a major form of domestic tourism in China.
