The 2nd Canadian Division (French: 2e Division du Canada) is a formation of the Canadian Army. It was first raised in the First World War, fought at battles including the Somme, Vimy Ridge and Passchendaele, and was re-raised in the Second World War, when it became known for its costly raid on Dieppe in 1942 and its fighting in Normandy and the Netherlands. Since 2013 it has been the army's regional formation for Quebec.

First World War

Formed in 1915, the division joined the Canadian Corps in France and fought through the major Canadian battles of the war's later years, including the Hundred Days offensive of 1918.

Second World War

Mobilised in 1939, the division supplied the bulk of the troops for the Dieppe Raid of 19 August 1942, suffering over 3,300 casualties among some 5,000 Canadians landed. Rebuilt, it landed in Normandy in July 1944 and fought through the Battle of Normandy, the clearing of the Channel ports, the Battle of the Scheldt, the Rhineland and the liberation of the northern Netherlands, ending the war in northern Germany.

Modern role

Reactivated in 2013 as the successor to Land Force Quebec Area, the 2nd Canadian Division administers Regular and Reserve units across Quebec, with headquarters in Montreal.
