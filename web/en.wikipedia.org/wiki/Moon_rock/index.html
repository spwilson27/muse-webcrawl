Moon rock

Moon rock or lunar rock is rock originating from Earth's Moon. The term is most commonly used for material collected during the Apollo program (1969–1972) and the Soviet Luna and Chinese Chang'e sample-return missions. In total about 382 kilograms of Apollo samples, some 300 grams of Luna samples, and about 1.7 kilograms from Chang'e 5 are curated on Earth.

Composition

Lunar rocks fall into two main groups: the basalts of the maria and the anorthositic rocks of the highlands, together with breccias formed by impact mixing. They resemble terrestrial rocks chemically but are notably depleted in water and volatile elements, enriched in refractory elements, and — remarkably — carry oxygen isotope ratios nearly identical to Earth's, key evidence for the giant-impact hypothesis of the Moon's origin. Surface samples contain tiny glass beads of volcanic and impact origin and are implanted with solar-wind gases. Radiometric dating gives ages up to about 4.46 billion years; the famous "Genesis Rock" (sample 15415) is an anorthosite about 4.1 billion years old.

Curation and value

Most Apollo samples are stored at the Lunar Sample Laboratory Facility at Johnson Space Center in Houston. Small display samples were given to nations and U.S. states as "goodwill Moon rocks"; many have since gone missing, and Moon rock is so tightly controlled that its rare court cases and sting operations (such as Operation Lunar Eclipse) have become part of its lore. Genuine Moon rock cannot legally be owned privately except in the form of lunar meteorites, which are a separate, naturally delivered source of lunar material.
