Nasi (Hebrew title)

Nasi (Hebrew: נָשִׂיא, nāśīʾ, "prince" or "president") is a Hebrew title meaning "prince" in biblical Hebrew and "president" in modern Hebrew. In the Hebrew Bible it denotes tribal chieftains and, in Ezekiel, the future ruler of Israel. In the Mishnah and Talmud it is the title of the head of the Sanhedrin, the supreme Jewish court and legislature in the Land of Israel.

The patriarchate

From the late 2nd century CE, the Nasi — known in Greek sources as the Patriarch — became the recognised political leader of the Jewish community under Roman rule. Holders of the office from Hillel the Elder's line included Gamaliel I, Simeon ben Gamaliel, and most famously Judah ha-Nasi ("Judah the Prince", c. 135–217), compiler of the Mishnah. The patriarch collected taxes from diaspora communities, appointed judges, and regulated the calendar by proclaiming leap years and new months.

Abolition and modern usage

The Roman emperors progressively restricted the office, which was abolished around 425 CE under Theodosius II after the death of Gamaliel VI. In modern Israel, Nasi is the official title of the President of the state (Nesi Medinat Yisra'el).
