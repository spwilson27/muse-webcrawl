Ethnography

Ethnography is a branch of anthropology and the systematic study of individual cultures. It explores cultural phenomena from the point of view of the subject of the study, typically through participant observation and fieldwork.

Methods

Ethnographers immerse themselves in a community for extended periods, keeping field notes, conducting interviews, and recording everyday life. Bronisław Malinowski's Argonauts of the Western Pacific (1922) set the classic model.

History

From early travellers' accounts, ethnography became a professional method in cultural anthropology with Malinowski and Franz Boas. Claude Lévi-Strauss added structural analysis in works like Tristes Tropiques.

Forms

Modern forms include autoethnography, digital/netnography, and ethnographies of organisations and online communities. Ethical issues include informed consent and representation — the 'writing culture' critique.
