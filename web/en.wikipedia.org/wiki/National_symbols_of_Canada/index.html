National symbols of Canada

The national symbols of Canada are the symbols used in Canada and by Canadians abroad to represent the country and its people. Chief among them is the maple leaf, which has been associated with Canada since the early 18th century and appears on the national flag, adopted in 1965, and on the coats of arms of Canada and several provinces.

Principal symbols

  • National flag – the red-and-white Maple Leaf flag, adopted 15 February 1965.
  • Coat of arms – the Arms of Canada, proclaimed in 1921 and modelled on the British royal arms with Canadian additions such as maple leaves.
  • National anthem – "O Canada", proclaimed in 1980; "God Save the King" remains the royal anthem.
  • National tree – the maple, recognised in 1996.
  • National animals – the North American beaver, an official emblem since 1975, and the Canadian horse.
  • National colours – red and white, proclaimed by King George V in 1921.
  • National sport – lacrosse (summer) and ice hockey (winter), declared in the National Sports of Canada Act 1994.

Other symbols

Additional widely recognised symbols include the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (the "Mounties"), the moose, the Canada goose, the common loon (depicted on the one-dollar "loonie" coin), and landmarks such as Parliament Hill in Ottawa and the Rocky Mountains. The monarch of Canada is also a national symbol, appearing on coinage and stamps. Provincial and territorial symbols — flags, flowers, birds and tartans — supplement the national set.
