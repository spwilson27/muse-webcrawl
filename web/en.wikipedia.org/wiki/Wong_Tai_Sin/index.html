Wong Tai Sin

Wong Tai Sin or Huang Daxian (Chinese: 黃大仙) is a Chinese deity popular in Jinhua, Zhejiang, and in Hong Kong, where he is worshipped as a god of healing and fortune-telling. According to tradition, he was a shepherd named Huang Chuping who lived in the fourth century during the Eastern Jin dynasty and became an immortal after practising Taoism on Mount Jinhua.

Legend

Legend holds that Huang Chuping, a poor and honest shepherd boy, began following Taoism at the age of fifteen and achieved immortality, gaining powers such as turning stones into sheep. His brother searched for him for forty years before they were reunited.

Worship in Hong Kong

The deity was brought to Hong Kong in 1915 by a Taoist priest from Guangdong. The Wong Tai Sin Temple, built in 1921 and administered by the Sik Sik Yuen, is one of Hong Kong's most popular temples, drawing huge crowds at Lunar New Year. Worshippers practise kau chim, shaking a cylinder of bamboo sticks to obtain a fortune to be interpreted. Wong Tai Sin is famed for the belief that he "grants whatever is requested" (有求必應).

Place names

The surrounding area of Kowloon takes its name from the temple: Wong Tai Sin District and its MTR station are named after the deity.
