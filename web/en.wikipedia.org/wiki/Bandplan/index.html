Bandplan

FieldRadio spectrum management
Used byAmateur radio operators, regulators
Coordinated byIARU regions; national societies

A bandplan or band plan is a plan for using a particular band of radio frequencies, designating which portions of the band are used for which transmissions. In amateur radio, bandplans are voluntary agreements, coordinated internationally by the International Amateur Radio Union (IARU) through its three regional organisations and implemented nationally by societies and administrations, that divide each amateur band into segments for different modes and activities.

Purpose

Bandplans reduce interference between incompatible transmissions. Narrow-bandwidth modes such as Morse code (CW) and weak-signal digital modes are typically grouped at the lower edge of each band, while wideband modes such as single sideband (SSB), FM, and amateur television occupy higher segments. Specific sub-bands are reserved for beacons, satellite uplinks and downlinks, emergency communications, contest activity, and repeater inputs and outputs.

Relation to regulation

A bandplan differs from a legal frequency allocation: regulators (such as the FCC in the United States or Ofcom in the United Kingdom) define which licences may transmit where, while bandplans organise usage within those legal limits by convention. Operating outside the bandplan is not usually illegal but is considered poor practice. Regional differences exist—most famously on the 40-metre band—because allocations differ among the three ITU regions.

Examples

On the 2-metre band in North America, segments are set aside for satellite operation, weak-signal SSB and CW, FM simplex, and repeater pairs offset by 600 kHz. On HF bands, the lowest portions are conventionally CW-only, with digital modes above them and phone (voice) segments higher still.
