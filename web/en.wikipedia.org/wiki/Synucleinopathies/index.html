Synucleinopathies

Synucleinopathies (also called α-synucleinopathies) are a group of neurodegenerative diseases characterised by the abnormal accumulation of aggregates of the protein alpha-synuclein in neurons, nerve fibres, or glial cells. The three main synucleinopathies are Parkinson's disease, dementia with Lewy bodies, and multiple system atrophy.

Pathology

In Parkinson's disease and dementia with Lewy bodies, alpha-synuclein aggregates form inclusions within neurons called Lewy bodies and Lewy neurites. In multiple system atrophy, aggregates occur predominantly in oligodendrocytes as glial cytoplasmic inclusions. Misfolded alpha-synuclein is thought to spread between cells in a prion-like manner, and mutations or multiplications of the SNCA gene cause rare familial forms of Parkinson's disease.

Other conditions

Alpha-synuclein pathology also appears in pure autonomic failure, REM sleep behaviour disorder (often a prodrome of a synucleinopathy), and as a co-pathology in Alzheimer's disease.
