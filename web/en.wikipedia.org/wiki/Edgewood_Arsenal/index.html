Edgewood Arsenal

LocationAberdeen Proving Ground, Maryland, U.S.
Established1917
RoleChemical and biological defence research
ParentUnited States Army

Edgewood Arsenal is a United States Army installation on the Gunpowder Peninsula in Harford County, Maryland, forming the Edgewood Area of Aberdeen Proving Ground. Established in 1917 as a chemical weapons production site during the First World War, it became the Army's principal centre for chemical and biological warfare research, defence and — historically — human experimentation with chemical and psychoactive agents.

History

The site was chosen in 1917 for the manufacture of chemical munitions such as chlorine, phosgene and mustard gas shells. Between the wars it hosted the Chemical Warfare School. During the Second World War it expanded greatly as a research and production centre.

Human experiments

From the 1950s to the 1970s, thousands of soldier volunteers at Edgewood were exposed to chemical agents and drugs — including nerve agents, mustard gas, and the incapacitating agents BZ and LSD — in experiments intended to study their effects and test protective equipment and treatments. The programme, ended in the 1970s amid public controversy, was later investigated by Congress, and the Army acknowledged the exposures; veterans have sought recognition and compensation for resulting health problems.

Present day

Today the Edgewood Area hosts organisations including the U.S. Army Combat Capabilities Development Command Chemical Biological Center, which works on detection, protection and decontamination technologies. Former chemical weapons stockpiles stored at the site, notably mustard agent, were destroyed under the Chemical Weapons Convention, with the destruction completed in the 2000s.
