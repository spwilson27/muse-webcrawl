Schläfli symbol

In geometry, the Schläfli symbol is a notation of the form {p, q, r, ...} that defines regular polytopes and tessellations. It is named after the 19th-century Swiss mathematician Ludwig Schläfli.

Definition

For a regular polygon, the symbol {p} denotes a polygon with p sides: {3} is an equilateral triangle, {4} a square and {5} a regular pentagon. For a regular polyhedron, {p, q} denotes a solid whose faces are regular p-gons with q faces meeting at each vertex. Thus the cube is {4, 3}, the tetrahedron {3, 3}, the octahedron {3, 4}, the dodecahedron {5, 3} and the icosahedron {3, 5}.

Extensions

The symbol extends recursively to higher dimensions: a regular 4-polytope {p, q, r} has cells {p, q} with r cells around each edge. Fractional entries such as {5/2} describe star polygons like the pentagram, and {5/2, 5} the great dodecahedron among the Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra. Modified Schläfli symbols with prefixes, such as t{p, q}, are used in the notation of truncated and other uniform polytopes.
