Tinea versicolor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tinea versicolor (also known as pityriasis versicolor) is a common fungal infection of the skin caused by yeasts of the genus Malassezia, which normally live on human skin. Under warm, humid or oily conditions the yeast overgrows and produces patches that may be lighter or darker than the surrounding skin, most often on the trunk, neck and upper arms.

Signs and symptoms

The patches are typically oval, slightly scaly and only mildly itchy, if at all. On lighter skin they may appear pinkish or coppery-brown; on darker or tanned skin the affected areas fail to tan and stand out as pale spots. The condition is not contagious in the usual sense, since the causative yeast is part of the normal skin flora, and it tends to recur, especially in tropical climates and in adolescents and young adults whose sebaceous glands are active.

Treatment

Treatment includes topical antifungal creams and shampoos containing agents such as ketoconazole, selenium sulfide or zinc pyrithione; extensive or recurrent cases may be treated with oral antifungals such as fluconazole or itraconazole. Even after successful treatment, the uneven pigmentation may take months to normalise.
