The 1934 FIFA World Cup was the second edition of the FIFA World Cup, the quadrennial international football championship for men's national teams. It was held in Italy from 27 May to 10 June 1934. The host nation, Italy, defeated Czechoslovakia 2–1 after extra time in the final at the Stadio Nazionale in Rome to win the tournament. It was the first World Cup for which teams had to qualify, and the first in which the defending champions — Uruguay — did not participate, in protest at the poor European attendance at their home tournament in 1930.

Background

Italy, under the fascist government of Benito Mussolini, was chosen as host after a long bidding process, and the regime invested heavily in stadiums and propaganda, treating the tournament as a showcase for the state. Thirty-two nations entered, so a qualifying round was held for the first time, reducing the field to sixteen. The format was a straight knockout tournament: a team losing a match was eliminated, with replays for drawn games.

Tournament summary

The tournament produced several surprises. Brazil and Argentina, weakened by disputes with professional leagues, were eliminated in the first round. Spain took Italy to a replayed quarter-final in Florence after a brutal 1–1 draw, losing the replay 1–0 amid controversy over refereeing. Austria's celebrated Wunderteam reached the semi-finals but lost 1–0 to Italy in Milan. In the other semi-final, Czechoslovakia beat Germany 3–1, with a hat-trick from Oldřich Nejedlý, who finished as the tournament's top scorer with five goals.

Czechoslovakia led through Antonín Puč with nineteen minutes remaining, but Raimundo Orsi equalised for Italy late in normal time, and Angelo Schiavio scored the winner early in extra time. Italy, coached by Vittorio Pozzo, became the first European winners of the World Cup, a triumph the Mussolini regime celebrated as a vindication of fascist Italy, although several of Italy's key players — Orsi, Luis Monti, Enrique Guaita and Attilio Demaría — were oriundi, South American-born players of Italian descent.

Legacy

Italy retained the trophy in France in 1938, making Pozzo the only coach to win two World Cups with the same team. The 1934 tournament remains discussed both for its football and for the political use made of it, including allegations of favourable refereeing for the hosts — a charge historians continue to debate.
