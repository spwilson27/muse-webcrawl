West Riding of Yorkshire

The West Riding of Yorkshire is one of the three historic subdivisions of Yorkshire, England, the others being the East Riding and the North Riding. The word "riding" derives from Old Norse þriðjungr, meaning a third part. The West Riding covered the western and southern parts of the county, including Leeds, Sheffield, Bradford, Halifax, Huddersfield, Wakefield and Harrogate.

History

The ridings date from the Viking era. The West Riding became a major centre of the woollen textile industry during the Industrial Revolution, with mill towns clustered along its rivers, alongside coal mining and steelmaking in the south around Sheffield. Wakefield was the county town and seat of the West Riding County Council, established in 1889.

Abolition and legacy

The administrative county was abolished on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, its territory divided mainly between West Yorkshire, South Yorkshire, North Yorkshire, and parts of Lancashire, Greater Manchester, Cumbria and Humberside. The riding's identity persists in institutions such as the West Riding County Football Association and in Yorkshire dialect and cricket culture.
