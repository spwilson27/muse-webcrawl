Real estate broker

Brokers and agents intermediate property transactions.

A real estate broker, real estate agent, or realtor is a person who represents sellers or buyers of real estate or real property. While a broker may work independently, an agent usually works under a licensed broker to represent clients. Brokers and agents are licensed by states (in the United States) or regulated bodies elsewhere and are required to meet education and examination standards; a broker's licence generally requires more experience and coursework than a salesperson/agent licence, and only brokers may operate a brokerage or employ agents.

Brokers typically earn commissions calculated as a percentage of the sale price, traditionally shared between the buyer's and seller's brokerages. In North America, multiple listing services (MLS) pool property listings, enabling cooperation among brokers. The term Realtor is a registered trademark of the National Association of Realtors (NAR), whose members subscribe to a code of ethics. Legal settlements in the United States in 2024 (following the Sitzer/Burnett verdict) changed long-standing practices around communicating buyer-broker compensation offers through the MLS.

Duties

Duties include marketing properties, advising on pricing, arranging viewings, negotiating offers, assisting with contracts and closing, and fiduciary duties of loyalty, disclosure, and accounting to clients. Property managers who rent and manage real estate may also require a broker's licence.
