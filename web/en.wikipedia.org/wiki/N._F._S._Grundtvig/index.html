Nikolaj Frederik Severin Grundtvig (8 September 1783 – 2 September 1872), most often referred to as N. F. S. Grundtvig, was a Danish pastor, theologian, poet, historian, philosopher and politician. He is regarded as one of the most influential people in Danish history, whose philosophy gave rise to a new form of nationalism in the second half of the 19th century, and whose ideas shaped Danish education and church life.

Grundtvig is credited with founding the folk high school movement: his writings on education for the common people inspired residential schools, first established in Denmark in the 1840s–1850s, which spread across Scandinavia and beyond. His educational ideal — "a school for life" — emphasised the "living word", dialogue, history and song rather than examinations and dead book-learning.

Life and work

Born in Udby in southern Zealand, Grundtvig studied theology at the University of Copenhagen and was ordained in 1811. His outspoken sermons and his critique of rationalist theology repeatedly brought him into conflict with the church establishment; he was censured and, for years, barred from preaching. A journey to England (1829–1831) to study the Anglo-Saxon manuscripts, including Beowulf, deepened his interest in Norse mythology and the cultural heritage of the North, which he celebrated in works such as Nordens Mytologi (Northern Mythology, 1808, revised 1832).

As a hymn writer Grundtvig was extraordinarily prolific, authoring or adapting roughly 1,500 hymns; a large share of the Danish Hymn Book still consists of his texts, sung to this day, including "Dejlig er jorden" (Pilgrim Song) in translation traditions. He served several terms in parliament after Denmark's first democratic constitution of 1849 and championed freedom of education, religion and the press.

Legacy

Grundtvig's thought — often summarised in the maxim "first a human being, then a Christian" (menneske først og kristen så) — influenced Danish democracy, the cooperative movement, and adult education worldwide. The Grundtvig International network continues to promote folk education based on his principles.
