SIPTU — the Services, Industrial, Professional and Technical Union — is Ireland's largest trade union, representing around 200,000 workers in the public and private sectors across the island of Ireland. It was formed in 1990 by the merger of the Irish Transport and General Workers' Union (ITGWU) and the Federated Workers' Union of Ireland. Its head office is Liberty Hall in Dublin, a building synonymous with the Irish labour movement.

History

SIPTU's roots lie in the ITGWU, founded by Jim Larkin in 1909 and central to the Dublin Lock-out of 1913 and the development of Irish trade unionism under leaders including James Connolly. The 1990 merger created a single general union spanning manufacturing, transport, health, local authorities, construction, energy and services. SIPTU played a central role in the era of "social partnership" national wage agreements between 1987 and 2009, and in campaigns over austerity, pay restoration and housing after the 2008 financial crisis.

Structure and role

The union is organised into divisions covering sectors such as health, public administration, utilities and construction. It is the largest affiliate of the Irish Congress of Trade Unions (ICTU) and is affiliated to the Labour Party, though that relationship has periodically been debated within the union. SIPTU is frequently involved in high-profile industrial disputes and national negotiations affecting health workers, transport staff and other public servants.
