Extra innings

Extra innings is the procedure by which a baseball or softball game is extended beyond the standard number of innings — nine innings in professional baseball — when the score is tied at the end of regulation play. Additional innings are played until one team has more runs than the other at the end of a completed inning, or until the home team takes the lead in the bottom of an inning, ending the game immediately (a "walk-off").

Rules

In a standard extra-innings format, each team bats in turn just as in regulation innings. If the visiting team scores in the top half but the home team matches or exceeds it, play continues or ends accordingly. Historically, games could in principle continue indefinitely and were stopped only by darkness, curfew, or exhaustion of players; famous marathon games include the 26-inning 1–1 tie between the Brooklyn Robins and Boston Braves in 1920 (called for darkness) and the 25-inning game between the Chicago White Sox and Milwaukee Brewers in 1984.

Automatic runner rule

To shorten games, many leagues adopted a rule placing an automatic runner on second base at the start of each half-inning in extras. Versions of this rule have long been used in international competition (World Baseball Softball Confederation events), in Nippon Professional Baseball and the KBO League alongside inning limits, and in Minor League Baseball from 2018. Major League Baseball adopted the rule for the 2020 season as a health-and-safety measure and retained it in subsequent seasons for regular-season games; it does not apply in the postseason.

Other formats

Some leagues cap regular-season games: in Japan's NPB, regular-season games are declared ties after 12 innings, and in South Korea's KBO after 12 innings as well (15 in the postseason). Japanese high school baseball historically allowed games of up to 18 innings before a replay, a format that produced famous marathon contests. In some amateur and softball contexts, the international tiebreaker (automatic runner) has been used for decades.

Statistics

Roughly 8–10% of Major League Baseball games go to extra innings in a typical season. The longest MLB game by innings in the modern era to produce a winner lasted 26 innings in total frames played for a single contest; the longest by time include the 1984 White Sox–Brewers game, which took 8 hours and 6 minutes over two days. In extra innings under the automatic-runner rule, the unearned runner who scores is not charged to the pitcher as an earned run, and the runner is recorded as having reached on a fielding error for bookkeeping purposes.
