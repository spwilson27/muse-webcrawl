The Slave Craton is an Archean craton in the northwestern part of the Canadian Shield, in the Northwest Territories and Nunavut, Canada. It is named after Great Slave Lake. The craton is one of the oldest and best-studied continental nuclei in the world.

Geology

The Slave Craton consists of ancient granitic and gneissic basement overlain by greenstone belts of metamorphosed volcanic and sedimentary rocks, intruded by younger granites. Its western part contains the Acasta Gneiss, dated at approximately 4.03 billion years — among the oldest known intact rocks on Earth — making the craton central to studies of early crust formation, including controversial evidence (from detrital zircons at Jack Hills and Acasta isotopic signatures) of a Hadean precursor crust.

Economic geology

The craton hosts Canada's diamond industry: kimberlite pipes discovered in the Lac de Gras region in 1991 led to the Ekati (1998), Diavik (2003), and Gahcho Kué mines. It also contains gold deposits (Yellowknife's Giant and Con mines) and prospective base-metal and lithium occurrences.
