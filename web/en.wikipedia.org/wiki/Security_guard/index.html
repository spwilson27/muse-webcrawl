Security guard

A security guard (also known as a security officer, security inspector, or in some jurisdictions a protective agent) is a person employed by a government or private party to protect the employing party's assets (property, people, equipment, money, etc.) from a variety of hazards (such as crime, waste, damages, unsafe worker behavior, etc.) by enforcing preventative measures. Security guards do this by maintaining a high-visibility presence to deter illegal and inappropriate actions, looking (either directly through patrols, or indirectly by monitoring alarm systems or video surveillance cameras) for signs of crime or other hazards (such as a fire), taking action to minimize damage (such as warning and escorting trespassers off property), and reporting any incidents to their clients, employers and emergency services (such as the police or emergency medical services), as appropriate.

Functions and duties

Many security firms and proprietary security departments practice the "detect, deter, observe and report" methodology. Security officers are not required to make arrests, but have the authority to make a citizen's arrest, or otherwise act as an agent of law enforcement, for example, at the request of a police officer or a sheriff.

A private security officer's responsibility is protecting their client from a variety of hazards (usually in the form of criminal acts). Security personnel enforce company rules and can act to protect lives and property, and they often have a contractual obligation to provide these actions. In addition to basic deterrence, security officers are often trained to perform specialized tasks such as arrest and control (including handcuffing and restraints), operate emergency equipment, perform first aid, CPR, take accurate notes, write detailed reports, and perform other tasks as required by the client they are serving.

History

Watchmen have existed since ancient times; Roman vigiles patrolled cities as firefighters and night watchmen. The modern private security industry grew substantially in the 19th and 20th centuries with agencies such as Pinkerton in the United States and the expansion of industrial and retail security.

Personnel types

Security guards are generally uniformed to represent their lawful authority to protect private property. They are generally governed by legal regulations, which set out the requirements for eligibility (e.g., a criminal record check) and the permitted authorities of a security guard in a given jurisdiction. Types of guards include in-house (proprietary) guards employed directly by the protected organization, contract guards supplied by an agency, armed and unarmed guards, and specialized roles such as loss prevention officers, executive protection agents (bodyguards), and mobile patrol officers.

Training and regulation

Licensing requirements vary widely by country and state. Many jurisdictions require a minimum number of hours of training in topics such as legal powers and limitations, emergency procedures, and communication, before a guard may be licensed; armed guards typically face additional firearms training and background checks.
