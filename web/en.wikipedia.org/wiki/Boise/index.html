Overview

Boise is the capital and most populous city of Idaho, in the state's southwestern corner along the Boise River at the foot of the Rocky Mountain foothills. Its name derives from the French 'les bois' — 'the woods' — supposedly exclaimed by French-Canadian trappers at the sight of the tree-lined river valley after crossing dry plains.

Fort Boise was established by the Hudson's Bay Company in 1834 and the townsite laid out in 1863 during a gold rush to the Boise Basin; it became territorial capital in 1864 and state capital in 1890. The sandstone Idaho State Capitol, modelled on the US Capitol, anchors downtown.

Modern city

Boise is Idaho's centre of government, technology (Micron Technology and HP's printing division have major roots here), and education, home to Boise State University and its famous blue-turf football stadium. A greenbelt trail follows the Boise River through the city, and the North End and Hyde Park districts preserve its early streetcar-suburb character. It has been among the faster-growing US metro areas.
