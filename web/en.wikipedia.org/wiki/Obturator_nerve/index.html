Obturator nerve

Obturator nerve
FromLumbar plexus, L2–L4
InnervatesMedial compartment of thigh
DivisionsAnterior and posterior

The obturator nerve in human anatomy arises from the ventral divisions of the second, third, and fourth lumbar nerves in the lumbar plexus. It descends through the fibres of the psoas major, emerges from its medial border, and exits the pelvis through the obturator canal to supply the medial (adductor) compartment of the thigh.

Structure and function

Within the obturator canal the nerve divides into anterior and posterior divisions surrounding the adductor brevis muscle. It provides motor innervation to the adductor muscles of the thigh — including adductor longus, adductor brevis, part of adductor magnus, gracilis, pectineus and obturator externus — and sensory innervation to skin on the medial side of the thigh. An accessory obturator nerve is present in a minority of people.

Clinical significance

Damage to the obturator nerve causes weakness of thigh adduction and gait disturbance. The nerve can be blocked with local anaesthetic (obturator nerve block) for certain knee and hip procedures. Pain referred from the hip joint may be felt in the knee via shared segments with the obturator nerve.
