Prayer in the New Testament

Prayer in the New Testament is presented as a central practice of Jesus and the early church. The New Testament records Jesus praying at key moments of his ministry, teaching his disciples how to pray — most famously in the Lord's Prayer — and depicts the first Christian communities devoting themselves to prayer after Pentecost. Paul's letters repeatedly exhort believers to pray "without ceasing" and preserve early hymns and intercessions.

The prayers of Jesus

The Gospels portray Jesus withdrawing to solitary places to pray, praying before choosing the Twelve, at his baptism, at the Transfiguration, and in thanksgiving. In the Garden of Gethsemane he prays "not my will, but yours be done", and from the cross he prays words drawn from the Psalms. The Gospel of John chapter 17 preserves a long prayer for his disciples, often called the High Priestly Prayer.

Teaching on prayer

In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus warns against ostentatious and empty prayer and gives the Lord's Prayer as a model: hallowing God's name, asking for the kingdom, daily bread, forgiveness (linked to forgiving others), and deliverance from evil. Parables such as the Friend at Midnight and the Persistent Widow teach persistence, while the Parable of the Pharisee and the Tax Collector contrasts humility with self-righteousness in prayer.

Prayer in Acts and the Epistles

Acts describes the Jerusalem community devoted to "the apostles' teaching and fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers", praying together for boldness, healing, and guidance in choosing leaders. Paul urges prayer for rulers and for his mission, gives thanks for his churches, and teaches that the Spirit intercedes for believers "with groanings too deep for words" (Romans 8). The Epistle of James teaches confession, prayer for the sick, and the "effective prayer of a righteous person".
