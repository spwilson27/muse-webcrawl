Go Nagai

Born6 September 1945, Wajima, Ishikawa, Japan
OccupationManga artist
Notable worksMazinger Z, Devilman, Cutie Honey, Harenchi Gakuen

Go Nagai (born 1945) is a Japanese manga artist and a prolific author of science fiction, fantasy and horror works, credited with reshaping several genres of manga and anime. He began his career as an assistant to Shotaro Ishinomori and made his professional debut in 1967. His comedy Harenchi Gakuen (1968) became a huge success but drew protests from parent-teacher associations for its risqué humour, an early sign of his career-long willingness to provoke.

Major works

In 1972 Nagai launched Mazinger Z, whose giant robot was piloted by a human from inside — an innovation that defined the super robot genre and influenced mecha fiction worldwide. The same year he created Devilman, a dark horror-action series whose apocalyptic themes made it a landmark of manga storytelling, and Violence Jack. In 1973 Cutie Honey introduced an android heroine who could transform between identities, an important forerunner of the magical girl genre. Other works include Getter Robo (with Ken Ishikawa), which pioneered the combining robot, and Grendizer.

Legacy

Nagai founded the production company Dynamic Planning in 1969 to manage adaptations of his work. His creations have been repeatedly revived in new anime and films, including Devilman Crybaby (2018) and Mazinger Z: Infinity (2017), and a museum dedicated to him stands in his home city of Wajima. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in the history of manga.
