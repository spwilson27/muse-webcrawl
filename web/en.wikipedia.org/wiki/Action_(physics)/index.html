Common symbolS

DefinitionTime integral of the Lagrangian

In physics, action is a scalar quantity that describes how a physical system changes over time. It is defined as the integral of the Lagrangian over time:

S = ∫ L dt, where L = T − V (kinetic minus potential energy).

The dynamics of a system follow from the principle of stationary action (often called the principle of least action): the path actually taken makes the action stationary (usually a minimum) with respect to small variations. This variational formulation yields the Euler–Lagrange equations and underlies Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics.

Of all possible trajectories between fixed endpoints, the physical path makes the action stationary.

History

The idea was anticipated by Maupertuis and Euler in the eighteenth century and given general form by Lagrange, Hamilton and Jacobi. In the twentieth century the action became central to field theory, general relativity (the Einstein–Hilbert action) and quantum mechanics: in Feynman's path integral formulation, the probability amplitude sums over all paths weighted by eiS/ħ, making action — measured on the scale of the Planck constant ħ — the key quantity linking classical and quantum physics.

Applications

Action principles provide a unified route to equations of motion in mechanics, electromagnetism, relativity and quantum field theory, and make conservation laws transparent through the connection (Noether's theorem) between symmetries of the action and conserved quantities.
