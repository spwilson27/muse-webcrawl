The Journal of the Royal Statistical Society is a peer-reviewed scientific journal of statistics, published by the Royal Statistical Society. First published in 1838 as the Journal of the Statistical Society of London, it is one of the oldest and most prestigious statistics journals in the world, and it currently appears in three series covering applied and social statistics, statistical methodology and applied statistical modelling.

History

The journal grew out of the Statistical Society of London, founded in 1834, and initially devoted itself to the collection and publication of social and economic data. Over the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries it became a primary forum for the development of statistical method: R. A. Fisher, Karl Pearson, Jerzy Neyman and many others published foundational work in its pages, and its tradition of reading papers at society meetings followed by published discussions — with a vote of thanks — became a distinctive institution of British statistics.

Series

Series A (Statistics in Society) publishes papers on statistical methods applied to substantive social, economic, governmental and health questions. Series B (Statistical Methodology) is among the most highly cited journals in statistics and presents advances in statistical theory and methodology; landmark papers include foundational works on the bootstrap, generalised linear models and multiple testing. Series C (Applied Statistics) focuses on novel applications of statistical methods to real problems in industry, medicine, agriculture and elsewhere. A supplementary outlet, JRSS discussion meetings, continues the society's read-paper tradition.

Notable papers

Famous contributions include Florence Nightingale-era sanitary statistics in the Victorian journal, Fisher's work on experimental design, and the 1972 paper of Nelder and Wedderburn introducing generalised linear models (Series A) and Efron's bootstrap paper (Series B context, 1979 debate) — milestones that shaped modern statistical practice.
