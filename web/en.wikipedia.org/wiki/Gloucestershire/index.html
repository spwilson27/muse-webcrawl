Gloucestershire

Gloucestershire is a ceremonial county in South West England. It comprises part of the Cotswold Hills, part of the flat fertile valley of the River Severn, and the Forest of Dean. The county town is the city of Gloucester, and other principal settlements include Cheltenham, Stroud, Cirencester and Tewkesbury. The ceremonial county also includes the area covered by the unitary authority of South Gloucestershire.

Geography

Three distinct landscapes make up the county. The Cotswolds, a range of limestone hills designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty, cover the east and are famous for villages built of honey-coloured oolitic stone. The Severn Vale runs through the middle, carrying the River Severn — the longest river in Great Britain — south-west to its estuary, where the famous Severn bore tidal wave occurs. West of the Severn lies the ancient Forest of Dean, a former royal hunting forest and centre of coal mining and iron working.

History

The area was inhabited in Roman times, with major settlements at Glevum (Gloucester) and Corinium (Cirencester), one of the largest towns of Roman Britain. In the Anglo-Saxon period the region formed part of the kingdom of the Hwicce and later Mercia. Gloucester's cathedral, begun in 1089, contains the tomb of Edward II. The county's wool trade underpinned the wealth of Cotswold market towns, and the docks at Gloucester, linked to the Severn by the Gloucester and Sharpness Canal, were a major inland port in the Victorian era.

Culture

Gloucestershire is known for traditions including the Cooper's Hill Cheese-Rolling near Brockworth, in which competitors chase a wheel of Double Gloucester cheese down a steep slope, and for Gloucestershire Old Spots pigs and Single Gloucester cheese. Cheltenham hosts famous National Hunt horse racing at the Cheltenham Festival, centred on the Gold Cup, and music, literature and science festivals. The composer Gustav Holst was born in Cheltenham, and the poet and composer Ivor Gurney in Gloucester.
