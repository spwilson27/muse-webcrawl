A boggart is a household spirit in English folklore, particularly associated with Lancashire and Yorkshire, which causes mischief and things to disappear. In some traditions a boggart is the malevolent counterpart of the helpful brownie: when a brownie is offended or mistreated it is said to turn into a boggart, breaking dishes, souring milk, tangling objects, and tormenting the household until it leaves or is appeased.

Folklore

Boggarts were blamed for poltergeist-like disturbances — noises in the night, missing tools, curdled cream — and could also haunt outdoor places: a "boggart hole" or haunted lane, field, or marsh. Folktales tell of families who moved house to escape a boggart, only to hear its voice from the luggage ("Aye, we're flitting"), concluding it was easier to live with it. The name may be related to Middle English bogge ("bugbear, spectre") and to words like "bogey" and "bogle."

Famous boggarts

Regional legends include the boggart of Longar Hede, the Grizlehurst boggart, and the boggart of Boggart Hole Clough, a park in Manchester that preserves the creature in its name. Manchester's folklore also links boggarts to the area around present-day Boggart Hole Brook.

In popular culture

Boggarts appear in modern fantasy: in J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter series a boggart is a shapeshifter that takes the form of the viewer's worst fear, defeated by the Riddikulus charm; other authors, including Susan Cooper and Brian Jacques, use boggarts as folk creatures of varying menace.
