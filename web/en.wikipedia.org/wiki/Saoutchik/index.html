Saoutchik

Saoutchik was a French coachbuilding company founded in 1906 by Jacques Saoutchik, a Russian-born cabinetmaker who had emigrated to France. Based in Neuilly-sur-Seine near Paris, it became one of the most celebrated carrossiers of the interwar period.

Work

Saoutchik built flamboyant, luxurious custom bodies on chassis from marques including Hispano-Suiza, Delahaye, Delage, Talbot-Lago, Bugatti and Mercedes-Benz. Its designs were known for extravagant curves, chrome trim and opulent interiors; the 1930s "torpedo" and cabriolet bodies for Delahaye and Talbot-Lago are among the most prized coachbuilt cars today.

Decline

Like other French coachbuilders, Saoutchik suffered after the Second World War as mass-produced unitary car bodies replaced separate chassis. The firm struggled with post-war designs and closed in the mid-1950s. Surviving Saoutchik cars are highlights of major collections and concours events.
