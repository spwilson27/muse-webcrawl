Carpet

A carpet is a textile floor covering typically consisting of an upper layer of pile attached to a backing. The pile is traditionally made from wool, but since the 20th century synthetic fibres such as nylon, polypropylene and polyester have become common. Carpets are used to insulate a person's feet from a cold tile or concrete floor, to make a room feel more comfortable, to reduce sound, and as decoration.

History

Knotted-pile carpet weaving originated in Central and West Asia. The oldest surviving pile carpet, the Pazyryk carpet (c. 5th–4th century BC), was preserved frozen in a Siberian burial mound. The great traditions of hand-knotted rugs — Persian, Turkish, Caucasian, Turkmen, Indian and Chinese — developed over many centuries, with carpets becoming prized trade goods in Europe. In Britain, industrial centres such as Kidderminster and Wilton mechanised production in the 18th and 19th centuries; the power-loom inventions of Erastus Bigelow and the later tufting industry centred on Dalton, Georgia, made wall-to-wall carpeting a mass-market product.

Construction

Carpets may be woven (Axminster, Wilton), knotted by hand, tufted by machine through a backing, needled, or flat-woven as kilims. Pile may be cut, looped (Berber), or combined. Fitted carpets are installed over underlay and held by tack strips, whereas rugs (area rugs) rest loosely on the floor.
