Argo Navis (the Ship Argo), or simply Argo, was a large constellation in the southern sky that depicted the ship used by Jason and the Argonauts in Greek mythology. It was one of the 48 constellations described by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy in the Almagest. Because of its size, it was divided by Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille in the 1750s into three modern constellations: Carina (the keel), Puppis (the stern), and Vela (the sails).

History

The constellation was known to Greek astronomers and was associated with the myth of the Argonauts' voyage to retrieve the Golden Fleece. Its brightest star, Canopus — named for the pilot of Menelaus's fleet — is the second-brightest star in the night sky. In the 1840s John Herschel proposed dividing the constellation, and Lacaille's division, published in his 1763 catalogue, was eventually adopted universally. The modern constellation Pyxis (the compass) occupies an area once associated with the ship's mast.

Stars

Because the division preserved the original Greek-letter designations, the stars of Argo Navis are shared among the three successor constellations: for example, Alpha and Beta belong to Carina, while Puppis begins with Zeta and Rho. The region is rich in bright stars and open clusters, lying across the Milky Way, and includes the famous variable star Eta Carinae and the Carina Nebula.

Legacy

Argo Navis is the only one of Ptolemy's 48 constellations no longer recognized in its original form by the International Astronomical Union. Its name survives in the division of its stars and in the "Argo" designations still used informally by astronomers.
