Battle of Warsaw (1920)

The Battle of Warsaw (12–25 August 1920), known in Poland as the "Miracle on the Vistula" (Cud nad Wisłą), was the decisive battle of the Polish–Soviet War. Poland's army under Józef Piłsudski defeated Mikhail Tukhachevsky's Red Army offensive, saving Polish independence and halting the westward advance of Bolshevism.

Background

After Poland's Kiev offensive failed, Soviet armies drove west to Warsaw, intending to carry revolution into Germany and Europe. Polish cryptologists, who had broken Red Army codes, gave Piłsudski vital insight into Soviet dispositions, including a gap between Tukhachevsky's fronts.

Battle

From 12 August Polish forces defended the Warsaw bridgehead, notably at Radzymin, while Sikorski's 5th Army counterattacked from the Modlin area across the Wkra. On 16 August Piłsudski personally led a bold counteroffensive from the Wieprz river into the Soviet flank and rear. The Red Army's front collapsed; one Soviet army was driven into East Prussia and interned, and tens of thousands were captured.

Aftermath

Polish pursuit won further victories at Komarów and the Niemen. The Treaty of Riga (1921) fixed the Polish-Soviet border far east of the Curzon Line. Lord d'Abernon called Warsaw the eighteenth decisive battle of the world.
