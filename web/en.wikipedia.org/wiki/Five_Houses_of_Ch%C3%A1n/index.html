Five Houses of Chán

TraditionChán Buddhism (Chinese Zen)
PeriodTang and Song dynasties

Genealogy of the five houses of Chán

The Five Houses of Chán (Chinese: 禪門五家) were the five major schools of Chinese Chán Buddhism that emerged during the late Tang dynasty and flourished under the Song. They are the Guiyang, Linji, Caodong, Yunmen, and Fayan houses, each named after its founding master or his mountain.

The five houses

The Guiyang house, founded by Guishan Lingyou and his disciple Yangshan Huiji, was the first to form and the first to disappear. The Linji house, founded by Linji Yixuan, became famous for shout and staff teaching methods and later for kōan practice; it survives in Japanese Rinzai Zen. The Caodong house, founded by Dongshan Liangjie and Caoshan Benji, emphasised "silent illumination" and survives as Japanese Sōtō Zen. The Yunmen house of Yunmen Wenyan was noted for terse, enigmatic single-phrase answers, and the Fayan house of Fayan Wenyi was the last to arise.

Later history

By the late Song dynasty the Yunmen, Fayan, and Guiyang houses had been absorbed into the Linji school, leaving Linji and Caodong as the two enduring lineages of Chán, transmitted to Korea, Vietnam, and Japan.
