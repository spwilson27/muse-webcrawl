Goahti

A goahti (Northern Sámi: goahti; also gamme, kåte or kota in Norwegian) is a traditional Sámi hut or tent. The goahti can be constructed of various materials, but typically of wooden poles and covered with either wood, peat, stone, turf, or canvas/fabric — with several goahti designs ranging from a turf hut to a more castle-like structure with wooden walls.

Three main forms are recognised: the fabric-covered lavvu-like goahti with curved poles, the turf goahti with a permanent wooden frame insulated with peat, and variants used along the coast and in forests. Turf goahtis were used as semi-permanent dwellings by reindeer-herding and coastal Sámi families well into the twentieth century, and survive today as cabins and museum structures across Sápmi.

The central fireplace, the árran, traditionally organised the interior, with distinct spaces for the occupants, guests and storage, and birch-bark and peat insulation made the dwellings warm in Arctic winters.
