Serbian (српски, srpski) is a South Slavic language and the official language of Serbia, co-official in Bosnia and Herzegovina and Montenegro. It is the standardized variety of the Shtokavian dialect also underlying Croatian, Bosnian, and Montenegrin, with which it is mutually intelligible — together once treated as Serbo-Croatian.

Writing systems

Serbian is unusual in Europe for its active digraphia: it is written both in a Cyrillic alphabet (ћирилица), official in Serbia, and in a Latin alphabet (latinica), both having thirty letters in a strictly phonemic one-letter–one-sound correspondence. The modern alphabets were standardized in the nineteenth century, the Cyrillic by the reformer Vuk Stefanović Karadžić and the Latin by Ljudevit Gaj. Serbian Cyrillic differs from Russian and other Cyrillic alphabets in letters such as Ђ, Ћ, Љ, Њ, and Џ.

Dialects and features

The standard language is based on the Eastern Herzegovinian Shtokavian dialect, with Ekavian pronunciation dominant in Serbia proper and Ijekavian in Bosnia, Montenegro, and among Serbs in Croatia. Serbian has seven cases, three genders, a pitch-accent system, and a rich aspect system in verbs. Its vocabulary bears layers of loanwords from Turkish, German, Hungarian, and more recently English.
