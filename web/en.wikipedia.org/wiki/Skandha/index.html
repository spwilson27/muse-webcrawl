Skandha

Skandha (Sanskrit: स्कन्ध, "heap, aggregate"; Pali: khandha) are a central concept in Buddhism, denoting the five ever-changing constituents of a sentient being: (1) rūpa (form, matter), (2) vedanā (feeling, sensation), (3) saṃjñā (perception), (4) saṃskāra (mental formations), and (5) vijñāna (consciousness). Taken together, the skandhas are what a person mistakenly identifies as a self.

The Buddha taught that each skandha is impermanent (anicca) and that clinging (upādāna) to any of them gives rise to suffering (dukkha). No permanent self (ātman) can be found within or apart from the aggregates — the core of the doctrine of anatta (non-self). Analysis of the skandhas appears throughout the early discourses and the Abhidharma literature, where each aggregate is subdivided in great detail.

The five aggregates

Form (rūpa)

Rūpa comprises the material body and external matter, analyzed into the four great elements: earth (solidity), water (fluidity), fire (heat), and air (motion).

Feeling (vedanā)

Vedanā is the affective tone accompanying every experience: pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral. Feelings arise from sense contact and condition craving.

Perception (saṃjñā)

Saṃjñā recognizes and labels objects — colors, sounds, odors, tastes, tangibles, and mental phenomena — enabling identification.

Mental formations (saṃskāra)

Saṃskāra covers volitions and all other mental factors: attention, intention, contact, desire, and the fifty-odd mental states catalogued in Abhidharma texts. These formations drive karmic activity.

Consciousness (vijñāna)

Vijñāna is awareness of an object through one of the six sense bases: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind. It arises dependently and ceases when its conditions cease.

In the suttas

In the Khandha Saṃyutta of the Saṃyutta Nikāya, the Buddha repeatedly asks monks to regard each aggregate as "this is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self." The Pheṇapiṇḍūpama Sutta compares form to a lump of foam, feeling to a bubble, perception to a mirage, formations to a banana trunk, and consciousness to a magician's illusion.
