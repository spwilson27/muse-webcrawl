Minestrone

Minestrone (Italian: [mineˈstroːne]) is a thick soup of Italian origin made with vegetables, often with the addition of pasta or rice. The name derives from minestra (soup) with the augmentative suffix -one, literally "the big soup." There is no fixed recipe: minestrone is traditionally made from whatever vegetables are in season, reflecting its origins as a peasant dish.

Ingredients and variations

Common ingredients include beans (especially borlotti), onions, celery, carrots, tomatoes, leafy greens such as chard or cabbage, and stock. Regional versions vary widely: Ligurian minestrone is finished with pesto, Milanese versions may include rice and pancetta, and Tuscan ribollita is a close relative thickened with bread. Some versions are entirely vegetarian; others use a beef or chicken stock or a ham bone for depth.

History

Hearty vegetable soups have been eaten in Italy since Roman times; Apicius's cookbook describes a vegetable soup thickened with grains. As new vegetables arrived from the Americas — tomatoes, potatoes, beans — they were absorbed into the dish. Minestrone remains a staple of Italian home cooking and of the cucina povera tradition of making filling meals from humble ingredients, akin to other classics of Italian cuisine.
