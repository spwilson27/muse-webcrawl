Jan Matthys

Jan Matthys (also spelled Jan Matthijs or Jan van Leyden's predecessor Jan Mathijs; c. 1500 – 5 April 1534) was a Dutch Anabaptist leader from Haarlem who became the dominant prophet of the radical Anabaptist movement in Münster during its early phase. A baker by trade, Matthys proclaimed himself a prophet — identifying with the biblical Enoch — and predicted the end of the world at Easter 1534.

Jan Matthys, Anabaptist prophet of Münster.

Life

Matthys was a baker in Haarlem who was rebaptised into the Anabaptist faith, which rejected infant baptism and anticipated an imminent apocalypse. His fiery preaching attracted followers across the Low Countries, and he dispatched "apostles" to spread his message; among those he baptised and sent out was Jan van Leiden (Jan of Leyden). Expelled from Amsterdam, Matthys eventually made his way to Münster in Westphalia, where Anabaptists under Bernhard Rothmann had taken control of the city in early 1534.

Münster

In Münster, Matthys displaced the local leadership and ruled the city as a prophet, ordering the expulsion of the "godless" (Catholics and Lutherans who refused rebaptism), the burning of books other than the Bible, and the communalisation of property and money. As the prince-bishop Franz von Waldeck besieged the city, Matthys prophesied that God would deliver Münster on Easter Sunday 1534. On Easter Day, 5 April 1534, he rode out of the gates with a tiny band of followers to face the besiegers, apparently expecting divine intervention. He was cut down and killed, and his head was displayed on a pike by the besieging army.

Aftermath

Matthys's death did not end the rebellion: his disciple and successor Jan van Leiden took control of Münster, proclaimed himself king, and held the city until its fall in June 1535. The catastrophe of the Münster kingdom discredited revolutionary Anabaptism and pushed the movement's surviving branches, under leaders such as Menno Simons, toward pacifism.
