Armed Forces of the Argentine Republic

Members of the Argentine armed forces during a ceremonial parade.

The Armed Forces of the Argentine Republic (Spanish: Fuerzas Armadas de la República Argentina) are the combined military forces of Argentina. They comprise the Argentine Army, the Argentine Navy, and the Argentine Air Force, and are controlled by the President of Argentina, the commander-in-chief, acting through the Ministry of Defense.

Structure

Operational coordination is provided by the Joint General Staff (Estado Mayor Conjunto). The Army is the land warfare branch; the Navy includes naval aviation and the Naval Infantry (marines); the Air Force, created in 1945, is one of the oldest independent air forces in Latin America. The Argentine National Gendarmerie and Naval Prefecture are security forces with military characteristics that can be placed under defence authority in wartime.

History

The armed forces trace their origins to the independence wars of the early 19th century, when forces under generals such as José de San Martín fought Spanish colonial rule. Through much of the 20th century the military intervened repeatedly in politics, ruling the country after coups in 1930, 1955, 1966 and 1976. The last military dictatorship (1976–1983) ended after Argentina's defeat by the United Kingdom in the Falklands War (Guerra de las Malvinas) of 1982, and democratic civilian control was restored.

Modern role

Conscription was abolished in 1994 (after the murder of conscript Omar Carrasco) and replaced by voluntary service. Argentina contributes troops to United Nations peacekeeping missions and maintains a presence in Antarctica, where the armed forces support scientific bases.
