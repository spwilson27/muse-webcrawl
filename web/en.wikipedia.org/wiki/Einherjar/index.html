Einherjar

In Norse mythology, the einherjar (Old Norse "single fighters" or "once-warriors") are warriors who have died in battle and are brought by the valkyries to Valhalla, the great hall of the god Odin in Asgard. There they spend their days fighting one another and their nights feasting, preparing for Ragnarök, the final battle.

Life in Valhalla

According to the Prose Edda and Poetic Edda, the einherjar battle each day on the fields of Asgard; those slain are restored to life by evening, when all feast together on the flesh of the boar Sæhrímnir, which is cooked daily and returns to life, and drink mead that flows from the udders of the goat Heiðrún. Their food is served by the valkyries.

Numbers and Ragnarök

The poem Grímnismál describes Valhalla as having 540 doors, through each of which 800 einherjar will march when they go out to fight the wolf Fenrir at Ragnarök. Despite their vast numbers, the einherjar and the gods are fated to fall in that battle, after which a renewed world will emerge.

Sources

The einherjar appear in the Poetic Edda poems Grímnismál, Vafþrúðnismál, and Helgakviða Hundingsbana II, and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.
