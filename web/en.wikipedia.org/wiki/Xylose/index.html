Xylose

Xylose
Formula: C₅H₁₀O₅
Molar mass: 150.13 g/mol
Type: Aldopentose monosaccharide
Appearance: White crystalline solid

Xylose (from Greek xylon, "wood") is a sugar first isolated from wood, after which it is named. It is classified as a monosaccharide of the aldopentose type, meaning it contains five carbon atoms and includes an aldehyde functional group. D-Xylose is one of the principal building blocks of hemicellulose — in the form of the polysaccharide xylan — and is therefore one of the most abundant sugars in plant biomass and in nature generally.

Occurrence and production

Xylose is obtained by hydrolysis of xylan-rich materials such as birch and other hardwoods, corn cobs, straw, and sugarcane bagasse. In solution it exists mainly in ring (pyranose) forms. It is not digested as a source of calories to the same extent as glucose; its hydrogenated derivative, xylitol, is widely used as a low-calorie sweetener.

Uses

Xylose is used in the production of furfural, an industrial solvent and platform chemical; in dyestuffs and tanning; in diagnostic medicine, where the D-xylose absorption test assesses intestinal malabsorption; and in biotechnology, where fermenting xylose from lignocellulosic feedstocks to ethanol and other products is a major research goal, since most common yeasts such as Saccharomyces cerevisiae do not naturally ferment it efficiently.
