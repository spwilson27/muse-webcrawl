Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller National Historical Park is a United States National Historical Park in Woodstock, Vermont, preserving the home and managed forest of three generations of conservationists. It is Vermont's only unit of the National Park System and the only national park dedicated to telling the story of conservation history and land stewardship in America.

History

The park is named for George Perkins Marsh, the author of Man and Nature (1864), one of the founding texts of the environmental movement, who was born on the property; Frederick Billings, a railroad magnate who purchased the estate in 1869 and reforested its denuded hillsides, planting thousands of Norway spruce, pine, and other trees guided by Marsh's ideas; and Laurance Rockefeller, Billings's grandson-in-law, who with his wife Mary French Rockefeller donated the property to the American people. The park was authorized in 1992 and opened to the public in 1998.

Features

The park includes the Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller Mansion, a Queen Anne-style house built in 1805 and remodelled in 1869 and 1885, filled with artworks including paintings by Hudson River School artists such as Albert Bierstadt. Around it stretches one of the oldest scientifically managed forests in the United States, with some 20 miles of carriage roads and trails. Across the road stands the Billings Farm & Museum, a working dairy farm and museum of rural life operated by the Woodstock Foundation.
