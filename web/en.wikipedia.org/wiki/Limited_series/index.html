Limited series

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A limited series is a format used in both comics publishing and television for a story told in a predetermined, finite number of installments, with a self-contained narrative arc and a definite conclusion.

In comics

In the comics industry, a limited series (also called a miniseries or maxiseries depending on length) is a comic book series with a planned number of issues, as opposed to an ongoing series. DC Comics popularised the form in the late 1970s and 1980s with titles such as Camelot 3000 (a twelve-issue "maxiseries") and Watchmen (1986–87). The format allows creators to tell complete stories about popular characters without disrupting ongoing continuity, and successful limited series are commonly reprinted as trade paperbacks or graphic novels.

In television

In television, a limited series (historically called a miniseries) is a programme that tells a complete story in a fixed number of episodes, typically between three and thirteen, with no intention of continuation. The format revived strongly in the 2010s with prestige productions; awards bodies such as the Primetime Emmy Awards maintain separate categories for limited or anthology series. Examples include Chernobyl, The Queen's Gambit, and Band of Brothers. Some limited series are later renewed or converted into anthology formats after unexpected success.
