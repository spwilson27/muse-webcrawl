Rerun

A rerun or repeat is a rebroadcast of an episode of a television or radio programme. There are two types: those that occur during a hiatus in the programme's current season, and those that occur after the series has ended — the latter feeding the market for broadcast syndication.

History

Early television was broadcast live and largely unrecorded, so repeats were rare until kinescope recording and then videotape made programmes reusable. The pivotal figure was Desi Arnaz, who insisted that I Love Lucy (1951) be filmed with three cameras on high-quality film stock so episodes could be rebroadcast; when Lucille Ball's pregnancy forced the show to repeat episodes in 1953, the strong ratings proved reruns viable. Networks soon built the practice into the television year: new episodes ran from autumn to spring, with repeats through the summer.

Economics

Reruns are central to the economics of television. A series that reaches roughly 100 episodes can be "stripped" in syndication — shown five days a week — generating revenue long after production ends; classics such as Star Trek and Seinfeld earned far more in reruns than in first run. Residual payments to actors, writers and directors for reruns are a major issue in entertainment labour contracts and were central to the 2023 Hollywood strikes. Cable channels and later streaming services were built substantially on libraries of previously aired programmes.

Terminology

Networks seeking to disguise repeats have rebranded them as "encore presentations". In British usage programmes are simply "repeats". The term should not be confused with a remake, in which a programme or episode is produced again from scratch.
