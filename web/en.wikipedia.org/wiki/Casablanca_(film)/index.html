Overview

Casablanca is a 1942 American romantic drama film directed by Michael Curtiz, starring Humphrey Bogart as Rick Blaine and Ingrid Bergman as Ilsa Lund. Set in Vichy-controlled Casablanca during the Second World War, it follows an expatriate nightclub owner torn between love and virtue: he must choose whether to help his former lover and her husband, a Czech resistance leader, escape to continue the fight against the Nazis.

Legacy

Made from the unproduced play Everybody Comes to Rick's and written largely on the fly, Casablanca premiered as Allied troops landed in North Africa — real events boosting its impact. It won three Academy Awards including Best Picture and has grown into one of the most beloved films ever made, its dialogue ("Here's looking at you, kid", "Round up the usual suspects", "We'll always have Paris", and the misquote "Play it again, Sam") woven into the language. In the final scene Rick sends Ilsa away and walks off with Captain Renault into the fog — "the beginning of a beautiful friendship". The film ranks consistently near the top of critics' and AFI polls of the greatest films.
