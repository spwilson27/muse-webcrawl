Native advertising

Forms: Sponsored content, in-feed ads, promoted listings, advertorials
Related: Content marketing, product placement
Regulators: FTC (US), ASA (UK)

Native advertising is a type of advertising in which the ad matches the form, style and function of the editorial content around it. Examples include sponsored articles on news sites, promoted posts in social media feeds, paid search results and "recommended stories" widgets. The word "native" refers to the ad's coherence with other content on the platform, in contrast to banner ads and pop-ups, which audiences increasingly ignore or block.

History

The practice has roots in twentieth-century advertorials and product placement, but grew rapidly in the 2010s as publishers sought revenue beyond display advertising and advertisers followed audiences to feeds. Social platforms such as Facebook and Twitter built their ad businesses on in-feed native formats, and publishers including major newspapers created in-house "brand studios" to produce sponsored content.

Criticism and regulation

Critics argue native advertising blurs the line between journalism and marketing and can mislead readers. Regulators require disclosure: the U.S. Federal Trade Commission's 2015 enforcement guidance requires that ads be identifiable before consumers engage with them, and sponsored content is typically labelled "Sponsored", "Paid post" or "Promoted". Studies consistently find that many readers fail to notice such labels.
