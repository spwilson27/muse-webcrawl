Charleston is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of West Virginia. Located at the confluence of the Elk and Kanawha rivers, it is the county seat of Kanawha County.

History

The area was settled in the late 18th century around Fort Lee and grew with the salt industry along the Kanawha River and later coal and chemical manufacturing. Charleston became the permanent state capital in 1877 after a statewide vote, following a period in which the capital alternated with Wheeling.

Economy and culture

Charleston is a centre for government, banking, and the chemical industry. Landmarks include the gold-domed State Capitol, designed by Cass Gilbert, the Clay Center for the Arts and Sciences, and the Capitol Market.
