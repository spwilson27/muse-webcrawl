Henan (Chinese: 河南; pinyin: Hénán) is a province in Central China and is part of the Central Plains region. The name Henan means "south of the (Yellow) River". With a population of about 99 million, Henan is the third-most populous province of China and, for much of its history, its political, economic, and cultural heartland. Its capital and largest city is Zhengzhou.

Henan is widely regarded as the cradle of Chinese civilization: some of the earliest Chinese dynasties had their capitals in the province, including at Luoyang and Kaifeng. More than 200 emperors across over twenty dynasties ruled from Henan over the course of three millennia. Four of the Eight Great Ancient Capitals of China — Luoyang, Kaifeng, Anyang, and Zhengzhou — are located in Henan.

History

Archaeological sites such as Yangshao and Dawenkou attest to Neolithic cultures on the North China Plain. The Erlitou culture near Luoyang is often identified with the semi-legendary Xia dynasty. Anyang was the capital of the late Shang dynasty, and its oracle bones preserve the earliest known Chinese writing. During the Zhou and Han dynasties, Luoyang served repeatedly as an imperial capital, and it was from Luoyang — the eastern terminus of the Silk Road — that the monk Xuanzang's contemporaries and Buddhist sculptors carved the Longmen Grottoes. Kaifeng became capital of the Northern Song dynasty in the 10th–12th centuries, when it was among the largest and most prosperous cities in the world.

The province suffered grievously in the modern era: the 1938 Yellow River flood, deliberately unleashed by the Nationalist government to slow the Japanese invasion, killed hundreds of thousands in Henan and the neighbouring provinces, and the famine of 1942–43 killed millions more. The Yellow River's catastrophic shifts of course across the Henan plain over the centuries earned the province a long association with flood and famine.

Geography

The eastern and central parts of Henan form part of the flat, fertile North China Plain, while mountains — the Taihang in the northwest, the Qinling and Funiu ranges in the west, and the Dabie Mountains in the south — occupy the province's fringes. The Yellow River crosses the north of the province; the Huai River rises in its south. Henan borders Hebei to the north, Shandong to the northeast, Anhui to the southeast, Hubei to the south, Shaanxi to the west, and Shanxi to the northwest. The climate is temperate continental, with hot, rainy summers and cold, dry winters.

Economy

Henan is one of China's largest agricultural producers, ranking first in wheat output and also producing maize, peanuts, sesame, and tobacco. Since the reform era, it has developed a large industrial base in food processing, textiles, machinery, metallurgy, and electronics; Zhengzhou hosts one of the world's largest smartphone assembly plants (Foxconn's "iPhone City"). The province is a major transport hub: Zhengzhou sits at the junction of China's north–south and east–west high-speed rail corridors. Henan's total GDP ranks among the top five of Chinese provinces, though GDP per capita remains below the national average.

Culture and sites

Famous sites include the Longmen Grottoes (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), the Shaolin Monastery in the Song Mountains — legendary birthplace of Chan (Zen) Buddhism and Shaolin kung fu — the White Horse Temple near Luoyang, China's first Buddhist temple, and the Yin Xu ruins at Anyang. Henan is also considered the heartland of Chinese martial arts, with both Shaolin kung fu and tai chi (from Chenjiagou village) originating there.
