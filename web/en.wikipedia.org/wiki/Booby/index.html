A booby is a seabird in the genus Sula, part of the family Sulidae. Boobies are closely related to the gannets (Morus), which were formerly included in Sula as well. They are large birds with long pointed wings and bills, famous for their spectacular plunge-diving for fish and their elaborate courtship dances on land.

Name

The name "booby" derives from the Spanish slang bobo, meaning stupid — a reference to the birds' tameness and clumsiness on land, where sailors could easily catch them. The genus name Sula comes from the Old Norse and Icelandic word for a gannet.

Species

Six living species are recognised: the blue-footed booby (Sula nebouxii) of the eastern Pacific and Galápagos, the Peruvian booby, the masked booby, the Nazca booby, the red-footed booby, and the brown booby. The Abbott's booby, once placed in Sula, is now in its own genus Papasula.

Behaviour

Boobies hunt by diving from heights of 10–30 metres (sometimes up to 100 m) into the ocean at high speed, pursuing fish and squid underwater with their wings and feet. They breed colonially on islands and coasts, laying one to three chalky blue eggs in a scrape on the ground. In species laying multiple eggs, obligate siblicide often occurs: the elder chick ejects or starves its sibling. The blue-footed booby's courtship involves a conspicuous high-stepping dance in which the male shows off his bright blue feet, whose colour signals health and is used in mate choice.
