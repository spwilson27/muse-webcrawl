Prague Conservatory

The Prague Conservatory (Czech: Pražská konzervatoř) is a music and drama school in Prague, Czech Republic, founded in 1808. It is one of the oldest conservatories in Europe, modelled on the Paris Conservatoire, and it offers secondary and tertiary education in instrumental and vocal performance, composition, conducting and acting.

History

The conservatory was founded by Prague aristocrats and musicians as an association for the cultivation of music, beginning instruction in 1811. It was housed at various locations before settling in the former Benedictine monastery quarter near the Vltava. Directors included Jan Bedřich Kittl and, most famously, Antonín Dvořák, who taught composition there from 1891 and served as director from 1901 to 1904.

Notable alumni and faculty

Graduates and teachers of the conservatory include composers Josef Suk, Vítězslav Novák and Bohuslav Martinů, violinists Jan Kubelík and Josef Suk the younger, and conductors such as Václav Talich. The school's orchestra and its alumni have played an important role in Czech musical life for two centuries.

Programme

Students enter after primary school and study for six years (eight for some fields), graduating with a diploma. In 1946 a dance department was added, which later became a separate conservatory, and a popular-music department followed in the 21st century.
