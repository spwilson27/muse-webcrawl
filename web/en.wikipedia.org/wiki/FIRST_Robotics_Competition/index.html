The FIRST Robotics Competition (FRC) is an international high-school robotics competition organised by FIRST (For Inspiration and Recognition of Science and Technology). Each year, teams of students and volunteer mentors design, build and programme industrial-sized robots to compete in a themed game, with a new challenge revealed each January.

History

FIRST was founded in 1989 by inventor Dean Kamen, and the first FRC competition was held in 1992 with 28 teams in a New Hampshire high-school gymnasium. The competition has since grown to thousands of teams from dozens of countries, with a championship event held annually in the United States.

Competition structure

Teams receive a kit of parts and have a limited build period to construct robots weighing up to about 125 pounds (57 kg) excluding battery and bumpers. At events, teams form alliances of three robots that cooperate against an opposing alliance, combining autonomous and driver-controlled periods. FIRST emphasises "gracious professionalism" and "coopertition" alongside competitive success.

Impact

FRC is the oldest and largest of FIRST's programmes, which also include FIRST LEGO League and FIRST Tech Challenge. Alumni are significantly more likely to pursue STEM degrees and careers.
