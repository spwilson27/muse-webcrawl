Fort Ontario Emergency Refugee Shelter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Fort Ontario Emergency Refugee Shelter was a camp at Fort Ontario in Oswego, New York, that housed nearly 1,000 European refugees from 1944 to 1946. Established by President Franklin D. Roosevelt, it was the only refugee shelter created in the United States during the Second World War for people fleeing Nazism.

Establishment

In June 1944, facing pressure to rescue Jewish refugees from Europe, Roosevelt announced that about 1,000 refugees would be brought from liberated southern Italy to Fort Ontario as "guests" of the United States, outside the immigration quota system. The 982 refugees who arrived in August 1944 — aboard the US Army transport Henry Gibbins — were mostly Jewish and came from 18 countries; many had survived concentration camps or life in hiding. The shelter was administered by the War Relocation Authority, directed on site by Ruth Gruber, who had accompanied the refugees across the Atlantic and documented their story.

Life in the camp

Residents were technically interned behind fences under guard, unable to leave freely or work outside the camp, though relations with the people of Oswego were warm and residents attended local schools and organised schools, newspapers, religious services and cultural life inside the fort. Uncertainty about whether they would be forced to return to Europe after the war caused considerable anxiety.

Resolution

In December 1945 President Harry S. Truman directed that the Fort Ontario refugees be given priority for immigration visas, allowing them to remain in the United States legally; the shelter closed in February 1946. The site is now commemorated by the Safe Haven Holocaust Refugee Shelter Museum.
