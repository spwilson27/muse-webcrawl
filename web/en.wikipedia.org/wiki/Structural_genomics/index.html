Structural genomics

Structural genomics seeks to describe the 3-dimensional structure of every protein encoded by a given genome. This genome-based approach allows for a high-throughput method of structure determination by a combination of experimental and modeling approaches. The principal difference between structural genomics and traditional structural prediction is that structural genomics attempts to determine the structure of every protein encoded by the genome, rather than focusing on one particular protein.

Methods

Structural genomics pipelines typically involve high-throughput gene cloning and protein expression, purification, crystallization or sample preparation, and structure determination by X-ray crystallography, NMR spectroscopy, or cryo-electron microscopy. Results are deposited in public databases such as the Protein Data Bank. Large coordinated efforts, such as the US Protein Structure Initiative (2000–2015) and Japan's RIKEN Structural Genomics/Proteomics Initiative, solved thousands of structures, prioritising proteins with novel folds and representatives of large sequence families so that homology modelling could extend coverage to many more proteins.

Significance

By mapping protein folds across genomes, structural genomics has clarified protein family relationships, aided functional annotation, and provided starting points for drug discovery. The rise of accurate computational prediction, notably AlphaFold, has since transformed the field's goals, shifting emphasis toward complexes, dynamics, and proteins that remain experimentally intractable.
