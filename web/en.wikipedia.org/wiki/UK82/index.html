UK82

UK82 is a subgenre of punk rock that emerged in the United Kingdom in the early 1980s, named after the year 1982 and a term popularized in connection with the Scottish band The Exploited. UK82 bands took the sound and ethos of 1970s punk and hardened them: faster tempos, heavier guitar distortion, shouted vocals, and a signature relentless drumming style later dubbed the "D-beat", pioneered by Discharge.

Characteristics and bands

The movement is closely associated with bands such as Discharge, The Exploited, GBH (Charged GBH), Chaos UK, The Varukers, English Dogs and Broken Bones. Compared with the more melodic Oi! scene or the politically elaborate anarcho-punk of Crass, UK82 emphasised aggression, speed and bleak imagery — war, nuclear annihilation and social decay — often matched by a visual style of tall mohawks and studded leather.

Influence

UK82 was enormously influential on later extreme music. Discharge's D-beat became a foundational rhythm of hardcore punk and crust punk in Scandinavia, Japan and the United States, and the genre fed directly into thrash metal; bands such as Metallica and Slayer cited UK82 groups among their influences. Though the original wave faded by the mid-1980s as many bands crossed over into metal, UK82 records remain central documents of punk's second wave.
