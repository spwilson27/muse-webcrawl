Denis Clive "Denny" Hulme OBE (18 June 1936 – 4 October 1992) was a New Zealand racing driver who won the Formula One World Drivers' Championship in 1967 with the Brabham team. Between his debut at Monaco in 1965 and his final race at the 1974 United States Grand Prix, he started 112 Grands Prix, winning eight. He remains the only New Zealander to have won the Formula One World Championship.

Nicknamed "The Bear" for his rugged appearance and gruff manner, Hulme drove barefoot in his earliest racing in New Zealand. He came to England in 1960 under a driver development scheme and worked as a mechanic at Jack Brabham's garage before racing for the Brabham team in Formula One.

Formula One career

Hulme joined Brabham full-time in 1966 and won the 1967 title through consistency, scoring two wins (Monaco and Germany) and defeating team-mate and team owner Jack Brabham. In 1968 he moved to Bruce McLaren's team, McLaren, where he stayed until his retirement from Formula One at the end of 1974. With McLaren he won six more Grands Prix and finished third in the championship in 1968 and 1972.

Can-Am and other racing

Hulme was a dominant force in the North American Can-Am sports car series, winning the championship in 1968 and 1970 for McLaren and finishing runner-up three times; he won 22 Can-Am races. He also competed at Indianapolis and in touring cars late in his career, winning the 1986 Tourism Trophy at Silverstone.

Death

Hulme died on 4 October 1992 after suffering a heart attack while driving a BMW M3 in the Bathurst 1000 in Australia. He managed to slow the car and bring it to a halt against a wall, avoiding injury to others; he was 56. The Denny Hulme Memorial Trophy is awarded in his honour, and he was inducted into the International Motorsports Hall of Fame.
