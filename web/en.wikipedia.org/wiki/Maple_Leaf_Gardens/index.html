Maple Leaf Gardens

Maple Leaf Gardens is a historic building at 60 Carlton Street in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. Opened in 1931, it was the home arena of the Toronto Maple Leafs of the National Hockey League from 1931 to 1999 and one of the most famous hockey arenas in the world. It was designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 2007.

History

Built in just five and a half months during the Great Depression under Maple Leafs managing director Conn Smythe, the arena opened on 12 November 1931 with a game against the Chicago Black Hawks. Its distinctive art deco exterior, domed roof and marquee made it a Toronto landmark. Beyond hockey it hosted basketball (including the first NBA game, in 1946, between the Toronto Huskies and the New York Knickerbockers), boxing, wrestling, political rallies, and concerts by artists including Elvis Presley, the Beatles and Bob Dylan.

After the Leafs

The Maple Leafs played their last game at the Gardens on 13 February 1999, moving to the Air Canada Centre. After years of uncertainty, the building was acquired jointly by Ryerson University (now Toronto Metropolitan University) and the grocery chain Loblaws. It reopened in 2012 as the Mattamy Athletic Centre, containing a supermarket at street level and a multi-purpose arena — home of the TMU Bold — under the restored dome, preserving the building's exterior and much of its character.
