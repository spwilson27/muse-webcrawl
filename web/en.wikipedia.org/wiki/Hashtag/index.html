Hashtag

A hashtag is a metadata tag used on social networks and microblogging services, formed by prefixing a word or phrase with the hash character (#), for example #hashtag. Hashtags make user-generated content searchable and group posts about the same topic into a single stream, allowing users to follow subjects and conversations of interest.

The hash sign gives the hashtag its name.

History

The use of the hash sign to label groups and topics began on Internet Relay Chat. In August 2007, designer Chris Messina proposed using the same convention on Twitter for grouping tweets; Twitter initially resisted but began hyperlinking hashtags in July 2009 after users embraced them during events such as the 2007 San Diego forest fires. The feature spread to Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, and most other social platforms, and "hashtag" was added to the Oxford English Dictionary in 2014.

Uses

Hashtags serve for commentary and humour, for tracking news and live events, for marketing campaigns, and for political and social movements — campaigns such as #MeToo, #BlackLivesMatter, and #IceBucketChallenge demonstrated their power to organise attention at global scale. Platforms typically surface "trending" hashtags, and etiquette guides advise against overloading posts with too many tags.
