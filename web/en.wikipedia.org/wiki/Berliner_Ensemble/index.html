Berliner Ensemble

The Berliner Ensemble is a German theatre company founded in 1949 by the playwright Bertolt Brecht and his wife, the actress Helene Weigel, in East Berlin. It is one of the most famous German-language theatre companies in the world, renowned for its productions of Brecht's plays and for the development and demonstration of his theory of "epic theatre".

History

Brecht and Weigel, returning from exile after the Second World War, founded the company in the Deutsches Theater in 1949, opening with Brecht's Mother Courage and Her Children, with Weigel in the title role — a production that became the model of Brechtian staging. In 1954 the company moved to the Theater am Schiffbauerdamm, a historic venue associated with the premiere of The Threepenny Opera in 1928.

Tours to Paris (1954, 1955) and London (1956) brought Brecht's staging methods international attention shortly before his death in 1956. Weigel directed the company until 1971. Later artistic directors included Ruth Berghaus, Manfred Wekwerth, Heiner Müller and Claus Peymann (1999–2017), under whom the company became a prominent, sometimes combative, presence in reunified Berlin's theatre scene. Oliver Reese became artistic director in 2017.

Style

The ensemble's classic productions embodied Brecht's Verfremdungseffekt ("alienation effect"): visible lighting, placards, songs interrupting the action, and acting that presented characters for critical judgement rather than empathy. The company's meticulous "model books" documenting its stagings influenced theatre practice worldwide.
