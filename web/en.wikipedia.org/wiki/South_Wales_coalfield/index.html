South Wales coalfield

The South Wales coalfield is a large region of south Wales that is rich in coal deposits, especially the South Wales Valleys. It lies in parts of the counties of Carmarthenshire, Swansea, Neath Port Talbot, Bridgend, Rhondda Cynon Taf, Merthyr Tydfil, Caerphilly, Blaenau Gwent, Torfaen and Monmouthshire.

Geology

The coalfield consists of Carboniferous rocks, including the South Wales Coal Measures Group, which are folded into a basin structure. Coal seams vary in quality from bituminous coals in the east to high-grade anthracite in the north-west. The field produces some of the finest steam coal in the world, prized historically for naval and railway use because it burns with little smoke.

Industrial history

Coal mining expanded dramatically during the 19th century as demand grew from ironworks, steamships and export markets. Ports at Cardiff, Swansea and Newport shipped Welsh coal around the globe. By 1913 output peaked at around 57 million tons and the industry employed more than 230,000 men. Towns such as Merthyr Tydfil and the Rhondda valleys grew from small villages into dense industrial communities.

Decline

The industry contracted steadily through the 20th century as oil replaced coal and seams became harder to work. The miners' strike of 1984–85 and subsequent pit closures under the National Coal Board ended deep mining at most collieries; Tower Colliery, the last deep mine, closed in 2008. The legacy of the coalfield remains visible in its terraced housing, chapels, institutes and the landscape of the South Wales Valleys.
