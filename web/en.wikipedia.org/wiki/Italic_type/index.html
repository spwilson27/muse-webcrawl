Italic type

In typography, italic type is a cursive font based on a stylised form of calligraphic handwriting. Because of the influence of calligraphy, italics normally slope slightly to the right. They are used to emphasise text, mark titles of works, denote foreign words, and set off quotations or names of ships.

History

Italic type was first used by the Venetian printer Aldus Manutius and his punchcutter Francesco Griffo in 1500, modelled on the humanist cursive hand. Originally entire books were printed in italic to save space; by the 17th century it had settled into its modern role as a companion to roman type.

True italics and obliques

A true italic is a separately designed style with letterforms that differ from the roman (for example, a single-storey a). An oblique is simply the roman design slanted mechanically, a substitute used in some sans-serif families. On computers, "italic" may refer to either, or to a synthetic slant applied by software.
