Henry Flitcroft (30 August 1697 – 25 February 1769) was an English architect of the Palladian school. A protégé of Richard Boyle, 3rd Earl of Burlington — the leading patron of English Palladianism — he was nicknamed "Burlington Harry" and became one of the most successful country-house architects of the mid-18th century.

Career

Flitcroft was apprenticed as a carpenter and joiner and worked at Burlington House, where, according to tradition, he fell from a scaffold and broke his leg; Lord Burlington noticed his talent while he convalesced, took him on to make architectural drawings, and sponsored his career. He assisted in preparing designs for publication and engraved plates for The Designs of Inigo Jones (1727). As a reward he received official posts, eventually becoming a leading figure in the Office of Works and Surveyor of Westminster Abbey.

Works

Flitcroft designed the palatial new Woburn Abbey for the 4th Duke of Bedford, and worked extensively at Wentworth Woodhouse and Wimpole Hall. At Stourhead in Wiltshire he designed the interiors of the house and many famous garden buildings, including the Temple of Ceres, the Temple of Apollo, and the Pantheon. His London church of St Giles-in-the-Fields (1731–34) is a notable Palladian church design, and he designed St Olave's, Southwark. He also provided interiors at York House and worked on numerous other country seats in a restrained, correct Palladian idiom.
