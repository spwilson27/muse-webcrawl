Old fashioned (cocktail)

The old fashioned is a cocktail made by muddling sugar with bitters and water, adding whiskey (typically rye or bourbon), and garnishing with an orange slice or zest and a cocktail cherry. It is traditionally served in an old fashioned glass, a short tumbler also known as a rocks glass. With its conception rooted in an earlier era, the cocktail is one of the International Bartenders Association's official cocktails.

History

The earliest known printed definition of a "cock-tail" appeared in 1806, describing a potent concoction of spirits, bitters, water, and sugar — a mixture that, by the 1860s, had come to be complicated by the addition of curaçao and other liqueurs. Purists who resisted the newer elaborations began asking barkeepers for a drink made "the old-fashioned way," and the name stuck. The Pendennis Club, a gentlemen's club founded in 1881 in Louisville, Kentucky, is often credited with popularizing the bourbon old fashioned.

Preparation

IngredientTypical amount
Bourbon or rye whiskey45–60 ml (1.5–2 oz)
Angostura bitters2–3 dashes
Sugar cube or simple syrup1 cube / 1 tsp
Watera few dashes
Garnishorange peel or slice, cocktail cherry

The sugar is muddled with the bitters and a little water until dissolved, the whiskey is added together with ice, and the drink is stirred and garnished. A Wisconsin variant, the brandy old fashioned, substitutes brandy for whiskey and is often served "sweet" or "sour" with soda. The cocktail enjoyed a resurgence in the early 21st century, helped by popular culture depictions of mid-century drinking.
