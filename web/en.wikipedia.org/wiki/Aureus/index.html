Aureus

Aureus
Country: Roman Republic / Roman Empire
Value: 25 denarii
Composition: Gold
Years of minting: 1st century BC – 4th century AD

The aureus (plural aurei) was a gold coin of ancient Rome valued at 25 silver denarii. It was issued from the 1st century BC to the beginning of the 4th century AD, when it was replaced by the solidus.

History

The aureus was first struck in quantity under Julius Caesar at a standard of 40 to the Roman pound (about 8 grams). Augustus set it at 45 to the pound. Over time the weight and purity were reduced: Nero lowered the weight to 45-to-the-pound standard of about 7.3 g, and Caracalla reduced it further. Diocletian issued aurei at 60 to the pound, but Constantine's solidus, struck at 72 to the pound, displaced the aureus as the standard gold coin. Aurei bear portraits of emperors and were used for large payments, taxes and military donatives.
