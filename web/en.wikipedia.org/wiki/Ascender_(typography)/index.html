Ascender (typography)

Ascender
DefinitionThe portion of a lowercase letter that rises above the mean line (x-height)
Examples (Latin)b, d, f, h, k, l, and sometimes t
Height referenceAscender line, above the cap line in many typefaces
CounterpartDescender

In typography and handwriting, an ascender is the part of a lowercase letter that extends above the mean line of a font—that is, above the x-height. The ascender is thus the upward stem or stroke seen in letters such as b, d, f, h, k, and l in the Latin alphabet; the letter t has a shortened ascender in most typefaces. The corresponding part extending below the baseline is the descender.

Terminology and metrics

The topmost reach of ascenders defines the font's ascender line or ascender height. In many typefaces the ascender height slightly exceeds the cap height (the height of capital letters), though in others the two coincide. Together, ascender height and descender depth determine the minimum line spacing (leading) needed to keep successive lines of text from colliding. In digital font formats such as OpenType, ascender and descender values are stored as font-wide metrics (for example, the hhea and OS/2 tables), and inconsistencies between these values are a common cause of clipped text or uneven line spacing across operating systems.

Design considerations

Long ascenders and a large x-height relative to them can make text faces look sturdy and open, while short ascenders give a compact, even texture suited to small sizes and tight leading. In scripts beyond Latin, analogous features occur: many letters in Arabic-based scripts have tall vertical strokes, and in Devanagari the headline (shirorekha) organises letter heights differently. Ascenders also matter in handwriting instruction, where children learn letter proportions in terms of sky, grass, and ground zones corresponding to ascender space, the x-height band, and descender space.

Ascenders in other letterforms

Some characters feature ascenders that rise above even the usual ascender line in certain fonts; historically, long s (ſ) and various ligatures occupied the ascender space. Certain fonts also give the letter f a full ascender, while others design it as an x-height letter with an overhanging terminal.
