Uludağ

Uludağ (Turkish pronunciation: [uˈɫudaː]), the ancient Mysian Olympus, is a mountain in Bursa Province, Turkey, with an elevation of 2,543 m (8,343 ft), making it the highest mountain of the Marmara region. Its name in Turkish means "Great Mountain" (ulu = great/grand, dağ = mountain). In classical antiquity it was one of several mountains called Olympus and was associated in myth with the council of the gods, notably in accounts of the Trojan War era.

Elevation: 2,543 m (8,343 ft)
Location: Bursa Province, Turkey
Ancient name: Mysian Olympus
Protected: Uludağ National Park (est. 1961)

Geography and nature

The mountain rises steeply south of the city of Bursa. Its slopes host rich flora and fauna, from maquis and beech and fir forest to alpine meadows; Uludağ National Park, declared in 1961, protects much of the massif, which is home to beech martens, wolves, jackals and rare butterflies, and is a noted site for endemic plants. In the Middle Ages the mountain was a centre of Orthodox monasticism, with many monasteries and hermitages, and was known as the "Mountain of Monks" (Keşiş Dağı) in the Ottoman period; the name Uludağ was officially adopted in 1925.

Tourism

Uludağ is Turkey's oldest and most famous winter-sports resort, with ski pistes, hotels and lifts developed from the 1930s and 1940s onward, reachable from Bursa by road and by one of the world's long-running gondola/cable-car lines (renewed in 2013–14). In summer it attracts hikers and campers. The city below is also famous for İskender kebab, chestnut candy and Turkish baths fed by local thermal springs.
