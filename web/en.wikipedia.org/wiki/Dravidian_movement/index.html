Dravidian movement

The Dravidian movement refers to a series of social and political movements in South India, particularly in Tamil Nadu, that sought to promote the interests, language and identity of Dravidian peoples, challenge Brahmin dominance and caste hierarchy, and advance social justice, rationalism and Tamil pride.

Origins

Its roots lie in the Justice Party, founded in 1916 as the South Indian Liberal Federation, and in E. V. Ramasamy (Periyar), whose Self-Respect Movement, launched in 1925, advocated atheism, women's rights, eradication of caste and opposition to Hindi imposition.

Political development

In 1944 Periyar transformed the Justice Party into the Dravidar Kazhagam. In 1949 C. N. Annadurai broke away to form the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK), which came to power in Tamil Nadu in 1967. The movement profoundly reshaped Tamil politics, language policy, reservation in education and employment, and state welfare schemes, and gave rise to both the DMK and its offshoot the AIADMK, which have alternated in power in the state since 1967.
