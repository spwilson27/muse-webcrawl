Ecological economics

Ecological economics, sometimes called eco-economics, is an interdisciplinary field concerned with the interdependence of human economies and natural ecosystems, emphasizing that the economy is a subsystem of Earth's larger ecosystem.

Ecological economics
Founders: Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen, Herman Daly, Robert Costanza
Journal: Ecological Economics (1989–)
Key concept: Steady-state, natural capital

History

Precursors include 19th-century concerns about limits, but the field coalesced in the 1980s, with the International Society for Ecological Economics founded in 1988. Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen's work on entropy and Herman Daly's steady-state economics were foundational.

Themes

Unlike environmental economics, which applies mainstream tools to environmental problems, ecological economics questions unlimited growth on a finite planet, stresses energy and material throughput, scale, distribution, and allocation, and values ecosystem services and natural capital. Robert Costanza's 1997 valuation of global ecosystem services drew wide attention.

Methods

Methods include energy analysis, material flow accounting, multi-criteria evaluation, and genuine progress indicators as alternatives to GDP.
