Stiletto

A stiletto is a knife or dagger with a long, slender blade and a needle-like point, primarily intended as a thrusting and stabbing weapon. The word derives from the Latin stilus, a slender pointed writing instrument.

The stiletto began to gain prominence in late medieval Italy, where it was favoured both as a weapon against heavily armoured opponents — its slim blade could pass through the gaps in armour or through mail — and, notoriously, as the weapon of assassins. Its effectiveness against armour led some jurisdictions to treat it as a particularly dangerous weapon, and it was sometimes banned.

In modern usage, "stiletto" also commonly refers to a type of automatic knife (switchblade) with a slender blade, particularly the Italian-style folding knives popularised in the 1950s, and to the stiletto heel, a long thin high heel on women's shoes named after the dagger.
