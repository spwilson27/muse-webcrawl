Grumman AA-5

AA-5 Traveler / Tiger / Cheetah
Role: Four-seat light aircraft
Manufacturer: Grumman American
First flight: 1970

The Grumman American AA-5 series is a family of four-seat, single-engine light aircraft produced in the 1970s and again in the 2000s. The line comprises the AA-5 Traveler, the AA-5A Cheetah, and the higher-performance AA-5B Tiger, the most popular variant.

Design

The AA-5 features a sliding canopy, bonded aluminium honeycomb construction, and a free-castering nosewheel. The Tiger, powered by a 180 hp Lycoming O-360 engine, cruises faster than many comparable four-seaters such as the Cessna 172, owing in part to its clean airframe and laminar-flow wing inherited from the BD-1 design by Jim Bede.

History

Production ran from 1971 to 1979 under American Aviation and then Grumman American; Gulfstream American built the last aircraft before the line was sold. In 1990 Fletchair acquired the design, and Tiger Aircraft produced the AG-5B Tiger from 2001 to 2006. The type remains popular with private owners and has an active owners' organization, the American Yankee Association.
