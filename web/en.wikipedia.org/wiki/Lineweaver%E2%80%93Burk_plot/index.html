Lineweaver–Burk plot

In biochemistry, the Lineweaver–Burk plot (or double reciprocal plot) is a graphical representation of the Michaelis–Menten equation of enzyme kinetics, published by Hans Lineweaver and Dean Burk in 1934. It plots the reciprocal of reaction velocity (1/v) against the reciprocal of substrate concentration (1/[S]).

Interpretation

The linearised equation gives a straight line whose y-intercept is 1/Vmax, whose x-intercept is −1/Km, and whose slope is Km/Vmax. Because the lines for inhibited and uninhibited reactions intersect in characteristic places, the plot was historically convenient for distinguishing competitive, non-competitive, and uncompetitive inhibition.

Criticism

Taking reciprocals grossly distorts the error structure of the data, giving undue weight to the least precise measurements at low substrate concentrations. For this reason, nonlinear regression on the untransformed Michaelis–Menten equation is now preferred for estimating parameters, although the plot remains common in teaching.
