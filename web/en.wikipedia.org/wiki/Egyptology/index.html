Egyptology is the scientific study of ancient Egypt — its language, history, religion, literature, art and material remains — from prehistory through the pharaonic period to the Roman era. It emerged as a discipline in the 19th century after Napoleon's Egyptian expedition publicised the country's antiquities in Europe.

Decipherment and discoveries

The Rosetta Stone, found in 1799, allowed Jean-François Champollion to decipher Egyptian hieroglyphs in the 1820s, unlocking primary texts. Landmark discoveries followed, including the tomb of Tutankhamun in 1922. Egyptologists today combine excavation, philology, conservation and scientific analysis, and fieldwork is centred on sites from Giza and Saqqara to Luxor and Aswan.
