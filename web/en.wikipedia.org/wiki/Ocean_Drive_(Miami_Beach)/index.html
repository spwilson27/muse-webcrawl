Ocean Drive (Miami Beach)

Ocean Drive is a major thoroughfare in the South Beach neighbourhood of Miami Beach, Florida, running along the Atlantic Ocean and famous for its concentration of Art Deco hotels and apartment buildings, its neon lighting, and its role as one of America's best-known nightlife and café streets.

Art Deco district

The street forms the eastern edge of the Miami Beach Architectural District (the Art Deco Historic District), listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1979. Facing the beach across Lummus Park, the pastel-coloured hotels of the 1930s — many designed by architects such as Henry Hohauser and L. Murray Dixon — display classic Streamline Moderne features: rounded corners, neon signage, eyebrow shades, and nautical motifs. The preservation movement led by Barbara Baer Capitman and the Miami Design Preservation League rescued the district from demolition in the 1970s and 1980s and revived South Beach.

Notable sites

Among the best-known buildings is the Casa Casuarina, built in 1930 and later the home of fashion designer Gianni Versace, who was murdered on its front steps in 1997. Other landmarks include the Colony, Clevelander, and Beacon hotels. Ocean Drive has served as a backdrop for films and television, including Scarface, The Birdcage, and Miami Vice. Since 2020 the city has experimented with closing parts of the street to traffic, creating a pedestrian promenade between the cafés and Lummus Park, and has debated measures to calm its raucous nightlife.
