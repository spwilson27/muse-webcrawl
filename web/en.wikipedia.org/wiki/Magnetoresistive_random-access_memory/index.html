Magnetoresistive random-access memory

Magnetoresistive random-access memory (MRAM) is a non-volatile computer memory technology that has been in development since the 1990s. Unlike conventional RAM, which stores data as electric charge, MRAM stores information in the magnetic orientation of elements on the chip, so data persists when power is removed.

Technical description

The basic MRAM cell is a magnetic tunnel junction (MTJ): two ferromagnetic layers separated by an ultrathin insulating layer, typically magnesium oxide. Electrons tunnel through the barrier at a rate that depends on whether the magnetizations of the two layers are parallel or antiparallel — the tunnel magnetoresistance (TMR) effect — giving two distinguishable resistance states for 0 and 1.

Writing methods

Field-switched (toggle) MRAM, the first generation to market, uses magnetic fields generated by currents in wires adjacent to the cell to flip the free layer. Spin-transfer torque (STT) writing instead drives a spin-polarized current directly through the junction; the angular momentum of the electrons torques the free layer into alignment. STT-MRAM scales to smaller cells and lower write energies, and is the basis of most current products. Research continues on spin–orbit torque and voltage-controlled switching for further gains.

Comparison with other memories

MRAM offers read/write speeds approaching SRAM and DRAM, far greater endurance than flash memory, and data retention measured in decades. Its historical disadvantages have been cell size and cost relative to DRAM and flash, confining it first to niche roles — battery-backed buffers, aerospace and automotive systems, and industrial controllers — before its adoption as embedded non-volatile memory in logic processes.

Commercial history

Honeywell pioneered radiation-hardened MRAM for military and space use. Freescale Semiconductor shipped the first commercial MRAM product, the 4 Mbit MR2A16A, in 2006, and its memory division became Everspin Technologies, the leading merchant supplier. Since the late 2010s, Samsung, TSMC, GlobalFoundries, and Intel have announced embedded STT-MRAM offerings.
