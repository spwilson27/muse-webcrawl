Chinese Lunar Exploration Program

The Chinese Lunar Exploration Program (CLEP), also known as the Chang'e Project after the Chinese Moon goddess Chang'e, is an ongoing series of robotic Moon missions by the China National Space Administration (CNSA). The program proceeds in phases: orbiting, landing and roving, sample return, and eventually robotic research stations and crewed landings.

Missions

MissionLaunchResult
Chang'e 12007Lunar orbiter; produced a full Moon map
Chang'e 22010Orbiter; later flew to asteroid 4179 Toutatis
Chang'e 32013First Chinese soft landing; Yutu rover
Chang'e 42018First soft landing on the far side of the Moon; Yutu-2 rover
Chang'e 52020Returned 1,731 g of lunar samples
Chang'e 62024First sample return from the far side (South Pole–Aitken basin)

Future plans

Subsequent missions — Chang'e 7 and Chang'e 8 — are planned to explore the lunar south polar region and test in-situ resource utilisation technologies, laying groundwork for the International Lunar Research Station planned with partner nations in the 2030s. China has also announced its intention to land taikonauts on the Moon before 2030.
