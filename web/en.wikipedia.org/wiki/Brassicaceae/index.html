Brassicaceae, also called Cruciferae, is a medium-sized and economically important family of flowering plants commonly known as the mustards, the crucifers, or the cabbage family. Most are herbaceous plants with characteristically four-petalled, cross-shaped (cruciform) flowers.

Characteristics

Members typically have alternate leaves, flowers with four sepals, four petals, and six stamens (four long and two short — tetradynamous), and fruits called siliques or silicles. Many produce glucosinolates, sulphur compounds responsible for the pungent flavours of mustard, horseradish and wasabi.

Important genera and uses

The genus Brassica gives many vegetables from a single species, Brassica oleracea — cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, kale and Brussels sprouts — as well as oilseed rape (canola) from B. napus. Other crops include radish, turnip, mustard seed and rocket. Arabidopsis thaliana, a small weed of the family, is the principal model organism of plant genetics, the first plant genome sequenced (2000).

Distribution

The family is distributed worldwide, with greatest diversity around the Mediterranean, Central Asia and western North America.
