CATOBAR

CATOBAR (Catapult Assisted Take-Off But Arrested Recovery, also written Catapult Assisted Take-Off Barrier Arrested Recovery) is a system used for the launch and recovery of aircraft from the deck of an aircraft carrier. Under this technique, aircraft launch using a catapult-assisted take-off and land using arrestor wires.

Description

Although this system is more costly than alternative methods, it provides greater flexibility in carrier operations, since it imposes less onerous design elements on carrier fixed-wing aircraft than alternative methods of launch and recovery such as STOVL or STOBAR, allowing for a greater payload for more ordnance and/or fuel. CATOBAR can launch aircraft that cannot attain a high thrust-to-weight ratio, and heavier aircraft can be launched in greater safety.

Users

The United States Navy operates the largest CATOBAR fleet, with the Nimitz-class and Gerald R. Ford-class carriers; the Ford class uses the Electromagnetic Aircraft Launch System (EMALS) in place of steam catapults. The French Navy's Charles de Gaulle is the only non-American CATOBAR carrier in service. China's Fujian was launched with electromagnetic catapults and arrestor gear, making it a CATOBAR carrier as well.

Types

The catapult system in use on most CATOBAR carriers historically was the steam catapult. The newer Electromagnetic Aircraft Launch System provides smoother acceleration, reduced maintenance and greater control. Recovery uses a tailhook engaging one of several arrestor wires stretched across the angled landing deck.
