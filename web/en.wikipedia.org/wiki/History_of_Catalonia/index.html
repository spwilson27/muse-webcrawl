History of Catalonia

The history of Catalonia traces the development of the territory in northeastern Iberia from prehistory to the modern autonomous community of Spain. Catalonia emerged as a distinct political entity in the Middle Ages through the union of the County of Barcelona with the Kingdom of Aragon, and developed its own institutions, law and language.

Antiquity and early Middle Ages

The region was inhabited by Iberian tribes, colonised by Greeks (Emporion) and Carthaginians, and thoroughly Romanised as part of Hispania Tarraconensis. After Visigothic rule and a brief Muslim period, Charlemagne's Franks created the Spanish March, a buffer zone of counties south of the Pyrenees. By the late 10th century the counts of Barcelona had become effectively independent of Frankish rule; tradition dates Catalan independence to Wilfred the Hairy (d. 897).

The Crown of Aragon

In 1137 the marriage of Ramon Berenguer IV of Barcelona to Petronilla of Aragon united Catalonia with the Kingdom of Aragon in the Crown of Aragon, while Catalan counties retained their own laws and institutions, including the Corts and, from 1359, the Generalitat. Catalan merchants and the Consulate of the Sea made medieval Catalonia a major Mediterranean power, extending influence to Sicily, Sardinia, Naples and Athens.

Early modern period

The dynastic union of Ferdinand II of Aragon with Isabella of Castile (1469) joined the Crown of Aragon to Castile but preserved Catalan institutions. Tensions with the Spanish monarchy erupted in the Reapers' War (1640–1652). During the War of the Spanish Succession Catalonia backed the Habsburg claimant; Barcelona fell on 11 September 1714, and Philip V's Nueva Planta decrees (1716) abolished Catalan institutions and imposed Castilian administration.

Industrialisation and Renaixença

Catalonia industrialised early in the 19th century, especially in textiles, and the Renaixença revived Catalan language and culture. Catalan nationalism took political form around 1900; the Mancomunitat (1914) restored a measure of self-government, and the Second Spanish Republic granted a Statute of Autonomy in 1932.

Civil War, Francoism and democracy

Catalonia was a Republican stronghold in the Spanish Civil War; after Franco's victory in 1939 autonomy was abolished and the Catalan language repressed. Following Spain's transition to democracy, the 1979 Statute restored the Generalitat, and the 2006 Statute expanded self-government. A disputed independence referendum on 1 October 2017 and a unilateral declaration of independence led Madrid to impose direct rule, and the status of Catalonia within Spain remains a central issue in Spanish politics.
