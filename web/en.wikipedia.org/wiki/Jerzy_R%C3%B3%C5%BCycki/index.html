Jerzy Witold Różycki (July 24, 1909 – January 9, 1942) was a Polish mathematician and cryptologist who, together with Marian Rejewski and Henryk Zygalski, worked in the Polish Cipher Bureau on breaking the German Enigma cipher machine in the years before and during World War II.

Work on Enigma

Różycki studied mathematics at the University of Poznań and was one of the students recruited in 1929 for a cryptology course organized by the Polish General Staff's Cipher Bureau. In 1932, the three mathematicians achieved the first breaks into the Enigma machine's wiring and daily keys, reconstructing the cipher mathematically and developing mechanical and paper methods—including the cyclometer and Różycki's "clock" method, a technique for exploiting the differing turnover positions of Enigma rotors to identify which rotor was in use.

In July 1939, shortly before the outbreak of war, the Poles shared their Enigma-breaking techniques and equipment with French and British intelligence at a meeting in the Kabaty Woods near Warsaw, giving the Allies the foundation on which the later work at Bletchley Park was built.

War years and death

After the German invasion of Poland, Różycki evacuated with other Cipher Bureau staff through Romania to France, where the team continued work at the PC Bruno station outside Paris and, after the fall of France, at Cadix near Uzès in the unoccupied zone. On January 9, 1942, Różycki died when the passenger ship Lamoricière, on which he was returning from a posting in Algiers, sank in the Mediterranean near the Balearic Islands.
