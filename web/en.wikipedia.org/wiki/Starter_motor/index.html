Starter motor

A starter (also self starter, cranking motor, or starter motor) is a device used to rotate (crank) an internal-combustion engine so as to initiate the engine's operation under its own power. Starters can be electric, pneumatic, or hydraulic. The starter can also be another internal-combustion engine in the case, for instance, of very large engines, or diesel engines in agricultural or excavation applications.

Electric starter

The electric starter motor is the most common type used on gasoline engines and many diesel engines. It is a high-torque electric motor, usually powered by the vehicle's battery, which engages a pinion gear with the engine's flywheel ring gear through a solenoid-operated mechanism. When the ignition key is turned, current flows to the solenoid, which pushes the pinion into mesh and closes contacts feeding the motor; once the engine starts, the pinion retracts.

History

Before electric starters, engines were cranked by hand, by spring, or by other laborious and sometimes dangerous methods. Charles F. Kettering developed the electric self-starter, first fitted to the 1912 Cadillac, which greatly improved the convenience and safety of automobiles and is often credited with removing the need for the hand crank.

Other types

Pneumatic starters use compressed air and are common on large diesel engines and gas turbines. Hydraulic starters are used in some marine and industrial applications. Inertia starters store energy in a flywheel spun up by hand or electric motor.
