The Quraysh (Arabic: قريش) were a grouping of Arab clans that historically inhabited and controlled the city of Mecca and its sanctuary, the Kaaba, in the Hejaz region of western Arabia. The Islamic prophet Muhammad was born into the Banu Hashim clan of the tribe. The Quraysh initially opposed Muhammad's preaching, but after years of conflict Mecca came under Muslim rule in 630 CE; thereafter the tribe supplied many of early Islam's leaders, and all of the first four ("Rightly Guided") caliphs, as well as the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphal dynasties, were of Qurayshi descent.

Origins and settlement in Mecca

According to traditional Arab genealogy, the tribe descends from Fihr ibn Malik, nicknamed Quraysh. In the 5th century CE, Qusayy ibn Kilab, an ancestor of Muhammad, is said to have gathered the scattered clans, taken control of Mecca, and organised the pilgrimage services—including providing water (siqaya) and food (rifada) for pilgrims—functions that brought the Quraysh prestige and income. Their control of the sanctuary and the associated trade fairs made Mecca a commercial hub linking Yemen with Syria.

Clans

Principal Qurayshi clans included the Banu Hashim (Muhammad's clan), Banu Umayya, Banu Makhzum, Banu Abd Shams, Banu Nawfal, Banu Zuhra and Banu Taym (the clan of Abu Bakr). Rivalry among these clans shaped Meccan politics in the 6th and 7th centuries.

Conflict with Muhammad and aftermath

After Muhammad began preaching monotheism around 610 CE, the Qurayshi leadership, fearing for the pilgrimage economy and ancestral religion, persecuted his followers and imposed a boycott on the Banu Hashim. The emigration (Hijra) to Medina in 622 was followed by battles at Badr (624), Uhud (625) and the Trench (627). In 630 Muhammad entered Mecca largely peacefully and granted a general amnesty. A Qurayshi chapter (surah) of the Quran, "Quraysh", takes its name from the tribe.

Legacy

A widely cited hadith holds that leadership of the Muslim community belongs to the Quraysh, a principle invoked in debates over the caliphate for centuries. Modern descendants and namesakes of the tribe remain prominent in the Hejaz and Jordan, where the Hashemite royal family traces its descent to Banu Hashim.
