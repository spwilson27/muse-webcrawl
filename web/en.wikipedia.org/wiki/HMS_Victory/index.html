HMS Victory

HMS Victory is a 104-gun first-rate ship of the line of the Royal Navy, ordered in 1758, laid down in 1759 and launched in 1765. She is best known for her role as Lord Nelson's flagship at the Battle of Trafalgar on 21 October 1805. She also served as Keppel's flagship at Ushant, Howe's at Cape Spartel and Jervis's at Cape St Vincent. Preserved in dry dock at Portsmouth since 1922, she is the world's oldest naval vessel still in commission, and is the flagship of the First Sea Lord.

Construction

Victory was designed by Sir Thomas Slade and built at Chatham Dockyard; some 6,000 trees — mostly oak — went into her hull. Her launch was delayed by the end of the Seven Years' War, and she spent 13 years in ordinary (reserve) before being commissioned in 1778 as the American War of Independence widened. She carried a crew of about 850 and was armed with 104 muzzle-loading guns across three decks, from 32-pounders on the lower deck to carronades on the quarterdeck.

Service

Victory fought at the First Battle of Ushant (1778), the relief of Gibraltar, and Cape St Vincent (1797). Rebuilt in 1800–1803, she became Nelson's flagship in the Mediterranean in May 1803. At Trafalgar in 1805 she led the weather column into the Franco-Spanish line, raking the French flagship Bucentaure; Nelson was mortally wounded on her quarterdeck by a shot from the French ship Redoutable. Victory lost 57 killed and 102 wounded in the battle. She later served in the Baltic under Saumarez before being retired to harbour duties at Portsmouth in 1812.

Preservation

By the early 20th century Victory was deteriorating in Portsmouth harbour. A public campaign led by the Society for Nautical Research saved her; she was placed in No. 2 Dry Dock — the world's oldest dry dock still in use — in 1922 and meticulously restored to her Trafalgar configuration. She remains a commissioned warship and museum, receiving hundreds of thousands of visitors annually, and is undergoing a long-term conservation programme to support her hull and renew her rigging.
