Aqua vitae

Aqua vitae (Latin for "water of life") is an archaic term for a concentrated aqueous solution of ethanol, i.e., distilled spirits. The term was used in medieval and early modern alchemy and medicine for the product of distilling wine or other fermented liquids.

Meaning: "Water of life" (Latin)
Refers to: Distilled spirits / ethanol solution
Related terms: Akvavit, uisce beatha (whisky), eau-de-vie

History

Distillation of alcohol spread through medieval Europe via alchemists and physicians, who prized aqua vitae as a medicine and preservative. The term survives in several spirit names: Scandinavian akvavit, the Gaelic uisce beatha (from which "whisky" derives, literally translating aqua vitae), and French eau-de-vie.

Alchemical significance

Alchemists regarded aqua vitae as a near-miraculous substance, associating it with the elixir of life. Repeated distillation was thought to purify the spirit; the related term aqua ardens ("burning water") described flammable distillate.
