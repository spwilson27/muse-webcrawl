Occupy movement

[Image: protesters' encampment with tents and hand-painted signs in a city financial district, 2011]

Occupy was a protest movement against social and economic inequality and the power of finance, which began with Occupy Wall Street in New York's Zuccotti Park on 17 September 2011 and spread to hundreds of cities worldwide. Inspired by the Arab Spring and Spain's indignados, and called into being by an Adbusters poster, it adopted encampments, leaderless general assemblies and the slogan 'We are the 99%' — framing politics as the 99% versus the richest 1%.

Ideas and legacy

Emerging from the aftermath of the Great Recession — bailouts for banks, foreclosures and stagnant wages for households — the movement issued no unified demands, instead modelling direct democracy and shifting public language: 'the 1%' entered everyday political vocabulary, and inequality rose up media and academic agendas, a context for the later success of Piketty's Capital. Most camps were cleared by authorities within months (Zuccotti in November 2011). Offshoots included Occupy Sandy relief (2012), Rolling Jubilee debt abolition, and lasting activist networks, while critics noted the absence of a policy programme or durable organisation.
