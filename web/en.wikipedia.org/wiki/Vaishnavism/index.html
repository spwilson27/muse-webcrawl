Vaishnavism

Vaishnavism is one of the major traditions within Hinduism, characterized by devotion (bhakti) to Vishnu as the supreme deity, along with his avatars, principally Rama and Krishna. Vaishnavas regard Vishnu as the preserver and protector of the universe within the Trimurti.

Vaishnavism
Supreme deity: Vishnu
Key texts: Vedas, Bhagavad Gita, Bhagavata Purana
Traditions: Sri, Gaudiya, Vallabha, Swaminarayan, ISKCON

Beliefs and practice

Central practices include chanting Vishnu's names, temple worship (puja), wearing the U-shaped tilaka mark, and observing festivals such as Janmashtami (Krishna's birth) and Rama Navami. Philosophies range from Ramanuja's Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism) to Madhva's Dvaita (dualism) and Chaitanya's Gaudiya tradition, spread worldwide by ISKCON (the Hare Krishna movement) from 1966.

History

Roots lie in Vedic worship of Vishnu and the hero-deities Vasudeva-Krishna and Rama, synthesized in the epics Mahabharata and Ramayana and the Puranas. The Tamil Alvars (6th–9th centuries) infused it with emotional bhakti.
