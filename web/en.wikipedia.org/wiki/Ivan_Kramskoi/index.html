Ivan Kramskoi

Ivan Nikolayevich Kramskoi (8 June 1837 – 6 April 1887) was a Russian painter and art critic, and the intellectual leader of the Russian democratic art movement of the 1860s–1880s. He was a founder and organiser of the Peredvizhniki (the Wanderers), the society of travelling art exhibitions that broke the monopoly of the Imperial Academy of Arts.

Career

Born in Ostrogozhsk to a poor family, Kramskoi entered the Imperial Academy in St Petersburg in 1857. In 1863 he led the "Revolt of the Fourteen", in which students refused the Academy's set competition subject and left to form an independent artists' artel. In 1870 he co-founded the Society for Travelling Art Exhibitions, whose shows took realistic art to the provinces.

Works

Kramskoi is renowned for expressive portraits of Russian cultural figures — Leo Tolstoy, Ivan Shishkin, Pavel Tretyakov and Fyodor Dostoevsky (the only portrait of the writer made from life) — and for paintings such as Christ in the Desert (1872) and Portrait of an Unknown Woman (1883), often linked with Anna Karenina. He died in St Petersburg while working at his easel.
