Frankie Warren Knuckles Jr. (January 18, 1955 – March 31, 2014), often credited simply as Frankie Knuckles, was an American DJ, record producer, and remixer. He was popularly known as the "Godfather of House Music" for his central role in developing and popularising house music in Chicago during the 1980s. In 1997 he won the Grammy Award for Remixer of the Year, the first year the award was given.

Early career

Born in the Bronx, New York, Knuckles began DJing in the early 1970s alongside Larry Levan, playing at the Continental Baths and the Gallery in New York. In 1977 he moved to Chicago to become the resident DJ at the Warehouse, a members-only club at 206 South Jefferson Street. His style — blending disco, European electronic music, R&B and his own reel-to-reel edits, played over a powerful sound system — defined a new sound that club-goers called "house" music, a term generally derived from the club's name.

Productions and influence

After leaving the Warehouse in 1982, Knuckles opened his own club, the Power Plant. He began producing records, releasing classic tracks such as "Your Love" (1987, with Jamie Principle), "Baby Wants to Ride," and "Tears" (1989, with Robert Owens and Satoshi Tomiie). As a remixer he reworked songs for artists including Michael Jackson, Whitney Houston, Diana Ross, Luther Vandross, and Pet Shop Boys. His 1991 album Beyond the Mix included the hit "The Whistle Song."

Legacy

Knuckles was inducted into the Dance Music Hall of Fame in 2005. The city of Chicago honoured him with a street, Frankie Knuckles Way, in 2004, and 25 August was declared Frankie Knuckles Day in Illinois. He died in 2014 of complications related to Type II diabetes. His recordings and DJ philosophy — treating the club as a spiritual, communal space — remain foundational to dance music culture worldwide.
