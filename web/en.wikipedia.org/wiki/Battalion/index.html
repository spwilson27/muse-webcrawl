Battalion

A battalion is a military unit, typically consisting of several hundred soldiers — commonly between 300 and 1,000 — commanded by a lieutenant colonel and subdivided into a number of companies. Battalions are usually part of a regiment, brigade or group, and two or more battalions form a regiment or brigade-level grouping in many armies.

An infantry battalion on parade.

Organisation

A typical battalion comprises a headquarters and headquarters company and three to five manoeuvre companies, together with combat support and service support elements. In the British Army and Commonwealth forces, an infantry battalion has traditionally had four rifle companies and support and headquarters companies; in the United States Army, a battalion is usually made up of three to five companies and is the smallest unit normally capable of independent operations of limited duration.

History

The term derives from the Italian battaglione, an augmentation of battaglia (battle). Battalions emerged as a tactical unit in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries as armies moved away from massive squares of pikemen toward more flexible formations. During the First and Second World Wars the battalion was the principal infantry fighting unit, and it remains so in most armies today.

Specialised battalions

Besides infantry, battalions exist in armour, artillery, engineers, signals, logistics and special forces: a tank battalion, an artillery battalion (called a regiment in some Commonwealth forces), and similar units use the battalion echelon for their primary fighting sub-units.
