Uber Eats is an online food ordering and delivery platform launched by Uber in 2014. Users can read menus, review and order food and convenience items from participating restaurants and stores, and pay using the app or website. Orders are delivered by couriers using cars, bikes, or on foot. Uber Eats operates in thousands of cities across dozens of countries and is one of the largest food delivery platforms in the world.

An Uber Eats courier making a delivery.

History

Uber launched its food delivery service in August 2014 as UberFRESH in Santa Monica, California, offering a limited lunch menu. In 2015 the service was renamed Uber Eats and a dedicated app was released, allowing users to order from full restaurant menus. The platform expanded rapidly across North America, Europe, Asia, and Latin America.

In 2020, Uber acquired rival delivery service Postmates for approximately $2.65 billion. During the COVID-19 pandemic, demand for food delivery surged, and Uber Eats expanded into grocery, alcohol, and convenience-store delivery. It has also experimented with autonomous delivery vehicles and sidewalk robots in several cities.

Business model

Uber Eats charges restaurants a commission on each order (commonly in the range of 15–30 per cent) and charges customers delivery and service fees. Couriers are generally engaged as independent contractors, a model that has been the subject of legal and political debate in several jurisdictions, including employment-status rulings in the United Kingdom and legislation in California and elsewhere.

Criticism

The platform has been criticised over courier pay and working conditions, commission fees charged to restaurants, and its role in the growth of "dark kitchens" (delivery-only restaurants). Consumer groups have raised concerns about opaque fee structures and tipping practices.
