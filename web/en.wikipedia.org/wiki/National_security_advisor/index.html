National security advisor

A national security advisor (NSA) is a senior official in a government who serves as the chief adviser to the head of government on matters of national security. The role exists in several countries, most prominently in the United States, where the official's formal title is Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs, and in the United Kingdom, India, Pakistan and other states with analogous posts.

United States

The American office was created in 1953 under President Dwight D. Eisenhower as part of the National Security Council (NSC) system established by the National Security Act of 1947. The advisor heads the NSC staff, coordinates foreign and defence policy among departments, and briefs the president daily. The post does not require Senate confirmation, and its influence has varied widely: advisors such as McGeorge Bundy, Henry Kissinger and Zbigniew Brzezinski became central figures in shaping foreign policy, at times rivalling the Secretary of State.

United Kingdom

The British National Security Adviser heads the National Security Secretariat in the Cabinet Office and chairs official-level preparations for the National Security Council created in 2010. The post has been held by senior diplomats and civil servants, including Sir Peter Ricketts, its first holder.

India

India created the post in 1998; the NSA is the chief executive of the National Security Council Secretariat and a principal adviser to the Prime Minister. Brajesh Mishra was the first incumbent, and the office gained cabinet rank in 2019 under Ajit Doval.
