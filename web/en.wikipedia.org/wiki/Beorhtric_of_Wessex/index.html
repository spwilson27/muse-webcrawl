Beorhtric (Old English for "magnificent ruler"; died 802) was King of Wessex from 786 to 802. His reign falls in the period of Mercian supremacy under Offa, whose daughter he married, and it ended with the accession of Ecgberht, the king whose descendants would unite England.

Accession

In 786 King Cynewulf of Wessex was murdered by the ætheling Cyneheard. Beorhtric, whose descent is not clearly recorded, succeeded with Mercian support, while Ecgberht, a rival claimant of the royal line, was driven into exile — first, after a failed return in 789, at the court of Charlemagne in Francia. The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle presents Beorhtric's reign as closely bound to Offa: in 789 he married Offa's daughter Eadburh, and the two kings cooperated, for example in banishing Ecgberht.

Reign

Few events of Beorhtric's reign are recorded. The Chronicle notes that in his days came the first recorded Viking raid on England: around 789–793 three ships of Northmen landed on the Dorset coast near Portland, whose crews killed the king's reeve when he attempted to bring them to the royal estate — an episode often treated as the opening of the Viking Age in England, preceding the famous attack on Lindisfarne in 793. Charters show Beorhtric acting jointly with Offa, and after Offa's death in 796 his influence waned in step with Mercia's.

Death and Eadburh

Beorhtric died in 802. The later chronicler Asser, in his Life of King Alfred, tells the dramatic story that Queen Eadburh accidentally poisoned her husband while intending the draught for a courtier the king favoured; she fled to Charlemagne's court, was placed in a nunnery, misbehaved, and died in poverty at Pavia. Historians treat the tale cautiously, but it explains Asser's claim that the wives of West Saxon kings thereafter were not styled queens. Ecgberht returned from exile in 802 and took the throne.
