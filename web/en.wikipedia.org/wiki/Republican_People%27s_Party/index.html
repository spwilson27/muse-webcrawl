The Republican People's Party (Turkish: Cumhuriyet Halk Partisi, CHP) is a Kemalist and social democratic political party in Turkey. Founded by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk in 1923, it is the oldest political party in the Republic of Turkey and was the country's sole legal party during the single-party era until 1946.

History

The CHP implemented Atatürk's reforms, including the adoption of the Latin alphabet, secular law codes, and women's suffrage. Under İsmet İnönü it led Turkey's transition to multiparty democracy, peacefully handing over power after losing the 1950 election. In the 1970s Bülent Ecevit reoriented the party toward democratic socialism and won pluralities.

Modern era

Closed after the 1980 coup and refounded in 1992, the CHP has been Turkey's main opposition party for most of the period since 2002, opposing the governing AKP. It achieved major municipal victories in 2019 and 2024, winning control of Istanbul, Ankara, and many other cities. Its emblem is six arrows representing the foundational principles of Kemalism.
