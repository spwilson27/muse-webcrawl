Media bias

[Image: A spectrum of newspaper front pages from the same day]

Media bias is the real or perceived slant in news reporting — in story selection, emphasis, framing and sourcing — toward a political position, ideology or interest. It is among the most contested ideas in journalism: audiences across the spectrum report high distrust, while researchers distinguish partisan slant, structural biases (toward conflict, novelty, official sources) and commercial pressures.

Types and evidence

Studies use text analysis and audience experiments to measure slant; findings typically show modest but real ideological lean in many outlets, alongside universal biases toward negativity, elite sources and horse-race election framing. 'Hostile media effect' research shows partisans perceive identical coverage as biased against their side.
