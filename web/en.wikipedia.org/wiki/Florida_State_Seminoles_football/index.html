Florida State Seminoles football

The Florida State Seminoles football team represents Florida State University in the sport of American football. The Seminoles compete in the NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) of the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) and the Atlantic Coast Conference (ACC). The team plays its home games at Doak Campbell Stadium in Tallahassee, Florida.

History

Although Florida State fielded teams in the early 1900s, the modern program dates to 1947, after the university became coeducational. The program rose to national prominence under head coach Bobby Bowden, who led the Seminoles from 1976 to 2009. During the "Dynasty" era from 1987 to 2000, Florida State finished every season ranked in the top four of the AP Poll, a record streak, and won national championships in 1993 and 1999.

Under Jimbo Fisher, the Seminoles won a third national championship in the 2013 season, defeating Auburn in the final BCS National Championship Game, led by Heisman Trophy-winning quarterback Jameis Winston. The school's other Heisman winners are Charlie Ward (1993) and Chris Weinke (2000).

Traditions and rivalries

Before home games, a student portraying the Seminole leader Osceola rides the horse Renegade to midfield and plants a flaming spear — a tradition begun in 1978 with the blessing of the Seminole Tribe of Florida, which endorses the university's use of the Seminoles name. Florida State's principal rivals are the Florida Gators and the Miami Hurricanes.
