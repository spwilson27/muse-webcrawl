Ogilvy is an American advertising, marketing and public relations agency founded in 1948 by David Ogilvy. Based in New York City, it is a subsidiary of the British conglomerate WPP and operates offices around the world, offering brand strategy, advertising, customer engagement, public relations, health marketing, and consulting.

History

David Ogilvy, a Scottish immigrant who had worked at the London agency Mather & Crowther and admired the research methods of George Gallup, founded the firm in Manhattan in 1948 with backing from British partners. Early campaigns that defined its reputation include the Hathaway shirt advertisements featuring "the man in the Hathaway shirt" with an eyepatch, the Schweppes campaign built around Commander Edward Whitehead, and Rolls-Royce's celebrated 1958 headline: "At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock."

In 1964 the firm merged with Mather & Crowther to form Ogilvy & Mather, and in 1966 it became one of the first advertising agencies to go public. It was acquired by WPP in 1989 after a contested takeover. In 2018 the group unified its sub-brands — including Ogilvy & Mather, Ogilvy Public Relations and OgilvyOne — under the single name Ogilvy.

Notable work

The agency is credited with long-running campaigns for American Express ("Don't leave home without it"), Dove's "Campaign for Real Beauty" (launched 2004), IBM, and Maxwell House ("Good to the last drop"). David Ogilvy's books Confessions of an Advertising Man (1963) and Ogilvy on Advertising (1983) remain influential texts in the industry.
