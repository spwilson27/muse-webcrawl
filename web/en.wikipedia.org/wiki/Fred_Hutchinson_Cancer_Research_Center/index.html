The Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center, also known as Fred Hutch, is a cancer research institute established in 1975 in Seattle, Washington. It is named after Fred Hutchinson, a Major League Baseball pitcher and manager who died of lung cancer at age 45 in 1964. The center was founded by his brother, surgeon William B. Hutchinson. In 2022 Fred Hutch merged with the Seattle Cancer Care Alliance and the University of Washington's cancer program to form the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center, an independent, private nonprofit organisation and the only National Cancer Institute-designated cancer center in the Pacific Northwest.

Research

Fred Hutch is renowned for pioneering bone marrow transplantation as a cure for leukaemia and other blood diseases; E. Donnall Thomas, who developed the technique there, received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1990. The institute's research spans basic science, clinical research, prevention, vaccine development and global health, and it played a central coordinating role in COVID-19 vaccine trials. Its campus sits on Seattle's First Hill and South Lake Union neighbourhoods.

Clinical care

The clinical arm treats patients with cancer and emphasizes an integrated model in which laboratory discoveries are translated rapidly into therapies. Fundraising events such as the Obliteride cycling ride support its work.
