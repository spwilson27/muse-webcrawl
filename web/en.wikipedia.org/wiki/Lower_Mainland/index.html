Lower Mainland

The Lower Mainland is a geographic and cultural region of the south-western corner of mainland British Columbia, Canada. It encompasses the lower Fraser Valley and the Greater Vancouver area, stretching roughly from the Strait of Georgia eastward through the Fraser Valley toward Hope, and contains the great majority of the province's population.

Geography

The region is bounded by the Coast Mountains to the north, the Cascade Mountains to the east, the Strait of Georgia to the west, and the Canada–United States border to the south. The Fraser River runs through its centre, and its low-lying flood plains are among Canada's most productive agricultural land, though they are exposed to flooding, as in the November 2021 Sumas Prairie floods.

Population and governance

Close to three million people live in the Lower Mainland, most within the Metro Vancouver Regional District; the eastern part falls in the Fraser Valley Regional District. Major cities include Vancouver, Surrey, Burnaby, Richmond, Abbotsford, Langley, and Chilliwack. The region is the traditional territory of Coast Salish peoples, including the Musqueam, Squamish, Tsleil-Waututh, Katzie, and Stó:lō nations.
