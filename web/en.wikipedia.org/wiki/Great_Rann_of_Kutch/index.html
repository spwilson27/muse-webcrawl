Great Rann of Kutch

The Great Rann of Kutch is a salt marsh in the Thar Desert in the Kutch District of Gujarat, India. It is about 7505 km² (2900 sq miles) in area and is reputed to be one of the largest salt deserts in the world. The area has been inhabited by the Kutchi people.

The Hindi word Rann and the Sindhi word Rinn come from the Sanskrit word iriṇa and Sanskrit/Prakrit raṇa, meaning "desert" or "salt marsh". The Rann of Kutch covers about 30,000 km² between the Gulf of Kutch and the mouth of the Indus River in southern Pakistan. The marsh can be accessed from the village of Kharaghoda in Surendranagar District.

Geography and climate

The Great Rann of Kutch, along with the Little Rann of Kutch and the Banni grasslands on its southern edge, is situated in the district of Kutch and comprises some 30,000 square kilometres (10,000 sq mi) between the Gulf of Kutch and the mouth of the Indus River in southern Pakistan. The marsh can be reached from the village of Kharaghoda in Surendranagar District. The Little Rann of Kutch is known for its wild ass sanctuary. To the north is the large alluvial fan of the Banni region, which was formed from the sediment deposited by the Indus, Luni, Rupen and other rivers.

The climate of the ecoregion is tropical and subtropical dry broadleaf forest. The temperature in summer can rise above 49 °C (120 °F) during noon time, whereas in winter it drops to near 0 °C (32 °F). The annual rainfall is less than 400 mm (16 in).

Ecology

The Rann of Kutch is the only large flooded grassland zone in the Indomalayan realm. The area has desert on one side and the sea on the other, enabling various ecosystems, including mangrove and desert vegetation. Its grassland and deserts are home to forms of wildlife that have adapted to its often harsh conditions. These include endemic and endangered animal and plant species.

The area is also famous for its Indian wild ass, nilgai, and migratory birds such as flamingos, which gather in large numbers during the winter months.

Rann Utsav

The Government of Gujarat hosts an annual festival called Rann Utsav (festival of the Rann) at Dhordo village, where tourists can see the natural beauty of the white salt desert under the full moon. The festival typically runs from November to February and features cultural programmes, folk music, handicrafts and adventure activities.
