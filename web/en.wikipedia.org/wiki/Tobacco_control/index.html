Tobacco control

Tobacco control is the field of public health policy and practice aimed at reducing tobacco use and exposure to tobacco smoke, the leading preventable cause of death worldwide, responsible for more than 8 million deaths a year.

Measures

Core measures include taxation to raise prices, smoke-free laws for enclosed public places and workplaces, advertising and sponsorship bans, large graphic health warnings and plain packaging, cessation support, and bans on sales to minors. The WHO summarises this package as MPOWER (Monitor, Protect, Offer help, Warn, Enforce, Raise taxes).

International framework

The WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (FCTC), adopted in 2003 and in force from 2005, was the first treaty negotiated under WHO auspices and has over 180 parties. Tobacco control has historically been contested by industry lobbying and litigation; global smoking prevalence has declined, though use of newer nicotine products presents ongoing policy questions.
