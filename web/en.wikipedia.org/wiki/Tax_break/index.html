Tax break

A tax break is a reduction in the amount of tax owed, granted through deductions, credits, exemptions or preferential rates.

A tax break is a benefit provided by a government that reduces a taxpayer's total tax liability. Tax breaks may be granted through deductions from taxable income, credits against tax owed, exclusions of particular income, exemptions for particular taxpayers or activities, or reduced rates. The term is informal and is used in public debate for provisions ranging from longstanding structural features of a tax code to targeted incentives.

Forms

  • Deductions reduce the income subject to tax (e.g., mortgage interest or business expenses).
  • Tax credits reduce tax owed directly, and may be refundable.
  • Exemptions and exclusions remove particular income or entities from tax entirely.
  • Preferential rates tax certain income, such as capital gains or dividends, at lower rates.
  • Deferrals postpone tax, as with retirement savings accounts.

Purpose and criticism

Governments use tax breaks to encourage behaviour considered desirable—saving for retirement, home ownership, charitable giving, research and development, or investment in particular regions or industries. Economists often classify the revenue forgone as a tax expenditure, equivalent in budgetary effect to direct spending. Critics argue that tax breaks can be poorly targeted, disproportionately benefit higher-income taxpayers, complicate the code and create opportunities for avoidance; supporters point to their administrative simplicity and their ability to harness private decisions.
