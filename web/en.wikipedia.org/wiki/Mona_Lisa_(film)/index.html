Mona Lisa (film)

Mona Lisa is a 1986 British neo-noir crime drama film directed by Neil Jordan, written by Jordan and David Leland, and starring Bob Hoskins, Cathy Tyson and Michael Caine. It was produced by George Harrison's Handmade Films.

Plot

George (Hoskins), a small-time London gangster recently released from prison, returns to work for his old boss, the underworld kingpin Mortwell (Caine), who assigns him to chauffeur Simone (Tyson), a high-class call girl. Their relationship begins in mutual contempt but deepens into an uneasy affection as George becomes entangled in Simone's search for her friend Cathy, a young prostitute held by a violent pimp. As George tries to protect Simone, he discovers the extent of Mortwell's exploitation and his own role as a pawn, leading to a violent confrontation.

Production and reception

The film takes its title from the Nat King Cole song "Mona Lisa", which features in the film. It premiered at the 1986 Cannes Film Festival, where Bob Hoskins shared the Best Actor award. Hoskins also won the BAFTA and Golden Globe for Best Actor and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actor. The film was praised for Jordan's atmospheric direction, its blend of crime thriller and character study, and Hoskins's performance, and it is now considered a landmark of 1980s British cinema. Michael Caine was BAFTA-nominated for his supporting role.
