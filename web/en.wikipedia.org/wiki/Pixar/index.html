Pixar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Pixar campus entrance in Emeryville, California, with Luxo Jr. — the studio's lamp mascot — as a statue by the sign]

Pixar Animation Studios is an American computer animation studio based in Emeryville, California, a subsidiary of Walt Disney Studios since 2006. Founded as the Graphics Group of Lucasfilm (1979), bought by Steve Jobs in 1986 for $10 million, it produced the first fully computer-animated feature, Toy Story (1995), and a run of films — Finding Nemo (2003), The Incredibles (2004), Ratatouille (2007), WALL-E (2008), Up (2009), Inside Out (2015), Coco (2017), Soul (2020) — that made it the most critically celebrated studio in animation history.

Disney acquired Pixar in 2006 for about $7.4 billion in stock, making Jobs Disney's largest shareholder and installing Pixar's Ed Catmull and John Lasseter over Disney Animation as well — a merger that revitalised both studios.

The Braintrust and the lamp

Pixar's method couples bleeding-edge technology (its RenderMan software, the Presto animation system) to a story culture of brutal candour — the 'Braintrust' meetings in which directors' films are dissected by peers, a process Catmull documented in Creativity, Inc. (2014). Luxo Jr., the hopping desk lamp from Lasseter's 1986 short, is the logo; the studio's campus, designed under Jobs, is built to force collisions between departments.

Standing and troubles

Nineteen Academy Awards for Best Animated Feature across its catalogue (by recent counts) mark Pixar's dominance; the 2020s brought turbulence — pandemic-era streaming releases of Soul, Luca and Turning Red undervalued the brand theatrically by its own leadership's admission, Lasseter departed in 2018 amid misconduct allegations, and Elemental's slow-burn success (2023) and Inside Out 2's record run (2024, animation's highest-grossing film) steadied the studio.
