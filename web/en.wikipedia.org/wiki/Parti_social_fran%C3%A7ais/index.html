The Parti social français (PSF; French Social Party) was a French nationalist political party founded in 1936 by Colonel François de La Rocque after the dissolution of his Croix-de-Feu league by the Popular Front government. It became one of the largest parties in France before the Second World War, claiming close to a million members.

Ideology and activity

The PSF combined nationalism, social Catholicism, corporatism, and anti-communism, advocating a strengthened executive and reconciliation between classes. Historians debate whether it should be classed as fascist: La Rocque rejected a coup, operated within republican legality, and competed in elections, where the party won municipal and by-election successes.

Wartime and aftermath

Banned under the occupation in practice, the party was dissolved in 1945. La Rocque himself was deported by the Germans in 1943 despite early contacts with Vichy, and died in 1946. Many PSF cadres later joined Gaullist movements, and the party is seen as a forerunner of the modern French right's mass party organisation.
