Halifax-class frigate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Image placeholder: Halifax-class frigate

The Halifax-class frigates are a class of multi-role patrol frigates that have served the Royal Canadian Navy since 1992. Twelve ships were built under the Canadian Patrol Frigate Project, the largest Canadian warship construction programme since the Second World War. Named for Canadian cities — beginning with HMCS Halifax — they form the backbone of Canada's surface fleet and are based at Halifax, Nova Scotia, and Esquimalt, British Columbia.

Design

Each ship displaces about 4,700 tonnes, is roughly 134 metres long, and uses a combined diesel and gas turbine (CODOG) propulsion system giving speeds above 29 knots. The original armament included Sea Sparrow surface-to-air missiles, Harpoon anti-ship missiles, a 57 mm Bofors gun, a Phalanx close-in weapon system, torpedo tubes and a CH-124 Sea King helicopter (later replaced by the CH-148 Cyclone).

Modernisation

Between 2010 and 2018 the class underwent the Halifax-class Modernization (HCM) / Frigate Life Extension (FELEX) programme, installing new radars, the evolved Sea Sparrow missile (ESSM), upgraded command and control systems and other improvements intended to keep the ships effective into the 2030s, when the Canadian Surface Combatant programme is expected to replace them.

Service

Halifax-class frigates have deployed worldwide, including to the Arabian Sea for counter-terrorism operations after 2001, NATO standing naval groups, counter-piracy patrols and domestic sovereignty missions in the Arctic and on both coasts.
