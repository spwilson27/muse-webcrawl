Born: 9 August 1922, Coventry, England
Died: 2 December 1985 (aged 63), Hull, England

Philip Arthur Larkin (9 August 1922 – 2 December 1985) was an English poet, novelist, and librarian. His first book of poetry, The North Ship, was published in 1945, followed by two novels, Jill (1946) and A Girl in Winter (1947). He came to prominence in 1955 with the publication of his second collection of poems, The Less Deceived, followed by The Whitsun Weddings (1964) and High Windows (1974).

Life and career

Larkin was born in Coventry and educated at King Henry VIII School and St John's College, Oxford, where he befriended Kingsley Amis. After graduating he worked as a librarian, first in Wellington, Shropshire, then at the University of Leicester and Queen's University Belfast. In 1955 he became Librarian at the University of Hull, a post he held for thirty years until his death. He never married, though he maintained long relationships, most notably with Monica Jones.

Poetry

Larkin is regarded as one of the greatest English poets of the second half of the twentieth century. Associated with The Movement, his work is marked by plain diction, formal craftsmanship, and a tone that mixes melancholy, scepticism and dry humour. Well-known poems include "The Whitsun Weddings", "Church Going", "An Arundel Tomb", "This Be The Verse", "Aubade" and "Annus Mirabilis", famous for its opening lines about sexual intercourse beginning in 1963. In 1984 he declined the position of Poet Laureate. Posthumous publication of his letters and a biography prompted debate about his private views, but his reputation as a poet has endured; in 2008 The Times named him the greatest British post-war writer.
