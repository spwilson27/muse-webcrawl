The Pink Lady is a Victorian-era Queen Anne style house in Eureka, California, United States. Painted in shades of pink — the source of its nickname — it stands directly across the street from its builder's own, larger residence, the Carson Mansion, and is one of the most photographed houses in California.

History

The house was built in 1889 by lumber baron William Carson as a wedding present for his son John Milton Carson and his bride. It was designed in the ornate Eastlake-influenced Queen Anne style by the firm of Newsom and Newsom, the architects of the Carson Mansion. The house remained in the Carson family for decades before passing into other hands; in 1964 it was sold and its furnishings were auctioned amid local controversy.

Architecture

The three-storey wooden house displays the asymmetry, patterned shingles, bay windows, decorative spindlework and wraparound porch typical of late-Victorian Queen Anne design. Its soft pink and maroon paint scheme is a later but now-canonical treatment that reinforces its popular name.

Today

The Pink Lady is a private residence, but its position opposite the famous Carson Mansion makes it a standard stop on tours of Eureka's Old Town. It has been used in the local wedding and events business and is protected within the city's historic building fabric.
