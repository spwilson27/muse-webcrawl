Vexillology is the study of the history, symbolism and usage of flags, or by extension any interest in flags in general. The word derives from the Latin vexillum, a square flag carried by Roman cavalry, combined with the Greek suffix -logia ("study"). A person who studies flags is a vexillologist; one who designs them is a vexillographer.

History

The study of flags was long treated as part of heraldry, but was established as a distinct discipline in the mid-20th century. American scholar Whitney Smith coined the term in 1957 and founded the Flag Research Center in 1962; he was instrumental in founding the International Federation of Vexillological Associations (FIAV) in 1967, which now links flag-study associations worldwide and holds biennial international congresses.

Terminology and design

Vexillologists describe flags using a technical vocabulary — canton, hoist, fly, field, charge, fimbriation — and classify flags by use (civil, state, war, ensigns) using the FIAV's usage symbols. The North American Vexillological Association popularised five principles of good flag design: simplicity, meaningful symbolism, two or three basic colours, no lettering or seals, and distinctiveness.

Modern interest

While once largely the province of specialists, vexillology has gained a broad public following through flag-design competitions, civic rebranding efforts, and online communities. New Zealand's 2015–2016 flag referendums and numerous US city flag redesigns in the 2010s and 2020s brought scholarly design principles to wide attention.
