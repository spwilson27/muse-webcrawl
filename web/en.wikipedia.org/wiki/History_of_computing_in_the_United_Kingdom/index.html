History of computing in the United Kingdom

The history of computing in the United Kingdom begins with Charles Babbage's designs for the Difference and Analytical Engines in the 19th century, with Ada Lovelace's notes on the latter often cited as the first computer program.

Wartime and early computers

At Bletchley Park, Alan Turing and colleagues built the Bombe and the Colossus machines to break German codes. After the war, Manchester's "Baby" (1948) was the first stored-program computer to run, followed by EDSAC at Cambridge (1949) and the commercial Ferranti Mark 1 and LEO computers.

Industry and home computing

British firms such as ICL competed internationally, while the 1980s saw a home-computing boom with the Sinclair ZX Spectrum, the BBC Micro, and Acorn's ARM architecture, which later dominated mobile processors. See also Alan Turing.
