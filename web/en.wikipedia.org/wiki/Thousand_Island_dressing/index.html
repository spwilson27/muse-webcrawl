Thousand Island dressing

TypeSalad dressing, condiment
Place of originUnited States / Canada (Thousand Islands region)

ColourPale pink to orange

Thousand Island dressing is an American salad dressing and condiment based on mayonnaise that can include olive oil, lemon juice, orange juice, paprika, Worcestershire sauce, mustard, vinegar, cream, chilli sauce, tomato purée, and Tabasco sauce. It also typically contains finely chopped ingredients, which can include pickles, onions, bell peppers, green olives, hard-boiled egg, parsley, pimento, chives, garlic, or chopped nuts such as walnuts or chestnuts. Its name comes from the Thousand Islands region along the upper St. Lawrence River between the United States and Canada, where the dressing was developed around the turn of the 20th century.

History

Several origin stories compete. According to the most popular account, the dressing was created by Sophia LaLonde, wife of a fishing guide in Clayton, New York, in the Thousand Islands, and served to visiting sportsmen; the actress May Irwin obtained the recipe and passed it to hotelier George Boldt, proprietor of the Waldorf-Astoria Hotel in New York, who put it on the hotel's menu around 1894 under maître d' Oscar Tschirky. Other versions credit chefs at hotels in Chicago or New Orleans with the invention. The earliest printed recipes date from the first years of the 1900s, and the dressing became a standard of American hotel and restaurant cooking.

Uses

In addition to being used on salads, Thousand Island dressing is used as a sandwich spread, notably in the Reuben sandwich and as the "special sauce" of hamburgers such as the Big Mac, whose sauce is closely related. It is a close relative of Russian dressing, which is sharper and traditionally made with horseradish and pimentos, and of fry sauce and other mayonnaise-and-ketchup-based condiments.

Variations

Low-fat versions substitute yoghurt or reduced-fat mayonnaise; commercial bottled versions are widely sold by major American brands. Outside the United States, similar pink dressings are served with prawn (shrimp) cocktails and seafood salads.
