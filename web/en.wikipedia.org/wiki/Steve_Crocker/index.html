Steve Crocker

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Steve Crocker
BornStephen David Crocker, 15 October 1944, Pasadena, California
Known forWriting RFC 1; ARPANET protocols; ICANN chair (2011–2017)
[Image: Portrait photograph of Steve Crocker speaking at an Internet governance meeting in the 2010s]

Stephen David Crocker (born 1944) is an American Internet pioneer. In 1969, as a graduate student at UCLA, he wrote the first Request for Comments — RFC 1, 'Host Software' — and convened the Network Working Group of graduate students who designed the ARPANET's host protocols, inventing along the way the open, discussion-based standards culture that still governs Internet engineering.

Crocker was part of the UCLA team that connected the first ARPANET node and sent the network's first message (the famous crashed 'LO' of 'LOGIN', 29 October 1969). He later worked at DARPA, founded the Internet Society's predecessor activities, and pursued network security ventures. In a symmetry much remarked upon, the student who wrote RFC 1 returned as a governor of the mature Internet: he served on, then chaired, the board of ICANN from 2011 to 2017, overseeing the period that included the 2016 IANA stewardship transition ending US government oversight of the DNS root — functions whose informal origins lay with his friend Jon Postel.
