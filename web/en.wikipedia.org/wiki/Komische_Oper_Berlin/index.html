The Komische Oper Berlin is an opera company and opera house in the Mitte district of Berlin, Germany, located on Behrenstraße near Unter den Linden. Founded in 1947 by the Austrian director Walter Felsenstein, it became renowned for its emphasis on opera as dramatic theatre, with meticulously rehearsed, text-driven productions sung in German translation. Along with the Staatsoper Unter den Linden and the Deutsche Oper Berlin, it is one of the city's three main opera houses.

The building

The theatre opened in 1892 as the Theater Unter den Linden, a variety and operetta house built to designs by Fellner & Helmer. Heavily used as the Metropol-Theater from 1898, famous for its revues, the building was badly damaged in the Second World War; its auditorium was reconstructed in a neo-baroque style in the 1960s, preserving the ornate historic interior while the façade took a plainer form.

The Felsenstein era and after

Under Felsenstein (1947–1975) the Komische Oper developed its concept of realistic music theatre, in which every singer was also an actor and productions were rehearsed over long periods. His successors included Joachim Herz and Harry Kupfer, whose productions — including a celebrated Don Giovanni — maintained the house's international reputation. From 2002 Andreas Homoki led the company, and from 2012 to 2022 Barrie Kosky served as chief director and intendant, broadening the repertoire to include operetta, musicals and rediscovered works of the Weimar era, all sung in German. The company has been led by co-intendants Susanne Moser and Philip Bröking, with productions temporarily staged at venues across Berlin during a multi-year renovation of the Behrenstraße house begun in 2023.
