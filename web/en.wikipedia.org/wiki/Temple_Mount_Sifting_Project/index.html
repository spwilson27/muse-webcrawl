Temple Mount Sifting Project

The Temple Mount Sifting Project (formerly the Temple Mount Salvage Operation) is an archaeological project begun in 2004 to recover artifacts from soil that was removed from the Temple Mount in Jerusalem during construction work carried out by the Jerusalem Islamic Waqf in 1996–2001 and dumped in the Kidron Valley.

Background

Between 1996 and 2001, large quantities of archaeologically rich earth were excavated from the area of Solomon's Stables and the el-Marwani Mosque and discarded without archaeological supervision. Archaeologists Zachi Dvira (Zweig) and Gabriel Barkay initiated the project to sift this soil, using wet sifting carried out largely by volunteers and tourists.

Finds

The sifting has yielded hundreds of thousands of finds spanning thousands of years, including First Temple period seal impressions (bullae), arrowheads, coins from many eras, Herodian architectural fragments, opus sectile floor tiles that helped reconstruct the Second Temple courts' flooring, and Ottoman-era items.
