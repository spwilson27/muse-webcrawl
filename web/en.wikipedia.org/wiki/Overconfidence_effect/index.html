Overconfidence effect

Overconfidence effect
Type: Cognitive bias
Forms: Overestimation, overplacement, overprecision
Related: Dunning–Kruger, planning fallacy, optimism bias

The overconfidence effect is a cognitive bias in which a person's subjective confidence in their judgements reliably exceeds their objective accuracy — a robust finding in psychology and behavioural economics.

Types

Researchers distinguish overestimation of one's performance, overplacement relative to others (the better-than-average effect), and overprecision — excessive certainty in beliefs, shown when 98% confidence intervals capture the true value far less than 98% of the time.

Consequences

Overconfidence contributes to the planning fallacy, excessive trading, entrepreneurial failure, diagnostic error, and war. Calibration training and considering alternatives can reduce, but rarely eliminate, it. It differs from the Dunning–Kruger effect, though the two are often conflated.
