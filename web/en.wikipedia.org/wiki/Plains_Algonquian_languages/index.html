The Plains Algonquian languages are a grouping of Algonquian languages spoken by Native American peoples of the Great Plains of North America. Despite their geographic proximity, the Plains Algonquian languages are not particularly closely related to one another; they are conventionally grouped as areally and culturally similar branches of the wider Algonquian family rather than as a single genetic subfamily.

Classification

The Plains Algonquian group is generally said to comprise three languages or subgroups: Blackfoot, which is its own divergent branch; Cheyenne; and the Arapahoan languages, including Arapaho and Gros Ventre (Atsina). Blackfoot is quite divergent from the others and is sometimes treated as an independent branch of Algonquian.

Features

Plains Algonquian languages share some areal features acquired through long contact on the Plains, alongside the inherited Algonquian traits of polysynthesis, animate and inanimate grammatical gender, and complex verb morphology. Historically, the speakers of these languages were peoples such as the Blackfeet Confederacy, the Cheyenne, the Arapaho, and the Gros Ventre.
