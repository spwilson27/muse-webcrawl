Paramount leader

Meaning"Highest leader"
StatusInformal political term, not an official title

Paramount leader (Chinese: 最高领导人; pinyin: Zuìgāo Lǐngdǎorén; lit. "highest leader") is an informal term for the most prominent political leader in the People's Republic of China (PRC). The paramount leader typically controls the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and the People's Liberation Army, often holding the posts of General Secretary of the CCP, Chairman of the Central Military Commission, and President of the PRC. The term is not an official position; it describes de facto supremacy within the leadership.

The Great Hall of the People in Beijing, seat of China's national congresses.

History

The concept is associated particularly with Deng Xiaoping, who exercised paramount authority from 1978 to the early 1990s without ever holding the top formal posts of head of state, head of government, or (after its abolition and restoration) party general secretary — demonstrating that real power in the PRC rests on control of the party and military rather than on any single formal office. Mao Zedong, as CCP Chairman and founder of the PRC, is generally regarded as the paramount leader from 1949 until his death in 1976.

Scholars note that Deng established norms of collective leadership and orderly succession intended to prevent a return to one-man rule; Xi Jinping's consolidation of power and the 2018 constitutional amendment removing presidential term limits have been widely analysed as a shift back toward personalised leadership.
