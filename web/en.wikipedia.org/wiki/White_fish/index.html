White fish

White fish (or whitefish) is a fisheries term for several species of demersal fish with fins, particularly Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua), whiting (Merlangius merlangus), haddock (Melanogrammus aeglefinus), hake (Merluccius), pollock (Pollachius), and others. The term also covers some freshwater species of the salmonid subfamily Coregoninae — see freshwater whitefish.

Cod and haddock, classic examples of white fish

Characteristics

White fish live on or near the seafloor and are distinguished from oily fish such as herring and mackerel by their low fat content: their oil is concentrated in the liver rather than distributed through the flesh, which is white, mild-flavoured, and flaky when cooked. Their flesh typically contains less than a few percent fat, compared with up to 30 percent in oily fish.

Fisheries

White fish have been a staple of North Atlantic fisheries for centuries, supporting the economies of fishing communities from New England to Norway. Major stocks have been heavily exploited; the collapse of the northern cod stock off Newfoundland in the early 1990s led to a moratorium and remains a landmark case in fisheries management. White fish are the standard fish used in British fish and chips, traditionally cod or haddock.
