Murayama Statement

The Murayama Statement is the landmark official apology issued by the prime minister of Japan, Tomiichi Murayama, on 15 August 1995, the 50th anniversary of the end of the Second World War. Formally titled "On the occasion of the 50th anniversary of the war's end", the statement was approved by the full cabinet and has since been affirmed as the official position of successive Japanese governments.

Content

Murayama declared that Japan, "following a mistaken national policy, advanced along the road to war… through its colonial rule and aggression, caused tremendous damage and suffering to the people of many countries, particularly to those of Asian nations." He expressed "deep remorse" and offered his "heartfelt apology" for this "irrefutable fact of history", and pledged that Japan would renounce war and pursue peace.

Significance

The statement set the standard wording for Japanese official apologies; later statements, such as that of Prime Minister Junichiro Koizumi in 2005, repeated its key phrases. It followed the 1993 Kono Statement on the "comfort women" issue and the Diet's contested 1995 war-renunciation resolution. Debates over whether later leaders have upheld, diluted or should revisit the Murayama wording remain a recurring feature of Japanese politics and of Japan's relations with China and South Korea.
