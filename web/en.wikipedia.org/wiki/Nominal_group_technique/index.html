Nominal group technique

The nominal group technique (NGT) is a group process involving problem identification, solution generation, and decision making. It can be used in groups of many sizes, who want to make their decision quickly, as by a vote, but want everyone's opinions taken into account (as opposed to traditional voting, where only the largest group is considered). The method of tallying is the difference. First, every member of the group gives their view in brief; the moderator records all views; duplicate solutions are eliminated from the list; then the group discusses and ranks or votes on the remaining ideas.

Procedure

Developed by Andre Delbecq and Andrew Van de Ven in 1968 and applied in studies of program planning, the technique typically involves five steps: (1) introduction and explanation of the question; (2) silent generation of ideas, written down by each participant alone; (3) sharing ideas one at a time in round-robin fashion, recorded publicly without debate; (4) group discussion for clarification and evaluation; and (5) individual ranking or rating of ideas, with the group's aggregated result taken as the decision.

Advantages and limitations

Research has found NGT groups often generate more and better ideas than unstructured interacting groups, because the structured format balances participation, prevents domination by a few members, and separates idea generation from evaluation. Limitations include the need for a trained facilitator, restriction to a single well-defined question per session, and the time required for preparation.
