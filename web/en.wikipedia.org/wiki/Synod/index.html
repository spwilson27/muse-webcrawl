Synod

A synod (from Greek σύνοδος, "assembly" or "meeting") is a council of a Christian church, usually convened to decide an issue of doctrine, administration, or application. The word is often synonymous with "council", though in some traditions — particularly in Presbyterian and Reformed polity — it denotes a specific intermediate governing body.

Usage by tradition

In the Roman Catholic Church, a Synod of Bishops advises the pope, while diocesan synods gather clergy and laity of a diocese. In Eastern Orthodoxy, the Holy Synod is the governing assembly of bishops of an autocephalous church. In Lutheran and Anglican churches, synods often include elected lay and clergy representatives and exercise legislative functions; in the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, for example, synods are regional judicatories led by bishops.

History

Regional synods met from the second century onward to address disputes such as the date of Easter. The first empire-wide gathering recognised as an ecumenical council was the Council of Nicaea in 325. Notable later synods include the Synod of Dort (1618–19) in the Reformed tradition and the controversial "Robber Synod" of 449.
