Low-noise amplifier

Low-noise amplifier
TypeElectronic amplifier
ApplicationRadio receivers, satellite and radar systems
Key parameterNoise figure

A low-noise amplifier (LNA) is an electronic amplifier that amplifies a very low-power signal without significantly degrading its signal-to-noise ratio. LNAs are a fundamental component of radio receivers, where they are placed as close as possible to the antenna so that losses before amplification contribute minimally to system noise.

Design considerations

According to the Friis formula for noise, the noise figure of a receiver chain is dominated by its first stage; hence the first amplifier must combine adequate gain with a very low noise figure, often below 2 dB at microwave frequencies and below 1 dB in cooled radio-astronomy systems. Designers must trade off noise figure against gain, input matching, linearity (third-order intercept point), and stability. Devices such as HEMTs, PHEMTs, and SiGe bipolar transistors are commonly used.

Applications

LNAs appear in cellular base stations and handsets, GPS receivers, satellite communications (including the low-noise block downconverters on satellite dishes), radar, radio telescopes, and medical imaging equipment. In radio astronomy, cryogenically cooled LNAs approach the quantum limit of noise performance.
