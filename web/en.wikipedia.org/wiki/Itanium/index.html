Itanium

Itanium is a discontinued family of 64-bit microprocessors developed by Hewlett-Packard and Intel, implementing the IA-64 architecture. First released in 2001 under the codename Merced, it was intended as a successor to 32-bit x86 for enterprise servers but lost the market to the simpler x86-64 extension.

LaunchedMay 2001
Discontinued2021 (final shipments)
DesignersHP and Intel
ArchitectureIA-64 (EPIC/VLIW)

Architecture

IA-64 used Explicitly Parallel Instruction Computing (EPIC), in which the compiler, rather than the hardware, schedules instructions into bundles for parallel execution. Poor performance on existing x86 code and repeated delays undermined adoption.

History

Successors included Itanium 2 (2002), the dual-core Montecito (2006), Tukwila (2010), Poulson (2012), and the final Kittson (2017). It found a niche in high-end systems running HP-UX and OpenVMS. Intel ended orders in 2020 and shipped the last processors in July 2021.
