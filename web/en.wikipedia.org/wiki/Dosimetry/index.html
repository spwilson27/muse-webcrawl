FieldRadiation protection, medical physics
Key quantitiesAbsorbed dose (gray), equivalent/effective dose (sievert)

Dosimetry is the measurement, calculation and assessment of the absorbed dose of ionising radiation received by the human body or by other materials. It is central to radiation protection for workers in medicine, nuclear power and industry, and to the safe delivery of radiotherapy, where the dose to a tumour and to surrounding healthy tissue must be known precisely.

Quantities and units

The fundamental quantity is the absorbed dose, the energy deposited per unit mass, measured in grays (Gy), where 1 Gy = 1 joule per kilogram. Weighting the absorbed dose by factors for radiation type gives the equivalent dose, and further weighting by tissue sensitivity gives the effective dose; both are measured in sieverts (Sv). Operational quantities such as personal dose equivalent are used for routine monitoring and are designed to conservatively estimate the protection quantities.

Personal dosimeters

Radiation workers wear personal dosimeters, traditionally film badges and later thermoluminescent dosimeters (TLDs) and optically stimulated luminescence (OSL) badges, which integrate exposure over weeks or months. Electronic personal dosimeters give immediate readouts and alarms. Extremity and eye-lens dosimeters monitor parts of the body that may receive higher doses, such as the hands of interventional clinicians.

Clinical and environmental dosimetry

In radiotherapy, ionisation chambers, diode detectors, gel dosimeters and treatment-planning calculations verify that the prescribed dose distribution is delivered. In environmental monitoring, dosimetry tracks exposures to the public from natural background radiation and from authorised discharges, forming the evidence base for dose limits recommended by bodies such as the ICRP and enforced by national regulators.
