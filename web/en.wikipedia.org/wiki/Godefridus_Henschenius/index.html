Godefridus Henschenius

Godefridus Henschenius (also Godfrey Henschen, in Dutch Godefroid Henschen; 1601–1681) was a Flemish Jesuit priest and hagiographer, one of the first of the Bollandists. As the principal collaborator of Jean Bolland, he helped establish the Acta Sanctorum, the vast critical collection of saints' lives arranged by feast day, on a scholarly footing.

Life and work

Henschenius was born at Venray in Limburg and entered the Society of Jesus in 1619. In 1635 he joined Jean Bolland in Antwerp to assist with the projected collection of the lives of all the saints. Together they travelled through Germany and Italy in 1660–1662, gathering manuscripts from libraries. The first two volumes of the Acta Sanctorum, covering January, appeared in 1643; volumes for February and March followed, setting the pattern of critical editions accompanied by learned commentary (the famous commentarius praevius) that characterised Bollandist scholarship.

Henschenius is credited with much of the scholarly method of the enterprise: the comparison of manuscript sources, scepticism toward legendary accretions, and chronological rigour. His studies of St Patrick and of early Irish and German saints were influential, though his critical treatment sometimes attracted controversy. With Daniel Papebroch, who joined in 1660, he carried the work forward after Bolland's death in 1665. Henschenius also helped found the Bollandists' library and museum in Antwerp, which supported the continuing publication.
