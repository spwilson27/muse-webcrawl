Kakutani fixed-point theorem

The Kakutani fixed-point theorem is a fixed-point theorem in mathematical analysis established by Shizuo Kakutani in 1941. It generalises the Brouwer fixed-point theorem from single-valued functions to set-valued functions (correspondences), and it is a foundational tool in economics and game theory.

Statement

Let S be a non-empty, compact, convex subset of a Euclidean space. Let φ: S → 2S be a set-valued function on S such that for every x in S, the set φ(x) is non-empty and convex, and φ has a closed graph (is upper hemicontinuous). Then φ has a fixed point: there exists x in S such that x ∈ φ(x).

Applications

John von Neumann used a result of this type in his 1937 work on expanding economies, and Kakutani's theorem supplied the standard tool when John Nash proved the existence of Nash equilibria in non-cooperative games in 1950: a profile of mixed strategies is an equilibrium exactly when it is a fixed point of the players' best-response correspondence. The theorem likewise underlies proofs of existence of competitive (Walrasian) equilibrium in general equilibrium theory, such as in the Arrow–Debreu model.
