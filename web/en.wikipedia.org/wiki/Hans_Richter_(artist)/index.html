Hans Richter (artist)

Born6 April 1888, Berlin, German Empire
Died1 February 1976 (aged 87), Minusio, Switzerland
MovementDada, abstract film, avant-garde
Notable worksRhythmus 21 (1921), Ghosts Before Breakfast (1928), Dreams That Money Can Buy (1947)

Hans Richter (6 April 1888 – 1 February 1976) was a German painter, graphic artist, and experimental filmmaker, one of the central figures of the European avant-garde. A participant in Zurich Dada during the First World War, he went on to make some of the earliest abstract films and to chronicle the Dada movement as its historian.

Early life and Dada

Richter studied art in Berlin and Weimar and was badly wounded in the First World War. In Zurich in 1916 he joined the circle around Hugo Ball, Tristan Tzara, and Hans Arp at the Cabaret Voltaire, becoming a leading Dadaist. With the Swedish painter Viking Eggeling he explored abstract "scroll" paintings — long rolls of sequential geometric forms conceived as music for the eye — which led directly to film.

Film work

Richter's Rhythmus 21 (1921), in which rectangles expand, contract, and slide across the screen, is regarded as one of the first abstract films. He followed it with Rhythmus 23 and Rhythmus 25, the playful Dada film Ghosts Before Breakfast (Vormittagsspuk, 1928), in which bowler hats and objects revolt against their owners, and Filmstudie (1926). In 1929 he published Filmgegner von heute — Filmfreunde von morgen, a manifesto for film as an art form, and organised the landmark "Film und Foto" exhibition in Stuttgart.

Emigration and later career

Richter's work was condemned by the Nazis and he left Germany in 1931, eventually settling in the United States in 1941. In New York he taught at the City College film institute and made Dreams That Money Can Buy (1947), an anthology film made with contributions from Max Ernst, Marcel Duchamp, Man Ray, Alexander Calder, and Fernand Léger, which won a prize at Venice. His book Dada: Art and Anti-Art (1964) remains a standard first-hand history of the movement. He spent his later years in Switzerland.
