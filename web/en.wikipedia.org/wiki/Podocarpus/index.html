Podocarpus

Podocarpus
Family: Podocarpaceae
Type species: Podocarpus elongatus
Species: about 100

Podocarpus is a genus of conifers, the most numerous and widely distributed genus in the podocarp family Podocarpaceae. The roughly 100 species are evergreen shrubs and trees found throughout the Southern Hemisphere and extending into the tropics and subtropics of Asia and the Americas. The name derives from the Greek pous (foot) and karpos (fruit), referring to the fleshy stalk that bears the seed.

Description

Unlike most conifers, podocarps do not bear their seeds in woody cones; instead the seed sits on a fleshy, often brightly coloured receptacle (the epimatium) that attracts birds and other dispersers. Leaves are typically strap-shaped or needle-like and spirally arranged. Species range from low shrubs to forest giants such as the New Zealand rimu relative Podocarpus totara and the South African yellowwoods, several of which are important timber trees.

Uses

Several species are valued timber sources, and many — notably Podocarpus macrophyllus, the Buddhist pine or kusamaki — are widely planted as ornamentals and hedges in warm-temperate gardens.
