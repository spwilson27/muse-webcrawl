Caiman (genus)

KingdomAnimalia
OrderCrocodilia
FamilyAlligatoridae
SubfamilyCaimaninae
GenusCaiman Spix, 1825

Caiman is a genus of caimans, small crocodilians in the family Alligatoridae, native to Central and South America. Members of the genus are relatively small alligatorids: males of the largest species, the black caiman's close relative the broad-snouted caiman, generally reach about 2 to 2.5 metres (6.6 to 8.2 ft), while the spectacled caiman typically reaches 1.5 to 2.5 metres. They inhabit slow-moving rivers, flooded forests, lakes, and wetlands, feeding on fish, amphibians, crustaceans, and other small animals.

Species

Three extant species are generally recognised:

  • Spectacled caiman (Caiman crocodilus) — also called the common caiman or white caiman; the most widespread species, ranging from southern Mexico through Central America to northern South America. It takes its name from the bony ridge between the eyes, which resembles a pair of spectacles.
  • Broad-snouted caiman (Caiman latirostris) — found in eastern and southern Brazil, Bolivia, Paraguay, Uruguay, and northern Argentina; distinguished by its unusually broad snout.
  • Yacare caiman (Caiman yacare) — found in central South America, including the Pantanal of Brazil, Bolivia, Paraguay, and Argentina; formerly treated as a subspecies of the spectacled caiman.

Several extinct species are known from the fossil record, mainly from Miocene deposits of South America.

Ecology and conservation

Caimans are nocturnal predators and are in turn preyed upon by jaguars, anacondas, and large birds of prey; juveniles fall victim to a wide range of predators. Females build mound nests of vegetation and guard their eggs and young. The spectacled caiman has adapted readily to human-modified habitats and has been introduced to Cuba, Puerto Rico, and Florida. Although caimans were heavily hunted for the leather trade in the mid-20th century, regulated trade and the species' adaptability mean the three recognised species are currently listed as Least Concern by the IUCN; millions of yacare caiman skins enter legal trade under CITES controls.
