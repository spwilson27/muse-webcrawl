Bicycle touring

Bicycle touring is the taking of self-contained cycling trips for pleasure, adventure or autonomy rather than sport or commuting. Touring can range from single-day trips to multi-year journeys around the world. Riders typically carry their gear on the bicycle using panniers, trailers, or frame bags (bikepacking).

History

Cycle touring began in the late 19th century with the advent of the safety bicycle; organizations such as the Cyclists' Touring Club (founded 1878 in Britain) promoted it. Notable long-distance tourists include Thomas Stevens, who rode a penny-farthing around the world in 1884–1886, and Annie Londonderry, the first woman to bicycle around the world (1894–1895). In 1976, thousands of riders crossed the United States on the Bikecentennial route, leading to the founding of the Adventure Cycling Association.

Styles and equipment

Common styles include loaded touring (fully self-supported, camping), credit-card touring (light loads, staying in hotels), supported tours with a vehicle carrying luggage, and ultralight bikepacking on unpaved routes. Touring bicycles traditionally have steel frames, relaxed geometry, wide gear ranges, and fittings for racks and mudguards. Long-distance tourists plan around water, food resupply, visas, and seasonal weather; hospitality networks such as Warm Showers connect travelling cyclists with hosts.
