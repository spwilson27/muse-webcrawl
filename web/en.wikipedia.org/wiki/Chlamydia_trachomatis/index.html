Chlamydia trachomatis

Scientific name: Chlamydia trachomatis

Type: Gram-negative obligate intracellular bacterium

Diseases: Chlamydia infection, trachoma, lymphogranuloma venereum

Chlamydia trachomatis is a Gram-negative bacterium that can replicate only within a host cell. It is one of the most common causes of sexually transmitted infection worldwide and is also the causative agent of trachoma, the world's leading infectious cause of blindness.

Life cycle

The organism has a distinctive biphasic developmental cycle. The infectious elementary body attaches to and enters a host epithelial cell, then transforms within a vacuole (inclusion) into the replicating reticulate body. After many rounds of division, the bacteria condense back into elementary bodies and are released to infect new cells.

Diseases

Different serovars cause different diseases. Serovars A–C cause trachoma, transmitted eye-to-eye, especially where water and sanitation are poor; repeated infection scars the conjunctiva and turns the eyelashes inward, damaging the cornea. Serovars D–K cause genital chlamydia — often asymptomatic, but capable of causing urethritis, cervicitis, pelvic inflammatory disease, infertility and ectopic pregnancy — as well as inclusion conjunctivitis and infant pneumonia. Serovars L1–L3 cause the invasive sexually transmitted disease lymphogranuloma venereum.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually by nucleic acid amplification testing of urine or swab specimens. Infections are treated with antibiotics such as doxycycline or azithromycin; sexual partners are treated concurrently, and screening of sexually active young people is recommended in many countries because of the frequency of silent infection.
