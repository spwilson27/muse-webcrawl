Energy Policy of the European Union

The energy policy of the European Union focuses on energy security, sustainability and competitiveness. Although each member state retains the right to determine its own energy mix under the Treaty of Lisbon, the EU sets binding targets for greenhouse gas emissions, renewable energy and energy efficiency, and operates an internal energy market intended to allow gas and electricity to flow freely across borders.

Renewable energy is central to the EU's energy and climate policy.

History

Energy was at the heart of European integration from the beginning: the European Coal and Steel Community (1951) and Euratom (1957) were among the first European communities. A common energy policy developed gradually, with major packages liberalising electricity and gas markets from the 1990s onward.

Targets and legislation

The European Green Deal aims to make the EU climate-neutral by 2050, and the European Climate Law sets a binding target of reducing net greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% by 2030 compared with 1990. Key instruments include the EU Emissions Trading System, the Renewable Energy Directive and the Energy Efficiency Directive. The REPowerEU plan, adopted in 2022 after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, accelerated efforts to end dependence on Russian fossil fuels.
