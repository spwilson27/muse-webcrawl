Gravity (2013 film)

An astronaut adrift above Earth, as depicted in the film.

Gravity is a 2013 science fiction thriller film directed by Alfonso Cuarón, who also co-wrote the screenplay with his son Jonás Cuarón. It stars Sandra Bullock and George Clooney as American astronauts stranded in space after the mid-orbit destruction of their Space Shuttle, who attempt to return to Earth.

Plot

Dr. Ryan Stone (Bullock), a biomedical engineer on her first space mission, is servicing the Hubble Space Telescope under the command of veteran astronaut Matt Kowalski (Clooney) when debris from a destroyed Russian satellite triggers a chain reaction of collisions — a Kessler syndrome cascade — that destroys their shuttle and kills the rest of the crew. Stone and Kowalski make their way to the International Space Station and then toward a Chinese space station, struggling against dwindling oxygen, fire, and repeated debris strikes, in a desperate bid for a capsule that can carry Stone home.

Production

The film was noted for its long, unbroken takes and its innovative visual-effects techniques, developed with cinematographer Emmanuel Lubezki and visual effects supervisor Tim Webber. Actors performed inside a "light box" surrounded by LED screens projecting the Earth and stars, with camera moves pre-programmed using robotic rigs. The production spent years in development while the technology to realize its weightless sequences was created.

Reception

Premiering at the 70th Venice International Film Festival, Gravity grossed over $723 million worldwide against a production budget of about $100 million. It won seven Academy Awards, including Best Director for Cuarón, Best Cinematography and Best Original Score, and was widely praised for its direction, visuals and Bullock's performance. Astronauts and scientists commended the film's depiction of orbital mechanics while noting dramatic liberties with distances between stations and the behaviour of debris fields.
