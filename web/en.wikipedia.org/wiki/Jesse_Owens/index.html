Jesse Owens

James Cleveland "Jesse" Owens (September 12, 1913 – March 31, 1980) was an American track and field athlete who won four gold medals at the 1936 Olympic Games in Berlin. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest and most famous athletes in track and field history.

Early life and college career

Owens was born in Oakville, Alabama, the grandson of slaves, and moved with his family to Cleveland, Ohio, as part of the Great Migration. A schoolteacher, mishearing his name "J.C.", called him "Jesse", and the name stuck. At Ohio State University, where he was known as the "Buckeye Bullet", Owens achieved his most famous single-day feat: at the Big Ten championships in Ann Arbor, Michigan, on May 25, 1935, he set three world records and tied a fourth within the space of about 45 minutes, in the 100-yard dash, the long jump, the 220-yard dash and the 220-yard low hurdles.

1936 Berlin Olympics

At the Berlin Games, held under the Nazi regime, Owens won gold medals in the 100 metres, the 200 metres, the long jump and the 4 × 100 metre relay. His long jump duel with Germany's Luz Long, who publicly advised and congratulated him, became one of the celebrated stories of the Games. Owens's triumphs are often said to have undermined Nazi propaganda about Aryan racial superiority, although historians note that his reception at home in the segregated United States was itself marked by discrimination: he received no congratulations from President Franklin D. Roosevelt and struggled to earn a living after the Games.

Later life

Barred from amateur competition after a dispute with the Amateur Athletic Union, Owens raced horses and cars for money, worked as a sports administrator and goodwill ambassador, and later ran a public relations firm. He was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1976 and, posthumously, the Congressional Gold Medal in 1990. Owens died of lung cancer in Tucson, Arizona, aged 66. His 1935 world record in the long jump stood for 25 years, and the Jesse Owens Award is USA Track & Field's highest accolade for the year's best athlete.
