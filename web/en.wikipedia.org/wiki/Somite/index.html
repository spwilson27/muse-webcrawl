Somite

The somites (from Greek sōma, "body") are paired blocks of paraxial mesoderm that form sequentially along the head-to-tail axis of the early vertebrate embryo, on either side of the neural tube. They are the first clear sign of the segmented (metameric) organisation of the vertebrate body plan, and they give rise to much of the axial skeleton, the skeletal muscles of the trunk and limbs' associated dermis, and other structures. In human embryos, somites appear beginning around day 20 of development, and about 42–44 pairs form by the end of the fifth week.

Somites bulge from the paraxial mesoderm flanking the neural tube of the embryo.

Formation

Somites bud off, in a regular rhythm, from the anterior end of the presomitic (unsegmented) paraxial mesoderm as the embryo elongates. The periodicity is governed by a molecular "segmentation clock" — oscillating waves of gene expression (involving the Notch, Wnt, and FGF signalling pathways) sweeping through the presomitic mesoderm, as described by the clock-and-wavefront model of Cooke and Zeeman (1976). As cells at the wavefront arrest their oscillation, they condense into an epithelial sphere — a new somite. In the chick, a pair forms roughly every 90 minutes; in the mouse about every 2 hours; in humans about every 5 hours. The final number is species-specific: about 50 in the chick, 65 in the mouse, and several hundred in some snakes.

Differentiation

Under signals from the notochord, neural tube, and lateral plate mesoderm, each somite differentiates into:

  • Sclerotome — the ventromedial part, whose cells migrate around the notochord and neural tube to form the vertebrae, ribs, and intervertebral discs.
  • Dermomyotome — the dorsal epithelial part, which further divides into the dermatome (forming the dermis of the back) and the myotome (forming the skeletal musculature of the trunk and, via migrating cells, of the limbs).
  • Syndetome — a small compartment giving rise to tendons.

Because each somite maps onto a body segment, the adult retains a segmental pattern in the vertebrae, spinal nerves, and dermatomes. Somites are numbered from anterior to posterior and classified as occipital, cervical, thoracic, lumbar, sacral, and coccygeal.
