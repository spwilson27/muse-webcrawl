Investment model

The investment model is a theoretical framework in social psychology, developed principally by Caryl Rusbult, that explains commitment in close relationships. Building on interdependence theory, it proposes that commitment to a relationship depends on three factors: satisfaction level, quality of alternatives, and investment size.

The three components

Satisfaction reflects the degree to which a relationship fulfils important needs, comparing outcomes against expectations. Quality of alternatives captures the perceived desirability of the best alternative — another partner, or being alone. Investments are the resources attached to the relationship that would be lost or diminished if it ended: time, emotional effort, shared memories, mutual friends, possessions and identity. Satisfaction increases commitment, alternatives decrease it, and greater investments increase it.

Evidence and extensions

Meta-analyses of many studies have found that the three variables predict commitment and relationship stability across romantic relationships, friendships and even jobs and brand loyalty. The model explains why people sometimes remain in dissatisfying relationships: poor alternatives and large investments can sustain commitment despite low satisfaction. Extensions have applied the framework to relationships with groups, organisations and places, and to decisions such as staying in abusive relationships, religious communities or academic programmes.
