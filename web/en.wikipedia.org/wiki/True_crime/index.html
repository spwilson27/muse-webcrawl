True crime

True crime is a nonfiction literary and media genre in which the author examines an actual crime and details the actions of real people associated with and affected by the event. It spans books, magazines, films, television series, documentaries and podcasts, and typically focuses on murder, though it also covers kidnapping, fraud and other offences.

History

The genre has roots in early modern crime pamphlets and "gallows literature", in volumes cataloguing notable criminals such as the Newgate Calendar, and in nineteenth-century sensation journalism. Truman Capote's In Cold Blood (1966) is widely credited with founding the modern genre by applying novelistic technique to the real 1959 Clutter family murders. Vincent Bugliosi's Helter Skelter (1974), about the Manson Family murders, and Ann Rule's The Stranger Beside Me (1980), about Ted Bundy, became landmark bestsellers.

Modern boom

Cable television channels devoted to true crime launched in the 1990s and 2000s. The 2014 podcast Serial, which re-examined the conviction of Adnan Syed, ignited a podcast and streaming boom, followed by acclaimed documentary series such as Making a Murderer (2015) and The Jinx (2015), in which productions sometimes influenced real investigations and court cases.

Audience and criticism

Audiences for true crime skew heavily female, and researchers have suggested motivations including empathy with victims, fascination with criminal psychology and a perceived sense of preparedness. Critics raise ethical questions about the exploitation of victims' families, sensationalism, "murderabilia", and the risk that coverage prejudices trials; some creators now consult victims' relatives and adopt more restrained approaches.
