Maestranza (Seville)

LocationSeville, Andalusia, Spain

Capacity12,000
OwnerReal Maestranza de Caballería

The Plaza de Toros de la Real Maestranza de Caballería de Sevilla, usually known simply as La Maestranza, is the oldest bullring in Spain still in regular use and one of the country's most celebrated arenas for bullfighting. It stands in the El Arenal district of Seville, near the Guadalquivir River.

History

Construction began in 1761 to replace an earlier rectangular wooden structure, making it one of the first bullrings built in a circular (actually polygonal) form. Work proceeded slowly and in stages for more than a century; the distinctive facade was completed in the 19th century and the ring itself was not finished until 1881. The arena is owned by the Real Maestranza de Caballería, a noblemen's cavalry guild founded in 1670.

Architecture and season

The building is noted for its late-Baroque facade painted in white and ochre, its irregular thirty-sided polygon shape and the Prince's Box (Palco del Príncipe). The bullfighting season centres on the Feria de Abril (April Fair), when the most prestigious corridas of the year are held, and the arena also houses a bullfighting museum.
