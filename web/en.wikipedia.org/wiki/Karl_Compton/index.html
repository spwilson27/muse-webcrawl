Karl Compton

Karl Taylor Compton (14 September 1887 – 22 June 1954) was an American physicist and the ninth president of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), serving from 1930 to 1948. He was the elder brother of physicist Arthur Compton and of Wilson Martindale Compton.

Career

Born in Wooster, Ohio, Compton studied at the College of Wooster and Princeton University, where he earned his PhD in physics in 1912 and later chaired the physics department. His research covered photoelectric effects, thermionic emission and spectroscopy. As president of MIT he refocused the institute on basic science and led its transformation into a central hub of wartime research during the Second World War.

Public service

Compton served on the National Defense Research Committee, as chief of the Office of Field Service of the OSRD, and as a scientific adviser attached to General MacArthur's staff in the Pacific. He was later a member of the Interim Committee on atomic energy and chairman of the Research and Development Board (1948–49). He received the Medal for Merit and numerous honorary degrees.
