Nearest-neighbor interpolation

Nearest-neighbor interpolation (also known as proximal interpolation) is a simple method of multivariate interpolation in one or more dimensions. The interpolated value at a point is taken to be the value of the nearest sample point, producing a piecewise-constant result.

Image scaling

In digital image processing, nearest-neighbor interpolation is the fastest method of resizing an image: each output pixel is assigned the colour of the closest input pixel. It introduces no new colours, which preserves hard edges and exact palettes — a property valued for pixel art — but produces blocky, aliased results when enlarging photographs, where bilinear or bicubic interpolation give smoother output.

Properties

The method is simple and fast and is the only interpolation method that never creates values outside the range of the samples. Its discontinuities at region boundaries, however, make it unsuitable where smooth gradients are required.
