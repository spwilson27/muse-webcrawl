List of national parks of China

China's protected area system historically included scenic areas, nature reserves and forest parks managed by different agencies. In 2015 China began piloting a unified national park system, and in October 2021 it formally established its first five national parks, covering about 230,000 km². The parks are administered by the National Forestry and Grassland Administration (also called the National Park Administration). China has announced plans for the world's largest national park system, with dozens of candidate areas covering roughly 10% of the country's land.

Pilot and candidate areas

The ten pilot areas designated in 2015–2017 included, in addition to the five established parks, the Great Wall, Qianjiangyuan (Zhejiang), Shennongjia (Hubei), Nanshan (Hunan) and Potala Palace area projects, some of which were folded into other protected-area schemes. Further candidate areas announced in 2022 include the Yellow River Estuary, Lishui, and regions in Xinjiang, Tibet and elsewhere, bringing the planned network to about 49 candidate parks.

Famous "national park" scenic areas

Before the unified system, several sites were popularly called national parks, including Zhangjiajie National Forest Park (China's first national forest park, 1982; its quartzite pillars inspired the floating mountains of the film Avatar), Jiuzhaigou, Huangshan, Mount Sanqing, Banff-like alpine scenery at Pudacuo in Yunnan (marketed as China's first "national park" in the IUCN sense in 2007), and sites such as Mount Taishan and the Three Parallel Rivers of Yunnan — many of which are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
