The Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan (BTC) pipeline is a 1,768-kilometre crude oil pipeline running from the Azeri–Chirag–Gunashli oil field in the Caspian Sea to the Mediterranean port of Ceyhan in Turkey, via Tbilisi in Georgia. At the time of its opening it was one of the longest oil pipelines in the world.

History and significance

The pipeline was officially inaugurated in 2006. It was the first pipeline to transport Caspian oil directly to world markets without crossing Russian territory, strategically important to the United States and Europe as an alternative energy corridor, and a significant source of revenue for Azerbaijan.

Operations

The pipeline is operated by BP on behalf of an international consortium. Oil is loaded onto tankers at the Ceyhan marine terminal. The pipeline passes through environmentally sensitive areas, including Georgia's Borjomi gorge, which drew criticism and additional safeguards. A section in Turkey was damaged by an explosion in 2008, temporarily halting flows.
