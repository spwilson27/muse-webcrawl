The Chiemgauer is a regional complementary currency used in the Chiemgau region of Upper Bavaria, Germany, around the towns of Prien am Chiemsee, Traunstein, and Rosenheim. Launched in 2003, it is one of the best-known and most successful examples of a Regiogeld (regional money) scheme, designed to encourage local spending, support small businesses and non-profit associations, and keep money circulating within the regional economy rather than leaking away to national and multinational chains.

How it works

Consumers exchange euros for Chiemgauer at a one-to-one rate through participating outlets and associations. The notes are accepted by hundreds of local businesses. When a business converts Chiemgauer back into euros, a fee of about 5% is charged; a portion of this supports local non-profit projects chosen by the consumers who exchanged the money, and the remainder funds administration of the scheme.

The Chiemgauer incorporates a demurrage mechanism inspired by the theories of Silvio Gesell: paper notes lose value (about 2% per quarter) unless a renewal stamp is purchased, encouraging holders to spend rather than hoard the currency and thereby increasing its velocity of circulation. An electronic version, the e-Chiemgauer, is managed through accounts with the regional co-operative banking network.

History

The currency was founded in 2003 by Christian Gelleri, a teacher at the Waldorf school in Prien, together with his students, as a practical school project in economics. It grew from a classroom experiment into a network of hundreds of businesses and thousands of users, with annual turnover in the millions of euros, and has been widely cited in studies of complementary currencies and local economic development.
