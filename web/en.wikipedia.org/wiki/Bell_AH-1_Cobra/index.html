Bell AH-1 Cobra

The Bell AH-1 Cobra is a single-engined attack helicopter developed and manufactured by the American rotorcraft manufacturer Bell Helicopter. It was the first dedicated attack helicopter to enter service and became one of the most widely recognized combat helicopters of the Vietnam War era. A member of the prolific Huey family, the AH-1 shares a common engine, transmission and rotor system with the Bell UH-1 Iroquois.

Development

During the early 1960s, the United States Army sought an interim gunship for service in Vietnam while the larger Lockheed AH-56 Cheyenne was in development. Bell proposed the Model 209, derived from its UH-1C Huey, with a slim tandem-seat fuselage, stub wings for weapons, and a chin-mounted turret. The prototype first flew on 7 September 1965, and the type was ordered into production as the AH-1G HueyCobra.

Design

The Cobra seated a pilot and gunner in tandem under a narrow armored canopy. Armament included a chin turret that could mount miniguns and grenade launchers, while stub wings carried rocket pods and, on later variants, anti-tank missiles. The single Lycoming T53 turboshaft engine gave early models a maximum speed of about 277 km/h (172 mph).

Operational history

The AH-1 arrived in Vietnam in 1967 and quickly became the Army's principal attack helicopter, flying escort, direct fire support and armed reconnaissance missions. Upgraded twin-engined variants served with the U.S. Marine Corps, and the family ultimately evolved into the AH-1Z Viper. Export Cobras saw service with many countries, including in the Iran–Iraq War.

Variants

Major variants include the AH-1G HueyCobra, the AH-1Q and AH-1S with TOW anti-tank capability, the twin-engined AH-1J and AH-1W SuperCobra for the Marine Corps, and the four-bladed AH-1Z Viper.
