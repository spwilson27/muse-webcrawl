Spunbond


Spunbond fabric coming off a production line.

Spunbond is a method of producing nonwoven fabrics in which a molten polymer is extruded through spinnerets to form continuous filaments, which are drawn, laid randomly on a moving conveyor belt to form a web, and then bonded — most commonly by heated calender rolls that fuse the filaments at their crossing points. The resulting fabric combines strength with light weight and low cost.

Materials and variants

Polypropylene is the most common spunbond polymer; polyester and polyamide are also used. Spunbond webs are frequently combined with meltblown layers to form SMS (spunbond–meltblown–spunbond) composites, in which the meltblown core provides barrier properties while the spunbond outer layers provide strength.

Applications

Spunbond fabrics are used in disposable medical and hygiene products (surgical gowns, masks, diaper covers), agriculture (crop-protection fleece), geotextiles, filtration, furniture and bedding interlinings, and packaging. Their disposability and durability have also made them a focus of debate about plastic waste.
