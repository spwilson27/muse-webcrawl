Fallujah (Arabic: الفلوجة, al-Fallūjah) is a city in Iraq's Al Anbar Governorate, located roughly 65 kilometers (40 mi) west of Baghdad on the Euphrates River. Known as the "city of mosques" for its many mosques and minarets, Fallujah has a long history as a trading post on the route between Mesopotamia and the Mediterranean.

History

The area near Fallujah was inhabited in Babylonian times; the nearby Jewish academy of Pumbedita flourished in the Talmudic era. Under the Ottoman Empire and the British Mandate the modern town grew around a bridge across the Euphrates, the scene of fighting in 1941 during the Anglo-Iraqi War.

Iraq War

Fallujah became internationally known during the Iraq War. In 2003, an incident in which American soldiers fired on protesters sparked unrest. In March 2004, four American private military contractors were killed and their bodies publicly desecrated, prompting the First Battle of Fallujah in April 2004. The Second Battle of Fallujah (Operation Phantom Fury), in November–December 2004, was among the heaviest urban combat fought by U.S. forces since the Vietnam War and left much of the city damaged.

Later years

In January 2014 the city fell to the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL), becoming one of the first Iraqi cities under its control. Iraqi government forces recaptured Fallujah in June 2016 after a major offensive. Reconstruction has proceeded slowly since, with residents gradually returning to the city.
