Space Shuttle Columbia disaster

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Columbia launches on STS-107, 16 January 2003.

The Space Shuttle Columbia disaster occurred on 1 February 2003, when the orbiter Columbia disintegrated as it re-entered the atmosphere over Texas at the end of mission STS-107, killing all seven crew members: Rick Husband, William McCool, Michael Anderson, Kalpana Chawla, David Brown, Laurel Clark and Ilan Ramon, the first Israeli astronaut.

The cause was damage during launch eighty-two seconds after liftoff, when a piece of insulating foam broke off the external tank and struck the leading edge of the left wing, punching a hole in its reinforced carbon-carbon heat shield. During re-entry superheated gas entered the wing, melting its internal structure until the orbiter broke apart. Engineers who reviewed launch imagery during the mission suspected damage but their requests for spy-satellite inspection were not acted on by management — a failure the Columbia Accident Investigation Board compared to the organisational causes of the Challenger disaster.

Aftermath

The Space Shuttle fleet was grounded for more than two years. The investigation led to on-orbit inspection and repair procedures, the stationing of a rescue shuttle for Hubble missions, and ultimately the decision to retire the shuttle after completing the International Space Station in 2011.
