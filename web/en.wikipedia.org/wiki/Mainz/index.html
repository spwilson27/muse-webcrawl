Mainz (German: [maɪnts]) is the capital and largest city of the German state of Rhineland-Palatinate. It lies on the west bank of the Rhine, opposite Wiesbaden and the confluence with the River Main. Founded by the Romans as the fortress of Mogontiacum, Mainz is famous as the home of Johannes Gutenberg, inventor of the movable-type printing press, and for its thousand-year-old cathedral and exuberant carnival.

History

The Roman general Drusus established the legionary camp of Mogontiacum in 13/12 BC; it guarded the Rhine frontier for four centuries. In the Middle Ages Mainz became one of the most powerful archbishoprics of the Holy Roman Empire — its archbishop was an imperial elector and Archchancellor of Germany — and a leading centre of Jewish scholarship, part of the "ShUM cities" with Speyer and Worms. Around 1450 Gutenberg developed his printing system here, producing the Gutenberg Bible and launching the European printing revolution. Mainz was besieged and fought over repeatedly in the revolutionary wars of the 1790s, and suffered heavy bombing in 1945, including the February 1945 raid that destroyed much of the old town.

Sights

  • Mainz Cathedral (St. Martin's), founded in 975 under Archbishop Willigis, a masterpiece of Romanesque architecture in red sandstone.
  • The Gutenberg Museum, housing two original Gutenberg Bibles.
  • St. Stephen's Church, with stained-glass windows by Marc Chagall.
  • The Roman-Germanic Central Museum and the remains of the Roman theatre and aqueduct.

Culture

Mainz is one of the strongholds of the Rhineland carnival (Fastnacht), televised nationwide, and a media city: the public broadcaster ZDF has its headquarters here. The city is also a gateway to the Rheinhessen wine region, Germany's largest wine-growing area.
