"Sing, Sing, Sing (with a Swing)" is a 1936 song written by Louis Prima, who first recorded it with the New Orleans Gang. It became one of the signature tunes of the swing era through the version by Benny Goodman and His Orchestra, whose driving arrangement — featuring Gene Krupa's tom-tom-heavy drumming — turned it into an anthem of big band jazz and one of the most recognisable swing recordings ever made.

Benny Goodman's band performing "Sing, Sing, Sing", with Gene Krupa on drums.

The Goodman recording

Goodman's band recorded the song on 6 July 1937 in an arrangement by Jimmy Mundy that famously interpolated Fletcher Henderson's arrangement of "Christopher Columbus". Because it ran far beyond a normal side, it was issued on both sides of a 12-inch 78 rpm record. Its centrepiece was Krupa's extended drum feature, built on a primitive, tom-tom-driven groove influenced by New Orleans rhythms — a showcase that elevated the drummer to co-star status and thrilled dancers.

The song's most celebrated performance came at Goodman's historic Carnegie Hall concert on 16 January 1938, the first jazz concert at that venue, where an extended version with solos by Krupa, trumpeter Harry James and Goodman himself became a landmark of recorded jazz. The 1937 recording has been inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry, and "Sing, Sing, Sing" remains a standard for swing bands and a staple of film soundtracks.
