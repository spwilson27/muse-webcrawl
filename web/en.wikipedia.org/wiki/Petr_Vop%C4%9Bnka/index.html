Petr Vopěnka

Petr Vopěnka (16 March 1935 – 20 March 2015) was a Czech mathematician and philosopher of mathematics, known for developing the Alternative Set Theory (AST) and for the large-cardinal notion of the Vopěnka cardinal and Vopěnka's principle, named after him.

Life

Born in Prague, Vopěnka studied at Charles University, where he worked on set theory and topology. After the 1968 Warsaw Pact invasion and during the normalization era he was restricted politically because of his dissident views, but he continued his research. After the Velvet Revolution he served briefly as the Czechoslovak (Czech) Minister of Education in the early 1990s, and later taught at the University of West Bohemia in Plzeň.

Work

In his 1979 book Mathematics in the Alternative Set Theory, Vopěnka proposed AST as a framework in which infinite sets are treated phenomenologically as arising from finite but unSurveyably large collections, introducing concepts such as semisets. He also wrote philosophical works interpreting mathematics, geometry, and the notion of infinity, and became a prominent public intellectual in the Czech Republic.
