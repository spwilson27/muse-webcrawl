The American Basketball Association (ABA) was a major professional basketball league that operated from 1967 to 1976. Founded as a challenger to the established National Basketball Association (NBA), the ABA was known for its flamboyant style of play, its red, white and blue basketball, the three-point arc, and stars such as Julius Erving, Artis Gilmore, George Gervin and Moses Malone.

History

The league was founded in 1967 by a group of businessmen, with former Minneapolis Lakers star George Mikan as its first commissioner. The ABA introduced innovations including the three-point field goal line, a slam dunk contest, and a faster, more wide-open style of play. Its most successful franchise was the Indiana Pacers, who won three championships, while the New York Nets, led by Julius "Dr. J" Erving, won two.

ABA–NBA merger

Years of financial strain led to the ABA–NBA merger in 1976. Four ABA teams — the Denver Nuggets, Indiana Pacers, New York Nets and San Antonio Spurs — joined the NBA, while the remaining teams folded. The merger brought the ABA's three-point line into the NBA in 1979 and helped usher in a more entertaining era of professional basketball.

Legacy

Though it survived only nine seasons, the ABA is remembered for transforming professional basketball with its emphasis on athleticism, showmanship and long-range shooting. A later minor league, founded in 1999, revived the ABA name but has no direct connection to the original league.
