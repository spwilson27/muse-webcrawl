Leyland National

The Leyland National is an integrally constructed single-decker bus built by Leyland between 1972 and 1985 at a purpose-built factory at Lillyhall, Workington, in Cumbria. A joint venture between British Leyland and the state-owned National Bus Company, it was intended as a standardised, mass-produced "bus for Britain" that would replace the diverse fleets of the nationalised industry with a single modern design.

Design

The National was advanced for its time: a unitary steel structure built on a production line using aircraft-style methods, with a rear-mounted horizontal engine under the floor at the back, power steering, and an integral heating system whose roof-mounted pod at the rear became its visual signature. Standard lengths were 10.3 and 11.3 metres. Its fixed-head Leyland 510 engine, however, gained a reputation for noise, thirst, and unreliability, and many operators later re-engined their fleets with Gardner or Leyland L11 units.

Production and service

More than 7,000 Nationals were built. They became the default town bus across England and Wales in National Bus Company fleets — famously in poppy red and leaf green liveries — and were also bought by London Transport (as the LS class), Scottish operators, municipal fleets, and export customers as far afield as Australia and Jamaica. Production ended in 1985 as British Leyland's bus business contracted; the model was succeeded by the Leyland Lynx. Many Nationals enjoyed long second lives with independent operators, and survivors are popular in preservation.

Assessment

Historians of the bus industry are divided: the National's build quality and standardization were landmark achievements, but its inflexibility, thirsty engine, and the enormous investment in a single model at a time of falling bus ridership helped seal the decline of Leyland's bus division.
