The Institut de France is a French learned society grouping five academies, created in 1795 during the French Revolution to bring together the nation's scientific, literary, and artistic elites. It is housed in the Palais de l'Institut on the Quai de Conti in Paris, the former Collège des Quatre-Nations, whose domed silhouette faces the Louvre across the Seine.

The five academies

The Institut comprises the Académie française (founded 1635, guardian of the French language), the Académie des inscriptions et belles-lettres (1663, humanities and antiquities), the Académie des sciences (1666), the Académie des beaux-arts (1816, from older art academies), and the Académie des sciences morales et politiques (1832). Members — the academicians, popularly called les immortels in the French Academy — are elected for life by their peers.

Role

The Institut administers prizes, foundations, museums and historic properties — including the Château de Chantilly and the Villa Medici in Rome — and advises the French state. Its grand prize ceremonies under the dome (sous la Coupole) are fixtures of French intellectual life.
