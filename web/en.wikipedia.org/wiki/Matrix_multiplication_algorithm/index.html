Matrix multiplication algorithm

Because matrix multiplication is such a central operation in many fields, various algorithms have been developed to multiply matrices efficiently. The naive algorithm, following the definition, multiplies two n×n matrices in Θ(n3) arithmetic operations. Faster algorithms reduce the exponent ω of matrix multiplication, defined as the infimum of exponents k such that two n×n matrices can be multiplied in O(nk) operations.

Strassen's algorithm

In 1969, Volker Strassen published the first algorithm faster than O(n3), achieving approximately O(n2.807) operations by using seven multiplications instead of eight when recursively multiplying 2×2 block matrices. Strassen's algorithm is of practical value for moderately large matrices.

Coppersmith–Winograd and later algorithms

The Coppersmith–Winograd algorithm (1990) achieved O(n2.376). Subsequent improvements by Stothers, Vassilevska Williams, Le Gall, and others brought the exponent down progressively; in the 2020s, work by Duan, Wu, Zhou, and by Alman, Duan, Vassilevska Williams, Xu, Xu, and Zhou reduced ω below 2.372. These "galactic" algorithms have large hidden constants and are not used in practice.

Practical considerations

In practice, cache-efficient blocking, the BLAS libraries, and hardware acceleration (such as GPUs and tensor cores) dominate real-world performance. Algorithms also exploit sparsity, structure, and parallelism. For rectangular matrices and chains of multiplications, the order of operations (matrix chain multiplication) further affects cost.
