First-person (video games)

In video games, first-person refers to a graphical perspective rendered from the viewpoint of the player character. The first-person perspective is used in a variety of genres, most famously the first-person shooter (FPS), but also in adventure games, racing games, and role-playing games. In contrast to a third-person perspective, in which the player character is visible on screen, the first-person view shows the game world as though through the character's own eyes, often displaying only the character's hands or weapon.

History

Early first-person games included the mainframe games Maze War (1973) and Spasim (1974). The perspective was popularized in the 1990s by id Software's Wolfenstein 3D (1992) and Doom (1993), which established the first-person shooter as a major genre. Later titles such as GoldenEye 007 (1997), Half-Life (1998) and Halo: Combat Evolved (2001) refined the perspective on consoles.

Genres

  • First-person shooters
  • First-person adventure and "walking simulator" games such as Dear Esther and Gone Home
  • Immersive sims such as Deus Ex and Dishonored
  • Racing and flight simulators with cockpit views
  • Virtual reality games, which almost universally use first-person views
