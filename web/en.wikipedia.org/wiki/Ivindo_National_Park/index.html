Ivindo National Park

Ivindo National Park is a national park of north-eastern Gabon in Central Africa, established in 2002. It protects some 3,000 km² of largely pristine rainforest in the Congo Basin and is renowned for its waterfalls, forest elephants, and western lowland gorillas. In 2021 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Geography and wildlife

The park straddles the equator and is drained by the Ivindo River, a major tributary of the Ogooué. Its dramatic Kongou Falls and Mingouli Falls rank among Central Africa's most spectacular waterfalls. The forest harbors forest elephants, western lowland gorillas, chimpanzees, sitatunga, African golden cats, and over 430 bird species, including the grey-necked rockfowl. Clearings (bais) such as Langoué Baï concentrate wildlife and are key research and ecotourism sites.

Conservation

Ivindo was one of thirteen national parks created by President Omar Bongo in 2002 covering about 10% of Gabon's territory. The park is threatened by illegal logging, poaching for bushmeat and ivory, and proposed infrastructure projects such as the Kongou Falls hydroelectric dam.
