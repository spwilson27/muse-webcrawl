Palearctic

The Palearctic (or Palaearctic) is the largest of Earth's eight biogeographic realms. It stretches across all of Eurasia north of the Himalayas and the Arabian Peninsula, together with North Africa down to the Sahara. Together with the Nearctic realm of North America, it forms the Holarctic, the largest realm complex on the planet.

Regions and fauna

The realm includes the Euro-Siberian region, the Mediterranean Basin, the deserts of Central Asia and Arabia, and the Sino-Japanese region. Its fauna was heavily shaped by the Pleistocene ice ages; characteristic mammals include red deer, brown bears, wolves, reindeer, and saiga antelope. Many Palearctic species are shared with the Nearctic across the Bering land bridge, and numerous Palearctic birds migrate south to Africa and South Asia for the winter.

Ecoregions

Major biomes range from Arctic tundra and boreal taiga through temperate broadleaf forest and steppe to Mediterranean scrub and hot desert.
