The name River Calder is borne by several rivers in the United Kingdom. The best known is the Calder in West Yorkshire, England, a major tributary of the River Aire; other Rivers Calder flow in Lancashire, Greater Manchester, Cumbria and Renfrewshire, Scotland.

West Yorkshire

The West Yorkshire Calder rises on the Pennine moors near Todmorden, close to the Lancashire border, and flows east for about 72 km (45 mi) through Hebden Bridge, Sowerby Bridge, Halifax (via its tributary the Hebble), Brighouse, Mirfield, Dewsbury and Wakefield before joining the River Aire at Castleford. Historically the river powered the textile mills of the Calder Valley and suffered severe industrial pollution; water quality has improved markedly since the late 20th century and salmon have returned. The lower river is navigable as part of the Calder and Hebble Navigation, a canalised waterway opened in stages from 1761 that connected Wakefield and Halifax with the Aire and Calder Navigation.

The valley was badly affected by flooding on Boxing Day 2015, when record water levels inundated thousands of homes and businesses in Mytholmroyd, Hebden Bridge and elsewhere.

Other Rivers Calder

  • The Lancashire Calder rises near Cliviger and flows through Burnley and Padiham to join the River Ribble near Great Mitton, passing beneath the Singing Ringing Tree panopticon hills.
  • The Greater Manchester Calder is a short tributary of the River Irwell near Middleton and Chadderton.
  • The Cumbrian Calder rises on Lank Rigg and flows past Calder Bridge and Sellafield to the Irish Sea, notable as a salmon river.
  • The Scottish Calder (Calder Water) in South Lanarkshire flows through East Kilbride to join the River Clyde near Bothwell.

Etymology

The name is generally thought to derive from Brittonic elements meaning "hard or violent water", a name shared with the numerous Rivers Calder and Caldew in northern Britain.
