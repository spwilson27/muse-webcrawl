The Derby du Rhône, also known as the Derby Rhône-Alpes, is a football rivalry between Olympique Lyonnais (Lyon) and AS Saint-Étienne, two of the most successful clubs in French football. The cities of Lyon and Saint-Étienne lie roughly 60 kilometres apart in the Rhône-Alpes region, and the fixture is considered one of the fiercest derbies in France.

History

The rivalry reflects a social contrast long drawn between Lyon, perceived as the prosperous bourgeois city, and Saint-Étienne, the industrial mining city of the working class — symbolised by the clubs' colours and by Saint-Étienne's nickname Les Verts (the Greens). Saint-Étienne dominated French football in the 1960s and 1970s, winning ten league titles; Lyon's era of dominance came in the 2000s, when the club won seven consecutive Ligue 1 titles from 2002 to 2008.

The first official meeting took place in 1951. Matches are marked by elaborate tifos, intense atmospheres and, at times, crowd trouble that has led to travelling-fan bans. A famous episode came in 2017, when Lyon captain Nabil Fekir celebrated a goal in the derby at Saint-Étienne by holding up his shirt to the home crowd, sparking a pitch invasion.

Honours comparison

Saint-Étienne's ten league titles long stood as a French record, while Lyon's seven straight titles set a record for consecutive championships. Both clubs count multiple Coupe de France wins; Saint-Étienne also reached the 1976 European Cup Final, losing to Bayern Munich in Glasgow — a match remembered for shots hitting the square wooden goalposts.
