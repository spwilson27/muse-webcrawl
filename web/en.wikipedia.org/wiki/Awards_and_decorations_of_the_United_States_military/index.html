Awards and decorations of the United States military

The awards and decorations of the United States military recognise service and personal accomplishments of members of the US Armed Forces. Together with military badges, they form a system of honours worn on uniforms, governed by an official order of precedence within each service.

Order of precedence

The highest award is the Medal of Honor, presented by the President in the name of Congress for conspicuous gallantry above and beyond the call of duty. Below it rank the service crosses — the Distinguished Service Cross (Army), Navy Cross and Air Force Cross — then the Distinguished Service Medals, the Silver Star, the Legion of Merit, the Distinguished Flying Cross and the Bronze Star. The Purple Heart is awarded to those wounded or killed by enemy action.

Beyond individual decorations, the system includes campaign and service medals (such as the Iraq and Afghanistan Campaign Medals), unit awards cited for collective gallantry or service, marksmanship awards, and ribbons for training and good conduct. Repeat awards are denoted by devices worn on the ribbon, including oak leaf clusters and service stars. Badges — such as the Combat Infantryman Badge and aviator wings — are related but distinct from decorations and are worn according to separate rules.
