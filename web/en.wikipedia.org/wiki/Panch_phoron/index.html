Panch phoron

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Panch phoron (Bengali: পাঁচ ফোড়ন, "five spices"; also spelled panch phoran) is a whole-spice blend used in the cuisines of eastern South Asia, especially Bengali, Assamese, and Odia cooking. It consists of equal parts of five seeds: fenugreek (methi), nigella (kalonji), cumin (jeera), black mustard (sarson), and fennel (saunf).

Use

Unlike ground spice mixes such as garam masala, panch phoron is almost always used whole and is tempered at the start of cooking: the seeds are fried briefly in hot mustard oil or ghee until they sputter and turn fragrant — a technique called phoron or tempering — and the flavoured oil becomes the base for vegetable dishes, dals, fish curries, and pickles. The blend is also sprinkled into the dough of some breads and snacks. Because fenugreek becomes bitter if over-fried, cooks watch the tempering carefully.

Characteristics

The flavour profile combines the pungency of mustard, the earthiness of cumin, the sweetness of fennel, the oniony note of nigella, and the slight bitterness of fenugreek. Proportions are traditionally equal, though regional and household variations exist; in Odisha the blend seasons the staple vegetable medleys and lentil dishes served in temple and home cooking alike.
