Belaying

Belaying
ActivityRock climbing, mountaineering
EquipmentBelay device, climbing rope, harness, locking carabiner

Belaying is a technique used in climbing in which a climber's rope is controlled by a partner (the belayer) so that a fall can be arrested. The belayer feeds rope out as the climber ascends, takes in slack, and locks off the rope using a belay device attached to their harness if the climber falls or needs to rest.

Technique

The belayer maintains a "brake hand" on the rope at all times; standard communication calls such as "on belay", "climbing", "take", and "slack" coordinate the pair. Belaying may be done from below (top-rope or lead climbing) or from an anchor above on multi-pitch routes. Devices range from simple plates and tubular devices such as the ATC to assisted-braking devices such as the Petzl GriGri.

Safety

Because the climber's safety depends entirely on the belayer, attentiveness is paramount: the belayer must manage slack carefully — too much increases fall distance, too little impedes the climber — and is usually anchored when belaying from the ground in exposed situations or when there is a large weight difference. See also abseiling and lead climbing.
