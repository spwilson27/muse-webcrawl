Document delivery

Document delivery is a service provided by libraries and commercial suppliers to supply copies of documents, such as journal articles, book chapters, conference papers and reports, to users who cannot access the original material locally.

Document delivery is closely related to interlibrary loan, but typically involves supplying a copy (photocopy or digital scan) which the user may keep, rather than lending the physical item. Services may be provided free of charge within reciprocal library networks, or for a fee by commercial providers.

Methods

Historically documents were delivered by post or fax. Since the 1990s electronic delivery via email, web download, and systems such as Ariel, Odyssey, and RapidILL has become standard. Copyright law in many jurisdictions permits limited copying for research or private study under fair use or fair dealing provisions, often administered through collective licensing.
