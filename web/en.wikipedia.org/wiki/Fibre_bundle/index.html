Fibre bundle

In mathematics, and particularly topology, a fibre bundle (Commonwealth English: fiber bundle, American English) is a space that is locally a product space, but globally may have a different topological structure. Specifically, the similarity between a space E and a product space B × F is defined using a continuous surjective map, the projection or submersion, π : E → B, that in small regions of E behaves just like a projection from corresponding regions of B × F to B.

Definition

A fibre bundle consists of a total space E, a base space B, a fibre F, and a projection π : E → B such that every point of B has a neighbourhood U over which π−1(U) is homeomorphic to U × F, with the homeomorphism commuting with the projection. The Möbius strip is a classic example: it is a nontrivial line bundle over the circle, locally indistinguishable from the cylinder but globally twisted.

Examples

Trivial bundles are plain products. Important examples include the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold, vector bundles in general, covering spaces (bundles with discrete fibre), the Hopf fibration of the 3-sphere over the 2-sphere with circle fibres, and principal bundles, in which the fibre is a Lie group acting on itself. Associated bundles and fibre bundles with structure groups provide the geometric language for gauge theories in physics.

Sections

A section of a bundle is a continuous choice of a point in each fibre; the existence and classification of sections is a central topic of algebraic topology, obstructed by characteristic classes such as the Euler class and Chern classes.
