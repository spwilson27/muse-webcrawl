Maria bint Shamʿun, better known as Maria al-Qibtiyya (Arabic: مارية القبطية), also called Maria the Copt, was an Egyptian Christian woman sent, along with her sister Sirin, as a gift from the Byzantine governor of Egypt, al-Muqawqis, to the Islamic prophet Muhammad in 628 CE. She became the mother of Muhammad's son Ibrahim, who died in infancy. Her status — whether wife or concubine — has been a subject of scholarly and traditional debate.

Life

In 628 CE (6–7 AH), Muhammad sent letters inviting neighbouring rulers to Islam. According to tradition, al-Muqawqis, the ruler of Egypt, responded with gifts, including two Coptic slave girls, Maria and her sister Sirin, a eunuch named Mabur, and various goods. On the journey to Medina, Maria and Sirin were invited to Islam and accepted. Muhammad gave Sirin to the poet Hassan ibn Thabit, and kept Maria in his household.

Ibrahim

Maria gave birth to Muhammad's son Ibrahim in 630 CE. The boy's birth raised her status considerably; according to tradition, Muhammad said that her son had set her free. Ibrahim died at about sixteen to eighteen months old in 632 CE, shortly before Muhammad's own death, causing him great grief. An eclipse of the sun on the day of Ibrahim's death was popularly connected with the event, which Muhammad is reported to have rejected, saying the sun and moon do not eclipse for the death of anyone.

Status debate

Sunni tradition is divided: some sources treat Maria as a concubine (umm walad, a slave who has borne her master's child and is freed on his death), while others count her among the wives of Muhammad. Shia tradition generally holds she was a concubine. The verses associated with the "incident of the honey" or the domestic dispute in the Quran (Surah At-Tahrim) are connected in some traditions with Maria.

Death

Maria died in 637 CE, during the caliphate of Umar, some five years after Muhammad. Umar himself led the funeral prayer, and she was buried in al-Baqi cemetery in Medina.
