Psychrometric constant

The psychrometric constant, denoted γ (gamma), relates the partial pressure of water vapour in air to the wet-bulb temperature depression measured by a psychrometer. It appears in the psychrometric equation used to compute actual vapour pressure from dry-bulb and wet-bulb thermometer readings.

Definition

The constant is defined as γ = (cp · P) / (ε · λ), where cp is the specific heat of moist air at constant pressure, P is atmospheric pressure, ε is the ratio of the molecular weights of water vapour to dry air (approximately 0.622), and λ is the latent heat of vaporization of water. At sea-level pressure its value is approximately 0.066 kPa per °C.

Applications

The psychrometric constant is fundamental in meteorology, agricultural science, and engineering. It appears in the Penman and Penman–Monteith equations for estimating evaporation and evapotranspiration, and in calculations involving Bowen ratio energy balance measurements, where the ratio of sensible to latent heat flux equals γ multiplied by the ratio of temperature and humidity gradients.
