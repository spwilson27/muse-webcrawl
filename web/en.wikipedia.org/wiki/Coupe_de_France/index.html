Coupe de France

The Coupe de France, also known in English as the French Cup, is the premier knockout cup competition in French football, organised by the French Football Federation (FFF). Founded in 1917 as the Coupe Charles Simon, it is open to all amateur and professional clubs in France and its overseas territories, regularly attracting more than 7,000 entrants.

History

The competition was created during the First World War and named after Charles Simon, a sports administrator killed in the conflict. Olympique de Pantin won the first edition in 1918. Uniquely, clubs from France's overseas departments and territories — such as Réunion, Guadeloupe, Martinique and New Caledonia — take part, sometimes travelling thousands of kilometres for ties.

Format

The cup is a single-elimination tournament. Lower-division and overseas clubs enter in the early regional rounds; Ligue 1 clubs join in the round of 64. Matches are played at the ground of the lower-division team when the sides are two divisions apart, a rule that has produced famous giant-killings, such as Calais RUFC, an amateur side that reached the final in 2000. The final is traditionally held at the Stade de France and attended by the President of France, who presents the trophy.

Records

Paris Saint-Germain is the most successful club in the competition's history, followed by Olympique de Marseille and AS Saint-Étienne. The winner qualifies for the UEFA Europa League and the Trophée des Champions.
