Earth Day

Earth Day is an annual event on April 22 to demonstrate support for environmental protection. First held on April 22, 1970, it now includes a wide range of events coordinated globally through earthday.org (formerly Earth Day Network) including 1 billion people in more than 193 countries. The official theme for 2024 was "Planet vs. Plastics".

History

Earth Day was founded by United States senator Gaylord Nelson of Wisconsin, who was inspired by the student anti-war movement and by witnessing the damage of the 1969 Santa Barbara oil spill. Nelson proposed a national "teach-in" on the environment, and recruited activist Denis Hayes as national coordinator. On April 22, 1970, roughly 20 million Americans — about 10% of the US population at the time — took part in rallies, marches, and teach-ins across the country.

The first Earth Day helped create political momentum that led to the creation of the United States Environmental Protection Agency and the passage of landmark legislation, including the Clean Air Act, the Clean Water Act, and the Endangered Species Act. In 1990, Earth Day went global, mobilising 200 million people in 141 countries. Earth Day 2000 combined the ambitions of the first event with modern internet organising, and on Earth Day 2016 the Paris Agreement on climate change was opened for signature.

Observance

Activities include cleanups, tree planting, rallies, school programs, and civic actions coordinated by governments, businesses, and civil society. Earthday.org selects an annual theme and runs year-round campaigns on issues such as plastic pollution, reforestation (the Canopy Project), climate literacy, and voting.
