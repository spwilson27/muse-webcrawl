Alberto Granado (8 August 1922 – 5 March 2011) was an Argentine-Cuban biochemist, doctor and writer, best known as the travelling companion of Ernesto "Che" Guevara on their 1952 motorcycle journey through South America, an experience recorded in his memoir Travelling with Che Guevara: The Making of a Revolutionary.

Early life

Granado was born in Hernando, in the province of Córdoba, Argentina. He studied biochemistry, pharmacology and natural sciences at the University of Córdoba and became interested in the treatment of leprosy, working at a leprosarium. In Córdoba he befriended the younger Ernesto Guevara, a medical student and fellow rugby player.

The motorcycle journey

In December 1951 Granado and Guevara set out from Buenos Aires aboard Granado's 1939 Norton 500cc motorcycle, nicknamed La Poderosa II ("The Powerful One II"), intending to ride the length of South America. After the machine broke down beyond repair, they continued by hitchhiking, raft and on foot through Chile, Peru — where they worked at the San Pablo leper colony on the Amazon — Colombia and Venezuela. The journey, during which the pair witnessed deep poverty and social injustice, was later chronicled in Guevara's The Motorcycle Diaries and in Granado's own account; it was dramatized in the 2004 film The Motorcycle Diaries, on which Granado served as an adviser.

Life in Cuba

Granado remained for a time in Venezuela, working at a leprosarium, before being invited by Guevara to join the new revolutionary Cuba. He moved to Havana in 1961, taught biochemistry at the University of Havana, helped found a medical school in Santiago de Cuba and worked at the National Institute of Animal and Plant Health.

Death

Granado died of natural causes in Havana on 5 March 2011, aged 88. His ashes were scattered in Cuba, Argentina and Venezuela, according to his wishes.
