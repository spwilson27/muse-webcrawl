Wind power in the United States

Wind power in the United States is a branch of the energy industry that has expanded rapidly in recent years. The United States is one of the world's largest producers of wind electricity, with total installed capacity exceeding 140 gigawatts, enough to supply more than ten percent of the nation's electricity in recent years and over a third of generation in states such as Iowa, Kansas, Oklahoma, and South Dakota.

Capacity and generation

Texas has the largest wind capacity of any state, followed by Iowa, Oklahoma, and Kansas. The wind corridor of the Great Plains, from North Dakota to Texas, benefits from strong, consistent winds and relatively low population density. Offshore wind development began with the Block Island Wind Farm in 2016 and has since expanded with larger projects off the Atlantic coast, including Vineyard Wind in Massachusetts.

Policy

Federal support has primarily come through the Production Tax Credit (PTC), first enacted in 1992, which provides a per-kilowatt-hour tax credit for the first ten years of a project's operation. Many states have additionally adopted renewable portfolio standards requiring utilities to source a share of their electricity from renewables.

Industry

Major turbine manufacturers operating in the U.S. market include GE Vernova, Vestas, and Siemens Gamesa. Leading project developers include NextEra Energy Resources. The industry employs well over 100,000 workers nationwide.
