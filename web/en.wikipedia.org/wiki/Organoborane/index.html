Organoborane

An organoborane or organoboron compound is a chemical compound of boron and carbon, of the general formula BRnH3−n or related species in which boron is bonded to organic groups. Organoboron compounds are important reagents in organic chemistry, most famously enabling the hydroboration reaction discovered by Herbert C. Brown, who received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1979 for his work with organoboranes.

Triethylborane, a simple organoborane.

Structure and bonding

Boron in organoboranes is typically trivalent and sp2-hybridized, with a vacant p orbital that makes the compounds Lewis acidic and susceptible to attack by nucleophiles and oxidation by air. Many simple trialkylboranes are pyrophoric. Boronic acids (RB(OH)2) and boronate esters are more stable, tetrahedral boronate complexes being formed with bases.

Synthesis

The principal route is hydroboration: the addition of a B–H bond of borane (BH3) or a substituted borane across a carbon–carbon multiple bond. Bulky reagents such as 9-BBN and disiamylborane give high regioselectivity for the less substituted carbon (anti-Markovnikov selectivity). Other routes include transmetalation from organolithium or Grignard reagents to boron halides and esters.

Reactions

Organoboranes undergo a rich reaction chemistry. Oxidation with hydrogen peroxide under basic conditions converts the C–B bond to a C–O bond with retention of configuration, giving alcohols — the second step of the hydroboration–oxidation sequence. Amination, halogenation, and the Zweifel olefination are also possible. Boronic acids participate in the palladium-catalysed Suzuki–Miyaura coupling, one of the most widely used carbon–carbon bond-forming reactions, for which Akira Suzuki shared the 2010 Nobel Prize.

Applications

Beyond synthesis, organoboron compounds are used in pharmaceuticals — the proteasome inhibitor bortezomib contains a boronic acid — in neutron capture therapy, in frustrated Lewis pair catalysis, and in materials such as boron-doped polymers and sensors for saccharides and fluoride.
