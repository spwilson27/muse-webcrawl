The Americano is an IBA official cocktail composed of Campari, sweet vermouth, and club soda. It is a gentle introduction to the bitter taste of Campari and is closely related to the Negroni, which replaces the soda water with gin.

History

The drink was first served in the 1860s at Gaspare Campari's bar in Milan, Italy. It was originally known as the "Milano-Torino" because of its ingredients: Campari, the bitter liqueur, is from Milan, and Punt e Mes, the vermouth, is from Turin. The cocktail was later renamed in honour of the American tourists who enjoyed it during the Prohibition era, when Americans travelled to Europe to drink legally.

In popular culture

The Americano is famously the first drink ordered by James Bond in Ian Fleming's first Bond novel, Casino Royale (1953), before he switches to the Vesper martini. It also appears in Fleming's From Russia, with Love.

Variations

Replacing the soda water with gin produces a Negroni. A "Negroni sbagliato" substitutes sparkling wine for the gin. Some bartenders serve the Americano in a highball glass topped up with more soda for a longer, lighter drink.
