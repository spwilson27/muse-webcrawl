The digamma function ψ(x) on the real line, with poles at 0, −1, −2, …

In mathematics, the digamma function is defined as the logarithmic derivative of the gamma function:

ψ(x) = d/dx ln Γ(x) = Γ′(x)/Γ(x).

It is the first of the polygamma functions. The digamma function, often denoted ψ0(x), ψ(0)(x) or Ϝ, is related to the harmonic numbers: ψ(n) = Hn−1 − γ, where Hn is the nth harmonic number and γ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant (≈ 0.57721).

Properties

The digamma function satisfies a recurrence ψ(x+1) = ψ(x) + 1/x, a reflection formula ψ(1−x) − ψ(x) = π cot(πx), and has simple poles at the non-positive integers with residue −1. Special values include ψ(1) = −γ and ψ(1/2) = −γ − 2 ln 2. Gauss's digamma theorem gives closed forms for ψ(p/q) at rational arguments in terms of elementary constants.

Appearances

Digamma functions arise in the evaluation of definite integrals and series, in number theory (for example in the theory of the Riemann zeta function), in statistics — as derivatives of log-normalising constants, e.g. for the Dirichlet and gamma distributions — and in physics, including quantum field theory and the statistical mechanics of disordered systems.
