The Dassault Falcon 900 is a large, long-range business jet manufactured by the French company Dassault Aviation. Like its predecessor the Falcon 50, it is a trijet, with three rear-mounted engines including one in the tail fed by an S-duct intake. The Falcon 900 has a wider, longer fuselage than the Falcon 50 and can carry up to 19 passengers, typically seating 8 to 14 in executive configuration.

The Falcon 900 is distinguished by its three rear-mounted engines.

Development and design

Announced in 1983, the Falcon 900 first flew on 21 September 1984 and entered service in 1986. Its composite-material wing and three Honeywell TFE731 turbofans gave it intercontinental range with the safety margin and short-field performance associated with trijets, including the ability to fly long overwater routes. Successive variants included the Falcon 900B with upgraded engines, the longer-range Falcon 900EX with TFE731-60 engines and increased fuel capacity, the 900EX EASy and 900LX with flight-deck and winglet improvements, and the Falcon 900LX featuring blended winglets and a range of about 4,750 nautical miles.

Operators

The type is flown by corporate operators worldwide and by government and military users, including the French Air and Space Force for VIP transport. More than 500 Falcon 900-series aircraft have been delivered.

Specifications (Falcon 900LX)

  • Crew: 2
  • Capacity: up to 19 passengers
  • Length: 20.21 m; wingspan: 21.38 m (with winglets)
  • Maximum speed: Mach 0.84
  • Range: approx. 8,800 km (4,750 nmi)
  • Service ceiling: 15,500 m (51,000 ft)
