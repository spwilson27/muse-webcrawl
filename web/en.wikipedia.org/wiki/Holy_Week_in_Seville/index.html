Holy Week in Seville

Holy Week in Seville (Spanish: Semana Santa en Sevilla) is one of the most famous and elaborate Holy Week celebrations in Spain, held in Seville, Andalusia, from Palm Sunday to Easter Sunday. It has been declared a Fiesta of International Tourist Interest.

Processions

Around sixty brotherhoods (hermandades and cofradías) carry ornate floats (pasos) bearing sculptures of Christ and the Virgin Mary through the streets to the cathedral. The floats are carried on the shoulders of costaleros hidden beneath them, accompanied by brass bands and by penitents called nazarenos wearing pointed hoods (capirotes). Particularly renowned processions take place in the early hours of Good Friday, the Madrugá, featuring images such as the Macarena and the Esperanza de Triana.

Traditions

The celebration includes the singing of saetas, impassioned flamenco-style laments addressed to the passing images, and draws hundreds of thousands of spectators each year.
