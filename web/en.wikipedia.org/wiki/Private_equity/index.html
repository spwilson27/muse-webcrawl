Private equity

[Image: the glass towers of a financial district housing private equity firms]

Private equity is Investment in companies that are not publicly traded, made through funds that raise committed capital from institutional investors and manage it for fees and a share of profits. The industry's core strategy is the leveraged buyout (LBO): buying a mature company largely with borrowed money secured on its own assets and cash flows, restructuring it, and selling or listing it years later at a profit.

Scale and debate

Giant firms such as Blackstone, KKR, Apollo and Carlyle — heirs to the 1980s buyout boom that culminated in the RJR Nabisco deal (1989) — now manage trillions across buyouts, growth equity, venture capital, real estate and private credit. Supporters argue private ownership aligns incentives, disciplines management and funds improvements without quarterly-market pressure; critics cite job losses, asset sales, debt burdens left on businesses such as retailers and care providers, and favourable tax treatment of 'carried interest'. Returns are reported net of high fees and compared, contentiously, with public-market alternatives.
