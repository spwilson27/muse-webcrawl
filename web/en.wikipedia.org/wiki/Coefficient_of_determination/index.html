Coefficient of determination

In statistics, the coefficient of determination, denoted R² or r² and pronounced "R squared", is the proportion of the variation in the dependent variable that is predictable from the independent variable(s). It is a statistic used in the context of statistical models whose main purpose is either the prediction of future outcomes or the testing of hypotheses, on the basis of related information.

Definition

For a set of observed values yi with fitted values ŷi and mean ȳ, R² is defined as

R² = 1 − SSres / SStot,

where SSres = Σ(yi − ŷi)² is the residual sum of squares and SStot = Σ(yi − ȳ)² is the total sum of squares. An R² of 1 indicates that the model explains all the variability of the response data around its mean, while an R² of 0 indicates that it explains none.

Interpretation

R² provides a measure of how well observed outcomes are replicated by the model, based on the proportion of total variation of outcomes explained by the model. It is commonly reported as a percentage; an R² of 0.70, for instance, is often read as "70% of the variance is explained". In simple linear regression, R² equals the square of the Pearson correlation coefficient between the observed and predicted values.

Caveats

R² does not indicate whether a model is adequate, whether the predictions are biased, or whether the chosen regressors are causally appropriate. Adding more independent variables to a model can never decrease R², which motivates the adjusted R², a modification that penalises additional predictors and can decrease when a new term does not improve the model enough. For models without an intercept, or fitted by methods other than ordinary least squares, R² can be negative and alternative definitions are used.

Generalizations

The concept extends to generalised linear models through pseudo-R² measures, to nonlinear regression with caution, and to measures such as the coefficient of determination for explained variation in analysis of variance, where it is closely related to the eta-squared statistic.
