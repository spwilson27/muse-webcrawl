Guanajuato
Capital of the state of Guanajuato, Mexico
Founded: 1559
UNESCO World Heritage Site (1988, with the adjacent mines)

Guanajuato is the capital city of the Mexican state of the same name. It stands in a narrow valley in central Mexico, surrounded by hills, with steep winding streets, alleys, and underground roadways built over a former river.

History

The city grew from a Spanish colonial mining settlement after rich silver deposits were discovered in the area in the 1550s; by the 18th century Guanajuato was one of the world's leading silver producers, anchored by the great Valenciana mine. Guanajuato played a key role in the Mexican War of Independence: in September 1810, Miguel Hidalgo's insurgent army captured the Alhóndiga de Granaditas granary, an early victory of the war. The city is the birthplace of artist Diego Rivera.

Culture

Guanajuato hosts the Festival Internacional Cervantino, one of Latin America's most important performing arts festivals, founded in 1972. Its historic town and adjacent mines were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1988. The Mummies of Guanajuato museum displays naturally mummified bodies exhumed from the municipal cemetery.
