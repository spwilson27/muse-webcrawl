Mittelburgenland (Central Burgenland) is the central region of the Austrian state of Burgenland, roughly corresponding to the district of Oberpullendorf. It borders Hungary to the east and is characterised by rolling hills lying between the foothills of the Alps and the Pannonian plain.

Wine region

Mittelburgenland is one of Austria's most important red wine regions and is nicknamed "Blaufränkischland" after its signature grape, Blaufränkisch. The DAC Mittelburgenland appellation covers full-bodied red wines, with notable wine villages including Deutschkreutz, Horitschon, Neckenmarkt and Lutzmannsburg. Heavy loam and clay soils and a sheltered, sunny Pannonian climate favour red grape varieties.

Population and culture

The region is home to much of Burgenland's Croatian-speaking minority, as well as Hungarian-speaking communities near the border. Settlements were historically shaped by fortified churches and castles defending the western borderlands of the Kingdom of Hungary.
