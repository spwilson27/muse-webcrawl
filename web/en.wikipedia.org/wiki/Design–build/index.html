Design–build

Design–build (or design/build, D–B) is a project delivery system used in the construction industry in which the design and construction services are contracted by a single entity known as the design–builder or design–build contractor. It relies on a single point of responsibility and is used to minimise risks for the project owner and to reduce the delivery schedule by overlapping the design phase and construction phase. Design–build contrasts with the traditional design–bid–build method, in which the owner contracts separately with a designer and a builder.

Overview

Historically, building was organised on the "master builder" model, under which one party took responsibility for both conception and execution; the separation of design and construction professions in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries displaced this with design–bid–build. Design–build revives the integrated model: the owner holds one contract, and the design–builder — which may be a contractor-led team, a designer-led team, an integrated firm or a joint venture — assembles architects, engineers and subcontractors under that single contract.

Advantages and criticisms

Supporters cite faster delivery through overlapping phases ("fast-tracking"), earlier certainty of cost, reduced adversarial relationships and litigation, and a single point of accountability. Studies by industry bodies have repeatedly found design–build projects to be delivered faster and with less cost growth than comparable design–bid–build projects. Critics argue that the owner loses the independent checking role of a designer acting as the owner's agent, that competitive design quality may suffer, and that the method requires an owner capable of defining its requirements clearly at the outset, since changes after contract are comparatively expensive. Variants include bridging (in which the owner takes design to a preliminary stage before tendering) and progressive design–build.

Use

Design–build has grown steadily in the United States and elsewhere since the 1980s, particularly for industrial facilities, transport infrastructure, water and wastewater plants and commercial buildings, and professional bodies such as the Design-Build Institute of America promote standard contract forms and best practice.
