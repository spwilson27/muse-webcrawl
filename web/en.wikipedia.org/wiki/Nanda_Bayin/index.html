Nanda Bayin (Burmese: နန္ဒဘုရင်, pronounced [nàɰ̃da̰ bəjɪ̀ɰ̃]; 9 November 1535 – 30 November 1600) was king of the Toungoo Empire from 1581 to 1599 and the penultimate emperor of the First Toungoo Empire, then the largest empire in the history of Southeast Asia. He inherited an empire stretching from Manipur and the Chinese Shan states to Lan Xang and Ayutthaya, assembled by his father Bayinnaung.

Reign

Nanda's reign was consumed by rebellion. Overconfident in the empire's vast resources, he launched five invasions of Siam between 1584 and 1593 to punish Ayutthaya's king Naresuan, who had declared independence. All failed disastrously: Burmese armies were repeatedly defeated, most decisively at the Battle of Nong Sarai in 1593, where the crown prince Mingyi Swa was killed in elephant combat with Naresuan.

The exhausting wars drained the manpower of Lower Burma, provoked mass flight of the population, and triggered revolts across the empire — in Ava, Prome, Toungoo, Lan Na, and Lan Xang. By 1597 most vassals had deserted, and in 1599 a combined force of Toungoo (his own father's home fief) and Arakan captured the capital Pegu (Bago). Nanda surrendered on 19 December 1599, and the great empire of Bayinnaung fragmented within months.

Captivity and death

Nanda was taken to Toungoo as a prisoner and was assassinated there on 30 November 1600 by Natshinnaung. The fall of Pegu, with its vast accumulated treasure looted and its population deported, is one of the classic examples in Southeast Asian history of imperial overextension.
