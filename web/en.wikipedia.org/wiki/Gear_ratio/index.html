Gear ratio

FieldMechanical engineering
Related conceptsMechanical advantage, torque, transmission

The gear ratio is the ratio of the rotations of a pair of meshing gears, or equivalently the ratio of the numbers of teeth on the two gears. If a driven (output) gear with N2 teeth is driven by a pinion with N1 teeth, the gear ratio is N2/N1, and the output gear turns that many times more slowly than the input. For example, a 40-tooth gear driven by a 10-tooth pinion has a gear ratio of 4:1, or 4.

gear ratio = Noutput / Ninput = ωinput / ωoutput

Mechanical advantage

Gearing trades speed for torque. A reduction ratio greater than 1 decreases output speed but multiplies output torque in (approximately) the same proportion, neglecting friction losses; this is the mechanical advantage of the gear pair. Conversely, an overdrive ratio less than 1 increases speed at the expense of torque.

Gear trains and transmissions

In a gear train with several meshes, the overall ratio is the product of the individual ratios; intermediate idler gears affect only the direction of rotation, not the ratio. Vehicle transmissions provide a set of selectable gear ratios — low ratios for starting and climbing, high ratios for efficient cruising — and a separate final drive ratio in the differential. Bicycle gearing is commonly expressed in "gear inches" or as a ratio of chainring to sprocket teeth.
