Sacroiliac joint

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The sacroiliac joint (abbreviated SI joint) is the joint between the sacrum and the ilium bones of the pelvis, which are connected by strong ligaments. In humans, the sacrum supports the spine and is supported in turn by each ilium. The joint is strong, supporting the entire weight of the upper body, and is a synovial plane joint with irregular elevations and depressions that produce interlocking of the two bones; movement is very limited, consisting of slight gliding and rotation.

Structure

There are two sacroiliac joints, one on each side. Their C-shaped articular surfaces are covered in cartilage and braced by the anterior, interosseous and posterior sacroiliac ligaments, among the strongest in the body. The joints transfer load between the spine and the lower limbs and act as shock absorbers for the pelvis.

Clinical significance

Dysfunction of the sacroiliac joint is an important cause of lower back and buttock pain; estimates attribute a significant share of chronic low back pain to it. Inflammation of the joint, sacroiliitis, is a hallmark of ankylosing spondylitis and other spondyloarthropathies and is graded on imaging in the modified New York criteria. The joint can also be affected by infection, trauma and the ligament laxity of pregnancy. Diagnosis relies on provocation tests and diagnostic injections; treatment ranges from physiotherapy and anti-inflammatory medication to radiofrequency ablation and, in refractory cases, surgical fusion.
