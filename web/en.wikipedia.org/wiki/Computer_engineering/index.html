Computer engineering

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Computer engineers design both the hardware and the low-level software of computing systems

Computer engineering (CoE or CpE) is a branch of engineering that integrates several fields of electrical engineering, electronics engineering, and computer science required to develop computer hardware and software. Computer engineers are involved in many hardware and software aspects of computing, from the design of individual microcontrollers, microprocessors, personal computers, and supercomputers, to circuit design and the writing of firmware and operating-system-level software.

Areas of work

Usual tasks involving computer engineers include designing processors and computer architecture; writing and testing software and firmware for embedded systems; designing digital circuits and mixed-signal integrated circuits (VLSI design); building computer networks and communications systems; robotics and control systems; and developing operating systems and the hardware/software interface. Two major focus areas are often distinguished:

  • Hardware engineering — logic design, microprocessor design, computer architecture, hardware description languages (such as Verilog and VHDL), and electronic design automation.
  • Software engineering (of computing systems) — operating systems, embedded and real-time software, compilers, and the engineering of dependable, efficient code close to the hardware.

Education

University computer engineering programs combine the core of electrical engineering (circuits, signals, electronics) with computer science subjects (data structures, algorithms, programming), joined by courses in digital logic, computer architecture, embedded systems, and a capstone design project. Many programs are accredited — in the United States, by ABET — and the field is served professionally by the IEEE Computer Society and ACM, which jointly publish curriculum guidelines for the discipline.

History

The discipline crystallized in the 1970s as universities began offering degrees distinct from both electrical engineering and computer science; among the first dedicated programs in the United States was that at Case Western Reserve University in 1971. Growth of the microprocessor industry and of embedded computing — processors hidden inside cars, appliances, aircraft, and industrial equipment — made computer engineering one of the largest engineering specialties, and its practitioners central to the design of the modern digital world.
