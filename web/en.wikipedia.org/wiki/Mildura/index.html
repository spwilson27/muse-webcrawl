Mildura

Mildura is a city in north-western Victoria, Australia, situated on the southern bank of the Murray River near its junction with the Darling River. It is the centre of the Sunraysia district, a major irrigated agricultural region known for dried fruit, citrus, wine grapes and table grapes.

History

The Mildura irrigation settlement was founded in 1887 by the Canadian-born engineers George and William Chaffey, who established one of Australia's first large-scale irrigation colonies. The region was previously home to the Latje Latje and related Aboriginal peoples. Mildura was proclaimed a city in 1934.

Geography and climate

Mildura has a semi-arid climate with hot summers and mild winters. The Murray River supports river tourism, houseboats and paddle-steamer cruises. The city is a transport hub for the surrounding fruit-growing region and a gateway to Mungo National Park across the river in New South Wales.

Economy

Agriculture dominates the local economy, particularly grapes, citrus, almonds and vegetables. Wine production in the Murray Darling region is significant, and the city hosts food and wine festivals.
