Trackball

A trackball is a pointing device consisting of a ball held in a socket containing sensors to detect its rotation; the user rolls the exposed ball with the fingers or palm while the device itself stays stationary. It functions like an upside-down ball mouse, whose mechanism it predates.

History

Trackballs were developed for military radar plotting systems in the 1940s–50s; the Canadian DATAR project (c. 1952) used a standard bowling ball as the rolling element. Trackballs became standard in air-traffic control, sonar, and CAD workstations, and later appeared in arcade games such as Atari's Centipede (1980) and in the Logitech TrackMan and Kensington Expert Mouse consumer lines.

Designs and uses

Modern trackballs are either finger-operated (a large central ball moved by the index and middle fingers) or thumb-operated (a smaller ball under the thumb), with optical sensors replacing mechanical rollers. They are valued where desk space is limited, for precision work, in industrial and marine environments, and by users who find them more comfortable than a mouse because the wrist and arm remain still.
