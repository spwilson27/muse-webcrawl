Chữ Quốc ngữ

Chữ Quốc ngữ (𡨸國語, "script of the national language") is the modern Latin-based writing system of the Vietnamese language. It uses the Latin alphabet augmented with diacritics to mark tones and vowel qualities, and is written with digraphs such as ph, th, kh, and ng.

History

The script was developed in the 17th century by Portuguese and Italian Catholic missionaries for evangelisation; the Jesuit Alexandre de Rhodes codified it in his 1651 Vietnamese–Portuguese–Latin dictionary. Under French colonial rule it was increasingly promoted in administration and education, and in the 20th century nationalist reformers embraced it as a tool for mass literacy. It is now the official and virtually universal script of Vietnam.

Structure

Vietnamese is tonal and monosyllabic in its native morphemes; chữ Quốc ngữ marks six tones with five diacritics (acute, grave, hook, tilde, dot below) plus unmarked mid tone, and modifies vowels with circumflex, breve, and horn marks to represent twelve vowel sounds. Literacy campaigns using the script raised Vietnamese literacy dramatically in the decades after 1945.
