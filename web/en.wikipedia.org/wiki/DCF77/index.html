DCF77

DCF77 is a German longwave time signal and standard-frequency radio station. It started service as a standard-frequency station on 1 January 1959; date and time information was added in June 1973. The transmitter is located at Mainflingen, near Frankfurt am Main, and broadcasts on 77.5 kHz with a power of about 50 kW, operated by Media Broadcast on behalf of the Physikalisch-Technische Bundesanstalt (PTB), Germany's national metrology institute.

Signal

The call sign DCF77 stands for D (Deutschland, Germany), C (longwave signal), F (Frankfurt), and 77 for the frequency 77.5 kHz. The carrier is amplitude-modulated with a coded time signal: at the start of each second the carrier amplitude is reduced for 0.1 or 0.2 seconds, encoding binary 0s and 1s that spell out the time, date, daylight-saving status, and warning bits in BCD format over one minute. Since 2003 the signal has also carried phase-modulated pseudo-random noise to improve receiver accuracy, and since 2006 civil-protection and weather information for parts of Europe has been embedded in an additional data channel.

Coverage and use

The signal can be received throughout Germany and much of Europe, at distances up to about 2,000 km at night. It is used to synchronize radio-controlled clocks and watches, railway and industrial clocks, and time switches across central Europe, and provides a widely available source of legal German time traceable to PTB's atomic clocks. Its accuracy is better than a few milliseconds when propagation delays are accounted for.
