North American Datum

The North American Datum (NAD) is a series of geodetic datums used to define the horizontal coordinates of points in North America for surveying, mapping, and navigation. A datum provides a reference framework—a model of the Earth's shape together with an origin and orientation—against which latitude and longitude are measured. Because the Earth's surface is irregular, different datums can assign noticeably different coordinates to the same physical location, sometimes differing by hundreds of metres.

The reference ellipsoid underlying the North American Datum

NAD27

The first widely used datum for the continent was the North American Datum of 1927 (NAD27), based on the Clarke ellipsoid of 1866. Its origin was fixed at Meades Ranch in Kansas, chosen because it lay near the geographic centre of the contiguous United States and close to where the ellipsoid best fit the continent. NAD27 underpinned topographic maps and charts for more than half a century, but its coordinates accumulated distortions because the underlying survey network was adjusted piecemeal rather than as a whole.

NAD83

The North American Datum of 1983 (NAD83) replaced NAD27 using satellite geodesy and Doppler observations. It is based on the Geodetic Reference System 1980 (GRS80) ellipsoid and is geocentric—its origin coincides with the Earth's centre of mass—whereas NAD27 was not Earth-centred. Coordinates for a given point can shift by tens to hundreds of metres between NAD27 and NAD83, so maps and datasets must state which datum they use. NAD83 remains the official horizontal datum of the United States, Canada, and Mexico, with later refinements such as the NSRS2007 and NAD83(2011) adjustments improving its accuracy.

Relation to WGS84

The World Geodetic System 1984 (WGS84), used by GPS, is very close to NAD83 but not identical; the two frames drift relative to one another by roughly one to two metres in North America because NAD83 is fixed to the North American tectonic plate while WGS84 is a global frame.
