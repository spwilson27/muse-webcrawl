Shahr-e Kord

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shahr-e Kord (Persian: شهرکرد, also romanised as Shahrekord and Shahr Kord) is a city in west-central Iran, the capital of Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari Province. Situated about 90 km southwest of Isfahan at an elevation of roughly 2,050 metres above sea level, it is among the highest provincial capitals in Iran, which gives it a relatively cool climate and makes it a summer retreat. Its population was around 190,000 in the 2016 census.

History

The city's older name was Deh-e Kord ("Village of the Kurd"), reflecting a tradition that the area was settled by Kurdish herders; the name was changed to Shahr-e Kord ("City of the Kurd") in 1935 after it gained city status. Coins and artefacts from the region suggest ancient settlement in the surrounding plain, though the modern town grew mainly in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

Economy and culture

Shahr-e Kord is a regional commercial and educational centre. It is home to Shahrekord University, an Islamic Azad University branch and medical and technical institutions. Local industries include brick and cement production, dairy processing and handicrafts; the region is known for Bakhtiari nomadic culture, felt-making and traditional carpets. The surrounding province is noted for its rivers, oak forests and the summer quarters (sardsir) of Bakhtiari nomads.

Geography and climate

The city lies on a high plain in the Zagros range. Winters are cold and snowy, while summers are mild and dry, with an average annual temperature around 12 °C. Nearby attractions include the Zayandeh Rud valley and the Cheshmeh Dimeh spring.
