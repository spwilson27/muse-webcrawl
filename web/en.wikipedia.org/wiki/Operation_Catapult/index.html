Operation Catapult

Operation Catapult was a British naval operation in July 1940, during the Second World War, to prevent the ships of the French Navy from falling into German hands after the armistice between France and Germany. Britain feared that the powerful French fleet might be seized by the Axis and used against the Royal Navy.

French warships in British ports were seized, mostly without bloodshed. At Mers-el-Kébir in Algeria, after an ultimatum was rejected, Force H opened fire on 3 July 1940, sinking the battleship Bretagne and damaging others, with the loss of nearly 1,300 French sailors. At Alexandria an agreement was reached to demilitarise the French squadron without fighting.

The operation demonstrated to the United States that Britain intended to fight on, but it badly damaged Anglo-French relations and was exploited by Vichy propaganda.
