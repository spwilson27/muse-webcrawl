Ringebu Stave Church

Ringebu Stave Church (Norwegian: Ringebu stavkyrkje) is a stave church located in Ringebu, in the valley of Gudbrandsdalen in Innlandet county, Norway. It is one of the 28 remaining stave churches in the country and among the largest.

History

The church was built around the year 1220, on the site of an earlier church. In 1630 the master builder Werner Olsen rebuilt and enlarged it, adding the distinctive transept with its red-painted exterior and the tower that give the church a cruciform plan unusual among stave churches. A further restoration took place in 1631. Excavations have revealed the post-holes of an earlier church and evidence of pre-Christian burials and an earlier cult site on the ground.

Interior

Inside, the church preserves medieval elements including a crucifix and a carved doorway, along with post-Reformation furnishings. A statue of Saint Laurence dates from the medieval period. The church remains in regular use as a parish church in the Church of Norway, and is a popular stop for travellers in the Gudbrandsdalen valley.
