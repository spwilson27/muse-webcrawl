Humphry Repton

Humphry Repton (21 April 1752 – 24 March 1818) was the last great English landscape designer of the eighteenth century, often regarded as the successor to Capability Brown. He coined the term "landscape gardener" for his profession and worked on some 400 commissions for the landed gentry.

Work

Repton is famous for his "Red Books", bound manuscripts for clients in which watercolour illustrations with hinged overlays showed views of an estate before and after his proposed improvements. Compared with Brown's sweeping parkland, Repton reintroduced terraces, flower beds and gravel walks near the house, anticipating Victorian garden design. Notable commissions include Woburn Abbey, Harewood House, Kenwood House and the grounds at Brighton Pavilion.

Legacy

His published works, including Sketches and Hints on Landscape Gardening (1795) and Observations on the Theory and Practice of Landscape Gardening (1803), codified the picturesque style of English landscape design.
