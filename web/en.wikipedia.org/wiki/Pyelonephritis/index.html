Pyelonephritis

Pyelonephritis is inflammation of the kidney, typically due to a bacterial infection. Symptoms most often include fever and flank tenderness, often together with the lower urinary tract symptoms of cystitis such as frequent, painful urination. Vomiting, nausea and rigors may also occur. It is a form of upper urinary tract infection, in contrast to infections confined to the bladder.

Cause and pathophysiology

Most cases result from bacteria ascending from the lower urinary tract through the ureters into the renal pelvis and parenchyma; the gut bacterium Escherichia coli accounts for the large majority of community-acquired cases. Less commonly, infection is blood-borne. Structural or functional urinary abnormalities — kidney stones, obstruction, vesicoureteral reflux, an indwelling catheter — increase the risk, as do diabetes and pregnancy.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is usually clinical, supported by urinalysis showing pyuria and bacteriuria and by urine culture identifying the organism. Blood tests may show a raised white-cell count and inflammatory markers. Imaging (ultrasound or CT) is reserved for complicated cases, men, pregnant patients, or those failing to improve, to detect obstruction or abscess.

Treatment and complications

Treatment is with antibiotics — orally for uncomplicated cases in otherwise well patients, intravenously and in hospital for severe illness, pregnancy or vomiting — together with fluids and antipyretics. Untreated or complicated pyelonephritis can lead to sepsis, renal abscess, papillary necrosis and, in recurrent cases, chronic pyelonephritis with renal scarring.
