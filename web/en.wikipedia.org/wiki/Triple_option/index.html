Triple option

The triple option is an offensive system in American football built around the option run, in which the quarterback makes post-snap decisions among three possible ball carriers on a single play. It is the signature scheme of the flexbone and wishbone offenses and has long been associated with the service academies — Army, Navy, and Air Force — and with programs such as Georgia Tech under Paul Johnson.

How it works

On the standard triple option (the "veer"), the quarterback first reads an unblocked defensive lineman and either hands off to the fullback on the dive or keeps the ball; he then reads a second defender, typically an outside linebacker or end, and either keeps it himself or pitches to a trailing slotback or halfback. By leaving defenders unblocked and reading them instead, the offense can neutralize superior defensive size and numbers.

History and use

The wishbone offense, popularized by Texas under Darrell Royal in the late 1960s, brought the triple option to national prominence and won multiple national championships. As passing offenses spread, the pure triple option became rare at the highest levels, but it remains effective for teams with smaller players and disciplined execution, since it controls the clock and limits opponents' possessions.
