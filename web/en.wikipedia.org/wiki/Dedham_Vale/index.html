Dedham Vale is a nationally important landscape on the Essex–Suffolk border in east England, centred on the valley of the River Stour around Dedham, East Bergholt, and Flatford. It is designated a National Landscape (formerly an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty) and is famously known as "Constable Country", the countryside painted throughout his career by the artist John Constable, who was born at East Bergholt in 1776.

Landscape

The vale is a low-lying river valley of water meadows, ancient woodland, hedgerows, and small villages with timber-framed and brick houses. The River Stour was made navigable between Sudbury and Mistley in the 18th century, and the valley's mills — including Flatford Mill and Willy Lott's Cottage — became emblematic subjects of English landscape painting.

Constable

Constable's major canvases, including The Hay Wain (1821), Dedham Vale (1802 and 1828 versions), and The White Horse, depict this small area within a few miles of his birthplace. His devotion to the valley shaped the English ideal of pastoral landscape, and the area now draws visitors to Flatford, Bridge Cottage, and the Constable exhibitions at Dedham and East Bergholt.

Conservation

The Dedham Vale Society, founded in 1938, campaigned for the area's protection, achieved with AONB designation in 1970. A surrounding "project area" and the Stour Valley Path long-distance footpath help manage visitors while preserving the valley's farmed character.
