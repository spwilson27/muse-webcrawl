Tōhoku region


Country: Japan (northern Honshu)
Prefectures: Akita, Aomori, Fukushima, Iwate, Miyagi, Yamagata
Largest city: Sendai

The Tōhoku region ("Northeast") comprises the northeastern part of Honshu, the largest island of Japan. It consists of six prefectures — Akita, Aomori, Fukushima, Iwate, Miyagi and Yamagata — and is traditionally regarded as a rural, mountainous region known for rice farming, heavy snows and summer festivals.

Geography

The Ōu Mountains run down the region's spine, separating the Pacific side from the Sea of Japan side. Fertile lowlands along the Kitakami and Mogami rivers support extensive agriculture, while the Sanriku coast features deeply indented ria shoreline.

2011 disaster

On 11 March 2011 the region was struck by the Great East Japan Earthquake and tsunami, which devastated coastal communities and caused the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear accident. Reconstruction has continued for years since, with seawalls, relocated towns and decommissioning work at the plant.
