MicroProse is an American video game publisher and developer founded in 1982 by Sid Meier and Bill Stealey in Hunt Valley, Maryland. The company became famous in the 1980s and 1990s for military simulation games and for strategy titles designed by Meier and his collaborators. MicroProse published many critically acclaimed and commercially successful games, and its name has been revived in the 21st century by a new company under Australian businessman David Lagettie.


The MicroProse logo used on its simulation and strategy titles.

History

Stealey, a former US Air Force pilot, and Meier, a programmer, founded the company — originally MicroProse Software — to publish flight simulation games for 8-bit home computers, beginning with Hellcat Ace (1982). Successful simulations followed, including F-15 Strike Eagle, Silent Service, and Gunship. The company expanded into strategy and other genres with Sid Meier's Pirates! (1987), Railroad Tycoon (1990), and Civilization (1991), as well as publishing titles from other developers such as X-COM: UFO Defense (1994) and Master of Orion. Through its UK office and the acquisition of studios, MicroProse also published the Geoff Crammond Grand Prix racing series.

Financial problems in the 1990s led to acquisition by Spectrum HoloByte in 1993; the combined company was itself acquired by Hasbro Interactive in 1998, and the MicroProse studios were closed in stages by Hasbro and its successor Infogrames (Atari) by 2003. In 2019–2020 the MicroProse brand was revived by Lagettie, a former Bohemia Interactive developer, and the new MicroProse began publishing simulation and strategy games such as Sea Power: Naval Combat in the Missile Age, working with independent studios including former MicroProse collaborators.
