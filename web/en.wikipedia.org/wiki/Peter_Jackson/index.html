Peter Jackson

Born: Peter Robert Jackson, 31 October 1961, Wellington, New Zealand

Occupation: Film director, producer, screenwriter

Known for: The Lord of the Rings trilogy; The Hobbit trilogy; King Kong

Awards: 3 Academy Awards

Sir Peter Robert Jackson (born 31 October 1961) is a New Zealand filmmaker. He is best known as the director, writer and producer of The Lord of the Rings trilogy (2001–2003) and The Hobbit trilogy (2012–2014), both adapted from the novels of J. R. R. Tolkien.

Early work

Jackson began with low-budget "splatstick" horror comedies — Bad Taste (1987), Meet the Feebles (1989) and Braindead (1992, released as Dead Alive in North America). He gained critical acclaim with Heavenly Creatures (1994), co-written with his partner Fran Walsh, which earned an Academy Award nomination for Best Original Screenplay.

Middle-earth and beyond

The Lord of the Rings films were shot simultaneously in New Zealand and grossed nearly $3 billion worldwide. The final instalment, The Return of the King (2003), won all 11 Academy Awards for which it was nominated, including Best Picture and Best Director for Jackson. He went on to direct King Kong (2005) and The Lovely Bones (2009), and produced District 9 (2009).

Jackson co-founded the visual effects company Weta Digital and the WingNut Films production company. His First World War documentary They Shall Not Grow Old (2018), built from restored and colourised archive footage, was widely acclaimed, as was the Beatles documentary series The Beatles: Get Back (2021). He was knighted in 2010 for services to film.
