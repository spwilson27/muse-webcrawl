Dynamic capabilities

Dynamic capabilities is a framework in strategic management describing a firm's ability to integrate, build and reconfigure internal and external competences to address rapidly changing environments. The concept was introduced by David Teece, Gary Pisano and Amy Shuen in their influential 1997 paper "Dynamic Capabilities and Strategic Management" in the Strategic Management Journal.

Framework

Extending the resource-based view of the firm, the framework holds that competitive advantage in fast-moving markets depends less on static resources than on the capacity to change them. Teece later elaborated the microfoundations as three clusters of activities: sensing (identifying and assessing opportunities and threats), seizing (mobilizing resources to capture value from opportunities) and transforming (continuous renewal and reconfiguration of the firm's asset base and structure).

Developments and criticism

Subsequent research by scholars such as Kathleen Eisenhardt and Jeffrey Martin recast dynamic capabilities as identifiable processes—like product development routines and alliance management—arguing that in high-velocity markets they are simple, experiential and fragile. The framework has been applied across innovation management, entrepreneurship and international business, though critics have charged it with vagueness and tautology—the tendency to define capabilities by the success they are meant to explain.
