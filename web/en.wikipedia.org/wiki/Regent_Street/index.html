Regent Street is a major shopping street in the West End of London. It is named after George, the Prince Regent (later George IV) and was laid out under the direction of the architect John Nash and James Burton. It runs from Waterloo Place in St James's at the southern end, through Piccadilly Circus and Oxford Circus, to All Souls Church. From there Langham Place and Portland Place continue the route to Regent's Park.

The street was completed in 1825 and was an early example of town planning in England, cutting through the existing street pattern of 17th- and 18th-century London. It was designed as a ceremonial route and commercial street, separating the fashionable districts of Mayfair from the less desirable Soho.

History

The original buildings were demolished and rebuilt between 1895 and 1927 in the grand Beaux-Arts style, with façades designed largely by Sir Reginald Blomfield and others, so that today the whole street presents a unified Portland stone frontage. Almost all of the street is owned by the Crown Estate.

Retail

Regent Street is one of London's principal shopping streets, home to flagship stores including Hamleys toy shop, Liberty (just off the street), and the Apple Store, the first in Europe when it opened in 2004. The street is also famous for its annual Christmas lights, a tradition dating from 1954.
