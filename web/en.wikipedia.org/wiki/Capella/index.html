Capella

Capella, designated Alpha Aurigae, is the brightest star in the constellation of Auriga, the sixth-brightest star in the night sky, and the third-brightest in the northern celestial hemisphere. Although it appears as a single star to the naked eye, Capella is a system of four stars in two binary pairs.

Capella
Constellation: Auriga
Apparent magnitude: +0.08
Distance: approx. 42.9 light-years
Spectral type: G-type giants

Properties

The primary pair consists of two yellow giant stars, Capella Aa and Ab, orbiting each other closely. A second, more distant pair of red dwarfs completes the system. Capella's name means "little goat" in Latin, and in mythology it represented the goat Amalthea.

Observation

Capella is circumpolar for observers at far northern latitudes and is a prominent feature of winter skies in the Northern Hemisphere, forming part of the Winter Hexagon asterism.
