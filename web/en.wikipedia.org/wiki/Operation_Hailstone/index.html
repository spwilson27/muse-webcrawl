Operation Hailstone

Date: 17–18 February 1944

Location: Truk Lagoon, Caroline Islands

Part of: Pacific War, Second World War

Belligerents: United States vs. Empire of Japan

Result: American victory

Operation Hailstone was a massive naval air and surface attack launched on 17–18 February 1944 by the United States Navy against the Japanese naval and air base at Truk Lagoon in the Caroline Islands. Truk, often called the "Gibraltar of the Pacific," was the principal forward anchorage of the Imperial Japanese Navy's Combined Fleet.

Background

Following the capture of Kwajalein in the Marshall Islands, Admiral Raymond Spruance's Fifth Fleet, built around Vice Admiral Marc Mitscher's Fast Carrier Task Force (TF 58), moved against Truk to neutralize it in support of the landings on Eniwetok. Anticipating attack, the Japanese had withdrawn their major warships to Palau and Singapore, but many auxiliaries, merchant ships, and aircraft remained.

The attack

Over two days, carrier aircraft from nine carriers flew more than 1,250 sorties. American fighters destroyed Japanese air power over the atoll, and bombers and torpedo planes sank shipping in the lagoon, while a surface group including the battleships Iowa and New Jersey intercepted vessels attempting to escape. A night torpedo attack by radar-equipped aircraft on the second night was among the first of its kind by US carrier planes.

Results

Japan lost some 250–275 aircraft and roughly 200,000 tons of shipping, including the light cruiser Naka, destroyers, submarines, and numerous supply and transport vessels — a haul sometimes compared to a reverse Pearl Harbor. American losses were modest, though the carrier Intrepid was damaged by a torpedo. Truk was effectively neutralized and bypassed for the remainder of the war; its garrison was cut off and starved until Japan's surrender.

The wrecks in Truk (now Chuuk) Lagoon became one of the world's most famous wreck-diving sites, sometimes called the "Ghost Fleet of Truk Lagoon."
