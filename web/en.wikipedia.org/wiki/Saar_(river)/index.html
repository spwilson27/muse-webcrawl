The Saar (French: Sarre) is a river in northeastern France and western Germany, a right-bank tributary of the Moselle. It is about 246 kilometres long and gives its name to the German state of Saarland, through which it flows.

The Saarschleife, the river's great incised meander near Mettlach.

Course

The Saar rises in the Vosges mountains from two headstreams, the Red Saar and the White Saar, and flows north through Sarreguemines and Sarralbe in France before crossing into Germany at Saarbrücken. It continues through the industrial Saar valley — Völklingen, Saarlouis and Dillingen — and then through Merzig and past the famous Saarschleife (Saar loop) near Mettlach, joining the Moselle at Konz near Trier.

History and economy

The river was central to the coal and steel industry of the Saar region, and its basin was administered as the Saar Protectorate after the Second World War before rejoining the Federal Republic of Germany in 1957. The canalised river remains navigable for freight, and the Saar Cycle Path along its banks is a popular long-distance cycling route.
