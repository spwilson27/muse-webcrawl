X-Men

The X-Men are a team of fictional superhero mutants appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Stan Lee and artist Jack Kirby, they first appeared in The X-Men #1 in September 1963. The X-Men are mutants — humans born with an "X-gene" that grants extraordinary abilities — who fight to protect a world that fears and hates them, under the guidance of their founder, Professor Charles Xavier.

Publication history

The original series struggled commercially and was placed on reprint status in 1970, but the 1975 relaunch in Giant-Size X-Men #1, introducing an international team including Wolverine, Storm and Colossus, transformed the title. Under writer Chris Claremont's long run (1975–1991), with artists Dave Cockrum and John Byrne, stories such as "The Dark Phoenix Saga" and "Days of Future Past" made Uncanny X-Men Marvel's best-selling comic. The franchise expanded into numerous spin-off titles, animated series (notably the 1992 X-Men: The Animated Series) and a long-running film series beginning with X-Men (2000).

Premise and themes

The X-Men's stories revolve around the dream of peaceful coexistence between mutants and ordinary humans, championed by Xavier, and the rival philosophy of Magneto, a mutant Holocaust survivor who believes coexistence is impossible. The mutant metaphor has been read as an allegory for civil rights, minority and LGBTQ experiences. The team operates from Xavier's school, which trains young mutants to control their powers, and uses the sub-basement Danger Room and the mutant-detecting computer Cerebro.

In other media

The 20th Century Fox film series (2000–2020) grossed over $6 billion worldwide and helped launch the modern superhero film era; following Disney's acquisition of Fox, the characters were integrated into the Marvel Cinematic Universe, and the animated revival X-Men '97 premiered in 2024.
