Polyester is a category of polymers containing ester functional groups in their main chain. The term most commonly refers to polyethylene terephthalate (PET), a thermoplastic used worldwide in synthetic fibres for clothing, in blow-moulded bottles and food containers, and in films and engineering resins. Polyester fibres are the most widely produced synthetic fibres in the world.

Polyester yarn, used in a large share of the world's textiles.

History

PET was patented in 1941 by John Rex Whinfield and James Tennant Dickson at the Calico Printers' Association in Manchester, building on Wallace Carothers' earlier polyester research at DuPont. ICI commercialised the fibre as Terylene and DuPont as Dacron. PET bottle resin was developed in the 1970s by Nathaniel Wyeth.

Properties and uses

Polyester fibres are strong, resilient, resistant to stretching, shrinking, and most chemicals, and dry quickly; they are blended with cotton and wool or used alone in sportswear, fleece, and home furnishings. Beyond textiles, PET is used for beverage bottles, food packaging, magnetic tape and photographic film (Mylar), sails, ropes, and tyre cord.

Environmental issues

Conventional polyester is derived from petrochemicals and is not biodegradable; washing polyester garments releases microplastic fibres. PET is, however, one of the most widely recycled plastics (resin code 1), and recycled PET (rPET) is increasingly used in bottles and clothing, though textile-to-textile recycling remains limited.
