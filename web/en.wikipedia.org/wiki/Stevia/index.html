Stevia is a plant genus in the sunflower family (Asteraceae), native to South America. The species Stevia rebaudiana, commonly known as sweetleaf, produces steviol glycosides in its leaves that are 200–300 times sweeter than sugar and have no calories. Extracts of the plant are widely used as a natural sugar substitute.

History

The Guaraní people of Paraguay have used stevia leaves, which they call kaʼa heʼẽ ("sweet herb"), for centuries to sweeten drinks such as yerba mate. Swiss botanist Moisés Santiago Bertoni described the plant scientifically around 1899, and in 1931 French chemists isolated the glycosides responsible for its sweetness, naming them stevioside and rebaudioside.

Japan began commercial cultivation in the 1970s, where stevia captured a large share of the sweetener market. Regulatory caution in the United States and Europe initially limited its use; the U.S. FDA granted GRAS (generally recognized as safe) status to high-purity rebaudioside A in 2008, opening the way for brands such as Truvia and PureVia.

Chemistry and products

The sweet compounds are steviol glycosides, principally stevioside and rebaudioside A. Rebaudioside A has the cleanest taste with the least bitter aftertaste. Extracts are sold as powders, liquids, and tabletop packets, often blended with erythritol or other bulking agents. Unlike saccharin, stevia extracts are marketed as natural because they are plant-derived.

Health effects

Steviol glycosides are not metabolized by the body for energy and do not raise blood glucose, making them suitable for people with diabetes. Toxicology reviews by international bodies have established an acceptable daily intake of 4 mg/kg body weight per day (as steviol equivalents). Whole-leaf and crude extracts have a different regulatory status from purified glycosides in some jurisdictions.

Cultivation

Stevia is grown commercially in Paraguay, China, and other warm regions. The plants are harvested just before flowering, when glycoside content peaks, and the leaves are dried and extracted with water.
