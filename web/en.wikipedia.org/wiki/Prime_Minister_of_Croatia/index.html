The Prime Minister of Croatia (Croatian: Predsjednik Vlade Republike Hrvatske, literally "President of the Government of the Republic of Croatia") is the head of government of Croatia and the country's most powerful political office. The prime minister leads the Government of Croatia (Vlada), proposes ministers, and directs the executive branch.

Appointment

Following parliamentary elections, the President of Croatia entrusts the mandate to form a government to the person who, based on the distribution of seats and consultations, is likely to command the confidence of a majority in the Croatian Parliament (Sabor). The prime minister-designate then presents a government and program to parliament, which must approve it by an absolute majority of all deputies. The government remains in office as long as it retains parliament's confidence.

Role

Croatia's 1990 constitution originally created a semi-presidential system with a strong president; constitutional amendments in 2000–2001 transformed the country into a parliamentary republic and made the prime minister the dominant executive figure. The prime minister represents the government, convenes and chairs its sessions, coordinates ministries, and is responsible for implementing laws and policy.

History

The office in its present form dates from 1990, when Stjepan Mesić became the first prime minister of the Socialist Republic of Croatia within Yugoslavia and then of the emerging independent state. Subsequent prime ministers have included Franjo Gregurić, who led the government of national unity during the early Homeland War; Nikica Valentić; Zlatko Mateša; Ivica Račan, the first prime minister under the parliamentary system after 2000; Ivo Sanader; Jadranka Kosor, the first woman to hold the office; Zoran Milanović; Tihomir Orešković; and Andrej Plenković, who took office in 2016 and led Croatia into the eurozone and Schengen area in 2023.
