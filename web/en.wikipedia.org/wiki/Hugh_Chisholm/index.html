Hugh Chisholm

Hugh Chisholm (22 February 1866 – 29 September 1924) was a British journalist and encyclopaedia editor, best known as the editor of the 11th edition of the Encyclopædia Britannica (1911), widely regarded as one of the finest general encyclopaedias ever published.

Life

Born in London and educated at Corpus Christi College, Oxford, Chisholm was called to the bar at the Middle Temple but turned to journalism, joining The Times in 1897. He had earlier edited the 10th-edition supplement of the Britannica and was appointed editor-in-chief for the 11th edition in 1903.

The eleventh edition

Under Chisholm, the 11th edition comprised 29 volumes written by more than 1,500 experts, combining scholarly authority with readability. Produced jointly by Cambridge University Press and The Times, it remains in the public domain and has been a major source for reference works, including early Wikipedia.

Later work

Chisholm later edited the 12th-edition supplementary volumes (1922) and The Britannica Year-Book. He died in London in 1924.
