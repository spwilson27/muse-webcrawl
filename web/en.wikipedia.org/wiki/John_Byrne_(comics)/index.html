John Byrne (comics)

Born: John Lindley Byrne, 6 July 1950, Walsall, England

Nationality: British-American

Occupation: Comics writer and artist

Notable works: Uncanny X-Men, Fantastic Four, The Man of Steel, Alpha Flight

John Lindley Byrne (born July 6, 1950) is a British-born American comic book writer and artist. Since the mid-1970s he has worked on nearly every major American superhero character and is regarded as one of the most influential creators in the medium.

Career

Born in England and raised in Canada, Byrne broke into American comics at Charlton and then Marvel. As penciller and co-plotter with writer Chris Claremont on Uncanny X-Men (1977–1981), he illustrated landmark stories including "The Dark Phoenix Saga" and "Days of Future Past". As writer-artist of Fantastic Four (1981–1986) he is credited with returning the title to prominence, and he created the Canadian team Alpha Flight.

In 1986 Byrne moved to DC Comics to relaunch Superman with the miniseries The Man of Steel, which redefined the character's origins for the modern era. Later work includes Next Men, Danger Unlimited and numerous projects for IDW Publishing. He was inducted into the Will Eisner Comic Book Hall of Fame in 2015.
