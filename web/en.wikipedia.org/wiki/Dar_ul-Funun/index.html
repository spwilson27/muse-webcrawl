Dar ul-Funun

Dar ul-Funun (Persian: دارالفنون, Dār al-Fonūn, "house of the sciences/techniques") was the first modern institution of higher learning in Iran, founded in Tehran in 1851 by Amir Kabir, the reforming chief minister of Naser al-Din Shah Qajar. It is regarded as the forerunner of the University of Tehran.

Foundation

Amir Kabir, impressed by European and Ottoman educational reforms, established the polytechnic to train Iranian officers, engineers, physicians and officials in modern sciences, reducing dependence on foreign experts. Initially organised on the model of a military academy, it enrolled about 100 students taught by Austrian, and later French and other European, instructors alongside Iranian scholars, in subjects including medicine, engineering, artillery, infantry and cavalry tactics, mining, mathematics, languages and, soon, the humanities and arts. Textbooks were translated into Persian, helping to create a modern scientific vocabulary.

Development

The school occupied a purpose-built complex near the Golestan Palace in Tehran, whose historic gate still stands. Dar ul-Funun educated many figures who shaped modern Iran, and its translation bureau seeded Persian journalism and publishing. In the early 20th century it added secondary schooling; its higher faculties were progressively spun off — medicine, law and other schools — and in 1934 these were merged into the newly founded University of Tehran. The remaining institution continued as a prestigious high school.
