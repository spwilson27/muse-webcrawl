Bracciano is a small town in the Italian region of Lazio, about 30 kilometres northwest of Rome, in the Metropolitan City of Rome. It stands on a hill overlooking Lake Bracciano (Lago di Bracciano), a volcanic lake that supplies drinking water to the capital, and is best known for its imposing Renaissance castle, the Castello Orsini-Odescalchi.

History

The area was settled in Etruscan times and passed through the hands of several Roman and medieval families. In the 15th century the powerful Orsini family acquired Bracciano and built the great pentagonal castle between about 1470 and 1490, one of the finest examples of Italian Renaissance military architecture, with six massive cylindrical towers. In 1696 the castle was sold to the Odescalchi family, who still own it. The town grew around the castle walls and the church of Santa Maria Novella, and later became a garrison and agricultural centre for the northern Roman countryside.

The castle

The Castello Orsini-Odescalchi contains richly frescoed apartments, including work associated with the Zuccari workshop and Antoniazzo Romano, and houses a museum of arms and furnishings. It is a popular venue for weddings and events — its celebrity weddings have included those of Tom Cruise and Katie Holmes in 2006 — and has served as a film location.

The town and lake today

Bracciano has a railway station on the Rome–Viterbo line. The adjoining military airfield at Vigna di Valle hosts the Italian Air Force Museum (Museo Storico dell'Aeronautica Militare), one of the largest aviation museums in Italy, on the lake's southern shore. The lake itself is protected as a regional park, and sailing, swimming and canoeing are popular; motorboats are restricted to preserve the water quality.
