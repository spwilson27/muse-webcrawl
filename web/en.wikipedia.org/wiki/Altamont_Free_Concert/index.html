The Altamont Free Concert was a counterculture rock concert held on Saturday, December 6, 1969, at the Altamont Speedway in northern California. Conceived as a West Coast answer to Woodstock, it was headlined by the Rolling Stones and also featured Santana, Jefferson Airplane, the Flying Burrito Brothers, and Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young. The event is remembered for its pervasive violence and is often described as the symbolic end of the 1960s hippie era.

The event

The concert was organized hastily after the Stones' 1969 American tour; the venue changed to Altamont only days before. Members of the Hells Angels motorcycle club were engaged — on the recommendation, participants later said, of the Grateful Dead's circle — to help with security, reportedly paid in beer. Fights broke out through the day: Angels beat audience members with pool cues, Jefferson Airplane's Marty Balin was knocked unconscious, and the Grateful Dead declined to perform.

Death of Meredith Hunter

During the Rolling Stones' set, 18-year-old Meredith Hunter, who had produced a gun near the stage, was stabbed to death by Hells Angel Alan Passaro within sight of the band; the killing was captured in the Maysles brothers' documentary Gimme Shelter (1970). Passaro was acquitted on grounds of self-defense. Three other attendees died — two in a hit-and-run, one by drowning — and many were injured.

Legacy

Altamont entered cultural memory as the dark mirror of Woodstock: where Woodstock stood for the utopian promise chronicled by writers like Theodore Roszak, Altamont stood for its collapse, and it changed how large rock festivals handled security thereafter.
