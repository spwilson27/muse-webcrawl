I.R.S. Records (International Record Syndicate) was a major American record label founded by Miles Copeland III, Jay Boberg and Carl Grasso in 1979. Copeland was the manager of The Police — whose drummer, Stewart Copeland, was his brother — and the label's initials playfully referenced the Internal Revenue Service. Distributed first by A&M Records and later by EMI, I.R.S. became a flagship label of the new wave and alternative rock era.

History

The label built its reputation on British and American alternative acts, releasing records by R.E.M., the Go-Go's, The Bangles, The English Beat, Wall of Voodoo, Oingo Boingo, The Alarm, and Fine Young Cannibals. Its first big success was the Go-Go's debut Beauty and the Beat (1981), the first album by an all-female band who wrote their own songs and played their own instruments to top the US charts. R.E.M.'s albums from Murmur (1983) through Document (1987) defined American college rock.

Subsidiaries included Miles Copeland's Faulty Products and the spoken-word/no-wave imprint. Financial difficulties led EMI to fold the label in 1996; its catalogue passed to Capitol and later Universal Music Group. The I.R.S. name was revived in the 2010s as a Nashville-oriented imprint under Capitol/UMG.
