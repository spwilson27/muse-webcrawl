Cullen skink

TypeSoup
Place of originScotland
Region or stateCullen, Moray
Main ingredientsSmoked haddock, potatoes, onion, milk or cream

Cullen skink is a thick Scottish soup made of smoked haddock, potatoes, and onions. An authentic Cullen skink will use finnan haddie, but it may be prepared with any other undyed smoked haddock.

This soup is a local speciality from the town of Cullen in Moray, on the north-east coast of Scotland. It is often served as a starter at formal Scottish dinners but is also widely served as an everyday dish across the north-east.

Etymology

Skink is a Scots word for a shin, knuckle, or hough of beef, which has developed the secondary meaning of a soup, especially one made from these. The soup was originally made with beef, but when beef became too expensive for everyday use, it was replaced with other cheaper ingredients, such as smoked haddock. The name is also related to the Gaelic word sgian.

Preparation

The haddock is poached in milk, then removed and flaked. Onions and potatoes are cooked in the same liquid until tender, and the flaked fish is returned to the pot. The soup is finished with the poaching milk or cream and seasoned. Recipes vary: some mash part of the potato to thicken the soup, while others add leek or a knob of butter.

World championship

The Cullen Skink World Championships are held annually in Cullen, with cooks competing to produce the best traditional version and the best "with a twist" variation.
