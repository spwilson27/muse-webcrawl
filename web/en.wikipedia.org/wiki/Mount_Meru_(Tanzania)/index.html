Mount Meru (Tanzania)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mount Meru from Arusha, with its ash cone visible in the caldera.

Mount Meru is a dormant stratovolcano in Arusha Region, Tanzania, about 70 kilometres (43 mi) west of Mount Kilimanjaro. At 4,562.13 metres (14,968 ft), it is the fifth-highest mountain of Africa and the second-highest in Tanzania. The mountain lies entirely within Arusha National Park. It is not to be confused with the sacred Mount Meru of Hindu and Buddhist cosmology.

Geology

Meru began erupting about 780,000 years ago. A major eruption roughly 7,800 years ago blew out the mountain's eastern wall, creating a horseshoe-shaped caldera and catastrophic debris avalanches and lahars; a smaller ash cone grew inside the crater, and minor eruptions occurred as recently as 1910. The crater rim's highest point, Socialist Peak, offers views across the caldera to Kilimanjaro.

Climbing

The standard climb takes three to four days via the Momella route, with huts at Miriakamba and Saddle, a summit ascent (often at night) along the crater rim, and a side walk to Little Meru (3,820 m) for acclimatisation. Because wildlife — buffalo, giraffe, warthog, and colobus monkeys — grazes the lower slopes, climbers are accompanied by an armed park ranger. Many trekkers use Meru as an acclimatisation climb before Kilimanjaro.
