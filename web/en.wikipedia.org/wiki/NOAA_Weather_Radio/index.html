NOAA Weather Radio


NOAA Weather Radio All Hazards
Operator: National Weather Service (NOAA)
Broadcast: VHF FM, 162.400–162.550 MHz
Coverage: United States and territories
Stations: ~1,000 transmitters

NOAA Weather Radio (NWR), officially NOAA Weather Radio All Hazards, is an automated 24-hour network of VHF FM weather radio stations in the United States that broadcast weather information directly from a nearby National Weather Service office. The broadcasts include watches, warnings, forecasts, and current weather observations, as well as non-weather emergency information.

Operation

Stations broadcast on one of seven frequencies in the VHF public service band (162.400 through 162.550 MHz), sometimes called the "weather band". Transmissions cycle through routine forecast products every few minutes, and are interrupted for severe weather warnings. Warning alarm tones and Specific Area Message Encoding (SAME) digital headers allow compatible receivers to activate automatically for warnings affecting a programmed county area.

Voices

Broadcasts historically used recorded staff voices and later synthesized voices (nicknamed "Paul" and others) generated by the Console Replacement System. A newer generation of synthesized voices was introduced in the 2010s.

Coverage and use

The network covers approximately 97 percent of the U.S. population. NOAA Weather Radio receivers are recommended by emergency managers as a complement to outdoor warning sirens and wireless emergency alerts, particularly for receiving tornado warnings at night.
