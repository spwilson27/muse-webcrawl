Retro-futurism

Retro-futurism (adjective retro-futuristic or retro-future) is a movement in the creative arts showing the influence of depictions of the future produced in an earlier era. If futurism is sometimes called a "science" bent on anticipating what will come, retro-futurism is the remembering of that anticipation. Characterized by a blend of old-fashioned "retro" styles with futuristic technology, retro-futurism explores the themes of tension between past and future, and between the alienating and empowering effects of technology.

Origins and meaning

The term was coined by Lloyd Dunn in 1983 in connection with the magazine and art projects he was involved in. Retro-futurism takes two forms: the future as seen from the past — the actual visions of earlier eras, such as the streamlined rocket ships, ray guns, and domed cities of 1930s–1950s pulp science fiction — and contemporary works created in deliberate imitation of those earlier visions.

Examples

Retro-futuristic aesthetics appear in films such as Brazil, The Rocketeer, and the Fallout video game series, whose world combines 1950s American optimism with post-apocalyptic technology. Related "-punk" subgenres — steampunk, dieselpunk, atompunk, and cassette futurism — each anchor their imagined technology to a particular historical period. Architecture and design have also drawn on the style, from Googie roadside architecture (itself the original futuristic style of the 1950s) to deliberately retro product design.
