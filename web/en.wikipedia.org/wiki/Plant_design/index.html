Plant design

Plant design is a field of industrial engineering and process engineering concerned with the conception, layout, and specification of industrial plants — facilities such as chemical plants, refineries, power stations, and manufacturing works — from initial feasibility studies through detailed engineering and commissioning.

Scope

Plant design covers process design (the selection and sizing of unit operations such as reactors, distillation columns, heat exchangers, and pumps), plant layout, piping design, instrumentation and control, electrical systems, and structural works. Deliverables typically include process flow diagrams (PFDs), piping and instrumentation diagrams (P&IDs), equipment specifications, and 3D models.

Process

Projects usually proceed through stages: conceptual design, front-end engineering design (FEED), detailed engineering, procurement, construction, and commissioning. Designers must integrate safety (such as HAZOP studies), environmental compliance, operability, and economics, often under the direction of an engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) contractor.

Tools

Modern plant design relies on process simulators and computer-aided design (CAD) software capable of producing intelligent 3D plant models from which drawings, material take-offs, and clash detection are derived.
