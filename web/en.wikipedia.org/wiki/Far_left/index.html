Far left

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Far left is a political term for ideologies and movements positioned to the left of mainstream left-wing politics on the Political spectrum. The far left generally seeks fundamental transformation of capitalism and existing institutions, and includes communists, revolutionary socialists, anarcho-syndicalists and some Trotskyist, Maoist and left-communist currents. Its characteristic goals are social ownership of the means of production, abolition of class society, and — depending on the current — either a revolutionary state or the immediate abolition of the state.

The boundary between “left” and “far left” is contested and defined variously by parties, scholars, media and security agencies (in Germany, for example, the domestic intelligence service monitors designated far-left organisations). Historically the far left achieved state power through the Russian Revolution of 1917 and the Chinese Revolution of 1949, and exerted wide cultural influence through anti-colonial and 1968 movements; since 1991 its organised strength has contracted in most countries, persisting in unions, social movements and some parties. Because the label is often pejorative, many groups so described reject it, preferring “revolutionary”, “anticapitalist” or their specific tradition's name.
