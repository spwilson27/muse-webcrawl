The president of Brazil (Portuguese: presidente do Brasil) is the head of state and head of government of the Federative Republic of Brazil. The president leads the executive branch of the federal government and is the commander-in-chief of the Brazilian Armed Forces.

Election and term

The president is elected by popular vote for a four-year term in a two-round system and may be re-elected for one consecutive additional term. Candidates must be Brazilian-born citizens at least 35 years old. Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, first elected in 2002 and again in 2022, is the incumbent president.

Powers

Presidential powers include appointing ministers of state, conducting foreign relations, vetoing legislation, issuing provisional measures and decrees, and granting pardons. The official residence is the Palácio da Alvorada, and the workplace is the Palácio do Planalto in Brasília.

History

The office dates to the proclamation of the republic in 1889. Brazil has passed through several republics and a military dictatorship (1964–85) before the current democratic constitution of 1988.
