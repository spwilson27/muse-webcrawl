Thrombocytopenia

Thrombocytopenia
Specialty: Hematology
Symptoms: Easy bruising, petechiae, purpura, bleeding
Definition: Platelet count below 150 × 109/L
ICD-10: D69.6

Thrombocytopenia is a condition characterised by an abnormally low level of platelets (thrombocytes) in the blood. A normal human platelet count ranges from 150,000 to 450,000 platelets per microlitre of blood; counts below this range indicate thrombocytopenia. Because platelets are essential for blood clotting, a low count increases the risk of bleeding, although serious spontaneous bleeding usually occurs only at counts below about 10,000–20,000 per microlitre.

A peripheral blood smear in thrombocytopenia, with very few platelets visible.

Causes

Causes are conventionally grouped into decreased platelet production, increased platelet destruction or consumption, and splenic sequestration. Decreased production occurs in bone-marrow disorders such as aplastic anemia, leukaemia, and marrow infiltration, and with some infections, alcohol use, and vitamin B12 or folate deficiency. Increased destruction is seen in immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, hemolytic uraemic syndrome, disseminated intravascular coagulation, and heparin-induced thrombocytopenia. An enlarged spleen can sequester a large fraction of circulating platelets.

Signs and symptoms

Mild thrombocytopenia often causes no symptoms and is found incidentally on a blood count. Lower counts produce easy bruising, petechiae (pinpoint red or purple spots), purpura, bleeding gums, nosebleeds, blood in urine or stool, and unusually heavy menstrual bleeding. Severe cases risk internal or intracranial haemorrhage.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on a complete blood count and examination of a blood smear to exclude pseudothrombocytopenia caused by platelet clumping in the sample. Treatment addresses the underlying cause; corticosteroids and other immunosuppressants are used for immune thrombocytopenia, and platelet transfusion is reserved for active bleeding, very low counts, or before surgery.
