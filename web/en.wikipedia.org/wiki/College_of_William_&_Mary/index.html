The College of William & Mary (abbreviated as W&M) is a public research university in Williamsburg, Virginia, United States. Founded in 1693 under a royal charter issued by King William III and Queen Mary II, it is the second-oldest institution of higher education in the United States after Harvard University, and the oldest south of Harvard.

The college educated American presidents Thomas Jefferson, James Monroe, and John Tyler. Its alumni also include sixteen signers of the Declaration of Independence. It was the first college in the United States to install an elected student honor code, and the home of the first law school in America, established in 1779. The university's main building, the Sir Christopher Wren Building, is the oldest college building still standing in the United States.

History

The college was founded under a royal charter obtained by the Reverend James Blair, who served as its first president for fifty years. It was named for the reigning monarchs, William III and Mary II. In 1776, students at the college founded the Phi Beta Kappa Society, the first Greek-letter fraternity in the United States. Classes were suspended during both the Revolutionary War and the Civil War. The college became coeducational in 1918 and a state-supported institution in 1906.

Academics

William & Mary is known for its strong liberal arts tradition, small class sizes, and emphasis on undergraduate teaching. It operates schools of law, business, education, and marine science (the Virginia Institute of Marine Science), along with the arts and sciences.
