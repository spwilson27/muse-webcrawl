Neoprene

Neoprene (polychloroprene)
Monomer: chloroprene
Invented: 1930 (DuPont)
First marketed: 1931 as "DuPrene"

Neoprene (also polychloroprene) is a family of synthetic rubbers produced by the polymerization of chloroprene. It displays good chemical stability and maintains flexibility over a wide temperature range. Neoprene is sold either as solid rubber or in latex form, and is used in a wide variety of consumer and industrial applications.

History

Neoprene was invented by DuPont scientists on 17 April 1930, after Elmer Bolton heard a lecture by Julius Nieuwland, whose work on acetylene chemistry led to divinylacetylene and ultimately chloroprene polymerization. It was the first mass-produced synthetic rubber compound in the United States.

Applications

Its most familiar use is in wetsuits, drysuits and other watersports apparel, where foamed neoprene provides thermal insulation. Other uses include gaskets, hoses, coatings, laptop sleeves, orthopedic braces, electrical insulation and fan belts.
