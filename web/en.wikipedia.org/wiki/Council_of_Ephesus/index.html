Council of Ephesus

The Council of Ephesus was a council of Christian bishops convened in Ephesus (near present-day Selçuk, Turkey) in AD 431 by the Roman emperor Theodosius II. It is counted as the third ecumenical council by the Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and many Protestant churches.

Background and decisions

The council was called to resolve the dispute between Nestorius, Patriarch of Constantinople, and Cyril of Alexandria over whether the Virgin Mary should be called Theotokos ("God-bearer") or only Christotokos ("Christ-bearer"). Presided over by Cyril, the council affirmed Mary as Theotokos, condemned Nestorius's teaching as heresy and deposed him, and confirmed the Nicene Creed, forbidding any alteration of it.

Aftermath

The council deepened division with the Church of the East, associated with Nestorius. A compromise Formula of Reunion followed in 433, but controversies continued to the Council of Chalcedon in 451. A rival gathering of bishops in 449, also at Ephesus, was branded the "Robber Council" (Latrocinium) by Pope Leo I and is not recognised as the Third Ecumenical Council.
