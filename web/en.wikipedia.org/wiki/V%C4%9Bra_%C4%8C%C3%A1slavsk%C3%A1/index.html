Věra Čáslavská (3 May 1942 – 30 August 2016) was a Czechoslovak artistic gymnast and Czech civil-rights advocate. She won eleven Olympic medals, including seven golds, at the 1964 Tokyo and 1968 Mexico City Games, and remains the most decorated Czech gymnast in history.

Gymnastics career

Čáslavská first competed internationally as a figure skater and dancer before switching to gymnastics. At the 1964 Tokyo Olympics she won gold in the all-around, vault, and balance beam. At Mexico City in 1968 she won four more gold medals, defeating Soviet rivals shortly after the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia. Her floor routine, performed to the music of "Jarabe Tapatío", made her a favourite of the Mexican crowd, and she married fellow Olympian Josef Odložil in Mexico City Cathedral during the Games.

Protest and persecution

Čáslavská had signed the "Two Thousand Words" manifesto supporting the Prague Spring. On the medal podium in Mexico City, during the Soviet anthem, she quietly turned her head down and to the right — a silent protest against the invasion. After returning home she was punished: forced to retire, denied work as a coach, and barred from travel for years.

Later life

After the Velvet Revolution of 1989 she was rehabilitated and served as president of the Czech Olympic Committee and an adviser to President Václav Havel. She died of pancreatic cancer in Prague in 2016.
