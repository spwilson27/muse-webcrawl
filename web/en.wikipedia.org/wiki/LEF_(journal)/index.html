LEF (journal)

LEF
Full titleLEF: Journal of the Left Front of the Arts (ЛЕФ)
Published1923–1925 (LEF); 1927–1928 (Novyi LEF)
EditorVladimir Mayakovsky (with Osip Brik)
PlaceMoscow, Soviet Union

LEF (Russian: ЛЕФ, "Left Front of the Arts") was a Soviet avant-garde journal published in Moscow from 1923 to 1925, and revived as Novyi LEF ("New LEF") from 1927 to 1928. Edited by the poet Vladimir Mayakovsky and the theorist Osip Brik, it was the organ of the LEF group of Constructivist and Futurist artists and writers.

Programme and contributors

LEF championed "art into life": productivism, factography, documentary film and photography, and literature of fact over fiction. Its contributors and associates included Alexander Rodchenko (who designed its striking covers), Dziga Vertov, Sergei Eisenstein, Viktor Shklovsky, Boris Arvatov, Sergei Tretyakov and Lilya Brik. The journal published film scenarios, photomontage, theoretical manifestos and agitational poetry.

Decline

Criticised as formalist by rival proletarian literary organisations and under growing political pressure, LEF folded in 1925; its successor Novyi LEF (1927–28) continued the programme before Mayakovsky resigned amid disputes in 1928. The journal remains a key document of the Soviet avant-garde.
