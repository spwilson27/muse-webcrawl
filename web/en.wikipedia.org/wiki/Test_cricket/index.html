Test cricket

Test cricket is a form of first-class cricket played at the international level between teams representing full member countries of the International Cricket Council (ICC). A Test match takes place over a period of up to five days, with each team batting up to two innings; it is considered the game's highest standard and its ultimate test of endurance and skill. Test matches are played in series, often for a trophy such as the Ashes between England and Australia.

History

The first officially recognized Test match took place in March 1877 between Australia and England at the Melbourne Cricket Ground; Australia won by 45 runs. Test status was gradually extended to South Africa (1889), the West Indies (1928), New Zealand (1930), India (1932), Pakistan (1952), Sri Lanka (1982), Zimbabwe (1992), Bangladesh (2000), and most recently Ireland and Afghanistan (2018), for twelve Test-playing nations in total.

Format

Matches are scheduled for five days of six hours' play, with a minimum of 90 overs per day. Teams wear white clothing and use a red ball (pink for day/night Tests, introduced in 2015). Results may be a win, a draw, or — rarely — a tie; only two Tests in history have been tied. Since 2019 most bilateral series have formed part of the ICC World Test Championship, which culminates in a final between the two top teams.
