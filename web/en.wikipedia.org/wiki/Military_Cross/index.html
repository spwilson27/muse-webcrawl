Military Cross

The Military Cross (MC) is the third-level military decoration awarded for gallantry during active operations against the enemy to members of the British Armed Forces and, formerly, to personnel of other Commonwealth countries. Established in December 1914 during the First World War, it sits below the Victoria Cross and the Distinguished Service Order in the order of wear, and recipients are entitled to the postnominal letters "MC".

History

The Military Cross was instituted by Royal Warrant on 28 December 1914, to recognize commissioned officers and warrant officers for distinguished and meritorious service in battle — previously, junior officers had lacked a gallantry award of their own. Its other-ranks counterpart, the Military Medal, followed in 1916. More than 37,000 MCs were awarded in the First World War, when recipients included the poet Siegfried Sassoon and, three times over with two bars, the remarkable "Mad Jack" Churchill in the Second World War, in which some 11,000 were awarded.

Following the 1993 review of gallantry awards, rank distinctions were removed and the MC became available to all ranks for gallantry on land operations, replacing the Military Medal for this purpose.

Description

The medal is an ornamental straight cross pattée in silver, bearing the royal cypher at its center and imperial crowns at the ends of its arms, suspended from a ribbon of equal stripes of white, purple, and white. Additional acts of gallantry are recognized by bars worn on the ribbon. The reverse is plain except for the name of the recipient engraved on some issues.
