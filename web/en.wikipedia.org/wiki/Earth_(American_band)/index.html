Earth is an American drone metal band formed in Olympia, Washington, in 1989 by guitarist Dylan Carlson. Generally regarded as the pioneers of drone metal, the band built its early sound from extremely slow, distorted, repetitive guitar riffs sustained over long durations. After a hiatus in the late 1990s, Earth returned in the 2000s with a transformed sound incorporating country, jazz and Americana influences, while retaining its characteristic minimalism and slow tempos.

History

Formation and Sub Pop years (1989–1997)

Carlson formed Earth in 1989, naming the band after the original name of Black Sabbath. The band's early lineup included Slim Moon and Greg Babior; during this era Carlson was a close friend of Kurt Cobain of Nirvana, and Cobain sang on the Earth song "Divine and Bright" on the Demons material. After demos (released later as Extra-Capsular Extraction, 1991), Earth signed to Sub Pop and released Earth 2: Special Low Frequency Version (1993), a landmark drone record consisting of three tracks of subterranean guitar drone. Phase 3: Thrones and Dominions (1995) and Pentastar: In the Style of Demons (1996) introduced more conventional rock structures; the band dissolved in 1997 amid Carlson's legal and personal troubles.

Return and reinvention (2003–present)

Earth reformed around Carlson and drummer Adrienne Davies, releasing Living in the Gleam of an Unsheathed Sword (2005) and the acclaimed Hex; Or Printing in the Infernal Method (2005), whose desolate, twang-inflected instrumentals drew comparisons to Ennio Morricone and Neil Young. The Bees Made Honey in the Lion's Skull (2008), featuring guitarist Bill Frisell, added jazz colour. The two-part Angels of Darkness, Demons of Light (2011–2012) introduced cello; Primitive and Deadly (2014) brought vocals back with guests Mark Lanegan and Rabia Shaheen Qazi; Full Upon Her Burning Lips (2019) returned to the core duo of guitar and drums.

Musical style and influence

Earth's early music reduced heavy metal to its essence: a single riff, played at punishing volume and glacial pace, exploring the physical properties of sound and feedback. Artists including Sunn O))), Boris and the wider drone and post-metal scenes cite Earth as a formative influence. Carlson has also recorded solo work, drawing on English folk and ballad traditions.
