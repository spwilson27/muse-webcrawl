The Prime Minister of Malaysia (Malay: Perdana Menteri Malaysia) is the head of government of Malaysia. The prime minister directs the executive branch, chairs the cabinet, and is appointed by the Yang di-Pertuan Agong (the king) from among the members of the Dewan Rakyat (House of Representatives) who, in the king's judgment, commands the confidence of the majority. The official residence is Seri Perdana in Putrajaya, and the prime minister's office is at Perdana Putra.

History

The office dates to independence in 1957, when Tunku Abdul Rahman, the "Father of Independence," became the first prime minister of the Federation of Malaya. His successors Tun Abdul Razak and Tun Hussein Onn were likewise fathers of later leaders. Mahathir Mohamad dominated the office from 1981 to 2003, presiding over rapid industrialization; he was followed by Abdullah Ahmad Badawi and Najib Razak, the latter defeated in 2018 amid the 1MDB scandal — the first change of government in Malaysian history. Remarkably, Mahathir returned as prime minister at age 92 in 2018, the world's oldest head of government. His government's collapse in 2020 brought Muhyiddin Yassin and then Ismail Sabri Yaakob to power, before longtime opposition leader Anwar Ibrahim was appointed prime minister on 24 November 2022, heading a unity government after a hung parliament.

Powers

The prime minister advises the king on most appointments, can request the dissolution of parliament, and controls the cabinet and the federal civil service. Although the constitution vests executive authority in the king, by convention the king acts on the prime minister's advice. Anti-party-hopping legislation enacted in 2022 was intended to stabilize governments after a period of defections.
