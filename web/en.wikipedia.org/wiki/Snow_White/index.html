"Snow White" (German: Sneewittchen) is a German fairy tale, first published by the Brothers Grimm in 1812 in the first edition of Grimms' Fairy Tales (tale no. 53). It tells of a beautiful princess whose jealous stepmother, the Evil Queen, repeatedly attempts to kill her because a magic mirror proclaims Snow White "the fairest of them all". The tale is classified as Aarne–Thompson–Uther type 709, and versions of it are known across Europe.

Plot

A queen sewing by a window in winter pricks her finger; seeing drops of blood on the snow, she wishes for a daughter with skin white as snow, lips red as blood, and hair black as ebony. The child, Snow White, is born, but the queen dies and the king remarries. The new queen possesses a magic mirror which she asks daily, "Mirror, mirror on the wall, who is the fairest of them all?" — and is enraged when the mirror names Snow White.

The queen orders a huntsman to kill the girl, but he spares her; Snow White flees through the forest and finds the cottage of seven dwarfs, who shelter her in exchange for housekeeping. The queen, learning from the mirror that Snow White lives, disguises herself three times: with tight stays (bodice laces) that suffocate her, with a poisoned comb, and finally with a poisoned apple. The dwarfs revive her after the first two attempts, but the apple lodges in her throat and she falls into a death-like sleep. The dwarfs place her in a glass coffin. A prince finds her; as the coffin is carried away, the piece of apple is dislodged from her throat and she awakens. Snow White marries the prince, and the queen, invited to the wedding, is forced to dance in red-hot iron shoes until she dies.

History and analysis

The Grimms revised the tale through successive editions; in the earliest manuscript version the villain was Snow White's biological mother, later changed to a stepmother. Scholars have debated possible historical models — including Margaretha von Waldeck (1533–1554) and Maria Sophia von Erthal (1725–1796) of Lohr am Main — but no identification is certain. The tale's motifs (the magic mirror, the poisoned apple, the glass coffin, the seven dwarfs) draw on widespread European folklore.

Adaptations

The most famous adaptation is Walt Disney's 1937 animated film Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs, the first full-length cel-animated feature film, which gave the dwarfs individual names and softened the queen's punishment. The story has inspired numerous other films, stage works, ballets and retellings in literature.
