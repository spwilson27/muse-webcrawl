Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2016

Long titleAn Act of the Scottish Parliament to make provision about land, and related matters
Citation2016 asp 18
Passed22 March 2016
Royal assent22 April 2016
Preceded byLand Reform (Scotland) Act 2003

The Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2016 is an Act of the Scottish Parliament intended to reform the system of land ownership in Scotland, where a small number of owners historically hold a large share of rural land. It followed the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 and the work of the Land Reform Review Group (2014).

Provisions

Key measures include:

  • A Land Rights and Responsibilities Statement setting out the Scottish Government's vision for land ownership and use.
  • Creation of the Scottish Land Commission to review land policy and promote reform.
  • A right to buy land to further sustainable development, extending community right-to-buy powers (previously centred on crofting and rural communities) to communities across Scotland, including, in defined circumstances, compulsory purchase without a willing seller.
  • Greater transparency of land ownership, including a public register of persons with controlling interests in land, and removal of business-rate exemptions for shooting and deerstalking estates.
  • Reforms to agricultural holdings, including a modernised regime for secure tenancies and an amnesty for tenants' improvements, plus a Tenant Farming Commissioner.
  • Powers to require information about land ownership and to intervene where land management breaches the Land Rights and Responsibilities Statement.
