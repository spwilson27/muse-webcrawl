Corset

A corset is a support garment commonly worn to hold and train the torso into a desired shape, traditionally a smaller waist or larger bottom, for aesthetic or medical purposes, either for the duration of the corset's use or with the aim of a lasting effect. Both women and men have worn corsets, although the garment has been associated primarily with women.

Corset

Type: Support / foundation garment

Materials: Fabric stiffened with boning (whalebone, steel, plastic)

Peak popularity: 19th century

History

Stiffened bodices and "stays" were worn from the sixteenth century; the nineteenth-century corset, shaped with gussets and later with spoon busks and steel boning, created the hourglass silhouette fashionable from the 1830s to the early 1900s. Tightlacing was practised by a minority and debated by doctors and dress reformers. Corsetry declined in the 1920s with changing fashions, but corsets remain in use in haute couture, historical re-enactment, orthopaedic support and modern waist training.

Construction

A corset is made of panels of strong fabric such as coutil, stiffened with boning channels and closed with lacing at the back and a split busk at the front. Fit and gradual reduction are considered essential to comfort and safety.
