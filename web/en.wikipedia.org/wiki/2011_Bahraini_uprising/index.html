2011 Bahraini uprising

Date14 February – 18 March 2011 (major phase)
LocationBahrain, centred on Manama
CausesPolitical marginalisation of the Shia majority, inequality, demands for constitutional reform
ResultUprising suppressed; state of emergency; reforms limited
Deaths93+ (per subsequent inquiries and activists)

The 2011 Bahraini uprising was a series of anti-government protests in the Gulf kingdom of Bahrain, inspired by the Arab Spring movements in Tunisia and Egypt. Beginning with a "Day of Rage" on 14 February 2011, tens of thousands of Bahrainis — drawn largely, though not exclusively, from the country's Shia Muslim majority — demanded political reform, an elected government, a constitutional monarchy and an end to discrimination.

Course of events

Protesters occupied the Pearl Roundabout in the capital, Manama, which became the symbolic centre of the movement. In the early hours of 17 February, police raided the sleeping camp, killing several protesters. After a period of mass demonstrations and a general strike, the government requested intervention by the Gulf Cooperation Council; on 14 March, Saudi-led Peninsula Shield Force troops entered Bahrain. A state of emergency was declared, the Pearl Roundabout was cleared by force, its monument demolished, and hundreds of activists, opposition figures and medics were arrested.

Aftermath

The Bahrain Independent Commission of Inquiry, appointed by the king, reported in November 2011 that security forces had used excessive force and torture; the government accepted its findings but opposition groups said implementation was inadequate. The 2011 Formula One Bahrain Grand Prix was cancelled amid the unrest. Sporadic protests and clashes continued for years, and the uprising left a lasting mark on Bahrain's politics and its relations with Iran, which the Bahraini government accused of interference.
