Heath

A heath (or heathland) is a shrubland habitat found mainly on free-draining, infertile, acidic soils, characterised by open, low-growing woody vegetation dominated by plants of the heather family (Ericaceae), such as heather (Calluna vulgaris), bell heather and gorse.

Distribution

Heathlands occur in north-western Europe (Britain, Ireland, the Netherlands, Denmark and northern Germany), coastal Australia, South Africa's fynbos, and other regions with poor soils. In lowland Britain much heathland is a semi-natural habitat created and maintained by centuries of grazing, burning and turf cutting following prehistoric woodland clearance.

Ecology and conservation

Heaths support specialised wildlife including nightjars, Dartford warblers, sand lizards and silver-studded blue butterflies. Large areas have been lost to agriculture, forestry and development, and remaining heaths are protected and managed by grazing and controlled burning to prevent succession to woodland.
