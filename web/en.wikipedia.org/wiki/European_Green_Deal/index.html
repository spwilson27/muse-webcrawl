The European Green Deal, approved in 2020, is a set of policy initiatives by the European Commission with the overarching aim of making the European Union (EU) climate neutral by 2050. Announced by Commission President Ursula von der Leyen in December 2019 and described by her as Europe's "man on the moon moment", it is the flagship environmental programme of her Commission.

Main elements

The Deal spans climate, energy, transport, industry, agriculture, and biodiversity. Its central legal instrument, the European Climate Law of 2021, wrote the 2050 climate-neutrality target and an intermediate target of cutting net greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% by 2030 (compared with 1990) into binding law. The "Fit for 55" package of 2021 proposed revisions to the EU Emissions Trading System, effort-sharing rules, renewable energy and energy efficiency directives, and CO₂ standards for cars.

Associated initiatives

  • The Farm to Fork Strategy for sustainable food systems
  • The EU Biodiversity Strategy for 2030
  • The Circular Economy Action Plan
  • The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), adopted in 2023, to price carbon on certain imports
  • The Just Transition Mechanism, supporting regions most affected by the move away from fossil fuels
  • The REPowerEU plan (2022), accelerating the shift after Russia's invasion of Ukraine

Criticism

The Deal has been criticised from several directions: environmental groups have argued its agricultural and biomass provisions are too weak, while farming organisations and some member states protested measures such as pesticide reduction targets, contributing to the farmers' protests of 2024 and the withdrawal of parts of the programme.
