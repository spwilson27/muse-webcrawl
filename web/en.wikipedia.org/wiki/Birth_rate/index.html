Birth rate

Countries by crude birth rate.

The birth rate, also known as natality, is the total number of live human births per 1,000 population in a given period, generally divided by the length of the period in years. The birth rate (along with mortality and migration) is used to calculate population growth. The estimated average population may be taken as the mid-year population.

The crude birth rate (CBR) is calculated as the number of live births in a year divided by the mid-year population, multiplied by 1,000. When the crude death rate is subtracted from the crude birth rate, the result is the rate of natural increase. Globally, the crude birth rate has declined from roughly 37 per 1,000 in 1950 to about 17–18 per 1,000 in the early 2020s.

Measurement

More refined measures include the general fertility rate (births per 1,000 women of childbearing age) and the total fertility rate (TFR), the average number of children a woman would have over her lifetime; a TFR of about 2.1 is the "replacement level" in low-mortality populations. Age-specific fertility rates describe births by the mother's age group.

Factors

Birth rates are influenced by socioeconomic development, urbanisation, female education and labour-force participation, access to contraception, cultural and religious norms, government pro- or anti-natalist policies, and economic conditions. The demographic transition model describes the historical shift from high birth and death rates to low birth and death rates as societies industrialise. The highest birth rates are found in sub-Saharan Africa — Niger's total fertility rate exceeds six children per woman — while many countries in Europe and East Asia have birth rates well below replacement level, producing population ageing and decline.
