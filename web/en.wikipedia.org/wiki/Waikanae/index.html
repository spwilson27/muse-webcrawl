Waikanae

Country: New Zealand

Region: Wellington Region

District: Kāpiti Coast District

Population: Around 13,000 (including Waikanae Beach)

Waikanae (Māori: Waikanae) is a town on the Kāpiti Coast of New Zealand's North Island, about 60 kilometres north of Wellington. It lies between the Waikanae River and the Tasman Sea coast, with the settlements of Waikanae Beach and Peka Peka nearby. Its name means "waters of the kanae" — the kanae being the yellow-eyed mullet once abundant in local waters.

History

The area was settled by Māori for centuries, and the Kāpiti Coast was the scene of fighting in the Musket Wars era involving Te Rauparaha and Ngāti Toa. European settlement followed in the 19th century, and the town developed as a farming community after the North Island Main Trunk railway arrived in 1886. The Kiwi actor and writer's residence aside, Waikanae long served as a seaside holiday and retirement destination for Wellingtonians.

Attractions and transport

Waikanae is known for its long sandy beach, the Waikanae River estuary — a scientific reserve important for migratory birds — and views across to Kapiti Island, a nature reserve about 5 kilometres offshore. The town is a gateway to the Tararua Range foothills and the Akatarawa Forest, and is home to the Nga Manu Nature Reserve. Waikanae railway station is the northern terminus of Wellington's Kapiti Line commuter services, with electrification extended from Paraparaumu in 2011, and the town is served by the Kāpiti Expressway (State Highway 1).
