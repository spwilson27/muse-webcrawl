Cuisine of Montreal


Iconic Montreal foods: poutine, smoked meat and hand-rolled bagels

The cuisine of Montreal, the largest city in Quebec, Canada, reflects the city's French colonial heritage, its large Jewish, Italian, and other immigrant communities, and its position as one of North America's most celebrated food cities. Montreal is known for several distinctive dishes, including poutine, Montreal-style smoked meat, and Montreal-style bagels.

Signature dishes

Montreal-style bagels

Montreal bagels are smaller, denser and sweeter than New York bagels. They are hand-rolled, boiled in honey-sweetened water, and baked in wood-fired ovens. The two most famous bakeries, St-Viateur Bagel and Fairmount Bagel, date to the mid-twentieth century and supply shops across the city; classic varieties are sesame and poppy seed.

Smoked meat

Montreal smoked meat is a kosher-style deli meat made by salting and curing beef brisket with spices, smoking it, and steaming it before serving it thick-sliced on rye bread with mustard. It is closely associated with delicatessens such as Schwartz's, founded in 1928, and is similar to but distinct from New York pastrami in its spice blend and preparation.

Poutine

Poutine — French fries topped with cheese curds and brown gravy — originated in rural Quebec in the late 1950s and became a Montreal institution, served in diners (casse-croûtes) and snack bars across the city, with La Banquise among the best-known late-night poutineries offering dozens of variations.

Culinary scene

Montreal has one of the highest numbers of restaurants per capita in North America. Its French-influenced fine dining, exemplified by restaurants such as Joe Beef and Toqué!, coexists with Portuguese grilled chicken shops on the Plateau, Haitian and Vietnamese restaurants, Italian cafés in Little Italy, and public markets such as Jean-Talon Market and Atwater Market that anchor the city's food culture.
