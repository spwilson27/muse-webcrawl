Phonics


Phonics is a method of teaching reading that emphasises the correspondence between letters (graphemes) and sounds (phonemes).

Phonics is a method for teaching people how to read and write an alphabetic language. It aims to develop phonemic awareness — the ability to hear, identify and manipulate phonemes — and to teach the systematic relationships between letters or letter groups (graphemes) and the sounds they represent, so that learners can decode unfamiliar printed words by sounding them out and blending the sounds together.

Approaches

Synthetic phonics teaches letter–sound correspondences in an explicit sequence, and pupils blend sounds from the start to read words. Analytic phonics begins with whole familiar words and analyses their component sounds by analogy with known words. Embedded phonics teaches letter sounds incidentally within meaningful text. Most programmes also teach a stock of "sight words" whose spellings are irregular.

Debate

Phonics has been one side of the long-running "reading wars" with whole-language approaches. Reviews such as the U.S. National Reading Panel report (2000) and the Rose Review in England (2006) concluded that systematic phonics instruction is an effective component of early reading instruction, and England introduced a statutory phonics screening check for six-year-olds in 2012. Critics caution that phonics alone does not guarantee comprehension and that English orthography's irregularities limit pure decoding, while proponents of the "science of reading" movement emphasise combining phonics with vocabulary, fluency and comprehension instruction.
