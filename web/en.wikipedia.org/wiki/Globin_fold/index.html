Globin fold

The globin fold is a common three-dimensional protein fold found in the globin family of proteins. It consists of eight alpha helices, conventionally labelled A through H, arranged around a haem (heme) group held in a hydrophobic pocket. The structure was first described for myoglobin, whose crystal structure was solved by John Kendrew in 1958 — the first atomic structure of a protein.

Members

Proteins with the globin fold include the haemoglobin subunits, myoglobin, neuroglobin, cytoglobin, leghemoglobins of plants, and numerous bacterial and archaeal haem sensors. The haem iron binds oxygen reversibly in the respiratory proteins and is coordinated by a proximal histidine residue.

Variations

Some family members are truncated globins with only six helices, and 2/2 globins found in bacteria and plants. Despite very low sequence identity between distant members, the fold and the haem-binding geometry are strongly conserved.
