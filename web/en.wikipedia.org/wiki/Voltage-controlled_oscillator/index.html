Voltage-controlled oscillator

A voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO) is an electronic oscillator whose oscillation frequency is controlled by a voltage input. The applied input voltage determines the instantaneous oscillation frequency, so by varying a DC or slowly varying control voltage the frequency of the output signal can be adjusted across a tuning range.

Types

VCOs are commonly divided into harmonic oscillators and relaxation oscillators. Harmonic VCOs — LC, crystal, and RC oscillators — use a resonant circuit whose frequency is varied, usually with a varactor diode whose capacitance changes with the applied reverse voltage. Relaxation VCOs, such as emitter-coupled and op-amp designs, generate a waveform by periodically charging and discharging a capacitor at a rate set by the control voltage.

Applications

VCOs are fundamental building blocks in:

  • Phase-locked loops (PLLs), where the VCO is steered until its output locks to a reference — used in frequency synthesisers, clock recovery, and demodulators.
  • Frequency and phase modulation: applying the modulating signal to the VCO input generates FM directly.
  • Function generators and sweep generators for test equipment.
  • Music synthesisers, where VCOs following the 1 volt-per-octave standard are the principal sound source of analogue synthesisers.
  • Clock generation and jitter cleanup in digital systems, including voltage-controlled crystal oscillators (VCXOs).

Performance measures

Key parameters include tuning range, tuning gain (KVCO, in Hz per volt), phase noise, linearity of the frequency-versus-voltage characteristic, and pushing and pulling (sensitivity to supply voltage and load). Phase noise performance distinguishes VCO designs used in demanding radio applications from simple general-purpose designs.
