Demoiselle crane

The demoiselle crane (Grus virgo, also placed in the genus Anthropoides) is a species of crane found in central Eurosiberia, ranging from the Black Sea to Mongolia and northeast China. It is the smallest species of crane, 85–100 cm long with a 155–180 cm wingspan, and is recognisable by its slate-grey plumage, black head and neck, striking white ear tufts, and red eyes.

Migration

The demoiselle crane is migratory. Birds from western Eurasia winter in Africa, while those from central and eastern Asia winter in the Indian subcontinent. Their migration is among the most arduous of any bird: Asian populations cross the Himalayas at altitudes of up to about 7,900 m, enduring thin air, freezing temperatures, and predation by eagles. The village of Khichan in Rajasthan, India, is famous for the thousands of demoiselle cranes that villagers feed each winter.

Behaviour and culture

Demoiselle cranes breed on dry steppe grasslands near water, laying two eggs in a simple ground nest. They feed on insects, seeds, lizards, and other small animals. The bird holds an honoured place in the cultures of North India and Pakistan — known as koonj, it appears in poetry and legend as a symbol of fidelity and courage, its name even traditionally given to formations of soldiers in battle.
