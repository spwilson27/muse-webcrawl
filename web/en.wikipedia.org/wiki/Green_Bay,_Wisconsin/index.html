Green Bay is a city in and the county seat of Brown County in the U.S. state of Wisconsin, at the head of Green Bay, a sub-basin of Lake Michigan, at the mouth of the Fox River. It is 581 feet (177 m) above sea level and 112 miles (180 km) north of Milwaukee. The population was 107,395 at the 2020 census. Green Bay is the principal city of the Green Bay Metropolitan Statistical Area, which covers Brown, Kewaunee, and Oconto counties.

Green Bay is home to the National Football League's Green Bay Packers, the only fan-owned major league franchise in the United States. Founded in 1919, the Packers are the third-oldest franchise in the NFL. The team plays its home games at Lambeau Field, one of the most storied stadiums in American sports.

History

The site of modern Green Bay was first visited by French explorer Jean Nicolet in 1634, who encountered the Ho-Chunk (Winnebago) people there. A French fur trading post, La Baye, was established in 1634. Green Bay is one of the oldest European settlements in the Americas, founded officially as a town in 1854 and incorporated as a city the same year.

The fur trade dominated the local economy through the early 19th century. By the mid-1800s, the city had become an important port and lumber trading center. The city nickname "Titletown" refers to the Packers' many NFL championships.

Geography

Green Bay is located in northeastern Wisconsin on the western shore of Green Bay at the mouth of the Fox River. According to the United States Census Bureau, the city has a total area of 55.96 square miles (144.94 km²), of which 45.47 square miles is land and 10.49 square miles is water.

Climate

Green Bay has a warm-summer humid continental climate. Winters are long and cold with significant snowfall, while summers are warm and humid. January average high temperatures are around 25 °F (−4 °C), while July averages reach about 81 °F (27 °C).

Economy

Green Bay's economy is dominated by the meatpacking industry, paper production (the city has been called the "Toilet Paper Capital of the World"), and shipping. Major employers include Georgia-Pacific, Schneider National, and Humana. The Port of Green Bay is a major Great Lakes shipping port handling bulk cargoes such as coal, salt, and cement.

Sports

The Green Bay Packers have won 13 league championships, more than any other NFL franchise, including four Super Bowls. Lambeau Field, opened in 1957, is the oldest continuously operating NFL stadium. The city also hosts the Green Bay Gamblers of the USHL and the University of Wisconsin–Green Bay Phoenix athletic teams.

Education

The city is served by the Green Bay Area Public School District. Higher education institutions include the University of Wisconsin–Green Bay, St. Norbert College (in nearby De Pere), and Northeast Wisconsin Technical College.
