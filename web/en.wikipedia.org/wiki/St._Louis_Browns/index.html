St. Louis Browns

The St. Louis Browns were an American League baseball team that played in St. Louis, Missouri, from 1902 through 1953. Originally the Milwaukee Brewers of 1901, the franchise relocated to St. Louis and took the Browns name, reviving the identity of the famous 1880s St. Louis Brown Stockings. The Browns were perennial underachievers, winning only one American League pennant—in the war-depleted 1944 season—before moving to Baltimore in 1954 and becoming the Baltimore Orioles.

History

The Browns arrived in St. Louis in 1902 and finished second in their first season, but rarely contended thereafter. Their best sustained era came in the 1920s, anchored by Hall of Fame first baseman George Sisler, who hit .420 in 1922, and outfielder Ken Williams. Despite stars, the club was overshadowed in its own city by the National League Cardinals, with whom it shared Sportsman's Park—and after the mid-1920s, almost always overshadowed in the standings as well.

The 1944 pennant and decline

The Browns' lone pennant came in 1944, when wartime manpower shortages levelled the league; they won the flag on the final day and lost the all-St. Louis World Series to the Cardinals in six games. The following year the Browns employed Pete Gray, a one-armed outfielder. Postwar the club sank again, and owner Bill Veeck resorted to famous stunts—most notoriously sending 3-foot-7-inch Eddie Gaedel to bat in 1951, wearing number ⅛. Veeck also signed the veteran Negro league star Satchel Paige. Unable to outdraw the Cardinals, Veeck sold the club, and after the 1953 season the franchise moved to Baltimore.

Legacy

The Browns are remembered as baseball's lovable losers—they finished in the second division in 41 of their 52 seasons and lost 100 or more games eight times. Their history is nonetheless rich in characters and curiosities, and their lineage continues today in the Baltimore Orioles. A dedicated society of fans, the St. Louis Browns Historical Society, kept the team's memory alive for decades.
