Internet culture

Internet culture is the set of cultures and cultural practices that have emerged from and within the Internet and online communities. It encompasses distinctive languages and slang, memes, participatory media, online communities, and norms of behaviour that developed on bulletin board systems, Usenet, forums, blogs, and social media platforms.

History

Early internet culture grew on Usenet newsgroups, IRC channels, and text-based communities, where practices such as flaming, trolling, FAQ documents, and netiquette developed. The rise of the World Wide Web in the 1990s brought personal homepages, webrings, and forums; broadband and Web 2.0 platforms in the 2000s enabled mass participation through blogs, wikis, YouTube, and social networks.

Elements

Internet memes — images, videos, phrases, and formats copied and remixed with variation — are a characteristic unit of online culture. Other elements include viral content, reaction images and GIFs, hashtags, copypasta, creepypasta, fan fiction and fan art, speedrunning and streaming cultures, and collaborative projects such as Wikipedia itself.

Communities and identity

Online communities form around shared interests rather than geography. Anonymity and pseudonymity shape behaviour, enabling both candid self-expression and disinhibition. Scholars study internet culture within sociology, anthropology, and media studies, examining how offline and online cultures increasingly interpenetrate.
