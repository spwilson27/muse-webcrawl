Legal sex

Legal sex is the sex or gender classification assigned to a person under the law of a jurisdiction. It is normally recorded at birth based on the appearance of the infant's external anatomy and entered on the birth certificate, and it appears thereafter on identity documents such as passports and driving licences.

Legal consequences

Legal sex may determine a person's treatment in areas including marriage, prisons and single-sex services, sex-segregated sport, military conscription, and statistical and anti-discrimination law. Most legal systems recognise only male and female, though a growing number provide a third or non-binary marker, such as the "X" marker available on passports in several countries.

Changing legal sex

Many jurisdictions permit transgender and intersex people to amend their legal sex. Requirements vary widely: some historically demanded surgery or sterilisation, a requirement the European Court of Human Rights condemned in 2017, while others — beginning with Argentina's Gender Identity Law of 2012 — allow change by simple self-declaration. The interaction between legal sex and gender identity remains an active area of legal and political debate.
