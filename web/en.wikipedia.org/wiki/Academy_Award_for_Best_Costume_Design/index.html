The Academy Award for Best Costume Design is an Academy Award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS) for the achievement of film costume design of the previous year. The award was first given for films made in 1948, when separate awards were made for black-and-white and colour films. The two categories were combined in 1957, briefly separated again in the mid-1960s, and have been combined ever since.

History

When the category was introduced at the 21st Academy Awards, the first winners were Roger K. Furse for the black-and-white film Hamlet and Dorothy Jeakins and Barbara Karinska for the colour film Joan of Arc. From 1967 the award has been a single category. Unlike some craft categories, winners need not be members of any particular guild, though the award is chosen by Academy members from five nominees selected by the costume designers branch.

Edith Head holds the records both for most wins (eight) and most nominations (thirty-five) in the category. More recent winners have included Mark Bridges (Phantom Thread), Ruth E. Carter (Black Panther and Black Panther: Wakanda Forever — the first person to win the award twice for the same franchise), Jenny Beavan (Cruella), Jacqueline Durran (Little Women), and Holly Waddington (Poor Things). Period and fantasy films win most often, and contemporary-set winners are comparatively rare.

Selection and rules

Candidates are nominated by the members of the Academy's Costume Designers Branch and voted on by the full membership. Eligibility generally requires that the costumes be conceived by a credited costume designer and executed specifically for the film. Since 2013, the branch has also presented the nomination process alongside a public exhibition of the nominated designs at the FIDM Museum in Los Angeles.
