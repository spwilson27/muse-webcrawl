Bermondsey

Bermondsey Street, now a centre of restaurants and galleries, once served the leather trade.

Bermondsey is a district in south-east London, in the London Borough of Southwark, on the south bank of the River Thames opposite the City of London and Wapping. Long associated with the leather industry and riverside wharves, it was a famously tight-knit working-class neighbourhood and has, since the late 20th century, become a centre for food, fashion and the arts.

History

The name derives from Beormund's eg — the island of a Saxon landowner — in the marshes south of the Thames. Bermondsey Abbey, a Cluniac priory founded in 1082, dominated the area until the Dissolution; its gatehouse and a fragment of wall survive near Bermondsey Square. From the 17th century, tanning and leather working clustered here, drawn by water, oak bark and distance from City regulation — London's leather market stood on Leathermarket Street — and the trade gave the streets a notorious smell but steady employment. The 19th century brought dense housing, riverside warehouses along Bermondsey Wall, and the first railway terminus in London at London Bridge / Spa Road in 1836.

The district's history of social reform is notable: under Ada and Alfred Salter — the "Bermondsey revolution" of the interwar years — the local council built model housing, planted trees and provided pioneering public health services. The Blitz and the decline of the docks and tanneries after 1945 changed the area profoundly.

Modern Bermondsey

Today Bermondsey Street is known for the Fashion and Textile Museum (founded by Zandra Rhodes), White Cube gallery, and independent shops and cafés, while Maltby Street Market, in the railway arches, is a celebrated food market. New Bermondsey and Bermondsey Spa regeneration schemes have brought extensive new housing, and the area is served by Bermondsey station on the Jubilee line.
