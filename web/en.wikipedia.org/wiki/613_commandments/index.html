613 commandments

The 613 commandments (Hebrew: taryag mitzvot) are the traditional enumeration of the commandments contained in the Torah (the Pentateuch). According to rabbinic tradition, the Torah contains 613 mitzvot: 248 positive commandments ("do") and 365 negative commandments ("do not").

Origins of the number

The number 613 derives from a sermon by Rabbi Simlai recorded in the Babylonian Talmud (Makkot 23b–24a), which states that 613 precepts were communicated to Moses: 365 prohibitions, corresponding to the days of the solar year, and 248 positive commandments, corresponding to the limbs of the human body. Simlai further described how later prophets reduced the principles to fewer summaries — David to eleven, Isaiah to six, Micah to three, and Habakkuk to one: "the righteous shall live by his faith."

Enumerations

Rabbi Simlai gave no list, and medieval authorities produced differing enumerations. The most influential is that of Maimonides in his Sefer ha-Mitzvot (Book of Commandments), who set out fourteen rules (shorashim) for deciding what counts as a biblical commandment. Other notable lists include those of the Halakhot Gedolot, Nachmanides (who wrote a critique of Maimonides' list), and the anonymous Sefer ha-Chinuch, which orders the commandments by the weekly Torah portion.

Observance

Many of the 613 commandments concern the Temple in Jerusalem, sacrifices, ritual purity or the civil law of an ancient agrarian society, and cannot be practised in the absence of the Temple. Rabbinic counts of the commandments applicable in the diaspora today usually arrive at figures between roughly 200 and 300. The commandments include such well-known precepts as observing the Sabbath, dietary laws (kashrut), charity, the wearing of tzitzit, and the prohibitions of murder, theft and idolatry.
