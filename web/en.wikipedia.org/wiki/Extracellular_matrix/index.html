Extracellular matrix

The extracellular matrix (ECM) is a large network of proteins and polysaccharides secreted by cells that provides structural support, adhesion, and biochemical signalling to surrounding cells in tissues.

Extracellular matrix
Components: Collagen, elastin, proteoglycans, fibronectin
Functions: Support, adhesion, signalling

Composition

Major components include collagens (tensile strength), elastin, proteoglycans and glycosaminoglycans such as hyaluronic acid forming hydrated gels, and glycoproteins fibronectin and laminin mediating cell attachment via integrins. The basement membrane is a specialised ECM sheet.

Functions and disease

ECM regulates development, wound healing and cell behaviour; its stiffness influences differentiation. Dysregulation contributes to fibrosis, cancer invasion and metastasis, and genetic disorders such as Marfan syndrome and Ehlers–Danlos syndromes.
