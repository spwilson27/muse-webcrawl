Passion fruit

Placeholder image

Passion fruit is the fruit of several plants in the genus Passiflora, most commonly Passiflora edulis. It is a pepo, a type of berry, round to oval, either yellow or dark purple at maturity, with a soft to firm, juicy interior filled with numerous seeds.

Description

The fruit is grown commercially in tropical and subtropical areas for its sweet, seedy fruit. The purple passion fruit is native to southern Brazil through Paraguay to northern Argentina. Passion fruit flowers are striking, with filaments that Spanish missionaries associated with the Passion of Christ, giving the plant its name.

Cultivation and use

Passion fruit is widely consumed fresh and as juice, often blended with other fruit juices to enhance aroma. It is used in desserts, sauces, cocktails such as the porn star martini, and confections. The fruit is a good source of vitamin C, dietary fiber, and antioxidants.

Major producing countries include Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Kenya, and Australia. Related species include the banana passionfruit and the granadilla; see Passiflora for the wider genus.
