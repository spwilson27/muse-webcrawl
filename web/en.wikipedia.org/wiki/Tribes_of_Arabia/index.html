Tribes of Arabia

The tribes of Arabia are the Arab clans and ethnic groups that have inhabited the Arabian Peninsula for millennia. Traditional Arab genealogy distinguishes the "extinct" Arabs (such as ʿĀd and Thamud), the "original" southern Arabs descended from Qahtan (Qahtanites) — including Himyar and Kindah — and the "Arabised" northern Arabs descended from Adnan (Adnanites), from whom the Quraysh of Mecca traced their line.

Organisation

Arabian society was organised by kinship: extended families formed clans, clans formed tribes (qabīla), and tribes could unite in confederations such as Quda'a, Rabi'a and Mudar. Tribal identity governed protection, marriage, blood-money (diyya) disputes and alliances. Both nomadic Bedouin herders and settled townspeople and agriculturalists were organised tribally, though their relations were often tense.

Pre-Islamic period

Before Islam, major tribes included Quraysh at Mecca, Thaqif at Taif, the Aws and Khazraj of Yathrib (Medina), Banu Hanifa in Yamama, and the Lakhmids and Ghassanids on the Byzantine and Sasanian frontiers. Tribal feuds such as the Basus War between Taghlib and Bakr became staples of Arabic oral poetry and lore.

Islamic era and after

Under Islam the tribes of Arabia supplied the armies of the early conquests, and tribal divisions shaped garrison towns such as Basra and Kufa. Tribal structures remained politically central in Arabia into the modern period: the Saudi state was built in alliance with tribes, and tribal affiliation still influences politics, law and social life across the peninsula.
