Spencer Perceval
Born: 1 November 1762, London
Died: 11 May 1812 (aged 49), Westminster, London
Prime Minister of the United Kingdom: 1809–1812
Political party: Tory

Spencer Perceval (1 November 1762 – 11 May 1812) was a British statesman and lawyer who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from October 1809 until his assassination in May 1812. He is the only British prime minister to have been assassinated, and the only solicitor-general or attorney-general to become prime minister.

Early life and career

Perceval was the second son of John Perceval, 2nd Earl of Egmont. Educated at Harrow School and Trinity College, Cambridge, he was called to the bar and built a successful legal practice, serving as counsel in the impeachment trial of Warren Hastings. He entered Parliament in 1796 as MP for Northampton.

Premiership

Perceval became Chancellor of the Exchequer in 1807 under the Duke of Portland and succeeded him as prime minister in 1809. His government prosecuted the Peninsular War against Napoleonic France, dealt with the madness of George III and the establishment of the Regency in 1811, and faced economic distress and Luddite unrest at home. A devout Evangelical Anglican, Perceval was a committed opponent of Catholic emancipation and of parliamentary reform.

Assassination

On 11 May 1812, Perceval was shot in the lobby of the House of Commons by John Bellingham, a merchant with a grievance against the government. Perceval died within minutes; Bellingham was hanged a week later. Perceval left a widow, Jane, and twelve children; Parliament voted the family a substantial grant and annuity.
