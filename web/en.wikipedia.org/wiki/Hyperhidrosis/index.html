Hyperhidrosis

Hyperhidrosis is a condition characterised by abnormally increased sweating, in excess of that required for regulation of body temperature. Although primarily a physical burden, it can significantly impair quality of life and lead to emotional distress and social avoidance.

Classification

Primary focal hyperhidrosis is excessive sweating, typically of the palms, soles, underarms or face, that has no underlying medical cause; it often begins in adolescence and may run in families. Secondary hyperhidrosis is caused by another condition or by medication, and may be generalised over the whole body; causes include infections, endocrine disorders such as hyperthyroidism, malignancy, menopause and certain drugs.

Treatment

Treatments range from strong topical antiperspirants containing aluminium chloride, through iontophoresis and botulinum toxin injections, to systemic anticholinergic medicines and, in severe refractory cases, surgery such as endoscopic thoracic sympathectomy, which carries a risk of compensatory sweating elsewhere. Microwave thermolysis and laser treatments are also used for underarm sweating.

Epidemiology

Estimates suggest that around 2–3% of the population, and in some surveys more, are affected by primary hyperhidrosis, with men and women affected roughly equally. The condition is under-reported because many sufferers do not seek medical help.
