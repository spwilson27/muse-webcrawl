Hormone replacement therapy (transgender)

Hormone replacement therapy (HRT), in the context of transgender health care, is a form of gender-affirming care in which sex hormones and other hormonal medications are administered to better align a transgender or gender-nonconforming person's secondary sexual characteristics with their gender identity. It is also known as gender-affirming hormone therapy (GAHT).

Feminizing therapy

For transgender women and transfeminine people, therapy typically combines an estrogen (usually estradiol, taken orally, transdermally, or by injection) with an antiandrogen such as spironolactone, cyproterone acetate, or a GnRH analogue. Effects include breast development, redistribution of body fat, softening of the skin, and reduced body hair growth.

Masculinizing therapy

For transgender men and transmasculine people, therapy centres on testosterone, given by injection, gel, or patch. Effects include deepening of the voice, increased muscle mass, growth of facial and body hair, and cessation of menstruation.

Guidelines and care

Care is commonly provided under guidelines issued by the World Professional Association for Transgender Health (WPATH) and the Endocrine Society. Access models vary by country and include informed-consent clinics. Regular blood monitoring is used to keep hormone levels within typical ranges and to watch for risks such as changes in blood lipids, prolactin, or haematocrit.
