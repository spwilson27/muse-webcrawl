Agrapha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Oxyrhynchus papyrus fragments preserve several agrapha

Agrapha (Greek: ἄγραφα, "unwritten things") are sayings attributed to Jesus that are not found in the canonical Gospels. The term is used by New Testament scholars for material preserved in the Acts of the Apostles, in patristic writings, in apocryphal gospels, in variant readings of New Testament manuscripts, and on papyri such as the Oxyrhynchus fragments of the Gospel of Thomas.

Examples

The best-known agraphon appears in Acts 20:35, where Paul quotes Jesus: "It is more blessed to give than to receive" — a saying found in no Gospel. Other widely discussed examples include:

  • "Become approved money-changers" (quoted by several Church Fathers).
  • "If you are at the altar and remember that your brother has something against you..." variants preserved in the Didache tradition.
  • "Let not him who seeks cease until he finds, and when he finds he shall be astonished" (from the Gospel of Thomas, logion 2, also quoted by Clement of Alexandria).
  • "Raise the stone and there you shall find me; cleave the wood and there am I" (Oxyrhynchus papyrus / Gospel of Thomas 77).
  • The story of the woman taken in adultery, though a narrative rather than a saying, is sometimes grouped with the agrapha as floating tradition.

Authenticity

Scholars assess individual agrapha by the same criteria used for canonical sayings — multiple attestation, dissimilarity, coherence, and Semitic features. A small number are judged possibly authentic by some scholars, while most are considered later compositions of the early church. The systematic collection of the material was undertaken by scholars such as Alfred Resch and J. H. Ropes in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.
