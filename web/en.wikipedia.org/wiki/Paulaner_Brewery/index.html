Paulaner Brewery

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Paulaner is a German brewery, established in 1634 in Munich by the Paulaner Order of friars. It ranks among the six breweries permitted to serve beer at Munich's Oktoberfest, and is one of the largest breweries in Germany, exporting to more than 70 countries.

History

The Minim friars of the Neudeck ob der Au monastery, followers of Saint Francis of Paola (hence "Paulaner"), brewed beer for their own use; an official complaint by Munich's commercial brewers in 1634 about the friars' sale of beer is treated as the brewery's founding document. For Lent the friars created a strong dark doppelbock, which they named Salvator — "Saviour" — in honour of Saint Francis and the electoral prince; the name became so famous that "-ator" remains the conventional suffix for doppelbocks brewed worldwide.

After secularisation in 1799 the monastery brewery passed into state and then private hands. Under Franz Xaver Zacherl in the 19th century Paulaner became a pioneer of refrigeration and steam brewing. The brewery's historic site is the Nockherberg in the Au district, famous for the annual Starkbierfest (strong beer festival) and the traditional tapping ceremony; production moved to a new large brewery at Langwied in 2016.

Beers

Paulaner's best-known products are its Hefe-Weißbier wheat beer, one of the world's best-selling wheat beers, its Münchner Hell lager, the Salvator doppelbock, and an Oktoberfest Bier. The Paulaner Brauerei Gruppe also owns brands including Hacker-Pschorr, and the company is majority-owned by the Schörghuber group with a minority stake held by Heineken.
