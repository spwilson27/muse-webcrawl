Overview

Spain–United Kingdom relations are the bilateral relations between the Kingdom of Spain and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. Their history runs from the dynastic union of Mary I and Philip II (1554–58) and the Armada war (1585–1604), through Britain's capture of Gibraltar (1704, ceded "in perpetuity" by Utrecht, 1713), alliance against Napoleon (the Peninsular War), and Spain's neutrality in the World Wars.

Modern relations

The standing dispute is Gibraltar: Spain claims sovereignty; Gibraltarians rejected shared sovereignty overwhelmingly in 2002 (as they had rejected transfer in 1967), and the UK commits to their self-determination. Franco closed the border entirely (1969–82/85). Both countries are NATO allies and major trade, tourism and investment partners — the UK is a top source of tourists to Spain and home to a large Spanish community; Spain's Santander, Iberdrola and Ferrovial are major UK investors. Brexit (Gibraltar voted 96% Remain) required a separate Gibraltar settlement: after the 2020 framework understanding, negotiators reached political agreement in principle in June 2025 on fluidity at the border/fence and airport arrangements, with treaty text to follow — as reported at the time.
