Mycorrhiza

A mycorrhiza (from Greek mykēs, fungus, and rhiza, root) is a symbiotic association between a fungus and a plant root, in which the fungus colonises the root and extends hyphae into the soil, improving the plant's uptake of water and nutrients, especially phosphorus, in exchange for photosynthetic sugars.

Types

Arbuscular mycorrhizas, formed by Glomeromycota, penetrate root cells and occur in most land plants. Ectomycorrhizas form a sheath around roots of many trees, with fungi including boletes and truffles.

Significance

Mycorrhizal networks can transfer nutrients and signals between plants, aid seedling establishment, and store carbon in soils. The symbiosis dates to the colonisation of land by plants over 400 million years ago.
