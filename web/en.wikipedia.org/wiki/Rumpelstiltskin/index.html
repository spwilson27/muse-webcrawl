Rumpelstiltskin (German: Rumpelstilzchen) is a German fairy tale collected by the Brothers Grimm in the 1812 edition of Children's and Household Tales (tale no. 55). It tells of a mysterious imp-like creature who helps a miller's daughter spin straw into gold in exchange for her firstborn child, unless she can guess his name within three days.

Plot

A miller, wishing to impress the king, falsely claims that his daughter can spin straw into gold. The king locks the girl in a tower room filled with straw and a spinning wheel and orders her to complete the task by morning on pain of death. As she despairs, a strange little man appears and offers to spin the straw for her in exchange for her necklace. The next night, in a larger room, he spins in exchange for her ring. On the third night, in a still larger room, the girl has nothing left to give, so the creature demands her firstborn child should she become queen — a condition she accepts in desperation. The king, delighted by the gold, marries her.

A year later the queen gives birth. The little man returns to claim the child, but moved by her tears he grants her three days to discover his name; if she guesses it, she may keep the child. The queen sends messengers throughout the land to gather names. On the third day a messenger reports having overheard, deep in the forest, a little man dancing around a fire and singing that his name is Rumpelstiltskin. When the queen speaks the name, Rumpelstiltskin in his fury tears himself in two (in earlier editions he stamps his foot through the floor and flies away on a cooking ladle).

Origins and variants

The tale is classified as Aarne–Thompson–Uther type 500, "The Name of the Helper," a type found across Europe and beyond, including the English "Tom-Tit-Tot" and the Scottish "Whuppity Stoorie." The German name Rumpelstilzchen derives from a type of goblin (also the name of a game), meaning roughly "little rattle-stilt." The Grimms collected the tale from the Hassenpflug family; between the 1812 and 1857 editions they progressively combined several variants into the familiar version.

Themes and analysis

Scholars have read the tale as dealing with the power of names, the miller's daughter's passage from object of exchange to agent, and the spinning of straw into gold as an image of female labour and alchemy. The motif of guessing a supernatural helper's name has parallels in traditions worldwide.

In popular culture

Rumpelstiltskin appears frequently in adaptations of the Grimms' tales, in stage musicals, and as a major character in the television series Once Upon a Time, among many other works.
