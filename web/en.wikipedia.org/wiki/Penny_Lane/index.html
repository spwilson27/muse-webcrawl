"Penny Lane" is a song by the English rock band the Beatles, released in February 1967 as a double A-side single with "Strawberry Fields Forever". It was written primarily by Paul McCartney and credited to the Lennon–McCartney partnership. The song takes its name from Penny Lane, a street in Liverpool, and evokes the everyday characters and places of a bus interchange near McCartney's childhood home.

Background and recording

Both sides of the single drew on the writers' Liverpool childhoods and were recorded for what became Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band, though EMI's demand for a single led to their exclusion from the album. "Penny Lane" features McCartney's piano, baroque orchestration arranged by George Martin, four flutes, and a celebrated piccolo trumpet solo by David Mason, inspired by a BBC broadcast of Bach's second Brandenburg Concerto, which Martin transcribed into a high, clarion solo near the song's climax.

The lyric describes the barber "showing photographs", the banker with a motor car, the fireman with an hourglass, and the "pretty nurse" selling poppies from a tray — a nostalgic snapshot animated by the refrain "Penny Lane is in my ears and in my eyes."

Reception and legacy

The single reached number one in the United States and number two in the United Kingdom — famously kept from the top by Engelbert Humperdinck's "Release Me", ending the Beatles' run of consecutive UK number ones. It was later included on the US version of the Magical Mystery Tour album (1967). Rolling Stone ranked it among the greatest songs of all time, and the street itself became a pilgrimage site; its signs were repeatedly stolen and are now painted on walls and buildings rather than mounted.
