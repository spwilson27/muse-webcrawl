Bombay Beach is a census-designated place (CDP) in Imperial County, California, United States, on the eastern shore of the Salton Sea. Once a popular resort destination in the 1950s and 1960s, it declined with the Salton Sea's ecological collapse, and has since become known for its ruins, art installations and small, eccentric community. At 223 feet (68 m) below sea level, it is one of the lowest-elevation settlements in North America.

History

During the Salton Sea's resort heyday, Bombay Beach drew boaters, water-skiers and anglers, with yacht clubs, marinas and waterfront homes. From the 1970s, rising salinity, agricultural runoff, flooding and massive fish and bird die-offs ruined the tourist trade; storms in 1976–77 flooded parts of the town, and a protective berm was built, leaving the lowest streets abandoned to the water. Many residents left, and trailers and ruins gave the town a famously post-apocalyptic appearance.

Art revival

Since the mid-2010s Bombay Beach has attracted artists drawn by its surreal landscape. The Bombay Beach Biennale, founded in 2015, is an annual art festival that has installed permanent works including an opera house, a drive-in theatre of wrecked cars and numerous sculptures. The influx has raised both the town's profile and local property prices, while the permanent population remains small (231 at the 2020 census, down from 295 in 2010). The town previously appeared in the 2010 documentary film Bombay Beach by Alma Har'el.

Geography

Bombay Beach lies along State Route 111 on the northeastern shore of the Salton Sea, south of the Salton Sea State Recreation Area. The "beach" is largely composed of the pulverised bones of dead fish rather than sand, and the shore remains affected by the sea's continuing shrinkage and salinity increase.
