Auguste and Louis Lumière

Lumière brothers
Auguste (1862–1954), Louis (1864–1948)
Born: Besançon, France
Occupation: Inventors, film pioneers, manufacturers
Known for: Cinématographe

Auguste Marie Louis Nicolas Lumière (19 October 1862 – 10 April 1954) and Louis Jean Lumière (5 October 1864 – 6 June 1948), the Lumière brothers, were French manufacturers and inventors who pioneered motion picture cameras and projection. Their Cinématographe, patented in 1895, functioned as camera, printer and projector in one device.

First films

Their first film, Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory, was shot in 1895. On 28 December 1895 at the Salon Indien du Grand Café in Paris they gave the first commercial public screening of projected motion pictures, showing a programme of short actuality films including The Arrival of a Train at La Ciotat. The brothers sent camera operators around the world to shoot and exhibit films, helping to establish cinema as a global medium.

Other work

The Lumières' company in Lyon was a major manufacturer of photographic plates; Louis also developed the Autochrome process, an early colour photography method marketed from 1907.
