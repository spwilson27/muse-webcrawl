Signalling System No. 7

Signalling System No. 7 (SS7) is a set of telephony signalling protocols developed in 1975 and defined by the ITU-T in its Q.700-series recommendations. It is used to set up and tear down telephone calls in most parts of the world on the public switched telephone network (PSTN).

Functionality

SS7 performs out-of-band signalling: call control information travels on separate channels from the voice traffic. Its functions include basic call setup, management and release; wireless services such as roaming and subscriber authentication; local number portability; toll-free (freephone) services; and enhanced call features. Protocols in the suite include MTP, SCCP, ISUP and TCAP.

Security

Because SS7 was designed for a closed network of trusted operators, it lacks strong authentication, and researchers have demonstrated attacks enabling call interception and location tracking. Successor signalling in modern networks uses protocols such as Diameter and SIP.
