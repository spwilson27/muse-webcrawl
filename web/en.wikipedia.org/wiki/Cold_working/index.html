Cold working

Cold working or cold forming is any metalworking process in which metal is shaped below its recrystallisation temperature, usually at ambient temperature. Major advantages include no heating required, better surface finish, improved dimensional control, and increased strength through strain hardening. Disadvantages include the higher forces required and reduced ductility of the worked material.

Processes

Common cold working processes include rolling, drawing, extrusion, forging, bending, shearing and cold heading. Repeated working raises the dislocation density in the metal, increasing yield strength and hardness while reducing ductility; annealing can restore ductility by allowing recrystallisation.
