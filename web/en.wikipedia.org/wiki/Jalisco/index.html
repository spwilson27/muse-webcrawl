Jalisco

Jalisco is one of the 31 states of Mexico, located on the Pacific coast of the west-central part of the country. Its capital and largest city is Guadalajara, whose metropolitan area is the second most populous in Mexico. Jalisco is the birthplace of iconic Mexican cultural traditions including mariachi music, the Mexican hat dance, and tequila.

Geography

Jalisco covers about 79,000 square kilometres and is bordered by Nayarit, Zacatecas, Aguascalientes, Guanajuato, Michoacán, and Colima, with a Pacific coastline. It contains Lake Chapala, Mexico's largest freshwater lake, and the Sierra Madre Occidental. The state is divided into 125 municipalities and has a population of over 8 million, making it one of Mexico's most populous states.

Culture and economy

The town of Tequila and the surrounding agave landscape are a UNESCO World Heritage Site; tequila and mezcal production are central to the state's identity and economy. Guadalajara is a major center for electronics and software (sometimes called Mexico's Silicon Valley), while Puerto Vallarta is a leading beach resort. Jalisco is also famous for charrería (the rodeo tradition from which mariachi dress derives) and for birria, a spicy meat stew.
