Joint lock

Joint lock
FocusGrappling
ClassificationSubmission technique
Practised inJudo, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, sambo, catch wrestling, aikido, mixed martial arts

A joint lock is a grappling technique involving the isolation and controlled manipulation of an opponent's joint so that it is moved beyond its normal range of motion, causing pain or the threat of injury and typically forcing a submission. Joint locks are central to arts such as judo (where they are called kansetsu-waza), Brazilian jiu-jitsu, sambo, catch wrestling and mixed martial arts.

Types

Common locks include the armbar (hyperextending the elbow), the kimura and americana (rotating the shoulder), wrist locks, leglocks such as the heel hook and kneebar, and spinal locks. Techniques are often grouped by the joint attacked: elbow, shoulder, wrist, ankle, knee, neck and small-joint manipulations (fingers and toes — frequently prohibited in competition).

Mechanics and safety

A lock works by using leverage to isolate a limb and applying force in a direction the joint cannot resist, with the attacker's body providing a fulcrum. In training and competition, locks are submitted to by "tapping out" — tapping the opponent or mat, or verbally submitting — before damage occurs. Because some locks, especially heel hooks and spinal locks, can cause severe injury with little warning pain, many rule sets restrict them by experience level. In judō competition, locks may be applied only to the elbow joint; police and military combatives include standing joint locks for restraint and control.
