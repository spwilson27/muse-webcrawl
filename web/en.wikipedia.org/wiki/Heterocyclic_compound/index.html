Heterocyclic compound

A heterocyclic compound or ring structure is a cyclic compound that has atoms of at least two different elements as members of its ring(s). Heterocyclic organic chemistry is the branch of organic chemistry dealing with the synthesis, properties, and applications of these heterocycles. The ring atoms other than carbon — most commonly nitrogen, oxygen, or sulfur — are called heteroatoms.

Examples

Well-known heterocycles include pyridine, pyrrole, furan, and thiophene (five- and six-membered aromatic rings), as well as the purine and pyrimidine bases of DNA. Heterocyclic compounds are widespread in nature and industry: many vitamins, alkaloids, antibiotics, and dyes contain heterocyclic rings, and the majority of modern pharmaceuticals are heterocycles.

Nomenclature

Systematic names for heterocycles are commonly formed by the Hantzsch–Widman nomenclature, which combines prefixes denoting the heteroatom (oxa-, thia-, aza-) with suffixes indicating ring size and saturation. Many heterocycles retain traditional names.
