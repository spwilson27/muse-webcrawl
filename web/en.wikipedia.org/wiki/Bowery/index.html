Bowery

The Bowery is a street and neighbourhood in Lower Manhattan, New York City, running from Chatham Square to Cooper Square. The name derives from Dutch bouwerij (farm), recalling Peter Stuyvesant's farm.

History

Once an elegant theatre district, by the late 19th and 20th centuries the Bowery became synonymous with flophouses, missions and skid row, serving homeless and alcoholic men. The Bowery Mission (founded 1879) remains active.

Culture and change

In the 1970s the club CBGB on the Bowery launched punk rock (Ramones, Talking Heads). Since the 1990s–2000s the area has gentrified with hotels, the New Museum (2007), restaurants and luxury housing, while debate continues over displacement and preservation of Bowery buildings.
