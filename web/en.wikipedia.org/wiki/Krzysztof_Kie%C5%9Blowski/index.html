Krzysztof Kieślowski

Born27 June 1941, Warsaw, Poland
Died13 March 1996, Warsaw
OccupationFilm director and screenwriter
Notable worksDekalog; The Double Life of Véronique; Three Colours trilogy

Krzysztof Kieślowski (27 June 1941 – 13 March 1996) was a Polish film director and screenwriter, regarded as one of the most influential European filmmakers of the late twentieth century. His films explore moral choice, chance, faith and the inner lives of ordinary people under communism and after it.

Career

Kieślowski studied at the Łódź Film School and began in documentary before turning to features such as Camera Buff (1979) and Blind Chance (1981, released 1987), associated with the Polish "Cinema of Moral Anxiety". International acclaim came with Dekalog (1988), a cycle of ten television films loosely based on the Ten Commandments, two of which were expanded into features, A Short Film About Killing and A Short Film About Love.

Later films

Working in France with co-writer Krzysztof Piesiewicz and composer Zbigniew Preisner, he made The Double Life of Véronique (1991) and the Three Colours trilogy — Blue (1993), White (1994) and Red (1994) — meditations on liberty, equality and fraternity. He announced his retirement after Red and died of a heart attack in 1996, aged 54.
