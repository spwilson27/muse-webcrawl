Anti-de Sitter space

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics and physics, anti-de Sitter space (AdS) is a maximally symmetric Lorentzian manifold with constant negative scalar curvature. It is the Lorentzian analogue of hyperbolic space, just as de Sitter space is the Lorentzian analogue of a sphere. Anti-de Sitter space is named after Willem de Sitter (1872–1934), who worked extensively on relativistic cosmology together with Albert Einstein.

Anti-de Sitter space is best known for its role in the AdS/CFT correspondence, a conjectured duality between a theory of quantum gravity in AdS space and a conformal field theory living on its boundary, proposed by Juan Maldacena in 1997. Because of this correspondence, AdS has become one of the most intensively studied backgrounds in string theory and theoretical high-energy physics.

Informal explanation

Whereas ordinary (Minkowski) spacetime is flat and de Sitter space is positively curved like a sphere, anti-de Sitter space is negatively curved, like a saddle or a hyperbolic plane extended into a spacetime. A distinctive feature of AdS is that it possesses a timelike boundary at spatial infinity: light can travel from the centre of the space to the boundary and back in a finite amount of coordinate time. This makes AdS behave somewhat like a box, and means that data on the boundary can encode the physics of the whole interior — the key intuition behind holography.

Definition and properties

Anti-de Sitter space in n dimensions, AdSn, can be constructed as the hyperboloid

−X02 − Xn2 + X12 + … + Xn−12 = −α2

embedded in a flat space with two time dimensions. The parameter α is the AdS radius, related to the (negative) cosmological constant by Λ = −(n−1)(n−2)/(2α2). The isometry group of AdSn is SO(2, n−1). The hyperboloid itself contains closed timelike curves, so physicists usually work with its universal cover, in which the timelike direction is unwrapped.

Poincaré coordinates

Only part of AdS is covered by the Poincaré patch, whose metric takes the form

ds2 = (α2/z2)(−dt2 + dx2 + dz2)

where z > 0 is a radial coordinate and the conformal boundary sits at z = 0. These are the coordinates most often used in the AdS/CFT correspondence, where the boundary theory is a conformal field theory on Minkowski space and the radial coordinate z plays the role of an energy scale in the boundary theory — the basis of the holographic renormalisation group.
