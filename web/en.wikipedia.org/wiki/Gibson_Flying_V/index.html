Gibson Flying V

Manufacturer: Gibson

Period: 1958–present (with interruptions)

Body type: Solid

Woods: Korina (original), mahogany

Pickup configuration: Two humbuckers

The Gibson Flying V is an electric guitar model first released by Gibson in 1958. Its radical V-shaped solid body was part of a line of futuristic designs introduced under company president Ted McCarty, alongside the Explorer and the ill-fated Moderne. Initially a commercial failure, the Flying V was reissued in the late 1960s and went on to become an iconic instrument strongly associated with rock and heavy metal guitarists.

History

Gibson developed the Flying V in 1957 as part of an effort to modernize its image in competition with Fender. The original 1958–1959 production run was built from limba wood (marketed as "korina") and shipped in small numbers — only about 98 Flying Vs were made. Poor sales led to discontinuation in 1959, and the remaining stock was assembled and sold in the early 1960s.

Blues guitarist Albert King and rock players such as Lonnie Mack had kept the model visible, and a successful 1967 reissue in mahogany with a larger pickguard and revised tailpiece established the guitar in Gibson's catalogue. Subsequent decades brought many variants, including the V2, the Flying V Pro, and signature models.

Design

The Flying V has a symmetrical V-shaped body with a set mahogany neck, a rosewood or ebony fingerboard, two humbucking pickups, and a Tune-o-matic bridge with either a stopbar or string-through-body tailpiece. The shape requires a guitar strap for comfortable playing while seated, but gives unimpeded access to the highest frets.

Notable players

Albert King famously played an original korina Flying V upside down (as a left-handed player using a right-handed instrument). Other prominent users include Jimi Hendrix, who owned a custom-painted 1967 model, Marc Bolan, Michael Schenker, Kirk Hammett, James Hetfield, Dave Mustaine, and Lenny Kravitz.
