Unsuk Chin, one of the most performed contemporary classical composers

Unsuk Chin (Korean: 진은숙; born 14 July 1961) is a South Korean composer of contemporary classical music, based in Berlin. Self-taught at the piano from an early age, she studied composition at Seoul National University and later with György Ligeti in Hamburg. Her music, noted for its shimmering orchestral colour, rhythmic playfulness, and fusion of avant-garde techniques with lyrical immediacy, is performed by leading orchestras and ensembles worldwide.

Life and career

Chin was born in Seoul in 1961. Largely self-taught in music theory as a child, she entered Seoul National University to study composition, supporting herself by playing piano in hotels and bars. In 1985 she won a prize at the Gaudeamus International Composers Award in Amsterdam and moved to Hamburg on a DAAD scholarship to study with Ligeti, whose emphasis on craftsmanship and freedom from dogma deeply influenced her. She later worked at the electronic music studio of the Technical University of Berlin and settled in Berlin.

Her breakthrough came with Akrostichon-Wortspiel (1991/1993), a set of playful scenes on texts from Lewis Carroll and fairy tales. Her Violin Concerto (2001), written for Viviane Hagner, won the prestigious Grawemeyer Award in 2004. Her opera Alice in Wonderland, to a libretto by David Henry Hwang and the composer, premiered at the Bavarian State Opera in Munich in 2007 under Kent Nagano. Chin has served as composer-in-residence with numerous orchestras and as artistic director of festivals and concert series, including work with the Seoul Philharmonic and the Philharmonia Orchestra's Music of Today series in London.

Music

Chin's style combines virtuosic instrumental writing, iridescent harmony, and intricate but lucid textures. Works such as Šu for sheng and orchestra (2009), the Cello Concerto (2009), Le Silence des Sirènes (2014), and her second violin concerto, Scherben der Stille (2020), reflect her interest in dreams, fairy tales, street theatre, and the interplay between Western and East Asian musical traditions.
