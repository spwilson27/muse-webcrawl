Content rating

A content rating (also known as a maturity rating) assesses the suitability of media content — films, television, video games, apps and books — for audiences based on its treatment of sex, violence, substance use, language and other mature themes. Ratings are usually expressed as a minimum recommended age.

Film

  • United States — MPA ratings: G, PG, PG-13, R, NC-17 (since 1968; PG-13 added 1984).
  • United Kingdom — BBFC: U, PG, 12A, 15, 18, R18.
  • Germany — FSK: 0, 6, 12, 16, 18.

Television and games

Television uses systems such as the US TV Parental Guidelines (TV-Y through TV-MA). Video games are rated by the ESRB in North America (E, E10+, T, M, AO) and by PEGI in Europe (3, 7, 12, 16, 18). Digital storefronts increasingly use the automated IARC questionnaire.

Purposes and criticism

Ratings inform parents and sometimes restrict sales or admission by law. Critics note inconsistency between boards, bias in judging content types, and "ratings creep" — material once rated for older audiences earning softer ratings over time.
