Florida State Parks

LocationFlorida, United States
AreaApproximately 800,000 acres
Established1935
Governing bodyFlorida Department of Environmental Protection, Division of Recreation and Parks

Florida State Parks is the collective name for the 175 state parks, trails and historic sites managed by the Florida Department of Environmental Protection, through its Division of Recreation and Parks. The system encompasses nearly 800,000 acres (320,000 ha) of land, including more than 100 miles (160 km) of beaches, and welcomes roughly 29 million visitors a year, making it one of the largest and most visited state park systems in the United States.

History

Florida's state park system began in 1935, when the Florida Park Service was created within the Florida Board of Forestry. Early parks were developed with help from the Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) during the Great Depression; CCC workers built roads, trails, cabins and other structures at parks such as Highlands Hammock, Myakka River, Hillsborough River and O'Leno. In 1949 the Florida Park Service became an independent agency, and in 1969 administration passed to the Department of Natural Resources, the predecessor of the Department of Environmental Protection.

The system has received the National Recreation and Park Association's Gold Medal Award for Excellence in Park and Recreation Management four times (1999, 2005, 2013 and 2019), more than any other state park system, and is frequently described as the "best state park system in America."

Parks and sites

The system includes state parks, state recreation areas, state geological sites, state archaeological sites, state historic sites, state gardens, state forests' recreation areas and rail trails. Notable units include Bahia Honda State Park in the Florida Keys, Edward Ball Wakulla Springs State Park, home to one of the world's largest and deepest freshwater springs, Ichetucknee Springs State Park, Jonathan Dickinson State Park (the largest park in Southeast Florida), and Paynes Prairie Preserve State Park near Gainesville, where wild bison and horses roam.

Trails

The Florida National Scenic Trail, administered in part through the state park system and the U.S. Forest Service, runs about 1,500 miles from Big Cypress National Preserve to the Alabama border. The Office of Greenways and Trails manages rail trails such as the General James A. Van Fleet State Trail and the Tallahassee–St. Marks Historic Railroad State Trail.
