Massasoit

Massasoit Sachem (c. 1581–1661), also known as Ousamequin, was the grand sachem of the Wampanoag Confederacy in the early 17th century. He is best known for establishing peaceful relations with the English colonists of Plymouth Colony in 1621, an alliance that endured for decades.

Alliance with Plymouth

Massasoit's people had been devastated by epidemics — probably introduced by European fishermen and traders — between 1616 and 1619. In March 1621 he visited Plymouth with Samoset and Squanto (Tisquantum) acting as interpreter, and concluded a treaty of mutual defence with Governor John Carver. Tradition associates Massasoit with the harvest feast of 1621, the event later commemorated as the first Thanksgiving.

In 1623, Governor William Bradford sent Edward Winslow to nurse Massasoit through a serious illness, an act of diplomacy that strengthened the alliance. Massasoit warned the colonists of a plot against them by other tribes, and the peace he maintained lasted until his death.

Legacy

After Massasoit's death in 1661, relations deteriorated under his sons: Wamsutta died in suspicious circumstances in 1662, and Metacomet ("King Philip") led the devastating King Philip's War of 1675–1676. Statues of Massasoit, including Cyrus Dallin's famous sculpture, stand in Plymouth, Massachusetts, and elsewhere.
