Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals

Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals (REACH) is a European Union regulation dating from 18 December 2006. REACH addresses the production and use of chemical substances, and their potential impacts on both human health and the environment. Its 849 pages took seven years to pass, and it has been described as the most complex legislation in the Union's history and the most important in 20 years. It is the strictest law to date regulating chemical substances and will affect industries throughout the world.

REACH entered into force on 1 June 2007, with a phased implementation over the next decade. The European Chemicals Agency (ECHA), based in Helsinki, Finland, manages the technical, scientific and administrative aspects of the regulation.

Aims

REACH aims to improve the protection of human health and the environment through the better and earlier identification of the intrinsic properties of chemical substances. It shifts the burden of proof to industry: manufacturers and importers must gather information on the properties of their chemical substances and register the information in a central database at ECHA. The regulation's motto is "no data, no market".

Requirements

Companies manufacturing or importing more than one tonne of a chemical substance per year must register it. Substances of very high concern (SVHC) — including carcinogens, mutagens, reproductive toxicants and persistent, bioaccumulative and toxic substances — may be subject to authorisation or restriction. REACH also regulates substances in articles and imposes duties to communicate information down the supply chain, including through safety data sheets.

Criticism

The regulation has been criticised for its cost and complexity, for the animal testing it may require, and for burdens on smaller companies, while supporters credit it with driving safer substitution and innovation.
