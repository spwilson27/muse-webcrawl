David (Michelangelo)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Michelangelo's David seen from a respectful distance in the Accademia tribune in Florence: the colossal marble figure in a domed apse, visitors small at its feet

David is a masterpiece of Italian Renaissance sculpture, created between 1501 and 1504 by the Italian artist Michelangelo. David is a 5.17-metre (17 ft 0 in) marble statue of the Biblical figure David, a favoured subject in the art of Florence. Originally commissioned as one of a series of prophets for Florence Cathedral, it was instead placed in the Piazza della Signoria and unveiled on 8 September 1504 as a symbol of the Florentine Republic.

Carved from a single block of Carrara marble abandoned by earlier sculptors, the statue shows David before the battle with Goliath — sling over his shoulder, body tensed in contrapposto, veins and tendons rendered with anatomical precision. In 1873 the original was moved to the Accademia Gallery for protection; a replica stands in the piazza. It remains one of the most recognised sculptures in the world.
