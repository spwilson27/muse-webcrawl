Politics of Croatia

The politics of Croatia are defined by a framework of a parliamentary, representative democratic republic, where the Prime Minister of Croatia is the head of government in a multi-party system. Executive power is exercised by the Government and the President of Croatia. Legislative power is vested in the Croatian Parliament (the Sabor). The judiciary is independent of the executive and the legislature.

Government

The President is head of state, directly elected to a five-year term. The Government is led by the Prime Minister, who heads a cabinet of ministers. The Sabor is a unicameral legislative body of 151 members elected to four-year terms.

Political history

Croatia declared independence from Yugoslavia in 1991 and fought the Croatian War of Independence until 1995. Major parties include the Croatian Democratic Union (HDZ) and the Social Democratic Party (SDP). Croatia joined NATO in 2009 and the European Union in 2013, adopting the euro and joining the Schengen Area in 2023.
