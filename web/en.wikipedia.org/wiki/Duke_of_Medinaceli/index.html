Duke of Medinaceli

Duke of Medinaceli is a Spanish noble title created in 1479 by Queen Isabella I of Castile for Luis de la Cerda y de la Vega, Count of Medinaceli. The head of the House of Medinaceli long ranked among the premier grandees of Spain, with vast estates in Castile and Andalusia centred on Medinaceli in the province of Soria.

History

The de la Cerda line descended from the infantes de la Cerda, sons of Alfonso X's eldest son Ferdinand. As Counts of Medinaceli from 1370 and Dukes from 1479, the family accumulated titles including the Dukedoms of Alcalá de los Gazules, Feria (by marriage in the 20th century), Segorbe and Cardona. The Medinaceli archive, housed for many years in the hospital of San Juan Bautista in Toledo, is one of the great private documentary collections of Spain.

Properties

Notable family properties include the Ducal Palace of Medinaceli, the Casa de Pilatos in Seville, and the Palacio de los Duques de Medinaceli in Madrid, later converted into the Hotel Ritz's neighbouring palace hotel. The Fundación Casa Ducal de Medinaceli manages much of the family's heritage.
