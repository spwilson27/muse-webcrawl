Attitude indicator
Also calledArtificial horizon; gyro horizon
ShowsPitch and bank relative to the horizon
Core sensorGyroscope / AHRS

The attitude indicator (AI), also known as the artificial horizon or gyro horizon, is a flight instrument that informs the pilot of the aircraft's orientation relative to Earth's horizon. It indicates pitch (fore-and-aft tilt) and bank (side-to-side tilt) at a glance, and is one of the six basic instruments in the traditional "six pack" of flight instruments. It is indispensable for flying in instrument meteorological conditions, when the natural horizon cannot be seen.

Operation

Traditionally the instrument is driven by a gyroscope, spun by vacuum or electricity and kept rigid in space, around which the instrument case moves with the aircraft; a miniature aeroplane symbol remains fixed to the case while a ball or bar representing the horizon moves behind it. Modern aircraft use solid-state attitude and heading reference systems (AHRS) that drive electronic displays, including the primary flight display of glass cockpits.

Reading the instrument

The display is divided by a horizon line, conventionally blue (or brown/black in older Western and some Eastern designs) above and brown or black below, with pitch ladder lines at intervals and a bank scale at the top. Failure modes of vacuum-driven gyros — gradual precession or tumbling in steep manoeuvres — make cross-checking with other instruments a core instrument-flying skill.
