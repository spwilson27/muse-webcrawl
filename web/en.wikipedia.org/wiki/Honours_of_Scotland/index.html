The Honours of Scotland (Scots: Honours o Scotland), informally known as the Scottish Crown Jewels, are the regalia that were worn by Scottish monarchs at their coronation. Kept in the Crown Room in Edinburgh Castle, they comprise the Crown of Scotland, the Sceptre, and the Sword of State — together the oldest surviving set of crown jewels in the British Isles.

The regalia

The Crown of Scotland was made in its present form in 1540 for James V by Edinburgh goldsmith John Mosman, who refashioned an earlier crown; it is of Scottish gold, encrusted with 22 gemstones, 20 precious stones, and pearls, and was first worn by James V at the coronation of Mary of Guise. The Sceptre, presented by Pope Alexander VI to James IV in 1494 and remodelled in 1536, is surmounted by a polished rock-crystal orb. The Sword of State, a gift from Pope Julius II to James IV in 1507, was made in Italy by Domenico da Sutri; its blade is etched with images of St Peter and St Paul.

History

The Honours were used at coronations from Mary, Queen of Scots (1543) to Charles II (1651, at Scone — the last coronation in Scotland). During the Wars of the Three Kingdoms they were hidden from Cromwell's army, buried at Kinneff Church. After the Union of 1707 they fell out of use and were locked in a chest in Edinburgh Castle, their very existence passing into legend until a commission including Sir Walter Scott rediscovered them on 4 February 1818. They were placed on public display, and in 1822 were presented to George IV during his visit to Edinburgh.

Modern use

Unlike the English regalia, the Honours are not used to crown the monarch, but the Crown of Scotland is traditionally present at ceremonial occasions: it was carried before Elizabeth II at the opening of the Scottish Parliament in 1999 and at the national service of thanksgiving in St Giles' Cathedral in 2023 for Charles III. The Stone of Scone (Stone of Destiny), returned to Scotland in 1996, is displayed alongside them. In 2023 a new Sword of State, the Elizabeth Sword, was commissioned because the 1507 sword had become too fragile.
