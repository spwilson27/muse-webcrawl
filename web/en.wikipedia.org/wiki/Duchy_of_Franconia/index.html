Duchy of Franconia

Franconia among the stem duchies, c. 1000

The Duchy of Franconia (German: Herzogtum Franken) was one of the five stem duchies of the East Frankish and early German kingdom, emerging around the beginning of the tenth century from the lands of the Franks along the Main and middle Rhine. Its territory roughly corresponded to modern Hesse, northern Bavaria (the region still called Franconia), northern Baden-Württemberg and parts of Thuringia and Rhineland-Palatinate.

History

Franconia occupied the Frankish heartland between the Saxons, Thuringians, Alemanni and Bavarians. The Conradine family rose to ducal authority in the late ninth century, and Conrad the Younger of Franconia was elected king of East Francia in 911 — the first non-Carolingian king. Under his successor, the Saxon Henry the Fowler, the ducal office in Franconia passed to Eberhard, Conrad's brother; Eberhard's revolt against Otto I ended with his death at the Battle of Andernach in 939. Otto thereafter kept the duchy in royal hands or gave it to close relatives, and no hereditary Franconian ducal dynasty became established.

Dissolution and legacy

From the mid-tenth century Franconia was progressively absorbed into direct royal administration and then fragmented among bishoprics (Würzburg, whose bishop later styled himself "Duke of Franconia", Bamberg, Mainz, Worms, Speyer) and secular lords, including the Hohenstaufen, whose family lands lay in the duchy and whose kings used Rothenburg and other Franconian residences. The region's name survives as one of the three administrative regions (Regierungsbezirke) of the modern German state of Bavaria: Upper, Middle and Lower Franconia.
