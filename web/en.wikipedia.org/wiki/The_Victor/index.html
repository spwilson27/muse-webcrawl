The Victor

The Victor (Serbian: Победник, Pobednik) is a monument in the Kalemegdan Park of Belgrade, Serbia, and one of the city's most recognisable landmarks. The statue, showing a nude male figure holding a falcon in his left hand and a sword in his right, was erected in 1928 to commemorate Serbia's victories in the Balkan Wars and the First World War.

History

The work of Croatian sculptor Ivan Meštrović, the monument was originally designed in 1913 for Terazije square in central Belgrade, but public objections to the figure's nudity led to it being placed instead atop a tall column on the bastion of Belgrade Fortress, facing the confluence of the Sava and Danube rivers. The figure stands on a pedestal designed by architect Petar Bajalović; the total height of the monument is about 14 metres.

Significance

The Victor has become a symbol of Belgrade, frequently used as an emblem of the city. In 2019–2020 the statue was removed from its column for restoration and returned after repair of the damaged pedestal.
