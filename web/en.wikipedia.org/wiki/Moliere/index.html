Moliere

Moliere — more usually written Molière — is the stage name of Jean-Baptiste Poquelin (baptized 15 January 1622 – 17 February 1673), the French playwright, actor and theatre manager regarded as the master of French comedy and one of the greatest writers in French literature. Life in brief

Poquelin, the son of a prosperous upholsterer to the royal household, abandoned a legal career in 1643 to co-found the Illustre Théâtre with Madeleine Béjart, taking the name Molière. After touring the provinces for thirteen years, the troupe triumphed before Louis XIV in 1658 and gained the use of Paris theatres. Molière wrote and starred in a stream of comedies mixing farce, commedia dell'arte, and comedy of manners.

Works and reputation

His masterpieces include The School for Wives (1662), Tartuffe (banned for five years under religious pressure, 1664–69), Don Juan, The Misanthrope (1666), The Miser (1668), The Bourgeois Gentleman (1670) and The Imaginary Invalid. During a performance of the last of these in 1673, Molière, playing the hypochondriac, collapsed with a haemorrhage and died hours later; legend holds he died on stage. Because actors were denied Christian burial, the king intervened so he could be interred in consecrated ground. French is still called "the language of Molière".
