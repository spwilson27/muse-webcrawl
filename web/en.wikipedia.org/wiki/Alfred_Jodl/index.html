Alfred Josef Ferdinand Jodl (10 May 1890 – 16 October 1946) was a German general who served as Chief of the Operations Staff of the Oberkommando der Wehrmacht (OKW, the German Armed Forces High Command) throughout the Second World War. He was among Adolf Hitler's closest military advisers and signed the German instrument of surrender in Reims on 7 May 1945. He was convicted of war crimes and crimes against humanity at the Nuremberg trials and hanged.

Career

Jodl joined the Bavarian Army as an artillery officer in 1910 and served in the First World War. Remaining in the small interwar Reichswehr, he rose through staff appointments and in 1939 became chief of the OKW's Operations Staff under Wilhelm Keitel. From that post he helped plan and direct nearly all German operations of the war, from the invasions of Norway and France to Operation Barbarossa and the defence of Germany. He was seriously wounded in the 20 July 1944 bomb plot against Hitler. Jodl signed orders that the tribunal later held criminal, including the Commando Order directing the execution of captured Allied commandos.

Trial and execution

Arrested in May 1945, Jodl was a principal defendant before the International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg. He defended himself on the basis of obedience to orders but was convicted on all four counts of the indictment and sentenced to death. His request to be shot rather than hanged was refused, and he was executed on 16 October 1946; his ashes were scattered to prevent the creation of a shrine.
