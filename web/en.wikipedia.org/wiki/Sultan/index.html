Sultan (Arabic: سلطان sulṭān, "strength, authority, ruler") is a position of authority originally used in the Islamic world for a provincial governor or military commander, and later the standard title of a sovereign ruler of a Muslim state. The female equivalent is sultana. A state ruled by a sultan is a sultanate.

History

The word appears in the Quran in the abstract sense of "authority" or "proof". In the eleventh century, as the Abbasid caliphs lost effective power, de facto rulers began to take the title: Mahmud of Ghazni is generally regarded as the first ruler to style himself sultan, and the Seljuk ruler Tughril was formally invested with the title by the caliph in Baghdad in 1055. The title was subsequently borne by the rulers of the Ayyubid, Mamluk and Ottoman states, among many others. The Ottoman sultans, beginning with Osman I, ruled one of the largest empires in history, and from 1517 also held the caliphate.

Modern usage

Today the title survives in a handful of states. The Sultan of Oman is an absolute monarch and head of state; the Sultan of Brunei likewise rules absolutely. In Malaysia, seven of the nine hereditary state rulers bear the title sultan, and the federal king (Yang di-Pertuan Agong) is elected from among them. Sultans also remain as ceremonial regional rulers in parts of Indonesia and the Philippines.
