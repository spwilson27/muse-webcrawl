Cocktail garnish

Cocktails garnished with citrus twists, cherries and olives

Purpose: Decoration and aroma/flavour

Common types: Citrus peel, cherries, olives, herbs, salt rims

Cocktail garnishes are decorative and functional additions placed in or on a mixed drink. Garnishes serve two purposes: they add visual appeal to the finished drink, and they contribute aroma and flavour — a well-chosen garnish is regarded by bartenders as an ingredient in its own right rather than mere ornament. The practice developed alongside the cocktail itself in the 19th century, when drinks such as the cobbler were presented with elaborate displays of seasonal fruit.

Common garnishes

Citrus twists and wheels: A strip of lemon, lime or orange peel, twisted over the drink to express its oils onto the surface, is standard for drinks such as the Martini and the Old Fashioned. Citrus wheels and wedges dress highballs, sours and tiki drinks.

Cherries: The maraschino cherry garnishes the Manhattan and many tiki and dessert drinks; modern craft bars often substitute house-brandied cherries.

Olives and onions: Green olives traditionally garnish the Martini, while a pickled cocktail onion converts it into a Gibson.

Herbs and flowers: A sprig of mint — slapped to release its aroma — crowns the Mojito and Mint Julep; basil, rosemary and edible flowers appear in contemporary drinks.

Rims: Salt on the rim of a Margarita, sugar on a Sidecar, and spiced rims on drinks such as the Bloody Caesar add flavour and texture with each sip.

Other garnishes include celery stalks (Bloody Mary), pineapple wedges and leaves, cucumber ribbons (Pimm's Cup), grated nutmeg, coffee beans (Espresso Martini and Sambuca), and novelty items such as paper umbrellas and cocktail picks.

Technique

Bartending guides stress that a garnish should complement the drink, be fresh and edible, and be prepared with proper tools — peelers, channel knives, zesters and cocktail picks. In contemporary craft bartending there is also a countervailing minimalist trend favouring a single precise garnish, or none at all, over elaborate decoration.
