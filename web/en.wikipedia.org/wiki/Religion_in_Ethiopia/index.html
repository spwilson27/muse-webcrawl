Religion in Ethiopia consists of a number of faiths. Among these mainly Abrahamic religions, the most numerous is Christianity (Ethiopian Orthodoxy, P'ent'ay, Catholic) at 62.8% of the population, followed by Islam at 33.9%. There is also a longstanding but small Jewish community. Some adherents of the Baháʼí Faith likewise exist in a number of urban and rural areas. Additionally, there are some followers of traditional faiths, who mainly reside in the southwestern part of the country.

History

According to the national census conducted in 2007, over 32 million people (43.5%) were reported to be Ethiopian Orthodox Christians, over 25 million (33.9%) were Muslims, just under 14 million (18.6%) were Protestants, and just under two million (2.6%) adhered to traditional beliefs. The Kingdom of Aksum in present-day Ethiopia and Eritrea was one of the first Christian countries in the world, having officially adopted Christianity as the state religion in the 4th century. Ethiopia was also one of the first regions in the world to officially adopt Islam, sheltering the followers of Muhammad who fled persecution in Mecca in the event known as the First Hijra.

Christianity

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church is the largest Christian denomination in Ethiopia and was part of the Coptic Orthodox Church until 1959. Ethiopia is one of the few regions of Africa where Christianity was not introduced primarily by European colonisation, and Ethiopian Christianity has distinctive practices, including its canon, its Ge'ez liturgy, and its calendar.

Islam

Islam in Ethiopia dates to the lifetime of the Prophet Muhammad. The city of Harar is regarded by many Ethiopian Muslims as a holy city, and Ethiopian Muslims are predominantly Sunni, with Sufi traditions also present.

Judaism

The Beta Israel ("House of Israel") are Ethiopian Jews who maintained an ancient form of Judaism. Most emigrated to Israel in the 1980s and 1990s in operations including Operation Moses and Operation Solomon.
