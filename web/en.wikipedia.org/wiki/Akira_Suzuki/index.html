Akira Suzuki

Akira Suzuki (鈴木 章, born 12 September 1930) is a Japanese chemist best known for discovering the palladium-catalysed cross-coupling reaction that bears his name. In 2010 he shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Richard F. Heck and Ei-ichi Negishi "for palladium-catalyzed cross couplings in organic synthesis".

Akira Suzuki, co-recipient of the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Career

Suzuki was born in Mukawa, Hokkaido, and studied at Hokkaido University, where he received his PhD in 1959 and spent most of his career, becoming full professor in 1973. From 1963 to 1965 he worked as a postdoctoral researcher with Herbert C. Brown at Purdue University, the pioneer of organoborane chemistry — an experience that shaped his later work. In 1979, with Norio Miyaura, he published the coupling of organoboron compounds with organic halides under palladium catalysis, now called the Suzuki (or Suzuki–Miyaura) coupling.

The Suzuki coupling

The reaction joins a carbon atom of an organoborane to a carbon atom of an aryl or vinyl halide, forming a new carbon–carbon bond under relatively mild conditions with commercially available, environmentally benign boron reagents. It has become one of the most widely used tools in pharmaceutical and materials synthesis, employed in the manufacture of drugs and advanced materials. After retiring from Hokkaido University in 1994, Suzuki held posts at Okayama University of Science and Kurashiki University of Science and the Arts. See also Suzuki reaction and Ei-ichi Negishi.
