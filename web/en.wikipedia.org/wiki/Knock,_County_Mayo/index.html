Knock (Irish: An Cnoc, meaning "the hill") is a village in County Mayo, Ireland. It is internationally known as the site of a reported Marian apparition in 1879 and is home to the Knock Shrine, one of the world's major Roman Catholic pilgrimage sites, which draws around a million and a half visitors per year.

The 1879 apparition

On the evening of 21 August 1879, fifteen local people, aged from 5 to 74, reported seeing an apparition on the gable wall of the parish church: the Virgin Mary, Saint Joseph, and Saint John the Evangelist, together with an altar bearing a lamb and a cross, surrounded by angels. The figures appeared in pouring rain for about two hours, yet witnesses said the ground beneath the gable remained dry. Two Church commissions of inquiry (1879 and 1936) found the testimony trustworthy. Unlike most apparition sites, the figures at Knock were silent, a fact often remarked upon.

The shrine today

The village has developed around the shrine: the original parish church, the Apparition Chapel enclosing the gable wall, and the great Basilica of Our Lady, Queen of Ireland (completed 1976), which seats about 10,000 people. Pope John Paul II visited in 1979 for the apparition's centenary, and Pope Francis visited in 2018. Ireland West Airport Knock, opened in 1985 about 20 km away, serves the pilgrimage traffic and the wider region. In 2021 the Vatican formally recognised Knock as an International Shrine of Eucharistic and Marian Devotion.
