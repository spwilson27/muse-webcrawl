The Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood, commonly shortened to Church of the Savior on Blood, is a Russian Orthodox church in Saint Petersburg built on the site where Emperor Alexander II was fatally wounded by an assassin's bomb on March 13, 1881. It is one of the city's principal sights, famous for its multicoloured onion domes and for containing more than 7,500 square metres of mosaics — among the largest mosaic collections in Europe.

Construction

Alexander III commissioned the church as a memorial to his father. Designed by Alfred Parland in deliberate contrast to the Baroque and Neoclassical architecture of St Petersburg, it imitates 16th- and 17th-century Russian churches such as St Basil's Cathedral in Moscow. Construction ran from 1883 to 1907, funded by the imperial family and private donors. Inside, a shrine marks the exact spot of the assassination, incorporating a section of the original cobblestoned embankment.

Soviet era and restoration

Closed in 1932, the church was used as a warehouse — during the Siege of Leningrad, as a morgue — and suffered serious damage. It was transferred to St Isaac's Cathedral museum in 1970 and, after a restoration lasting 27 years, reopened in 1997 as a museum of mosaics; it is not used for regular worship.
