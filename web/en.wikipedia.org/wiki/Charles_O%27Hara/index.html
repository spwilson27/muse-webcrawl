Charles O'Hara (1740 – 25 February 1802) was a British Army officer who served in the Seven Years' War, the American War of Independence, and the French Revolutionary Wars. He is best remembered for surrendering the British sword at both Saratoga (by proxy for General Burgoyne) and at Yorktown in 1781, where he presented it on behalf of Lord Cornwallis.

American War

O'Hara served under Cornwallis in the southern campaigns and was wounded and captured at Yorktown. In the surrender ceremony he first attempted to give his sword to the French commander Rochambeau, who redirected him to George Washington; Washington in turn directed him to Benjamin Lincoln.

Later career

He later commanded in Toulon in 1793, where he was captured by French forces including the young Napoleon Bonaparte, and subsequently served as Lieutenant Governor of Gibraltar, where he died.
