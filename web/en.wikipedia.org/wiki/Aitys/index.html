Aitys

Aitys (Kazakh: айтыс; Kyrgyz: айтыш) is a traditional improvised oral contest between two akyns — singer-poets of the Kazakh and Kyrgyz peoples — who take turns composing and performing verses in response to one another, accompanying themselves on the dombra (Kazakh) or komuz (Kyrgyz). In 2015 aitys was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity as a joint nomination by Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan.

Performance

Two akyns sit facing each other before an audience and improvise alternating verses on themes that may be set in advance or chosen on the spot: praise of one's own region, satire of the opponent, social criticism, philosophy or current events. Speed of wit, poetic virtuosity, memory and humour are prized; a jury or the audience's acclaim decides the winner. Contests can last from minutes to hours.

History

Aitys grew out of the nomadic oral culture of the Central Asian steppe, where akyns served as entertainers, chroniclers and social commentators. Famous historical akyns include the Kazakh poets Birzhan Sal, Akan Sery and Zhambyl Zhabayev. During the Soviet period the form was channelled into officially organised contests; after independence it revived as a nationally televised art, with national competitions drawing large audiences and new generations of performers, including prominent women akyns.
