The Scottish Parliament (Scottish Gaelic: Pàrlamaid na h-Alba; Scots: Scots Pairlament) is the devolved, unicameral legislature of Scotland, located in the Holyrood area of Edinburgh. Often referred to simply as Holyrood, it was established by the Scotland Act 1998 following a referendum in 1997 in which Scottish voters backed devolution.

The Scottish Parliament Building, opened in 2004.

Composition and elections

The Parliament has 129 Members of the Scottish Parliament (MSPs): 73 elected in single-member constituencies and 56 by a regional additional-member proportional system across eight electoral regions. Elections are normally held every five years. Matters reserved to the UK Parliament at Westminster include foreign affairs, defence and most macroeconomic policy; the Scottish Parliament legislates on devolved areas such as health, education, justice, transport and much taxation.

History

The original Parliament of Scotland was the legislature of the independent Kingdom of Scotland, meeting from the 13th century until it was adjourned in 1707 by the Acts of Union. Campaigns for a revived Scottish legislature ran through the 20th century; the new Parliament first met on 12 May 1999, when Winnie Ewing famously declared it "reconvened". It sat initially in the Church of Scotland's General Assembly Hall before moving to the new Holyrood building, designed by Enric Miralles, in 2004.
