Paleoproterozoic

The Paleoproterozoic Era is the first of three subdivisions of the Proterozoic eon in the geologic timescale, extending from 2,500 million years ago to 1,600 million years ago (2.5 to 1.6 Ga). During this era the continents first stabilised into cratons, the atmosphere became oxygenated in the Great Oxidation Event, the Earth may have frozen over in the Huronian glaciation, and the first eukaryotic cells evolved.

Great Oxidation Event

Early in the era, oxygen produced by photosynthetic cyanobacteria accumulated in the atmosphere (about 2.45–2.32 Ga), precipitating dissolved iron from the oceans as banded iron formations and likely triggering the Huronian glaciation — possibly a "Snowball Earth" — by destroying atmospheric methane. The rise of oxygen was poisonous to most anaerobic life but paved the way for aerobic metabolism and, eventually, complex life.

Life and continents

The first confirmed eukaryotes — cells with nuclei, including the large coiled alga Grypania — appear in the Paleoproterozoic record, as do possible early multicellular organisms of the Francevillian biota of Gabon (about 2.1 Ga). On land, growing cratons collided to assemble the supercontinent Columbia (Nuna) by around 1.8 Ga, accompanied by major mountain-building episodes such as the Trans-Hudson orogeny in North America.
