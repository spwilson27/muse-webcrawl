San Sebastián

San Sebastián (Basque: Donostia) is a coastal city in Spain's Basque Country, capital of Gipuzkoa, near the French border. Set around the shell-shaped La Concha bay — regularly ranked among Europe's finest city beaches — it is renowned as a capital of gastronomy, with one of the world's densest clusters of Michelin-starred restaurants and a beloved pintxos (tapas) culture in its old town. Population about 188,000.

History

A monastic settlement chartered as a town around 1180 by Sancho VI of Navarre, San Sebastián served Castile as a naval and trading port and was repeatedly burned and besieged — most notoriously in 1813, when Wellington's Anglo-Portuguese troops stormed the French-held city and sacked it, a trauma burned into local memory during the Peninsular War. Rebuilt in neoclassical style, it became from the 1840s the fashionable summer court of Spain — Queen María Cristina's regency fixed its Belle Époque character of grand hotels and promenades. In the Spanish Civil War it fell early to the Nationalists. European Capital of Culture in 2016, it balances tourism with a famously high quality — and cost — of life.
