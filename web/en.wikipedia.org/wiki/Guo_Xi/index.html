Guo Xi (Chinese: 郭熙; c. 1020 – c. 1090) was a Chinese landscape painter of the Northern Song dynasty, one of the most celebrated masters of the shanshui ("mountain-water") tradition.

Life and work

Guo Xi served as a court painter under Emperor Shenzong, who prized his monumental landscapes. His best-known surviving work, Early Spring (1072), now in the National Palace Museum, Taipei, depicts towering peaks emerging from mist in early spring using multiple viewpoints and layered ink washes.

Theory

His treatise Linquan Gaozhi ("The Lofty Message of Forest and Streams"), compiled by his son Guo Si, sets out his theory of the "three distances" (high, deep and level distance) and argues that a great landscape should be one the viewer can "wander in and dwell in", not merely look at. His distinctive "crab-claw" brushstrokes for leafless branches and "cloud-head" rocks became highly influential.
