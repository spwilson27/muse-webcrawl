Chiffon cake

Chiffon cake is a very light sponge cake made with vegetable oil, eggs, sugar, flour, baking powder and flavourings. Unlike traditional sponge cakes, which rely on butter or air beaten into eggs alone, chiffon cake combines a batter of yolks and oil with separately whipped egg whites (meringue), giving it a rich yet airy, moist crumb. It was invented in California in 1927 and popularised across the United States after its formula was sold to General Mills.

History

Insurance salesman and caterer Harry Baker developed the recipe in Los Angeles in 1927 and kept it secret for twenty years, baking the cakes for Hollywood stars and the Brown Derby restaurant. In 1947 he sold the formula to General Mills, which promoted it in 1948 as "Chiffon Cake" — hailed as "the first really new cake in 100 years" — using Betty Crocker advertising that made the cake a national sensation.

Method

Egg yolks are whisked with oil, liquid and dry ingredients; egg whites are beaten with cream of tartar to stiff peaks and folded in. The batter is baked in an ungreased tube pan so the cake can climb the sides, then inverted to cool so it does not collapse. Chiffon cakes are made in many flavours (lemon, orange, chocolate, coffee) and became especially popular in East and Southeast Asia, where pandan chiffon cake is an iconic dessert in Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia.
