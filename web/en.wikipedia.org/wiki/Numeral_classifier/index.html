Numeral classifier

A numeral classifier is a word or affix that accompanies a numeral to indicate the class or type of the noun being counted. Classifier languages require such a unit whenever a noun is quantified: rather than "three horses", a speaker says the equivalent of "three [animal-classifier] horse".

Distribution

Numeral classifiers are prominent in East and Southeast Asian languages, including Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Thai and Malay, as well as in many languages of the Americas, Oceania and South Asia (Bengali, for example). Mandarin has, among many others, gè (general), zhāng for flat objects, tiáo for long flexible things, and běn for bound volumes. Japanese has -hon, -mai, -hiki and dozens more counters.

Function and typology

Classifiers categorize nouns by properties such as animacy, shape, size, function and social status. They are related to but distinct from measure words, which denote containers or quantities ("three cups of rice"). Classifier systems are a classic topic in linguistic typology and cognitive linguistics, illustrating how grammar encodes categorization of the world.
