Radiosity (computer graphics)

In computer graphics, radiosity is an application of the finite element method to solving the rendering equation for scenes with purely diffuse (Lambertian) surfaces. Developed at Cornell University in 1984, it models the steady-state interreflection of light between surfaces.

Method

Surfaces are divided into patches; form factors describe the fraction of light transferred between each pair. Solving the resulting linear system yields each patch's radiosity, producing view-independent soft shadows and colour bleeding. Radiosity was widely used for architectural visualisation and as precomputed lightmaps in games before real-time global illumination techniques emerged.
