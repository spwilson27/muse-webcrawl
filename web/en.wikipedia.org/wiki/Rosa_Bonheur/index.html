Rosa Bonheur (born Marie-Rosalie Bonheur; 16 March 1822 – 25 May 1899) was a French artist known best as a painter of animals (animalière). She also made sculptures in a realist style. Bonheur is widely considered to be the most famous female painter of the nineteenth century, and her monumental canvas The Horse Fair brought her international fame in France, Britain and the United States.

Life

Bonheur was born in Bordeaux, the eldest child of the artist Raymond Bonheur, who trained her after her difficulties in conventional schooling. She exhibited at the Paris Salon from 1841 and won a first medal in 1848. To study animals at first hand she visited slaughterhouses and horse markets, and obtained official permission (permission de travestissement) from the Paris police to wear men's clothing for this work. She lived for decades with the painter Nathalie Micas, and later with the American artist Anna Elizabeth Klumpke, her biographer and heir.

Work

Ploughing in the Nivernais (1849), a government commission, established her reputation for accurate, unsentimental depictions of working animals. The Horse Fair, over 2.4 m high and nearly 5 m wide, was exhibited at the Salon of 1853 and toured Britain and America to acclaim; Queen Victoria requested a private viewing. Bonheur sold the engraving rights profitably and became financially independent, buying the Château de By at Thomery in 1860, where she kept a menagerie including lions. In 1865 Empress Eugénie presented her with the Legion of Honour — she was the first female artist to receive the award — and she was promoted to Officer in 1894. In her last years she befriended Buffalo Bill Cody, whose Wild West show she painted, and corresponded with artists and collectors worldwide.

Legacy

After her death her studio sale revealed thousands of works. Interest revived in the late 20th century as feminist art historians reassessed her career; major retrospectives, including the 2022 bicentenary exhibition at the Musée d'Orsay, have restored her reputation as a central figure of French Realism.
