Radio London

Radio London, also known as Big L, was a commercial offshore pirate radio station that operated from 23 December 1964 to 14 August 1967, broadcasting from the MV Galaxy, a former Second World War minesweeper anchored in international waters off Frinton-on-Sea, Essex, England.

History

The station was financed by Texas businessman Don Pierson, inspired by Radio Caroline and by the American Top 40 format of stations such as KLIF in Dallas. Transmitting on 266 metres medium wave with a powerful 75 kW transmitter, Radio London rapidly gained an audience estimated in the millions, far exceeding that of the BBC's Light Programme.

Programming and influence

Radio London pioneered in Britain the American-style format of disc jockeys, jingles produced by PAMS of Dallas, and chart shows such as the "Fab 40." Its DJs included Kenny Everett, Tony Blackburn, Dave Cash, and John Peel, many of whom moved to BBC Radio 1 when it launched in September 1967. The station popularized pirate-radio catchphrases and is remembered as the model for the fictional station in the film The Boat That Rocked.

Closure

The Marine, &c., Broadcasting (Offences) Act 1967 made it illegal for British citizens to work for or advertise on offshore stations, and Radio London closed at 3 p.m. on 14 August 1967 with a farewell programme hosted by Paul Kaye. The name was later revived several times, most successfully as a 1980s oldies project by its original programme director, Philip Birch.
