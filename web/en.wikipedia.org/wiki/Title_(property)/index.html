Title (property)


A deed evidences a transfer of title

In property law, a title is a bundle of rights in a piece of property in which a party may own either a legal interest or equitable interest. The rights in the bundle may be separated and held by different parties. It is also the formal evidence, such as a deed, that a person or entity has the legal right to ownership of the property.

Concepts

Title is not itself a physical document but a legal concept: the abstract fact of ownership. A deed or certificate of title records and evidences it. Ownership may be qualified by encumbrances such as mortgages, easements, liens, and covenants, which are said to "cloud" the title if they cast doubt on the owner's rights. Buyers commonly investigate the chain of title — the sequence of historical transfers — and purchase title insurance against hidden defects.

Registration systems

Jurisdictions differ in how title is proved. Deeds registration systems record the documents of transfer, leaving the parties to assess their validity. In Torrens title systems, used in Australia and parts of the Commonwealth, the state maintains a register and guarantees the title it records. Motor vehicles and other valuable movables may likewise have certificates of title issued by the state.
