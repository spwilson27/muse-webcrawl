Arirang (Korean: 아리랑) is a Korean folk song that is often considered to be the unofficial national anthem of Korea. There are about 3,600 variations of the song across some 60 versions, all sharing a refrain of lament and longing. The song was included twice on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list, first for South Korea in 2012 and then for North Korea in 2014.

Lyrics and refrain

The refrain is well known throughout Korea: "Arirang, arirang, arariyo... Arirang gogaero neomeoganda" — "Over the Arirang Pass." Verses commonly express sorrow at a lover's departure and the hardships of crossing mountain passes. Well-known regional versions include Jeongseon Arirang, Jindo Arirang, and Miryang Arirang; the version from Gyeonggi Province, often called Bonjo Arirang, is the one most widely known today.

History

The song's origins are uncertain and probably centuries old. It gained wide currency during the late Joseon period and the Japanese colonial era, when it became an anthem of Korean identity and quiet resistance; the 1926 film Arirang by Na Woon-gyu made its theme song famous nationwide. The name "Arirang" itself appears in the names of the Arirang Mass Games in Pyongyang, the Arirang TV network, and numerous cultural institutions.

Symbolism

Arirang is sung at celebrations, protests, and sporting events — it was performed as a unifying song by joint Korean delegations at international competitions, including the 2018 Winter Olympics — and it is often said to embody the Korean sentiment of han (deep sorrow and longing).
