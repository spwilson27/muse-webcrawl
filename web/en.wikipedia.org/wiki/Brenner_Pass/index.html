Brenner Pass

Elevation: 1,370 m (4,495 ft)

Location: Austria–Italy border

Range: Alps

The Brenner Pass (German: Brennerpass; Italian: Passo del Brennero) is a mountain pass through the Alps which forms the border between Italy and Austria. At 1,370 metres (4,495 ft) above sea level, it is one of the lowest of the major Alpine passes and the most important north–south crossing in the Eastern Alps, carrying heavy road and rail traffic between Innsbruck and Bolzano.

History

The pass has been used since prehistoric times and was a major route in the Roman era, followed by the Via Claudia Augusta and later imperial roads. Through the Middle Ages it was the principal link between the Holy Roman Empire and Italy, travelled by emperors, armies, pilgrims, and merchants. The Brenner railway, opened in 1867, was one of the first mountain railways in the Alps. After the First World War, the pass became the border between Austria and Italy following the Treaty of Saint-Germain (1919), which assigned South Tyrol to Italy.

Transport

The Brenner motorway (A13 in Austria, A22 in Italy) is one of Europe's busiest freight corridors, carrying millions of lorries each year between Germany and Italy, which has raised environmental and political concerns in Tyrol. The Europabrücke (Europe Bridge) near Innsbruck, opened in 1963, was for years Europe's tallest bridge pier. The Brenner Base Tunnel, a 55-kilometre rail tunnel under construction between Innsbruck and Fortezza, is intended to shift freight from road to rail when completed.

Geography

The pass separates the Ötztal and Stubai Alps to the west from the Zillertal Alps to the east. The village of Brenner (Brennero) lies just south of the summit on the Italian side, and the Eisack (Isarco) river flows south from the pass toward Bolzano while the Sill flows north toward Innsbruck.
