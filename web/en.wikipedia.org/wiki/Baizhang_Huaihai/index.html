Baizhang Huaihai (Chinese: 百丈懷海; 720–814) was a Chinese Zen (Chan) master of the Tang dynasty. A disciple of Mazu Daoyi, he is traditionally credited with establishing the first independent Chan monasteries and with drawing up a code of monastic rules, summarised in the famous saying "a day without work is a day without food" (一日不作，一日不食), which emphasised manual labour — including the master's own participation — as part of monastic practice.

Life

Huaihai was born in Changle in present-day Fujian and became a monk as a young man, studying the Vinaya before joining the community of Mazu Daoyi in Jiangxi. Along with Xitang Zhizang and Nanquan Puyuan he was one of Mazu's leading heirs. He took up residence at Mount Baizhang near modern Fengxin in Jiangxi, from which his common name derives. According to tradition, he continued working in the fields into old age; when his students hid his tools to force him to rest, he refused to eat, giving rise to his celebrated maxim.

Teachings and legacy

Baizhang's recorded sayings and dialogues are preserved in later Chan collections. He is the protagonist of several famous koans, including "Baizhang's fox", the second case of the Mumonkan (The Gateless Barrier), in which a monk is reborn as a wild fox for five hundred lifetimes for wrongly denying cause and effect, and is released when Baizhang gives the turning word. The monastic regulations attributed to him — the "Pure Rules of Baizhang" — shaped the organisation of Chan communities for centuries, even though the surviving text of that name dates from a later period. Among his dharma heirs was Huangbo Xiyun, teacher of Linji Yixuan, founder of the Linji (Rinzai) school.
