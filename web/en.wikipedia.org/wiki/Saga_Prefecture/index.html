Saga Prefecture

Country: Japan
Region: Kyushu
Island: Kyushu
Capital: Saga
Area: 2,440 km²
Population: approx. 800,000

Saga Prefecture (佐賀県, Saga-ken) is a prefecture of Japan located on the island of Kyushu. Saga Prefecture has a population of roughly 800,000 and an area of 2,440 square kilometres, making it one of Japan's smallest prefectures by area. Its capital and largest city is Saga.

Saga Prefecture borders Fukuoka Prefecture to the northeast and Nagasaki Prefecture to the southwest, and faces the Genkai Sea to the north and the Ariake Sea to the south. The broad Saga Plain, formed by rivers flowing into the Ariake Sea — which has Japan's largest tidal range — is a major rice-growing area.

History

The prefecture corresponds largely to the former Hizen Province and the Saga Domain of the Nabeshima clan, whose pottery-producing towns of Arita and Imari made "Imari ware" famous in Europe after porcelain production began in Arita in the early 17th century, using techniques brought by Korean potters. The Yoshinogari site, a large Yayoi-period settlement, lies in the prefecture.

Economy and culture

Saga is known for Arita and Imari porcelain, Karatsu ware, nori seaweed from the Ariake Sea, and green tea from Ureshino. The Saga International Balloon Fiesta, held each autumn along the Kase River, is one of Asia's largest hot-air balloon events. Karatsu's Kunchi festival is a nationally famous autumn festival featuring large festival floats.
