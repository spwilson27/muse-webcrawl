Alfred Rosenberg

Alfred Ernst Rosenberg (12 January 1893 – 16 October 1946) was a Baltic German Nazi theorist and ideologue, the chief exponent of Nazi racial doctrine and author of The Myth of the Twentieth Century (1930). As head of the Nazi Party's foreign affairs office and later Reich Minister for the Occupied Eastern Territories, he presided over the looting of art and the murderous administration of occupied Soviet territory. He was convicted of war crimes and crimes against humanity at Nuremberg and hanged in 1946.

Early life

Rosenberg was born in Reval (Tallinn) in the Governorate of Estonia, the son of a Baltic German merchant. He studied architecture in Riga and engineering in Moscow, graduating in 1917. Caught up in the Russian Revolution, he emigrated to Germany in 1918 and joined the German Workers' Party, the forerunner of the Nazi Party, in 1919 — among its earliest members and a conduit for the anti-Semitic conspiracy literature of White Russian émigré circles.

Ideologue of the party

Rosenberg edited the party newspaper Völkischer Beobachter and codified Nazi racial doctrine in The Myth of the Twentieth Century, a rambling work of pseudo-history that ranked second only to Mein Kampf in official esteem. During the Third Reich he directed the Einsatzstab Reichsleiter Rosenberg, which systematically plundered art, libraries, and archives across occupied Europe.

Eastern ministry and trial

Appointed in July 1941 to administer the occupied Soviet territories, Rosenberg's ministry oversaw a regime of exploitation, forced labour, and mass murder, in constant bureaucratic conflict with Himmler's SS. Arrested after the war, he was found guilty on all four counts at Nuremberg and executed on 16 October 1946.
