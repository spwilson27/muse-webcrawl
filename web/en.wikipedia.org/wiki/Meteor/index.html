A meteor is the visible streak of light produced when a small rock or dust particle (a meteoroid) from space burns up in Earth's atmosphere — a 'shooting star'. Most are grain-sized and perish high above ground; surviving fragments are meteorites.

Showers and impacts

When Earth passes through comet debris streams, meteor showers occur (Perseids in August, Geminids in December). Rare large impacts are catastrophic — a meteorite impact ended the dinosaurs' reign 66 million years ago (Chicxulub). The Moon's craters preserve the same bombardment record that Earth's weather erases; observatories and the Hubble telescope track hazardous near-Earth objects.
