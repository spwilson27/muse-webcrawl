Adult animation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Adult animation, also known as mature animation, is any type of animated motion work that is catered specifically to adult interests and is mainly targeted and marketed towards adults and sometimes older adolescents, as opposed to works that are made for children or for all ages. Animated works in this medium may be for entertainment, explorations of philosophy or psychology, or products of avant-garde and experimental filmmaking. Adult-oriented animation is predominantly produced in the United States and Japan, though output has grown in many other countries since the 2010s.

History

Early animated films sometimes featured risqué humour, but from the 1930s the enforcement of the Hays Code in the United States pushed American animation towards children's and family fare. Underground and independent animators such as Ralph Bakshi challenged this in the 1970s with films like Fritz the Cat (1972), the first animated feature to receive an X rating in the United States.

The modern adult-animation boom in television is generally dated to The Simpsons (1989– ), which proved that an animated sitcom could succeed in prime time with adults. It was followed by series such as Beavis and Butt-Head, King of the Hill, South Park, Family Guy and Futurama. Cable networks created dedicated late-night blocks, most famously Cartoon Network's Adult Swim (launched 2001), while streaming services later expanded the field with series such as BoJack Horseman, Rick and Morty and Arcane.

Genres and themes

Adult animated works commonly include sitcoms and other comedy series, but also drama, horror, science fiction and action. Outside the United States, Japanese anime has long included works aimed at adult men and women (the seinen and josei demographics), including acclaimed films by directors such as Satoshi Kon and works from Studio Ghibli aimed at older audiences.
