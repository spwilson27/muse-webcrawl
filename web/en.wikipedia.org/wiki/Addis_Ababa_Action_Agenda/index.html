The Addis Ababa Action Agenda is a global framework for financing sustainable development adopted by consensus at the Third International Conference on Financing for Development, held in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, from 13 to 16 July 2015. Endorsed by the United Nations General Assembly, the Agenda provides a comprehensive set of policy actions and commitments intended to mobilise the financial resources needed to implement the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), which were adopted two months later in September 2015 as part of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development.

[Image: Delegates at the Third International Conference on Financing for Development, Addis Ababa, 2015]

Background

The Agenda built on the outcomes of two earlier conferences: the Monterrey Consensus of 2002 and the Doha Declaration of 2008. Negotiations were closely watched because the scale of investment required for the SDGs — estimated in the trillions of dollars annually — far exceeded the roughly $135 billion then provided each year in official development assistance. The outcome document contains more than 100 concrete measures covering domestic public finance, private business and finance, international development cooperation, trade, debt, technology and systemic issues.

Key commitments

  • Strengthening domestic resource mobilisation, including fair and efficient tax systems and international cooperation against tax evasion and illicit financial flows.
  • A recommitment by developed countries to the target of 0.7 per cent of gross national income as official development assistance.
  • Measures to align private investment with sustainable development, including infrastructure investment and support for small and medium-sized enterprises.
  • A new Technology Facilitation Mechanism, including an online platform and a multi-stakeholder forum on science, technology and innovation for the SDGs.
  • Agreement to hold an annual ECOSOC Forum on Financing for Development Follow-up to review implementation, supported by an Inter-Agency Task Force report.

Reception

The Agenda was welcomed as a landmark framework linking finance to the post-2015 development agenda, although some civil-society groups criticised the failure to establish an intergovernmental tax body under the United Nations and the reliance on voluntary commitments. Its follow-up process feeds into the annual Financing for Sustainable Development Report.
