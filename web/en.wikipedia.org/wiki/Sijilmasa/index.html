Sijilmasa

Sijilmasa was a medieval Moroccan city and trade entrepôt at the northern edge of the Sahara, on the Ziz River near present-day Rissani in the Tafilalt oasis. Founded in 757/758 by Kharijite migrants, it became the northern terminus of the trans-Saharan trade routes linking the Mediterranean world with West Africa.

History

Ruled initially by the Midrarid dynasty, Sijilmasa prospered on trade in gold, salt, slaves and ivory. It was fought over by the Fatimids and Umayyads of Córdoba, later controlled by the Almoravids, Almohads, Marinids and Alaouites. Leo Africanus visited in the 16th century. The city fell into ruin in the 19th century; its ruins, largely of mudbrick, are a major archaeological site in Morocco.
