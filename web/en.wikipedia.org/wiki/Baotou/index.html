Baotou (Chinese: 包头; Mongolian: ᠪᠤᠭᠤᠲᠤ, "place with deer") is a prefecture-level city in the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region of China and the region's largest city by population, exceeding the capital Hohhot. It lies on the northern bank of the Yellow River at the edge of the Gobi Desert, roughly 650 km west of Beijing.

Economy

Baotou is one of China's principal heavy-industry centres. The Baotou Iron and Steel Company (Baogang), founded in the 1950s as a key project of China's First Five-Year Plan, built the modern city around steelmaking and heavy machinery, including armoured vehicles. North of the city lies the Bayan Obo mining district, the world's largest known deposit of rare-earth elements, whose ores are processed in Baotou; the city consequently dominates global production of light rare earths such as neodymium and cerium. The industry's tailings pond west of the city is one of the largest of its kind.

History and sights

The area was a minor garrison and trading town under the Qing dynasty and grew rapidly after 1949 with planned industrial development and broad, Soviet-influenced avenues. Sites include the Wudangzhao Lamasery, one of the largest Tibetan Buddhist monasteries in Inner Mongolia, the Genghis Khan Mausoleum region to the south across the river, and the Saihantala grassland park within the city.
