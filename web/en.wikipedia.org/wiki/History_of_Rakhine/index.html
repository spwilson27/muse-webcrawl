History of Rakhine

Rakhine (Arakan)
LocationWestern coast of Myanmar, Bay of Bengal
Historic capitalsDhanyawadi, Vesali, Mrauk U
Key periodsAncient kingdoms; Kingdom of Mrauk U (1430–1785); Burmese conquest 1785; British rule
Modern stateRakhine State, Myanmar

The history of Rakhine (historically known as Arakan) concerns the coastal region of western Myanmar along the Bay of Bengal. Separated from central Burma by the Arakan Mountains, Rakhine developed a distinct culture with strong ties to both Southeast Asia and the Indian subcontinent.

Ancient kingdoms

According to Rakhine chronicles, early kingdoms arose at Dhanyawadi and later Vesali (Waithali), trading with India and adopting Buddhism and Hinduism from across the bay. Ancient coins and inscriptions attest to links with Bengal and the wider Indian Ocean world.

Kingdom of Mrauk U

In 1430 Narameikhla founded the kingdom of Mrauk U, which grew into a powerful maritime state in the 16th and 17th centuries. At its height it controlled parts of southeastern Bengal including Chittagong, and its kings minted coins in Bengali, Persian, and Arakanese. The Dutch and Portuguese traded there, and Arakanese fleets, often with Portuguese mercenaries, raided the Bengal coast.

Burmese conquest and British rule

In 1784–85 the Burmese Konbaung dynasty conquered Mrauk U, carrying off the revered Mahamuni Buddha image to Mandalay. Britain annexed Arakan after the First Anglo-Burmese War (1824–26), and it later became part of British Burma.

Modern era

Communal tensions between Buddhist Rakhine and Muslim communities, including the Rohingya, erupted repeatedly in the 20th and 21st centuries, notably in 2012 and during the 2017 crisis, when violence drove hundreds of thousands of Rohingya into Bangladesh. The region has also been a theater of Myanmar's civil conflicts, with the Arakan Army emerging as a major armed actor.
