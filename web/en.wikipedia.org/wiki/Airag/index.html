Airag (Mongolian: айраг) is a traditional fermented drink made from mare's milk, widely consumed in Mongolia and across the Central Asian steppe, where it is known in Turkic languages as kumis. Slightly sparkling, sour and mildly alcoholic, it is the ceremonial drink of the Mongolian summer and a symbol of hospitality.

Production

Fresh mare's milk is poured into a large leather sack or wooden vat, often hung beside the door of the ger (yurt), and churned repeatedly with a wooden paddle over one or two days. Naturally present yeasts and lactic acid bacteria ferment the milk sugars — mare's milk is unusually rich in lactose — producing lactic acid, carbon dioxide and a small amount of alcohol. Passers-by traditionally stir the churn as they enter, keeping the fermentation going.

Culture

Airag is offered to guests, sprinkled as a blessing at departures and ceremonies, and drunk in quantity during the Naadam festival season. Because mares are milked only in summer, airag is a seasonal product; a good household's airag is a matter of pride. Distilled further, it yields a stronger milk spirit called arkhi or shimiin arkhi.
