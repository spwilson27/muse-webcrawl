Kane County is a county in the northeastern part of the U.S. state of Illinois. Its county seat is Geneva, and its largest city is Aurora, part of which also extends into neighbouring counties. Kane County is one of the collar counties of the Chicago metropolitan area. It was founded in 1836 and named for Elias Kane, a lawyer and the first Secretary of State of Illinois, later a U.S. senator.

Geography

The Fox River runs north–south through the eastern part of the county, and a chain of river towns — Elgin, St. Charles, Geneva, Batavia and Aurora — grew up along its mills and bridges. The western two-thirds of the county remain largely agricultural.

Demographics and economy

The 2020 census counted about 516,000 residents, making Kane one of Illinois's most populous counties. Its economy mixes manufacturing, logistics along the Interstate 88 corridor, health care, and farming; Metra commuter rail links the Fox River cities and Elgin with Chicago.
