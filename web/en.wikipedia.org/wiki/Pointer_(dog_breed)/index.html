Pointer (dog breed)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Pointer, sometimes called the English Pointer, is a medium-sized breed of pointing dog developed in England. Pointers are used by hunters to locate game birds: when the dog scents its quarry it stops and stands rigid, head and nose directed toward the hidden birds — the characteristic "point" from which the breed and the whole pointing-dog group take their names.

History

The breed was developed in the 17th and 18th centuries, probably from pointing dogs imported from Spain (the Spanish pointer), crossed with setters, foxhounds and other breeds to increase speed and stamina as wing shooting over dogs became fashionable. The Pointer became the archetypal bird dog of the English shooting field and was exported worldwide, becoming especially influential in the United States, where it dominates field-trial competition.

Characteristics

Pointers are athletic, clean-lined dogs with a short dense coat, a long tapered head and a tail carried level with the back when working. They are energetic and wide-ranging hunters requiring substantial exercise, and are generally even-tempered and biddable in the home. Health issues can include hip dysplasia and hereditary sensory neuropathy. The breed is distinct from the German Shorthaired Pointer and other continental pointing breeds, and from pointing breeds generically — in American field usage "pointer" often refers specifically to this breed, reflected in the FDSB's registrations.
