The National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA) is a statutory body of the Government of India under the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, established to strengthen tiger conservation in the country, which holds the majority of the world's wild tigers.

Establishment

The authority was set up in December 2005 following recommendations of the Tiger Task Force, and given statutory force by the 2006 amendment to the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972. It is chaired by the Union Minister for Environment and Forests.

Functions

The NTCA approves tiger conservation plans prepared by state governments, lays down standards for tourism and project activity in tiger reserves, oversees the notification of new reserves, and conducts the quadrennial All India Tiger Estimation — one of the largest wildlife surveys in the world, carried out with the Wildlife Institute of India.

Project Tiger

The authority administers Project Tiger, launched in 1973, under which a network of more than fifty tiger reserves covering over 75,000 square kilometres receives central funding and protection standards. India's estimated wild tiger population rose to around 3,700 in the 2022 estimation.
