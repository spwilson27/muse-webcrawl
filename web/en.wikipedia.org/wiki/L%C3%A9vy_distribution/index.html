In probability theory and statistics, the Lévy distribution, named after Paul Lévy, is a continuous probability distribution for a non-negative random variable. It is a special case of the inverse-gamma distribution and one of the few distributions, along with the normal and Cauchy distributions, whose density function can be expressed in closed form among the stable distributions.

Definition

The probability density function of the Lévy distribution over the domain x ≥ μ is f(x; μ, c) = sqrt(c / (2π)) · exp(−c / (2(x−μ))) / (x−μ)3/2, where μ is the location parameter and c is the scale parameter.

Properties

The Lévy distribution is heavy-tailed: its mean and variance are both infinite. It is a stable distribution with stability parameter α = 1/2 and skewness β = 1. It arises as the distribution of the time taken by a Brownian motion to first reach a given level, and it is related to the inverse Gaussian distribution.

Applications

Lévy distributions are used to model phenomena with heavy tails, including certain financial returns, first-passage times in diffusion processes, and the distribution of waiting times in some physical systems.
