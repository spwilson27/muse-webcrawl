Secretariat of State (Mexico)

In the federal government of Mexico, a Secretariat of State (Spanish: Secretaría de Estado) is a ministry of the executive branch, headed by a Secretary of State (Secretario de Estado) appointed and freely removable by the President of the Republic. The secretariats form the core of the centralised federal public administration under the Organic Law of the Federal Public Administration (Ley Orgánica de la Administración Pública Federal), and their holders make up, together with the Legal Counsel of the Executive, the president's cabinet.

List of secretariats

The number and names of the secretariats have changed repeatedly since independence as portfolios were created, merged, or renamed. The principal secretariats in the modern era include:

  • Interior (Gobernación, SEGOB) — domestic politics, elections, internal security policy, and relations with the states and Congress.
  • Foreign Affairs (Relaciones Exteriores, SRE).
  • National Defense (Defensa Nacional, SEDENA) and Navy (Marina, SEMAR) — the two military ministries.
  • Finance and Public Credit (Hacienda y Crédito Público, SHCP).
  • Security and Citizen Protection (Seguridad y Protección Ciudadana, SSPC) — created in 2018 to take over federal policing functions.
  • Public Education (Educación Pública, SEP), Health (Salud), Labour and Social Welfare (Trabajo y Previsión Social), Economy (Economía), Agriculture and Rural Development, Environment and Natural Resources (Medio Ambiente, SEMARNAT), Infrastructure, Communications and Transportation, Tourism, Culture (created 2015), and Welfare (Bienestar).

Role and organisation

Each secretariat is organised into undersecretariats, official directorates, and decentralised bodies, and is responsible for formulating and executing federal policy in its sector, drafting bills and regulations, and administering federal programmes in coordination with state and municipal governments. Secretaries must be Mexican citizens by birth, at least 30 years old, and are accountable to Congress, which may summon them to report. Because cabinet posts are entirely at the president's discretion, secretariats are frequent instruments of presidential reorganisations at the start of each six-year term (sexenio).
