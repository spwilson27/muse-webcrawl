Removal of Sam Altman from OpenAI

On November 17, 2023, the board of directors of the artificial intelligence research organisation OpenAI announced that chief executive Sam Altman would leave the company, stating that he had not been "consistently candid" in his communications with the board. The decision triggered a crisis at the company, and within five days Altman was reinstated as CEO with a reconstituted board.

Background

OpenAI is governed by a non-profit board controlling a for-profit subsidiary — a structure intended to prioritise its mission of safe artificial general intelligence over profit. Tensions had reportedly grown between Altman and some directors, including chief scientist Ilya Sutskever, over the pace of commercialisation and AI safety.

Events

After Altman's removal, president Greg Brockman resigned, and Mira Murati was appointed interim CEO, followed two days later by Emmett Shear. Microsoft, OpenAI's largest investor, announced it would hire Altman and Brockman to lead a new AI division. In response, more than 700 of OpenAI's roughly 770 employees signed a letter threatening to resign and join Microsoft unless the board resigned and Altman was reinstated. On November 21, an agreement was reached: Altman returned as CEO, and a new initial board was formed including Bret Taylor (chair), Larry Summers and Adam D'Angelo.

Aftermath

The episode prompted widespread discussion of OpenAI's unusual governance and of corporate control over powerful AI systems. Sutskever and other directors stepped down from the board, and an internal investigation by the law firm WilmerHale concluded in 2024.
