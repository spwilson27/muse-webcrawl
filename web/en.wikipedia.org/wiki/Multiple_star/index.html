Multiple star

A multiple star is a system of three or more stars that appear to be close together in the sky. Systems of two stars are called binary stars. A multiple star may be a physical multiple system, in which the stars are gravitationally bound and orbit a common centre of mass, or an optical multiple, in which unrelated stars merely lie near the same line of sight.

Hierarchical systems

Most multiple systems are organised hierarchically: close binary pairs orbit each other, and the pairs in turn orbit with more distant companions. This arrangement helps systems remain stable over long periods. Examples include Castor, a sextuple system, and Alpha Centauri, a triple system containing Proxima Centauri.

Classification

Systems are described as triple, quadruple, quintuple, sextuple and so on according to the number of components. Triple star systems are the most common type of multiple system.
