Galata Tower

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Galata Tower
Built: 1348, by the Genoese colony of Galata
Height: about 67 m
Location: Beyoğlu, Istanbul

The Galata Tower is a medieval stone tower in the Beyoğlu district of Istanbul, on the northern shore of the Golden Horn. Built in 1348 as the great tower of the Genoese colony of Galata (they called it Christea Turris, the Tower of Christ), it is one of the city's most recognisable landmarks.

The conical-capped tower, about 67 metres tall, once anchored the northern end of the great chain that could be drawn across the Golden Horn. Under the Ottomans it served as a fire-watch tower and, in legend, as the launch point of Hezârfen Ahmed Çelebi's claimed glider flight across the Bosphorus in the 1630s — a story recorded by the traveller Evliya Çelebi and best treated as legend.

Restored several times after fires and earthquakes — its conical roof was lost to a storm in 1875 and rebuilt in the 1960s, and a major restoration concluded in 2020 — the tower now houses a museum and observation deck with panoramic views over the old city, the Golden Horn and the Bosphorus.
