Latin liturgical rites

Latin liturgical rites are the liturgical traditions of the Latin Church of the Catholic Church, distinguished from the Eastern Catholic liturgical rites. The overwhelmingly dominant form is the Roman Rite, but several other local and religious-order rites survived, especially those more than two centuries old at the time of the Council of Trent.

Principal rites

  • Roman Rite — used by most Latin Catholics; its post–Vatican II form is the Mass of Paul VI, while the 1962 form is often called the Traditional Latin Mass.
  • Ambrosian Rite — Milan, associated with St Ambrose.
  • Mozarabic Rite — Toledo and Salamanca, inherited from Visigothic Spain.
  • Braga Rite — archdiocese of Braga, Portugal.
  • Rites of religious orders — Dominican, Carmelite and Carthusian rites.
  • Gallican and Celtic rites — historically used in Gaul and the British Isles, largely superseded by the Roman Rite; Anglican Use is a variant within the Latin Church for former Anglicans.

Characteristics

Latin rites traditionally used Latin as their liturgical language, though vernacular use became general after Vatican II. Their liturgical year, sacramental forms and chant (notably Gregorian chant in the Roman Rite) distinguish them from Byzantine and other Eastern traditions.
