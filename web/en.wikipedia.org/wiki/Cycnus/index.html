Cycnus

Cycnus ("swan" in Greek: Κύκνος) is the name given to several figures of Greek mythology. Most famously, Cycnus was a close friend — in some versions a lover or relative — of Phaethon, who was transformed into a swan while mourning Phaethon's death, and was later placed among the stars as the constellation Cygnus.

Cycnus, friend of Phaethon

In Ovid's Metamorphoses, Cycnus, king of Liguria and son of Sthenelus, lamented the death of Phaethon — who had fallen into the river Eridanus (the Po) after losing control of the sun god's chariot — so intensely that as he grieved on the riverbank his hair turned to feathers and he became a swan, thereafter avoiding fire and haunting lakes and streams. The story links the swan's mournful song and the bird's association with the constellation Cygnus.

Other figures named Cycnus

  • Cycnus, son of Ares — a brutal robber who challenged travellers and built a temple from their skulls; he was killed by Heracles, a combat narrated in the pseudo-Hesiodic Shield of Heracles.
  • Cycnus of Kolonai — a son of Poseidon, king in the Troad, invulnerable to iron weapons; during the Trojan War he was killed by Achilles, who strangled or stoned him, whereupon Poseidon turned him into a swan.
  • Cycnus, son of Apollo — a beautiful youth who, spurned by his lover Phylius after setting him impossible labours, threw himself from a rock and was changed into a swan.
