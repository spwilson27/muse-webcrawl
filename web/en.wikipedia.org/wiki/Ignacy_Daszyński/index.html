Ignacy Ewaryst Daszyński (26 October 1866 – 31 October 1936) was a Polish socialist politician, journalist and statesman, a co-founder of the Polish Social Democratic Party and later of the Polish Socialist Party (PPS). He was the first prime minister of an independent Polish government in 1918 and one of the most prominent parliamentarians of the Second Polish Republic.

Early activism

Born in Zbaraż in Austrian Galicia, Daszyński was expelled from school and university for socialist agitation, and in 1892 he co-founded the Polish Social Democratic Party of Galicia. A gifted orator, he edited the party newspaper Naprzód and in 1897 was elected to the Austrian Imperial Council in Vienna, where he championed universal suffrage, an eight-hour working day and Polish independence.

1918 and the Second Republic

On 7 November 1918, in Lublin, Daszyński proclaimed the Provisional People's Government of the Republic of Poland, whose manifesto promised a democratic republic, universal suffrage (including for women), free education and sweeping social reforms. He stepped aside days later so that Józef Piłsudski's government in Warsaw could unify the country. Daszyński served as deputy prime minister in 1920–21 during the Polish–Soviet War, and as Marshal (Speaker) of the Sejm from 1928 to 1930, becoming a leading defender of parliamentary democracy against Piłsudski's authoritarian Sanation camp; he famously refused to open a Sejm session under the pressure of armed officers in 1929.

Legacy

Daszyński died in 1936 and was buried in Kraków. He is remembered as a founding figure of Polish democratic socialism and of the independent Polish state.
