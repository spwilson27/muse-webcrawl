Harrison Colyar White (21 March 1930 – 19 May 2024) was an American sociologist who was the Giddings Professor of Sociology at Columbia University. White is widely regarded as one of the most influential living (pre-2024) structural sociologists and a principal founder of the modern programme of social network analysis, sometimes summarised by the slogan of the "Harvard structuralists" in the 1970s.

Education and career

White trained first as a theoretical physicist, earning a doctorate from MIT in 1955, and then a second doctorate in sociology from Princeton in 1960. He taught at Harvard University (1963–1986), where he trained a generation of network analysts, then at the University of Arizona, and from 1992 at Columbia.

Contributions

White's Anatomy of Kinship (1963) applied algebraic models to kinship structures. His work on blockmodels (with Breiger and Boorman) formalised the idea of structural equivalence and social roles. Chains of Opportunity (1970) modelled mobility through vacancy chains in organisations. His book Identity and Control (1992; 2nd edn 2008) is a sweeping theoretical statement in which identities emerge from efforts to gain control amid network contingencies. His market theory — elaborated in "Where Do Markets Come From?" (1981) — treats markets as self-reproducing role structures among producers watching one another, an approach foundational to economic sociology; his student Mark Granovetter's embeddedness programme grew partly from this milieu.
