Willem Jacob van Stockum

Willem Jacob van Stockum (1910–1944), Dutch mathematician and physicist.

Willem Jacob van Stockum (20 November 1910 – 10 June 1944) was a Dutch mathematician and theoretical physicist who made an early contribution to general relativity and served as a bomber pilot in the Second World War.

Work in relativity

Van Stockum studied mathematics at Trinity College Dublin, graduating with a gold medal, and completed his doctorate at the University of Edinburgh. In 1937 he published one of the earliest exact solutions of the Einstein field equations describing a rotating configuration of matter — an infinite cylinder of rotating dust, now called the van Stockum dust. Later analysis showed that this solution permits closed timelike curves at sufficiently high rotation, making it one of the earliest known spacetimes containing such curves and a touchstone in discussions of time travel in general relativity.

War service and death

At the outbreak of the Second World War van Stockum, then teaching in the United States, resolved to fight against Nazi Germany. He joined the Royal Canadian Air Force and transferred to the Royal Air Force, flying Halifax bombers with No. 10 Squadron. He was killed on 10 June 1944 when his aircraft was shot down during a raid on Laval, France, shortly after the Normandy landings.
