Rhine Province

The Rhine Province (German: Rheinprovinz), also known as Rhenish Prussia, was a province of the Kingdom of Prussia and later of the Free State of Prussia, existing from 1822 to 1945. Its capital was Koblenz, and it comprised the territories along the middle and lower Rhine, including the cities of Cologne, Düsseldorf, Bonn, Aachen, Trier, Essen and Wuppertal. In 1939 it had a population of nearly eight million.

History

The province was formed in 1822 by merging the provinces of the Lower Rhine and Jülich-Cleves-Berg, territories Prussia had gained at the Congress of Vienna in 1815. It became one of Prussia's most industrially important regions, containing the Ruhr coal and steel district at its northern edge. After the First World War the Rhineland was demilitarised and partly occupied by the Allies, and the Saar Basin was detached. After the Second World War the province was divided: its northern part joined the British-zone state of North Rhine-Westphalia (1946), the southern part went to Rhineland-Palatinate in the French zone, and the district of Wetzlar to Hesse.
