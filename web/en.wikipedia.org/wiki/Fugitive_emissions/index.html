Fugitive emissions

Fugitive emissions
Common sourcesValves, flanges, seals, compressors, storage tanks, pipelines, coal mines
Typical pollutantsMethane, volatile organic compounds, refrigerant gases
MitigationLeak detection and repair (LDAR) programmes

Fugitive emissions are emissions of gases or vapours from pressurised equipment caused by leaks and other unintended or irregular releases. They differ from planned, controlled emissions through stacks or vents, and they occur across the chemical, petrochemical, oil and gas, and refrigeration industries, as well as from coal mining, where methane escapes from seams and abandoned mines.

Significance

Although individual leaks are small, their sheer number — an industrial plant may contain hundreds of thousands of valves, connectors, pump seals and flanges — makes fugitive emissions a major share of total releases. Fugitive methane from oil and gas operations is an important source of greenhouse gas emissions, while fugitive volatile organic compounds contribute to ground-level ozone formation and workplace exposure hazards.

Detection and control

Regulators in many jurisdictions require leak detection and repair (LDAR) programmes, in which components are periodically surveyed with portable analysers or optical gas imaging cameras that visualise hydrocarbon leaks in infrared. Detected leaks must be repaired within prescribed deadlines. Satellite and aircraft monitoring has increasingly been used to identify large "super-emitter" events, and improved seals, low-emission valves and vapour recovery systems can reduce fugitive losses at source.
