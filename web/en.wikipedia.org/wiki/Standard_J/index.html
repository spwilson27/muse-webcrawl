Standard J

The Standard J was a two-seat basic training biplane produced in the United States during the First World War by the Standard Aircraft Corporation of Plainfield, New Jersey. Together with its more famous contemporary, the Curtiss JN-4 "Jenny", it trained a generation of American military pilots. The definitive version, the J-1, first flew in 1916–17.

Design and service

Developed from the earlier Sloan and Standard H-series designs, the J was a conventional wood-and-fabric biplane with tandem open cockpits. The J-1 was initially powered by the unreliable Hall-Scott A-7a engine, whose failings — including a tendency to catch fire — gave the type a poor reputation compared with the JN-4, and many J-1s were later re-engined with the Curtiss OXX-6. About 1,600 were built by Standard and subcontractors.

Post-war use

Surplus J-1s were sold cheaply after the war and became popular with barnstormers and early civil operators, though the type never achieved the fame or longevity of the Jenny. A small number survive in museums, including examples at the National Museum of the United States Air Force.
