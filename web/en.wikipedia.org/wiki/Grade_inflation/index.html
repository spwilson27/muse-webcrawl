Grade inflation

Grade inflation is the awarding of higher academic grades for the same level of achievement than students received in the past, causing the average grade point average (GPA) at a school to rise over time without a corresponding increase in student performance. It has been documented at many colleges and universities in the United States and other countries since the mid-20th century.

Causes

Proposed causes include student evaluations of teaching that reward lenient grading, competition between institutions, consumer-oriented views of education, efforts to improve retention and graduation rates, and changes in grading norms following the Vietnam War era, when low grades could affect draft status.

Effects and criticism

Critics argue that grade inflation devalues the signal sent by grades, makes it harder to distinguish excellent students, and may disadvantage students at institutions with stricter standards. Defenders counter that part of the rise in grades may reflect genuinely improved teaching, better prepared students, and changes in course composition.

Responses

Some institutions have adopted grade quotas, median grade reporting on transcripts, or distribution targets in response to concerns about inflation.
