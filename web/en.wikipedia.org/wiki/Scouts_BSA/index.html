Scouts BSA is the flagship programme of Scouting America (known until 2025 as the Boy Scouts of America), serving young people aged 11 to 17. Until February 2019 the programme — then called Boy Scouting — was restricted to boys; it was renamed Scouts BSA when girls were admitted, with troops remaining single-gender but following the same programme and requirements.

Organisation

Youth join a troop, chartered to a community organisation such as a school, faith group or civic club, and are organised into small groups called patrols. The programme emphasises the "patrol method", in which youth lead their own activities under the mentorship of adult leaders (Scoutmasters). Troops typically meet weekly and camp monthly, with a week-long summer camp as a highlight.

Advancement

Scouts advance through the ranks Scout, Tenderfoot, Second Class, First Class, Star, Life and Eagle Scout, earning merit badges (more than 130 are offered, from First Aid and Camping to newer subjects in science and technology) and completing leadership service and, for Eagle, a substantial community service project. The first class of female Eagle Scouts was recognised in 2021. Religious and civic awards, camping honours and high-adventure treks supplement the rank structure.

History

The programme descends directly from the original Boy Scouting established in the US in 1910. Its expansion to include girls formed part of a broader modernisation under the "Scout Me In" campaign, amid declining membership and competition for families' time. Its sister programme for younger children is Cub Scouts, and older youth may continue into Venturing or Sea Scouts.
