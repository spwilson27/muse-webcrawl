Shanhai Pass

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shanhai Pass or Shanhaiguan (Chinese: 山海关; pinyin: Shānhǎi Guān, "Mountain-Sea Pass") is a major fortified pass of the Great Wall of China, located in present-day Qinhuangdao, Hebei, where the wall reaches the Bohai Sea. Built under the Ming dynasty beginning in 1381, it guarded the narrow corridor between Manchuria and the North China Plain and was celebrated as the "First Pass Under Heaven" (天下第一关), an inscription that adorns its main gate tower.

History

The Ming general Xu Da established the pass in the early Hongwu reign, and the fortress town grew into a rectangular walled city with four gates. The site's fame rests above all on the events of 1644: the Ming general Wu Sangui, defending the pass against the rebel Li Zicheng, chose instead to open the gates to the Manchu armies of Dorgon. The ensuing Battle of Shanhai Pass destroyed Li's army and opened the way for the Qing dynasty's conquest of China.

Layout

The fortifications include the main Zhendong gate tower, the surrounding town walls, subsidiary forts, and the stretch of wall known as Laolongtou ("Old Dragon's Head") that projects into the sea. Parts of the complex were damaged or rebuilt variously through the Qing period, the Second Sino-Japanese War and the twentieth century; the pass is now protected as a national heritage site and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of the Great Wall.
