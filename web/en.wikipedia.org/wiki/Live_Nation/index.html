Live Nation Entertainment, Inc. is an American multinational entertainment company that was founded in 2010 following the merger of Live Nation and Ticketmaster. The company promotes, operates, and manages ticket sales for live entertainment worldwide, and owns or operates hundreds of venues including amphitheatres, clubs, theatres, and festivals. It is the world's largest concert promoter and live entertainment company.

History

Live Nation's roots lie in SFX Entertainment, built by Robert F. X. Sillerman through the roll-up of regional concert promoters in the late 1990s and sold to Clear Channel Communications in 2000. Clear Channel spun off its live entertainment division as Live Nation in 2005, with Michael Rapino as chief executive. In 2009 Live Nation and Ticketmaster announced a merger; the U.S. Department of Justice approved it in 2010 subject to conditions including the divestiture of Ticketmaster's Paciolan subsidiary and a ten-year consent decree governing competitive conduct.

Operations

The company operates in three main segments: Concerts (promotion and venue operation), Ticketing (through Ticketmaster), and Sponsorship & Advertising. It promotes tens of thousands of events annually, sells hundreds of millions of tickets, and manages major festivals and venues such as the House of Blues chain and numerous amphitheatres. Artist-management interests have been held through Front Line Management and Artist Nation.

Criticism and legal issues

Live Nation–Ticketmaster has faced persistent criticism over service fees, market power, and dynamic "platinum" pricing. The 2022 presale collapse for Taylor Swift's Eras Tour drew congressional hearings and renewed antitrust scrutiny. In May 2024 the U.S. Department of Justice and numerous state attorneys general filed an antitrust lawsuit seeking to break up the company, alleging monopolisation of live music markets; the company denies the claims. The 2010 consent decree had previously been extended after findings of violations.
