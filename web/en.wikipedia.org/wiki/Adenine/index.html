Adenine

Adenine
Formula: C5H5N5
Purine nucleobase

Adenine (symbol A) is a purine nucleobase and a component of DNA, RNA and energy-carrying molecules such as ATP.

Role in nucleic acids

In DNA, adenine pairs with thymine via two hydrogen bonds; in RNA it pairs with uracil. In the ribosome and genetic code it is one of the four standard bases. Adenine attached to ribose forms adenosine; phosphorylated derivatives (AMP, ADP, ATP) are central to cellular energy transfer.

Other functions

Adenine derivatives occur in coenzymes NAD, FAD and coenzyme A, and in the signalling molecule cyclic AMP. Historically adenine was called vitamin B4, though it is no longer classified as a vitamin.
