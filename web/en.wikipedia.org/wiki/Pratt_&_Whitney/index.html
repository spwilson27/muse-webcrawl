Pratt & Whitney

Pratt & Whitney is an American aerospace manufacturer with global service operations, headquartered in East Hartford, Connecticut. It is one of the world's largest makers of aircraft engines — the "big three" alongside GE Aerospace and Rolls-Royce — and a business unit of RTX Corporation (formerly Raytheon Technologies). Its engines power military fighters such as the F-35 (the F135), tankers and transports, and a large share of the world's commercial airliners and regional aircraft; its Canadian subsidiary, Pratt & Whitney Canada, dominates the small turboprop and turboshaft market.

History

The company began in 1925 when Frederick Rentschler, backed by the Pratt & Whitney Machine Tool Company of Hartford, developed the air-cooled Wasp radial engine, which revolutionised naval aviation. In 1929 Rentschler merged the company into the United Aircraft and Transport Corporation combine; after that holding was broken up in 1934, Pratt & Whitney continued within United Aircraft (later United Technologies). Its Twin Wasp powered many Second World War aircraft, and its J57 turbojet powered the F-100 and B-52. The JT9D was one of the launch engines of the Boeing 747.

Recent decades

Pratt & Whitney's PW1000G geared turbofan — which lets the fan and turbine spin at different optimal speeds — entered service in 2016 on the Airbus A320neo family, offering substantial fuel-burn improvements; however, from 2023 a contamination problem in powdered-metal parts forced accelerated inspections and groundings of many PW1100G engines, a costly remediation programme. United Technologies merged with Raytheon in 2020 to form Raytheon Technologies, renamed RTX in 2023.
