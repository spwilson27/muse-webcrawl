Cordon sanitaire

Cordon sanitaire (French for "sanitary cordon") is a term with two main uses: in public health, a barrier used to contain the spread of an infectious disease, and in politics, a refusal by established parties to cooperate with a party considered extremist or unacceptable.

Overview

Literally: "sanitary cordon" (French)

Uses: Quarantine barrier; political exclusion strategy

Public health

A cordon sanitaire is a guarded line around an area experiencing an outbreak of infectious disease, restricting movement into and out of the area to prevent the disease from spreading. The practice dates to plague-era Europe and was used during the Ebola epidemic in West Africa in 2014, when Liberia imposed a cordon around the West Point neighbourhood of Monrovia. Critics note that such measures can be counterproductive if they trap healthy people with the sick and erode trust in authorities.

Political usage

In Belgian politics, the cordon sanitaire refers to the agreement among mainstream parties, in place since 1989, not to form coalitions or cooperate with the far-right Vlaams Blok (later Vlaams Belang). The term is also used in France and other countries for similar strategies of excluding extremist parties from government.
