Lead(II) azide, Pb(N3)2, is an inorganic compound that is a primary explosive — more powerful and less stable than lead styphnate and widely used as an initiator in detonators and blasting caps. It is the lead salt of hydrazoic acid.

Properties and production

Lead azide is a white crystalline powder, practically insoluble in water. It is prepared by metathesis between sodium azide and lead nitrate or lead acetate in aqueous solution. Commercial material ("dextrinated lead azide") is coated with dextrin to reduce its extreme sensitivity during handling; even so, it must never contact copper or brass, with which it can form the even more sensitive copper azide.

Uses and hazards

Because it detonates reliably from a small stimulus and has good thermal stability (decomposing explosively around 350 °C), lead azide has for decades been the standard primary charge in military and commercial detonators. Its sensitivity, toxicity as a lead compound, and environmental concerns have driven research into alternatives such as copper(I) 5-nitrotetrazolate (DBX-1). Like other azides it is also acutely toxic.
