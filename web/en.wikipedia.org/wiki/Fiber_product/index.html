Fiber product

In category theory, a branch of mathematics, a pullback (also called a fiber product, fibre product, fibered product or Cartesian square) is the limit of a diagram consisting of two morphisms f : X → Z and g : Y → Z with a common codomain. The pullback is often written P = X ×Z Y and comes equipped with two projection morphisms.

Universal property

Explicitly, the pullback of the morphisms f and g consists of an object P and two morphisms p1 : P → X and p2 : P → Y for which the diagram commutes, f ∘ p1 = g ∘ p2, and which is universal with this property: for any object Q with morphisms q1, q2 satisfying the same equation, there exists a unique morphism u : Q → P making the projections commute. As with all universal constructions, the pullback, if it exists, is unique up to unique isomorphism.

Examples

In the category of sets, the pullback of f : X → Z and g : Y → Z is the set of pairs (x, y) ∈ X × Y such that f(x) = g(y), with projections given by the coordinate maps. A special case is the fiber of a single map: the pullback of f along a point inclusion gives the preimage of that point. In algebraic geometry, fiber products of schemes underlie constructions such as base change and the intersection of subschemes. The pullback of differential forms along a smooth map is a related but distinct use of the term.

Properties

Pullbacks are dual to pushouts. In categories with a terminal object, the pullback of two morphisms with common codomain generalizes the product (recovered when Z is terminal) and the equalizer. A monomorphism stable under pullback is a key notion in topology and algebraic geometry, leading to the concept of base change.
