Horned grebe
Scientific namePodiceps auritus
FamilyPodicipedidae
ConservationVulnerable (IUCN)

The horned grebe or Slavonian grebe (Podiceps auritus) is a small waterbird in the grebe family Podicipedidae. In breeding plumage it is unmistakable, with a black head bearing fan-shaped tufts of golden-yellow feathers — the "horns" — bright red eyes, and chestnut flanks. In winter it is a plain black-and-white bird.

Distribution and habitat

The species breeds on freshwater lakes and marshes across northern North America and the northern Palearctic, from Iceland and Scotland east across Scandinavia and Russia, and winters mainly on sheltered coasts and large inland waters. Two subspecies are recognised: P. a. auritus in Eurasia and P. a. cornutus in North America.

Behaviour

Like other grebes, it is an expert diver, feeding on small fish, crustaceans, molluscs, and aquatic insects. Pairs build a floating nest of plant material anchored to emergent vegetation and perform elaborate courtship displays. Chicks are carried on the parents' backs.

Conservation

The horned grebe has declined substantially in parts of its range, owing to habitat loss, eutrophication, introduced predators, and competition; it is classified as Vulnerable by the IUCN. The small British breeding population, centred in Scotland, is closely monitored.
