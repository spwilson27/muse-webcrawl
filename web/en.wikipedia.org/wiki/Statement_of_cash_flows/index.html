Statement of cash flows

A typical statement of cash flows with its three sections.

In financial accounting, a statement of cash flows (also called a cash flow statement) is a financial statement that shows how changes in balance sheet accounts and income affect cash and cash equivalents, breaking the analysis down into operating, investing, and financing activities. It is one of the core financial statements, alongside the balance sheet and income statement.

The statement reports the cash generated and used during a specified period, helping investors and creditors assess a company's liquidity, solvency, and ability to generate future cash flows. Unlike the income statement, it is prepared on a cash basis, so it is unaffected by non-cash accruals such as depreciation.

Sections

SectionContents
Operating activitiesCash receipts from customers, payments to suppliers and employees, interest and taxes paid
Investing activitiesPurchase and sale of property, plant and equipment, securities, and loans to other entities
Financing activitiesIssuing and repaying debt, issuing shares, dividend payments, share repurchases

Methods of preparation

The operating section may be presented using the direct method, which lists gross cash receipts and payments, or the indirect method, which starts from net income and adjusts for non-cash items and changes in working capital. Most companies use the indirect method, although standard setters such as the FASB and IASB encourage the direct method. Under IAS 7 and ASC 230 the statement is a required part of a complete set of financial statements.

History

Cash flow reporting developed from earlier "funds statements." In the United States, SFAS 95 (1987) made the statement of cash flows mandatory, replacing the statement of changes in financial position. Its international counterpart, IAS 7, was first issued in 1977 and revised in 1992.
