World Lacrosse is the international governing body for the sport of lacrosse, responsible for overseeing the men's, women's, and indoor (box) versions of the game, as well as the newer sixes format. It organizes the World Lacrosse Championships across multiple age groups and disciplines.

History

World Lacrosse was formed in 2008 through the merger of the International Lacrosse Federation (ILF), which had governed men's lacrosse since 1974, and the International Federation of Women's Lacrosse Associations (IFWLA), founded in 1972. The unification brought all forms of the sport under a single global body. The organization was originally known as the Federation of International Lacrosse (FIL) before adopting the name World Lacrosse in 2019.

Recognition

World Lacrosse is recognized by the International Olympic Committee, having received provisional recognition in 2018 and full recognition in 2021. The sport returned to the Olympic program at the Los Angeles 2028 Games in its sixes format. World Lacrosse is also a member of the Global Association of International Sports Federations (GAISF) and the International World Games Association.

Competitions

World Lacrosse sanctions world championships for men's field lacrosse, women's field lacrosse, men's and women's box lacrosse, under-20 championships, and the World Lacrosse Sixes Championships. The men's World Lacrosse Championship has been dominated historically by the United States and Canada, while the Haudenosaunee Nationals compete as a sovereign team representing the Haudenosaunee Confederacy, the Indigenous originators of the game.

Sixes

In 2018 World Lacrosse introduced World Lacrosse Sixes, a fast-paced six-a-side discipline played on a smaller field with a 30-second shot clock, designed to increase accessibility and global growth of the sport.
