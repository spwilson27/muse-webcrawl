École de Nancy

École de Nancy Founded: 1901 (Alliance provinciale des industries d'art)
Location: Nancy, Lorraine, France
Movement: Art Nouveau
Key figures: Émile Gallé, Louis Majorelle, Daum brothers, Victor Prouvé
Glasswork in the naturalistic École de Nancy style.

The École de Nancy, or School of Nancy, was an alliance of Art Nouveau artists and manufacturers based in the city of Nancy in north-eastern France, active roughly between 1890 and 1914. Formally organised in 1901 as the Alliance provinciale des industries d'art, it united glassmakers, cabinetmakers, ironworkers and architects around a shared programme of uniting art and industry.

Its leading figure was the glassmaker and botanist Émile Gallé, whose cameo glass and marquetry furniture drew heavily on the flora of Lorraine. Other major members included the glassworks of the Daum brothers, the furniture designer Louis Majorelle and the painter and sculptor Victor Prouvé. The school's hallmark was its intense naturalism: insects, orchids, thistles and dragonflies rendered in glass, wood intarsia and wrought iron, informed by scientific botanical study and by Japanese art.

Legacy

Nancy became, alongside Paris, a principal centre of French Art Nouveau, and the city retains an exceptional concentration of its buildings, including the Villa Majorelle and the Chamber of Commerce building. The Musée de l'École de Nancy, opened in 1964 in the former home of the collector Eugène Corbin, displays one of the finest collections of the school's glass, furniture and ceramics.
