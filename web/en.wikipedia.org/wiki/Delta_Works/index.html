The Delta Works (Dutch: Deltawerken) is a series of construction projects in the southwest of the Netherlands to protect a large area of land around the Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta from the sea. Constructed between 1954 and 1997, the works consist of dams, sluices, locks, dykes, levees and storm surge barriers located in the provinces of South Holland and Zeeland. The American Society of Civil Engineers has declared the Delta Works, together with the Zuiderzee Works, one of the Seven Wonders of the Modern World.

History

The catastrophic North Sea flood of 1953, which killed 1,836 people in the Netherlands, spurred the creation of the Delta Commission and the Delta Act of 1958. The plan shortened the Dutch coastline by about 700 kilometres and raised flood protection standards in the delta to a 1-in-4,000-year event. Rather than simply damming all estuaries, environmental and fishing concerns led to the Oosterscheldekering being built as an open storm surge barrier with movable gates — the largest and most celebrated part of the project, completed in 1986.

Principal components

The works include the Hollandse IJssel barrier (1958), the Zandkreekdam, Veerse Gatdam, Grevelingendam, Haringvlietdam, Brouwersdam, Oosterscheldekering, Philipsdam, Volkerakdam, Oesterdam, Bathse Spuisluis, Hartelkering and the Maeslantkering near Rotterdam (completed 1997), one of the largest moving structures on Earth, whose two floating arms close automatically when a storm surge threatens the city and port. Together with the Afsluitdijk of the Zuiderzee Works, the Delta Works underpin the Dutch system of flood defence that protects the low-lying western Netherlands, much of which lies below sea level.
