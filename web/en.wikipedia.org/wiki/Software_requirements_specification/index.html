Software requirements specification

A software requirements specification (SRS) is a document that describes what a software system is to do and how it is expected to perform. It lays out functional and non-functional requirements, constraints, and assumptions, and serves as an agreement between stakeholders, developers, and testers about the product to be built. The SRS is a key deliverable of requirements engineering within the software development process.

Contents

A typical SRS, following templates derived from the former IEEE 830 standard (superseded by ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148), includes:

  • Introduction — purpose, scope, definitions, references, and overview.
  • Overall description — product perspective, functions, user characteristics, constraints, and assumptions.
  • Specific requirements — detailed functional requirements; external interface requirements (user, hardware, software, communications); and non-functional requirements such as performance, security, reliability, availability, and maintainability.
  • Appendices and index — supporting information and traceability matrices.

Qualities of a good SRS

Requirements in an SRS should be correct, unambiguous, complete, consistent, ranked for importance or stability, verifiable, modifiable, and traceable. Each requirement is typically given a unique identifier so that it can be traced forward to design elements and test cases, and backward to stakeholder needs.

Role in development models

In plan-driven (waterfall) projects, a complete SRS is produced and baselined before design begins, and changes proceed through formal change control. In agile projects, the equivalent information is often distributed among a product backlog, user stories with acceptance criteria, and lightweight documentation, though regulated industries still commonly require formal specifications.
