AFC Cup

The AFC Cup was an annual continental club football competition organised by the Asian Football Confederation (AFC) from 2004 to 2024. It was contested by clubs from AFC member associations that did not receive direct slots in the AFC Champions League, making it Asia's second-tier club tournament. In 2024 it was replaced by the AFC Champions League Two.

History

The AFC Cup was created in 2004 as part of the AFC's "Vision Asia" restructuring, initially for clubs from fourteen "developing" football nations. Kuwaiti clubs were especially successful: Al-Kuwait and Kuwait SC rivals Qadsia SC won multiple titles, and Al-Quwa Al-Jawiya of Iraq won three consecutive editions from 2016 to 2018. The last edition, in 2023–24, was won by Central Coast Mariners of Australia, the first Australian club to win an AFC club title.

Format

The competition used zonal groups (West, ASEAN, Central, South, and East Asia) followed by zonal semi-finals, inter-zone play-offs, and a single-match final. Champions typically earned a qualifying play-off place in the following season's AFC Champions League.
