Gävle goat

The Gävle goat (Swedish: Gävlebocken) is a traditional Christmas display erected annually in the city of Gävle, Sweden: a giant version of the Swedish Yule goat figure made of straw on a wooden frame. It has been erected each year since 1966 at Slottstorget in central Gävle.

History and vandalism

The goat stands about 13 metres (43 ft) high and 7 metres long, and weighs roughly 3.6 tonnes. It has become famous worldwide because of the many times it has been destroyed — most frequently by arson — despite security measures including guards, fences, cameras and flame-retardant treatment. Vandals have also rammed it with a car and, in 2023, its straw was eaten away by jackdaws attracted to seeds in the straw. Betting on whether the goat will survive the season has been offered by bookmakers, and the goat has its own social media presence; each year, its survival (or burning) attracts international media attention, and the city has come to embrace the notoriety as promotion.

Yule goat tradition

The display derives from the Scandinavian Yule goat, a straw goat figure associated with Christmas traditions in Sweden and neighbouring countries.
