Permanent University Fund

The Permanent University Fund (PUF) is a sovereign wealth fund established in the Constitution of Texas of 1876 to support the University of Texas System and the Texas A&M University System. It is one of the largest university endowments in the United States, built principally on oil and gas revenues from public lands in West Texas.

History and lands

The fund traces its origins to land set aside for public education by the Republic of Texas and to the constitutional provisions of 1876, which dedicated public lands to the support of a university. The PUF land estate comprises about 2.1 million acres in West Texas, much of it in the Permian Basin. The discovery of oil on university lands — famously the Santa Rita No. 1 well in Reagan County, which struck oil in 1923 — transformed the fund from largely grazing land into an immense endowment.

Management and distribution

The surface and mineral interests of the lands are managed under the University of Texas System, and the fund's financial assets are invested by the University of Texas Investment Management Company (UTIMCO), created in 1996. Income is distributed through the Available University Fund (AUF), which pays debt service on construction bonds and supports academic excellence at the two university systems. By the fund's constitutional allocation, the University of Texas System receives two-thirds of the distributions and the Texas A&M System one-third. The fund's market value has grown to tens of billions of dollars, ranking the combined UT/PUF endowment among the very largest in American higher education.
