Skylab

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Skylab with its parasol sunshade, 1973.

Skylab was the first space station launched and operated by NASA. Built from a converted upper stage of the Saturn V rocket, it was launched on 14 May 1973 and occupied by three successive three-person crews between 1973 and 1974, for a total of 171 days.

The station was damaged during launch: its micrometeoroid shield tore away, taking one solar array with it and jamming the other. The first crew saved the station by deploying a parasol sunshade through a scientific airlock to cool the overheating workshop, and later freed the jammed array during a spacewalk. Skylab crews conducted extensive solar astronomy, Earth observation and medical experiments, proving that humans could live and work in space for months.

Re-entry

With the Space Shuttle delayed, Skylab's orbit decayed and it re-entered the atmosphere on 11 July 1979, scattering debris across the Indian Ocean and sparsely populated Western Australia. The incident drew worldwide attention and a tongue-in-cheek littering fine from the shire of Esperance, paid decades later by a radio listener fundraiser.
