Stiefel-Whitney class

This article title uses an ASCII hyphen. The same topic, using a typographic en dash, is at Stiefel–Whitney class.

In algebraic topology, a Stiefel-Whitney class is a characteristic class associated to a real vector bundle. The classes are named after Eduard Stiefel and Hassler Whitney, who developed them in the 1930s and 1940s in their work on the topology of manifolds and fibre bundles.

Definition

Given a real vector bundle E over a topological space B, the Stiefel-Whitney classes wi(E) are elements of the mod-2 cohomology groups Hi(B; Z/2). They can be characterized axiomatically by naturality, the Whitney sum formula w(E ⊕ F) = w(E)w(F), and a normalization condition. In particular, w1(E) = 0 if and only if the bundle is orientable, and w2(E) is related to the existence of spin structures.

Relation to other classes

Stiefel-Whitney classes are the mod-2 analogues of Chern classes for complex vector bundles: the Chern classes of the complexification of a real bundle reduce mod 2 to (squares of) Stiefel-Whitney classes. The Bockstein homomorphism applied to w2 yields the third integral Stiefel-Whitney class, the obstruction to a spinc structure.
