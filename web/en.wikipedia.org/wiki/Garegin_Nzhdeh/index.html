Garegin Nzhdeh

Garegin Ter-Harutyunyan (Armenian: Գարեգին Տեր-Հարությունյան), better known by his nom de guerre Garegin Nzhdeh (1 January 1886 – 21 December 1955), was an Armenian statesman, military commander, and national hero. As a member of the Armenian Revolutionary Federation, he was involved in the national liberation struggle and revolutionary activities during the First Balkan War and World War I.

Nzhdeh was one of the key political and military leaders of the First Republic of Armenia (1918–1921), and is widely known for being the founder and commander of the Republic of Mountainous Armenia in 1921, which prevented the region of Syunik from being absorbed into Soviet Azerbaijan.

Early life and career

Nzhdeh was born in the village of Kznut in Nakhichevan, in the Russian Empire. He studied law in Saint Petersburg before joining the Armenian Revolutionary Federation and taking part in revolutionary activity in the Balkans and the Caucasus. During the First Balkan War he fought in a volunteer unit on the Bulgarian side against the Ottoman Empire. In World War I he commanded Armenian volunteer units on the Caucasus front and was decorated by the Russian command.

Mountainous Armenia

After the collapse of the First Republic and the Sovietization of Armenia in late 1920, Nzhdeh led the defense of the Zangezur (Syunik) region, proclaiming the Republic of Mountainous Armenia in April 1921. His resistance secured Syunik's inclusion within Soviet Armenia. In July 1921, after the Soviet government pledged that the region would remain part of Armenia, he and his soldiers left for Persia.

Later life

Nzhdeh spent the interwar years in Bulgaria and worked among the Armenian diaspora. During World War II he sought support for Armenia's cause from various powers. In 1944 he was arrested by SMERSH in Sofia and sentenced to 25 years' imprisonment in the Soviet Union, where he died in Vladimir prison in 1955. He remains a celebrated figure in Armenia, where monuments, including a major square and statue in Yerevan, commemorate him; aspects of his legacy are debated by historians.
