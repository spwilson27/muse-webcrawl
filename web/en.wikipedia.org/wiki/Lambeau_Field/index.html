Lambeau Field is an American football stadium in Green Bay, Wisconsin, the home field of the Green Bay Packers of the National Football League (NFL). Opened in 1957 as City Stadium, it was renamed in 1965 in memory of Packers founder, player, and long-time head coach Curly Lambeau, who had died earlier that year. With a capacity of 81,441 it is among the largest stadiums in the NFL, remarkable for a metropolitan area of only about 300,000 people.

History

The stadium replaced the old City Stadium as the Packers' home and has been expanded and renovated repeatedly, most comprehensively in a 2003 redevelopment that added the Lambeau Field Atrium, the Packers Pro Shop, and the Green Bay Packers Hall of Fame while preserving the historic bowl. The surrounding Titletown District has since developed as an entertainment and commercial area.

Lambeau Field is revered in American sports culture for its small-town setting amid residential streets, its decades-long season-ticket waiting list, and its legendary cold-weather games, above all the 1967 NFL Championship Game — the "Ice Bowl" — played in temperatures of −13 °F (−25 °C) with a wind chill far lower, won by the Packers on Bart Starr's quarterback sneak. The stadium is also the origin of the "Lambeau Leap", the touchdown celebration in which a scoring player jumps into the stands, popularised by LeRoy Butler in 1993.
