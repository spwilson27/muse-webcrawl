Random number generation

Random number generation is the process of producing a sequence of numbers that cannot be predicted better than by random chance. A device or algorithm performing it is a random number generator (RNG).

Methods

True random number generators extract randomness from physical phenomena — dice, coin flipping, radioactive decay, thermal noise, or atmospheric noise — and historically supplied tables of random digits for statistics and lotteries. Computers more commonly use pseudorandom number generators (PRNGs): deterministic algorithms, such as the Mersenne Twister or linear congruential generators, that produce long sequences approximating the properties of random numbers from a seed. Cryptographically secure PRNGs add resistance to prediction even given partial output.

Applications

Random numbers underpin gambling and lotteries, statistical sampling, Monte Carlo simulation, randomised algorithms, and cryptography, where unpredictable keys and nonces are essential to security. Faulty or predictable generation has caused notable failures, from biased lottery draws to compromised cryptographic keys.
