Heimat (film series)

Heimat is a series of German films directed by Edgar Reitz, widely regarded as one of the landmarks of post-war German cinema and television. The series chronicles the life of the Simon family in the fictional village of Schabbach in the Hunsrück region of the Rhineland, and through them the social history of Germany across the 20th century. The German word Heimat, denoting "homeland" and the place to which one belongs, is notoriously difficult to translate, and the series explores its emotional pull and its betrayals.

The series

  • Heimat: A Chronicle of Germany (1984), 11 episodes, over 15 hours, following the family from 1919 to 1982, in a mixture of black-and-white and colour cinematography.
  • Heimat II: A Chronicle of a Generation (1992), 13 episodes, following Hermann Simon through the Munich art and music scene of the 1960s.
  • Heimat III: A Chronicle of Beginnings and Endings (2004), 6 episodes, set from the fall of the Berlin Wall to the millennium.
  • Home from Home: Chronicle of a Vision (2013), a feature-length prequel set in the 1840s, concerning emigration from the Hunsrück to Brazil.

Reception

The original series was a major television event in Germany and Britain, praised for its epic scale combined with intimate domestic realism, and it influenced the development of long-form television drama internationally. Reitz shot the works principally in villages of the Hunsrück, which has since become a destination for devoted viewers.
