Patella

The patella (pl. patellae or patellas), also known as the kneecap, is a flat, circular-triangular bone which articulates with the femur (thigh bone) and covers and protects the front of the knee joint. It is the largest sesamoid bone in the human body — a bone embedded within a tendon — sitting inside the tendon of the quadriceps femoris muscle.

Structure and function

The patella increases the leverage of the quadriceps muscle by holding its tendon away from the joint axis, improving the efficiency of knee extension by up to about 30 percent. Its posterior surface is covered with articular cartilage and glides in the trochlear groove of the femur. The patellar ligament connects the apex of the patella to the tibial tuberosity of the shin bone.

Development

The patella ossifies from a cartilage model between the ages of about three and six years. In some individuals it remains in two pieces (bipartite patella), a normal variant that can occasionally be mistaken for a fracture.

Clinical significance

The patella can fracture from direct blows or sudden quadriceps contraction, and can dislocate laterally, particularly in adolescents. Conditions affecting the joint surface behind the patella include chondromalacia patellae and patellofemoral pain syndrome ("runner's knee"). Severe damage may require patellectomy (removal), which reduces extension strength.
