Roy E. Disney

Roy Edward Disney (January 10, 1930 – December 16, 2009) was an American businessman and executive of The Walt Disney Company. The son of company co-founder Roy O. Disney and nephew of Walt Disney, he was for decades the last member of the Disney family actively involved in running the company, and twice led shareholder revolts that reshaped its leadership.

Career

Disney joined the company after graduating from Pomona College in 1951, working first as an assistant editor on the True-Life Adventures nature films, contributing as a writer and producer. He was elected to the board of directors in 1967. After his father's death in 1971 he watched the company drift, and in 1977 he resigned his executive position over disagreements about its direction, while retaining his board seat.

The 1984 and 2003 revolts

In 1984 Disney resigned from the board and, with his business partner Stanley Gold and fellow dissident shareholder Sid Bass, engineered the ouster of chief executive Ron Miller and the recruitment of Michael Eisner and Frank Wells — the management team credited with the company's renaissance. Disney returned as vice chairman and head of the animation department, championing the revival that produced The Little Mermaid, Beauty and the Beast, Aladdin, and The Lion King, and serving as executive producer of Fantasia 2000.

Twenty years later he again broke with management, resigning in November 2003 with a blistering letter attacking Eisner's leadership and launching, with Gold, the "Save Disney" campaign. At the 2004 annual meeting 43% of shares were withheld from Eisner, who shortly afterwards gave up the chairmanship and stepped down as CEO in 2005. Disney returned as a consultant and director emeritus.

Personal life

Disney was a passionate competitive sailor: he set records in the Transpacific Yacht Race to Hawaii and campaigned the maxi-yacht Pyewacket. He supported the California Institute of the Arts (CalArts) and numerous charities. He died of stomach cancer in Newport Beach, California, in 2009, a day after the anniversary of his uncle Walt's death.
