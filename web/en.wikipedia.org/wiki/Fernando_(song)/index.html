Fernando (song)

"Fernando" is a song written by Benny Andersson, Björn Ulvaeus, and Stig Anderson and recorded by Swedish pop group ABBA. First recorded in Swedish by ABBA member Anni-Frid Lyngstad for her 1975 solo album, it was re-recorded by ABBA in English and released as a stand-alone single in March 1976, becoming one of the group's biggest sellers — with reported sales of around ten million copies — and reaching number one in at least thirteen countries, including fourteen weeks at the top in Australia.

Background

The song began as "Fernando," a track on Frida's Swedish solo album Frida ensam (1975), in which the narrator addresses a heartbroken friend. When adapted for ABBA, Stig Anderson's English lyric was recast — at Ulvaeus's suggestion — as two elderly Mexican revolutionaries reminiscing about a battle in their youth, inspired by a picture of old veterans. The result, with its pan-flute colouring and nostalgic lyric ("There was something in the air that night, the stars were bright, Fernando"), was recorded at Polar's Stockholm studio sessions and released as a non-album single, later included on the Greatest Hits and international editions of Arrival.

Chart performance

"Fernando" was a phenomenon in Australia, where it held number one for fourteen weeks — then a record — propelled by ABBA's hugely successful 1976 tour and television specials. It also topped charts in the UK, Germany, France, Ireland, and many other countries, and reached number 13 in the United States. It is frequently cited among ABBA's best-selling singles worldwide.

In popular culture

The song appears in the film Muriel's Wedding (1994), is performed in the Mamma Mia! stage and film franchise (in Mamma Mia! Here We Go Again it is sung by Cher and Andy García), and has been covered by numerous artists. A Spanish-language version, recorded by ABBA for their Gracias Por La Música album, was a hit in Latin America.
