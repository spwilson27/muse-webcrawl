Lisa Marie Simpson is a fictional character in the animated television series The Simpsons. She is the middle child and elder daughter of Homer and Marge Simpson, and the younger sister of Bart and older sister of Maggie. Voiced by Yeardley Smith, Lisa first appeared in the Tracy Ullman Show short "Good Night" in 1987.

Lisa Simpson with her saxophone.

Character

Lisa is an exceptionally intelligent eight-year-old—an overachieving student at Springfield Elementary School whose intellectual curiosity sets her apart from her family and classmates. She is a talented baritone saxophonist, a vegetarian, a Buddhist, an environmental and social activist, and frequently serves as the moral centre of the show, in contrast to her father's hedonism and her brother's mischief. Her star-shaped hair, red-orange dress and white pearl necklace are iconic elements of her design; creator Matt Groening named her after his own sister.

Reception

Lisa has been widely praised as one of the greatest animated characters of all time, and Yeardley Smith won a Primetime Emmy Award for her performance in 1992. Episodes centred on Lisa, such as "Lisa's Substitute", "Lisa the Vegetarian" and "Lisa's Wedding", are consistently ranked among the series' best, and the character has been credited with bringing vegetarianism and environmentalism into mainstream popular culture.
