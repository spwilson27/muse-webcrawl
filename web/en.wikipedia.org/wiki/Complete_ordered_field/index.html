Complete ordered field

A complete ordered field is an ordered field in which every non-empty subset that has an upper bound has a least upper bound (supremum) in the field. The field of real numbers is the classic example, and a fundamental theorem of real analysis states that any two complete ordered fields are isomorphic — so the real numbers are, up to isomorphism, the unique complete ordered field.

Definition

An ordered field is a field equipped with a total order compatible with addition and multiplication (positive elements stay positive when added, and the product of two positive elements is positive). Such a field is complete if it satisfies the least-upper-bound property (also called Dedekind completeness). Completeness implies the Archimedean property: for any elements x and y > 0 there is a natural number n with n·y > x, so a complete ordered field contains no infinitesimals or infinitely large elements in the Archimedean sense.

Examples and non-examples

The rational numbers form an ordered field that is not complete: the set of rationals whose square is less than 2 is bounded above but has no rational supremum. The real numbers, constructed from the rationals by Dedekind cuts or Cauchy sequences, fill exactly these gaps. Non-Archimedean ordered fields such as the hyperreal numbers are not complete (in this order-theoretic sense), which is why completeness pins down the reals uniquely.

Importance

Completeness is the property that makes the real numbers suitable for calculus: it implies the monotone convergence theorem, the intermediate value theorem, the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem, and the equivalence of Cauchy completeness with Dedekind completeness for Archimedean fields. The axiomatic characterisation of the reals as the (unique) complete ordered field is a standard foundation for real analysis.
