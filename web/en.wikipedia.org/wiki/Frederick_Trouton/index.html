Frederick Trouton

Frederick Thomas Trouton FRS (24 November 1863 – 21 September 1922) was an Irish physicist known for Trouton's rule in thermodynamics, the Trouton–Noble experiment, which sought to detect motion through the luminiferous aether, and the Trouton–Rankine experiment.

Life and work

Trouton was born in Dublin and educated at Trinity College Dublin, where he became assistant to George Francis FitzGerald. In 1884 he announced Trouton's rule, the empirical observation that the entropy of vaporization of many liquids is approximately the same, about 85–88 joules per kelvin per mole. He later became professor of physics at University College London.

With H. R. Noble, Trouton devised an experiment using a charged, suspended capacitor whose plates were expected to rotate as the Earth moved through the aether; the null result, obtained around 1903, was one of the experiments that challenged aether theory and was later explained by special relativity. The Trouton–Rankine experiment of 1908 similarly sought, without success, to measure changes in electrical resistance due to aether motion.
