The Sapphire Jubilee of Elizabeth II marked the 65th anniversary of the accession of Queen Elizabeth II to the thrones of the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth realms, reached on 6 February 2017. The Queen was the first British monarch ever to celebrate a sapphire jubilee.

Commemoration

Unlike the Golden and Diamond Jubilees, the Sapphire Jubilee was marked without large-scale public celebrations, in keeping with the Queen's wishes; she spent the day privately at Sandringham House, as 6 February is also the anniversary of the death of her father, George VI. A 41-gun salute was fired by the King's Troop, Royal Horse Artillery in Green Park, London, and gun salutes were also held at other locations.

Commemorative issues included a set of stamps and coins issued by the Royal Mail and the Royal Mint; a £5 coin bore a sapphire-blue design. The portrait photograph of the Queen wearing sapphire jewellery, taken by David Bailey in 2014, was reissued for the occasion. In September 2017 a new community centre in Romford was named the Sapphire Jubilee Community Centre in honour of the milestone.
