West Falkland
Spanish: Gran Malvina
Area: 4,532 km² (1,750 sq mi)
Highest point: Mount Adam, 700 m
Population: under 200

West Falkland (Spanish: Isla Gran Malvina) is the second largest of the Falkland Islands in the South Atlantic Ocean, separated from East Falkland by the Falkland Sound. It covers about 4,532 square kilometres (1,750 square miles) and is sparsely populated, with fewer than 200 inhabitants, most living in the settlement of Port Howard on the east coast.

Geography

The island's western side is hilly, with the Hornby Mountains running along the Falkland Sound. Its highest point is Mount Adam at 700 metres (2,300 ft). The main settlements are Port Howard and Fox Bay; much of the land is farmed for sheep.

History

Early maps show West Falkland was known to 18th-century sealers and whalers. During the Falklands War of 1982 the island saw little fighting, though Argentine forces were stationed at Port Howard. The island's economy rests on sheep farming and some tourism, including Mount Harriet and wildlife watching.
