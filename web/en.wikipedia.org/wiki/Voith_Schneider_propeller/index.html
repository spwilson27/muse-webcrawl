Voith Schneider propeller

The Voith Schneider propeller (VSP), also known as a cycloidal drive, is a specialized marine propulsion system. It consists of a circular array of vertical blades projecting from the bottom of a vessel; each blade oscillates about its own axis as the rotor turns, and by varying the blades' pitch pattern the direction and magnitude of thrust can be changed almost instantaneously in any horizontal direction.

Principle

Invented by Austrian engineer Ernst Schneider and developed by the Voith company from the late 1920s, the VSP works analogously to a trochoidal propeller. Because thrust can be vectored through 360 degrees without a rudder, vessels fitted with VSPs — typically two units — have exceptional maneuverability.

Applications

VSPs are used on tugboats (including Voith Water Tractors), double-ended ferries, minesweepers, buoy tenders, and offshore support vessels, where precise station-keeping and rapid thrust reversal are more valuable than top speed. Their efficiency is lower than that of large screw propellers at high speed, limiting adoption on fast ships.
