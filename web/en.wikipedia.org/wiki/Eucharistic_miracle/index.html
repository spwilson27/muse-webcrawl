Eucharistic miracle

A Eucharistic miracle is any miracle involving the Eucharist, most often in the Catholic tradition a reported visible transformation of the consecrated host or wine, such as a host bleeding or turning into flesh. Such claims are connected with the doctrine of transubstantiation, in which the bread and wine become the body and blood of Christ while retaining their outward appearances.

Famous cases

Among the best-known reported cases is the Miracle of Lanciano in Italy, traditionally dated to the 8th century, in which a host is said to have turned into flesh and wine into blood; the relics are preserved in Lanciano. Other widely cited cases include those of Bolsena (1263), associated with the institution of the feast of Corpus Christi, and more recent reports in places such as Buenos Aires and Sokółka, Poland.

Church assessment

The Catholic Church investigates reported cases through diocesan processes and sometimes scientific examination, but recognition of a Eucharistic miracle is not required of the faithful, and the Church distinguishes between such extraordinary phenomena and the doctrine of the real presence itself.
