Tāwhiao

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tāwhiao (born Matutaera Tāwhiao, c. 1822–1894) was the second Māori King, leader of the Waikato tribes and of the Kīngitanga (Māori King Movement) in New Zealand. He succeeded his father, Pōtatau Te Wherowhero, in 1860 and reigned for 34 years.

Reign

Tāwhiao's reign was dominated by the New Zealand Wars. After the government invasion of the Waikato in 1863–64, he and his followers were driven into exile in the King Country (Te Rohe Pōtae), where they remained for some twenty years behind the aukati (boundary line) while their lands were confiscated. Tāwhiao pursued peaceful strategies, developing a prophetic and religious dimension to his leadership often called Tāwhiao's "covenant".

Later years

In 1881 Tāwhiao formally laid down arms, and in 1884 he travelled to London to petition Queen Victoria over Treaty of Waitangi grievances, without success. He established institutions including a parliament (Te Kauhanganui) and a newspaper, and worked to preserve Māori self-governance. His motto, "Ki te kāhore he whakakitenga ka ngaro te iwi" ("Without vision, the people perish"), is widely quoted.

Death

Tāwhiao died on 26 August 1894 and was succeeded by his son Mahuta. His annual coronation celebrations (Koroneihana) at Tūrangawaewae marae in Ngāruawāhia remain central to the Kīngitanga.
