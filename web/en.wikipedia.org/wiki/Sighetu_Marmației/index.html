Sighetu Marmației (often shortened to Sighet; Hungarian: Máramarossziget; Yiddish: סיגעט, Siget) is a city in Maramureș County, northern Romania, near the confluence of the Iza and Tisa rivers on the border with Ukraine. Once the seat of the historic Maramureș county and a centre of the salt trade, it is widely known as the birthplace of writer and Nobel laureate Elie Wiesel and as the site of the Sighet prison, one of the most notorious political prisons of communist Romania.

History

The settlement is first mentioned in the 14th century and grew as a market town for the salt of the surrounding mines. For centuries it belonged to the Kingdom of Hungary; after the First World War it passed to Romania, returned to Hungary under the Second Vienna Award in 1940, and was restored to Romania after 1944–45. Before the Second World War, Jews made up a large share of the town's population and Sighet was a centre of Hasidic Judaism; in May 1944 the community was deported to Auschwitz, among them the teenage Elie Wiesel, whose memoir Night recounts the experience.

Sighet Prison and memorial

Between 1948 and 1955 the former county prison held much of Romania's pre-war political and religious elite; many, including former prime minister Iuliu Maniu, died there. The building now houses the Memorial to the Victims of Communism and to the Resistance, established in the 1990s under the poet Ana Blandiana — one of the first memorials of its kind in post-communist Europe.

Main sights and culture

The Maramureș Village Museum displays traditional wooden houses and churches from the region. The Elie Wiesel Memorial House marks the writer's birthplace. Sighet serves as a gateway to the wooden churches of Maramureș, several of them UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
