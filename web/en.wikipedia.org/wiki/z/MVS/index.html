MVS

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Multiple Virtual Storage, more commonly called MVS, is the most commonly used operating system on the System/370, System/390 and IBM Z mainframe computers. IBM developed MVS, along with OS/VS1 and SVS, as a successor to OS/360. It is unrelated to IBM's other mainframe operating systems, such as VSE and VM.

First released in 1974 as OS/VS2 Release 2, MVS introduced true virtual storage addressing to the OS/360 line: each job received its own private address space while sharing system code. Successive releases — MVS/XA (31-bit addressing), MVS/ESA, and its renaming into OS/390 and then z/OS — extended the architecture to 64-bit while preserving compatibility with MVS-era applications, datasets and JCL. Core MVS facilities include JES2/JES3 job entry subsystems, TSO interactive computing, CICS and IMS transaction managers, and RACF security.
