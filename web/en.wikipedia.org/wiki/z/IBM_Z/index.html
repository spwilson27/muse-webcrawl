IBM Z

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

IBM Z is a family name used by IBM for all of its z/Architecture mainframe computers, beginning with the z900 in 2000 and continuing through machines such as the z16 (2022) and z17 (2025). The "Z" stands for "zero downtime", reflecting the line's design goal of extremely high availability. IBM Z systems run operating systems including z/OS, z/VM, z/VSE and Linux on IBM Z, and are widely used by banks, insurers, airlines and governments for high-volume transaction processing.

The family descends from the System/360 of 1964, whose architectural compatibility the line still maintains: applications written for earlier generations can run on current hardware. Modern systems emphasise encryption (pervasive encryption), AI acceleration on the Telum processor, and very large I/O and memory capacity. The related IBM LinuxONE line uses the same hardware with a Linux-only focus.
