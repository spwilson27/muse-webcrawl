Inner light

The Inner Light is a core concept in the theology of the Religious Society of Friends (Quakers), referring to the light of Christ or the presence of God within each person, which guides conscience, reveals truth, and makes direct, unmediated religious experience possible. Variants of the phrase used by Friends include "the Light Within", "that of God in everyone", and the "Inward Light".

Origins

The doctrine was central to the preaching of George Fox, the founder of Quakerism in seventeenth-century England. Fox taught that believers did not need ordained clergy, set liturgies or outward sacraments, because Christ could speak directly to the soul: "There is one, even Christ Jesus, that can speak to thy condition." Early Friends drew on scriptural language such as John 1:9, "the true Light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world".

Theology and practice

Belief in the Inner Light underlies the Quaker practices of silent, unprogrammed worship, in which participants wait expectantly for the promptings of the Spirit, and of consensus-based decision making in meetings for business. Because the Light is present in every person, the doctrine supports the Quaker testimonies of equality, peace and integrity: it motivated early Friends to refuse oaths and military service, to campaign against slavery, and to accord women an unusual degree of equality in ministry.

Interpretations

Friends have interpreted the Inner Light in differing ways. Conservative and evangelical Friends emphasise the Light as specifically the presence of Christ and test inward leadings against Scripture, while liberal Friends often understand it in more universal terms, as a divine spark or the continuum of spiritual experience accessible across faiths. The term overlaps with, but is distinct from, the Christian mystical tradition of divine illumination and the concept of the "inner Christ".
