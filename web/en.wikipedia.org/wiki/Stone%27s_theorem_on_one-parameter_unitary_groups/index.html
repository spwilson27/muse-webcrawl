Stone's theorem on one-parameter unitary groups

In mathematics, Stone's theorem, formulated by Marshall Harvey Stone, is a basic result in functional analysis. It establishes a correspondence between strongly continuous one-parameter unitary groups on a Hilbert space and (possibly unbounded) self-adjoint operators.

Statement

Let U(t) be a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group on a Hilbert space H. Then there exists a unique self-adjoint operator A such that U(t) = eitA for all real t. Conversely, for every self-adjoint operator A, the family eitA forms such a group.

Applications

The theorem is fundamental to quantum mechanics: time evolution of a quantum system is given by a one-parameter unitary group, and Stone's theorem identifies its generator as the Hamiltonian, the observable of energy. It also underlies the theory of Schrödinger operators and quantum dynamics.
