Red heifer

The red heifer (Hebrew: פרה אדומה, para adumma) is a specific type of cow mentioned in the Hebrew Bible. According to the Book of Numbers (Numbers 19), the ashes of a red heifer were used in a ritual of purification for people who had come into contact with a corpse, a state of ritual impurity known as tumat hamet.

Biblical account

The commandment instructs that a red heifer "without defect, in which there is no blemish, and upon which a yoke has never come" be taken outside the camp and slaughtered. The animal was burned together with cedar wood, hyssop, and scarlet wool, and its ashes were collected and mixed with water to produce the "water of purification" (mei niddah). Sprinkling this water on the third and seventh days purified a person who had touched a dead body, a grave, or human bones.

Requirements

Traditional Jewish law imposes extremely strict conditions on the qualifying animal: it must be entirely red, with even two black or white hairs disqualifying it; it must never have borne a yoke or performed labor; and it must be at least three or four years old. Because of these rare conditions, a valid red heifer is described in rabbinic literature as an extraordinarily uncommon event — the Mishnah records that only nine red heifers were prepared from the time of Moses until the destruction of the Second Temple.

In Jewish tradition

The red heifer ritual is the archetypal example of a chok, a divine statute whose rationale is beyond human comprehension: paradoxically, the ashes purify the impure while rendering those who prepare them impure. The Mishnah tractate Parah is devoted to its laws. In modern times, some groups, notably the Temple Institute in Jerusalem, have sought to breed and identify a ritually valid red heifer, viewing it as a prerequisite for resuming the full Temple service; candidate animals have been reported in Israel and the United States.

In Christianity

The New Testament Epistle to the Hebrews (9:13–14) refers to the ashes of the red heifer as a foreshadowing of the purifying sacrifice of Christ, a reading developed in later Christian theology.
