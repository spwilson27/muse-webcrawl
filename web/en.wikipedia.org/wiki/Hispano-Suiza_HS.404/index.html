Hispano-Suiza HS.404

The Hispano-Suiza HS.404 was a 20 mm autocannon widely used by the French, British, and American air forces in the Second World War. Designed in the 1930s by the French subsidiary of Hispano-Suiza from the work of engineer Marc Birkigt, it was based on mechanisms derived from the Oerlikon FF series.

Design and use

The HS.404 was gas-operated with a delayed blowback action, fired from drum magazines (later belt feed), and offered a good combination of muzzle velocity, rate of fire, and explosive shell power. The British produced it as the Hispano Mk II and later Mk V, arming fighters such as the Spitfire and Typhoon. The United States manufactured it as the M1, AN/M2, and M3 cannon, though American versions suffered reliability problems with their chambers.

Legacy

Post-war developments of the HS.404 included the British ADEN and French DEFA revolver cannons, and the design influenced aircraft armament for decades.
