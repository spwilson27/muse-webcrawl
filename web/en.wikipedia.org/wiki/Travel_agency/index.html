Travel agency

A travel agency is a private retailer or public service that provides travel and tourism-related services to the general public on behalf of accommodation or travel suppliers. Travel agencies can provide outdoor recreation, arranging logistics for luggage and medical items, arranging public transport services, car rentals, bureau de change, insurance, and package tours.

History

The modern travel agency traces to Thomas Cook, who in 1841 organized a railway excursion in England and founded the firm that became Thomas Cook & Son. Agencies proliferated with commercial aviation in the 20th century, earning commissions from airlines, hotels, and tour operators, and using global distribution systems (GDS) such as Amadeus, Sabre, and Travelport to book inventory.

Types and the online shift

Agencies include high-street retail agencies, business travel management companies, niche specialists (cruise, adventure, luxury), and host agencies supporting independent contractors. From the mid-1990s, online travel agencies (OTAs) such as Expedia and Booking.com, plus direct supplier websites, disintermediated much of the traditional trade, causing airline commissions to be cut or eliminated. Traditional agencies have responded by charging service fees and emphasizing expertise, complex itineraries, and corporate accounts. Accreditation in many markets runs through bodies such as IATA (for airline ticketing) and national associations.
