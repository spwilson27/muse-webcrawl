In the United States Armed Forces, a general is a four-star general officer rank in the Army, Marine Corps, Air Force, and Space Force. A general is equivalent to an admiral in the Navy and Coast Guard and ranks above a lieutenant general (three-star) and below the special wartime grades of General of the Army and General of the Air Force.

Appointment and limits

Generals are nominated by the President of the United States and confirmed by the Senate. The grade is typically tied to specific senior positions, such as service chiefs, combatant commanders, and the Chairman and Vice Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff; officers generally revert to a lower permanent grade when leaving a four-star billet unless placed on the retired list. Statute limits the number of four-star officers on active duty in each service.

Insignia

Four-star officers wear four silver stars as their insignia of grade. The rank originated in the American Civil War era; Ulysses S. Grant became the first modern three- and four-star holder as General of the Army in 1866, and the modern four-star "general" grade was standardised during the twentieth century.
