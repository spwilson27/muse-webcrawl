The Third Battle of the Aisne (27 May – 6 June 1918) was a German offensive, codenamed Blücher-Yorck, during the German spring offensive of the First World War. Intended as a diversion to draw Allied reserves south before a decisive blow in Flanders, it instead achieved one of the deepest advances of the war on the Western Front.

The attack

On 27 May 1918, more than 4,000 German guns opened a short, intense bombardment along the Chemin des Dames ridge, followed by stormtrooper infiltration that overwhelmed the depleted French Sixth Army and the British IX Corps resting in the sector. The Aisne was crossed on the first day, Soissons fell, and within three days German forces had advanced some 55 kilometres to the river Marne at Château-Thierry — the closest approach to Paris since 1914.

Outcome

The advance created a vulnerable salient, outran its artillery and supplies, and was halted by French resistance stiffened by arriving American divisions, notably at Belleau Wood and Château-Thierry. Allied casualties were around 137,000 and German casualties comparable. The battle set the stage for the Allied counterstroke in the Second Battle of the Marne in July, which began the final Allied advance of the war.
