Personal protective equipment

Personal protective equipment
ExamplesGloves, safety glasses, helmets, respirators, gowns
Used inHealthcare, construction, manufacturing, laboratories

Personal protective equipment (PPE) is protective clothing, helmets, goggles, or other garments or equipment designed to protect the wearer's body from injury or infection. The hazards addressed by protective equipment include physical, electrical, heat, chemical, biohazards, and airborne particulate matter. Protective equipment may be worn for job-related occupational safety and health purposes, as well as for sports and other recreational activities.

Types

PPE is commonly categorised by the part of the body protected: eye and face protection (safety glasses, face shields), hearing protection (earplugs, earmuffs), respiratory protection (dust masks, respirators such as N95 masks), hand protection (gloves), body protection (aprons, gowns, high-visibility clothing), and fall protection (harnesses). In healthcare, PPE such as gloves, gowns, masks and eye protection forms a barrier against infectious agents and is a central element of standard precautions.

Regulation

Workplace PPE is regulated in many jurisdictions. In the United States, the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) requires employers to assess hazards, provide appropriate PPE at no cost to employees, and train workers in its use. In the European Union, PPE is governed by Regulation (EU) 2016/425, which sets design and manufacturing requirements.

Limitations

PPE is considered the last line of defence in the hierarchy of hazard controls, because it does not remove the hazard and its effectiveness depends on correct selection, fit, use and maintenance. Shortages of PPE during the COVID-19 pandemic highlighted supply-chain vulnerabilities in healthcare systems worldwide.
