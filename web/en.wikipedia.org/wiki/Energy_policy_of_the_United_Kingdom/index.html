Energy policy of the United Kingdom

The energy policy of the United Kingdom refers to the United Kingdom's efforts in providing security of energy supply, reducing carbon emissions, raising the population's standards of living, and reducing energy poverty. Since 2000, UK energy policy has been directed towards reducing greenhouse gas emissions while maintaining reliable and affordable energy supplies, overseen by the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero.

Climate targets

The Climate Change Act 2008 made the UK the first country to set legally binding carbon budgets, originally committing to an 80% cut in emissions by 2050 relative to 1990 levels; in 2019 this was amended to a "net zero" target of 100%. The independent Committee on Climate Change advises the government and monitors progress against five-yearly carbon budgets.

Electricity generation

The UK electricity mix has shifted dramatically away from coal: coal provided around 40% of electricity in 2012 but fell to near zero, with the last coal-fired power station closing in 2024. Renewables, led by offshore and onshore wind power, have grown to supply roughly half of electricity generation, alongside nuclear power and gas. Policy mechanisms have included the Renewables Obligation, feed-in tariffs, and Contracts for Difference.

Energy security

The UK became a net importer of energy in 2004 as North Sea oil and gas production declined from its peak around 1999–2000. Energy security policy has emphasised diversification of supply, electricity interconnectors with neighbouring countries, and, following the 2022 energy price crisis, accelerated deployment of domestic renewables and nuclear power.
