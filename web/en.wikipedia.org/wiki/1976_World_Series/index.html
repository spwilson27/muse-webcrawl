1976 World Series

DatesOctober 16–21, 1976
ChampionsCincinnati Reds
Runner-upNew York Yankees
Series score4–0
MVPJohnny Bench

The 1976 World Series was the championship series of Major League Baseball's 1976 season. The defending champion Cincinnati Reds, known as the Big Red Machine, swept the New York Yankees in four games. It was the Reds' second consecutive World Series title and the first World Series appearance for the Yankees under owner George Steinbrenner.

Summary

Cincinnati won the first two games at Riverfront Stadium, then took two more at Yankee Stadium. Catcher Johnny Bench dominated the series, batting .533 with two home runs in the deciding game and winning Most Valuable Player honours. The Reds' lineup of Bench, Pete Rose, Joe Morgan, Tony Pérez and George Foster overwhelmed New York pitching, while Cincinnati's bullpen closed out close games.

Significance

The sweep confirmed the 1975–76 Reds as one of baseball's great teams and marked the last World Series played entirely in daytime starts or traditional scheduling before night games became standard for the event. The Yankees rebounded to win the next two World Series. See also Cincinnati Reds, New York Yankees, Johnny Bench and Big Red Machine.
