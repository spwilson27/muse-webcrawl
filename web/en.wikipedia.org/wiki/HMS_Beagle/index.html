HMS Beagle

HMS Beagle was a Cherokee-class 10-gun brig-sloop of the Royal Navy, launched on 11 May 1820. She is famous for her second survey voyage (1831–1836), on which the young naturalist Charles Darwin served as the captain's companion and gentleman naturalist — the voyage that helped shape his theory of evolution by natural selection.

Voyages

Her first voyage (1826–1830) surveyed Patagonia and Tierra del Fuego under Phillip Parker King. The second, under Robert FitzRoy, circumnavigated the globe, charting South America, the Galápagos Islands, Tahiti, Australia and South Africa. A third voyage (1837–1843) surveyed much of the Australian coast.

Later life

After 1845 she was moored as a coastguard watch vessel in Essex and was sold and broken up in 1870. Darwin's account, The Voyage of the Beagle, became a classic of travel and science writing.
