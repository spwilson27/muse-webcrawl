Brick Lane is a street in the East End of London, in the London Borough of Tower Hamlets. It runs from Swanfield Street in Bethnal Green in the north, crosses the Bethnal Green Road, passes through Spitalfields and is linked to Whitechapel High Street in the south by the short stretch of Osborn Street. The street is famous for its many curry houses, its Sunday markets, and its vibrant street art.

History

The street's name derives from brick and tile manufacture that began in the area in the 15th century, using the local brick earth deposits. Brick Lane was first developed in the 17th century as the London suburbs spread eastward.

The area around Brick Lane has successively been home to immigrant communities. French Huguenot weavers settled in Spitalfields in the late 17th century, followed by Irish immigrants in the 19th century and Jewish immigrants fleeing Eastern Europe later in the same century. The Old Truman Brewery, once one of the largest breweries in the world, operated on Brick Lane from the 17th century until 1989.

From the mid-20th century the neighbourhood became a centre of London's Bangladeshi community, particularly migrants from the Sylhet region. The southern part of the street is lined with curry restaurants, and the area is sometimes called "Banglatown." The street sign is displayed in both English and Bengali.

Markets and culture

Brick Lane Market, held on Sundays, sells a wide range of goods including vintage clothing, furniture, bric-a-brac and food. Nearby are the Old Truman Brewery complex, which hosts markets, exhibitions and events, and a number of galleries and music venues. The street is also known for its regularly changing graffiti and street art, attracting artists from around the world.

The street gave its name to Monica Ali's 2003 novel Brick Lane, which depicts the life of a Bangladeshi woman living in the area, and to the 2007 film adaptation of the novel.

Landmarks

  • The Jamme Masjid (Brick Lane Mosque), a building that has served successively as a Huguenot chapel, a synagogue and a mosque.
  • The Old Truman Brewery complex.
  • The Beigel Bake, a well-known 24-hour bakery.
