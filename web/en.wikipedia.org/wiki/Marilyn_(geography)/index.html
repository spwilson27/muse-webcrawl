Marilyn (geography)

Illustration: A typical Marilyn hill (placeholder image)

A Marilyn is a mountain or hill in the British Isles (including Ireland) with a topographical prominence of at least 150 metres (492 feet), regardless of its absolute height. The concept was devised by Alan Dawson and published in his 1992 book The Relative Hills of Britain. The name is a punning counterpart to the Scottish "Munros" (mountains over 3,000 feet), evoking the actress Marilyn Monroe and the word "Munro".

The list

The Marilyns were defined as a complete list of the relative hills of Britain: every summit with 150 m or more of drop on all sides. The original list contained 1,542 hills in Great Britain and a further set in Ireland; the total for the British Isles is around 2,000, and the list is continually refined as surveys improve (one hill on the Isle of Man and several in England, including hills in lowland counties such as Norfolk and Leicestershire, qualify). The Marilyns include all the higher British lists — the Munros, Corbetts, Grahams, and (in England and Wales) the Hewitts are all subsets by prominence criteria — but also embrace small hills of the lowlands and islands, making "Marilyn bagging" an unusually varied pursuit.

Sub-Marilyns and regional lists

Hills with prominence between 140 and 150 metres are termed Sub-Marilyns and are candidates for promotion upon resurvey. Related Dawson lists include the Humps (hundred-metre prominence hills). The Marilyns are published and maintained through the Relative Hills of Britain database and the Database of British and Irish Hills, and climbing all of them — including remote sea stacks and island summits requiring boat access — is a long-term challenge completed by only a small number of hillwalkers. The highest Marilyn is Ben Nevis; the lowest are barely 150 m of prominence in flat eastern counties, such as Melton Hill in Suffolk.
