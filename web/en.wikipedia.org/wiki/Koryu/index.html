Koryū (Japanese: 古流, "old school") is the term for traditional schools of Japanese martial arts founded before the Meiji Restoration of 1868, as distinct from the modern gendai budō such as judo, karate-do, and kendo. A school qualifies as koryū if its lineage of transmission (ryūha) can be traced to the feudal period and its curriculum was designed for the battlefield or duelling arts of the samurai.

Characteristics

Koryū typically teach a comprehensive curriculum (sōgō bujutsu) combining weapons such as the sword (kenjutsu, iaijutsu), spear, naginata, and staff with grappling (jujutsu). Training centres on pre-arranged forms (kata) transmitted through licensing systems such as menkyo kaiden, and students historically swore blood oaths (keppan) to the school. Unlike modern budō, koryū have no sport competitions, standardised ranks, or central governing bodies; authority rests with the headmaster (sōke) of each lineage.

Surviving schools include Tenshin Shōden Katori Shintō-ryū, one of the oldest extant traditions, and the various lines of Muso Jikiden Eishin-ryū and Shindō Musō-ryū. Koryū are demonstrated publicly at annual exhibitions such as the Meiji Shrine dedication in Tokyo and are practised by small communities in Japan and abroad.
