Paraparaumu is a town in the south-western North Island of New Zealand. It lies on the Kāpiti Coast, 55 kilometres (34 mi) north of the capital, Wellington. Like other towns in the area, it has a partner settlement at the coast, Paraparaumu Beach, which lies directly opposite Kapiti Island. Together, Paraparaumu and Paraparaumu Beach have a population of around 29,000. The wider Kāpiti Coast District had about 58,000 residents as of the early 2020s.

The name derives from Māori: parapara means "scraps" and umu an earth oven — traditionally explained as a reference to a feast at which only scraps remained for late arrivals.

History

The area was settled by Māori iwi including Ngāti Toa under Te Rauparaha in the 1820s. In the 1940s the construction of an airfield at Paraparaumu — later Kapiti Coast Airport — brought development; the airport served as Wellington's main airport from 1949 until 1959, when Wellington International Airport opened. After the Second World War the town grew rapidly as a commuter and retirement settlement for Wellington.

Geography and climate

Paraparaumu lies on a coastal plain between the Tasman Sea and the Tararua Range. The Wharemauku Stream flows through the town. The climate is temperate maritime, with mild summers, cool winters and reliable rainfall; the coast is somewhat sheltered by Kapiti Island, which is a nature reserve visible from the beach.

Transport

The town is served by the North Island Main Trunk railway; Paraparaumu station is the northern terminus of the Kapiti Line suburban service to Wellington. The Transmission Gully motorway, opened in 2022, improved road access to Wellington. Kapiti Coast Airport offers domestic services.

Commerce and community

Paraparaumu is the commercial centre of the Kāpiti Coast District, with the Coastlands shopping centre one of the earliest large shopping malls in New Zealand. The Southward Car Museum, one of the largest private automobile collections in the Southern Hemisphere, is located near the town. Local education is provided by schools including Paraparaumu College and Kapiti College.
