IS-95

IS-95 (cdmaOne)
Family: CDMA2000
Developed by: Qualcomm
Released: 1995
Status: Legacy

Interim Standard 95 (IS-95), marketed as cdmaOne, is a digital cellular telephone standard that uses code-division multiple access (CDMA). Developed by Qualcomm and standardised by the Telecommunications Industry Association (TIA), it was the first commercial cellular technology based on CDMA and a major second-generation (2G) standard in North America, South Korea and elsewhere.

Technical overview

IS-95 operates in 1.25 MHz channel bandwidths, spreading each call with a unique code so many users share the same frequency band. It uses variable-rate vocoders (8 and 13 kbit/s), power control to manage the near-far problem, soft handoff between cells, and rake receivers to combine multipath signals. Data services in the original revision were limited to about 14.4 kbit/s; IS-95B raised packet data rates to up to 115 kbit/s.

Deployment and successors

Commercial service began in Hong Kong in 1995 and spread widely through the late 1990s. IS-95 networks were largely succeeded by CDMA2000 1xRTT and EV-DO (3G) systems, and most carriers shut down their CDMA networks in the early 2020s as they moved to LTE and 5G.
