Clay County, Minnesota

The Clay County Courthouse in Moorhead, the county seat.

Clay County is a county in the U.S. state of Minnesota. As of the 2020 census, the population was 65,318. Its county seat is Moorhead. Clay County is part of the Fargo–Moorhead metropolitan statistical area, which straddles the Minnesota–North Dakota border along the Red River of the North.

The county was created in 1862 and organised in 1872. It was originally named Breckenridge County; voters changed the name in 1862 in honour of Henry Clay (1777–1852), the American statesman and Secretary of State under John Quincy Adams.

Geography

Clay County covers about 1,053 square miles in northwestern Minnesota. Its western border is the Red River of the North, opposite Fargo and Cass County, North Dakota. The eastern part of the county is rolling glacial terrain with numerous lakes and rises toward the Leaf Hills, while the west lies in the flat bed of glacial Lake Agassiz — the Red River Valley — renowned for its rich black soil. The Buffalo River flows westward across the county to join the Red River. Interstate 94 and U.S. Highways 10 and 75 cross the county.

Communities

Moorhead, the largest city, is home to Minnesota State University Moorhead and Concordia College. Other cities include Dilworth, Barnesville, Hawley, Glyndon, Ulen, Hitterdal, Felton, Georgetown, Comstock and Sabin. The county also contains the western edge of the Detroit Lakes lakes country resorts around its eastern townships.

Demographics and economy

Agriculture — sugar beets, corn, soybeans, wheat and potatoes — dominates the county's economy, supported by the Red River Valley's fertile soil; the American Crystal Sugar cooperative operates a major processing plant in Moorhead. Education and health care are leading urban employers. The county's population has grown steadily with the Fargo–Moorhead metro area, and Moorhead's population passed 44,000 in the 2020 census.
