Loch Lomond (Scottish Gaelic: Loch Laomainn) is a freshwater Scottish loch, the largest inland stretch of water in Great Britain by surface area, straddling the Highland Boundary Fault.

Geography

The wild, narrow northern fjord-like arm contrasts with the broad lowland south dotted with islands including Inchmurrin, the largest freshwater island in the British Isles. Ben Lomond (974 m) rises on the east shore, traversed by the West Highland Way. The loch drains via the River Leven to the Clyde at Dumbarton.

Culture and protection

Celebrated in the song "The Bonnie Banks o' Loch Lomond", the loch forms the heart of Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park (established 2002), popular for boating, watersports and wildlife.
