University of Oxford

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Radcliffe Camera in Oxford: a circular domed stone library against a blue sky, surrounded by college buildings]

The University of Oxford is a collegiate research university in Oxford, England. There is evidence of teaching there as early as 1096, making it the oldest university in the English-speaking world and the world's second-oldest university in continuous operation (after Bologna). (The title 'Oxford University' redirects to this article.)

Oxford is made up of 39 constituent colleges and a range of academic departments organised into four divisions. It consistently ranks among the world's leading universities, and its alumni include dozens of British prime ministers, heads of state, Nobel laureates and Olympians.

History

The university grew rapidly after 1167, when Henry II banned English students from attending the University of Paris. The collegiate system, the tutorial method of teaching, and institutions such as the Bodleian Library (1602) and the Ashmolean Museum (1683) define its character.

Admissions and life

Undergraduate admission is famously competitive and includes interviews; students belong both to a college and to the university. The Oxford-Cambridge rivalry finds expression in the annual Boat Race on the Thames.
