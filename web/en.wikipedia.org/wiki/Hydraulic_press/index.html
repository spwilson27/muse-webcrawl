Hydraulic press

A hydraulic press is a machine press using a hydraulic cylinder to generate a compressive force. It uses the hydraulic equivalent of a mechanical lever, and was invented by Joseph Bramah, who patented it in 1795; it is therefore also known as the Bramah press.

Principle

The press works on Pascal's principle: pressure exerted on a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions. A small force applied by a pump piston over a small area creates pressure in the fluid; this pressure acts on the much larger area of the main ram piston, producing a greatly multiplied force, at the cost of a correspondingly shorter movement of the ram. Press capacities range from a few tonnes for bench and workshop presses to tens of thousands of tonnes for forging presses.

Applications

Hydraulic presses are used for forging, stamping, moulding, clinching, deep drawing, metal forming, powder compacting and assembly operations such as press-fitting bearings and gears. Laboratory presses prepare samples such as KBr pellets for spectroscopy, and hydraulic presses are familiar from the popular "Hydraulic Press Channel" demonstration videos of objects being crushed.
