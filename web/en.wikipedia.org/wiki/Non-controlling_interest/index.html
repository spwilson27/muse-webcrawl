Non-controlling interest

A non-controlling interest (NCI), formerly known as a minority interest, is the portion of a subsidiary's equity that is not owned by the parent company. When a parent controls a subsidiary but owns less than 100 per cent of it, the consolidated financial statements include all of the subsidiary's assets and liabilities, and the share belonging to outside owners is reported separately.

Accounting treatment

Under IFRS 3 and IFRS 10, and under US GAAP (ASC 810), the non-controlling interest is presented within equity, separately from the equity of the parent's owners — a change from earlier practice, when minority interest was often shown outside equity as a liability or mezzanine item. Consolidated profit or loss is allocated between the parent and the NCI even if this leaves the NCI with a deficit balance.

Measurement

At acquisition, IFRS 3 allows NCI to be measured either at fair value or at the NCI's proportionate share of the subsidiary's identifiable net assets; US GAAP requires fair value. Subsequent changes in a parent's ownership that do not result in loss of control are treated as equity transactions, with no gain or loss recognised in profit or loss.
