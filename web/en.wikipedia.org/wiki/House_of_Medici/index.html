The House of Medici was an Italian banking family and political dynasty that rose to power in Florence during the early 15th century. Through the Medici Bank—once the largest in Europe—the family dominated Florentine politics and became hereditary Grand Dukes of Tuscany, while their patronage fueled the Italian Renaissance.

Rise to power

Giovanni di Bicci de' Medici founded the bank in 1397. His son Cosimo ("the Elder") became de facto ruler of Florence in 1434. Lorenzo de' Medici, "the Magnificent" (r. 1469–1492), presided over a golden age of art and learning, patronizing Botticelli, Michelangelo, and Leonardo da Vinci. The family survived exile (1494–1512) and the assassination attempt of the Pazzi conspiracy (1478).

Grand Dukes and royalty

Alessandro and then Cosimo I established hereditary rule; Cosimo I became the first Grand Duke of Tuscany in 1569. Medici popes Leo X and Clement VII shaped the turbulent era of the Reformation. Catherine de' Medici became queen consort and regent of France; Marie de' Medici also became queen of France. The line ended with Gian Gastone in 1737, and his sister Anna Maria Luisa bequeathed the family's treasures to Florence in 1743.

Legacy

Medici wealth built or embellished the Uffizi, the Pitti Palace, the Laurentian Library, and numerous churches; their patronage of Galileo, Brunelleschi, and the Platonic Academy makes the family synonymous with the Renaissance.
