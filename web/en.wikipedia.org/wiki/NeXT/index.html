NeXT, Inc. (later NeXT Computer, Inc. and NeXT Software, Inc.) was an American computer and software company founded in 1985 by Steve Jobs after he left Apple Computer. Based in Redwood City, California, NeXT developed high-end workstations aimed at higher education and business, and later the pioneering WebObjects platform.

History

Jobs founded NeXT with several former Apple colleagues and an initial personal investment of million; Canon later invested 00 million. The NeXT Computer, launched in 1988 and nicknamed "the cube" for its one-foot magnesium cubic case, introduced a built-in magneto-optical drive, a Motorola DSP for audio, and the NeXTSTEP operating system, built on the Mach kernel and BSD Unix with an object-oriented application framework written in Objective-C. Tim Berners-Lee used a NeXT workstation at CERN to create the first web browser and web server in 1990–91.

Legacy

Although the hardware sold in limited volumes and NeXT exited the hardware business in 1993, its software had lasting influence. Apple acquired NeXT in 1997 for about 29 million, bringing Jobs back to Apple and making NeXTSTEP the foundation of Mac OS X (now macOS), and through it iOS. NeXT's WebObjects was used to build early versions of the Apple Store and iTunes Store.
