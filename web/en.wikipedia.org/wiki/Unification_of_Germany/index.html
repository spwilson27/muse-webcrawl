Unification of Germany

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The unification of Germany (German: Deutsche Einigung) was a process of building the first nation-state for Germans with federal features based on the concept of Lesser Germany (one without Austria). It commenced on 18 August 1866 with adoption of the North German Confederation Treaty establishing the North German Confederation, and ended on 18 January 1871 with the proclamation of Wilhelm I as German Emperor in the Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of Versailles.

Prior to 1803, the German-speaking lands of Central Europe consisted of more than 300 political entities, most of them part of the loose Holy Roman Empire or independent inheritances of the Habsburg hereditary lands. After the Empire's dissolution in 1806, the Congress of Vienna created the German Confederation of 39 states, dominated jointly by Prussia and Austria.

Background

Economic integration through the Zollverein customs union (1834), industrialisation and railways, and the spread of liberal nationalism eroded particularism. The Revolution of 1848 briefly produced the Frankfurt Parliament, which offered the imperial crown to Frederick William IV of Prussia; his refusal ended the liberal attempt at unification "from below".

Bismarck and the wars of unification

Under Minister President Otto von Bismarck, Prussia achieved unification "from above" through three wars: the Second Schleswig War against Denmark (1864); the Austro-Prussian War (1866), which expelled Austria from German affairs and produced the Prussian-dominated North German Confederation (1867); and the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71), which brought the south German states into alliance and culminated in victory at Sedan and the siege of Paris.

Result

The German Empire proclaimed in 1871 was a federation of 25 states dominated by Prussia, whose king became German Emperor and whose minister president became Chancellor. Austria was excluded (the kleindeutsche Lösung), leaving a German question that would resurface in the twentieth century, until German reunification in 1990 completed unification in another form.
