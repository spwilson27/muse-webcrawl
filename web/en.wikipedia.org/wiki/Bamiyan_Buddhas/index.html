Buddhas of Bamiyan

The 55-metre Western Buddha before its destruction

The Buddhas of Bamiyan were two monumental statues of Gautama Buddha carved into the cliffs of the Bamyan valley in the Hazarajat region of central Afghanistan, about 130 kilometres northwest of Kabul. Built in the 6th and 7th centuries CE in the Gandhara style, the larger statue stood 55 metres (180 ft) tall and the smaller 38 metres (125 ft); they were for centuries among the tallest standing Buddha statues in the world, landmarks on the Silk Road described by the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang.

Destruction and aftermath

In March 2001, the Taliban government of Mullah Mohammed Omar demolished the statues with explosives and artillery, declaring them idols, despite international condemnation and appeals from UNESCO and many governments. The act became a worldwide symbol of cultural heritage destruction. The empty niches remain, and the Bamyan valley's cultural landscape was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List in 2003, simultaneously on the List of World Heritage in Danger. Fragments of the statues were recovered and catalogued, and debate continues over whether and how to reconstruct them; a 3D light projection briefly recreated the larger Buddha's image in its niche in 2015.
