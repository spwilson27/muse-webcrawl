Kythera

Kythera (Greek: Κύθηρα) is a Greek island lying opposite the south-eastern tip of the Peloponnese, north-west of Crete, traditionally counted among the Ionian Islands. The island has an area of about 280 km² and a population of roughly 3,600, concentrated in the capital, Chora, and the port of Diakofti.

History

Kythera was sacred to Aphrodite, who in one tradition was born from the sea foam near the island — hence her epithet Cytherea; a famous sanctuary of Aphrodite Urania stood there. The island passed through Spartan, Athenian, Roman and Byzantine hands, was long held by Venice (which called it Cerigo) and belonged to the Venetian Ionian Islands until they were ceded to Greece in 1864. Many Kytherians emigrated in the 19th and 20th centuries, forming a large diaspora, especially in Australia.

Culture

The island's chief sights include the Venetian castle of Chora, the medieval town of Palaiochora, the monastery of Myrtidiotissa and the beaches of Kapsali and Kaladi. The Watteau painting Embarkation for Cythera immortalised the island as an allegory of love.
