Valley of Hinnom

The Valley of Hinnom (Hebrew: Gēʾ ben-Hinnōm, "Valley of the Son of Hinnom") is a valley in Jerusalem running along the southern and western sides of the Old City, joining the Kidron Valley near the Siloam pool area. In the Hebrew Bible it is associated with the Topheth, a site of child sacrifice to Moloch, and in later Jewish and Christian tradition its Greek name, Gehenna, became a term for the place of punishment of the wicked after death — a synonym for hell.

Biblical references

The valley is first mentioned in the Book of Joshua as part of the boundary between the tribes of Judah and Benjamin. The Books of Kings and Jeremiah condemn the kings Ahaz and Manasseh for burning their sons as offerings at Topheth in the valley, and describe Josiah defiling the site to end the practice. Jeremiah prophesied that the valley would be renamed the "Valley of Slaughter" because of the burials there.

Gehenna

In Second Temple Judaism and the New Testament, the name Gehenna (Greek Geenna) developed from its topographical sense into an eschatological one: the place or state of final punishment. The valley itself was used as a burial ground, including the field Akeldama, traditionally purchased with the thirty pieces of silver returned by Judas Iscariot. Archaeologically the valley contains numerous rock-cut tombs from the First and Second Temple periods, and in modern Jerusalem it hosts parks, concerts and cultural events at the Hinnom Valley park and the Sultan's Pool.
