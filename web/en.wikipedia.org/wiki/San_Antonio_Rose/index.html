San Antonio Rose

"San Antonio Rose" is a Western swing standard written by Bob Wills and first recorded by Bob Wills and his Texas Playboys in 1938 as an instrumental. A vocal version with lyrics, issued as "New San Antonio Rose" in 1940, became one of the group's biggest hits and is often regarded as Wills's signature song.

History

The tune is an adaptation of the Mexican melody "Spanish Two Step". Wills's 1938 instrumental recording featured his fiddle alongside the steel guitar of Leon McAuliffe. For the 1940 vocal version, Tommy Duncan sang lyrics praising a girl from San Antonio — "Deep within my heart lies a melody, a song of old San Antone" — backed by an expanded arrangement with a horn section that gave the record a big-band flavour unusual for a country act.

Legacy

The song has been recorded by hundreds of artists, including Bing Crosby, who had a hit with it in 1941, as well as Patsy Cline, Willie Nelson, and Clint Eastwood. It was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame, and its success helped establish Western swing as a nationally popular style. The melody's Mexican influence reflects the border musical culture of Texas that shaped Wills's sound.
