Dismal Swamp Canal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Dismal Swamp Canal is a canal located along the eastern edge of the Great Dismal Swamp in Virginia and North Carolina in the United States. Opened in 1805, it is the oldest continually operating man-made canal in the United States. The canal runs between Chesapeake, Virginia, and South Mills, North Carolina, connecting the Elizabeth River and Chesapeake Bay with Albemarle Sound.

History

In the colonial era, water transport was the major method of moving goods in the Tidewater region of Virginia and North Carolina. Surveying of the swamp began in 1763, and a young George Washington was among the early promoters of draining and developing the area; the Washington Ditch still bears his name. The Dismal Swamp Canal Company began digging in 1793, largely using enslaved labor. Hand dug under difficult conditions, the 22-mile (35 km) canal opened in 1805.

Although only about five feet deep at first, the canal carried timber products, shingles, and other goods. It was widened and improved over the nineteenth century, and locks were rebuilt. During the American Civil War the canal had strategic value and was fought over by Union and Confederate forces. In 1929 the federal government purchased the canal, and the United States Army Corps of Engineers has maintained it since, deepening and widening it to its present dimensions.

The canal today

The canal forms part of the Atlantic Intracoastal Waterway and is used chiefly by recreational boaters travelling between Chesapeake Bay and the sounds of North Carolina. It is maintained with a controlling depth of about six feet and has locks at Deep Creek, Virginia, and South Mills, North Carolina. The canal is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and is also recognized as part of the Underground Railroad story of the Great Dismal Swamp, where escaped enslaved people found refuge in maroon communities.
