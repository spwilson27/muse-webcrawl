Gamete

A gamete is a haploid cell that fuses with another haploid cell during fertilisation in organisms that reproduce sexually. In animals, the female gamete is the ovum (egg cell) and the male gamete is the sperm; in flowering plants, the male gametes are contained in pollen and the female gametes in the ovule. The word comes from the ancient Greek gametḗ, "wife", and gamétēs, "husband".

Haploidy

Gametes carry half the genetic information of an individual — one copy of each chromosome type. They are produced by a specialised cell division called meiosis, which also shuffles genetic material through recombination. When two gametes fuse (syngamy), the resulting zygote restores the full diploid chromosome number and can develop into a new organism.

Anisogamy and isogamy

Most familiar species have two markedly different gamete sizes: anisogamy. The small, motile male gamete is optimised to find the egg; the large, immobile female gamete is stocked with nutrients for the early embryo. Biologists define "male" and "female" precisely by which gamete size an individual produces. Some algae and fungi instead show isogamy, where the fusing gametes are indistinguishable in size and shape, sometimes divided into "+" and "−" mating types. The evolution of anisogamy from an isogamous ancestor is a classic problem in evolutionary biology, usually explained by disruptive selection between numerous small gametes and few well-provisioned ones.

Production

In animals gametes form in the gonads through gametogenesis: spermatogenesis in the testes produces sperm continuously from puberty in most male mammals, while oogenesis in the ovaries produces a finite stock of eggs, typically released one (or a few) per reproductive cycle. In plants, gametes are produced by the haploid gametophyte generation — in flowering plants reduced to the pollen grain and the embryo sac.

In medicine

Donor gametes and IVF allow conception where a person's own gametes are absent or non-viable, raising legal and ethical questions about anonymity and parentage that are regulated differently in different countries.
