Al-Kamil

Al-Malik al-Kāmil
Reign: 1218 – 1238 (Sultan of Egypt)
Predecessor: al-Adil I
Successor: al-Adil II
Born: c. 1177, Cairo
Died: 6 March 1238, Damascus
Dynasty: Ayyubid

Al-Kamil Muhammad (Arabic: الكامل محمّد, full name: al-Malik al-Kāmil Naṣr al-Dīn Muḥammad; c. 1177 – 6 March 1238) was a Muslim ruler and the fourth Ayyubid sultan of Egypt. During his tenure as sultan, the Ayyubids defeated the Fifth Crusade and, controversially, negotiated the return of Jerusalem to Crusader control in 1229.

Reign

Al-Kamil was the son of al-Adil I, Saladin's brother. Before succeeding his father in 1218 he governed Egypt as viceroy and defended Damietta against the Fifth Crusade; although Damietta fell in 1219, flooding of the Nile and disease forced the Crusaders to surrender in 1221. In 1229, after a decade of negotiation with the Holy Roman Emperor Frederick II, al-Kamil concluded the Treaty of Jaffa, ceding Jerusalem, Bethlehem and Nazareth to the Christians while retaining Muslim control of the Temple Mount sanctuaries — a settlement criticised by many contemporaries on both sides but one that kept the peace for a decade.

Meeting with St Francis

In 1219, during the siege of Damietta, al-Kamil famously received St Francis of Assisi, who had crossed the battle lines to preach to him. The meeting, remembered in Christian tradition as a model of interfaith encounter, ended with the sultan listening politely and sending Francis back safely.
