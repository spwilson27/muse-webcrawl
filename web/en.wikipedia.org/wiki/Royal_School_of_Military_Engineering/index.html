Royal School of Military Engineering
Location: Brompton Barracks, Chatham, Kent, England
Established: 1812
Role: Training centre for the Corps of Royal Engineers
Motto: Ubique (Everywhere)

The Royal School of Military Engineering (RSME) is the British Army's principal training establishment for the Corps of Royal Engineers. Based at Brompton Barracks in Chatham, Kent, it delivers military engineering, construction, technical and specialist training to soldiers and officers of the Royal Engineers and other services.

History

The school traces its origins to the Royal Engineer Establishment founded at Chatham in 1812 under Lieutenant Colonel Charles Pasley, who argued for systematic training in siegecraft, bridging and field works. It became a centre of innovation in military engineering, including early experiments in photography, telegraphy, diving and ballooning. The institution was granted the title Royal School of Military Engineering in 1962, on its 150th anniversary.

Organisation

The RSME Group includes combat engineer schools, a construction engineering school, and specialist units such as the Defence Explosive Ordnance Disposal, Munitions and Search Training Regiment and the Defence Animal Centre at Melton Mowbray, which trains military working dogs and horses. Training is delivered in partnership with civilian contractors and awarding bodies, and many courses lead to recognised civilian qualifications in trades such as carpentry, plumbing, electrical engineering and plant operation.

The school also supports the Royal Engineers Museum at Gillingham, which holds the corps' archives and collections.
