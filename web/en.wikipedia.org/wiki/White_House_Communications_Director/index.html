The White House Communications Director, officially the Assistant to the President for Communications, is a senior member of the president of the United States' staff responsible for developing and promoting the president's agenda and leading the administration's overall media campaign. The communications director heads the White House Office of Communications and works closely with — but is distinct from — the White House Press Secretary, who handles day-to-day interaction with the press corps and briefings.

Role

The communications director is charged with long-range message strategy: planning speeches and presidential travel around policy themes, coordinating communications across cabinet departments and agencies, arranging interviews and surrogate appearances, and overseeing the White House speechwriting office and research team. While the press secretary serves as the public face at the podium, the communications director is typically a behind-the-scenes strategist, among the president's closest communications advisers.

History

The White House Office of Communications was established by President Richard Nixon in 1969, with journalist and Nixon aide Herb Klein as its first director. The office expanded under successive administrations as television, cable news, and later digital and social media transformed presidential communication. Notable holders have included David Gergen, who served under several presidents; George Stephanopoulos under Bill Clinton; Karen Hughes and Dan Bartlett under George W. Bush; and, in later administrations, figures such as Jen Psaki (who subsequently became press secretary) and Kate Bedingfield under Joe Biden. Some presidents have split the role or merged it with that of a senior counsellor; in the Trump administration the post had several occupants, including Anthony Scaramucci, whose tenure lasted only days.

Relationship to the press secretary

The two roles have been combined or ranked in different ways over time. In some administrations the communications director outranks the press secretary and the latter reports through the former; in others both report directly to the chief of staff. The post does not require Senate confirmation, as it is a White House staff position rather than an executive branch agency office.
