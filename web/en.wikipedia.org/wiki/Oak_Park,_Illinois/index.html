Oak Park is a village in Cook County, Illinois, United States, directly west of and adjacent to the city of Chicago. It is the 29th-most populous municipality in Illinois, with a population of 54,583 at the 2020 census. Oak Park is famous worldwide for its association with the architect Frank Lloyd Wright and the writer Ernest Hemingway.

Architecture

Wright lived and worked in Oak Park from 1889 to 1909, building his home and studio there and designing dozens of houses in the surrounding streets — the largest concentration of his work anywhere in the world, now protected as the Frank Lloyd Wright–Prairie School of Architecture Historic District. His Unity Temple (1908), a pioneering cast-concrete Unitarian church, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Many other Prairie School architects and artists also worked in the village.

History and community

Settled in the 1830s and incorporated in 1902, Oak Park developed as a commuter suburb served by rail and elevated lines to downtown Chicago. In the 1960s and 1970s the village became nationally known for deliberately managed integration policies aimed at maintaining racial diversity, an approach frequently studied by sociologists. Hemingway was born in Oak Park in 1899; his birthplace home and a museum preserve his early years, though his oft-quoted description of the village as a place of "broad lawns and narrow minds" is probably apocryphal.
