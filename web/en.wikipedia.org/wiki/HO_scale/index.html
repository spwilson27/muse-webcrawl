HO or H0 scale is the most popular scale for model railways in the world. The name derives from "half O", HO being half the size of O scale. In HO, 3.5 millimetres represent 1 foot, giving a ratio of 1:87. Standard-gauge prototype track (1,435 mm) is modelled with rails spaced 16.5 mm apart.

History

HO scale emerged in the 1930s, first in the United Kingdom and Germany, as manufacturers sought a scale smaller — and therefore cheaper and less space-hungry — than O. After the Second World War it overtook O in popularity, particularly in North America, and by the latter half of the 20th century dominated the hobby thanks to mass-produced ready-to-run models from makers such as Athearn, Märklin, Fleischmann and Hornby. Its size is considered a good compromise: small enough for a substantial layout in a spare room, large enough for fine detail and reliable operation.

Related scales

The same track gauge is used by British OO scale (1:76), which uses oversize bodies on HO track to accommodate early British mechanisms and remains the dominant scale in the UK. Narrow-gauge prototypes are modelled in HO using narrower track, for example HOn30 (or H0e), which runs on 9 mm (N scale) track.
