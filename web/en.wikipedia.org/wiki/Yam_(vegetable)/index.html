Yam (vegetable)

Yam
Genus: Dioscorea
Family: Dioscoreaceae
Main producing region: West Africa
[Image: Large rough-skinned yam tubers stacked at a West African market]

Yam is the common name of some plant species in the genus Dioscorea that form edible tubers. Yams are perennial herbaceous vines cultivated for their starchy tubers in Africa, Asia, Latin America, the Caribbean and Oceania. West Africa accounts for the great majority of world production, and the yam is a culturally central staple in Nigeria and Ghana, celebrated at New Yam Festivals marking the harvest.

Species and use

Important species include the white yam (D. rotundata), yellow yam (D. cayennensis), water yam (D. alata, also called ube-type purple yam in some forms), and the Chinese yam (D. polystachya). Tubers are boiled, roasted, fried or pounded into a smooth dough (pounded yam, or fufu) eaten with soups and stews. Some wild yams are bitter and toxic unless processed, containing alkaloids such as dioscorine; certain species are also sources of diosgenin, used industrially in steroid synthesis.

Confusion with sweet potato

In the United States and Canada, "yam" is commonly applied to orange-fleshed sweet potatoes, a usage dating from marketing in the mid-20th century; US regulators require such products also to be labelled "sweet potato". True yams differ in their rough, bark-like skin, drier starchier flesh and much larger potential size. Both are distinct from the tamarind-pod legumes and other tropical staples with which they share market stalls.
