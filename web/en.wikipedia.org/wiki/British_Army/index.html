The British Army is the principal land warfare force of the United Kingdom, a part of the British Armed Forces along with the Royal Navy and the Royal Air Force. Tracing its origins to the standing armies of England and Scotland united in 1707, it is administered by the Ministry of Defence and commanded by the Chief of the General Staff. The army is a volunteer force; conscription (National Service) ended in 1960.

History

A permanent English standing army dates from the Restoration era, and regiments such as the Coldstream Guards trace their lineage to the Commonwealth period. The army of the 18th and 19th centuries fought in the American Revolutionary War, the Napoleonic Wars, and the Crimean War, and garrisoned a worldwide empire, notably in India alongside the forces of the East India Company and later the British Indian Army. Cardwell and Childers reforms in the late 19th century reorganized regiments into county-linked structures. In the First World War the army expanded into a mass force of millions; the Battle of the Somme (1916) remains its bloodiest day. In the Second World War it fought in France, North Africa, Italy, Burma, and from D-Day in North-West Europe. Post-1945 it fought in Korea, Malaya, Cyprus, the Falklands (1982), the Gulf War (1991), the Balkans, Sierra Leone, Iraq, and Afghanistan, and maintained a large presence in West Germany (British Army of the Rhine) and Northern Ireland during the Troubles.

Structure

The army is organized into combat arms (infantry, Royal Armoured Corps, Army Air Corps, Royal Artillery, Royal Engineers) and combat support and service arms (Royal Signals, Intelligence Corps, Royal Logistic Corps, Royal Army Medical Service, Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers, Adjutant General's Corps, and others). The basic unit is the regiment or corps; deployable formations are brigades and divisions. Special forces include the Special Air Service (SAS) and Special Boat Service. The Army Reserve provides part-time volunteer reserves.

Equipment

Principal equipment includes the Challenger 2 main battle tank (being upgraded to Challenger 3), Warrior and Boxer armoured vehicles, the Ajax reconnaissance family, Apache AH-64E attack helicopters, AS90 and M270 artillery, and the SA80 (L85) individual weapon.

Bases and deployments

Major garrisons include Aldershot, Catterick, Tidworth, Colchester, and Edinburgh. Overseas, the army maintains training areas in Canada (BATUS) and Kenya (BATUK), a jungle warfare school in Brunei, garrisons in Cyprus, Gibraltar, and the Falkland Islands, and a permanent presence in Germany reduced after 2020.
