Hegra

Hegra (Ancient Greek: Ἕγρα), also known as Mada'in Salih (Arabic: مَدَائِن صَالِح, "Cities of Salih"), is an archaeological site located in the area of Al-Ula within Medina Province in the Hejaz region, Saudi Arabia. A majority of the remains date from the Nabataean Kingdom (1st century AD). The site constitutes the kingdom's southernmost and second largest city after Petra, its capital, in what is now Jordan.

In 2008, UNESCO proclaimed Mada'in Salih as a site of patrimony, becoming Saudi Arabia's first World Heritage Site. It was chosen for its well-preserved remains from late antiquity, especially the 100 monumental rock-cut tombs with their elaborately ornamented façades.

History

The site was settled as early as the Iron Age and was an important station on the incense trade route. The Nabataeans developed Hegra into a prosperous city during the 1st century BC and 1st century AD, constructing an extensive hydraulic system of wells and rainwater channels. After the Roman annexation of the Nabataean kingdom in AD 106, the city declined, though it remained inhabited and was later associated in Islamic tradition with the story of the prophet Salih and the people of Thamud.

Archaeology

The necropolis contains 111 monumental tombs, 94 of them decorated with carved façades featuring eagles, griffins, masks and inscriptions in Nabataean Aramaic that record legal details of ownership and burial rights. The residential area, surrounded by a mud-brick rampart, includes houses, temples and a diwan — a rock-cut triclinium used for ritual banquets. Excavations by French and Saudi teams since 2001 have revealed much of the ancient town.
