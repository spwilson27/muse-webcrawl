The Bureau of Indian Education (BIE) is a division of the United States Department of the Interior that provides education services to Native American children. The BIE funds about 180 elementary and secondary schools, located on or near 64 reservations in 23 states, serving roughly 46,000 students. About two-thirds of the schools are tribally controlled under grants or contracts with tribes, while the remainder are operated directly by the bureau. The BIE also supports two postsecondary institutions — Haskell Indian Nations University in Kansas and Southwestern Indian Polytechnic Institute in New Mexico — and provides funding to tribally controlled colleges.

History

Federal involvement in Native education grew out of treaties in which tribes ceded land in exchange for services including schooling. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the Bureau of Indian Affairs ran a system of off-reservation boarding schools, such as Carlisle Indian School, whose assimilationist policies — separating children from their families and suppressing Native languages — caused lasting harm now widely acknowledged. Twentieth-century reforms, from the Meriam Report of 1928 to the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act of 1975, shifted control toward tribes and day schooling. The education functions were formally separated from the Bureau of Indian Affairs in 2006, creating the BIE as its own bureau.

Challenges and reform

BIE schools have repeatedly been criticised for poor facilities, teacher shortages, and academic outcomes below national averages, and the bureau has been the subject of government accountability reports and reform initiatives, including a 2014 restructuring intended to turn the BIE from a school operator into a support organisation for tribal education departments. In 2021–2022 the Department of the Interior's Federal Indian Boarding School Initiative investigated the legacy of the boarding school system the bureau's predecessors administered.
