German battleship Scharnhorst

Launched3 October 1936
Commissioned7 January 1939
Displacementc. 38,700 tonnes (full load)
Armament9 × 28 cm guns; 12 × 15 cm guns
FateSunk 26 December 1943, Battle of the North Cape

Scharnhorst was a German battleship (often classed as a battlecruiser) of the Kriegsmarine, lead ship of her class together with Gneisenau. Armed with nine 28 cm (11 in) guns and capable of 31 knots, she was designed for commerce raiding and operations against enemy cruisers.

Service history

Commissioned in January 1939, Scharnhorst took part in operations off Norway in 1940, sinking the British aircraft carrier HMS Glorious and her escorting destroyers on 8 June 1940 — one of the few occasions battleships sank a carrier by gunfire. With Gneisenau she raided Atlantic convoys in Operation Berlin (early 1941), then survived the famous "Channel Dash" home to Germany in February 1942.

Sinking

Based in northern Norway to threaten Arctic convoys, Scharnhorst sortied on 25 December 1943 against convoy JW 55B. In the Battle of the North Cape the next day she was intercepted by the British battleship HMS Duke of York, cruisers and destroyers, hit by numerous shells and torpedoes, and sank with the loss of 1,932 of her 1,968 crew. Her wreck was located in 2000 off the North Cape.
