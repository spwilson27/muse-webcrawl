The Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM; English: International Motorcycling Federation) is the global governing and sanctioning body for motorcycle racing and the international advocate for motorcycling. Founded in Paris in 1904 as the Fédération Internationale des Clubs Motocyclistes (FICM), it is headquartered in Mies, near Geneva, Switzerland, and brings together around 120 national motorcycle federations across six continental unions.

History

The FICM was founded on 21 December 1904 at the Restaurant Ledoyen in Paris by representatives of ten nations. Dissolved during the First World War, it was re-founded in 1919. The organisation moved to Switzerland and in 1949 adopted its present name, Fédération Internationale Motocycliste, changed in 1998 to Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme. In 1959 the headquarters moved from Geneva to Mies. The FIM was the first international sports federation to adopt an environmental code (1994) and works in road safety, touring and leisure motorcycling as well as sport.

Sanctioned championships

The FIM sanctions world championships across the main motorcycle disciplines, including:

  • Circuit racing — the Grand Prix motorcycle racing world championship (MotoGP, Moto2, Moto3), the Superbike World Championship and endurance racing.
  • Motocross — the Motocross World Championship (MXGP), Motocross of Nations and supercross.
  • Off-road — the Enduro World Championship, hard enduro, cross-country rallies (including the Dakar Rally for motorcycles) and the International Six Days Enduro.
  • Trial — the Trial World Championship, indoors and outdoors.
  • Track racing — the Speedway Grand Prix and ice racing.
  • Other — supermoto, sidecar racing, e-bike and electric (MotoE / FIM E-Xplorer) competitions.

Role beyond racing

Beyond sport, the FIM represents motorcycling before bodies such as the United Nations and the European Union, promotes rider training and road safety, homologates protective equipment and circuits, and maintains anti-doping and technical regulations for competition machines.
