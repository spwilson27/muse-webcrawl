Points of the compass

A 32-point compass rose

The points of the compass are the named directions on a compass rose that divide the horizon into a set of equally spaced bearings. Traditionally, mariners used a system of 32 points, each separated by 11.25 degrees, derived by repeatedly bisecting the angles between the principal winds.

The 32 points

The four cardinal points are north, east, south, and west. Bisecting these gives the four intercardinal (ordinal) points: northeast, southeast, southwest, and northwest, forming the eight principal winds. Bisecting again yields eight half-winds, such as north-northeast and east-southeast, and a final bisection gives sixteen quarter-winds named with the prefix "by", as in "north by east". Each point corresponds to a bearing measured clockwise from north; north is 0°, east 90°, south 180°, and west 270°.

History and use

The classical Mediterranean tradition named points after winds such as Tramontana, Greco, Levante, and Scirocco. The 32-point system was standard in European navigation for centuries, and the practice of "boxing the compass" — reciting all 32 points in order — was a basic skill for sailors. Modern navigation generally uses degrees, but compass points remain in common use for general directions and in weather reporting, where 8- or 16-point descriptions are typical.
