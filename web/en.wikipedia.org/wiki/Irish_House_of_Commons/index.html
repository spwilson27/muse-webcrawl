Irish House of Commons

The Irish House of Commons was the lower house of the Parliament of Ireland, which existed from 1297 until 1800. It met in Dublin, most famously in the purpose-built Parliament House on College Green, and had 300 members elected from counties, boroughs, and Dublin University.

History

For most of its existence the Commons was subordinate to the English (later British) government through Poynings' Law (1494), which allowed the English Privy Council to vet Irish legislation. In 1782, under Henry Grattan's leadership, the parliament won "legislative independence", inaugurating the era remembered as Grattan's Parliament. Catholics, though the majority of the population, could neither sit nor (until 1793) vote.

Abolition

After the rebellion of 1798, the British government of William Pitt the Younger promoted union. The Acts of Union 1800 abolished the Irish Parliament, its passage secured amid allegations of bribery with peerages and compensation for pocket boroughs; 100 Irish MPs thereafter sat at Westminster. The Commons chamber was subsequently converted for the Bank of Ireland.
