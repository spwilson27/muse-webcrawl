Moroni (Book of Mormon prophet)

According to the Book of Mormon, Moroni (pronounced /məˈroʊnaɪ/) was the last Nephite prophet, historian, and military commander who lived in the Americas in the late fourth and early fifth centuries AD. He was the son of Mormon, the prophet after whom the Book of Mormon is named, and succeeded him as keeper of the golden plates — the record from which Joseph Smith is said to have translated the Book of Mormon. Moroni is a central figure in the theology of the Latter Day Saint movement: as a resurrected being (angel), he is believed to have visited Joseph Smith repeatedly beginning in 1823 and guided him to the plates.

Narrative in the Book of Mormon

Moroni participated in the final wars between the Nephites and Lamanites and survived the destruction of the Nephite nation at the Battle of Cumorah (about AD 385), in which his father was killed. For decades afterwards he wandered alone, avoiding the Lamanites who sought his life, completing his father's abridgement of the Nephite records, translating and adding the record of the Jaredites (the Book of Ether), and writing the final portion of the record, the Book of Moroni. That book includes instructions on ordinances, the sacrament prayers, and the well-known invitation in its final chapter (Moroni 10:3–5) to ask God whether the record is true. About AD 421 Moroni sealed up the plates and hid them in the Hill Cumorah.

Angel Moroni

In Latter Day Saint belief, the resurrected Moroni appeared to Joseph Smith on the night of 21–22 September 1823 and on subsequent occasions, informing him of the plates' location and preparing him to receive them in 1827. A statue of the Angel Moroni, trumpet in hand — symbolizing the preaching of the restored gospel — has traditionally crowned the tallest spire of many temples of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, though newer temple designs increasingly omit the figure. Moroni should not be confused with Captain Moroni, an earlier Nephite military leader in the Book of Alma.
