Brane cosmology

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Brane cosmology refers to several theories in particle physics and cosmology motivated by superstring theory, brane theory, and M-theory. The central idea is that the visible, three-dimensional universe is restricted to a brane embedded in a higher-dimensional space, the "bulk". The extra dimensions may be small and compactified, as in the original Kaluza–Klein picture, or large — in some models, infinite — with gravity alone propagating through them, while the Standard Model fields are confined to the brane.

Models

Arkani-Hamed–Dimopoulos–Dvali (ADD)

The ADD model (1998) proposed large extra dimensions accessible only to gravity, explaining the weakness of gravity relative to the other forces (the hierarchy problem): gravity appears weak because it "leaks" into the bulk. The extra dimensions are compactified at sub-millimetre scales, tested by short-range gravity experiments.

Randall–Sundrum models

The Randall–Sundrum models (1999) employ a warped extra dimension bounded by two branes (RS1) or a single brane in an infinite warped bulk (RS2). Warping of the geometry can generate the Planck–electroweak hierarchy, and the cosmology of a moving brane in an anti-de Sitter bulk modifies the Friedmann equations at high energies, giving corrections relevant to the very early universe.

Ekpyrotic and cyclic models

The ekpyrotic universe, proposed by Paul Steinhardt, Neil Turok and collaborators, envisions the Big Bang as the collision of two branes in the bulk, which heats the universe and initiates the hot expanding phase; its extension, the cyclic model, repeats such collisions indefinitely. These scenarios were offered as alternatives to cosmic inflation for explaining the flatness and homogeneity of the universe.

Observational status

Brane-world scenarios predict possible deviations from Newtonian gravity at short distances, modified expansion history in the early universe, and (in some models) characteristic signatures in the cosmic microwave background and gravitational waves. No positive evidence for extra dimensions has been found to date; experiments and collider searches continue to constrain the parameter space.
