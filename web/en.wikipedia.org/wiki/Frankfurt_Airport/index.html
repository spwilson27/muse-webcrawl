Frankfurt Airport (German: Flughafen Frankfurt Main) is Germany's busiest airport and one of Europe's major aviation hubs, located about 12 km southwest of central Frankfurt in the Frankfurt Rhine-Main region. It is the main hub of Lufthansa and the Lufthansa Group, and a base for Condor, with four runways and two main passenger terminals; a third terminal has been under construction.

History

The airport opened in 1936 in the Frankfurt City Forest as the Flughafen Rhein-Main, and was also a major base for Zeppelin airships, including the Graf Zeppelin and the Hindenburg. After the Second World War it served as a principal U.S. Air Force base (Rhein-Main Air Base, closed in 2005) and a key departure point for the Berlin Airlift. The modern Terminal 1 opened in 1972 and Terminal 2 in 1994; the long-debated Northwest Runway opened in 2011, accompanied by continuing noise protests and a night-flight ban settlement.

Frankfurt Airport is a major employer and cargo hub (among Europe's busiest by freight). It is connected to the long-distance rail network via the Frankfurt Airport long-distance station on the Cologne–Frankfurt high-speed line, and to the city by S-Bahn and motorways (the Frankfurter Kreuz interchange nearby is one of Germany's busiest).
