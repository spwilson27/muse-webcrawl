Usambara Mountains

The Usambara Mountains of northeastern Tanzania are part of the ancient Eastern Arc chain of mountains. The Usambaras consist of two ranges, the western and eastern, separated by the Lwengera Valley. The mountains are known for their exceptional biodiversity and are considered one of the most biologically important regions in Africa.

Geology and geography

The ranges are formed of Precambrian metamorphic rocks uplifted in long fault blocks. The West Usambaras reach about 2,300 metres, while the lower East Usambaras lie closer to the Indian Ocean coast and receive heavier rainfall. The town of Lushoto is the main centre of the West Usambaras.

Biodiversity

The forests of the Usambaras are a centre of endemism, home to species found nowhere else, including the Usambara eagle-owl, the Usambara weaver, and many endemic plants and invertebrates. The African violet (Saintpaulia) originates from these mountains. The Amani Nature Reserve in the East Usambaras protects a notable tract of submontane forest.

History and people

The Shambaa (Sambaa) people are the traditional inhabitants of the West Usambaras, ruled historically by the Kilindi dynasty. During the German colonial period, the mountains became a favoured hill station and agricultural research centre; the biological station at Amani dates from 1902.
