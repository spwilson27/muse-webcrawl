The ankus (Sanskrit: अङ्कुश, aṅkuśa), also called an elephant goad or bullhook, is a tool used by mahouts in the handling and training of elephants. It consists of a hook, usually of bronze or steel, mounted on a handle about 60–90 cm long and ending in a tapered point.

Use

The mahout applies the hook to sensitive areas of the elephant's skin — behind the ear, under the chin, or around the feet — to guide the animal or reinforce commands. Ankuses have been used in Asia for many centuries, in war, ceremony, logging and transport, and are depicted in ancient Indian art and texts.

Symbolism

In Hindu iconography the ankusha is an attribute of the elephant-headed god Ganesha, who often holds one in an upper hand; it also appears among the eight auspicious symbols (ashtamangala) and as an emblem of royal authority over the elephant, itself a symbol of kingship.

Welfare controversy

Because the point can injure or frighten elephants, the bullhook is controversial. Many modern zoos have moved to "protected contact" management without goads, and some jurisdictions — including California and several cities in the United States — have banned the use of bullhooks on elephants, a factor in the Ringling Bros. circus retiring its elephant acts in 2016.
