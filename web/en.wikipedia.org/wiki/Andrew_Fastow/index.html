Andrew Fastow, chief financial officer of Enron from 1998 to 2001.

Andrew Stuart Fastow (born 22 December 1961) is an American businessman who served as chief financial officer of the energy company Enron from 1998 until shortly before its collapse in 2001. He was a central figure in the Enron scandal, having designed the web of off-balance-sheet special-purpose entities — including partnerships named LJM and the Raptors — that concealed Enron's debt and inflated its earnings.

Enron and the fraud

Fastow joined Enron in 1990 and rose quickly, becoming CFO in 1998. He structured partnerships that he himself managed while serving as Enron's finance chief, an arrangement approved by the board despite the obvious conflict of interest; he reportedly earned more than $30 million from them. When Enron's accounting unravelled in the autumn of 2001, Fastow was forced out on 24 October 2001, and the company filed for bankruptcy that December.

Conviction

Indicted on numerous counts of fraud, money laundering, and conspiracy, Fastow pleaded guilty in January 2004 to two counts and agreed to cooperate with prosecutors, testifying against Kenneth Lay and Jeffrey Skilling. He was sentenced to six years in prison and served his term at a federal facility in Louisiana, being released in December 2011. Since his release he has spoken publicly about fraud and business ethics.
