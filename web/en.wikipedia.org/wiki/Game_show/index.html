Game show

A game show is a genre of broadcast viewing entertainment where contestants compete for rewards such as cash, trips, or consumer goods. Game shows are usually presented by a host and may involve answering questions, solving puzzles, performing physical stunts, or games of chance. The genre began on radio in the 1930s and became a staple of television from its earliest days.

History

Early radio quiz programs such as Professor Quiz (1936) and Truth or Consequences established the format. Television game shows flourished in the 1950s, though the decade ended with the quiz show scandals, in which it was revealed that producers had secretly fed answers to contestants on shows like Twenty-One. The scandal led to US federal legislation making it illegal to rig quiz shows. Formats later diversified into daytime staples such as The Price Is Right (premiered in its current form in 1972), Jeopardy! (1964; revived 1984), and Wheel of Fortune (1975).

Formats

Quiz shows test knowledge (Jeopardy!, Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?); panel and word games test wit and language (Password, Countdown); pricing and chance games dominate daytime television (The Price Is Right, Deal or No Deal); dating, talent, and reality-competition formats blur the boundary with reality television. Many formats are licensed internationally and adapted market by market.

Prime-time revival

The 1999 debut of Who Wants to Be a Millionaire? in the United States sparked a prime-time game show revival, with large cash prizes and dramatic production values. Streaming and syndication have kept classic formats continuously in production worldwide.
