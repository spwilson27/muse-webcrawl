Tōdai-ji


Location: Nara, Japan
Founded: 8th century (dedicated 752)
Known for: Great Buddha (Daibutsu), Daibutsuden hall
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site

Tōdai-ji ("Great Eastern Temple") is a Buddhist temple complex in the city of Nara, Japan. Its Great Buddha Hall (Daibutsuden) houses one of the world's largest bronze statues of the Buddha Vairocana, known in Japanese as the Daibutsu, and was for centuries the largest wooden building in the world.

History

Emperor Shōmu ordered the temple's construction as the head of a national network of provincial temples; the Great Buddha was dedicated in 752. The hall burned twice and the present Daibutsuden, dating to 1709, is about two-thirds the width of the original yet remains immense.

Grounds

Visitors pass through the great south gate (Nandaimon), guarded by towering Niō statues, into grounds where sika deer roam freely. The Shōsōin repository at the complex preserves eighth-century treasures from the Silk Road era.
