Bounce music

Bounce music is a regional style of hip hop music that originated in New Orleans, Louisiana, in the late 1980s and early 1990s. It is characterised by call-and-response chanting, sexually explicit lyrics, heavy use of samples from a small canon of local records, and dance styles centred on twerking and other vigorous hip movements.

Sound and structure

The foundational bounce beat derives from the "Triggaman" sample, taken from the Showboys' 1986 song "Drag Rap", combined with elements of "Brown Beat" by Cameron Paul and Derek B. Bounce tracks typically layer chants naming New Orleans neighbourhoods and housing projects over the Triggaman loop, with shout-outs, repeated hooks and instructions to dancers rather than narrative verses. Early releases such as Tucker's "Where Dey At" (1991) and DJ Jimi's It's Jimi (1992) established the template.

Development and influence

Bounce remained largely local until New Orleans artists including Juvenile, Mystikal and Master P carried elements of it into mainstream hip hop in the late 1990s. A "sissy bounce" scene of openly queer performers, notably Big Freedia and Katey Red, became central to the culture and brought bounce national attention after Hurricane Katrina, when the music became a vehicle for the city's recovery culture. Bounce's rhythms and twerking dances have influenced mainstream pop, including recordings by Beyoncé, Drake and Lizzo.
