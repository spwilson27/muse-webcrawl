William Osler

Sir William Osler, 1st Baronet (July 12, 1849 – December 29, 1919) was a Canadian physician and one of the "Big Four" founding professors of Johns Hopkins Hospital. He created the first medical residency programme and was a pioneer of bedside clinical teaching, taking students out of the lecture hall to learn at the patient's side. He is often called the "Father of Modern Medicine".

Career

Osler studied medicine at McGill University in Montreal, graduating in 1872, and after postgraduate study in Europe became professor of medicine at McGill at the age of 24. In 1884 he moved to the University of Pennsylvania, and in 1889 he became the first physician-in-chief of the new Johns Hopkins Hospital in Baltimore. His textbook The Principles and Practice of Medicine (1892) was the standard text of internal medicine for decades and inspired the creation of the Rockefeller Institute.

At Johns Hopkins Osler established the residency system, in which young physicians lived in the hospital and trained for years under supervision, and the clerkship that put medical students in direct contact with patients. In 1905 he became Regius Professor of Medicine at Oxford, where he remained until his death. He was made a baronet in 1911.

Legacy

Osler's name survives in Osler's nodes (painful finger lesions of infective endocarditis), hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia (Osler–Weber–Rendu syndrome) and Osler's manoeuvre. His essays, such as Aequanimitas, remain classics of medical humanism, and his vast medical-history library, the Bibliotheca Osleriana, went to McGill.
