Baldwin IV of Jerusalem

Baldwin IV, the "Leper King" of Jerusalem

Baldwin IV (1161 – 16 March 1185), known posthumously as the Leper King, was the king of Jerusalem from 1174 until his death in 1185. Despite suffering from leprosy, which was detected in his childhood by his tutor William of Tyre, he became an active and respected military commander in the Crusader struggle against Saladin.

Reign

Baldwin succeeded his father Amalric I at the age of thirteen. During his minority, regents — first Miles of Plancy, then Raymond III of Tripoli — governed the kingdom. Once of age, Baldwin personally led campaigns, most famously winning the Battle of Montgisard in 1177, where his heavily outnumbered army routed Saladin's forces. As his disease progressed, he became blind and unable to walk or use his hands, yet continued to rule, being carried into battle on a litter. He arranged the succession carefully: his sister Sibylla's son, Baldwin V, was crowned co-king in 1183, and Baldwin IV attempted to annul Sibylla's marriage to Guy of Lusignan, whose recklessness he distrusted. He died in the spring of 1185, aged about twenty-four, and was succeeded by Baldwin V. His reign is often considered the last period of effective resistance by the Kingdom of Jerusalem before its collapse at Hattin in 1187.
