Joan Miró

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A Miró constellation: black calligraphic signs, red and blue biomorphic shapes and stars scattered on a deep ground]

Joan Miró i Ferrà (20 April 1893 – 25 December 1983) was a Catalan painter, sculptor and ceramist. A museum dedicated to his work, the Fundació Joan Miró, was established in his native city of Barcelona in 1975, and another, the Fundació Pilar i Joan Miró, was established in his adoptive city of Palma in 1981. Earning international acclaim, his work has been interpreted as Surrealism, though he kept a lifelong independence from any movement.

From the meticulous magic-realist The Farm (1921–22) Miró developed a private language of signs — birds, women, stars, ladders of escape — in paintings such as The Harlequin's Carnival (1924–25) and the wartime Constellations (1940–41). His later public art — murals, tapestries, sculptures and the Barcelona foundation — spread his joyful cosmology worldwide.
