Oriental studies

Oriental studies is the academic field that historically studied the cultures, languages, history and societies of Asia and North Africa — the regions Europeans called "the Orient". Practitioners were known as Orientalists. The field encompassed disciplines such as Egyptology, Assyriology, Sinology, Indology, Iranian studies, Turkology and Islamic studies, and was institutionalised in European and American universities from the late 18th century onwards.

19th-century Orientalist scholarship combined philology, archaeology and history.

History

Western study of Eastern languages has roots in medieval and early modern biblical scholarship, missionary activity and trade. The field expanded dramatically with colonial expansion: the Asiatic Society of Bengal, founded by William Jones in 1784, promoted the study of Sanskrit, leading Jones to propose the Indo-European language family. In 1795 the École spéciale des langues orientales opened in Paris; chairs of Arabic, Hebrew and Sanskrit were founded across Europe, and the decipherment of Egyptian hieroglyphs (1822) and cuneiform (mid-19th century) opened entire civilisations to study.

Orientalism and criticism

In his influential 1978 book Orientalism, Edward Said argued that Western scholarship about the Middle East was inseparable from imperial power: it constructed the Orient as exotic, irrational and inferior, thereby justifying colonial rule. The book reshaped the field and contributed to postcolonial studies. Critics of Said, such as Bernard Lewis, countered that he caricatured a diverse scholarly tradition and ignored genuine erudition and anti-imperial Orientalists. The debate continues over how far scholarship can be separated from the politics of its era.

Modern reorganisation

Since the later 20th century, most universities have replaced "Oriental studies" with area-studies designations — Asian studies, Middle Eastern studies, Near Eastern studies — organised by region and drawing on modern social sciences as well as philology. Oxford retains a Faculty of Oriental Studies (renamed the Faculty of Asian and Middle Eastern Studies in 2022), and the term persists in some European institutions and in the names of learned societies and institutes.
