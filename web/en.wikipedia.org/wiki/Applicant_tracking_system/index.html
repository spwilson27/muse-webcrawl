Applicant tracking system

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An applicant tracking system (ATS) is a software application that enables the electronic handling of recruitment and hiring needs. An ATS allows an organisation to collect, store, and organise job applications, track candidates through each stage of the hiring process, and automate tasks such as posting job advertisements, screening résumés, scheduling interviews, and communicating with applicants. ATS platforms are used by the great majority of large employers, and are increasingly common among small and medium-sized businesses, often delivered as cloud-based software as a service.

Functions

Core functions of an applicant tracking system include:

  • Posting openings to job boards and company career pages from a single interface
  • Collecting applications and parsing résumés into structured candidate profiles
  • Searching, filtering, and ranking candidates, including by keyword matching and screening questions ("knockout questions")
  • Tracking each candidate's progress through pipeline stages (application, screening, interview, offer)
  • Scheduling interviews, collecting interviewer feedback and scorecards
  • Sending automated and personalised communications to candidates
  • Generating reports on hiring metrics and compliance records, such as those needed for equal-opportunity reporting

Many systems integrate with human resource information systems (HRIS), background-check providers, assessment tools, and video interviewing platforms, and increasingly incorporate artificial-intelligence features such as résumé matching, chatbots for candidate questions, and automated sourcing from talent pools.

History

Applicant tracking emerged in the late 1980s and 1990s as employers began receiving résumés electronically; early systems were desktop databases used by recruiters to store and search candidate records. With the growth of online job boards in the late 1990s and 2000s, ATS products became central infrastructure for corporate recruiting departments, and major vendors such as Taleo (acquired by Oracle), iCIMS, Greenhouse, Lever, and Workday Recruiting came to dominate segments of the market. By the 2010s, a large fraction of Fortune 500 companies used an ATS for most hiring.

Criticism and candidate experience

Applicant tracking systems are criticised for screening out qualified candidates whose résumés do not match the keywords or formats the system expects — so-called "résumé black holes" in which applications receive no human review. Job seekers are commonly advised to tailor résumés with the exact terminology of the job posting and to avoid complex formatting that parsers handle poorly; an industry of "ATS optimisation" advice and services has grown around this. Advocates respond that the volume of applications for many postings makes manual review impractical, and that well-configured systems can reduce some forms of bias by applying consistent criteria. Concerns about algorithmic screening have prompted regulation in some jurisdictions, such as requirements for bias audits of automated employment decision tools.
