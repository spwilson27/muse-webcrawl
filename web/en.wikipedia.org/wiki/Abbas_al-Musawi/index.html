Abbas al-Musawi (Arabic: عباس الموسوي; 26 October 1952 – 16 February 1992) was a Lebanese Shia cleric and the second secretary-general of Hezbollah. He was assassinated by Israel in 1992.

Early life and education

Al-Musawi was born in the village of Al-Nabi Shayth in the Beqaa Valley. He studied theology in the Iraqi holy city of Najaf, where he was influenced by Ayatollah Muhammad Baqir al-Sadr and came into contact with the ideas that would later shape Hezbollah. He returned to Lebanon in 1978 and founded religious schools in Baalbek.

Role in Hezbollah

Al-Musawi was one of the founding members of Hezbollah, established in 1982 during the Lebanese Civil War and the Israeli invasion of Lebanon. In May 1991 he was elected secretary-general, succeeding Subhi al-Tufayli. Under his leadership Hezbollah expanded its political and military activities in southern Lebanon.

Assassination

On 16 February 1992, Israeli AH-64 Apache helicopters attacked a motorcade carrying al-Musawi in southern Lebanon, killing him, his wife, his five-year-old son, and several others. He was succeeded by Hassan Nasrallah. The assassination was followed by the 1994 AMIA bombing in Buenos Aires, which Argentina attributed to Hezbollah acting in retaliation.
