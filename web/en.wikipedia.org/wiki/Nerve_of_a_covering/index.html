Nerve of a covering

In topology, the nerve of an open covering is an abstract simplicial complex that encodes how the sets of the covering overlap. Given an open cover of a topological space X by sets {Ui} indexed by a set I, the nerve N(U) has as its simplices the finite subsets J ⊆ I for which the intersection of the sets Uj, j ∈ J, is non-empty.

Construction

Each set in the cover contributes a vertex; each pair of overlapping sets contributes an edge; each triple with a common point contributes a 2-simplex, and so on. If no point of X lies in more than two sets, the nerve is simply an intersection graph.

Nerve theorem

The nerve theorem (or nerve lemma), associated with Jean Leray, states that if the cover is "good" — every non-empty intersection of finitely many sets in the cover is contractible — then the nerve is homotopy equivalent to the covered space X itself. This result lets one compute the homology of a space combinatorially from a covering, and underlies tools such as the Mayer–Vietoris sequence and much of modern applied topology and topological data analysis.

Related constructions

The nerve construction is closely related to the Čech complex used in data analysis, where vertices represent data points and simplices record overlapping balls around them.
