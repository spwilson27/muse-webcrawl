Valproate (VPA), and its derivatives valproic acid, sodium valproate and divalproex sodium, are medications primarily used to treat epilepsy and bipolar disorder and to prevent migraine headaches. Valproate is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Valproate is a broad-spectrum anticonvulsant effective against absence seizures, tonic–clonic seizures and myoclonic seizures, and is a first-line treatment for generalised epilepsy. In psychiatry it is used as a mood stabiliser for the treatment of acute mania and maintenance therapy in bipolar disorder. It is also licensed for the prophylaxis of migraine. Off-label uses have included neuropathic pain and some movement disorders.

Mechanism of action

The mechanism of action is not fully understood. Valproate is believed to increase levels of the inhibitory neurotransmitter gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) in the brain by inhibiting its degradation, and to block voltage-gated sodium channels and T-type calcium channels. It is also a histone deacetylase inhibitor, which underlies both experimental anticancer interest and some of its developmental toxicity.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include nausea, drowsiness, tremor, weight gain and hair loss. Serious adverse effects include liver toxicity (hepatotoxicity, especially in young children), pancreatitis and low platelet counts. Valproate is a known teratogen: exposure during pregnancy is associated with neural tube defects such as spina bifida and with reduced IQ and autism spectrum disorders in exposed children, leading regulators to restrict its use in women and girls of childbearing potential unless other treatments are ineffective or not tolerated.

History

Valproic acid was first synthesised in 1882 by Beverly S. Burton and was used for decades as an organic solvent. Its anticonvulsant properties were discovered serendipitously in 1962 by Pierre Eymard, who used it as a vehicle for other compounds being screened for antiepileptic activity; it was first marketed in France in 1967.
