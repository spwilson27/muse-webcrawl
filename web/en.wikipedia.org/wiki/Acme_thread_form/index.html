Acme thread form

The Acme thread form is a trapezoidal screw thread profile developed in the late 19th century for power transmission. It has a 29° thread angle with flat crests and roots, making it stronger and easier to manufacture than the older square thread form it largely replaced.

Characteristics

Acme threads are standardised in ASME B1.5. The general-purpose Acme thread has clearances on the major diameter so that a split nut can disengage, as on a lathe lead screw. A shorter "stub Acme" variant is used where a shallower thread depth is acceptable. Because of the flank angle, Acme threads generate some radial (bursting) load on the nut, but this same geometry allows easy machining and die threading.

Uses

Typical applications include lead screws and feed screws of machine tools, jacks, vises, and valve stems — anywhere a screw must transmit motion or force rather than merely fasten parts.
