Gediminas Tower

LocationVilnius, Lithuania
Part ofVilnius Castle Complex (Upper Castle)
BuiltEarly 15th century (present brick tower)
Named forGrand Duke Gediminas

Gediminas Tower (Lithuanian: Gedimino pilies bokštas) is the remaining part of the Upper Castle in Vilnius, Lithuania. Standing on Gediminas Hill above the old town, the red-brick octagonal tower is one of the most important symbols of Vilnius and of the Lithuanian state, and the flag of Lithuania flying from its top is a national landmark.

History

The first fortifications on the hill were wooden, associated with Grand Duke Gediminas, who founded Vilnius as his capital in the early 14th century. According to legend, Gediminas dreamed of an iron wolf howling on the hill and was told by the priest Lizdeika that it foretold a great city on the site. The brick Upper Castle was built in the early 15th century under Vytautas the Great. The castle was damaged in wars with Muscovy and in the 17th century, and much of the complex fell into ruin; the surviving western tower was restored in 1930 and again in later decades.

The flag

The Lithuanian tricolour was first raised on the tower in 1919. On 1 January 1989, during the Singing Revolution, the flag was ceremonially raised again, beginning an annual tradition: each 1 January, Flag Day, the worn flag of the previous year is lowered and a new one hoisted. The tower houses an exhibition of the National Museum of Lithuania and offers panoramic views over Vilnius Old Town.
