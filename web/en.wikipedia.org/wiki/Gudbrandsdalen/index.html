Gudbrandsdalen

Gudbrandsdalen is a valley and traditional district in Innlandet county, Norway. It stretches about 230 kilometres from Lake Mjøsa in the south to the Dovrefjell mountains in the northwest, following the Gudbrandsdalslågen river, and is among the longest valleys in Norway.

The valley is divided into northern, central and southern parts and includes the town of Lillehammer and settlements such as Vinstra, Otta, Dombås and Ringebu, with its famous stave church. Historically Gudbrandsdalen lay on the main pilgrim and royal road between Oslo and Trondheim; it features prominently in Norwegian sagas and folklore, in Ibsen's Peer Gynt (set partly in the valley), and in the legend of the Battle of Kringen (1612), where local farmers ambushed Scottish mercenaries.

Today agriculture, forestry and tourism dominate, with several national parks — Dovrefjell, Rondane and Jotunheimen — accessible from the valley.
