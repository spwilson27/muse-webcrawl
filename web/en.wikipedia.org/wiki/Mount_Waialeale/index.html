Mount Waialeale

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The perpetually misty summit of Waiʻaleʻale, Kauaʻi.

Mount Waiʻaleʻale is a shield volcano and the second-highest point on the island of Kauaʻi in the Hawaiian Islands, rising to 1,569 metres (5,148 ft). Its name literally means "rippling water" or "overflowing water" in Hawaiian.

Rainfall

Waiʻaleʻale is famous as one of the wettest places on Earth: its summit receives an average of about 373 inches (9,500 mm) of rain per year, measured over a long period, and in 1982 it recorded 683 inches (17,300 mm). The peak's steep cliffs and its position in the path of the trade winds force moist air upward, producing near-constant rain; the summit is cloud-covered on most days and accessible only with difficulty. Rain falling on the mountain feeds the Alakaʻi Wilderness Preserve, one of the world's wettest bogs, and the headwaters of the Waimea, Wailua, and Hanalei rivers.

Features

The summit plateau's near-permanent cloud and rain support mosses, ferns, and ʻōhiʻa forest adapted to extreme wetness, while flash-flooding streams cut the dramatic fluted cliffs of the "Wall of Tears" on the crater's eastern side. A rain gauge has been maintained near the summit since the early 20th century, supplying the records that made the mountain famous.
