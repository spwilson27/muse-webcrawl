Emirate

An emirate is a territory ruled by an emir, a title of high office in the Islamic world. From the Arabic amārah, via amīr (أمير), "commander" or "prince", the word originally denoted a province or command under an appointed governor; over time many emirates became effectively independent hereditary states.

Historical emirates

Historically notable emirates include the Emirate of Córdoba in Islamic Spain (756–929), elevated to a caliphate by Abd al-Rahman III; the Emirate of Sicily (831–1091); the Emirate of Granada (1238–1492), the last Muslim state in Iberia; and the Emirate of Bukhara in Central Asia, which survived into the 20th century as a Russian protectorate. The title was also used for frontier commands and successor states across North Africa and the Middle East.

Modern emirates

  • United Arab Emirates: a federation of seven emirates — Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah — formed in 1971–72. Each is ruled by an emir, and the ruler of Abu Dhabi traditionally serves as the federation's president.
  • Qatar and Kuwait are sovereign states officially styled emirates, ruled by an emir as head of state.
  • The Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan is the official name used by the Taliban government.
  • Nigeria's northern states contain traditional emirates (such as Kano and Sokoto) whose emirs are influential non-sovereign traditional rulers.
