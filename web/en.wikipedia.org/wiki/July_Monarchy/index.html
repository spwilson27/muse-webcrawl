The July Monarchy (French: Monarchie de Juillet) was the liberal constitutional monarchy of France under Louis Philippe I, lasting from the July Revolution of 1830 until the Revolution of 1848. Louis Philippe styled himself "King of the French" by the will of the people rather than King of France by divine right.

Origins

The July Days (Trois Glorieuses, 27–29 July 1830) overthrew Charles X after his reactionary July Ordinances. The liberal bourgeoisie installed the Orléanist Louis Philippe under a revised Charter of 1830 that expanded — though still narrowly limited — suffrage based on tax payment.

Character and fall

The regime favoured the upper middle class; François Guizot, its dominant minister in the 1840s, famously urged the French to "enrich yourselves" through work and saving. The period saw industrialisation, railway building, colonisation of Algeria, and flourishing romantic culture, but also corruption, refusal to extend the franchise, and economic crisis from 1846. The banquet campaign for reform triggered the February 1848 revolution; Louis Philippe abdicated and fled to England, and the Second Republic was proclaimed.
