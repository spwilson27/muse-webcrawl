Úrsula Hilaria Celia de la Caridad Cruz Alfonso (21 October 1925 – 16 July 2003), known simply as Celia Cruz, was a Cuban singer and one of the most popular Latin artists of the 20th century. Cruz rose to fame in Cuba during the 1950s as lead vocalist of the Sonora Matancera orchestra, becoming known throughout Latin America as the "Guarachera de Cuba." After the Cuban Revolution she settled in the United States, where collaborations with Tito Puente and the Fania All-Stars made her a defining figure of salsa music and earned her the title "Queen of Salsa."

Early life and career

Cruz was born in the Santos Suárez neighborhood of Havana, the second of four children of Catalina Alfonso and Simón Cruz, a railway stoker. Against her father's wish that she become a teacher, she studied music at Havana's National Conservatory and sang in radio talent contests. In 1950 she joined Sonora Matancera, with whom she recorded dozens of albums over fifteen years.

Exile and salsa years

In July 1960, while touring in Mexico, Cruz declined to return to Cuba and eventually settled in the United States, never being permitted to return to the island in her lifetime. On 14 July 1962 she married Pedro Knight, the orchestra's trumpeter and later her manager. In the 1970s her recordings for Fania Records, including "Quimbara" and the album Celia y Johnny (1974) with Johnny Pacheco, helped define the salsa sound. Her signature cry of "¡Azúcar!" ("Sugar!") became one of the most recognizable exclamations in Latin music.

Later years and death

Cruz won multiple Grammy and Latin Grammy Awards, received the National Medal of Arts in 1994, and recorded more than 70 albums. She died of brain cancer on 16 July 2003 at her home in Fort Lee, New Jersey. Her funeral drew tens of thousands of mourners in Miami and New York.

Legacy

Cruz is regarded as one of the most influential figures in Latin music history. The Smithsonian and the United States Mint have honored her; she was selected as the first Afro-Latina to appear on a U.S. quarter, issued in 2024 as part of the American Women Quarters program.
