Schoolhouse Blizzard
Date: January 12–13, 1888
Location: Great Plains, United States
Deaths: 235 (estimated)

The Schoolhouse Blizzard, also known as the Schoolchildren's Blizzard or the Children's Blizzard, struck the Great Plains of the United States on January 12–13, 1888. The storm arrived suddenly on an unusually mild winter morning, so many children had gone to school lightly dressed. When temperatures plunged and whiteout conditions struck during the school day, teachers faced the decision of keeping pupils in schoolhouses that were running out of fuel or sending them home into the blizzard.

Impact

An estimated 235 people died, many of them schoolchildren caught while trying to reach home. Stories of heroism became famous: in Dakota Territory, teacher Minnie Freeman led her pupils to safety after the schoolhouse roof blew off; in Nebraska, Lois Royce kept her pupils alive by burning desks, though some children were lost. The disaster is remembered in David Laskin's book The Children's Blizzard (2004) and in Laura Ingalls Wilder's depiction of plains blizzards.

Context

The blizzard struck a region only recently settled by homesteaders, including many Scandinavian and German-Russian immigrants, and became one of the defining weather disasters of the pioneer era.
