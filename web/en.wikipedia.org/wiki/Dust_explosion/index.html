Dust explosion

A dust explosion is the rapid combustion of fine particles suspended in the air within an enclosed location. Dust explosions can occur where any dispersed powdered combustible material is present in high-enough concentrations in the air or another oxidising medium. They are a frequent hazard in coal mines, grain elevators and factories handling flour, sugar, plastics or metal powders.

Conditions

Five elements, the "dust explosion pentagon", are required: combustible dust, dispersion in air, an oxidant, confinement, and an ignition source. A primary explosion often lofts accumulated dust layers, creating a far more destructive secondary explosion — the cause of the catastrophic damage in incidents such as the 2008 Imperial Sugar refinery explosion in Georgia, USA, which killed 14 workers.

Prevention

Prevention rests on controlling dust accumulation through housekeeping, ventilation and collection; eliminating ignition sources; inerting; explosion venting and suppression; and, in underground coal mining, the use of rock dust to inert coal dust. Regulations include OSHA standards in the US and the ATEX directives in the European Union.
