Battle of Sardarapat

Date21–29 May 1918
LocationSardarabad (now Armavir), Armenia
ResultArmenian victory
BelligerentsArmenian National Council vs. Ottoman Empire
CommandersTovmas Nazarbekian; Movses Silikyan; Daniel Bek-Pirumyan vs. Yakub Shevki Pasha; Kazim Karabekir

The Battle of Sardarapat was fought from 21 to 29 May 1918 near Sardarabad in the Ararat plain, between Armenian regular units and militia of the Armenian National Council and the invading army of the Ottoman Empire, during the Caucasus campaign of the First World War. The Armenian victory halted the Ottoman advance on Yerevan and is credited with saving eastern Armenia from destruction and enabling the proclamation of the First Republic of Armenia.

Background

After the Russian collapse in the Caucasus and the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, Ottoman armies advanced into Armenian-populated territory in the spring of 1918, taking Kars and Alexandropol (Gyumri). In mid-May Ottoman columns drove toward Yerevan and the Echmiadzin, the spiritual centre of the Armenian Church, in a region crowded with refugees from the Armenian genocide.

The battle

General Movses Silikyan organised a desperate defence, mobilising every available man; Armenian clergy rang church bells to summon the population, and civilians — including refugees — supplied and reinforced the army. After Ottoman forces took the Sardarabad railway station, Daniel Bek-Pirumyan's counterattack on 22 May recaptured it, and in the following days Armenian forces drove the Ottomans back in a series of attacks, advancing to recapture ground toward Alexandropol before being checked. Simultaneously, Armenian forces fought the battles of Bash Abaran and Karakilisa on the other approaches to Yerevan.

Aftermath

On 28 May 1918, while the fighting continued, the Armenian National Council proclaimed the independence of the First Republic of Armenia. The cost was heavy, and the Treaty of Batum (4 June) still imposed harsh terms, but the republic survived. The battle is commemorated by the monumental Sardarapat Memorial, opened in 1968, and is regarded in Armenia as a national deliverance.
