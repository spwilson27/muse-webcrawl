Last speaker

The last speaker of a language is the final known fluent native speaker of that language; when such a person dies without the language having been transmitted or fully documented, the language becomes extinct.

Related fieldLanguage documentation
OutcomeLanguage extinction

Notable cases

Famous last speakers include Dolly Pentreath (Cornish, d. 1777, though the language was later revived), Ishi (Yahi, d. 1916), Ned Maddrell (Manx, d. 1974 — Manx has since been revived by learners), Tevfik Esenç (Ubykh, d. 1992), and Cristina Calderón (Yaghan, d. 2022). Some, like Boas Sr. of the Eyak language's last speaker Marie Smith Jones (d. 2008), worked with linguists to record their languages.

Significance

Last speakers often become symbols of language loss and of revitalisation efforts. The term can be contested: semi-speakers and later learners may complicate claims, and some communities reject the finality the label implies.
