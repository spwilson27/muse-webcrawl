Al-Fath

Al-Fatḥ (Arabic: الفتح)
Classification: Medinan
Number: 48th surah
Verses: 29
Position: Juz' 26

Al-Fath (Arabic: الفتح, al-fatḥ; meaning: "Victory", "Conquest") is the 48th chapter (surah) of the Qur'an with 29 verses (ayat). According to tradition, the chapter was revealed to Muhammad shortly after the Treaty of al-Hudaybiyyah (628 CE), on the return journey from the attempted pilgrimage to Mecca, and it describes that apparently unfavourable treaty as a "clear victory" (fatḥan mubīnan).

Content

The surah opens with the announcement of the victory granted to the Prophet, the forgiveness of his sins, and the completion of God's favour upon him. It speaks of the divine tranquillity (sakīnah) sent down into the hearts of the believers, condemns the hypocrites and the polytheists who thought ill of God, and praises those who pledged allegiance to Muhammad under the tree at Hudaybiyyah. It also mentions the spoils of Khaybar and promises future gains, and it ends with a description of Muhammad and his companions, said to be prefigured in the Torah and the Gospel.

Context

The Treaty of Hudaybiyyah, concluded between Muhammad and the Quraysh of Mecca, initially appeared to many of his followers to be a setback, but it paved the way for the peaceful spread of Islam and the eventual conquest of Mecca two years later. The surah is often cited in discussions of sincerity, allegiance, and the spiritual fruits of patience.
