Mark Adamo (born 1962) is an American composer and librettist, best known for his operas. His first opera, Little Women (1998), based on Louisa May Alcott's novel, became one of the most frequently performed American operas of recent decades.

Career

Adamo was born in Philadelphia and studied at New York University and the Catholic University of America. Before turning full-time to composition he worked as a music critic. Little Women, commissioned and premiered by Houston Grand Opera in 1998 with a libretto by the composer, was televised nationally and has since received dozens of productions internationally; its success established Adamo's reputation for lyrical, theatrically direct writing.

Works

His subsequent operas include Lysistrata, or The Nude Goddess (2005), after Aristophanes, premiered by Houston Grand Opera; The Gospel of Mary Magdalene (2013), premiered by San Francisco Opera, which drew on the Gnostic gospels and scholarly research into the historical Mary Magdalene; and Becoming Santa Claus (2015), premiered by Dallas Opera. As a librettist he has collaborated with other composers, notably writing the libretto for his husband John Corigliano's opera The Lord of Cries (2021). Adamo has also composed choral and orchestral works and has served on the faculty of New York University's Tisch School of the Arts. See also Little Women and John Corigliano.
