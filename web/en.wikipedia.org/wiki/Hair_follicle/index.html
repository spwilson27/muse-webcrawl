Hair follicle

A hair follicle is an organ found in mammalian skin that produces hair. It lies in the dermis and consists of the hair shaft, inner and outer root sheaths, the hair bulb with its dermal papilla and matrix cells, and associated structures including the sebaceous gland and arrector pili muscle.

Structure

At the base of the follicle, the bulb encloses the dermal papilla, which supplies blood and signalling molecules. Rapidly dividing matrix cells keratinise to form the growing hair. The bulge region of the outer root sheath contains stem cells that regenerate the follicle each cycle.

Hair growth cycle

Follicles cycle through anagen (growth), catagen (regression) and telogen (rest), followed by shedding (exogen). Scalp anagen lasts years, which determines maximum hair length; body hair cycles are much shorter.

Clinical significance

Disorders include alopecia, folliculitis, hirsutism and ingrown hairs. Follicles are also important in wound healing and skin regeneration research.
