Koi-Koi
TypeFishing / matching card game

Koi-Koi (Japanese: こいこい) is a popular card game in Japan played with hanafuda ("flower cards"). The name means "come on, come on" and refers to the choice a player makes to continue a round after completing a scoring combination. The objective is to form scoring sets (yaku) from cards captured from a shared field and from one's hand.

Setup

The game uses the 48-card hanafuda deck, twelve suits of four cards, one for each month of the year. Each player is dealt eight cards, eight cards are placed face up on the table, and the remainder forms a draw pile. Scoring is usually played over a set of rounds, often twelve, one for each month.

Gameplay

On a turn, a player plays a card from the hand to match a table card of the same month (suit), capturing both; if no match is possible the card is discarded to the table. The player then flips the top card of the draw pile and may capture in the same way. When a player completes a yaku, they may end the round and bank the points, or call "koi-koi" and play on in pursuit of additional yaku — at the risk of the opponent scoring first and taking the round, in which case the caller's points are lost and the opponent may gain a multiplier.

Scoring combinations

Well-known yaku include the Five Brights (gokō) and other combinations of bright cards; the Boar, Deer and Butterfly (ino-shika-chō) set of animal cards; seed (animal) and ribbon sets; and lunar and seasonal viewing combinations such as moon-viewing (tsukimi-zake) and cherry-blossom-viewing (hanami-zake).

In popular culture

Koi-Koi appears in numerous video games and anime — Nintendo, which began as a hanafuda manufacturer in 1889, has included the game in its Clubhouse Games series — and it features prominently in the 2009 film Summer Wars.
