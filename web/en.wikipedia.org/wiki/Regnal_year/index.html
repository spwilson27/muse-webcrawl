Regnal year

A regnal year is a year of the reign of a sovereign, from the Latin regnum meaning kingdom or rule. Regnal years considered a formal epoch (in contradistinction to an era) were used by many ancient civilisations, and dating by regnal years remains common in some contexts, notably in British legal citations of Acts of Parliament and in the era names of East Asian monarchy.

Usage

Traditionally, in many English law contexts, Acts of Parliament were cited by the regnal year in which they were passed, for example "1 Geo. 1" for a statute of the first year of George I. Japan still uses imperial era names (nengō), such as Reiwa, counting years from the accession of the emperor; similar systems were historically used in China, Korea, and Vietnam. In ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia, chronologies were reconstructed from king lists recording events by the year of each king's reign.
