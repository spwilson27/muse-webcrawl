Ford Falcon (Australia)

Ford Falcon
Manufacturer: Ford Australia
Production: 1960–2016
Class: Full-size car
Assembly: Broadmeadows, Victoria, Australia

The Ford Falcon is a full-size car that was manufactured by Ford Australia from 1960 to 2016. Over seven generations it became one of Australia's best-known and best-selling nameplates, offered as a sedan, wagon, utility (ute), panel van and in high-performance variants. Its long rivalry with the Holden Commodore defined the Australian family car market for decades.

History

The first XK Falcon of 1960 was closely based on the contemporary North American Falcon, but early durability problems on rough Australian roads led to extensive local engineering. Subsequent generations — including the XY of 1970–72, whose GTHO Phase III was among the fastest four-door production cars of its time — were increasingly Australian in design. Later series included the XD (1979), the EA (1988), the BA (2002), which won Wheels Car of the Year, and the final FG X range.

Motorsport

The Falcon was a mainstay of Australian touring car racing and the Bathurst 1000, driven by stars such as Allan Moffat, Dick Johnson and Marcos Ambrose, and later competed in the V8 Supercars championship.

End of production

Ford ended Australian manufacturing in October 2016, closing the Broadmeadows plant and ending Falcon production after 56 years and more than 3.5 million vehicles.
