Volozhin Yeshiva

The Volozhin Yeshiva, also known as Etz Chaim Yeshiva, was a prestigious Lithuanian yeshiva (talmudical college) located in the town of Volozhin (now Valozhyn, Belarus). Founded in 1803 by Rabbi Chaim of Volozhin, a disciple of the Vilna Gaon, it is often called the "mother of all Lithuanian yeshivas" because most later Lithuanian yeshivas traced their lineage to it.

History

Chaim of Volozhin founded the yeshiva as the first institution of its kind organised independently of the local community, supported by donations from across the Jewish world, with its own administration and a rosh yeshiva as both spiritual and academic head. Students studied Talmud around the clock in shifts, a practice rooted in Chaim's belief in continuous Torah study.

The yeshiva was later led by the Netziv, Rabbi Naftali Zvi Yehuda Berlin, for nearly forty years. Its students included many leading rabbis and figures of the Haskalah and Zionist movements, including the poet Hayim Nahman Bialik.

Closure

The Russian government demanded the introduction of secular studies and Russian-language instruction; when the yeshiva refused to comply fully, it was officially closed in 1892. A smaller yeshiva continued informally until 1939, when the region fell under Soviet control and the students fled to Vilnius.

Legacy

The Volozhin model — a large, independent, full-time talmudical academy with a charismatic rosh yeshiva — became the template for yeshivas across Eastern Europe and later in Israel and America.
