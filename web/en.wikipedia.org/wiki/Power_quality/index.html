Power quality

Power quality refers to the degree to which the voltage, frequency, and waveform of an electrical power supply conform to established specifications. Good power quality can be defined as a steady supply voltage that stays within the prescribed range, a stable AC frequency close to the rated value, and a smooth sinusoidal voltage waveform. It is the result of interaction between the power system and connected loads: modern electronic equipment is both more sensitive to disturbances and a greater source of them.

Disturbances

Common power quality problems include voltage sags (dips) and swells, transients and surges, interruptions, harmonic distortion caused by non-linear loads such as variable-speed drives and switched-mode power supplies, flicker, frequency variation, and poor power factor. Standards such as IEC 61000 and IEEE 519 set limits and measurement methods.

Mitigation

Poor power quality can cause equipment malfunction, data loss, overheating, and reduced efficiency, costing industry substantial sums. Mitigation measures include surge protective devices, uninterruptible power supplies, harmonic filters, voltage regulators, dynamic voltage restorers, and proper earthing and wiring practice.
