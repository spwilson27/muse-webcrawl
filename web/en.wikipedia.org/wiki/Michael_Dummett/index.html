Sir Michael Anthony Eardley Dummett (27 June 1925 – 27 December 2011) was an English philosopher, the Wykeham Professor of Logic at the University of Oxford from 1979 to 1992. A leading figure in analytic philosophy, he was the foremost interpreter of Gottlob Frege and a principal advocate of anti-realism in debates about meaning and truth.

Philosophy

Dummett's Frege: Philosophy of Language (1973) and The Interpretation of Frege's Philosophy (1981) reshaped Anglophone understanding of Frege. Drawing on intuitionistic logic and the idea that meaning is use, he argued that a theory of meaning must be a theory of understanding: speakers' knowledge of meaning must be manifestable in their use of language, undermining realist notions of evidence-transcendent truth. His books Elements of Intuitionism (1977) and The Logical Basis of Metaphysics (1991) extended this programme, making him a central figure in metaphysical debates about realism.

Other interests

Dummett was also the world's leading scholar of tarot and historical card games, writing the standard history The Game of Tarot (1980), and he campaigned vigorously against racism in Britain, helping to shape debates on immigration and voting reform; he wrote extensively on proportional representation. He converted to Roman Catholicism in 1944 and remained a practising Catholic. He was knighted in 1999 and received the Rolf Schock Prize in Logic and Philosophy in 1995.
