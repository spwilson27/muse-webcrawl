Bill Cosby

William Henry Cosby Jr. (born July 12, 1937) is an American stand-up comedian and actor. One of the most popular entertainers of the twentieth century, he starred in the groundbreaking drama I Spy (1965–1968) and created and starred in The Cosby Show (1984–1992), the top-rated American television series of its era, in which he played obstetrician Cliff Huxtable.

Career

Cosby began in stand-up in the early 1960s, releasing a string of best-selling comedy albums and winning multiple Grammy Awards. He became the first Black actor to co-star in a dramatic television series with I Spy, winning three consecutive Emmy Awards. His animated series Fat Albert and the Cosby Kids ran through the 1970s, and The Cosby Show revived the sitcom genre and reshaped American television's portrayal of an affluent Black family.

Sexual assault allegations and conviction

Beginning in the 2000s and escalating in 2014, dozens of women publicly accused Cosby of sexual assault over several decades; he denied the allegations. In 2018 he was convicted in Pennsylvania of aggravated indecent assault and sentenced to three to ten years in prison. In 2021 the Pennsylvania Supreme Court overturned the conviction on due process grounds — finding that a prior prosecutor's commitment had barred the prosecution — and Cosby was released. A 2022 civil jury in California found him liable for sexually abusing a minor in the 1970s.

Legacy

Many honours once bestowed on Cosby, including honorary degrees and awards, were rescinded following the allegations, and his career and public reputation collapsed.
