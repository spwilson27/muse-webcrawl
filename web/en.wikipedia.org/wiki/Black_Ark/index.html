Black Ark

The Black Ark was a recording studio in Kingston, Jamaica, built and run by reggae and dub producer Lee "Scratch" Perry. Operating from 1973 until it burned down in the early 1980s, the studio was the creative centre of Perry's most influential work, built behind his family home at 5 Cardiff Crescent in the Washington Gardens area of Kingston.

Sound and technique

Working with a modest four-track Teac recorder, a Soundcraft mixer, and effects such as the Mutron phaser and Roland Space Echo, Perry developed a dense, psychedelic production style — layering echo, reverb, found sounds, and repeated overdubs that pushed tracks toward saturation. The studio band was The Upsetters. Classic recordings made at the Black Ark include Junior Murvin's "Police and Thieves", Max Romeo's War Ina Babylon, The Heptones' Party Time, The Congos' Heart of the Congos, and Perry's own Super Ape.

Fire and legacy

Around 1980–1983 the studio burned down; Perry maintained that he set the fire himself to purge negative influences, though an electrical fault has also been blamed. The Black Ark's output is regarded as one of the foundations of dub and a lasting influence on electronic and experimental music worldwide.
