Durante degli Alighieri, known as Dante (Italian: [ˈdante]; c. 1265 – 14 September 1321), was an Italian poet, writer, and philosopher. His Divine Comedy, originally called Comedìa and later christened Divina by Boccaccio, is widely considered one of the most important poems of the Middle Ages and the greatest literary work in the Italian language. Dante is known for establishing the use of the vernacular in literature at a time when most poetry was written in Latin, and he is often called the "father" of the Italian language; his Tuscan dialect became the basis of standard Italian.

Life

Dante was born in Florence into a family of modest means aligned with the Guelph faction. His great love, Beatrice Portinari — met when both were children, dead at 24 in 1290 — is the subject of his early masterpiece La Vita Nuova (c. 1294) and guides him through Paradise in the Comedy. He studied rhetoric and philosophy, fought at the Battle of Campaldino (1289), married Gemma Donati, and entered politics, serving as one of Florence's priors in 1300. When the Black Guelphs seized Florence with the aid of Boniface VIII, Dante, then on an embassy to Rome, was exiled on fabricated charges in 1302, condemned to death if he returned. He never saw Florence again, spending his exile in Verona, Lucca, and finally Ravenna, where he died in 1321, probably of malaria contracted on a diplomatic mission to Venice.

The Divine Comedy

Composed between about 1308 and 1321, the Comedy is an allegorical journey through the afterlife in 14,233 lines of terza rima, divided into Inferno, Purgatorio, and Paradiso, each of 33 cantos plus an introductory canto. The Roman poet Virgil — heir to the epic line of Homer — guides Dante through Hell and Purgatory; Beatrice guides him through Paradise. The poem ranges across theology, astronomy, politics, and invective against Dante's enemies, and its vision of Hell has shaped Western imagination ever since. Its standing beside the epics of Homer, the Shahnameh, and the works of Shakespeare is rarely disputed, and Florence, which exiled him, maintains an empty tomb in Santa Croce while Ravenna keeps his bones.
