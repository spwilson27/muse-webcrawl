RF power amplifier

RF power amplifier
TypeElectronic amplifier
Frequency rangeRadio frequency (roughly 3 kHz – 300 GHz)
ApplicationsTransmitters, broadcasting, radar, mobile networks

An RF power amplifier (RFPA) is a type of electronic amplifier that converts a low-power radio-frequency signal into a higher-power signal, typically to drive the antenna of a transmitter. Power amplifiers are the final active stage of most radio transmitters and are key components in broadcast stations, cellular base stations, radar, satellite links and amateur radio equipment.

Design considerations

Designers trade off output power, efficiency, linearity and bandwidth. Amplifier classes A, AB, B and C describe the conduction angle of the output device, with higher-efficiency classes generally giving poorer linearity; modern systems use techniques such as Doherty architectures and digital predistortion to recover linearity. Devices range from vacuum tubes in high-power broadcast service to silicon LDMOS and gallium nitride (GaN) transistors in modern equipment.

Parameters

Important specifications include gain, saturated output power, power-added efficiency, harmonic and intermodulation distortion, and ruggedness under load mismatch, commonly expressed as a maximum tolerable VSWR.
