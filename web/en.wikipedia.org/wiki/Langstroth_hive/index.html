Langstroth hive

A Langstroth hive is any vertically modular beehive that has key features inspired by the work of Lorenzo Lorraine Langstroth (1810–1895): movable frames, the correct "bee space" between frames, and top-opening boxes of standardised size. Langstroth patented his design in 1852, and it has become the standard hive used in much of the world.

Design

Langstroth recognised that honey bees will not fill a gap of about 6–9 mm (the bee space) with comb or seal it with propolis, so frames spaced by this amount can be removed without cutting comb. A typical hive consists of a floor, brood boxes, honey supers, queen excluder, inner cover and roof, all built to common dimensions — commonly ten-frame or eight-frame boxes in deep, medium or shallow depths.

Impact

Movable-frame hives allowed inspection for disease, honey harvest without destroying the colony, and mass production of interchangeable equipment, which together made modern commercial beekeeping and migratory pollination practicable.
