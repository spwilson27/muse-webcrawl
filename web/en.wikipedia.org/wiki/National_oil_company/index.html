National oil company

Examples: Saudi Aramco, Gazprom, Rosneft, PetroChina, Petrobras, Pemex, PDVSA, Equinor, ADNOC, QatarEnergy
Share of world reserves: A majority, by most estimates
Counterpart: International oil companies (IOCs)

A national oil company (NOC) is an oil and gas company that is wholly or majority owned by a national government. NOCs control a large share of the world's petroleum production and the great majority of proven reserves, and they are central instruments of energy and fiscal policy in their home states.

History

State oil companies emerged in the early twentieth century — Mexico created what became Pemex after expropriating foreign oil firms in 1938 — but their decisive rise came with the nationalisations of the 1970s, when Middle Eastern and Latin American producers took control of concessions formerly run by the "Seven Sisters". Saudi Aramco, fully Saudi-owned by 1980, became the world's most valuable and productive oil company. Some NOCs later partially listed shares (Petrobras, Equinor, PetroChina, Saudi Aramco in 2019) while remaining state-controlled.

Characteristics

Compared with investor-owned international oil companies, NOCs often carry non-commercial obligations: subsidising domestic fuel, employing large workforces, funding state budgets and pursuing national development goals. Their efficiency and governance vary widely, from highly commercial operators such as Norway's Equinor to companies undermined by political interference.
