Indian Council of Agricultural Research

The Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) is an autonomous body responsible for co-ordinating agricultural education and research in India. It reports to the Department of Agricultural Research and Education (DARE), Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers' Welfare, Government of India. Its headquarters are at Krishi Bhavan in New Delhi, and its president is the Union Minister of Agriculture.

History

ICAR was established on 16 July 1929 as the Imperial Council of Agricultural Research, following the recommendations of the Royal Commission on Agriculture (1926–1928). It was set up as a registered society to promote, guide and co-ordinate agricultural research across the country. After independence it was renamed the Indian Council of Agricultural Research, and it underwent major reorganisations in 1966 and 1973, which brought most national agricultural research institutes and agricultural universities under a unified national system.

Organisation and work

ICAR oversees one of the largest national agricultural research systems in the world, comprising more than 100 ICAR institutes and over 70 agricultural universities across India, together with hundreds of Krishi Vigyan Kendras (farm science centres) that transfer technology to farmers. Its institutes conduct research on crops, horticulture, animal sciences, fisheries, natural resource management and agricultural engineering. ICAR played a central role in the Green Revolution, developing and disseminating high-yielding varieties of wheat and rice, and it coordinates the All India Coordinated Research Projects that test technologies across agro-climatic zones.

Education

Through its education division, ICAR accredits agricultural universities, funds infrastructure and fellowships, and conducts the All India Entrance Examinations for admission to undergraduate and postgraduate agricultural programmes.
