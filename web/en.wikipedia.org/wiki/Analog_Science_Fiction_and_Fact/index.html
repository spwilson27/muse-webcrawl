Analog Science Fiction and Fact is an American science fiction magazine published continuously since 1930, making it the longest-running magazine in the genre. It began as Astounding Stories of Super-Science, a pulp magazine published by Clayton Magazines, and later appeared as Astounding Stories and Astounding Science-Fiction. Under editor John W. Campbell from late 1937, Astounding became the central magazine of the so-called Golden Age of Science Fiction. The title was changed to Analog Science Fact & Fiction in 1960 to signal a more mature image, and later shortened to its present form.

History

The first issue, dated January 1930, was edited by Harry Bates. After Clayton's bankruptcy the magazine was acquired by Street & Smith, and F. Orlin Tremaine became editor in 1933. John W. Campbell took over in late 1937 and, over the following years, published early work by Isaac Asimov, Robert A. Heinlein, A. E. van Vogt, L. Sprague de Camp, Lester del Rey, and many others, insisting on rigorously reasoned stories grounded in plausible science — the style that came to be called hard science fiction.

Campbell edited the magazine until his death in 1971. Ben Bova (1972–1978) and Stanley Schmidt (1978–2012) followed, each winning Hugo Awards for Best Editor. Trevor Quachri became editor in 2012. The magazine has been published by Condé Nast (from 1980), then Dell Magazines and Penny Publications. In 1992 its companion publication, Isaac Asimov's Science Fiction Magazine, has shared its publishing house; both remain leading markets for short science fiction.

Content and influence

Analog publishes novellas, novelettes, short stories, serials, science-fact articles, and the long-running "Alternate View" and editorial features. It maintains a reputation for "hard" science fiction emphasising scientific accuracy and problem-solving protagonists, though it also publishes softer work. Stories first published in its pages have won numerous Hugo, Nebula, and Locus awards, and the magazine itself has been central to the careers of writers including Lois McMaster Bujold, Orson Scott Card, and Robert J. Sawyer.
