The alphorn is a long, straight wooden natural horn traditionally used by mountain dwellers of the Swiss, Austrian, French, and Bavarian Alps. Under this spelling the instrument is most often discussed in English-language sources and in its modern international revival, where alphorn ensembles, competitions, and festivals are organized in Switzerland and abroad, including North America and Japan. (See also the article under the German-derived spelling Alpenhorn.)

Modern practice

Today the alphorn is played far beyond its original herding context. Players gather in groups for open-air concerts, church services, and contests at folk festivals; in Switzerland the Federal Yodelling Association oversees alphorn instruction and adjudication. Solo and ensemble repertoire includes traditional herding melodies (Ranz des vaches), chorales, and new compositions written specifically for the instrument's natural harmonic series.

Modern instruments are built in three or four detachable sections of spruce, ash, or carbon fibre, usually in F♯/G♭, with a wooden cup mouthpiece. Players exploit the peculiar eleventh harmonic of the natural series — the so-called "alphorn F" lying between F and F♯ — as a characteristic colour of the instrument's traditional melodies.

Cultural significance

In 2020 and following years, alphorn music figured in international media as a symbol of Alpine identity and of communal music-making during the COVID-19 pandemic, when players performed from balconies and meadows. The alphorn is depicted on Swiss souvenirs, postage stamps, and tourism imagery alongside the Matterhorn and the Swiss flag.
