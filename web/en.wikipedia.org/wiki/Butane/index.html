Butane

FormulaC4H10
Molar mass58.12 g/mol
Boiling point−0.5 °C (n-butane)
CAS number106-97-8

Butane is widely used as a fuel in lighters and portable stoves.

Butane is an alkane with the formula C4H10. It exists in two structural isomers: n-butane, a straight chain, and isobutane (methylpropane), a branched chain. Butane is a colourless, highly flammable gas at room temperature that liquefies readily under modest pressure, a property central to its uses.

Uses

Blended with propane, butane forms liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), used for heating, cooking, and as vehicle fuel. Nearly all disposable lighters and portable camping stoves run on butane canisters. Isobutane is used in refrigeration (as refrigerant R-600a), as a propellant in aerosols, and as a feedstock for producing isobutylene and alkylate for petrol blending.

Properties and safety

Butane burns cleanly to carbon dioxide and water. It is an asphyxiant in high concentrations and is abused as an inhalant, a dangerous practice. Because it becomes sluggish to vaporise near its boiling point around 0 °C, pure butane performs poorly in very cold weather, which is why winter LPG blends contain more propane.
