École Normale Supérieure

École normale supérieure (ENS) Motto: (unofficial) "The school of the Rue d'Ulm"
Established: 1794
Location: 45 rue d'Ulm, Paris, France
Type: Grande école, public research institution
Students: about 2,400
The main building of the ENS on the rue d'Ulm.

The École normale supérieure (ENS) is a French grande école in Paris, regarded as one of the most prestigious and selective higher-education institutions in France. Founded in 1794 during the French Revolution to train teachers for the Republic, it has become a leading centre for the training of researchers and academics in the sciences and the humanities.

Admission is by a famously competitive national examination (concours) taken after two or more years of preparatory classes (classes préparatoires). Students admitted through the examination, the normaliens, hold civil-servant trainee status and receive a monthly salary in exchange for a commitment to work for the French state for ten years after graduation.

History and alumni

The school moved to its present home at 45 rue d'Ulm in the Latin Quarter in 1847. Its alumni include an exceptional number of Fields Medalists and Nobel laureates, as well as philosophers and writers such as Jean-Paul Sartre, Simone de Beauvoir, Michel Foucault and the scientist Louis Pasteur. The ENS is a constituent member of PSL University.
