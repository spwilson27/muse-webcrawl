The War of the Worlds

The War of the Worlds is a science fiction novel by English author H. G. Wells, first serialised in 1897 and published in book form in 1898. One of the earliest stories of conflict between humanity and an extraterrestrial race, it is among the most influential works in science fiction, popularising the idea of a Martian invasion of Earth.

Plot

An unnamed narrator in Surrey recounts the invasion of southern England by Martians who arrive in cylinders fired from Mars. The Martians assemble towering three-legged fighting machines armed with heat-rays and poisonous black smoke, sweeping aside the British Army and driving terrified refugees from London. Humanity appears doomed, but the Martians — lacking immunity to terrestrial microbes — are killed by Earthly bacteria, and the invasion collapses. The novel is narrated in the first person and interleaves the narrator's experiences with those of his brother fleeing London.

Themes and adaptations

Wells drew on contemporary fears of invasion literature, British imperialism — inverting the experience of colonised peoples — and evolutionary theory. The book inspired countless adaptations, most famously Orson Welles's 1938 radio drama, whose news-bulletin format reportedly panicked some listeners; Byron Haskin's 1953 film; Jeff Wayne's 1978 musical version; and Steven Spielberg's 2005 film starring Tom Cruise. Its image of the tripod war machine remains an enduring icon of the genre.
