Cycloalkene

A cycloalkene or cycloolefin is a type of alkene hydrocarbon which contains a closed ring of carbon atoms and one or more double bonds, but has no aromatic character. Some cycloalkenes, such as cyclobutene and cyclopentene, can be used as monomers to produce polymer chains. Other cycloalkenes can be valuable intermediates in chemical synthesis.

Endonuclear cycloalkenes have the double bond located within the ring; exocyclic cycloalkenes have the double bond outside the ring itself.

Nomenclature

Cycloalkenes follow IUPAC nomenclature conventions similar to alkenes. The ring carbons bearing the double bond are assigned positions 1 and 2, and numbering proceeds so that substituents receive the lowest possible locants.

Properties

Because of Bredt's rule, a bridgehead double bond cannot be placed in a small ring system: having a double bond at a bridgehead position of a small bicyclic system is extremely strained unless the ring is large enough (at least eight atoms in the largest ring).

Examples

  • Cyclopropene — the smallest cycloalkene, highly strained.
  • Cyclobutene
  • Cyclopentene
  • Cyclohexene — common solvent and hydrogenation precursor to cyclohexane.
  • Cycloheptene
  • Norbornene — a bicyclic alkene used in ring-opening metathesis polymerization.

Synthesis

Cycloalkenes may be prepared by partial hydrogenation of aromatic compounds (e.g. cyclohexene from benzene), elimination reactions of cycloalkanes or cycloalkanols, the Diels–Alder cycloaddition, and ring-closing metathesis.

Reactivity

Cycloalkenes undergo the typical reactions of alkenes — electrophilic addition, catalytic hydrogenation, epoxidation, and ozonolysis — often with outcomes influenced by ring size and strain.
