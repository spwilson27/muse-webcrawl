National dish

A national dish is a culinary dish strongly associated with a particular country. Such dishes may be selected — officially by a government or informally by popular consensus — as emblems of a nation's cuisine and cultural identity, often featuring local ingredients and traditional methods of preparation.

National dishes act as edible symbols of national identity.

Characteristics

National dishes are often tied to a country's history, geography and agriculture. They may be everyday staples eaten across social classes, festive dishes served on national holidays, or plates popularised abroad by emigrants and restaurants. Because most national cuisines are diverse, the choice of a single dish is frequently contested and several dishes may share the status.

Examples

Widely recognised examples include sushi and ramen for Japan, kimchi for Korea, pho for Vietnam, pad thai for Thailand, biryani for parts of South Asia, feijoada for Brazil, ceviche for Peru, poutine for Canada, hamburgers for the United States, fish and chips and roast beef for England, pasta and pizza for Italy, paella for Spain, borscht for Ukraine, hummus and falafel across the Levant, and injera with wat for Ethiopia.

Cultural significance

Food scholars note that national dishes are partly modern constructions, promoted by cookbooks, tourism and state campaigns, yet they remain powerful markers of belonging — celebrated at festivals, defended in cross-border origin disputes, and recognised by UNESCO listings of culinary heritage.
