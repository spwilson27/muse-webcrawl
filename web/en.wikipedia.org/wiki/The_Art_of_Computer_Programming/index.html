The Art of Computer Programming

The Art of Computer Programming (TAOCP) is a comprehensive multi-volume monograph on computer programming and the analysis of algorithms, written by Donald Knuth. Knuth began the project in 1962, intending a single book of twelve chapters; the first volume appeared in 1968. More than half a century later the work remains unfinished, and it is regarded as one of the foundational texts of computer science.

Volumes

  • Volume 1: Fundamental Algorithms (1968) — mathematical preliminaries, information structures.
  • Volume 2: Seminumerical Algorithms (1969) — random numbers, arithmetic.
  • Volume 3: Sorting and Searching (1973).
  • Volume 4A: Combinatorial Algorithms, Part 1 (2011); fascicles of Volume 4B began appearing in 2022.
  • Volumes 4B, 4C and further volumes on combinatorial algorithms, and Volumes 5 (syntactic algorithms) and 6 (theory of context-free languages) remain in preparation; a Volume 7 on compilers is also planned.

Content and style

Programs in the books are written for the hypothetical MIX computer and its successor MMIX, architectures Knuth designed so that the examples would not depend on any real machine. The text is famous for its rigorous mathematical depth, its graded exercises — rated by difficulty and marked where they contain research problems — and its author's wit. Knuth famously offered a reward cheque for the first finder of any error in his books; the "Knuth reward check" became a prized trophy among programmers. Frustrated by the typesetting of a new edition, Knuth interrupted the project in the 1970s to create the TeX typesetting system and the Metafont font-design system, with which all subsequent volumes were produced.

Bill Gates wrote of the series: "If you think you're a really good programmer… read (Knuth's) Art of Computer Programming… You should definitely send me a résumé if you can read the whole thing."
