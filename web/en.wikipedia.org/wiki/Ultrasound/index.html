Ultrasound

Ultrasound is sound with frequencies higher than the upper audible limit of human hearing (about 20 kHz). It is used in many fields: most famously in medical ultrasonography — the real-time imaging of fetuses, organs and blood flow — but also in industrial flaw detection, cleaning, sonar-adjacent ranging, flow measurement and pest deterrence.

Medical ultrasound typically uses 2–18 MHz piezoelectric transducers: the same crystal that sends a pulse listens for echoes, whose timing and strength build an image. It is non-ionising (unlike X-ray), inexpensive and portable, which is why it is the first-line imaging in obstetrics and much of cardiology (echocardiography) and emergency medicine. Some animals — bats, dolphins — navigate and hunt by ultrasonic echolocation, a natural precedent discovered through studies from Spallanzani to Donald Griffin.
