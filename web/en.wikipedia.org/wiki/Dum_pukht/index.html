Dum pukht

Dum pukht (Persian: دم‌پخت; Urdu: دم پخت), also spelled dampokhtak, is a slow-cooking technique associated with Awadhi and Mughlai cuisine, in which food is cooked in a heavy-bottomed, sealed vessel (a handi) over a low flame. Dum means "to breathe in" and pukht means "to cook," so the term describes food cooked in its own steam and juices, with the lid traditionally sealed with dough.

History

Legend attributes the technique to Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula of Awadh (r. 1775–1797): during a famine, large sealed cauldrons of rice and meat were cooked slowly for workmen, and the Nawab, impressed by the aroma when a pot was opened, ordered the method adopted in the royal kitchens of Lucknow. The technique became central to Awadhi cuisine and its famous biryanis and kebabs.

Method

Ingredients — meat, rice, spices, saffron, herbs and aromatics — are layered in the handi, the vessel is sealed so no steam escapes, and the food cooks gently in its own moisture. The pot is often opened only at the table, releasing a burst of fragrance. Famous dishes include dum pukht biryani, shahi tukda variants, and the dum pukht preparations popularised by restaurants such as Bukhara and Dum Pukht in India.
