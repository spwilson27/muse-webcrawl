Nissan S30

The Nissan S30, sold as the Datsun 240Z (260Z and 280Z in later forms) and as the Nissan Fairlady Z in Japan, is the first generation of the Z-car sports car line, produced from 1969 to 1978. Conceived under Yutaka Katayama ("Mr. K") of Nissan USA and styled by Yoshihiko Matsuo's team, it offered European-style GT looks and performance at a fraction of rivals' prices.

Specifications

The 240Z used a 2.4-litre L24 overhead-cam inline-six producing about 151 hp, independent suspension all round, front disc brakes, and a four- or five-speed manual gearbox. The US received enlarged 2.6-litre (260Z, 1974) and fuel-injected 2.8-litre (280Z, 1975–78) versions as emissions rules tightened; Japan also got the limited Fairlady Z432 with the S20 twin-cam engine and the long-nose ZG.

Legacy

The S30 won the East African Safari Rally in 1971 and 1973 and dominated SCCA racing in the US. Roughly 550,000 were sold across its run, permanently changing perceptions of Japanese cars. It was succeeded by the S130 280ZX. See also Nissan Fairlady Z.
