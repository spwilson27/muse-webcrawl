South West France (wine)

South West France, or in French the Sud-Ouest, is a wine region in southwestern France lying between Bordeaux and the Pyrenees. It is a mosaic of small appellations along the Garonne, Dordogne, and Lot river systems, including Bergerac, Cahors, Gaillac, Madiran, Jurançon, and Irouléguy. The region is noted for distinctive local grape varieties often found nowhere else.

History

Viticulture in the region predates Bordeaux's fame, with wines shipped downriver through Bordeaux. The Bordeaux merchants' historical privilege of blocking upriver wines until their own had sold stunted the region's development for centuries. Many appellations were revived only in the 20th century.

Wines and grapes

Cahors is famed for powerful reds from Malbec (locally Auxerrois or Côt); Madiran for tannic Tannat reds; Jurançon for dry and sweet whites from Gros and Petit Manseng; Gaillac for ancient varieties such as Mauzac and Braucol; and Bergerac for Bordeaux-style blends and the sweet wines of Monbazillac. The region preserves dozens of indigenous varieties neglected elsewhere.
