Mineral

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A mineral is a naturally occurring, inorganic, solid substance with a definite chemical composition and an ordered internal structure (a crystal lattice). Minerals are the building blocks of rocks: granite, for example, is an aggregate of the minerals quartz, feldspar and mica. More than 5,000 mineral species are recognised by the International Mineralogical Association, though only a few dozen are common. By definition a mineral must be natural (synthetic gems do not count), solid, inorganic in origin (coal and amber are excluded), and chemically definite within limits.

Properties and identification

Mineralogists identify minerals by physical properties: crystal form, hardness (measured on Mohs scale, from talc at 1 to diamond at 10), lustre, colour and streak (the colour of the powdered mineral), cleavage (how it splits along planes of weakness), fracture, density and taste or magnetism in special cases. Quartz (silicon dioxide, SiO2) is one of the most abundant minerals in Earth's crust and is used in glass, electronics and watches for its piezoelectric properties.

Classification and uses

Minerals are classified chemically: native elements (gold, copper, diamond), sulfides (pyrite, galena), oxides (hematite, magnetite), carbonates (calcite), and the vast silicate class, which makes up about 90% of the crust. Economically important minerals — ores — supply metals, fertilisers (phosphate, potash), industrial chemicals and gemstones. The study of minerals is mineralogy, a branch of geology and Earth science.
