Saint-Maurice, Switzerland

Saint-Maurice is a municipality in the canton of Valais in Switzerland, located where the Rhône emerges from a narrow gorge between the Dent de Morcles and the Dent du Midi. Its population is about 4,600. The town is famous for the Abbey of St Maurice, Agaunum, founded in 515 and among the oldest continuously operating monasteries in the Western world.

History

The Roman station of Acaunum or Agaunum guarded the road over the Great St Bernard Pass. According to tradition, Saint Maurice and the Theban Legion — Christian soldiers from Egypt — were martyred here around 287 under Maximian for refusing to sacrifice to the Roman gods. A shrine grew over their reputed burial place, and in 515 the Burgundian king Sigismund founded the abbey, instituting the laus perennis, an unbroken round of choral prayer maintained for centuries. The abbey accumulated a celebrated treasury of early medieval goldsmith work.

Geography and sights

Saint-Maurice commands the defile between French- and German-speaking Valais. Its castle housed the canton's military museum, and nearby are the Fairy Cave (Grotte aux Fées), a fortified cliff system hollowed out during the Second World War, and the pilgrimage chapel of Notre-Dame du Scex carved into the rock face above the town.
