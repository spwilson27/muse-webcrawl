The Vande Bharat Express is a series of Indian semi-high-speed electric multiple unit (EMU) trains operated by Indian Railways. Designed and manufactured by the Integral Coach Factory (ICF) in Chennai under the government's Make in India initiative, the train was first introduced in 2019 on the New Delhi–Varanasi route. It is named after the patriotic salute "Vande Mataram" (literally, "I praise you, Mother").

History

The first trainset, originally developed under the name Train 18, was rolled out in October 2018 after roughly 18 months of design and construction. Prime Minister Narendra Modi flagged off the inaugural service on 15 February 2019. A second-generation version, Vande Bharat 2.0, entered service in 2022 with upgraded features, followed by further refinements including an orange-and-grey livery and shortened eight-car formations for shorter routes. A sleeper version of the trainset was unveiled in 2024 for overnight long-distance services.

Features

Vande Bharat trainsets are self-propelled EMUs with distributed traction, allowing rapid acceleration and regenerative braking. Amenities include fully air-conditioned chair car and executive class coaches, automatic doors, sealed gangways, onboard Wi-Fi and infotainment, CCTV cameras, and bio-vacuum toilets. The trains are fitted with the KAVACH indigenous train collision avoidance system. Although capable of speeds up to 160–180 km/h depending on the version, operational speeds on most routes are limited by track conditions.

Network

Since 2019 the service has expanded rapidly, and by the mid-2020s well over a hundred Vande Bharat services connected major cities across nearly all electrified broad-gauge states in India, running on routes such as New Delhi–Varanasi, Mumbai–Ahmedabad, Delhi–Katara (Shri Mata Vaishno Devi Katra), and Chennai–Bengaluru–Mysuru.
