The Estádio da Luz (Portuguese pronunciation: [ɨˈʃtaðju ðɐ ˈluʃ]), officially named Estádio do Sport Lisboa e Benfica, is a multi-purpose stadium located in Lisbon, Portugal. It is used mostly for association football matches, hosting the home games of Portuguese club S.L. Benfica, its owner.

Opened on 25 October 2003 with an exhibition match between Benfica and Uruguayan club Nacional, it replaced the original Estádio da Luz, which had been in use since 1954 and was demolished to make way for the new stadium. The new ground was built as part of Portugal's preparations to host UEFA Euro 2004, and it staged the tournament final, in which Greece defeated Portugal 1–0.

History

Following Portugal's successful bid to host Euro 2004, Benfica decided to demolish its ageing stadium and construct a modern arena on adjacent land. Designed by the architecture firm Populous (then HOK Sport), the stadium is noted for its polycarbonate roof arches, which allow sunlight to illuminate the pitch while giving the building its distinctive silhouette. The name "da Luz" ("of Light") derives from the parish of Nossa Senhora da Luz in which the stadium stands.

The stadium hosted the 2014 UEFA Champions League Final, in which Real Madrid beat Atlético Madrid 4–1 after extra time, and the final rounds of the 2019–20 Champions League, held in Lisbon as a single-leg tournament during the COVID-19 pandemic, including the final between Bayern Munich and Paris Saint-Germain.

Characteristics

With a capacity of around 65,000 spectators it is the largest stadium in Portugal. The seating bowl is arranged over three tiers, and the stadium complex includes a shopping centre, a museum dedicated to the club's history (the Museu Benfica – Cosme Damião), and a statue of the club's legendary player Eusébio outside the main entrance.
