Chaleo Yoovidhya (17 August 1923 – 17 March 2012) was a Thai businessman best known as the creator of the energy drink Krating Daeng and the co-founder, together with the Austrian entrepreneur Dietrich Mateschitz, of Red Bull GmbH, the company behind the Red Bull energy drink sold worldwide.

Early life

Chaleo was born in Phichit province in central Thailand to a poor family of Chinese immigrants. His father traded ducks and fruit, and Chaleo received little formal education. He moved to Bangkok as a young man, working first as an insurance salesman before going into business for himself.

Business career

In 1962 Chaleo founded TC Pharmaceutical Limited Partnership in Bangkok, importing pharmaceutical products and later manufacturing its own lines. In the mid-1970s his company developed Krating Daeng (Thai for "red gaur"), a sweet, uncarbonated tonic drink containing caffeine, taurine, B vitamins and sugar, aimed at truck drivers, labourers and other manual workers who needed to stay awake through long shifts. The drink, sold in small brown glass bottles with a logo of two red bulls charging at a yellow sun, became a major success in Thailand.

In 1982 the Austrian marketing manager Dietrich Mateschitz, travelling in Asia, tried Krating Daeng and found it relieved his jet lag. He approached Chaleo with a proposal to market the drink internationally. In 1984 the two men founded Red Bull GmbH in Austria, each holding 49 per cent of the shares, with the remaining 2 per cent held by Chaleo's son Chalerm. The formula was adapted for Western tastes — carbonated and less sweet — and Red Bull was launched in Austria in 1987, growing into the world's best-selling energy drink brand.

Wealth and death

Thanks to his Red Bull stake and the family's Thai businesses, Chaleo was for many years listed by Forbes among Thailand's richest people, with a fortune estimated in the billions of US dollars. He was famously reclusive and rarely gave interviews. Chaleo died in Bangkok on 17 March 2012, aged 88. His family's shareholding in Red Bull passed to his heirs, led by his son Chalerm Yoovidhya.
