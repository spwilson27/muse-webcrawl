V8 Supercars

V8 Supercars
Category: Touring car racing
Country: Australia (and New Zealand)
Inaugural season: 1960 (as Australian Touring Car Championship)
Current name: Supercars Championship

V8 Supercars was the popular name of Australia's premier touring car racing category, sanctioned by Motorsport Australia and contested since 1960 as the Australian Touring Car Championship. The formula took its modern shape in 1993 with the Group 3A rules restricting the championship to V8-powered Ford Falcons and Holden Commodores; from 2017 the series has been officially known as the Supercars Championship, and since 2023 it has run Gen3 cars based on the Ford Mustang and Chevrolet Camaro.

History

Australian touring car racing grew out of the great Ford–Holden rivalry of the 1960s and 1970s, centred on the Bathurst endurance race, where legends such as Peter Brock — the "King of the Mountain", winner of nine Bathurst 1000s — became national heroes. In the early 1990s the category consolidated around five-litre V8 sedans, and in 1997 it was rebranded the Shell Championship Series; the "V8 Supercars" identity followed in 1999 under the AVESCO/TEGA administration.

The cars

The classic V8 Supercar was a purpose-built, silhouette-style race car: a production body shell over a control chassis, powered by a naturally aspirated 5.0-litre V8 producing roughly 460 kW (620 hp), with a six-speed sequential gearbox and rear-wheel drive. The 2013 "Car of the Future" rules admitted other manufacturers — Nissan, Mercedes-AMG (via Erebus) and Volvo joined for a time — before the field returned to Ford and General Motors products. The Gen3 formula, introduced in 2023, restored a closer link to road-car styling with Mustang and Camaro bodywork.

Events and drivers

The championship's crown jewel is the Bathurst 1000 at Mount Panorama, held each October over 1,000 km. Other marquee events include the Adelaide 500 street race and the Sandown 500. Celebrated champions include Mark Skaife, Craig Lowndes, Jamie Whincup — the most successful driver in the category's history with seven titles — and Scott McLaughlin.
