The Suffolk Coast and Heaths is an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty (AONB) — since 2023 branded a National Landscape — in the county of Suffolk, England. Designated in 1970, it covers approximately 403 square kilometres along the North Sea coast, from the Stour estuary near Felixstowe in the south to Kessingland, north of Southwold.

Landscape

The area is characterised by low, flat coastal scenery: shingle beaches and spits such as Orford Ness, saltmarshes, estuaries (the Blyth, Alde, Deben, Orwell and Stour), ancient heathlands of the Sandlings, grazing marshes, and small historic towns and villages including Aldeburgh, Southwold, Woodbridge and Orford. The coastline is dynamic and subject to erosion, famously illustrated by the lost medieval port of Dunwich.

Wildlife

The area contains nature reserves of international importance, including the RSPB reserve at Minsmere, famed for avocets, marsh harriers and bitterns, and the estuaries are designated Ramsar sites and Special Protection Areas for their wintering and breeding birds. The Sandlings heaths support species such as nightjar, woodlark and Dartford warbler.

Management

A dedicated partnership and staff unit, hosted by Suffolk County Council, coordinates conservation and visitor management across the area, working with local authorities, landowners, Natural England and voluntary organisations. The Suffolk Coast Path and long-distance routes such as the Sandlings Walk cross the landscape.
