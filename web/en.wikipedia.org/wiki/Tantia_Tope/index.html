Tantia Tope

Tantia Tope (c. 1814 – 18 April 1859), born Ramchandra Pandurang Tope, was a general in the Indian Rebellion of 1857 and one of its most notable commanders. A trusted aide of Nana Sahib, the adopted son of the last Peshwa, Tantia Tope fought British forces across central India for nearly two years, conducting a celebrated guerrilla campaign long after the main rebel centres had fallen.

Role in the rebellion

When the rebellion broke out in 1857, Tantia Tope became the military commander of Nana Sahib's forces at Kanpur (Cawnpore). After the British recaptured Kanpur, he joined forces with the Rani of Jhansi, Lakshmibai, and in 1858 the two seized the fortress city of Gwalior, briefly proclaiming Nana Sahib as Peshwa. Defeated there by Sir Hugh Rose, Tantia Tope escaped and waged a mobile guerrilla war across Rajputana and central India, repeatedly eluding British columns and marching, by contemporary estimates, thousands of miles.

Capture and death

In April 1859 he was betrayed by Man Singh, the raja of Narwar, a former ally who handed him over to the British while he slept. He was court-martialled and hanged at Shivpuri on 18 April 1859. British officers who fought against him acknowledged his skill; he is remembered in India as a hero of the first war of independence.
