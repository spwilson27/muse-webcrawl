DVD recordable

DVD recordable and DVD rewritable are optical disc recording technologies. The principal formats are DVD-R and DVD+R (write once), their dual-layer variants, and the rewritable DVD-RW, DVD+RW and DVD-RAM. A standard single-layer disc holds 4.7 GB.

Formats

DVD-R was developed by Pioneer in 1997 and is supported by the DVD Forum; DVD+R was introduced in 2002 by the DVD+RW Alliance with improved linking and error management. Most later drives support both ("±" drives). DVD-RAM, with hardware defect management and many rewrite cycles, found use in camcorders and set-top recorders.

Recording

Write-once discs use an organic dye recording layer changed by the laser; rewritable discs use a phase-change alloy that can be melted and recrystallised many times. Recordable DVDs largely replaced VHS for home recording in the 2000s before being displaced by streaming and flash storage.
