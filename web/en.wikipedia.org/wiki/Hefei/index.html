Hefei

Hefei 合肥
Country: China
Province: Anhui
Status: Prefecture-level city; provincial capital
Population (2020 census): approx. 9.4 million (prefecture-level)
Area: about 11,400 km²

Hefei (Chinese: 合肥; pinyin: Héféi) is the capital and largest city of Anhui Province, China. A prefecture-level city, it is the political, economic and cultural centre of the province. Located in the central part of Anhui, between the Yangtze and Huai rivers and beside Lake Chaohu, Hefei has grown rapidly in the 21st century into a major hub for science, research and manufacturing, and functions as a key transport centre on China's high-speed rail network.

History

The site of Hefei has been inhabited since the Han dynasty, and the name Hefei dates to that period. During the Three Kingdoms era the area was the scene of several battles between the states of Cao Wei and Eastern Wu, most famously the Battle of Xiaoyao Ford (215 AD), in which the Wei general Zhang Liao with a small force repulsed a much larger Wu army. The city later served as a prefectural seat under successive dynasties. After 1949 Hefei was designated the capital of Anhui Province (confirmed in 1952) and began industrial development; its population at the time was only around 50,000–80,000.

Science and education

Hefei is one of China's leading research cities, home to the University of Science and Technology of China (relocated here in 1970), the Hefei Institutes of Physical Science of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, and national laboratories working in fusion energy (the EAST tokamak), quantum information and plasma physics. The city is sometimes styled China's "capital of science and education" after Beijing and Shanghai.

Economy

Manufacturing is dominated by automobiles (including electric vehicle production), home appliances, flat-panel displays and electronics; the appliance industry around Hefei is among the largest in China. The city has attracted large foreign and domestic investment and its GDP has grown to over one trillion yuan.

Transport

Hefei Xinqiao International Airport serves the city, and the Hefei Metro opened its first line in 2016. High-speed rail lines connect Hefei with Shanghai, Beijing, Nanjing, Wuhan and other major cities.
