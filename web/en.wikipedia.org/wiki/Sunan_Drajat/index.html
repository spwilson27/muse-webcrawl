Sunan Drajat (born Raden Qasim, also known as Raden Syarifuddin; c. 1470 – c. 1522) was one of the Wali Sanga, the nine saints honoured for spreading Islam in Java, Indonesia. He was a son of Sunan Ampel and younger brother of Sunan Bonang.

Life and mission

Raden Qasim studied under his father at Ampel Denta, Surabaya, and undertook missionary work along the north coast of East Java. Tradition relates that after surviving a shipwreck — and, in local legend, being helped ashore on the back of a large fish or shark — he settled at the coastal village of Drajat in present-day Paciran, Lamongan Regency, where he established a pesantren. The village prospered and became a centre of Islamic learning.

Teachings

Sunan Drajat is remembered especially for his emphasis on social welfare and his philosophy often summarised in Javanese as memangun resep tyasing Sasama — making others happy — and in teachings such as Jroning suka kudu éling lan waspada ("in joy one must remain mindful and vigilant"). His preaching stressed care for orphans and the poor, communal charity, hard work, and generosity. He is also credited with composing tembang (Javanese sung poetry), including the macapat metre Pangkur, used to convey Islamic teaching through local art forms.

Legacy

His tomb at Drajat, Paciran, Lamongan, is a pilgrimage site, and a museum dedicated to him (Museum Sunan Drajat) stands nearby, displaying gamelan instruments and carvings attributed to his era.
