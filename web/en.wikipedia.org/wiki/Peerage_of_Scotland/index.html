Peerage of Scotland

The Peerage of Scotland comprises all peerages created by the King of Scots before the Act of Union 1707. It shares the five ranks of duke, marquess, earl, viscount, and lord of Parliament — the last corresponding to the English baron.

Distinctive features

Scottish peerages have several peculiarities: the lowest rank is styled "Lord" rather than baron; many titles can descend through female lines in the absence of male heirs, depending on the terms of the grant; and ancient earldoms descend from the Celtic mormaers. Before the Union, Scottish peers sat in the single-chamber Parliament of Scotland; afterwards they elected sixteen representative peers to sit in the House of Lords at Westminster, a system that lasted until the Peerage Act 1963 admitted all Scottish peers.

History

The first Scottish dukedom, Rothesay, was created in 1398. Notable creations include the Dukes of Hamilton, Buccleuch, Argyll, and Atholl. Since 1707 no new Scottish peerages have been created, but the dignities continue to descend according to their remainders.
