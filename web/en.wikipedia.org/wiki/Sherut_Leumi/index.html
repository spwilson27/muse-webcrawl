Sherut Leumi (Hebrew: שירות לאומי, literally "National Service") is an alternative voluntary form of national service in Israel, available to citizens who are exempt from or do not serve in the Israel Defense Forces. Participants serve for one or two years in public institutions such as schools, hospitals, welfare services, museums, environmental organisations and government ministries, receiving a modest monthly stipend, housing assistance and benefits comparable to those of discharged soldiers.

Background

Under Israeli law, military service is generally compulsory for Jewish citizens, but exemptions exist for various groups, including most religious women who declare that military service conflicts with their way of life, medical exemptions, and — by long-standing arrangement, the subject of continuing political debate — most Arab citizens and many Haredi men. Sherut Leumi was developed to provide a recognised civilian framework for those exempted, particularly religious young women (bnot sherut), and has since broadened to include other populations.

Administration and placements

The programme is coordinated by the Authority for National-Civic Service within the Israeli government and implemented through authorised associations that recruit, place and support volunteers. Placements range from assisting teachers and special-needs educators to hospital support roles, work with new immigrants, emergency services auxiliary roles and community projects in development towns.

Debates

Proposals to expand civilian national service — and to require it of groups currently exempt from the draft, in the name of "sharing the burden" — have been a recurring theme in Israeli politics, notably in the Tal Law (2002) and its successors. Arab-Israeli participation is contested, with many Arab leaders opposing service as a step toward militarisation or as conditional citizenship, while participants cite civic and employment benefits.
