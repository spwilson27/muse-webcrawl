The Papiermark (German: [paˈpiːɐ̯ˌmaʁk], "paper mark") was the fiat currency of Germany from 4 August 1914, when the link between the Goldmark and gold was abandoned, until 15 November 1923, when it was superseded by the Rentenmark. The Papiermark is indelibly associated with the hyperinflation of 1921–1923 in the Weimar Republic, one of the most extreme currency collapses in modern history.

History

At the outbreak of the First World War, Germany suspended the gold standard and financed much of the war effort through borrowing and printing money. After the war, the burden of reparations imposed by the Treaty of Versailles, together with large budget deficits, placed continuous downward pressure on the mark. Inflation accelerated from 1921; by the summer of 1923 prices were doubling within days, and workers were paid twice daily so they could spend their wages before they lost value.

At the height of the crisis in November 1923, one United States dollar exchanged for about 4.2 trillion Papiermark. Banknotes were issued in denominations up to 100 trillion marks, and municipalities and firms printed emergency money (Notgeld). The old currency was ultimately exchanged at a rate of one trillion Papiermark to one Rentenmark.

Coins and banknotes

The Reichsbank and the German treasury issued an enormous variety of notes, with denominations climbing from a few marks in 1914 to billions (Milliarden) and trillions (Billionen in German usage) by late 1923. Because printing could not keep pace, many notes were printed on one side only, and existing stocks were overprinted with new, higher values. Coins largely disappeared from circulation as their metal content came to exceed their face value.

Stabilisation

Under finance minister Hans Luther and Reichsbank president Hjalmar Schacht, the Rentenbank was established in October 1923 and began issuing the Rentenmark, nominally backed by mortgages on agricultural and industrial land. Combined with fiscal reforms and the end of passive resistance in the Ruhr, the new currency restored confidence almost immediately. In 1924 the Rentenmark was replaced by the Reichsmark at par, restoring a gold-backed unit.
