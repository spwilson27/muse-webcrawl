Compost

Compost is a mixture of ingredients used as plant fertilizer and to improve soil's physical, chemical, and biological properties. It is commonly prepared by decomposing plant and food waste, recycling organic materials, and manure. The resulting mixture is rich in plant nutrients and beneficial organisms, such as bacteria and fungi. Compost improves soil fertility in gardens, landscaping, horticulture, urban agriculture, and organic farming, reducing dependency on commercial chemical fertilizers.

Composting process

Composting is an aerobic method of decomposing organic solid wastes performed by microorganisms under controlled conditions. A compost pile typically combines carbon-rich "browns" (dry leaves, straw, wood chips) with nitrogen-rich "greens" (grass clippings, food scraps, manure). During the active, thermophilic phase, pile temperatures may reach 55–70 °C (131–158 °F), which kills many pathogens and weed seeds. Turning the pile supplies oxygen and speeds decomposition; finished compost smells earthy and resembles dark topsoil.

Uses

Compost is used as a soil conditioner and fertilizer in agriculture and gardening, for erosion control and land reclamation, and increasingly in municipal programs that divert food waste from landfills, where it would otherwise generate methane.
