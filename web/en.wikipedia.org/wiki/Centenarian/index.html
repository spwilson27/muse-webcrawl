Centenarian

A centenarian is a person who has reached the age of 100 years. Because life expectancies everywhere are well below 100, the term is invariably associated with longevity. Centenarians were once rare, but improving survival has made them the fastest-growing age segment in many developed countries: Japan, for example, counts tens of thousands of centenarians, and the United States had an estimated 80,000–100,000 around the early 2020s.

Demographics and research

Women form the large majority of centenarians — roughly four or five women for every man at age 100 in most statistics. Long-running cohort studies, such as the New England Centenarian Study and research in Okinawa, Sardinia and other so-called "Blue Zones", investigate the genetics, lifestyle and disease resistance of the very old. Many centenarians show "compression of morbidity", remaining healthy and independent until very late in life; researchers distinguish "escapers" who avoid major age-related disease entirely from "delayers" and "survivors".

Celebration and records

In the United Kingdom, people receive a congratulatory card from the monarch on their 100th birthday; similar greetings are issued by heads of state elsewhere, and in Japan centenarians receive a silver sake cup and letter from the prime minister. A person reaching 110 is called a supercentenarian; the longest verified human lifespan is that of Jeanne Calment of France (1875–1997), who lived 122 years and 164 days. Age claims beyond the verified record require rigorous documentary validation, a field maintained by organisations such as the Gerontology Research Group.
