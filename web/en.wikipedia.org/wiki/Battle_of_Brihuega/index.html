The Battle of Brihuega was fought on 8–9 December 1710 during the War of the Spanish Succession, in which a British force under James Stanhope, 1st Earl Stanhope, was surrounded and forced to surrender at the town of Brihuega in Castile by a Franco-Spanish army under the Duke of Vendôme.

Background

After victories at Almenar and Saragossa in the summer of 1710, the Allied army supporting the Habsburg claimant, Archduke Charles, briefly occupied Madrid. Unable to hold the hostile capital, the Allies retreated toward Catalonia in separate columns during the winter. Stanhope's British contingent, about 4,000–5,000 men, lagged behind the main body under the Austrian commander Guido Starhemberg and took up position in Brihuega.

The battle

Vendôme, marching rapidly with the army of Philip V, caught Stanhope isolated on 8 December 1710 and invested the town. The British defended the walls through the day and into the next, repelling assaults but exhausting their ammunition; a storm on 9 December broke into the town, and street fighting followed. With no prospect of relief, Stanhope surrendered his entire force. Around 600 British soldiers were killed and the remainder — including Stanhope himself — taken prisoner.

Aftermath

The following day Vendôme attacked Starhemberg at the nearby Battle of Villaviciosa (10 December 1710) with less success, but the Allied retreat continued and the campaign effectively ended the Habsburg attempt to win the Spanish crown from Madrid. Philip V's throne was thereafter secure, a result confirmed by the Treaty of Utrecht in 1713.
