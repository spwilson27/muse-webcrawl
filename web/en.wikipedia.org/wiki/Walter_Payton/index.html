Walter Payton

Walter Jerry Payton (July 25, 1953 – November 1, 1999) was an American professional football running back who played in the National Football League (NFL) for 13 seasons with the Chicago Bears. Nicknamed "Sweetness", he is widely considered one of the greatest running backs of all time and retired as the NFL's all-time leading rusher.

Career

Payton was selected fourth overall in the 1975 NFL draft after a record-setting career at Jackson State University. With the Bears he set numerous records, including most career rushing yards (16,726, later surpassed by Emmitt Smith), most rushing touchdowns at his retirement, and a then-record 275 rushing yards in a single game against the Minnesota Vikings in 1977 — a performance achieved while suffering from influenza. He was named the NFL Most Valuable Player in 1977 and was selected to nine Pro Bowls.

Super Bowl XX

Payton was a central figure of the celebrated 1985 Chicago Bears, who won Super Bowl XX by defeating the New England Patriots 46–10. In a lasting controversy, head coach Mike Ditka did not give Payton a rushing attempt near the goal line in the game, later calling it his biggest regret.

Death and legacy

Payton died in 1999 of complications from primary sclerosing cholangitis, a rare liver disease, and bile duct cancer. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1993. The NFL's Man of the Year Award was renamed the Walter Payton NFL Man of the Year Award in his honour, recognising players' charitable work off the field.
