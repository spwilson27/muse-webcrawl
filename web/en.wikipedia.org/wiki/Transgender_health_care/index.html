Transgender health care

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Transgender health care includes the prevention, diagnosis and treatment of physical and mental health conditions for transgender individuals. A major component is gender-affirming care, the medical aspects of gender transition, which may include hormone replacement therapy, surgeries, voice therapy, and mental health support. Issues faced by this population include barriers to accessing care, discrimination within health systems, and disparities in outcomes for common conditions.

Gender-affirming care

Gender-affirming medical care is provided under clinical guidelines such as the Standards of Care published by the World Professional Association for Transgender Health (WPATH) and guidelines of the Endocrine Society. For adolescents, care may involve puberty blockers; for adults, hormone therapy with estrogen or testosterone is common, and some people pursue surgeries such as chest reconstruction, genital surgery or facial procedures. Access rules, insurance coverage and legal availability vary widely between countries and are the subject of ongoing public and professional debate.

Health disparities

Transgender people experience higher rates of some mental health conditions, HIV in certain populations, and avoidance of routine care due to anticipated stigma. Professional bodies in many countries recommend clinician training and non-discriminatory provision of general medical services such as cancer screening appropriate to a patient's anatomy.
