San Antonio River Walk


Location: San Antonio, Texas, United States
Length: 15 miles (24 km)
Opened: 1941 (downtown reach)

The San Antonio River Walk is a city park and special-case pedestrian street in San Antonio, Texas, one level below the automobile street. The River Walk winds and loops under bridges as two parallel sidewalks lined with retail shops, restaurants, hotels, and other commercial services, connecting the major tourist draws from the Shops at Rivercenter, to the Arneson River Theatre, to Marriage Island, to La Villita, to HemisFair Park, to the Tower Life Building, and later extensions to the San Antonio Missions National Historical Park.

History

The River Walk's design was the vision of architect Robert H. H. Hugman, who proposed "The Shops of Aragon and Romula," a landscaped pedestrian riverfront, after a disastrous flood in 1921 killed more than 50 people and prompted calls to pave over the river bend. Construction under the Works Progress Administration began in 1939, and the first section opened in 1941. Initially quiet, the River Walk flourished after HemisFair '68 brought hotels and the Tower of the Americas to its banks.

Extensions

The Museum Reach, opened in 2009, extended the walk north to the San Antonio Museum of Art and the Pearl Brewery complex. The Mission Reach, completed in 2013, restored an eight-mile stretch of the river ecosystem southward to connect with the Spanish colonial missions, bringing the total River Walk system to about 15 miles (24 km). Today it is one of the most visited attractions in Texas, hosting events such as the Fiesta River Parade and the Ford Holiday River Parade.
