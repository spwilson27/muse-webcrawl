Battle of Villaviciosa

The Battle of Villaviciosa was fought on 10 December 1710 during the War of the Spanish Succession, near Villaviciosa de Tajuña in Spain. A Franco-Spanish army under the Duke of Vendôme engaged the Allied army of Guido Starhemberg, which had taken Madrid but was retreating toward Aragon. Tactically hard-fought, the battle enabled the Bourbon forces to continue their pursuit and confirmed Philip V on the Spanish throne.

Background

The Allied army under Starhemberg and Stanhope had occupied Madrid in September 1710, but found the capital hostile and unsupplied, and began a retreat toward Aragon in separate columns. Vendôme, having surprised and captured Stanhope's detachment at Brihuega on 9 December, caught up with Starhemberg's main body the following day.

The battle

Fighting began in the afternoon. The Bourbon right, including cavalry led by the Marquis of Valdecañas and the Count of Aguilar, broke the Allied left and captured artillery and baggage, while on the other flank the Allied troops under Starhemberg repulsed the Bourbon left. Both sides claimed the field at nightfall, but Starhemberg, heavily outnumbered after Brihuega and short of supplies, continued his retreat in good order, abandoning guns and wounded.

Aftermath

Coming the day after Brihuega, Villaviciosa completed the ruin of the Allied campaign of 1710. The Archduke Charles's cause in Spain never recovered; Catalonia held out until 1714, but the Treaty of Utrecht recognised Philip V as king of Spain.
