Pandemic

A pandemic is an epidemic of an infectious disease that has spread across a large region — multiple continents or worldwide — affecting a substantial number of people. The World Health Organization traditionally described pandemic phases for influenza; for COVID-19 it 'characterised' the outbreak as a pandemic on 11 March 2020.

History's great pandemics include the Plague of Justinian, the Black Death (14th century, killing a large fraction of Europe's population), the 1918 influenza pandemic ('Spanish flu', tens of millions of deaths), HIV/AIDS, and COVID-19. Pandemic preparedness involves surveillance, the International Health Regulations, vaccine platform readiness and stockpiles — all scrutinised after COVID-19 exposed gaps.

Pandemic vs epidemic

An epidemic is a sudden rise in cases in a community or region; an outbreak is a smaller, localised version; a disease constantly present at baseline levels is endemic. Pandemics are usually caused by novel pathogens to which populations have little immunity.
