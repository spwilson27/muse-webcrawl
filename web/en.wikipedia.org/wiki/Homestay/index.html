Homestay

Homestay
TypeLodging / hospitality arrangement
GuestsStudents, tourists, language learners
Related conceptsBed and breakfast, guest house, CouchSurfing

A homestay is a form of hospitality and lodging whereby visitors pay to stay in the home of a local family rather than in a hotel or other commercial accommodation. Homestays are especially common for students — including international students and language learners — who live with a host family for an extended period, often sharing meals and daily life with the household.

Characteristics

A homestay typically provides a private or shared bedroom, access to common areas, and in many arrangements some or all meals. Because the guest lives within a family home, homestays are valued for cultural immersion and language practice. Host families may be vetted by schools, agencies, or community organizations, particularly where minors are involved.

Student homestays

Homestays are a major component of study-abroad and student exchange programs. Schools and universities commonly maintain homestay programs for international students, matching them with local families. Staying with a host family is often promoted as a way for students to improve their language skills, adapt to a new culture, and receive support away from home. In countries such as Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, homestay hosting is also a modest source of household income.

Tourism homestays

In tourism, homestays offer travelers an alternative to hotels, marketed on authenticity and local contact. Some countries have formal homestay programs — for example, Malaysia's national homestay programme and community-based homestays in Nepal and Vietnam — intended to spread tourism income to rural communities. The rise of online platforms for short-term rentals has blurred the line between traditional homestays, guest houses, and services such as Airbnb.

Regulation and welfare

Where hosts accommodate minors or vulnerable students, background checks, home inspections, and safeguarding rules may apply. Payment arrangements, house rules, and expectations about meals, curfews, and privacy are usually agreed in advance through a school, agency, or written contract.
