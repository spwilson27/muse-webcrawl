Samuel R. Delany

Samuel R. Delany (born 1 April 1942), also known as "Chip" Delany, is an American author and literary critic. His work includes science fiction, memoirs, and essays on sexuality and society. He is a major figure in science fiction and in the modern memoir of gay life.

Early life

Delany was born in Harlem, New York, to a prominent African American family. His father ran a funeral home. He attended the Bronx High School of Science. His mother was a clerk at the New York Public Library.

Career

His early novels, beginning with The Jewels of Aptor (1962), published when he was twenty, won acclaim. Babel-17 (1966) and The Einstein Intersection (1967) won the Nebula Award. His most famous novel, Dhalgren (1975), is a long, experimental work set in the fictional city of Bellona and has sold over a million copies. Other major works include the Return to Nevèrÿon series and Stars in My Pocket Like Grains of Sand (1984).

Nonfiction and memoir

Delany's memoir The Motion of Light in Water (1988) won the Hugo Award. Times Square Red, Times Square Blue (1999) examines urban sexuality and public space. He taught at several universities, including the University of Massachusetts Amherst and Temple University, retiring in 2015.

Personal life

Delany married poet Marilyn Hacker in 1961; they later divorced. He is openly gay and has written extensively about his experiences.
