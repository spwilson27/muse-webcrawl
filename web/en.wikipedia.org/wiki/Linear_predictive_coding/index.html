Linear predictive coding

Linear predictive coding (LPC) is a method used mostly in audio signal processing and speech processing for representing the spectral envelope of a digital signal of speech in compressed form, using the information of a linear predictive model. It is one of the most useful methods for encoding quality speech at a low bit rate.

Principle

LPC models each speech sample as a linear combination of previous samples; the predictor coefficients are chosen to minimize the mean squared prediction error over a short frame of speech. The residual signal — voiced (periodic) or unvoiced (noise-like) excitation — plus the coefficients suffice to resynthesize intelligible speech. Analysis typically uses autocorrelation with the Levinson–Durbin algorithm.

Applications

LPC underlies speech codecs such as CELP used in mobile telephony, secure voice communications, and early speech synthesis chips such as those in the Speak & Spell toy.
