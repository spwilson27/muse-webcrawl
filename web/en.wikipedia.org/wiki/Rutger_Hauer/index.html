Rutger Oelsen Hauer (23 January 1944 – 19 July 2019) was a Dutch actor. He rose to fame in the Netherlands through his collaborations with director Paul Verhoeven, including the television series Floris (1969) and the films Turkish Delight (1973) and Soldier of Orange (1977), and achieved international recognition as the replicant Roy Batty in Ridley Scott's science-fiction film Blade Runner (1982).

Career

Hauer's American debut came in Nighthawks (1981) opposite Sylvester Stallone. In Blade Runner, his performance as the eloquent, doomed android culminated in the partly improvised "tears in rain" monologue, one of the most celebrated scenes in science-fiction cinema. He followed it with leading roles in Ladyhawke (1985), The Hitcher (1986), Blind Fury (1989), and the television film Escape from Sobibor (1987), for which he won a Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor.

From the 1990s onward Hauer worked prolifically in independent and genre films, and appeared in mainstream productions including Batman Begins (2005), Sin City (2005), and Hobo with a Shotgun (2011). He published an autobiography, All Those Moments (2007), donating proceeds to his foundation supporting people with HIV/AIDS, and was an environmental activist who supported organisations such as Greenpeace and Sea Shepherd.

Death and legacy

Hauer died at his home in Beetsterzwaag on 19 July 2019 after a short illness. Fittingly, 2019 was the year in which his Blade Runner character Roy Batty was set to die, a coincidence widely noted in tributes. He is remembered as one of the most prominent Dutch actors of his generation, with a screen presence that combined physical intensity and unexpected tenderness.
