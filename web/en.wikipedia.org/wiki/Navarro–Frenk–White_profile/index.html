Navarro–Frenk–White profile

The characteristic shape of the NFW density profile

The Navarro–Frenk–White (NFW) profile is a spatial mass distribution of dark matter fitted to dark matter halos identified in N-body simulations by Julio Navarro, Carlos Frenk, and Simon White. The profile is the most commonly used parametrisation of dark matter halo density in astrophysics and cosmology.

Form

The density as a function of radius r is:

ρ(r) = ρ₀ / [ (r/Rs) (1 + r/Rs)² ]

where Rs is a characteristic scale radius and ρ₀ is a characteristic density. The profile diverges as r−1 at the centre (an inner "cusp") and falls as r−3 at large radii. The halo is specified by its virial mass and a concentration parameter c = Rvir/Rs.

Properties and significance

The NFW profile's remarkable feature is its near-universality: halos spanning many orders of magnitude in mass, from dwarf galaxies to galaxy clusters, are described by the same functional form with only two parameters. It underlies analytic models of galaxy formation, gravitational lensing mass estimates, and halo occupation modelling.

Its predicted central cusp contrasts with the flatter "cores" inferred from the rotation curves of some dwarf and low-surface-brightness galaxies — the core–cusp problem — motivating alternatives such as the Einasto profile, the Burkert profile, and baryonic feedback or self-interacting dark matter as physical remedies.
