Educational technology

Educational technology (commonly abbreviated as edutech, or edtech) is the combined use of computer hardware, software, and educational theory and practice to facilitate learning and teaching. When referred to by its abbreviation, edtech, it often refers to the industry of companies that create educational technology. In education, educational technology must be supplemented by a clear understanding of learning theory, as technology itself does not improve learning outcomes without sound pedagogy.

Besides practical classroom experience, educational technology is based on theoretical knowledge from various disciplines such as communication, education, psychology, sociology, artificial intelligence, and computer science. It encompasses several domains including learning theory, computer-based training, online learning, and m-learning (mobile learning).

Definition

The Association for Educational Communications and Technology (AECT) has defined educational technology as "the study and ethical practice of facilitating learning and improving performance by creating, using and managing appropriate technological processes and resources." It denotes all the valid and reliable applied education sciences, such as equipment, as well as processes and procedures that are derived from scientific research, and in a given context may refer to theoretical, algorithmic or heuristic processes.

History

Helping people learn in ways that are efficient and appealing has long been pursued by teachers employing tools such as visual aids. In the mid-1960s, Stanford University psychology professors Patrick Suppes and Richard C. Atkinson experimented with using computers to teach arithmetic and spelling to elementary school children. The development of the PLATO system at the University of Illinois in the 1960s pioneered many concepts of modern e-learning, including forums, message boards, online testing, and instant messaging.

The arrival of inexpensive personal computers, the World Wide Web in the 1990s, and later mobile devices transformed the field. Massive open online courses (MOOCs), popularised in the early 2010s by platforms such as Coursera, edX, and Khan Academy, made recorded lectures and interactive exercises freely available to millions of learners worldwide.

Learning management systems

A learning management system (LMS) is a software application for the administration, documentation, tracking, reporting, automation, and delivery of educational courses, training programs, materials or learning and development programs. Widely used systems include Moodle, Canvas, Blackboard Learn, and Google Classroom. LMS platforms typically support discussion forums, assignment submission, automated grading, and learning analytics dashboards.

Theoretical foundations

Educational technology draws on behaviourism (programmed instruction, mastery learning), cognitivism (multimedia learning theory, cognitive load theory), and constructivism (project-based and inquiry-based learning with digital tools). Richard Mayer's cognitive theory of multimedia learning and the TPACK framework — technological, pedagogical, and content knowledge — are influential models guiding the design of technology-supported instruction.

Criticism and evidence

Researchers have frequently found "no significant difference" between well-designed technology-based and face-to-face instruction, prompting debate over whether media themselves influence learning or whether instructional method is decisive — a debate associated with Richard E. Clark and Robert Kozma. Concerns include the digital divide, student distraction, data privacy, screen time, and the quality of commercial products marketed without rigorous evaluation. Randomised evaluations suggest technology is most effective when it supplements good teaching, personalises practice, and expands access to content that would otherwise be unavailable.
