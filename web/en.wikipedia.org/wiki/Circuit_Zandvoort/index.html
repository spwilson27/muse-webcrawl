Circuit Zandvoort, officially Circuit Park Zandvoort, is a motorsport race track located in the dunes north of Zandvoort, Netherlands, near the North Sea coast. It hosted the Formula One Dutch Grand Prix from 1952 to 1985 and, after renovation, again from 2021, drawing enormous home crowds following the success of Dutch driver Max Verstappen.

History

Racing on the streets of Zandvoort began in 1939, and a permanent circuit using a mix of purpose-built track and public roads opened on August 7, 1948. The circuit joined the Formula One world championship calendar in 1952. The layout, with fast sweeping corners such as Scheivlak winding through the dunes, was popular with drivers but saw serious accidents, including the fatal crashes of Piers Courage (1970) and Roger Williamson (1973), which raised safety concerns.

Modifications including a chicane at Panoramabocht followed, but financial and noise problems ended the Grand Prix after 1985. Parts of the land were sold for housing, and a shorter club circuit operated for national racing. In 1998–99 the track was rebuilt to a new layout of about 4.3 km that restored much of its length on a smaller footprint.

Formula One return

Zandvoort returned to the Formula One calendar in 2021 (the 2020 return having been cancelled because of COVID-19). The renovation added steeply banked corners — the final Arie Luyendykbocht at 18 degrees and the Hugenholtzbocht — to aid overtaking. The race became one of the best-attended events on the calendar, with Verstappen winning the first three editions of the revived Grand Prix. Organizers later announced the event would conclude after 2026.
