Erik Prince

Erik Dean Prince
Born: 6 June 1969, Holland, Michigan, U.S.
Education: Hillsdale College; U.S. Naval Academy (attended)
Occupation: Businessman, investor
Known for: Founder of Blackwater

Erik Dean Prince (born June 6, 1969) is an American businessman, investor, and former U.S. Navy SEAL officer. He is best known as the founder of the private military company Blackwater, which became one of the largest private security contractors to the U.S. government during the Iraq War and was embroiled in controversy after the 2007 Nisour Square shooting in Baghdad.

Early life and military service

Prince was born in Holland, Michigan, the son of Edgar Prince, founder of the auto-parts manufacturer Prince Corporation. He attended the U.S. Naval Academy and graduated from Hillsdale College in 1992. Commissioned as a naval officer, he served as a Navy SEAL with SEAL Team 8, deploying to Haiti, the Middle East and the Balkans. After his father's sudden death in 1995, Prince left the Navy; the family sold Prince Corporation in 1996 for about $1.35 billion.

Blackwater

In 1997 Prince founded Blackwater USA, building a training facility in North Carolina's Great Dismal Swamp. After the bombing of USS Cole (2000) and especially after the September 11 attacks, Blackwater won large federal contracts to guard diplomats and facilities in Iraq and Afghanistan, growing into a company of thousands of contractors. On 16 September 2007, Blackwater guards opened fire in Baghdad's Nisour Square, killing 17 Iraqi civilians; four guards were later convicted in U.S. courts (and pardoned in 2020). The scandal brought intense scrutiny of contractor oversight. Prince resigned as CEO in March 2009 and sold the company in 2010; it was renamed Xe Services and later Academi.

Later ventures

Prince later chaired the Hong Kong–listed logistics and security firm Frontier Services Group and has been involved in resource and security ventures in Africa and elsewhere. He is a prominent donor to conservative causes; his sister, Betsy DeVos, served as U.S. Secretary of Education under President Donald Trump. Prince's meetings and proposals — including reported plans for private military operations in Afghanistan and elsewhere — have periodically drawn public and congressional attention.
