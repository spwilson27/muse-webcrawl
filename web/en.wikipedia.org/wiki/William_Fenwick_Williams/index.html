William Fenwick Williams


Born 4 August 1800, Annapolis, Nova Scotia
Died 26 July 1883 (aged 82), London, England
Allegiance British Empire
Branch British Army
Rank Lieutenant-General

Sir William Fenwick Williams, 1st Baronet, of Kars (4 August 1800 – 26 July 1883) was a Nova Scotia-born British military leader best remembered for his heroic defence of the Ottoman town of Kars during the Crimean War.

Early life

Williams was born in Annapolis Royal, Nova Scotia, the son of a commissary-general. He entered the Royal Artillery as a second lieutenant in 1825 and served in Ceylon and elsewhere before being seconded to the Ottoman Empire in the 1840s, where he helped reform the Turkish artillery and served as a commissioner for the settlement of frontiers.

Defence of Kars

During the Crimean War, Williams, holding the rank of colonel, was British commissioner attached to the Turkish army in Anatolia. In 1855 he directed the defence of Kars against a much larger Russian force under General Muravyov. Despite starvation and cholera within the garrison, Williams held the fortress for five months, repulsing a major assault on 29 September 1855 and inflicting heavy losses. The garrison finally capitulated with honourable terms on 28 November 1855, after supplies were exhausted.

Honours and later life

On his return to England Williams was created a baronet, received the thanks of Parliament, and was granted a pension. He served as Lieutenant-Governor of Nova Scotia from 1865 to 1867 and as Governor of Gibraltar from 1870 to 1876. He died in London in 1883.
