The Western African Ebola epidemic was the most widespread outbreak of Ebola virus disease in history. It began in Guinea in late 2013 and spread to neighbouring Liberia and Sierra Leone, eventually affecting several other countries and causing cases in the United States and Europe. It was declared a public health emergency of international concern by the World Health Organization in August 2014.

Course of the epidemic

The outbreak is believed to have begun in a two-year-old boy in Meliandou, a village in Guinea's Guéckédou Prefecture, in December 2013. Transmission was sustained by weak health systems, population movement across porous borders, and traditional burial practices involving contact with the body. By 2015, thousands of cases were reported each month, overwhelming treatment centres. International response teams, supported by organizations including Médecins Sans Frontières and the WHO, conducted treatment, contact tracing, and safe burials.

Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone were declared free of Ebola transmission in phases during early 2016, although sporadic flare-ups occurred. In total, more than 28,000 cases and about 11,300 deaths were reported, making the epidemic by far the largest Ebola outbreak on record; the economic and social costs to the affected countries were severe.
