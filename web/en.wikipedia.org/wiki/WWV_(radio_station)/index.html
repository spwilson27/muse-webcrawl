WWV (radio station)

WWV is a shortwave ("high frequency") time signal radio station operated by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), located near Fort Collins, Colorado. WWV continuously broadcasts time signals on several HF frequencies, with regular voice announcements of the time. First established in 1919, it is one of the oldest continuously operating radio stations in the United States and has operated continuously in its time-service role since the 1920s and 1940s.

Broadcasts

WWV transmits simultaneously on 2.5, 5, 10, 15, and 20 MHz. Each broadcast consists of standard-frequency carriers, second pulses (ticks), tones, and voice announcements of the time "at the tone" in Coordinated Universal Time, given by a male voice. The schedule also includes propagation forecasts, marine storm warnings, and other announcements. Its sister station WWVH in Hawaii uses a female voice so the two can be distinguished when both are heard.

History

The WWV call sign was assigned in 1919 to a Bureau of Standards station in Washington, D.C., which initially broadcast music and reports before concentrating on standard frequency transmissions. The station moved through sites in Maryland before relocating to Fort Collins, Colorado, in 1966, sharing the site of the low-frequency station WWVB. The famously recognizable voice announcements were automated in the 1970s; the WWV announcements were recorded by professional announcer John Doyle and later digitally synthesized.
