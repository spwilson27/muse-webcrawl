Le Cordon Bleu is an international network of hospitality and culinary schools teaching French cooking techniques and, more recently, hotel and restaurant management. Founded in Paris in 1895, it takes its name from the blue ribbon of the old French Order of the Holy Spirit, whose knights were famed for splendid feasts — "cordon bleu" became French shorthand for an accomplished cook.

History

The school grew out of the magazine La Cuisinière Cordon Bleu, founded by Marthe Distel, which began offering cooking lessons by professional chefs in 1895–96. Under later owners, notably the Brassart family from 1945 and André Cointreau from 1984, the school expanded abroad: London, Tokyo, Ottawa, Sydney, and many other cities. Its most famous alumna is Julia Child, who enrolled in Paris in 1949 and carried its techniques to an American audience; the 2009 film Julie & Julia dramatised her time there.

Programmes

The flagship curriculum pairs the Diplôme de Cuisine and Diplôme de Pâtisserie — each progressing through basic, intermediate, and superior certificates — which together form the Grand Diplôme. Many campuses add diplomas in boulangerie, wine, gastronomy, and bachelor's and master's degrees in hospitality management delivered with partner universities. Instruction follows the traditional model of chef demonstrations followed by student practical sessions.
