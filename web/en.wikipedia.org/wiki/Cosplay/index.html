Cosplay

Cosplay, a portmanteau of "costume play," is an activity and performance art in which participants, called cosplayers, wear costumes and accessories to represent a specific character, usually from anime, manga, video games, comic books, film, or television. The term was coined in Japan in 1984 by Nov Takahashi after attending the Los Angeles science fiction convention; the practice itself grew out of costuming at science fiction conventions in the United States, where attendees dressed as characters as early as the first Worldcon in 1939.

Cosplayers at a fan convention.

Practice

Cosplayers often make their own costumes, developing skills in sewing, armour and prop fabrication (commonly with EVA foam and thermoplastics such as Worbla), wig styling, and makeup; others buy or commission outfits. Characters are portrayed at conventions, photo shoots, and competitions, and increasingly shared through social media. Contest categories at major conventions reward craftsmanship and performance, and the World Cosplay Summit, held annually in Nagoya, Japan, draws national teams from dozens of countries.

Culture

Cosplay is a central feature of fan conventions worldwide, including Comic-Con in San Diego and Comiket in Tokyo. Related practices include crossplay (portraying a character of another gender) and dressing in styles such as Lolita fashion, though the latter is generally considered fashion rather than cosplay. Since the 2010s, professional cosplayers have built careers through sponsorships, print sales, and subscription platforms.
