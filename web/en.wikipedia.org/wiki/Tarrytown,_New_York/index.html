Tarrytown, New York

Tarrytown is a village in the town of Greenburgh, Westchester County, New York, United States, on the eastern bank of the Hudson River about 25 miles (40 km) north of New York City. The population was about 11,900 at the 2020 census. Together with its northern neighbour Sleepy Hollow, it forms part of the storied "Sleepy Hollow country" made famous by Washington Irving.

History

The area was inhabited by the Weckquaesgeek band of the Lenape. Dutch farmers settled in the seventeenth century; one explanation of the name derives it from the Dutch tarwe ("wheat"), giving "wheat town", though legend attributes it to wives complaining that their husbands "tarried" at the village tavern. Tarrytown was a modest farming and milling community and a stop on the Albany Post Road.

During the American Revolutionary War the area was contested "neutral ground". Nearby, in 1780, militiamen captured the British officer Major John André, exposing Benedict Arnold's treason. In 1820 Washington Irving published "The Legend of Sleepy Hollow", set in the valley just north of the village; Irving's home, Sunnyside, lies to the south. The village was incorporated in 1870.

Landmarks

Tarrytown is the western landing of the Governor Mario M. Cuomo Bridge (opened 2017–2018), successor to the 1955 Tappan Zee Bridge, which crosses the Hudson at one of its widest points to Nyack. Other landmarks include the hilltop mansion Lyndhurst, a Gothic Revival estate; the Tarrytown Music Hall (1885), one of the oldest theatres in Westchester; the Old Dutch Church of Sleepy Hollow (c. 1685), just north of the village line; and Kykuit, the Rockefeller family estate in neighbouring Pocantico Hills. The village has a Metro-North Railroad station on the Hudson Line.

Economy and education

Historically Tarrytown hosted manufacturing, including a Chevrolet assembly plant (closed 1996, in North Tarrytown/Sleepy Hollow). Today it is a residential suburb with offices, the Tarrytown House Estate conference centre, and Marymount College's former campus. It is served by the Union Free School District of the Tarrytowns.
