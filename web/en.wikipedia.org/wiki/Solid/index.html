Solid

Solid is one of the four fundamental states of matter (the others being liquid, gas and plasma). The molecules in a solid are closely packed together and contain the least amount of kinetic energy. A solid is characterised by structural rigidity and resistance to a force applied to the surface: unlike a liquid, a solid object does not flow to take on the shape of its container, nor does it expand to fill the entire available volume like a gas.

Classes

Crystalline solids have atoms arranged in a repeating lattice (e.g. metals, salts, diamond) and melt at a sharp temperature. Amorphous solids, such as glass and many polymers, lack long-range order and soften over a range of temperatures through a glass transition. Quasicrystals show ordered but non-periodic structure.

Properties

Solids exhibit elasticity, plasticity, strength, hardness and toughness, studied through concepts like Young's modulus and dislocation motion. Their electrical behaviour spans insulators, semiconductors and superconductors; thermal and magnetic properties underpin most of modern technology. Phase transitions — melting, sublimation, and structural transformations — connect the solid to other states.

Solid-state physics

The branch of condensed-matter physics dealing with solids grew rapidly after the invention of the transistor (1947), and explains properties through crystal structure, band theory and phonons. Amorphous materials produced by routes such as the sol–gel process are an active research area.
