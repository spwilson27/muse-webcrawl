Dagar vani

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dagar vani (also written Dagarbani) is one of the four principal vanis (styles or schools) of dhrupad, the oldest surviving genre of Hindustani classical music. It is the hereditary musical tradition of the Dagar family, whose members trace their lineage through centuries of court and temple musicians, and it is distinguished by an exceptionally slow, meditative and highly refined elaboration of the raga in the alap section.

Characteristics

The hallmarks of Dagar vani include:

  • A very slow and expansive alap in which the raga unfolds note by note, with emphasis on microtonal nuance (shruti) and smooth glides (meend).
  • Pronunciation of the nom-tom syllables with great care, regarded as carrying spiritual as well as musical significance.
  • A serene, contemplative aesthetic in which melodic development takes precedence over rhythmic display, though the composed portion (bandish) is sung with pakhawaj accompaniment.
  • Jugalbandi performance—two vocalists singing together, a format popularised by the senior Dagar brothers.

History

The Dagars claim descent from Swami Haridas, the legendary teacher of Tansen, through Baba Gopal Das (Behram Khan). In the twentieth century the tradition was carried by the celebrated pairs of brothers: the senior Dagar brothers, Nasir Moinuddin Dagar and Nasir Aminuddin Dagar, and the junior Dagar brothers, Zahiruddin Dagar and Faiyazuddin Dagar. Ustad Zia Mohiuddin Dagar revolutionised the rudra veena, while his brother Ustad Zia Fariduddin Dagar became a revered teacher. The brothers' concerts in Europe and America from the 1960s onwards played a major role in reviving international interest in dhrupad.

Later exponents include Ustad Wasifuddin Dagar and the Gundecha Brothers, students of the Dagar family, who founded the Dhrupad Sansthan in Bhopal to teach the style.
