DESTINY+ (Demonstration and Experiment of Space Technology for INterplanetary voYage with Phaethon fLyby and dUst Science) is a planned medium-class mission of the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) to fly by the active asteroid 3200 Phaethon, the parent body of the Geminid meteor shower, and to analyse interplanetary and interstellar dust en route.

Mission profile

DESTINY+ is designed as a technology demonstration of low-cost, high-performance deep-space exploration using solar electric propulsion — ion engines powered by large, thin solar array panels. Launched on an Epsilon S rocket, the spacecraft will spiral up from Earth orbit using its ion engines, perform lunar swing-bys, and cruise to a high-speed flyby of Phaethon, imaging its surface and measuring its dust environment. An extended mission may visit additional asteroids.

Instruments

The payload includes a telescopic camera and a multiband camera for imaging Phaethon, and the DESTINY+ Dust Analyzer (DDA), developed with German partners, which will measure the composition, mass and trajectories of interplanetary and interstellar dust grains encountered during the cruise. Scientific goals include understanding the origin and nature of the dust supplied to Earth, and the activity of Phaethon — a "rock comet" whose perihelion activity and the Geminid stream it feeds make it a key object for understanding near-Earth material.

Development

The mission builds on JAXA's experience with the Hayabusa and Hayabusa2 asteroid missions and the IKAROS solar-sail demonstrator's electric propulsion heritage. Launch, originally planned for the mid-2020s, has been subject to delays related to the Epsilon S launch vehicle programme.
