Battle of Jutland

The Battle of Jutland (German: Skagerrakschlacht) was a naval battle fought between the British Royal Navy's Grand Fleet and the Imperial German Navy's High Seas Fleet on 31 May – 1 June 1916 in the North Sea near Jutland, Denmark. It was the largest naval battle of the First World War and the only full-scale clash of battleships in that war.

Course of the battle

Admiral Scheer's plan was to lure out and destroy part of the Grand Fleet using Hipper's battlecruisers as bait. British signals intelligence warned Admiral Jellicoe, and the entire Grand Fleet was already at sea. In the "run to the south", Hipper drew Beatty's battlecruisers onto the German battleships; two British battlecruisers, Indefatigable and Queen Mary, blew up under German fire.

In the "run to the north" Beatty in turn led the Germans onto Jellicoe's main body. Jellicoe deployed his fleet across the German "T" twice, inflicting heavy damage, but Scheer executed skilful battle turns away under cover of smoke and torpedo attacks. A third British battlecruiser, Invincible, exploded during the main action. Confused night fighting followed as the German fleet slipped past the British rear and reached home.

Assessment

Germany inflicted heavier losses and claimed victory on that basis, but the High Seas Fleet never again seriously challenged British command of the North Sea, and the British blockade of Germany remained intact. Germany consequently turned to unrestricted submarine warfare. British battlecruiser losses were traced to thin armour and unsafe cordite handling practices; the battle has been debated ever since.
