The Arafura Sea (Indonesian: Laut Arafura) is a shallow sea of the western Pacific Ocean lying between the north coast of Australia and the south coast of New Guinea. It is bounded by the Gulf of Carpentaria and the Torres Strait to the east, the Timor Sea to the west, and the Banda and Ceram seas to the northwest.

Geography

The sea overlies the Arafura Shelf, part of the Sahul continental shelf; during the last Ice Age, when sea levels were far lower, the shelf formed a land bridge between Australia and New Guinea. The sea is mostly shallow (rarely deeper than 200 metres), warm, and subject to tropical cyclones. Its shores include Arnhem Land and the Cobourg Peninsula in Australia's Northern Territory and the Digul river delta region of Indonesian New Guinea.

Fisheries and ecology

The Arafura Sea supports productive fisheries for prawns, demersal fish, and pelagic species, managed cooperatively by Australia and Indonesia, though illegal fishing has been a persistent problem. Its mangrove-fringed coasts are habitat for saltwater crocodiles, dugongs, and migratory shorebirds.
