Programmer

A programmer, also called a software developer or coder, is a person who writes, tests, debugs, and maintains computer programs. Programmers work in programming languages such as Python, Java, C, JavaScript, and many others, translating algorithms and specifications into instructions that a computer can execute.

History

Ada Lovelace is often regarded as the first programmer for her nineteenth-century notes on Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine, which included an algorithm intended for machine execution. Programming became a profession with the stored-program computers of the 1940s and 1950s; early programmers such as the ENIAC's six original operators and pioneers including Grace Hopper, who developed the first compiler, established many foundations of the field.

Nature of the work

Programmers analyze requirements, design program structure, write source code, test for correctness, fix defects, and document and maintain software over its lifetime. They work across domains including operating systems, web and mobile applications, games, embedded systems, data analysis, and artificial intelligence. The occupation overlaps with software engineering, which places greater emphasis on systematic design and project processes, and many job titles use the terms interchangeably.

Employment

Programmers are employed in nearly every industry, with the largest concentrations in software publishing, finance, manufacturing, and information services. Demand for programmers has grown steadily for decades, and the work is frequently performed remotely or in freelance arrangements.
