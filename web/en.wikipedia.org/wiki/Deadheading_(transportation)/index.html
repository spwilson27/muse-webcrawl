Deadheading (transportation)

FieldTransport logistics
MeaningOperating a vehicle without revenue passengers or cargo

Deadheading is the practice of operating a commercial vehicle — such as a bus, train, aircraft or truck — while empty of fare-paying passengers or freight, typically to reposition it at the start or end of a service or to return it to a depot. The term also describes crew members travelling as passengers to position themselves for a duty.

Causes and costs

Deadheading arises from imbalances between schedules and demand: a bus must travel from its depot to the start of a route, or a truck must return from a delivery point with no backhaul load. These empty miles generate fuel, labour and maintenance costs without revenue, and operators try to minimise them through scheduling and load-matching.

In aviation

In the airline industry, "deadheading" commonly refers to pilots and cabin crew travelling on duty as passengers so they can operate a flight from another airport; such assignments are governed by union contracts and duty-time regulations. Aircraft themselves flying empty for repositioning are more often called ferry flights.
