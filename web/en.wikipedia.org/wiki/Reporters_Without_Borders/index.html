Reporters Without Borders

Reporters Without Borders (French: Reporters sans frontières; RSF) is an international non-profit and non-governmental organisation with the stated aim of safeguarding the right to freedom of information. It describes its advocacy as founded on the premise that everyone requires access to the news and information, in line with Article 19 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, which recognises the right to receive and share information regardless of frontiers, along with other international rights charters. RSF is headquartered in Paris, France.

History

Reporters Without Borders was founded in Montpellier, France, in 1985 by Robert Ménard, Rémy Loury, Jacques Molénat and Émilien Jubineau. Ménard served as the organisation's first secretary-general, succeeded by Jean-François Julliard in 2008 and Christophe Deloire in 2012. Deloire died in June 2024, and Thibaut Bruttin became secretary-general later that year.

The organisation initially worked to promote alternative journalism, but the founders disagreed on the organisation's direction, and Ménard steered it toward its current focus on press freedom and the defence of journalists. RSF holds consultative status at the United Nations, UNESCO, the Council of Europe and the International Organisation of the Francophonie.

World Press Freedom Index

RSF is best known for its annual World Press Freedom Index, first published in 2002, which ranks some 180 countries and territories according to the level of freedom available to journalists. The index is compiled from a questionnaire answered by experts and an analysis of abuses and acts of violence against journalists. Since a methodological overhaul in 2022, the index measures five indicators: political context, legal framework, economic context, sociocultural context, and safety.

Countries in northern and western Europe, such as Norway, Ireland, Denmark, Sweden and the Netherlands, regularly occupy the top positions, while countries such as North Korea, Eritrea, China, Afghanistan and Syria have ranked near the bottom.

Activities

RSF monitors attacks on journalists, publishes reports and press releases on press freedom violations, and campaigns for the release of imprisoned journalists and bloggers. It operates a network of correspondents in numerous countries and sections in several European countries and elsewhere. The organisation also provides assistance to journalists in danger, including legal aid and support for their families, and runs the Collateral Freedom project, which mirrors censored news websites to make them accessible in countries where they are blocked.

Criticism

RSF has faced criticism over its funding sources, its rankings methodology, and accusations of political bias, including scrutiny of its historical receipt of funding from bodies linked to the United States government. The organisation states that its funding is transparent and does not affect its independence.
