Lam Cho (Chinese: 林祖; 1910–2012) was a Chinese martial artist and grandmaster of the Hung Ga (Hung Kuen) style of kung fu. He was the adopted son, nephew and foremost disciple of the celebrated master Lam Sai-wing, and is regarded as one of the most influential teachers of the Lam family Hung Ga lineage in the twentieth century.

In 1930 Lam Cho established his own martial arts school and dit da (bone-setting) clinic in Hong Kong, where he taught for more than eight decades. He preserved and transmitted the Lam family curriculum, including the famous Iron Wire Set (Tit Sin Kuen), and accepted students from many later lineages of Hung Ga in Hong Kong and overseas.

Life and training

Born in Guangdong in 1910, Lam Cho began training under Lam Sai-wing as a boy. When Lam Sai-wing moved to Hong Kong, Lam Cho followed and continued his apprenticeship, also studying traditional Chinese bone-setting medicine, which practitioners of Southern kung fu commonly combined with martial instruction.

Teaching career

Lam Cho's school in Sham Shui Po / Mong Kok area of Hong Kong became a landmark of the Hung Ga community. Among his students were several teachers who later spread Lam family Hung Ga internationally. He was widely respected as a living link to the pre-war generation of Southern Chinese martial arts and continued teaching into extreme old age, dying in 2012 at the age of 102 or 103.

Legacy

Lam Cho is remembered for standardising aspects of the Lam family Hung Ga forms, for maintaining the association between Hung Ga practice and dit da medicine, and for the affection with which his students addressed him as "Uncle Cho." His lineage continues through schools in Hong Kong, Europe, and North America.
