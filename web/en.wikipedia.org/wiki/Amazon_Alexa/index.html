Amazon Alexa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Amazon Alexa, or simply Alexa, is a virtual assistant technology developed by Amazon, based largely on a speech synthesizer developed by the Polish company Ivona, acquired by Amazon in 2013. It was first used in the Amazon Echo smart speaker and is now available across a wide range of devices, including Echo speakers and displays, Fire TV, and third-party products. Alexa can perform tasks such as voice interaction, music playback, making to-do lists, setting alarms, streaming podcasts and audiobooks, and providing weather, traffic, sports, and news information. It can also control smart home devices in a home automation system.

History

Amazon announced Alexa alongside the Echo in November 2014, initially available to Amazon Prime members by invitation before a wider release in June 2015. The name was reportedly chosen partly because it contains the hard consonant "X", making it easier for the system to recognize, and as an homage to the Library of Alexandria. In 2016 Amazon opened Alexa to third-party developers through the Alexa Skills Kit and made the voice service available to other device makers. At its peak the platform supported more than 100,000 "skills".

Features

  • Voice profiles and routines – recognition of individual users and chained automations.
  • Skills – third-party apps enabled by voice, spanning games, news "flash briefings", banking, and ordering services.
  • Smart home – control of lights, locks, thermostats, and cameras from compatible vendors.
  • Calling and messaging – voice calls, Drop In intercom features, and announcements between Echo devices.
  • Shopping – voice ordering and reordering from Amazon.

Most devices use a wake word—"Alexa" by default, with alternatives including "Echo", "Amazon", "Computer", and "Ziggy"—after which speech is streamed to Amazon's cloud for recognition and processing.

Alexa+

In February 2025 Amazon announced Alexa+, a substantially upgraded version powered by generative artificial intelligence, including large language models from Amazon's Nova family and Anthropic's Claude, offering more conversational interaction, personalization, and agentic task completion such as booking reservations and ordering deliveries. Alexa+ is free for Prime members and priced as a standalone subscription for others, with the original service continuing as "Alexa Classic".

Privacy concerns

Alexa's always-listening design has raised privacy concerns. Amazon employees and contractors were reported in 2019 to review a sample of recordings to improve recognition. Devices have occasionally recorded and transmitted private conversations in error, and recordings have been sought as evidence in criminal cases. Amazon provides controls for reviewing and deleting recordings, and Echo devices include a microphone mute button.
