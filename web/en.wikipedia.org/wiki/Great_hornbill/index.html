Scientific nameBuceros bicornis
FamilyBucerotidae (hornbills)
RangeSouth and Southeast Asia

The great hornbill (Buceros bicornis), also known as the concave-casqued hornbill or great Indian hornbill, is one of the larger members of the hornbill family. It is found in the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia. Reaching 95–130 cm in length with a wingspan up to 152 cm, it is famous for the huge yellow-and-black casque on top of its massive bill.

Behaviour

Great hornbills are mainly frugivorous, feeding especially on figs, though they also take small animals. During breeding, the female seals herself inside a cavity in a large tree trunk, leaving only a narrow slit through which the male passes food; she remains sealed in for several weeks while incubating the eggs and moulting her flight feathers.

Cultural significance

The species is the state bird of Kerala and Arunachal Pradesh in India. Its feathers and casques have traditionally been used in the ceremonial dress of some tribal peoples, and it is regarded as a symbol of status in parts of its range.

Conservation

The great hornbill is listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List owing to habitat loss from logging of large forest trees and hunting pressure.
