Věra Čáslavská

Věra Čáslavská (3 May 1942 – 30 August 2016) was a Czechoslovak artistic gymnast and Czech sports official. Individually she won a total of eleven Olympic medals between 1960 and 1968, including seven golds, and is one of the most successful gymnasts of all time. She remains the only gymnast, male or female, to have won Olympic gold on every individual apparatus event.

Career

Čáslavská won team silver at the 1960 Rome Olympics, then dominated the 1964 Tokyo Games with golds in the all-around, vault, and balance beam. At the 1966 World Championships she led Czechoslovakia to the team title over the Soviet Union.

Mexico City 1968 and protest

Months after signing the "Two Thousand Words" manifesto and seeing Soviet-led forces crush the Prague Spring, Čáslavská won four gold medals at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics, including the all-around title, tying Larisa Latynina's record of golds. In a silent protest against the invasion, she turned her head down and away during the Soviet anthem at two medal ceremonies — an act that made her a heroine at home but led to her being ostracized by the normalization regime, barred from sport and foreign travel for years.

Later life

After the Velvet Revolution of 1989 she was rehabilitated, serving as an adviser to President Václav Havel and as president of the Czech Olympic Committee, and as a member of the International Olympic Committee (1995–2001). She died of pancreatic cancer in Prague in 2016.
