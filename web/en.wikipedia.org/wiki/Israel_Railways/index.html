Israel Railways Ltd. is the state-owned railway company of Israel, responsible for inter-city and suburban passenger rail and for freight services across the country. Its network of around 1,300 km of standard-gauge track links the coastal cities with Jerusalem, Beersheba, the Galilee and the Negev.

History

Railways in the region began under Ottoman rule with the Jaffa–Jerusalem line (1892) and the Hejaz Railway's branch to Haifa (1905, the "Valley Train"). Under the British Mandate, Palestine Railways operated lines to Egypt, Lebanon and Syria; after 1948 Israel's railways were cut off from its neighbours. For decades the network was small and underinvested, but from the 1990s a major expansion added suburban services around Tel Aviv and Haifa, a rebuilt line to Beersheba, the Ayalon corridor through Tel Aviv, and new lines to Ashdod, Ashkelon, Karmiel and the Jordan Valley (Bet She'an).

Modernisation

The electrified fast line between Tel Aviv and Jerusalem (via Ben Gurion Airport and the A1 link) opened in stages from 2018, cutting journey times to about half an hour. A system-wide electrification programme is converting main lines from diesel to 25 kV AC operation, with Siemens Desiro HC electric multiple units entering service in the 2020s. Annual ridership recovered to roughly 65–70 million passenger journeys a year in the years before the COVID-19 pandemic.
