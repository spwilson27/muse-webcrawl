Melt blowing

Melt blowing is a manufacturing process in which a molten thermoplastic polymer is extruded through a die and immediately drawn into very fine fibres by converging streams of hot, high-velocity air. The fibres, typically 1–5 micrometres in diameter, are collected on a moving belt as a random nonwoven web. Melt-blown nonwovens are best known as the filtration layer in medical face masks and respirators.

Melt blowing

Melt-blown web forming

Material: polypropylene (typical)
Fibre diameter: ~1–5 µm
Main uses: mask filtration, sorbents, insulation

Process

Polymer pellets are melted in an extruder and pumped through a die nose containing hundreds of tiny capillaries. Hot air jets flanking the die stretch the emerging streams, attenuating them to microfibres that cool and land on a collector screen, bonding to one another while still tacky. The resulting web is soft, drapable and an excellent filter because of its fine fibre size and high surface area. When an electrostatic charge is applied (electret treatment), filtration efficiency rises dramatically without increasing breathing resistance.

Applications

Melt-blown polypropylene is the critical filter medium in N95 respirators and surgical masks; demand surged during the COVID-19 pandemic, when melt-blown fabric was a global bottleneck. Other uses include oil-absorbent pads and booms, battery separators, thermal and acoustic insulation, and filtration for air-conditioning and industrial processes.
