Baisakhi (Vaisakhi)

Observed bySikhs and Hindus, chiefly in Punjab
TypeReligious, harvest festival, solar new year
DateUsually 13 or 14 April (first of Vaisakh)
SignificanceFounding of the Khalsa (1699); spring harvest; solar new year in several regional calendars

Baisakhi, also spelled Vaisakhi, is a major spring festival celebrated primarily in the Punjab region of India and Pakistan and by Sikh and Hindu communities worldwide. Falling on the first day of the month of Vaisakh — usually 13 or 14 April — it marks the start of the harvest season and, in several regional solar calendars, the new year. For Sikhs it is one of the most important dates in the religious calendar, commemorating the founding of the Khalsa in 1699.

Sikh significance

On Baisakhi 1699, the tenth Guru, Guru Gobind Singh, gathered Sikhs at Anandpur Sahib and founded the Khalsa, the community of initiated Sikhs. According to tradition, he called for volunteers willing to give their lives; five men — the Panj Pyare (Five Beloved Ones) — came forward and were initiated with amrit (sweetened water stirred with a double-edged sword) in the first Amrit Sanchar ceremony, after which the Guru himself received initiation from them. The Khalsa adopted the Five Ks and the names Singh and Kaur.

Celebrations

Gurdwaras hold special services, kirtan and readings; communities stage colourful nagar kirtan processions led by the Panj Pyare and the Guru Granth Sahib, with gatka martial-arts displays, music and free food distributed to all. Amrit ceremonies are commonly held on the day. The festival also retains its harvest character: farmers celebrate the rabi crop, folk dances such as bhangra and gidda are performed, and fairs (melas) are held across Punjab. Baisakhi is observed elsewhere in India under related names — including Pohela Boishakh in Bengal, Puthandu in Tamil Nadu, Vishu in Kerala and Bihu in Assam — as a solar new year festival. In 1919, Baisakhi day in Amritsar was marked by the Jallianwala Bagh massacre, in which British troops fired on an unarmed crowd.
