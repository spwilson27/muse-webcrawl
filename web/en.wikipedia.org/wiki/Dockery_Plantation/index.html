Dockery Plantation is a historic cotton plantation and former company town in the Mississippi Delta, located on Mississippi Highway 8 near Cleveland, Mississippi. Founded in 1895 by Will Dockery, it grew to cover some 28,000 acres (about 11,000 hectares) and employed thousands of workers. It is widely regarded as the birthplace of Delta blues music.

Blues heritage

During the early twentieth century, Dockery was home to influential blues musicians including Charley Patton, often called the "Father of the Delta Blues," who lived and worked there for decades and taught younger players. Musicians associated with the plantation include Robert Johnson, Son House, Howlin' Wolf, Willie Brown and Pops Staples. Drawn by its relatively fair treatment of workers and its social life centered on the plantation's commissary and Saturday dances, itinerant musicians gathered there, developing the Delta blues style.

Later history

Mechanization reduced the need for labor after the 1940s, and the plantation declined as a company town. The property remains in the Dockery family, and its surviving buildings — including the cotton gin, commissary, seed house and a service station bearing the plantation's name — are preserved as a historic site and a stop on the Mississippi Blues Trail.
