Federal Chancellery (Austria)

The Federal Chancellery (German: Bundeskanzleramt, BKA) is the ministry and official office of the Chancellor of Austria, located on Ballhausplatz in Vienna, adjacent to the Hofburg Palace. It coordinates the work of the Austrian federal government and handles matters including European Union policy, public administration and equal treatment.

The building

The Baroque building on Ballhausplatz was constructed in 1717–1719 to designs by Johann Lucas von Hildebrandt as the Privy Court Chancellery. It became the seat of Austria's foreign ministry and the workplace of statesmen including Prince Metternich, whose diplomacy at the Congress of Vienna made "Ballhausplatz" a byword for Austrian foreign policy. Since 1918 it has housed the Federal Chancellery of the Republic of Austria. The square outside was the scene of major political events, including celebrations of the Anschluss in 1938 and later demonstrations.

The ministry

As a federal ministry, the Chancellery supports the Chancellor in coordinating government business, prepares cabinet meetings, and is responsible for areas such as EU coordination, the civil service, media policy, women's affairs and minorities. Its head is the Chancellor, assisted by chancellery ministers and state secretaries as governments choose to appoint them.
