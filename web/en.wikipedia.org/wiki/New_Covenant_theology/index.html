New Covenant theology

New Covenant theology (or NCT) is a Christian theological position teaching that the person and work of Jesus Christ is the central focus of the Bible and that the New Covenant in Christ fulfills and supersedes the Old (Mosaic) Covenant. It is often described as a middle way between dispensationalism and covenant theology, drawing elements from both while remaining distinct from each.

Beliefs

The Law of Christ

New Covenant theology holds that the entire Mosaic Law, including the Ten Commandments as a covenant document, has been fulfilled and abrogated in Christ. Believers are instead under the "law of Christ" (Galatians 6:2) — the teaching and example of Jesus and his apostles in the New Testament. Moral principles repeated in the New Testament remain binding, but not because they belong to the Mosaic code.

The New Covenant and Israel

NCT teaches that the New Covenant promised in Jeremiah 31 is made with the church, understood as the continuation and fulfillment of the people of God, rather than with ethnic Israel as a separate entity. The church is not viewed as an afterthought or a "parenthesis" in God's plan, in contrast to dispensational teaching.

Eschatology

Most New Covenant theologians hold an amillennial or postmillennial view of the end times, though eschatology is not considered a defining feature of the system.

History

New Covenant theology emerged in the late 20th century, partly out of discussions within Reformed Baptist circles. Teachers associated with its development include Jon Zens, John G. Reisinger, and Fred Zaspel. Reisinger's book Abraham's Four Seeds (1998) and Zens's writings in Baptist Reformation Review and Sword and Trowel were influential in articulating the position. The movement has been especially significant among some Baptist and independent churches dissatisfied with both covenant theology's treatment of the Sabbath and infant baptism, and dispensationalism's separation of Israel and the church.

Comparison with other systems

  • Covenant theology sees one covenant of grace administered differently across history and retains the Ten Commandments as an abiding moral law; NCT sees the Mosaic covenant as a unit that has expired.
  • Dispensationalism distinguishes sharply between Israel and the church; NCT sees the church as the fulfillment of Old Testament promises.
  • Progressive covenantalism is a related, more recent system sharing many NCT emphases.
