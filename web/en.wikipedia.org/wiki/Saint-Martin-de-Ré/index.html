Saint-Martin-de-Ré

Saint-Martin-de-Ré is a commune on the Île de Ré, in the Charente-Maritime department of western France. It is the island's principal port and largest town, with about 2,300 permanent residents, and is renowned for its star-shaped Vauban fortifications, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2008 as part of the Fortifications of Vauban.

History

The harbour town was fortified repeatedly because of its strategic position off La Rochelle. In 1627 it withstood a siege by English forces under the Duke of Buckingham, who landed on the island in support of the Huguenots of La Rochelle but failed to take Saint-Martin's citadel and withdrew with heavy losses. Under Louis XIV, Vauban rebuilt the defences as a complete star fort with a citadel, enclosing the town in ramparts that survive largely intact. From the 19th century the citadel housed a prison — convicts were embarked here for the penal colonies of French Guiana and New Caledonia, among them Alfred Dreyfus (held briefly before transfer) and Henri Charrière, "Papillon". A central prison still operates within the citadel.

Today

Saint-Martin is now a fashionable holiday town: its quays are lined with restaurants and hotels, and it serves as the island's hub for cycling, oyster culture and salt production. Its population swells many-fold in summer.
