Chang'e program

The Yutu-2 rover photographed on the far side of the Moon by the Chang'e 4 lander.

The Chang'e program (Chinese: 嫦娥工程), also known as the Chinese Lunar Exploration Program (CLEP), is a series of robotic missions to the Moon conducted by the China National Space Administration (CNSA). The program is named after Chang'e, the Chinese goddess of the Moon. Since its first mission in 2007 it has progressed through three phases: orbiting, landing and roving, and sample return, with a crewed lunar landing planned as a longer-term goal.

Missions

Chang'e program
Country: China
Organization: CNSA
First launch: Chang'e 1, 24 October 2007
Key firsts: first far-side landing (2019); first far-side sample return (2024)
Rovers: Yutu, Yutu-2

Chang'e 1 (2007) and Chang'e 2 (2010) were lunar orbiters that mapped the Moon's surface. Chang'e 3 landed in Mare Imbrium in December 2013 and deployed the Yutu rover, China's first soft landing on another world. Chang'e 4, launched in December 2018, made the first-ever soft landing on the far side of the Moon, in Von Kármán crater, on 3 January 2019, supported by the Queqiao relay satellite at the Earth–Moon L2 point; its rover, Yutu-2, became the longest-lived lunar rover. Chang'e 5 (2020) returned about 1.7 kg of samples from Oceanus Procellarum — the first lunar sample return since the Soviet Luna 24 in 1976. Chang'e 6 (2024) returned the first samples from the far side, collected in the Apollo basin of the South Pole–Aitken basin.

Future

Follow-on missions Chang'e 7 and Chang'e 8 are planned to explore the lunar south pole and test in-situ resource utilization, as precursors to the International Lunar Research Station that China plans with partners. China has announced its intention to land taikonauts on the Moon before 2030, using the Mengzhou crewed spacecraft and Lanyue lander then in development.
