Quality improvement

Quality improvement (QI) is a systematic, formal approach to the analysis of practice performance and efforts to improve it. The term is used most prominently in healthcare, where QI denotes the combined efforts of clinicians, patients, researchers, planners and educators to make changes leading to better patient outcomes, better system performance and better professional development.

Run charts and statistical process control help distinguish real improvement from noise.

Methods

The most widely used framework is the Model for Improvement, developed by Associates in Process Improvement and popularised by the Institute for Healthcare Improvement. It asks three questions — What are we trying to accomplish? How will we know that a change is an improvement? What change can we make that will result in improvement? — and tests changes through rapid plan–do–study–act (PDSA) cycles. Other established approaches include Lean, Six Sigma, total quality management and continuous quality improvement (CQI).

In healthcare

Quality improvement in healthcare addresses safety, effectiveness, patient-centredness, timeliness, efficiency and equity — the six aims identified by the Institute of Medicine's 2001 report Crossing the Quality Chasm. Activities range from hand-hygiene campaigns and surgical checklists to redesigning appointment systems. Unlike research, QI usually applies existing knowledge locally and is judged by whether measured performance changes over time, often using run charts and control charts rather than randomised trials.

Distinctions

Quality improvement differs from quality assurance, which sets and monitors compliance with standards, and from quality control, which inspects output. QI emphasises iterative learning, front-line engagement and measurement for improvement.
