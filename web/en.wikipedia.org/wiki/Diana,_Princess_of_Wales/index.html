Diana, Princess of Wales (born Diana Frances Spencer; 1 July 1961 – 31 August 1997), was a member of the British royal family. She was the first wife of Charles III (then Prince of Wales) and mother of Princes William and Harry. Her activism and glamour made her an international icon, and earned her enduring popularity.

Early life and marriage

Diana was born into the Spencer family, a family of British nobility, and grew up close to the royal family on their Sandringham estate. In 1981, while working as a nursery school assistant, she became engaged to Prince Charles, the eldest son of Queen Elizabeth II. Their wedding, held at St Paul's Cathedral on 29 July 1981, reached a global television audience of over 750 million people. As Princess of Wales, she undertook royal duties on behalf of the Queen and represented her at functions across the Commonwealth realms.

Charity work and public life

Diana initially gained recognition for her shy demeanour, but her charisma and friendliness captivated the public. She became prominent for her unprecedented public empathy with people living with HIV/AIDS — in 1987 she was photographed shaking hands with an AIDS patient without gloves, challenging the belief that the condition spread by touch. In the last year of her life she campaigned internationally for a ban on landmines, famously walking through a cleared Angolan minefield. She also supported charities for the homeless, young people, drug addicts, and the elderly, and was president or patron of over 100 charities at the height of her work.

Separation, divorce, and death

The marriage broke down amid incompatibility and extramarital affairs; the couple separated in 1992 and divorced in 1996. Diana died in a car crash in the Pont de l'Alma tunnel in Paris on 31 August 1997. Her public funeral at Westminster Abbey drew an estimated 2.5 billion television viewers, and the unprecedented public outpouring of grief — including the sea of flowers outside Kensington Palace — became a defining cultural moment in modern Britain.
