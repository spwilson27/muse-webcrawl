Bergen-Belsen displaced persons camp

The DP camp occupied the former Wehrmacht barracks at Hohne, a few kilometres from the concentration camp.

The Bergen-Belsen displaced persons camp was a camp for displaced persons (DPs) established by the British authorities in 1945 in a former German military barracks complex near the liberated Bergen-Belsen concentration camp in Lower Saxony. It became the largest Jewish DP camp in the British zone of occupied Germany and a centre of Jewish political and cultural life after the Holocaust, operating until 1950.

Establishment

When British forces liberated the Bergen-Belsen concentration camp on 15 April 1945, they found some 60,000 starving and diseased prisoners. The concentration camp itself, a focus of typhus, was burned to the ground in May 1945, while survivors were moved for medical care and housing into the nearby Panzer Training School barracks at Hohne — the nucleus of the DP camp. At its peak the camp held more than 11,000 people; unusually, many residents refused repatriation and remained for years awaiting emigration.

Jewish communal life

Bergen-Belsen DP camp became the capital, in effect, of the She'erit Hapletah ("the surviving remnant") in the British zone. In September 1945 its residents organised the first congress of Jewish survivors in Germany and elected the Central Committee of Liberated Jews in the British Zone, led by Josef Rosensaft. The camp sustained Yiddish newspapers, schools, a theatre, religious institutions, kibbutzim training young people for Palestine, and an active Zionist political life — along with a remarkable baby boom, with hundreds of weddings and births as survivors rebuilt families.

Emigration and closure

Residents campaigned against British restrictions on Jewish immigration to Mandatory Palestine; the camp was closely associated with the Exodus affair of 1947, when passengers of the ship Exodus 1947 were forcibly returned to Germany. After the establishment of Israel in 1948 and changes in American immigration law, most residents emigrated to Israel and the United States. The camp closed in August 1950, the last of the major Jewish DP camps in Germany to do so.
