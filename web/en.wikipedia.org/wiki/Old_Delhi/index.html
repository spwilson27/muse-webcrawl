Old Delhi

Old Delhi is the historic walled city of Delhi, India, founded in 1639 by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan under the name Shahjahanabad. It served as the capital of the Mughal Empire from 1648 until the empire's fall in 1857, and remains one of the most densely populated and commercially vibrant historic quarters of modern Delhi, adjoining the newer districts of New Delhi to the south.

History

Shah Jahan laid out the city on the western bank of the Yamuna River, enclosing it within walls pierced by gates such as the Delhi Gate, Kashmiri Gate, and Ajmeri Gate. At its eastern edge he built the Red Fort (Lal Qila), the imperial palace complex, and opposite the fort's Lahore Gate ran Chandni Chowk, the great market street, with a canal down its centre designed by Ali Mardan Khan. The Jama Masjid, one of India's largest mosques, was completed in 1656 on a hillock overlooking the city. After the Indian Rebellion of 1857, the British demolished large areas near the fort and altered the city's fabric, but Shahjahanabad continued as a commercial and cultural centre.

Character and landmarks

Old Delhi is renowned for its maze of narrow lanes (gali), historic havelis, temples, mosques, and the Sikh Gurdwara Sis Ganj Sahib. Its bazaars — including Khari Baoli, Asia's largest wholesale spice market, and the jewellery and wedding markets of Dariba Kalan and Kinari Bazaar — draw traders from across India. The area is equally famous for its Mughlai cuisine, from kebabs and biryanis to jalebis and parathas sold in centuries-old shops. Urdu literary culture flourished here, home to poets such as Mir Taqi Mir and Mirza Ghalib, whose haveli is preserved as a museum.

Present day

Today Old Delhi falls largely within the Shahdara and Central Delhi administrative districts and is served by several Delhi Metro stations, including Chawri Bazar and Chandni Chowk. Redevelopment of the Chandni Chowk precinct in the early 2020s pedestrianised the main street, while conservationists continue to debate how to protect the quarter's heritage amid intense commercial pressure.
