Opacity (optics)

Property ofMaterials

Opacity is the measure of impenetrability to electromagnetic radiation, especially visible light. An opaque material transmits no light through it in the ordinary sense: light incident on it is absorbed, scattered, or reflected at its surface. Common examples of opaque materials are metals, wood, and stone.

Description

Whether a material is opaque depends on the wavelength of the radiation and the thickness of the sample. X-rays pass through flesh but not bone; a sheet of glass transparent to visible light is opaque to some ultraviolet wavelengths. Opacity is quantified through the attenuation (extinction) coefficient in the Beer–Lambert law, which relates the transmitted intensity to path length and concentration of absorbing species.

Applications

The concept is central to radiography and medical imaging (radiopacity of contrast agents), astrophysics (opacity of stellar interiors, governing radiative energy transport), and remote sensing, where the opacity of the atmosphere determines observing windows.
