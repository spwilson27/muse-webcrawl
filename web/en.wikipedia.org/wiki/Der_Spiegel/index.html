Der Spiegel is a German weekly news magazine published in Hamburg, founded in 1947, and continental Europe's best-known newsweekly — 'the assault gun of democracy' in its founder Rudolf Augstein's phrase. Its investigative tradition produced the 1962 Spiegel affair, a defining press-freedom battle in post-war Germany.

Character

Spiegel's long, novelistic features are written by staff writers in a distinctive style. Its English site, Spiegel International, translates selected pieces; the magazine and its digital arm (spiegel.de) form one of Germany's most influential news brands, majority-owned by a staff trust arrangement since Augstein's era.
