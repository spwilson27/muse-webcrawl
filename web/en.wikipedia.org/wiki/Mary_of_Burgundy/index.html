Mary of Burgundy (French: Marie de Bourgogne; Dutch: Maria van Bourgondië; 13 February 1457 – 27 March 1482), nicknamed the Rich, was a member of the House of Valois-Burgundy who ruled a collection of states that included the Duchy of Burgundy, the Free County of Burgundy, and the majority of the Low Countries. She was the only child of Charles the Bold and his wife Isabella of Bourbon, and inherited the Burgundian lands upon her father's death in the Battle of Nancy on 5 January 1477.

Succession crisis

On becoming Duchess at nineteen, Mary faced the invasion of Louis XI of France, who seized the Duchy of Burgundy proper, and a revolt of her Netherlandish subjects. To secure their support she signed the Great Privilege in February 1477, restoring the liberties her father and grandfather had curtailed. Her two leading counsellors were executed in Ghent despite her pleas.

Marriage

Among many suitors, Mary chose Archduke Maximilian of Austria, the future Holy Roman Emperor Maximilian I. They married at Ghent on 19 August 1477. The marriage preserved most of the Burgundian Netherlands for her heirs and founded the long Habsburg presence in the Low Countries.

Death and legacy

In March 1482 Mary was thrown from her horse while hawking near Wijnendale Castle and died of her injuries on 27 March, aged 25. She was buried in the Church of Our Lady in Bruges beside her father. Her son Philip the Handsome succeeded to the Netherlands, and through him the Burgundian inheritance passed eventually to Charles V and the Spanish Habsburgs.
