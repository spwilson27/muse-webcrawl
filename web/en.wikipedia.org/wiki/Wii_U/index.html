The Wii U is a home video game console developed by Nintendo as the successor to the Wii. Released in late 2012, it was the first eighth-generation console and the first Nintendo console to support high-definition graphics. Its defining feature is the Wii U GamePad, a controller with an embedded 6.2-inch touchscreen that can display supplementary information or the main game itself (Off-TV Play), enabling asymmetric multiplayer gameplay.

History and reception

The Wii U was unveiled at E3 2011 under the codename "Project Café". Despite a strong launch line-up including New Super Mario Bros. U and third-party ports, sales were poor: consumers were confused by the branding (many thought the GamePad was a Wii accessory), third-party support faded, and the console was outsold by the competing PlayStation 4 and Xbox One. Lifetime sales of 13.56 million units made it Nintendo's least successful home console, though it built a well-regarded library including Mario Kart 8, Super Smash Bros. for Wii U, Splatoon, Super Mario Maker, and The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild, its final first-party release.

Services

The console introduced the Nintendo Network ID system and the Miiverse social network (closed in 2017), as well as the Nintendo eShop and Virtual Console emulation of earlier systems. Production ended in January 2017, shortly before the launch of its successor, the Nintendo Switch, and many of its games were later ported to that platform.
