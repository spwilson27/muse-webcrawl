Identity parade

An identity parade, commonly known as a police lineup in the United States, is a process by which a crime victim or witness's putative identification of a suspect is confirmed to a level that can count as evidence at trial. The suspect, along with several other "fillers" of similar appearance, stands in a line; the witness is then asked whether any of those present resemble the perpetrator of the crime.

Procedure

Fillers are matched to the witness's description of the perpetrator and, increasingly, to the suspect's appearance, so that the suspect does not stand out. In modern British practice, identity parades are often replaced by video identification procedures, in which the witness views recorded moving images of the suspect among at least eight others. Guidelines such as the Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 (PACE) Code D in England and Wales regulate how procedures must be conducted.

Reliability

Eyewitness misidentification is a leading cause of wrongful convictions later overturned by DNA evidence. Reforms advocated "double-blind" administration — the officer conducting the parade does not know who the suspect is — and sequential presentation, in which members are viewed one at a time rather than simultaneously.
