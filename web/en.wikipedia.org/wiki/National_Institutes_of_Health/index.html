National Institutes of Health

The National Institutes of Health (NIH) is the primary agency of the United States government responsible for biomedical and public health research. Based in Bethesda, Maryland, it is part of the Department of Health and Human Services and consists of 27 separate institutes and centres. The NIH is the world's largest single funder of biomedical research, with an annual budget of roughly $45 billion in the 2020s, most of which supports research grants to universities and medical schools through its extramural programme, while about ten per cent funds research conducted in its own laboratories (the intramural programme).

History

The NIH traces its origins to the Hygienic Laboratory, established in 1887 at the Marine Hospital on Staten Island, New York, to investigate infectious diseases such as cholera and yellow fever. The laboratory moved to Washington, D.C., and in 1930 the Ransdell Act renamed it the National Institute of Health and authorised fellowships and expanded research. In 1937 the National Cancer Institute was created as the first of the disease-specific institutes, and in 1948 the organisation became the National Institutes of Health (plural) as additional institutes were added. The campus at Bethesda grew around land donated by Luke and Helen Wilson. After the Second World War, NIH funding expanded dramatically under directors such as James Shannon, making the agency the engine of the postwar biomedical research enterprise. The NIH Clinical Center, opened in 1953, is the largest hospital in the world devoted entirely to clinical research.

Organisation

The institutes include the National Cancer Institute (NCI), the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID), the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI), the National Institute of General Medical Sciences (NIGMS), the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS), and the National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI), among others. Centres include the National Center for Biotechnology Information, which operates resources such as PubMed and GenBank, and the Clinical Center.

Research and funding

Roughly 80 per cent of the NIH budget is awarded competitively through peer-reviewed grants — principally the R01 investigator-initiated research grant — to more than 300,000 researchers at universities, medical schools and other institutions. NIH-supported research has contributed to numerous landmark advances, from the development of vaccines and antiretroviral therapies to the Human Genome Project, which was led in part by NHGRI and whose director, Francis Collins, later served as NIH director from 2009 to 2021. The agency also played a central role in the rapid development of COVID-19 vaccines.
