Cope

The cope (from Latin cappa, "cape") is a liturgical vestment: a long, semicircular cloak or mantle open at the front, worn by clergy and lay ministers in the Roman Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran, and some other Western churches. It is typically fastened at the chest with a clasp called a morse, and often has an ornamental hood or shield at the back.

Use

The cope is worn for processions, Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament, solemn Vespers and Lauds, weddings, funerals, and other ceremonies outside the celebration of Mass — or, in the case of bishops and other prelates, on formal occasions generally. Because it is not an Eucharistic vestment proper to the celebrant, it may also be worn by cantors, acolytes, and other ministers. Richly embroidered copes, often made of silk with gold thread ("cloth of gold"), are among the most elaborate surviving works of medieval textile art; the cope is frequently paired with a matching stole and a veil (the humeral veil) when used at Benediction.

History

Derived from the late antique hooded cloak, the cope developed in the early Middle Ages from a practical rain cape into a ceremonial garment. By the high Middle Ages its hood had become a decorative shield-shaped panel on the back.
