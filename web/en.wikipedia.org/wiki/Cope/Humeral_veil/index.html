Humeral veil

The humeral veil is one of the liturgical vestments of the Roman Rite, used also in some Anglican and Lutheran churches. It consists of a cloth about 2.75 metres (9 feet) long and 90 cm wide draped over the shoulders (Latin humerus) and down the front, normally of silk or cloth of gold. At the ends there are sometimes pockets in the back for hands to go into so that the wearer can hold items without touching them with bare hands.

Use

The humeral veil is used at Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament, when the priest or deacon blesses the congregation with the monstrance while holding it through the veil, and in processions of the Blessed Sacrament such as those on Corpus Christi. In the traditional Roman Rite, the subdeacon also wore the humeral veil at Solemn Mass to hold the paten during part of the Canon. The veil is usually white or gold, though it may match the liturgical colour of the day for the subdeacon's use.

Related items

The vimpa, a simpler veil or shoulder cloth sometimes worn by altar servers holding a bishop's mitre and crosier, is related in form. The stole, worn beneath the veil, distinguishes the ordained ministers who use it.
