March Madness is the popular name for the NCAA Division I men's and women's basketball tournaments held each spring in the United States. The single-elimination tournaments feature 68 teams each and are among the most watched sporting events in the country. The phrase is closely associated with the excitement of upsets, "Cinderella" runs by low-seeded teams, and the widespread practice of filling out prediction brackets.

History

The men's tournament was first held in 1939, organised by the National Association of Basketball Coaches; the NCAA took over the following year. The expression "March Madness" was first applied to a basketball tournament by Illinois high-school official Henry V. Porter in 1939 and became attached to the NCAA tournament through broadcaster Brent Musburger's use of it in the 1980s. The women's NCAA Division I tournament began in 1982.

Format

Thirty-two conference champions receive automatic bids; the remaining at-large selections are announced on "Selection Sunday." After the First Four play-in round, the field of 64 plays through the Sweet Sixteen, Elite Eight, and Final Four rounds, culminating in the national championship game. Teams are seeded 1–16 in four regions, and early-round upsets by double-digit seeds are a hallmark of the event.

Cultural impact

Tens of millions of Americans complete brackets predicting the results, and no perfect bracket has ever been verified through the first weekend of play. Television rights are worth billions of dollars; the tournament's theme, "One Shining Moment," closes the championship broadcast.
