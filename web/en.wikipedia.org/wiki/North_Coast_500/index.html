Overview

The North Coast 500 (NC500) is a scenic touring route of about 516 miles (830 km) around the north coast of Scotland, starting and finishing at Inverness Castle in Inverness. Launched in 2015 by the North Highland Initiative, it links many features of the northern Highlands and has been marketed as Scotland's answer to Route 66.

Route

The route travels west from Inverness to Applecross, then north along the coast through Torridon, Ullapool, Durness, and Tongue to John o' Groats at the northeastern tip of mainland Britain, before returning south along the east coast through Wick and Dingwall. Highlights include the Bealach na Bà mountain pass, white-sand beaches, sea cliffs, castles, and whisky distilleries.

Impact

The NC500 substantially boosted tourism in the Highlands, bringing new business to hotels and restaurants, but it also raised local concerns about traffic on narrow single-track roads, litter, and pressure on small communities. Visiting drivers are encouraged to use passing places correctly and to travel responsibly.
