Peritectic

A peritectic transformation (or peritectic reaction) is an isothermal reaction in a two-component system in which, on cooling, a liquid and a solid phase react together to form a new, single solid phase: Liquid + Solid1 → Solid2. The temperature and composition at which this occurs are the peritectic point and peritectic composition.

Description

Peritectic reactions are important in alloy systems, most famously the iron–carbon system: at around 1495 °C, liquid iron reacts with delta ferrite to form austenite, a step central to the solidification of steels. Because the new solid forms at the interface between liquid and the primary solid, it can coat the primary phase and slow further reaction, so peritectic solidification often remains incomplete under real casting conditions, producing cored, non-equilibrium microstructures.

Related reactions

The peritectic reaction is one of several invariant reactions in phase diagrams, alongside the eutectic (liquid → two solids), eutectoid (solid → two solids), monotectic and peritectoid (two solids → one solid) reactions.
