Acquis communautaire

The acquis communautaire (French for "community patrimony"), often shortened to the acquis, is the accumulated body of European Union law, comprising the treaties, legislation, declarations, resolutions, international agreements and the case law of the Court of Justice of the European Union.

Enlargement

Countries applying to join the EU must accept the acquis in full and transpose it into their national legal systems. Accession negotiations are organised into chapters corresponding to areas of the acquis — such as free movement of goods, competition policy and the environment — and each chapter must be provisionally closed before membership. New member states may negotiate transitional periods but not permanent exemptions.

Scope

The acquis runs to tens of thousands of pages in the Official Journal. The principle that it must be preserved as a whole reflects the idea that the achievements of European integration are not open to renegotiation by individual states.
