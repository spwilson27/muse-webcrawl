Richard of York, 3rd Duke of York

Richard of York, 3rd Duke of York (21 September 1411 – 30 December 1460), also named Richard Plantagenet, was an English magnate, a great-grandson of Edward III through his father, and a descendant of Edward III's second surviving son through his mother. His claim to the throne precipitated the Wars of the Roses.

Early life

Richard inherited the Earldom of Cambridge after his father was executed for the Southampton Plot against Henry V, and the Dukedom of York after his uncle died at Agincourt. He also inherited the vast Mortimer estates and the Earldom of March through his mother Anne Mortimer.

France and protectorate

He served as Lieutenant of France and later of Ireland, building a reputation for competent government in contrast to Henry VI's court. During Henry VI's mental illness, York served as Lord Protector in 1454–55 and again after the First Battle of St Albans (1455).

Claim and death

By the Act of Accord (1460), York was recognised as Henry VI's heir, disinheriting the Prince of Wales. Weeks later he was killed at the Battle of Wakefield, fighting Queen Margaret of Anjou's forces. His head was displayed on Micklegate Bar in York wearing a paper crown. His sons became kings Edward IV and Richard III.
