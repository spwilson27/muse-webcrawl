Ferdinand Porsche

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Ferdinand Porsche in the 1930s beside a racing car, black and white photograph]
Ferdinand Porsche, 1930s.

Ferdinand Porsche (1875–1951) was an Austro-Bohemian-German automotive engineer and founder of the Porsche car company. He designed the Volkswagen Beetle for Nazi Germany, the Lohner-Porsche hybrid (1900) — the first hybrid car — and led the Auto Union and Mercedes-Benz racing programmes of the 1930s. His wartime work (Tiger tank designs, the V-1 era at Peenemünde in an advisory sense, the Maus super-heavy tank) led to imprisonment in France after the war; released in 1947, he saw his son Ferry launch the Porsche 356 before his death.

Early career

A self-taught electrical engineer, Porsche built the Egger-Lohner electric car (1898) and the Mixte hybrid, then designed for Austro-Daimler (including artillery tractors and aircraft engines) and Daimler-Motoren-Gesellschaft, where his compressor Mercedes SSK sports cars dominated racing.

Volkswagen and war

His Stuttgart design bureau (founded 1931) won the contract for Hitler\'s people\'s car; the Wolfsburg factory used forced labour during the war, a record scrutinised by historians. Porsche also designed the Tiger (P) tank (losing to Henschel) and the Elefant tank destroyer derived from it.

Legacy

Porsche AG, led by Ferry Porsche, became one of the world\'s great sports-car and engineering firms; Ferdinand\'s grandson Ferdinand Piëch later chaired Volkswagen Group. Porsche\'s engineering genes — air cooling, torsion bars, synchromesh gears (his patent) — influenced generations of cars.
