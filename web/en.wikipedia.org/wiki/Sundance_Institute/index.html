The Sundance Institute is an American nonprofit organisation founded by actor Robert Redford in 1981 in the mountains of Sundance, Utah. Dedicated to the discovery and development of independent artists and audiences, the Institute provides creative and financial support to emerging filmmakers, theatre artists and composers through a series of residential labs, grants and mentorship programmes. It is best known for organising the annual Sundance Film Festival, the largest festival for independent film in the United States.

History

The Institute began in June 1981 with its first Feature Film Program Lab, in which a small group of independent filmmakers developed projects under the guidance of established creative advisors. Redford, who had bought land in Utah's Provo Canyon in 1969 and named the area after his character in Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid, envisioned a retreat where new voices could work outside the commercial pressures of Hollywood. In 1985 the Institute took over management of the struggling US Film Festival in Park City, renaming it the Sundance Film Festival in 1991.

Programmes

The Institute runs labs for screenwriters, directors, documentary filmmakers, editors, composers, producers, theatre-makers and episodic storytellers, together with initiatives supporting Indigenous, women and other underrepresented artists; the Documentary Film Program and Fund; the Sundance Collab online learning platform; and the Ignite programme for filmmakers aged 18 to 25. Alumni include Quentin Tarantino, Steven Soderbergh, Darren Aronofsky, Taika Waititi, Ava DuVernay and Ryan Coogler.
