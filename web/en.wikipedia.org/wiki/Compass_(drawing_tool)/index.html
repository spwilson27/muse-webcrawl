Compass (drawing tool)

[Image: A drawing compass]

A compass, more accurately known as a pair of compasses, is a technical drawing instrument that can be used for inscribing circles or arcs. As dividers, it can also be used as a tool to mark out distances, in particular, on maps. Compasses can be used for mathematics, drafting, navigation and other purposes.

The instrument consists of two legs joined by a hinge at the top. One leg ends in a sharp needle point that is fixed at the centre of the circle to be drawn; the other holds a pencil, pen or scriber that traces the circumference as the compass is rotated about the point. The distance between the legs sets the radius. Variants include beam compasses for large circles, bow compasses adjusted by a screw wheel, proportional compasses for scaling distances, and caliper compasses used in machining and medicine.

History

Compasses have been used since antiquity: bronze examples survive from the Roman world, and the instrument is fundamental to the constructions of Euclid's Elements (c. 300 BC), where the first three postulates define what can be done with a straightedge and a "collapsing" compass. Islamic and medieval European craftsmen refined the design, and the sector and proportional compass were developed in the late sixteenth century, with contributions by Fabrizio Mordente and Galileo Galilei.

Uses

Beyond geometry and technical drawing, compasses are used by masons, carpenters and metalworkers for scribing, by navigators (as dividers) for measuring distances on charts, and symbolically: the square and compasses form the best-known emblem of Freemasonry.
