Overview

The Mudrarakshasa (Sanskrit: मुद्राराक्षस, Mudrārākṣasa, "Rākṣasa's Ring" or "The Signet Ring of Rakshasa") is a Sanskrit political drama by Viśākhadatta, notable among classical Indian plays for its basis in history and its plot of pure political intrigue, without a love story. Its date is debated — proposals range from the 4th to the 8th century CE — but it dramatises events around the founding of the Mauryan Empire in the 4th century BCE.

Plot

Chandragupta Maurya, guided by his minister Chanakya (Kautilya), has overthrown the Nanda dynasty. Rākṣasa, the loyal minister of the deposed Nandas, plots with allied kings to destroy the usurper. Chanakya's aim is not to kill Rākṣasa but to win him over, since his ability and integrity would strengthen the new regime. Through forged letters, double agents and a signet ring (the mudrā) that falls into Chanakya's hands, the spymaster sows suspicion between Rākṣasa and his allies, engineers the ruin of the conspiracy, and finally traps Rākṣasa into accepting the ministership of Chandragupta to save the life of his friend Candanadāsa.

Significance

The play is the principal dramatic source for the legend of Chanakya and reflects the amoral statecraft of the Arthaśāstra tradition: espionage, assassination and disinformation are its machinery. Unusually for Sanskrit drama, the antagonist Rākṣasa is drawn with dignity and loyalty that rival the hero's side. The Mudrarakshasa has been translated into many languages, adapted for modern Indian stage and television, and remains a standard text in the study of Sanskrit literature and ancient Indian political thought.
