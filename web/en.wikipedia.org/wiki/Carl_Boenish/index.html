Carl Boenish

Carl Ronald Boenish (3 April 1941 – 7 July 1984) was an American freefall cinematographer and jump pioneer regarded as the father of BASE jumping. Boenish, who filmed many of the landmark skydiving and fixed-object jumps of the 1970s and early 1980s, coined the acronym "BASE" (Building, Antenna, Span, Earth) together with his wife Jean and others, and founded the sport's first numbering system for jumpers who had completed all four categories. He died in a cliff jump accident in Norway in 1984.

Boenish's helmet-camera films brought fixed-object jumping to a wide audience.

Early career

Boenish trained as an electrical engineer and worked in the aerospace industry before devoting himself to skydiving cinematography. An accomplished jumper despite having contracted polio as a child, which left him with a weakened leg, he became known for filming freefall with helmet- and body-mounted cameras, producing footage for films and television — including the 1976 classic skydiving documentary work and jumps from El Capitan in Yosemite National Park in 1978, which helped popularise cliff jumping.

BASE jumping

In 1978 Boenish filmed jumps from El Capitan using ram-air parachutes and freefall technique, demonstrating that fixed-object jumping could be done in a controlled, repeatable way. With Jean Boenish, Phil Smith and Phil Mayfield, he formalised the BASE concept: the acronym appeared in print in 1981, and Boenish began issuing BASE numbers to jumpers who completed one jump from each of the four object types — his own being BASE #1. He published BASE Jump magazine and promoted the discipline worldwide, filming record formations such as multi-jumper exits from the Troll Wall (Trollveggen) in Norway.

Death and legacy

On 6 July 1984 Boenish joined a record group jump from the Troll Wall for a television programme; the next day he returned for a further jump with a camera team and struck the cliff shortly after exit. He died instantly, and the accident — at the very site of the sport's greatest publicity success — became a defining cautionary moment in BASE history. Jean Boenish continued to administer the BASE number system, and Carl Boenish is remembered in the sport's culture and in films such as the documentary Sunshine Superman (2014), which drew on his extensive film archive.
