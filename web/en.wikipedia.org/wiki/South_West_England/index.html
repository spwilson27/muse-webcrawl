South West England

South West England, or the South West of England, is one of the nine official regions of England. It consists of the counties of Cornwall (including the Isles of Scilly), Dorset, Devon, Gloucestershire, Somerset and Wiltshire, together with the city and county of Bristol. The region is the largest of England's nine regions by area, covering 23,800 square kilometres, but is relatively sparsely populated with about 5.7 million residents.

Geography

The region is dominated by the long southwestern peninsula between the Bristol Channel and the English Channel, with large areas of high moorland — Dartmoor, Exmoor and Bodmin Moor — and a long, varied coastline famous for resorts and surfing beaches. Major settlements include Bristol, Plymouth, Bournemouth, Exeter, Gloucester, Swindon, Torquay and Bath, the last a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Land's End in Cornwall is the westernmost point of mainland England.

Economy and culture

Tourism, agriculture and food production (including cider, Cheddar cheese and Cornish pasties) are major economic activities, along with aerospace and defence engineering around Bristol, marine industries at Plymouth and public-sector employment. The region has strong cultural identities, notably Cornish identity and language, and is known for folk traditions, seaside resorts, and areas of outstanding natural beauty. Stonehenge and Avebury in Wiltshire are among Britain's most famous prehistoric sites.

Transport

The M4 and M5 motorways and the Great Western Main Line connect the region to London and the Midlands; the A30 and A303 are the principal routes into Devon and Cornwall. Bristol Airport is the main international airport.
