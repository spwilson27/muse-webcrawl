Rakhigarhi

Rakhigarhi, also spelled Rakhi Garhi, is a village and archaeological site in Hisar district, Haryana, India. It is one of the largest known settlements of the Indus Valley Civilisation, with an estimated area of more than 350 hectares spread across several mounds, occupied from about 6500 BCE in its early phases through the Mature Harappan period (c. 2600–1900 BCE).

Excavations

The site was first noted by surveyors in the 1960s and excavated by the Archaeological Survey of India under Amarendra Nath in 1997–2000, and again by teams from Deccan College, Pune, in the 2010s. Excavations have revealed planned streets, mud-brick and burnt-brick houses, drainage, granaries, craft areas producing beads and pottery, and burials.

Ancient DNA

A 2019 study reported the first ancient DNA genome from the Indus Valley Civilisation, obtained from a woman buried at Rakhigarhi. The genome showed ancestry related to Iranian agriculturalists and South Asian hunter-gatherers, without the steppe pastoralist ancestry common in the region later, a result widely discussed in debates about Bronze Age migrations.

Significance

Its size has led some scholars to describe Rakhigarhi as larger than Mohenjo-daro, and the Government of India has proposed a major on-site museum for the site.
