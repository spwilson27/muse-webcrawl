Cretan War (1645–1669)

The Cretan War (1645–1669), also known as the War of Candia, was fought between the Republic of Venice and the Ottoman Empire for control of Crete, the last major Venetian overseas possession. It ended with the Ottoman conquest of the island after the twenty-one-year Siege of Candia, one of the longest sieges in history.

Course

The war began in 1645 when an Ottoman expedition landed near Chania, ostensibly in response to Maltese corsairs using Venetian harbours. Most of Crete fell quickly, but the capital, Candia (Heraklion), held out from 1648 to 1669, supported by Venetian sea power and volunteers from across Europe. Venetian fleets won repeated victories in the Aegean and blockaded the Dardanelles, yet could not break the siege.

Outcome

Venice's captain-general Francesco Morosini surrendered Candia in September 1669 on terms allowing the population to evacuate. Venice retained only three island fortresses — Gramvousa, Souda and Spinalonga — and lost its last great colony, hastening its decline as a Mediterranean power.
