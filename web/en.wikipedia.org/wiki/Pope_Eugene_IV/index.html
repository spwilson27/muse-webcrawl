Pope Eugene IV (Latin: Eugenius IV; 1383 – 23 February 1447), born Gabriele Condulmer, was head of the Catholic Church from 1431 to his death. His pontificate was dominated by conflict with the Council of Basel, which asserted conciliar supremacy over the pope, and by his efforts to reunite the Latin and Greek churches, achieved briefly at the Council of Ferrara–Florence.

Early life

Condulmer was born in Venice to a wealthy merchant family and became an Augustinian canon at San Giorgio in Alga. His uncle, Pope Gregory XII, made him Bishop of Siena and then cardinal in 1408. He served as papal treasurer under Martin V before his election in 1431.

Pontificate

Eugene inherited the Council of Basel, summoned by his predecessor, and attempted to dissolve it in 1431, provoking a prolonged crisis: the council refused, reaffirmed the conciliarist decree Haec sancta, and in 1439 deposed Eugene and elected the antipope Felix V — the last antipope in history. Eugene countered by transferring the council to Ferrara and then Florence, where in 1439 the decree Laetentur Caeli proclaimed union with the Greek Orthodox Church, attended by the Byzantine emperor John VIII. The union was later repudiated in Constantinople but enhanced Eugene's prestige in the West. Driven from Rome by revolt in 1434, he spent nine years in exile, mainly in Florence, where he consecrated Brunelleschi's dome of Florence Cathedral in 1436 and patronised artists including Fra Angelico. Returning to Rome in 1443, he spent his last years restoring papal authority; the Basel schism ended only after his death, in 1449.
