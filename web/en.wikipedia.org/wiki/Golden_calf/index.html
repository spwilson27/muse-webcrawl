Golden calf

The golden calf (עגל הזהב, ʿēgel hazzāhāv) was a cult image made by Aaron for the Israelites during Moses' absence on Mount Sinai, as described in the Book of Exodus. When Moses came down from the mountain and saw the calf and the dancing, he broke the two tablets of the Law and destroyed the idol. The episode is one of the most famous incidents in the Hebrew Bible and has become a symbol of apostasy and idolatry.

The Exodus narrative

According to Exodus 32, while Moses was on Mount Sinai receiving the Law, the people, despairing of his return, asked Aaron to make them a god. Aaron collected their golden earrings, fashioned a molten calf, and built an altar before it, proclaiming a feast to the LORD. Moses, informed by God, descended, shattered the stone tablets in anger, burned the calf, ground it to powder, scattered it on water, and made the Israelites drink it. The Levites, answering Moses' call, slew about three thousand of the offenders, and a plague followed.

Jeroboam's calves

In 1 Kings 12, Jeroboam I, first king of the northern kingdom of Israel, set up golden calves at Bethel and Dan, using a formula echoing Aaron's words ("Behold your gods, O Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt"). Biblical writers present this as the archetypal "sin of Jeroboam." Some scholars argue the Exodus story was written or shaped as a polemic against Jeroboam's shrines; others see bull imagery as an older, originally legitimate symbol of Yahweh or of El.

Interpretation

The episode is traditionally read as a warning against idolatry and impatience with divine absence. In Islamic tradition the story appears in the Qur'an, where the calf is made by al-Samiri during Musa's absence. In modern usage, "golden calf" denotes any false object of worship, especially money or material success.
