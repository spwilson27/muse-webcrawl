Longinus

Longinus is the name given in Christian tradition to the Roman soldier who pierced the side of Jesus Christ with a lance as he hung on the Cross, an event recorded in the Gospel of John (19:34). The name is also that of the reputed author of the classical treatise On the Sublime, and of the 3rd-century philosopher Cassius Longinus. This article concerns the legendary soldier and saint.

The name Longinus probably derives from the Greek lonchē (λόγχη), meaning "spear" or "lance". In medieval legend, Longinus was said to have been healed of an eye affliction when blood and water from Christ's side fell upon his face, and to have converted to Christianity. Later accounts describe him as preaching the faith and suffering martyrdom.

Relics and veneration

The lance used at the Crucifixion — the Holy Lance or Spear of Destiny — is among the most famous relics attributed to Longinus, with examples preserved in Vienna, Rome, and elsewhere. In St Peter's Basilica in the Vatican, a colossal statue of St Longinus by Gian Lorenzo Bernini stands in one of the piers supporting the dome, and the basilica preserves a relic said to be the tip of his lance.

Longinus is regarded as a saint in both Eastern and Western tradition, though modern scholarship treats the details of his legend as unverifiable. His story appears in apocryphal works such as the Gospel of Nicodemus and in the medieval Golden Legend.

Other uses of the name

  • Cassius Longinus (c. 213–273 AD), Greek rhetorician and philosopher, adviser to Queen Zenobia of Palmyra.
  • Pseudo-Longinus, the unknown author of On the Sublime (Peri Hypsous), a landmark work of literary criticism.
