Zapatista Army of National Liberation

EZLN
Ejército Zapatista de Liberación Nacional
Founded: 1983 (public emergence 1994)
Region: Chiapas, Mexico
Ideology: Zapatismo, indigenous rights, anti-neoliberalism

The Zapatista Army of National Liberation (Spanish: Ejército Zapatista de Liberación Nacional, EZLN) is a left-wing revolutionary group based in Chiapas, the southernmost state of Mexico. It takes its name from Emiliano Zapata, the agrarian revolutionary of the Mexican Revolution.

1994 uprising

The EZLN emerged publicly on 1 January 1994, the day the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) came into force, declaring war on the Mexican state and briefly occupying towns including San Cristóbal de las Casas. After about twelve days of fighting, a ceasefire was declared. The group's spokesman, Subcomandante Marcos, became internationally known for his communiqués combining political argument with literary flourish.

Autonomous communities

Rather than seeking state power, the movement built autonomous municipalities and later regional governing bodies known as Juntas de Buen Gobierno ("Good Government Councils"), centred on community assemblies called caracoles. In late 2023 the EZLN announced a restructuring of its autonomous structures. Its principles emphasise indigenous self-government, collective land, and resistance to what it calls neoliberal capitalism, summarised in the slogan of governing by obeying (mandar obedeciendo).

Influence

The Zapatistas became an early emblem of the alter-globalisation movement, making pioneering use of the internet to circulate their declarations and build international solidarity networks. Negotiations with the government produced the San Andrés Accords of 1996 on indigenous rights, which the movement says were never fully implemented.
