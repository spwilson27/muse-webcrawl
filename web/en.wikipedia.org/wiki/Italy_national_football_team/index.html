The Italy national football team (Italian: Nazionale di calcio dell'Italia) represents Italy in men's international football and is controlled by the Italian Football Federation. Known as the Azzurri for their Savoy blue shirts, Italy is one of the most successful national teams in the history of the World Cup, having won four titles.

History

Italy played its first international in 1910, defeating France 6–2 in Milan. Under coach Vittorio Pozzo the team won the 1934 World Cup on home soil and retained the trophy in France in 1938, also winning Olympic gold in 1936. The all-conquering Grande Torino side that formed the backbone of the national team was lost in the 1949 Superga air disaster.

Italy returned to the summit by winning the European Championship in 1968 and reaching the 1970 World Cup final, losing to Brazil. In 1982, Paolo Rossi's goals carried Enzo Bearzot's team to a third world title in Spain. A fourth star followed in 2006, when Marcello Lippi's side beat France on penalties in Berlin, captain Fabio Cannavaro lifting the trophy. Roberto Mancini's team won Euro 2020, beating England on penalties at Wembley, and set a world-record unbeaten run of 37 matches, though the side then surprisingly failed to qualify for the 2022 World Cup.

Playing style and identity

Italy is traditionally associated with strong defensive organisation, historically expressed in the catenaccio system, as well as tactical flexibility and tournament resilience. Famous players include Giuseppe Meazza, Giacinto Facchetti, Dino Zoff, Franco Baresi, Paolo Maldini, Alessandro Del Piero, Francesco Totti, Andrea Pirlo and Gianluigi Buffon.

Records

Gianluigi Buffon is Italy's most capped player with 176 appearances. Luigi Riva is the all-time top scorer with 35 goals. Italy's traditional rivals include Germany, France, Spain, Brazil and England.
