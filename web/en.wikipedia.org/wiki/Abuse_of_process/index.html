Abuse of process

Abuse of process
Legal areaTort; civil and criminal procedure
DefinitionUse of legal process for an ulterior, improper purpose
Key elements(1) An ulterior purpose; (2) a wilful act in the use of process not proper in the regular conduct of proceedings
Related doctrinesMalicious prosecution; vexatious litigation; SLAPP

Abuse of process is a cause of action in common law jurisdictions for the misuse of court process — civil or criminal — for an end other than that which the process was designed to accomplish. Unlike malicious prosecution, which concerns proceedings wrongfully initiated, abuse of process concerns the perversion of a regularly issued process, such as using a lawsuit or arrest to extort a settlement, gain leverage in an unrelated dispute, or harass an opponent.

Elements

In United States law the tort generally requires (1) an ulterior purpose or motive in using the process, and (2) a wilful act in using it that is not proper in the regular conduct of the proceeding. Merely filing a suit with a bad motive is not enough where the process is used only for its intended purpose; the misuse must occur in the manner of its deployment — for example, threatening criminal prosecution to force payment of a civil debt.

Abuse of process as a judicial doctrine

The term is also used more broadly in Commonwealth courts for proceedings that, while formally valid, misuse the court's procedures — including relitigating decided issues (Henderson v Henderson abuse), bringing claims for a collateral purpose, or delay tactics. Courts hold an inherent power to stay or strike out such proceedings to protect the integrity of the judicial process. Related concepts include vexatious litigation, frivolous lawsuits and strategic lawsuits against public participation (SLAPPs).
