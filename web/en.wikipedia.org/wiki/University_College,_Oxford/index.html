University College, Oxford

University College, formally the Master and Fellows of the College of the Great Hall of the University of Oxford and commonly called Univ, is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England. Founded in 1249 by William of Durham, it is among the oldest colleges of the university.

History

William of Durham left 310 marks on his death in 1249 to support masters studying theology at Oxford; the college counts this as its foundation, and celebrated its 750th anniversary in 1999. For centuries legend attributed the foundation to King Alfred in 872, a claim the college long indulged. Its main quadrangle was rebuilt in the 17th century, and the Radcliffe Quad was added in the early 18th century.

People

Notable alumni include the poet Percy Bysshe Shelley (expelled in 1811 for publishing a pamphlet on atheism, and now commemorated by the Shelley Memorial in college), US President Bill Clinton (a Rhodes Scholar at Univ), physicist Stephen Hawking, and prime ministers Clement Attlee and Harold Wilson. The college's visitor is the Lord Chancellor by convention of its statutes.
