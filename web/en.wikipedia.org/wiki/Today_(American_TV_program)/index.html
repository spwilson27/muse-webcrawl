Today (also called The Today Show) is an American morning television show airing on NBC. It debuted on January 14, 1952, and was the first program of its genre on American television and in the world. The program principally broadcasts from Studio 1A in Rockefeller Center, New York City, combining news, interviews, weather, and lifestyle features.

History

Conceived by NBC executive Sylvester "Pat" Weaver, the show was originally hosted by Dave Garroway, with a chimpanzee mascot, J. Fred Muggs, becoming an unlikely 1950s attraction. Later hosts included John Chancellor, Hugh Downs, Tom Brokaw, Jane Pauley, Bryant Gumbel, Katie Couric, Matt Lauer, Meredith Vieira, and Savannah Guthrie. The weekday broadcast typically runs four hours, with later hours devoted to lifestyle and entertainment segments; weekend editions also air.

Format and influence

Today established the "magazine" format of morning television copied by rivals such as ABC's Good Morning America and CBS Mornings, blending hard news early with softer features, live music on the plaza, and celebrity interviews. It has consistently been one of the most-watched morning programmes in the United States.
