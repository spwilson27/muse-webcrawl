KNX

TypeBuilding automation standard
StandardISO/IEC 14543-3; EN 50090
Governing bodyKNX Association
PredecessorsEIB, EHS, BatiBUS

KNX is an open standard for commercial and residential building automation, covering control of lighting, blinds, heating, ventilation, air conditioning, security, and energy management. It is administered by the KNX Association and standardised internationally as ISO/IEC 14543-3.

Architecture

KNX is a decentralised system: sensors (such as switches and thermostats) and actuators communicate as peers over a bus, so no central computer is required for operation. Devices exchange standardised telegrams containing datapoints, and are configured with the ETS (Engineering Tool Software). Physical media include twisted pair (KNX TP), powerline, radio frequency (KNX RF), and IP networks (KNXnet/IP).

History

KNX was formed in 1999 by merging three earlier European systems — the European Installation Bus (EIB), BatiBUS, and the European Home Systems Protocol (EHS). Thousands of certified products from hundreds of manufacturers are interoperable under the standard.
