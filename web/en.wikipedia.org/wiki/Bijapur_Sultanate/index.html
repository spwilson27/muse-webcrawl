The Bijapur Sultanate was a kingdom in the western Deccan of southern India, ruled by the Adil Shahi dynasty from 1490 to 1686. Founded by Yusuf Adil Shah, a former governor of the Bahmani Sultanate, it was one of the five Deccan sultanates that emerged from the Bahmani breakup. At its height under Ibrahim Adil Shah II and Mohammed Adil Shah, it stretched from the Arabian Sea toward the Bay of Bengal approaches and contested territory with Vijayanagara, the Portuguese at Goa, the Marathas, and the Mughals.

History

Yusuf Adil Shah declared independence around 1490. His successors alternated between Sunni and Shia court alignments and fought shifting wars and alliances with the sister sultanates of Ahmadnagar, Golconda, Bidar, and Berar; the Deccan alliance famously crushed Vijayanagara at the Battle of Talikota in 1565. Ibrahim II (r. 1580–1627) made Bijapur a celebrated centre of art and music and promoted religious tolerance. In the seventeenth century, Maratha leader Shivaji, whose father Shahaji served Bijapur, carved out his own kingdom from its western territories, while Mughal pressure intensified until Aurangzeb captured Bijapur in 1686 after a long siege, ending the dynasty.

Culture and architecture

Bijapur's court culture produced the Deccani school of painting, Ibrahim II's songbook the Kitab-i Nauras, and monumental architecture: the Gol Gumbaz, with one of the largest domes of the pre-modern world and its whispering gallery; the Ibrahim Rauza tomb complex; and the great unfinished Jami Masjid. The city—modern Vijayapura in Karnataka—retains many of these monuments.
