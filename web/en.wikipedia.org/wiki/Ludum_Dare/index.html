Ludum Dare (from Latin "to give a game", sometimes shortened to LD) is a recurring game jam competition in which participants create a video game from scratch within a single weekend, working to a theme announced at the start of the event. It was founded by Geoff Howland and was first held in April 2002. It is one of the oldest and largest game jams in the world, regularly attracting thousands of entries per event.

Format

The event traditionally comprises two parallel competitions. In the Compo, a single developer must create a complete game, including all art, music and code, alone within 48 hours, and release the source code. In the Jam, teams are allowed, pre-made and third-party assets may be used, and entrants have 72 hours. In both formats the game must fit the event's theme, which is chosen by community vote from a shortlist of suggestions.

After the creation period ends, participants play and rate each other's games over a judging period of several weeks in categories such as innovation, fun, theme, graphics, audio, humour and mood, producing overall rankings.

History

The first Ludum Dare was held in April 2002 with a small number of entrants. The community steadily grew, and the event moved from its original ld48.org home to a new site, ldjam.com, launched in 2016 with Ludum Dare 38. Notable games that began as Ludum Dare entries include McPixel, Evoland, Mini Metro, and early prototypes by developers such as Markus "Notch" Persson and Mike Bithell. Well-known independent developers including Phil Fish and Terry Cavanagh have also participated.
