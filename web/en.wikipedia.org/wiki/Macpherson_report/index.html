Macpherson report

The Macpherson report is the published result of The Stephen Lawrence Inquiry, a public inquiry chaired by Sir William Macpherson of Cluny into the investigation of the racist murder of the black British teenager Stephen Lawrence, who was stabbed to death in Eltham, south-east London, in April 1993. The report was published in February 1999.

Findings

The inquiry concluded that the Metropolitan Police Service's investigation was "marred by a combination of professional incompetence, institutional racism and a failure of leadership by senior officers". Its use of the term institutional racism — defined as the collective failure of an organisation to provide a professional service because of colour, culture or ethnic origin, detectable in processes, attitudes and behaviour amounting to discrimination through unwitting prejudice, ignorance, thoughtlessness and racist stereotyping — became the report's most influential legacy.

Recommendations and aftermath

The report made 70 recommendations, including targets for recruiting ethnic-minority officers, reforms in stop-and-search recording, racism awareness training, and that consideration be given to allowing prosecution after acquittal — i.e. reforming the double jeopardy rule in serious cases, which was enacted in the Criminal Justice Act 2003. In 2012, two of Lawrence's killers, Gary Dobson and David Norris, were convicted under the new law on the basis of new forensic evidence.

Home Secretary Jack Straw described the report as a landmark. The inquiry and report profoundly influenced British policing, public-sector race relations and equality law, including the extension of race-relations duties to public bodies in the Race Relations (Amendment) Act 2000.
