Tennessee-Tombigbee Waterway

The Tennessee-Tombigbee Waterway (popularly known as the Tenn-Tom Waterway or Tenn-Tom) is a 234-mile (377 km) artificial U.S. waterway built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers between the Tennessee River at Pickwick Lake and the Tombigbee River at Demopolis, Alabama. Opened in 1985, it provides a shorter navigation route from the interior United States to the Gulf of Mexico at Mobile than the Mississippi River.

Construction

Authorised in 1946, the project was delayed by funding disputes and environmental litigation before construction began in 1972. It required excavation of about 310 million cubic yards of earth — more than the Panama Canal — including the 27-mile Divide Cut through the divide between the two watersheds. The waterway comprises a river section, a canal section and the Divide Cut, with ten locks lifting traffic a total of 341 feet.

Use

Commercial traffic — chiefly coal, timber, grain and aggregates — runs below early projections, and the waterway has been both praised as regional infrastructure and criticised as a pork-barrel project. It is also heavily used for recreation, with marinas and Corps campgrounds along its length.
