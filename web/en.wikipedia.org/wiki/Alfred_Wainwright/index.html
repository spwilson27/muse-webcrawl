Alfred Wainwright

Alfred Wainwright MBE (17 January 1907 – 20 January 1991), who preferred to be known as A. Wainwright, was a British fellwalker, guidebook author, and illustrator. His seven-volume Pictorial Guide to the Lakeland Fells, handwritten and drawn entirely by hand and published between 1955 and 1966, is regarded as a classic of British outdoor literature, and the 214 Lakeland summits he described are known as "the Wainwrights".

Life

Wainwright was born in Blackburn, Lancashire, the son of a stonemason, and left school at thirteen to work as an office boy while studying accountancy at night school. In 1930 a week's holiday in the Lake District — his first sight of the fells, from Orrest Head above Windermere — changed his life. He moved to Kendal in 1941 to work in the borough treasurer's office, becoming borough treasurer in 1948, and spent his weekends walking the fells of the Lakes, the Pennines, and the Peak District.

Guidebooks

Beginning in 1952, Wainwright devoted thirteen years to the Pictorial Guides: seven volumes covering 214 fells, every page produced in his meticulous handwriting and pen-and-ink drawings, with maps, ascent diagrams, and personal commentary. He later wrote the guide to the Pennine Way (1968) and devised and described the Coast to Coast Walk (1973), a 192-mile route from St Bees in Cumbria to Robin Hood's Bay in Yorkshire across the Lakes, the Pennines, and the North York Moors. His ashes were scattered on Innominate Tarn on Haystacks, the fell he loved most.
