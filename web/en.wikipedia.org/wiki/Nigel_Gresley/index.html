Nigel Gresley

Born19 June 1876, Edinburgh, Scotland
Died5 April 1941, Hertfordshire, England
OccupationLocomotive engineer

Sir Herbert Nigel Gresley (19 June 1876 – 5 April 1941) was a British railway engineer and chief mechanical engineer of the London and North Eastern Railway (LNER) from 1923 until his death. He is regarded as one of Britain's most celebrated steam locomotive designers.

Designs

Gresley designed the A1/A3 Pacific class, including Flying Scotsman, and the streamlined A4 class, whose most famous member, Mallard, set the world speed record for steam locomotives at 126 mph (203 km/h) in 1938 — a record that still stands. His locomotives featured conjugated valve gear and elegant, wind-tunnel-tested streamlining.

Career

After apprenticeships at Crewe and elsewhere, Gresley rose through the Great Northern Railway, becoming its chief mechanical engineer in 1911, and continued in the role after the 1923 Grouping created the LNER. He was knighted in 1936.
