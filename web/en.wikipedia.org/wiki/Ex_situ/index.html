Ex situ

Ex situ is a Latin phrase meaning "off site" or "out of place". It is most commonly used in conservation biology, in the term ex situ conservation: the process of protecting an endangered species, variety, or breed outside its natural habitat — for example in zoos, botanical gardens, aquariums, seed banks, and gene banks.

Ex situ conservation

Ex situ methods complement in situ conservation (protection within the natural habitat). Facilities such as the Svalbard Global Seed Vault store genetic material as insurance against extinction in the wild. Captive breeding programs have reintroduced species such as the Arabian oryx and the California condor. Critics note that ex situ populations may lose genetic diversity and adaptations to the wild, and that ex situ conservation should not substitute for habitat protection.

Other uses

In medicine and materials science, ex situ refers to procedures or measurements carried out outside the original system, as opposed to in situ (in place) or in vivo (in a living organism). In mining and remediation, ex situ treatment of contaminated soil involves excavating and treating it elsewhere.
