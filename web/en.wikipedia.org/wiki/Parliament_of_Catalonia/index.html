The Parliament of Catalonia (Catalan: Parlament de Catalunya) is the unicameral legislature of the autonomous community of Catalonia, Spain. It represents the people of Catalonia, passes legislation within the community's competences, approves its budget, and elects and oversees the President and Government (Generalitat) of Catalonia.

History

The Parliament traces its origins to the Catalan Courts (Corts Catalanes), one of Europe's oldest parliamentary institutions, which met from the medieval period until their abolition after the War of the Spanish Succession in 1714. A modern Parliament was established under the Statute of Autonomy of 1932 during the Second Spanish Republic and sat until the end of the Civil War in 1939. It was restored following the Statute of Autonomy of 1979, with the first democratic elections held on 20 March 1980.

Composition and elections

The Parliament has 135 deputies elected for four-year terms by proportional representation (D'Hondt method) in four provincial constituencies: Barcelona (85 seats), Tarragona (18), Girona (17) and Lleida (15). Sessions are held in the Palace of the Parliament, a former arsenal building in the Ciutadella Park in Barcelona.

Functions

The Parliament legislates in areas devolved to Catalonia, including education, health, culture, language policy and civil law; it invests the President of the Generalitat, can pass motions of no confidence, and controls the executive through questions, committees and investigative commissions. In 2017 the Parliament passed laws on a self-determination referendum and a unilateral declaration of independence, which were annulled by the Spanish Constitutional Court; the chamber was dissolved and direct rule imposed until elections that December.
