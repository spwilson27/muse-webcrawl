Alligator gar

The alligator gar (Atractosteus spatula) is a large ray-finned fish in the gar family, native to the lower Mississippi River basin and Gulf Coast drainages of North and Central America. It is the second-largest freshwater fish in North America, commonly reaching 2 metres and over 45 kg, with historical reports of specimens near 3 metres and 159 kg.

Description

The species takes its name from its broad, short snout filled with two rows of sharp teeth in the upper jaw, giving it an alligator-like profile. Its body is armoured with hard ganoid scales. Like other gars it has a vascularised swim bladder that lets it breathe air in oxygen-poor water, and its roe is toxic to mammals and birds.

Ecology and conservation

An ambush predator, the alligator gar feeds mainly on fish but also takes waterfowl and small mammals. Once persecuted as a supposed threat to sport fisheries and drastically reduced by habitat loss, it has become the focus of reintroduction and management programmes, including restocking in its former range in Illinois, and is prized by some anglers as a catch-and-release trophy fish.
