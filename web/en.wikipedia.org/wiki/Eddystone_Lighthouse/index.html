Eddystone Lighthouse

LocationEddystone Rocks, English Channel, off Cornwall, England
First lit1698 (first tower); current tower 1882
Height49 m (161 ft)
DesignerJames Douglass (current tower)

The Eddystone Lighthouse is a lighthouse on the treacherous Eddystone Rocks, about 14 kilometres (9 miles) south of Rame Head in Cornwall, England, in the English Channel. It has been the site of four successive lighthouse towers, and its history is one of the most famous in lighthouse engineering, demonstrating the progressive mastery of building on wave-swept rocks.

The four towers

Winstanley's tower (1698)

The first lighthouse, an elaborate timber-and-stone structure built by Henry Winstanley, was lit in 1698. Winstanley was in the tower during the Great Storm of 1703 when it was swept away entirely, killing him and five others.

Rudyard's tower (1709)

John Rudyard's replacement, a timber tower on a stone base, served for nearly fifty years before being destroyed by fire in 1755.

Smeaton's tower (1759)

Engineer John Smeaton built the third tower of interlocking granite blocks, shaped like the trunk of an oak tree. It became the prototype for modern offshore lighthouses and stood for over a century. When replaced, its upper portion was re-erected on Plymouth Hoe as a memorial.

Douglass's tower (1882)

The present tower, designed by James Douglass, was built on an adjacent rock and first lit in 1882. It stands 49 metres high and was automated in 1982, among the first Trinity House lighthouses to be converted.
