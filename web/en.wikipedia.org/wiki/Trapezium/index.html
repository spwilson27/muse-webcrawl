Trapezium

A trapezium is a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides, though the precise definition differs between British and American usage. In British English (and in most of the world), a trapezium has at least one pair of parallel sides — in North American usage this figure is called a trapezoid, while the North American "trapezium" means a quadrilateral with no parallel sides, which British usage calls a trapezium in the older sense or an irregular quadrilateral. This article follows the inclusive definition: a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides.

Properties

The parallel sides of a trapezium are called its bases, and the non-parallel sides are its legs; the perpendicular distance between the bases is the height (or altitude). The line segment joining the midpoints of the legs is the midsegment, whose length is the average of the lengths of the bases. The area of a trapezium is given by A = (a + b)/2 × h, where a and b are the lengths of the parallel sides and h is the height. An isosceles trapezium has congruent legs, base angles equal in pairs, and an axis of symmetry.

Terminology

The conflicting definitions arose from a transposition in the writings of the ancient mathematician Proclus, whose usage was then propagated differently: British authors from the 19th century generally followed one assignment and American dictionaries the reverse, a confusion that persists in school geometry today.
