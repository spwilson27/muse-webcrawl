Belite

Chemical formulaCa2SiO4 (2CaO·SiO2)
NameDicalcium silicate
Natural mineralLarnite (β form)
Typical content in Portland cement15–30%
NamedBy Alfred Elis Törnebohm, 1897

Belite is an industrial mineral of dicalcium silicate, Ca2SiO4, that is important in the manufacture of Portland cement. It is one of the four principal phases of Portland cement clinker, alongside alite (tricalcium silicate), tricalcium aluminate and ferrite. The Swedish geologist Alfred Elis Törnebohm named the clinker phases alphabetically in 1897 — alite, belite, celite and felite — without knowing their compositions.

Properties and hydration

Belite hydrates much more slowly than alite. Its reaction with water produces calcium silicate hydrate (C–S–H, the main strength-giving phase of hardened cement paste) and calcium hydroxide, but the strength contribution comes mainly at later ages — after about 28 days — whereas alite dominates early strength. Belite-rich cements therefore develop strength slowly but durably, generate less heat of hydration, and are valued for massive pours and for improved long-term durability. Because belite forms at lower kiln temperatures than alite, high-belite and belite–sulfoaluminate cements are also studied as lower-CO2 alternatives to ordinary Portland cement.

Polymorphism

Dicalcium silicate exists in several polymorphs, designated α, α′, β and γ. The β form — corresponding to the natural mineral larnite — is the form normally stabilised in clinker by minor-element substitution (for example with Al, Fe, Mg, K or sulfur). The γ form is hydraulically inactive, and its formation during cooling is accompanied by a volume increase that can cause clinker nodules to fall to dust, a defect known as "dusting", avoided by rapid cooling and stabilisation of the β phase.
