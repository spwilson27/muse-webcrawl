Division (business)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Divisions within a corporate structure

A division, sometimes called a business sector or business unit, is one of the parts into which a business, organization or company is divided. A division forms a distinct part of a company, often organized around a product line, market, or geographic area, but remains legally part of the parent corporation - unlike a subsidiary, which is a separate legal entity.

Characteristics

Divisions typically have their own management, profit-and-loss responsibility, and functional departments such as marketing, production, and accounting, while ultimate control rests with the corporate parent. This structure allows large, diversified companies to manage distinct businesses with appropriate focus while retaining centralized ownership and strategy. Divisional results are often disclosed as reportable segments in financial statements, for example under IFRS 8 and US GAAP segment reporting rules.

Examples

Historical examples include the divisional structure of General Motors, organized around brands such as Chevrolet and Cadillac, a model developed under Alfred P. Sloan in the 1920s ("a car for every purse and purpose"), and the divisions of conglomerates such as General Electric, whose businesses ranged from aviation to healthcare and finance.
