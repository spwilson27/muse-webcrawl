Planica is an alpine valley in northwestern Slovenia, in the Municipality of Kranjska Gora near the village of Rateče, close to the borders with Austria and Italy. It is world-famous as a venue for ski jumping and ski flying, and since 2015 has hosted the Planica Nordic Centre, with facilities for ski jumping, ski flying, cross-country skiing, and other Nordic disciplines. The valley lies beneath the peaks of the Julian Alps, including Ciprnik and Sleme, and leads into the Tamar valley.

History

The first ski jumping hill at Planica was built before 1930, and in 1934 the Bloudkova velikanka, designed by engineer Stanko Bloudek, opened as the largest hill in the world. Planica hosted the first ski jumps beyond 100 metres: Sepp Bradl landed the first-ever jump over 100 m there in 1936, and the valley became known as the cradle of ski flying. The Mamut ("Mammoth") hill tradition continues in the Letalnica bratov Gorišek, the Gorišek brothers' ski flying hill, on which numerous world records have been set, including the first flights past 200 metres (Toni Nieminen, 1994) and, in the 21st century, distances beyond 250 metres.

Events

Planica traditionally hosts the final event of the FIS Ski Jumping World Cup season each March, drawing tens of thousands of spectators, and it has staged the FIS Ski Flying World Championships many times (1972, 1979, 1985, 1994, 2004, 2010, 2020). In 2023 it hosted the FIS Nordic World Ski Championships. The chant of crowds celebrating long flights in the valley is inseparable from Slovenian sporting identity, and Slovene jumpers such as Primož Peterka, Peter Prevc, and Domen Prevc have been home favourites.

The valley

Beyond the jumps, Planica is the starting point for hikes into the Tamar valley and toward Jalovec, and the Planica glacier once covered the area during the last ice age. The Nordic Centre includes an indoor cross-country tunnel, allowing year-round training on snow.
