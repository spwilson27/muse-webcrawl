Nubuck

Nubuck is top-grain cattle leather that has been sanded or buffed on the grain (outer) side to produce a fine, velvet-like nap of short fibres. The name is thought to derive from "new" + "buck(skin)". Nubuck is similar in appearance to suede but is made from the stronger outer side of the hide, making it more durable and more expensive, though still delicate.

Characteristics and uses

Because it is made from top-grain leather, nubuck is thicker and tougher than suede, which is made from the inner split of the hide. Its buffed surface gives a soft, matte, slightly fuzzy texture that shows light markings when touched, since the nap is disturbed. Nubuck is commonly used for premium footwear (including hiking boots and dress shoes), handbags, jackets, upholstery and gloves.

Care

Nubuck is vulnerable to water, oil and staining, and it cannot be polished like smooth leather. Care involves specialised nubuck brushes, erasers and protector sprays to raise the nap and repel moisture; over time, heavy wear may require conditioning or re-dyeing. Damage to the nap is difficult to reverse, which limits the material's use in rough conditions despite its durability relative to suede.
