All-terrain vehicle

An all-terrain vehicle (ATV), also known as a quad bike or four-wheeler, is a vehicle that travels on low-pressure tires, with a seat straddled by the operator and handlebars for steering control. As the name implies, it is designed to handle a wider variety of terrain than most other vehicles. ATVs are used for recreation, agriculture, ranching, and utility work.

Definition and types

The American National Standards Institute defines an ATV as a vehicle on four low-pressure tires with a straddled seat and handlebar steering. Two broad classes exist: sport models, optimised for performance and often rear-wheel drive, and utility models with racks, towing capacity, and four-wheel drive. Engine sizes range from under 50 cc youth models to 1,000 cc machines. Related but distinct are side-by-sides (UTVs), which have car-style seats and a steering wheel; most jurisdictions classify and regulate them separately.

History

Honda introduced the first three-wheeled ATC (the US90) in 1970. Three-wheelers were withdrawn from the US market under a 1988 consent decree because of their rollover risk, and four-wheelers — pioneered by Suzuki's QuadRunner in 1983 and Honda's TRX200 — became the standard configuration.

Safety and regulation

ATVs have high fatality and injury rates relative to other vehicles, particularly rollovers and incidents involving children on adult-sized machines. Safety advocates recommend helmets, rider training, age-appropriate engine sizes, and not riding on paved roads (ATVs' solid rear axles and high centres of gravity make them unstable on pavement). Laws on registration, licensing, and road use vary widely by country and state.
