Emperor Go-Daigo (後醍醐天皇, Go-Daigo-tennō; 26 November 1288 – 19 September 1339) was the 96th emperor of Japan, reigning from 1318 to 1339. He attempted to overthrow the Kamakura shogunate and restore direct imperial rule, an effort culminating in the Kenmu Restoration (1333–1336) — the first imperial government in over 150 years — before its collapse split Japan into rival Northern and Southern Courts.

Struggle with the shogunate

Go-Daigo came to the throne in 1318 under the Bunpō succession compromise between the Daikakuji and Jimyōin imperial lines. Determined to rule in his own right, he plotted against the Kamakura bakufu; after two failed conspiracies (1324 and 1331) he was exiled to the Oki Islands in 1332. He escaped in 1333, and the warrior Ashikaga Takauji and Nitta Yoshisada turned against the shogunate, destroying it that year.

Kenmu Restoration and downfall

Go-Daigo's restoration government tried to revive court-centered rule, but its rewards policy alienated the warrior class. Ashikaga Takauji rebelled in 1335–36, seized Kyoto, and installed a rival emperor of the Northern Court. Go-Daigo fled to Yoshino, where he maintained the Southern Court until his death in 1339, beginning the Nanboku-chō period. The schism was not resolved until 1392.
