Vicia faba

Vicia faba, commonly known as the broad bean, fava bean or faba bean, is a species of flowering plant in the legume family Fabaceae, cultivated for its large, flat seeds, eaten as a vegetable and used as animal feed and a nitrogen-fixing cover crop.

Cultivation and use

One of the oldest domesticated legumes, with remains in the Near East dating back some 9,000 years, the broad bean is a hardy annual with erect stems and white-and-black flowers, grown in cool seasons across Europe, the Mediterranean, China and the Americas. The seeds are rich in protein; they are eaten fresh, dried (as in Egyptian ful medames and Italian traditions with pecorino), or ground into flour. The plant also hosts the black bean aphid and is a host of the parasitic weed broomrape in some regions.

Favism

In people with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency, eating fava beans (or inhaling their pollen) can trigger favism, a haemolytic reaction caused by the compounds vicine and convicine; the condition is most common around the Mediterranean, where the deficiency itself is frequent.
