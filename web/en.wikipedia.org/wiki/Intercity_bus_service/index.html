Intercity bus service

A long-distance coach operating an intercity route.

An intercity bus service (North American English) or intercity coach service is a public transport service using coaches to carry passengers significant distances between different cities, towns, or other population centres. Unlike transit buses serving a single metropolitan area, intercity buses typically travel longer distances, use highway coaches with luggage bays and reclining seats, and stop less frequently.

History

Horse-drawn stagecoaches provided early intercity land transport. Motor bus networks expanded rapidly in the 1920s; Greyhound Lines, founded in 1914, grew into the largest North American operator. Intercity buses carried large numbers of passengers through the mid-20th century but declined with the rise of private cars, interstate highways used for personal travel, and subsidised air travel. In Europe and much of Asia, deregulated coach markets and networks such as FlixBus revived the sector in the 2000s–2010s.

Operations

Services range from scheduled rural routes to frequent express "curbside" buses between major cities. Facilities include bus stations, roadside stops, and online booking. Intercity buses are among the most energy-efficient and lowest-carbon motorised modes per passenger-kilometre, and are often the only public transport serving small communities.

By region

In the United States the sector is dominated by Greyhound and FlixBus (which acquired Greyhound in 2021), plus Megabus and regional carriers. China and India operate some of the world's largest intercity bus networks, and long-distance coaches are a mainstay of transport in Latin America.
