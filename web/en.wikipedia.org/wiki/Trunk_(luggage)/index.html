Trunk (luggage)

A trunk is a large rectangular container for transporting possessions on long journeys, typically made of wood covered with canvas, leather or metal, and fitted with a hinged lid, handles and a lock. Trunks were the dominant form of luggage through the age of steamship and rail travel in the 19th and early 20th centuries. Variants include the flat-top trunk, the domed or "camelback" trunk, and the steamer trunk, designed to fit beneath a ship's berth.

TypeLuggage
MaterialsWood, canvas, leather, metal
Era of peak use19th–early 20th century
VariantsSteamer trunk, Saratoga trunk, wardrobe trunk

Construction

Traditional trunks were built on a pine or poplar frame, covered for waterproofing and strength, and lined with paper or fabric, often with a shallow inner tray for small items. The wardrobe trunk opened upright with drawers and a hanging section, acting as a portable closet. As travel shifted to lighter suitcases after the Second World War, trunks declined, though they persist as storage chests, camp trunks and decorative furniture.
