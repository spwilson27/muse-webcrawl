Amine Gemayel (Arabic: أمين الجميل; born 22 January 1942) is a Lebanese politician who served as President of Lebanon from 1982 to 1988. He succeeded his younger brother, President-elect Bachir Gemayel, who was assassinated days before taking office. A member of the prominent Maronite Christian Gemayel family, he led the Kataeb Party (Phalange) and remains an influential figure in Lebanese politics.

Early life and career

Amine Gemayel was born in Bikfaya, the eldest son of Pierre Gemayel, founder of the Kataeb Party. He studied law at Saint Joseph University in Beirut and worked as a lawyer before entering parliament in 1970, winning a by-election in the Metn district. During the early years of the Lebanese Civil War he was seen as more moderate than his brother Bachir, who commanded the Lebanese Forces militia.

Presidency

Following Bachir's assassination on 14 September 1982, Amine was elected president by the National Assembly on 21 September 1982. His presidency was dominated by the civil war, the Israeli invasion of 1982 and subsequent occupation of the south, and the presence of Syrian forces. His government negotiated the US-brokered 17 May Agreement with Israel in 1983, which was abrogated the following year under Syrian and domestic pressure. The last months of his term were marked by a constitutional crisis: in 1988, unable to secure the election of a successor, he appointed an interim military government under General Michel Aoun, while a rival civilian government under Salim al-Hoss claimed legitimacy — a dual-government standoff that lasted until the Taif Agreement.

Later life

Gemayel went into exile after 1988, returning to Lebanon in 2000 following the Israeli withdrawal and Syria's reduced dominance. He resumed leadership of the Kataeb Party and served again as a deputy. His son Pierre Amine Gemayel, a minister, was assassinated in 2006.
