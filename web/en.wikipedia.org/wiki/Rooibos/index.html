Rooibos

Rooibos (Afrikaans for "red bush"; Aspalathus linearis) is a plant in the legume family grown in the Cederberg region of South Africa's Western Cape. Its needle-like leaves are harvested and oxidised to produce the reddish-brown herbal tea also called rooibos, bush tea or redbush tea.

Production

Rooibos grows only in a small area around Clanwilliam in the Cederberg. The leaves and stems are cut, bruised and left to ferment (oxidise) in heaps, developing the characteristic red colour and sweet flavour, then dried in the sun and pasteurised. An unoxidised "green" rooibos is also produced. The tea is naturally caffeine-free and low in tannins compared with black tea, and contains antioxidants such as aspalathin, a compound found almost nowhere else.

History and status

The Khoisan peoples of the region used rooibos for centuries; commercial production began in the early 20th century, and seeds were famously collected with the help of ants' nests. Rooibos was granted a Protected Designation of Origin in the European Union in 2021 — the first African food product to receive the status — and a 2019 benefit-sharing agreement recognised the Khoi and San peoples' traditional knowledge, entitling them to a share of the industry's proceeds.
