Walt Disney Pictures

Walt Disney Pictures is an American film production company and studio within Walt Disney Studios, a major division of the Walt Disney Company. Based at the Walt Disney Studios in Burbank, California, it is the flagship live-action and family-film label of the company, releasing films intended primarily for general and family audiences under the Disney brand.

History

The Disney company was founded in 1923 by Walt and Roy Disney. Although the studio was famous first for animation, it moved into live-action with Treasure Island (1950) and productions such as 20,000 Leagues Under the Sea (1954) and Mary Poppins (1964). The Walt Disney Pictures banner was formally established in 1983, when Disney reorganised its film output into separate labels, with Touchstone Pictures created the following year for more mature content.

Output

Walt Disney Pictures releases the studio's family-oriented films, both animated (in partnership with Walt Disney Animation Studios and Pixar, whose films carry the Disney label in distribution) and live-action. Its most successful properties include the Pirates of the Caribbean franchise, launched in 2003, and a long-running series of live-action remakes and adaptations of animated classics, including Beauty and the Beast (2017), Aladdin (2019), and The Lion King (2019), each of which grossed more than $1 billion worldwide.

Logo

The studio's production logo, depicting a fairytale castle with fireworks and a shooting star, is one of the most recognisable in cinema. Introduced in 1985 with The Black Cauldron, it has been revised several times, most notably in a CGI version created by Weta Digital in 2006.
