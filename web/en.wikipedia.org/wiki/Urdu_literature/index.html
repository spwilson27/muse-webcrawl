Urdu literature

The ghazal and marsiya are hallmark forms of Urdu poetry

Urdu literature encompasses the body of literary works written in the Urdu language, which developed in northern India from the interaction of Persian, Arabic, and Turkish vocabulary with local Indo-Aryan speech. It flourished from the 17th century onward in the courts of the Deccan, Delhi, and Lucknow, and today is centred in Pakistan and India.

Poetry

Poetry is the most celebrated part of Urdu literature. Its principal forms include the ghazal (lyric poem of autonomous couplets), the marsiya (elegy, especially on the martyrdom of Husayn at Karbala), the masnavi (long narrative poem), and the qasida (ode). Major poets include Wali Mohammed Wali, Mir Taqi Mir, Mirza Ghalib, and Muhammad Iqbal; the poetic symposium, or mushaira, remains a living institution.

Prose

Urdu prose developed through translations and romances (dastan) in the 19th century, followed by the novel — pioneered by Nazir Ahmad's Mirat-ul-Uroos (1869) — and the short story, in which Premchand, Saadat Hasan Manto, Ismat Chughtai, and Qurratulain Hyder are central figures.
