Fritz Perls

Frederick Salomon Perls (July 8, 1893 – March 14, 1970), better known as Fritz Perls, was a German-born psychiatrist, psychoanalyst and psychotherapist. Along with his wife Laura Perls and Paul Goodman, he developed Gestalt therapy, a form of psychotherapy emphasizing personal responsibility, present-moment awareness and the relationship between therapist and client.

Fritz Perls
Born: July 8, 1893, Berlin, Germany
Died: March 14, 1970, Chicago, United States
Known for: Gestalt therapy

Life

Perls was born in Berlin and studied medicine, serving as a medical officer in the First World War. He underwent psychoanalytic training and worked with figures such as Kurt Goldstein, whose holistic neurology influenced Gestalt therapy's emphasis on the whole person and the organism-environment field. In the 1930s he and Laura fled Nazi Germany for South Africa, where they founded a psychoanalytic institute, and in 1946 moved to New York.

Gestalt therapy

The foundational text Gestalt Therapy: Excitement and Growth in the Human Personality (1951), co-authored with Ralph Hefferline and Paul Goodman, set out the approach: awareness of the here and now, direct experiencing rather than talking about problems, and techniques such as the empty-chair dialogue. In the 1960s Perls became a prominent figure at the Esalen Institute in California, and his demonstration sessions made him a well-known figure of the human potential movement. His concise "Gestalt prayer" ("I do my thing and you do your thing...") became famous, though Perls himself downplayed it.
