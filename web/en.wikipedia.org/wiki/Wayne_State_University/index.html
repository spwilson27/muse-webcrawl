Wayne State University

Type: Public research university
Established: 1868
Location: Detroit, Michigan, U.S.
Students: ~24,000
Mascot: Warriors
Colors: Green and gold

Wayne State University (WSU) is a public research university in Detroit, Michigan. Founded in 1868, it consists of 13 schools and colleges offering approximately 350 programs to nearly 24,000 graduate and undergraduate students. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity".

The university originated in 1868 with the founding of the Detroit Medical College, now the School of Medicine. Other predecessor institutions included the Detroit College of Medicine, the Detroit City College and the Detroit Teachers College, which were combined into the Wayne University in 1933. It became Wayne State University in 1956 when it joined Michigan's system of state-supported universities.

Campus

The main campus covers more than 200 acres in Detroit's Midtown Cultural Center, adjacent to institutions such as the Detroit Institute of Arts and the Detroit Public Library. The campus includes more than 100 buildings, among them Old Main, the McGregor Memorial Conference Center designed by Minoru Yamasaki, and the university's medical campus in the Detroit Medical Center.

Academics

Wayne State comprises 13 schools and colleges, including the College of Liberal Arts and Sciences, the School of Medicine, the Law School, the College of Engineering and the Eugene Applebaum College of Pharmacy and Health Sciences. Its medical school is one of the largest single-campus medical schools in the United States and is recognised for research in neuroscience, oncology and population health.

Athletics

The university's athletic teams, the Wayne State Warriors, compete in NCAA Division II as members of the Great Lakes Intercollegiate Athletic Conference.
