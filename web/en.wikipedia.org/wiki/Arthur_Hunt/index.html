Arthur Surridge Hunt (1 March 1871 – 18 September 1934) was an English papyrologist. With his lifelong collaborator Bernard Pyne Grenfell, he excavated and published the Oxyrhynchus Papyri, one of the greatest recoveries of ancient literature and documents in history, transforming knowledge of the classical and early Christian worlds.

Life and career

Hunt was educated at Queen's College, Oxford, where he met Grenfell. From 1896–97, under the Egypt Exploration Fund, the two began excavating the rubbish mounds of Oxyrhynchus (el-Bahnasa) in Egypt, uncovering hundreds of thousands of papyrus fragments dating from the Ptolemaic and Roman periods. Hunt succeeded Grenfell as the driving force of the project after Grenfell's health failed; he became Oxford's first Professor of Papyrology in 1913 (a chair created for him) and edited volume after volume of The Oxyrhynchus Papyri.

Discoveries

Among the texts Grenfell and Hunt recovered were lost poems of Sappho, Pindar and Sophocles' Ichneutae; the "Sayings of Jesus" fragments (later identified with the Gospel of Thomas); early Christian texts; and an unmatched archive of everyday documents — tax receipts, private letters, contracts — that revolutionised social and economic history of Roman Egypt.

Honours

Hunt was elected a Fellow of the British Academy and received honorary degrees and foreign orders; a street in Oxford (Hunt's former home area) and the Grenfell–Hunt legacy underpin the modern discipline of papyrology at Oxford, where the Oxyrhynchus project continues.
