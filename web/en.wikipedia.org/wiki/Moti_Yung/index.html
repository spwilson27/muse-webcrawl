Moti Yung

Mordechai M. "Moti" Yung is a cryptographer and computer scientist known for work on cryptography, including the concept of cryptovirology (with Adam Young), threshold cryptosystems, and secure multiparty computation. He has held research positions at IBM, CertCo, RSA Laboratories, Snap Inc., and Google, and is a research professor at Columbia University.

Career

Yung received his PhD from Columbia University in 1988 under Zvi Galil. He worked at IBM's T.J. Watson Research Center, then in industry at CertCo, Bankers Trust, RSA, and later as research director at Snap and a research scientist at Google working on privacy and security.

Contributions

With Adam Young he coined cryptovirology, studying how cryptography can be used in malware such as ransomware and kleptography. He contributed to threshold signatures, distributed key generation, zero-knowledge proofs, and the security of cryptographic protocols against adaptive adversaries.
