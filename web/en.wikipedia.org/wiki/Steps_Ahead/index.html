Steps Ahead is an American jazz fusion group founded by vibraphonist Mike Mainieri in 1979. The band emerged from the loose collective of New York session musicians who had played together in the Brecker Brothers band and in various studio contexts, and it became one of the most technically accomplished and influential electric jazz ensembles of the 1980s.

History

The group originally performed under the name Steps, recording live albums in Japan in 1979 and 1980. The classic lineup coalesced around Mainieri, saxophonist Michael Brecker, pianist Don Grolnick, bassist Eddie Gómez and drummer Steve Gadd; Peter Erskine later took over the drum chair. After signing with Elektra Musician, the band changed its name to Steps Ahead because another group held rights to the name Steps.

Their self-titled American debut, Steps Ahead (1983), and the follow-up Modern Times (1984) showcased a sophisticated blend of acoustic jazz virtuosity and contemporary electric textures. Brazilian pianist Eliane Elias joined for Magnetic (1986). Personnel rotated frequently in later years as members pursued solo careers, but Mainieri kept the band active through albums such as N.Y.C. (1989), Yin-Yang (1992), Vibe (1994) and Steppin' Out (2016).

Musical style

Steps Ahead's music combines bebop-derived improvisation with funk rhythms, Latin influences and electronic instrumentation. Mainieri's vibraphone, often processed electronically, gives the group a distinctive timbre, while Brecker's tenor saxophone work is widely regarded as among the finest of his career.
