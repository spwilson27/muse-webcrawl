Lester Bowie (October 11, 1941 – November 8, 1999) was an American jazz trumpet player and composer. A founding member of the Art Ensemble of Chicago and a leading figure of the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM), Bowie was known for combining avant-garde techniques with a deep grounding in the whole history of jazz trumpet, from New Orleans to free improvisation.

Early life and career

Bowie was born in Frederick, Maryland, and raised in St. Louis, Missouri, in a musical family; his father was a professional trumpeter. He studied trumpet and played in rhythm and blues bands, backing artists such as Little Milton and Albert King, before moving to Chicago in 1966. There he joined the AACM and studied with Muhal Richard Abrams.

Art Ensemble of Chicago

In 1968 Bowie helped form the Art Ensemble of Chicago with Roscoe Mitchell, Joseph Jarman, and Malachi Favors. The group became famous for its theatrical performances, face paint, and slogan "Great Black Music: Ancient to the Future." Bowie often performed in a white laboratory coat, a visual signature of his wit and stagecraft.

Later work

In the 1980s Bowie founded Brass Fantasy, a brass ensemble that applied avant-garde jazz methods to pop and soul material by artists such as Whitney Houston and Michael Jackson. He also led Lester Bowie's New York Organ Ensemble and recorded extensively as a leader. Bowie died of liver cancer in Brooklyn in 1999.
