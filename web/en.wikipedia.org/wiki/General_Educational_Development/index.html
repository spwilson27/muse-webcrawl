General Educational Development (GED) tests are a group of four subject tests which, when passed, certify that the test-taker has United States or Canadian high-school-level academic skills. The GED credential is widely accepted as a high school equivalency diploma for people who did not graduate from high school.

The modern GED is administered by computer at official testing centres.

History

The GED programme began in 1942, when the United States Armed Forces Institute asked the American Council on Education to develop tests to measure the academic skills of servicemen returning from the Second World War, many of whom had left school to enlist. Passing allowed veterans to obtain credentials for civilian employment and further education. The tests were opened to civilians in later decades, and major revisions followed in 1978, 1988, 2002, and 2014, when the test became computer-based and aligned to college- and career-readiness standards.

Structure and scoring

The four modules are Reasoning through Language Arts, Mathematical Reasoning, Science, and Social Studies. Each is scored on a scale of 100 to 200; 145 is the passing standard, with higher bands (165 and 175) denoting college readiness and possible college credit. More than 20 million people have earned the GED credential since its inception.
