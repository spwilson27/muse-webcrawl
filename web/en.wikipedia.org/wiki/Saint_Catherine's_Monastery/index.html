Saint Catherine's Monastery

Saint Catherine's Monastery (Arabic: دير القديسة كاترين; Greek: Μονὴ τῆς Ἁγίας Αἰκατερίνης), officially the Sacred Autonomous Royal Monastery of Saint Catherine of the Holy and God-Trodden Mount Sinai, is a Greek Orthodox monastery located on the Sinai Peninsula in Egypt, at the mouth of a gorge at the foot of Mount Sinai, near the town of Saint Catherine. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited Christian monasteries in the world.

History

The site was sacred long before the monastery: tradition identifies it with the Burning Bush seen by Moses, and a chapel was built there by Saint Helena in 330 AD. The fortified monastery itself was constructed by order of the Byzantine emperor Justinian I between 548 and 565, incorporating the Chapel of the Burning Bush. The monastery is named for Saint Catherine of Alexandria, whose relics, according to tradition, were carried by angels to nearby Mount Catherine and later brought to the monastery.

Library and icons

The monastery's library preserves the second-largest collection of early codices and manuscripts in the world, outnumbered only by the Vatican Library. It includes the 4th-century Codex Sinaiticus (the larger part of which is now in the British Library) and the Syriac Sinaiticus palimpsest. The monastery also holds one of the world's most important collections of early icons, including the 6th-century encaustic Christ Pantocrator icon, which survived iconoclasm because of the monastery's remoteness. The Sinai Palimpsests Project has used spectral imaging to recover erased texts from hundreds of its palimpsest leaves.

The site today

The monastery is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and remains home to a small community of monks under its own archbishop, consecrated by the Patriarch of Jerusalem. Pilgrims and tourists visit the basilica, the Chapel of the Burning Bush, and the monastery's museum of icons and manuscripts, and many climb Mount Sinai (Jebel Musa) at night to reach the summit at dawn.
