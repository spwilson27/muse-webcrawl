Johnson solid

DefinitionStrictly convex polyhedron whose faces are regular polygons, but which is not uniform
Count92
Named afterNorman Johnson

A Johnson solid is a strictly convex polyhedron each of whose faces is a regular polygon, but which is not a uniform polyhedron — that is, it is not a Platonic solid, Archimedean solid, prism or antiprism. There are exactly 92 such solids.

History and enumeration

In 1966 Norman W. Johnson published a list of 92 solids and gave them names and numbers (J1 through J92), conjecturing that the list was complete. In 1969 Victor Zalgaller proved that there are no others. Examples include the square pyramid (J1), the pentagonal cupola (J5) and the elongated pentagonal orthobicupola (J38). Elementary Johnson solids cannot be decomposed into other Johnson solids by coplanar merging; others are constructed by augmentation, diminution and gyrations of simpler pieces.

Johnson solids arise in chemistry, architecture and puzzle design, and their classification completes, in a sense, the enumeration of convex polyhedra with regular faces begun with the Platonic and Archimedean solids.
