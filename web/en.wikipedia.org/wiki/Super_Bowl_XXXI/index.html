Super Bowl XXXI

Super Bowl XXXI was an American football game played on January 26, 1997, at the Louisiana Superdome in New Orleans, to decide the champion of the National Football League (NFL) for the 1996 season. The National Football Conference (NFC) champion Green Bay Packers defeated the American Football Conference (AFC) champion New England Patriots, 35–21, winning their third Super Bowl and first NFL championship since Super Bowl II in 1968.

Game summary

Green Bay quarterback Brett Favre threw an 81-yard touchdown pass to Andre Rison on the Packers' second play and ran for another score, earning game MVP honours. New England, behind Drew Bledsoe touchdown passes to Keith Byars and Ben Coates, briefly led 14–10. The turning point came in the third quarter: after the Patriots cut the lead to 27–21, Desmond Howard returned the ensuing kickoff 99 yards for a touchdown — still a Super Bowl record — and Favre's two-point conversion pass to Mark Chmura set the final margin. Howard, with 244 return yards, became the first special teams player named Super Bowl MVP.

The win capped a celebrated season in which Green Bay fielded the league's top-ranked offense and defense, and defensive end Reggie White recorded three sacks in the game, a Super Bowl record at the time.

Entertainment

Luther Vandross sang the national anthem, and the halftime show featured the Blues Brothers (Dan Aykroyd, John Goodman, and James Belushi performing as the Elwood character) with ZZ Top and James Brown. The game was the first Super Bowl broadcast by Fox under its NFC television contract.
