Calouste Gulbenkian Museum

Calouste Gulbenkian Museum
Location: Lisbon, Portugal
Established: 1969
Collection: about 6,000 items
Founder: Calouste Gulbenkian

The Calouste Gulbenkian Museum (Museu Calouste Gulbenkian) in Lisbon, Portugal, houses the private art collection of the Armenian-British oil magnate and philanthropist Calouste Gulbenkian (1869–1955), who bequeathed it to Portugal, where he had spent his final years. The museum, part of the Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation complex, opened in 1969.

Collection

The roughly 6,000-piece collection spans some 5,000 years, from ancient Egyptian and Mesopotamian art through Greek and Roman antiquities, Armenian manuscripts, Islamic art, Chinese and Japanese objects, and European painting and decorative arts. European highlights include works by Rembrandt, Rubens, Turner, Monet, Renoir, and Degas, along with an outstanding group of jewels and glass by the French Art Nouveau designer René Lalique, a personal friend of Gulbenkian.

The building

The museum stands in landscaped gardens within the foundation's Lisbon headquarters, a landmark of Portuguese modernist architecture. A separate Modern Collection building (the Centro de Arte Moderna, opened 1984 and redesigned in 2024 by Kengo Kuma) displays Portuguese modern and contemporary art.
