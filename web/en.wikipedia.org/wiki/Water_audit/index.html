Water audit

A water audit is a systematic accounting of water entering, used within, and leaving a facility or supply system.

A water audit is a method of quantifying and accounting for all water flows into and out of a facility, building, irrigation system or public water supply network, in order to identify losses, inefficiencies and opportunities for water conservation.

Method

An audit typically begins with a water balance: metered inputs are compared with the sum of end uses — such as cooling, process water, sanitation, irrigation and domestic use — and with losses. In municipal supply systems, the difference between water produced and water billed is called non-revenue water, which includes leakage from mains, metering inaccuracies and unauthorised consumption. Standard methodologies, such as those promoted by the American Water Works Association and the International Water Association, break the balance into standard components so results can be benchmarked between utilities.

Applications

Water audits are used by utilities to target leak detection and pipe renewal, by industries to reduce process water and discharge costs, and by large buildings to find faulty fixtures and cooling-tower losses. Many jurisdictions require periodic audits by large water suppliers as part of water-efficiency regulation.
