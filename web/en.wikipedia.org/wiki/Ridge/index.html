Ridge

A ridge is a long, narrow raised landform — a chain of hills or mountains, a strip of high ground, or an elongated crest — whose sides slope down to lower terrain. Ridges are among the most common features of mountainous landscapes.

Types

Mountain ridges form by tectonic uplift and erosion; knife-edge ridges between glacial cirques are called arêtes. Mid-ocean ridges are vast submarine mountain systems where new oceanic crust is created at divergent plate boundaries. Other ridges include eskers and drumlin-like features deposited by glaciers, beach ridges, and pressure ridges in sea ice. In meteorology, a ridge is an elongated area of relatively high atmospheric pressure.

Significance

Ridges often form drainage divides between river basins, natural boundaries, and defensive positions; ridge trails are popular with hikers because they offer views to both sides.
