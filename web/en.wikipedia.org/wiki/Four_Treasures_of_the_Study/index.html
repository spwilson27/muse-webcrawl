Four Treasures of the Study

The Four Treasures of the Study (Chinese: 文房四寶; pinyin: wénfáng sìbǎo) is an expression used to refer to the brush, ink, paper and ink stone used in Chinese calligraphy and painting, and in the broader literati cultures of Korea, Japan and Vietnam. The expression was coined during the Northern and Southern dynasties period.

The four treasures

  • Brush (筆, bǐ) — made of animal hair (weasel, goat, wolf) set in a bamboo or wooden handle.
  • Ink (墨, mò) — ink sticks of soot and glue, ground with water on the ink stone; famous production centres include Huizhou.
  • Paper (紙, zhǐ) — especially Xuan paper from Jing County, Anhui, prized for absorbency and durability.
  • Ink stone (硯, yàn) — carved stones, such as Duan stone from Guangdong and She stone from Jiangxi, used to grind ink.

Cultural role

Mastery of the Four Treasures was essential to the scholar-official class; connoisseurship of fine brushes, inks, papers and ink stones became an art in itself, and celebrated examples were collected as treasures.
