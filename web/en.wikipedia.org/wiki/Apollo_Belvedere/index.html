Apollo Belvedere
Type: Marble sculpture
Height: 2.24 m
Location: Vatican Museums
Copy of: Greek bronze, attributed to Leochares tradition

The Apollo Belvedere (Apollo of the Belvedere) is a celebrated marble sculpture, a Roman copy (c. AD 120–140) of a Greek bronze original, depicting Apollo having just shot an arrow. Housed in the Cortile del Belvedere, Vatican Museums.

History and influence

Rediscovered in the late 15th century, placed in the Vatican Belvedere by 1511. Admired by Winckelmann as the highest ideal of Greek art, it profoundly influenced Neoclassicism, though 20th-century critics were cooler.

Description

Apollo strides forward, right arm extended (holding a bow, now lost/fragmentary), wearing a cloak draped over his arm, quiver over shoulder, hair in classical knots.
