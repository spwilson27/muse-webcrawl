Nils Liedholm

Nils Erik Liedholm (8 October 1922 – 5 November 2007) was a Swedish football midfielder and coach. Nicknamed Il Barone ("The Baron") in Italy, he was famed for his passing accuracy — legend held that he once went two years without misplacing a pass for AC Milan — and formed the celebrated "Gre-No-Li" forward line with compatriots Gunnar Gren and Gunnar Nordahl.

Nils Liedholm

Born: 8 October 1922, Valdemarsvik, Sweden

Died: 5 November 2007, Cuccaro Monferrato, Italy

Position: Midfielder

Clubs: IFK Norrköping, AC Milan

National team: Sweden (23 caps)

Playing career

Liedholm began his career with IFK Norrköping, winning two Swedish championships, and won a gold medal with Sweden at the 1948 London Olympics. In 1949 he joined AC Milan, where he played 359 league matches, scored 81 goals and won four Serie A titles. He captained Sweden to the final of the 1958 World Cup on home soil, scoring the opening goal against Brazil in the final, which Sweden lost 5–2.

Coaching career

After retiring in 1961, Liedholm became a highly successful coach in Italy, most notably with Roma, whom he led to the Scudetto in 1983 and to the European Cup final in 1984, and with Milan, Fiorentina and Verona. Renowned as an early proponent of zonal marking, he settled in Italy, running a vineyard in Piedmont, and died in Cuccaro Monferrato in 2007.
