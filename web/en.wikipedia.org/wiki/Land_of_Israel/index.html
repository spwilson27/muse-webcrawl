Land of Israel

The Land of Israel (Hebrew: אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל, Eretz Yisrael) is the traditional Jewish name for the region of the southern Levant between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean Sea. In biblical tradition it is the land promised by God to Abraham and his descendants, also called the Promised Land and, in earlier passages, the land of Canaan. Its exact extent is defined differently in different biblical passages and later Jewish law; the term is used in Judaism with religious and legal significance, and in modern times also in political discourse about the State of Israel and the Palestinian territories.

Biblical definitions

Genesis 15 describes the covenant boundaries as stretching "from the river of Egypt to the great river, the Euphrates", the maximal extent. Numbers 34 gives a more restricted boundary for the land to be divided among the tribes, roughly from the Brook of Egypt and the Mediterranean in the west to the Jordan and the Dead Sea in the east, with a northern limit near the entrance of Hamath. Ezekiel 47 gives yet another scheme for the restored land. Rabbinic halakha distinguishes areas settled in the time of Joshua from those settled by returnees from Babylon, because commandments "dependent on the land" — such as tithes and the sabbatical year — apply with differing force in each.

History

The name "Israel" first appears in an external source on the Egyptian Merneptah Stele (c. 1208 BC). The biblical kingdoms of Israel and Judah occupied parts of the region in the early first millennium BC; after the Babylonian exile and under Persian, Hellenistic, and Roman rule, Jewish political life centred on the province of Judea until the failed revolts against Rome (AD 66–73 and 132–135), after which Rome renamed the province Syria Palaestina. Jewish religious attachment to the land persisted through nearly two millennia of diaspora, expressed in liturgy, the direction of prayer, and periodic return migration. The region subsequently came under Byzantine, early Islamic, Crusader, Mamluk, and Ottoman rule; from the late 19th century the Zionist movement promoted Jewish national revival there, leading to the British Mandate for Palestine and, in 1948, the State of Israel.

Modern usage

In Israeli usage Eretz Yisrael can denote the State of Israel within its recognised borders, or in some political movements (notably the Greater Israel ideology) the whole area between the Jordan and the sea, including the West Bank and sometimes the Gaza Strip — territories whose status remains disputed. The term is also used neutrally in historical and archaeological scholarship for the region in antiquity.
