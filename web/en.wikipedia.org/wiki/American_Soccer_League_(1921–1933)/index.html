The first American Soccer League (ASL) was a professional soccer league based in the northeastern United States that existed from 1921 to 1933. It was the first genuinely professional soccer league of consequence in the United States and a high point of the sport's early popularity in the country, drawing strong immigrant-supported clubs and paying salaries competitive with those in the Scottish and English leagues.

History

The league was formed in 1921 by the merger of teams from the National Association Football League and the Southern New England Soccer League. Clubs were centred on industrial cities of the Northeast, including Fall River, New Bedford, Bethlehem, Brooklyn, and New York. The Fall River Marksmen dominated the league, winning seven titles. Bethlehem Steel, one of the great American teams of the era, drew large crowds, and the league imported notable players, including many Scottish internationals.

The league's success rested on factory-town support and ethnic communities, and several of its players formed the core of the United States team that reached the semifinals of the first FIFA World Cup in 1930. However, the "Soccer War" of 1928–1929 — a dispute with the United States Football Association over participation in the National Challenge Cup — led clubs to boycott the cup and briefly form a rival league, draining finances and spectators. The onset of the Great Depression in 1929 then collapsed attendance and sponsorship; the league struggled on in weakened form and folded in 1933. A second, semi-professional American Soccer League was founded the same year.

Legacy

Historians regard the first ASL as the high-water mark of American soccer before the modern era; its collapse set the sport back for decades in the United States until the North American Soccer League of the 1970s.
