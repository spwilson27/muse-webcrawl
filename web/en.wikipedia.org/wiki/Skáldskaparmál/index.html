Skáldskaparmál

Skáldskaparmál (Old Norse for "the language of poetry") is the second major part of the Prose Edda, written by the Icelandic scholar and statesman Snorri Sturluson around 1220. It is a handbook teaching the diction of traditional skaldic poetry, especially the elaborate periphrases known as kennings and the synonyms known as heiti.

Structure and content

The work is framed as a dialogue in which Ægir, a sea-giant renowned for brewing ale for the gods, visits Asgard and questions the god Bragi about the origins of the kennings used in poetry. Bragi's answers narrate many myths — among them the abduction of Iðunn and her apples, the story of Thor's duel with Hrungnir, the tale of the mead of poetry, and the theft of the necklace Brísingamen — because most kennings allude to these stories and cannot be understood without them.

Following the dialogue, Skáldskaparmál continues with systematic lists of kennings and heiti for gods, people, places, the sea, ships, weapons, gold and many other concepts, illustrated with quotations from named skalds. These catalogues preserve verses by dozens of poets that would otherwise have been lost, making the work one of the most important sources for Old Norse mythology and skaldic verse.

Purpose

Snorri's project in the Prose Edda as a whole was to preserve the traditional art of the skalds in a Christian Iceland where the pagan myths underlying its imagery were being forgotten. Skáldskaparmál supplements the mythological summary of Gylfaginning with the detailed story material and worked examples that a practising poet needed.
