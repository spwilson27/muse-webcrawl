Geoffrey Plantagenet, Count of Anjou

Geoffrey V (24 August 1113 – 7 September 1151), called the Fair (le Bel) or Plantagenet, was Count of Anjou, Touraine and Maine by inheritance from 1129, and also Duke of Normandy by conquest from 1144.

Geoffrey Plantagenet
Born: 24 August 1113
Died: 7 September 1151, Château-du-Loir
Spouse: Empress Matilda
Issue: Henry II of England, Geoffrey of Nantes, William FitzEmpress

Life

Geoffrey was the elder son of Fulk V of Anjou. His nickname comes from the sprig of broom (Latin planta genista) he reportedly wore in his hat, giving the Plantagenet name to his dynasty. In 1128 he married Matilda, widowed Holy Roman Empress and daughter and heir of Henry I of England.

Career

During the Anarchy — the civil war between Matilda and Stephen of Blois — Geoffrey conquered Normandy (1141–1144) rather than campaigning in England, securing the duchy that his son inherited. His eldest son became Henry II of England in 1154, founding the Angevin Empire. Geoffrey died suddenly at Château-du-Loir in 1151, aged 38, and was buried in Le Mans Cathedral, where his enamel funerary plaque survives.
