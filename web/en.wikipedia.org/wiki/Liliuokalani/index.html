Liliʻuokalani (born Lydia Liliʻu Loloku Walania Kamakaʻeha; 2 September 1838 – 11 November 1917) was the last sovereign monarch of the Kingdom of Hawaii, reigning from January 1891 until the overthrow of the monarchy on 17 January 1893. She was also a composer of many musical works, the best known being the song "Aloha ʻOe".

Early life

Liliʻuokalani was born in Honolulu to the high chiefs Caesar Kapaʻakea and Analea Keohokālole, and was a younger sister of King Kalākaua. Under the Hawaiian custom of hānai she was raised by Abner Pākī and his wife Laura Kōnia. She was educated at the Chiefs' Children's School (the Royal School) and in 1862 married John Owen Dominis, an American official who later became Governor of Oʻahu; the couple had no children of their own but raised three hānai children. She served as regent during her brother's world tour of 1881 and again during his final illness.

Reign and overthrow

Liliʻuokalani ascended the throne on her brother's death in 1891. Determined to restore powers the monarchy had lost under the 1887 "Bayonet Constitution", she drafted a new constitution restoring voting rights and royal authority. On 17 January 1893 a group of American and European businessmen, backed by the United States minister John L. Stevens and troops landed from the USS Boston, deposed her and proclaimed a Provisional Government. The queen yielded under protest to what she called the superior force of the United States, expecting Washington to undo the act. President Grover Cleveland's Blount Report condemned the overthrow, but the Provisional Government refused restoration and proclaimed the Republic of Hawaii in 1894.

After a failed royalist counter-rebellion in 1895, Liliʻuokalani was arrested and imprisoned in an upstairs bedroom of ʻIolani Palace. To secure the release of her supporters, she formally abdicated and signed a statement renouncing the throne. She later travelled to Washington to plead against annexation, and in 1898 published her memoir, Hawaii's Story by Hawaii's Queen.

Later life and legacy

Hawaii was annexed by the United States in 1898 during the Spanish–American War. Liliʻuokalani lived at her residence, Washington Place, until her death in 1917, and was given a state funeral. Her trust, established in 1909, still operates an orphanage and services for Hawaiian children. In 1993, the United States Congress passed the Apology Resolution, signed by President Bill Clinton, formally apologizing for the overthrow of the Kingdom of Hawaii.
