Dream yoga

Dream yoga or milam (Tibetan: rmi lam, "dream practice") is a set of techniques, developed within Tibetan Buddhism and Bön traditions, for attaining lucidity and control within the dream state. It forms one of the Six Yogas of Naropa and aims at the realization that waking and dreaming experience alike are illusory, ultimately serving preparation for the bardo state after death.

Background

Dream yoga is part of the completion stage practices of Vajrayana Buddhism. In the Six Yogas of Naropa, it follows practices such as inner heat (tummo) and precedes teachings on luminosity and the intermediate state. The practice is also found in Bön, Tibet's indigenous religious tradition.

Practice

The traditional progression of dream yoga includes:

  • Recognizing the dream — becoming lucid within the dream state.
  • Overcoming fear — doing in the dream what frightens one, to see dream content as harmless.
  • Transformation — changing the size, number and nature of dream objects at will.
  • Realizing the dream body — using the dream body to visit places and realms.
  • Non-difference — realizing the identity of dream and waking experience.

Purpose

The ultimate aim is not dream control for its own sake but insight into the illusory nature of all experience (maya), and the ability to maintain awareness through sleep and death. Dream yoga is considered a training ground for the bardo, the intermediate state between death and rebirth.
