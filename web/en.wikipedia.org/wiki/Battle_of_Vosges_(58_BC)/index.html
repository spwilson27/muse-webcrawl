Battle of Vosges (58 BC)

The Battle of Vosges, also known as the Battle of Vesontio, was fought in September 58 BC between the Roman army of Julius Caesar and the Germanic Suebi led by Ariovistus, near the foothills of the Vosges mountains in eastern Gaul (in the vicinity of modern Belfort or Mulhouse). Caesar's victory expelled the Suebi from Gaul and was one of the major battles of the Gallic Wars.

Background

Ariovistus had led his Suebi across the Rhine years earlier and had been recognised by the Roman Senate as a king and friend of the Roman people. By 58 BC Gallic tribes appealed to Caesar against his dominance. After a failed parley, Caesar seized Vesontio (Besançon) and marched on the Germanic host, overcoming a panic in his own army by a famous speech to his officers.

The battle

For several days Ariovistus manoeuvred to cut Caesar's supply lines before finally offering battle. The Germans advanced rapidly in tribal phalanxes to close before the Romans could use their javelins. The fighting was hardest on the Roman left, until the young Publius Licinius Crassus, commanding the cavalry, sent in the third line on his own initiative and turned the tide. The Suebi broke and fled some fifty miles to the Rhine; many, including two of Ariovistus's wives and a daughter, were killed in the pursuit. Ariovistus himself escaped across the river in a boat and died of his wounds not long after.

Aftermath

Caesar's account in De Bello Gallico presents the battle as the deliverance of Gaul from Germanic invasion. With the Suebi driven back across the Rhine, Caesar put his legions into winter quarters among the Sequani — a presence that led directly to the next campaigns of the Gallic Wars.
