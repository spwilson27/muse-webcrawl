Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk

Died: 14 September 1937, Lány, Czechoslovakia

Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk (7 March 1850 – 14 September 1937) was a Czechoslovak statesman, sociologist and philosopher who served as the first President of Czechoslovakia from 1918 until 1935. He is regarded as the founding father of the Czechoslovak state.

Early life and scholarship

Masaryk was born in Hodonín, Moravia, the son of a Slovak coachman and a Moravian mother. He studied philosophy at the University of Vienna and became a professor at Charles University in Prague. His wife, Charlotte Garrigue, was an American whose surname he adopted as his middle name.

Road to independence

During the First World War Masaryk campaigned abroad for the dissolution of Austria-Hungary and the creation of an independent Czechoslovak state, organising the Czechoslovak Legions and winning Allied recognition. The republic was proclaimed on 28 October 1918.

Presidency and legacy

Re-elected three times, Masaryk embodied the democratic and humanist ideals of the First Republic. He resigned in 1935 due to ill health and was succeeded by Edvard Beneš. Masaryk University in Brno and numerous streets and squares bear his name.
