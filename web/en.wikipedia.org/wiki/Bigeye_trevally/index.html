The bigeye trevally (Caranx sexfasciatus), also known as the bigeye jack, great trevally, six-banded trevally and dusky jack, is a species of widespread large marine fish classified in the jack family Carangidae. The bigeye trevally is distributed throughout the tropical waters of the Indian and Pacific Oceans, ranging from South Africa in the west to the eastern coast of the Americas in the east. It inhabits coastal and offshore waters, moving from deep reefs to shallow coastal waters as it ages.

The species is distinguished by its large eyes and the six dark vertical bands that give it its specific name, sexfasciatus, meaning "six-banded". It is a powerful predatory fish, feeding on a variety of fish, crustaceans and cephalopods, and is known to form large daytime schools around reef structures, dispersing at night to feed.

Taxonomy and naming

The bigeye trevally was first scientifically described in 1825 by the French naturalists Jean René Constant Quoy and Joseph Paul Gaimard, based on a specimen collected from Waigeo Island in Indonesia. The species has been placed in the genus Caranx since its description, though it has occasionally been moved to other carangid genera by various authors.

Description

The bigeye trevally is a large fish, growing to a maximum known length of 120 cm and a weight of 18 kg. The body is moderately deep and compressed, with a steep, blunt head profile dominated by a large eye. Adults are generally silvery-grey to brassy in colour, while juveniles display the characteristic six dark bars across the body that fade with age.

Distribution and habitat

The bigeye trevally has a wide Indo-Pacific distribution, extending into the eastern Pacific as far as the Galápagos Islands and the coast of the Americas. Adults prefer deeper offshore reefs at depths of up to 100 m, while juveniles inhabit shallow coastal waters, estuaries and even rivers.

Biology and ecology

Bigeye trevally are nocturnal predators, forming aggregations over reefs during the day and moving into shallower water at night to hunt small fish, squid and crustaceans. Spawning occurs in summer months in large aggregations. The species is an important food fish throughout its range and is also valued as a game fish.

Relationship to humans

The bigeye trevally is taken by commercial fisheries throughout its range using nets and lines, and is marketed fresh, salted and dried. It is also a popular target for recreational anglers. There have been occasional reports of ciguatera poisoning associated with larger specimens.
