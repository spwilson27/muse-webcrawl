German wine classification

The German wine classification system is a set of regulations, defined primarily by the German Wine Law of 1971 (and its subsequent revisions), that classifies wines according to the ripeness of the grapes at harvest, the region of origin, and other factors. Unlike the French appellation system, which emphasises geography, the German system has traditionally placed its greatest weight on the must weight — the sugar content of the grape juice at harvest — as the principal measure of quality.

Quality categories

German wines are divided into four broad quality categories, in ascending order:

TafelweinTable wineThe lowest category; largely replaced in practice by the EU-level categories.
LandweinCountry wineFrom one of the designated Landwein regions; must be dry or off-dry.

Prädikat levels

Within the Prädikatswein category, wines are graded by increasing must weight into six Prädikate:

  • Kabinett – light, delicately fruity wines made from fully ripe grapes.
  • Spätlese – "late harvest"; richer wines from riper grapes.
  • Auslese – "selected harvest"; from selected ripe bunches, sometimes affected by noble rot.
  • Beerenauslese – "selected berries"; rare, sweet wines from individually selected overripe, often botrytised berries.
  • Eiswein – ice wine, made from grapes frozen on the vine, pressed while frozen; must reach Beerenauslese must weights.
  • Trockenbeerenauslese – "selected dry berries"; the rarest and sweetest category, from shrivelled botrytised berries.

Sweetness designations

Separate from the quality classification, labels may indicate the wine's residual sweetness: trocken (dry), halbtrocken (off-dry), and the unofficial terms feinherb and lieblich (mildly sweet). These designations are independent of the Prädikat level, so a Kabinett may be dry, medium, or sweet.

VDP classification

The Verband Deutscher Prädikatsweingüter (VDP), an association of leading estates, operates its own vineyard-based classification modelled partly on Burgundy, with tiers including Gutswein, Ortswein, Erste Lage and Grosse Lage, the latter bottled as Grosses Gewächs when dry.
