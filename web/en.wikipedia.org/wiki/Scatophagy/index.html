Scatophagy

Dung beetles are among the best-known scatophagous animals

Scatophagy (also spelled skatophagy) is the consumption of faeces. The term derives from the Greek skōr (stem skat-), meaning dung or excrement, and phagein, to eat. It is closely related to the term coprophagia, which is often used interchangeably.

In animals

Scatophagy is widespread among invertebrates. Dung beetles, many fly species, and other insects feed on the droppings of larger animals, playing an essential ecological role in nutrient cycling, soil aeration, and the suppression of parasites. Among vertebrates, some species practise forms of scatophagy for nutritional reasons: rabbits and hares produce soft caecal pellets (caecotrophs) that are re-ingested to extract further nutrients, a behaviour known as caecotrophy. Dogs and other animals may consume faeces opportunistically.

In medicine

In humans, persistent consumption of faeces is generally associated with psychiatric or developmental disorders and with the eating disorder pica. It carries significant health risks, including bacterial and parasitic infection.
