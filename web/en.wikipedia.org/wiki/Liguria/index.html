Liguria
Country: Italy
Capital: Genoa
Area: 5,422 km²
Population: about 1.5 million
Provinces: Genoa, Imperia, La Spezia, Savona

Liguria (Italian: [liˈɡuːrja]) is a region of north-western Italy, a narrow strip of land between the Ligurian Sea and the Maritime Alps and Apennines. It is known as the Italian Riviera. Its capital and largest city is Genoa, historically one of Europe's great maritime republics. The region takes its name from the ancient Ligures people.

Geography

The region curves around the Gulf of Genoa in a crescent about 350 km long. The coast divides into the Riviera di Ponente to the west of Genoa — with resorts such as Sanremo and Alassio — and the Riviera di Levante to the east, home to Portofino and the five cliff villages of the Cinque Terre, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Because mountains plunge steeply into the sea, settlement and agriculture occupy terraced slopes; olive groves and vineyards cling to the hillsides.

History

The Republic of Genoa dominated Liguria for centuries and rivalled Venice in Mediterranean trade. After the Napoleonic era the region passed to the Kingdom of Sardinia in 1815 and became part of unified Italy in 1861. Its ports were heavily bombed in the Second World War, and post-war Genoa developed as a major industrial and shipping centre.

Economy and culture

Tourism, shipping and port services, shipbuilding and speciality agriculture (olives, flowers, basil) drive the economy. Ligurian cuisine is famous for pesto alla genovese, focaccia, and seafood; the dialect belongs to the Gallo-Italic group. The region's mild climate made it a beloved winter resort of 19th-century northern Europeans.
