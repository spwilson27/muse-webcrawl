The College Football Playoff National Championship is a post-season college football game, used to determine a national champion of the NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS), which began play in the 2014 season. The game is held at a neutral site determined through bids, and pits the winners of the two College Football Playoff semifinal games against each other. It replaced the BCS National Championship Game as the culminating event of the college football season.

History

For decades, the FBS (formerly Division I-A) champion was named by polls and, from 1998 to 2013, by the Bowl Championship Series title game. In 2014 the four-team College Football Playoff debuted, with semifinals hosted by rotating New Year's Six bowl games. The first championship game, played 12 January 2015 at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas, saw Ohio State defeat Oregon 42–20. With the 2024 season the playoff expanded to twelve teams, with on-campus first-round games and quarter- and semifinals at bowl sites; the 2025 championship game was won by Ohio State over Notre Dame.

Trophy

The champion receives the College Football Playoff National Championship Trophy, a 26.5-inch, 35-pound trophy of gold-plated metal featuring a full-size football, introduced with the playoff in 2014.
