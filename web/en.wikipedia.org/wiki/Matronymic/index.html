Matronymic

A matronymic is a personal name or surname component based on the name of one's mother or a female ancestor, formed in contrast to the much more common patronymic, which is based on the father's name. Matronymics are found in many cultures, historically often used when the mother's identity or status was more significant, when the father was unknown, or in certain naming traditions and legal circumstances.

Examples

In Icelandic naming, most people use patronymics, but matronymics such as those formed with -dóttir ("daughter") from the mother's name are also used, for example footballer Heiðar Helguson's contemporaries with names derived from their mothers. The Welsh surname Maddox derives from the matronymic "son of Madoc" tradition, while English surnames such as Marriott (from Mary), Megson (from Margaret), and Emmett (from Emma) are matronymic in origin. In many Arab and Muslim cultures the name ibn Maryam ("son of Mary") is famously used of Jesus. Jewish tradition invokes matronymics in prayers for the sick, naming a person as son or daughter of their mother. In parts of Spain and in Sephardic tradition, maternal surnames form the second family name.

Modern usage

In contemporary Western naming, some parents deliberately choose matronymic surnames or combine both parents' names, and feminist writers have sometimes adopted matronymics as a matter of principle. Historically, children of unmarried mothers in various European countries were commonly given matronymic surnames.
