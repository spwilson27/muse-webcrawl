Hilary of Poitiers (Latin: Hilarius Pictaviensis; c. 310 – c. 367) was Bishop of Poitiers and a leading Latin theologian of the 4th century, honoured as a Doctor of the Church. He is sometimes called the "Athanasius of the West" for his vigorous defence of Nicene Trinitarianism against Arianism.

Life

Born into a wealthy pagan family in Poitiers and well educated in Latin rhetoric and philosophy, Hilary converted to Christianity as an adult through his reading of Scripture and was elected bishop of his native city around 353. His opposition to the Arianising policy of the emperor Constantius II led to his exile to Phrygia (356–360), where he wrote his main theological work, De Trinitate (On the Trinity), as well as his Tractatus super Psalmos. Returning to Gaul, he campaigned successfully against Arian bishops in the West.

Legacy

Hilary is among the earliest Latin hymn-writers whose works survive, and his writings were highly influential on later Western theology. Pope Pius IX proclaimed him a Doctor of the Church in 1851. The Hilary term at Oxford and Cambridge is named after his feast day.
