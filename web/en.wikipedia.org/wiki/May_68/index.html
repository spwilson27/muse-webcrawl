May 68 (French: Mai 68) was a period of civil unrest in France in May and June 1968, marked by student occupations and protests, massive general strikes, and the occupation of factories and universities across the country. At its height some ten million workers were on strike, the economy ground to a halt, and the government of President Charles de Gaulle briefly appeared close to collapse. Although the movement failed to overthrow the government, it had a profound and lasting effect on French society, culture, and politics.

Background

France in the 1960s combined rapid economic growth with authoritarian political structures, overcrowded universities, and low wages for young workers. Student activism, energised by campaigns against the Vietnam War and by figures such as Daniel Cohn-Bendit at Nanterre, challenged university discipline and broader social mores.

Events

In early May, clashes between students and police at the Sorbonne escalated into the "Night of the Barricades" on 10–11 May. Trade unions called a general strike on 13 May; workers occupied factories, including Renault plants, and strikes spread nationwide. On 29 May de Gaulle briefly left Paris for the French military headquarters in Germany before returning to announce new elections. The Grenelle accords of late May delivered large wage increases, and in the June legislative elections the Gaullists won a landslide.

Legacy

May 68 liberalised French cultural and social life, weakened the prestige of de Gaulle (who resigned in 1969 after losing a referendum), and inspired protest movements internationally. Its slogans — "Sous les pavés, la plage" ("Under the paving stones, the beach") and "Il est interdit d'interdire" ("It is forbidden to forbid") — remain iconic.
