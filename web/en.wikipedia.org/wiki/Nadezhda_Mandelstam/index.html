Nadezhda Yakovlevna Mandelstam (Russian: Надежда Яковлевна Мандельштам; 31 October 1899 – 29 December 1980) was a Russian writer and memoirist, the wife of the poet Osip Mandelstam. Her two great memoirs, Hope Against Hope and Hope Abandoned, are among the classic testimonies of life under Stalin's terror and of the destruction of Russia's literary culture.

Life

Born Nadezhda Khazina in Saratov, she met Osip Mandelstam in Kiev in 1919 and married him in 1922. When he was arrested in 1934 for his epigram on Stalin, she accompanied him into exile in Voronezh; after his second arrest in 1938 he died in a transit camp near Vladivostok. For two decades she lived precariously, teaching English in provincial towns, while secretly preserving her husband's poetry — committing it to memory and hiding manuscripts with trusted friends — because possession of the papers was dangerous.

Memoirs

Hope Against Hope (published in English in 1970; her first name means "hope") recounts the years of persecution and the moral collapse and resistance of the intelligentsia, portraying figures such as Anna Akhmatova. The sequel, Hope Abandoned, covers her earlier life and later years. Both circulated in samizdat and were published abroad; they were first legally published in the Soviet Union at the end of the 1980s.
