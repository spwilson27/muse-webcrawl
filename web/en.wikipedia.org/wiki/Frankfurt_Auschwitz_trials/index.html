Frankfurt Auschwitz trials

The Frankfurt Auschwitz trials (1963–1965) were a series of trials in West Germany in which 22 defendants were charged under German criminal law for their roles in the Holocaust at Auschwitz-Birkenau. Driven by prosecutor Fritz Bauer, they were the largest and most public German trials of Auschwitz personnel and confronted the West German public with the camp’s crimes.

Frankfurt Auschwitz trials
Dates: 20 Dec 1963 – 19 Aug 1965
Location: Frankfurt am Main, West Germany
Defendants: 22 (SS and functionaries)
Prosecutor: Fritz Bauer (Attorney General of Hesse)

Proceedings

Held in Frankfurt’s Bürgerhaus Gallus, the trial heard hundreds of witnesses, many of them survivors. Because West German law required proof of individual murder beyond following orders, many defendants received relatively light sentences: of the 22, six received life imprisonment, others terms of years, and several were acquitted.

Impact

Tape recordings and transcripts (now UNESCO Memory of the World) educated a generation; Peter Weiss’s play The Investigation (1965) was drawn from the proceedings. The trials spurred debate over the statute of limitations for Nazi murders and broader West German reckoning with the past.
