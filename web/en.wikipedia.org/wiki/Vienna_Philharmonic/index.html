The Vienna Philharmonic (German: Wiener Philharmoniker) is an Austrian orchestra based in Vienna, founded in 1842, and consistently ranked among the finest orchestras in the world. Uniquely, its members are drawn exclusively from the orchestra of the Vienna State Opera, and the ensemble is self-governing: decisions, including the admission of members and the choice of guest conductors, are made democratically by the musicians themselves.

The orchestra's first concert, organized by composer-conductor Otto Nicolai in 1842, established the "philharmonic idea" of an orchestra run by its own members. Over its history it has been led in subscription concerts by conductors including Hans Richter, Gustav Mahler (1898–1901), Felix Weingartner, Wilhelm Furtwängler, and Herbert von Karajan, who maintained a close association without holding a permanent title — the orchestra famously has no single music director.

Traditions

The Vienna Philharmonic is best known internationally for its annual New Year's Concert in the Golden Hall of the Musikverein, devoted to the waltzes and polkas of the Strauss family and broadcast worldwide since 1959. It is also the resident orchestra of the Salzburg Festival. The orchestra's distinctive sound is associated with Viennese instruments and playing traditions, including the Viennese oboe, rotary-valve trumpets, and the Viennese horn. Women were not admitted as full members until 1997, a change that drew international attention and criticism of the orchestra's long-standing exclusivity.
