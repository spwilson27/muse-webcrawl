Dizi (instrument)

A dizi, the Chinese transverse flute.

The dizi (Chinese: 笩子) is a major Chinese transverse flute, usually made of bamboo. In addition to the embouchure hole and six finger holes, the dizi has an extra hole, the mokong, between the embouchure and finger holes, which is covered with a thin membrane (dimo) made from the inner lining of bamboo or reed. The vibrating membrane gives the instrument its characteristic bright, resonant and slightly buzzing timbre.

Types and technique

The dizi comes in many sizes and pitches. The two most common types are the higher-pitched bangdi, used in northern Chinese music and opera accompaniment, and the lower, mellower qudi, associated with the Jiangnan region and Kunqu opera. Very large bass dizi also exist. Players use techniques such as tonguing, trills, glissando and flutter-tonguing, and the instrument has a range of about two and a half octaves.

History and repertoire

Flutes have a long history in China; bone flutes from the Neolithic site of Jiahu date back some 8,000–9,000 years. The dizi became a central instrument in Chinese folk music, opera and the modern Chinese orchestra, and twentieth-century virtuosos developed an extensive solo repertoire. A related end-blown flute is the xiao, and a similar transverse folk flute is the daegeum of Korea.
