Apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic fiction

Apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic fiction is a subgenre of science fiction in which the Earth's (or another planet's) civilization is collapsing or has collapsed. The apocalypse is typically caused by a catastrophic event such as climatic change, pandemic, artificial intelligence, war (especially nuclear warfare), asteroid impact, or other disasters; post-apocalyptic stories are set afterward, focusing on the survivors and the world that remains.

History

The genre has ancient roots in flood myths and religious eschatology. Modern works begin with Mary Shelley's The Last Man (1826), in which a plague destroys humanity, and Richard Jefferies' After London (1885), depicting an England returned to wilderness. H. G. Wells contributed The War of the Worlds (1898) and other catastrophe tales. The atomic bomb transformed the genre: the 1950s produced Nevil Shute's On the Beach (1957), John Wyndham's The Day of the Triffids (1951), Pat Frank's Alas, Babylon (1959) and Walter M. Miller Jr.'s A Canticle for Leibowitz (1960), in which monks preserve knowledge through a new dark age. Richard Matheson's I Am Legend (1954) combined plague and vampires and influenced zombie fiction.

Themes and settings

Post-apocalyptic works commonly explore the fragility of civilization, the psychology of survival, the rebuilding or failure of social order, and humanity's moral regression or regeneration. Settings range from ruined cities and deserts (Mad Max) to rewilded landscapes (Earth Abides, 1949), the interiors of generation ships and bunkers, and slow civilizational collapse as in Emily St. John Mandel's Station Eleven (2014) and Cormac McCarthy's The Road (2006). Zombie apocalypses, popularized by George A. Romero's films and later The Walking Dead, use the undead as the catastrophe's mechanism. In the 21st century, climate fiction has increasingly merged with the genre, and young-adult dystopian series such as The Hunger Games often presuppose a past apocalypse.

Video games including the Fallout series, The Last of Us (which became a prominent television adaptation), Metro 2033 and S.T.A.L.K.E.R. have made explorable post-apocalyptic worlds a staple of interactive media.
