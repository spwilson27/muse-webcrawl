The British Jumping Derby is a prestigious show jumping competition held annually at the All England Jumping Course, Hickstead, in West Sussex, England. First run in 1961, it is the feature event of the Hickstead Derby Meeting and one of the most famous jumping competitions in the world, renowned for its unusually testing and idiosyncratic course.

The course

Unlike the flat, technically demanding courses of modern championship jumping, the Derby course at Hickstead includes natural obstacles rarely seen elsewhere: the famous Derby Bank, a 3.2-metre (10 ft 6 in) drop down a grass bank; a water ditch; a double of water trays; and the "Devil's Dyke", a triple combination. The course is 1,195 metres long and frequently produces very few clear rounds — in some years no rider finishes clear at all.

History

The Derby was founded by Douglas Bunn, who bought the Hickstead estate in 1959 and established it as a show jumping centre. The event quickly became a fixture of the British equestrian calendar and a staple of television coverage. The only British rider to have won it more than four times is Harvey Smith, whose three victories in the 1960s and 1970s made him a national figure; Eddie Macken of Ireland won it four consecutive times between 1976 and 1979.
