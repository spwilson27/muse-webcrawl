Wellington boot

The Wellington boot, usually shortened to wellies or wellingtons and also known as rain boots or gumboots, is a type of waterproof boot. It was based upon leather Hessian boots and named after Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington.

History

The Duke of Wellington instructed his shoemaker to modify the Hessian boot into a plainer, hard-wearing style suited to both battle and evening wear; the resulting leather boot became fashionable in Regency Britain and took his name. In the 1850s, manufacturers including Hiram Hutchinson and the North British Rubber Company (later Hunter Boot Ltd) began producing Wellington boots from vulcanised rubber, making them fully waterproof and affordable.

Use

Rubber Wellingtons became standard footwear for farming, fishing, wet-weather work, and the trenches of the First World War, where they were essential against trench foot. Modern wellies are made of rubber or PVC, often lined, and produced in safety versions with steel toecaps for industry. In New Zealand, where they are called gumboots, they are a national rural symbol celebrated with events such as Gumboot Day in Taihape. In recent decades designer versions have also become festival and fashion items in Britain.
