Delémont

Delémont (German: Delsberg) is a town in north-western Switzerland, the capital of the canton of Jura and of the district of Delémont. Situated in the valley of the Sorne river at the foot of the Jura mountains, it has a population of about 12,700 and is the largest town in Switzerland's youngest canton.

History

First mentioned in 736 as Delemonte, the town grew under the prince-bishops of Basel, who built a castle there that served as their summer residence. After the Congress of Vienna in 1815, Delémont was assigned to the canton of Bern. Throughout the 20th century, French-speaking Jurassians campaigned for separation from Bern; following a series of plebiscites, the canton of Jura was created in 1979 with Delémont as its capital.

The town today

The old town retains medieval gates, fountains, and the former bishops' castle. Delémont is a railway junction on the line between Basel and Biel/Bienne and is known for the Jurassian Museum of Art and History and for knife-making — the Swiss Army knife manufacturer Wenger was based in Delémont for over a century.
