Democratic socialism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Democratic socialism is a left-wing set of political philosophies that supports political democracy and some form of a socially owned economy, with a strong emphasis on economic democracy, self-management and workers' control of the means of production. Democratic socialists argue that capitalism is inherently incompatible with freedom, equality and solidarity, and that these ideals can be achieved only through a socialist society — reached democratically rather than by one-party rule, which distinguishes them from Marxist–Leninist traditions and, in emphasis, from social democrats who accept a predominantly capitalist economy with humanised terms.

History and presence

The tradition includes the democratic wing of the early socialist movement, figures such as Eugene Debs, Norman Thomas, George Orwell and Michael Harrington, and parties and currents within labour movements worldwide. Interest revived in the Anglosphere in the 2010s — the Democratic Socialists of America grew rapidly, and "democratic socialist" became a live label in US and UK politics — while European democratic-socialist parties and factions continue to advocate public ownership, decommodification of essentials and industrial democracy within constitutional frameworks.
