Duality (projective geometry)

In projective geometry, duality is a principle by which theorems and definitions in a projective plane can be systematically interchanged by swapping the roles of points and lines, and of operations such as joining points and intersecting lines, to obtain equally true dual statements.

Principle of duality

For example, "two points determine a unique line" dualises to "two lines determine a unique point". Brianchon's theorem is the dual of Pascal's theorem, and Desargues's theorem is self-dual. In three-dimensional projective space, points are dual to planes, and lines to lines.

Pole and polar

A concrete duality is given by a polarity, such as pole and polar with respect to a conic, which maps each point to a line and each line to a point while preserving incidence.
