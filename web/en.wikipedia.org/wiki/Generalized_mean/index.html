Generalized mean

In mathematics, the generalized mean (or power mean, or Hölder mean) is a family of functions for aggregating sets of numbers, which includes as special cases the Pythagorean means: the arithmetic, geometric and harmonic means.

Definition

For a real number p ≠ 0, the power mean of positive numbers x1,…,xn is Mp = ( (x1p + ⋯ + xnp) / n )1/p. Limiting cases give the geometric mean (p → 0), the maximum (p → ∞) and the minimum (p → −∞).

Special cases

p = −1 is the harmonic mean, p = 1 the arithmetic mean, and p = 2 the quadratic mean (root mean square). The power mean is monotonically increasing in p, which yields the familiar chain of inequalities: harmonic ≤ geometric ≤ arithmetic ≤ quadratic.

Generalizations

Weighted versions replace the average of powers with a weighted average, and the quasi-arithmetic (f-) mean generalizes further by applying an arbitrary invertible function before and after averaging. Power means appear in statistics, information theory (Rényi entropy) and inequality theory.
