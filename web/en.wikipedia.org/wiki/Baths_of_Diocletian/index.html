The Baths of Diocletian (Latin: Thermae Diocletiani) were the largest of the imperial public baths of ancient Rome. Built between AD 298 and 306 on the Viminal Hill, they could accommodate around 3,000 bathers at once and followed the standard plan of a central frigidarium, tepidarium and caldarium flanked by palaestrae, gardens and libraries.

Later history

The baths fell out of use after the Goths cut Rome's aqueducts in 537. In 1561 Pope Pius IV commissioned Michelangelo to convert the great frigidarium hall into the church of Santa Maria degli Angeli e dei Martiri, preserving its colossal granite columns and vaults. Other parts house the Museo Nazionale Romano (National Roman Museum), and the octagonal hall served for a time as Rome's planetarium. Roma Termini railway station takes its name from the baths (terme).
