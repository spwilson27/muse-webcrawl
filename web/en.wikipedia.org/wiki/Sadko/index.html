Sadko is the hero of a Russian bylina, an orally transmitted epic tale belonging to the Novgorod cycle. Sadko is a poor musician who plays the gusli (a multi-stringed psaltery); with the aid of the Sea Tsar he becomes a fabulously wealthy merchant of Novgorod, and later visits the Tsar's underwater kingdom.

The tale

In the best-known version, Sadko plays by the shore of Lake Ilmen, and the Sea Tsar, delighted by his music, tells him how to win a wager with the merchants of Novgorod: three golden-feathered fish will be caught in the lake, and by staking his head against their shops Sadko wins a fortune and becomes the richest man in the city. Pride follows: Sadko boasts that he can buy up all the goods of Novgorod, and is humbled when the city's trade proves inexhaustible. Later, becalmed at sea, his ships must cast lots to find whom the Sea Tsar demands as tribute; the lot falls on Sadko himself, who descends to the sea floor. There he plays for the Tsar's dancing, which raises a storm wrecking ships, until an apparition — Saint Nicholas in many versions — teaches him to break his strings and end the dance. Offered a bride, Sadko chooses the maiden Chernava on advice, returns miraculously to Novgorod, and builds a church in thanksgiving.

Adaptations

Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov used the bylina for his opera Sadko (premiered in Moscow in 1898), whose "Song of India" became famous, and he had earlier written a symphonic poem of the same name. The story was also filmed in the Soviet Union (1952) and inspired artworks including Ilya Repin's painting Sadko (1876), depicting the hero in the underwater kingdom.
