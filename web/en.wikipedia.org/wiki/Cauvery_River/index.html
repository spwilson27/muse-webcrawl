Cauvery River

Cauvery
Length: about 800 km
Source: Talakaveri, Karnataka
Mouth: Bay of Bengal, Tamil Nadu
Basin: about 81,155 km²

The Cauvery (also spelled Kaveri) is a major river of southern India. It rises at Talakaveri in the Brahmagiri range of the Western Ghats in Karnataka, flows generally southeast through Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and empties into the Bay of Bengal near Poompuhar.

Course

Fed by the southwest and northeast monsoons, the river is unusual in receiving rain in both seasons. It flows past Srirangapatna and the Shivanasamudra Falls, forms part of the Karnataka–Tamil Nadu border, passes through the Mettur Dam reservoir, and then crosses the fertile Tamil Nadu plains before splitting into a delta network.

Significance

The Cauvery delta, sometimes called the garden of southern India, has supported irrigated rice cultivation for two millennia; the ancient Kallanai (Grand Anicut) dam dates to roughly the 2nd century CE. The river is sacred in Hindu tradition and central to festivals such as Aadi Perukku and Kaveri Pushkaram. Allocation of its waters between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu has been the subject of a long-running dispute adjudicated by a tribunal and the Supreme Court of India.
