The Peddars Way is a long-distance footpath in Norfolk (with a short section in Suffolk), England, that follows the line of a Roman road. It runs for 46 miles (74 km) from Knettishall Heath near Thetford to Holme-next-the-Sea on the North Norfolk coast, where it meets the Norfolk Coast Path; together the two form the Peddars Way and Norfolk Coast Path National Trail.

History

The Roman road was built around AD 61, apparently in the aftermath of Boudica's revolt, as a military route through the territory of the Iceni, linking the Roman settlements of the region toward the port near modern Holme. Its strikingly straight alignment, dead-straight for most of its length, survives in hedgerows, tracks, and short stretches of modern road. The name "Peddars Way" is traditional and may refer to a later use by pedlars — it is not a Roman name.

Route

From Knettishall Heath the way passes through Thompson, near the famous pingos of Thompson Water, by the village of Castle Acre with its Norman castle and priory, and through the Breckland and west Norfolk farmland to the coast at Holme, close to the site where the Bronze Age timber circle known as "Seahenge" was exposed in 1998. The route is waymarked with an acorn symbol as a National Trail and is accompanied by artworks at intervals along its length.
