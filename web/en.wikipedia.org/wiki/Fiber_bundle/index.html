Fiber bundle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, and particularly topology, a fiber bundle (or fibre bundle) is a space that is locally a product space but globally may have a different topological structure. Specifically, a fiber bundle consists of a total space E, a base space B, a projection map π: E → B, and a typical fiber F, such that every point of B has a neighbourhood over which the bundle is homeomorphic to a product U × F, compatibly with the projection.

Definition and examples

Formally, a fiber bundle is a structure (E, B, π, F) where π: E → B is a continuous surjection and F is a topological space, with local trivialisations covering B. The set π−1(x) over a base point x is a fiber, homeomorphic to F. A Möbius strip is the classic non-trivial example: it is a bundle over a circle with fiber an interval, twisted so that the total space is not a product (unlike the cylinder). The Klein bottle is likewise a circle bundle over a circle that is not the torus.

Special cases

  • Vector bundles — the fiber is a vector space and transition maps are linear; tangent and cotangent bundles are examples.
  • Principal bundles — the fiber is a Lie group acting on itself; fundamental to gauge theory in physics.
  • Covering spaces — bundles with discrete fiber.
  • Fiber bundles with structure group — transition functions take values in a specified group G, encoding how local pieces are glued.

Sections and applications

A section assigns to each base point a point in its fiber (e.g. a vector field is a section of the tangent bundle); obstructions to global sections are measured by characteristic classes. Fiber bundles pervade differential geometry and mathematical physics, describing gauge fields, spin structures and the topology of configuration spaces.
