Overview

The Norfolk and Western Railway (reporting mark NW) was a US class I railroad headquartered in Roanoke, Virginia. It was renowned for hauling coal from the Appalachian fields of Virginia, West Virginia, and Kentucky to the port of Norfolk, Virginia, and for building its own steam locomotives at its Roanoke Shops.

History

The company was created in 1881 when the Atlantic, Mississippi and Ohio Railroad was purchased at foreclosure and reorganised. Under presidents Frederick J. Kimball and later executives, the road expanded westward to the Ohio River and Great Lakes, becoming a major carrier of bituminous coal.

The N&W was the last major American railroad to operate steam locomotives, retaining them in regular service until 1960 because of its close ties to the coal industry and the quality of its home-built engines such as the Class J 4-8-4s and Class A 2-6-6-4s.

Mergers and legacy

In 1964 the N&W absorbed the Virginian Railway, the Nickel Plate Road, the Wabash Railroad, and other lines, greatly expanding its network in the Midwest. In 1982 the N&W combined with the Southern Railway to form Norfolk Southern Railway. The Roanoke Shops and the company's steam heritage are commemorated by the Virginia Museum of Transportation, home to restored Class J No. 611.
