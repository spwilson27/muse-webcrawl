Danube school

The Danube school or Donau school (German: Donauschule or Donaustil) is the name given to a circle of painters of the first third of the 16th century in Bavaria and Austria, around the valley of the river Danube. They are noted for their innovative treatment of landscape, which for the first time in northern European art became an independent and dominant subject rather than a mere background to religious or historical scenes.

Characteristics

Danube school works typically feature dramatic, panoramic landscapes with steep cliffs, dense fir forests, misty river valleys and dramatic skies, populated by small figures from biblical or historical narratives. The leading figures include Albrecht Altdorfer, whose Battle of Alexander at Issus (1529) is a famous example, Wolf Huber, Jörg Breu the Elder and the younger Lucas Cranach's early work is also associated with the style.

Influence

The school's expressive landscapes influenced later northern landscape painting and are connected with early printed landscape etchings by artists such as Augustin Hirschvogel. The term itself was coined by later art historians rather than by the painters, who never formed an organised group.
