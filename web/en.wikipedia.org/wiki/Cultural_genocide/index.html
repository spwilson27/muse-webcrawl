Cultural genocide

Cultural genocide or cultural cleansing is a concept which was proposed by lawyer Raphael Lemkin in 1944 as a component of genocide. It describes the systematic destruction of the cultural heritage, language, religion and identity of a group, without necessarily physically exterminating its members — though the two often overlap.

History of the concept

Lemkin included cultural destruction in his original conception of genocide. The 1948 Genocide Convention, however, limited the legal definition mainly to physical and biological acts, after debate among states including colonial and settler states. Cultural destruction is addressed in other instruments, including the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (2007).

Examples discussed

Scholars and commissions have applied the term to residential schools for Indigenous children in Canada (the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's 2015 finding), forced assimilation policies in Australia, Tibet and Xinjiang debates, and destruction of heritage in armed conflict. Usage remains politically and legally contested.
