Wolverine

The wolverine (Gulo gulo), also known as the glutton, carcajou, or quickhatch, is the largest land-dwelling member of the weasel family (Mustelidae). A stocky, muscular carnivore of remote northern wilderness, it is renowned for strength and ferocity out of proportion to its size, and is found across the boreal forests and tundra of North America and Eurasia.

Description

Adults measure 65–109 cm in body length and weigh 9–25 kg, with a broad head, short legs, and long, coarse dark-brown fur frosted with paler bands along the flanks. Wolverines have powerful jaws and specialised upper molars that allow them to tear frozen meat and crush bone. Large, snowshoe-like paws help them travel over deep snow, and they are tireless travellers, sometimes covering 20–30 km in a day within home ranges of hundreds of square kilometres.

Ecology and behaviour

Wolverines are solitary and primarily scavengers of winter-killed or predator-killed ungulates, but they also hunt prey from hares and beavers to caribou calves, and cache food in snow and rock crevices — behaviour behind the Latin name Gulo, "glutton". Females give birth in late winter in snow dens to kits of two or three. Reproduction is slow and densities naturally very low, making populations vulnerable to trapping, habitat fragmentation, and the loss of persistent spring snowpack under climate change.

Conservation

The species is listed as Least Concern globally by the IUCN but has declined across much of its southern range; the distinct population segment in the contiguous United States was listed as threatened under the Endangered Species Act in 2023. In culture, the wolverine is the state animal of Michigan (the "Wolverine State") and a symbol of wilderness tenacity.
