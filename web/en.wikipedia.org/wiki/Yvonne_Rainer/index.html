Yvonne Rainer

Yvonne Rainer (born 24 November 1934) is an American dancer, choreographer and filmmaker whose work helped define postmodern dance. A cofounder of the Judson Dance Theater in New York, she famously rejected spectacle, virtuosity and narrative in favour of ordinary movement, task-like action and neutrality of performance.

Dance

Rainer trained in New York with Martha Graham and at the Merce Cunningham studio before founding, with other Cunningham students, the Judson Dance Theater in 1962. Her 1965 "No Manifesto" declared "No to spectacle, no to virtuosity, no to transformations and magic and make-believe…" Her best-known dance, Trio A (1966) — a continuous, uninflected sequence of everyday movements performed without eye contact with the audience — became an icon of minimalist art. Other early works include The Mind Is a Muscle (1968). After health problems, she increasingly turned to film in the 1970s.

Film

Rainer's experimental feature films include Lives of Performers (1972), Film About a Woman Who… (1974), Kristina Talking Pictures (1976), Journeys from Berlin/1971 (1980), The Man Who Envied Women (1985), Privilege (1990) and MURDER and murder (1996). The films combine narrative fragments, text, documentary and theory, and are central works of feminist cinema. In 2000 she returned to choreography, creating new dances and restaging early pieces, including works for the Baryshnikov Dance Foundation and the retrospective performances associated with the Museum of Modern Art.

Recognition

Rainer received a MacArthur Fellowship ("genius grant") in 1990 and numerous retrospectives, including at the Getty Research Institute and Tate Modern. Her memoir Feelings Are Facts: A Life was published in 2006.
