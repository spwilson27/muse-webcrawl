Being There

Peter Sellers as Chance, the gardener.

Being There is a 1979 American satirical comedy-drama film directed by Hal Ashby and based on the 1970 novella by Jerzy Kosiński, who also wrote the screenplay. It stars Peter Sellers as Chance, a simple-minded, middle-aged gardener who has lived his entire life in a Washington, D.C. townhouse and knows the world only from television.

Plot

When his employer dies, Chance is turned out onto the streets. Struck a glancing blow by a limousine, he is taken in by the wealthy Eve Rand and her dying husband Benjamin, a power broker who mistakes Chance's name, "Chance, the gardener", for "Chauncey Gardiner" and interprets his plain talk about gardening as profound economic metaphor. Chance becomes a celebrity adviser to the President, while those around him project wisdom onto his literal statements; the film ends with his name floated as a presidential candidate.

Reception

The film was acclaimed as a satire of media and politics and is famous for its closing shot of Chance apparently walking on water. Sellers won a Golden Globe and received an Academy Award nomination for the role; Melvyn Douglas won the Oscar for Best Supporting Actor as Benjamin Rand. In 2015 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
