Sac (biology)

In biology, a sac is a hollow, bag-like anatomical structure that encloses or contains a fluid, a gas, or another structure. Sacs occur throughout the animal and plant kingdoms and serve a wide variety of functions, including storage, protection, buoyancy, respiration, and reproduction. Many important anatomical structures are described as sacs, including the air sacs of birds, the amniotic sac of amniote embryos, and the yolk sac that nourishes early embryos.

In animals

Respiration and buoyancy

The air sacs of birds are thin-walled sacs connected to the lungs that act as bellows, enabling the unidirectional flow of air through the respiratory system. In fish, the swim bladder is a gas-filled sac that regulates buoyancy; in many species it develops as an outpocketing of the gut. The alveoli of the vertebrate lung are microscopic air sacs, clustered at the ends of the bronchioles like bunches of grapes, across whose thin walls gas exchange takes place.

Reproduction and development

The amniotic sac is the fluid-filled membrane-bound cavity in which the amniote embryo develops; its rupture at birth is known colloquially as the breaking of the waters. The yolk sac supplies nutrients to the embryo of egg-laying animals and, in mammals, is an early site of blood-cell formation. Many invertebrates enclose their eggs in protective sacs: female spiders spin silk egg sacs, and the egg cases of some sharks and skates are toughened sacs sometimes called mermaid's purses.

Other examples

  • The ink sac of cephalopods stores ink that is ejected to confuse predators.
  • The lacrimal sac collects tears from the lacrimal canaliculi before they drain into the nasolacrimal duct.
  • The scrotum is a sac of skin and muscle housing the testes.
  • The venom sac of some animals stores venom at the base of a sting or fang.
  • The bursa is a small fluid-filled sac that reduces friction between moving tissues, such as tendons and bone.

In plants and fungi

In botany, the pollen sac is the part of the anther in which pollen grains develop; there are typically four pollen sacs (microsporangia) per anther. The embryo sac is the female gametophyte of flowering plants, usually a seven-celled, eight-nucleate structure formed within the ovule. In fungi of the phylum Ascomycota, the ascus is a sac-like cell in which the sexual ascospores are formed, giving the group its common name, the sac fungi.
