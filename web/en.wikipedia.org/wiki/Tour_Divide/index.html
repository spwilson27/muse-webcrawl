Tour Divide

Date: Second Friday of June (grand depart)
Location: Banff, Canada to Antelope Wells, New Mexico, US
Distance: approx. 2,745 miles (4,418 km)
Type: Self-supported bikepacking race

The Tour Divide is an annual self-supported, ultra-distance bicycle race along the length of the Great Divide Mountain Bike Route, the world's longest off-road cycle route. The race follows the Continental Divide from Banff, Alberta, Canada, to Antelope Wells, New Mexico, on the United States–Mexico border, covering roughly 2,745 miles (4,418 km) with over 200,000 feet (60,000 m) of elevation gain.

Format

The Tour Divide is not a stage race and has no entry fees, prizes, or official support. Riders must be entirely self-supported, carrying their own food, water, and equipment, and may only use services available to the general public along the route. The clock never stops. The race traditionally begins with a "grand depart" on the second Friday of June, though riders may also attempt the route as an individual time trial at other times of year.

Records

The men's course record was set by Mike Hall in 2016 with a time of 13 days, 22 hours and 51 minutes. The women's record was held for many years by Lael Wilcox, who completed the route in 15 days, 10 hours and 59 minutes in 2015. Weather, snow on high passes, mud, and grizzly bears in Montana and Wyoming are among the principal hazards.
