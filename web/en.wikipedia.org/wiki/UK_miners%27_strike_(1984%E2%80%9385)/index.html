UK miners' strike (1984–85)

Date6 March 1984 – 3 March 1985
LocationUnited Kingdom
PartiesNational Union of Mineworkers vs. National Coal Board / Conservative government
OutcomeStrike ended without agreement; pit closures followed

The miners' strike of 1984–85 was a major industrial action within the British coal industry in an attempt to prevent colliery closures. It was led by Arthur Scargill of the National Union of Mineworkers (NUM) against the National Coal Board (NCB), a government agency chaired by Ian MacGregor, under the Conservative government of Margaret Thatcher.

Course

The strike began in March 1984 after the announcement of the closure of Cortonwood Colliery and plans to close twenty pits. It lasted a year, marked by mass picketing, violent clashes with police — most famously at the "Battle of Orgreave" in June 1984 — and deep divisions within the NUM, including the absence of a national ballot and the breakaway Union of Democratic Mineworkers in Nottinghamshire. Support groups, notably Women Against Pit Closures, sustained mining communities.

Outcome and legacy

In March 1985 the NUM voted to return to work without a settlement. The defeat of the strike weakened the British trade union movement, accelerated the decline of the coal industry — most pits closed within the following decade — and became a defining episode of Thatcher-era politics, leaving lasting bitterness in mining communities.
