Thalamegos

The thalamegos (Greek: θαλαμηγός, "chamber-carrier") was a gigantic Nile river yacht and floating palace built for Ptolemy IV Philopator, ruler of Ptolemaic Egypt, around 200 BC. It is described in detail by Callixenus of Rhodes, preserved in the Deipnosophistae of Athenaeus.

TypeRiver yacht / floating palace
OwnerPtolemy IV Philopator
Lengthc. 115 m (377 ft)
Beamc. 14 m (46 ft)
PropulsionOars, towed

Description

The vessel was essentially a double-hulled, catamaran-like barge carrying a two-storey palace superstructure. Its rooms included banqueting halls, bedrooms, shrines to Aphrodite and Dionysus, colonnades and gardens, decorated with ivory, cedar, cypress and marble. A main reception hall could seat diners on twenty couches.

Purpose

Too large for the open sea, the thalamegos cruised the Nile, serving as a royal residence and a display of Ptolemaic wealth and power during progresses through Egypt. It required a large crew and was reportedly towed or rowed by thousands of men.

Sources

The description by Callixenus survives only in Athenaeus's quotation, and some dimensions may be exaggerated, but the account remains a key source for Hellenistic shipbuilding and court luxury.
