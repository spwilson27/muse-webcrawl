Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery

Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery (Latin: Medicinae Baccalaureus, Baccalaureus Chirurgiae), abbreviated MBBS or MBChB among other forms, is the primary medical degree awarded by medical schools in the United Kingdom and many countries that follow the British tradition of medical education, including much of the Commonwealth, Ireland, and parts of Asia, Africa, and the Middle East.

History and nomenclature

The double bachelor's degree reflects the historical separation of medicine and surgery into distinct professions in Britain. Despite the name, the two degrees are awarded together as a single qualification on completion of an undergraduate medical programme, usually of five or six years. Variants include MBChB (used in Scotland and elsewhere), MBBCh, BMBS, and BM BCh (Oxford and Cambridge). Some universities historically awarded a separate Doctor of Medicine (MD) as the first degree, but undergraduate MBBS-style degrees are the norm.

Programme structure

Courses typically combine pre-clinical study of the basic medical sciences (anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, pharmacology, pathology) with clinical teaching in hospitals and community settings. Many schools now use integrated curricula with early patient contact, problem-based learning, and clinical placements throughout.

Registration and equivalence

The degree is the standard route to provisional and then full registration with the UK General Medical Council, followed by the two-year Foundation Programme. Internationally, MBBS is broadly equivalent in level to the MD awarded in North America as a first professional degree, although the titles differ in origin.
