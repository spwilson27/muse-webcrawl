"Mull of Kintyre" is a song by the British-American rock band Wings, written by Paul McCartney and Denny Laine. Released as a non-album single in November 1977, it became one of the best-selling singles of all time in the United Kingdom, and was the first single to sell over two million copies in the UK alone.

Background

The song is a tribute to the Kintyre peninsula in Argyll and Bute, Scotland, where McCartney had owned a farm, High Park Farm, since 1966. The Mull of Kintyre is the southwesternmost tip of the peninsula. McCartney wrote the song in praise of the area's natural beauty and as an expression of his longing for it while away.

The recording featured the Campbeltown Pipe Band, a local bagpipe band from the Kintyre area, whose contribution gave the song its distinctive Scottish character. It was recorded in August 1977 at Spirit of Ranachan studio on McCartney's farm.

Commercial success

"Mull of Kintyre" was a huge commercial success. It spent nine weeks at number one on the UK Singles Chart over the Christmas period of 1977 and into January 1978, becoming the Christmas number one of 1977. It was the first-ever single to sell over two million copies in the UK, overtaking the previous record held by the Beatles' "She Loves You". Globally, the single sold an estimated six million copies.

The B-side of the single was "Girls' School". Despite its enormous success in the UK and many other countries, the song was only a modest hit in the United States, where the sides were reversed and "Girls' School" was promoted as the A-side.

Legacy

The song remains one of McCartney's best-known solo works and a fixture of British popular culture. The Campbeltown Pipe Band's performance brought the sound of the bagpipes to a mass pop audience. The record held the UK all-time sales record until 1984, when it was surpassed by Band Aid's "Do They Know It's Christmas?"
