Elder (Christianity)

Elders serve as spiritual overseers of a local congregation
Elder
Greekπρεσβύτερος (presbyteros)
RoleTeaching, oversight, pastoral care
Key textsActs 14:23; 1 Timothy 5:17; Titus 1:5–9; 1 Peter 5:1–4

In Christianity, an elder is a person valued for their wisdom and recognised as holding an office of leadership and oversight in a local church. The English word derives from the New Testament Greek presbyteros (πρεσβύτερος), meaning "older man", and is closely related to the office of episkopos (overseer or bishop). Depending on the denomination, elders may be ordained clergy, elected lay leaders, or both.

Biblical background

The office has roots in the elders of Israel, the recognised leaders of towns and tribes in the Old Testament. In the New Testament, elders were appointed in the churches founded by Paul and Barnabas (Acts 14:23), are listed among the leaders of the Jerusalem church alongside the apostles, and receive instructions in the pastoral epistles. First Timothy 5:17 speaks of elders who "rule well", some labouring in preaching and teaching, while Titus 1 and 1 Timothy 3 set out character qualifications. Peter exhorts elders to shepherd the flock willingly and not domineeringly.

By tradition

Presbyterian and Reformed churches are governed by elders: teaching elders (ministers) and ruling elders (elected laypeople) sit together in sessions, presbyteries and assemblies — the system known as presbyterian polity, from which the Presbyterian name derives. In Baptist and many evangelical churches, elders share pastoral leadership with the congregation. In the Churches of Christ and Plymouth Brethren, elders (also called overseers or shepherds) lead autonomously. In Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and Anglican usage, "elder" generally corresponds to the ordained presbyter (priest), while some monastic traditions honour a spiritual elder (Greek geron, Russian starets). In the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, Elder is a priesthood office and title for missionaries and senior leaders; Jehovah's Witnesses are led congregationally by appointed elders.

Qualifications and ordination

Churches typically require elders to be mature in faith, of good reputation, able to teach, and exemplary in family life. Ordination or appointment is usually marked by prayer and the laying on of hands (1 Timothy 4:14), following the biblical pattern.
