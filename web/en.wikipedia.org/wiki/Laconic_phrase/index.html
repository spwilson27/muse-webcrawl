Laconic phrase

A laconic phrase or laconism is a concise or terse statement, especially a blunt or elliptical rejoinder. The term derives from Laconia, the region of ancient Greece whose capital was Sparta, whose inhabitants were famous for their austere, pithy speech.

Famous examples

When Philip II of Macedon threatened Sparta with a long list of what he would do "if I invade Laconia," the Spartans replied with a single word: "If." At Thermopylae, Leonidas is said to have answered Xerxes' demand for the Greeks' weapons with "Molon labe" — "Come and take them." Dienekes, told that Persian arrows would blot out the sun, replied that the Spartans would then "fight in the shade."

Use

Laconic speech was cultivated by the Spartans as a mark of discipline and courage, and the word has passed into general use for any tersely effective utterance.
