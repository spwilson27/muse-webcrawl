Graduated neutral-density filter

[Image placeholder: Square graduated ND filter in a holder]

A graduated neutral-density filter, also called a graduated ND filter, split neutral-density filter, or just a graduated filter, is an optical filter used in photography that has a variable light transmission. Typically it is half clear and half darkening, with the transition between the two areas either abrupt (hard edge) or gradual (soft edge). It is used to bring an overly bright part of a scene into the dynamic range of film or sensor.

Use

The classic application is landscape photography at sunrise or sunset, when the sky is far brighter than the foreground. Placing the dark portion of the filter over the sky reduces its exposure without affecting the rest of the scene, so a single exposure can hold detail in both. Graduated filters are commonly supplied as square or rectangular resin or glass plates held in front of the lens in an adjustable holder, allowing the horizon line of the transition to be positioned precisely.

Types

Filters are described by the density of their dark half (for example ND0.3, ND0.6 or ND0.9, corresponding to one, two or three stops of reduction) and by the hardness of the transition. A "reverse" graduated filter is darkest at the transition line and clears toward the top, suiting sunsets where the brightest area sits at the horizon.

Alternatives

Digital techniques — exposure bracketing and high-dynamic-range blending, or selective adjustment in post-processing — can achieve similar results, but many photographers still prefer optical graduated filters for single-exposure work.
