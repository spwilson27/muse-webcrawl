The canton of Glarus (German: Kanton Glarus) is one of the 26 cantons forming the Swiss Confederation. Situated in east-central Switzerland, it comprises the valley of the river Linth and is one of the most mountainous and least populous cantons. Its capital is the town of Glarus.

Geography

The canton's territory is dominated by the Glarus Alps, including the Tödi (3,614 m), the highest peak of the range. The Linth flows northward through the main valley to the Walensee. The Sardona Tectonic Arena, where older rock strata were thrust over younger ones, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a classic locality for the study of mountain building.

History

Glarus joined the Swiss Confederation in 1352. It became famous for its Landsgemeinde, the open-air assembly of citizens voting by show of hands, which survives to this day — the canton is one of only two (with Appenzell Innerrhoden) retaining the institution. In the 19th century Glarus pioneered factory legislation, limiting working hours in its textile mills, and developed textile printing and later machinery and plastics industries. The Battle of Näfels (1388), a victory over the Habsburgs, is commemorated annually by the Näfelser Fahrt pilgrimage.

Government

Since a 2011 municipal reform the canton has just three municipalities: Glarus Nord, Glarus, and Glarus Süd. Cantonal business is decided by the Landsgemeinde held each May in Glarus town.
