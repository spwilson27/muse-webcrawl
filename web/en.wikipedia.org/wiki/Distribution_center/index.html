Distribution center

A distribution center for a set of products is a specialized building, stocked with products, to be redistributed to retailers, to wholesalers, or directly to consumers. A distribution center is a part of the order fulfillment process. Normally consisting of a warehouse, it is distinguished from a plain warehouse in that it is oriented toward processing and shipping orders rather than long-term storage: products typically move through a distribution center quickly.

Operations

A typical distribution center performs receiving, put-away, storage, order picking, packing, and shipping. Many use warehouse management systems (WMS) and automation such as conveyor networks, automated storage and retrieval systems, and goods-to-person workstations to increase throughput. Cross-docking terminals transfer incoming goods directly to outgoing transport with minimal storage. Facilities dedicated to online retail orders are often called fulfillment centers, while those handling returns processing are specialised reverse-logistics operations.

Network role

Companies design distribution networks by deciding how many centers to operate and where to locate them, trading off transportation cost, delivery speed, and facility expense. Large retailers and e-commerce companies operate dozens of regional distribution centers; Amazon's network of fulfillment centers is a prominent example. A distribution center's processing capacity is commonly measured in units or orders per day, and labor is often its largest operating cost.
