The Sukhoi Su-25 Grach (Russian: Грач, "rook"; NATO reporting name: Frogfoot) is a subsonic, single-seat, twin-engine jet aircraft developed in the Soviet Union by Sukhoi. It was designed to provide close air support for Soviet ground forces, in a role comparable to the American A-10 Thunderbolt II.

Design

The Su-25 is heavily armoured: the pilot sits in a welded titanium-alloy "bathtub" resistant to small-arms and shrapnel, and critical systems are duplicated or shielded to survive battle damage. It is armed with a 30 mm GSh-30-2 cannon and can carry up to about 4,000 kg of unguided rockets, bombs, and guided missiles on ten underwing hardpoints. Its twin Tumansky R-95/R-195 turbojets give it a top speed of about 950 km/h and it is designed to operate from rough forward airstrips.

Operational history

The Su-25 entered service in 1981 and was extensively used in the Soviet–Afghan War, where it proved rugged though vulnerable to shoulder-fired missiles. It has since flown in numerous conflicts, including the Iran–Iraq War, the Gulf War, wars in the Caucasus, the Syrian Civil War, and the Russo-Ukrainian War, in which both Russia and Ukraine have operated it. More than 1,000 were built, and upgraded variants such as the Su-25SM remain in service.
