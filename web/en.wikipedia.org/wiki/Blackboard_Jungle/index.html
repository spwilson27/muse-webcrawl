Blackboard Jungle is a 1955 American social drama film directed by Richard Brooks and based on the 1954 novel by Evan Hunter. It stars Glenn Ford as Richard Dadier, a new English teacher at an inner-city vocational high school whose students — led by Vic Morrow's Artie West and including Sidney Poitier as the gifted Gregory Miller — test his determination to reach them.

Plot

Dadier, a war veteran, takes a job at North Manual High School, where the faculty has largely given up on discipline. After he intervenes to stop an assault on a fellow teacher, he becomes the target of the class delinquents, who vandalise his car and make an anonymous complaint that nearly costs him his marriage and career. Refusing to quit, Dadier gradually wins over Miller and most of the class, confronting West in a final classroom showdown.

Cultural impact

The film is famous for its use of Bill Haley & His Comets' "Rock Around the Clock" over the opening credits, widely credited with popularising rock and roll among teenagers and launching the song to number one. Its frank depiction of juvenile delinquency alarmed some officials — the film faced bans and cuts abroad — but it was a major box-office success and received four Academy Award nominations, including for Brooks's screenplay.
