Andreas Floer (23 August 1956 – 15 May 1991) was a German mathematician who introduced the infinite-dimensional Morse theory now known as Floer homology, a central tool in symplectic geometry and low-dimensional topology. Despite his early death, his work transformed the study of the Arnold conjecture and three-manifold invariants.

Life

Floer studied mathematics at the University of Bielefeld and completed his doctorate at the Ruhr University Bochum in 1982 under Eduard Zehnder, working on periodic solutions of Hamiltonian systems. After positions at Berkeley and elsewhere, he became a professor at Bochum in 1990. He died in 1991 at the age of 34.

Floer homology

In a series of papers in the late 1980s, Floer applied the ideas of Morse theory to infinite-dimensional spaces of paths and connections. For symplectic manifolds he defined a homology theory generated by periodic orbits of Hamiltonian systems, whose non-triviality implies the Arnold conjecture on fixed points of symplectomorphisms. For three-manifolds he defined an instanton Floer homology based on flat connections and the Chern–Simons functional. Related constructions — Lagrangian Floer homology and the Atiyah–Floer conjecture among them — remain active research areas and underpin much of modern symplectic topology, including Fukaya categories and mirror symmetry.
