Religion in Australia

Religion in Australia is diverse. Australia has no official religion, and Section 116 of the Constitution of Australia prohibits the Federal Parliament from making any law to establish any religion, impose any religious observance, or prohibit the free exercise of any religion.

In the 2021 Australian census, 43.9% of the population identified as Christian, 38.9% reported "no religion", 3.2% identified as Muslim, 2.7% as Hindu, and 2.4% as Buddhist. Christianity remains the largest faith group, though its share has declined steadily since the 1960s, while the proportion reporting no religion has grown rapidly. Hinduism is the fastest-growing religion, largely through immigration from India and Nepal.

History

Aboriginal spirituality, centred on the Dreaming, has been practised on the continent for tens of thousands of years. The First Fleet of 1788 brought Anglican Christianity, and the Church of England was long the largest denomination. Catholic numbers grew with Irish convicts and later with post-war immigration from Italy, Croatia, Poland and elsewhere. After the Second World War, migration brought Orthodox Christians, Muslims, Buddhists, Hindus, Sikhs and Jews in larger numbers, making Australia one of the most religiously diverse countries in the world.

Christianity

The largest Christian traditions are Catholicism (about 20% of the population in 2021) and Anglicanism (about 9.8%), followed by the Uniting Church, Presbyterian and Reformed churches, Eastern Orthodoxy, Baptists and Pentecostal churches. Church attendance is low by historical standards, with only a minority of self-identified Christians attending regularly.

Other religions

Islam is the second-largest religion, followed by Hinduism and Buddhism. Judaism has a long history in Australia dating to the First Fleet. Smaller communities include Sikhs, Baha'is, Jains, and practitioners of traditional Aboriginal religion. Interfaith dialogue is promoted by bodies such as the Faith Communities Council of Victoria.

Freedom of religion

Freedom of religion is protected by the Constitution and by anti-discrimination law, although debates continue over religious exemptions in employment and education, and over proposals for a federal Religious Discrimination Act.
