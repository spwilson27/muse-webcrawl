Gröbner basis

In mathematics, and more specifically in computer algebra, computational algebraic geometry, and computational commutative algebra, a Gröbner basis is a particular kind of generating set of an ideal in a polynomial ring over a field. A Gröbner basis allows many important properties of the ideal and of the associated algebraic variety to be deduced easily, such as the dimension and the number of zeros when it is finite.

History and definition

The concept is named after the Austrian mathematician Wolfgang Gröbner; his student Bruno Buchberger developed the theory and, in his 1965 PhD thesis, gave Buchberger's algorithm for computing Gröbner bases. Given a monomial ordering, the Gröbner basis of an ideal is a generating set whose leading terms generate the ideal of all leading terms of the ideal. A reduced Gröbner basis is unique for a given ideal and monomial ordering.

Applications

Gröbner bases provide algorithms for solving systems of polynomial equations, deciding ideal membership, computing elimination ideals, and analysing algebraic varieties. They are implemented in computer algebra systems such as Mathematica, Maple, Macaulay2, and Singular, and are used in fields ranging from robotics and coding theory to automated theorem proving. Their worst-case complexity can be very high, which motivates ongoing research into faster algorithms such as F4 and F5.
