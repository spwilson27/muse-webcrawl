Japanese kitchen knife

[Image: Japanese chef's knife]

Japanese kitchen knives (包丁, hōchō) are a family of kitchen knives produced in Japan, renowned for their sharpness and the craftsmanship of their construction. They have become highly prized by professional chefs and home cooks worldwide, and are produced in traditional knife-making centres such as Sakai, Seki, and Echizen.

History

Japanese blade-making dates to the swordsmithing tradition that flourished from the Kamakura period. When sword production declined in the nineteenth century, many smiths turned to making kitchen knives, adapting their techniques of forging and sharpening to culinary blades.

Types

  • Gyūtō — the Japanese chef's knife, a general-purpose blade for slicing meat, fish, and vegetables.
  • Santoku — a versatile all-purpose knife, whose name means "three virtues" (meat, fish, vegetables).
  • Yanagiba — a long, thin knife for slicing raw fish for sashimi, traditionally sharpened on one side only.
  • Deba — a heavy knife for breaking down fish and poultry.
  • Nakiri and Usuba — vegetable knives, the nakiri double-bevelled and the usuba single-bevelled.

Construction

Many Japanese knives are made from high-carbon steel, which holds a fine edge but requires care to prevent rust; stainless and Damascus-clad variants are also common. Single-bevel (kataba) knives are sharpened on one side and are associated with traditional Japanese cuisine, while double-bevel (ryōba) knives resemble Western blades.
