Convoy

A convoy is a group of vehicles, typically motor vehicles or ships, traveling together for mutual support and protection. Often, a convoy is organized with armed defensive support and may help maintain cohesion within a unit. The term is also used for groups of trucks travelling together on highways and, by extension, for protest movements of vehicles.

Naval convoys

Convoying merchant ships under warship escort is an ancient practice — used by the Hanseatic League and in the Spanish treasure fleets — but it reached its greatest scale in the two world wars. In the First World War, Allied convoys dramatically reduced losses to German U-boats after their adoption in 1917. In the Second World War, the Battle of the Atlantic saw convoys of dozens of merchantmen escorted across the Atlantic, hunted by U-boat "wolfpacks". The Arctic convoys to Murmansk and the Malta convoys in the Mediterranean were among the most hazardous operations of the war.

Land convoys

Military land convoys move supplies and personnel and are vulnerable to ambush and improvised explosive devices, as seen in Iraq and Afghanistan. Civilian "truck convoys" coordinate long-distance haulage, and the word entered popular culture through the 1975 song "Convoy" by C. W. McCall and the 1978 film of the same name. Vehicle convoys have also been used as a form of protest, notably the 2022 Canadian trucker convoy protests.
