UEFA Euro 2004

The 2004 UEFA European Football Championship, commonly referred to as Euro 2004, was the twelfth UEFA European Championship, held in Portugal from 12 June to 4 July 2004. In one of the greatest upsets in football history, the tournament was won by Greece, who defeated the hosts Portugal 1–0 in the final in Lisbon.

Tournament summary

Greece, managed by the German coach Otto Rehhagel, opened the tournament with a shock 2–1 win over Portugal and advanced with disciplined, defensive football. In the knockout rounds Greece defeated holders France 1–0 in the quarter-finals and the Czech Republic — the tournament's most admired attacking side — in the semi-final with a "silver goal" from Traianos Dellas. In the final at the Estádio da Luz, Angelos Charisteas headed the only goal in the 57th minute to give Greece its first major title, despite Greece having been considered outsiders of up to 150–1 before the tournament.

Venues

Matches were played in ten stadiums across eight Portuguese cities, including the Estádio da Luz and Estádio José Alvalade in Lisbon, the Estádio do Dragão in Porto, and stadiums in Aveiro, Braga, Coimbra, Faro-Loulé, Guimarães, and Leiria, several of them newly built for the event.
