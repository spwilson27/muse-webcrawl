Job search
Also calledJob hunting
Key toolsRésumé/CV, cover letter, job boards, networking, interviews
Related conceptsRecruitment, unemployment, career development

A job search, or job hunt, is the act of looking for employment, typically because of unemployment, underemployment, dissatisfaction with a current position, or a desire for a better position. The usual process involves identifying suitable vacancies, preparing application materials such as a résumé or curriculum vitae and a cover letter, applying, and attending job interviews.

Methods

Job seekers find work through many channels:

  • Online job boards and search engines such as Indeed and LinkedIn, which aggregate vacancies and host applications
  • Company websites and direct applications
  • Networking — personal and professional contacts, widely considered the most effective route, since many positions are filled without public advertisement (the "hidden job market")
  • Recruitment agencies and headhunters
  • Public employment services, job centres and careers fairs
  • Newspapers and trade journals, historically dominant and now a minor channel

Process and research

Job-search theory in economics treats hunting as a search process under imperfect information: workers sample wage offers and accept when an offer exceeds their reservation wage. Research in organisational psychology studies the intensity and quality of search behaviour, the role of self-efficacy, and the psychological toll of repeated rejection. Employers increasingly screen applicants with applicant tracking systems that filter résumés by keywords before human review.
