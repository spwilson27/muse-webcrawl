Vouvray is a French wine region in the Loire Valley located in the Touraine district, east of Tours in the commune of Vouvray and surrounding villages. Its wines are made exclusively from Chenin blanc grown on tuffeau limestone soils, and the appellation, granted AOC status in 1936, is renowned for producing wines in a remarkable range of styles, from bone-dry to lusciously sweet, still and sparkling.

Styles

Labels indicate sweetness: sec (dry), demi-sec (off-dry), moelleux (sweet) and doux (very sweet, from the ripest or botrytised grapes). In cool vintages much of the harvest becomes sparkling Vouvray, made by the traditional method. The region's cellars are often carved directly into the tuffeau cliffs, where wines mature at constant cool temperatures.

Characteristics and ageing

Young Vouvray shows aromas of quince, apple, acacia and honey with Chenin's characteristic high acidity, which allows fine examples — especially moelleux from great years — to age for decades, developing flavours of honey, lanolin and dried fruit. Notable producers include Domaine Huet and Domaine du Clos Naudin (Foreau).

Geography

The appellation covers about 2,200 hectares on the right bank of the Loire. The combination of tuffeau soils, a continental-maritime climate and south-facing slopes above the river produces grapes that ripen slowly while retaining acidity.
