Traffic collision

Deaths: ~1.19 million per year worldwide (WHO estimate)

Also called: Car accident, road traffic accident

A traffic collision, also called a motor vehicle collision or car accident, occurs when a vehicle collides with another vehicle, a pedestrian, an animal, or a stationary object. Road traffic injuries are a leading cause of death worldwide, especially for people aged 5–29.

Causes

Human factors dominate: speeding, alcohol and drug impairment, distracted driving (including phones), fatigue and failure to use seat belts and helmets. Vehicle defects and road design also contribute. Risk analysis often uses the Haddon matrix of host, vehicle and environment before, during and after a crash.

Prevention

The Safe System / Vision Zero approach designs roads and vehicles to forgive human error: speed management, separated cycling infrastructure, crashworthy cars with airbags and electronic stability control, graduated licensing, enforcement and post-crash care.

Classification and law

Collisions are classed as rear-end, head-on, side-impact, rollover and single-vehicle, among others. Liability, insurance and criminal charges (such as dangerous driving) follow investigation of fault and causation.
