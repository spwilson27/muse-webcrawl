Alessandria is a city and commune in Piedmont, Italy, and the capital of the province of Alessandria. It is situated on the alluvial plain between the Tanaro and Bormida rivers, about 90 kilometres southeast of Turin. The city is an important rail and industrial hub.

History

Alessandria was founded in 1168 by the Lombard League as a fortified outpost against the Holy Roman Emperor Frederick Barbarossa, and was named in honour of Pope Alexander III. It famously withstood Barbarossa's siege in 1174–75. The city later passed between Milan, Savoy and France; its massive star-shaped citadel, built in the 18th century under the House of Savoy, remains one of Europe's best-preserved fortresses of its kind. The Battle of Marengo (1800), Napoleon's decisive victory over Austria, was fought just outside the city.

Economy and culture

Alessandria is known for hat-making — the Borsalino company, maker of the famous fedora, was founded there in 1857 — as well as jewellery, food processing and logistics. The writer and philosopher Umberto Eco was born in Alessandria in 1932. In November 1994 the city suffered severe flooding when the Tanaro burst its banks.
