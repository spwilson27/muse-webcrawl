Kassala

Kassala (Arabic: كسلا) is the capital city of Kassala State in eastern Sudan, near the border with Eritrea. It lies on the seasonal Gash River and is overshadowed by the granite domes of the Taka Mountains, including Jebel Kassala. The city is a regional trade and agricultural centre, long known for its fruit gardens and markets.

History

Kassala was founded around 1840 as a military outpost by the Turco-Egyptian rulers of Sudan. During the Mahdist War it was besieged and captured by the Mahdists in 1885, then occupied by Italy in 1894 under an agreement with Britain before being returned to Anglo-Egyptian control in 1897–98. In the Second World War, Italian forces from Eritrea briefly occupied Kassala in 1940–41 before the East African campaign pushed them back. The region saw further fighting during the Eritrean independence war and Sudan's own civil conflicts, and eastern Sudan has continued to be affected by regional instability.

People and economy

The area is home to Beja peoples including the Hadendoa — famously the "Fuzzy-Wuzzies" of Kipling's verse and the soldiers who broke a British square at Tamai — as well as Rashaida and other groups; the city has a distinctive camel market and sword-making tradition among the Hadendoa. Irrigated agriculture on the Gash spate-irrigation scheme produces sorghum, cotton and fruit. Kassala is connected by road and rail to Khartoum and Port Sudan.
