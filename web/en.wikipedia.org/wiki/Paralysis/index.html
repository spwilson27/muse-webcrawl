Paralysis

Paralysis of the lower limbs (paraplegia) often requires the use of a wheelchair.

Paralysis (from Greek parálysis, "disabling of the nerves") is a loss of motor function in one or more muscles in the body. It is generally caused by damage within the nervous system, especially the spinal cord, or by damage to the brain. Other causes include peripheral nerve injury, certain toxins, and diseases such as poliomyelitis.

Classification

Paralysis may be localised to one area or generalised, and may be partial (paresis) or complete. Common patterns include:

  • Monoplegia — one limb
  • Hemiplegia — one side of the body, often from stroke
  • Paraplegia — both legs, usually from spinal cord injury
  • Quadriplegia (tetraplegia) — all four limbs
  • Diplegia — the same region on both sides of the body

Causes

Major causes include stroke, traumatic spinal cord injury, cerebral palsy, multiple sclerosis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), Guillain–Barré syndrome, Bell's palsy (facial paralysis), and infections such as polio. Paralysis may also be temporary, as in sleep paralysis or periodic paralysis associated with potassium disturbances.

Management

There is no cure for most forms of paralysis, but management includes physical and occupational therapy, assistive devices, prevention of complications such as pressure sores and contractures, and treatment of the underlying condition where possible. Research into nerve regeneration, stem cells and brain–computer interfaces is ongoing.
