Nicosia International Airport (Greek: Διεθνές Αεροδρόμιο Λευκωσίας) is a largely deserted airport located 8.2 km (5.1 mi) west of Nicosia, Cyprus. It was the island's main international airport until 1974, when it ceased commercial operations during the Turkish invasion and fell within the United Nations Buffer Zone. The site remains under UN control and is used as the headquarters of the United Nations Peacekeeping Force in Cyprus (UNFICYP).

History

The airfield began as a Royal Air Force station in the 1930s, known as RAF Nicosia. After the Second World War it developed into Cyprus's principal civil airport, and a new terminal designed by a West German firm opened in 1968. During the 1974 invasion the airport was the scene of heavy fighting; a Cyprus Airways Trident was destroyed on the apron, and the UN negotiated a ceasefire around the site to prevent its capture. Commercial traffic subsequently moved to Larnaca International Airport in the south.

Present state

The terminal building, control tower, and hangars stand largely as they were left in 1974, decaying inside the buffer zone. A derelict Cyprus Airways Hawker Siddeley Trident remains parked on the apron. The site is off-limits to the public apart from occasional organised visits.
