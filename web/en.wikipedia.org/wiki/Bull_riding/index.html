Bull riding

Bull riding is a rodeo sport that involves a rider getting on a bucking bull and attempting to stay mounted for eight seconds while the animal tries to buck off the rider. It has been called "the most dangerous eight seconds in sports". Bull riding originated in the ranching contests of the American West and in Mexican charreada traditions, and is now a professional sport with major organisations in the United States, Brazil, Mexico, Canada and Australia.

Rules and scoring

The rider mounts the bull in a chute, gripping a braided bull rope wrapped around the animal's chest with one hand; the other hand must remain free and cannot touch the bull, the rope or the rider's own body. When the gate opens, the bull bucks, spins and twists. If the rider remains mounted for the full eight seconds, two judges score the ride out of 100 points in total: up to 50 for the rider's control, timing and style, and up to 50 for the bull's bucking power and difficulty. Scores of 90 points or more are considered exceptional.

The bulls are specially bred for bucking ability and are valuable athletes in their own right; famous bucking bulls such as Bodacious and Bushwacker became celebrities. A flank strap encourages the bull to buck, and animal welfare in the sport is a subject of continuing debate.

Professional competition

The premier organisation is Professional Bull Riders (PBR), founded in 1992 by twenty bull riders who broke away from traditional rodeo to establish bull riding as a standalone sport; its top series culminates in the PBR World Finals. The Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA) includes bull riding as one of the events of full rodeos. Brazilian riders have been particularly successful internationally. Because of the extreme injury risk, riders commonly wear protective vests and increasingly helmets rather than the traditional cowboy hat.
