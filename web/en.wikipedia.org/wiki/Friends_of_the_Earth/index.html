Friends of the Earth

Friends of the Earth International (FoEI) is an international network of grassroots environmental organizations in 73 countries. It was founded in 1971 by four organizations from France, Sweden, the UK and the United States, who had agreed at a small meeting in Rosay, France, to coordinate their actions. The founding groups had in turn been formed shortly earlier: the United States branch was founded in 1969 by David Brower after he left the Sierra Club, and the UK branch in 1970.

Structure

The network operates from a small secretariat in Amsterdam. Member groups are autonomous and decide their own campaigns, but commit to the network's mission of protecting the environment and promoting sustainable, socially just societies. FoEI takes positions combining scientific evidence with a justice-oriented analysis, campaigns on climate change, forests and biodiversity, food sovereignty, and economic justice, and opposes what it sees as environmentally harmful corporate and trade practices.

Campaigns

FoEI is known for campaigns against climate change, including calls for binding cuts in greenhouse gas emissions and opposition to fossil fuel financing. It criticizes carbon offsetting and some market-based mechanisms, arguing that they allow polluters to avoid real reductions. Other long-running themes include defending land rights of indigenous peoples and small farmers, halting deforestation, and challenging the power of transnational corporations, for example through support for a binding international treaty on business and human rights.

Notable member groups

Member organizations include Friends of the Earth (US), Friends of the Earth England, Wales and Northern Ireland, and groups across Africa, Asia-Pacific, Europe and Latin America. The US branch, founded by Brower, campaigned on nuclear power, pesticides and energy policy in its early decades.
