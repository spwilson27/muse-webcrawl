Ottava rima

Ottava rima is a rhyming stanza form of Italian origin. Originally used for long poems on heroic themes, it later came to be popular in the writing of mock-heroic works. Its earliest known use is in the writings of Giovanni Boccaccio.

The ottava rima stanza in English consists of eight iambic lines, usually iambic pentameters, each of ten syllables (or eleven in the Italian hendecasyllabic original), rhyming ABABABCC:

I want a hero: an uncommon want,
When every year and month sends forth a new one,
Till, after cloying the gazettes with cant,
The age discovers he is not the true one;
Of such as these I should not care to vaunt,
I'll therefore take our ancient friend Don Juan—
We all have seen him, in the pantomime,
Sent to the Devil somewhat ere his time.
— Lord Byron, Don Juan (Canto I)

History

Boccaccio used the form in Teseida and Il Filostrato. It became the standard stanza of Italian chivalric epic: Boiardo's Orlando Innamorato, Ariosto's Orlando Furioso and Tasso's Gerusalemme Liberata are all in ottava rima. In English it was adopted by Wyatt, and used for serious poetry by Milton's contemporaries, but reached its greatest fame in Byron's Beppo and Don Juan, whose closing couplets lent themselves to comic, digressive wit. Keats used it in Isabella, Yeats in "Sailing to Byzantium" and "Among School Children", and it remains a living form.
