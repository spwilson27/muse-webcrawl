Copa do Brasil

Founded: 1989
Region: Brazil
Number of teams: 92 (recent editions)
Current champions: decided annually
Most successful club: Cruzeiro (6 titles)

The Copa do Brasil (English: Brazil Cup) is a knockout football competition played by Brazilian representative teams of their respective states. It is the domestic cup competition of Brazil, roughly equivalent to the FA Cup in England. The competition was founded in 1989 to give clubs from smaller states a better chance to compete with big teams from major states.

Format

The tournament is played in single-elimination knockout ties. In its modern format, 92 teams take part, qualifying through state championships and the CBF ranking. Early rounds are played over single matches or two legs depending on the round, and the final is played over two legs. The champion qualifies for the following season's Copa Libertadores.

History

Grêmio won the inaugural edition in 1989. Cruzeiro is the most successful club with six titles, and the competition has become noted for upset victories by smaller clubs, including Santo André's win over Flamengo in 2004 and Paulista's title in 2005. Prize money grew substantially in the 2010s, making the Copa do Brasil one of the richest domestic cup competitions in the world.

Winners

Major winners include Cruzeiro, Grêmio, Corinthians, Palmeiras, Flamengo and Atlético Mineiro. Flamengo and Corinthians have each won multiple editions and contested famous finals.
