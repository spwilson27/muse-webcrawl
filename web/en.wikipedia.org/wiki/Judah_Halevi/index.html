Judah Halevi

Judah Halevi (also Yehuda Halevi or ha-Levi; Hebrew: יהודה הלוי; Arabic: يهوذا اللاوي; c. 1075–1141) was a Spanish Jewish physician, poet and philosopher. He was born in Spain, either in Toledo or Tudela, in 1075 or 1086, and died shortly after arriving in the Holy Land in 1141, at that point the Crusader Kingdom of Jerusalem.

Halevi is considered one of the greatest Hebrew poets, celebrated both for his religious and secular poems, many of which appear in present-day liturgy. His philosophical magnum opus is the Kuzari, completed in Arabic and translated by Judah ibn Tibbon into Hebrew.

Biography

Halevi was born to a wealthy family in Tudela or Toledo. He studied in Lucena at the yeshiva of Isaac Alfasi, and wrote poems in Hebrew as a youth. He travelled through Spain, was received in Muslim Al-Andalus by Jewish courtiers, and practised medicine. In his later years he resolved to emigrate to the Land of Israel, leaving Spain in 1140, travelling via Alexandria and Cairo, and arriving in Jerusalem in 1141. Legend holds that he was killed by an Arab horseman at the gates of Jerusalem while reciting his ode to Zion.

Poetry

His secular verse includes panegyrics, love poems, and drinking songs written in the Arabic-influenced forms of Andalusian Hebrew poetry. His liturgical poems, or piyyutim, entered the prayer books of many communities. His Zionides, including "My heart is in the East, and I in the uttermost West," express longing for Zion.

Philosophy

The Kuzari, subtitled "The Book of Refutation and Proof on Behalf of the Despised Religion," is framed as a dialogue between the king of the Khazars and representatives of philosophy, Christianity, Islam, and Judaism. The Jewish sage argues for the superiority of revealed religion grounded in historical experience over abstract metaphysics.
