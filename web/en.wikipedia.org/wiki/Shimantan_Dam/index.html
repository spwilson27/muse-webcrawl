Shimantan Dam

The Shimantan Dam is a dam on the Hong River (Honghe), a tributary of the Huai River, near Wugang in Henan Province, China. Completed in the late 1950s during the Great Leap Forward, it became infamous as one of the dams that failed catastrophically during Typhoon Nina in August 1975 — a disaster often cited among the deadliest dam failures in history.

Construction

Shimantan and the nearby Banqiao Dam were built with Soviet assistance in the early 1950s and reinforced in the following years. The Shimantan reservoir had a storage capacity of roughly 94 million cubic metres and served irrigation and flood-control purposes in the Huai River basin.

The 1975 failure

Typhoon Nina stalled over Henan in August 1975 and dropped extraordinary rainfall — over a metre in places within a few days. On the night of 7–8 August 1975 the Banqiao Dam failed, and the Shimantan Dam also collapsed under the flood. Dozens of dams failed across the region in a cascading sequence. The resulting flood inundated vast areas of the Huai plain; death-toll estimates vary widely, from tens of thousands immediately to totals above 150,000 including subsequent famine and disease. The disaster was not publicly acknowledged in China for many years.

Aftermath

The surviving dams of the region were rebuilt or reinforced in later decades. The Banqiao–Shimantan failures remain a standard case study in dam-safety engineering on the dangers of designing for inadequate flood standards.
