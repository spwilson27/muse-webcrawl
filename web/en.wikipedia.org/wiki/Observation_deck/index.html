Observation deck

Observation deck (placeholder image)

An observation deck, observation platform, or viewing platform is an elevated sightseeing platform, usually on the top floor or roof of a tall building or tower, that offers visitors a long-distance view and a good place to take photos of the surroundings. Many observation decks are among the most popular tourist attractions in the cities where they are located.

Observation decks may be enclosed behind glass or open-air, sometimes with glass floors or outdoor walkways extending beyond the building's facade. The tallest observation deck in the world is on the 118th floor of the Shanghai Tower, at about 562 metres; other famous examples include those of the Burj Khalifa in Dubai, the CN Tower in Toronto, and the Empire State Building in New York City.

History

purpose-built viewing platforms became common with the rise of the skyscraper in the early twentieth century, following precedents such as the observation galleries of the Eiffel Tower (1889). Observation decks have since become a standard feature of supertall buildings and telecommunications towers, often combined with restaurants, exhibitions, and thrill attractions such as glass slides or ledge boxes.
