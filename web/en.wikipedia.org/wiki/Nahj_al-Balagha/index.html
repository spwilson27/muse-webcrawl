Nahj al-Balagha

Nahj al-Balagha (Arabic: نهج البلاغة, "The Peak of Eloquence") is the best-known collection of sermons, letters and sayings attributed to Ali ibn Abi Talib, the fourth Rashidun caliph and first Shia imam. It was compiled in the 10th–11th century by the Shia scholar Sharif al-Radi (d. 1015).

Contents

The work contains over 200 sermons, about 80 letters, and nearly 500 short sayings, addressing theology, ethics, governance and the transience of the world. Famous pieces include the Sermon of the Shaqshaqiya and Ali's letter of instructions to Malik al-Ashtar, his governor of Egypt, often cited as a model of just rule. The book is revered in Shia Islam and admired across Arabic literature for its eloquence; questions about the authenticity of some attributions have been debated by scholars for centuries.
