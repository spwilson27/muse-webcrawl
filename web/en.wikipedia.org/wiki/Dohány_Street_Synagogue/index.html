Dohány Street Synagogue

The Dohány Street Synagogue (Hungarian: Dohány utcai zsinagóga), also known as the Great Synagogue or Tabakgasse Synagogue, is a historical building in Erzsébetváros, the 7th district of Budapest, Hungary. It is the largest synagogue in Europe, seating 3,000 people, and a centre of Neolog Judaism.

The synagogue was built between 1854 and 1859 in the Moorish Revival style, based on the design of Ludwig Förster. The complex includes the Great Synagogue, the Heroes' Temple, the graveyard, the Memorial, and the Jewish Museum, built on the site where Theodor Herzl's house stood — Herzl, the founder of modern political Zionism, was born in the adjacent house in 1860.

Architecture

The building features twin onion-domed towers 43 metres high, a rose window above the main entrance, and an interior modelled in part on Catholic basilicas, with a central aisle, pulpits and an organ. Franz Liszt and Camille Saint-Saëns played the original 5,000-pipe organ.

History

During the Second World War the synagogue stood within the Budapest Ghetto and served as a shelter; it was seriously damaged by aerial bombing. Under Communism it again served a much-reduced Jewish community. Restoration, funded partly by the Hungarian state and by donations associated with actor Tony Curtis, took place between 1991 and 1998. The adjacent Raoul Wallenberg Holocaust Memorial Park contains the Memorial of the Hungarian Jewish Martyrs, a weeping willow sculpture whose leaves bear victims' names.
