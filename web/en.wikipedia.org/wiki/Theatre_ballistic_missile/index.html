Overview

A theatre ballistic missile (TBM) is a ballistic missile with a range of roughly 300 to 3,500 kilometres, intended for use within a theatre of operations — that is, against regional targets such as airfields, ports, troop concentrations, logistics hubs, and command facilities — rather than for intercontinental strategic attack. The category corresponds broadly to short-range ballistic missiles (SRBMs) and medium-range ballistic missiles (MRBMs).

Characteristics and defence

Theatre ballistic missiles usually follow a ballistic trajectory after a powered boost phase, re-entering the atmosphere at high speed. They may carry conventional, cluster, or in some cases chemical or nuclear warheads. Their use in regional wars — notably Scud attacks during the Iran-Iraq War and the 1991 Gulf War — spurred the development of theatre missile defence systems such as Patriot, THAAD, the S-300/S-400 family, and Arrow, designed to intercept missiles in the terminal or upper-atmosphere phases of flight.

Modern precision-guided TBMs, including quasi-ballistic designs that manoeuvre in flight, have blurred the line between ballistic missiles and cruise missiles and remain a central concern of regional military planning.
