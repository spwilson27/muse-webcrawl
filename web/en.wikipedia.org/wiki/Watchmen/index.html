Watchmen is a comic book limited series by the British creative team of writer Alan Moore, artist Dave Gibbons and colourist John Higgins. Published by DC Comics as a twelve-issue series from 1986 to 1987, it was later collected into a single-volume graphic novel and is regarded as one of the most significant works in the medium's history.

Premise

Set in an alternate 1985 in which costumed vigilantes emerged in the 1940s and 1960s, radically altering history — the United States won the Vietnam War with the help of the godlike Doctor Manhattan, and Richard Nixon remains president — the story opens with the murder of Edward Blake, the Comedian, a government-backed former hero. The masked vigilante Rorschach investigates, suspecting a plot to eliminate former costumed adventurers, and gradually uncovers a conspiracy that leads to a catastrophic climax in Antarctica.

Origins

Moore originally proposed using characters DC had acquired from Charlton Comics, but DC, unwilling to see those characters rendered unusable, encouraged him to create originals instead: Rorschach (after the Question), Nite Owl (Blue Beetle), Doctor Manhattan (Captain Atom), the Comedian (Peacemaker), Silk Spectre (Nightshade) and Ozymandias (Thunderbolt).

Structure and themes

The series is famous for its formal rigor: each issue follows a strict nine-panel grid, images and text mirror one another across chapters, and supplementary prose documents — memoirs, psychiatric files, newspaper clippings — end each issue. Chapter V, "Fearful Symmetry," is laid out as a visual palindrome. Themes include the psychology of vigilantism, Cold War nuclear anxiety, the nature of time, and the moral ambiguity summarised by the title's source, Juvenal's question Quis custodiet ipsos custodes? — "Who watches the watchmen?"

Reception and legacy

Watchmen won a Hugo Award in 1988 and appears on Time's "All-Time 100 Novels" list. Together with works such as The Dark Knight Returns, it helped usher in a darker, more adult era in mainstream comics. A film adaptation directed by Zack Snyder was released in 2009, and an HBO television series by Damon Lindelof, set decades after the comic's events, aired in 2019 to critical acclaim. Moore, as with other adaptations of his work, declined involvement and credit.
