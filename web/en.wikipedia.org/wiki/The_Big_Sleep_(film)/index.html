The Big Sleep is a 1946 American film noir directed by Howard Hawks and starring Humphrey Bogart as private detective Philip Marlowe and Lauren Bacall as Vivian Rutledge. The screenplay, adapted from Raymond Chandler's 1939 novel of the same name, was written by William Faulkner, Leigh Brackett, and Jules Furthman.

Plot

Marlowe is hired by the wealthy, dying General Sternwood to resolve a blackmail attempt against his younger daughter, Carmen. The case entangles Marlowe in a web of gambling, pornography, and murder, while he spars and flirts with the elder daughter, Vivian. The plot is famously convoluted: legend holds that during filming, Hawks and the writers cabled Chandler to ask who had killed one of the victims, and Chandler replied that he did not know either. The film is remembered less for its mystery than for its atmosphere and the crackling dialogue between Bogart and Bacall.

Production and versions

The film was completed in 1945 but its release was delayed while Warner Bros. cleared a backlog of war-related films; the extra time allowed reshoots that expanded Bacall's role after the success of To Have and Have Not. The 1945 prerelease version was rediscovered and released in the 1990s, and the two versions are frequently compared by scholars.

Legacy

The Big Sleep is regarded as one of the greatest film noirs and one of the finest film adaptations of Chandler's work. In 1997 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant.
