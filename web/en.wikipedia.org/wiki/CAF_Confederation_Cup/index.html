CAF Confederation Cup

The CAF Confederation Cup, known as the TotalEnergies CAF Confederation Cup for sponsorship reasons, is an annual association football club competition established in 2004 by the Confederation of African Football (CAF). It is the second-tier club competition in African football, ranking below the CAF Champions League. The tournament was formed by merging the African Cup Winners' Cup and the CAF Cup.

History

CAF created the Confederation Cup in 2004 on the model of the UEFA Cup (now Europa League), absorbing the Cup Winners' Cup (begun 1975) and the CAF Cup (begun 1992). Entry is granted to domestic cup winners and high-placed league finishers from CAF's member associations, with places allocated according to CAF's 5-year association ranking; teams eliminated at certain stages of the Champions League also drop into the competition.

Format

The competition begins with preliminary and first-round knockout ties played over two legs. Surviving teams enter a group stage (four groups of four), followed by quarter-finals, semi-finals, and a final — in recent seasons played as a two-legged tie (and as a single match in some editions). The champion qualifies to play the CAF Champions League winner in the CAF Super Cup.

Winners

Tunisia's CS Sfaxien is the most successful club with three titles (2007, 2008, 2013). Other multiple winners include Étoile du Sahel, TP Mazembe, Raja Casablanca, RS Berkane, and Zamalek. Moroccan, Tunisian, Egyptian, and Congolese clubs have dominated the honours list.
