Marriage certificate

A marriage certificate (in some jurisdictions called a marriage licence record or certificate of marriage) is an official statement that two people are married, issued by a government authority after a marriage ceremony and its registration.

Contents

Certificates typically record the names of the spouses, the date and place of marriage, the officiant and witnesses, and biographical details such as dates of birth and parents' names. In many countries the underlying marriage register entry is a vital record used to prove marital status.

Uses

A marriage certificate is commonly required to change a surname, claim spousal benefits, obtain visas, settle inheritance, file taxes jointly, or apply for divorce. Historically, parish registers performed this function before civil registration systems began in the 19th century.

By country

In the United States certificates are issued by counties and states following a licence obtained before the ceremony. In England and Wales, civil registration began in 1837, and couples receive a certificate after the register is signed. Similar registry systems operate throughout Europe and much of the world.
