Left-hand path and right-hand path

In Western esotericism the left-hand path and right-hand path are a dichotomy between two opposing approaches to magic and religion. The terms derive from Indian tantra, where vāmācāra ("left-hand attainment") denoted heterodox practices that deliberately transgressed social and religious taboos, in contrast to dakṣiṇācāra ("right-hand attainment"), which conformed to orthodox norms.

Terminology and origins

The terms were introduced into Western occultism by Helena Blavatsky and the Theosophical Society in the nineteenth century, drawing on Hindu tantric categories. In the Western reinterpretation, the right-hand path came to denote religions and magical systems that submit to divine authority and conventional morality, aiming at union with God or dissolution of the ego, while the left-hand path denoted systems emphasising self-deification, individualism, and the preservation and strengthening of the ego.

Characteristics

Right-hand path traditions are typically associated with white magic, communal worship, and ethical restraint. Left-hand path traditions are associated with black magic in popular usage, although practitioners such as those of the Church of Satan, the Temple of Set, and the Typhonian Order reject the equation of the left-hand path with evil, instead describing it as a path of self-determination and antinomian spirituality. The scholar Dave Evans and others have noted that self-identification with the left-hand path became prominent only in the twentieth century.

Criticism

Historians of esotericism observe that the dichotomy is a simplified and largely Western construction that fits Indian tantra poorly, and that many traditions combine elements of both. Some practitioners reject the distinction entirely or use the terms in inverted senses.
