The United States Open Championship, commonly known as the U.S. Open, is the national open golf championship of the United States and one of the sport's four major championships. Organised by the USGA since 1895, it is held in mid-June at rotating American courses and is famous for the severity of its test: narrow fairways, thick penal rough, fast greens and winning scores often close to par. It is the third major of the season, following the Masters and the PGA Championship and preceding The Open Championship.

History

Amateur Francis Ouimet's playoff victory over Harry Vardon and Ted Ray in 1913 made front-page news and ignited American golf's boom. Bobby Jones won four titles in the 1920s, and Ben Hogan four in the post-war years, including the 1950 event at Merion barely a year after a near-fatal car crash. Jack Nicklaus also won four; Tiger Woods won three, most famously the 2000 championship at Pebble Beach by a record 15 strokes and the 2008 title at Torrey Pines on a broken leg. Recurring anchor sites in the modern rota include Pebble Beach, Oakmont, Shinnecock Hills and Pinehurst.

Qualification

The U.S. Open is genuinely open: roughly half the 156-man field earns its place through local and sectional qualifying, allowing unknown club professionals and amateurs to compete alongside the world's best — a pathway that has produced champions and legends since Ouimet's day.
