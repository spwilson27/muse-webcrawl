Kirtland Air Force Base

Kirtland Air Force Base (IATA: ABQ, ICAO: KABQ) is a United States Air Force base in Albuquerque, New Mexico, in the Albuquerque Basin. At roughly 51,558 acres it is the third-largest installation in Air Force Global Strike Command and the sixth-largest in the Air Force. The base shares its runways with Albuquerque International Sunport, the state's main civil airport.

History

The base began in 1941 as Albuquerque Army Air Base, a training field built beside the municipal airport, and was renamed for Colonel Roy C. Kirtland, one of the Army's earliest pilots, who died that year. During the Second World War it trained bombardiers and supported the nearby Manhattan Project: scientists and ordnance from Los Alamos passed through the field, and the base's 509th Composite Group links it to the atomic missions of 1945.

Current missions

Kirtland hosts the Air Force Nuclear Weapons Center, responsible for nuclear systems sustainment; the 377th Air Base Wing as host unit; and major tenant organisations including the Air Force Research Laboratory's Directed Energy and Space Vehicles directorates, the Defense Threat Reduction Agency, and the Kirtland Underground Munitions Maintenance and Storage Complex, one of the largest nuclear weapons storage facilities in the United States. The 58th Special Operations Wing trains aircrew on MC-130, CV-22 and HH-60 aircraft. Sandia National Laboratories sits adjacent to the base and is its largest associated employer.
