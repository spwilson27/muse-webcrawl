Ballot

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A ballot is a device used to cast votes in an election — originally a small ball (as in blackballing in clubs), now usually a paper document or an electronic record. On the ballot, each voter indicates choices among candidates and, in referendums and initiatives, among propositions. Ballot design is a specialised field: the order of candidates, layout, typography, instructions and language access all measurably affect error rates and outcomes.

Forms and controversies

Forms include hand-counted paper ballots, machine-readable optical-scan sheets, direct-recording electronic (DRE) voting machines with or without paper trails, and postal ballots. Famous design failures include Palm Beach County's "butterfly ballot" in the 2000 US presidential election, credited with diverting thousands of votes and spawning litigation that reached the Supreme Court (Bush v. Gore). Spoilt/invalid ballots, residual-vote rates, ballot exhaustion in ranked systems, and administrative questions (ballot harvesting, drop boxes, chain of custody) are standing topics in election administration and law.
