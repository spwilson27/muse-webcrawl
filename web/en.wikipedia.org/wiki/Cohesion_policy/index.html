Cohesion policy

Cohesion policy
Policy area: European Union regional development
Administered by: European Commission (DG REGIO)
Main funds: ERDF, ESF+, Cohesion Fund

Cohesion policy is the European Union's main investment policy, aimed at reducing economic, social, and territorial disparities between regions and member states. It accounts for roughly a third of the EU budget and supports job creation, business competitiveness, economic growth, sustainable development, and improvements in citizens' quality of life.

Funds

Cohesion policy is delivered mainly through the European Regional Development Fund (ERDF), the European Social Fund Plus (ESF+), and the Cohesion Fund, which supports large transport and environment projects in less wealthy member states. Additional instruments include the Just Transition Fund, which helps regions dependent on fossil fuels move toward climate neutrality.

Eligibility

Funding is concentrated on less developed regions, defined principally as those whose gross domestic product per capita is below 75 per cent of the EU average. Transition regions and more developed regions receive smaller allocations. Programmes are negotiated between the Commission and national or regional authorities for seven-year programming periods aligned with the EU's multiannual financial framework.

Objectives

For the 2021–2027 period, the policy pursues a smarter, greener, more connected, more social, and closer-to-citizens Europe, with substantial shares of funding earmarked for innovation, digitalisation, and the low-carbon transition.

Criticism

The policy has been criticised for administrative complexity, uneven absorption of funds, and questions about measurable added value in some recipient regions, though evaluations also credit it with supporting convergence and infrastructure modernisation.
