East Harlem

East Harlem, also known as Spanish Harlem or El Barrio, is a neighborhood of Upper Manhattan in New York City, north of the Upper East Side and bounded by the Harlem River to the east, Fifth Avenue and Central Park to the west, and roughly 96th Street to the south. It is one of the largest predominantly Hispanic communities in New York City, mostly made up of Puerto Ricans as well as an increasing number of Dominican and Mexican immigrants.

History

The area was rural farmland through the nineteenth century and developed rapidly with the extension of elevated railways and the subway. It was first home to German, Irish, Scandinavian, and Jewish communities, then a major Italian enclave — Italian Harlem — in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. After the Second World War, Puerto Rican migration transformed the neighborhood, giving rise to the Nuyorican cultural movement, with institutions such as El Museo del Barrio (founded 1969) and a rich tradition of salsa music and mural art.

Culture and landmarks

East Harlem is known for its public murals, community gardens, and festivals including the annual Museum Mile Festival on Fifth Avenue. Landmarks include the Thomas Jefferson Park, the Harlem River Park, La Marqueta marketplace under the Park Avenue viaduct, and the Graffiti Hall of Fame. In recent decades the neighborhood has experienced gentrification pressure and significant new development, alongside persistent high poverty rates and extensive public housing, including the East River Houses and Johnson Houses.
