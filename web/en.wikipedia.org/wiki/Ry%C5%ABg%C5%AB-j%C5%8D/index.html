Ryūgū-jō

Ryūgū-jō (Japanese: 竜宮城, "Dragon Palace Castle") is the undersea palace of Ryūjin, the dragon king of the sea, in Japanese mythology. It is described as a magnificent castle built of red and white coral, inhabited by the king's sea-creature attendants, in which each of its four sides belongs to a different season.

In legend

Ryūgū-jō is best known from the tale of Urashima Tarō, a fisherman who rescues a sea turtle and is carried to the palace, where he is entertained by the princess Otohime. After what seems a stay of a few days, he returns home with a mysterious box (tamatebako) and discovers that centuries have passed on land; when he opens the box despite warnings, old age overtakes him at once. In some versions, visitors to the palace experience time at a vastly different rate than in the human world.

Cultural references

The name Ryūgū-jō has been given to a railway station in Chiba Prefecture (Katakai station's nickname and the Ryūgūjō-inspired design of stations on the Chōshi Electric Railway), and the palace's image — a turtle approaching a coral castle — is a common motif in Japanese art. The asteroid 162173 Ryugu, visited by the Hayabusa2 spacecraft, was named after the palace.
