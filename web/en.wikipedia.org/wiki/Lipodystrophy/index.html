Lipodystrophy

Lipodystrophy syndromes are a group of disorders characterized by the selective loss of adipose tissue (body fat) from various regions of the body, often accompanied by abnormal deposition of fat elsewhere and severe insulin resistance.

Classification

The syndromes may be congenital (such as congenital generalized lipodystrophy, or Berardinelli–Seip syndrome, caused by mutations in genes including AGPAT2 and BSCL2) or acquired, whether generalized or partial. A well-known acquired form was associated with older antiretroviral therapy for HIV. Localized lipodystrophy can also follow repeated insulin injections at the same site (lipohypertrophy or lipoatrophy).

Complications and treatment

Fat loss leads to low leptin levels, ectopic fat storage in liver and muscle, diabetes mellitus, hypertriglyceridemia, and fatty liver disease. Management includes diet, metformin and other anti-diabetic agents, and metreleptin (recombinant leptin) for generalized forms.
