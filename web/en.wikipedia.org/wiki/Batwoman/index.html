Batwoman

Batwoman is a name used by several fictional superheroes appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. The original Batwoman, Kathy Kane, was created by writer Edmond Hamilton and artist Sheldon Moldoff and first appeared in Detective Comics #233 (1956), introduced in part as a love interest for Batman. The modern incarnation, Kate Kane, debuted in 2006 in the weekly series 52 and became one of DC's most prominent LGBTQ characters.

Kathy Kane

The Silver Age Batwoman was a wealthy heiress and former circus performer who fought crime alongside Batman and the first Bat-Girl, her niece Betty Kane. The character was retired in 1964 during editor Julius Schwartz's overhaul of the Batman line, briefly returned in the 1970s, and was later killed by the League of Assassins; post-Crisis continuity revived and redefined her as "Kathy Webb-Kane".

Kate Kane

Reintroduced in 2006, Kate Kane is a wealthy Gotham heiress and Bruce Wayne's cousin who was expelled from West Point under "Don't Ask, Don't Tell" after refusing to conceal that she is a lesbian. Inspired by an encounter with Batman, she took up crimefighting with her father's military resources. Her solo series, written by J. H. Williams III and W. Haden Blackman, won GLAAD Media Awards and critical acclaim; her 2011 origin arc "Elegy" (by Greg Rucka and Williams) is widely regarded as a modern classic.

In other media

Kate Kane, played by Ruby Rose, headlined The CW's Batwoman television series (2019–2022), the first live-action superhero series led by an openly gay character; after Rose's departure the role passed to a new character, Ryan Wilder (Javicia Leslie). Batwoman has also appeared in animated films such as Batman: Bad Blood (2016).
