High-performance computing

High-performance computing (HPC) is the use of supercomputers and computer clusters to solve advanced computation problems at the highest operational rate. HPC systems aggregate enormous numbers of processors — CPUs and increasingly GPUs and other accelerators — connected by high-bandwidth, low-latency networks, and are used for scientific simulation, weather forecasting, molecular modelling, nuclear stockpile stewardship, cryptanalysis, financial modelling and artificial intelligence training.

Performance measurement

Performance is traditionally measured in FLOPS (floating-point operations per second) using the LINPACK benchmark, published twice yearly as the TOP500 list. The milestone scales are petaflops (1015), reached by IBM Roadrunner in 2008, and exaflops (1018), first officially reached by Frontier at Oak Ridge National Laboratory in 2022.

Architecture

HPC systems typically consist of thousands of compute nodes, each with multiple multicore processors and accelerators, interconnected by fabrics such as InfiniBand or proprietary networks (e.g. Slingshot, Tofu). Jobs are written with parallel programming models including MPI for distributed memory, OpenMP for shared memory, and CUDA or HIP for GPUs, and are scheduled by batch systems such as Slurm. Power consumption of tens of megawatts and liquid cooling are major design constraints at the exascale.
