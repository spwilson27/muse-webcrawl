Jigokudani Monkey Park

Jigokudani Monkey Park (地獄谷野猿公苑, Jigokudani Yaen Kōen) is a park in the valley of the Yokoyu River in Yamanouchi, Nagano Prefecture, Japan, famous for its wild Japanese macaques ("snow monkeys") that bathe in a man-made hot-spring pool. Established in 1964, it lies within Jōshin'etsu-kōgen National Park, at about 850 metres altitude in a steep, snow-covered valley whose name means "Hell's Valley."

Location: Yamanouchi, Nagano, Japan
Established: 1964
Known for: Bathing Japanese macaques (snow monkeys)

The monkeys

A troop of roughly 150–200 Japanese macaques lives in the surrounding mountains. After monkeys observed humans bathing at a local ryokan's outdoor onsen — and began entering the water themselves in the 1960s — a dedicated pool fed by the hot spring was built for them, and the monkeys are provisioned with barley and soybeans to anchor the troop. The bathing behaviour, learned and passed down, is unique among non-human primates and peaks in winter, when snow covers the monkeys' heads as they soak.

Visiting

The park is reached by a roughly 1.6–2 km forest trail from Kanbayashi Onsen near Shibu-Yudanaka hot springs; there is no road access. Visitors must not feed, touch or show food to the monkeys, and flash photography and staring into monkeys' eyes are discouraged. A live camera broadcasts the pool online. The site became world-famous through photographs, including a Life magazine cover of 1970, and drew crowds during the 1998 Nagano Winter Olympics.
