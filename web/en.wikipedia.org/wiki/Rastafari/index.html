Rastafari is a religious and social movement that developed in Jamaica in the 1930s, after the coronation of Haile Selassie I as emperor of Ethiopia — whom followers regard as God (Jah) incarnate, the returned messiah foretold in prophecy. Born among poor Afro-Jamaicans under colonial rule, it proclaims Africa (Zion) as the true home, denounces the Western world-system as Babylon, and sanctifies dreadlocks, ital (natural) food, and — in many mansions (denominations) — cannabis as a sacrament.

Spread and legacy

Reggae, above all Bob Marley, carried Rastafari worldwide in the 1970s; its language (I-and-I), colours and dreadlocked iconography became global culture. There is no central authority: mansions (Nyahbinghi, Bobo Ashanti, Twelve Tribes) differ in doctrine, and Haile Selassie's own Ethiopian Orthodox faith denied his divinity — a tension believers resolve variously. Ethiopia's Shashamane land grant hosts a small repatriated community.
