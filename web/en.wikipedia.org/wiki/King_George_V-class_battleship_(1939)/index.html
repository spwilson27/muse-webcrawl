The King George V class was a group of five battleships built for the Royal Navy in the lead-up to and during the Second World War. Designed within the limits of the Washington and London naval treaties, the ships carried ten 14-inch guns in a distinctive arrangement of two quadruple turrets and one twin turret, and were heavily protected, with speed sacrificed less than armour.

Ships and service

The five ships were King George V, Prince of Wales, Duke of York, Anson and Howe. Prince of Wales famously fought the German battleship Bismarck in May 1941 while still working up, and was sunk by Japanese aircraft off Malaya in December 1941 — a landmark demonstration of air power over capital ships. Duke of York sank the German battlecruiser Scharnhorst at the Battle of the North Cape in 1943. The surviving ships served in the Arctic, Mediterranean and Pacific, and were scrapped in the 1950s.

Design

The class's quadruple turrets proved mechanically troublesome, and the choice of the 14-inch gun — made in hopes of keeping treaty limits in force — was criticised, though the shells were effective and the ships' protection was excellent.
