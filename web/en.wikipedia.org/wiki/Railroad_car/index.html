Railroad car

A covered hopper car, a common type of freight car.

A railroad car, railway wagon, or railway carriage is a vehicle used for the carrying of cargo or passengers on a rail transport system. Cars are typically unpowered and are hauled by locomotives, though self-propelled passenger vehicles (railcars and multiple units) also exist.

Freight cars

Freight cars are specialised by cargo: boxcars for general merchandise, hopper cars for bulk solids such as grain and coal, tank cars for liquids and gases, flatcars for containers, vehicles, and machinery, gondolas for loose bulk materials, and refrigerated cars (reefers) for perishables. In North America cars run on two-axle swivelling trucks (bogies) and are joined by automatic knuckle couplers.

Passenger cars

Passenger cars include coaches, sleeping cars, dining cars, baggage cars, and observation cars. Modern passenger stock is often formed into semi-permanently coupled trainsets with through gangways, air conditioning, and power-operated doors.

Reporting marks and classification

Freight cars carry reporting marks identifying their owner and are classified by type codes issued by the Association of American Railroads in North America and by UIC classifications in Europe. See also locomotive and bogie.
