Eduroam

Type: Wireless roaming service

Launched: 2002 (Netherlands)

Operator: National research and education networks; coordinated by GÉANT in Europe

Website: eduroam.org

Eduroam (stylised eduroam, a portmanteau of "education roaming") is an international roaming service for users in research, higher education and further education. It provides researchers, teachers, and students easy and secure network access when visiting an institution other than their own. Authentication of users is performed by their home institution, using a federated RADIUS infrastructure, so that visitors can log on with the same credentials they use at home.

History

Eduroam began in the Netherlands in 2002, when SURFnet staff explored using IEEE 802.1X authentication combined with RADIUS to let users of one institution roam onto the wireless networks of others. The service spread through the European research and education community via the TERENA (later GÉANT) mobility task forces, and subsequently to North America, Asia-Pacific, Latin America and Africa. By the 2020s eduroam was available in more than one hundred countries and territories, with billions of authentications performed each year.

Technology

Eduroam networks broadcast the SSID "eduroam". A user's device authenticates using IEEE 802.1X with an EAP method such as EAP-TTLS, PEAP or EAP-TLS. The authentication request, which carries a realm based on the user's home institution domain, is proxied through a hierarchy of RADIUS servers — institutional, national and regional or global root servers — to the home institution, which verifies the credentials. Traffic is commonly tunnelled or placed on a local visitor VLAN according to local policy.

Governance

Global coordination is overseen by the Global Eduroam Governance Committee, with regional confederations and national roaming operators responsible for their federations. In Europe the service is operated under the GÉANT Association; in other regions operators include Internet2 (United States) and national research and education networks elsewhere.
