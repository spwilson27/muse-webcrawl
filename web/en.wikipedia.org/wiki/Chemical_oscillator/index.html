Chemical oscillator

A chemical oscillator is a complex mixture of reacting chemical compounds in which the concentration of one or more components exhibits periodic changes. Chemical oscillators are a hallmark of far-from-equilibrium thermodynamics and nonlinear chemical dynamics.

Examples

The best-known example is the Belousov–Zhabotinsky (BZ) reaction, in which an acidic bromate solution oxidises an organic substrate in the presence of a metal-ion catalyst, producing striking oscillating colour changes and travelling waves. Other classic oscillators include the Briggs–Rauscher reaction, the Bray–Liebhafsky reaction, and the Field–Noyes model (the Oregonator) developed to describe BZ dynamics.

Oscillating reactions require an open system or a large reservoir of reactants, since oscillations in a closed system eventually die out as equilibrium is approached. Chemical oscillators have been used as models for biological rhythms and pattern formation.
