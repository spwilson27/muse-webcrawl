Sabino Arana Goiri (1865–1903) was a Spanish writer and politician from Biscay, regarded as the founder of Basque nationalism. In 1895 he founded the Basque Nationalist Party (EAJ-PNV), designed the Basque flag (the ikurriña), and coined many of the terms and symbols — including the name Euzkadi for the Basque homeland — still used by Basque nationalists.

Life

Arana was born in Abando, now part of Bilbao, into a Carlist family that lost its property and was exiled after the Third Carlist War. Self-taught in the Basque language (Euskara), which he had not spoken as a child, he devoted himself to its study and codification, writing grammars and proposing orthographic and lexical reforms. He was repeatedly prosecuted and imprisoned for his political writings, including a sentence for a congratulatory telegram he sent to US President Theodore Roosevelt after the Spanish–American War.

Ideology and legacy

Arana's nationalism centred on the Basque "race", the Catholic faith and the restoration of the fueros (historic laws) under the motto "Jaungoikoa eta Lege-zarra" (God and the Old Laws). His early writings contained strong anti-Spanish and anti-immigrant rhetoric directed at industrial workers arriving in Biscay. He died of Addison's disease in 1903, aged 38, but the party he founded became the dominant force in Basque politics and continues to play a central role in the autonomous Basque Country.
