Spodumene

CategoryInosilicate (single chain), pyroxene group
FormulaLiAl(SiO3)2
Crystal systemMonoclinic
Hardness6.5–7 on the Mohs scale
ColourColourless to yellowish, lilac (kunzite), emerald-green (hiddenite), white, grey

Spodumene is a pyroxene mineral consisting of lithium aluminium inosilicate, LiAl(SiO3)2, and is an important source of lithium. It occurs in lithium-rich granite pegmatites and aplites, where it can form enormous crystals; single crystals up to about 14 metres (47 ft) long have been reported from the Black Hills of South Dakota. The name derives from the Greek spodumenos, "burnt to ashes", referring to the opaque ash-grey appearance of the material from which lithium was first prepared.

Varieties

Two gem varieties are well known: kunzite, the pink-to-lilac form coloured by traces of manganese, named after the gemmologist George Frederick Kunz, and hiddenite, the intense emerald-green form coloured by chromium, named after William Earl Hidden. Both are strongly pleochroic and are cut so that the deepest colour is visible through the table of the stone.

Uses

Spodumene is the world's principal hard-rock ore of lithium, mined extensively in Western Australia (for example at Greenbushes and Pilgangoora) and elsewhere for conversion into lithium carbonate and hydroxide used in batteries. It is also used in ceramics and glass, where it lowers thermal expansion, historically notably in ovenware.
