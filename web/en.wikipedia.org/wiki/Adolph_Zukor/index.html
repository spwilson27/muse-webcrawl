Adolph Zukor (January 7, 1873 – June 10, 1976) was a Hungarian-American film producer best known as one of the three founders of Paramount Pictures. A central figure in the creation of the Hollywood studio system, he built Paramount into one of the dominant American film companies and helped establish the feature-length film and the movie star as commercial institutions.

Early life

Zukor was born into a Jewish family in Ricse, in the Kingdom of Hungary, and was orphaned as a child. He emigrated to the United States in 1889, arriving in New York nearly penniless, and worked his way up in the fur trade before investing in penny arcades and then nickelodeon theatres with partners including Marcus Loew.

Paramount

In 1912 Zukor founded the Famous Players Film Company, importing the French film Queen Elizabeth starring Sarah Bernhardt to demonstrate that American audiences would watch feature-length films with stage stars — his slogan was "Famous Players in Famous Plays." In 1916 Famous Players merged with Jesse L. Lasky's company and the distributor Paramount Pictures, creating the Famous Players–Lasky Corporation, renamed Paramount Pictures. Under Zukor, Paramount controlled production, distribution, and a large theatre chain, and built stars such as Mary Pickford, Rudolph Valentino, and Clara Bow.

Later years

Zukor lost control of the studio during its bankruptcy reorganization in the 1930s but remained as a senior executive and later chairman emeritus. He received an honorary Academy Award in 1948 for his services to the industry. He died in Los Angeles in 1976 at the age of 103.
