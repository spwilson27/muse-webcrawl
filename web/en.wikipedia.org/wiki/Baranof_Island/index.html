Baranof Island

Baranof Island is an island in the northern Alexander Archipelago in the Alaska Panhandle, in the U.S. state of Alaska. It is the tenth-largest island in the United States and one of the ABC islands of Alaska. The island is about 160 kilometres (100 miles) long and 48 kilometres (30 miles) wide, with an area of roughly 4,160 square kilometres. It is named after Alexander Andreyevich Baranov, the first governor of Russian America.

Geography and communities

The island is mountainous and heavily forested, lying mostly within the Tongass National Forest. Its largest community is Sitka, the former capital of Russian America (as New Archangel), with a population of around 8,500; other communities include Baranof Warm Springs, Port Alexander, and Port Armstrong. Baranof Island has one of the highest densities of brown bears in North America, and its waters support important salmon fisheries.

History

The island was first charted by Europeans in the 1790s. The Russians established a settlement at what is now Sitka in 1799, destroyed by the Tlingit in 1802 and re-established in 1804 after the Battle of Sitka. Sitka remained the capital of Russian America until the Alaska Purchase of 1867, and later served as the first capital of the U.S. territory of Alaska.
