Ammonium nitrate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ammonium nitrate prills, a major nitrogen fertilizer worldwide.

Ammonium nitrate is a chemical compound with the formula NH4NO3, the nitrate salt of ammonia. It is a white crystalline solid, highly soluble in water, produced industrially by reacting ammonia with nitric acid. It is the dominant high-nitrogen fertilizer in world agriculture and a key ingredient of mining explosives.

Production and uses

Ammonium nitrate is made by neutralizing nitric acid (itself produced by the Ostwald process) with ammonia, then evaporating, prilling, or granulating the concentrated solution. As a fertilizer it supplies nitrogen in both ammonium and nitrate forms. Mixed with fuel oil it forms ANFO, the most widely used bulk industrial explosive; it is also used in instant cold packs, exploiting its endothermic dissolution.

Safety and disasters

Ammonium nitrate is a strong oxidizer. Under confinement, contamination, or fire it can decompose explosively, and it has been implicated in catastrophic accidents including the Texas City disaster of 1947, the Toulouse AZF explosion of 2001, the West, Texas explosion of 2013, and the Beirut explosion of 2020. These events led many jurisdictions to tighten storage and handling rules. Its misuse in improvised explosive devices has also made its sale regulated in numerous countries.

Properties

The compound melts at about 169 °C and decomposes on stronger heating, ultimately to nitrous oxide and water or, in detonation, to nitrogen, oxygen, and water vapor. It undergoes several solid–solid phase transitions near ambient temperatures, which complicate storage of prills.
