The Digital Services Act (DSA) is a regulation in EU law (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065) that updates the rules for intermediary services in the European Union, modernising the framework of the e-Commerce Directive 2000. Together with the Digital Markets Act, it forms the centrepiece of the European Commission's digital strategy under the motto "what is illegal offline is illegal online".

Content

The DSA imposes obligations scaled to the size and role of providers. All intermediaries must act on orders against illegal content, publish transparency reports, and provide complaint channels. Hosting services must operate notice-and-action mechanisms. Online platforms face additional duties: internal complaint-handling, out-of-court dispute settlement, trusted-flagger priority, protection of minors, and a ban on manipulative interface design ("dark patterns") and on targeted advertising based on profiling of minors or sensitive data. Very large online platforms and search engines (VLOPs/VLOSEs, with more than 45 million monthly EU users) must also assess and mitigate systemic risks — such as disinformation and harms to mental health — undergo independent audits, share data with regulators and vetted researchers, and offer a recommender option not based on profiling. Advertising repositories and recommender-system transparency are required throughout.

Enforcement

The European Commission enforces the rules directly against VLOPs, while national Digital Services Coordinators supervise smaller services. Fines can reach 6% of worldwide turnover, with periodic penalties of up to 5% of average daily turnover. The first designated set of 19 VLOPs and VLOSEs was announced in April 2023; obligations for them applied from August 2023, and for all other covered services from 17 February 2024.
