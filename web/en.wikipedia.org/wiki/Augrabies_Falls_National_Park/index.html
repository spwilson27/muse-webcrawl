Augrabies Falls National Park

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Augrabies Falls National Park is a national park located around the Augrabies Falls, about 120 km west of Upington in the Northern Cape Province, South Africa. It was established in 1966.

The Augrabies Falls, after which the park is named, are in the Orange River. The falls are around 60 metres (200 ft) high. The original Khoikhoi name for the falls was Aukoerebis, meaning "place of great noise", from which the Trek Boers, who settled here later, derived the name Augrabies. The falls are within a gorge some 18 kilometres long and up to 240 metres deep, carved into granite rock.

Geography and wildlife

The park covers an area of 820 km² and stretches along the Orange River. The landscape is dominated by arid granite gorges, klipspringer habitat and quiver trees (Aloidendron dichotomum). Animals found in the park include springbok, gemsbok (oryx), giraffe, Hartmann's mountain zebra, leopard and the klipspringer, after which several features are named. The riverine vegetation at the falls contrasts sharply with the surrounding semi-desert of the Kalahari fringe.

Climate

The region is extremely arid, receiving around 100 mm of rain a year, mostly in late summer. Summer temperatures can exceed 40 °C, while winter nights are cold. The flow of the Orange River peaks in late summer, when the falls are at their most spectacular; during record floods the volume has reached several thousand cubic metres per second.

Tourism

The park is administered by South African National Parks (SANParks). Viewing platforms overlook the main falls, and trails such as the Dassie Nature Trail and the longer Klipspringer Hiking Trail traverse the gorge. Night drives offer the chance to see aardwolf and other nocturnal species.
