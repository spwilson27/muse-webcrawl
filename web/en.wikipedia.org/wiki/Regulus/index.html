Regulus

Constellation: Leo
Bayer designation: Alpha Leonis
Apparent magnitude: 1.40
Distance: About 79 light-years

Regulus, designated Alpha Leonis, is the brightest star in the constellation of Leo and one of the brightest stars in the night sky. Its name is Latin for "little king". Regulus is a multiple star system about 79 light-years from the Sun, lying almost exactly on the ecliptic, so it is regularly occulted by the Moon and, rarely, by planets.

The system

The primary, Regulus A, is a blue-white main-sequence star of spectral type B7 that rotates extremely rapidly — once in about 16 hours — which distorts it into a pronounced oblate spheroid. It has a white dwarf companion in a close 40-day orbit. A more distant pair of fainter stars, Regulus B and C, completes the system.

Observation and history

Regulus forms the base of the "Sickle" asterism that outlines the Lion's head. It was one of the four "royal stars" of Persian astronomy, associated with the south and the summer solstice. Because of its position on the ecliptic, the star has been used historically in astrology as a marker of kingship and success.
