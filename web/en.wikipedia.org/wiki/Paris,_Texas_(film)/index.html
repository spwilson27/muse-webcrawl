Paris, Texas is a 1984 road movie directed by Wim Wenders, written by Sam Shepard (with adaptation by L. M. Kit Carson), and starring Harry Dean Stanton and Nastassja Kinski. The film follows Travis Henderson, a man who walks out of the Texas desert after having been missing for four years, and his attempts to reconnect with his brother, his young son, and his estranged wife. It won the Palme d'Or at the 1984 Cannes Film Festival and is widely regarded as one of the finest films of the 1980s.

Travis (Harry Dean Stanton) wanders the West Texas desert at the film's opening.

Plot

Travis Henderson emerges from the desert near the Mexican border, mute and disoriented, wearing a ragged suit and a red cap. His brother Walt, a billboard worker in Los Angeles, is summoned to retrieve him. Four years earlier Travis had vanished, abandoning his wife Jane and their son Hunter, who has been raised by Walt and his wife Anne. Travis gradually begins to speak and to bond with Hunter. On learning that Jane makes monthly deposits at a Houston bank, Travis takes Hunter on a road trip to find her. He discovers Jane working at a peep-show club and speaks to her through a one-way mirror in two celebrated monologue scenes, recounting their destructive marriage. Travis reunites Jane with Hunter at a hotel and then drives away alone into the night.

Production and style

Shot by Robby Müller, the film is noted for its luminous photography of the American Southwest and of neon-lit urban spaces, and for Ry Cooder's slide-guitar score. The title refers not to the French capital but to a vacant lot Travis owns in Paris, Texas — the place where, he believes, he was conceived. Wenders and Shepard began shooting with an incomplete script; the celebrated peep-show monologues were written during production.

Reception

The film won the Palme d'Or, the FIPRESCI Prize, and the Prize of the Ecumenical Jury at Cannes. Initial American distribution was delayed, but its critical reputation grew steadily; it is praised for Stanton's restrained performance, its treatment of masculinity, guilt, and redemption, and its portrait of American landscapes. It was added to many critics' lists of the greatest films ever made and was restored and re-released for its 40th anniversary in 2024.
