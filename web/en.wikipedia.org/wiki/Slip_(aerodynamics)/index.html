Slip (aerodynamics)

A slip is an aerodynamic state in which an aircraft moves somewhat sideways as well as forward relative to the oncoming airflow. It occurs whenever the aircraft's longitudinal axis is not aligned with its direction of travel — a condition measured by the sideslip angle and shown to the pilot by the slip/skid ball of the turn coordinator. Pilots use slips deliberately as manoeuvres and must also correct unintentional slips to keep flight coordinated.

Types

In a forward slip, the pilot banks the aircraft with aileron while applying opposite rudder, so the fuselage presents itself broadside to the airflow. This greatly increases drag without increasing airspeed, allowing a steep descent — particularly useful for landing without flaps or for losing altitude on approach. In a sideslip (or slip in a turn), the aircraft's nose stays aligned with its path while it slides sideways toward the low wing; this is the technique used to counteract crosswind drift during landing, keeping the aircraft aligned with the runway while its ground track remains on the centreline.

Physics and handling

Slipping exposes the side of the fuselage to the airflow, generating substantial parasite drag and a side force. Because the relative wind strikes one wing differently, dihedral tends to roll the aircraft out of the slip, and the pilot holds it in with aileron. A slip is the opposite condition to a skid, in which the tail slides outward in a turn; slips are generally benign, whereas skids at low speed can precipitate a spin.
