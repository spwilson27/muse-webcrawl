St Mary's Hospital, Paddington.

St Mary's Hospital is a teaching hospital in Paddington, London, founded in 1845. It is part of Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust and is the major trauma centre for north-west London. The hospital is historically associated with its medical school, which became part of Imperial College London in 1997, and with Alexander Fleming's discovery of penicillin there in 1928.

History

St Mary's opened its doors to patients in 1851 in a building designed with the involvement of the young surgeon Isaac Baker Brown and others, and its medical school was established the same year. The Clarence Memorial Wing, opened in the 1890s, housed the pathology department where Fleming, working under Almroth Wright, observed in September 1928 that a Penicillium mould contaminating a culture plate had killed surrounding staphylococci — the observation that led to penicillin. The Fleming Museum at the hospital preserves his laboratory.

Notable associations

The hospital is the traditional birthplace for members of the royal family treated in its private Lindo Wing, including Princes William and Harry and the children of the Prince and Princess of Wales. Its medical school alumni include Roger Bannister, the first man to run a sub-four-minute mile, who trained and later worked as a neurologist at St Mary's. During the Second World War, staff of the hospital contributed to the development of penicillin into a usable drug, and the hospital remains a major centre for infectious disease, trauma, and vascular surgery research.
