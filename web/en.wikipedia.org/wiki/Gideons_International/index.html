Gideons International

Gideons International is an evangelical Christian association founded in 1899 in the United States. Its members, traditionally Christian businessmen and professionals, are best known for distributing free copies of the Bible — and, since the late twentieth century, primarily the New Testament with Psalms and Proverbs — to hotels, motels, hospitals, schools, military personnel, prisons and other institutions.

History

The association was founded by John H. Nicholson, Samuel E. Hill and Will J. Knights, travelling salesmen who met in Boscobel and Janesville, Wisconsin, in 1898–1899. Taking their name from Gideon, the judge in the Hebrew Bible's Book of Judges, they organised Christian commercial travellers for mutual support and evangelism. The idea of placing Bibles in hotel rooms followed in 1908, beginning at the Superior Hotel in Iron Mountain, Montana. The familiar hotel-room Bible became a fixture of American travel culture during the twentieth century.

Work

Gideons distribute scriptures in more than 190 countries and territories, in over a hundred languages, and claim cumulative distribution in the billions of copies. The work is carried out by local "camps" (chapters) of members, supported by an auxiliary of members' spouses. Distribution is funded by donations from members, churches and the public. In many countries the organisation operates under the name Gideons International or a national equivalent; in the United Kingdom it rebranded as Good News for Everyone in 2018–2021.

Membership

Historically membership was restricted to men who were businessmen or professionals and members of evangelical churches; in 2019 the association voted to admit women as full members in some jurisdictions. Members pay for many of the testaments they distribute themselves, and the organisation emphasises that scriptures are given, never sold.

Controversies

Distribution in public schools has periodically drawn legal challenges in the United States on Establishment Clause grounds, particularly where distribution occurred on school grounds during school hours. Some hotels and hospital systems have declined or removed Gideon Bibles in favour of on-request copies of multiple faiths' texts.

In popular culture

The Beatles' song "Rocky Raccoon" (1968) references "Gideon's Bible" found in a hotel room — one of the best-known pop-cultural nods to the organisation.
