John of Leiden (born Jan Beukelszoon; 2 February 1509 – 22 January 1536) was a Dutch Anabaptist leader who led the Münster Rebellion of 1534–35 and proclaimed himself king of the "New Jerusalem".

Münster

A tailor's apprentice and innkeeper from Leiden, Beukelszoon was baptised into the Anabaptist movement influenced by Melchior Hoffman and travelled to Münster in 1533, where Jan Matthys had established an Anabaptist regime expecting the imminent end of the world. After Matthys was killed in a sortie in April 1534, John claimed a revelatory vision and assumed leadership. He instituted communal property, expelled or executed opponents, introduced polygamy — taking sixteen wives — and in September 1534 had himself crowned king.

Fall and execution

The city's prince-bishop, Franz von Waldeck, besieged Münster with Catholic and Protestant forces. After months of starvation the city fell in June 1535 through betrayal. John was captured, tortured to death with red-hot tongs on 22 January 1536, and his body, with those of two followers, was displayed in an iron cage hung from St Lambert's Church — cages that remain there to this day. The episode haunted the Anabaptist movement and is dramatised in Meyerbeer's opera Le prophète.
