Leon County is a county located in the north-central portion of the U.S. state of Florida, in the eastern Florida Panhandle and Big Bend region. As of the 2020 census, its population was 292,198. Its county seat and only incorporated municipality is Tallahassee, which is also the capital of Florida. Leon County is the core of the Tallahassee metropolitan statistical area.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Apalachee people, whose principal town, Anhaica, stood near present-day Tallahassee; Spanish missions were established in the seventeenth century as part of the province of Apalachee. Leon County was created in 1824, shortly after the United States acquired Florida from Spain, and was named for the Spanish explorer Juan Ponce de León. Tallahassee was selected as the territorial capital the same year, as a midpoint between the old capitals of Pensacola and St. Augustine, and grew with the cotton plantations of the county's fertile red hills. During the Civil War, Tallahassee was the only Confederate state capital east of the Mississippi not captured by Union forces, protected in part by the Union repulse at the Battle of Natural Bridge in 1865.

Geography

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the county covers about 702 square miles, of which some 667 square miles is land. Unlike most of Florida's flat coastal landscape, Leon County has rolling red-clay hills rising to around 200 feet. It is bordered by Georgia to the north, and by Gadsden, Liberty, Wakulla, and Jefferson counties. Lakes include Lake Jackson, Lake Talquin, Lake Lafayette, and Lake Iamonia. Part of the Apalachicola National Forest lies in the county's southwest.

Government, education, and economy

State government is the county's dominant employer, and two universities anchor its educational life: Florida State University and Florida A&M University, the latter a historically Black public university. The county is also a regional center for health care, law, and nonprofit associations. Leon County is governed by an elected seven-member Board of County Commissioners; a consolidated city–county government was approved by local voters decades ago but has never been implemented.

Transport

Interstate 10 crosses the county east–west, and U.S. highways 27, 90, 319, and 363 serve Tallahassee. Tallahassee International Airport provides commercial air service.
