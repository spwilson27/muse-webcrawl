The toothed whales (systematic name Odontoceti) are a parvorder of cetaceans that includes dolphins, porpoises, and all other whales that have teeth rather than baleen, such as the sperm whale, orcas, and belugas. About 77 species are recognised, ranging from the vaquita (under 2 m) to the sperm whale (up to 20 m).

Echolocation

Toothed whales navigate and hunt using echolocation: they produce clicks and whistles through the melon, a fatty organ in the forehead, and interpret the returning echoes. This allows them to hunt in darkness and turbid water. They are among the most vocal of animals, with complex communication systems — the sperm whale's clicking and the orca's calls are subjects of extensive research.

Families

Major families include the oceanic dolphins (Delphinidae), the largest family, which includes orcas and pilot whales; the sperm whales (Physeteridae), the largest toothed whale; the beaked whales (Ziphiidae), among the least-known deep-diving mammals; and the monodontids (Monodontidae), the narwhal and beluga.

Conservation

Many species were heavily whaled for oil and ambergris in the 18th–20th centuries. Today threats include bycatch, ocean noise, plastic pollution, and climate-driven shifts in prey. The sperm whale is listed as vulnerable.
