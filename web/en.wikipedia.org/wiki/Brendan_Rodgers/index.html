Brendan Rodgers

Born: January 26, 1973, Carnlough, Northern Ireland

Position: Defender (as player)

Teams managed: Watford, Reading, Swansea City, Liverpool, Celtic, Leicester City, Celtic

Brendan Rodgers (born 26 January 1973) is a Northern Irish professional football manager and former player. Known for his possession-based, attacking style of play, he has managed clubs including Swansea City, Liverpool, Celtic, and Leicester City.

Early career

Rodgers began his playing career as a defender at Ballymena United before joining Reading, but a genetic knee condition forced him to retire from playing at age 20. He moved into coaching, working in Reading's academy, and in 2004 was appointed head of Chelsea's youth academy by José Mourinho, later becoming reserve team manager.

Management career

Rodgers became manager of Watford in 2008 and then Reading in 2009, though his spell at Reading was short. At Swansea City, appointed in 2010, he won promotion to the Premier League via the Championship play-offs in 2011 — making Swansea the first Welsh club in the Premier League — and kept them up playing an admired passing game.

In 2012 he succeeded Kenny Dalglish at Liverpool. In the 2013–14 season, Liverpool, inspired by Luis Suárez, Steven Gerrard, and Daniel Sturridge, scored 101 league goals and finished second, narrowly missing the title. He was dismissed in October 2015.

At Celtic from 2016, Rodgers won back-to-back domestic trebles, including an unbeaten "Invincibles" season in 2016–17. In February 2019 he left for Leicester City, where he won the FA Cup in 2021 — the club's first — and the FA Community Shield. He left Leicester by mutual consent in April 2023 and returned to Celtic that June, winning further league titles and cups.

Managerial style

Rodgers is associated with short-passing, high-pressing football, developing young players, and detailed man-management. His approach was influenced by Dutch and Spanish coaching models and by his time working under Mourinho.
