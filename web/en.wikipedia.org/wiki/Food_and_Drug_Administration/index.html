Food and Drug Administration

The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) is a federal agency of the US Department of Health and Human Services responsible for protecting public health by ensuring the safety, efficacy and security of human and veterinary drugs, biological products, medical devices, food, cosmetics and tobacco.

FormedJune 30, 1906 (roots 1862)
HeadquartersSilver Spring, Maryland
ParentDept. of Health and Human Services

History

Its modern authority stems from the 1906 Pure Food and Drug Act, the 1938 Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act after the sulfanilamide disaster, and the 1962 efficacy amendments after thalidomide.

Role

The FDA reviews drug development applications, inspects facilities, issues recalls and labelling rules, and has played a central role in the response to the opioid epidemic.
