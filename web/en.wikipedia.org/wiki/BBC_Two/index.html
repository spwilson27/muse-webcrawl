BBC Two

BBC Two is a British free-to-air public broadcast television channel owned and operated by the BBC. It is the corporation's second flagship channel, covering a wide range of subject matter, with a remit "to broadcast programmes of depth and substance" in contrast to the more mainstream BBC One. The channel was the first in Britain to broadcast regularly in colour, from 1967, and was the birthplace of programmes such as Monty Python's Flying Circus (via BBC One repeats), Top Gear, The Office and numerous acclaimed dramas and documentaries.

History

BBC2 launched on 20 April 1964, although a power failure across west London blacked out most of the opening night, and the formal launch took place the following morning. The channel broadcast on the new 625-line UHF system, requiring viewers to buy new sets and aerials. Under controller David Attenborough from 1965, BBC2 commissioned landmark series including Civilisation and introduced colour broadcasting in July 1967, beginning with the Wimbledon tennis championships — ahead of BBC One and ITV.

Programming

The channel's output mixes arts, science, history and natural history documentaries, comedy, drama, current affairs (Newsnight), business (Dragon's Den), gardening and cookery, and sport. Programmes that began on BBC Two and became major hits include The Office, Top Gear (in its modern format), QI, The Great British Bake Off (later moved to Channel 4) and dramas such as Wolf Hall and Line of Duty (later moved to BBC One).

Idents and branding

BBC Two is known for creative channel idents built around the numeral "2", introduced in 1991 and revived in modified forms since. The 2018 "Curve" rebrand commissioned a wide range of animated idents from different artists and studios.
