On-call

On-call or on call is a work arrangement in which an employee must be available to respond to work demands outside their regular scheduled hours, typically by remaining reachable by phone or pager and able to come in or log on within a specified time. On-call duty is common in medicine, emergency services, utilities, and information technology.

In medicine

Physicians, especially residents and surgeons, work scheduled on-call shifts during which they handle admissions, emergencies and consultations outside normal hours. The length and frequency of call has been a longstanding concern in medical training; in the United States, accrediting bodies limit resident duty hours — partly in response to cases such as the death of Libby Zion — capping work weeks and the number of consecutive hours on call.

In information technology

In site reliability engineering and DevOps, engineers rotate through on-call schedules to respond to automated alerts about service outages. Incident-management platforms page the on-call engineer, who acknowledges the alert, investigates, and escalates if needed. Healthy on-call culture emphasises blameless post-incident reviews, limits on pages per shift, and compensation or time off in lieu, because frequent overnight interruptions are associated with burnout and attrition.

Compensation and law

Whether on-call time counts as paid working time varies by jurisdiction and by how restricted the employee's movements are while waiting. Labour law in many countries distinguishes between being on call at the workplace, on call at home, and merely contactable, with correspondingly different pay entitlements.
