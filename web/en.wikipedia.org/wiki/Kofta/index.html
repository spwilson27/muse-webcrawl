Kofta

Kofta is a family of meatball and meatloaf dishes found throughout the Balkans, the Middle East, North Africa, the Caucasus, and South and Central Asia. The word derives from Persian kūfta, "pounded (meat)".

Preparation and varieties

Kofta are usually made of minced or ground meat — beef, lamb, chicken or pork — mixed with spices, onions and sometimes rice, bulgur or breadcrumbs, then shaped into balls, patties or cylinders around skewers and grilled, fried, steamed or simmered in sauce. Variants include Turkish köfte, Greek keftedes, Levantine kafta, South Asian kofta curries (including vegetable and paneer versions), and Azerbaijani and Armenian forms.

Serving

Kofta are served with flatbreads, rice, salads and yoghurt or tomato sauces, as street food, meze, or main courses.
