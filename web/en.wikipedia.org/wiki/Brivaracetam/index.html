Brivaracetam, sold under the brand name Briviact, is an anticonvulsant medication used to treat partial-onset (focal) seizures in people with epilepsy. Developed by the Belgian company UCB, it was approved in the European Union and the United States in 2016. It is the 4-n-propyl analogue of levetiracetam and belongs to the racetam family of drugs.

Mechanism of action

Brivaracetam binds selectively and with high affinity to synaptic vesicle glycoprotein 2A (SV2A), a protein involved in neurotransmitter release — the same target as levetiracetam, but with roughly 15–30 times higher binding affinity and greater selectivity. Modulating SV2A is thought to reduce abnormal synchronous neuronal firing; brivaracetam also shows some inhibition of voltage-gated sodium channels at higher concentrations.

Medical use

It is indicated as adjunctive (and in the US also as monotherapy) treatment of focal seizures in adults and children from one month of age (US) or two years (EU, adjunctive), depending on jurisdiction. Formulations include film-coated tablets, an oral solution and an intravenous injection. Typical doses are 50–200 mg per day in two divided doses.

Adverse effects

The most common side effects are somnolence, dizziness, fatigue, nausea and irritability. Like other antiepileptic drugs it carries warnings about suicidal thoughts and behaviour, and psychiatric symptoms such as aggression or psychosis occur uncommonly. Brivaracetam has low potential for drug interactions, being mainly hydrolysed and partly metabolised by CYP2C19. In the United States it is a Schedule V controlled substance owing to limited reports of euphoria.
