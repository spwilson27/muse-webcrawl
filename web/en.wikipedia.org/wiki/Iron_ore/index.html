Iron ores are rocks and minerals from which metallic iron can be economically extracted. The ores are usually rich in iron oxides — principally hematite and magnetite — and vary in colour from dark grey and bright yellow to deep purple and rusty red. Iron ore is the raw material for pig iron, which is converted into steel; about 98 per cent of mined iron ore is used to make steel, making it one of the most traded bulk commodities in the world.

Ore minerals and grades

The main ore minerals are hematite (Fe₂O₃, 69.9% iron), magnetite (Fe₃O₄, 72.4% iron), goethite and limonite (hydrated oxides, around 55–63% iron from deposits such as channel iron deposits), and siderite (FeCO₃, 48.2% iron). "Direct shipping ores" (DSO) from Australia's Pilbara and Brazil typically contain 58–65% iron and can be fed to blast furnaces after crushing and screening, whereas low-grade ores such as taconite (25–35% iron) require beneficiation — grinding, magnetic separation and pelletising — to reach a marketable concentrate of 65% or more. Phosphorus, silica, alumina and sulphur content affect ore value.

Deposits

Most large deposits are hosted in banded iron formations (BIFs), sedimentary rocks laid down mostly between 2.7 and 2.4 billion years ago as photosynthetic oxygen reacted with dissolved iron in the oceans. Supergene enrichment near the surface has produced the high-grade hematite ores of the Hamersley Range (Western Australia), the Iron Quadrangle and Carajás (Brazil), the Mesabi Range (Minnesota, USA), Krivoy Rog (Ukraine) and the Singhbhum belt (India).

Mining and trade

Iron ore is overwhelmingly mined by open-pit methods using some of the largest trucks and excavators in existence, then moved by heavy-haul railways and capesize bulk carriers. World production is around 2.5 billion tonnes per year. Australia is the largest producer and exporter (chiefly from the Pilbara by Rio Tinto, BHP and Fortescue), followed by Brazil (Vale). Prices are quoted against benchmark indices such as the 62% Fe CFR China index published by price-reporting agencies.

Smelting

Ore is smelted with coke and limestone in a blast furnace to produce pig iron, or reduced with natural gas or hydrogen in direct-reduction processes before electric-arc melting. Growing interest in low-carbon steelmaking has increased demand for high-grade concentrates and pellets suitable for direct reduced iron and hydrogen-based reduction.
