Jónsbók

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: manuscript of Jónsbók]

Jónsbók is a medieval Icelandic law code enacted in 1281, after Iceland had come under the rule of the Norwegian Crown. It is named after the lawspeaker Jón Einarsson, who is traditionally credited with presenting the code to the Althing, though it was compiled under the direction of King Magnus VI of Norway, the "Law-mender."

Contents

Jónsbók replaced the earlier Járnsíða code and covered areas including land law, inheritance, trade, church law, and the obligations of tenants and householders. It was largely based on Norwegian law but adapted to Icelandic conditions.

Legacy

Remarkably, portions of Jónsbók remained in force in Iceland for more than 700 years; some provisions relating to land and livestock were only finally repealed in the 20th and even 21st centuries, making it one of the longest-lived law codes in European history. It is also an important source for the history of the Old Icelandic language.
