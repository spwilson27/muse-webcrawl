Michael Martin Hammer (April 13, 1948 – September 3, 2008) was an American engineer, management author and professor of computer science at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), best known as one of the founders of business process reengineering (BPR).

Career

Hammer earned his bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees in electrical engineering and computer science at MIT, where he taught and researched computer systems. In the 1980s he turned his engineering mindset to business management, arguing that companies were applying information technology merely to automate broken processes — "paving the cow paths" — rather than redesigning work from scratch.

Reengineering

His 1990 Harvard Business Review article "Reengineering Work: Don't Automate, Obliterate" and the 1993 bestseller Reengineering the Corporation, co-written with James Champy, launched a worldwide management movement. Hammer defined reengineering as the fundamental rethinking and radical redesign of business processes to achieve dramatic improvements in cost, quality, service and speed. BPR swept through large corporations in the 1990s and was often associated with restructuring and layoffs, which Hammer later acknowledged had led him to underemphasise the human side; his later work, including The Agenda (2001), stressed process management, measurement and execution. He died in 2008 of complications from a brain haemorrhage.
