Barış Manço

Barış Manço (born Tosun Yusuf Barış Manço; 2 January 1943 – 1 February 1999) was a Turkish rock musician, singer, songwriter, composer, actor, television producer and show host. A pioneer of Anatolian rock, he fused Turkish folk music with Western rock instrumentation and became one of the most beloved public figures in Turkey, remembered for his long hair, flamboyant rings and costumes, and his advocacy for children, culture and peace — his first name, Barış, means "peace" in Turkish.

Career

Manço began performing while a student at Galatasaray High School, later studying at the Royal Academy of Fine Arts in Liège, Belgium. His first major Turkish hit was "Dağlar Dağlar" (1970). With his band Kurtalan Ekspres, formed in 1972, he released landmark albums including 2023 (1975) and Sözüm Meclisten Dışarı (1981), and songs such as "Gülpembe", "Alla Beni Pulla Beni", "Dönence" and the English-language "Nick the Chopper". He wrote numerous children's songs, among them "Arkadaşım Eşek" ("My Friend the Donkey").

Television and legacy

From 1988, his TRT travel-and-children's programme 7'den 77'ye ("From 7 to 77") and its segment Adam Olacak Çocuk made him a national institution; he visited more than 150 countries for the show. Manço died of a heart attack on 1 February 1999, shortly before the release of his final album Mançoloji. His home in Moda, Istanbul, is now a museum, and he received a state funeral attended by tens of thousands.
