Campaign hat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A campaign hat, also called a campaign cover, drill instructor cover, or (in popular usage) a "Smokey Bear hat", is a broad-brimmed felt or straw hat with a tall crown pinched into a distinctive "Montana peak" crease. It is associated with the United States Army and Marine Corps of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and today with drill instructors, state police troopers, park rangers, and Scout leaders.

History

The hat evolved from slouch hats worn by American soldiers during the Civil War and Indian Wars. The Army standardised a campaign hat in the 1870s–1880s, and the 1911 pattern — with the Montana peak giving four dents — became iconic in the First World War. Its design was influenced by Mexican vaquero and earlier wide-brimmed working hats suited to sun and rain protection on campaign.

Modern wear

Today the campaign hat is worn by U.S. Marine Corps drill instructors and Army drill sergeants, many U.S. state highway patrols (giving rise to the "Smokey Bear" nickname, shared with the U.S. Forest Service mascot who wears the same style), the Royal Canadian Mounted Police ceremonial dress, and National Park Service rangers. In Scouting, the hat is associated with Robert Baden-Powell, who adopted it from his military service.
