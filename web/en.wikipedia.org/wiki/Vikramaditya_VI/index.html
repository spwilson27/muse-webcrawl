Vikramaditya VI (r. 1076–1126 CE) was the most famous king of the Western Chalukya Empire of the Deccan, in modern-day India. His fifty-year reign marked the high point of Chalukya power, and he introduced a new era, the Chalukya-Vikrama era, beginning with his accession in 1076.

A Western Chalukya temple of the kind patronised during Vikramaditya VI's reign.

Reign

Vikramaditya came to the throne after a struggle with his elder brother, Someshvara II, allying with the Cholas before turning against them. His long reign involved repeated wars with the Chola Empire over the province of Vengi, and campaigns against the Hoysalas, the Yadavas of Devagiri, and other Deccan powers. Toward the end of his reign the Hoysala king Vishnuvardhana began to assert independence, but the empire remained the dominant power in the Deccan.

Court and culture

Vikramaditya's court at Kalyani was a celebrated centre of learning. The Kashmiri poet Bilhana wrote the Vikramankadevacharita, a biography of the king, and the jurist Vijnaneshvara composed the Mitakshara, an influential commentary on Hindu law, under his patronage. The king granted land for temples and supported scholars in both Sanskrit and Kannada.

Legacy

Vikramaditya VI is remembered as the greatest of the Western Chalukya monarchs. The era he founded remained in use in parts of the Deccan for centuries, and his reign is a benchmark in the political and cultural history of medieval Karnataka.
