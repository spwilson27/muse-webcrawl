Digital art

Digital art
MediumComputers, tablets, software
FormsDigital painting, 3D graphics, pixel art, fractal art, generative art, net art
Emerged1960s–1980s

Digital art refers to any artistic work or practice that uses digital technology as part of the creative or presentation process. It can also refer to computational art that uses and engages with digital media. Since the 1960s, various names have been used to describe digital art, including computer art, electronic art, multimedia art, and new media art.

History

Early experiments included plotter drawings by Georg Nees and Frieder Nake in the 1960s and the work of pioneers such as Vera Molnár and Harold Cohen, whose AARON program painted autonomously. The development of affordable personal computers, graphics tablets, and software such as Adobe Photoshop (1990) and Corel Painter brought digital painting to a wide audience. The rise of the internet enabled net art, while 3D modelling transformed film, games, and animation.

Forms

Digital painting recreates traditional media with a stylus and tablet. Pixel art works at the level of individual pixels, as in early video games. Fractal and algorithmic art generates imagery from mathematical rules. Generative art and, more recently, AI art produced with text-to-image models have raised new questions about authorship. Digital art is also central to video games, virtual reality, and installation art.

Impact and reception

Museums increasingly collect digital works, though long-term preservation is complicated by obsolete formats and hardware. The 2021 sale of Beeple's NFT collage Everydays: The First 5000 Days for $69 million at Christie's drew worldwide attention to crypto art.
