The Handley Page Victor is a British jet-powered strategic bomber developed and produced by Handley Page. It was the third and final of the Royal Air Force's "V bombers", alongside the Vickers Valiant and Avro Vulcan, built to carry Britain's airborne nuclear deterrent during the Cold War.

Design

The Victor was distinguished by its crescent-shaped wing, designed to keep a favourable critical Mach number across the span, and its high T-tail. It entered service in 1958 as the B.1 bomber, followed by the more powerful B.2. After the RAF's deterrent role passed to the Royal Navy's Polaris submarines, most Victors were converted into aerial refuelling tankers (K.2 standard).

Operational history

As tankers, Victors supported operations including the Falklands War of 1982 — where they made possible the long-range "Black Buck" Vulcan raids — and the 1991 Gulf War. The type was retired in 1993, the last of the V bombers to leave service. It was never used to drop nuclear weapons in anger, though RAF Victors did drop conventional bombs during the Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation in the 1960s while held in reserve.
