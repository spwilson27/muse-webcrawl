Hilary Mantel

Dame Hilary Mary Mantel (née Thompson; 6 July 1952 – 22 September 2022) was a British writer known for historical fiction, personal memoirs and short stories. She twice won the Booker Prize — for Wolf Hall (2009) and its sequel Bring Up the Bodies (2012) — the first woman and first Briton to win twice. The trilogy concluded with The Mirror & the Light (2020).

Hilary Mantel
Born: 6 July 1952, Glossop, Derbyshire
Died: 22 September 2022, Exeter
Occupation: Novelist, essayist, critic
Notable works: Wolf Hall trilogy, Beyond Black, A Place of Greater Safety

Early life

Born in Glossop, raised Catholic in Hadfield and Romiley, she studied law at the London School of Economics and Sheffield, worked as a social worker, and lived in Botswana and Saudi Arabia with her husband Gerald McEwen, a geologist. Chronic illness from endometriosis shaped her life and writing.

Career

Early novels include Every Day is Mother's Day (1985), Eight Months on Ghazzah Street (1988) drawn from Jeddah life, Fludd (1989) and the French Revolution epic A Place of Greater Safety (1992). A Change of Climate (1994), An Experiment in Love (1995), The Giant, O'Brien (1998) and the memoir Giving Up the Ghost (2003) and comic-gothic Beyond Black (2005) widened her reputation.

Wolf Hall trilogy

The trilogy follows Thomas Cromwell, Henry VIII's fixer, from blacksmith's son to Earl of Essex, in close present-tense narration. Wolf Hall and Bring Up the Bodies were adapted for stage by the RSC and for BBC television (2015), starring Mark Rylance.

Later life and honours

She was appointed CBE (2006) and DBE (2014), was president of the Royal Society of Literature debate on historical fiction, and died suddenly in 2022 of a stroke, aged 70.
