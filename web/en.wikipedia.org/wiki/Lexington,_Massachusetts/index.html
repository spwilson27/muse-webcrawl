Lexington, Massachusetts

Lexington is a suburban town in Middlesex County, Massachusetts, United States, in the Greater Boston metropolitan area. Its population was 34,454 at the 2020 census.

Lexington is best known as the site of the first shots fired in the American Revolutionary War, in the Battle of Lexington on April 19, 1775, as the "shot heard round the world" when news spread. It is home to the Minuteman National Historical Park.

History

Settled in 1641 as part of Cambridge and incorporated as a separate parish in 1691 and town in 1713, Lexington was a farming community for much of its early history. On the morning of 19 April 1775, about 77 Lexington militiamen under Captain John Parker confronted British regulars on the town common; eight militiamen were killed. The battles of Lexington and Concord began the Revolutionary War.

Geography and demographics

Lexington covers about 16.5 square miles northwest of Boston along Route 2. It is an affluent suburb with highly rated public schools and a large historic district centred on the Battle Green.
