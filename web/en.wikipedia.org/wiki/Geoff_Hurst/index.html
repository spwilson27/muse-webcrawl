Geoff Hurst

Sir Geoffrey Charles Hurst

Born: 8 December 1941, Ashton-under-Lyne, England

Position: Striker

Clubs: West Ham United, Stoke City, West Bromwich Albion

England caps: 49 (24 goals)

Sir Geoffrey Charles Hurst (born 8 December 1941) is an English former professional footballer. A striker, he remains the only man to score a hat-trick in a World Cup final, when England recorded a 4–2 victory over West Germany at Wembley Stadium in 1966.

Club career

Hurst spent most of his career at West Ham United, scoring 242 goals in 500 first team appearances for the club. He won the FA Cup in 1964 and the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1965 with West Ham. He later played for Stoke City and West Bromwich Albion before a spell in the United States and non-league football.

International career

Hurst won 49 caps for England and scored 24 goals. He was a late addition to Alf Ramsey's squad for the 1966 World Cup and replaced the injured Jimmy Greaves in the quarter-final against Argentina, scoring the only goal. In the final against West Germany he scored three goals, including the famous disputed second goal that struck the crossbar and bounced down on or over the line, and a final goal in the last minute accompanied by Kenneth Wolstenholme's commentary line "some people are on the pitch... they think it's all over... it is now!"

Later life

After retiring, Hurst had a brief managerial career, including a spell as assistant to Ron Greenwood with England and as manager of Chelsea from 1979 to 1981. He was knighted in 1998. Following the deaths of his fellow 1966 team members over the years, Hurst became the last surviving member of England's World Cup-winning starting eleven.
