Gun (Chinese mythology)

Gun (Chinese: 鯀; pinyin: Gǔn) is a figure in Chinese mythology, best known as the father of Yu the Great and for his failed attempt to control the Great Flood. He was a descendant of the Yellow Emperor and a member of the imperial clan of the legendary sage-king Yao.

Tasked by Emperor Yao with controlling the devastating floods, Gun spent nine years building dikes and dams using xirang, a magical self-expanding soil he had stolen from the Supreme Deity. His approach of blocking and damming the waters failed, and the flood grew worse. Under Yao's successor Shun, Gun was executed (or exiled, in some versions) at Feather Mountain (Yushan) for his failure.

His son Yu succeeded where he had failed, dredging channels to lead the waters to the sea over thirteen years of labour, and went on to found the Xia dynasty. In some traditions Gun was deified after death, variously as a yellow bear, a dragon, or a god of city walls.
