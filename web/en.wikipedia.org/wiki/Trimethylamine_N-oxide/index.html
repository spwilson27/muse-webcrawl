Trimethylamine N-oxide

Trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) is an organic compound with the formula (CH3)3NO. It is the amine oxide of trimethylamine and is commonly encountered as its dihydrate. TMAO is a colorless solid, soluble in water, and is best known as an osmolyte that accumulates in the tissues of many marine animals.

Biological role

In sharks, rays, and other marine organisms, TMAO stabilizes proteins against the denaturing effects of urea and, in deep-sea fish, against high hydrostatic pressure; its concentration in tissues increases with the depth at which the species lives. In humans, TMAO is produced in the liver by oxidation of trimethylamine, which gut bacteria generate from dietary choline, carnitine, and related nutrients.

Health research

Elevated blood levels of TMAO have been associated in a number of clinical studies with increased risk of cardiovascular disease and chronic kidney disease, making it a subject of active research into the gut microbiome's influence on metabolism. In chemistry, TMAO is also used as an oxidizing agent, for example in the decarbonylation of metal carbonyl complexes and as a co-oxidant in dihydroxylation reactions.
