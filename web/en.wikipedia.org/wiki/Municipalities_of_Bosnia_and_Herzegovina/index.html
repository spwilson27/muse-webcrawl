Municipalities of Bosnia and Herzegovina

The municipalities of Bosnia and Herzegovina (općine/opštine) are the lowest level of administrative subdivision in the country. Following the Dayton Agreement of 1995, the country is divided between two entities — the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and Republika Srpska — together with the self-governing Brčko District, and municipalities operate within that structure.

Organization

The Federation is organized into ten cantons, which are subdivided into municipalities; in Republika Srpska, municipalities report more directly to the entity government. In total the country has well over one hundred municipalities, plus the cities of Sarajevo, Banja Luka, Mostar, and others with special status. The City of Sarajevo, for example, comprises four municipalities.

Government

Each municipality is governed by a mayor (načelnik) and a municipal council, both directly elected. Municipalities are responsible for local services such as primary utilities, local roads, land use, and communal infrastructure, within competencies defined by entity and, in the Federation, cantonal law.

Largest municipalities

By population, the largest municipal areas include Banja Luka, Sarajevo's constituent municipalities such as Novi Grad, Tuzla, Zenica, Bijeljina, Prijedor, and Mostar.
