Royal Society for the Protection of Birds

The Royal Society for the Protection of Birds (RSPB) is a charitable organisation registered in England and Wales and in Scotland. It was founded in 1889. It works to promote conservation of birds and the wider environment through public awareness campaigns, petitions and through the operation of nature reserves throughout the United Kingdom. The RSPB has over a million members, making it the largest nature conservation charity in Europe, and it manages more than 200 nature reserves covering well over 100,000 hectares.

History

The society began in Manchester in 1889 as the Society for the Protection of Birds, founded by Emily Williamson to campaign against the trade in feathers and plumage for women's hats, which was devastating populations of species such as the great crested grebe. It gained its royal charter in 1904. Early campaigns helped secure the Importation of Plumage (Prohibition) Act 1921. The society bought its first nature reserve at Cheyne Court in 1930 and expanded its reserve network after the Second World War, opening flagship sites such as Minsmere in Suffolk and Loch Garten in Scotland, famous for its ospreys.

Activities

The RSPB carries out scientific research on bird populations, campaigns on issues including climate change, agriculture and marine protection, and runs public engagement events such as the annual Big Garden Birdwatch, one of the world's largest citizen-science surveys. Its magazine, Nature's Home, is sent to members. The society is the UK partner of BirdLife International and works internationally, including on the recovery of endangered species such as the spoon-billed sandpiper and on combating illegal bird trapping in the Mediterranean.

Reserves

Notable reserves include Minsmere, Snettisham, Leighton Moss, Bempton Cliffs, South Stack, Loch Garten and Belfast Lough. Reserves protect habitats ranging from reedbeds and heathland to seabird cliffs and estuarine mudflats.
