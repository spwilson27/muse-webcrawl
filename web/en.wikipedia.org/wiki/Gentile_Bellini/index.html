Gentile Bellini (c. 1429 – 23 February 1507) was an Italian painter of the Venetian school. He worked in the service of the Republic of Venice and is celebrated for his large narrative canvases of Venetian life and for his sojourn at the court of the Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II in Constantinople.

Life and career in Venice

Gentile was the elder son of Jacopo Bellini and brother of Giovanni Bellini, and was named after his father's teacher, Gentile da Fabriano. He became official portraitist to the doges and painted, with Giovanni, the great narrative cycle of the Scuola Grande di San Giovanni Evangelista. His surviving Venetian masterpieces include the Procession in Piazza San Marco (1496) and the Miracle of the Cross at the Bridge of San Lorenzo (1500), now in the Gallerie dell'Accademia; their meticulous depiction of contemporary architecture and dress makes them invaluable documents of Renaissance Venice.

Constantinople

In 1479 the Venetian Senate sent Gentile to Constantinople as part of the peace settlement with the Ottoman Empire, because Mehmed II had requested an able portraitist. He remained until 1480/81, producing court portraits including the celebrated Portrait of Sultan Mehmed II (now in the National Gallery, London) and drawings of Ottoman courtiers, soldiers and costumes that influenced European images of the East for decades.

Assessment

Long overshadowed by his younger brother Giovanni, Gentile is now valued as a pioneer of the Venetian narrative veduta style and as a unique artistic bridge between Renaissance Italy and the Ottoman world.
