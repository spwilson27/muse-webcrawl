Set-top box

A set-top box (STB), also known as a cable box, receiver, or set-top unit, is an information appliance device that generally contains a TV tuner input and displays output to a television set, turning the source signal into content that can be displayed. The name derives from the practice of placing the unit on top of the television cabinet.

Types

Set-top boxes are used for cable television, satellite television and terrestrial digital broadcasts: cable boxes demodulate QAM and often decrypt encrypted channels using a CableCARD or embedded conditional access; satellite receivers use DVB-S/S2 tuners; and terrestrial boxes decode DVB-T, ATSC or ISDB signals. With the growth of broadband video, the category expanded to streaming media players (such as Roku, Apple TV, Amazon Fire TV and Android TV devices) and hybrid boxes combining broadcast tuners with internet-delivered services. Many include personal video recorder (PVR/DVR) functionality with internal or external storage for recording programmes.

Functions

Typical features include an electronic programme guide (EPG), parental controls, subtitles and audio description support, pay-per-view ordering, interactive television services and software updates delivered over the broadcast or broadband connection. Conditional access modules control entitlement to subscription channels.
