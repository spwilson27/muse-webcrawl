The mile (symbol: mi) is a unit of length in the British imperial and United States customary systems, equal to 5,280 feet, or 1,760 yards, and standardised as exactly 1,609.344 metres. It remains the standard unit for road distances and speed limits in the United States and the United Kingdom, and is familiar worldwide through athletics events and expressions such as "miles per hour".

History

The mile derives from the Roman mille passus — "a thousand paces" — roughly 1,480 metres. English usage standardised various local miles before the statute mile of 1,760 yards was fixed by an Act of Parliament in 1593, defined in terms of furlongs (eight per mile) of 220 yards. In 1959 the international yard and pound agreement set the yard at exactly 0.9144 metres, fixing the mile at 1,609.344 metres.

Related units

The nautical mile, used in navigation and aviation, is a different unit — one minute of latitude, exactly 1,852 metres — and gives rise to the knot. The Scandinavian mil equals 10 kilometres. Historical variants include the Scots mile and Irish mile, longer than the statute mile, and the Roman mile from which the word derives.
