David Politzer

Hugh David Politzer (born 31 August 1949) is an American theoretical physicist and the Richard Chace Tolman Professor of Theoretical Physics at the California Institute of Technology. He shared the 2004 Nobel Prize in Physics with David Gross and Frank Wilczek for the discovery of asymptotic freedom in quantum chromodynamics, announced in his 1973 paper "Reliable perturbative results for strong interactions?"

Biography

Politzer was born in New York City, graduated from the Bronx High School of Science in 1966, received his bachelor's degree from the University of Michigan in 1969, and his PhD from Harvard in 1974, where he worked under Sidney Coleman. After a postdoctoral period at Harvard, he joined the faculty of Caltech in 1979 and has remained there throughout his career, serving as chair of the Division of Physics, Mathematics and Astronomy in the 1990s.

Research

Politzer's 1973 calculation showed that the beta function of a non-Abelian gauge theory like QCD is negative, meaning the coupling weakens at high energy — asymptotic freedom. Published back-to-back with Gross and Wilczek's paper, the result supplied the theoretical foundation of the modern theory of the strong interaction. His subsequent work included influential predictions on charmonium (bound states of charm and anticharm quarks) with Thomas Appelquist in the wake of the J/ψ discovery, and research on heavy-quark physics and on hydrodynamic behaviour in condensed matter systems. Politzer is also known to film audiences for a cameo as Manhattan Project physicist Robert Serber in the 1989 film Fat Man and Little Boy.
