Equinor ASA is a Norwegian state-owned multinational energy company headquartered in Stavanger. Founded in 1972 as Statoil (Den norske stats oljeselskap), it developed Norway's petroleum resources on the Norwegian continental shelf and grew into one of the world's largest offshore oil and gas operators. The Norwegian government holds a majority stake of about 67 percent. In 2018 the company changed its name to Equinor — combining "equi" (equality/equilibrium) and "nor" (Norway) — to reflect a broader energy strategy.

History

Statoil was created as Norway's state oil company following the discovery of oil in the North Sea. It became operator of major fields including Statfjord and Gullfaks and was partially privatized and listed in 2001. In 2007 Statoil merged with the oil and gas division of Norsk Hydro, creating one of the world's largest offshore operators. The company later expanded internationally, including into US shale, Brazilian offshore fields, and offshore wind.

Operations

Equinor is the dominant operator on the Norwegian continental shelf, responsible for a large share of Norway's oil and gas production, including the giant Johan Sverdrup field. It has substantial refining, marketing, and midstream operations, and is a major supplier of natural gas to Europe. Its renewables portfolio includes the Hywind floating offshore wind farms off Scotland and Norway and large fixed-bottom projects such as Dogger Bank (with partners) off England and Empire Wind off New York.

Criticism

The company has faced criticism over the pace of its energy transition, its continued exploration for new oil and gas, and past incidents including cost overruns and the 2020 Hammerfest LNG fire. It maintains an ambition of net-zero emissions by 2050.
