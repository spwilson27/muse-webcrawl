Publish What You Pay

Publish What You Pay (PWYP) is a global coalition of civil-society organisations campaigning for transparency in the extractive industries — oil, gas and mining — so that citizens of resource-rich countries can hold their governments to account for revenues earned from natural resources. Founded in 2002, it grew to a network of more than a thousand member organisations in over fifty countries.

Origins

The coalition was launched by organisations including Global Witness, the Open Society Foundations' network, CAFOD, Oxfam, Save the Children and Transparency International, drawing on Global Witness's 1999 report A Crude Awakening on oil corruption in Angola. Its original demand was simple: require extractive companies listed on stock exchanges to disclose payments to governments as a condition of listing.

Achievements

PWYP was a driving force behind the Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative (EITI, launched 2003), which pairs company disclosure with government receipt reporting. Its "publish what you pay" logic entered law in the Dodd–Frank Act §1504 (the Cardin–Lugar provision, 2010) in the United States, EU Accounting and Transparency Directives (2013), and Canadian legislation (2014), requiring project-level payment disclosure. The coalition later widened its agenda to contract transparency, beneficial ownership of companies, and "publish what you earn and how you spend it" along the whole extractive value chain.

Context

The campaign is a leading response to the resource curse: the paradox that many countries rich in oil and minerals suffer corruption, conflict and poor development outcomes. Implementation has been uneven — the US rule was repealed and reissued in changing forms — but mandatory disclosure is now a settled feature of extractives governance.
