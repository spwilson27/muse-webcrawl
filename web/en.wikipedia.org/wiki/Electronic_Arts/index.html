Electronic Arts Inc. (EA) is an American video game company headquartered in Redwood City, California. Founded in May 1982 by former Apple employee Trip Hawkins, the company was a pioneer of the early home computer games industry and promoted the designers and programmers responsible for its games as "software artists". EA is one of the largest game companies in the world by revenue and market capitalization.

History

Hawkins founded the company, originally named Amazin' Software, with the idea of treating game creators as artists and selling their works in album-style packaging. Renamed Electronic Arts, it published landmark early titles such as Pinball Construction Set (1983), M.U.L.E. (1983) and Archon. In the late 1980s and 1990s EA expanded into console games, built the EA Sports label (launched with the Madden NFL series), and grew through acquisitions, including Maxis (The Sims), Westwood Studios, BioWare and Respawn Entertainment.

Games and studios

EA's major franchises include the sports series EA Sports FC (formerly FIFA) and Madden NFL, the life-simulation series The Sims, the shooters Battlefield and Apex Legends, and role-playing series Mass Effect and Dragon Age. Its internal studios include DICE, BioWare, Maxis, Respawn Entertainment and EA Tiburon. EA also operates subscription services and the EA app (formerly Origin) digital distribution platform.

Criticism

The company has repeatedly been voted among the most disliked companies in America in consumer polls, owing in part to aggressive monetisation practices such as loot boxes in its sports titles' Ultimate Team modes, studio closures, and the handling of the Star Wars Battlefront II (2017) microtransaction controversy, which prompted regulatory scrutiny of loot boxes in several countries.
