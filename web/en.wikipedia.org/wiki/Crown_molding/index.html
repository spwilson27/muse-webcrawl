Crown molding

Crown molding (also spelt crown moulding) is a form of cornice created out of decorative molding installed atop an interior wall. It is also used at the top of cabinets, pilasters, and other joinery. The molding is typically cut with a compound profile that springs outward from the wall, bridging the transition between wall and ceiling.

Materials and profiles

Crown molding is most commonly made of wood, but is also produced in plaster, medium-density fibreboard (MDF), PVC, polyurethane foam, and even pressed metal. Profiles descend from classical orders: the cyma recta, cyma reversa, cavetto and ovolo curves combine in traditional profiles. The angle at which the molding sits between wall and ceiling is known as its spring angle, commonly 38°, 45°, or 52°.

Installation

Because the molding meets at inside and outside corners, joints are cut as mitres or coped joints; coping, in which one piece is cut to fit the profile of the other, is preferred for inside corners because it tolerates seasonal movement. The molding is nailed to wall studs and ceiling joists or to a backing block, and gaps are caulked before painting. See also Cornice and Molding (decorative).
