Cosworth

[Image: Cosworth]

Cosworth is a British automotive and motorsport engineering company founded in London in 1958 by Mike Costin and Keith Duckworth. Based in Northampton, it specialises in high-performance internal combustion engines, powertrains and electronics for racing and road cars.

History

Costin and Duckworth — the company name combines their surnames — began by tuning Ford engines for racing. With Ford backing, Cosworth developed the DFV (Double Four Valve) V8 Formula One engine, introduced in 1967 in the Lotus 49. The DFV won 155 Grands Prix and remained competitive into the 1980s, making it the most successful engine in Formula One history.

Road cars

Cosworth produced high-performance versions of road cars including the Ford Sierra RS Cosworth and Escort RS Cosworth, the Mercedes-Benz 190 E 2.3-16, and engines for the Opel/Vauxhall range. In later years it developed hybrid hypercar powertrains, including the naturally aspirated V12 for the Aston Martin Valkyrie and the V16 for the Bugatti Tourbillon, and the engine of the Gordon Murray Automotive T.50.

Motorsport

Beyond Formula One, Cosworth engines powered winners in IndyCar, Champ Car, the World Rally Championship and Le Mans. The company supplied engines to Formula One teams as recently as 2013, and continues to supply electronics and powertrain technology across motorsport.
