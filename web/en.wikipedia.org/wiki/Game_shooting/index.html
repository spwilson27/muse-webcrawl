Game shooting


Game shooting is the shooting of game birds and animals for sport and food.

Game shooting is the sport of shooting game — birds such as pheasant, partridge, grouse, and duck, and ground game such as deer and rabbit — typically for food and sport, under licence and within defined open seasons. It is a traditional field sport particularly associated with the United Kingdom, where it is a significant rural industry.

Forms

In driven shooting, beaters flush birds over a line of stationary "Guns," who shoot them in flight; the classic British driven pheasant and partridge shoot, and the driven grouse shoots of the northern moors beginning on the "Glorious Twelfth" of August, follow this pattern. In walked-up or rough shooting, hunters walk with dogs (including pointers, setters, spaniels, and retrievers) and shoot game they flush. Wildfowling targets ducks and geese on coasts and marshes, and deer stalking pursues deer on foot.

Management and controversy

Gamekeepers rear and release millions of pheasants and partridges annually and manage habitat — including heather moorland burning for grouse — which supporters credit with conservation benefits for other wildlife, while critics raise concerns about raptor persecution, lead shot, and the environmental effects of large-scale releases. In the UK, the activity is regulated under the Game Acts and closed seasons protect breeding birds. Shot game enters the food chain through game dealers and retailers.
