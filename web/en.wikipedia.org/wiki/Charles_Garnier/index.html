Jean-Louis Charles Garnier (6 November 1825 – 3 August 1898) was a French architect, best known as the designer of the Palais Garnier and the Monte Carlo Casino. His exuberant, eclectic style — mixing historicist elements, rich polychromy, and lavish ornament — became emblematic of the French Second Empire.

Life and career

Born in Paris to a working-class family, Garnier studied at the École des Beaux-Arts and won the Prix de Rome in 1848, spending several years studying in Italy and Greece. On his return, the largely unknown 35-year-old won the 1861 competition for a new Paris opera house commissioned as part of Haussmann's renovation of Paris under Napoleon III.

Construction of the Palais Garnier lasted from 1861 to 1875, interrupted by the Franco-Prussian War and the fall of the Empire. Asked by the Empress Eugénie what style the building was in, Garnier famously replied, "It is Napoleon III, Madame!" The building, with its great staircase, gilded foyer, and underground lake, opened in 1875.

Other works

Garnier designed the Salle Garnier housing the Monte Carlo Casino (1878–79), the Nice Observatory with Gustave Eiffel's dome, the Villa Garnier at Bordighera, and numerous buildings on the French and Italian Riviera. He died in Paris in 1898.

Legacy

His name became so identified with his opera house that it is commonly called simply "the Garnier". The building later inspired the setting of Gaston Leroux's novel The Phantom of the Opera.
