MP 38

The MP 38 and MP 40 (Maschinenpistole 38/40) were submachine guns developed in Nazi Germany and used extensively by the Axis powers during World War II. The MP 38 was produced from 1938 by the Erma works (Erfurter Maschinenfabrik, ERMA), adapting the design of Heinrich Vollmer.

Design

The MP 38 is a blowback-operated, open-bolt, fully automatic weapon chambered for the 9×19mm Parabellum cartridge, fed from a 32-round detachable box magazine inserted beneath the receiver. Making extensive use of machined steel, it features a folding metal stock, a bakelite lower frame, and a cyclic rate of about 500 rounds per minute, with an effective range of around 100–200 metres. There is no selector for semiautomatic fire on standard guns; trained shooters could fire single shots by briefly pressing the trigger.

Development into the MP 40

Combat experience led to simplifications for mass production: the MP 40 replaced many machined components with stamped steel parts, greatly increasing production. Approximately 1.1 million MP 40s were built during the war. Allied soldiers often incorrectly called the gun the "Schmeisser", after designer Hugo Schmeisser, who in fact designed the magazine but not the weapon itself.

Users

The MP 38/40 equipped German infantry squad leaders, paratroopers, tank crews, and police formations, and influenced later designs such as the American M3 "Grease Gun". It remained in use in various countries after 1945, including in Norway into the 1990s in some roles.
