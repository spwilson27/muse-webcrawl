Tomorrowland is a large-scale annual electronic dance music festival held at De Schorre provincial recreational park in Boom, Flanders, Belgium. First held in 2005, it has grown into one of the world's largest and most notable music festivals, taking place over two weekends in July and welcoming around 400,000 attendees from more than 200 countries.

History

The festival was created by brothers Manu and Michiel Beers. The first edition in 2005 drew about 9,000 people to a single day. Expansion was rapid: by 2011 it had grown to three days and some 180,000 attendees. The 2013 edition's tickets sold out in seconds. In 2014, for its tenth anniversary, Tomorrowland expanded to two weekends — a format continued since. International editions have included TomorrowWorld in Georgia, USA (2013–2015), Tomorrowland Brasil (2015–2016, revived in 2023), and the winter edition Tomorrowland Winter in Alpe d'Huez, France, from 2019. During the COVID-19 pandemic the festival staged virtual editions under the name "Tomorrowland Around the World".

Stages and theme

Tomorrowland is famed for its elaborate fairytale-themed stage designs — giant book-shaped main stages, volcanoes, magic kingdoms — each year built around a story theme such as "The Book of Life" or "Adscendo". Sixteen or more stages host hundreds of DJs, including headliners such as Martin Garrix, Armin van Buuren, Dimitri Vegas & Like Mike, and Charlotte de Witte. The festival village, DreamVille, camps tens of thousands. Its anthem-like motto is "Live Today, Love Tomorrow, Unite Forever".
