Blockade runners of the American Civil War

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The blockade runners of the American Civil War were fast, purpose-built steamships that evaded the Union blockade of Confederate ports between 1861 and 1865. They carried cotton out to neutral entrepôts such as Bermuda, Nassau and Havana, and returned with rifles, ammunition, medicines and luxury goods essential to the Confederacy.

Ships and strategy

Successful runners were long, low, grey-painted steamers, often built on the Clyde in Scotland, burning smokeless Welsh anthracite coal to avoid detection. They timed their runs for moonless nights and could make 17 knots or more. Roughly five of every six attempts succeeded early in the war, though Union capture rates improved sharply by 1864.

Impact

Historians credit the runners with delivering some 600,000 small arms to the Confederacy, yet the trade could not offset the South's industrial disadvantage, and cotton exports fell to a fraction of pre-war levels. Famous vessels included the CSS Advance, the Banshee and the Hope. See also Union blockade and the Anaconda Plan.
