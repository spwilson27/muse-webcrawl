Jataka tales

The Jataka tales are a voluminous body of Buddhist literature concerning the previous births of Gautama Buddha, in both human and animal form. The collection forms part of the Khuddaka Nikaya of the Pali Canon and comprises 547 stories in the traditional Theravada version.

Content

In each tale the future Buddha, as Bodhisatta, embodies a virtue such as generosity, patience or wisdom, often as an animal — a hare, a monkey king, a golden deer — or as a prince, merchant or ascetic. Frame stories set the tale in the Buddha's present life, and verses preserved in the canon are embedded in later prose commentary.

History and influence

Jataka scenes are among the earliest Buddhist art, carved at Bharhut and Sanchi in the centuries BCE. The tales spread throughout Asia, inspired local literature and art, and influenced story collections far beyond Buddhism; some parallels appear in the Panchatantra and in European fable.
