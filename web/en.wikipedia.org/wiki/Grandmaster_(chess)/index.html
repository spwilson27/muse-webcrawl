Grandmaster (chess)

Grandmasters competing in a top-level event

Grandmaster (GM) is a title awarded to chess players by the world chess organisation FIDE. Apart from World Champion, Grandmaster is the highest title a chess player can attain. Once achieved, the title is held for life, though it may be revoked for cheating or fraud.

Qualification

The usual way to obtain the title is to achieve three favourable results (known as norms) in tournaments meeting specified standards, covering at least 27 games, together with a FIDE rating of at least 2500 at some point. Titles are also awarded automatically for certain achievements, such as winning the Women's World Championship or the World Junior Championship. Below Grandmaster rank the titles of International Master (IM) and FIDE Master (FM). Separate women-only titles, headed by Woman Grandmaster (WGM), have lower requirements but women may and do qualify for the open titles.

History

The term was used informally for leading players from the 19th century; legend holds that Tsar Nicholas II called the five finalists of the St Petersburg 1914 tournament "grandmasters". FIDE formally created the title in 1950, initially awarding it to 27 players. The number of grandmasters has grown greatly since, reaching well over 1,700, as the required playing strength has been reached by more players and the title has become easier to pursue in an era of frequent international events and computer preparation. The youngest players to qualify have done so at around twelve years of age; Abhimanyu Mishra became the youngest ever in 2021, at 12 years, 4 months and 25 days.
