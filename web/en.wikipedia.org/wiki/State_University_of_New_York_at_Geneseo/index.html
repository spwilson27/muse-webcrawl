The State University of New York College at Geneseo (SUNY Geneseo) is a public liberal arts college in Geneseo, New York. It is a member of the State University of New York (SUNY) system and is frequently described as a "public Ivy" for combining liberal arts education with public university tuition. The college enrolls roughly 4,000–4,500 undergraduates and a smaller number of graduate students.

History

Geneseo was founded in 1871 as the Wadsworth Normal and Training School, one of the normal schools created by New York State to train teachers. It became part of the SUNY system at the system's founding in 1948 and evolved over the following decades into a selective liberal arts college. The campus overlooks the Genesee River valley.

Academics

Geneseo offers bachelor's degrees in more than 50 majors, with strong programmes in the sciences, education, business, and the humanities. It has one of the oldest chapters of Phi Beta Kappa among SUNY campuses (the Alpha-Gamma of New York chapter was established in 2004). The college is known for high graduation rates and for sending a large share of graduates to graduate and professional schools.

Campus and student life

The college's mascot is the Knights. Geneseo competes in NCAA Division III athletics in the SUNYAC conference. Campus landmarks include the gazebo on the college green, Sturges Hall's clock tower, and the medieval-style castle-like water tower visible across the village. Traditions include the Great Battle of the Books, the annual Geneseo Dance Ensemble performances, and watching sunsets over the Genesee Valley.
