Neurotechnology

Neurotechnology is any technology that interacts directly with the nervous system — recording its activity, stimulating it, or both — in order to understand, monitor, repair or enhance brain and nerve function. It spans medical devices, research tools and, increasingly, consumer products.

Brain–computer interfaces are a flagship neurotechnology.

Categories

Established neurotechnologies include cochlear implants, deep brain stimulation for movement disorders such as Parkinson's disease, spinal cord stimulators for chronic pain, and diagnostic recording techniques such as EEG and MEG. Invasive and non-invasive brain–computer interfaces aim to let people control computers or prosthetic limbs by thought, and companies and research groups are developing high-bandwidth implanted interfaces for paralysis and communication.

Ethics and governance

Because neurotechnology can access or influence mental processes, it raises distinctive questions of mental privacy, agency and identity, grouped under "neurorights". Chile became the first country to legislate on neuroprotection in 2021, and UNESCO and the OECD have issued recommendations on responsible innovation in the field.

Outlook

Advances in miniaturised electronics, materials and machine learning continue to expand the field, with applications anticipated in treating depression, epilepsy, blindness and memory disorders, alongside debates about enhancement and equitable access.
