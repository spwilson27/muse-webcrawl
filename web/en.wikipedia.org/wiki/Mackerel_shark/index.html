Mackerel shark

Mackerel sharks are sharks of the order Lamniformes, a group that includes some of the best-known large predatory sharks. The order contains the great white shark, the mako sharks, the porbeagle and salmon shark, as well as the filter-feeding basking shark and megamouth shark, and the unusual goblin shark.

Characteristics

Lamniform sharks typically have two dorsal fins, an anal fin, five gill slits and large eyes without nictitating membranes. Many are fast, active swimmers with streamlined bodies and crescent-shaped tails. Several species are regionally endothermic, maintaining body temperatures above that of the surrounding water, which supports their high-speed predatory lifestyle.

Families

The order comprises families including Lamnidae (the mackerel sharks proper: white, mako, porbeagle and salmon sharks), Cetorhinidae (basking shark), Megachasmidae (megamouth), Mitsukurinidae (goblin shark), Alopiidae (thresher sharks) and Odontaspididae (sand tiger sharks).

Evolution

Lamniformes have a fossil record extending back to the Cretaceous. The extinct megalodon (Otodus megalodon), the largest predatory shark known, is usually placed within or near this order.
