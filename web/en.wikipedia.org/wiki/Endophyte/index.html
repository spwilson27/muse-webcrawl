Endophyte

An endophyte is an endosymbiont, often a bacterium or fungus, that lives within a plant for at least part of its life cycle without causing apparent disease. Endophytes are ubiquitous and have been found in all species of plants studied to date; however, most of the endophyte–plant relationships are not well understood.

Fungal endophyte hyphae growing intercellularly in a grass leaf.

Ecology

Some endophytes enhance host growth and nutrient acquisition and improve the plant's ability to tolerate abiotic stresses such as drought and salinity, as well as decreasing susceptibility to biotic stresses by producing defensive alkaloids. Grass endophytes of the genus Epichloë, for example, produce compounds toxic to grazing livestock and insect herbivores. Transmission may be vertical (through seed) or horizontal (through spores).

Applications

Endophytes are studied for their potential in agriculture, phytoremediation and the production of novel bioactive compounds, including antimicrobials and anticancer agents such as taxol, originally isolated from the endophytic fungus of the Pacific yew.
