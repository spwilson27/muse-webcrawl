Pac-Man

Pac-Man, originally called Puck Man in Japan, is a 1980 maze action video game developed and released by Namco for arcades. The player controls Pac-Man, who must eat all the dots inside an enclosed maze while avoiding four colored ghosts—Blinky, Pinky, Inky, and Clyde. Eating large flashing dots, called "Power Pellets", causes the ghosts to temporarily turn blue, allowing Pac-Man to eat them for bonus points.

Development

The game was designed by Toru Iwatani, who wanted to create a non-violent game appealing to a broader audience, including women, at a time when most arcade games were space shooters. The character's shape was inspired by a pizza with a slice missing. The name came from the Japanese phrase paku paku taberu, describing the sound of eating. The ghost AI was designed so that each ghost has a distinct targeting behavior: Blinky chases the player directly, Pinky tries to ambush from ahead, Inky's movement depends on both Blinky and Pac-Man, and Clyde alternates between chasing and retreating.

Reception and legacy

Released first in Japan in May 1980 and in North America by Midway Manufacturing in October 1980, Pac-Man became an unprecedented commercial success and a popular culture phenomenon, spawning a franchise of sequels, merchandise, a hit song, and an animated television series. It is considered one of the most influential video games of all time and an icon of 1980s popular culture.

The game's maximum possible score, 3,333,360 points, was first achieved by Billy Mitchell in 1999. The arcade original has a famous bug: level 256, the "split-screen" or "kill screen," corrupts half the maze due to an 8-bit overflow of the level counter, making the game unbeatable beyond that point.
