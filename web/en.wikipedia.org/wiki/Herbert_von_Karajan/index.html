Herbert von Karajan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Herbert von Karajan (born Heribert Ritter von Karajan; 5 April 1908 – 16 July 1989) was an Austrian conductor. He was principal conductor of the Berlin Philharmonic for 34 years (1955–1989) and is generally regarded as one of the greatest conductors of the 20th century. His extensive discography, of more than 300 recordings, helped define the classical record market, and he was a central figure at the Salzburg Festival.

Early career

Born in Salzburg into a family of Greek and Slovene origin, Karajan was a child prodigy pianist who studied at the Mozarteum. He made his conducting debut in 1929 and held posts in Ulm and Aachen, joining the Nazi Party in 1933 — a membership that remained controversial. His triumph conducting Tristan und Isolde in Berlin in 1938 launched his ascent.

Berlin Philharmonic

After the war, cleared by a denazification tribunal, Karajan recorded extensively with the Philharmonia Orchestra in London before succeeding Wilhelm Furtwängler in Berlin in 1955. He built the orchestra into a byword for polished, powerful sound, toured worldwide, and embraced new recording technologies, including compact disc.

Later years and legacy

Karajan resigned from the Berlin Philharmonic in April 1989 and died that July in Anif, near Salzburg. His perfectionism, business acumen, and recordings of Beethoven, Brahms, Strauss, and Wagner left an indelible mark on classical music.
