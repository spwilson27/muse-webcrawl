Musée Condé

The Musée Condé is an art museum in the Château de Chantilly in Chantilly, Oise, France, about 50 kilometers north of Paris. It houses one of the finest collections of paintings in France after the Louvre, and is remarkable because its hang has been essentially frozen since the 19th century: the collector who bequeathed it to the nation stipulated that the presentation of the works never be altered and that nothing be loaned out.

The collector

The collection was assembled by Henri d'Orléans, Duke of Aumale, son of King Louis-Philippe, who rebuilt the château and filled it with paintings, drawings, illuminated manuscripts, and decorative arts. In 1884 he donated the estate to the Institut de France, and the museum opened to the public in 1898, two years after his death.

Collection

Highlights include the Très Riches Heures du Duc de Berry, the celebrated illuminated manuscript; works by Raphael (including the Three Graces), Poussin, Watteau, Ingres, and Delacroix; and the largest collection of portraits by Jean Clouet and his son François. The library holds around 19,000 volumes, including a Gutenberg Bible fragment and hundreds of medieval manuscripts. The museum also preserves the duke's apartments and galleries of portraits of the Princes of Condé, from whom the museum takes its name.
