La Folia

La Folia (Spanish La Folía; also Follia in Italian and Folies d'Espagne in French) is one of the oldest remembered European musical themes, a chord progression and melody that has been used in countless compositions since the late 15th century. Its name — "folly" or "madness" — originally described a fast, frenzied Portuguese folk dance.

The theme

The "later Folia", the standard form codified in the 17th century, is a progression in D minor (i–V–i–VII–III–VII–i–V–i) in slow triple time, associated with a stately, melancholic character quite different from the wild dance of the "early Folia". The earliest notated examples appear in Iberian sources around 1490–1520, and the tune spread across Europe in the 17th century.

Composers who used it

More than 150 composers have written variations on La Folia. Landmark sets include Lully's Folies d'Espagne, Marin Marais's variations for viola da gamba, Corelli's Violin Sonata Op. 5 No. 12 (1700) — often simply called "La Follia" — Vivaldi's trio sonata on the theme, Handel's sarabande, Liszt's Rhapsodie espagnole, and Rachmaninoff's Variations on a Theme of Corelli (1931). The progression also survives in modern film scores and folk traditions of the Canary Islands and Latin America.
