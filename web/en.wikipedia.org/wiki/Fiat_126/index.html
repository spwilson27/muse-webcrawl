Fiat 126

The Fiat 126 is a small rear-engined city car produced by the Italian manufacturer Fiat from 1972 to 1980 in Italy, and under licence in Poland — as the Polski Fiat 126p — until 2000. It replaced the Fiat 500, retaining much of its mechanical layout while offering a more modern body and improved safety.

Overview

Launched at the Turin Motor Show in 1972, the 126 used an air-cooled, rear-mounted two-cylinder engine, initially of 594 cc and later enlarged to 652 cc, driving the rear wheels through a four-speed gearbox. The bodywork provided more interior space and a larger luggage compartment than the 500, together with an all-synchromesh transmission on later models and improved crash protection.

Polish production

The Polish-built Polski Fiat 126p, produced by FSM in Bielsko-Biała and Tychy, became one of the most popular cars in Poland and a symbol of mass motorisation there; it was nicknamed the "Maluch" ("little one"). Over 3.3 million were built in Poland, and millions more in Italy and other countries. A water-cooled "126 BIS" version was produced from 1987 to 1991.
