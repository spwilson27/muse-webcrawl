Talking Cricket

The Talking Cricket (Italian: il Grillo Parlante) is a fictional character in Carlo Collodi's 1883 children's novel The Adventures of Pinocchio. A wise, long-lived cricket who has lived in Geppetto's house for over a hundred years, he acts as Pinocchio's would-be conscience, offering the puppet moral advice that Pinocchio repeatedly ignores — with disastrous results.

In Collodi's novel

The cricket first appears early in the book, warning Pinocchio that disobedient boys who rebel against their parents will come to grief. Infuriated, Pinocchio throws a mallet at him and kills him. The cricket nonetheless returns later in the story — in one celebrated episode appearing as a ghost, and later as one of the animal doctors summoned to Pinocchio's bedside alongside a crow and an owl, and finally alive again, offering his house to Pinocchio and Geppetto at the novel's end. The Cricket's counsel — on honesty, work, study and gratitude — represents the voice of adult wisdom against Pinocchio's impulsiveness.

Adaptations

In Disney's 1940 animated film Pinocchio, the character was transformed into Jiminy Cricket, a cheerful, formally dressed companion formally appointed by the Blue Fairy as Pinocchio's official conscience — a much softer figure than Collodi's sardonic insect, and the singer of the Oscar-winning song "When You Wish Upon a Star". The name "Jiminy Cricket" derives from a minced oath in use long before the film. The character has since become one of Disney's most recognisable figures, appearing in Fun and Fancy Free, Mickey's Christmas Carol and other productions. Other adaptations vary: in Guillermo del Toro's 2022 stop-motion film, the cricket (Sebastian J. Cricket) is an aspiring memoirist living in the chest cavity of the wooden puppet.
