Silvio Berlusconi

Died12 June 2023, Milan, Italy
OfficePrime Minister of Italy (1994–1995, 2001–2006, 2008–2011)

Silvio Berlusconi (29 September 1936 – 12 June 2023) was an Italian media tycoon and politician who served as Prime Minister of Italy in three governments, holding the office for a total of nine years — longer than any other post-war Italian prime minister. He was the controlling shareholder of Mediaset (formerly Fininvest), Italy's largest commercial broadcaster, and the founder of the centre-right party Forza Italia.

Business career

Born in Milan to a middle-class family, Berlusconi studied law at the University of Milan and worked first in property development, building large residential districts such as Milano Due. In the late 1970s he moved into television, assembling local stations into national commercial networks — Canale 5, Italia 1 and Rete 4 — that broke the state broadcaster RAI's monopoly. His holding company Fininvest grew to encompass publishing (Mondadori), advertising (Publitalia), banking, insurance, and the AC Milan football club, which he owned from 1986 to 2017 and under whom the club won numerous domestic and European titles.

Political career

Amid the collapse of Italy's traditional parties in the Tangentopoli corruption scandal, Berlusconi announced his entry into politics in January 1994 — the famous "discesa in campo" (taking the field) — and won the general election two months later at the head of a centre-right coalition. His first government fell within the year, but he returned to power in 2001, serving a full five-year term, and again from 2008 to 2011, when he resigned during the European sovereign debt crisis amid pressure over Italy's borrowing costs.

Controversies and legal cases

Berlusconi's career was dogged by conflicts of interest between his media empire and public office, and by numerous criminal proceedings. In 2013 he was definitively convicted of tax fraud in the Mediaset rights case, expelled from the Senate, and barred from public office for a period. He was also tried in the so-called "Rubygate" case concerning allegations of paying for sex with a minor; after an initial conviction he was acquitted on appeal, the acquittal being upheld in 2015. Supporters praised his entrepreneurial flair and longevity; critics accused him of reshaping Italian politics and media around his personal interests.

Later life and death

After serving his ban, Berlusconi returned to politics, winning a seat in the European Parliament in 2019 and a Senate seat in 2022, when Forza Italia joined the governing coalition led by Giorgia Meloni. He died in Milan on 12 June 2023, aged 86, after treatment for leukaemia. Italy declared a national day of mourning and he was given a state funeral at Milan Cathedral.
