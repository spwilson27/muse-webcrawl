South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc., 585 U.S. ___ (2018), was a United States Supreme Court decision holding that states may require out-of-state sellers to collect and remit sales tax even if the seller has no physical presence in the state.

Background

Under National Bellas Hess v. Illinois (1967) and Quill Corp. v. North Dakota (1992), the Dormant Commerce Clause barred states from imposing use-tax collection duties on retailers without a physical presence in the state. As e-commerce grew, states lost substantial revenue and pressed for the rule to be reconsidered. South Dakota enacted a law requiring collection by remote sellers with over $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions in the state, deliberately setting up a test case against online retailers Wayfair, Overstock.com, and Newegg.

Decision

In a 5–4 decision written by Justice Anthony Kennedy, the Court overruled Quill and Bellas Hess, calling the physical-presence rule "unsound and incorrect" in the era of internet commerce, and upheld South Dakota's economic-nexus threshold. Chief Justice Roberts dissented, arguing that Congress, not the Court, should change the rule.

Impact

Following the decision, nearly all states with sales taxes adopted economic-nexus and marketplace-facilitator laws, reshaping online retail taxation across the United States.
