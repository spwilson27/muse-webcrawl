ChapStick

ChapStick is a brand of lip balm manufactured by the consumer healthcare company Haleon (formerly GSK Consumer Healthcare in the United States) and sold worldwide. The name has become a genericised trademark for lip balm in many markets. ChapStick products are applied to the lips to moisturise and relieve chapping.

History

Lip balm was invented in the early 1880s by Dr. Charles Browne Fleet, a pharmacist in Lynchburg, Virginia, who created a waxy balm wrapped in foil resembling a candle without a wick. Fleet sold the rights in 1912 for five dollars to John Morton, who with his wife refined the formula and packaging — pouring the pink mixture into brass tubes — and founded the Morton Manufacturing Corporation. The chapped-lips logo, created in 1936 by commercial artist Frank J. Jr. with stylised lips, remains associated with the brand. The product line passed through the A.H. Robins Company, Wyeth, Pfizer and GlaxoSmithKline before becoming part of Haleon.

Products and ingredients

Classic ChapStick is made principally of petrolatum, lanolin, camphor, menthol, phenol and waxes. Variants include flavoured, medicated and sun-protection (SPF) versions. ChapStick has been famously endorsed by Olympic skier Suzy Chaffee, who appeared in its advertisements as "Suzy Chapstick."
