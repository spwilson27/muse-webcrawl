Health for All

Health for All is the goal of attaining a level of health that permits all people to lead socially and economically productive lives, adopted by the World Health Organization's member states in 1977 and elaborated in the Alma-Ata Declaration of 1978. The strategy set the target "Health for All by the Year 2000" and made primary health care the key vehicle for achieving it.

Alma-Ata Declaration

The International Conference on Primary Health Care at Alma-Ata (now Almaty, Kazakhstan) declared health a fundamental human right and defined primary health care around essential services: health education, nutrition, safe water and sanitation, maternal and child health, immunisation, control of endemic diseases, treatment of common illnesses, and provision of essential drugs.

Legacy

Although the year-2000 target was not met, Health for All shaped global health policy for decades and is regarded as a forerunner of the Millennium Development Goals, the Sustainable Development Goals, and the modern universal health coverage movement.
