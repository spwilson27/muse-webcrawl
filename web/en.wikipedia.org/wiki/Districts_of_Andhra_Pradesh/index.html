Districts of Andhra Pradesh

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Districts of Andhra Pradesh after the 2022 reorganisation.

The Indian state of Andhra Pradesh is divided into 26 districts, following a comprehensive reorganisation that took effect on 4 April 2022. Before the reorganisation, the state had 13 districts, a number inherited when Telangana was separated in 2014 to form a separate state. The restructured districts were drawn largely along the boundaries of parliamentary constituencies, with the stated aims of bringing administration closer to citizens and improving governance.

Each district is headed by a District Collector (District Magistrate), an officer of the Indian Administrative Service, and is subdivided into revenue divisions and mandals. The districts group into the three broad regions of the state: Uttarandhra (north coastal), Coastal Andhra, and Rayalaseema in the south-west.

History

Andhra Pradesh was formed in 1956 by merging the Telugu-speaking districts of the former Madras and Hyderabad states. Various districts were created, divided, and renamed over the following decades. Upon the bifurcation of 2014 the residuary state retained 13 districts. Proposals for comprehensive reorganisation were enacted under the Andhra Pradesh Districts (Formation) Act, and on 4 April 2022 the number rose to 26, including newly created districts such as Anakapalli, NTR (headquartered at Vijayawada), Sri Satya Sai, and Tirupati.
