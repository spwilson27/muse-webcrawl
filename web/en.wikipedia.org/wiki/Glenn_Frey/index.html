Glenn Frey

Glenn Lewis Frey (November 6, 1948 – January 18, 2016) was an American musician and actor, best known as a founding member of the rock band the Eagles. With Don Henley, Frey was the band's principal songwriter and one of its lead vocalists, singing lead on hits such as "Take It Easy," "Peaceful Easy Feeling," "Tequila Sunrise," "Lyin' Eyes," "New Kid in Town," and "Heartache Tonight."

Early life and the Eagles

Born in Detroit and raised in Royal Oak, Michigan, Frey played in local bands in the 1960s and sang backing vocals on Bob Seger's early recordings. Moving to Los Angeles, he met Henley when both were hired for Linda Ronstadt's backing band in 1971; that year they formed the Eagles with Bernie Leadon and Randy Meisner. The band became one of the best-selling acts of all time, and its Their Greatest Hits (1971–1975) is the best-selling album in United States history. Frey and Henley's perfectionism and business disputes contributed to the band's bitter breakup in 1980, but the Eagles reunited in 1994 for the Hell Freezes Over tour and continued performing until Frey's death.

Solo career and acting

As a solo artist Frey scored hits in the 1980s with "The Heat Is On" (from Beverly Hills Cop), "You Belong to the City" (written for Miami Vice, in which he also guest-starred), "Smuggler's Blues," "True Love," and "Soul Searchin'." He appeared as an actor in the television series Miami Vice and Nash Bridges and in the film Jerry Maguire. Frey won six Grammy Awards as a member of the Eagles and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the band in 1998.

Death and legacy

Frey died in New York City in January 2016 from complications of rheumatoid arthritis, acute ulcerative colitis, and pneumonia. The Eagles continued after his death with his son Deacon Frey and Vince Gill joining the lineup, and in 2016 the surviving members performed a tribute to him at the Grammy Awards.
