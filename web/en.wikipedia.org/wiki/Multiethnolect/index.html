Multiethnolect

Multiethnolect
FieldSociolinguistics
Term introducedJacqueline Lindenfeld (1990s usage); developed by Pia Quist, Bente Ailin Svendsen and others
SettingMultiethnic urban neighbourhoods, mainly in Western Europe

A multiethnolect is a language variety that emerges in multiethnic, working-class urban neighbourhoods, typically among adolescents, from contact between the majority language and the many immigrant languages spoken in the community. Unlike an ethnolect, which is tied to a single ethnic group, a multiethnolect is shared across ethnic lines: its speakers may have very different family languages, but they converge on a common youth variety of the city's language, marked by distinctive pronunciation, grammar and vocabulary drawn from several sources.

Examples and features

Well-studied examples include Multicultural London English, which blends features of Jamaican Creole, South Asian and West African Englishes and Cockney; Kiezdeutsch in Berlin, influenced by Turkish and Arabic; Rinkebysvenska in Stockholm; straattaal ("street language") in Dutch cities; and multiethnic youth varieties in Copenhagen and Oslo. Common traits across these varieties include new discourse markers and loanwords, simplified or restructured grammatical gender and agreement, distinctive intonation, and — importantly for their speakers — heavy use as a marker of local, multiethnic identity rather than of any single heritage.

Scholarly and public debate

Linguists emphasise that multiethnolects are rule-governed, creative varieties, and that their speakers are usually bidialectal, able to shift toward the standard in formal settings. Public commentary, by contrast, has sometimes stigmatised them as "broken" language or a threat to the standard, a framing researchers criticise as a moral panic about immigration and youth. Over time, features of multiethnolects diffuse into mainstream urban speech, making them a significant engine of language change in contemporary European cities.
