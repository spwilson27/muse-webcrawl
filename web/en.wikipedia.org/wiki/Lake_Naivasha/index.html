Lake Naivasha (from Maasai Nai'posha, "rough water") is a freshwater lake in the Kenyan Great Rift Valley, near the town of Naivasha. Unlike most Kenyan Rift lakes it is fresh rather than alkaline, because underground drainage through fractured volcanic rock flushes out accumulated salts.

Ecology

The lake's fringing papyrus swamps and fever-tree forests support hippos, waterbuck, giraffe, and over 400 bird species, including African fish eagles and ospreys. Crescent Island and the nearby Hell's Gate and Mount Longonot national parks make the area a major tourism destination. The lake level fluctuates dramatically with rainfall; it nearly dried up in the early 20th century and rose sharply again in the 2010s–2020s.

Economy

Naivasha is the centre of Kenya's floriculture industry: large greenhouses around the shore grow roses and carnations for export to Europe, employing tens of thousands of workers. The irrigation and water abstraction this requires, along with geothermal power development at Olkaria nearby, have made the lake a focus of debate over sustainable water use. It was designated a Ramsar site in 1995.
