Software maintenance

Software maintenance is the modification of a software product after delivery to correct faults, to improve performance or other attributes, or to adapt the product to a modified environment. It is one of the phases of the systems development life cycle and typically consumes the largest share of a system's total lifetime cost — studies commonly attribute well over half of total software expenditure to maintenance.

Categories

The standard taxonomy, associated with E. Burton Swanson and codified in IEEE and ISO/IEC standards (such as ISO/IEC 14764), divides maintenance into four categories:

  • Corrective maintenance — fixing defects discovered after delivery.
  • Adaptive maintenance — adapting the software to changes in its environment, such as new hardware, operating systems, or regulations.
  • Perfective maintenance — improving performance, maintainability, or adding new features requested by users.
  • Preventive maintenance — changes made to prevent future problems, such as restructuring or documenting code.

Challenges

Maintenance is complicated by poor documentation, staff turnover, and the accumulation of technical debt. Lehman's laws observe that a system must continually change or become progressively less useful, and that its complexity grows unless work is done to reduce it. Techniques such as refactoring, automated regression testing, and reverse engineering are used to control these costs.
