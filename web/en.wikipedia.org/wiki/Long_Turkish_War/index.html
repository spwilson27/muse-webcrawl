Long Turkish War

The Long Turkish War or Thirteen Years' War was an indecisive land war between the Habsburg Monarchy and the Ottoman Empire, primarily over the principalities of Wallachia, Transylvania, and Moldavia, lasting from 1593 to 1606. It ended with the Peace of Zsitvatorok, in which the Ottomans acknowledged the Habsburg emperor as an equal for the first time.

Background

The war followed years of border raiding in Ottoman Hungary. The Ottoman defeat of a Habsburg-Croatian force at the Battle of Sisak in June 1593 — where the Ban of Croatia's troops destroyed an Ottoman raiding army — prompted Sultan Murad III to declare war. Habsburg strategy rested on the fortress frontier of Hungary, subsidised by the Spanish Monarchy and the German princes through the "Turkish tax".

Course of the war

The war was marked by gruelling fortress sieges: the Ottomans captured Győr (1594) and Eger (1596), winning the great field battle of Keresztes (1596) but failing to exploit it. Michael the Brave of Wallachia revolted against Ottoman suzerainty in 1594, won at Călugăreni (1595), and in 1600 briefly united Wallachia, Transylvania, and Moldavia under his rule before his assassination in 1601. In 1598 imperial forces under Adolf von Schwarzenberg retook Győr, but Buda resisted repeated sieges. Exhaustion, the Bocskai uprising in Hungary (1604–1606), and the Ottoman–Safavid war in the east pushed both sides to negotiate.

Peace of Zsitvatorok

The peace, signed on 11 November 1606, replaced the humiliating annual Habsburg "tribute" for Royal Hungary with a single payment, recognised the emperor as the sultan's diplomatic equal, and left territorial changes minimal. It stabilised the frontier for half a century and is seen as a milestone marking the limits of Ottoman expansion in Europe.
