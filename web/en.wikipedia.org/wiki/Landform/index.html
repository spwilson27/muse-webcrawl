Landform

Relief features of the Earth's surface

A landform is a natural or anthropogenic feature of the solid surface of the Earth or another planetary body. Landforms together make up a given terrain, and their arrangement in the landscape is known as topography. Landforms include hills, mountains, canyons, and valleys, as well as shore features like bays, peninsulas, and seas, including submerged features such as mid-ocean ridges and the great ocean basins.

The science of landform formation and classification is geomorphology. Landforms are categorised by characteristic physical attributes such as elevation, slope, orientation, structure, rock exposure, and soil type, and grouped into orders of relief from continental-scale features down to individual hills and gullies.

Types

Common classes of landform include:

  • Mountainous — mountains, ridges, plateaus, hills;
  • Fluvial — valleys, floodplains, levees, deltas, terraces;
  • Coastal — cliffs, beaches, spits, lagoons, estuaries;
  • Glacial — moraines, drumlins, cirques, fjords;
  • Aeolian — dunes, loess plains, yardangs;
  • Volcanic — calderas, lava plateaus, cinder cones;
  • Karst — sinkholes, caves, limestone pavements;
  • Tectonic — fault scarps, rift valleys, horsts and grabens.

Formation

Landforms are produced by the interplay of constructive forces — tectonic uplift and volcanism — and destructive processes of weathering, erosion and deposition driven by water, ice, wind and gravity. Most landforms evolve over geological timescales, though some (landslides, volcanic cones, flood deposits) form in single events.
