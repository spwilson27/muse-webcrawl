Emerald Tablet

The Emerald Tablet, also known as the Smaragdine Tablet or Tabula Smaragdina, is a compact and cryptic Hermetic text attributed to Hermes Trismegistus. It was highly regarded by European alchemists as the foundation of their art and its Hermetic tradition. Its most famous line, "As above, so below," distills the doctrine of correspondence between the macrocosm and the microcosm.

History

No original emerald tablet has ever been found. The earliest surviving versions appear in Arabic works of the late eighth or ninth century, notably the Book of the Secret of Creation attributed to Balinas (pseudo-Apollonius of Tyana). The text was translated into Latin in the twelfth century and circulated widely among medieval and Renaissance scholars; Isaac Newton produced his own translation, preserved among his alchemical papers.

Content

In brief aphorisms, the Tablet describes a single primal substance and the operations — separation, purification, and reunion of the elements — by which "the miracle of the one thing" is accomplished, often interpreted as the creation of the philosopher's stone. It speaks of the Sun as its father, the Moon as its mother, and the wind and earth as its carriers and nurse.

Influence

The Tablet shaped alchemy, occultism, and later esoteric movements, and its aphorisms remain staples of Hermetic and New Age literature.
