Western Armenian

Native to: historically Western Armenia (eastern Anatolia); now the Armenian diaspora
Writing system: Armenian alphabet (traditional orthography)
Status: Endangered (UNESCO)

Western Armenian (Արեւմտահայերէն, Arevmdahayeren) is one of the two standardised literary forms of the Armenian language, the other being Eastern Armenian. It developed in the 19th century from the Armenian dialects of Istanbul and western Anatolia and became the language of the Armenian communities of the Ottoman Empire.

History

Following the Armenian genocide of 1915, Western Armenian survived chiefly among the diaspora communities of the Middle East, Europe and the Americas, where it is maintained in schools, churches and newspapers. Unlike Eastern Armenian, it retains the traditional (Mashtotsian) orthography, since the Soviet spelling reform of the 1920s applied only in Soviet Armenia.

Differences from Eastern Armenian

The two standards differ in pronunciation — Western Armenian has undergone a shift of the classical voiced consonants, so that letters written for /b/, /d/, /g/ are pronounced voiceless, and vice versa — as well as in some vocabulary and grammatical preferences. UNESCO classifies Western Armenian as an endangered language, as younger diaspora generations increasingly shift to the languages of their host countries.
