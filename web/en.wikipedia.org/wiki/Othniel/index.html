Othniel (Hebrew: עָתְנִיאֵל, ʿOṯnîʾēl ben Qənaz) was the first of the biblical judges of Israel, according to the Book of Judges. The younger brother (or nephew) of Caleb, he is the only judge associated with the tribe of Judah, and his story establishes the pattern that the rest of the book follows: Israel's apostasy, oppression by a foreign power, crying out to God, and deliverance by a Spirit-empowered judge, followed by a period of rest.

Biblical narrative

Othniel first appears in the Book of Joshua (15:17) and in Judges 1, where Caleb offers his daughter Achsah in marriage to whoever captures the city of Debir (Kiriath-sepher). Othniel takes the city and marries Achsah, who then persuades her father to add springs of water to her dowry.

In Judges 3, after the death of Joshua, the Israelites "did evil in the sight of the Lord" and were given into the hand of Cushan-rishathaim, king of Aram-Naharaim (Mesopotamia), whom they served for eight years. When the Israelites cried out, "the Spirit of the Lord came upon" Othniel, who went to war, defeated Cushan-rishathaim, and gave the land rest for forty years, until his death. He was succeeded, after a new cycle of apostasy, by Ehud.

Interpretation

Commentators often treat Othniel as the model or idealised judge: uniquely among the major judges he is given no character flaw, no ambiguous episode, and no violent death, in contrast to later figures such as Gideon, Jephthah, and Samson. His position at the head of the list may reflect editorial framing as much as chronology. The meaning of the name is uncertain; it has been linked to roots meaning "lion of God" or "God has answered me".

A tomb at Hebron has traditionally been venerated as the burial place of Othniel ben Kenaz.
