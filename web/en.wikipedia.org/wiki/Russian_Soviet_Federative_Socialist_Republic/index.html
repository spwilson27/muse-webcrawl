The Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic (RSFSR; Russian: Российская Советская Федеративная Социалистическая Республика), previously known as the Russian Soviet Republic, was an independent federal socialist state from 1917 to 1922, and afterwards the largest and most populous Soviet socialist republic of the USSR from 1922 to 1991, until becoming a sovereign part of the Soviet Union with priority of Russian laws over Union laws in 1990 and 1991, during the last two years of the existence of the USSR.

Overview

Proclaimed after the October Revolution of 1917, the RSFSR was governed by the Bolsheviks under Vladimir Lenin, with its constitution adopted in 1918. On 30 December 1922 it joined the Ukrainian, Byelorussian, and Transcaucasian republics to form the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics. The RSFSR comprised about three-quarters of the USSR's territory and over half its population, contained its capital, Moscow, and included within it numerous autonomous republics, oblasts, and krais for non-Russian peoples. Yet unlike other union republics it lacked some institutions of its own — for most of the Soviet period it had no separate Communist Party branch, KGB, or Academy of Sciences — because union-level institutions largely doubled as Russian ones.

End of the Soviet Union

On 12 June 1990 the RSFSR's Congress of People's Deputies adopted a declaration of state sovereignty, and on 12 June 1991 Boris Yeltsin won the first popular election for the republic's presidency. After the failed August 1991 coup and the Belovezha Accords, the RSFSR was renamed the Russian Federation on 25 December 1991, becoming the legal successor state of the USSR. Yeltsin remained president of the new federation.
