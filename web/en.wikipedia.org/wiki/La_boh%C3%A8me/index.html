La bohème

La bohème
Composer: Giacomo Puccini
Librettists: Luigi Illica and Giuseppe Giacosa
Premiere: 1 February 1896, Teatro Regio, Turin

La bohème is an opera in four acts by Giacomo Puccini to an Italian libretto by Luigi Illica and Giuseppe Giacosa, based on Henri Murger's Scènes de la vie de bohème. It premiered in Turin in 1896, conducted by the young Arturo Toscanini, and has become one of the most frequently performed operas worldwide.

Plot

In the Latin Quarter of Paris, the poet Rodolfo falls in love with the seamstress Mimì, who is ill with tuberculosis. Their friends — the painter Marcello, the musician Schaunard, the philosopher Colline, and the singer Musetta — share poverty and high spirits. In the final act the friends reunite around the dying Mimì, who expires in Rodolfo's arms.

Music

Famous numbers include Rodolfo's "Che gelida manina," Mimì's "Sì, mi chiamano Mimì," their duet "O soave fanciulla," Musetta's waltz "Quando m'en vo'," and Colline's farewell to his coat, "Vecchia zimarra."

Legacy

The opera inspired the Broadway musical Rent. Leoncavallo composed a rival La bohème based on the same source in 1897, which is now rarely performed.
