Energy balance

Energy balance is the relationship between energy consumed (from food and drink) and energy expended (through basal metabolism and physical activity) in a living organism. When the energy consumed equals the energy expended, energy balance is maintained and body weight remains stable. A positive energy balance (consuming more than is expended) leads to storage of the surplus, primarily as fat, while a negative energy balance leads to the use of stored energy and weight loss.

Components

Energy intake comes from the macronutrients in food: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, and alcohol. Energy expenditure consists of the basal metabolic rate (the largest component, typically 60–75%), the thermic effect of food (about 10%), and physical activity, which varies widely between individuals. The concept derives from the first law of thermodynamics applied to biological systems; see also energy homeostasis.

Health implications

Long-term positive energy balance is the fundamental driver of obesity, while prolonged negative balance causes underweight and malnutrition. Energy balance is regulated by complex hormonal and neural signals involving appetite hormones such as leptin and ghrelin, and is influenced by genetics, environment, sleep, and behaviour.
