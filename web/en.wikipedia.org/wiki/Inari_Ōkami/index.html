Inari Ōkami (Japanese: 稲荷大神), also read as Inari Daimyōjin, is the Japanese kami (deity) of rice, agriculture, fertility, prosperity, industry, worldly success, and foxes, and is one of the most widely worshipped deities of Shinto. Inari is served by fox messengers (kitsune) and is associated with the colour red, vermilion torii gates, and offerings of fried tofu (aburaage, said to be the foxes' favourite food and the origin of the sushi dish inarizushi).

Worship

Some 10,000 shrines in Japan — roughly a third of all Shinto shrines — are dedicated to Inari, headed by the Fushimi Inari-taisha in Kyoto, founded in 711 on Mount Inari and famous for its thousands of vermilion torii gates (the Senbon Torii). Inari's sex is variously depicted — as an old man, a young woman, or an androgynous figure — reflecting the deity's many aspects and identifications, including merged forms with Buddhist deities such as Dakiniten.

History

Worship of Inari grew with rice agriculture and spread in the Edo period, when Inari shrines were installed in homes, businesses, and feudal residences as protectors of prosperity. Today companies as well as individuals maintain Inari shrines, and Inari remains a guardian of enterprises from sake brewing to high technology.
