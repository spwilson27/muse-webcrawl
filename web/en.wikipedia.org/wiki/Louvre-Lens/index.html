Louvre-Lens is an art museum in Lens, in the Pas-de-Calais department of northern France, built as a regional branch of the Louvre. Inaugurated by President François Hollande on 4 December 2012, it stands on a former coal mine, symbolising the cultural regeneration of the mining basin, and belongs to the wave of French museum decentralisation that also produced the Mucem and Centre Pompidou-Metz.

Building and display

Designed by the Japanese firm SANAA, the museum is a chain of five low, glass-and-polished-aluminium pavilions set in a park by landscape architect Catherine Mosbach, with slag heaps visible on the horizon. Its centrepiece, the Galerie du Temps, displays some 200 works from the Louvre's collections in a single open hall, arranged chronologically from antiquity to the nineteenth century — with Delacroix's Liberty Leading the People as an early emblematic loan — so visitors of all departments see art across periods rather than separated by curatorial department. A glass pavilion hosts temporary thematic exhibitions, and visible storage areas show works in reserve.

Impact

The museum drew around 900,000 visitors in its first year, far beyond expectations in a town of some 36,000 people, and is credited with changing perceptions of the former mining region, whose basin was listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2012. Loans rotate regularly, keeping the Galerie du Temps display in slow evolution.
