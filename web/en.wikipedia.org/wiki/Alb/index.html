The alb (from the Latin albus, meaning "white") is a long white liturgical vestment worn by clergy and lay ministers in the Roman Catholic Church and in several Protestant traditions. Reaching to the ankles, it is one of the most ancient Christian vestments, derived from the ordinary white tunic (tunica alba) of the Graeco-Roman world.

Description

The alb is typically made of white linen or a linen-like fabric and is worn over ordinary clothing, tied at the waist with a girdle called a cincture. A priest celebrating Mass wears the alb beneath the stole and chasuble; deacons and altar servers may also wear the alb, sometimes as the outermost liturgical garment.

Symbolism

The white colour symbolises purity and the baptismal garment. In the traditional vesting prayers, the celebrant putting on the alb asks to be made clean in heart, echoing the white robes of the Book of Revelation. Simplified alb-like garments such as the surplice developed from it for use over the cassock in choir offices.
