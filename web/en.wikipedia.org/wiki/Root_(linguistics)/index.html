Root (linguistics)

In linguistics, a root is the core of a word that carries its principal meaning and cannot be reduced into smaller meaningful parts. A root is a type of morpheme: prefixes, suffixes, and other affixes attach to it, and inflectional endings modify it, but the root itself remains the lexical foundation of the word. For example, in English the word unhappiness consists of the root happy with the prefix un- and the suffix -ness; removing all affixes leaves the root.

Roots, stems, and bases

Linguists distinguish the root from the stem and the base. A stem is the form of a word to which inflectional affixes attach — in dogs, the stem is dog; in a derived word such as friendliness, the base for the suffix -ness is friendly, while the root remains friend. A root may itself be a free form that can stand alone as a word (as friend does) or a bound form that cannot, such as -ceive in receive, perceive, and conceive, which descends from Latin and carries meaning only in combination.

Semitic roots

The concept of the root is especially central to the Semitic languages, where most words are built from a consonantal root — usually three consonants (a triliteral root) — into which vowel patterns and affixes are interleaved. In Arabic, the root k-t-b carries the core notion of writing: kitāb "book", kātib "writer", maktaba "library", kataba "he wrote". Hebrew shows the same pattern, and dictionaries of these languages are traditionally organised by root. Grammarians of Arabic and Hebrew developed root analysis centuries before European linguistics adopted the term.

Historical linguistics

In historical linguistics, roots are reconstructed for proto-languages. Proto-Indo-European roots are conventionally written with an asterisk, such as *bʰer- "to carry", which yields English bear, Latin ferre, and Greek pherein. Such roots are abstractions reconstructed from systematic sound correspondences across descendant languages. Root structure constraints — for example, rules about which consonants could co-occur in a Proto-Indo-European root — are an important tool in reconstruction.

Root compounds and derivation

Roots combine with each other in compounding and with affixes in derivation to create new words. In languages such as German and Sanskrit, long compound words may contain several roots. Study of roots belongs to morphology, the branch of linguistics concerned with the internal structure of words.
