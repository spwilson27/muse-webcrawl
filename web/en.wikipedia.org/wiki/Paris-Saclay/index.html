Paris-Saclay

TypeResearch and technology cluster
LocationSaclay plateau, Essonne and Yvelines, France
Established2010s (public development operation)

Paris-Saclay is a research and technology cluster under construction south of Paris, on the Saclay plateau in the departments of Essonne and Yvelines. Conceived as a French "Silicon Valley", it brings together universities, grandes écoles, public research institutes and the research centres of major companies.

The site already hosts the CEA Saclay nuclear research centre, the SOLEIL synchrotron, and institutes that relocated there from the 1970s onward, such as École Polytechnique (moved to Palaiseau in 1976). Under the Grand Paris development programme, the French state created a public development authority to build a unified scientific campus around the new Paris-Saclay University and the Institut Polytechnique de Paris, served in the future by line 18 of the Grand Paris Express metro.

Institutions

Members and residents include Paris-Saclay University (formed from Paris-Sud University and associated institutions), École Polytechnique, ENSTA, Télécom Paris, HEC Paris, CentraleSupélec, ENS Paris-Saclay, AgroParisTech, INRAE, CNRS laboratories, and corporate research sites of companies such as Thales, EDF, Danone and Horiba. The cluster accounts for a significant share — often cited around 15 percent — of French public and private research capacity.

Development and debate

The project has involved major construction on fertile plateau farmland, prompting opposition from farmers and environmental groups concerned about the urbanisation of the plateau and the loss of agricultural land, as well as debate over transport links and the long delays to the metro line serving the campus.
