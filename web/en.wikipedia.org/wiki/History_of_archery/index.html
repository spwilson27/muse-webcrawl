The history of archery covers the use of the bow and arrow for hunting and warfare from the Stone Age to the firearm era, and its survival as a sport.

Origins

Stone arrowheads from sites such as Sibudu Cave (South Africa) suggest archery may be tens of thousands of years old. Bows spread worldwide; the oldest surviving bows are Mesolithic elm longbows from Holmegaard, Denmark (c. 7000 BCE).

Warfare

Egyptian, Assyrian and Persian armies used archers extensively; the composite bow of Central Asian horse archers — Scythians, Huns, Mongols — transformed steppe warfare. In medieval Europe the English and Welsh longbow proved decisive at Crécy (1346) and Agincourt (1415). Ottoman and Japanese (kyūdō) traditions also elevated archery to high art.

Decline and revival

Firearms displaced the war bow by the 17th century. Archery survived as recreation, was an Olympic sport in 1900–1920, and returned permanently at Munich 1972; modern target and field archery use recurve and compound bows.
