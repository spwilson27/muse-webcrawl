Balto

Balto with his musher, Gunnar Kaasen, in 1925

Balto (1919 – 14 March 1933) was a Siberian husky and sled dog belonging to musher and breeder Leonhard Seppala. He achieved fame as the lead dog of the sled team, driven by Gunnar Kaasen, that carried diphtheria antitoxin on the final leg of the 1925 serum run to Nome, Alaska.

The serum run

In January 1925 a diphtheria epidemic threatened Nome, and the only serum was in Anchorage, nearly 1,000 miles away. Ice and blizzards prevented air delivery, so a relay of more than twenty dog sled teams carried the serum 674 miles from Nenana in about five and a half days — the "Great Race of Mercy". Seppala's team, led by Togo, covered the longest and most dangerous leg, but it was Kaasen's team, led by Balto, that delivered the serum to Nome on 2 February 1925 and received most of the publicity. Balto toured the United States, and a statue of him by Frederick Roth was unveiled in New York's Central Park in December 1925. Later neglected in a travelling sideshow, Balto and his surviving teammates were rescued by a public fundraising campaign and brought to the Cleveland Metroparks Zoo, where Balto lived until 1933. His preserved body is displayed at the Cleveland Museum of Natural History. The annual Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race commemorates the run, and Balto's story was retold in the 1995 animated film Balto.
