Niqqud

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Pointed Hebrew text from a prayer book

In Hebrew orthography, niqqud or nikkud (Hebrew: נִקּוּד, "dotting, pointing") is a system of diacritical signs used to represent vowels and distinguish between alternative pronunciations of a letter that are not conveyed by the basic consonantal Hebrew alphabet. The signs are small marks placed above, below, or inside the consonant letters.

History

The system was developed by the Masoretes of Tiberias in the early Middle Ages (roughly the 7th to 10th centuries CE) to preserve the precise pronunciation of the Hebrew Bible for liturgical reading. The Tiberian system triumphed over the rival Babylonian and Palestinian systems, and the pointed text became the basis of the Masoretic Text. Niqqud was later applied to poetry, prayer books, children's books, and textbooks for learners of Hebrew.

Modern usage

Modern Israeli Hebrew is normally written without niqqud ("full spelling" uses the letters yod and vav as vowel aids instead). Pointed text appears in the Bible, poetry, dictionaries, and materials for children and new immigrants. Proposals to simplify or reform the system have been debated by the Academy of the Hebrew Language.
