Political integration of India

Integration of princely states

The political integration of India was the process, following independence and Partition in August 1947, by which the 562 princely states of British India acceded to either India or Pakistan and were merged into the new nations. In India, the campaign was directed by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, Minister for States, and his secretary V. P. Menon, whose diplomacy — backed by Viceroy-turned-Governor-General Lord Mountbatten — persuaded nearly all rulers within the geographical reach of India to sign Instruments of Accession before or shortly after 15 August 1947.

Princely states (yellow) amid British Indian provinces at independence, 1947.

Method

States were offered accession on just three subjects — defence, external affairs, and communications — with guarantees of privy purses and privileges. Smaller states were grouped into unions (such as Saurashtra and PEPSU) or merged into provinces, producing the state map later reorganised on linguistic lines in 1956.

Hard cases

Three accessions were contested. Junagadh's Muslim Nawab acceded to Pakistan over a Hindu-majority population; India took control and a 1948 plebiscite confirmed accession to India. Hyderabad, the largest state, resisted until Indian forces annexed it in "Operation Polo" (September 1948). The Maharaja of Jammu and Kashmir acceded to India in October 1947 amid a tribal invasion from Pakistan, triggering the First Kashmir War and a dispute that persists today. Residual integrations followed: French and Portuguese enclaves (Pondicherry 1954/1962, Goa 1961) and Sikkim in 1975.
