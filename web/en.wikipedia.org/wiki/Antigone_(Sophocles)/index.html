Antigone (Sophocles)

Antigone
Author: Sophocles
Written: c. 441 BC
Genre: Tragedy
Setting: Thebes

Antigone is an Athenian tragedy written by Sophocles in 441 BC or earlier. It is chronologically the third of the three Theban plays but was written first.

Plot

After Oedipus's sons Eteocles and Polynices kill each other, King Creon forbids burial of Polynices. Their sister Antigone defies the edict, performing burial rites. Creon condemns her to be walled up alive; prophet Tiresias warns of divine anger. Creon relents too late: Antigone has hanged herself, Haemon (Creon's son and her betrothed) dies by his own hand, and Queen Eurydice commits suicide.

Themes

Divine law versus human law, civil disobedience, family loyalty, gender, and the dangers of pride and tyranny. The play has inspired adaptations by Anouilh, Brecht, and many others.

Characters

  • Antigone, Ismene, Creon, Haemon, Tiresias, Eurydice
