Bourgogne-Franche-Comté

Bourgogne-Franche-Comté is a region in eastern France created in 2016 by merging Burgundy and Franche-Comté. Its prefecture and largest city is Dijon; Besançon is seat of the regional council.

Geography and economy

The region spans the Saône plain, Burgundy vineyards (Côte d'Or), the Morvan hills and the Jura Mountains bordering Switzerland. It is renowned for wine (Pinot Noir, Chardonnay), mustard, Comté cheese, watchmaking and automotive industry (Peugeot at Sochaux).

History

It unites the historic Duchy of Burgundy — a major medieval power — with the Free County (Franche-Comté), once part of the Holy Roman Empire and Spanish Habsburg lands before French annexation in 1678.
