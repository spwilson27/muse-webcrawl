Wakehurst

Location: Ardingly, West Sussex, England
Area: about 214 ha (535 acres)
Operated by: Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew
Known for: Millennium Seed Bank

Wakehurst, previously known as Wakehurst Place, is a house, garden and country estate in Ardingly, West Sussex, England, managed by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew. The estate covers more than 500 acres of woodland, conservation areas, and ornamental gardens, and is home to the Millennium Seed Bank, the largest wild plant seed storage facility in the world.

History

The Elizabethan mansion at the heart of the estate was built by Sir Edward Culpeper in 1590. The designed landscape was largely created under Gerald Loder, Lord Wakehurst, who bought the estate in 1903 and devoted some forty years to developing the gardens and planting notable trees. The estate later passed through the hands of Sir Henry Price, who bequeathed it to the nation; it was accepted by the National Trust in 1963 and leased to the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, the following year.

The gardens

Wakehurst's gardens reflect its position in the Sussex High Weald, with sandstone outcrops, steep valleys, and lakes. Plantings include national collections, a walled garden, water gardens, the Himalayan Glade, and extensive woods of native and exotic trees adapted to the local acid soils and high rainfall. It is often described as "Kew in the country", holding living collections that complement the smaller London site.

Millennium Seed Bank

The Millennium Seed Bank, opened in 2000 within the Wellcome Trust Millennium Building, stores seeds of wild plant species in underground vaults as insurance against extinction and for use in research and habitat restoration. Working with partners in dozens of countries, it has banked seeds from tens of thousands of species, including the great majority of the United Kingdom's native flora. The building also houses laboratories, a public exhibition, and seed-processing facilities.
