Reichstag building

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Reichstag building (German: Reichstagsgebäude) is a historic government building on Platz der Republik in Berlin. Constructed between 1884 and 1894 to designs by architect Paul Wallot, it housed the Reichstag, the parliament of the German Empire and later of the Weimar Republic. Since 1999 it has been the seat of the German federal parliament, the Bundestag.

History

The Neo-Renaissance building was opened in 1894 and bore the inscription Dem deutschen Volke ("To the German People"), added in 1916. On 27 February 1933 it was severely damaged by the Reichstag fire, which the Nazi regime used as a pretext to suspend civil liberties. The ruin stood in West Berlin during the Cold War, partially restored in the 1960s but without a functioning parliament. In 1995 the building was famously wrapped in fabric by artists Christo and Jeanne-Claude.

Reconstruction

Following German reunification, the building was reconstructed under architect Norman Foster between 1995 and 1999. The project added the now-iconic glass dome, which offers public views over the chamber and the city, and preserved historical features including Soviet soldiers' graffiti from 1945. The Bundestag held its first sitting in the rebuilt chamber in April 1999, and the dome has become one of Berlin's most visited landmarks.
