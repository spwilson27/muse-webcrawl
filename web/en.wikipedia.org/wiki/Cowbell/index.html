Cowbell

A cowbell is a bell worn around the neck of free-roaming livestock so herders can keep track of an animal via the sound of the bell. The term also refers to a hand percussion instrument — an idiophone — used in many kinds of popular music, named for its resemblance to the livestock bell.

Livestock bells

Bells for cattle, sheep and goats have been made for centuries in bronze, iron and other metals, often in distinctive regional shapes. Switzerland is especially known for its large, finely tuned treicheln and trycheln used in alpine transhumance ceremonies.

As a musical instrument

The percussion cowbell is struck with a stick and produces a bright, penetrating metallic tone. It is a staple of Latin music such as salsa and of rock and funk; a famous 2000 Saturday Night Live sketch about the recording of "(Don't Fear) The Reaper" made "more cowbell" a popular catchphrase.
