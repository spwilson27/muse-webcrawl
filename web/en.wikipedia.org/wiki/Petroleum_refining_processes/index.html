Petroleum refining processes

Petroleum refining processes are the chemical engineering processes and other facilities used in petroleum refineries (also referred to as oil refineries) to transform crude oil into useful products such as liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), gasoline (petrol), kerosene, jet fuel, diesel oil, and fuel oils.

Separation processes

The first step in refining is fractional distillation. Crude oil is heated and fed into a distillation column, where hydrocarbons separate according to boiling point: light gases and naphtha rise to the top, kerosene and diesel are drawn from the middle, and heavy gas oils, lubricating fractions, bitumen, and residual fuel oil collect at the bottom. Desalting and atmospheric and vacuum distillation are the principal separation units.

Conversion processes

Because crude oil rarely yields the desired proportions of products, conversion processes change the size and structure of hydrocarbon molecules. Fluid catalytic cracking (FCC) breaks heavy gas oil into gasoline and lighter gases over a hot catalyst. Hydrocracking performs a similar transformation under high hydrogen pressure. Coking thermally cracks heavy residues, while visbreaking mildly cracks residues to reduce viscosity. Catalytic reforming rearranges naphtha molecules to raise octane, and alkylation combines isobutane with butylenes to form high-octane alkylate.

Treatment processes

Treating processes remove impurities and improve product quality. Hydrotreating (hydrodesulfurisation) uses hydrogen and catalysts to remove sulfur, nitrogen, and metals. Sweetening processes such as Merox remove or convert mercaptans, and amine gas treating removes hydrogen sulfide from refinery gases.
