The San Francisco 49ers are a professional American football team in the National Football League, based in the San Francisco Bay Area. Founded in 1946 as a charter member of the All-America Football Conference, they joined the NFL in 1950; their name honours the prospectors of the 1849 California Gold Rush.

Dynasty years

Under coach Bill Walsh, inventor of the West Coast offense, and quarterbacks Joe Montana and later Steve Young, the 49ers won five Super Bowls between 1982 and 1995 — a dynasty featuring players such as Jerry Rice, widely considered the greatest wide receiver in NFL history, and Ronnie Lott.

Recent history

The team moved from Candlestick Park to Levi's Stadium in Santa Clara in 2014 and returned to the Super Bowl under coach Kyle Shanahan after the 2019 and 2023 seasons, losing both times to Kansas City.
