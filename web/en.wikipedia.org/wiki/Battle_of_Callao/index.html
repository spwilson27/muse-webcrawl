Battle of Callao

The Battle of Callao (Spanish: Combate del Callao), also known as the Battle of May 2 (Combate del Dos de Mayo), was fought on 2 May 1866 between a Spanish naval squadron under Admiral Casto Méndez Núñez and the coastal defenses of Callao, Peru, during the Chincha Islands War. After bombarding Valparaíso, Chile, the Spanish fleet attacked Callao, Peru's principal port; the Peruvian batteries, manned by regulars and civilian volunteers, fought the squadron to a standstill, and the Spanish withdrew. The battle is celebrated in Peru as a national triumph and is the subject of the Peruvian national holiday of 2 May.

Background

Spain, which had not recognized Peruvian independence, seized the guano-rich Chincha Islands in 1864, provoking war with Peru and Chile. After Rear Admiral Pareja's suicide following setbacks, Méndez Núñez took command, bombarded the undefended Chilean port of Valparaíso on 31 March 1866, and sailed for Callao, determined — in his famous phrase — that Spain preferred "honor without ships to ships without honor".

Battle

Callao had been hastily but powerfully fortified under President Mariano Ignacio Prado, with Armstrong and Blakely guns, the turret monitors Loa and Victoria, and batteries including the Merced tower, where Minister of War José Gálvez directed the defense. On 2 May the Spanish squadron, led by the ironclad Numancia, opened fire around midday. The duel lasted some five to eight hours: the Spanish ships were repeatedly hit, Méndez Núñez was wounded but refused to leave the bridge, and Gálvez was killed when the Merced tower exploded. By evening the Spanish withdrew, having failed to silence the defenses or destroy the port.

Aftermath

The Spanish squadron sailed for the Philippines days later, effectively ending Spain's Pacific campaign; formal peace treaties followed years later. In Peru, the battle — the Dos de Mayo — became a cornerstone of national identity, commemorated annually and giving its name to a province and numerous streets.
