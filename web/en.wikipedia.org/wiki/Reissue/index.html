Reissue

Applies to: Music recordings, films, books
Common features: Remastering, bonus material, new packaging
Related: Remaster, rerelease, special edition

A reissue (also a rerelease) is the renewed publication of a previously released work — most commonly a music album, film or book — after its original release. Reissues range from straight repressings to elaborate new editions with remastered sound or picture, bonus tracks, restored footage, and new artwork and liner notes.

Music

In the recording industry, reissues allow labels to keep catalogue titles available and to exploit anniversaries and renewed interest in an artist. The compact disc era from the 1980s onward produced a wave of album reissues, often remastered and expanded with bonus tracks; deluxe and box-set editions became a significant market for collectors. Reissues may correct earlier errors, restore original mixes, or present material in formats such as vinyl that had fallen out of use.

Film and books

Films are reissued for anniversaries, restoration premieres and home-video editions, sometimes with altered or extended cuts. Publishers reissue classic books in new editions with fresh typesetting, introductions and scholarly apparatus.
