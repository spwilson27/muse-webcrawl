The Māori are the indigenous Polynesian people of mainland New Zealand (Aotearoa). They originated with settlers from East Polynesia, who arrived in New Zealand in several waves of canoe voyages between roughly 1320 and 1350.

History

Settlement of New Zealand by Polynesian ancestors of Māori occurred in the late 13th century, following earlier exploration by legendary navigators such as Kupe. Iwi (tribes) developed distinct regional cultures. In 1840 the Treaty of Waitangi was signed between the British Crown and many Māori chiefs.

Culture

Māori culture is known for the haka (ceremonial posture dance), tā moko (tattooing), whakairo (carving), and the marae (communal meeting ground). Traditional society was organised by whānau (extended family), hapū (subtribe) and iwi.

Language

Te reo Māori is an Eastern Polynesian language closely related to Cook Islands Māori, Tahitian and Hawaiian. It became an official language of New Zealand in 1987 and has seen a major revitalisation through kōhanga reo immersion schools.

Demographics

Māori make up roughly 17–18% of New Zealand's population, with large communities also in Australia. Urbanisation since the mid-20th century moved most Māori from rural areas into cities.
