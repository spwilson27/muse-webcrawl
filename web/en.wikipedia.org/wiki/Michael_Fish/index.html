Michael Fish

Born: 27 April 1944, Eastbourne, Sussex
Occupation: Meteorologist and weather presenter
Employer: Met Office / BBC (1960s–2004)

Michael Fish MBE FRMetS (born 27 April 1944) is a British weather forecaster, best known as the longest-serving broadcast meteorologist on British television and for his remarks on the eve of the Great Storm of 1987.

Career

Fish joined the Meteorological Office after school and began broadcasting weather forecasts on BBC radio and television in the 1970s. He became one of the BBC's most recognisable presenters, appearing on national forecasts for over three decades until his final regular broadcast in 2004; at his farewell he was presented with a commemorative weather symbol. He later continued to make occasional forecasts, including for regional programmes, and to appear in documentaries about British weather.

The 1987 storm

On 15 October 1987, hours before the worst storm to hit south-east England in centuries, Fish told viewers: "Earlier on today, apparently, a woman rang the BBC and said she heard there was a hurricane on the way; well, if you're watching, don't worry, there isn't." That night winds gusting over 100 mph killed 18 people in England, felled millions of trees and caused enormous damage. Fish later maintained that his comment had referred to a separate storm system affecting Florida, and that he had warned of very windy weather to come; he also pointed out that the Met Office had in fact issued severe weather warnings before the storm peaked. The clip nonetheless became one of the most replayed moments in British broadcasting history, and "doing a Michael Fish" entered slang for a confidently wrong prediction.

Honours

Fish was appointed MBE in 2004 and is an honorary fellow of the Royal Meteorological Society.
