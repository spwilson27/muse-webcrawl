Sorbet is a frozen dessert made from sweetened water flavored with fruit (typically juice or purée), wine, or liqueur. Unlike ice cream, sorbet contains no dairy, and unlike sherbet (in the American sense) it contains little or no milk fat. The word derives, via Italian sorbetto and Turkish şerbet, from the Arabic sharbat, a chilled sweet drink.

History

Iced drinks flavored with fruit were enjoyed in Persia, China, and the Roman Empire; snow mixed with honey and fruit was a luxury of antiquity. Sorbet in its modern churned form developed in 17th-century Italy and France. In French haute cuisine, a small portion of sorbet is traditionally served between courses as a palate cleanser.

Preparation

A sorbet base of fruit juice or purée and sugar syrup is churned in an ice-cream maker as it freezes, incorporating air and keeping ice crystals small; the result is smoother than the scraped Sicilian granita. Sugar concentration—about 20–30%—controls texture: too little makes it icy, too much prevents freezing. Alcohol or a small amount of glucose syrup can improve scoopability. Because it contains no fat, sorbet's fruit flavor tastes especially vivid.

Variations

Popular flavors include lemon, raspberry, mango, and blood orange. Wine sorbets (e.g., Champagne) are served as elegant desserts. Sorbet is naturally vegan when made without egg-white stabilizers, and pairs with fresh fruit and meringue.
