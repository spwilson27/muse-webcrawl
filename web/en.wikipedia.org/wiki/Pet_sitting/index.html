Pet sitting

Pet sitting is the act of temporarily taking care of another person's pet for a given time frame. It commonly involves visiting the pet in its own home to feed it, provide water, spend time playing with it, and take it for walks, typically while the owner is on vacation or otherwise away. Pet sitting differs from boarding, in which the animal is taken to kennels or the sitter's home, and from dog walking, which usually covers only exercise.

Industry

Pet sitting has grown into a substantial professional industry, with sitters offering services through agencies, online platforms and independent businesses. Professional pet sitters may be insured and bonded, and may hold certifications in pet first aid. Industry bodies such as Pet Sitters International promote standards and provide accreditation. Because the animal stays in its familiar environment, pet sitting is often considered less stressful for the pet than boarding.

Duties

Beyond feeding and exercise, sitters may administer medication, clean litter trays, bring in mail, water houseplants and adjust lights, giving the home a lived-in appearance. Services are usually arranged through an initial consultation at which the sitter meets the animal and collects instructions and keys.
