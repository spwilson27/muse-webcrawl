Anglo-Spanish War (1585–1604)

The Anglo-Spanish War (1585–1604) was an intermittent conflict between the Kingdom of England and the Kingdom of Spain that was never formally declared. It began with England's military expedition to the Spanish Netherlands under the Earl of Leicester in 1585.

Date1585–1604
LocationAtlantic, English Channel, Netherlands, Spain, Americas
ResultTreaty of London (1604); status quo

Causes

Causes included religious division after the Reformation, English privateering against Spanish treasure fleets, and English support for Dutch rebels against Spanish rule. Philip II of Spain sought to restore Catholicism in England under Elizabeth I.

Spanish Armada

In 1588 Spain launched the Spanish Armada, a huge fleet intended to invade England. It was defeated by English naval tactics, fireships at Gravelines, and storms on the return voyage around Scotland and Ireland. England launched a counter-Armada in 1589, which also failed.

Later war

Fighting continued at sea, in Ireland, France and the Netherlands, including the English capture and sack of Cádiz in 1596 and Spanish landings in Ireland culminating at Kinsale in 1601.

Treaty of London

The war ended with the Treaty of London in 1604, negotiated between Philip III and James I, restoring trade and the pre-war status quo. See also Drake passage for Francis Drake's earlier circumnavigation.
