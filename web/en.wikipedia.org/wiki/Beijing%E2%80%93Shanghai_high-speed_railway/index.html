The Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway (Chinese: 京沪高速铁路) is a 1,318-kilometre (819 mi) high-speed railway connecting Beijing and Shanghai, China's two largest cities. Construction began in April 2008 and the line opened to commercial service on 30 June 2011. It is one of the world's busiest high-speed rail lines, carrying well over 100 million passengers a year.

Route

The line runs from Beijing South station through Tianjin, Jinan, Xuzhou, Bengbu, Nanjing, Suzhou, and other cities to Shanghai Hongqiao station, passing through seven provinces and municipalities. Much of the route is elevated on viaducts; the 164.8 km Danyang–Kunshan Grand Bridge is the world's longest bridge. Principal structures include the Tianjin Grand Bridge and the Nanjing Dashengguan Yangtze River Bridge.

Service

Trains operate at up to 350 km/h; the fastest services complete the journey in about 4 hours 18 minutes. Fuxing and Hexie trainsets run the route, with classes ranging from second class to business class. The line became profitable within a few years of opening — a rarity among high-speed projects — and its operating company listed on the Shanghai Stock Exchange in 2020.

Significance

The corridor it serves contains roughly a quarter of China's population and a large share of its GDP, and the railway is a flagship of China's high-speed network, the largest in the world.
