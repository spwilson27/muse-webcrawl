The greater bilby (Macrotis lagotis), commonly referred to simply as the bilby, is a long-eared, rabbit-like marsupial native to Australia. It is the only surviving member of its genus, the lesser bilby having become extinct in the 1950s. Once widespread across arid and semi-arid Australia, it now survives in scattered populations in the Northern Territory, Western Australia and Queensland.

Description and behaviour

Bilbies have soft blue-grey fur, very long ears, a pointed snout and a long black-and-white tail. They are nocturnal omnivores that dig deep spiral burrows to escape the desert heat, feeding on insects, larvae, seeds and bulbs located largely by smell and hearing. They do not need to drink water, obtaining sufficient moisture from food.

Conservation and culture

Numbers were devastated by introduced predators such as foxes and feral cats, and by competition with rabbits. Conservation programmes have reintroduced bilbies to predator-proof reserves. Since the 1990s the bilby has been promoted in Australia as a native alternative to the Easter Bunny — the "Easter Bilby".
