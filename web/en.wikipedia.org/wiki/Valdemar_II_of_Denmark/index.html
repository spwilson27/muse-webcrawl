Valdemar II of Denmark

Valdemar II (1170 – 28 March 1241), called Valdemar the Victorious, was King of Denmark from 1202 to his death. The son of Valdemar I and brother of Canute VI, he presided over Denmark at the height of its medieval power as a Baltic empire.

Reign

Before becoming king, Valdemar was Duke of Schleswig and conquered Holstein and other territories on the southern Baltic shore. As king he extended Danish rule over Pomerania, Mecklenburg, and parts of northern Germany. In 1219 he led a crusade to Estonia; according to legend, the Danish flag, the Dannebrog, fell from the sky during the Battle of Lindanise, assuring victory. The site became Reval (Tallinn), and Denmark held northern Estonia until 1346.

In 1223 Valdemar and his son were kidnapped by Count Henry of Schwerin while hunting on the island of Lyø, and were released only after heavy ransom and territorial concessions. His attempt to recover the lost German lands ended in defeat at the Battle of Bornhöved in 1227. In his later years he was a great lawgiver, associated with the Law of Jutland (Jyske Lov, 1241), and he ordered the Liber Census Daniae, a survey of royal revenues.
