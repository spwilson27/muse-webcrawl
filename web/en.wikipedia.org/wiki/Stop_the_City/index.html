Stop the City

Stop the City was a series of linked demonstrations held in the City of London, the British financial centre, in 1983 and 1984. Organised by a loose network of anarchists, peace activists, and anti-militarists — many drawn from the peace camps at Greenham Common and elsewhere — the protests aimed to disrupt the financial institutions they held responsible for funding the arms trade and nuclear weapons.

The demonstrations

The first Stop the City took place on 29 September 1983, coinciding with protests in other cities internationally. Several thousand participants blockaded streets, picketed banks and the Stock Exchange, and superglued locks and staged "die-ins." Several hundred arrests were made, but little violence occurred. A second demonstration followed on 29 March 1984, and a third in September 1984 drew fewer people amid a heavy police presence. The events were organised without formal leaders or permission, using affinity groups and leaked consensus planning — methods that anticipated later anti-globalisation protests.

Legacy

Stop the City is regarded as a forerunner of the 1990s anti-roads movement, Reclaim the Streets, the J18 Carnival Against Capitalism of 1999, and the broader alter-globalisation movement. Its mixture of direct action against finance and festive street protest strongly influenced subsequent British protest culture, including later actions targeting the City during the 2009 G20 protests and the Occupy movement.
