Multi-purpose vehicle

A multi-purpose vehicle (MPV), also known as a minivan in North America or a people carrier, is a passenger vehicle designed to maximise interior space and seating flexibility, typically with a tall, one-box or two-box body, sliding rear doors, and seats that fold or can be removed to carry cargo.

History

Early forerunners included the Volkswagen Type 2 and various station wagons. The modern MPV segment was defined in the 1980s by the Dodge Caravan/Plymouth Voyager (1983) in North America and the Renault Espace (1984) in Europe, which packaged car-like driving with van-like capacity for families.

Characteristics

MPVs usually seat six to eight passengers in three rows, with high rooflines for headroom and easy access. Compact MPVs based on small-car platforms offered similar flexibility in smaller footprints. Although SUVs and crossovers have supplanted MPVs in many markets, the type remains popular in parts of Asia and for taxi and shuttle use.

Terminology

"Minivan" is the usual North American term; European markets distinguish full-size MPVs from smaller "mini MPVs" and "compact MPVs".
