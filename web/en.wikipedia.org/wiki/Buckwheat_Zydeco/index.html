Buckwheat Zydeco was the stage name of American accordionist and zydeco musician Stanley Dural Jr. (November 14, 1947 – September 24, 2016). Together with his band, Ils Sont Partis ("They're off!" in French — the call shouted at Louisiana horse races as the horses leave the gate), he brought the Creole dance music of southwest Louisiana to international audiences and became the most commercially successful zydeco artist of his era.

Early life

Dural was born in Lafayette, Louisiana, into a French-speaking Creole farming family. He acquired the nickname "Buckwheat" as a child because his braided hair resembled the character Buckwheat from the Little Rascals films. He began his career on the Hammond organ, playing funk and soul with his band Buckwheat and the Hitchhikers, and backed touring R&B acts.

Career

In the 1970s Dural took up the accordion after working with zydeco king Clifton Chenier, forming Ils Sont Partis in 1979. In 1987 his album On a Night Like This became the first zydeco record released by a major label (Island Records) and earned a Grammy nomination. He collaborated with Eric Clapton, Keith Richards, Dwight Yoakam, Willie Nelson, and Paul Simon, among others, and appeared in films including The Big Easy.

Dural was noted for fusing traditional zydeco with rock, soul, and country influences, and for his exuberant stage presence. His children's album Choo Choo Boogaloo (1994) introduced the genre to young listeners. In 2010, his album Lay Your Burden Down won the Grammy Award for Best Zydeco or Cajun Music Album.

Death

Dural died of lung cancer on September 24, 2016, in Lafayette, at the age of 68. His son, Sir Reginald Dural, later led a successor version of the band.
