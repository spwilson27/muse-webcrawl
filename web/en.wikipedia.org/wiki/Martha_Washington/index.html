Martha Washington

Martha Washington
Born: Martha Dandridge, June 13, 1731, Chestnut Grove, Virginia
Died: May 22, 1802 (aged 70), Mount Vernon, Virginia
Spouse(s): Daniel Parke Custis (m. 1750; died 1757); George Washington (m. 1759; died 1799)
Known for: First Lady of the United States (1789–1797)

Martha Washington (née Dandridge; June 13, 1731 – May 22, 1802) was the wife of George Washington, the first president of the United States. Although the title was not coined until later in the 19th century, she is regarded as the first First Lady of the United States.

Early life and first marriage

Martha Dandridge was born at Chestnut Grove plantation in New Kent County, Virginia. In 1750 she married Daniel Parke Custis, a wealthy planter, with whom she had four children, two of whom survived childhood. Custis died in 1757, leaving Martha one of the wealthiest widows in Virginia.

Marriage to George Washington

She married George Washington on January 6, 1759, and moved to his estate at Mount Vernon. During the American Revolutionary War she spent winters at Washington's encampments, including Valley Forge, helping to sustain morale and organizing relief for soldiers. As First Lady in New York and Philadelphia she hosted weekly receptions that helped define the social rituals of the new republic.

Later life

After George Washington's death in 1799, Martha freed the enslaved people he had owned in her own right ahead of the schedule in his will. She died at Mount Vernon in 1802 and is buried beside her husband in the Washington family tomb.
