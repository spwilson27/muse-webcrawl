The Gave de Pau is a river of south-western France. It takes its name from the city of Pau, through which it flows. The word gave is a generic name given to many torrential rivers in the western Pyrenees, derived from a pre-Celtic root meaning watercourse.

Geography

The Gave de Pau rises in the Cirque de Gavarnie in the Pyrenees, close to the Spanish border, at an altitude of roughly 2,600 metres. It flows generally west-northwest through the departments of Hautes-Pyrénées, Pyrénées-Atlantiques and Landes. Among the towns on its course are Luz-Saint-Sauveur, Lourdes, Pau, Orthez and Peyrehorade.

At Peyrehorade it joins the Gave d'Oloron to form the Gaves réunis, a short tributary of the Adour. The river is about 193 kilometres (120 mi) long and drains a basin of about 2,600 square kilometres.

History and culture

The river is closely associated with Lourdes, where the Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes stands on its bank near the Massabielle grotto, site of the Marian apparitions reported by Bernadette Soubirous in 1858. The gave at Lourdes has flooded on several occasions, notably in 2012 and 2013.

The Gave de Pau is also known for whitewater sports; a slalom course near Pau has hosted international canoe slalom competitions, and the river supports populations of Atlantic salmon and trout fished by anglers along much of its length.
