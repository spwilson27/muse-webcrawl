White House Office

The White House Office is an entity within the Executive Office of the President of the United States (EOP) that comprises the president's immediate staff — the officials who work most closely with the president on a day-to-day basis in the West Wing and the Eisenhower Executive Office Building. It should not be confused with the White House itself, the building.

History

The office was created in 1939 as part of the creation of the Executive Office of the President under Franklin D. Roosevelt, following the recommendations of the Brownlow Committee, which famously concluded that "the President needs help." Its staff has grown from a handful of aides to several hundred.

Organization

The White House Office is headed by the White House chief of staff. Major subordinate units typically include:

  • Office of the Chief of Staff and Deputy Chiefs of Staff
  • Office of Communications and the Press Secretary
  • Office of Legislative Affairs
  • Office of Political Affairs and the Office of Public Engagement
  • Office of the White House Counsel
  • Office of Management and Administration, including the Oval Office Operations
  • Office of the Staff Secretary, which controls the flow of documents to the president
  • National Security Council staff and the Domestic Policy Council staff (formally separate EOP units, closely integrated)

Staff of the White House Office are appointed by the president without Senate confirmation and serve at the president's pleasure. Their salaries are reported annually to Congress; senior advisors, assistants to the president, and special assistants form the recognized tiers of seniority.
