Smörgåsbord

Smörgåsbord
Country: Sweden
Type: Buffet-style meal
Typical dishes: Pickled herring, gravlax, meatballs, crispbread
Occasions: Christmas (julbord), Midsummer, Easter

A smörgåsbord is a Swedish buffet-style meal of hot and cold dishes served on a table from which guests help themselves. The word combines smörgås (an open-faced sandwich, literally "butter goose") and bord (table). It descends from the 16th–17th-century brännvinsbord, an appetiser table of schnapps, bread, cheese, and herring served before dinner, and developed into a full meal by the 20th century.

Order of eating

Tradition dictates several trips to the table in a set order: first pickled herring and other fish with boiled potatoes; then cold fish such as gravlax and smoked salmon; then cold meats, pâtés, and salads; then warm dishes including Swedish meatballs, Janssons frestelse (a potato and anchovy gratin), sausages, and ribs; finally cheese and desserts. Aquavit and beer are the customary drinks, with singing of drinking songs (snapsvisor).

Festive variants

The Christmas version, the julbord, adds a Christmas ham, lutfisk, and rice pudding, while at Midsummer, new potatoes with dill and sour cream, and strawberries, are essential. Easter has its own påskbord featuring eggs and lamb.

International use

The smörgåsbord was introduced to a wide international audience at the 1939 New York World's Fair, where the Swedish pavilion's restaurant popularised it abroad. In English, the word has acquired a figurative meaning: any abundant, varied assortment — "a smorgasbord of options".
