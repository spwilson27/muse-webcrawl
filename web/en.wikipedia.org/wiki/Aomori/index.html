Aomori

Aomori (Japanese: 青森市, Aomori-shi) is the capital city of Aomori Prefecture, in the Tōhoku region of northern Honshu, Japan. As of recent estimates the city has a population of around 260,000–280,000, declining from a peak above 300,000. Situated on Aomori Bay at the northern tip of Honshu, the city is a major transport hub: it is the Honshu terminus of the Seikan Tunnel to Hokkaido, a historic ferry port, and the site of the annual Aomori Nebuta Matsuri, one of Japan's most famous summer festivals.

History

The area around Aomori was inhabited from ancient times; the nearby Sannai-Maruyama site, one of the largest and best-preserved Jōmon period settlements in Japan, dates back more than 5,000 years and is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2021) as part of the Jōmon Prehistoric Sites in Northern Japan.

The modern port town was founded in 1624 during the Edo period as a shipping point for the Tsugaru domain of Hirosaki. Aomori grew with the opening of ferry routes to Hokkaido and became a city in 1898. It suffered devastating air raids in July 1945 that destroyed most of the wooden city. In 1954 the ferry Tōya Maru sank in a typhoon in the Tsugaru Strait with over a thousand deaths, one of Japan's worst maritime disasters, spurring construction of the Seikan Tunnel, opened in 1988. The Tōhoku Shinkansen reached Shin-Aomori Station in 2010, and the Hokkaido Shinkansen began services through the tunnel in 2016.

Geography and climate

Aomori lies on the southern shore of Aomori Bay, backed by the Hakkōda Mountains to the south. It is famous as one of the snowiest major cities in the world, receiving on average around 6.7 metres (over 20 feet) of snow each winter from sea-effect snow off the Sea of Japan, despite relatively moderate winter temperatures for its latitude. Summers are mild and short.

Culture

The Nebuta Matsuri, held 2–7 August, draws over two million visitors who watch enormous illuminated paper floats depicting warriors and mythological figures parade through the city, accompanied by dancers chanting "Rassera! Rassera!" The Nebuta Museum Wa Rasse preserves winning floats. Aomori Prefecture is Japan's largest apple-producing region, and the city is a gateway to attractions including Lake Towada, the Oirase Gorge, and the Shirakami-Sanchi beech forests.

Transport

Shin-Aomori Station serves the Tōhoku and Hokkaido Shinkansen lines; Aomori Station serves local lines including the Ōu Main Line and the Aoimori Railway. Ferries connect the port with Hakodate and Muroran in Hokkaido, and Aomori Airport offers domestic and limited international flights.
