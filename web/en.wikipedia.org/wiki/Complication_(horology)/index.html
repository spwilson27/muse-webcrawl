Complication (horology)

In horology, a complication is any feature of a mechanical timepiece beyond the simple display of hours, minutes, and seconds. Complications increase a watch's complexity, value, and collectibility.

Types

Common complications include date displays, chronographs (stopwatches), moon-phase indicators, alarms, power-reserve indicators, and GMT/second time zone functions. Striking complications include repeaters and sonneries that sound the time.

Grand complications

Watches combining several major complications — typically a perpetual calendar, minute repeater, and split-seconds chronograph — are called "grand complications." Famous examples include Patek Philippe's Calibre 89 and Henry Graves Supercomplication.

Tourbillon

The tourbillon, patented by Abraham-Louis Breguet in 1801, is a rotating escapement cage intended to average out gravitational errors, and is often classed among prestigious complications.
