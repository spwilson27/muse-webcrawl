"Old Folks at Home", also known by its opening line "Swanee River", is a song written by Stephen Foster in 1851. Written for the minstrel troupe Christy's Minstrels, it was published by Firth, Pond & Co. of New York and became one of the most popular American songs of the 19th century. Since 1935 it has been the official state song of Florida, though its lyrics were revised in 2008.

Composition

Foster needed a river name of two syllables to fit the melody; his brother Morrison suggested the Yazoo River or the Pee Dee River, but Foster settled on the Suwannee River in Florida — a state he never visited — shortening "Suwannee" to "Swanee" for the metre. The original publication credited E. P. Christy, the minstrel troupe leader, as the author; Foster was not publicly identified as its composer until later editions. The narrator of the song is an enslaved person longing for the plantation of his youth, written in the dialect conventions of the minstrel stage.

Florida state song

Florida adopted "Old Folks at Home" as its state song in 1935. Because of the song's origins in blackface minstrelsy and its dialect lyrics, its status was long controversial. In 2008, following a statewide competition, the Florida Legislature adopted a revised version with modernized lyrics — changes championed in part by the scholar and musician revision — while also designating "Florida (Where the Sawgrass Meets the Sky)" as the state anthem. The revised lyrics replace dialect terms such as "darkeys" with neutral wording.

Legacy

The melody is among the most widely recognized of Foster's compositions and has been recorded by countless artists, quoted in cartoons and films, and used as a musical shorthand for the American South and for nostalgia itself. Its opening phrase is instantly identifiable in American popular culture.
