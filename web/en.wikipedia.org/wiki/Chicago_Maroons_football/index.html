The Chicago Maroons football team represents the University of Chicago in college football. The program was one of the early powers of the sport in the late 19th and early 20th centuries under legendary coach Amos Alonzo Stagg, winning national championships in 1905 and 1913 as a member of the Western Conference (the future Big Ten). The university abolished varsity football in 1939, but the sport was revived in 1969 at the NCAA Division III level, where the Maroons compete today.

History

Stagg era (1892–1932)

The University of Chicago hired Amos Alonzo Stagg as its athletic director and football coach in 1892, the year the university opened. Stagg built the Maroons into a national power, pioneering innovations including the huddle, the man-in-motion, the lateral pass, and numbered jerseys. Chicago won seven Western Conference championships under Stagg, and the 1905 team was named national champion after defeating Michigan 2–0 in a famous game that ended Michigan's 56-game winning streak.

End John "Jay" Berwanger, who played in the mid-1930s under coach Clark Shaughnessy, became the first winner of the Heisman Trophy in 1935.

Abolition and revival

University president Robert Maynard Hutchins, a prominent critic of big-time college athletics, abolished the football program in 1939, and Chicago withdrew from the Big Ten in 1946. Football returned as a club sport in 1963 and regained varsity status in 1969. The modern Maroons play at a new Stagg Field, built on the site where the first sustained nuclear chain reaction was achieved in 1942 as part of the Manhattan Project (Chicago Pile-1), beneath the stands of the original stadium.

Stagg Field

The original Stagg Field hosted the Maroons from 1893 until the program's abolition. The current Stagg Field, a modest facility for Division III play, opened in 2003 adjacent to the university's Ratner Athletics Center.
