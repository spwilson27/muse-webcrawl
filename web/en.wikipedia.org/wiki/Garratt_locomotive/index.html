Garratt locomotive

A Garratt locomotive is a type of articulated steam locomotive designed by the British engineer Herbert William Garratt and built predominantly by Beyer, Peacock and Company of Manchester, hence the name Beyer-Garratt. The design places the boiler and cab on a central cradle frame carried between two separate engine units, each with its own cylinders, wheels and fuel/water supplies.

Design

In the Garratt arrangement, the boiler is slung low between the two engine units, free of driving wheels beneath it. This allows a boiler of large diameter and a wide, deep firebox without the constraints of a conventional locomotive frame. The front engine unit carries a water tank and the rear unit carries fuel (coal or oil) and further water. The articulation allows the locomotive to traverse sharply curved track while developing the power of a much larger rigid-framed engine.

Use

Garratts were exported widely, especially to Africa, Australia, South America and parts of Asia, where they hauled heavy trains over steep, lightly built lines. They became the backbone of steam operations in countries such as South Africa, Rhodesia (Zimbabwe), Kenya and New South Wales. The largest Garratts were among the most powerful steam locomotives ever built. Many were retired as dieselisation proceeded from the 1960s, though some remained in industrial and heritage service, and examples have been restored for preserved railways.
