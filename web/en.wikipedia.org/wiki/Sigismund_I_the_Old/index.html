Sigismund I the Old (Polish: Zygmunt I Stary; 1467–1548) was King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1506 until his death in 1548, one of the longest-reigning Polish monarchs. His 42-year reign was a period of political stability, territorial consolidation and brilliant Renaissance culture, often regarded as part of the Polish Golden Age.

Sigismund I, king during Poland's Renaissance golden age.

Reign

The fifth son of Casimir IV Jagiellon, Sigismund succeeded his brother Alexander in 1506. He strengthened royal finances, created a standing conscription army funded by the kwarta tax on royal lands, and defeated the Teutonic Order's ambitions in the Polish–Teutonic War (1519–1521), which ended with the Treaty of Kraków (1525): the Order's Grand Master, Albert of Brandenburg, secularised Prussia and did homage to Sigismund as the first Duke of Prussia — the famous "Prussian Homage" in Kraków's market square. In 1526 he incorporated the Duchy of Mazovia into the Crown. He also fought repeated wars against Muscovy over the eastern borderlands and against Moldavia and the Tatars in the south.

His second wife, the Italian princess Bona Sforza, whom he married in 1518, brought Renaissance culture to Kraków: the Wawel Castle was rebuilt in Renaissance style, housing the magnificent Sigismund Chapel, and Bona amassed vast estates and political influence. The great Sigismund Bell, cast in 1520 and still rung at Wawel Cathedral on major occasions, bears his name. In 1529 his son was crowned Sigismund II Augustus vivente rege, an unprecedented step to secure the succession.
