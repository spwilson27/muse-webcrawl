The Nürburgring 24 Hours (German: 24-Stunden-Rennen auf dem Nürburgring) is a 24-hour annual touring car and GT endurance racing event held on the combined Nürburgring Nordschleife and Grand Prix circuits in Nürburg, Germany. Held since 1970, the event is one of the largest motor races in the world, regularly attracting fields of well over 150 cars and more than 200,000 spectators.

The race uses a variant of the Grand Prix circuit together with most of the Nordschleife, forming a lap of about 25.4 km (15.8 mi). Because of the enormous field, cars start in several groups. The event is organised by the ADAC and features a wide mix of professional factory teams in GT3 cars and amateur entries in production-based classes.

History

The first 24-hour race at the Nürburgring was held in 1970. The race has been interrupted or shortened at times by weather, notably by fog. Porsche, BMW, Audi and Mercedes-AMG are among the most successful manufacturers; Manthey Racing is among the most successful teams. The race is part of the Intercontinental GT Challenge in some years and counts toward the Nürburgring Langstrecken-Serie (NLS) championship environment.
