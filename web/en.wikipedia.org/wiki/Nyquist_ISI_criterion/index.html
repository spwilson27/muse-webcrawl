Nyquist ISI criterion

In communications, the Nyquist ISI criterion describes the conditions which, when satisfied by a communication channel (including the combined effects of the transmit and receive filters), result in no intersymbol interference (ISI). It provides a method for constructing band-limited functions to overcome the effects of intersymbol interference.

Statement

When consecutive symbols are transmitted one after another over a channel with a certain sequence of pulses, the received signal suffers from the effect of each symbol overlapping its neighbours. The criterion, derived by Harry Nyquist, states that a pulse satisfies the zero-ISI condition if its sampled values are zero at all sampling instants corresponding to other symbols, while being non-zero at its own sampling instant. Equivalently, in the frequency domain, the folded spectrum of the channel must be flat — a condition satisfied, for example, by raised-cosine filters commonly used in digital communications.
