Gorillas in the Mist is a 1988 American biographical drama film directed by Michael Apted and starring Sigourney Weaver as the primatologist Dian Fossey. It is adapted from Fossey's 1983 autobiographical book of the same name, which recounts her study of mountain gorillas in Rwanda and her campaign against poaching.

Plot

Occupational therapist Dian Fossey travels to Africa inspired by the anthropologist Louis Leakey, and establishes the Karisoke Research Center in the Virunga Mountains of Rwanda. Over years of patient observation she habituates gorilla groups and documents their behaviour, while her increasingly uncompromising opposition to poachers and to aspects of local tourism brings her into conflict. She is murdered in her cabin in 1985, a crime that remains unsolved.

Production

The film was shot on location in Rwanda and Kenya, working with habituated gorillas alongside actors in gorilla suits. Weaver learned to communicate with the animals and performed scenes with real mountain gorillas.

Reception

The film was a critical and commercial success. Weaver won a Golden Globe Award and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actress; the film received five Oscar nominations in total. It significantly raised public awareness of the plight of the mountain gorilla.
