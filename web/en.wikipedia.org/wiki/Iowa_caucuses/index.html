The Iowa caucuses are electoral events held in Iowa in which the state's Democratic and Republican parties select delegates to their national presidential nominating conventions. Held early in the election year — traditionally first in the United States — they have been an outsized focus of campaigning and media attention since the 1970s.

Process

Unlike a primary election run by the state, caucuses are party-run meetings at precinct sites across Iowa's 99 counties. Democratic caucuses historically required voters to physically group by candidate preference, with groups below a viability threshold (usually 15 percent) realigning to other candidates; Republicans vote by secret straw ballot. In 2024 Democrats moved to a mail-in preference process, while Republicans kept the traditional January caucus.

Significance and criticism

Because Iowa votes first, strong showings have launched candidacies — Jimmy Carter in 1976 and Barack Obama in 2008 — while poor results often end campaigns. Critics note that Iowa's population is less ethnically diverse than the United States as a whole and that caucus turnout is low; the delayed and chaotic 2020 Democratic results further damaged the state's first-in-the-nation status, and Democrats demoted Iowa from the opening position in their 2024 calendar. Republicans have kept Iowa first.
