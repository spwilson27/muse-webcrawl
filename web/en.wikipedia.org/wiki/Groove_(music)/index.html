Groove (music)

In music, groove is the sense of an effect ("feel") of changing pattern in a propulsive rhythm or sense of "swing". In jazz, it can be felt as a persistently repeated pattern; in funk, hip hop and dance music, groove generally refers to the interplay of rhythm section parts that compels listeners to move or dance.

Concept

Musicians use the term "in the groove" (or "in the pocket") to describe playing that feels rhythmically locked together and satisfying. Groove emerges from the alignment and small timing deviations of bass, drums and other instruments — patterns of syncopation, ghost notes, swung subdivisions and microtiming that may sit slightly ahead of or behind the metronomic beat. Genres such as funk (James Brown, P-Funk), soul, disco, hip hop and house music are built around establishing and sustaining a groove, often with minimal harmonic movement.

Groove in music production

In electronic music production and digital audio workstations, "groove" also denotes timing templates — quantization settings that shift notes by characteristic amounts derived from classic drum machines such as the Akai MPC or E-mu SP-1200 — applied to programmed parts to give them a human feel. Drummers such as Bernard Purdie, Clyde Stubblefield and Steve Gadd are celebrated for signature grooves.
