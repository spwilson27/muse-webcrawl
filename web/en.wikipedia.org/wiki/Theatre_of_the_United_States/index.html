Theatre of the United States

[Hero image placeholder: a Broadway theatre marquee in New York City.]

The theatre of the United States encompasses the tradition of drama and performance in the country, from its beginnings in the colonial era to the modern dominance of Broadway, regional theatre, and experimental companies.

History

Professional theatre in America dates to the mid-18th century, when English touring companies such as Lewis Hallam's London Company performed in the colonies. The 19th century saw the rise of melodrama, minstrelsy, vaudeville and touring "combination companies". In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, producers such as the Shubert brothers built national networks, while New York's Broadway became the commercial centre of American theatre.

Twentieth century

The 20th century produced playwrights of international stature, including Eugene O'Neill, Tennessee Williams, Arthur Miller, Edward Albee and Lorraine Hansberry, and saw the flowering of the American musical with Rodgers and Hammerstein, Stephen Sondheim and others. The mid-century regional theatre movement, along with Off-Broadway and Off-Off-Broadway, decentralised production and nurtured new work. Contemporary American theatre includes August Wilson's Century Cycle, Tony Kushner's Angels in America, and the hip-hop musical Hamilton.
