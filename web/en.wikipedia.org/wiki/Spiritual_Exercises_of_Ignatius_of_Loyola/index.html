The Spiritual Exercises (Latin: Exercitia spiritualia) is a set of Christian meditations, contemplations and prayers composed by Ignatius of Loyola, the Basque priest who founded the Society of Jesus (Jesuits). Written first from Ignatius's own conversion experience during his convalescence and retreat at Manresa in 1522–1523, the work was refined over the next two decades and first published in Rome in 1548. It forms the foundational spiritual text of Ignatian spirituality and remains the basis of retreats given worldwide.

Structure

The book is not meant to be read straight through but used as a director's manual: it gives instructions for a retreat master ("the one who gives the Exercises") guiding a retreatant through about thirty days of prayer, divided into four flexible "weeks":

First Week — reflection on sin, its effects, and God's mercy, beginning from the "Principle and Foundation": that human beings are created to praise, reverence and serve God. Second Week — contemplation of the life of Christ from the Incarnation to the Passion, framed by the call to follow Christ and the "Two Standards" meditation on the opposing camps of Christ and Satan. Third Week — contemplation of the Passion of Christ. Fourth Week — contemplation of the Resurrection and the love of God, concluding with the "Contemplation to Attain Love."

Method

Distinctive features include "composition of place" — imaginatively placing oneself within a Gospel scene — and repeated application of the senses to the scene contemplated; the daily examen, a prayerful review of the day; and the "discernment of spirits," a set of rules for interpreting consolation and desolation in prayer. Central to the Exercises is the "election": a considered decision about one's state of life or reform of life, made in prayer. Ignatius insists the director should remain unobtrusive, "permitting the Creator to deal directly with the creature."

Influence and adaptations

The Exercises became a defining instrument of Catholic renewal in the Counter-Reformation and the standard retreat for Jesuit formation. Because a thirty-day silent retreat is impractical for most people, Ignatius himself provided for adaptations — including the "Exercises in daily life" (the 19th Annotation), in which the retreatant prays over an extended period while continuing ordinary work; this form has become widespread since the late twentieth century. The Exercises have also attracted interest beyond Catholicism among other Christian traditions and spiritual directors.
