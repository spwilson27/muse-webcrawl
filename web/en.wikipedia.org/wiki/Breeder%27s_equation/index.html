Breeder's equation

The breeder's equation is a fundamental equation in quantitative genetics that predicts the response of a trait to selection. It states that the change in the mean value of a trait in the next generation, R, equals the product of the trait's narrow-sense heritability, h², and the selection differential, S: R = h²S.

Terms

The selection differential S is the difference between the mean trait value of the selected parents and the mean of the whole population. Heritability h² is the proportion of phenotypic variance attributable to additive genetic variance.

Use

The equation is a cornerstone of animal and plant breeding, used to forecast gains from selective breeding programmes, and it underlies much of evolutionary quantitative genetics. Extensions, including multivariate versions using the G-matrix, predict correlated responses among multiple traits.
