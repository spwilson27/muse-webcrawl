Sheepshead (card game)

Sheepshead is a trick-taking card game, usually played with five players, that originated from the German game Schafkopf. It is most closely associated with Wisconsin — where it is played extensively in Milwaukee and eastern Wisconsin and is sometimes called the unofficial state card game — and with German-American communities in the Upper Midwest. Sheepshead is played with a shortened 32-card deck, using the 7s through aces.

Basics

Fourteen cards are permanent trumps: all four queens (the highest trumps), all four jacks, and all six diamonds, ranked queen of clubs down through the 7 of diamonds. The remaining cards of clubs, spades, and hearts are "fail" suits. Card points total 120 per deal: aces 11, tens 10, kings 4, queens 3, jacks 2, and all other cards 0.

Each player receives six cards, with two cards placed face down as the "blind". The player who picks up the blind becomes the picker and (in the standard partner variant) calls an ace of a fail suit; the holder of that ace is secretly the picker's partner. The picker buries two cards. The picker's team must capture at least 61 points; defenders need 60. If the picker's side takes all tricks ("schneider"/"schwarz" bonuses) or fails, scores are adjusted accordingly. Players passed over in calling may also play variations such as a "leaster" (winning with the fewest points when no one picks) or a "doubler".

Variations

Regional rules vary: four-player and three-player versions, Jack-of-diamonds partner instead of called ace, "crack" and "recrack" declarations that raise stakes, and six-player games with modified trump counts are all common. A standard phrase of Wisconsin play is "Sheepshead: it's a game of skill".
