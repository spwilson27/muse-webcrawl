International Force East Timor

Active20 September 1999 – 28 February 2000
Lead nationAustralia
CommanderMajor General Peter Cosgrove
Authorised byUN Security Council Resolution 1264

The International Force East Timor (INTERFET) was a multinational peacekeeping task force, mandated by the United Nations and led by Australia, deployed to East Timor from 20 September 1999 until 28 February 2000 to address the humanitarian and security crisis that followed the territory's independence referendum.

Background and deployment

In the referendum of 30 August 1999, supervised by UNAMET, East Timorese voters overwhelmingly chose independence from Indonesia. Pro-Indonesian militias, with support from elements of the Indonesian military, responded with a campaign of violence, killing an estimated thousand or more people, displacing hundreds of thousands and destroying most infrastructure. Under international pressure Indonesia accepted a peacekeeping force; the Security Council authorised INTERFET under Chapter VII in Resolution 1264 on 15 September 1999.

Operations

At its peak the force numbered about 11,500 personnel from 22 countries, commanded by Australian Major General Peter Cosgrove. It restored security, enabling humanitarian relief and the return of refugees, and in February 2000 handed responsibility to the UNTAET peacekeeping force, which administered East Timor until independence in May 2002.
