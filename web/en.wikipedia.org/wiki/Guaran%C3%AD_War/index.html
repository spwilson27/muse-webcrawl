Guaraní War

Date: 1754–1756

Location: Jesuit missions, present-day Paraguay, Argentina and Brazil

Combatants: Guaraní of the missions vs Spain and Portugal

The Guaraní War (Spanish: Guerra Guaranítica) of 1754–1756 was an armed resistance by the Guaraní people of the seven Jesuit reductions (Sete Povos das Missões) east of the Uruguay River against Spanish and Portuguese forces enforcing the Treaty of Madrid (1750).

Background

The Treaty of Madrid transferred the territory of the seven missions from Spain to Portugal in exchange for Colonia del Sacramento, and ordered some 30,000 Guaraní to abandon their towns and resettle west of the Uruguay. The Guaraní, led by figures such as Sepé Tiaraju, refused to leave lands they had farmed for generations.

War and aftermath

Joint Spanish-Portuguese expeditions defeated the Guaraní, most bloodily at the Battle of Caiboaté in February 1756, where around 1,500 Guaraní were killed. The missions were emptied, and the Treaty of Madrid was annulled in 1761; the episode later inspired the film The Mission (1986).
