Romanian New Wave

The Romanian New Wave (Romanian: Noul val românesc) is a genre of realist and often minimalist films made in Romania beginning with the mid-2000s. The movement won widespread international acclaim at film festivals and is characterised by austere, long-take cinematography, deadpan black humour, and stories drawn from the late Communist period and the difficult transition that followed.

Key films and directors

The wave is usually dated from Cristi Puiu's The Death of Mr. Lazarescu (2005), which won the Un Certain Regard prize at Cannes. Cristian Mungiu's 4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days (2007) won the Palme d'Or, the first Romanian film to do so. Other major figures include Corneliu Porumboiu (12:08 East of Bucharest, Police, Adjective), Radu Muntean, Radu Jude, and Călin Peter Netzer, whose Child's Pose won the Golden Bear at Berlin in 2013.

Themes

The films frequently examine the moral compromises of life under the Ceaușescu regime, the dysfunction of post-communist institutions such as hospitals and police, and everyday ethical dilemmas faced by ordinary people, all presented with minimal music and an unadorned visual style.
