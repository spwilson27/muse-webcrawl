Joe Byrne

Joseph Byrne (21 November 1856 – 28 June 1880) was an Australian bushranger, best known as Ned Kelly's closest friend and second-in-command of the Kelly gang. Literate and intelligent, Byrne is credited with drafting the famous Jerilderie Letter and designing much of the gang's armour.

Byrne, photographed before the gang's outlawry.

Early life

Byrne was born at Woolshed, near Beechworth in Victoria, the son of Irish immigrants. He was educated and fluent in Cantonese, having grown up around Chinese gold miners, and formed a close friendship with Aaron Sherritt. After convictions for stock theft and brushes with the police, Byrne joined Kelly's circle and was present when the gang killed three policemen at Stringybark Creek in October 1878, which led to their being declared outlaws.

The Kelly gang

Byrne took part in the gang's bank robberies at Euroa and Jerilderie; at Jerilderie he dictated assistance for Kelly's manifesto letter. He is generally credited with the idea of forging suits of armour from plough mouldboards. In June 1880 the gang murdered Sherritt, Byrne's former friend who had become a police informer, to provoke a confrontation. At the siege of Glenrowan on 28 June 1880, Byrne, wearing armour without leg protection, was shot in the groin while drinking at the bar of the Glenrowan Inn and bled to death; his body was later photographed strung up outside the Benalla police station.

Legacy

Byrne is remembered in Australian folklore as the brains of the Kelly gang — the letter-writer, strategist and armourer — and appears in numerous films and books about Ned Kelly.
