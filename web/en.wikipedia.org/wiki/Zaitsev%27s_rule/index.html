Zaitsev's rule

Named after: Alexander Mikhaylovich Zaitsev

Field: Organic chemistry (elimination reactions)

Zaitsev's rule (also spelled Saytzeff's rule) is an empirical rule in organic chemistry, formulated by Russian chemist Alexander Zaitsev in 1875, for predicting the regioselectivity of elimination reactions. In a dehydrohalogenation of an alkyl halide, the hydrogen is preferentially removed from the neighbouring carbon that has the fewest hydrogens, so that the major alkene product is the most highly substituted — and generally the most stable — alkene.

Explanation

The preference arises because more substituted alkenes are thermodynamically more stable, owing to hyperconjugation and the electron-donating effect of alkyl groups on the double bond. With bulky bases, steric hindrance can override the rule, giving the less substituted Hofmann product instead.
