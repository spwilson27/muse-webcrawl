1968 Mexican movement

The 1968 Mexican movement (Movimiento de 1968) was a broad social and student movement in Mexico that challenged the authoritarian rule of the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) under President Gustavo Díaz Ordaz. It culminated in the Tlatelolco massacre of 2 October 1968, ten days before the Mexico City Olympic Games.

Course of the movement

Beginning in July 1968 after police repression of student clashes in Mexico City, students formed the National Strike Council (CNH), occupied campuses, and issued demands including the release of political prisoners, abolition of the riot police, and democratic reform. The movement drew in workers, teachers, and professionals, and held mass marches through the capital.

Tlatelolco massacre

On 2 October, soldiers and snipers opened fire on a peaceful rally in the Plaza de las Tres Culturas at Tlatelolco. The death toll remains disputed — officially a few dozen, but widely estimated in the hundreds. Hundreds more were arrested.

Legacy

The movement and massacre became a watershed in modern Mexican history, eroding the PRI's legitimacy and seeding later democratisation. Official acknowledgement and investigations came only decades later.
