The Gloriosa, full name Maria Gloriosa, is a bell in the central tower of Erfurt Cathedral in Thuringia, Germany. Cast in 1497 by the Dutch bell-founder Geert van Wou of Kampen, it is regarded as one of the most beautifully toned medieval bells in existence and is the largest free-swinging medieval bell in the world.

Description

The bell weighs 11,450 kg, is 2.56 metres in diameter and approximately 2.5 metres high, and sounds the note E. Its casting on the night of 7–8 July 1497, in the churchyard beside the cathedral, was a celebrated technical achievement of its time; van Wou is considered the foremost bell-founder of the late Middle Ages, and the Gloriosa is his masterpiece. The bell bears Latin inscriptions praising the Virgin Mary and naming its founder.

History

The Gloriosa is rung only on high feast days and special occasions, by hand, traditionally by a team of ringers. A crack was discovered in 1984; after years of silence the bell was lowered in 2004, repaired by welding at the Lachenmeyer workshop in Nördlingen, and returned to the tower in October 2004, where it was rung again on Christmas Eve that year.
