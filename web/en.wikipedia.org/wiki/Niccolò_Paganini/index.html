Niccolò Paganini (27 October 1782 – 27 May 1840) was an Italian violinist and composer. He was the most celebrated violin virtuoso of his time, and left his mark as one of the pillars of modern violin technique. His 24 Caprices for Solo Violin, Op. 1, are among the best known of his compositions and have served as a major source of inspiration for later composers.

Biography

Paganini was born in Genoa, the third of six children of Antonio and Teresa Paganini. He was taught the mandolin by his father at age five and began learning violin at seven, giving his first public performance at eleven. After studies in Parma, he embarked on a touring career that eventually made him famous throughout Europe.

Career and legend

Paganini's extraordinary technique — including flying staccato, double stops, left-hand pizzicato, and scordatura tuning — together with his tall, gaunt appearance, fed rumours that he had made a pact with the Devil. Audiences sometimes demanded he repeat passages to prove there was no trickery. His concert tours of the 1820s and 1830s made him wealthy and internationally celebrated; Franz Liszt attended a Paganini concert in 1832 and was inspired to become the Paganini of the piano.

Instruments

His favourite instrument was the 1743 Guarneri del Gesù violin known as Il Cannone ("The Cannon") for its powerful tone. He bequeathed it to the city of Genoa, where it is kept at the Palazzo Tursi and played only on rare occasions.

Compositions

Paganini's works include six violin concertos, the 24 Caprices, numerous sonatas, quartets with guitar, and variations — famously on "La Campanella" and on Rossini themes. The famous theme of the 24th Caprice has been used for variations by Liszt, Brahms, Rachmaninoff and many others.

Death and legacy

Plagued by ill health, possibly worsened by mercury treatment for syphilis, Paganini died in Nice in 1840. Because of the devil legends, the Church initially refused him burial in consecrated ground; his remains were moved several times before final resting in Parma in 1876. The Premio Paganini international violin competition, held in Genoa, is named in his honour.
