Vasuki

Vasuki (Sanskrit: वासुकि, romanized: Vāsuki) is the king of the Nāgas in Hindu and Buddhist mythology. He is described as having a gem called Nagamani ("serpent jewel") on his head. Vasuki is the son of the sage Kashyapa and Kadru, and the brother of the serpent Shesha and of Manasa, the goddess of snakes.

In Hindu mythology

Vasuki is most famous for his role in the Samudra Manthana, the churning of the Ocean of Milk. The devas and asuras used Vasuki as the rope wound around Mount Mandara, pulling him back and forth to churn the ocean and produce the nectar of immortality (amrita). The poison halahala that emerged during the churning was swallowed by the god Shiva to save the world; in some versions, Vasuki is the serpent worn around Shiva's neck.

Vasuki also appears in the Mahabharata and the Puranas. His sister Jaratkāru was married to the sage Jaratkaru to save the serpent race from King Janamejaya's snake sacrifice, and their son Astika persuaded the king to halt the sacrifice.

In Buddhism and Jainism

In Buddhist tradition Vasuki is one of the great nāga kings who attended the Buddha's sermons and protected the Dharma. In Jainism, Vasuki is associated with the Tirthankara Parshvanatha, whose serpent hood is formed by nāgas. The serpent king also entered Chinese and Japanese mythology as one of the eight great dragon kings.
