Black Rock Desert

LocationNorthern Nevada, United States
AreaAbout 1,000 sq mi (playa and surroundings)
Known forBurning Man; land speed records

The Black Rock Desert is a semi-arid region of northern Nevada, part of the Great Basin. Its centrepiece is a vast flat playa — the dry bed of prehistoric Lake Lahontan — which is one of the largest flat surfaces on Earth.

Geography and history

The desert takes its name from Black Rock Point, a dark volcanic outcrop at the southern end of the Black Rock Range. Emigrant trails to California and Oregon crossed the playa in the nineteenth century, and wagon ruts and emigrant inscriptions survive. The region is now largely protected within the Black Rock Desert–High Rock Canyon Emigrant Trails National Conservation Area.

Uses

The playa hosts the annual Burning Man festival, which builds the temporary Black Rock City. Its extreme flatness has also made it a site for land speed attempts: the world land speed record of 763 mph was set there by Thrust SSC in 1997. Amateur rocketry launches are held on the playa as well.
