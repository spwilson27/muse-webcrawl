Bradwell-on-Sea is a village and civil parish in Essex, England, on the Dengie peninsula at the mouth of the River Blackwater. It lies about 9 miles (14 km) north-east of Southminster and is the site of the Saxon Shore fort of Othona and one of the oldest churches in England, the Chapel of St Peter-on-the-Wall. In the 20th century the parish became known for Bradwell nuclear power station, one of Britain's first generation of Magnox stations, which operated from 1962 to 2002 and has been in decommissioning since.

History

The Romans built a fort here—known as Othona—in the 3rd century AD as part of the Saxon Shore defences guarding the estuary. After the Roman withdrawal the site became the landing place of St Cedd, a missionary from Lindisfarne, who in 654 founded a monastery and built the chapel of St Peter-on-the-Wall from the stone of the ruined fort. The little church, still standing on the sea wall, is one of the oldest substantially intact churches in England and remains a place of pilgrimage, associated with the nearby Othona Community, an ecumenical retreat community founded in 1946.

The parish church of St Thomas stands inland in the village. For centuries the local economy rested on farming the marshland, oyster fishing in the Blackwater, and coastal trade from Bradwell Quay. The writer and broadcaster about farming in the parish, and the Dengie marshes around Bradwell, remain noted for their wild, open landscape and birdlife.

Bradwell power station

Bradwell nuclear power station, built on the site of the former RAF Bradwell Bay airfield, was one of the first commercial nuclear power stations in the United Kingdom. Its two Magnox reactors generated electricity from 1962 until closure in 2002. The station has since been defuelled and placed into a long "care and maintenance" phase of decommissioning; in 2019 its turbine hall was demolished. The Bradwell site has been identified in government policy as potentially suitable for new nuclear development (sometimes designated Bradwell B), a proposal that has been the subject of long-running local and national debate.

Geography

Bradwell-on-Sea sits at the eastern end of the Dengie peninsula, bounded by the North Sea and the Blackwater estuary. The coastline is one of saltmarsh, mudflats, and sea walls, internationally important for overwintering wildfowl and waders and protected as part of the Dengie Special Protection Area. At low tide the Buxey Sands extend far offshore. The village is connected by minor roads to Southminster, Tillingham, and Burnham-on-Crouch.
