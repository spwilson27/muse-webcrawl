Flint glass

Flint glass is optical glass that has a relatively high refractive index and low Abbe number (high dispersion). Flint glasses have refractive indices ranging between 1.45 and 2.00 and are conventionally defined as having Abbe numbers below 50. In modern classification, flint glasses are those with Abbe numbers below 55 or so, depending on the convention used.

Composition and history

Originally, flint glass was made by the English with calcined flints as a source of silica. Around 1675, glassmaker George Ravenscroft developed lead crystal glass by adding lead oxide, which improved working properties and brilliance. Lead flint glass became prized for tableware as well as optics; modern optical flints may use oxides of titanium, zirconium or other elements to replace some or all of the lead.

Applications

Because of its high dispersion, flint glass is paired with crown glass in achromatic doublets to correct chromatic aberration in lenses and telescope objectives. Dense flint glasses are also used in prisms and high-index optical components.
