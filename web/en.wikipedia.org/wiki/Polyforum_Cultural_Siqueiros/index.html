Polyforum Cultural Siqueiros

The Polyforum in the Benito Juárez borough of Mexico City
LocationAvenida de los Insurgentes Sur, Mexico City, Mexico
Inaugurated15 December 1971
ArtistDavid Alfaro Siqueiros
Key workThe March of Humanity

The Polyforum Cultural Siqueiros is a cultural centre in Mexico City, located next to the World Trade Center Mexico City on Avenida de los Insurgentes Sur. The building is covered inside and out with murals by David Alfaro Siqueiros, one of the three great Mexican muralists, and houses what is often described as the largest mural in the world, The March of Humanity (La Marcha de la Humanidad).

History

The project was commissioned by businessman Manuel Suárez y Suárez as the centrepiece of a planned hotel and cultural complex. Siqueiros, then in his seventies, began work in the mid-1960s with a team of artists and developed the concept of "polyforum"—a twelve-sided structure whose every surface, including the exterior walls and the interior of the great hall on the top floor, would be integrated into a single sculptural-pictorial work. The centre was inaugurated on 15 December 1971 by President Luis Echeverría.

The March of Humanity

The mural, executed with acrylics and sculptural elements over roughly 8,700 square metres in total between the interior and exterior surfaces, narrates the history and future of humankind: the struggles of peoples against oppression, the march of the bourgeois-democratic revolution, and a vision of liberation and the conquest of the cosmos. The exterior panels depict themes such as leadership, the tree of life, and the atom. The Polyforum contains a theatre, exhibition spaces, and the great hall where the main mural is viewed from a rotating platform accompanied by a recorded commentary based on texts by Siqueiros.
