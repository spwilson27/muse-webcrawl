Anteaters are the four extant mammal species in the suborder Vermilingua ("worm tongue"), within the order Pilosa, known for eating ants and termites. The individual species are the giant anteater (Myrmecophaga tridactyla), the silky anteater (Cyclopes didactylus) and the southern and northern tamanduas (Tamandua species). Together with the sloths, they form the superorder Xenarthra.

Anatomy and feeding

Anteaters have elongated snouts, no teeth, very long sticky tongues — up to 60 cm in the giant anteater — and powerful foreclaws for tearing open insect nests. A giant anteater may lick up tens of thousands of ants a day, feeding briefly at each nest so the colony survives. Anteaters have unusually low body temperatures for mammals, around 32–34 °C, reflecting their low-calorie diet.

Distribution and behaviour

Anteaters are native to Central and South America. The giant anteater is terrestrial and largely solitary; tamanduas are semi-arboreal; and the silky anteater is nocturnal and almost entirely arboreal. Females carry a single pup, often riding on the mother's back. Giant anteaters are listed as vulnerable, threatened mainly by habitat loss and road kills.
