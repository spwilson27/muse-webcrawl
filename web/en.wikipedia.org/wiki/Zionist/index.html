Zionist

Zionist is a term denoting a person who supports Zionism, the movement for the self-determination and statehood of the Jewish people in their ancestral homeland, the Land of Israel. The term derives from "Zion", a hill in Jerusalem and a traditional synonym for the land itself. Since the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948, the term has most commonly described supporters of Israel's continued existence as a Jewish state, although Zionists differ widely in their political, religious and cultural outlooks.

Theodor Herzl (1860–1904), founder of modern political Zionism, photographed at the Zionist Congress in Basel.

History of the term

The word "Zionism" was coined around 1890, commonly attributed to the Austrian writer Nathan Birnbaum, and was popularised by Theodor Herzl, whose 1896 pamphlet Der Judenstaat ("The Jewish State") argued that Jewish emancipation in Europe had failed and that a sovereign Jewish state was necessary. Herzl convened the First Zionist Congress in Basel, Switzerland, in 1897, which founded the World Zionist Organization. A "Zionist" thereafter meant a member or supporter of that movement and its programme of securing a publicly recognised, legally secured home for the Jewish people in Palestine.

Varieties

Zionists have never formed a single ideological bloc. Major currents include:

  • Political Zionism — Herzl's diplomatic approach, seeking international legal guarantees for a Jewish state.
  • Labour Zionism — associated with figures such as David Ben-Gurion; combined national revival with socialist settlement, the kibbutz movement and the Histadrut labour federation.
  • Revisionist Zionism — founded by Ze'ev Jabotinsky, emphasising Jewish military strength and claiming both banks of the Jordan River.
  • Religious Zionism — rooted in the teachings of Rabbi Abraham Isaac Kook, viewing return to the land as part of a divine redemptive process.
  • Cultural Zionism — championed by Ahad Ha'am, prioritising a Hebrew cultural and spiritual centre over immediate statehood.

After 1948

With the founding of Israel, the practical focus of Zionists shifted to supporting the state: encouraging Jewish immigration (aliyah), fundraising, political advocacy and strengthening ties between Israel and the Jewish diaspora. Surveys and communal usage generally treat belief in Israel's right to exist as a Jewish state as the core criterion of being a Zionist. The term is also used critically by opponents of Israel, and its pejorative use in political polemic — particularly since the 1970s — remains a subject of controversy.
