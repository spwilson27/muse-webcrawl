The Way of a Pilgrim

The Way of a Pilgrim (Russian: Откровенные рассказы странника духовному своему отцу, "Candid tales of a pilgrim to his spiritual father") is a 19th-century Russian Orthodox devotional work by an unknown author. First published in Kazan in 1884, it describes a wandering pilgrim's quest to learn how to pray without ceasing.

Content

The narrator, a poor peasant wanderer in his thirties who has lost his wife and home, hears the Apostle Paul's injunction to "pray without ceasing" (1 Thessalonians 5:17) and travels across Russia seeking someone who can teach him how this is possible. A starets (spiritual elder) introduces him to the Philokalia and instructs him in the Jesus Prayer — "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner" — repeated in rhythm with the breath and heartbeat until it becomes, in the hesychast tradition, self-acting within the heart. Carrying only dry bread, a Bible and the Philokalia, the pilgrim journeys to monasteries and villages, recounting his experiences and conversations about prayer. Later sequels describe further travels and teachings.

Influence

Translated into English in 1930 by R. M. French, the book became one of the most widely read introductions to Eastern Orthodox spirituality in the West and greatly popularised the Jesus Prayer. It features prominently in J. D. Salinger's Franny and Zooey (1961), where Franny Glass carries and obsessively discusses the book.
