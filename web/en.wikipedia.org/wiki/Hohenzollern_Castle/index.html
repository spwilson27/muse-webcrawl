Hohenzollern Castle (German: Burg Hohenzollern [buʁk hoːənˈtsɔlɐn]) is the ancestral seat of the imperial House of Hohenzollern. The third of three hilltop castles built on the site, it is located atop Mount Hohenzollern, above and south of Hechingen, on the edge of the Swabian Jura of central Baden-Württemberg, Germany.

History

The first castle on the mountain was built in the early 11th century and was completely destroyed in 1423 after a ten-month siege by the free imperial cities of Swabia. A larger and sturdier second castle was built from 1454 to 1461 and served as a refuge for the Catholic Swabian Hohenzollerns during wartime, including the Thirty Years' War. By the end of the 18th century, however, it had lost its military significance and fell into disrepair.

The third, present-day castle was built for King Frederick William IV of Prussia between 1850 and 1867 under the direction of the architect Friedrich August Stüler, in an English Gothic Revival style inspired by the châteaux of the Loire Valley. The castle was intended as a dynastic monument and family seat rather than a working royal residence; no king regularly lived there, although Crown Prince Wilhelm, the last Prussian crown prince, lived there during the final months of the Second World War after fleeing Potsdam.

Collections

The castle houses significant historical artefacts, including the Crown of Wilhelm II, some of the personal effects of Frederick the Great (including his flute and his uniform with the snuff box that famously saved his life at the Battle of Kunersdorf), and a letter from US President George Washington thanking the Hohenzollern-descended Baron von Steuben for his service in the American Revolutionary War. The Prussian royal vault in the castle was the resting place of the remains of Frederick William I and Frederick the Great from 1952 until their reburial at Potsdam in 1991.

Today the castle remains privately owned by the House of Hohenzollern and is one of Germany's most visited castles, receiving well over 300,000 visitors a year.
