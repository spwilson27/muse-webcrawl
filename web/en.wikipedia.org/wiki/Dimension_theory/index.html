Dimension theory

Dimension theory is the branch of general topology devoted to assigning a natural number — the dimension — to topological spaces in a way that accords with intuition for Euclidean spaces. A space has dimension n if, roughly, n coordinates are needed to describe points in it locally.

Definitions

The main classical notions are the small inductive dimension (ind), the large inductive dimension (Ind), and the Lebesgue covering dimension (dim). For separable metric spaces the three coincide, and the covering dimension of Euclidean space Rn is exactly n. The theory was developed in the early twentieth century by Brouwer, Menger, Urysohn, and others, including the invariance of dimension theorem showing Rm and Rn are not homeomorphic for m ≠ n.

Related notions

Other dimension concepts include the Hausdorff dimension for fractals, the Krull dimension in algebra, and dimensions of vector spaces and manifolds. For the algebraic theory see the dimension theory of commutative rings.
