Solar panels on spacecraft


Solar panels on spacecraft supply electrical power for most satellites and many space probes.

Spacecraft operating in the inner Solar System usually rely on solar panels to generate electricity from sunlight. Photovoltaic arrays are the primary power source for Earth-orbiting satellites and for probes travelling to Venus, Earth, Mars, and, with large specialised arrays, even Jupiter.

History

The first satellite to use solar cells was Vanguard 1, launched in 1958, whose small array powered a transmitter for years. Solar power became standard for communications satellites through the 1960s, and array sizes grew from tens of watts to tens of kilowatts.

Design

Modern arrays use high-efficiency multi-junction cells, often gallium arsenide based, mounted on deployable wings that fold for launch and unfold on orbit. The International Space Station's arrays and later roll-out designs such as ROSA produce over a hundred kilowatts in total. For missions far from the Sun, where sunlight is weak, radioisotope thermoelectric generators are used instead.
