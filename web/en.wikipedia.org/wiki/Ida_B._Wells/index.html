Ida B. Wells

Ida B. Wells
Born16 July 1862, Holly Springs, Mississippi
Died25 March 1931, Chicago
OccupationJournalist and activist
[Image: A portrait of Wells in the 1890s]

Ida B. Wells (1862–1931) was an American investigative journalist, editor and civil-rights leader born into slavery in Mississippi, who became the foremost anti-lynching crusader of her era: her pamphlets Southern Horrors (1892) and The Red Record (1895) documented lynching's true patterns with data, after a Memphis mob destroyed her newspaper's offices.

Legacy

Wells co-founded the NAACP, fought for women's suffrage, and was posthumously awarded a Pulitzer Prize special citation in 2020 for her investigative reporting on lynching.
