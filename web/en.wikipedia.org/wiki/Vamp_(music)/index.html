Vamp (music)

In music, a vamp is a repeating chord progression, short passage or riff that serves as an introduction, accompaniment or holding pattern, typically repeated until a cue signals the music to move on. The term derives from the theatrical instruction to improvise filler — related to "vamping" as padding or patching — so that a performer can enter, speak, or prepare. A vamp section is often notated as "vamp till ready" in theatre scores.

Uses

In musical theatre, vamps cover entrances, scene changes and dialogue, and underscore songs while singers wait to begin; the famous repeating figure under "Cell Block Tango" numbers and underscoring loops in pit orchestras are standard examples. In jazz, a vamp is a short repeated progression — often two chords, such as a modal i–♭VII oscillation — used as an intro, an interlude or an open-ended soloing backdrop; John Coltrane's "My Favorite Things" and much modal jazz rest on vamp structures. Gospel, funk, soul and R&B use vamps extensively for extended endings ("vamping out") while a singer improvises over the top.

Related concepts

The vamp is closely related to the ostinato and to the loop in electronic music, and overlaps with the rock "groove" or "jam" section. Composers such as Steve Reich built minimalist textures from vamp-like repetition, while in popular music the two-chord vamp underlies countless songs. A "safety" vamp in live performance can be cut at a conductor's signal, making it a flexible tool for coping with unpredictable stage timing.
