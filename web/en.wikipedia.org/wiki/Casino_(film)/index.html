Casino is a 1995 American epic crime film directed by Martin Scorsese, adapted by Scorsese and Nicholas Pileggi from Pileggi's non-fiction book Casino: Love and Honor in Las Vegas. It stars Robert De Niro as Sam "Ace" Rothstein, a gambling expert recruited by the Chicago Outfit to run the Tangiers casino in Las Vegas.

Plot

Rothstein runs the Tangiers profitably while the mob skims cash from the count room. His childhood friend Nicky Santoro (Joe Pesci) arrives as an enforcer and becomes a violent liability, while Rothstein's marriage to hustler Ginger McKenna (Sharon Stone) disintegrates amid addiction and betrayal. Federal investigations, Nicky's rampage, and Ginger's decline bring the mob's Las Vegas empire down; Nicky and his brother are beaten and buried alive in an Indiana cornfield, and Rothstein ends his career as an elderly sports handicapper in San Diego after surviving a car bombing.

Reception

Sharon Stone won the Golden Globe for Best Actress and was nominated for the Academy Award. The film is noted for its extensive voice-over narration, period soundtrack, and detailed depiction of casino operations and the skim. It is the third collaboration between Scorsese and Pileggi after Goodfellas and is loosely based on the life of Frank "Lefty" Rosenthal.
