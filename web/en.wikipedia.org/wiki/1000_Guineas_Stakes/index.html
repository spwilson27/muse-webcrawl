1000 Guineas Stakes

The 1000 Guineas Stakes is a Group 1 flat horse race in Great Britain open to three-year-old fillies. It is run on the Rowley Mile at Newmarket over a distance of 1 mile (1,609 metres), and it is scheduled to take place each year in late April or early May on the Sunday following the 2000 Guineas Stakes.

History

The 1000 Guineas was first run on 28 April 1814, five years after the inaugural running of its counterpart for colts, the 2000 Guineas. The two races were established by the Jockey Club under the direction of Sir Charles Bunbury. Both races were named after their original prize fund of 1,000 guineas. The race became the first of the five English Classics for fillies and the opening leg of the Fillies' Triple Crown, followed by the Oaks and the St Leger.

Records

Leading jockey honours in the race are shared among riders of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, while the great trainer Robert Robson saddled early multiple winners. In the modern era, notable winners have included Oh So Sharp (1985), Salsabil (1990), Russian Rhythm (2003), Special Duty (2010), Minding (2016) and Love (2020). Fillies that complete the 1000 Guineas–Oaks double are regarded among the finest of their generation.

The Classics

Together with the 2000 Guineas, the Derby, the Oaks and the St Leger, the 1000 Guineas forms the set of five British Classic races that define the championship season for three-year-old Thoroughbreds. It is staged during Newmarket's Guineas Festival.
