Hypnotherapy

Hypnotherapy, also known as hypnotic medicine, is the use of hypnosis in psychotherapy. A hypnotherapist guides a client into a trance-like state of focused attention and heightened suggestibility, then uses therapeutic suggestions and techniques aimed at treating psychological or physical conditions.

Approaches

Approaches include suggestion therapy, analytical hypnotherapy, cognitive-behavioural hypnotherapy, and Ericksonian hypnotherapy, named after Milton H. Erickson. Gut-directed hypnotherapy is a specialised protocol used for irritable bowel syndrome.

Evidence and regulation

Evidence supports hypnotherapy for some applications, such as pain management and IBS, while claims for many other uses remain contested. Regulation varies widely by country; hypnotherapists may be licensed health professionals or lay practitioners. Hypnotherapy is distinct from stage hypnosis performed for entertainment.
