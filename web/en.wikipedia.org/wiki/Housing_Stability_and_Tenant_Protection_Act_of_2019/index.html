Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act of 2019

The Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (HSTPA) is a New York State law that strengthened rent regulation and tenant protections for roughly one million rent-stabilized apartments, principally in New York City. Signed by Governor Andrew Cuomo on June 14, 2019, it was the most sweeping overhaul of New York's rent laws in decades and, unlike previous renewals of the rent laws, was made effectively permanent.

Key provisions

The Act repealed vacancy decontrol, under which apartments left the stabilization system when rents crossed a threshold, and ended the "vacancy bonus" that had allowed large increases between tenancies. It sharply limited surcharges for Major Capital Improvements and Individual Apartment Improvements, capped annual preferential-rent increases, restricted the use of rent arrears and eviction practices, limited security deposits to one month's rent statewide, and extended new protections against retaliatory eviction and unlawful lockouts. It also allowed municipalities elsewhere in New York State to opt into rent stabilization under the Emergency Tenant Protection Act.

Background and reaction

The law followed the 2018 elections, in which Democrats won full control of the state government and tenant advocates campaigned for "universal rent control". Landlord groups and the real-estate industry argued the Act discouraged maintenance and investment and challenged it in court as an unconstitutional taking; the United States Supreme Court declined to hear challenges to New York's rent stabilization regime in 2023–2024, leaving the law in place. Tenant organisations hailed it as a landmark protection, while subsequent state budgets made modest adjustments to the improvement-surcharge rules.
