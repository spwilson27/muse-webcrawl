Metric tensor

In differential geometry, a metric tensor (or simply a metric) is a structure on a manifold that allows distances and angles to be measured, generalising the dot product of Euclidean space. Formally it is a symmetric, nondegenerate (0,2)-tensor field g that assigns an inner product to the tangent space at each point of the manifold, varying smoothly from point to point.

Definition and use

In coordinates, the metric has components gij, and the squared length of an infinitesimal displacement is ds² = gij dxi dxj (Einstein summation convention). Lengths of curves follow by integration, and the inverse metric gij permits "raising" and "lowering" tensor indices. A manifold equipped with a positive-definite metric is a Riemannian manifold; a metric of Lorentzian signature (−,+,+,+) is used for spacetime in general relativity, where the metric is the dynamical gravitational field, satisfying the Einstein field equations.

Examples

In Euclidean 3-space the metric is the identity matrix in Cartesian coordinates. On the 2-sphere of radius a, the metric in spherical polar coordinates is ds² = a²(dθ² + sin²θ dφ²). In special relativity, the Minkowski metric of flat spacetime has signature (−,+,+,+), giving the spacetime interval.
