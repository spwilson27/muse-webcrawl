Indo-Pakistani War of 1971

The Indo-Pakistani War of 1971 was a military confrontation between India and Pakistan from 3 to 16 December 1971, during the Bangladesh Liberation War. It ended with Pakistan's eastern command surrendering in Dhaka and the creation of Bangladesh.

Indo-Pakistani War of 1971
Date3–16 December 1971
ResultIndian victory; independence of Bangladesh
Prisoners~93,000 Pakistani personnel surrendered

Background

Pakistan's military crackdown in East Pakistan from March 1971 (Operation Searchlight) caused mass atrocities and some ten million refugees fleeing to India. India supported the Mukti Bahini guerrillas; Pakistan launched pre-emptive air strikes (Operation Chengiz Khan) on 3 December.

Course

Indian forces advanced rapidly on Dhaka, bypassing strongpoints, while the navy blockaded both wings — including the first use of anti-ship missiles in the region at Karachi. Lt. Gen. A. A. K. Niazi signed the Instrument of Surrender on 16 December.

Aftermath

The Simla Agreement (1972) followed. The war is a central event in Bangladeshi national identity and reshaped South Asian geopolitics.
