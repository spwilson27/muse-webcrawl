Heaven, or the heavens, is a common religious cosmological or transcendent supernatural place where beings such as deities, angels, souls, saints, or venerated ancestors are said to originate, be enthroned, or reside. According to the beliefs of some religions, heavenly beings can descend to Earth or incarnate, and earthly beings can ascend to heaven in the afterlife, or in exceptional cases enter heaven while still alive.

Heaven is often described as a "highest place", the holiest place, a paradise, in contrast to hell or the underworld or the "low places", and universally or conditionally accessible by earthly beings according to various standards of divinity, goodness, piety, faith, or other virtues or right beliefs, or simply by the will of God.

Etymology and concepts

The modern English word heaven derives from the Old English heofon, referring to the sky and the firmament. In classical antiquity, the sky was imagined as a series of concentric spheres in which the celestial bodies moved, and the outermost sphere was the dwelling place of the gods. Many cultures distinguish between a physical sky and a spiritual heaven, although the two are often closely linked.

Heaven in the Abrahamic religions

Judaism

In the Hebrew Bible, the word shamayim denotes both the sky and the dwelling place of God. Early Israelite cosmology pictured a firmament separating the waters above from the waters below. In later Second Temple and rabbinic Judaism, heaven became associated with the world to come (Olam Ha-Ba) and with Gan Eden, the Garden of Eden, where the righteous would be rewarded.

Christianity

In Christianity, heaven is traditionally the location of the throne of God and the angels of God, and in most forms of Christianity it is the abode of the righteous dead in the afterlife. Believers hold that the saved will be resurrected and live eternally with God. The New Testament describes heaven as a place of rest and reward, and the Book of Revelation depicts a renewed heaven and earth at the end of time. Christian concepts of heaven draw on imagery of a garden, a city (the New Jerusalem), and a wedding feast.

Islam

In Islam, heaven is known as Jannah (Arabic: جنّة, "garden"), described in the Quran as an eternal abode of peace and bliss prepared for the righteous. Jannah is portrayed with rivers of water, milk, honey, and wine, shaded gardens, and the company of the prophets and the faithful. Above the seven heavens stands the throne of God (al-Arsh).

Heaven in Asian religions

In Chinese tradition, Tian (天) can mean both the sky and a supreme power governing the universe; the emperor ruled by the Mandate of Heaven. In Hinduism, the heavens known as Svarga are temporary abodes of reward within the cycle of rebirth, distinct from liberation (moksha). In Buddhism, various heavenly realms (deva worlds) exist within samsara, but they too are impermanent, and beings there are still subject to rebirth.
