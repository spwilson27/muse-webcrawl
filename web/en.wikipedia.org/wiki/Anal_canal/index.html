Anal canal

Part ofLarge intestine
PrecursorHindgut / proctodeum
SystemDigestive system

The anal canal is the terminal segment of the large intestine, extending from the rectum to the anus. It is about 2.5 to 4 centimetres long in adults and is surrounded by the internal and external anal sphincters, which keep it closed except during defecation.

Structure

The canal is divided by the pectinate (dentate) line into an upper part derived from the embryonic hindgut and a lower part derived from the ectoderm of the proctodeum. Above the line, the lining is columnar epithelium with autonomic innervation and venous drainage to the portal system; below it, the lining is stratified squamous epithelium with somatic innervation and systemic drainage. The upper canal bears longitudinal folds called anal columns, whose bases are joined by small valves.

Function

The anal canal regulates the controlled passage of faeces and flatus. Its sphincter complex and the sensory epithelium below the dentate line allow the body to distinguish gas from solid and liquid stool.

Clinical significance

Common disorders of the anal canal include haemorrhoids, anal fissures, fistulae and abscesses. Its dual venous drainage makes the region a portosystemic anastomosis of clinical importance in portal hypertension.
