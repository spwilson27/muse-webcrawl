Strongly regular graph

The Petersen graph, srg(10, 3, 0, 1).

In graph theory, a strongly regular graph (SRG) is a regular graph with the property that the number of common neighbours of two vertices depends only on whether the two vertices are adjacent or not. Strongly regular graphs were introduced by Raj Chandra Bose in 1963 and are closely related to finite geometries, block designs, association schemes, and algebraic graph theory.

Definition

Let G be a regular graph with v vertices and degree k. G is strongly regular with parameters (v, k, λ, μ) if there exist integers λ and μ such that:

  • every two adjacent vertices have exactly λ common neighbours;
  • every two non-adjacent vertices have exactly μ common neighbours.

A graph with parameters (v, k, λ, μ) is denoted srg(v, k, λ, μ). The complement of a strongly regular graph is also strongly regular, with parameters (v, v−k−1, v−2−2k+μ, v−2k+λ).

Examples

  • The cycle C5: srg(5, 2, 0, 1).
  • The Petersen graph: srg(10, 3, 0, 1).
  • The 4×4 rook's graph (line graph of K4,4): srg(16, 6, 2, 2); its complement, the Shrikhande graph, has the same parameters but is not isomorphic to it — a famous pair.
  • The Paley graph of order 13: srg(13, 6, 3, 3).
  • The Clebsch graph: srg(16, 5, 0, 2).
  • The Hoffman–Singleton graph: srg(50, 7, 0, 1), one of the Moore graphs.

Properties

The parameters must satisfy the feasibility relation (v − k − 1)μ = k(k − λ − 1). The adjacency matrix A satisfies A² = (λ−μ)A + (k−μ)I + μJ, so a strongly regular graph has exactly three distinct eigenvalues (for a primitive SRG): k with multiplicity 1, and two others r and s whose multiplicities are determined by the parameters (the Krein conditions further restrict possible parameter sets). Conference graphs have parameters (v, (v−1)/2, (v−5)/4, (v−1)/4) and arise from conference matrices.

Strongly regular graphs connect to two-weight linear codes, finite generalized quadrangles, and mutually orthogonal Latin squares. Whether a Moore graph of degree 57 on 3,250 vertices exists is a famous open problem; its existence would give an srg(3250, 57, 0, 1).
