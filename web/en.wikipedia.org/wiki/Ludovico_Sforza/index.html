Ludovico Sforza

Ludovico "il Moro" Sforza
Born27 July 1452, Vigevano
Died27 May 1508, Loches, France
ReignDuke of Milan, 1494–1499

Ludovico Maria Sforza (27 July 1452 – 27 May 1508), known as Ludovico il Moro ("the Moor"), was an Italian Renaissance nobleman who ruled as Duke of Milan from 1494 until his capture by the French in 1500.

Life

The fourth son of Francesco Sforza, Ludovico seized effective power in Milan in 1479–1480 as regent for his nephew Gian Galeazzo Sforza, whose sudden death in 1494 — widely attributed to Ludovico's poisoning — left him duke in his own right. Seeking to secure his usurpation, he encouraged Charles VIII of France to press his claim to Naples, helping trigger the Italian Wars that devastated the peninsula for decades.

Patronage

Ludovico's court at the Castello Sforzesco was one of the most splendid in Europe. He employed Leonardo da Vinci, who painted The Last Supper for the refectory of Santa Maria delle Grazie and designed court festivities, and the architect Donato Bramante. His wife, Beatrice d'Este, was a notable patron in her own right before her early death in 1497.

Fall

In 1499 Louis XII of France invaded Milan, claiming the duchy through his grandmother Valentina Visconti. Ludovico briefly regained the city in 1500 but was betrayed by Swiss mercenaries at Novara and captured. He spent the remaining eight years of his life as a prisoner in France, dying at the Château de Loches in 1508.
