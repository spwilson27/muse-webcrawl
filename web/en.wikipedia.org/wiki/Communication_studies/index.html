Communication studies

Communication studies is an academic discipline that deals with processes of human communication and behaviour, patterns of communication in interpersonal relationships, social interactions, and communication in different cultures. Communication is commonly defined as giving, receiving, or exchanging ideas, information, signals, or messages through appropriate media, enabling individuals or groups to persuade, to seek information, to give information, or to express emotions effectively.

Scope

The field spans interpersonal, group, organisational, intercultural, health, political, and mass communication, as well as rhetoric, media studies, and the study of digital and networked communication. Scholars draw on both social-scientific methods (experiments, surveys, content analysis) and humanistic approaches (rhetorical criticism, semiotics, critical and cultural theory).

History

The study of communication descends from classical rhetoric — Aristotle's Rhetoric analysed persuasion over two millennia ago. As an institutionalised discipline it emerged in the United States in the mid-20th century, shaped by figures such as Harold Lasswell, Paul Lazarsfeld, Carl Hovland, and Kurt Lewin (sometimes called its founding fathers), by Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver's mathematical theory of communication (1948), and by Wilbur Schramm, who founded key institutes and helped define the field. Professional associations include the National Communication Association (founded 1914 as a speech-teachers' association) and the International Communication Association (founded 1950).

Key concepts

  • Models of communication — linear (sender–message–channel–receiver), interactional, and transactional models; noise and feedback.
  • Effects research — agenda-setting, framing, cultivation, uses and gratifications.
  • Rhetoric and persuasion — ethos, pathos, logos; argumentation and public address.
  • Meaning-making — semiotics, symbolic interactionism, speech-act and coordinated management of meaning theories.

Careers and applications

Graduates work in journalism, public relations, advertising, broadcasting, organisational communication, health campaigns, and media production; the field also underlies practical training in public speaking and media literacy.
