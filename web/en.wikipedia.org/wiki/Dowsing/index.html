Dowsing

Dowsing is a type of divination employed in attempts to locate ground water, buried metals or ores, gemstones, oil, claimed radiations (radiesthesia), gravesites, malign earth vibrations and other objects without scientific apparatus.

Dowsing
Type: Divination practice
Tools: Forked rod, L-rods, pendulum
Status: Pseudoscience
Also called: Water witching, divining

Practice and history

The traditional dowser walks over ground holding a forked (Y- or V-shaped) twig — often hazel or willow — or paired L-shaped metal rods, which are said to dip, twitch or cross over targets. Pendulum dowsing over maps is also practised. Water divining is described from the 16th century in German mining regions and spread worldwide, condemned at times as superstition yet persistently used by farmers and even some utilities.

Scientific assessment

Controlled double-blind studies, including large German government-sponsored trials whose claims did not survive reanalysis, have consistently found dowsing performs no better than chance. The rod's movements are explained by the ideomotor effect — unconscious muscular action influenced by expectation and prior knowledge of terrain.
