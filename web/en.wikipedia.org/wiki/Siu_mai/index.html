Siu mai

TypeDumpling / dim sum
OriginChina
Main ingredientsPork, shrimp, wheat wrapper
ServingSteamed, in bamboo basket

Siu mai (also shui mai or shumai) is a traditional Chinese dumpling served in dim sum, typically made of seasoned ground pork and shrimp wrapped in a thin wheat-flour wrapper that is open at the top, often garnished with crab roe, a pea or diced carrot. In Cantonese cuisine it is one of the most standard dim sum items, alongside har gow, together nicknamed 'the big two'.

Origin

Siu mai is said to have originated in Hohhot, Inner Mongolia, as a side product sold in tea houses along the Silk Road, before becoming a Cantonese dim sum staple in Guangzhou and Hong Kong. The name, literally 'to sell hot', reflects its sale as a freshly steamed snack.

Varieties

Cantonese siu mai uses pork and shrimp; Muslim Chinese versions use mutton or beef; Japanese shumai, introduced via Yokohama's Chinatown, is similar to the Cantonese style. A glutinous rice version is popular in Shanghai and Jiangsu, and wonton-like variants exist across Southeast Asia.

Preparation

The filling is coarsely minced, seasoned with soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, sesame oil and white pepper, gathered into a pleated cup of wrapper, and steamed in bamboo baskets for several minutes until just cooked.
