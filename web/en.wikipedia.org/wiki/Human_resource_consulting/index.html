Human resource consulting

The human resource consulting industry provides advice and implementation services to organisations on the management of their human capital. HR consultants advise on areas including talent acquisition and recruitment strategy, compensation and benefits benchmarking, organisational design, performance management, leadership development, employee engagement, HR technology selection and implementation, and compliance with employment law.

Industry structure

The market ranges from the HR advisory practices of the large generalist consulting firms and the global HR specialists — firms such as Mercer, Aon and Willis Towers Watson, historically strong in pensions, benefits and actuarial work — through mid-sized specialists to independent practitioners and interim HR executives. Clients engage consultants for expertise not available in-house, for surge capacity during transactions such as mergers and restructurings, or for independent review of sensitive matters such as executive pay and workplace investigations.

Trends

Demand has been shaped by the digitisation of HR — the adoption of human capital management software suites and people analytics — and by shifting work models, including the expansion of remote and hybrid work after 2020. Consultancies increasingly deliver services through subscription platforms and managed services, blurring the boundary with HR outsourcing providers. Professional standards are informal: HR consultants commonly hold qualifications such as SHRM or CIPD certification, and engagements are typically governed by statements of work specifying deliverables and fees on a project, retainer or day-rate basis.
