Bandwidth (computing)

In computing, bandwidth is the maximum rate of data transfer across a given path. Bandwidth may be characterised as network bandwidth, data bandwidth, or digital bandwidth. It is typically measured in bits per second (bit/s) or multiples thereof such as megabits per second (Mbit/s) and gigabits per second (Gbit/s).

Definition

The term originates in signal processing, where bandwidth denotes the width, in hertz, of a frequency band occupied by a signal. For a communication channel, the maximum achievable data rate is bounded by the Shannon–Hartley theorem, which relates capacity to bandwidth and signal-to-noise ratio, and by the Nyquist rate for a noiseless channel. In practice, users experience throughput — the rate actually achieved — which is usually lower than the nominal bandwidth because of protocol overhead, congestion and retransmissions.

Usage

Internet service providers advertise connection bandwidths, commonly distinguishing download and upload rates, which are often asymmetric on consumer technologies such as ADSL and cable. Bandwidth is also consumed by hosted services, where providers may meter the total volume of data transferred per month, a usage likewise informally called "bandwidth". Network administrators manage bandwidth by traffic shaping, quality of service rules, caching and content delivery networks.
