The Battle of Rarańcza was fought on 15–16 February 1918, when the II Brigade of the Polish Legions, commanded by Józef Haller, broke through the Austro-Hungarian front at Rarańcza in Bukovina in protest against the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk.

Background

The separate peace signed between the Central Powers and Ukraine ceded the Chełm Land to Ukraine, outraging Polish opinion. The Polish Auxiliary Corps, successor to the Legions in Austrian service, resolved to leave the front and join Polish forces forming in Russia.

The breakthrough

On the night of 15–16 February the brigade attacked Austrian positions near Rarańcza; the infantry broke through, though much of the corps' artillery and trains were captured. Haller's men eventually joined the Polish II Corps in Russia.

Aftermath

The Austrians interned many legionaries at Huszt and Marmaros-Sziget and court-martialled them for treason. Rarańcza remains a symbol of Polish refusal to accept the Brest-Litovsk settlement; many of its veterans later fought at Kaniów.
