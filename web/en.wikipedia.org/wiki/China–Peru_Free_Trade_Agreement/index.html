China–Peru Free Trade Agreement

The agreement governs trade between China and Peru

The China–Peru Free Trade Agreement is a bilateral trade agreement between the People's Republic of China and the Republic of Peru. Negotiations were formally launched in 2007 and concluded in November 2008. The agreement was signed in Beijing on April 28, 2009, ratified by Peru's Congress, and entered into force on March 1, 2010. It was China's second free trade agreement with a Latin American country, after the China–Chile agreement of 2005.

Provisions

The agreement provides for the progressive elimination of tariffs on more than 90 percent of goods traded between the two countries over a period of up to 17 years. It covers trade in goods and services, investment, customs procedures, trade remedies, sanitary and phytosanitary measures, and intellectual property. Sensitive Peruvian products, including some agricultural goods, textiles, and footwear, received longer transition periods or exclusions.

Trade effects

Peru exports to China are dominated by minerals, particularly copper, zinc, and iron ore, along with fishmeal and agricultural products such as grapes and avocados. China became Peru's largest trading partner in the years following the agreement. In 2023–2024 the two countries negotiated an upgrade of the agreement, concluding talks in mid-2024.
