"The Legend of Sleepy Hollow" is a Gothic short story by American author Washington Irving, first published in 1820 in his collection The Sketch Book of Geoffrey Crayon, Gent. Along with its companion piece "Rip Van Winkle", it is among the earliest examples of American fiction still widely read. The story is set in 1790 in the secluded Dutch settlement of Tarry Town (in the real village of Sleepy Hollow, New York) and tells of Ichabod Crane, a lanky schoolmaster who competes with the brawny Brom Bones for the hand of Katrina Van Tassel, and who, riding home from a harvest party, is pursued by the Headless Horseman, the ghost of a Hessian soldier decapitated by a cannonball.

Plot

Ichabod Crane, a schoolteacher from Connecticut, boards with local farmers and courts Katrina Van Tassel, the eighteen-year-old daughter of a wealthy Dutch farmer — drawn as much by her father's property as by Katrina herself. His rival is Abraham "Brom Bones" Van Brunt, a rowdy local hero. After a party at the Van Tassel homestead, where tales of the Headless Horseman are exchanged, Ichabod is rejected by Katrina. Riding home through the woods he encounters a cloaked rider with his head on his saddle, races him to the bridge by the Old Dutch Church — which legend says the ghost cannot cross — but the figure hurls its head at him. The next morning Ichabod has vanished; only his horse, his hat, and a shattered pumpkin are found. The story leaves it ambiguous whether the Horseman was supernatural or Brom in disguise, as Brom later marries Katrina and laughs knowingly whenever the tale is told.

Origins and themes

Irving wrote the story while living in England, drawing on German folktales, the folklore of the Hudson Valley's Dutch settlers, and an actual local legend of a headless Hessian soldier. Themes include the clash between Puritan New England and old Dutch New York, superstition, greed, and the anxieties of the young republic. The frame narrator, "Diedrich Knickerbocker", adds a layer of comic unreliability.

Legacy and adaptations

The story became a cornerstone of American mythology and Halloween tradition; the village of North Tarrytown officially renamed itself Sleepy Hollow in 1996. Adaptations include Disney's 1949 animated segment of The Adventures of Ichabod and Mr. Toad, narrated by Bing Crosby; Tim Burton's 1999 film Sleepy Hollow with Johnny Depp; and the television series Sleepy Hollow (2013–2017).
