Eben Moglen (born July 13, 1959) is an American legal scholar, professor of law and legal history at Columbia University, and a prominent advocate of free software. He is the founder, director-counsel, and chairman of the Software Freedom Law Center, established in 2005.

Early life and education

Moglen was born in New Haven, Connecticut. He graduated from Swarthmore College in 1980 and received his law degree and a PhD in history from Yale University. He clerked for Judge Edward Weinfeld of the United States District Court for the Southern District of New York and for Justice Thurgood Marshall of the United States Supreme Court.

Free software work

Beginning in 1993, Moglen served as pro bono general counsel of the Free Software Foundation, where he worked closely with Richard Stallman on the enforcement of the GNU General Public License and on drafting version 3 of the license (GPLv3), released in 2007. His 2003 essay "Anarchism Triumphant" and his "dotCommunist Manifesto" examined the economics and politics of free software. In 2010 he founded the FreedomBox Foundation, promoting personal servers designed to protect privacy and decentralize the internet.

Academic career

Moglen joined the Columbia Law School faculty in 1994. His scholarship covers the history of copyright, the First Amendment, and the legal implications of networked technology. He has been a vocal critic of software patents and of mass surveillance.
