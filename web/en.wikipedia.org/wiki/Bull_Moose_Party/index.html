Progressive Party (United States, 1912)

The Progressive Party, popularly nicknamed the Bull Moose Party, was a third party in the United States formed in 1912 by former president Theodore Roosevelt after he lost the Republican presidential nomination to incumbent William Howard Taft. Roosevelt declared himself "fit as a bull moose," giving the party its nickname.

1912 election

Running on the "New Nationalism" platform — women's suffrage, tariff reduction, regulation of business, social insurance, and direct democracy measures — Roosevelt won 27.4% of the popular vote and 88 electoral votes, the strongest third-party presidential showing in US history. The split in the Republican vote helped Democrat Woodrow Wilson win. Roosevelt survived an assassination attempt in Milwaukee and delivered his speech with the bullet still lodged in his chest.

Decline

The party elected some congressmen and governors, including Hiram Johnson of California, but faded after 1914. Roosevelt declined its 1916 nomination and supported the Republican candidate; the party dissolved by 1920.
