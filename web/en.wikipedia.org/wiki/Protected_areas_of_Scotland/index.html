Protected areas of Scotland are areas within Scotland that have been designated for the protection of the natural environment, landscape and wildlife. Several overlapping designations exist, ranging from Scotland's two national parks to local nature reserves and sites protected under international conventions.

National parks

Scotland has two national parks, established under the National Parks (Scotland) Act 2000. Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park was created in 2002 and covers 1,865 km² around Loch Lomond. The Cairngorms National Park, created in 2003 and extended in 2010, is the largest national park in the United Kingdom at about 4,528 km², encompassing the Cairngorms mountain range and parts of Highland, Aberdeenshire, Moray, Angus and Perth and Kinross. Scottish national parks have four statutory aims: conserving natural and cultural heritage, promoting sustainable use of natural resources, promoting public enjoyment and understanding, and promoting sustainable economic and social development of communities.

National scenic areas

There are 40 national scenic areas (NSAs), covering roughly 13% of Scotland's land area. Introduced in 1980, NSAs protect landscapes of outstanding scenic quality, including areas such as Glen Coe, the Cuillin Hills, Wester Ross, Jura, and the Shetland and Orkney coasts. Development within NSAs is subject to special planning controls.

Sites of Special Scientific Interest

Sites of Special Scientific Interest (SSSIs) are the basic building block of site-based nature conservation in Great Britain. There are around 1,400 SSSIs in Scotland, covering about 12.6% of its area, notified for their flora, fauna, geological or physiographical features. Many SSSIs underpin European designations: Special Areas of Conservation (SACs) for habitats and species and Special Protection Areas (SPAs) for birds.

National and local nature reserves

National nature reserves (NNRs) are declared by NatureScot (formerly Scottish Natural Heritage) under the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949 and the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981; examples include Ben Lawers, the Isle of May, St Kilda and the Cairngorms. Local authorities and bodies such as the Scottish Wildlife Trust and the RSPB also manage many local nature reserves and voluntary reserves.

Marine protected areas

Scotland's seas are protected through a network of marine protected areas (MPAs), including nature conservation MPAs designated under the Marine (Scotland) Act 2010, covering sites such as the waters around St Kilda, South Arran and the Flannan Isles, alongside marine SACs and SPAs.

Ramsar sites and World Heritage

Wetlands of international importance such as Caerlaverock and the Montrose Basin are listed under the Ramsar Convention. St Kilda and the Heart of Neolithic Orkney are also UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
