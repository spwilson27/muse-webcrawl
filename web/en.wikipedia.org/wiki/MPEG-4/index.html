MPEG-4 is a group of international standards for the compression and delivery of digital audio and video, developed by the Moving Picture Experts Group (MPEG) and published as ISO/IEC 14496. Introduced in the late 1990s, it extended the earlier MPEG-1 and MPEG-2 standards with object-based coding, interactivity, and support for low-bit-rate streaming.

Parts

The standard is organised into numbered parts. Part 2 (MPEG-4 Visual) defined the video codec used by implementations such as DivX and Xvid. Part 3 covers audio, principally Advanced Audio Coding (AAC). Part 10, developed jointly with ITU-T as H.264/AVC, roughly doubled the efficiency of earlier codecs and became one of the most widely used video formats in history, underpinning Blu-ray, streaming, and broadcast. Part 14 defines the MP4 container file format, based on Apple's QuickTime format. Other parts address systems and scene description (BIFS), the delivery multimedia integration framework, conformance testing, and intellectual property management.

Licensing

Many MPEG-4 technologies were covered by patent pools administered by MPEG LA, a factor that influenced adoption decisions and motivated the development of royalty-free alternatives in later years.
