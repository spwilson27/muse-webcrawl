Verghese Kurien (26 November 1921 – 9 September 2012), best known as the "Father of the White Revolution" in India, was a social entrepreneur whose Amul cooperative model and the Operation Flood programme transformed India from a milk-deficient country into the world's largest milk producer.

Early life

Kurien was born in Calicut (now Kozhikode), Kerala, and studied physics at Loyola College, Madras, followed by mechanical engineering. On a government scholarship he trained in dairy engineering in the United States. In 1949 he was posted to a government creamery at Anand, Gujarat, intending to leave quickly; instead he was drawn into helping the local Kaira District Co-operative Milk Producers' Union, founded under the leadership of Tribhuvandas Patel, in its struggle against the monopolistic Polson dairy.

The Anand model and Amul

Working with Patel and engineer H. M. Dalaya — who pioneered the manufacture of milk powder from buffalo milk — Kurien built the Kaira cooperative (whose products were marketed under the Amul brand, established 1955) into a farmer-owned enterprise in which village milk societies collect milk twice daily, district unions process it, and a state federation markets it, with the bulk of the consumer rupee returning to farmers. This three-tier structure became known as the Anand pattern.

Operation Flood

In 1965 Kurien became founding chairman of the National Dairy Development Board (NDDB), created to replicate the Anand pattern nationwide. Its programme Operation Flood (1970–1996), financed partly by monetising donated European dairy surpluses, linked millions of village producers to urban markets through a national milk grid. By its end India had become the world's largest milk producer, and dairying had become a major source of rural income, especially for women and landless farmers. Kurien also founded the Institute of Rural Management Anand (IRMA) in 1979 to train managers for farmer organisations.

Legacy

Kurien received the Ramon Magsaysay Award (1963), the World Food Prize (1989) and India's Padma Vibhushan (1999). His birthday, 26 November, is celebrated in India as National Milk Day. His autobiography is titled I Too Had a Dream.
