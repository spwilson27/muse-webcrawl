Edson Arantes do Nascimento (23 October 1940 – 29 December 2022), better known as Pelé, was a Brazilian professional footballer who played as a forward. Widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time, he is the only footballer to have won three FIFA World Cups, with Brazil in 1958, 1962, and 1970.

Pelé spent most of his club career with Santos, winning numerous state and national titles as well as two Copa Libertadores and two Intercontinental Cups. He finished his career with the New York Cosmos of the North American Soccer League, helping to popularise the sport in the United States.

International career

Pelé debuted for Brazil in 1957 at the age of sixteen. At the 1958 World Cup in Sweden he scored six goals, including two in the final. He scored 77 goals in 92 official internationals, a Brazilian record he held for decades, and was named FIFA Player of the Century in 2000 jointly with Diego Maradona.

Playing style and legacy

Known for his dribbling, passing, bicycle kicks, and powerful shot with either foot, Pelé popularised the phrase "the Beautiful Game". After retiring he served as Brazil's Minister of Sports and as a global ambassador for football and charitable causes.
