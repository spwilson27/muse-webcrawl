National Museum of the United States Air Force

The National Museum of the United States Air Force (formerly the United States Air Force Museum) is the official museum of the United States Air Force, located at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base near Dayton, Ohio. Founded in 1923, it is the largest and oldest military aviation museum in the world, with more than 360 aircraft and missiles on display and around a million visitors a year. Admission is free.

History

The museum began in 1923 as an engineering collection at McCook Field, Dayton, preserving technical artefacts and foreign aircraft from the First World War. It moved to Wright Field in 1927 and, after periods in temporary quarters, opened its first purpose-built building in 1971. Successive hangars — huge steel structures on the former Wright Field flight line — expanded the museum in 1988, 2003, and 2016, the last adding a fourth building for space, presidential, and experimental aircraft.

Collection

The exhibits trace American military aviation from the Wright brothers' 1909 Military Flyer — the world's first military aeroplane — through the world wars, Korea, Vietnam, the Cold War, and the space age. Highlights include the B-29 Bockscar, which dropped the atomic bomb on Nagasaki; the XB-70 Valkyrie supersonic bomber prototype; an F-117 Nighthawk stealth fighter; the Apollo 15 command module Endeavour; and a gallery of presidential aircraft, including SAM 26000, the Boeing 707 that served as Air Force One for presidents Kennedy through Clinton and carried John F. Kennedy's body back from Dallas. A separate hangar area preserves the Memphis Belle B-17, unveiled in 2018 after a long restoration.

The museum also administers the Air Force's heritage programme, loaning aircraft to museums around the world, and operates an annex historically housed in hangars across the base, including the former Research and Development Gallery.
