Marco Polo Bridge Incident

The Marco Polo Bridge, site of the clash of 7 July 1937

The Marco Polo Bridge Incident, also known as the Lugou Bridge Incident (卢沟桥事变) or the July 7 Incident (七七事变), was a battle between the Republic of China's National Revolutionary Army and the Imperial Japanese Army that occurred on the night of 7–8 July 1937. It is generally regarded as the start of the Second Sino-Japanese War and, in Chinese historiography, of China's War of Resistance against Japan within the Second World War.

The incident

Japanese forces stationed near the town of Wanping, southwest of Beiping (Beijing), under the terms of the Boxer Protocol, conducted night exercises near the historic Lugou Bridge — known in the West as the Marco Polo Bridge. After an exchange of fire, the Japanese demanded entry into Wanping to search for a missing soldier, Private Kikujiro Shimura, who in fact returned to his unit shortly afterwards. The Chinese commander Qin Dechun refused the demand to search the town, and fighting escalated. Chinese forces under General Song Zheyuan's 29th Army resisted, but were outmatched.

Escalation

Attempts at a local truce failed, and both sides reinforced. Japanese forces bombarded Wanping and captured the bridge. Beiping fell on 29 July and Tianjin on 30 July 1937. Within weeks the conflict had expanded into full-scale war, with fighting spreading to Shanghai in August, beginning eight years of war that would merge into the wider Second World War.

Legacy

The anniversary of 7 July is commemorated in China as the start of the War of Resistance. The Museum of the War of Chinese People's Resistance Against Japanese Aggression stands near the bridge in Wanping.
