Indigenous peoples of California

The Indigenous peoples of California are the diverse Native American groups who have inhabited the region now constituting the US state of California for at least 12,000 years. Pre-contact California was one of the most culturally and linguistically diverse areas in North America, with perhaps 300,000 inhabitants speaking as many as 100 distinct languages, organised into hundreds of small polities sometimes called tribelets — including the Chumash, Pomo, Miwok, Maidu, Yurok, Hupa, Ohlone, Tongva, Cahuilla and Kumeyaay.

History

Spanish colonisation beginning in 1769 brought the mission system, which coerced Indigenous people into missions where disease and harsh conditions caused catastrophic population decline. The Mexican era secularised the missions without restoring land, and after US annexation (1848), the Gold Rush era brought state-sanctioned violence and enslavement — the California genocide — reducing the Indigenous population to roughly 30,000 by 1870. Eighteen treaties negotiated in 1851–52 were never ratified, leaving most California tribes landless.

Today

California has more federally recognised tribes than any other state — over 100 — though many communities remain unrecognised. Tribes operate governments, cultural programmes and, on some reservations, gaming enterprises, and language and cultural revitalisation is widespread.
