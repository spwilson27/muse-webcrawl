Thrill killing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A thrill killing is a premeditated murder committed by a person who kills simply for the excitement of the experience. In criminology and forensic psychology, thrill killings are distinguished from other motive categories — such as lust killings, mission-oriented killings, or gain-motivated murders — by the absence of any material or ideological purpose: the act itself, and the emotional arousal it produces, is the reward.

Characteristics

Investigative literature, including typologies developed by the FBI's Behavioral Science Unit, describes thrill killers as often seeking a sense of power, dominance or euphoria. Offences may be committed by individuals or, frequently, by pairs or small groups in which social reinforcement plays a role. Victims are often strangers selected opportunistically, and offenders may keep souvenirs or records of the crime, revisit the scene, or seek media coverage.

Notable cases

The 1924 murder committed by Nathan Leopold and Richard Loeb in Chicago is frequently cited as a paradigmatic thrill killing: two wealthy students kidnapped and murdered a 14-year-old boy, motivated in part by a desire to commit a "perfect crime" and experience the act itself. The case became a landmark in American criminal history and inspired works including the play and film Rope and the film Compulsion.

Other cases discussed in criminological literature under this category include offences committed by pairs of offenders who reported excitement as a principal motive. Analysts caution that stated motives may shift after arrest and that thrill motives frequently coexist with antisocial personality traits, psychopathy, and other factors.

In criminology

Thrill killing is one category in the Holmes and DeBurger typology of serial murder, alongside visionary, mission-oriented, hedonistic and power/control categories; thrill killing is generally grouped within the hedonistic family. Researchers note that pure thrill motivation is comparatively rare and that most homicides arise from arguments, profit, or intimate relationships.
