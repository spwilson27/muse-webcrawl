Supply chain finance

Supply chain finance
Reverse factoring: the buyer approves invoices, the financier pays the supplier early
FieldCorporate finance, trade finance
Key techniqueReverse factoring (payables finance)
ParticipantsBuyer, suppliers, banks and fintech platforms

Supply chain finance (SCF) is a set of technology-based business and financing processes that link buyers, sellers and financing institutions to lower financing costs and improve efficiency for all parties in a supply chain. Its best-known technique is reverse factoring, in which a large, creditworthy buyer arranges for its suppliers to be paid early by a bank or platform at a discount rate based on the buyer's credit rating, while the buyer itself pays the financier at the normal due date or later.

How reverse factoring works

The buyer approves supplier invoices and uploads them to a financing platform. Suppliers can then elect to sell their approved receivables and receive payment within days rather than waiting the contractual 30–120 days, paying a financing charge keyed to the buyer's (usually strong) credit rather than their own. The buyer benefits from extended payment terms and a more resilient supply base; the financier earns a low-risk return on short-term assets, since the obligation is an approved payable of a known buyer.

Development and controversy

SCF expanded rapidly after the 2008 financial crisis, driven by bank platforms and fintech firms such as Greensill Capital. The field drew scrutiny because extended payables financed this way can flatter a buyer's reported cash position while resembling debt; accounting standard-setters (IASB and FASB) have since introduced disclosure requirements for supplier finance arrangements. The collapse of Greensill in 2021, and the earlier failure of Carillion — which had used supply chain finance while stretching supplier payments — intensified debate about transparency and the treatment of small suppliers.

Related techniques

The broader SCF toolkit includes inventory finance, pre-shipment finance, distributor finance, dynamic discounting (where the buyer itself pays early in exchange for a discount), and receivables securitisation.
