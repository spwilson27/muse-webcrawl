Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam (15 October 1931 – 27 July 2015) was an Indian aerospace scientist and statesman who served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. Known popularly as the "Missile Man of India" and the "People's President", he played a leading role in the development of India's ballistic missile and nuclear weapons programmes.

Early life and education

Kalam was born into a Tamil Muslim family in Rameswaram, on Pamban Island in present-day Tamil Nadu. His father was a boat owner and imam of a local mosque. Kalam sold newspapers as a boy to supplement the family income. He studied physics at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, and then aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology.

Career as a scientist

After a brief period at the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO), Kalam moved in 1969 to the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), where he directed the SLV-III project that deployed the Rohini satellite into orbit in 1980. Returning to DRDO, he headed the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, which produced the Agni and Prithvi missiles. He served as scientific adviser to the Defence Minister and was a key figure in the Pokhran-II nuclear tests of 1998. He received India's highest civilian honour, the Bharat Ratna, in 1997.

Presidency

Elected President in 2002 with support from both the ruling and opposition parties, Kalam brought an unusually public, youth-oriented style to Rashtrapati Bhavan, travelling widely and addressing students across India. After one term he returned to teaching and writing in 2007.

Later years and death

Kalam's books, including the autobiography Wings of Fire (1999) and India 2020, inspired generations of students. He collapsed while lecturing at the Indian Institute of Management Shillong on 27 July 2015 and died the same day. His tomb stands at Pei Karumbu in Rameswaram.
