River Blackwater (Munster)

The River Blackwater (Irish: An Abha Mhór, "the great river") is a river in Ireland, one of the rivers collectively known as the three sisters of Munster's south. At about 169 km (105 miles) it is among the longest rivers wholly within the Republic of Ireland. It should not be confused with the Northern Ireland Blackwater or the Essex river of the same name.

Course

The Blackwater rises in the Mullaghareirk Mountains of County Kerry and flows east through counties Cork and Waterford, passing the towns of Mallow, Fermoy, Lismore and Cappoquin. At Cappoquin it turns abruptly south and enters the Celtic Sea at Youghal, where its wide estuary forms a noted wildfowl habitat. Its main tributaries include the Awbeg (immortalised by Edmund Spenser, who lived at Kilcolman Castle nearby), the Funshion, the Araglin and the Bride.

Fishing and ecology

The river is renowned as one of Ireland's finest salmon fisheries, and also supports trout, lamprey, shad and the freshwater pearl mussel. Much of the river and its estuary is designated a Special Area of Conservation and Special Protection Area under European nature law.
