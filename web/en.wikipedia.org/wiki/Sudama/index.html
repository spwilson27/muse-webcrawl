Sudama

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sudama is a figure in Hindu tradition, known as the childhood friend and devotee of the god Krishna. His story, told principally in the Bhagavata Purana, is a beloved exemplum of humble devotion (bhakti) and of Krishna's grace toward his friends. Sudama is also called Kuchela in traditions of Kerala.

Story

Sudama was a poor Brahmin who, as a boy, studied alongside Krishna and Balarama at the ashram of the sage Sandipani in Ujjain. In adult life Sudama fell into deep poverty. Urged by his wife, he travelled to Krishna's capital at Dvaraka to seek help, carrying only a small bundle of beaten rice (poha) as a gift, which his wife had tied in a cloth. At the palace Krishna welcomed the ragged Brahmin with great honour, washing his feet and seating him on his own couch, and ate the humble rice with delight. Sudama, overwhelmed, could not bring himself to ask for anything and returned home empty-handed — only to find that Krishna had transformed his hut into a splendid mansion filled with riches.

Significance

The tale emphasises that sincere devotion and true friendship matter more than wealth, and it is celebrated especially on occasions connected with friendship and during recitations of the Bhagavata. The city of Porbandar in Gujarat is traditionally associated with Sudama, where a temple dedicated to him stands.
