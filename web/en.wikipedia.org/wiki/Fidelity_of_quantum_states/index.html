Fidelity of quantum states

Fidelity
FieldQuantum information theory
DefinitionMeasure of closeness between two quantum states
Range0 (orthogonal) to 1 (identical)
RelatedBures distance, trace distance, Uhlmann's theorem

In quantum mechanics, especially in quantum information theory, the fidelity is a measure of the "closeness" of two quantum states. For density operators ρ and σ, the fidelity is defined as

F(ρ, σ) = [ tr √( √ρ σ √ρ ) ]²

using the definition of Uhlmann. The quantity lies between 0 and 1: it equals 1 if and only if the states are identical, and 0 if they have orthogonal support.

Pure states

If at least one of the states is pure, the fidelity reduces to the squared modulus of the inner product, F = |⟨ψ|φ⟩|², i.e. the transition probability between the two states. For two pure states it coincides with the overlap of their state vectors.

Properties

Fidelity is symmetric, invariant under unitary transformations applied to both states, and monotonically non-decreasing under quantum operations (completely positive trace-preserving maps). It is jointly concave in its arguments. By Uhlmann's theorem, the fidelity equals the maximum squared overlap between any purifications of the two states in a larger Hilbert space.

Relation to other measures

The Bures distance is derived from fidelity as dB = √(2(1 − √F)). Fidelity bounds the trace distance: 1 − √F ≤ dtr ≤ √(1 − F) (the Fuchs–van de Graaf inequalities). Fidelity is widely used as a benchmark in quantum computing, where gate and state-preparation fidelities quantify how closely a device reproduces target states.
