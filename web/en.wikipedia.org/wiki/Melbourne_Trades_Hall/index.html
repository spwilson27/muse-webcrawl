Melbourne Trades Hall

The Melbourne Trades Hall is a historic building in Carlton, Melbourne, Victoria, Australia, and the headquarters of the Victorian Trades Hall Council, the peak body of the state's trade union movement. Built in stages from 1874, it is regarded as the oldest continuously operating trades hall in the world and stands on the traditional meeting place of Melbourne's working class at the corner of Lygon and Victoria Streets.

Melbourne Trades Hall

Trades Hall, Carlton

Location: 54 Victoria Street, Carlton, Melbourne
Built: 1874–1926
Architect: Joseph Reed
Style: Neoclassical

History

The site was granted to Melbourne's trades unions in 1858, recognising the role of the Eight Hours movement, which in 1856 won the eight-hour day for building workers — a world-first achievement commemorated by the Eight Hour Day monument opposite the building. The first hall opened in 1859; the present grand building, designed by Joseph Reed in a classical style reminiscent of a parliament house, was constructed progressively as funds allowed from union levies.

Role

Trades Hall has been the organisational heart of Victorian campaigns for the eight-hour day, equal pay, occupational safety and workers' rights, and its council chamber has hosted generations of union delegates. The building also houses the Victorian branch of the Labor Party's historical roots and remains a venue for meetings, rallies and cultural events. It is listed on the Victorian Heritage Register.
