Drive time

Drive time
PeriodsMorning drive: approx. 6–10 a.m.; Afternoon drive: approx. 3–7 p.m. (local)
IndustryRadio broadcasting
SignificancePeak radio audiences; premium advertising rates

Drive time is the period during the radio broadcasting day when the largest number of people are listening, because they are commuting by car. There are two drive-time dayparts: morning drive (typically 6–10 a.m.) and afternoon drive (typically 3–7 p.m.), coinciding with rush hours. Because in-car listening dominates these hours, drive time commands radio's highest advertising rates and its flagship programming.

Programming

Morning drive is home to personality-led "morning zoo" and talk shows mixing news, weather, traffic reports and entertainment; afternoon drive emphasises music, commuter services and brief news to accompany the trip home. Traffic and travel updates — often delivered from helicopters or traffic centres — are a signature drive-time service, and syndicated national hosts build their shows around drive-time slots in each time zone.

Measurement and change

Audience measurement firms such as Nielsen Audio treat drive times as distinct dayparts in ratings, and stations schedule their strongest talent accordingly. Since the 2010s, the pattern has been eroded and reshaped by podcasting, streaming in cars, satellite radio and, after 2020, by changed commuting habits associated with hybrid and remote work, which flattened the peaks somewhat without eliminating them.
