Underwater hockey

Underwater hockey — also known as Octopush in the United Kingdom — is a limited-contact sport in which two teams manoeuvre a puck across the bottom of a swimming pool into the opposing team's goal. Players wear mask, snorkel, fins, a protective glove and a water-polo style cap, and play the puck with a short stick of about 30 cm. Because the action takes place on the pool floor, it is largely a three-dimensional game of breath-hold diving and teamwork.

History

The sport was invented in 1954 in Southsea, England, by Alan Blake and fellow members of the Southsea Sub-Aqua Club, as a winter activity to maintain divers' fitness and interest when open-water diving was impossible. The original game used eight players (hence "Octopush"), a lead puck called a "squid", and small hockey sticks. The sport spread through the British Sub-Aqua Club network to Commonwealth countries and beyond; the first world championship was held in Canada in 1980. The international governing body is the Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques (CMAS).

Play

Matches are typically played in a pool 25 m long with a water depth of about 2–3.5 m, with metal or fibreglass goals (gullies) 3 m wide at each end resting on the pool floor. Teams field six players at a time with rolling substitutes. The puck, weighing about 1.3 kg and coated in plastic, slides along the tiles and can be flicked short distances. Players dive down to contest possession, surface for air, and rely on positioning since no player can stay on the bottom long. Games consist of two halves of 10–20 minutes. Referees in the water and on deck signal with hand gestures and audible signals; video review is used at elite level.

Characteristics

Underwater hockey is unusual in being almost invisible and silent to spectators above the surface, yet fast and physical within the water. It is played by men, women and mixed teams, with strong national teams in New Zealand, Australia, South Africa, France, Great Britain, the United States and Colombia. Because success depends more on teamwork, fitness and breath-hold economy than on individual possession, the sport is often praised for its emphasis on passing and spatial awareness.
