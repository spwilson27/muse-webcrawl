Genodermatosis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A genodermatosis is an inherited skin disease or a hereditary disorder in which skin manifestations are a prominent feature. Genodermatoses may be caused by mutations in a single gene or may be part of multisystem genetic syndromes in which the skin provides visible clues to the underlying diagnosis. Hundreds of distinct genodermatoses have been described, and advances in molecular genetics have identified the causative genes for most of the major ones.

Classification

Genodermatoses are commonly grouped by their principal clinical feature: disorders of keratinization (such as the ichthyoses), mechanobullous disorders in which skin blisters with minor trauma (the epidermolysis bullosa group), pigmentary disorders (such as oculocutaneous albinism and piebaldism), disorders of the hair, nails, and sweat glands (the ectodermal dysplasias), and tumor-predisposition syndromes with cutaneous signs (such as neurofibromatosis and Gorlin syndrome). Others affect connective tissue, including Ehlers–Danlos syndromes, or involve premature ageing, as in xeroderma pigmentosum, a defect of DNA repair.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis combines careful clinical examination and family history with skin biopsy, and increasingly with molecular genetic testing, including targeted gene panels and whole exome sequencing. Identifying the precise genetic cause can guide prognosis — for example, the cancer risks associated with certain syndromes — and enable prenatal or preimplantation diagnosis for families.

Management

Most genodermatoses have no cure; treatment is supportive, emphasizing skin care, wound management, infection prevention, sun protection where relevant, and surveillance for systemic complications. Gene therapy and protein-replacement approaches are under investigation for conditions such as recessive dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa, and topical gene therapy for that condition received regulatory approval in the United States in 2023.
