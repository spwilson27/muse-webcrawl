Mahishasura

Mahishasura is a buffalo demon (asura) in Hindu mythology, famous for his battle with the goddess Durga. His name combines mahisha (buffalo) and asura (demon). According to legend, he was the son of the demon king Rambha and a water buffalo, and possessed the ability to change form at will.

Legend

After performing severe penance, Mahishasura obtained a boon from Brahma that he could not be killed by any man or god. Empowered by this blessing, he defeated the gods, drove Indra from heaven, and established his rule over the three worlds. Unable to defeat him, the gods combined their energies to create the goddess Durga, who rode a lion and bore weapons given by each god.

Durga fought Mahishasura and his armies for nine nights. The demon repeatedly changed shape — into a buffalo, lion, man, and elephant — but on the final day Durga pinned the buffalo form and beheaded him as he emerged from its mouth, earning the epithet Mahishasuramardini (slayer of Mahishasura).

Significance

The victory is celebrated during the festivals of Navaratri and Durga Puja, and forms the central narrative of the Devi Mahatmyam in the Markandeya Purana. The city of Mysuru (Mysore) in Karnataka takes its name from Mahishasura, and a statue of the demon stands on Chamundi Hill overlooking the city.
