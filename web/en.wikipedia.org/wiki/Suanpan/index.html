Suanpan

The suanpan (Mandarin: 算盤; pinyin: suànpán) is a Chinese abacus used for arithmetic calculation. Typically constructed of wood with metal rods, a traditional suanpan has seven or more columns of beads divided by a horizontal beam: two beads in the upper deck (each worth five) and five beads in the lower deck (each worth one) for each column, an arrangement that also supports hexadecimal weight calculations historically used in China.

History

Bead reckoning in China dates back many centuries; the suanpan in recognisable form appeared by the Song dynasty and was in widespread use during the Ming dynasty, when treatises such as Cheng Dawei's Suanfa Tongzong (1592) described its methods in detail. Suanpan techniques allow rapid addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and even extraction of square and cube roots.

Influence

The suanpan spread to neighbouring countries, giving rise to the Japanese soroban (which reduced the bead count to one upper and four lower beads) and the Korean jusan. Skilled suanpan operators could rival electronic calculators in speed for addition and subtraction, and abacus arithmetic (zhusuan) was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2013.
