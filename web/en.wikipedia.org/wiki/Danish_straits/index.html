Danish straits

The Danish straits between Denmark, Sweden and Germany.

The Danish straits are the straits connecting the Baltic Sea to the North Sea via the Kattegat and Skagerrak. Historically, the Danish straits were internal waters of Denmark, though in the modern era their legal status is governed by treaties guaranteeing free passage for shipping.

The three main straits

  • The Øresund (the Sound), between the Danish island of Zealand and Sweden, about 4 km wide at its narrowest point between Helsingør and Helsingborg.
  • The Great Belt (Storebælt), between the Danish islands of Zealand and Funen, crossed by the Great Belt Fixed Link.
  • The Little Belt (Lillebælt), between Funen and the Jutland peninsula.

History

Control of the straits gave Denmark great strategic and economic leverage for centuries. From 1429 to 1857 Denmark levied the Sound Dues on foreign ships passing through the Øresund, a major source of Crown revenue. The dues were abolished by the Copenhagen Convention of 1857, which declared the straits international waterways open to all shipping; the conventions governing passage remain in force and govern transit by warships and merchant vessels alike.

Geography

The straits form the only natural outlet of the Baltic Sea. Their shallow sills limit the inflow of saline North Sea water, which strongly influences the Baltic's low salinity and its sensitive ecology.
