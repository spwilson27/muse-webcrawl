The 2012 Summer Paralympics, the fourteenth Summer Paralympic Games, were held in London, United Kingdom, from 29 August to 9 September 2012, following the 2012 Summer Olympics. They were the largest Paralympic Games ever held at the time.

Overview

Some 4,302 athletes from 164 nations competed in 503 events across 20 sports. The Games returned to the country of the Paralympics' origins: the Stoke Mandeville Games for injured servicemen were first held in England in 1948. Intellectually disabled athletes returned to competition for the first time since 2000.

Highlights

British cyclist Sarah Storey won four gold medals, David Weir won four golds in wheelchair racing, and South African sprinter Oscar Pistorius — who had also competed at the Olympics — won gold in the 400 m T44. China topped the medal table with 231 medals, including 95 golds, followed by Russia and Great Britain.

Reception

Ticket sales exceeded expectations, with 2.7 million tickets sold, and the Games were credited with shifting public attitudes toward disability in the United Kingdom.
