Liquid oxygen

AppearancePale blue liquid
Boiling point−182.96 °C (90.19 K)
Density1.141 g/cm³
Expansion ratio≈ 860 : 1 to gas

Liquid oxygen, abbreviated LOx or LOX, is the liquid form of molecular oxygen. It has a pale blue colour and is strongly paramagnetic. Liquid oxygen is produced industrially by the fractional distillation of liquefied air.

Uses

Liquid oxygen is a common cryogenic oxidiser propellant for spacecraft rocket applications, typically combined with liquid hydrogen, kerosene (RP-1), or methane. It is also used in medicine, in steelmaking and metal cutting, in submarines and aviation breathing systems, and historically in liquid-oxygen explosives.

Hazards

As a powerful oxidiser, liquid oxygen can cause organic materials to burn rapidly and energetically; asphalt and other hydrocarbons can become shock-sensitive explosives on contact. Its very low temperature also causes severe cold burns.
