Urination


The urinary system

Urination, also known as micturition or voiding, is the release of urine from the urinary bladder through the urethra to the outside of the body. It is the urinary system's form of excretion and, in healthy humans and many other animals, is under combined voluntary and autonomic control coordinated by the pontine micturition centre in the brainstem.

Physiology

As the bladder fills — typically to 300–500 mL in an adult — stretch receptors in its wall signal the spinal cord and brain. When voiding is appropriate, the parasympathetic nervous system contracts the detrusor muscle of the bladder wall while the internal and external urethral sphincters relax, allowing urine to flow. Infants void by a simple spinal reflex; continence develops as cortical control matures.

Clinical aspects

Disorders of urination include urinary incontinence, retention, nocturia (waking at night to void), dysuria (painful voiding, often from infection), and increased frequency in conditions such as diabetes, benign prostatic hyperplasia and overactive bladder. Urinary flow rate and post-void residual volume are standard urological measurements.

In other animals

Many mammals use urination for scent marking and territorial communication — canids and felids deposit urine on prominent objects, and some rodents lay down ultraviolet-visible urine trails. Frequency, posture and volume vary widely across species.
