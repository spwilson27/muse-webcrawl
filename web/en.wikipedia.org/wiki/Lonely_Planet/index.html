Lonely Planet is a travel guidebook publisher founded in 1973 by Tony and Maureen Wheeler in Melbourne, Australia. Their first book, Across Asia on the Cheap (1973), was a stapled-together account of their overland journey from Turkey through Asia to Australia along the "hippie trail". It was followed by a guide to South-East Asia — nicknamed the "Yellow Bible" by backpackers — which established the company's practical, budget-oriented style.

Growth

Lonely Planet grew into the world's largest travel guidebook publisher, producing guides to nearly every country, along with phrasebooks, maps, travel literature and, later, a major website and digital products. Its blue-spined guides became a defining accessory of independent backpacker travel, and the company maintained a tradition of headquarters in Footscray, Melbourne, with the relaxed corporate culture symbolised by its founding ethos.

Ownership changes

In 2007 BBC Worldwide acquired a 75% stake in the company, buying the remainder in 2011. The BBC sold Lonely Planet in 2013 at a substantial loss to NC2 Media, the company of American billionaire Brad Kelley. In 2020 NC2 sold the business to Red Ventures. Through these changes Lonely Planet has continued publishing printed guides while expanding digital content and television productions.

Criticism

The guides have been criticised for contributing to the over-tourism of once-quiet destinations and for occasional inaccuracies; a 2008 controversy concerned an author's admission that he had written parts of a Colombia guide without visiting the country, which the company disputed in scope but which drew attention to guidebook research practices.
