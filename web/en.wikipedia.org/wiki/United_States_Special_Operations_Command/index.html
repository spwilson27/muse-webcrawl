The United States Special Operations Command (USSOCOM or SOCOM) is the unified combatant command charged with overseeing the various special operations component commands of the Army, Marine Corps, Navy and Air Force of the United States Armed Forces. It is headquartered at MacDill Air Force Base near Tampa, Florida.

History

SOCOM was established in 1987 under the Nunn–Cohen Amendment to the Goldwater–Nichols Act, prompted by failures such as the 1980 Iran hostage rescue attempt (Operation Eagle Claw). Uniquely among combatant commands, it has service-like responsibilities for organising, training and equipping special operations forces, with its own budget authority (Major Force Program 11).

Components

Service components are U.S. Army Special Operations Command (USASOC), Naval Special Warfare Command (NAVSPECWARCOM), Air Force Special Operations Command (AFSOC) and Marine Forces Special Operations Command (MARSOC). Subordinate joint headquarters include Joint Special Operations Command (JSOC), which controls the most elite special mission units, and theatre special operations commands aligned to the geographic combatant commands.

Missions

Core activities include direct action, special reconnaissance, counterterrorism, unconventional warfare, foreign internal defence, civil affairs, psychological operations, counter-proliferation and hostage rescue. SOCOM forces expanded greatly after the 2001 September 11 attacks, conducting sustained campaigns in Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria and elsewhere.
