May 1968 events in France

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Students and workers brought France to a standstill in May 1968.

The May 1968 events in France (French: Mai 68) were a period of civil unrest throughout France lasting some seven weeks, punctuated by demonstrations, general strikes and the occupation of universities and factories. At its height, roughly ten million workers — about two-thirds of the French workforce — were on strike, and the economy ground to a halt. The protests shook the government of President Charles de Gaulle, who briefly fled the country, and remain a watershed in post-war French social and cultural history.

Background

The movement began among students protesting overcrowded, authoritarian universities, consumer capitalism and American imperialism, against the backdrop of the Vietnam War. The March 22 Movement at Nanterre University, associated with Daniel Cohn-Bendit, agitated against university regulations; when police entered the Sorbonne on 3 May to arrest student protesters, riots erupted in the Latin Quarter.

The crisis

On the night of 10–11 May, students built barricades in the Latin Quarter and fought pitched battles with riot police — the "Night of the Barricades". Trade unions then called a general strike, and factory occupations spread nationwide. Demands broadened from university reform to wage increases and fundamental social change. On 29 May de Gaulle secretly flew to the French military headquarters at Baden-Baden in Germany to assure himself of the army's loyalty, then returned to announce the dissolution of the National Assembly. A massive Gaullist demonstration on the Champs-Élysées on 30 May marked the turning of the tide.

Outcome

The Grenelle agreements of late May conceded substantial wage rises, including a 35 percent increase in the minimum wage. The June elections produced a landslide victory for the Gaullists. Yet the events' cultural legacy — challenges to authority in education, the workplace, gender relations and the media — profoundly reshaped French society, and "May 68" became a byword for youthful revolt worldwide.
