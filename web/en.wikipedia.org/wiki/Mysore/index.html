Mysore

Mysore (officially Mysuru; Kannada: ಮೈಸೂರು) is a city in the southern Indian state of Karnataka. It was the capital of the Kingdom of Mysore from 1399 to 1956 and remains the cultural capital of Karnataka, famed for its palaces, the Dasara festival, Mysore silk, sandalwood products, and yoga schools. The city lies at the base of the Chamundi Hills, about 145 km southwest of Bengaluru.

History

Tradition derives the name from Mahishūru, "abode of Mahisha", after the demon Mahishasura slain by the goddess Chamundeshwari, patron deity of the city. The Wodeyar dynasty founded the Kingdom of Mysore in 1399 and, apart from the period of rule by Hyder Ali and his son Tipu Sultan (1761–1799), governed until Indian independence. Under Hyder Ali and Tipu, Mysore fought four wars against the British East India Company; Tipu was killed defending Srirangapatna in 1799. The Wodeyars were restored under British paramountcy, and under rulers such as Krishnaraja Wodeyar IV and his diwans (including M. Visvesvaraya) Mysore became a model princely state known for education, industry, and public works. The state acceded to India in 1947, and the city was officially renamed Mysuru in 2014.

Attractions and culture

The city's centrepiece is the Mysore Palace (Amba Vilas), an Indo-Saracenic palace completed in 1912 and among India's most visited monuments. During the ten-day Dasara festival the palace is lit by some 100,000 bulbs and a royal procession recalls the Wodeyar era. Other landmarks include the Chamundeshwari Temple atop Chamundi Hills, St. Philomena's Cathedral, the Jaganmohan Palace art gallery, the Mysore Zoo (est. 1892), and Brindavan Gardens at the Krishnarajasagara dam. Mysore gives its name to Mysore pak (a ghee sweet), Mysore masala dosa, Mysore silk sarees, and the Ashtanga yoga tradition taught by K. Pattabhi Jois.

Economy and education

Tourism, information technology (notably Infosys's large training campus), silk and sandalwood industries, and education anchor the economy. The University of Mysore (1916) was the first university in Karnataka, and the city hosts institutions such as the Central Food Technological Research Institute and the Defence Food Research Laboratory.
