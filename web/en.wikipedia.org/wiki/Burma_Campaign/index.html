The Burma Campaign was a series of battles fought in the British colony of Burma (now Myanmar) during the Second World War, principally between the forces of the British Empire and China against the Empire of Japan. It was one of the longest campaigns of the war, lasting from the Japanese invasion of Burma in early 1942 until the Japanese surrender in 1945.

Japanese invasion, 1942

Japan invaded Burma to close the Burma Road, China's supply line from Rangoon to Kunming, and to shield its conquests in Malaya and the East Indies. The British, Indian, and Burmese forces were driven back in the longest retreat in British military history, some 1,000 miles to India, and the Chinese expeditionary force in Burma was also defeated. Rangoon fell in March 1942.

Stalemate and counteroffensives

For much of 1942–1943 the front stabilized along the India–Burma border. Allied operations included the first Arakan offensive, the long-range penetration operations of Orde Wingate's Chindits behind Japanese lines, and American-led efforts in the north under Joseph Stilwell to build the Ledo Road. The Japanese U-Go offensive of 1944, aimed at invading India, was decisively defeated at the battles of Imphal and Kohima by the Fourteenth Army under William Slim — generally regarded as among the greatest defeats suffered by the Japanese Army on land.

Reconquest, 1944–1945

In Operation Capital and its sequel Operation Dracula, the Fourteenth Army crossed the Chindwin and Irrawaddy rivers, captured Mandalay and Meiktila, and raced south to retake Rangoon in May 1945 before the monsoon. Chinese and American forces in the north reopened the land route to China. The last Japanese forces in Burma surrendered following the atomic bombings and the Japanese capitulation in September 1945.
