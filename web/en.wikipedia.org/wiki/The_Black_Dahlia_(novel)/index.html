The Black Dahlia (novel)

Author: James Ellroy
Published: 1987
Genre: Crime fiction, neo-noir
Series: L.A. Quartet (#1)

The Black Dahlia is a crime fiction novel by American author James Ellroy, published in 1987. It is a fictional treatment of the unsolved 1947 murder of Elizabeth Short in Los Angeles, and the first book in Ellroy's L.A. Quartet. The novel is widely credited with launching Ellroy's reputation as a major writer of American crime fiction.

Plot summary

In 1946, former boxers turned LAPD officers Dwight "Bucky" Bleichert and Lee Blanchard fight a publicity boxing match and become partners and friends. When the mutilated body of Elizabeth Short — dubbed "the Black Dahlia" by the press — is found in January 1947, the case consumes both men. Bleichert's investigation draws him into an obsessive pursuit through the city's underworld of pornography, corruption, and a wealthy, decadent family, ultimately leading him to a fictional solution to the crime.

Background

Ellroy has linked his fascination with the Short case to the murder of his own mother, Geneva Ellroy, in 1958, which was also unsolved; he dedicated the novel to her memory. The book blends real figures and events of post-war Los Angeles with invented characters and answers the real, still-open case with a work of fiction.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted into the 2006 film The Black Dahlia, directed by Brian De Palma and starring Josh Hartnett, Scarlett Johansson, Aaron Eckhart, and Hilary Swank. A graphic novel adaptation by Matz and David Fincher, with art by Miles Hyman, was published in 2013.
