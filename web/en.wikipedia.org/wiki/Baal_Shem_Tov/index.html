Israel ben Eliezer (c. 1698/1700 – May 22, 1760), known as the Baal Shem Tov (Hebrew: "Master of the Good Name", often abbreviated Besht), was a Jewish mystic and healer from Poland who is regarded as the founder of Hasidic Judaism.

Biography

Israel was born to poor parents, Eliezer and Sara, in Okopy in Podolia, in what is now Ukraine. Orphaned young, he was raised by the local Jewish community. Biographical details of his early life are heavily overlaid with legend: he worked as a teacher's assistant, a synagogue beadle, and later in a clay and lime quarry in the Carpathian Mountains, where he spent long periods in solitary meditation in nature.

Around 1734–1736, tradition holds, he "revealed" himself publicly as a baal shem — a master of the divine name who could heal the sick and perform wonders through prayer and knowledge of Kabbalah. He settled in the town of Medzhybizh (Międzybóż), which became the centre of his movement.

Teachings

The Besht taught that God is present in all things (panentheism), and that every person, however simple or unlearned, can cleave to God (devekut) through sincere prayer, joy and the sanctification of everyday acts. He opposed excessive asceticism and self-mortification, which he held lead to melancholy, and emphasized worship through joy (simcha), song and storytelling. His doctrine elevated prayer and intention over scholarly study alone, a democratizing message that spread rapidly among the impoverished and persecuted Jews of Eastern Europe in the aftermath of pogroms and the failed messianic movement of Sabbatai Zevi.

He left no writings of his own; his teachings were preserved by his disciples, notably in the collection Shivhei ha-Besht ("In Praise of the Baal Shem Tov", 1815), a compilation of hagiographic tales, and through the works of his successors such as Jacob Joseph of Polonne and Dov Ber of Mezeritch.

Legacy

From the circle of the Besht's disciples, Hasidism grew into a mass movement that reshaped East European Jewish life. His emphasis on the charismatic leader (the tzadik), heartfelt prayer and sacred storytelling remains central to Hasidic communities worldwide. His daughter Adele's descendants include Rabbi Nachman of Breslov.
