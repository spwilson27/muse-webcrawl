Thabo Mvuyelwa Mbeki (born 18 June 1942) is a South African politician who served as the second president of South Africa from 14 June 1999 to 24 September 2008. A leading figure in the African National Congress (ANC), he was Nelson Mandela's deputy president from 1994 to 1999.

Early life and exile

Mbeki was born in the Transkei to a family of ANC activists; his father Govan Mbeki was a leader of the ANC and the South African Communist Party. Thabo joined the ANC Youth League at 14, went into exile in 1962, and studied economics at the University of Sussex in Britain. He rose through the ANC in exile in Lusaka, Zambia, becoming a key diplomat of the liberation movement and a negotiator in the talks that ended apartheid.

Presidency

As president, Mbeki championed the "African Renaissance", founded the New Partnership for Africa's Development (NEPAD), and helped create the African Union. Domestically he maintained orthodox macroeconomic policies and presided over strong growth and an expanding black middle class, though unemployment and inequality remained high. His presidency was overshadowed by his government's stance on HIV/AIDS — questioning the link between HIV and AIDS and delaying antiretroviral treatment, a policy widely condemned by scientists and activists — and by the arms deal corruption controversy.

Resignation

After losing the ANC presidency to Jacob Zuma at the Polokwane conference in December 2007, and following a court judgment suggesting political interference in Zuma's prosecution, the ANC recalled Mbeki, and he resigned in September 2008. He has since remained active in African diplomacy and mediation.
