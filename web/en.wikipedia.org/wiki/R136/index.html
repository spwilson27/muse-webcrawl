R136 (formally Radcliffe 136) is a super star cluster near the centre of 30 Doradus — the Tarantula Nebula — in the Large Magellanic Cloud, about 163,000 light-years from Earth. It is one of the most massive and densely packed young star clusters known, and contains some of the most massive and luminous stars ever observed.

Properties

R136 is only one to two million years old yet contains hundreds of massive O-type stars and Wolf–Rayet stars packed into a region a few parsecs across. Its total mass is estimated at around 450,000 solar masses, and it is considered a probable progenitor of a future globular cluster. The cluster's radiation and winds have blown a cavity in the surrounding nebula and power much of 30 Doradus's glow.

Notable stars

The brightest member, R136a1, is a Wolf–Rayet star that is the most massive and most luminous star known, with an estimated current mass of around 200 solar masses (earlier estimates reached about 265) and a luminosity of several million times that of the Sun. Other members, including R136a2 and R136a3, also exceed 100 solar masses, challenging models of star formation that once suggested an upper mass limit near 150 solar masses.

Observation

R136 was once catalogued as a single supermassive object — even speculated in the 1980s to be a single star of up to thousands of solar masses — until speckle interferometry in 1985 resolved it into a dense cluster. The Hubble Space Telescope has since imaged its core in detail.
