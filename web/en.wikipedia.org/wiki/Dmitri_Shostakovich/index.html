Dmitri Shostakovich

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Dmitri Shostakovich: round glasses, frail and watchful, fire-watching on a Leningrad rooftop during the siege]

Dmitri Dmitriyevich Shostakovich (25 September 1906 – 9 August 1975) was a Soviet composer and pianist who became internationally famous as one of the most celebrated composers of the 20th century. His music is characterised by sharp contrasts, elements of the grotesque, and ambivalent tonality; his output includes fifteen symphonies and fifteen string quartets, six concertos, operas, ballets and film scores.

Shostakovich's career was a survival act under Stalin: denounced in Pravda in 1936 ('Muddle Instead of Music', over his opera Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk) and again in 1948, living for years with a packed suitcase by the door. His Fifth Symphony (1937) was presented as 'a Soviet artist's reply to just criticism'; the Seventh, the 'Leningrad', was composed during the siege and flown out on microfilm to worldwide performance. The meaning of his irony — dissent or compliance — remains fiercely debated since the disputed memoir Testimony (1979).
