The Libro de buen amor ("Book of Good Love") is a long narrative poem written in Castilian in the fourteenth century by Juan Ruiz, known as the Archpriest of Hita. Composed in more than 1,700 stanzas, mostly in the cuaderna vía form, it is considered one of the masterpieces of medieval Spanish literature.

Overview

The poem presents itself as an autobiographical account of the Archpriest's amorous adventures, framed by a stated didactic purpose: to teach readers the difference between buen amor ("good love," the love of God and virtuous conduct) and loco amor ("mad love," carnal and worldly passion). The tension between the declared moral purpose and the vivid, comic depiction of worldly love has generated centuries of scholarly debate about the author's true intent.

Contents

Within its frame, the work gathers a rich miscellany of medieval genres: fables borrowed from Aesop and the medieval bestiary tradition, exempla, allegorical episodes such as the battle between Don Carnal (Carnival) and Doña Cuaresma (Lent), debates between the poet and Don Amor (Love), satirical portraits of clergy and society, parodies of epic and courtly literature, and a famous sequence in which the go-between Trotaconventos (a name later echoed by the Celestina of Fernando de Rojas) arranges the narrator's love affairs.

Influence

Three manuscripts preserve the poem. Its mixture of learned and popular registers, its irony, and its gallery of vivid characters established it as a foundational text of Spanish literature, a bridge between medieval clerical verse (the mester de clerecía) and the prose fiction of the Renaissance.
