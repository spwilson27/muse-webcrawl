Nitisinone, sold under the brand name Orfadin among others, is a medication used to treat hereditary tyrosinemia type 1 (HT-1) and, in some jurisdictions, alkaptonuria. Also known by its developmental name NTBC (2-(2-nitro-4-trifluoromethylbenzoyl)-1,3-cyclohexanedione), it was originally developed as a herbicide of the triketone class before its pharmacological value was recognized.

Medical uses

In HT-1, a deficiency of fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase causes accumulation of toxic metabolites that destroy the liver and kidneys and can cause liver cancer. Nitisinone blocks the tyrosine degradation pathway upstream, at the enzyme 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate dioxygenase, preventing formation of the toxic compounds. Combined with a diet restricted in tyrosine and phenylalanine, it transformed the prognosis of HT-1: children treated early, especially when identified by newborn screening, usually avoid liver failure and transplantation. Nitisinone is also used in alkaptonuria, where blocking the same pathway reduces production of homogentisic acid by more than 95%; it was approved for this indication in the European Union in 2020 following the DevelopAKUre trials.

Adverse effects

Because the drug raises blood tyrosine levels, excessive tyrosine can cause corneal crystals and eye irritation, skin lesions, and low platelet and white-cell counts; patients are monitored with regular blood tests, eye examinations, and dietary control. Other reported effects include hair thinning and gastrointestinal upset.

History

Triketone herbicides were found to inhibit 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate dioxygenase in plants. Swedish researchers led by Sven Lindstedt reasoned that the same inhibition could protect patients with tyrosinemia, and the first patients were treated in 1991–92 with dramatic success. Nitisinone received U.S. FDA approval in 2002 and European approval in 2005 as an orphan drug.
