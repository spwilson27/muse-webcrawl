Volvo

Volvo (Latin for "I roll") is a Swedish multinational group of companies headquartered in Gothenburg. The name is used by two separate enterprises that share the Volvo trademark: AB Volvo, which manufactures trucks, buses, construction equipment, and marine and industrial engines; and Volvo Cars, the passenger-car maker.

History

Volvo was founded in 1927 as a subsidiary of ball-bearing maker SKF; the first car, the ÖV 4, left the Gothenburg factory that year. Truck production began in 1928. Volvo built a reputation for safety, introducing the three-point seat belt in 1959 and making the patent freely available.

The two companies

AB Volvo sold its car division to Ford in 1999; Ford in turn sold Volvo Cars to China's Zhejiang Geely Holding in 2010. Volvo Cars, maker of models such as the XC90 and the electric EX30, listed in Stockholm in 2021, while AB Volvo remains one of the world's largest heavy-truck manufacturers, owning brands including Volvo Trucks, Renault Trucks, and Mack Trucks. The two firms jointly own the trademark through Volvo Trademark Holding AB.
