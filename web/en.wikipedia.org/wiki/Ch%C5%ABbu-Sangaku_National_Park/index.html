Chūbu-Sangaku National Park

Chūbu-Sangaku National Park (中部山岳国立公園) is a national park in the Chūbu region of Japan, centred on the Hida Mountains — the "Northern Alps" — and extending across parts of Nagano, Gifu, Toyama, and Niigata prefectures. Established in 1934, it is one of Japan's oldest national parks and protects some of the country's highest and most rugged alpine scenery.

LocationChūbu region, Honshu, Japan
Established4 December 1934
AreaAbout 1,743 km²

Features

The park includes celebrated peaks such as Mount Hotaka (3,190 m), Mount Yari, Mount Tate, and Mount Shirouma, along with the dramatic gorge of Kurobe and the mountain resort of Kamikōchi on the Azusa River, a gateway for climbers. The Tateyama–Kurobe Alpine Route crosses the park, famous for its spring snow corridor. Wildlife includes Japanese serow, Asiatic black bears, and rock ptarmigan.
