ADAMTS13 (a disintegrin and metalloproteinase with a thrombospondin type 1 motif, member 13) — also known as von Willebrand factor-cleaving protease (VWFCP) — is a zinc-containing metalloprotease enzyme that cleaves von Willebrand factor (VWF), a large protein involved in blood clotting. It is secreted into the blood, principally by hepatic stellate cells and endothelial cells, and breaks down the ultra-large multimers of VWF that would otherwise promote excessive platelet aggregation.

Function

Under the shear stress of flowing blood, VWF unfolds to expose a cleavage site (the Tyr1605–Met1606 bond in its A2 domain), which ADAMTS13 cuts, trimming the multimer to smaller, less adhesive forms. This regulation keeps platelet adhesion at sites of vascular injury proportionate to need.

Clinical significance

Severe deficiency of ADAMTS13 activity — typically below 10% of normal — causes thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), a life-threatening thrombotic microangiopathy in which uncleaved ultra-large VWF multimers trigger widespread platelet-rich microthrombi. The deficiency may be inherited, as in congenital TTP (Upshaw–Schulman syndrome, caused by mutations in the ADAMTS13 gene), or acquired through autoantibodies that inhibit the enzyme or accelerate its clearance. Measurement of ADAMTS13 activity and inhibitor titres is central to diagnosis, and plasma exchange — which removes antibody and replenishes the enzyme — is the mainstay of treatment for acquired disease. A recombinant form of ADAMTS13 has been developed for congenital TTP.
