Bruce Springsteen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Bruce Springsteen on stage with the E Street Band, guitar raised, leading a stadium singalong in his seventies]

Bruce Frederick Joseph Springsteen (born 23 September 1949) is an American singer, songwriter and bandleader, nicknamed 'the Boss', whose work with the E Street Band made him rock's laureate of working-class America. His breakthrough Born to Run (1975), the stark Nebraska (1982), and the blockbuster Born in the U.S.A. (1984) anchor a catalogue of some 21 studio albums and marathon concerts routinely running past three hours.

Springsteen's songs — 'Thunder Road', 'The River', 'Dancing in the Dark', 'The Rising' (2002, his response to 9/11) — are cited here by title. His honours include 20 Grammy Awards, an Academy Award ('Streets of Philadelphia', 1993), a Tony Award for Springsteen on Broadway (2017–21) and the Presidential Medal of Freedom (2016).

Asbury Park to Born to Run

Raised in Freehold, New Jersey, in a household strained by his father's unemployment and depression, Springsteen played the Jersey Shore bar circuit for a decade before Columbia's John Hammond (who had signed Dylan) took him on as a 'new Dylan' folk singer. Two fast albums failed to sell; Born to Run — eight tracks of operatic escape, produced in agony with Jon Landau (who had written of seeing 'rock and roll future' in him, in the famous review) — saved his career at 25.

The elder statesman

The River's double-album breadth, Nebraska's home-recorded murder ballads, Born in the U.S.A.'s stadium anthems (and its title track's famous misreading as uncomplicated patriotism, against the veteran's complaint at its heart) made him the eighties' American mirror. Later landmarks: The Ghost of Tom Joad (1995), the E Street reunion (1999), The Rising, the Seeger Sessions, the memoir Born to Run (2016) and Broadway residency, and the 2023– sellout world tours in his seventies.
