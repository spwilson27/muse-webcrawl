S-300 missile system

TypeLong-range surface-to-air missile / anti-ballistic missile system
Place of originSoviet Union / Russia
In service1978–present
DesignerAlmaz / NPO Almaz (now Almaz-Antey)
NATO reporting namesSA-10 Grumble, SA-12 Gladiator/Giant, SA-20 Gargoyle, SA-23

The S-300 (Russian: С-300) is a series of long-range surface-to-air missile systems developed by the Soviet Union and Russia, principally by NPO Almaz. First deployed in 1978, the S-300 was designed to defend large areas and strategic targets against aircraft and cruise missiles, with later variants gaining substantial capability against ballistic missiles. It is considered one of the most potent air-defence systems of its era and has been widely exported.

Variants

The family divides into branches developed for the Soviet air defence forces (PVO) and for ground forces air defence. Key lines include the S-300P (SA-10 Grumble), the mobile S-300V (SA-12 Gladiator/Giant) optimised against ballistic missiles, the improved S-300PMU series (SA-20 Gargoyle) widely exported, and the naval S-300F (SA-N-6). Successor and derivative systems include the S-350, S-400 Triumf, and S-500.

Design and operation

An S-300 battery typically combines surveillance and target-acquisition radars, a command post, engagement radar, and several transporter-erector-launchers carrying four (or more) vertically launched missiles in sealed canisters. Depending on variant and missile type (such as the 48N6), engagement ranges extend from a few kilometres to roughly 150–200 km against aerodynamic targets, at altitudes up to about 27–30 km. The system's radars can track and engage multiple targets simultaneously.

Operators and combat use

The S-300 has been operated by Russia, Ukraine, China, Iran, Syria, and numerous other states. It has seen extensive combat use in the Russo-Ukrainian War, where both sides field variants, and older systems have been adapted by Russia for surface-to-surface strike roles. The controversial deliveries of the S-300PMU-2 to Iran and the S-300 to Syria drew sustained international attention.
