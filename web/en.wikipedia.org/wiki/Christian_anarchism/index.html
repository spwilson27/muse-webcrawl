Christian anarchism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christian anarchism is a Christian movement in political theology that claims anarchism is inherent in Christianity and the Gospels. Its adherents hold that there is only one source of authority to which Christians are ultimately answerable — God — expressed in the teachings of Jesus, and that earthly powers such as government and established churches do not, and should not, hold authority over them.

[Hero image: cover of Leo Tolstoy's The Kingdom of God Is Within You — image placeholder]

Basis

Christian anarchists draw especially on the Sermon on the Mount, with its teachings on non-resistance to evil and turning the other cheek, and on the communal life of the early church described in the Acts of the Apostles. Many reject the interpretation of Romans 13 ("let every person be subject to the governing authorities") as requiring obedience to unjust states, arguing that submission means non-violent refusal to participate in evil rather than compliance.

Notable figures

The movement's most famous advocate is Leo Tolstoy, whose book The Kingdom of God Is Within You (1894) argued that the church had corrupted Jesus's pacifist teaching and influenced Gandhi. Other figures include Adin Ballou, Dorothy Day and the Catholic Worker Movement, Ammon Hennacy, and the theologian Jacques Ellul. Movements such as the Diggers of the English Civil War period are seen as precursors.

Practices

Christian anarchists commonly practise pacifism, voluntary poverty, tax resistance to military spending, and communal living, and they are often active in peace and anti-war campaigns.
