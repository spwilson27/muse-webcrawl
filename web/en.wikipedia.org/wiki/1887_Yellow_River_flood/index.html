The 1887 Yellow River flood was a catastrophic flood of the Yellow River (Huang He) in Qing-dynasty China, beginning in late September 1887 when the river breached its dikes near Zhengzhou, Henan. It is considered one of the deadliest floods — and one of the worst natural disasters — in recorded history.

Course of the flood

Heavy rains raised the river, whose bed had silted up above the surrounding plain, until the dikes gave way. An enormous sheet of water spread over the low-lying North China Plain, inundating thousands of square kilometres and countless villages. Some estimates put the flooded area at up to 130,000 km².

Toll and aftermath

Death toll estimates range from about 900,000 to 2 million, including deaths from the famine and disease that followed. The river was not brought back under control until 1889, after two years of dike-rebuilding works. The disaster strained Qing finances and relief capacity and became a symbol of dynastic decline.
