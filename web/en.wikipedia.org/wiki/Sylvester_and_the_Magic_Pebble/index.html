Sylvester and the Magic Pebble

Author / illustratorWilliam Steig
Published1969
PublisherWindmill Books / Simon & Schuster
AwardsCaldecott Medal (1970)

Sylvester and the Magic Pebble is a children's picture book written and illustrated by William Steig, published in 1969. It won the Caldecott Medal in 1970 for the most distinguished American picture book for children, and is considered a classic of the form.

Plot

Sylvester Duncan, a young donkey who collects pebbles, finds a shining red pebble that grants wishes. Startled by a hungry lion on his way home, he panics and wishes himself into a rock — and, as a rock, cannot hold the pebble to wish himself back. His parents search in vain and grieve through the seasons, while Sylvester sits frozen on Strawberry Hill, conscious but unable to communicate. A year later his parents, out on a picnic, find the pebble and wish for their son; the family is joyfully reunited, locks the pebble in an iron safe, and decides that what they already have is all they want.

Reception

Steig's watercolor illustrations and the story's gentle treatment of fear, loss and gratitude have kept the book in print for decades. It drew some controversy in the 1970s when police organisations objected to the book's depiction of police officers as pigs, leading to challenges in some school libraries.
