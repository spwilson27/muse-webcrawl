Nadia Murad


Nadia Murad Basee Taha
Born: 10 March 1993, Kocho, Iraq
Occupation: Human rights activist
Awards: Nobel Peace Prize (2018)

Nadia Murad (born 1993) is an Iraqi Yazidi human rights activist who lives in Germany. In 2014 she was abducted from her home village of Kocho in the Sinjar region of Iraq by the Islamic State (IS), during its genocidal campaign against the Yazidis, and held as a sex slave for three months before escaping.

Activism

After reaching safety, Murad became a leading voice for survivors of genocide and sexual violence. In December 2015 she addressed the United Nations Security Council on human trafficking and conflict, and in 2016 she was appointed the first UN Goodwill Ambassador for the Dignity of Survivors of Human Trafficking. She founded Nadia's Initiative, which advocates for the rebuilding of the Yazidi homeland in Sinjar and supports survivors.

Nobel Peace Prize

In 2018, Murad was jointly awarded the Nobel Peace Prize with Denis Mukwege "for their efforts to end the use of sexual violence as a weapon of war and armed conflict". She also received the Sakharov Prize (2016) and the Václav Havel Human Rights Prize. Her memoir, The Last Girl: My Story of Captivity, and My Fight Against the Islamic State (2017), recounts her experience. Murad has campaigned for legal accountability for IS crimes, including the work of the UN investigative team UNITAD.
