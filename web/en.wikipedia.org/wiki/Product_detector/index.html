Product detector

A product detector is a type of demodulator used for AM and SSB signals. It multiplies the incoming signal by a locally generated carrier signal rather than detecting the envelope of the received signal, as an envelope detector does. Product detectors are used in radio receivers for single-sideband (SSB) voice and Morse code (CW) reception, where the envelope of the signal does not directly represent the audio.


A product detector mixes the signal with a local oscillator.

Operation

The received signal is fed to a mixer together with a signal from a beat frequency oscillator (BFO) at or near the original carrier frequency. The product of the two signals contains sum and difference frequencies; a low-pass filter keeps the difference term, which is the recovered audio. For AM reception, the local oscillator must be phase-synchronised with the carrier for undistorted output, a refinement sometimes called synchronous detection.

Advantages

Compared with envelope detection, a product detector offers better signal-to-noise performance at low signal levels, less distortion from selective fading, and the ability to receive suppressed-carrier signals that an envelope detector cannot demodulate at all. Modern implementations use balanced mixers, diode rings, or digital signal processing in software-defined radios.
