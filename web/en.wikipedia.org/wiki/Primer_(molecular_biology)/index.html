Primer (molecular biology)

Primer
TypeShort nucleic acid sequence
Typical length18–24 bases (in vitro)
FunctionStarting point for DNA synthesis
Used inPCR, DNA sequencing, replication in vivo

A primer is a short single-stranded nucleic acid used by all living organisms in the initiation of DNA synthesis. A group of enzymes called primases synthesize primers in living organisms; DNA polymerases can only add nucleotides to the 3′ end of an existing nucleic acid, so a primer annealed to the template strand provides the free 3′-hydroxyl group from which synthesis begins.

In living organisms, primers are short strands of RNA, typically 5 to 10 nucleotides long, synthesized by primase on both the leading strand (one primer) and the lagging strand (one primer per Okazaki fragment). The RNA primers are later removed and replaced with DNA.

Laboratory primers

In the laboratory, scientists design synthetic primers of single-stranded DNA, usually 18–24 bases long, that are chemically synthesized and exactly complementary to sites flanking a target region. Pairs of primers are the key reagents of the polymerase chain reaction (PCR), where they define the segment to be amplified, and of Sanger sequencing, where a single primer initiates the sequencing reaction.

Primer design

Good primer design balances several properties:

  • Length — long enough for specificity (typically 18–24 bases) but short enough to anneal efficiently.
  • Melting temperature (Tm) — usually 50–65 °C; both primers of a pair should have similar Tm.
  • GC content — typically 40–60%, with a GC "clamp" at the 3′ end promoting stable binding.
  • Specificity — the sequence, especially at the 3′ end, should match only the intended target, checked with tools such as BLAST.
  • Secondary structure — primers should avoid self-complementarity, hairpins and primer–dimer formation.

Degenerate primers contain mixtures of bases at some positions and are used to amplify related sequences, for example from different species. Primers may also carry non-templated tails adding restriction sites, promoters or barcodes to the product.
