Change detection

Change detection is the process of identifying differences in the state of an object or phenomenon by observing it at different times. The term is used in several fields, most prominently in remote sensing and geographic information systems, where it describes techniques for comparing imagery or spatial data acquired over the same area at different dates in order to map changes such as deforestation, urban growth, flooding, or crop condition.

Change maps derived from multi-temporal satellite imagery

In remote sensing

In remote sensing, change detection generally involves the digital comparison of co-registered images from two or more dates. Common approaches include:

  • Image differencing and ratioing – subtracting or dividing pixel values between dates and thresholding the result.
  • Change vector analysis – measuring both the magnitude and the direction of change in multi-spectral space.
  • Post-classification comparison – classifying each date independently and comparing the resulting thematic maps.
  • Index differencing – comparing derived indices such as the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) between dates.
  • Time-series analysis – using dense stacks of images, for example from Landsat or Sentinel-2, to detect abrupt changes and gradual trends.

Accuracy depends on precise geometric registration, correction for atmospheric and illumination differences, and consistent sensor calibration. Results are typically summarized in a change matrix that records how much area converted from one land-cover class to another.

Applications

Change detection underpins forest monitoring and deforestation alerts, disaster damage assessment, urban sprawl studies, glacier retreat measurement, agricultural monitoring, and military reconnaissance. Automated systems now issue near-real-time alerts of forest disturbance based on satellite time series.

Other uses

In statistics and quality control, change detection (or change point detection) refers to identifying the time at which the statistical properties of a sequence change, a problem addressed by methods such as cumulative sum (CUSUM) charts and Bayesian online change point detection. In software engineering, the term describes algorithms that identify modifications between versions of files or data structures.
