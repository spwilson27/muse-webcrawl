Planetary system

A planetary system is a set of gravitationally bound non-stellar objects in or out of orbit around a star or star system. Generally speaking, systems with one or more planets constitute a planetary system, although such systems may also consist of bodies such as dwarf planets, asteroids, natural satellites, meteoroids, comets, planetesimals and circumstellar discs. The Solar System is an example of a planetary system, in which Earth and other planets orbit the Sun.

History

The idea that other stars might host planetary systems has a long history, from ancient speculations about a plurality of worlds to the heliocentric model of Nicolaus Copernicus. The first confirmed exoplanets were found in the 1990s, and since then thousands of exoplanets have been detected, revealing a wide diversity of system architectures.

Formation and evolution

Planetary systems are thought to form from protoplanetary discs of gas and dust surrounding young stars, through processes such as core accretion and gravitational instability. Systems evolve over time through planetary migration, scattering, and orbital resonances, which shape their final configurations.

Structure

Planetary systems vary widely: some stars host compact systems of small rocky planets in tight orbits, others host gas giants on wide or eccentric orbits, and many contain both. Systems around multiple stars (circumbinary planets) also exist. As of the mid-2020s, more than 5,000 exoplanets in several thousand systems have been confirmed.
