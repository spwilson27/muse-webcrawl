Rapid eye movement sleep behavior disorder

Rapid eye movement sleep behavior disorder (RBD) is a sleep disorder (a parasomnia) in which people act out their dreams. During normal REM sleep, the body is paralysed (atonia); in RBD this paralysis is incomplete or absent, so the sleeper physically enacts dream content, sometimes violently, risking injury to themselves or a bed partner.

Symptoms and diagnosis

Episodes involve talking, shouting, punching, kicking or jumping from bed in response to dream content, which is often vivid or frightening. The person usually awakens quickly and can recall the dream. Diagnosis requires video polysomnography demonstrating elevated muscle tone during REM sleep (REM sleep without atonia) together with documented dream-enactment behaviour.

Causes and associations

RBD may be idiopathic or secondary to narcolepsy, certain antidepressants, or neurological disease. Idiopathic RBD is strongly associated with later development of alpha-synuclein neurodegenerative disorders — Parkinson's disease, dementia with Lewy bodies and multiple system atrophy — with a majority of patients eventually converting over 10–15 years. It is therefore regarded as an important prodromal marker for these diseases.

Treatment

Management includes making the sleep environment safe (removing sharp objects, padding furniture), melatonin, and in some cases clonazepam. Offending medications may be reviewed.
