Time's Up

Time's Up is a movement and organisation founded in 2018 by women in the entertainment industry to combat sexual harassment and gender inequality in workplaces. It was announced on January 1, 2018, in an open letter published as a full-page advertisement in The New York Times and signed by more than 300 actresses, agents, writers, directors, producers, and entertainment executives, in response to the Harvey Weinstein abuse allegations and the wider #MeToo movement.

Legal Defense Fund

The centrepiece of the initiative was the Time's Up Legal Defense Fund, administered by the National Women's Law Center, which raised more than $20 million in its first months — the largest GoFundMe campaign of its time — to subsidise legal representation for people, especially low-wage workers, who had experienced sexual harassment or assault at work. A network of volunteer attorneys took on cases spanning industries from Hollywood to agriculture and food service. At the 75th Golden Globe Awards in January 2018, most attendees wore black and many wore "Time's Up" pins in support.

Organisation and later history

Time's Up developed into a nonprofit advocacy organisation with affiliated chapters in other industries and countries, campaigning for pay equity, diverse leadership, and stronger harassment policies. It also faced internal scrutiny: in 2021 its chief executive Tina Tchen resigned after reporting that leaders had advised New York governor Andrew Cuomo, who himself faced harassment allegations, and the organisation subsequently restructured its board and operations. The celebrity-led organisation wound down its central operations in early 2023, with the Legal Defense Fund continuing at the National Women's Law Center.
