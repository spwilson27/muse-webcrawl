The M2 Bradley, named after General Omar Bradley, is an American infantry fighting vehicle that is a member of the Bradley Fighting Vehicle family. It is manufactured by BAE Systems Land & Armaments (formerly United Defense) and entered United States Army service in 1981.

Design

The M2 carries a crew of three — commander, gunner and driver — plus six or seven infantry dismounts. Its turret mounts a 25 mm M242 Bushmaster chain gun and a twin TOW anti-tank guided missile launcher, with a coaxial 7.62 mm machine gun. The vehicle is protected by aluminum and spaced laminate armor, later supplemented by explosive reactive armor tiles, and it is amphibious with preparation.

Development and controversy

The Bradley's long development during the 1970s was marked by shifting requirements and controversy over cost, survivability and testing practices — a process later dramatized in the book and film The Pentagon Wars. Despite the criticism, the vehicle proved effective in service.

Combat history

Bradleys performed prominently in the 1991 Gulf War, where they destroyed more Iraqi armored vehicles than the M1 Abrams in some accounts, and again in the 2003 invasion of Iraq. Upgraded variants including the M2A2 and M2A3 added improved armor, digital fire control and thermal sights. The related M3 Bradley is the cavalry/scout variant. The United States has supplied Bradleys to Ukraine following Russia's 2022 invasion, and to other partners.
