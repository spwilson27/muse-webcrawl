E-scooter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An e-scooter or electric scooter is a two-wheeled vehicle powered by an electric motor, most commonly a standing kick scooter with a small deck, handlebars and a rechargeable battery. E-scooters became a prominent form of micromobility in the late 2010s, both as privately owned vehicles and through dockless sharing schemes in cities worldwide.

Design

Typical e-scooters have a hub motor in the front or rear wheel, a lithium-ion battery pack under the deck, a thumb or twist throttle, and electronic or drum braking, often supplemented by a rear fender foot brake. Most consumer models weigh between 12 and 25 kg, fold for carrying, have a range of roughly 15–40 km per charge, and are limited to speeds of about 25 km/h (15.5 mph) in many jurisdictions, though performance models can be much faster.

Sharing systems

Dockless scooter-sharing companies such as Bird and Lime launched from 2017 onwards, deploying fleets that users unlock with a smartphone app and pay for by the minute. The rapid, sometimes unpermitted rollouts provoked regulatory responses: cities introduced permits, fleet caps, parking rules and speed limits, and some banned rental scooters from pavements or altogether.

Safety and regulation

Studies of emergency-department visits found head injuries and fractures common among injured riders, with helmet use rare. Legal status varies widely: some countries treat e-scooters like bicycles, others like mopeds, and in some places privately owned scooters remain illegal on public roads while rental schemes operate under trial licences. Concerns have also been raised about pavement clutter, pedestrian conflicts, battery fires and the environmental footprint of short-lived shared scooters.
