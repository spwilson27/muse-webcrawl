José Francisco de San Martín y Matorras (1778–1850) was an Argentine general and statesman who led the southern part of South America's struggle for independence from the Spanish Empire. He is regarded as a national hero of Argentina, Chile, and Peru.

Campaigns

After service in the Spanish army against Napoleon, San Martín returned to South America in 1812. As commander of the Army of the Andes, he achieved in 1817 the famous Crossing of the Andes from Argentina into Chile, defeating royalist forces at Chacabuco and Maipú and securing Chilean independence. In 1820 he sailed to Peru and proclaimed Peruvian independence in Lima in 1821. After his 1822 meeting with Simón Bolívar in Guayaquil, he withdrew from public life and spent his later years in exile in France.
