Laxdæla saga

AuthorUnknown
LanguageOld Norse
GenreIcelandic saga (family saga)

Laxdæla saga (Icelandic pronunciation: [ˈlaksˌtaiːla ˈsaːɣa]) is one of the Icelanders' sagas (Íslendingasögur), probably written in western Iceland sometime between 1245 and 1270. It tells the story of the people of Laxárdalur ("Salmon River Valley") in Iceland across several generations, from the settlement era in the late ninth century to the early eleventh century. It is notable for its unusually prominent and well-drawn female characters.

Synopsis

The saga begins with Ketill Flatnose and his daughter Auðr the Deep-Minded (Unnr djúpúðga), who leads her family to Iceland after the death of her husband and son. The narrative traces the fortunes of her descendants, including Höskuldr Dala-Kollsson and his sons Óláfr pái (the Peacock) and Þorleikr.

The central tragedy of the saga concerns Guðrún Ósvífrsdóttir, the most famous of the saga's women, and her loves: she is married four times, and her great love Kjartan Óláfsson and his foster-brother and cousin Bolli Þorleiksson are drawn into a fatal feud, inflamed by Guðrún herself, that ends with Kjartan's killing by Bolli and Bolli's subsequent death at the hands of Kjartan's brothers. In old age, asked by her son which husband she loved most, Guðrún gives the saga's most famous answer: "I treated worst the one I loved best" (Þeim var ek verst er ek unna mest).

Themes and reception

Laxdæla saga stands out among the family sagas for its romantic and tragic tone, its descriptions of clothing, dreams and courtly manners, and its sophisticated narrative structure. It survives principally in the fourteenth-century compilation Möðruvallabók. It has been translated into English several times and remains one of the most widely read of all the Icelandic sagas.
