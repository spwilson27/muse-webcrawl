Muscovy

Muscovy is an historical name, used mainly in the West, for the Grand Duchy of Moscow and later the Tsardom of Russia, particularly in the period from the 13th to the 17th centuries.

The Grand Duchy grew from a small principality under the rule of Daniel of Moscow and his descendants, gradually absorbing other Rus' principalities, throwing off Mongol-Tatar overlordship in 1480, and expanding under Ivan III and Ivan IV, who took the title Tsar in 1547.

Usage

The Muscovy Company of London, chartered in 1555, traded with Russia via the White Sea. The term "Muscovite" was also applied to inhabitants of the state. Modern historians use "Muscovy" chiefly for the pre-Petrine Russian state before Peter the Great proclaimed the Russian Empire in 1721.
