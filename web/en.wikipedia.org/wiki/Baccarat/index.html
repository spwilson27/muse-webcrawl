Baccarat

Baccarat (also written baccara) is a card game played at casinos, comparing the hands of two participants — the "player" and the "banker" — whose card totals are counted modulo ten. The most common version, punto banco, is a pure game of chance: neither hand's play involves any decision. Baccarat has long been associated with high-stakes private salons and generates the majority of casino revenue in Macau, the world's largest gambling market.

Rules

Aces count one, cards 2–9 count face value, and tens and face cards count zero. A hand's value is the units digit of the sum of its cards, so a hand of 7 and 8 is worth 5. A two-card total of 8 or 9 is a "natural". Under punto banco's fixed drawing rules (the tableau), the player hand draws a third card on totals of 0–5 and stands on 6–7; whether the banker draws depends on the banker's total and the player's third card. Bets on the banker win pay a 5% commission because the banker bet wins slightly more often than it loses.

History and culture

The game's origins are disputed, with claims of an Italian game invented by Felix Falguière in the 1400s and its development in 19th-century France. The variant chemin de fer, in which players make drawing decisions and bet against each other, remains popular in France. Ian Fleming made chemin de fer James Bond's game of choice, featuring famously in Casino Royale. In Macau, baccarat accounts for the overwhelming majority of casino gaming revenue, dominated by VIP rooms.
