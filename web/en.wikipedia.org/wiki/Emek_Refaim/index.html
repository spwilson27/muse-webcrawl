Emek Refaim

Emek Refaim (Hebrew: עמק רפאים, "Valley of the Giants") is a neighborhood and main street in Jerusalem. The neighborhood, established in the 19th century, forms the center of the German Colony, founded by members of the German Templar Society in the 1870s. The biblical Valley of Rephaim runs nearby, southwest of the city.

History

The German Templers built one- and two-story stone houses along the valley floor following the layout of their settlements in Germany. After the Templers were deported by the British during World War II, the neighborhood became home to Jewish residents, and many of the original houses have been preserved for their distinctive architecture.

Today

Emek Refaim street is now a popular commercial strip of cafes, restaurants, and boutiques, and the neighborhood is among Jerusalem's most affluent. Plans to run a line of the Jerusalem Light Rail along the street were the subject of extended public debate over the impact on the street's historic trees and character.
