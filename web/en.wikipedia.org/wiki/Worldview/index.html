Worldview

A worldview (or world-view) is the fundamental cognitive orientation of an individual or society encompassing their knowledge, culture, philosophy, values, emotions and ethics. The term is a calque of the German Weltanschauung, composed of Welt ("world") and Anschauung ("perception" or "view").

Concept

A worldview functions as a framework through which people interpret experience and interact with the world. It answers — explicitly or implicitly — questions about the nature of reality, how knowledge is possible, what is good, and what it means to be human. Worldviews may be held consciously, as in a formal philosophy or religion, or may operate as unexamined assumptions absorbed from one's culture and upbringing.

History of the term

Immanuel Kant used Weltanschauung in his Critique of Judgment (1790), and the concept was developed by Wilhelm Dilthey, who distinguished types of worldview (naturalism, idealism of freedom, and objective idealism), and by Karl Jaspers in his Psychologie der Weltanschauungen (1919). In linguistics, the related idea that language shapes worldview is associated with Wilhelm von Humboldt and the Sapir–Whorf hypothesis.

Worldviews and systems

Comparative study of worldviews is a theme in philosophy of religion, anthropology and cognitive science. Scholars such as Ninian Smart have analysed religions and secular ideologies alike as systems of dimensions — doctrinal, mythic, ethical, ritual, experiential, social and material — that together constitute a worldview.
