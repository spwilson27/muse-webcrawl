Bagrationi dynasty

The Bagrationi dynasty (Georgian: ბაგრატიონი, bagrationi) is a royal dynasty that reigned in Georgia from the Early Middle Ages until the early 19th century, being among the oldest extant Christian ruling dynasties in the world. In modern usage, the name is applied to the royal family descended from the bloodline of the Bagratuni dynasty of Armenia, which moved to Georgia.

Origins

The Georgian Bagrationi claimed descent, in later tradition, from King David of Israel. Historically, the family emerged in the 8th–9th centuries in the region of Tao-Klarjeti in southwestern Georgia. Adarnase and his son Ashot I established the principality of Iberia, and in 888 Adarnase IV was crowned King of the Iberians, laying the basis for the unified Georgian monarchy achieved under Bagrat III in 1008.

Golden age and fragmentation

Under David IV "the Builder" and Queen Tamar the Great, Georgia became a dominant regional power in the Caucasus in the 12th and early 13th centuries. Mongol invasions and later Timur's campaigns fragmented the kingdom, and by the late 15th century it had split into the kingdoms of Kartli, Kakheti and Imereti, each ruled by Bagrationi branches.

End of the monarchy

In 1801 the Russian Empire annexed Kartli-Kakheti in violation of the 1783 Treaty of Georgievsk, and Imereti followed in 1810. The Bagrationi were deprived of their thrones and many were deported to Russia, where branches of the family were recognised as princely families of the Russian nobility. Descendants remain active in Georgian public life, and the dynasty's senior line is associated with the country's monarchist tradition.
