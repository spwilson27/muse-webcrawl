Katherine Paterson

Katherine Womeldorf Paterson (born 31 October 1932) is an American author of children's and young adult books, best known for the novel Bridge to Terabithia (1977). She has won two Newbery Medals and two National Book Awards, and received the Hans Christian Andersen Award (1998) and the Astrid Lindgren Memorial Award (2006) for her body of work.

Life

Paterson was born in Huai'an, China, to Christian missionary parents, and spent parts of her childhood in China and the American South. She studied English and American literature at King College, Tennessee, and taught and worked as a missionary in Japan before turning to writing.

Works

Bridge to Terabithia, inspired by a tragedy in her son's life, won the Newbery Medal in 1978; Jacob Have I Loved (1980) won it again in 1981. Her historical novels include Of Nightingales That Weep (1974), The Great Gilly Hopkins (1978), Lyddie (1991) and Jip: His Story (1996). She served as National Ambassador for Young People's Literature from 2010 to 2011.
