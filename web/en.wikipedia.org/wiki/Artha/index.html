Artha

Artha — material prosperity, one of the four aims of life in Hindu thought

Artha (Sanskrit: अर्थ) is one of the four aims of human life in Indian philosophy, the puruṣārthas. The word literally means "meaning", "object", "purpose" or "wealth", and in the framework of the four aims it denotes material prosperity, economic security and worldly success pursued within the bounds of dharma (moral duty).

The four aims

The puruṣārthas are usually listed as dharma (righteousness, duty), artha (prosperity), kāma (pleasure, desire) and mokṣa (liberation). The first three are sometimes grouped as the trivarga, the goals of worldly life, with mokṣa standing as the ultimate aim. Traditions differ on their ranking: texts on statecraft and worldly life, such as Kautilya's Arthaśāstra, exalt artha as foundational, arguing that livelihood and a well-ordered economy underpin both dharma and kāma, while renunciant traditions subordinate artha to mokṣa.

Scope

Artha covers the acquisition and stewardship of wealth, property, livelihood, political power and social standing — the material means necessary for a stable household and society. It is not greed: classical literature distinguishes artha pursued in accordance with dharma from wealth amassed by unjust means, which is condemned. The concept is most fully developed in the arthaśāstra literature, which addresses economics, law, administration and statecraft.

Stages of life

In the classical āśrama system, the pursuit of artha belongs especially to the householder stage (gṛhastha), when a person is expected to earn, support a family, pay debts to gods, sages and ancestors, and give in charity. In later stages of life, artha is progressively relinquished in favour of liberation.
