Gustav Friedrich Schmoller, from 1908 von Schmoller (24 June 1838 – 27 June 1917), was a German economist and social scientist, and the leading figure of the younger German historical school of economics. His work combined detailed historical and statistical research with advocacy of social reform within the monarchy.

Schmoller in later life

Career and ideas

Schmoller taught at the universities of Halle, Strasbourg and, from 1882, Berlin. In 1873 he co-founded the Verein für Socialpolitik (Association for Social Policy), whose members — the so-called Kathedersozialisten, "socialists of the chair" — pressed for state action on housing, factory inspection, insurance and other social questions, influencing Bismarck-era social legislation.

Methodologically, Schmoller championed inductive, historically grounded economics against the deductive theory of the Austrian school, a dispute that erupted as the Methodenstreit with Carl Menger. His magnum opus, the Grundriss der allgemeinen Volkswirtschaftslehre (1900–1904), surveyed economic life from its historical and institutional foundations. Schmoller's seminar trained a generation of scholars, including students from America and Japan, and his approach fed into American institutional economics.
