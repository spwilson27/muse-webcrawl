Standardized uptake value

The standardized uptake value (SUV) is a semi-quantitative measure used in positron emission tomography (PET) imaging to express the concentration of a radiotracer in a region of tissue relative to the average concentration that would result if the injected dose were uniformly distributed through the body. It is the most widely used metric in clinical PET, especially in oncology with the glucose analogue tracer FDG.

Definition

The SUV at a point in the image is defined as:

SUV = (activity concentration in tissue, kBq/mL) ÷ (injected dose decay-corrected to scan time, kBq) × (patient body weight, g)

assuming 1 g ≈ 1 mL of tissue. SUV is nominally dimensionless. The most commonly reported variant, SUVmax, is the highest voxel value within a region of interest; SUVmean and SUVpeak are also used. Because body weight includes fat, which takes up little FDG, normalisations by lean body mass (SUL) or body surface area are sometimes preferred, and PERCIST response criteria are based on SULpeak.

Clinical use

Malignant tumours typically show high glucose metabolism and therefore high FDG uptake, so SUV helps distinguish malignant from benign lesions, stage disease, select biopsy sites and assess response to therapy: a substantial fall in SUV after chemotherapy generally indicates treatment response, codified in the EORTC and PERCIST criteria. An SUVmax of about 2.5 has historically been used as a rough threshold for malignancy in solitary pulmonary nodules, though with many exceptions.

Limitations

SUV varies with blood glucose level, uptake time after injection, patient motion and body composition, reconstruction parameters and scanner calibration, and it is sensitive to partial-volume effects in small lesions. Inflammation and infection also accumulate FDG, so high uptake is not specific to cancer. These factors complicate comparisons between centres and motivate harmonisation programmes such as the EANM/EARL accreditation scheme.
