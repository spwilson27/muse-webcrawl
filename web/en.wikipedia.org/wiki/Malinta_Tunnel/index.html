Malinta Tunnel

The Malinta Tunnel is a tunnel complex built by the United States Army on Corregidor island in the Philippines. Completed in 1932, it served as a bomb-proof storage area, headquarters and hospital during the Second World War, most famously during the Battle of Corregidor in 1942.

Construction

Construction began in 1922 and took about ten years, carried out largely by Filipino convict laborers under the direction of the United States Army Corps of Engineers. The main tunnel runs east to west through Malinta Hill for about 253 metres (831 feet), with a diameter of roughly 7.3 metres (24 feet). A network of lateral tunnels branches off from the main passage, providing space for storage, offices and a 1,000-bed hospital.

Second World War

During the Japanese invasion of the Philippines, the tunnel served as the headquarters of General Douglas MacArthur and the seat of the Commonwealth government of the Philippines under President Manuel L. Quezon. Thousands of soldiers and civilians sheltered inside during the heavy bombardments that preceded the island's fall in May 1942.

When American and Filipino forces retook Corregidor in February 1945, Japanese troops who had taken refuge in the tunnel detonated explosives inside it, killing hundreds of their own men. After the war the tunnel was cleared and eventually developed as a historical site.

Present day

Today the Malinta Tunnel is part of the Corregidor Island historical park, and a light-and-sound show inside the tunnel presents the story of the island's wartime ordeal to visitors.
