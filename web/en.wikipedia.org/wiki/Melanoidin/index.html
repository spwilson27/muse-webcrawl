Melanoidin

Melanoidins are brown, high-molecular-weight, heterogeneous polymers formed in the final stages of the Maillard reaction — the non-enzymatic browning reaction between reducing sugars and amino acids that occurs during cooking. They are responsible for the brown colour of many foods, including bread crust, roasted coffee, beer, and soy sauce, and contribute significantly to flavour and aroma.

Formation and structure

Melanoidins form when early Maillard products (Amadori compounds) undergo further reactions — dehydration, fragmentation and polymerisation — at high temperatures or over long storage. Their exact structures are still poorly characterised because they are complex mixtures rather than single compounds; in coffee, for example, melanoidins incorporate polysaccharides and proteins cross-linked with coloured Maillard products.

Properties and health effects

Melanoidins contribute bitterness, body and colour to foods, and they can bind metal ions and act as antioxidants in vitro. Coffee melanoidins have been studied for possible prebiotic effects, since a portion survives digestion and reaches the colon. They are also implicated in the formation of acrylamide-related compounds in heated foods, though melanoidins themselves are not considered harmful and are a normal part of the human diet.
