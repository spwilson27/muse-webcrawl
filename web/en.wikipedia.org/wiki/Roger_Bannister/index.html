Roger Bannister

Sir Roger Gilbert Bannister
Born: 23 March 1929, Harrow, London
Died: 3 March 2018, Oxford
Known for: First sub-four-minute mile

Sir Roger Gilbert Bannister CH CBE FRCP (23 March 1929 – 3 March 2018) was an English middle-distance athlete and neurologist who ran the first sub-four-minute mile. On 6 May 1954, at the Iffley Road track in Oxford, Bannister completed the mile in 3 minutes 59.4 seconds, breaking one of the most famous barriers in sport. Paced by Chris Brasher and Chris Chataway, the run was announced to the crowd by Norris McWhirter, whose deliberately delayed announcement of "three..." became part of the legend.

Athletics career

Bannister studied at Exeter College, Oxford, and St Mary's Hospital Medical School in London. He finished fourth in the 1500 metres at the 1952 Helsinki Olympics, a disappointment that drove his pursuit of the four-minute mile, a target that had eluded runners since Gunder Hägg's 4:01.4 in 1945. His record lasted only 46 days before Australia's John Landy ran 3:57.9 in Finland; the two met at the 1954 British Empire and Commonwealth Games in Vancouver in the "Miracle Mile", which Bannister won. Weeks later he won the European Championships 1500 m in Bern and retired from athletics to concentrate on medicine.

Medical career

Bannister became a distinguished neurologist, specialising in the autonomic nervous system and autonomic failure, and served as Master of Pembroke College, Oxford (1985–1993). He was the first chairman of the Sports Council and was knighted in 1975. He published the memoir The Four Minute Mile (1955) and the autobiography Twin Tracks (2014), and edited standard texts on autonomic disorders; he regarded his medical work as more significant than his running.

Legacy

The "four-minute mile" remains a byword for a supposedly impossible barrier. The Iffley Road track is marked by a blue plaque, and Bannister's achievement was voted among the greatest sporting moments of the twentieth century. He died in Oxford in 2018, aged 88, having been diagnosed with Parkinson's disease in 2011.
