EastEnders
Creators: Julia Smith and Tony Holland
First episode: 19 February 1985
Network: BBC One
Setting: Albert Square, Walford, East London
Episodes: 7,000+

EastEnders is a British television soap opera created by Julia Smith and Tony Holland, first broadcast on BBC One on 19 February 1985. Set in Albert Square in the fictional borough of Walford in the East End of London, it follows the lives of working-class local residents and their families.

Albert Square, with the Queen Vic pub at its heart, is the centre of life in Walford.

Overview

The programme was launched to rival ITV's Coronation Street and quickly became one of Britain's most watched and talked-about shows, with a grittier, more issue-led approach than its rival. Its storylines have tackled HIV, domestic abuse, alcoholism, rape, mental illness and euthanasia, and its Christmas Day episodes—such as the 1986 episode in which Den Watts served Angie divorce papers, watched by over 30 million viewers—are a national institution.

Iconic characters and storylines

Long-running families include the Beales, Fowlers, Mitchells, Slaters, Carters and Brannings. Landlady Peggy Mitchell (Barbara Windsor), villainous "Dirty Den" Watts, and the Mitchell brothers are among British television's best-known fictional characters. The 2001 "Who Shot Phil?" storyline and the live episodes broadcast for the 25th and 30th anniversaries drew enormous audiences and press coverage.

Production

EastEnders is filmed at the BBC Elstree Centre in Borehamwood, where Albert Square is a permanent outdoor set; a replacement set was built and brought into use in the 2020s. The programme is broadcast several times a week and has won numerous British Soap Awards and BAFTAs.
