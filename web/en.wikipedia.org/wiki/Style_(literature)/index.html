Style (literature)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In literature, style is the manner in which an author writes, encompassing the distinctive use of language, sentence structure, tone, imagery, and other devices that characterise a particular writer's or work's expression. Style is often described as the "how" of writing, as distinct from its content or subject matter — the "what". Two writers may treat the same subject very differently because of differences in style.

Elements of style

Literary style is composed of numerous interrelated elements, including diction (word choice), syntax (sentence structure), rhythm and cadence, figurative language such as metaphor and simile, point of view, and tone. An author's style may be ornate or plain, formal or colloquial, lyrical or journalistic, and it typically develops and changes over the course of a career.

Style and genre

Style is closely related to genre and to period: the elevated diction of epic poetry, the naturalistic detail of the nineteenth-century novel, and the clipped prose associated with hard-boiled detective fiction are all recognisable stylistic conventions. Writers may adopt, adapt, or deliberately violate the stylistic expectations of their genre.

Study of style

The systematic study of style is known as stylistics. Classical rhetoric treated style (Latin: elocutio) as one of the five canons of oratory, alongside invention, arrangement, memory, and delivery. Modern stylistics draws on linguistics to analyse texts objectively, while traditional literary criticism evaluates style in aesthetic terms. The French naturalist Buffon famously declared that "style is the man himself" (le style est l'homme même), reflecting the view that style expresses an author's individual personality.

Style guides

In nonfiction and journalism, "style" can also refer to a codified set of conventions for spelling, punctuation, and usage, as set out in style guides such as The Chicago Manual of Style or the Associated Press Stylebook. This editorial sense of style is concerned with consistency rather than with literary artistry.
