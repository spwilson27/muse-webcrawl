Health technology assessment

Health technology assessment (HTA) is the systematic evaluation of the properties, effects and impacts of health technology. It is a multidisciplinary process that summarises information about the medical, social, economic and ethical issues related to the use of a health technology in a systematic, transparent, unbiased and robust manner, in order to inform policy decisions about whether and how a technology should be used within a health system.

Scope

"Health technology" is broadly defined and includes pharmaceuticals, medical devices, diagnostics, surgical procedures, screening programmes, vaccines and health service delivery systems. An HTA typically considers clinical effectiveness, safety, cost-effectiveness, and budget impact, as well as patient, organisational, legal and ethical implications.

Methods

HTA draws on systematic reviews of clinical evidence, statistical meta-analysis, and health economic modelling. Economic evaluation often takes the form of cost-utility analysis, expressing outcomes in quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) and producing incremental cost-effectiveness ratios that decision-makers compare with a threshold representing what the health system can afford.

Organisations

Many countries have dedicated HTA agencies. Examples include the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) in England, the Canadian Agency for Drugs and Technologies in Health (CADTH), the Pharmaceutical Benefits Advisory Committee (PBAC) in Australia, and Germany's Institute for Quality and Efficiency in Health Care (IQWiG). International collaboration is coordinated through networks such as EUnetHTA in Europe and the International Network of Agencies for Health Technology Assessment (INAHTA).

History

The field emerged in the 1970s, partly from the work of the United States Congress Office of Technology Assessment, and spread as health systems sought evidence-based methods to manage the adoption of expensive new technologies.
