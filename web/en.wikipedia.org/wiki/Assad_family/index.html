The Assad family ruled Syria from 1971, when Hafez al-Assad became president, until December 2024, when his son Bashar al-Assad was overthrown at the end of the Syrian civil war. Originally from Qardaha in the Alawite heartland of north-western Syria, the family rose through the Ba'ath Party and the Syrian armed forces, institutions whose ideology drew on Arab nationalism and Arab socialism.

Hafez al-Assad

Hafez al-Assad (1930–2000), an air force officer, participated in the 1963 Ba'athist coup and seized full power in the 1970 "Corrective Revolution". As president he built a highly centralised authoritarian state dominated by Alawites in the security services, crushed the Muslim Brotherhood uprising at Hama in 1982, and made Syria a significant regional power involved in Lebanon and in conflicts with Israel.

Bashar al-Assad

On Hafez's death in 2000 the constitution was amended to lower the minimum presidential age so his son Bashar (born 1965), a London-trained ophthalmologist recalled after the death of his elder brother Basil in a 1994 car accident, could succeed him. Initial hopes of reform (the "Damascus Spring") faded. His violent suppression of the 2011 protest movement escalated into a civil war that killed hundreds of thousands and displaced millions, with his government accused of war crimes including chemical weapons attacks. Supported by Russia and Iran, the regime regained much of the country, but a rapid rebel offensive in late 2024 captured Damascus; Bashar fled to Russia on 8 December 2024, ending 53 years of family rule.

The wider family

Other prominent members included Rifaat al-Assad, Hafez's brother, exiled after a 1984 succession challenge; Bushra al-Assad, Bashar's sister; and Maher al-Assad, commander of the Republican Guard and Fourth Armoured Division. Asma al-Assad, Bashar's British-born wife, served as First Lady. The family's wealth and business networks, including associates such as Rami Makhlouf, were central to Syria's political economy.
