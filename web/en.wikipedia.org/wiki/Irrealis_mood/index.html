Irrealis mood

In linguistics, irrealis moods (abbreviated IRR) are the main set of grammatical moods that indicate that a certain situation or action is not known to have happened at the moment the speaker is talking. This contrasts with the realis moods.

Every language has grammatical ways of expressing unreality. Linguists tend to reserve the term "irrealis" for particular grammatical markers that distinguish irrealis verb forms from other, less frequently discussed markers. While many languages have various grammatical moods such as the conditional, the subjunctive, or the optative, irrealis is used for a morpheme that is used to express uncertain situations in general.

List of irrealis moods

  • Subjunctive — used in many languages, particularly in subordinate clauses.
  • Conditional — used with the conditional probability and cross-functional mapping.
  • Optative — expresses a wish or hope.
  • Jussive — expresses pleas, insistence, imploring, self-encouragement, wish, desire, intent, command, purpose or consequence.
  • Potential — indicates that, in the speaker's judgment, a certain situation is likely to happen.
  • Imperative — expresses a direct command.
  • Prohibitive — the negative imperative.
  • Desiderative — expresses a wish or desire.
  • Dubitative — expresses doubt or uncertainty.
  • Hypothetical — expresses a possibility.
  • Presumptive — expresses a hypothesis.
  • Permissive — expresses permission.
  • Admonitive — expresses a warning.
  • Volitive — expresses a wish or desire.
  • Deductive — expresses a deduction.
  • Negationalis — expresses negation.
  • Eventive — used in some languages.
  • Speculative — used in Japanese.

Examples

Cheyenne

The Cheyenne language marks irrealis with forms dependent on the specific context.

Turkish

Turkish uses several moods classified under irrealis, including the conditional and the optative.
