The Battle of Neerwinden was fought on 18 March 1793 during the War of the First Coalition, part of the French Revolutionary Wars. An Austrian army under Prince Josias of Saxe-Coburg-Saalfeld defeated the French Army of the North commanded by General Charles François Dumouriez near the village of Neerwinden in the Austrian Netherlands. The defeat ended Dumouriez's invasion, forced the French to evacuate the Austrian Netherlands, and precipitated Dumouriez's defection to the Austrians.

Background

Following the French victory at Jemappes in November 1792, Dumouriez overran the Austrian Netherlands, but his invasion of the Dutch Republic stalled, and an Austrian counter-offensive under Coburg retook ground in early 1793. Dumouriez hurried back to meet Coburg's army advancing across the Gete river line near Neerwinden and Landen.

The battle

Dumouriez attacked Coburg's positions on 18 March. French columns initially captured Neerwinden and other villages, but Austrian cavalry and infantry counter-attacks, led on one flank by Archduke Charles, drove the French back across the river in disorder. A second French effort late in the day also failed.

Aftermath

The French retreated toward Brussels and then France, abandoning the Austrian Netherlands. Dumouriez, blamed for the failure and plotting against the Convention, defected to the Austrians on 5 April 1793. The battle site had earlier hosted a French victory in 1693 during the Nine Years' War.
