Cowbridge Grammar School

Cowbridge Grammar School was a selective grammar school for boys in Cowbridge, in the Vale of Glamorgan, Wales. Founded in the 17th century, it educated generations of pupils from the town and surrounding countryside until it was reorganised into a comprehensive school in the 1970s.

History

The school traced its origins to a foundation of 1608 associated with Sir Edward Stradling and other local benefactors, with teaching likely taking place in Cowbridge from an even earlier period. Its endowment was reorganised in the 17th century, notably through gifts connected with Sir Leoline Jenkins, a notable alumnus who became a judge, diplomat and Secretary of State. The school stood on a site in the town near the church, and its historic buildings included early schoolrooms that are now protected as listed structures.

Over the centuries the school developed a strong reputation in south Wales, teaching Latin, Greek and mathematics and sending pupils to Oxford, Cambridge and Jesus College, Oxford in particular. It admitted boarders as well as day boys, and during the 19th and 20th centuries it educated a number of Welsh academics, clergy, sportsmen and public figures, including the poet and scholar Sir Ifor Williams, who was a pupil there.

Closure and legacy

With the reorganisation of secondary education in the Vale of Glamorgan, the grammar school merged into the new Cowbridge Comprehensive School in 1973–74, ending more than three and a half centuries of the grammar school's separate existence. The original school buildings in the town centre were later adapted for other uses, and the school's records and traditions are preserved partly through local history collections.
