Insurrection of 31 May – 2 June 1793

The insurrection of 31 May – 2 June 1793 was an uprising during the French Revolution in which Parisian sans-culottes and National Guards surrounded the National Convention and forced the arrest of the leading Girondin deputies, consolidating the power of the Montagnards allied with Maximilien Robespierre and the Jacobins.

Background

Conflict between the moderate Girondins and the radical Montagnards had intensified during the trial of Louis XVI and amid military defeats, inflation and food shortages. The Girondin-dominated Convention's arrest of the journalist Jacques Hébert and attacks on Jacques Roux provoked the Paris sections to mobilise.

Events

On 31 May 1793, insurgent crowds invaded the Convention demanding the expulsion of the Girondins; the deputies resisted. On 2 June, tens of thousands of National Guards under François Hanriot surrounded the Tuileries with cannon, and the Convention voted to arrest 29 Girondin deputies and two ministers.

Consequences

The purge marked a turning point toward the Reign of Terror: power passed to the Committee of Public Safety, and many expelled Girondins fled to the provinces, where their supporters launched federalist revolts.
