Bilibili (Chinese: 哔哩哔哩; pinyin: bīlī bīlī), nicknamed B Site (B站), is a Chinese online video sharing website based in Shanghai, themed around animation, comics, and games (ACG), where users can submit, view and add overlaid commentary on videos. Bilibili hosts videos on various themes, including anime, music, dance, science and technology, movies, drama, fashion and video game Let's Plays, but it is also known for its extensive kuso-style parodies by submitters.

Bilibili is known for its scrolling danmaku ("bullet curtain") commenting system, in which viewer comments fly across the screen over the video itself. Since the mid-2010s, Bilibili began to expand to a broader audience, moving from a niche subculture market to mainstream audiences, and offering a wide variety of content including documentaries, variety shows and original programming.

History

Bilibili was founded by Xu Yi in 2009, initially as a website for fans of the anime A Certain Scientific Railgun, whose character Mikoto Misaka carries the nickname "Bilibili" (a reference to the sound of electric sparks). The site was originally named Mikufans. It grew out of the community surrounding the earlier video site AcFun. In 2014, Chen Rui joined as chairman and CEO, and the company professionalised its operations while licencing increasing amounts of official content. Bilibili Inc. held its initial public offering on the NASDAQ in March 2018 under the ticker BILI, and a secondary listing on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange followed in 2021.

Features

Besides hosting videos, Bilibili offers live streaming, mobile games publishing, e-commerce and fan community features. Its mascot characters, the sisters 22 and 33, were selected from a community design contest. The bullet curtain comment system, adapted from Japan's Niconico, remains a signature feature that fosters a strong sense of community participation among viewers.

Users and culture

Bilibili's user base skews young, with a large proportion of users from Generation Z. To post comments and access full features, users historically had to pass a membership exam testing knowledge of ACG culture and community etiquette. The site has become a major platform for Chinese internet culture, popularising terms and memes, and is widely used for educational content, including lectures and language-learning videos.

Business

Bilibili's revenue comes principally from mobile games, value-added services such as premium memberships and live streaming, advertising, and e-commerce. The company has invested heavily in licensed and original anime and documentary content, and sponsors esports teams and events, including ownership of the Hangzhou Spark in the Overwatch League and Bilibili Gaming in League of Legends competition.
