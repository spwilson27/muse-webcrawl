Junípero Serra

Died28 August 1784, Mission San Carlos Borromeo, California

Saint Junípero Serra Ferrer (1713–1784) was a Spanish Franciscan friar who founded a chain of missions in Alta California, including San Diego, San Carlos Borromeo (Carmel) and San Francisco. He is known as the "Apostle of California".

Early life and Mexico

Born Miquel Josep Serra in Mallorca, he joined the Franciscans, took the name Junípero, and became a noted theology lecturer before sailing to the Americas in 1749. He served in missions in the Sierra Gorda of Mexico and later took charge of the former Jesuit missions of Baja California.

California missions

In 1769 Serra accompanied the expedition of Gaspar de Portolá to Alta California and founded Mission San Diego de Alcalá, the first of nine missions he established. From his headquarters at Carmel he directed efforts to convert Indigenous peoples to Christianity and introduce European agriculture.

Controversy and canonization

Serra's legacy is contested: admirers praise his devotion and defense of Indigenous people against military abuses, while critics point to coercion, punishment and disease within the mission system that devastated Native populations. Pope Francis canonized him in 2015, the first canonization performed in the United States, prompting protests from some Native American groups.
