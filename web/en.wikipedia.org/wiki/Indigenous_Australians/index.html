Indigenous Australians

Indigenous Australians are the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples of Australia, whose ancestors inhabited the continent and its islands for at least 50,000–65,000 years before British colonisation in 1788.

RegionsAll states and territories; large populations in NSW, Queensland, NT

History

Diverse nations with distinct languages, law and land management — including fire-stick farming and aquaculture such as Budj Bim — were devastated by disease, dispossession and frontier violence after 1788. Policies of removal created the Stolen Generations.

Rights and recognition

Milestones include the 1967 referendum, the Mabo decision (1992) recognising native title, and the 2008 national Apology. Debates continue over treaty, truth-telling and representation, including the unsuccessful 2023 Voice referendum.

Culture

Indigenous cultures encompass the Dreaming, songlines, art, and Torres Strait Islander traditions, with hundreds of language groups.
