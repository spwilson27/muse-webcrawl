Sullom Voe

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sullom Voe is an inlet of the North Sea in the Shetland Islands, Scotland. The voe (a Shetland term for a sea inlet, from Old Norse vágr) separates the Northmavine peninsula from the rest of Mainland Shetland and is best known as the site of the Sullom Voe Terminal, one of the largest oil and gas terminals in Europe.

The terminal

The terminal was built during the 1970s to receive oil and gas from the East Shetland Basin fields of the North Sea, including the Brent and Ninian fields. Construction began in 1975 and the first oil arrived by pipeline in 1978; the terminal was officially opened in 1981. At its peak in the 1980s it handled over a million barrels of oil per day, shipped out by tankers from its jetties, which can accommodate very large crude carriers. The complex includes storage tanks, processing plant, a power station, and facilities that also handle gas production associated with the Brent system and, later, fields west of Shetland via the Sullom Voe gas terminal connections.

History and wartime use

Before the oil era, Sullom Voe was notable as an RAF flying-boat station during the Second World War, from which Catalina and Sunderland aircraft patrolled the North Atlantic. The small settlement of Graven sits near the head of the voe.

Environment

The terminal's operations are subject to environmental monitoring by an independent advisory group, reflecting the sensitivity of Shetland's marine environment; otters, seals, and seabirds are found around the voe. With the decline of North Sea production, throughput has fallen substantially from its peak, and the site has been discussed in connection with future energy uses, including carbon capture and hydrogen.
