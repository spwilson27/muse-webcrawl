German occupation of Norway

Period9 April 1940 – 8 May 1945
Governing authorityReichskommissariat Norwegen
Reich CommissionerJosef Terboven
Norwegian governmentIn exile in London (King Haakon VII)

The German occupation of Norway began on 9 April 1940, when Nazi Germany launched Operation Weserübung against the neutral kingdom. After a two-month campaign, during which Allied forces withdrew from northern Norway, Germany controlled the country until the capitulation of German forces in Europe on 8 May 1945.

Administration

Norway was ruled as the Reichskommissariat Norwegen under Josef Terboven, acting alongside Vidkun Quisling's collaborationist government, formally installed in 1942. Quisling's party, Nasjonal Samling, was the only legal political party, though it never gained broad popular support.

Resistance

The Norwegian resistance — coordinated groups such as Milorg, the sabotage organisation Oslogjengen, and intelligence network XU — worked with the London-based government-in-exile and the large Norwegian merchant fleet (Nortraship). Notable operations included the heavy-water sabotage at Vemork, which hampered the German nuclear programme, and the sinking of the ferry SF Hydro in 1944.

Liberation

German forces in Norway, numbering some 350,000–400,000 men, surrendered without a final battle in May 1945. King Haakon VII returned on 7 June 1945, exactly five years after his departure. Quisling was tried and executed for treason in October 1945.
