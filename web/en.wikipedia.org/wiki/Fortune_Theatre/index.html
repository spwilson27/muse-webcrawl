Fortune Theatre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Fortune Theatre is a West End theatre in Russell Street, Covent Garden, London, with a capacity of 432 seats. Opened in 1924, it is one of the smallest West End houses and is best known as the long-time home of the thriller The Woman in Black. The name recalls the original Fortune Theatre, a famous Elizabethan playhouse built in 1600 for the Admiral's Men, whose surviving builder's contract is a key source for historians of the early modern stage.

The modern theatre

Designed by architect Ernest Schaufelberg and built on the site of the Albion Tavern, the Fortune opened on 8 November 1924 with Sinners by Lawrence Anderson. Its Italianate facade is Grade II listed. During the Second World War it hosted ENSA productions for troops. Subsequent productions ranged from revue (Beyond the Fringe began its London life in the West End after Edinburgh) to drama and musicals.

The Woman in Black

Stephen Mallatratt's stage adaptation of Susan Hill's novel The Woman in Black transferred to the Fortune in 1989 and ran there for more than 33 years, becoming the second-longest-running play in West End history, before closing in March 2023. The production's ghost-story atmosphere made distinctive use of the theatre's intimate, slightly claustrophobic auditorium.

Elizabethan predecessor

The first Fortune Theatre was built in Finsbury in 1600 by impresario Philip Henslowe and actor Edward Alleyn for the Admiral's Men. Its contract with carpenter Peter Street survives and specifies a square timber frame building — unusual among the polygonal playhouses — modelled in many respects on the Globe. It burned down in 1621, was rebuilt in brick, and was finally closed by Parliament in 1642.
