Celtic knot

Celtic knots are decorative knots and stylised representations of knots used for ornament in the Celtic style of Insular art. These endless knots are most famously found in illuminated manuscripts such as the Book of Kells, in the carving of stone crosses, and in metalwork produced in Ireland and Britain from about the 7th century onward.

History

Interlace patterns have older roots in late Roman and Byzantine ornament, but Celtic artists developed them into complex plaitwork filling panels, borders and the bodies of beasts. After the medieval period the style faded, then returned during the 19th-century Celtic Revival, and Celtic knots remain popular in jewellery, tattoos and graphic design.

Symbolism

Because the strands have no beginning or end, the knots are often interpreted as symbols of eternity, continuity, and the interconnection of life, though specific historical meanings for individual motifs are largely speculative.
