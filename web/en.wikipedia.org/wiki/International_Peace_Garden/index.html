International Peace Garden

LocationCanada–United States border, Manitoba / North Dakota
Areaabout 2,300 acres (930 ha)
Established14 July 1932

The International Peace Garden is a large park straddling the Canada–United States border between the province of Manitoba and the state of North Dakota, near the towns of Boissevain, Manitoba, and Dunseith, North Dakota. Covering approximately 2,300 acres (930 hectares) of the Turtle Mountain plateau, it commemorates the long-standing peace between Canada and the United States along the world's longest undefended international border. It was formally dedicated on 14 July 1932.

History

The garden was the idea of Dr Henry J. Moore, a horticulturist from Ontario, who proposed a memorial garden on the border at a meeting of the National Association of Gardeners in 1928. Land was secured on both sides of the boundary, and the non-profit International Peace Garden, Inc. was established to develop and operate the site. A cairn at the dedication bears the pledge of the two peoples "that these two countries shall forever live in peace ... along these pictured borders."

Features

The formal gardens are centred on an axis crossing the border, with twin floral flag displays — the Stars and Stripes formed in flowers on the American side and the Canadian flag on the Canadian side — among annual plantings that have used tens of thousands of bedding plants each season. Other features include the Peace Tower, a set of four 120-foot (37 m) concrete pillars straddling the boundary (later deemed structurally unsound and closed), the Peace Chapel — the only building in the park that crosses the border — whose walls are inscribed with quotations about peace, a carillon bell tower, and the International Music Camp, founded in 1956, which hosts young musicians from both countries each summer. The 9/11 Memorial displays steel girders recovered from the World Trade Center.

Visiting

The garden is accessed through a single entrance from the border highway; visitors pass through customs formalities when arriving and departing because the site lies between the two countries' border posts. It attracts tens of thousands of visitors annually, as well as campers using its campground and trails through the surrounding woodlands and lakes of the Turtle Mountains.
