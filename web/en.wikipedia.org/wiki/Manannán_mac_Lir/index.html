Manannán mac Lir is a sea god, warrior and ruler of the Otherworld in Irish mythology. He is associated with the sea, weather, the Isle of Man, and the boundary between this world and the Otherworld of Tír na nÓg. His name means "son of Lir" (Ler, "the Sea"), and the Isle of Man is traditionally said to take its name from him. In the tales he is a master of magic and illusion, a gifted shape-shifter, and a generous bestower of gifts upon heroes.

Mythology

Manannán possesses a number of famous magical treasures. His self-navigating ship, the Scuabtuinne ("Wave-sweeper"), sails without sail or oar. His horse, Aonbharr, can gallop over sea as over land. He owns a cloak of invisibility, a helmet of flame, the sword Fragarach ("the Answerer"), which compels truth from anyone at whose throat it is held, and the crane bag (corrbolg), made from the skin of a woman transformed into a crane, which contains his treasures and is visible only at high tide. He also keeps the pigs whose flesh renews itself daily, feeding the feasts of the Otherworld, and an otherworldly orchard of apple trees associated with Emain Ablach.

Manannán appears in many of the great Irish tales. In Echtra Cormaic he tests King Cormac mac Airt and gives him a cup of truth. In Immram Brain, the Voyage of Bran, he appears riding his chariot over the sea and sings of the delights of Mag Mell. He is the foster-father of the god Lugh, and in later tradition he is credited with assigning the síde (fairy mounds) to the defeated Tuatha Dé Danann and concealing them from mankind beneath a mist of invisibility, the féth fíada.

Manx tradition

On the Isle of Man, Manannán (Manannan beg mac y Leirr, "little Manannan son of Leirr") is remembered as the island's first ruler and protector, who could wrap the island in mist to hide it from enemies and shapeshift at will. Until modern times, islanders left bundles of green rushes as a tribute for him at the Barrule on St John's Eve. He is commemorated by the Manx Museum and in Manx folklore as a wizard, necromancer and lawgiver.

Welsh counterpart

Manannán is generally considered the Irish counterpart of the Welsh figure Manawydan fab Llŷr, a central character of the Third Branch of the Mabinogion, both descending from a common Celtic sea deity.

In popular culture

Manannán appears in modern fantasy literature, music and games, including as a character in works drawing on Celtic mythology and as the namesake of vessels, a Manx cat breed association, and the Irish Coast Guard's folklore. A large statue of the god stood overlooking Lough Foyle before it was stolen and later replaced.
