Gaudeamus igitur

"Gaudeamus igitur" ("So let us rejoice"), also known by its opening words or as "De brevitate vitae" ("On the shortness of life"), is a popular academic commercium song in many Western countries, sung at university graduation ceremonies and other academic occasions. The Latin lyrics reflect the medieval student tradition of celebrating youth while acknowledging the brevity of life.

History

The song is based on a Latin manuscript of 1287, with the melody in its modern form dating to the eighteenth century. The first verses begin "Gaudeamus igitur, iuvenes dum sumus" ("Let us rejoice, then, while we are young") and continue "post iucundam iuventutem, post molestam senectutem, nos habebit humus" ("after joyful youth, after troublesome old age, the ground will have us").

Content

Further verses toast the university ("Vivat academia"), its professors, students, the city and its patrons, and include the mildly irreverent "Pereat tristitia" ("Down with sadness"). Johannes Brahms quoted the melody in the finale of his Academic Festival Overture (1880).

Modern use

The song remains the anthem of student organisations and is sung at graduations in Europe, Latin America and elsewhere, often as the closing piece of the ceremony.
