Melodic death metal (often abbreviated to melodeath) is a subgenre of death metal that employs highly melodic guitar riffs, often borrowing from traditional heavy metal and the New Wave of British Heavy Metal. The genre emerged in the early 1990s, most famously in Gothenburg, Sweden.

Characteristics

The style combines the aggression and harsh vocals of death metal with melodic lead guitar lines, twin-guitar harmonies, and occasional acoustic passages and keyboards. Vocals are typically screamed or growled, though some bands incorporate clean singing.

History

The "Gothenburg sound" was pioneered by bands such as In Flames, Dark Tranquillity and At the Gates, whose mid-1990s albums laid the genre's foundations. The English band Carcass is also commonly cited as an early influence with its 1993 album Heartwork. Melodic death metal later became a major influence on American metalcore.
