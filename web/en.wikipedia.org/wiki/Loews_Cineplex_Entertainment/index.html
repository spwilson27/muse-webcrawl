Loews Cineplex Entertainment

Loews Cineplex Entertainment, also known as Loews Incorporated, was an American theatre chain that was the oldest continuously operating company of its kind in the United States and one of the largest movie theatre chains in North America. Founded in 1904 by Marcus Loew as a vaudeville and nickelodeon business, Loews grew into the parent company of Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer (MGM). In January 2006, AMC Theatres announced it would merge with Loews, a transaction completed that year, making AMC the largest theatrical exhibitor at the time.

History

Marcus Loew began in 1904 with penny arcades and nickelodeons in New York, consolidating into Loew's Consolidated Enterprises and then Loew's Incorporated (1919). The company built ornate "movie palaces" and, to supply them with films, acquired Metro Pictures, Goldwyn Pictures, and Louis B. Mayer Productions, which were merged in 1924 to form MGM — while Loew's theatres remained the exhibition arm.

Following the 1948 Supreme Court antitrust decision in United States v. Paramount Pictures, Inc., Loew's was required to divorce its theatre chain from MGM, a separation completed in 1959. In later decades control passed through investors including the Tisch family; Sony acquired Loews theatres in the late 1980s, and the circuit combined with Cineplex Odeon in 1998 to form Loews Cineplex Entertainment. After a 2001 bankruptcy restructuring, the chain was owned by Onex and Oaktree, and then by Bain Capital, Carlyle Group, and Spectrum Equity.

Merger with AMC

On 21 June 2005 AMC and Loews announced a merger; antitrust conditions required divestitures of theatres in several markets. The merged company operated under the AMC name, though the Loews name persisted on some theatres for a period.
