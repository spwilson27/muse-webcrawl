Chinese alligator

The Chinese alligator (Alligator sinensis), also known as the Yangtze alligator, is a small crocodilian endemic to eastern China. It is one of only two living species in the genus Alligator, the other being the much larger American alligator. The species is critically endangered, with only a few hundred individuals surviving in the wild in the lower Yangtze River region.

Description

The Chinese alligator is fully armoured, with a broad, slightly upturned snout and bony plates even on the eyelids and belly. Adults are dark grey or black, while juveniles show yellowish crossbands. Unlike most crocodilians it spends the cold winter brumating in complex burrow systems.

Conservation

Habitat conversion to rice paddies reduced the wild population drastically during the 20th century. The Anhui Research Centre of Chinese Alligator Reproduction has bred many thousands of animals, and reintroductions into restored wetlands began in the 2000s; reintroduced alligators have survived and reproduced in the wild.
