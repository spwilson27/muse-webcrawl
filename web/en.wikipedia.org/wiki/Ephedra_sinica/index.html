Ephedra sinica

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ephedra sinica (Chinese: 麻黄, ma huang) is a species of Ephedra native to Mongolia, Russia (Buryatiya, Chita, Primorye), and northeastern China. It is a shrub with jointed, photosynthetic green stems and minute scale-like leaves, adapted to dry steppe and desert environments.

Traditional use

The dried stems of E. sinica are the source of the drug ma huang, used in traditional Chinese medicine for at least two millennia, including in the treatment of asthma, colds, and fever. The plant contains the alkaloids ephedrine and pseudoephedrine, powerful sympathomimetic stimulants acting on the adrenergic system.

Regulation

Because ephedra alkaloids can raise blood pressure and have been linked to cardiovascular adverse events — and because ephedrine is a precursor in the illicit manufacture of methamphetamine — ephedra-containing dietary supplements were banned in the United States by the FDA in 2004, and the sale of ephedrine is restricted in many countries. Pharmaceutical ephedrine remains in medical use, for example to treat hypotension during anaesthesia.
