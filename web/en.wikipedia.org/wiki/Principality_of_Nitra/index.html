Principality of Nitra

The Principality of Nitra was an early medieval Slavic polity in present-day Slovakia and part of Hungary, centred on the city of Nitra. It was the first known state of the Slovaks' ancestors. Its best-known ruler was Pribina, who governed it in the early 9th century.

History

The principality emerged in the late 8th or early 9th century among the Slavs of the middle Danube region, after the fall of the Avar Khaganate. Around 828, Pribina had a church consecrated at Nitra by Archbishop Adalram of Salzburg — the first documented Christian church among the West Slavs. Around 833, Prince Mojmír I of Moravia conquered the principality and expelled Pribina, uniting Nitra with Moravia to form Great Moravia. Within Great Moravia, Nitra retained a degree of separate status as an appanage principality, ruled for example by Svatopluk I before he became king.

Legacy

After the fall of Great Moravia in the early 10th century, the territory was absorbed into the Kingdom of Hungary, where a duchy (ducatus) centred on Nitra survived for a time as an appanage for members of the Árpád dynasty. The Principality of Nitra is regarded in Slovak historiography as a foundational precursor of Slovak statehood.
