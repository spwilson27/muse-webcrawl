Tabaristan

Tabaristan or Tabarestan (Persian: طبرستان) was the name of a mountainous region on the southern shore of the Caspian Sea, corresponding largely to the modern Iranian province of Mazandaran. Protected by the Alborz mountains, it long resisted Arab conquest and preserved Iranian rule and Zoroastrian religion well into the Islamic era.

History

Under the Sasanian Empire the region was governed locally, and after the Arab conquest of Persia in the 7th century, local dynasties such as the Dabuyids maintained independence, striking their own coins in imitation of Sasanian issues. The Abbasid Caliphate only subdued Tabaristan in the 760s, and even then local houses like the Bavandids and Qarinvandids continued to rule parts of the mountains for centuries. In the 9th century the Alid Hasan ibn Zayd established a Zaydi Shi'a state in Tabaristan, an important episode in the spread of Shi'a Islam in Iran.

Legacy

The name survives in the Tabari language (Mazandarani) and in the historian al-Tabari, whose family came from Amol in Tabaristan.
