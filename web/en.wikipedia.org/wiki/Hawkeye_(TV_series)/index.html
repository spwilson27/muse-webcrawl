Hawkeye (TV series)

Hawkeye is an American television miniseries created by Jonathan Igla for the streaming service Disney+, based on the Marvel Comics character Clint Barton / Hawkeye. It is the fifth television series in the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU) produced by Marvel Studios, sharing continuity with the films of the franchise.

Jeremy Renner reprises his role as Clint Barton from the film series, with Hailee Steinfeld joining him as Kate Bishop. The series premiered on November 24, 2021, and ran for six episodes, concluding on December 22, 2021. It is part of Phase Four of the MCU.

Premise

Set in New York City during the Christmas season following the events of Avengers: Endgame (2019), Clint Barton must work with the young archer Kate Bishop to confront enemies from his past — including the Tracksuit Mafia and echoes of his time as the vigilante Ronin — so he can return to his family in time for Christmas.

Cast

Jeremy Renner stars as Clint Barton / Hawkeye, and Hailee Steinfeld as Kate Bishop, a 22-year-old Hawkeye fan who becomes Barton's protégée. Florence Pugh reprises her role as Yelena Belova, Vincent D'Onofrio returns as Wilson Fisk / Kingpin, and Alaqua Cox appears as Maya Lopez / Echo, a role that led to the spin-off series Echo (2024). Vera Farmiga plays Eleanor Bishop, Kate's mother, and Tony Dalton plays Jack Duquesne.

Episodes

The six episodes are "Never Meet Your Heroes", "Hide and Seek", "Echoes", "Partners, Am I Right?", "Ronin", and "So This Is Christmas?". The series was directed by Rhys Thomas and the directing duo Bert & Bertie.

Production

The series draws visual and narrative inspiration from Matt Fraction and David Aja's acclaimed Hawkeye comic run (2012–2015), including the character of Lucky the Pizza Dog, the Tracksuit Mafia, and the colour palette of Barton's costume. The fictional Rogers: The Musical, a Broadway musical about Steve Rogers depicted within the series, became a notable cultural talking point.

Reception

Hawkeye received generally positive reviews, with praise for Steinfeld's performance, the chemistry between the leads, the street-level tone, and the Christmas setting. It holds a high approval rating on review aggregator Rotten Tomatoes.
