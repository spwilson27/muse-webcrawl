Human nervous system

Human nervous system
DivisionsCentral nervous system (brain, spinal cord); Peripheral nervous system (cranial and spinal nerves, ganglia)
Cell typesNeurons (~86 billion in the brain), glial cells
FunctionSensation, integration, movement, regulation of body systems, cognition
BranchesSomatic and autonomic (sympathetic, parasympathetic, enteric)

The human nervous system is the part of the body that coordinates voluntary and involuntary actions and transmits signals to and from different parts of the body. It consists of two main parts: the central nervous system (CNS), comprising the brain and spinal cord, and the peripheral nervous system (PNS), comprising nerves and ganglia outside the CNS.

Central nervous system

The brain, containing about 86 billion neurons, is the seat of cognition, memory, emotion, and consciousness; its major divisions are the cerebrum, cerebellum, and brainstem. The spinal cord, running within the vertebral column, relays signals between brain and body and mediates reflexes. Both are protected by the meninges and bathed in cerebrospinal fluid.

Peripheral nervous system

Thirty-one pairs of spinal nerves and twelve pairs of cranial nerves connect the CNS to the rest of the body. Functionally the PNS divides into the somatic system, governing voluntary movement and sensation, and the autonomic system, which regulates involuntary functions through its sympathetic ("fight or flight"), parasympathetic ("rest and digest"), and enteric divisions—the last controlling the gastrointestinal tract.

Cells and signaling

Neurons transmit information by electrochemical signals: electrical action potentials along axons and chemical neurotransmitters (such as glutamate, GABA, dopamine, and serotonin) across synapses. Glial cells—including astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, Schwann cells, and microglia—support, insulate, and protect neurons. Disorders of the nervous system range from stroke and epilepsy to neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases and immune conditions such as multiple sclerosis.
