Dermatome (anatomy)

A dermatome is an area of skin that is mainly supplied by afferent nerve fibres from the dorsal root of a single spinal nerve. There are eight cervical nerves (C1 being an exception with no dermatome), twelve thoracic nerves, five lumbar nerves, and five sacral nerves, and each relays sensation from a particular region of skin to the brain. Along the thorax and abdomen the dermatomes form a stack of horizontal strips, while along the limbs they run longitudinally, reflecting the rotation of the limbs during development.

Clinical significance

Dermatome maps are essential clinical tools. Because each dermatome corresponds to a spinal segment, a clinician can localise a lesion of the spinal cord or a nerve root by identifying the pattern of sensory loss. Conditions assessed with dermatomes include:

  • Shingles (herpes zoster), whose rash characteristically erupts within a single dermatome when the varicella-zoster virus reactivates in a dorsal root ganglion.
  • Radiculopathy, in which a compressed or inflamed nerve root causes pain, numbness, or tingling in its dermatome — for example, sciatica following the S1 dermatome down the back of the leg.
  • Spinal cord injury, where the lowest unaffected dermatome helps define the level of injury.

Important dermatomes

  • C2 — back of the skull
  • C6 — thumb; C7 — middle finger; C8 — little finger
  • T4 — nipple line; T10 — umbilicus (navel)
  • L1 — groin; L4 — medial leg and foot; L5 — dorsum of the foot
  • S1 — back of the calf and sole of the foot

Published maps (such as those of Keegan and Garrett, and of Foerster) differ in detail because of overlap between adjacent dermatomes and individual variation.
