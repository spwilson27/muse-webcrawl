Great St Bernard Hospice

LocationGreat St Bernard Pass, Valais, Switzerland
Altitude2,469 m (8,100 ft)
Foundedc. 1049

The Great St Bernard Hospice (French: Hospice du Grand St-Bernard) is a hospice and hostel at the summit of the Great St Bernard Pass in the Pennine Alps, on the border between Switzerland and Italy. Situated at 2,469 metres (8,100 ft) above sea level, it was founded around 1049 by Saint Bernard of Menthon to shelter travellers crossing the dangerous Alpine pass, and it has been staffed ever since by canons regular of the Congregation of Great Saint Bernard.

History

The pass had been an important, if hazardous, route since Roman times. Bernard, archdeacon of Aosta, established the hospice to aid pilgrims travelling to Rome and to rescue travellers caught by storms and robbers. The hospice received support from donors across Europe, and its church is first mentioned in 1125.

St Bernard dogs

The hospice is famous for the St. Bernard dogs bred there by the canons from the 17th century onwards. The large dogs, historically equipped with small casks around their necks in popular imagination, assisted in locating and rescuing travellers lost in snow. The most famous, Barry (1800–1814), is credited with saving more than forty lives. Since 2005 the breeding of the dogs has been maintained by the Fondation Barry in Martigny.

Today

The hospice remains open year-round as a place of lodging and spiritual retreat, accessible in winter only by snow vehicle or on skis since the pass road closes. A museum at the hospice displays its history, the dogs, and the crossing of the Alps by Napoleon in 1800, when his army passed with the canons' assistance.
