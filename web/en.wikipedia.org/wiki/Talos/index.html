Talos

In Greek mythology, Talos (Ancient Greek: Τάλως) was a giant automaton made of bronze who patrolled the island of Crete, circling its shores three times each day to protect the island — and, in some traditions, Europa — from pirates and invaders. He hurled stones at approaching ships and, in some versions, heated his bronze body red-hot to embrace and burn intruders.

Mythology

Talos was said to be the last survivor of the bronze race of men born from ash trees (the meliae), or, in the account given in the Argonautica of Apollonius of Rhodes, a gift of Hephaestus to Zeus, who gave him to Europa to guard Crete. A single vein ran through his body from neck to ankle, carrying ichor, the life-fluid of the gods, and was sealed by a bronze nail or a thin membrane of skin at his ankle.

When Jason and the Argonauts approached Crete on their return voyage, Talos kept the Argo at bay with thrown boulders. Medea destroyed him: in Apollonius's version she bewitched him with incantations and evil spirits so that he grazed his ankle on a rock, and the ichor flowed out "like molten lead" until he fell. Other versions have the archer Poeas shoot the vulnerable spot, or Medea remove the nail after promising Talos immortality.

Interpretation

Talos is frequently discussed as an early mythological precursor of the automaton or robot, and his single vulnerable vein is often compared with the "Achilles' heel" motif. His name may derive from the Cretan word talōs, equivalent to the Greek hēlios, "sun."
