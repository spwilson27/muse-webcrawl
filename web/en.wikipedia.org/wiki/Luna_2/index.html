Luna 2

Luna 2 (also known as Lunik 2) was the second spacecraft of the Soviet Union's Luna programme and the first human-made object to reach the surface of the Moon. It impacted the lunar surface on 14 September 1959, east of Mare Imbrium near the craters Aristides, Archimedes and Autolycus.

Mission

The spherical probe, launched on 13 September 1959, was similar in design to Luna 1 but carried additional instruments including scintillation counters, Geiger counters, a magnetometer and micrometeorite detectors. It had no propulsion system of its own after separation from its upper stage, following a direct trajectory to the Moon lasting about 33.5 hours.

Results

En route, Luna 2 confirmed Luna 1's finding that the Moon has no appreciable magnetic field and detected no radiation belts around it. On 13 September it released a bright orange sodium vapour cloud to make its trajectory observable and to study gas behaviour in a vacuum. The probe carried two spherical "pennants" studded with hexagonal medallions bearing the Soviet coat of arms and the inscription USSR, which were designed to scatter on impact; a replica was later presented to US President Dwight D. Eisenhower.

Luna 2's success was a major Soviet achievement in the early Space Race, coming less than a month before Luna 3 photographed the far side of the Moon.
