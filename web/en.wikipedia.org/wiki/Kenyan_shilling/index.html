The Kenyan shilling (Swahili: shilingi; symbol: KSh; code: KES) is the currency of Kenya. It is divided into 100 cents. The Kenyan shilling is among the most stable and widely used currencies in East Africa, and is used informally in neighbouring regions such as South Sudan and parts of Somalia.

History

The Kenyan shilling replaced the East African shilling at par on 14 September 1966, following Kenya's independence in 1963 and the dissolution of the East African Currency Board system. Early coins and banknotes bore the portrait of Kenya's first president, Jomo Kenyatta; later issues featured President Daniel arap Moi, and after 2003 Kenyatta's image returned. The Constitution of Kenya 2010 prohibits the use of portraits of individuals on currency, leading to a new generation of banknotes launched in 2019 featuring images of Kenyan wildlife and landmarks, including the "Big Five" animals and the Kenyatta International Convention Centre.

Coins and banknotes

Coins circulate in denominations of 1, 5, 10 and 20 shillings (with smaller cent coins rarely used). Banknotes circulate in denominations of 50, 100, 200, 500 and 1,000 shillings. The 2019 series introduced enhanced security features and designs celebrating Kenya's wildlife and its constitutional themes.

Exchange rate

The shilling floats freely and is traded on the Nairobi foreign exchange market. It has historically been one of the stronger currencies in sub-Saharan Africa, though it depreciated significantly in the early 2020s before partially recovering.
