Anti-Defamation League

The Anti-Defamation League (ADL), formerly known as the Anti-Defamation League of B'nai B'rith, is a Jewish non-governmental organization based in the United States that specializes in civil rights law. Founded in late September 1913 by B'nai B'rith, in the wake of the antisemitic trial and lynching of Leo Frank, its stated mission is to stop the defamation of the Jewish people and to secure justice and fair treatment to all.

History

The ADL was founded by attorney Sigmund Livingston in Chicago. Its founding charter stated that its immediate object was "to stop, by appeals to reason and conscience and, if necessary, by appeals to law, the defamation of the Jewish people." Over the following decades it broadened its work to combating antisemitism, bigotry, extremism and hate crimes generally, and became known for its research on extremist groups, its annual Audit of Antisemitic Incidents, and educational programmes such as "No Place for Hate".

Activities

The ADL monitors extremist activity, provides information to law enforcement, publishes reports on antisemitism and hate, advocates for civil-rights legislation, and runs anti-bias education in schools. It has also been involved in controversies over the balance between its civil-rights mission and its advocacy relating to Israel, and over its data-collection practices.

Leadership

Long-serving national director Abraham Foxman led the organization from 1987 to 2015; he was succeeded by Jonathan Greenblatt.
