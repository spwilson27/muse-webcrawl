Castell Coch

Castell Coch, the “Red Castle,” above Tongwynlais near Cardiff.

Castell Coch (Welsh for “Red Castle”) is a 19th-century Gothic Revival castle near Tongwynlais, north of Cardiff, Wales. It was designed by architect William Burges for John Crichton-Stuart, 3rd Marquess of Bute, and built between 1875 and 1891 on the ruins of a 13th-century fortification.

History

The medieval castle on the site, probably built by the de Clare lords of Glamorgan, was abandoned after the Welsh revolts of the early 14th century. In the 1870s the immensely wealthy Marquess of Bute, who was simultaneously transforming Cardiff Castle, commissioned Burges to recreate it as a romantic summer retreat. Burges died in 1881 and his team completed the interiors over the following decade.

Architecture

The castle’s conical-roofed towers and drawbridge evoke a French medieval fortress, while the interiors are lavishly decorated, with painted ceilings, carved fireplaces, and themes drawn from fable and nature, notably in Lady Bute’s bedroom and the drawing room. Though rarely occupied by the family, Castell Coch is regarded as one of the finest examples of Burges’s High Victorian Gothic work. It is now cared for by Cadw and open to the public.
