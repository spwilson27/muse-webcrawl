Scandinavian krone

Gold coins issued under the Scandinavian Monetary Union

The Scandinavian krone was the common currency unit introduced by the Scandinavian Monetary Union, the monetary union between Sweden, Denmark, and — from 1875 — Norway. The union was established by treaty in 1873 and the krone (Swedish and Norwegian krone, Danish krone, plural kroner) replaced the earlier rigsdaler and speciedaler currencies at par.

History

Under the terms of the union, the krone was defined on a gold standard, with one krone equal to 1/2480 of a kilogram of pure gold (approximately 0.403 grams). Coins minted in any member state were legal tender in all three, and banknotes were mutually accepted. The union functioned smoothly for several decades and is often cited as one of the most successful monetary unions in history, in part because the member economies were closely integrated and the participating states maintained convertibility.

The outbreak of the First World War in 1914 strained the system: Sweden suspended gold convertibility, and divergent monetary policies during and after the war led to the union's effective dissolution, although it was never formally abolished until much later. Each country retained the krone as its national currency name, and today the Swedish krona, Danish krone, and Norwegian krone are separate currencies with independent values.

Denominations

Common coin denominations under the union included 1, 2, 5, 10, 25, and 50 øre, and 1 and 2 kroner in base metals, with gold coins of 5, 10, and 20 kroner. The subunit, the øre (Swedish öre), was one hundredth of a krone in all three countries.
