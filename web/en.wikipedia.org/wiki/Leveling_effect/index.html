Leveling effect

The leveling effect (or solvent leveling) in chemistry describes the effect of a solvent on the properties of acids and bases. The strength of a strong acid is limited ("leveled") by the basicity of the solvent. Similarly, the strength of a strong base is leveled by the acidity of the solvent.

Explanation

No acid stronger than the conjugate acid of the solvent can exist in that solvent, and no base stronger than the conjugate base of the solvent can exist. In water, all acids stronger than hydronium (H3O+) react with water to form hydronium, so they appear to have equal strength. For example, perchloric acid, hydrochloric acid and nitric acid all appear fully dissociated in water.

Differentiating solvents

Solvents such as glacial acetic acid can differentiate between strong acids that are leveled in water, allowing their relative strengths to be measured. The choice of solvent is therefore important in acid–base titrations of very strong or very weak acids and bases.
