London Southend Airport (IATA: SEN, ICAO: EGMC) is an international airport situated on the outskirts of Southend-on-Sea in Essex, England, approximately 36 miles (58 km) east of central London. It is sited between the districts of Rochford and Southend and has its own railway station on the Shenfield–Southend line, giving journey times of under an hour to London Liverpool Street.

History

The site began as a Royal Flying Corps station in 1915 and served as RAF Rochford during both world wars; the Battle of Britain fighter squadrons operated from its satellite role alongside RAF Hornchurch. After the Second World War it became a municipal airport, and in the 1960s it was briefly the third-busiest airport in the United Kingdom, handling cross-Channel car-ferry flights.

Traffic declined as larger jets outgrew its runway, but a regeneration programme by the Stobart Group after 2008 extended the runway to 1,856 metres, built a new control tower, terminal and railway station, and relaunched scheduled services in 2012 ahead of the London Olympics. Passenger numbers peaked at over two million in 2019, fell sharply during the COVID-19 pandemic, and have since recovered with based low-cost services, principally easyJet.

Facilities

The airport has a single asphalt runway (05/23) equipped with ILS, a passenger terminal opened in 2012 and extended in 2014, and extensive maintenance and aviation-services businesses. It markets itself on short check-in-to-gate times and its direct rail link.
