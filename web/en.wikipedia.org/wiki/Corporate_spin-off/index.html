Corporate spin-off

A corporate spin-off (also known as a spin-out or starburst) is a type of corporate action where a company "spins off" a section as a separate business or creates a new independent company. In a spin-off, the parent company distributes shares of the new entity to its existing shareholders on a pro-rata basis, and the spun-off unit becomes an independent, publicly traded company. It is a form of divestiture.

Reasons

Companies spin off divisions to unlock shareholder value when markets undervalue a conglomerate's parts, to separate businesses with different growth profiles or capital needs, to remove an unwanted or underperforming unit, or to satisfy regulators. Studies frequently find that spin-offs and their parents outperform the market on average, a result attributed to improved managerial focus and accountability.

Spin-off versus split-off and carve-out

In a split-off, shareholders must choose between holding the parent or the separated company, exchanging their shares. In an equity carve-out, the parent sells a minority stake in a subsidiary to the public via an IPO while retaining control. Under US tax law, a spin-off meeting the requirements of Internal Revenue Code Section 355 is tax-free to shareholders.

Examples

Notable spin-offs include PayPal from eBay (2015), AbbVie from Abbott Laboratories (2013), and the break-up of General Electric into GE HealthCare, GE Vernova and GE Aerospace (2023–2024).
