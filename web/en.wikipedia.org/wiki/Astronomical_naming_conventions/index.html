Astronomical naming conventions
Authority: International Astronomical Union (IAU)
Systems: Bayer, Flamsteed, provisional designations, catalogue numbers

Astronomical naming conventions are the systems by which astronomers designate celestial objects. Since 1919 the International Astronomical Union (IAU) has been the recognised authority for naming stars, planets, minor planets and their surface features, although in practice many names originate in catalogues and tradition.

Stars

Bright stars carry traditional proper names (such as Sirius) and Bayer designations combining a Greek letter with the constellation (Alpha Centauri); fainter stars are known by Flamsteed numbers or catalogue identifiers like HD and HIP numbers. The IAU's Working Group on Star Names standardises proper names.

Minor planets and other bodies

Newly discovered minor planets receive provisional designations encoding the year and order of discovery, then a permanent number once the orbit is secure, after which the discoverer may propose a name. Comets are named for their discoverers; trans-Neptunian objects and planetary features follow thematic rules set by IAU working groups.
