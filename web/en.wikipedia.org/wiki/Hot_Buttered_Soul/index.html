Hot Buttered Soul

Hot Buttered Soul
Artist: Isaac Hayes
Released: June 23, 1969
Studio: Ardent Studios, Memphis
Label: Enterprise (Stax)
Genre: Progressive soul, funk
Producer: Al Bell, Marvell Thomas, Allen Jones

Hot Buttered Soul is the second studio album by American soul artist Isaac Hayes, released on June 23, 1969, on the Enterprise subsidiary of Stax Records. Consisting of only four lengthy tracks, the album was a landmark in soul music, expanding its boundaries with long, orchestral, jazz- and funk-inflected arrangements and paving the way for the progressive soul of the 1970s.

Background

After the assassination of Martin Luther King Jr. and the loss of much of the Stax roster to Atlantic Records in 1968, executive Al Bell ordered a bulk release of new albums to rebuild the label. Given unprecedented creative freedom, Hayes — previously a staff songwriter and producer with David Porter — recorded long reinterpretations of pop standards with the Bar-Kays as his rhythm section and lush orchestration.

Music

The album opens with an 12-minute version of Burt Bacharach and Hal David's "Walk On By", featuring fuzz guitar and sweeping strings. "Hyperbolicsyllabicsesquedalymistic" is an original funk workout; "One Woman" is a ballad of romantic conflict; and the closing "By the Time I Get to Phoenix", by Jimmy Webb, runs over 18 minutes, with Hayes's celebrated spoken introduction ("rap") lasting nearly nine minutes before the song proper begins.

Reception and legacy

The album reached No. 1 on the Billboard R&B chart and No. 8 on the pop chart, was certified gold, and made Hayes a star. Critics regard it as a foundational record of progressive soul, influencing Curtis Mayfield, Marvin Gaye, and Barry White. It has been preserved in retrospective lists of the greatest albums of all time and sampled extensively in hip hop.
