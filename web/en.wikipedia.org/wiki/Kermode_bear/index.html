Kermode bear

The Kermode bear (Ursus americanus kermodei), also known as the spirit bear, is a subspecies of the American black bear found in the coastal rainforests of British Columbia, Canada. It is famous because about one in ten individuals has a cream- or white-coloured coat caused by a recessive gene.

Description

The white coat is not albinism but a single-nucleotide mutation in the melanocortin 1 receptor gene, the same gene responsible for red hair in humans. White Kermode bears may have an advantage when fishing for salmon in daylight, since their pale silhouette is harder for fish to spot.

Habitat and conservation

Kermode bears inhabit Princess Royal Island, Gribbell Island, and the surrounding Great Bear Rainforest. The spirit bear is a symbol of the region and was named British Columbia's official mammal in 2006. It holds great significance for the Tsimshian and Gitga'at First Nations.
