Sustainable city

A sustainable city, eco-city, or green city is a city designed with consideration for the social, economic, and environmental impact and resilient habitat for existing populations, without compromising the ability of future generations to experience the same. Related concepts include urban sustainability, smart cities, and the 15-minute city.

Features

Sustainable cities aim to reduce their ecological footprint through compact, mixed-use development, public transport, walking and cycling infrastructure, energy-efficient buildings, renewable energy, waste recycling, urban agriculture, and green spaces that support biodiversity and manage stormwater. Water-sensitive design, district heating, and circular-economy practices are also common. Social sustainability — affordable housing, access to services, and participatory governance — is increasingly treated as equally essential.

Examples and assessment

Cities frequently cited in indices and studies include Copenhagen, with its cycling culture and district heating; Singapore, with its water management and greenery; Curitiba, noted for its bus rapid transit; and Freiburg's Vauban district. Purpose-built eco-city projects such as Masdar City in the UAE and various Chinese eco-cities have had mixed results, sometimes criticised for cost, slow occupancy, or "greenwashing". Ranking systems such as the Arcadis Sustainable Cities Index and ISO 37120 attempt to measure performance across people, planet, and profit.
