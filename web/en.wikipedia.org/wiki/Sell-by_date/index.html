Sell-by date

A sell-by date is a date printed on packaged food products indicating the last day a retailer should display the product for sale. It is one of several forms of date marking used in the food industry, alongside use-by dates and best-before dates. The sell-by date is primarily a stock-control tool for retailers; the food may remain safe and edible for some time afterwards if stored properly, and it generally allows a margin for home storage after purchase.

Related date marks

A use-by date is a safety-related mark found on highly perishable foods, after which the food should not be eaten. A best-before date concerns quality rather than safety: food past its best-before date may lose flavour or texture but is not necessarily unsafe. Terminology and legal requirements vary between countries; in the United States, date labels on most foods are not federally standardized (infant formula being an exception), while the European Union requires use-by or best-before marking under its food information rules.

Food waste

Confusion among consumers between sell-by, best-before, and use-by dates is widely identified as a contributor to avoidable food waste, since edible food is often discarded based on a date that refers only to quality or stock rotation.
