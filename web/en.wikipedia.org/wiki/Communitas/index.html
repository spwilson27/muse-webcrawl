Communitas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Communitas is a Latin noun referring to an unstructured community in which people are essentially equal, or to the spirit of community and solidarity that emerges in such a group. In anthropology the term was developed by Victor Turner in his work on ritual and liminality, most fully in The Ritual Process: Structure and Anti-Structure (1969).

Turner's theory

Building on Arnold van Gennep's model of rites of passage, Turner argued that during the liminal (threshold) phase of a ritual, participants are stripped of their normal social status and experience an intense, egalitarian bond — communitas — that stands in anti-structure to the hierarchical ordering of everyday society. Pilgrimages, initiation rites, and festivals are classic settings. Turner distinguished three forms: spontaneous or existential communitas (a fleeting, direct sense of human unity), normative communitas (organised attempts to sustain the experience, such as religious orders), and ideological communitas (utopian models proposed for society as a whole).

Applications

The concept has been applied to studies of pilgrimage, music festivals, Burning Man, disaster response, and online communities, where temporary suspension of ordinary status produces feelings of shared identity. Thinkers such as Roberto Esposito have used communitas in political philosophy, contrasting the communal sharing it implies (from cum-munus, "together in a gift or duty") with immunitas, exemption from communal obligation.
