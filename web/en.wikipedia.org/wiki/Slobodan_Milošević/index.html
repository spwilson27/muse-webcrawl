Slobodan Milošević (Serbian Cyrillic: Слободан Милошевић; 20 August 1941 – 11 March 2006) was a Serbian and Yugoslav politician who served as President of Serbia from 1989 to 1997 and President of the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia from 1997 to 2000. A former banker and Communist apparatchik, he rose to power on a wave of Serbian nationalism, presiding over the violent breakup of Yugoslavia, and became the first sitting head of state indicted for war crimes by an international tribunal.

Rise to power

Milošević, the son of a Serbian Orthodox theologian father and a schoolteacher mother (both of whom died by suicide), studied law at the University of Belgrade, where he met his wife Mirjana Marković. He rose through the League of Communists and ran the state gas company Tehnogas and Beobanka. In April 1987, sent to Kosovo Polje to defuse clashes between Kosovo Serbs and Albanian-majority police, he declared "No one should dare to beat you" — a moment broadcast nationally that launched his nationalist ascent. He seized control of the Serbian party in the "anti-bureaucratic revolution," revoked Kosovo's and Vojvodina's autonomy in 1989, and marked the 600th anniversary of the Battle of Kosovo with the Gazimestan speech.

Wars of Yugoslav succession

As Slovenia and Croatia declared independence in 1991 and Bosnia and Herzegovina in 1992, Milošević backed Serb separatist entities and paramilitaries. Serbia was hit by UN sanctions and hyperinflation. He negotiated for the Bosnian Serbs at the Dayton Accords (1995), which ended the Bosnian War and briefly recast him as a peacemaker. In 1998–99, his forces' campaign against the Kosovo Liberation Army and ethnic Albanian civilians — including mass expulsions — triggered NATO's 78-day bombing of Yugoslavia, ending with Serbian withdrawal from Kosovo.

Downfall and trial

After disputing the September 2000 election result against Vojislav Koštunica, mass protests — the Bulldozer Revolution of 5 October 2000 — forced Milošević from power. He was arrested in April 2001 and transferred in June to the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY) in The Hague, indicted for war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide across Kosovo, Croatia and Bosnia. His trial, at which he defended himself defiantly, began in February 2002 but was repeatedly delayed by his ill health. He was found dead in his cell on 11 March 2006, of a heart attack; the trial was terminated without a verdict. In 2016, the ICTY's Karadžić judgment noted Milošević had shared the goals of a joint criminal enterprise; the ICJ, in Bosnia's genocide case against Serbia (2007), found Serbia not directly responsible for genocide but in breach of its duty to prevent it at Srebrenica.
