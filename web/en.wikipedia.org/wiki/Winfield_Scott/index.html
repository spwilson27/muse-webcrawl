Winfield Scott (June 13, 1786 – May 29, 1866) was an American military commander and political candidate. He served as Commanding General of the United States Army from 1841 to 1861, a tenure longer than any other person in that position, and was active in the War of 1812, the Mexican–American War, the early American Civil War, and numerous conflicts with Native Americans. Scott was the Whig Party's presidential nominee in the 1852 election, losing to Democrat Franklin Pierce.

Early career and War of 1812

Born in Virginia, Scott briefly practised law before joining the army in 1808. In the War of 1812 he was captured at the Battle of Queenston Heights (1812), exchanged, and then won distinction training the army at Buffalo and fighting at Chippewa (July 5, 1814) and Lundy's Lane (July 25, 1814), where he was severely wounded. His insistence on drill and discipline—using translated French manuals—helped transform the army into a professional force. His fondness for elaborate uniforms earned him the nickname "Old Fuss and Feathers".

Mexican–American War

In 1847 Scott commanded the expedition against Mexico City: he landed near Veracruz in the first major amphibious operation in U.S. history, besieged and took the city, then marched inland winning the battles of Cerro Gordo, Contreras, Churubusco, Molino del Rey, and Chapultepec, occupying the Mexican capital in September 1847. The campaign is widely regarded as a masterpiece of operational art.

Civil War and the Anaconda Plan

At the outbreak of the Civil War in 1861 the 74-year-old Scott, a Virginian who remained loyal to the Union, devised the strategic framework known as the Anaconda Plan: a naval blockade of the Confederacy combined with a thrust down the Mississippi River to divide and slowly strangle the South. Initially ridiculed, the plan broadly anticipated the Union's eventual winning strategy. Infirm and unable to ride a horse, Scott retired on November 1, 1861, succeeded by George B. McClellan. He died at West Point in 1866. Scott was the last surviving general of the War of 1812 era and one of the most significant American soldiers of the 19th century.
