George R. R. Martin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: George R. R. Martin signing books at a convention, wearing his signature cap and suspenders]
George R. R. Martin signing books at a convention, wearing his signature cap and suspenders

George Raymond Richard Martin (born 20 September 1948), also known as GRRM, is an American novelist, screenwriter and producer, best known for the Epic fantasy series A Song of Ice and Fire, adapted by HBO as Game of Thrones and House of the Dragon. A leading figure of modern fantasy, he is noted for gritty realism, vast casts of viewpoint characters and the killing of protagonists. Time named him one of the 100 most influential people in the world in 2011.

Early career

Martin was born in Bayonne, New Jersey, and sold his first stories in the early 1970s, winning Hugo and Nebula Awards for works such as 'A Song for Lya' and 'Sandkings'. The commercial failure of his fourth novel, The Armageddon Rag (1983), pushed him into television (The Twilight Zone, Beauty and the Beast) for a decade, while he edited the long-running shared-world anthology series Wild Cards (from 1987).

A Song of Ice and Fire

Returning to prose with few length constraints, Martin published A Game of Thrones in 1996; the series' slow expansion — a planned trilogy becoming five published volumes of seven — and the HBO adaptation (2011–19) made him the world's best-known living fantasist. The gap between A Dance with Dragons (2011) and the still-unfinished The Winds of Winter is a cultural saga of its own, debated by a fandom he alternately charms and exasperates.

Other work and life

Based in Santa Fe, Martin co-owns the Jean Cocteau Cinema and the railway Santa Fe Southern, funds the Wild Spirit Wolf Sanctuary, and co-developed the lore of the video game Elden Ring (2022) with FromSoftware. He blogs prolifically ('Not a Blog') on writing, football and politics.
