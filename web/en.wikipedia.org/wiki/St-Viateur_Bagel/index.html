St-Viateur Bagel

St-Viateur Bagel is a famous Montreal-style bagel bakery in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. Founded in 1957 by Myer Lewkowicz, a Polish-Jewish immigrant and Holocaust survivor, the original shop on Saint-Viateur Street in the Mile End neighbourhood is regarded as an institution of Montreal food culture.

The Montreal bagel

St-Viateur's bagels exemplify the Montreal style: compared with New York bagels they are smaller, denser and sweeter, with a larger hole. The dough contains egg and honey, and each bagel is hand-rolled, proofed, boiled in honey-sweetened water, and baked in a wood-fired oven on long wooden boards. Sesame seed and poppy seed are the classic varieties, and the ovens burn around the clock, so bagels are sold hot at all hours.

History and expansion

Lewkowicz arrived in Montreal after the Second World War and opened the bakery with partners in 1957. The business grew to include additional locations in the city and mail-order distribution across Canada. In 2019 the original Mile End location was threatened by a dispute over its wood-fired oven emissions following a change in landlord, drawing public support for the bakery; the shop continued operating. St-Viateur bagels are a symbol of Montreal's Jewish culinary heritage alongside smoked meat.
