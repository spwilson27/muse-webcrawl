Social entrepreneurship

Social entrepreneurship is the practice of developing, funding and implementing innovative solutions to social, cultural or environmental problems, applying entrepreneurial methods while prioritising social impact over (or alongside) profit. Social enterprises may be organised as nonprofits, cooperatives, or for-profit companies with a social mission — including legal forms such as the UK community interest company and the US benefit corporation.

History

The idea was popularised from the 1980s by Bill Drayton, founder of Ashoka, which selects and supports "social entrepreneurs" as fellows. Frequently cited examples include Muhammad Yunus's Grameen Bank and microfinance in Bangladesh, Wikipedia as a knowledge commons, and enterprises providing off-grid solar power or low-cost healthcare in developing countries.

Characteristics and debate

Scholars such as J. Gregory Dees emphasise social entrepreneurs' focus on sustainable, system-changing innovation and measurable social value. Debates continue over how social impact should be measured, whether earned income is essential to the definition, and how social enterprises relate to traditional charity, corporate social responsibility and public provision.
