Grágás

Grágás ("grey goose") is the common name given to the law code of the Icelandic Commonwealth (930-1262). The name, of uncertain origin and possibly jocular, appears in later sources. The laws were originally recited aloud by the Lawspeaker at the Althing at Þingvellir; they were first written down in the winter of 1117-1118.

Contents and manuscripts

Grágás survives principally in two manuscripts of the later 13th century, the Konungsbók and Staðarhólsbók versions, along with various fragments. Rather than a single enacted code, it is a collection of legal provisions, probably drawn from private compilations, covering Christian law, assembly procedure, homicide, property, marriage, inheritance, outlawry, and the treatment of dependents. Its detailed rules on wergild-like compensation and the prosecution of killings illuminate the feud culture described in the Icelandic sagas.

End of the Commonwealth

When Iceland submitted to Norwegian rule in 1262-1264, Grágás was progressively replaced by the Norwegian-influenced codes Ironside (Járnsíða) and then Jónsbók (1281).
