Soong sisters

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Soong sisters (Chinese: 宋氏姐妹) were three sisters from Shanghai — Soong Ai-ling, Soong Ching-ling, and Soong Mei-ling — who, together with their husbands and their brother T. V. Soong, stood at the centre of Chinese politics in the first half of the 20th century. Daughters of the wealthy Methodist businessman Charlie Soong, all three were educated in the United States, at Wesleyan College in Macon, Georgia.

The sisters

Soong Ai-ling (1889–1973), the eldest, married H. H. Kung, a banker descended from Confucius who served as finance minister and premier of the Republic of China. Ai-ling was known for her business acumen and amassed a great fortune.

Soong Ching-ling (1893–1981) married Sun Yat-sen, the founder of the Republic of China. After his death she broke with the Kuomintang and later became an honoured figure of the People's Republic of China, serving as its honorary President shortly before her death.

Soong Mei-ling (1898–2003) married Chiang Kai-shek, leader of the Republic of China, and became a prominent diplomat and public face of Nationalist China, especially in the United States.

Legacy

The sisters were encapsulated in a famous saying: "One loved money, one loved power, and one loved her country" (referring to Ai-ling, Mei-ling, and Ching-ling respectively). Their family — including brother T. V. Soong, a leading financier and premier — was counted among the "four great families" of Republican China. The Chinese Civil War split the family politically: Ching-ling sided with the Communists while Mei-ling and Ai-ling followed the Nationalists, so the sisters never reunited after 1949.
