The Burma Road (Chinese: 滇缅公路) was a road linking Burma (now Myanmar) with the southwest of China. Its terminals were at Lashio in Burma and Kunming in Yunnan province, China. Built during the Second Sino-Japanese War, the road became famous as the principal overland supply route by which the Western Allies delivered materiel to the Republic of China in its war against Imperial Japan.

Construction

The road was built between 1937 and 1938 by some 200,000 Chinese labourers, working largely by hand through mountainous jungle terrain. The Chinese section ran from Kunming to Wanding on the Burmese border; the Burmese section connected Wanding (via Ruili) to Lashio, where it met the railway to the port of Rangoon. Supplies shipped to Rangoon could thus travel by rail and road into unoccupied China.

World War II

After the outbreak of full-scale war in 1937, the Burma Road was China's lifeline once Japan seized the Chinese coastline. Under diplomatic pressure from Tokyo, Britain briefly closed the road in July 1940, reopening it three months later. Following the Japanese conquest of Burma in 1942, the road was cut, and the Allies were forced to supply China by air over the eastern Himalayas, a route known as the Hump.

To restore the overland link, Allied engineers under General Joseph Stilwell built the Ledo Road from Assam in India to join the old Burma Road near Bhamo. The combined route, renamed the Stilwell Road, was completed in January 1945, allowing the first convoy to reach Kunming in February 1945.
