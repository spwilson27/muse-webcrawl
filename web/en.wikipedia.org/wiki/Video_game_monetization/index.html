Video game monetization

Video game monetization refers to the ways publishers generate revenue from games beyond or instead of an upfront purchase price. Schemes include downloadable content, microtransactions, loot boxes, battle passes, subscriptions, advertising and the free-to-play model.

History

Expansion packs in the 1990s gave way to downloadable content in the 2000s — famously the 2006 Oblivion horse armour. Mobile and social games popularised free-to-play designs funded by a minority of high-spending players ("whales"). Loot boxes spread from Asian online games into Western AAA titles in the 2010s, prompting regulatory scrutiny in Belgium, the Netherlands and elsewhere over gambling concerns.

Models

Premium, freemium, subscription (Xbox Game Pass, MMO fees), season/battle passes and cosmetic-only stores each balance revenue against player goodwill. Live-service games monetise continuously updated content.

Reception

Critics argue some schemes exploit psychological triggers and minors; defenders note they fund ongoing development and lower entry barriers.
