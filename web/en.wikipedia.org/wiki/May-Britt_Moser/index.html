May-Britt Moser

May-Britt Moser (born 4 January 1963) is a Norwegian psychologist and neuroscientist. She shared the 2014 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Edvard Moser and John O'Keefe "for their discoveries of cells that constitute a positioning system in the brain."

Career

Moser studied psychology at the University of Oslo and earned her PhD in neurophysiology in 1995; she did postdoctoral work with Richard Morris in Edinburgh and visited John O'Keefe's laboratory at University College London. She became a professor at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU) in Trondheim, where she and Edvard Moser founded the Centre for the Biology of Memory (2002) and the Kavli Institute for Systems Neuroscience (2007), which she directs.

Research

Building on O'Keefe's discovery of place cells in the hippocampus, the Mosers identified grid cells in the entorhinal cortex (2005) — neurons that fire in a hexagonal pattern as an animal moves, forming an internal coordinate system. Their group subsequently characterized head-direction cells, border cells, and speed cells, mapping how the brain computes position and navigation. Their work transformed the understanding of spatial cognition and memory.
