The Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi (Kazakh: Қожа Ахмет Ясауи кесенесі) is an unfinished mausoleum in the city of Turkistan in southern Kazakhstan. Commissioned in 1389 by Timur, ruler of the Timurid Empire, it replaced a smaller 12th-century tomb over the grave of the celebrated Sufi teacher and poet Khoja Ahmed Yasawi (1093–1166), the first great Turkic-speaking Sufi mystic.

Architecture

The rectangular structure measures about 45.8 by 62.7 metres and is crowned by a ribbed dome some 44 metres high — the largest brick dome in Central Asia — over the main hall (the Kazandyk), which contains a huge bronze ritual cauldron cast in 1399. An unadorned portal, the tallest in Central Asia, was left unfinished when construction stopped after Timur's death in 1405; scaffolding holes remain visible in the facade. Inside are the tomb chamber (Gurkhana) with Yasawi's tombstone, a mosque with a notable mihrab, a library, refectory, and well rooms, decorated with glazed tilework in geometric and epigraphic patterns.

Significance

The mausoleum is a masterpiece of Timurid architecture and a prototype for later buildings including the Gur-e Amir in Samarkand and, more distantly, Mughal monuments such as the Taj Mahal. It remains a major pilgrimage site — visiting three times is said locally to equal the Hajj — and in 2003 it became Kazakhstan's first UNESCO World Heritage Site.
