Chloride

Chloride
FormulaCl−
Parent elementChlorine (Cl, atomic number 17)
Common saltsSodium chloride (table salt), potassium chloride, calcium chloride
RolesElectrolyte; gastric acid (HCl); nerve signalling

Chloride is the anion Cl− formed when the element chlorine gains an electron, occurring in ionic compounds called chlorides. The most familiar is sodium chloride — common salt — and chloride salts are generally highly soluble in water, making chloride the most abundant anion in seawater.

Chemistry

Chloride is the conjugate base of hydrochloric acid and a weak reducing agent. In analysis it is detected by precipitation with silver nitrate, forming white silver chloride. Industrially, chlorides are produced by and used in the chloralkali process, and chloride-induced corrosion of steel is a major concern in reinforced concrete and marine environments.

Biological role

Chloride is an essential electrolyte: it is the principal anion of extracellular fluid, helps maintain osmotic balance and blood pressure, and is required to produce gastric hydrochloric acid. Chloride channels, including the CFTR channel defective in cystic fibrosis, mediate its movement across cell membranes, and inhibitory neurotransmission in the nervous system depends on chloride gradients.
