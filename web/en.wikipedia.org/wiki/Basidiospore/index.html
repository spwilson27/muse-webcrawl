Basidiospore

A basidiospore is a reproductive spore produced by Basidiomycete fungi, including mushrooms, puffballs, rusts and smuts. Basidiospores are borne externally on a specialised spore-producing cell called a basidium, typically four spores per basidium.

Formation and discharge

Within the basidium, two haploid nuclei fuse (karyogamy) and immediately undergo meiosis, producing four haploid nuclei that migrate into spores budded at the tips of slender projections called sterigmata. In gilled mushrooms the mature spores are forcibly discharged by a surface-tension catapult mechanism known as ballistochory, in which a droplet (Buller's drop) grows at the spore's base and propels it a fraction of a millimetre — just far enough to clear the gill and fall free.

Numbers and significance

A single large mushroom may release billions of basidiospores over a few days, visible as a coloured dust or spore print whose colour is an important identification character. In the rust and smut fungi, basidiospores form part of complex life cycles that parasitise crops such as wheat.
