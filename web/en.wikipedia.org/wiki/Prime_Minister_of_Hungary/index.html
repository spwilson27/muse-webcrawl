The Prime Minister of Hungary (Magyarország miniszterelnöke) is the head of government of Hungary and leader of the Hungarian cabinet. Under the Fundamental Law of Hungary, the prime minister is nominated by the president of the republic and formally elected by the National Assembly, which also votes on the government's programme. The prime minister determines the general policy of the government and may be removed only by a constructive vote of no confidence, which simultaneously elects a successor.

History

The office was created during the Hungarian Revolution of 1848, when Count Lajos Batthyány formed the first responsible Hungarian government within the Austrian Empire. After the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867, Hungary again had its own prime ministers, beginning with Gyula Andrássy. The office survived — under varying constitutions and titles — through the Kingdom of Hungary, the interwar regency, and the communist period, when the head of government was titled chairman of the Council of Ministers. The modern post dates from the democratic transition of 1989–90, when József Antall became the first freely elected prime minister.

Recent holders

Post-communist prime ministers include József Antall (1990–93), Péter Boross, Gyula Horn, Viktor Orbán (1998–2002), Péter Medgyessy, Ferenc Gyurcsány, and Gordon Bajnai. Viktor Orbán of Fidesz returned to power in 2010 with a two-thirds parliamentary supermajority and has won consecutive elections since, becoming Hungary's longest-serving prime minister; his governments rewrote the constitution, centralized media and electoral institutions, and repeatedly clashed with the European Union over rule-of-law questions.
