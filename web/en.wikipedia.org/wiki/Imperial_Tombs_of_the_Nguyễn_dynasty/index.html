Imperial Tombs of the Nguyễn dynasty
LocationHuế, Vietnam
Built19th–early 20th century
UNESCOPart of the Complex of Huế Monuments (World Heritage, 1993)

The Imperial Tombs of the Nguyễn dynasty are a group of mausoleums of the emperors of Vietnam's Nguyễn dynasty, built in and around the former imperial capital of Huế in central Vietnam, mostly along the Perfume River. Each tomb was planned during its emperor's lifetime as both a palace for the living and a residence for the afterlife, combining fortified enclosures, temples, courtyards of stone mandarins and animals, stelae pavilions, lakes, and landscaped gardens laid out according to feng shui.

The tombs

  • Gia Long (Thiên Thọ Lăng) — the most remote, a vast, serene complex founded in 1814.
  • Minh Mạng (Hiếu Lăng) — admired for its classical, symmetrical layout along a 700-metre axis of lakes and temples.
  • Thiệu Trị (Xương Lăng) — comparatively modest, modelled on his father's tomb.
  • Tự Đức (Khiêm Lăng) — the most poetic, built as a retreat with a lake, island, and theatre where the emperor lived for long periods.
  • Dục Đức (An Lăng) — the smallest and simplest of the imperial tombs.
  • Đồng Khánh (Tư Lăng) — showing early Western influence.
  • Khải Định (Ứng Lăng) — the last built, a striking blend of Vietnamese and European styles in reinforced concrete, with elaborate glass-and-porcelain mosaics inside.

Heritage

The tombs form part of the "Complex of Huế Monuments", inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993 together with the Imperial City and other monuments. They survived the wars of the 20th century largely intact and are among Vietnam's most visited cultural sites.
