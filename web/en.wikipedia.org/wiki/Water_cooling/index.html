Water cooling

TypeHeat removal method
MediumWater or coolant
ApplicationsEngines, computers, power stations, industry

Water cooling is a method of heat removal from components and industrial equipment. As opposed to air cooling, water is used as the heat conductor. Water cooling is commonly used for cooling automobile internal combustion engines, large industrial facilities such as power stations, and increasingly for high-performance computers.

Principle

Water has a high specific heat capacity and thermal conductivity, allowing it to absorb and transport large amounts of heat. In a typical closed loop, a pump circulates coolant through a block in contact with the heat source, then through a radiator or heat exchanger where the heat is rejected to the environment.

Applications

In internal combustion engines, a water jacket surrounds the cylinders and the coolant is cooled in a radiator. In computing, liquid cooling ranges from sealed all-in-one kits to custom loops cooling CPUs and GPUs in gaming PCs and data centres. Power stations and industrial plants often use once-through cooling from a river or sea, or recirculating cooling towers.
