Coldstream Guards

Coldstream Guards
Active: 1650–present
Country: United Kingdom
Branch: British Army
Role: Light infantry / public duties
Motto: Nulli Secundus ("Second to None")
Nickname: "The Lilywhites"

The Coldstream Guards is the oldest continuously serving regular regiment in the British Army. As part of the Guards Division, it is one of the Foot Guards regiments, whose primary ceremonial role is the protection of the sovereign and royal palaces, alongside an operational role as infantry. The regiment takes its name from the Scottish village of Coldstream, where its founder, General George Monck, began his march on London in 1660.

History

The regiment was raised by Monck in 1650 during the English Civil War era, from five companies of other regiments. In 1660 Monck's regiment marched from Coldstream to London and played a decisive part in the Restoration of Charles II; after Monck's death in 1670 it was renamed the Coldstream Regiment of Foot Guards. It has served in nearly every major British conflict since, including the American War of Independence, the Napoleonic Wars — notably the defence of Hougoumont at Waterloo — the Crimean War, both World Wars, and more recent operations in Iraq and Afghanistan.

Traditions and uniform

Coldstream Guardsmen are recognised on parade by the red plume worn on the right side of the bearskin cap and by buttons on the scarlet tunic arranged in pairs. The regimental motto, Nulli Secundus ("Second to None"), reflects the regiment's claim to seniority: although the Grenadier Guards are first in the order of precedence as the older royal bodyguard, the Coldstreamers insist they are "second to none", pointing to their earlier continuous service as a regular regiment. The regimental band is famous for performances at the Changing of the Guard and Trooping the Colour.

Modern role

The regiment's 1st Battalion serves as light infantry, while No. 7 Company provides public duties in London and Windsor. Coldstream Guards have won numerous gallantry awards, including Victoria Crosses, and the regiment maintains strong recruiting ties with the North East of England and the Scottish Borders.
