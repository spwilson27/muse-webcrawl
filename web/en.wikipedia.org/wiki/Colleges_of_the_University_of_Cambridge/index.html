Colleges of the University of Cambridge

The University of Cambridge is a collegiate university: alongside its central departments and faculties, it comprises 31 semi-autonomous constituent colleges, each a self-governing community of scholars with its own property, income and internal governance. Every student and most academics belong to a college, which provides accommodation, dining, pastoral care and small-group teaching (supervisions), while the university confers degrees and organises lectures and examinations.

The colleges

The oldest college is Peterhouse, founded in 1284; the newest is Robinson College, founded in 1977. Sixteen colleges date from before 1600 ("old" colleges) and fifteen from later centuries ("new" colleges). Among the largest and most famous are Trinity College (founded 1546, the wealthiest, with more Nobel laureates than most countries), St John's College, and King's College, noted for its chapel and choir. Three colleges — Murray Edwards, Newnham and Lucy Cavendish (the latter now mixed) — admit only women; several colleges admit only mature students or only postgraduates, including Clare Hall and Wolfson College.

Role and rivalry

Colleges select their own undergraduates, employ fellows, and compete in sport and in the informal academic league tables such as the Tompkins Table. Despite their independence, the colleges collectively fund university activities through assessed contributions, and their combined endowments make Cambridge the wealthiest university in Europe.
