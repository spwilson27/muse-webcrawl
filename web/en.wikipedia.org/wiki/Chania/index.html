Chania

Chania (Greek: Χανιά, pronounced [xaˈɲa]; Venetian: Canea) is a city in Greece and the capital of the Chania regional unit. It lies along the north coast of the island of Crete, about 70 km (43 mi) west of Rethymno and 145 km (90 mi) west of Heraklion. The city is the second most populous on Crete, with a population of around 88,000 in the municipality.

Chania can be divided in two parts: the old town and the modern city. The old town is centred on the Venetian harbour, with narrow lanes, Venetian and Ottoman-era buildings, the famous Venetian lighthouse, and the mosque of the Janissaries (the Hassan Pasha Mosque), built after the Ottoman conquest of 1645.

History

Ancient and medieval

The site of Chania was the location of the Minoan settlement of Kydonia (Greek: Κυδωνία), one of the most important cities of ancient Crete, mentioned by Homer. After the decline of the Minoan civilisation the city continued as a Greek city-state. It was later ruled by Rome and then by the Byzantine Empire.

After the Fourth Crusade, Crete passed to Venice, and from 1212 Chania (as Canea) developed as a Venetian stronghold. The Venetians built the harbour, fortifications, the Firkas fortress and the shipyards (neoria) that still line the port.

Ottoman period

After a two-month siege, the Ottoman Empire captured Chania in 1645, the first Cretan city to fall in the Cretan War. Under Ottoman rule the city acquired mosques, baths and fountains, and many churches were converted to mosques. During the 19th century, after the Greek War of Independence, Chania became the capital of Ottoman Crete.

Modern era

In 1898, during the transition to the autonomous Cretan State, Chania became the capital of the island, a role it kept until 1971, when Heraklion became the administrative capital of Crete. In May 1941 the area was the scene of the Battle of Maleme during the Battle of Crete; the nearby Maleme airfield was a key objective of the German airborne invasion. The German commander General Bernhard Ramke and other German military personnel were later tried in Chania, and the German war cemetery stands on a hill near Maleme.

Geography and climate

Chania lies on the north-western coast of Crete, backed by the White Mountains (Lefka Ori). It has a hot-summer Mediterranean climate, with long, dry summers and mild, wetter winters. The city is served by Chania International Airport "Ioannis Daskalogiannis" on the Akrotiri peninsula.

Culture

The city hosts the Archaeological Museum of Chania, the Maritime Museum of Crete, and the Municipal Art Gallery. Chania was the birthplace of Eleftherios Venizelos, the prominent Greek statesman, whose tomb lies on the Akrotiri peninsula overlooking the city. The covered municipal market (Agora), built in 1913 in the shape of a cross, is a landmark of the modern city.
