Combination drug

A combination drug or fixed-dose combination (FDC) is a medicine that includes two or more active ingredients combined in a single dosage form, such as a tablet or capsule. Combination products may also be produced by combining drug ingredients with devices or biological products. Fixed-dose combinations are widely used in the treatment of HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria, hypertension, and diabetes, where therapy inherently requires several drugs.

One dosage form delivering multiple active ingredients simplifies complex regimens.

Rationale

Combining agents in one pill simplifies treatment regimens, improving patient adherence, reducing pill burden, and lowering the chance of missed doses. Adherence is clinically crucial where partial treatment breeds drug resistance, as in tuberculosis and HIV. Combinations may also be designed so the ingredients act synergistically, or so that one ingredient counteracts a side effect of the other (for example, combining a prostaglandin with a protective agent).

Examples

Well-known fixed-dose combinations include:

  • Co-trimoxazole (trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole) — antibacterial.
  • Antiretroviral combinations such as tenofovir/emtricitabine and complete regimens in a single tablet (e.g. bictegravir/tenofovir alafenamide/emtricitabine) for HIV.
  • Artemisinin-based combination therapies (ACTs) for malaria.
  • Antihypertensive combinations such as amlodipine/valsartan.
  • Combined oral contraceptives (an estrogen plus a progestin).
  • Levodopa/carbidopa for Parkinson's disease.

Disadvantages and regulation

Fixed ratios prevent individual dose titration of the components, and a patient needing only one ingredient is exposed to the others and their side effects. If an adverse reaction occurs, it may be harder to identify the responsible component. Regulators such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration generally require that each ingredient contribute to the claimed effect; the World Health Organization maintains a list of recommended FDCs on its Essential Medicines List.
