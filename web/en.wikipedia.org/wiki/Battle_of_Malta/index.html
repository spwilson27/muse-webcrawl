Battle of Malta

The Battle of Malta is a name applied to several engagements around the island of Malta; this article concerns the naval battle of 8 July 1283 — also known as the action off Malta — fought during the War of the Sicilian Vespers, in which an Aragonese-Catalan galley fleet under Roger of Lauria defeated an Angevin (Neapolitan) fleet in the Grand Harbour of Malta.

Background

After the Sicilian Vespers revolt of 1282, Peter III of Aragon intervened in Sicily against Charles I of Anjou. Malta, held by an Angevin garrison at the Castrum Maris (Fort St Angelo) but besieged by Maltese rebels and Aragonese supporters, became a flashpoint. An Angevin relief fleet of galleys from Naples entered the Grand Harbour in July 1283.

The battle

Roger of Lauria arrived with his galleys and attacked at dawn on 8 July. Lauria kept his ships chained or closely ordered, letting the Angevins exhaust themselves, then used his Catalan and Sicilian crossbowmen and the shock of the almogavars boarding to overwhelm the enemy. The Angevin admirals were killed and some ten galleys captured; the rest fled. The victory cemented Aragonese sea power in the central Mediterranean.

Aftermath

Though the Angevin castle garrison at Malta held out until 1284–85, Lauria's victory off Malta was the first of his string of triumphs (followed by the Gulf of Naples, 1284, and Les Formigues, 1285) that made him the most celebrated admiral of the age and secured Aragonese-Sicilian control of the sea lanes.
