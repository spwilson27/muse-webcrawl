Membrane potential

Membrane potential (also transmembrane potential or membrane voltage) is the difference in electric potential between the interior and the exterior of a biological cell. Typical values range from –40 mV to –80 mV in animal cells.

Resting potential

When a cell is not conducting an impulse, its membrane potential is known as the resting potential, maintained largely by the sodium–potassium pump and potassium leak channels. The equilibrium potential for an ion is given by the Nernst equation.

Action potentials

Changes in membrane potential underlie signalling in neurons and muscle cells. An action potential is a rapid, transient depolarisation of the membrane.
