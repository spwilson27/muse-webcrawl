Yul Brynner

Yul Brynner (born Yuliy Borisovich Briner; 11 July 1920 – 10 October 1985) was a Russian-born actor best known for his portrayal of King Mongkut of Siam in the Rodgers and Hammerstein musical The King and I, a role he played 4,625 times on stage and in the 1956 film, for which he won the Academy Award for Best Actor. With his shaved head and commanding presence, Brynner became one of the most distinctive leading men of 1950s Hollywood, starring in The Ten Commandments (1956), The Magnificent Seven (1960) and Westworld (1973).

Early life

Brynner was born in Vladivostok to a family of Russian, Swiss and Buryat descent. After his father abandoned the family, his mother moved with the children to Harbin, China, and later Paris, where Brynner worked as a trapeze artist and musician before an injury turned him to acting. He emigrated to the United States in 1940 and worked for the Office of War Information broadcasting to occupied France.

Career

Brynner's Broadway debut in The King and I (1951) made him a star; he shaved his head for the role, a look that became his trademark. The film version earned him an Oscar. In the same year he played Pharaoh Rameses II opposite Charlton Heston in Cecil B. DeMille's The Ten Commandments. He headlined the western The Magnificent Seven, the biblical epic Solomon and Sheba, and later the science-fiction thriller Westworld, in which his implacable robot Gunslinger anticipated the Terminator. He toured in The King and I revivals almost until his death.

Death and legacy

A lifelong heavy smoker, Brynner died of lung cancer in 1985, the same day as Orson Welles. He recorded an anti-smoking public service announcement released posthumously, in which he said: "Now that I'm gone, I tell you: don't smoke." His son Rock Brynner wrote a biography, Yul: The Man Who Would Be King.
