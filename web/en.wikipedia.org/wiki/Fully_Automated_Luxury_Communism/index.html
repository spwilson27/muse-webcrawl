Fully Automated Luxury Communism

Fully Automated Luxury Communism (FALC) is a political ideology and slogan that envisions a post-scarcity society in which automation and advanced technology provide abundant goods and services for all, eliminating the need for wage labour while maintaining a high standard of living. It draws on Marxist ideas updated for an age of robotics, artificial intelligence, renewable energy, and asteroid mining.

Origins

The phrase circulated on left-wing internet forums in the early 2010s and was popularised by the British media collective Novara Media. Journalist Aaron Bastani developed the idea at length in his 2019 book Fully Automated Luxury Communism: A Manifesto, arguing that a "communism of abundance" could arise from the falling costs of information, energy, and automated production — a series of disruptions he calls the Third Disruption.

Ideas and criticism

Proposals associated with FALC include a universal basic income or universal basic services, drastically reduced working hours, and public ownership of automated infrastructure. Critics question its environmental feasibility, its optimism about technological progress, and whether abundance can be reconciled with ecological limits.
