The Binnenhof (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈbɪnə(n)ˌɦɔf]; lit. 'inner court') is a complex of buildings in the centre of The Hague, Netherlands, next to the Hofvijver pond. It has been the meeting place of the States General, the parliament of the Netherlands, since 1446, and is among the oldest parliament buildings in the world still in use. The complex also houses the Ministry of General Affairs and the office of the Prime Minister of the Netherlands, the Torentje ("little tower").

History

The complex began as a castle of the Counts of Holland: Count Floris IV purchased the site around 1229, and his son William II began construction of what became the Ridderzaal (Hall of Knights), completed under Floris V around 1290. The Ridderzaal is the ceremonial centre of the complex, where the monarch delivers the Speech from the Throne on Prinsjesdag each September.

During the Dutch Republic the Binnenhof was the seat of the States General and the residence of the stadtholders. The Trêveszaal, overlooking the Hofvijver, is now the meeting room of the Council of Ministers.

Layout

The rectangular courtyard is surrounded by the buildings of the Senate (Eerste Kamer) on one side and the House of Representatives (Tweede Kamer) chamber, which moved into a modern extension designed by Pi de Bruijn in 1992. The complex has been undergoing a major renovation begun in 2021, during which parliament temporarily relocated elsewhere in The Hague.
