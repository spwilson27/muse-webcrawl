Karl Ove Knausgård (born 6 December 1968) is a Norwegian author best known for the six-volume autobiographical novel sequence My Struggle (Norwegian: Min kamp, 2009–2011), which became a literary sensation in Scandinavia and internationally and helped define the genre of autofiction.

Career

Knausgård grew up on Tromøya island and in Kristiansand, and studied literature and art history at the University of Bergen. His debut novel Out of the World (Ute av verden, 1998) won the Norwegian Critics Prize for Literature — the first debut to do so — and its partly autobiographical portrait of a small-town childhood was followed by A Time for Everything (2004), a retelling of biblical stories centred on angels.

My Struggle

The six volumes of Min kamp — some 3,600 pages in total — narrate Knausgård's life in extraordinary, unfiltered detail, from childhood and his fraught relationship with his father to marriage, fatherhood and writing. The books sold roughly one copy for every ten Norwegians and provoked intense debate and legal threats from relatives depicted in them; the provocative title echoes Hitler's Mein Kampf, a connection the author has addressed in the final volume's long essay. English translations by Don Bartlett (and Martin Aitken for the last volume) made the series a global phenomenon; Zadie Smith and other writers praised its hypnotic immediacy.

Later works include the seasonal quartet Autumn, Winter, Spring and Summer (2015–2016), written as letters to his children, and the novels The Morning Star (2020) and its sequels.
