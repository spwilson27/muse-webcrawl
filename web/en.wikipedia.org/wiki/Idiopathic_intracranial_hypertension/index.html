Idiopathic intracranial hypertension

Idiopathic intracranial hypertension
Other name: Pseudotumor cerebri
Specialty: Neurology
Symptoms: Headache, visual disturbance, pulsatile tinnitus

Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH), also known as pseudotumor cerebri, is a condition characterised by raised intracranial pressure without a detectable cause on brain imaging — no tumour, hydrocephalus or infection. It most commonly affects women of childbearing age with obesity.

Signs and symptoms

Typical features include headache, transient visual obscurations, pulsatile tinnitus (a whooshing sound in time with the pulse), double vision from sixth nerve palsy, and swelling of the optic discs (papilledema), which can lead to permanent vision loss if untreated.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on normal neuroimaging (often with signs such as an empty sella or distended optic nerve sheaths), a raised opening pressure on lumbar puncture, and normal cerebrospinal fluid composition. Management centres on weight loss, the carbonic anhydrase inhibitor acetazolamide, and, where vision is threatened, procedures such as optic nerve sheath fenestration or cerebrospinal fluid shunting and venous sinus stenting.
