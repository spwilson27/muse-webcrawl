The Emirate of Transjordan (Arabic: إمارة شرق الأردن, "Emirate of the East Bank of the Jordan") was a British protectorate established on 11 April 1921, formally part of the League of Nations Mandate for Palestine but administered separately. It became the independent Hashemite Kingdom of Transjordan — later Jordan — in 1946.

Establishment

After the First World War and the collapse of Faisal's Arab Kingdom of Syria in 1920, Abdullah ibn al-Husayn, second son of Sharif Hussein of Mecca, advanced into the territory east of the Jordan River. At the Cairo Conference of March 1921, Winston Churchill agreed that Abdullah should rule the territory under British oversight. The Transjordan memorandum of 1922 excluded the emirate from the Jewish settlement provisions of the Palestine Mandate.

Government and development

Abdullah ruled from Amman with the advice of a British Resident, initially building a rudimentary administration and the Arab Legion, commanded from 1939 by Glubb Pasha. The 1928 Organic Law provided a constitution and an elected legislative council. Borders with Saudi Arabia, Iraq and Syria were settled through agreements in the 1920s and 1930s, sometimes involving territorial exchanges such as the Ma'an and Aqaba districts in 1925.

Independence

By the Treaty of London, signed on 22 March 1946, Britain recognised Transjordan's independence; on 25 May 1946 Abdullah was proclaimed King of the Hashemite Kingdom of Transjordan. After the 1948 Arab–Israeli War and the annexation of the West Bank, the state was renamed the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan in 1949.
