Liaison (French)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In French phonology, liaison is the pronunciation of a word-final consonant that is normally silent, when the following word begins with a vowel (or mute h) and the two words form a close syntactic unit. The consonant is "latent": it appears only in this linking environment. Thus les alone is pronounced /le/, but in les amis ("the friends") the final s surfaces as /z/: /le.za.mi/.

Types of liaison

Liaisons are classified as obligatory, optional and forbidden. Obligatory liaisons occur, for example, between a determiner and a noun (les enfants), between a pronoun and a verb (nous avons), and after certain prepositions and in fixed phrases. Optional liaisons, whose frequency varies with register and formality, occur for instance after plural nouns and some verb forms. Liaisons are forbidden after the conjunction et, before words beginning with aspirate h, and in various other contexts. The consonant realised depends on the letter: s and x give /z/, d gives /t/, f gives /v/, and nasal vowels may denasalise with /n/.

Related phenomena

Liaison is related to enchaînement (resyllabification of an always-pronounced final consonant) and contrasts with elision, in which a final vowel is dropped before a following vowel, as in l'ami. Mastering liaison contexts is a well-known difficulty for learners of French.
