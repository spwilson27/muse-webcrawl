Mountain Railways of India

UNESCO World Heritage Site

Components: Darjeeling Himalayan Railway; Nilgiri Mountain Railway; Kalka–Shimla Railway

Inscribed: 1999 (Darjeeling), extended 2005 and 2008

Criteria: Cultural (ii), (iv)

The Mountain railways of India are a group of railway lines built in the mountainous regions of India during the British colonial period. Three of them — the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, the Nilgiri Mountain Railway and the Kalka–Shimla Railway — are collectively listed by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site, recognised as outstanding examples of the engineering solutions developed to establish rail connections through difficult mountain terrain.

Darjeeling Himalayan Railway

Opened in stages between 1879 and 1881, the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway runs about 88 km (55 mi) from New Jalpaiguri to Darjeeling in West Bengal, climbing from about 100 m to over 2,200 m. The narrow-gauge (2 ft / 610 mm) line uses loops and zig-zag reverses to gain altitude and is famed for its Batasia Loop. It was the first component inscribed by UNESCO, in 1999, and its small B-class steam locomotives remain in regular use.

Nilgiri Mountain Railway

The Nilgiri Mountain Railway in Tamil Nadu runs 46 km (29 mi) from Mettupalayam to Udhagamandalam (Ooty) through the Nilgiri Hills. Completed in 1908, it is India's only rack railway, using the Abt rack and pinion system on its steepest section, where gradients reach 1 in 12. The metre-gauge line was added to the World Heritage listing in 2005.

Kalka–Shimla Railway

The Kalka–Shimla Railway, completed in 1903, climbs 96 km (60 mi) from Kalka in Haryana to Shimla, the former summer capital of British India, rising from 656 m to 2,075 m. The 2 ft 6 in (762 mm) gauge line has 102 tunnels and 864 bridges, including striking multi-arched masonry viaducts. It was added to the listing in 2008.

Other mountain lines

Other notable Indian hill railways include the Kangra Valley Railway in Himachal Pradesh and the Matheran Hill Railway in Maharashtra, which are not part of the UNESCO inscription.
