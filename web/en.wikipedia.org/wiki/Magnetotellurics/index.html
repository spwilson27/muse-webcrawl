Magnetotellurics

Magnetotellurics (MT) is an electromagnetic geophysical method for inferring the Earth's subsurface electrical conductivity from measurements of natural geomagnetic and geoelectric field variations at the surface. The method was proposed in the early 1950s independently by Andrey Tikhonov and Louis Cagniard.

Principle

Time-varying magnetic fields originating in the ionosphere and magnetosphere, and from distant lightning, induce currents in the conducting Earth. Measuring the horizontal electric and magnetic fields at the surface over a range of frequencies yields an impedance from which apparent resistivity and phase are computed. Because lower frequencies penetrate deeper (the skin effect), MT can image structures from tens of metres to hundreds of kilometres deep.

Applications

Magnetotellurics is used in geothermal exploration, hydrocarbon and mineral exploration, groundwater studies, and research into the structure of the crust and upper mantle, including subduction zones and cratons. Variants include audio-frequency magnetotellurics (AMT) for shallower targets and long-period MT for deep lithospheric studies. The method is passive — it uses natural field sources — which makes surveys logistically simple though sometimes noisy near power lines and other cultural interference.
