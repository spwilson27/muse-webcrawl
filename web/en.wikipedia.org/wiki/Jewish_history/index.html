Jewish history is the history of the Jewish people, their nation, religion, and culture, extending over more than three thousand years from the ancient Israelites of the Levant through the global Jewish diaspora to the modern State of Israel.

Ancient period

According to tradition, Jewish history begins with the patriarch Abraham, the Exodus from Egypt under Moses, and the kingdoms of Israel and Judah. The Babylonian exile (586 BC), the return under Persian rule, and the Second Temple period — ended by Rome's destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70 after the Jewish–Roman wars — fixed the pattern of sovereignty and exile.

Diaspora

In dispersion, Jewish communities flourished and suffered across Europe, North Africa, and the Middle East: the scholarship of Babylonian and medieval Iberian Jewry, the expulsions from England (1290) and Spain (1492), the Haskalah enlightenment, and emancipation in the 19th century. Twentieth-century Jewish history is dominated by the Holocaust, in which Nazi Germany murdered six million Jews, and by Zionism's culmination in the State of Israel (1948), which drew immigrants from Europe, the Arab world (see the Jewish exodus from Arab and Muslim countries), Ethiopia, and the former Soviet Union.
