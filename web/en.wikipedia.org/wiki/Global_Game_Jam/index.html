The Global Game Jam (GGJ) is an annual distributed game jam in which participants at hundreds of physical sites around the world form small teams and create a complete video game or board game within 48 hours, all working to a common secret theme revealed at the start of the event.

History

The event was founded in 2008 by Susan Gold, Gorm Lai and Ian Schreiber, inspired by the Nordic Game Jam and Ludum Dare, and the first jam took place in January 2009 at 53 sites in 23 countries, producing 370 games. It grew rapidly: by 2020, more than 48,000 participants at 934 sites in 118 countries created over 9,600 games, making it the largest game jam in the world according to Guinness World Records.

Format

Participants gather at a local site on a Friday afternoon, watch a keynote, and learn the theme (past themes include "deception", "extinction", "what home means to you" and "lost and found"). Teams then pitch ideas, divide roles — programming, art, audio, design — and work through the weekend, submitting their games by Sunday afternoon to the GGJ website under a Creative Commons licence. The event is non-competitive and aimed at collaboration, experimentation and learning.
