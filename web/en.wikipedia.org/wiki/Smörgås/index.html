Smörgås

A smörgås (Swedish: [ˈsmœ̂rɡoːs], literally "buttered goose", from smör "butter" and gås "goose" — butter was once said to float on milk like a goose) is a Swedish open-faced sandwich consisting of a slice of bread, typically rye or crispbread, covered with butter and a topping (pålägg). Unlike a closed sandwich, the smörgås is eaten with knife and fork and displays its ingredients.

History and custom

The open sandwich spread across Scandinavia in the 19th century as buttered bread with toppings became a staple meal and a vehicle for preserved foods — pickled herring, cured salmon, cheeses and cold meats — suited to northern winters. The smörgås is the building block of the smörgåsbord, the Swedish buffet of hot and cold dishes whose name means literally "sandwich table".

Typical toppings

  • Räksmörgås — shrimp with mayonnaise, egg, lemon and dill, the classic "prawn sandwich" of west-coast Sweden.
  • Laxsmörgås — cured or smoked salmon (gravlax) with mustard-dill sauce.
  • Sill — pickled herring in many marinades.
  • Leverpastej — liver pâté with pickles; köttbullar — meatballs with beetroot salad; eggs with Kalles kaviar (creamed smoked cod roe).
  • Smörgåstårta — a large, layered "sandwich cake" of multiple smörgås layers frosted with mayonnaise or cream cheese, served at celebrations.

In Scandinavia

Denmark's smørrebrød and Norway's smørbrød are close relatives, with their own rules of composition and garnish; the Danish version, with prescribed combinations on dark rye, is the most codified. In Swedish everyday life the smörgås is a breakfast and fika item as often as a lunch.
