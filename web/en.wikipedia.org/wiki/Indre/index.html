The river Indre gives the department its name.

Indre (French pronunciation: [ɛ̃dʁ]) is a department in central France named after the river Indre. It lies in the Centre-Val de Loire region and corresponds largely to the historic province of Berry. Its prefecture is Châteauroux, and it is one of France's most rural and least densely populated departments.

Geography

The department is a landscape of bocage, plateaus, and the marshy ponds of the Brenne, a regional natural park known as the "land of a thousand lakes" and an important wetland for birds. The Indre river crosses it from south-east to north-west before joining the Loire in neighbouring Indre-et-Loire. George Sand's country around Nohant and La Châtre, in the south, inspired many of her novels.

History

Created on 4 March 1790 during the French Revolution from parts of Berry, Touraine, and Poitou, Indre was among the original 83 departments. It industrialised modestly around Châteauroux (textiles, tobacco, and a large American air base at Déols in the 1950s) but has seen slow population decline since the late 19th century.
