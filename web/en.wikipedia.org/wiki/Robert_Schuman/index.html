Robert Schuman

Robert Schuman
Born: 29 June 1886, Luxembourg City, Luxembourg
Died: 4 September 1963, Scy-Chazelles, France
Offices: Prime Minister of France (1947–1948); Foreign Minister (1948–1953); first President of the European Parliamentary Assembly (1958–1960)
Known for: Schuman Declaration (1950); founding father of the European Union

Robert Schuman (29 June 1886 – 4 September 1963) was a Luxembourg-born French statesman regarded as one of the founding fathers of the European Union. As France's foreign minister he issued the Schuman Declaration of 9 May 1950, which proposed pooling French and West German coal and steel production and led directly to the European Coal and Steel Community, the forerunner of today's European Union. Europe Day is celebrated on 9 May in commemoration.

Early life and career

Schuman was born in Luxembourg to a family from Lorraine, then annexed to the German Empire, and studied law in Germany. After the First World War, when Lorraine returned to France, he entered French politics as a deputy for Moselle. He served as finance minister and as prime minister in 1947–48, during the strikes and the beginnings of the Cold War, and then as foreign minister from 1948 to 1953.

European integration

Working with Jean Monnet, Schuman proposed that Franco-German production of coal and steel be placed under a common High Authority open to other European countries, making war between France and Germany "not only unthinkable, but materially impossible". The resulting Treaty of Paris (1951) created the European Coal and Steel Community among six states. Schuman later served as the first president of the European Parliamentary Assembly (1958–1960), the predecessor of the European Parliament.

Legacy

Schuman died at Scy-Chazelles near Metz in 1963. His house there is a museum, and numerous institutions, a Brussels district and metro station, and universities bear his name. A cause for his beatification in the Roman Catholic Church has been advanced, and in 2021 Pope Francis recognised his heroic virtues, declaring him Venerable.
