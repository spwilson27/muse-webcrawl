Kielbasa

Kiełbasa (Polish: [kʲɛwˈbasa]) is a general Polish word for sausage. In Poland it names dozens of distinct varieties — smoked or fresh, coarse or fine, pork, beef, turkey, lamb, veal or mixed — while in English "kielbasa" usually refers to a smoked, U-shaped pork sausage seasoned with garlic and marjoram, similar to the Polish kiełbasa wiejska ("country sausage").

Varieties in Poland

Regional and protected varieties include kiełbasa krakowska (a thick smoked sausage from Kraków), kabanosy (long, thin, dry sticks), kiełbasa biała (white sausage eaten at Easter in soup, żurek), kaszanka (blood sausage with buckwheat) and kiełbasa lisiecka, which has EU protected geographical status. Sausage-making in Poland dates back centuries, and medieval accounts describe smoked sausages sent as tribute and gifts.

In the diaspora

Polish immigrants brought kielbasa to the United States, Canada and Britain, where it became a staple of Polish-American communities in cities such as Chicago, Detroit, Cleveland and Pittsburgh, and a classic of ballpark and barbecue cooking. North American kielbasa is typically fully cooked and smoked, sold in links or rings, and served grilled, in soups, or with sauerkraut. The dish is a familiar part of European cuisine far beyond Poland's borders.
