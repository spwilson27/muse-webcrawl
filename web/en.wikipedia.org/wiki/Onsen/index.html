Onsen

A roten-buro (outdoor bath) in winter.

An onsen (温泉) is a Japanese hot spring and the bathing facilities and traditional inns around them. As a volcanically active country, Japan has thousands of onsen scattered across all of its major islands. By law, onsen water must be at least 25 °C at its source and contain specified minerals; inn signs reading 温泉 or the character ゆ (yu) mark them.

Types

Onsen come in indoor (uchiyu) and outdoor (roten-buro or noten-buro) forms. Waters are classified by their mineral content — sulphur, sodium chloride, hydrogen carbonate, iron, acidic and simple springs among them — and each type is traditionally associated with particular therapeutic benefits, the practice of therapeutic bathing being called tōji.

Etiquette

Bathers must wash and rinse thoroughly before entering the communal bath and enter naked; swimsuits are prohibited, and only a small modesty towel is carried, kept out of the water. Historically mixed bathing (kon'yoku) was normal, but most modern facilities are sex-segregated. People with tattoos were long refused entry due to associations with the yakuza, though many establishments have relaxed this rule as international tourism has grown.

Famous onsen towns

  • Beppu, Ōita — one of Japan's largest hot spring resorts
  • Kusatsu, Gunma — highly acidic waters, famous yubatake cooling field
  • Hakone, Kanagawa — popular resort near Tokyo
  • Noboribetsu, Hokkaidō — sulphurous "hell valley" source
  • Dōgo Onsen, Matsuyama — among the oldest springs, legendary inspiration for the bathhouse in Spirited Away
