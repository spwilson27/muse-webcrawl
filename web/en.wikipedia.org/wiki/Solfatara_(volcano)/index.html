Solfatara (volcano)

Solfatara (Italian: Solfatara di Pozzuoli) is a shallow volcanic crater at Pozzuoli, near Naples, in southern Italy. It is part of the Phlegraean Fields (Campi Flegrei) volcanic area, a large caldera west of Naples. The crater is characterised by numerous fumaroles emitting steam and sulfurous gases, pools of boiling mud, and brightly coloured mineral deposits.

Geology

Solfatara formed roughly four thousand years ago during one of the later eruptive episodes of the Phlegraean Fields. The crater is about 770 metres across. Its floor is largely flat and covered with whitish, altered volcanic soil, yellow sulfur crusts around the vents, and fumaroles whose gases reach temperatures of up to about 160 °C. The largest fumarole, the Bocca Grande ("large mouth"), has been famous since antiquity.

The name derives from the Latin sulpha terra, "land of sulfur". Sulfur was mined here in earlier centuries, and the crater's vapours were long used for supposed medicinal purposes; a natural sauna-like cave, the Stufe di Nerone, lies nearby. In Roman mythology the area was associated with the god Vulcan, and the crater was regarded as the entrance to the underworld.

Activity and hazards

Solfatara lies within a restless caldera subject to bradyseism — slow ground uplift and subsidence. Periods of uplift, such as those of 1969–72 and 1982–84, were accompanied by earthquake swarms and increased gas emissions. In September 2017, three members of a visiting family died after falling into a restricted, fenced-off area of the crater where the ground collapsed; the site was subsequently closed to the public for an extended period while safety work was carried out.

Scientists monitor the crater continuously: changes in gas composition, fumarole temperature, and ground deformation are used to track the state of the underlying hydrothermal system.
