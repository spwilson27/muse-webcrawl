The Gran Telescopio Canarias (GTC), also known as the GranTeCan, is a 10.4-metre reflecting telescope located at the Roque de los Muchachos Observatory on the island of La Palma in the Canary Islands, Spain. At the time of its inauguration it was the largest single-aperture optical telescope in the world.

Design

The telescope's primary mirror consists of 36 hexagonal segments of Zerodur glass-ceramic that work together as a single reflective surface, controlled by an active optics system. The project was led by the Instituto de Astrofísica de Canarias, with partnership from Spain, Mexico and the University of Florida.

Science

The GTC is used to study exoplanets, distant galaxies, supernovae, and the structure of the Milky Way. Its instruments have included the OSIRIS spectrograph and imager and the CanariCam mid-infrared instrument.
