Durango

Durango, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Durango, is one of the 31 states of Mexico, located in northwestern Mexico. Its capital and largest city is Victoria de Durango. With a population of about 1.8 million spread over one of Mexico's larger state territories, Durango is among the least densely populated states, dominated by the Sierra Madre Occidental mountains, pine forests and high desert plateaus.

History

The region was inhabited by peoples including the Tepehuán, Acaxee and Xixime before Spanish colonization; the city of Durango was founded in 1563 by Francisco de Ibarra, who named it after Durango in his native Basque Country, Spain. The state played a role in the Mexican War of Independence and the Revolution; it is the home state of revolutionary leader Francisco "Pancho" Villa, born at La Coyotada in the municipality of San Juan del Río.

Geography and economy

Durango is one of Mexico's leading timber-producing states and has a long mining tradition in gold, silver, iron and other minerals. Its dramatic canyon and desert landscapes—especially around Mexiquillo and the Zone of Silence (Zona del Silencio)—served as locations for numerous Hollywood westerns, earning the area the nickname "land of cinema". The state is also famous for scorpions, which feature in local folklore and souvenirs.
