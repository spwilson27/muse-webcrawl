Ganymede (moon)

Ganymede is a satellite of Jupiter and the largest and most massive moon in the Solar System. It is slightly larger than the planet Mercury (though much less massive) and is the only moon known to have its own internally generated magnetic field. Discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610, it is the third of the Galilean moons and orbits Jupiter in roughly seven days.

Composition and interior

Ganymede is composed of roughly equal amounts of silicate rock and water ice. It is a fully differentiated body with a molten iron core. Evidence from the Galileo spacecraft and the Hubble Space Telescope indicates a subsurface saltwater ocean lying about 200 km beneath the crust, containing more water than all of Earth's surface oceans combined. Ganymede has a thin oxygen atmosphere and an oxygen ionosphere.

Surface

The surface shows two types of terrain: dark, heavily cratered regions saturated with impact craters, and somewhat younger, light terrain marked by grooves and ridges, the result of tectonic and cryovolcanic activity. Ganymede is the primary target of ESA's JUICE (Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer) mission, launched in 2023 and scheduled to arrive at Jupiter in 2031 and enter orbit around Ganymede in the mid-2030s — the first spacecraft to orbit a moon of another planet.

Name

Ganymede is named for the Trojan prince of Greek mythology carried off to Olympus to be cupbearer to Zeus. Simon Marius independently observed the moon and published the names of the Galilean moons, taken from lovers of Zeus in mythology.
