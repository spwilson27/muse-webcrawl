Thessaloniki (Greek: Θεσσαλονίκη) is the second-largest city in Greece and the capital of the region of Central Macedonia, a major port on the Thermaic Gulf of the Aegean Sea. With a metropolitan population of over one million, it is a principal economic, university, and cultural centre of northern Greece, sometimes called the country's "co-capital".

History

The city was founded in 315 BC by Cassander, who named it after his wife Thessalonike, half-sister of Alexander the Great. Under Rome it flourished on the Via Egnatia, and it was visited by St Paul, whose letters to its church are among the earliest Christian writings. In the Byzantine era it was the empire's second city, producing monuments — the Rotunda, the Arch of Galerius, and great churches such as Hagia Sophia and Hagios Demetrios — now UNESCO-listed. After nearly five centuries of Ottoman rule (from 1430), it joined Greece in 1912. A great fire in 1917 destroyed much of the centre, which was rebuilt to a modern plan; its large Sephardic Jewish community, once a majority of the population, was destroyed in the Holocaust.

The city today

Landmarks include the White Tower, Aristotelous Square, and the Museum of Byzantine Culture. Aristotle University of Thessaloniki is among the largest universities in the Balkans. The city is known for its food culture, waterfront promenade, film festival, and lively café life.
