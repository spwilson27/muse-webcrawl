Extrapolation

In mathematics, extrapolation is the process of estimating, beyond the original observation range, the value of a variable on the basis of its relationship with another variable. It is similar to interpolation, which produces estimates between known observations, but extrapolation is subject to greater uncertainty and a higher risk of producing meaningless results. Extrapolation may also mean extension of a method, assuming similar methods will be applicable.

Methods

Common extrapolation methods include linear extrapolation, polynomial extrapolation, and conic extrapolation. Polynomial extrapolation beyond the data range can diverge dramatically (a phenomenon related to Runge's phenomenon), and exponential or trend-based extrapolation is common in forecasting. In statistics, extrapolation is at the heart of regression-based prediction, and its reliability depends on the assumption that the underlying relationship persists outside the observed range.

Applications and risks

Extrapolation is used in economics, demography, engineering, and climate modelling to project future values. Its risks were famously discussed in connection with population forecasts, technological predictions, and financial models, where extending patterns beyond their domain of validity produces systematically wrong results.
