Chablis wine

Chablis (pronounced [ʃabli]) is the northernmost wine district of the Burgundy region in France. The wines are made exclusively from Chardonnay grapes, but Chablis is known for a leaner, less oaky style than other Burgundian Chardonnays, reflecting its cool climate and Kimmeridgian limestone soils.

Classification

The Chablis appellation is divided into four levels: Petit Chablis, Chablis, Chablis Premier Cru (with 40 named climats), and Chablis Grand Cru, the latter comprising seven climats on a single hillside above the town of Chablis, including Les Clos, Vaudésir, and Bougros. Wines from the Grand Cru and Premier Cru vineyards are considered the finest expression of the region.

The town of Chablis sits on the River Serein. Historically, its wines were shipped to Paris via the Yonne river, making Chablis widely known in the capital.
