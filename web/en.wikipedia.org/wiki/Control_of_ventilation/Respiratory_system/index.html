Respiratory system

The respiratory system (also respiratory apparatus or ventilatory system) is a biological system consisting of specific organs and structures used for gas exchange in animals and plants. In land vertebrates including humans, the respiratory system generally includes the airways, lungs, and the respiratory muscles that move air in and out. In the lungs, oxygen from inhaled air diffuses into the blood and carbon dioxide diffuses out, a process called external respiration.

Anatomy

The respiratory tract is divided into the upper and lower tracts. The upper respiratory tract includes the nose and nasal cavities, the paranasal sinuses, the pharynx, and the portion of the larynx above the vocal cords. The lower respiratory tract begins with the larynx below the cords and continues through the trachea, which divides into the two main bronchi; these branch repeatedly into bronchioles, ending in clusters of alveoli where gas exchange occurs. Humans have roughly 300 to 500 million alveoli with a combined surface area of about 70 square metres.

Ventilation

The diaphragm and intercostal muscles drive ventilation. Inhalation expands the thoracic cavity and draws air in; exhalation at rest is largely passive, driven by elastic recoil. Gas exchange in the alveoli occurs by diffusion down partial-pressure gradients, and the blood carries oxygen to tissues bound mainly to hemoglobin.

Other functions

Beyond gas exchange, the respiratory system filters, warms, and humidifies inhaled air; provides the airflow for speech and smell; helps regulate blood pH through control of carbon dioxide; and forms part of the body's defense through mucus, cilia, and immune cells in the airways.

Diseases

Disorders of the respiratory system include obstructive diseases such as asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), restrictive diseases such as pulmonary fibrosis, infections including pneumonia and tuberculosis, and lung cancer, one of the leading causes of cancer death worldwide.
