Breathing

Breathing (or ventilation) is the process of moving air into and out of the lungs to facilitate gas exchange with the internal environment, mostly by bringing in oxygen and expelling carbon dioxide. All aerobic creatures need oxygen for cellular respiration, which uses the oxygen to burn food molecules and produce energy in the form of ATP, generating carbon dioxide as a waste product.

Mechanics

In mammals, breathing is driven mainly by the diaphragm, a dome-shaped sheet of muscle separating the thoracic cavity from the abdomen. When the diaphragm contracts and flattens, the volume of the chest cavity increases and air is drawn into the lungs (inhalation). When it relaxes, elastic recoil of the lungs and chest wall expels air (exhalation). The intercostal muscles between the ribs also contribute, especially during deep or forced breathing.

At rest, a healthy adult breathes roughly 12 to 20 times per minute, moving about 500 mL of air with each breath (the tidal volume). Total lung capacity is around 6 litres in an average adult male.

Control of breathing

The rhythm of breathing is generated by respiratory centers in the medulla oblongata and pons of the brainstem. Central chemoreceptors respond mainly to the pH of cerebrospinal fluid, which reflects carbon dioxide levels, while peripheral chemoreceptors in the carotid and aortic bodies respond to oxygen, carbon dioxide, and pH in arterial blood. This feedback system keeps arterial carbon dioxide within narrow limits and adjusts ventilation during exercise, altitude exposure, and sleep. Breathing can also be modified voluntarily, as in speech, singing, and breath-holding, though automatic control eventually overrides voluntary inhibition.

Gas exchange

In the lungs, oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse across the thin walls of the alveoli and surrounding capillaries. Oxygen binds to hemoglobin in red blood cells for transport to the tissues, while carbon dioxide is carried dissolved, as bicarbonate, and bound to proteins. Breathing is thus one part of respiration, which also includes circulation and cellular metabolism.
