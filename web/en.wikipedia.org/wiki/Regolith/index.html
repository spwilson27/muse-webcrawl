Regolith

Regolith is a blanket of loose, heterogeneous superficial deposits covering solid rock. It includes dust, broken rocks, and other related materials and is present on Earth, the Moon, Mars, some asteroids, and other terrestrial planets and moons. The term was coined in 1897 by the geologist George P. Merrill from the Greek words rhegos (blanket) and lithos (rock).

Formation

On Earth, regolith is formed by weathering and biological processes acting on bedrock, and forms the basis of most soil profiles. On the Moon, regolith has been produced over billions of years by micrometeorite impacts and the thermal cycling of the surface, producing a layer typically several metres thick of pulverised rock and impact glass. Lunar regolith is of interest as a potential resource for oxygen, water, and construction material for future exploration.
