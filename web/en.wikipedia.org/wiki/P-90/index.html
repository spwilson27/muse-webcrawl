P-90


Type: single-coil electric guitar pickup. Introduced: 1946, Gibson.

The P-90 (sometimes written P90) is a single-coil electric guitar pickup produced by Gibson since 1946. With a wider, squatter coil than a Fender-style single coil, it produces a sound often described as sitting between a conventional single coil and a humbucker: bright and articulate but with greater midrange body and output.

History

The P-90 was Gibson's standard pickup after the Second World War, fitted to models such as the ES-330, the early Les Paul goldtop, and the Les Paul Junior and Special. In 1957 it was largely displaced on flagship models by Seth Lover's humbucking PAF pickup, but it remained in production on student and mid-range models and has enjoyed repeated revivals.

Design

The pickup uses a flat, wide bobbin wound with roughly 10,000 turns of wire around two Alnico bar magnets beneath adjustable steel pole screws. Its wide coil senses a longer section of string, contributing to its thicker tone; like all true single coils it is susceptible to mains hum. Two housing styles are common: the "soapbar," named for its rectangular cover, and the "dog-ear," with mounting tabs at each end.

Notable users

Players associated with the P-90 include Leslie West of Mountain, whose Les Paul Junior tone is a touchstone of rock guitar, as well as John Lennon and George Harrison, who used P-90-equipped Gibson-built Epiphone Casinos, and punk and indie players drawn to its raw, snarling character.
