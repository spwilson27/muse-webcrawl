Caret

The caret (/ˈkærɪt/) is a V-shaped grapheme, usually inverted and sometimes extended, used in proofreading and typography to indicate that additional material needs to be inserted at the point indicated in the text. The same symbol is also used as a modifier letter in some languages and, in computing, as an operator and a generic name for a cursor.

^

Etymology and history

The term comes from the Latin caret, "it lacks", from carēre, "to lack; to be separated". The symbol was originally used by scribes and printers: a small mark placed below the line, with the missing text written in the margin. In proofreading, the caret is placed below the line of text at the point where an insertion is intended, and the inserted material is written above or in the margin.

Uses

In mathematics, the caret is sometimes used as a notation for a unit vector (the "hat" symbol over a variable is related). In computing, the circumflex character ^ (U+005E CIRCUMFLEX ACCENT) is used as an exponentiation operator in several programming languages and calculators, to represent the bitwise XOR operator in C-like languages, to mark the beginning of a line in regular expressions, and to denote the Control key in caret notation such as ^C. In text editors, "caret" also refers to the blinking text insertion cursor, even when it is drawn as a vertical bar rather than a circumflex.

Unicode

The caret proper is encoded separately from the ASCII circumflex accent: U+2038 ‸ CARET is the proofreader's mark, while U+005E ^ is the circumflex accent/spacing modifier. A fullwidth form and various modifier letters (such as ʌ and ˆ) also exist.
