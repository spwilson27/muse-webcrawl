Mount Emei (Chinese: 峨眉山; pinyin: Éméi shān) is a mountain in Sichuan Province, China, rising to 3,099 metres at its Golden Summit. It is one of the Four Sacred Buddhist Mountains of China, the traditional pilgrimage site (bodhimaṇḍa) of the bodhisattva Samantabhadra (Puxian), who is usually depicted riding a six-tusked elephant.

Religious history

Buddhism reached the mountain during the Eastern Han dynasty, and the first Buddhist temples in China were built in its vicinity. By the Ming dynasty the mountain held a large complex of temples, of which about thirty survive, including Baoguo Temple at the foot, Wannian Temple — housing a Ming-dynasty bronze statue of Samantabhadra on an elephant — and the summit temples of the Golden Summit (Jinding), crowned by a great gilded statue of Samantabhadra with ten heads facing ten directions.

Geography and nature

Mount Emei lies at the western rim of the Sichuan Basin. Its steep forested slopes, spanning subtropical to subalpine climates, are noted for exceptional biodiversity, with over 3,000 plant species and wildlife including Tibetan macaques that famously interact with visitors. The mountain is also associated with the Emei school of Chinese martial arts, one of the historic branches of wushu.

Heritage status

The Mount Emei Scenic Area, together with the nearby Leshan Giant Buddha, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1996.
