Alphonse Mucha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Gismonda poster (1894): Sarah Bernhardt in a Byzantine mosaic halo and ornate gown, tall narrow lithograph in gold and pastel]

Alfons Maria Mucha (24 July 1860 – 14 July 1939) was a Czech painter, illustrator and graphic artist. Living in Paris during the Art Nouveau period, he was widely known for his distinctively stylised and decorative theatrical posters, particularly those of Sarah Bernhardt. He produced illustrations, advertisements, decorative panels and designs, which became among the best-known images of the period.

His breakthrough came on Boxing Day 1894, when he improvised the poster for Bernhardt's Gismonda — the long, narrow 'Mucha style' of Byzantine halos, whiplash hair and floral arabesques defined Parisian Art Nouveau (called le style Mucha). After 1910 in independent Czechoslovakia he devoted himself to his nationalist masterwork, The Slav Epic (1910–28), twenty monumental canvases of Slavic history.
