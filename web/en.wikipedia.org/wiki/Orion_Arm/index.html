Orion Arm

Galaxy: Milky Way

Also called: Orion–Cygnus Arm; Local Arm; Orion Spur

Contains: Solar System; Orion Nebula; Gould Belt

Neighbours: Perseus Arm; Sagittarius Arm

The Orion Arm, also known as the Orion–Cygnus Arm or Local Arm, is a minor spiral arm of the Milky Way galaxy, roughly 3,500 light-years across and about 10,000 light-years long. It lies between the larger Sagittarius Arm (toward the Galactic Centre) and the Perseus Arm (outward), and it contains the Solar System.

The Solar System's position

The Sun sits near the inner edge of the Orion Arm, inside the Local Bubble — a cavity of hot, thin gas about 300 light-years wide carved by ancient supernovae — at a distance of roughly 27,000 light-years from the Galactic Centre. The Sun takes some 225–250 million years to orbit the galaxy once.

Contents

The arm takes its name from the constellation Orion, whose bright stars and the Orion Nebula (M42) lie within it. Other notable objects include the Pleiades and Hyades clusters, the Ring Nebula, the North America Nebula in Cygnus, and the stars Betelgeuse and Rigel. The Gould Belt, a ring of young stars tilted to the galactic plane, is a prominent local structure within the arm.

Structure debate

Astronomers long debated whether the Orion Arm is merely a "spur" bridging the two major arms. Measurements of maser parallaxes by the BeSSeL Survey and VLBA indicate it is a substantial structure in its own right, longer and denser than once thought, though not as massive as the Perseus or Scutum–Centaurus arms.
