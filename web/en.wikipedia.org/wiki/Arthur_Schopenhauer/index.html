Arthur Schopenhauer (1788–1860) was a German philosopher best known for The World as Will and Representation (1818), which portrays the world as the objectification of a blind, striving Will. Influenced by Immanuel Kant and by Indian thought — the Upanishads and Buddhism — Schopenhauer concluded that desire is the root of suffering and that release lies in aesthetic contemplation, compassion and ascetic denial of the will.

Influence

Long ignored while Hegel dominated German universities, Schopenhauer found fame late, after his essay collection Parerga and Paralipomena (1851). His philosophy of music, pessimism and the will shaped Wagner, Friedrich Nietzsche (who later rebelled against it), Freud and much modernist literature. His thought is a major bridge between Western philosophy and Asian religions.
