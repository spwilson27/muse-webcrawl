Serge Koussevitzky

Serge Koussevitzky
Born: 26 July 1874, Vyshny Volochyok, Russia
Died: 4 June 1951, Boston, Massachusetts
Occupation: Conductor, double bassist, composer

Serge Alexandrovich Koussevitzky (1874–1951) was a Russian-born conductor, composer and double-bassist, long-time music director of the Boston Symphony Orchestra (1924–1949). Born into a Jewish family of musicians, he first won fame as a virtuoso double bassist — he wrote a popular concerto for the instrument — before turning to conducting.

Career

He founded his own orchestra in Moscow, championed contemporary Russian composers, and after the Revolution moved to Paris and then Boston. In Boston he built one of the world's great orchestras, founded the Tanglewood festival and the Berkshire Music Center, and commissioned landmark works including Bartók's Concerto for Orchestra, Britten's Peter Grimes (through the Koussevitzky Foundation), Copland's Third Symphony, and Stravinsky's Symphony of Psalms.

Legacy

He mentored Leonard Bernstein and championed American music at a time when European repertoire dominated programming.
