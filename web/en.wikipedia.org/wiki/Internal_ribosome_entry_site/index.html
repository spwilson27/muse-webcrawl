Internal ribosome entry site

An internal ribosome entry site, abbreviated IRES, is an RNA element that allows for translation initiation in a cap-independent manner, as part of the greater process of protein synthesis. In eukaryotic translation, initiation typically occurs at the 5' cap of messenger RNA; IRES elements instead recruit the ribosome to an internal position.

Discovery

IRES elements were first discovered in 1988 in poliovirus and encephalomyocarditis virus RNA. These viruses use IRES-mediated translation to produce their proteins while shutting down the host cell's cap-dependent translation.

Occurrence

IRES elements are common in viral genomes, including those of picornaviruses, hepatitis C virus, and some retroviruses. Cellular IRES elements have also been described in mRNAs encoding certain stress-response, apoptosis, and growth-regulatory proteins, though their physiological role remains debated.

Applications

IRES elements are widely used in molecular biology to express two proteins from a single transcript in bicistronic vectors, for example co-expressing a gene of interest with a fluorescent reporter.
