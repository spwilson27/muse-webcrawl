Airfix

Airfix is a British brand of plastic scale model kits, founded in 1939 by Hungarian-born businessman Nicholas Kove. Originally a manufacturer of inflatable rubber toys and plastic combs, Airfix became famous from the 1950s onward for its inexpensive injection-moulded kits of aircraft, ships, vehicles and figures, sold in toyshops and — in a famous early arrangement — through Woolworths stores. The name itself was chosen, Kove said, so that it would appear near the front of any alphabetical catalogue, and because his first products were air-filled.

Classic Airfix box art for a 1:72 scale Spitfire kit.

History

Airfix's first model kit, a Ferguson tractor produced in 1949 as a promotional item, is often cited as the first British plastic construction kit. A kit of the Golden Hind followed in 1952, and in 1953 the company issued its first aircraft kit, the Supermarine Spitfire, in 1:72 scale — the scale Airfix would popularise worldwide. Through the 1960s and 1970s the range expanded to hundreds of subjects, graded in "Series" by price and complexity, with box art by artists such as Roy Cross becoming collectable in its own right.

The company passed through financial difficulties and several owners in the 1980s and 1990s, including Humbrol. In 2006 Airfix was acquired by Hornby Hobbies, which revived the brand with new tooling produced to modern standards alongside reissues of classic kits. See also scale model and Hornby.
