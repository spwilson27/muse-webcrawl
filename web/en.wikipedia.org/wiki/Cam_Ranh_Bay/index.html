Cam Ranh Bay (Vietnamese: Vịnh Cam Ranh) is a deep-water bay in Khánh Hòa Province on the southeastern coast of Vietnam, on the South China Sea. It is considered one of the finest deep-water shelters in Southeast Asia, and has been an important naval anchorage for more than a century.

History

The French developed port facilities at the bay during the colonial era. During the Russo-Japanese War, the Russian Baltic Fleet sheltered there in 1905 on its voyage to Tsushima. In the Second World War the Imperial Japanese Navy used it as a staging base.

During the Vietnam War the United States built Cam Ranh Air Base and a vast logistics port from 1965, turning the bay into one of the largest American support complexes in South Vietnam; the facilities were handed to South Vietnam in 1972 and captured by North Vietnamese forces in April 1975. Under a 1979 agreement the Soviet Union, and later Russia, leased the base rent-free; Soviet naval and signals-intelligence forces operated there until Russia withdrew in 2002.

Today

Vietnam now operates the naval base itself and hosts visiting foreign warships, while civil development includes Cam Ranh International Airport and resort development along Bãi Dài beach. Vietnam's principal submarine base, serving its Kilo-class submarines, is located at Cam Ranh.
