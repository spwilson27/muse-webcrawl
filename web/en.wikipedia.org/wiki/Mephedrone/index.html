Mephedrone, also known as 4-methylmethcathinone (4-MMC), is a synthetic stimulant drug of the amphetamine and cathinone classes. It was first synthesised in 1929 but remained largely obscure until it was rediscovered around 2003 and sold online as a "legal high" and research chemical in the late 2000s. Slang names include meow meow, MCAT, and plant food.

Effects

Users report effects similar to those of MDMA and cocaine, including euphoria, stimulation, increased sociability, and empathy, arising from increased release and reduced reuptake of dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine. Adverse effects include tachycardia, hypertension, agitation, hyperthermia, and, with heavy use, neurotoxicity concerns and deaths were reported in the media around 2009–2010.

History

Mephedrone gained rapid popularity in the UK and Europe in 2008–2010, sold in head shops and online as "plant food" or "bath salts" to evade medicines law. Following intense media coverage and reported harms, it was classified as a Class B drug in the United Kingdom in April 2010 and controlled across the European Union later that year; it is also a Schedule I controlled substance in the United States.

Chemistry

Mephedrone is a substituted cathinone, closely related to methcathinone, differing by a methyl group at the 4-position of the phenyl ring. It is typically produced as a white or off-white powder or crystals, as the hydrochloride salt.
