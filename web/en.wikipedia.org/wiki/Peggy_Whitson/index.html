Peggy Whitson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Peggy Whitson
Born9 February 1960, Mount Ayr, Iowa
OccupationAstronaut, biochemist
RecordsMost cumulative days in space by an American (695); first woman to command the ISS (twice)
Whitson aboard the ISS.

Peggy Annette Whitson (born 1960) is an American biochemist and the most experienced American astronaut, with 695 cumulative days in space across NASA and commercial Axiom Space missions. She was the first woman to command the International Space Station — and the only person to command it twice — and at 57 became the oldest woman to fly in space, records she kept extending.

A rice-farmer's daughter from Iowa with a doctorate in biochemistry, Whitson joined NASA as a scientist in 1986 and the astronaut corps in 1996. Her three long ISS expeditions (2002, 2008, 2016–17) made her the US endurance record-holder and its most prolific female spacewalker, with ten EVAs.

Axiom era

Retiring from NASA in 2018 as its chief astronaut emerita, Whitson returned to orbit as commander of the private Axiom Mission 2 in 2023 and Axiom Mission 4 in 2025 — ferrying international crews to the ISS aboard Crew Dragon and pushing her own cumulative record past 690 days. Her career spans the Shuttle era, the station's assembly, and the commercial era she now helps lead.
