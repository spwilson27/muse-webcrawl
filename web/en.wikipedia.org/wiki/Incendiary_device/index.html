Incendiary device

Incendiary devices are weapons designed to start fires or destroy sensitive equipment using fire. They are a type of chemical weapon, formed of chemical mixtures (often including oxidizers) that burn at extremely hot temperatures. Incendiary weapons were used extensively in both World Wars.

Types

Common incendiary agents include thermite, white phosphorus, napalm, and magnesium. Devices range from improvised petrol bombs to purpose-built munitions such as incendiary bombs dropped from aircraft, often in clusters of small bomblets.

International law

The use of incendiary weapons against civilians, or against military targets within concentrations of civilians, is restricted by Protocol III of the 1980 Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons. Air-delivered incendiary weapons are prohibited against military objectives within a concentration of civilians.
