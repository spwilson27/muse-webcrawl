Pennsylvanian (train)


Operator: Amtrak
First service: April 27, 1980
Route: New York City – Pittsburgh
Distance: 444 miles (715 km)

The Pennsylvanian is a 444-mile (715 km) daily Amtrak passenger train running between New York City and Pittsburgh via Philadelphia, Harrisburg, and Altoona. It is the only Amtrak service across Pennsylvania's Allegheny Mountains and traverses the historic Horseshoe Curve west of Altoona.

Route

The train departs New York Penn Station, travels the Northeast Corridor to Philadelphia, then follows the former Pennsylvania Railroad Main Line through Lancaster and Harrisburg. West of Harrisburg it crosses the Susquehanna River and follows the Juniata River valley to Altoona, climbs the Horseshoe Curve over the Allegheny Front, and continues through Johnstown to Pittsburgh Union Station. The scheduled journey takes about nine hours end to end.

History

Amtrak introduced the Pennsylvanian in 1980 with financial support from the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania under the federal "403(b)" programme, restoring daily service between Harrisburg and Pittsburgh. The train was briefly extended to Chicago in various forms during the 1990s and 2000s before reverting to the New York–Pittsburgh route. Pennsylvania continues to subsidise the service.

Equipment and service

The train typically operates with an electric locomotive between New York and Harrisburg and a diesel locomotive west of Harrisburg, with Amfleet coaches and a café car. A second daily round trip has long been proposed as part of expanded Keystone Corridor service west of Harrisburg.
