Pressurized water reactor (PWR)

Type: Light-water reactor
Coolant/moderator: Pressurised water
Primary pressure: ~155 bar

A pressurized water reactor (PWR) is a type of light-water nuclear reactor. The PWR is the most common type of nuclear power reactor worldwide and is used for electricity generation and in nuclear marine propulsion.

Design

Water in the primary coolant loop is kept under high pressure (~150–160 bar) so it does not boil, despite core temperatures around 315 °C. Heated water transfers heat in steam generators to a secondary loop, whose steam drives turbines. A separate tertiary loop cools condensers. The pressuriser, reactor pressure vessel, control rods using boric acid and rod absorbers, and containment building are key components.

History

Developed at Oak Ridge and Westinghouse for US Navy submarines (USS Nautilus) and first commercial plant at Shippingport (1957) under Admiral Hyman Rickover's programme.

Safety

Negative temperature coefficient, multiple barriers, emergency core cooling; the 1979 Three Mile Island accident was a PWR partial meltdown. Modern designs (EPR, AP1000) add passive safety.
