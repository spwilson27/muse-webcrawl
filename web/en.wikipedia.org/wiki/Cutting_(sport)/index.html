Cutting (sport)

Placeholder image: Cutting (sport)

Cutting is a western-style equestrian competition in which a horse and rider work together before a judge or panel of judges to demonstrate the horse's ability to separate a single cow from a herd of cattle and keep it away for a short period of time. The sport evolved from the work of ranch horses separating cattle for branding, doctoring, or sorting.

Competition

In a cutting run, which lasts two and a half minutes, the rider selects and "cuts" one cow from the herd and drives it to the center of the arena. The rider then loosens the reins and allows the horse to work on its own, mirroring the cow's movements to prevent it from returning to the herd; the rider may use leg aids and seat cues but is penalized for visible reining ("hot quitting" and other infractions result in penalties). A run may involve working two or three cows in sequence. Judges score runs typically between 60 and 80 points, with 70 considered average, based on the horse's responsiveness, style, and ability to control the cow.

Organizations

The National Cutting Horse Association (NCHA), founded in 1946 and headquartered in Fort Worth, Texas, is the principal governing body, organizing major events such as the NCHA Futurity for three-year-old horses, one of the richest events in western performance horse competition. Cutting is also sanctioned internationally and recognized within the wider western horse show world.

Horses

Cutting horses are typically American Quarter Horses, selectively bred for "cow sense" — an instinctive ability to read and react to cattle. Famous sires such as Doc Bar and Peppy San Badger have shaped modern cutting bloodlines.

Related disciplines

Cutting is related to other cattle events including reined cow horse (working cow horse), team penning, and ranch sorting.
