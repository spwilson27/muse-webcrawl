Erfurt Cathedral

Erfurt Cathedral (German: Erfurter Dom), formally the Cathedral Church of St Mary (Hohe Domkirche St. Marien), is a Gothic church on the Domberg in Erfurt, Thuringia, Germany. Together with the neighbouring Severikirche it crowns the Cathedral Hill, reached by the broad Domstufen steps from the Cathedral Square below.

History

A church dedicated to St Mary stood on the site from about 752, when St Boniface founded the diocese of Erfurt. The present building is chiefly Gothic: the choir was built in the fourteenth century and consecrated in 1372, while the nave was completed in the fifteenth century. The central tower houses the Gloriosa, for centuries the largest free-swinging medieval bell in the world, cast in 1497.

Features

The cathedral preserves important medieval stained glass in the choir, a Romanesque Wolfram candelabrum of about 1160, and the mid-thirteenth-century stucco figure of the Virgin known as the Maria gravida. Martin Luther was ordained a priest in the cathedral in 1507. Since 1994 it has again been the seat of a Roman Catholic bishop, of the restored Diocese of Erfurt.
