The Diavik Diamond Mine is a diamond mine in the North Slave Region of the Northwest Territories, Canada, about 300 kilometres (190 mi) northeast of Yellowknife. It is located on a 20 km² island in Lac de Gras, informally called East Island, and is connected to the mainland by an ice road in winter.

History

The kimberlite pipes at Diavik were discovered in 1994, following the staking rush triggered by the nearby Ekati discovery. Construction of the mine required building water-retaining dikes in Lac de Gras so the open pits could be mined from the lakebed. Production began in 2003. Originally a joint venture between Rio Tinto (60%) and Dominion Diamond (40%), the mine came under full Rio Tinto ownership in 2021 after Dominion's insolvency proceedings.

Operations

The mine exploits several kimberlite pipes (A154 South, A154 North, A418, and the later A21 pipe) by open-pit and underground methods. It has produced on the order of six to seven million carats of diamonds per year in its peak years, noted for a high proportion of large, gem-quality white stones. Ore is processed on site and the diamonds are sorted in Yellowknife. The mine is a major employer in the Northwest Territories, with significant Indigenous employment through agreements with local First Nations and Inuit organisations. Commercial production is expected to conclude in the mid-2020s, after which the site will be reclaimed and the dikes breached to reflood the pits.
