National Coal Board

National Coal Board
Industry: Coal mining
Founded: 1946 (vesting day 1 January 1947)
Defunct: 1987 (became the British Coal Corporation)
Headquarters: London, United Kingdom

The National Coal Board (NCB) was the statutory corporation created to run the nationalised coal mining industry in the United Kingdom. It was established under the Coal Industry Nationalisation Act 1946 and took over the country's collieries on "vesting day", 1 January 1947.

History

At nationalisation the NCB took control of more than 950 collieries employing around 700,000 miners. Its first chairman was Lord Hyndley. Over the following decades the industry contracted as cheaper fuels and falling demand led to successive rounds of pit closures. The NCB was centrally involved in the miners' strikes of 1972, 1974, and the year-long strike of 1984–85. In 1987 the NCB was renamed the British Coal Corporation; the remaining industry was privatised in 1994.
