Exhalation: Stories is a collection of short fiction by American writer Ted Chiang, published in 2019 by Alfred A. Knopf. It is Chiang's second collection, following Stories of Your Life and Others (2002), and gathers nine stories — seven previously published and two original to the volume — that explore time, memory, free will, and the consequences of technology.

Contents

  • "The Merchant and the Alchemist's Gate" (2007) — a fable of time travel in medieval Baghdad.
  • "Exhalation" (2008) — a robot scientist in a pneumatic universe discovers the thermodynamics of his own existence; winner of the Hugo and Locus awards for best short story.
  • "What's Expected of Us" (2005) — a flash piece on a device that undermines belief in free will.
  • "The Lifecycle of Software Objects" (2010) — a novella about raising artificial intelligences as digital pets over two decades; winner of the Hugo and Locus awards for best novella.
  • "Dacey's Patent Automatic Nanny" (2011) — a steampunk-inflected story about mechanical child-rearing.
  • "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling" (2013) — parallel narratives on writing and on a technology of perfect memory.
  • "The Great Silence" (2015) — written with artist Allora & Calzadilla, narrated by a parrot reflecting on the Fermi paradox.
  • "Omphalos" (original) — a world where young-earth creationism is scientifically demonstrable.
  • "Anxiety Is the Dizziness of Freedom" (original) — a novella about devices that allow brief communication with parallel selves, and the moral choices that follow.

Reception

The collection was widely praised for the precision of its prose and the philosophical depth of its premises. It was named one of the ten best books of 2019 by The New York Times Book Review and appeared on Barack Obama's list of favourite books of the year. Reviewers frequently compared Chiang's careful, idea-driven style to that of Jorge Luis Borges and noted the rarity of the volume: Chiang has published only a small body of short fiction across three decades, yet is regarded as one of the most decorated writers in modern science fiction.

Awards

Several stories in the collection won or were shortlisted for major genre awards, including the Hugo Award, the Nebula Award, the Locus Award, and the BSFA Award. "Anxiety Is the Dizziness of Freedom" was a finalist for the Hugo Award for Best Novella.
