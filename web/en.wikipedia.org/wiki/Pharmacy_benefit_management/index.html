Pharmacy benefit management

Pharmacy benefit managers administer prescription drug programs for health plans and employers.

Pharmacy benefit management is the administration of prescription drug programs, typically performed by a third-party administrator known as a pharmacy benefit manager (PBM). PBMs act as intermediaries between health insurers, employers, pharmacies, and drug manufacturers, negotiating drug prices, building pharmacy networks, maintaining drug formularies, and processing prescription claims.

Functions

PBMs perform a range of functions, including negotiating rebates and discounts with pharmaceutical manufacturers, deciding which drugs appear on a plan's formulary and at what cost-sharing tier, processing and paying pharmacy claims, operating mail-order and specialty pharmacies, and running drug utilization review programs intended to promote safe and cost-effective prescribing.

Major companies

The largest PBMs in the United States include CVS Caremark (part of CVS Health), Express Scripts (part of Cigna), and OptumRx (part of UnitedHealth Group). Together these three firms process a large majority of prescription claims in the United States, and their vertical integration with insurers and pharmacies has attracted regulatory scrutiny.

Criticism and regulation

Critics argue that PBMs profit from opaque rebate arrangements and "spread pricing" — charging health plans more for a drug than they reimburse the pharmacy — and that their practices can raise costs for patients and squeeze independent pharmacies. Defenders respond that PBMs use their bargaining power to restrain drug spending. State and federal lawmakers have increasingly proposed transparency requirements and restrictions on PBM practices, and the Federal Trade Commission has studied the industry's effect on drug prices.
