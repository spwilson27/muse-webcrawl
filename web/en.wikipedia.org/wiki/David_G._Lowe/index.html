David G. Lowe is a Canadian computer scientist and professor emeritus at the University of British Columbia, known for inventing the scale-invariant feature transform (SIFT), one of the most widely used and influential algorithms in computer vision.

Work

Lowe received his PhD from Stanford University in 1985 under Thomas Binford, and taught at New York University's Courant Institute before joining UBC. His 1999 paper and 2004 journal article on SIFT described a method for detecting and describing local image features invariant to scale, rotation and partially to illumination and viewpoint change; it became one of the most cited papers in computer science, with applications in object recognition, image stitching, robotics and 3D reconstruction. Lowe later worked with Google on machine perception. He has received major honours including the IC CV Helmholtz Prize for fundamental contributions to computer vision.
