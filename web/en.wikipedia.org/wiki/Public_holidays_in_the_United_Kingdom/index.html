Public holidays in the United Kingdom

Public holidays in the United Kingdom, commonly called bank holidays, are days on which most businesses and non-essential services close. The nations of the UK have different holidays: England and Wales share eight, Scotland nine, and Northern Ireland ten. Rights to time off or extra pay depend on employment contracts, there being no automatic statutory right to either, though the 5.6 weeks' statutory annual leave may include bank holidays.

The Bank Holidays Act 1871 formalised the system, naming the holidays after the days banks closed; the Banking and Financial Dealings Act 1971 now sets the schedule, with substitute days when fixed-date holidays fall at weekends. Additional one-off holidays are declared by royal proclamation for events such as royal weddings, jubilees and, in 2022, the state funeral of Elizabeth II and in 2023 the coronation of Charles III.
