San Gorgonio Mountain


Elevation: 11,503 ft (3,506 m)
Prominence: 8,294 ft (2,528 m)
Location: San Bernardino County, California, United States
Parent range: San Bernardino Mountains

San Gorgonio Mountain, also known locally as Mount San Gorgonio or Old Greyback, is the highest peak in Southern California and the Transverse Ranges at 11,503 feet (3,506 m). It lies within the San Gorgonio Wilderness, part of the Sand to Snow National Monument managed by the San Bernardino National Forest.

Geography

The mountain sits about 82 miles (132 km) east of Los Angeles and rises abruptly above San Gorgonio Pass, which separates the San Bernardino Mountains from the San Jacinto Mountains to the south. Its large, broad summit is one of the most topographically prominent peaks in the contiguous United States, and the summit is visible from much of the Los Angeles Basin and the Inland Empire on clear days. Though it is not the highest point in California (that is Mount Whitney), its prominence makes it stand out.

Recreation

Several trails ascend to the summit, the most popular beginning at Vivian Creek and the South Fork trailhead. The summit is above the treeline, with alpine conditions and strong winds common even in summer; patches of snow may persist into summer, and the peak is the only spot in Southern California with a true alpine tundra zone. The Pacific Crest Trail passes along its northern flanks.
