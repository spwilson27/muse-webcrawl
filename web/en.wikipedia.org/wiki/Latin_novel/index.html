Latin novel

The Latin novel (or Roman novel) refers to extended prose fiction written in Latin in the Roman Empire, chiefly Petronius' Satyricon and Apuleius' Metamorphoses (The Golden Ass).

LanguageLatin
Period1st–2nd century AD
Surviving worksSatyricon; Metamorphoses

Works

Petronius' Satyricon (c. AD 60s), surviving only in fragments, satirises the vulgar wealth of freedmen through the wanderings of Encolpius, including the famous Dinner of Trimalchio. Apuleius' Metamorphoses (late 2nd century), the only Latin novel to survive complete, follows Lucius, transformed into a donkey, through a series of embedded tales — notably Cupid and Psyche — to salvation through the goddess Isis.

Context

The Latin novels adapt the Greek romance and Menippean satire to Roman settings, mixing high and low registers, verse and prose. Their episodic, first-person narratives influenced later European picaresque fiction and the development of the modern novel.
