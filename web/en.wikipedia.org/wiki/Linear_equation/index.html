Linear equation

A linear equation is an equation for a straight line, in which the highest power of any variable is one and no variable is multiplied by another. Such an equation in n variables has the general form

a1x1 + a2x2 + … + anxn + b = 0,

where the ai are coefficients and b is a constant term. Linear equations form the basis of linear algebra, and their solution sets are lines, planes, or hyperplanes.

Two variables

In two variables, a linear equation Ax + By + C = 0 represents a straight line in the plane. Useful forms include the slope–intercept form y = mx + b, where m is the slope and b the y-intercept; the point–slope form y − y1 = m(x − x1); and the standard form Ax + By = C.

Solutions

A single linear equation in one variable, ax + b = 0 with a ≠ 0, has the unique solution x = −b/a. Systems of linear equations can have no solution, exactly one solution, or infinitely many, according to whether the lines (or planes) are parallel, intersect at a point, or coincide; they are solved systematically by methods such as substitution, elimination, and Gaussian elimination using matrices.

Applications

Linear equations model rates, mixtures, supply and demand, and linear relationships throughout science and economics, and are the first step toward the study of more general polynomial and differential equations.
