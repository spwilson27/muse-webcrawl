Saturn (astrology)

Saturn is the ruling planet of the signs Capricorn and, traditionally, Aquarius in Western astrology. Named for the Roman god Saturn, the planet is associated with discipline, responsibility, limitation, structure, time, and authority. Its glyph (♄) is traditionally read as the sickle of the harvest god or as the cross of matter above the crescent of the soul.

Saturn
Domicile: Capricorn (and Aquarius, traditional)
Detriment: Cancer (and Leo)
Exaltation: Libra
Fall: Aries
Orbital period: ~29.5 years

Significations

Astrologers link Saturn to boundaries, hard work, patience, ageing, and the consequences of one's actions — earning it the traditional name "the Greater Malefic" in Hellenistic astrology, though modern practitioners more often describe it as a stern teacher. Saturn's placement in a natal chart is said to indicate where a person encounters difficulty, duty, and the need for mastery through sustained effort.

Saturn return

Because Saturn takes about 29.5 years to orbit the Sun, it "returns" to its natal position around ages 29–30, 58–60, and 87–88. The Saturn return is popularly regarded as a milestone of maturation — a period of reassessment of career, relationships, and life direction. Astrologers also note Saturn's rulership of the skeletal system, skin, and teeth in medical astrology, and its transit through the houses as a marker of consolidation and testing.

In Vedic astrology

In Hindu (Jyotisha) astrology Saturn is Shani, a powerful and often feared deity whose seven-and-a-half-year transit across the Moon's sign and its neighbours, called sade sati, is widely believed to bring hardship and karmic lessons, mitigated by charity, discipline, and devotion to Hanuman.
