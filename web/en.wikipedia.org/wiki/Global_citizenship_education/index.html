Global citizenship education

Global citizenship education (GCED) is a form of civic learning that aims to equip learners of all ages with the knowledge, skills, values and attitudes needed to thrive in, and contribute to, an increasingly interconnected world. Promoted chiefly by UNESCO, it encompasses respect for human rights, cultural diversity, social justice, peace and sustainability, and is embedded in target 4.7 of the Sustainable Development Goals, which calls on countries to ensure that learners acquire the knowledge and skills needed to promote sustainable development and global citizenship.

Framework

UNESCO's framework identifies three core conceptual dimensions: the cognitive (knowledge of local, national and global issues and systems), the socio-emotional (a sense of belonging to a common humanity, empathy and respect for diversity), and the behavioural (acting effectively and responsibly at local, national and global levels). GCED draws on and overlaps with human rights education, education for sustainable development, peace education and intercultural education.

History

The concept gained prominence through the UN Secretary-General's Global Education First Initiative (2012), which made "fostering global citizenship" one of three education priorities. UNESCO subsequently published guidance including Global Citizenship Education: Topics and Learning Objectives (2015), and, with the Asia-Pacific Centre of Education for International Understanding, has promoted the concept through the youth-led GCED movement and clearinghouse resources.

Debates

Scholars distinguish "soft" approaches focused on awareness and charity from "critical" approaches that interrogate the structural causes of global inequality. The idea of global citizenship itself is debated, since citizenship is normally tied to states, and critics question whether GCED can promote political commitments without becoming ideological; supporters frame it as preparing learners for global challenges that transcend national borders.
