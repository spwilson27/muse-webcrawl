Tracing (art)

Tracing in art is the technique of copying a drawing or image by following its lines on a sheet placed over it. The copy sheet is typically translucent — tracing paper, vellum, or a digital layer — and the source is viewed by transmitted light, historically from a window and now usually from a light box or backlit tablet.

Uses

Artists use tracing to transfer sketches to finished supports, to refine and iterate on a composition without redrawing it, to reproduce lettering and technical illustrations, and in animation, where it historically underpinned rotoscoping and the inbetweening process. Architects and engineers used tracing paper ("trace") for drafting overlays well into the computer-aided design era.

Attitudes

Tracing is a standard learning and production tool, but presenting traced work as freehand original drawing is generally considered deceptive in illustration and fan-art communities. Photography-based tracing aids such as the camera lucida and camera obscura were used by Old Masters, a claim popularised by the Hockney–Falco thesis, which argues that some early modern painters used optical projections — a theory that remains debated among art historians.
