"The Chaos" is a poem demonstrating the irregularity of English spelling and pronunciation, written by the Dutch writer Gerard Nolst Trenité (1870–1946), also known under the pseudonym Charivarius. It first appeared in his 1920 book of English exercises, Drop Your Foreign Accent: engelsche uitspraakoefeningen, and has circulated widely ever since among teachers of English as a foreign language.

The poem begins: "Dearest creature in creation, / Studying English pronunciation, / I will teach you in my verse / Sounds like corpse, corps, horse and worse..." In its longest version it contains some 800 of the most irregularly spelled and pronounced words in English, arranged in rhyming couplets so that the reader, following the rhyme, is repeatedly tripped up by words that look alike but sound different, or sound alike but are spelled differently.

History and versions

Trenité, a Dutch teacher of English who had traveled extensively, wrote the poem to help learners confront the chaotic relationship between English orthography and phonology. Several versions of the poem exist; the original contained 274 lines, and a fuller version of 164 lines in its most commonly reproduced form ends with the challenge: "Finally, which rhymes with enough, / Though, through, bough, cough, hough, sough, tough?? / Hiccough has the sound of sup... / My advice is: GIVE IT UP!"

Legacy

The poem is frequently cited in discussions of English spelling reform and is used as a demonstration of why English is considered one of the hardest European languages to master in pronunciation. A simplified rendering of the poem was set to a reading challenge that has been reproduced in many pronunciation textbooks and on speech-training websites.
