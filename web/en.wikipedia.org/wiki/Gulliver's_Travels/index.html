Gulliver's Travels, or Travels into Several Remote Nations of the World. In Four Parts. By Lemuel Gulliver, First a Surgeon, and then a Captain of Several Ships, is a 1726 satirical novel by the Anglo-Irish writer and clergyman Jonathan Swift. It is a satire on human nature and the traveller's-tale literary subgenre, and Swift's best-known full-length work and a classic of English literature.

The book recounts the voyages of Lemuel Gulliver, a ship's surgeon, to four fantastical lands. Although widely adapted — often in abridged and bowdlerised form — as children's literature, the original is a fierce satire of politics, science, colonialism and human pride, and has never been out of print.

Synopsis

  • Part I — Lilliput: Shipwrecked, Gulliver wakes bound by the six-inch-tall Lilliputians, whose pompous court politics (including a war over which end of an egg to crack) mock the disputes of European states and religious sects.
  • Part II — Brobdingnag: In a land of giants, Gulliver is the miniature one; the king of Brobdingnag, hearing his account of England, concludes that the English are "the most pernicious race of little odious vermin that nature ever suffered to crawl upon the surface of the earth".
  • Part III — Laputa and other islands: A satire on abstract science and the Royal Society: the flying island of Laputa, the Academy of Lagado with its absurd experiments, immortal but decrepit Struldbruggs, and the summoning of historical ghosts in Glubbdubdrib.
  • Part IV — The Houyhnhnms: Gulliver encounters rational horses, the Houyhnhnms, served by brutish, Yahoo-like humans. He comes to despise humanity and, returning home, can barely tolerate his own family — a misanthropy whose sincerity critics still debate.

Themes and legacy

The novel satirises party politics, warfare, colonial exploitation, pride in reason, and the corruption of language and learning. Its coinages — Lilliputian, Yahoo (as a term for a brutish person), Brobdingnagian, and the names of computer-era terms such as "endian" (from the egg controversy) and the moons of Mars predicted for Laputa — have entered the language. The book influenced writers from Fielding and Thackeray to George Orwell, and remains one of the most widely read prose satires in English.
