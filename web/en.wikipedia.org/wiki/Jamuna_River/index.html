The Jamuna River (Bengali: যমুনা, romanized: Yamunā) is one of the three principal rivers of Bangladesh and the main distributary channel of the Brahmaputra River within the country. It flows generally south for about 245 kilometres (152 mi) to its confluence with the Ganges at Goalundo Ghat, where the combined stream takes the name Padma, and onward to the Lower Meghna and the Bay of Bengal. The Jamuna is among the widest rivers in the world, averaging some 12 kilometres (7.5 mi) wide in the rainy season.

Course and character

Until the late 18th century the Brahmaputra flowed in a more easterly course past Mymensingh. A series of floods, notably in 1787, and possibly tectonic shifts associated with the great earthquake of 1762, diverted its main flow westward into the present Jamuna channel. The river is a classic braided river: in the dry season it splits into innumerable shifting channels separated by sandbars and chars, which are farmed and settled but frequently eroded or flooded. Its banks migrate dramatically, making the Jamuna a major focus of river-training and erosion research.

Bridges and transport

The river long divided Bangladesh's northwest from Dhaka, crossed mainly by ferries. The Jamuna Bridge (Bangabandhu Bridge), opened in 1998, is a 4.8-kilometre multi-purpose road and rail bridge near Sirajganj and Bhuapur and was among the longest river bridges in South Asia at completion. A dedicated Jamuna railway bridge was constructed alongside it in the 2020s. Ferries and country boats remain vital for local transport and fisheries.
