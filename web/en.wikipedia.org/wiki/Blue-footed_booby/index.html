Blue-footed booby

Scientific nameSula nebouxii
FamilySulidae
RangeEastern Pacific, incl. Galápagos Islands
Conservation statusLeast Concern

The blue-footed booby (Sula nebouxii) is a marine bird native to subtropical and tropical regions of the eastern Pacific Ocean. It is one of six species of the genus Sula, known as boobies, and is easily recognised by its distinctive bright blue feet, which are a sexually selected trait.

Description and behaviour

The species averages about 81 cm (32 in) in length with a wingspan of roughly 1.5 m. The plumage is mainly brown and white, with a streaked head and neck. The brilliant blue of the feet comes from carotenoid pigments obtained from the bird's diet of fresh fish; the colour fades in birds that are hungry or unhealthy, and females preferentially mate with males showing brighter feet.

Breeding

About half of all breeding pairs nest on the Galápagos Islands. Courtship involves an elaborate dance in which the male lifts his feet alternately, points his bill skyward and whistles. The female lays one to three eggs in a shallow scrape on the ground, and both parents incubate using their large webbed feet. When food is scarce, siblicide may occur among chicks. Blue-footed boobies hunt cooperatively, plunge-diving from heights of up to 25 m to catch sardines, anchovies and squid.
