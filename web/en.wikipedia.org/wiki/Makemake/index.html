Makemake (minor-planet designation 136472 Makemake) is a dwarf planet and the largest of what is known as the classical population of Kuiper belt objects. With a diameter of roughly two-thirds that of Pluto, Makemake is the third-largest known trans-Neptunian object. It has one known moon, nicknamed MK2. Its extremely low average temperature, about 40 K (−233 °C), means its surface is covered with ices of methane, ethane and possibly nitrogen.

Discovery and naming

Makemake was discovered on March 31, 2005, by a team led by Michael E. Brown at the Palomar Observatory, and was announced on July 29, 2005, the same day as Eris. Before receiving its official name it was nicknamed "Easterbunny" by its discoverers because it was found shortly after Easter. In July 2008 it was named after Makemake, the creator of humanity and god of fertility in the mythology of the Rapa Nui people of Easter Island, partly to preserve the object's connection with Easter.

Physical characteristics

Stellar occultation observations in 2011 showed that Makemake lacks a substantial atmosphere comparable to Pluto's. Its surface is reddish in colour, attributed to tholins produced by irradiation of methane ice. In 2015 the Hubble Space Telescope revealed a small, dark moon, MK2, orbiting at a distance of at least 21,000 km with an orbital period of about 12 days or more.
