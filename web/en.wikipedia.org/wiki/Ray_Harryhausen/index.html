Raymond Frederick Harryhausen (29 June 1920 – 7 May 2013) was an American-British animator and special-effects artist, the creator of "Dynamation", a form of stop-motion model animation that combined animated models with live-action footage. His creature work on films including Mighty Joe Young (1949), The 7th Voyage of Sinbad (1958), Jason and the Argonauts (1963) and Clash of the Titans (1981) profoundly influenced generations of filmmakers, among them Steven Spielberg, George Lucas, Peter Jackson and Tim Burton.

Early career

Inspired at thirteen by Willis O'Brien's work on King Kong (1933), Harryhausen taught himself animation, later meeting O'Brien and working under him on Mighty Joe Young, which won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects. During the Second World War he served in Frank Capra's Army Signal Corps film unit. In the 1950s he animated the giant octopus of It Came from Beneath the Sea and the alien saucers of Earth vs. the Flying Saucers for producer Charles H. Schneer, beginning their long collaboration.

Dynamation classics

With Schneer, Harryhausen perfected rear-projection composites that let his models interact with actors. His celebrated sequences include the skeleton sword-fight in Jason and the Argonauts — often cited as one of the greatest effects scenes ever made, which took over four months to animate — the bronze giant Talos, the Hydra, the duel with Kali in The Golden Voyage of Sinbad, and the prehistoric creatures of One Million Years B.C. and The Valley of Gwangi. His final feature was Clash of the Titans (1981), featuring Medusa, the Kraken and Pegasus.

Legacy

Harryhausen received an Honorary Academy Award in 1992, presented by Tom Hanks, who said that some called Casablanca the greatest film ever made but that for him it was Jason and the Argonauts. He spent his later years in London, published memoirs, and his collection of models and artwork is preserved by the Ray and Diana Harryhausen Foundation. The 2020 exhibition "Ray Harryhausen: Titan of Cinema" at the Scottish National Gallery of Modern Art celebrated his centenary.
