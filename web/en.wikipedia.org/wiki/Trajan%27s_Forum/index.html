Trajan's Forum

Built106–112 AD
BuilderEmperor Trajan
ArchitectApollodorus of Damascus

Trajan's Forum (Latin: Forum Traiani; Italian: Foro di Traiano) was the last and largest of the Imperial fora of Rome, built on the order of Emperor Trajan with the spoils of war from the conquest of Dacia, and inaugurated in 112 AD. Its architect was Apollodorus of Damascus.

Layout

The forum centred on a vast rectangular plaza, about 200 by 120 metres, paved in coloured marble and dominated by a colossal gilded bronze equestrian statue of Trajan. The square was flanked by porticoes and exedrae decorated with statues of captured Dacians. Behind the plaza stood the Basilica Ulpia, the largest basilica in Rome, and beyond it the Column of Trajan, flanked by Greek and Latin libraries. The complex was completed by the Temple of Trajan, added after the emperor's death and deification.

Construction

Building the forum required extensive excavation: the saddle of land joining the Quirinal and Capitoline hills was cut away, a task commemorated by the column, whose height of about 30 metres (98 feet) was said to equal the depth of the cutting. Trajan's Market, a multi-level complex of shops and offices built into the excavated hillside, formed part of the same project.

Later history

The forum remained in use through late antiquity and was admired by visiting emperors for centuries. Much of its marble was later reused or burned for lime, and the area was built over in the medieval and Renaissance periods; excavations in the 20th century, including those for the Via dei Fori Imperiali, exposed the remains visible today.
