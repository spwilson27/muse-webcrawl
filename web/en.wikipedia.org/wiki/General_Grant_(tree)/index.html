General Grant (tree)

[Image placeholder: General Grant tree]

The General Grant tree is a giant sequoia (Sequoiadendron giganteum) in the General Grant Grove section of Kings Canyon National Park in California. It is the second-largest known living single-stem tree on Earth by trunk volume, after the nearby General Sherman tree, with a volume of about 46,600 cubic feet (1,320 m³), a height of roughly 267 feet (81 m), and a ground circumference of about 108 feet (33 m). Its age is estimated at around 1,650 years.

History and significance

The tree was named in 1867 by Joseph Hardin Thomas after Ulysses S. Grant, the Civil War general and later U.S. president. In 1926, President Calvin Coolidge proclaimed it the "Nation's Christmas Tree", and annual Christmas ceremonies have been held at its base since 1925. In 1956, President Dwight D. Eisenhower declared the tree a National Shrine — a memorial to Americans who died in war — the only living object so designated.

The tree stands in Grant Grove, an area protected since 1890, when General Grant National Park (later absorbed into Kings Canyon National Park, created in 1940) was established. A large fire scar on its trunk is a reminder of the sequoias' adaptation to periodic wildfire.
