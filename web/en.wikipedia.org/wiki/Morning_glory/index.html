Morning glory (also written morning-glory) is the common name for over 1,000 species of flowering plants in the family Convolvulaceae, whose current taxonomy and systematics remain in flux. The best-known morning glories belong to the genus Ipomoea. Their flowers typically unfurl into full bloom in the early morning, giving the plants their name, and often fade by the afternoon.

Description

Most morning glory species are twining, herbaceous annual or perennial vines with heart-shaped leaves and slender stems. The flowers are showy, trumpet- or funnel-shaped, and commonly blue, purple, violet, pink, or white, often with a paler throat. Many species bloom in the morning hours and close as the day heats up. Because they grow quickly and climb readily, they are popular ornamental plants for fences, trellises, and arbors, though in warm climates several species — such as Ipomoea indica — can become invasive weeds.

Cultivation and uses

Morning glories have been cultivated for centuries. In Japan, Ipomoea nil (asagao) became a much-loved garden flower during the Edo period, with growers breeding hundreds of ornamental varieties. Common garden species include Ipomoea tricolor ('Heavenly Blue'), Ipomoea purpurea (common morning glory), and Ipomoea alba (moonflower), whose white flowers open at night. The plants prefer full sun and well-drained soil; their hard-coated seeds germinate faster if nicked or soaked before sowing.

Chemistry

The seeds of certain species, notably Ipomoea tricolor and Turbina corymbosa (ololiúqui), contain ergoline alkaloids such as ergine (lysergic acid amide, LSA), which are hallucinogenic. Such seeds were used ritually by Aztec and other Mesoamerican peoples as entheogens. Ingestion is hazardous and the compounds are controlled in many jurisdictions.

Related plants

The family Convolvulaceae also includes the sweet potato (Ipomoea batatas), water spinach, bindweeds, and the parasitic dodder. Several plants in other genera of the family, such as Calystegia and Merremia, also bear the name morning glory.
