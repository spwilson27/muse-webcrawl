Square foot gardening

Square foot gardening is a method of intensive, small-scale gardening popularised by American gardener and engineer Mel Bartholomew, whose book Square Foot Gardening was published in 1981 and became one of the best-selling gardening books in the United States. The method was further promoted through Bartholomew's PBS television series of the same name.

Method

Plants are grown in raised beds, typically 4 feet (1.2 m) square, divided by a visible grid into one-square-foot (30 cm × 30 cm) sections. Each square is planted with a different crop, with the number of plants per square determined by the mature size of the plant: for example, one tomato plant, four lettuce plants, or sixteen carrot plants per square.

Bartholomew recommended a custom soil mixture, often called "Mel's Mix", of one-third peat moss, one-third coarse vermiculite, and one-third blended compost. Beds are usually about 6 inches (15 cm) deep, making the method suitable for areas with poor native soil and accessible to gardeners who cannot dig.

Advantages and criticism

Supporters cite efficient use of space, reduced weeding and watering, and accessibility for beginners and gardeners with limited mobility. Critics note the cost of the soil ingredients and that intensive spacing can require careful attention to watering and fertility. A revised edition, All New Square Foot Gardening (2006), updated the system and founded a charitable foundation promoting the method internationally.
