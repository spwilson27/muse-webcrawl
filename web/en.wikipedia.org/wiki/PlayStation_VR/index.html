PlayStation VR

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

PlayStation VR, known by its code name Project Morpheus during development, is a virtual reality headset developed by Sony Interactive Entertainment for the PlayStation 4 home video game console. It was released worldwide on 13 October 2016. It is fully functional with the PlayStation 4 and is forward compatible with the PlayStation 5 via an adapter. A successor, PlayStation VR2, was released in February 2023 for the PlayStation 5 only.

Hardware

The headset uses a single 5.7-inch OLED panel with a resolution of 1920×1080 pixels, providing 960×1080 per eye, and can display games at 90 or 120 frames per second. Nine blue LEDs on the headset are tracked by the PlayStation Camera to determine the wearer's head position. A separate processor unit connects the headset to the console, handles 3D audio processing, and outputs a social-screen image to a television so others can watch or join in. Games could be played with the DualShock 4 controller, PlayStation Move motion controllers, or the PlayStation VR Aim Controller.

Sales and reception

Sony announced in February 2017 that over 915,000 units had been sold; by December 2019 cumulative sales had passed five million units, making PlayStation VR the best-selling tethered VR headset of its generation. Reviewers praised its comfort, price relative to PC-based rivals such as Oculus Rift and HTC Vive, and its library of games including Resident Evil 7: Biohazard, Astro Bot Rescue Mission and Beat Saber, while criticising its comparatively low resolution and camera-based tracking.
