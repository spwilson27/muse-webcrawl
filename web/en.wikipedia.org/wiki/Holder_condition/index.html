Hölder condition

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, a real or complex-valued function f on d-dimensional Euclidean space satisfies a Hölder condition, or is Hölder continuous, when there are constants C ≥ 0 and 0 < α ≤ 1 such that |f(x) − f(y)| ≤ C‖x − y‖α for all x and y in the domain of f. The condition is named after Otto Hölder, who introduced it in 1882.

Properties

For α = 1 the condition is the Lipschitz condition; for α > 1 on an interval it forces f to be constant. Hölder-continuous functions with 0 < α < 1 are uniformly continuous but need not be differentiable anywhere: the Weierstrass function is Hölder continuous for some α < 1. Every Lipschitz function on a bounded set is Hölder continuous for every smaller exponent, and on bounded domains higher exponents give stronger conditions.

Hölder spaces

The vector spaces of functions satisfying such conditions, equipped with the Hölder norm ‖f‖ = ‖f‖∞ + sup |f(x) − f(y)| / ‖x − y‖α, are Banach spaces denoted C0,α. These spaces play a central role in the theory of partial differential equations: Schauder estimates give Hölder regularity of solutions to elliptic equations, and the De Giorgi–Nash–Moser theorem shows that weak solutions of certain elliptic and parabolic equations are automatically Hölder continuous.
