Alton Ellis

Alton Nehemiah Ellis OD (1 September 1938 – 10 October 2008) was a Jamaican singer-songwriter. One of the innovators of rocksteady, he was given the informal title "Godfather of Rocksteady". A gifted vocalist, he was a leading star of Jamaican music in the 1960s, recording for the top producers of the day, and continued performing into the 2000s.

Early career

Born in Trenchtown, Kingston, Ellis first performed as part of the duo Alton & Eddie (with Eddie Parkins), recording for Coxsone Dodd's Studio One label, including the hit "Muriel" (1959). After Parkins emigrated, Ellis formed the Flames and recorded ska and rhythm-and-blues sides for Duke Reid's Treasure Isle label.

Rocksteady years

As the tempo of Jamaican music slowed in 1966, Ellis became the defining voice of rocksteady. At Treasure Isle he cut enduring classics such as "Girl I've Got a Date", "Cry Tough", "Rock Steady" (1967) — the song that gave the genre its name — "You Make Me Happy", and "Why Birds Follow Spring". His Studio One recordings from the same period include "I'm Just a Guy" and "I'm Still in Love with You", the latter later a major hit for Althea & Donna (1978) and Sean Paul (2002). In 1967–68 Ellis toured the United Kingdom, where rocksteady was popular.

Later life

Ellis moved to England in the early 1970s, recording reggae and lovers rock (including "Too Late to Turn Back Now") and founding his own label. He remained a revered performer on the oldies circuit, influencing generations of Jamaican and British singers. In 2004 he was awarded the Order of Distinction by the Jamaican government. He died of cancer in London on 10 October 2008, aged 70.
