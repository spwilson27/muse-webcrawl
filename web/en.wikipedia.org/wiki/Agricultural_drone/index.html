Agricultural drone

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A spraying drone in operation over farmland

An agricultural drone is an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) used in precision agriculture to monitor crop and soil conditions, apply inputs such as pesticides and fertilizers, plant seeds, and survey farmland. Agricultural drones are typically equipped with multispectral, hyperspectral, thermal, or ordinary visible-light cameras, and many spraying models carry liquid tanks and nozzles driven by GPS-guided autopilots.

Uses

  • Crop monitoring: aerial imagery and vegetation indices such as NDVI reveal plant stress, nutrient deficiency, irrigation problems, and pest or disease outbreaks before they are visible from the ground.
  • Spraying: large multirotor or fixed-wing drones apply pesticides, herbicides, and liquid fertilizer with variable rates, reducing chemical use and operator exposure; manufacturers such as DJI (Agras series) and XAG dominate this market.
  • Planting and seeding: drones can broadcast seed or fire seed pods into the soil, used in reforestation and cover-crop seeding.
  • Livestock and infrastructure: drones are used to locate animals, inspect fencing, irrigation lines, and farm buildings.

History and regulation

Yamaha's R-MAX unmanned helicopter was used for crop dusting in Japan from the 1990s, making Japan an early leader. The widespread adoption of cheap multirotors in the 2010s brought drone scouting and spraying to farms worldwide, with especially rapid uptake in China. Operations are regulated as aviation: in the United States, commercial agricultural drone use falls under the FAA's Part 107 rules, with additional certification for aerial application of pesticides; other countries maintain their own licensing schemes.
