Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez

Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez, 531 U.S. 533 (2001), is a United States Supreme Court case concerning the First Amendment. The Court held that a federal restriction prohibiting Legal Services Corporation-funded attorneys from challenging the constitutionality of existing welfare laws was an unconstitutional viewpoint-based restriction on speech.

Background

The Legal Services Corporation (LSC) distributes federal funds to local programs providing legal aid to the poor. In 1996 Congress barred LSC-funded lawyers from representing clients in efforts to amend or challenge existing welfare law. Carmen Velazquez, a recipient of benefits, and attorneys including those of Bronx Legal Services challenged the restriction.

Decision

In a 5–4 decision written by Justice Anthony Kennedy, the Court struck down the restriction. Unlike the subsidy in Rust v. Sullivan, where the government spoke through doctors, the LSC program was designed to fund private speech — the attorney speaking on the client's behalf — and barring challenges to welfare law distorted the adversarial legal system by prohibiting vital arguments from reaching the courts. Justice Antonin Scalia dissented, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices O'Connor and Thomas.

Significance

The case is a key precedent on government funding and speech, distinguishing subsidies of private expression from government speech and reinforcing the rule against viewpoint discrimination in funding programs intended to facilitate a diversity of private views.
