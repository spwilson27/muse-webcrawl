Desmond G. Higgins is an Irish bioinformatician best known as the originator of the Clustal family of programs for multiple sequence alignment, among the most widely used and cited tools in computational biology. He is a professor at University College Dublin, where his group works on methods for aligning and analysing biological sequences.

Career

Higgins studied at Trinity College Dublin. While working at the European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL) in Heidelberg in the late 1980s, he developed the first Clustal program, which aligned protein and DNA sequences using a progressive alignment strategy based on a guide tree. Successive versions — Clustal V, Clustal W and Clustal X, developed with colleagues including Julie Thompson and Toby Gibson, and later Clustal Omega — added improved algorithms, graphical interfaces and the ability to align very large sequence sets.

Impact

The papers describing Clustal W (1994) and Clustal Omega (2011) are among the most highly cited in bioinformatics, and the software has been a standard tool in molecular evolution, phylogenetics and comparative genomics for over three decades.
