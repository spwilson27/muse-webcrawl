Great North Run

LocationNewcastle upon Tyne to South Shields, England
Established1981
DistanceHalf marathon, 13.1 miles (21.1 km)
FounderBrendan Foster
ParticipantsAbout 60,000

The Great North Run is the largest half marathon in the world, taking place annually in North East England each September. Participants run between Newcastle upon Tyne and South Shields, a distance of 13.1 miles (21.1 km).

The race was devised by former Olympic 10,000 m bronze medallist and BBC Sport commentator Brendan Foster. The first run, held on 28 June 1981, attracted 12,000 participants; the event grew steadily and now takes around 60,000 runners each year, with entry by ballot. The course crosses the Tyne Bridge at the start and finishes along the coast at South Shields. The Red Arrows traditionally perform a flypast.

Records and notable winners

Past winners include Mo Farah, who won three consecutive editions (2014–2016), and Kenenisa Bekele (2013). The event raises tens of millions of pounds annually for charities, and its millionth finisher was celebrated in 2014, the first mass-participation event in the world to reach that mark.
