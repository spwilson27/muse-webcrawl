Lake Huron (French: Lac Huron) is one of the five Great Lakes of North America. Hydrologically, it is the eastern portion of Lake Michigan–Huron, which has the largest surface area of any freshwater lake in the world. It is bounded on the east by the Canadian province of Ontario and on the west by the state of Michigan in the United States. The name of the lake is derived from early French explorers who named it for the Huron people inhabiting the region.

Geography

By surface area, Lake Huron is the second-largest of the Great Lakes, with 59,600 km², and the third-largest freshwater lake on Earth. Its shoreline, reckoned to include its many islands, is the longest of any of the Great Lakes at about 6,157 km. The lake's northeastern lobe, Georgian Bay, is so large it is sometimes called the "sixth Great Lake"; North Channel separates Georgian Bay from the main body. Within the lake lies Manitoulin Island, the largest lake island in the world.

Lake Huron receives the outflow of Lake Superior via the St. Marys River and of Lake Michigan via the Straits of Mackinac, and drains southward through the St. Clair River into Lake St. Clair and thence Lake Erie. Its retention time is about 22 years.

History

The lake was the first of the Great Lakes seen by European explorers; Étienne Brûlé reached Georgian Bay around 1615, and it appeared on French maps as Mer Douce, the "sweet" (fresh) sea. The region later became a centre of the fur trade and of French–English conflict. The lake is famous among maritime historians for its shipwrecks — thousands of vessels have sunk in its waters; the Great Lakes storm of 1913, the "White Hurricane", sank twelve ships on Lake Huron alone. Michigan's Thumb coast preserves part of the lake bed as the Thunder Bay National Marine Sanctuary.

Ecology

Lake Huron supports major fisheries for lake trout, walleye, whitefish and introduced salmon. Invasive species including zebra and quagga mussels, round gobies and alewives have reshaped its food web; the collapse of the alewife population in 2003–04 sharply reduced the lake's Chinook salmon fishery. Water levels fluctuate naturally and dropped to record lows in 2013 before rising to record highs in 2020.

Settlements

Major shore communities include Sarnia, Owen Sound, Collingwood and Midland in Ontario, and Bay City, Port Huron, Alpena and Cheboygan in Michigan.
