Three Little Pigs (film)

Three Little Pigs is an American animated short film produced by Walt Disney and directed by Burt Gillett, released on 27 May 1933 as part of the Silly Symphony series. Based on the fable of the same name, it was one of the most successful animated shorts of its era.

Plot

Fifer Pig and Fiddler Pig build flimsy houses of straw and sticks and spend their time playing music, while the diligent Practical Pig builds a house of bricks. The Big Bad Wolf blows down the first two houses, but the pigs take refuge with Practical Pig, whose brick house withstands the wolf. The wolf is finally scalded when he slides down the chimney into a pot of boiling water.

Reception and song

The film's song "Who's Afraid of the Big Bad Wolf?", written by Frank Churchill, became a popular hit and an anthem of resilience during the Great Depression. The cartoon won the Academy Award for Best Animated Short Film at the 6th Academy Awards and is credited with advancing character animation through the distinct personalities of the three pigs, achieved by animators including Fred Moore and Art Babbitt.

Legacy

The short spawned several sequels and was selected in 2007 for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically or aesthetically significant.
