George Green (mathematician)

George Green (14 July 1793 – 31 May 1841) was a British mathematical physicist who wrote An Essay on the Application of Mathematical Analysis to the Theories of Electricity and Magnetism in 1828.

Largely self-taught and working as a miller in Nottingham, Green introduced several fundamental concepts, including Green's theorem, Green's functions and the term 'potential'. His essay, published privately to a small circle of subscribers, was rediscovered after his death and became foundational in mathematical physics.

Legacy

Green entered Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge in 1833. His work underpins modern potential theory and influenced Thomson (Lord Kelvin) and Maxwell. Green's Mill in Sneinton, Nottingham, is now a science centre.
