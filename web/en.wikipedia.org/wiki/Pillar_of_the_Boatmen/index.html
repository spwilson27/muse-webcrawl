Pillar of the Boatmen

The Pillar of the Boatmen (French: Pilier des nautes) is a square-section stone pillar erected in Roman Lutetia (modern Paris) in the first century AD. Dedicated by the guild of boatmen (nautae) of the Parisii tribe, it is the oldest monument in Paris and a key source for the religion of Roman Gaul.

Discovery and description

The blocks of the monument were found in 1710 beneath the choir of Notre-Dame Cathedral, where they had been reused in late antique wall foundations, and are now displayed in the Musée de Cluny in Paris. The pillar is assembled from carved blocks bearing images and inscriptions. An inscription records that the boatmen dedicated the monument publicly to Jupiter in the reign of Tiberius (AD 14–37).

Iconography

The reliefs mingle Roman and Celtic gods side by side: Jupiter, Mars, Fortuna, and the Dioscuri appear alongside the Gaulish deities Esus, Tarvos Trigaranus (the bull with three cranes), Cernunnos, and Smertrios. This blend makes the pillar a celebrated example of religious syncretism in the Roman provinces and one of the few monuments to name several Celtic gods in inscriptions.
