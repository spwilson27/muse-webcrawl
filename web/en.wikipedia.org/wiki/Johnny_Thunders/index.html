Johnny Thunders (born John Anthony Genzale, 15 July 1952 – 23 April 1991) was an American guitarist, singer, and songwriter. He came to prominence in the early 1970s as the lead guitarist of the New York Dolls, and later fronted the Heartbreakers. His slashing, Chuck Berry-derived guitar style and swaggering stage image made him one of the defining influences on punk rock.

Early life

Genzale grew up in Jackson Heights, Queens, New York, the son of Josephine and John Anthony Genzale Sr., who left the family when he was young. A promising teenage baseball player, he turned to music, playing in several local bands and adopting the stage name Johnny Volume before taking "Johnny Thunders" from a comic-book character.

New York Dolls (1971–1975)

Thunders co-founded the New York Dolls in 1971 with singer David Johansen, bassist Arthur Kane, drummer Billy Murcia (soon replaced by Jerry Nolan) and guitarist Rick Rivets, replaced by Sylvain Sylvain. With their thrift-shop glam outfits, teased hair and raw rock and roll, the Dolls recorded two influential albums for Mercury Records, New York Dolls (1973) and Too Much Too Soon (1974), produced by Todd Rundgren and Shadow Morton respectively. Commercial success eluded them, and managed briefly by Malcolm McLaren, the band disintegrated on a Florida tour in 1975.

The Heartbreakers

Thunders and Nolan formed the Heartbreakers with bassist Richard Hell, soon replaced by Billy Rath. The band became a fixture at CBGB and Max's Kansas City. In 1977 they joined the Sex Pistols' Anarchy tour of the United Kingdom; signed to Track Records, they released their only studio album, L.A.M.F. (1977), whose muddy original mix blunted its impact. Thunders's best-known song, "Born to Lose", dates from this period, as does his signature number "Chinese Rocks", written largely by Dee Dee Ramone and Richard Hell.

Solo career

Thunders's first solo album, So Alone (1978), recorded in London with guests including Steve Marriott, Phil Lynott and the Sex Pistols' Steve Jones and Paul Cook, contains his best-loved ballad, "You Can't Put Your Arms Around a Memory". Years of heroin addiction undermined his career thereafter, though he recorded further albums including Hurt Me (1983), an acoustic record of notable vulnerability, and toured widely in Europe and Japan. He reunited periodically with the Heartbreakers and with Sylvain Sylvain.

Death and legacy

Thunders died on 23 April 1991 at the St. Peter House hotel in New Orleans, in circumstances that have never been fully explained; his room had been ransacked and his possessions stolen. Officially his death was attributed to drug-related causes. Though he sold few records in his lifetime, his guitar style and image profoundly influenced punk and glam-metal — Guns N' Roses and countless punk bands cited him — and his songs have been widely covered.
