The Pusan Perimeter (부산 교두보) was a large defensive perimeter around the southeastern Korean city of Pusan (now Busan), established by United Nations forces in the summer of 1950 during the Korean War. It was the final defensive position to which UN and Republic of Korea forces retreated after a series of defeats at the hands of the advancing North Korean Korean People's Army (KPA) in the opening weeks of the war.

Stretching roughly 140 miles (225 km), the perimeter ran from the Korea Strait north along the Naktong River and around the mountainous terrain near Taegu (Daegu) before turning east to the Sea of Japan. Behind it, reinforcements and supplies poured into the port of Pusan, allowing the UN force to build up strength while repelling repeated North Korean assaults.

Background

North Korea invaded South Korea on 25 June 1950. United States ground forces, rushed from occupation duty in Japan, were under-equipped and suffered defeats at Osan, Pyongtaek, Chonan and Taejon in July. South Korean forces likewise fell back steadily. By the end of July, UN forces under Lieutenant General Walton Walker, commander of the US Eighth Army, had been pushed into the southeastern corner of the peninsula. Walker issued his famous "stand or die" order, declaring there would be no retreat to Pusan.

The perimeter

The perimeter took advantage of natural obstacles: the Naktong River guarded the western and northern approaches, while mountains covered much of the line. UN forces, though outnumbered at the outset, enjoyed growing advantages in artillery, armour, air power and naval gunfire support. The perimeter enclosed the key ports and airfields around Pusan and Masan, and its relatively short front allowed reserves to be shifted rapidly to threatened sectors using interior lines of communication.

Fighting

Fighting along the perimeter from August to mid-September 1950 was intense and nearly continuous. Major engagements included the Battle of Taegu, the fighting around the Naktong Bulge, the Battle of Kyongju, and repeated attacks along the river line near Yongsan and Changnyong. North Korean units, their supply lines stretched and battered by UN air interdiction, launched a final all-out offensive at the beginning of September — sometimes called the Great Naktong Offensive — which made dangerous penetrations but failed to break the perimeter.

Breakout

On 15 September 1950, UN forces under General Douglas MacArthur landed at Inchon, far behind North Korean lines. The following day, Eighth Army forces attacked out of the perimeter. Facing collapse, North Korean forces disintegrated and retreated northward in disorder. By late September the perimeter had ceased to exist as UN forces advanced up the peninsula, linking up with the Inchon landing force near Osan on 26–27 September.

Legacy

The successful defence of the Pusan Perimeter was the turning point of the war's first phase. It bought the time needed to build up UN strength in Korea and made possible the Inchon landing and the subsequent advance to the Yalu River. The battle remains a classic example of a delaying defence conducted against a numerically superior attacker.
