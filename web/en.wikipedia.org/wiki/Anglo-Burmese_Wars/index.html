Anglo-Burmese Wars

Date1824–1886
LocationBurma (Myanmar), northeast India
CombatantsBritish Empire (East India Company) vs. Konbaung dynasty
ResultBritish victory; Burma annexed to British India

The Anglo-Burmese Wars were a series of three wars fought between the British Empire and the Burmese Konbaung dynasty in the 19th century, ending in the complete annexation of Burma into British India.

First Anglo-Burmese War (1824–1826)

Provoked by Burmese expansion into Assam and border clashes with British India, the First Anglo-Burmese War was the longest and most expensive war in British Indian history to that date. Under the Treaty of Yandabo (1826), Burma ceded Assam, Manipur, Arakan and the Tenasserim coast and paid a large indemnity.

Second Anglo-Burmese War (1852–1853)

The Second War arose from commercial disputes and the treatment of British merchants in Rangoon. British forces under General Godwin seized Rangoon and the Irrawaddy delta; Lord Dalhousie annexed the province of Pegu (Lower Burma) by proclamation, without a formal treaty.

Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885)

The Third War, triggered by a dispute over a fine imposed on the Bombay Burmah Trading Corporation and British fears of French influence at Mandalay, lasted only weeks. King Thibaw was deposed and exiled to Ratnagiri in India, and Upper Burma was annexed on 1 January 1886, though a guerrilla resistance continued for several years. Burma was administered as a province of British India until 1937.
