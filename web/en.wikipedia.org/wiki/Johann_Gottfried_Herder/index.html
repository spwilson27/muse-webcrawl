Johann Gottfried Herder

Born: 25 August 1744, Mohrungen, Prussia

Died: 18 December 1803 (aged 59), Weimar, Saxe-Weimar

Occupation: Philosopher, theologian, poet, literary critic

Era: Sturm und Drang; Weimar Classicism

Johann Gottfried von Herder (25 August 1744 – 18 December 1803) was a German philosopher, theologian, poet and literary critic. Associated with the Sturm und Drang movement and Weimar Classicism, he is a foundational figure of historicism and of modern thought about language, culture and nationality.

Thought

Herder argued that language and thought are inseparable, that each people (Volk) has its own distinctive genius expressed in its language, poetry and folk traditions, and that cultures must be understood in their own terms rather than judged by universal standards — a stance often summarised by the term Einfühlung (empathy). His Treatise on the Origin of Language (1772) and his unfinished masterwork Ideas for the Philosophy of History of Humanity (1784–1791) developed these themes.

Influence

Herder's championing of folk song, Shakespeare and medieval literature influenced Goethe, whom he mentored in Strasbourg, and helped spur cultural nationalism across Europe. His philosophy of history influenced Hegel and the development of anthropology and comparative linguistics.
