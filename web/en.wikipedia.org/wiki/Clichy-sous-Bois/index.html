Clichy-sous-Bois is a commune in the eastern suburbs of Paris, France, in the Seine-Saint-Denis department of Île-de-France. It is located 16 km (10 mi) from the centre of Paris. Despite its name ("sous-Bois" = "under the woods"), the commune is a densely populated suburban area largely made up of post-war housing estates.

History

The settlement, known historically as Clippiacum, was associated with the Merovingian kings and later with hunting estates of the French crown. It remained a small rural village until the 1960s and 1970s, when large housing estates (grands ensembles) were built to accommodate repatriates from Algeria and immigrant workers. The commune has one of the youngest populations in France and a high proportion of residents of immigrant origin.

The 2005 riots

Clichy-sous-Bois came to national and international attention on 27 October 2005, when two teenagers, Zyed Benna and Bouna Traoré, were electrocuted in an electricity substation while fleeing police. Their deaths triggered three weeks of riots in banlieues across France and led the government to declare a state of emergency. The events exposed deep grievances over unemployment, discrimination and policing in France's outer suburbs, and prompted sustained debate about integration policy.

Transport and renewal

Long one of the most poorly connected communes in the Paris region — with no railway station — Clichy-sous-Bois gained a tramway link (line T4 extension) in 2019, and a station on the Grand Paris Express metro Line 16 is planned. Major urban renewal programmes have demolished and rebuilt parts of its housing estates.
