Free Church of Scotland (1843–1900)

The Free Church of Scotland was a Scottish denomination founded in 1843 by a large withdrawal from the established Church of Scotland in an event known as the Disruption. In 1900 the vast majority of its congregations united with the United Presbyterian Church to form the United Free Church of Scotland, though a minority — the continuing Free Church — rejected the union.

Founded: 18 May 1843, Edinburgh
Key figure: Thomas Chalmers (first Moderator)
Cause: Patronage and the spiritual independence of the Church
Merged: 1900 into the United Free Church of Scotland

The Disruption

The crisis grew out of the "Ten Years' Conflict" over lay patronage — the right of landowners to impose ministers on congregations against their will — and the civil courts' insistence on enforcing presentees. After the courts and Parliament rejected the Church's Claim of Right (1842), at the General Assembly in Edinburgh on 18 May 1843 over 450 ministers, led by Thomas Chalmers and David Welsh, walked out and constituted the Free Church at Tanfield Hall. The seceders — about a third of the ministry and perhaps half the laity — surrendered their churches, manses and stipends.

Building a church

Through Chalmers' Sustentation Fund, a voluntary central fund equalising ministerial incomes, the Free Church within a few years built some 700 new churches, hundreds of schools and its own theological college, New College, Edinburgh. It was strongly evangelical, supported foreign missions in India and Africa, and included a large Highland membership that gave it a distinctive Gaelic-speaking constituency.

Union and legacy

Doctrinal relaxation and shared evangelical work drew the Free Church and the United Presbyterian Church together; they united on 31 October 1900 as the United Free Church of Scotland. A small minority refused to enter the union and retained the Free Church name — a claim upheld in the famous House of Lords judgment of 1904 (Bannatyne v. Overtoun), which awarded the Free Church the denomination's property and led Parliament to redistribute the assets by the Churches (Scotland) Act 1905. Most of the United Free Church joined the Church of Scotland in 1929.
