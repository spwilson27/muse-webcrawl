Indonesian mass killings of 1965–66

The Indonesian mass killings of 1965–66 were large-scale killings and civil unrest that occurred in Indonesia following the abortive coup attempt of the 30 September Movement on 1 October 1965. The army, under Major General Suharto, blamed the Communist Party of Indonesia (PKI) and organised a purge of communists, alleged sympathisers, and others.

Scale

Over several months, soldiers and local militias killed an estimated 500,000 to over one million people, with the worst violence in Central and East Java, Bali, and North Sumatra. Hundreds of thousands more were imprisoned without trial. The PKI — then the largest communist party in the non-communist world — was destroyed and banned, and ethnic Chinese Indonesians were also targeted in some areas.

Consequences and foreign involvement

The killings facilitated Suharto's rise to power and the establishment of the authoritarian "New Order" regime, which ruled until 1998. Western governments, including the United States and United Kingdom, supported the Indonesian army during the purge; declassified documents show that U.S. officials provided lists of PKI cadres to the Indonesian military. The events remain a sensitive and contested subject in Indonesia, with no official reckoning or prosecutions of perpetrators.
