Elections in Turkey

Elections in Turkey are held to elect the President of Turkey and the 600 members of the Grand National Assembly, as well as local administrations. Since the 2017 constitutional referendum, Turkey has operated under a presidential system, with presidential and parliamentary elections held simultaneously every five years.

Presidential elections

The president is elected by popular vote for a five-year term, renewable once. If no candidate wins an absolute majority in the first round, a runoff is held between the two leading candidates two weeks later. The first direct presidential election was held in 2014; Recep Tayyip Erdoğan won the elections of 2014, 2018 and 2023, the last after a runoff against Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu.

Parliamentary elections

Members of the Grand National Assembly are elected by proportional representation using the D'Hondt method in multi-member provincial districts. A national threshold — 10% until 2022, when it was lowered to 7% — applies to parties, though candidates running within electoral alliances can benefit from the alliance's combined vote. Voting is compulsory for citizens over 18, and turnout is typically among the highest in the world.

Local elections

Local elections, held every five years, choose metropolitan and provincial mayors, municipal councils and neighbourhood headmen (muhtars). The local elections of 2019 and 2024 saw major gains for the opposition Republican People's Party (CHP), which won control of Istanbul, Ankara and other large cities.
