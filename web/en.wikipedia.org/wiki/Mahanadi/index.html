Mahanadi

Mahanadi River
LengthAbout 858 km (533 mi)
SourceSihawa, Dhamtari district, Chhattisgarh
MouthBay of Bengal, near Paradip, Odisha
Basin sizeAbout 141,600 km²

The Mahanadi is a major river in East Central India. It drains an area of around 141,600 square kilometres and flows for about 858 kilometres through the states of Chhattisgarh and Odisha before emptying into the Bay of Bengal. Its name combines the Sanskrit words maha ("great") and nadi ("river").

Course

The river rises in the highlands of Chhattisgarh near Sihawa and flows eastward across the plains of Odisha. Its principal tributaries include the Seonath, Jonk, Hasdeo, Mand, Ib, Ong and Tel. Below Naraj, near Cuttack, it splits into a deltaic network of distributaries — among them the Kathajodi, Kuakhai, Bhargavi and Daya — which reach the sea near Paradip and Puri.

Hirakud Dam

Just upstream of Sambalpur stands the Hirakud Dam, one of the longest earth dams in the world, completed in 1957. It provides flood control, irrigation and hydroelectric power, forming a reservoir of some 743 km². The river's delta is one of India's most fertile agricultural regions, though it is also prone to destructive flooding.

Water disputes

The sharing of the Mahanadi's waters between Chhattisgarh and Odisha has been politically contentious, with Odisha objecting to upstream barrages and industrial diversions in Chhattisgarh; a tribunal was constituted by the Government of India in 2018 to adjudicate the dispute.
