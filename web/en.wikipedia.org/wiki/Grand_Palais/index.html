The Grand Palais des Champs-Élysées, commonly known as the Grand Palais, is a large historic exhibition hall and museum complex in the 8th arrondissement of Paris, France. Built for the Universal Exhibition of 1900, it is renowned for its enormous glass-domed nave — one of the largest glass roofs in Europe — and its Beaux-Arts stone façade.

Architecture

The building combines a classical stone façade with Ionic colonnades and Art Nouveau ironwork. The nave, about 240 m long, is capped by a steel-and-glass barrel vault crowned by a dome. Quadriga sculptures by Georges Réciponce adorn the corners facing the Seine and the Champs-Élysées.

Uses

The Grand Palais hosts major temporary exhibitions, art fairs, fashion shows (famously Chanel's runway spectacles), and sporting events; it served as a venue for fencing and taekwondo at the 2024 Summer Olympics after an extensive renovation completed in stages between 2021 and 2025. Its west wing long housed the Palais de la Découverte science museum. Across the avenue stands its smaller counterpart, the Petit Palais.
