P. L. Travers

Pamela Lyndon Travers (born Helen Lyndon Goff; 9 August 1899 – 23 April 1996) was an Australian-born British writer best known as the creator of Mary Poppins, the magical nanny who first appeared in the novel Mary Poppins (1934) and in seven sequels published over the following half-century. She wrote under the pen name P. L. Travers, adopting the surname from her father, Travers Goff.

Early life

Travers was born in Maryborough, Queensland, the daughter of a bank manager whose fortunes declined before his early death. As a teenager she published poems and toured Australia and New Zealand as an actress with a Shakespearean company before emigrating to England in 1924. There she wrote journalism, poetry and criticism, and fell in with the circle of the Irish poet AE (George Russell); later she became a close associate of the spiritual teacher George Gurdjieff, whose teachings influenced her writing for the rest of her life.

Mary Poppins

The first Mary Poppins book appeared in 1934 and was an immediate success. Travers's nanny is considerably sterner and stranger than the character familiar from film — vain, sharp-tongued and touched with cosmic mystery. Sequels including Mary Poppins Comes Back (1935) and Mary Poppins Opens the Door (1943) followed, with the final volume, Mary Poppins and the House Next Door, appearing in 1988.

Walt Disney pursued the film rights for two decades; the resulting film Mary Poppins (1964), starring Julie Andrews and Dick Van Dyke, was a triumph, but Travers disliked its sentimentality, its animation sequences and its changes to her character, and refused permission for a sequel during her lifetime. Her battles with the studio were dramatised in the film Saving Mr. Banks (2013).

Later life

Travers lived mainly in London, taught and lectured in the United States, and continued writing essays and studies in myth and symbolism. She was appointed an Officer of the Order of the British Empire (OBE) in 1977. She died in London in 1996, aged 96.
