Restatement of the Law

The Restatements of the Law are a series of treatises published by the American Law Institute (ALI) that aim to clarify, modernize, and otherwise improve the law, principally the common law of the United States. The Restatements set out principles of law in black-letter rules, accompanied by comments, illustrations, and reporters' notes describing the state of the existing law.

History

The American Law Institute was founded in 1923 in response to perceived uncertainty and complexity in American common law. Its first major project was the preparation of Restatements in core subjects such as contracts, torts, agency, property, and trusts. The first series of Restatements was completed in the 1930s and 1940s; many subjects have since been revised in second, third, and fourth series.

Content and influence

Each Restatement section states a general legal rule in concise black-letter text, followed by comments explaining the rule and hypothetical illustrations of its application. Although Restatements are not binding law, they are highly persuasive secondary authority and are frequently cited by American courts. Some provisions, such as section 402A of the Restatement (Second) of Torts, which articulated strict liability for defective products, have been enormously influential in shaping doctrine.

Prominent Restatements include those on Contracts, Torts, Property, Agency, Trusts, Conflict of Laws, Judgments, Restitution and Unjust Enrichment, and the Law Governing Lawyers. Some projects, notably aspects of the Restatement of Employment Law, have drawn controversy over whether they restate existing law or advocate reform.
