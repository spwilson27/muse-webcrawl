War of the Mantuan Succession

The War of the Mantuan Succession (1628–1631) was a conflict fought over the inheritance of the Duchy of Mantua and the Marchionate of Montferrat in northern Italy. Part of the wider struggle between France and the Habsburgs during the Thirty Years' War, it drew in Imperial and Spanish forces on one side and France and its Italian allies on the other.

Background

When Duke Vincenzo II Gonzaga died in December 1627 without a male heir, the senior Gonzaga line became extinct. The nearest relative by primogeniture was Charles of Gonzaga-Nevers, a French-based cadet of the family, who took possession of Mantua as Charles I. Spain and the Emperor Ferdinand II backed a rival claimant, Ferrante II Gonzaga of Guastalla, and disputed Nevers' right to the strategically vital Montferrat, which controlled routes between Spanish Milan and the Mediterranean.

Course of the war

In 1628 Imperial and Spanish forces invaded, besieging Casale in Montferrat. France, under Cardinal Richelieu's direction, intervened: Louis XIII and Richelieu personally led an army across the Alps in 1629, forcing Savoy to terms at the Treaty of Susa and relieving Casale. In 1630 Imperial troops stormed and sacked Mantua itself — a catastrophe for the city, aggravated by a plague carried by soldiers that killed a large share of its population. France returned to the offensive, and Emperor Ferdinand II, weakened by the Swedish intervention in Germany, sought peace.

Peace of Cherasco

The Treaty of Cherasco (June 1631) confirmed Charles of Nevers as Duke of Mantua and awarded the greater part of Montferrat to him, while Savoy received some Montferrat territory and France retained the fortress of Pinerolo, giving it a permanent gateway into Italy. The settlement was a diplomatic victory for France at Habsburg expense.
