Periodc. 60–230 AD

The Second Sophistic was a literary and oratorical movement in the Greek world under the Roman Empire, roughly from the 1st to the early 3rd century AD. The term comes from Philostratus's Lives of the Sophists.

Characteristics

Sophists were celebrity rhetoricians who performed improvised declamations on historical and mythical themes, cultivated an archaizing Attic Greek modelled on classical Athens, and served as ambassadors and benefactors for their cities. Centres included Athens, Smyrna, and Ephesus. Figures such as Dio Chrysostom, the orator Aelius Aristides (author of the Sacred Tales), the satirist Lucian, and the wealthy Herodes Atticus exemplify the culture.

Significance

The movement expressed Greek identity within Roman rule, reviving classical paideia. Its literature — novels, speeches, Philostratus's biography of Apollonius of Tyana — shaped later Greek and Byzantine rhetoric. It is distinguished from the 5th-century BC sophists such as Gorgias and Protagoras. See secret history for a later Greek literary tradition.
