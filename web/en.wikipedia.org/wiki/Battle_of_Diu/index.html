The Battle of Diu was a naval battle fought on 3 February 1509 in the harbour of Diu, India, between a Portuguese fleet under the viceroy Francisco de Almeida and a combined force of the Mamluk Sultanate of Egypt, the Gujarat Sultanate and the Zamorin of Calicut, supported by Ottoman assistance. It is considered one of the most important naval battles in history, marking the beginning of European naval dominance in the Indian Ocean.

Background

Portuguese intrusion into the Indian Ocean spice trade threatened the established Mamluk–Venetian trading system. A Mamluk fleet under Amir Husain Al-Kurdi had defeated a Portuguese squadron at Chaul in 1508, killing the viceroy's son Lourenço de Almeida. Francisco de Almeida refused to hand over power to his successor until he had avenged his son, and sailed for Diu, where the allied fleet lay under the protection of Malik Ayyaz, governor of Diu.

Battle

Almeida's eighteen ships entered the harbour on 3 February. Superior Portuguese gunnery and seamanship allowed their heavy carracks to stand off and batter the allied vessels, many of which were anchored or crowded near the shore; a boarding action completed the rout. The Mamluk fleet was effectively destroyed, and Malik Ayyaz subsequently surrendered Diu's cooperation to the Portuguese.

Aftermath

The victory secured Portuguese control of the sea lanes of the western Indian Ocean for the following century and led to the establishment of a Portuguese fortress at Diu in 1535. Almeida handed over the viceroyalty to Afonso de Albuquerque later in 1509.
