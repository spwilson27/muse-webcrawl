Mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) is an eight-week evidence-based program that offers secular, intensive mindfulness training to assist people with stress, anxiety, depression and pain. Developed at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center in the 1970s by Professor Jon Kabat-Zinn, MBSR uses a combination of meditation, body awareness, and yoga.

Program

The standard course runs for eight weeks, with weekly group sessions of about two and a half hours, one all-day retreat, and daily home practice of roughly 45 minutes. Core practices include the body scan, sitting meditation focused on breath and sensation, gentle hatha yoga, and informal mindfulness in daily activities. Kabat-Zinn defined mindfulness as "paying attention in a particular way: on purpose, in the present moment, and nonjudgmentally."

Evidence

Meta-analyses have found moderate evidence that MBSR improves anxiety, depression and pain, with effects on stress and quality of life, though reviewers note methodological limitations in many trials and call for more rigorous, adequately controlled research. Derivatives include mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT), developed by Zindel Segal, Mark Williams and John Teasdale to prevent depressive relapse.

Spread

From its origins as the Stress Reduction Clinic (founded 1979), MBSR has been adopted by hospitals, schools, businesses and prisons worldwide, and helped bring mindfulness practice into mainstream medicine and psychology.
