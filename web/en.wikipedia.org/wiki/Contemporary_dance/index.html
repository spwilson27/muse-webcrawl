Contemporary dance

Contemporary dance is a genre of dance performance that developed during the mid-20th century and has since grown to include influences from modern dance, ballet, jazz and world dance forms. It emphasizes versatility, improvisation, floor work and expressive, often unconventional movement.

Origins

Contemporary dance emerged as modern dance pioneers such as Martha Graham (contraction and release), Merce Cunningham (chance procedures) and Lester Horton freed dance from classical ballet conventions. The postmodern experiments of Judson Dance Theater in the 1960s further expanded it, including contact improvisation.

Techniques

  • Floor work and use of gravity, falls and recovery.
  • Release technique — efficient, breath-driven movement.
  • Improvisation as composition and performance tool.
  • Partnering, including weight-sharing drawn from contact improvisation.
  • Fusions with hip-hop, capoeira, yoga and martial arts.

Major figures

Internationally significant choreographers include Pina Bausch (Tanztheater), William Forsythe, Anne Teresa De Keersmaeker, Akram Khan, Ohad Naharin and Crystal Pite.
