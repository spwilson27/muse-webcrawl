Arthroscopy

Arthroscopy
SpecialtyOrthopedic surgery
Other namesArthroscopic surgery, keyhole joint surgery
Common sitesKnee, shoulder, elbow, wrist, ankle, hip

Arthroscopy (also called arthroscopic surgery) is a minimally invasive surgical procedure in which an orthopedic surgeon examines — and, where necessary, treats — the interior of a joint using an arthroscope, a slender fibre-optic or digital camera inserted through a small incision. Additional small incisions admit surgical instruments, and the joint is distended with sterile fluid so that its structures can be seen on a monitor. Arthroscopy is most commonly performed on the knee and shoulder.

History

The Japanese surgeon Masaki Watanabe is credited with developing modern arthroscopy in the mid-20th century, building on early work by Kenji Takagi and by the Danish physician Severin Nordentoft, who presented a knee endoscope in 1912. The technique spread rapidly in the 1970s and 1980s as camera and instrument technology improved, largely replacing open arthrotomy for diagnosis and for many therapeutic procedures.

Procedures

In the knee, arthroscopy is used to repair or remove torn menisci, reconstruct torn cruciate ligaments, trim damaged articular cartilage, remove loose bodies and treat synovial disease. In the shoulder it is used for rotator cuff repair, treatment of instability and labral tears, and subacromial decompression. Arthroscopy of the wrist, elbow, ankle and hip is also well established. Patients typically return home the same day, and recovery is usually faster than after open surgery.

Risks and evidence

Complications are uncommon but include infection, bleeding, blood clots, stiffness and damage to cartilage or nerves. For some long-standing indications — notably arthroscopic partial meniscectomy and subacromial decompression for degenerative conditions — randomised trials and sham-surgery comparisons have questioned the benefit over physiotherapy and exercise, leading guidelines in several countries to restrict their use in older patients with osteoarthritis.
