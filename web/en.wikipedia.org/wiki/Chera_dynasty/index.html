The Chera dynasty was one of the principal lineages of kings that ruled southwestern India, in present-day Kerala and parts of Tamil Nadu, from early historic times until the 15th century CE. Along with the Cholas and the Pandyas, the Cheras formed the three crowned dynasties (the "Three Crowned Kings") of ancient Tamilakam, celebrated in Sangam literature.

Early Cheras

The early Cheras controlled the central and northern parts of present-day Kerala and the Kongu region of western Tamil Nadu. Their ports, most famously Muziris, traded pepper and other spices with the Roman world; Roman coin hoards found in the region attest to the scale of this commerce. Sangam poems name celebrated rulers such as Uthiyan Cheralathan and Senguttuvan, the hero of the epic Silappatikaram.

Medieval Cheras

After a period of obscurity, a new Chera kingdom arose in the 9th century CE under the Kulasekhara dynasty, ruling from Mahodayapuram (near modern Kodungallur). The medieval Chera state, often called the Kulasekhara Empire, dominated Kerala until the early 12th century, when it fragmented into successor chiefdoms including those of Venadu, Calicut, and Cochin.
