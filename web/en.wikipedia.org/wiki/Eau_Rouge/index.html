Eau Rouge and the Raidillon climb at Spa-Francorchamps.

Eau Rouge

Eau Rouge is a corner sequence on the Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps in Belgium, home of the Belgian Grand Prix. The complex consists of a short downhill left-hander at the bottom of the valley followed by the sweeping uphill right-hand climb known as Raidillon, which rises to the Kemmel Straight. The name derives from the stream that runs beneath the track, whose iron-rich water has a reddish colour.

Characteristics

The sequence descends into the Eau Rouge valley and then climbs steeply — a gradient of around 18 percent — with significant compression at the bottom and a crest at the top that historically made cars go light. Formula One cars take the complex at well over 300 km/h, and it is considered one of the most challenging and famous corners in motor racing.

History and safety

The corner has featured in the circuit layout since the 1920s, with Raidillon created in 1939 to speed up the track. Several fatal and serious accidents have occurred at or near the sequence, including the deaths of Stefan Bellof in 1985 and Formula 2 driver Anthoine Hubert in 2019, prompting safety modifications such as enlarged run-off areas and reprofiled barriers ahead of the 2022 season.
