Adobe Inc. is an American multinational computer software company headquartered in San Jose, California. Founded in December 1982 by John Warnock and Charles Geschke, who left Xerox PARC to commercialise the PostScript page-description language, Adobe created the Portable Document Format (PDF) and makes the dominant tools of digital design and publishing: Photoshop for image editing, Illustrator for vector graphics, Acrobat for PDF, Premiere Pro for video and the Creative Cloud subscription suite that bundles them, sold through adobe.com.

History

PostScript, licensed by Apple for its LaserWriter printer in 1985, ignited desktop publishing. Adobe released Illustrator in 1987 and acquired Photoshop from its creators, the Knoll brothers, releasing it in 1990. It standardised PDF in 1993 and later gave the format to ISO as an open standard. In 2005 Adobe acquired its rival Macromedia for about $3.4 billion, gaining Flash, Dreamweaver and other web tools. From 2012–2013 the company moved its creative software from boxed licences to the Creative Cloud subscription, a transition that initially angered some customers but greatly increased recurring revenue. A proposed $20 billion acquisition of the design platform Figma, announced in 2022, was abandoned in December 2023 after regulatory opposition in the EU and UK.

Products

Beyond Creative Cloud, Adobe sells the Document Cloud (Acrobat and e-signatures) and the Experience Cloud for marketing and analytics. Since 2023 it has integrated Firefly, its family of generative AI models marketed as trained on licensed content, across its applications.

Criticism

Adobe has been criticised for subscription pricing and cancellation fees — the subject of a 2024 US government lawsuit over subscription practices — and for the security history of its discontinued Flash Player, which was retired at the end of 2020.
