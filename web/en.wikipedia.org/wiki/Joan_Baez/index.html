Joan Chandos Baez (born January 9, 1941) is an American singer, songwriter, musician and activist. Her contemporary folk music often includes songs of protest and social justice, and she has been one of the most prominent voices of the American folk revival since her debut at the Newport Folk Festival in 1959.

Early life

Baez was born on Staten Island, New York, to a Mexican-born physicist father, Albert Baez, co-inventor of the X-ray microscope, and a Scottish-born mother of Quaker faith. The family's beliefs and her experiences of racial prejudice shaped her lifelong commitment to nonviolence and civil rights.

Career

Baez's debut album, Joan Baez (1960), released by Vanguard Records when she was nineteen, showcased her pure soprano voice and traditional ballad repertoire, including songs from Child's collection. She was a central figure in popularising Bob Dylan, with whom she had a personal and musical relationship in the mid-1960s. She performed at the March on Washington in 1963, singing "We Shall Overcome", and at Woodstock in 1969.

Her greatest commercial success came in the 1970s with the album Diamonds & Rust (1975), whose title track reflects on Dylan, and a hit cover of "The Night They Drove Old Dixie Down". She has recorded in both English and Spanish, and has campaigned for civil rights, against the Vietnam War, and for human rights in Latin America and elsewhere. In 2018–2019 she undertook a farewell tour, "Fare Thee Well". Baez was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2017.
