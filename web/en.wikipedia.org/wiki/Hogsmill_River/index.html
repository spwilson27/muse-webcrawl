Hogsmill River

The Hogsmill River in London, England, is a small chalk stream tributary of the River Thames. It rises from springs at Ewell in Surrey and flows about 10 km (6 miles) north and east through Epsom and Ewell, Worcester Park's Surrey fringe, and Kingston upon Thames, joining the Thames at Kingston just downstream of Kingston Bridge.

Course and tributaries

The Hogsmill is fed by springs at the junction of the chalk of the North Downs and the London Clay. Its main tributaries include the Bonesgate Stream, which joins near Tolworth, the Horton Stream, and the Green Lanes Stream. Along its lower course it passes the Hogsmill Valley Sewage Treatment Works at Berrylands, and the treated effluent significantly augments the river's flow.

History and culture

The river powered several mills, historically including gunpowder mills near Ewell and paper and flour mills. It is associated with the Pre-Raphaelite painters: John Everett Millais painted the background of his famous Ophelia (1851–1852) beside the Hogsmill at Ewell, and William Holman Hunt painted The Hireling Shepherd in the same area. In literature, the river appears in local history as a boundary marker dating back to Saxon charters.

Ecology

As a chalk stream, the Hogsmill supports wildlife including kingfishers, water voles, and various fish species. Conservation groups and the South East Rivers Trust have worked on restoring the river's habitats, removing weirs, and improving water quality after historic pollution episodes.
