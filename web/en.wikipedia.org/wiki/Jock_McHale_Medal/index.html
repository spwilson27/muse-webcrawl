The Jock McHale Medal is an Australian rules football honour awarded to the coach of the winning team in the Australian Football League (AFL) Grand Final. It is named after Jock McHale, who coached Collingwood to eight premierships between 1919 and 1939 and is regarded as one of the greatest coaches in the sport's history.

The medal was first presented in 2001 to Leigh Matthews, coach of the Brisbane Lions. In 2004 the AFL decided to award it retrospectively to the premiership coaches dating back to 1950, the year after McHale's retirement as a coach.

Recipients

Multiple winners include Leigh Matthews, Kevin Sheedy, Alastair Clarkson, Damien Hardwick and Chris Scott. Norm Smith is among the retrospective recipients, honoured for his Melbourne premierships of the 1950s and 1960s.
