Vesak

Vesak lanterns in Sri Lanka.

Vesak (Pali: Vesākha; Sanskrit: Vaiśākha), also known as Buddha Jayanti, Buddha Purnima, and Buddha Day, is a holiday traditionally observed by Buddhists in South Asia and Southeast Asia, as well as in Tibet and Mongolia. It is among the most important Buddhist festivals. The festival commemorates the birth, enlightenment (Nibbāna), and passing away (Parinirvāna) of Gautama Buddha — events Theravada tradition holds to have all occurred on the same day of the year.

Date

Vesak is celebrated on the full moon day of the month of Vesākha in the Buddhist and Hindu calendars, usually falling in April or May in the Gregorian calendar (occasionally early June in leap years). In 1999 the United Nations General Assembly recognised the day internationally, and it is officially observed at UN headquarters and offices worldwide.

Observance

Devotees gather at temples before dawn to sing hymns, raise the Buddhist flag, and make offerings of flowers, candles, and incense. Observances include listening to sermons, meditating, observing the Eight Precepts, giving alms and releasing captive animals, and decorating temples and streets with lanterns. In Sri Lanka two days are set aside for the celebration, and elaborate illuminated pandals depict stories from the Buddha's life.
