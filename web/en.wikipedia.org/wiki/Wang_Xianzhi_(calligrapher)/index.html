Wang Xianzhi (Chinese: 王獻之; 344–386) was a Chinese calligrapher of the Eastern Jin dynasty, the seventh and youngest son of the celebrated calligrapher Wang Xizhi. Together the pair are known as the "Two Wangs", and Xianzhi is revered as a sage of calligraphy second only to his father.

The Duck-Head Pill Note, attributed to Wang Xianzhi

Life and work

Born into the eminent Wang clan of Langye, Xianzhi served as an official at the Jin court, eventually becoming a secretary in the palace secretariat. Trained initially by his father, he is said to have been told to seize mastery by first building a solid foundation; legend relates that his father once tried to snatch the brush from behind him as a boy and could not pull it from his grip.

Style

Xianzhi excelled in semi-cursive (xingshu) and cursive (caoshu) scripts and is credited with developing the "one-stroke" cursive technique (yi bi shu), in which a whole character or column flows in a single continuous movement. Where his father's style was restrained and inward, later critics described Xianzhi's as fluid, dashing, and outwardly expressive. Famous works attributed to him include the Duck-Head Pill Note (Ya Tou Wan Tie) and the Mid-Autumn Note (Zhongqiu Tie), though, as with his father's writings, no certain autograph originals survive; they are known through tracing copies.

Legacy

In the Tang dynasty, Emperor Taizong's devotion to Wang Xizhi eclipsed the son, but later connoisseurs re-established Xianzhi's standing, and the father–son pair defined classical calligraphy pedagogy for centuries.
