John Gilbert (bushranger)

John Gilbert (1842 – 13 May 1865) was an Australian bushranger who was born in Canada and came to Australia as a young man. Along with Ben Hall and John Dunn, Gilbert formed a gang that committed many robberies in central New South Wales during the 1860s.

Early life

Gilbert was born in Hamilton, Canada West, and emigrated to Australia with his family during the Victorian gold rushes. By the age of twenty, he had fallen in with bushranger Frank Gardiner's gang.

Bushranging career

After Gardiner's capture, Gilbert joined Ben Hall's gang, which held up mail coaches, homesteads, and towns across the Lachlan district. In 1864–65 the New South Wales parliament declared Hall, Gilbert, and Dunn outlaws under the Felons Apprehension Act. Gilbert is credited with participating in over sixty robberies, one of the most prolific records of any Australian bushranger.

Death

On 13 May 1865, Gilbert and Dunn were surprised by police near Binalong. Gilbert was shot dead while attempting to escape; Dunn escaped but was captured and hanged the following year.
