Agreement on the Application of Sanitary and Phytosanitary Measures

SPS Agreement
Signed15 April 1994, Marrakesh
Effective1 January 1995
PartiesMembers of the World Trade Organization

The Agreement on the Application of Sanitary and Phytosanitary Measures, commonly called the SPS Agreement, is an international treaty of the World Trade Organization (WTO). Concluded during the Uruguay Round and in force from 1 January 1995, it sets out rules for how governments may apply food safety and animal and plant health measures (sanitary and phytosanitary measures) without using them as disguised restrictions on trade.

Provisions

The agreement affirms members' right to protect human, animal, and plant life or health, but requires that measures be applied only to the extent necessary, be based on scientific principles and sufficient scientific evidence, and rest on a risk assessment. Members are encouraged to base measures on international standards set by the Codex Alimentarius Commission (food safety), the World Organisation for Animal Health, and the International Plant Protection Convention; measures conforming to these standards are presumed consistent with the agreement. Other provisions address harmonization, equivalence, transparency, and special treatment for developing countries.

Disputes

The SPS Agreement has featured in prominent WTO disputes, including the EC–Hormones case over the European Union's ban on hormone-treated beef, the Japan–Agricultural Products and Japan–Apples cases over quarantine requirements, and Australia–Salmon. These rulings clarified the evidentiary burden on countries maintaining stricter-than-international standards.
