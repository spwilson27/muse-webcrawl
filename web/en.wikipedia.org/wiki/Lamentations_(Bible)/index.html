Book of Lamentations

The Book of Lamentations (Hebrew: אֵיכָה, ʾĒikhāh) is a collection of poetic laments mourning the destruction of Jerusalem and the First Temple by Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon in 586 BC. It is one of the Five Megillot (scrolls) of the Hebrew Bible and appears among the Ketuvim ("Writings").

Structure

The book consists of five poems (chapters). The first four are acrostics, with successive verses beginning with successive letters of the 22-letter Hebrew alphabet; the third chapter is a triple acrostic of 66 verses. The poems vividly describe the siege, famine, destruction and exile, and express grief, confession of sin, and hope in God's mercy: "Great is Thy faithfulness" (Lamentations 3:23).

Authorship and use

Tradition ascribes the work to the prophet Jeremiah, and it is still sometimes called the Lamentations of Jeremiah; modern scholars generally regard it as arising from the exilic community. In Judaism the book is read publicly on Tisha B'Av, the fast commemorating the destruction of both Temples; in Christianity passages are read during Holy Week.
