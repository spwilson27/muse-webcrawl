Hyksos

The Hyksos were a people of mixed Levantine origin who settled in the eastern Nile Delta and came to rule Lower Egypt during the Second Intermediate Period, forming the Fifteenth Dynasty (c. 1650–1550 BC). Their name derives from the Egyptian heka khasut, "rulers of foreign lands", later misread by Greek writers as "shepherd kings".

Origins and rule

Excavations at their capital, Avaris (Tell el-Dab'a), show a largely Canaanite material culture: Levantine-style houses, temples, burials, and weapons, indicating gradual immigration from the Levant rather than a single invasion, as later Egyptian propaganda claimed. From Avaris the Hyksos kings — including Khyan and Apepi — controlled Lower and at times Middle Egypt and traded widely with the Levant, Cyprus, and Nubia, while the native Egyptian Seventeenth Dynasty ruled from Thebes in the south.

Innovations and expulsion

The Hyksos period is credited with introducing or popularizing in Egypt the horse-drawn chariot, the composite bow, new bronze-working techniques, and musical instruments such as the lyre. The Theban kings Seqenenre Tao and Kamose began the war of liberation, completed by Ahmose I, who captured Avaris and pursued the Hyksos to Sharuhen in the southern Levant, founding the New Kingdom. Later retellings, echoed by Manetho and Josephus, linked the Hyksos to the Exodus story, a connection most modern scholars treat with caution.
