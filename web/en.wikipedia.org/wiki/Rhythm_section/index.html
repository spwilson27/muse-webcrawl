Rhythm section

A rhythm section is a group of musicians within a musical ensemble or band that provides the underlying rhythm, harmony, and pulse of the music, accompanying and supporting the lead instruments or vocalists.

Composition

In jazz and popular music, a basic rhythm section typically consists of a chordal instrument (piano, guitar, Hammond organ, or vibraphone), a bass instrument (double bass, electric bass, or tuba), and a drum kit. Larger ensembles may add percussion, a second chordal instrument, or a horn-free lineup in rock and funk contexts.

Role

The rhythm section establishes tempo and groove, "comps" behind soloists, and in jazz historically "walked" the bass line through the chord changes. In studio recording, top session rhythm sections — such as the Wrecking Crew in Los Angeles and the Funk Brothers at Motown — played on thousands of hit records.
