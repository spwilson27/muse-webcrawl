Forlorn hope

A forlorn hope is a band of soldiers chosen as the first wave in an assault on a defended position, such as the storming of a breach in a fortress wall, where the risk of casualties is very high. The term entered English in the sixteenth century from the Dutch verloren hoop, "lost troop".

Role

Volunteers for a forlorn hope were typically motivated by the promise of reward, promotion or honour: officers who survived leading such an attack could expect advancement, and soldiers could gain pay, plunder or recognition. In British sieges of the Peninsular War, such as Ciudad Rodrigo, Badajoz and San Sebastián, the forlorn hope led storming parties into the breaches under concentrated fire.

Idiomatic use

By extension, "a forlorn hope" has come to mean any undertaking with a very slight chance of success.
