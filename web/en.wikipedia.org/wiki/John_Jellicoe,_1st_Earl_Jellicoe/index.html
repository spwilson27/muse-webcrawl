John Rushworth Jellicoe, 1st Earl Jellicoe (5 December 1859 – 20 November 1935) was a Royal Navy officer and statesman. He commanded the Grand Fleet at the Battle of Jutland in May–June 1916, the largest naval battle of the First World War — Winston Churchill called him "the only man on either side who could lose the war in an afternoon."

Early career

Jellicoe joined the navy at 12, specialised in gunnery, survived the sinking of HMS Victoria in 1893, and served as chief of staff on the China Station during the Boxer Rebellion, where he was severely wounded. A protégé of Admiral Fisher, he served as Director of Naval Ordnance and Third Sea Lord, driving dreadnought construction.

Grand Fleet and Jutland

Appointed to command the Grand Fleet on the eve of war in August 1914, Jellicoe kept the German High Seas Fleet contained by blockade. At Jutland (31 May – 1 June 1916) his fleet twice crossed the German fleet's T, but nightfall, torpedo threats and cautious deployment decisions allowed the Germans to escape after inflicting heavier losses. Historians continue to debate his caution and his handling of the battle, though Britain retained command of the sea.

First Sea Lord and later life

Jellicoe became First Sea Lord in November 1916 and, after initial resistance, oversaw the introduction of convoys against the U-boat campaign in 1917; he was dismissed by Lloyd George on Christmas Eve 1917. He undertook imperial naval missions, served as Governor-General of New Zealand (1920–1924), was created Earl Jellicoe in 1925, and died in London in 1935. He is buried in St Paul's Cathedral.
