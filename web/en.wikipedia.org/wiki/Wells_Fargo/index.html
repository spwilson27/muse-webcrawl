Wells Fargo

Wells Fargo & Company is an American multinational financial services company with a significant presence in all 50 U.S. states. The firm is one of the 'Big Four banks' of the United States, alongside JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America and Citigroup. Founded in 1852 during the California Gold Rush, it is famous for its stagecoach history and logo.

History

Henry Wells and William G. Fargo, co-founders of American Express, created Wells Fargo to provide banking and express transport in Gold Rush California, operating stagecoach lines that became legendary. After separating banking from express in 1905, the bank expanded through acquisitions, including Norwest Corporation (1998, taking the Wells Fargo name) and Wachovia in 2008, which gave it a coast-to-coast branch network built in part on branch-banking ideas pioneered by A. P. Giannini.

Fake accounts scandal

In 2016 it was revealed employees, under intense sales pressure, had opened millions of unauthorised accounts. The scandal led to fines, clawbacks, the resignation of CEO John Stumpf, and a Federal Reserve asset cap imposed in 2018 that remained a major constraint for years.
