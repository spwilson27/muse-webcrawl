Bosnian (bosanski jezik) is a South Slavic language and one of the three official languages of Bosnia and Herzegovina, alongside Croatian and Serbian. It is spoken by about 2.5–3 million people, principally ethnic Bosniaks, in Bosnia, the Sandžak region, and a large diaspora. Bosnian is based on the Shtokavian dialect, specifically the Eastern Herzegovinian subdialect with Ijekavian pronunciation.

Characteristics

Bosnian is mutually intelligible with Croatian, Serbian and Montenegrin, from which it is distinguished chiefly by its standardisation choices, including a layer of vocabulary borrowed from Ottoman Turkish, Arabic and Persian during the Ottoman period (for example kahva "coffee", komšija "neighbour"). Unlike standard Croatian and Serbian, Bosnian preserves the phoneme /h/ in words such as lahko ("easy", compare Serbian lako). The language is officially written in both the Latin and Cyrillic alphabets, although Latin predominates in everyday use.

History

Medieval Bosnia used a distinctive Cyrillic script known as Bosančica. During Ottoman rule, Bosnian was also written in an adapted Arabic script called Arebica. Vuk Karadžić's nineteenth-century reforms laid the basis for a shared Shtokavian standard, and in the Yugoslav period the language was treated as part of "Serbo-Croatian". After Bosnia's independence in 1992, Bosnian was codified as a separate standard, with the Sarajevo linguistic school playing a key role in its modern codification.
