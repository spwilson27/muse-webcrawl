Adler Planetarium

The Adler Planetarium is a public museum in Chicago, Illinois, dedicated to astronomy and astrophysics. It was founded in 1930 by Chicago businessman Max Adler. Located on Northerly Island at the shore of Lake Michigan, it is part of Chicago's Museum Campus together with the Shedd Aquarium and the Field Museum. The Adler was the first planetarium in the Western Hemisphere and holds one of the world's finest collections of historic astronomical instruments.

History and building

The twelve-sided granite building, designed by Ernest A. Grunsfeld Jr., is decorated with bronze plaques of the zodiac signs and was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1987. Its original Zeiss Mark II projector, installed in 1930, projected the night sky onto the dome of the Sky Theater. Major expansions added the glass Sky Pavilion (1999) and modern digital theatres, including the Grainger Sky Theater, one of the highest-resolution digital planetarium theatres of its time.

Collections

The Adler's Webster Institute for the History of Astronomy curates sundials, telescopes, astrolabes and rare books dating back to the 12th century, including one of the world's largest collections of antique astronomical instruments. Exhibits also cover the Gemini and Apollo space programmes, meteorites, and contemporary astrophysics research conducted with institutional partners.
