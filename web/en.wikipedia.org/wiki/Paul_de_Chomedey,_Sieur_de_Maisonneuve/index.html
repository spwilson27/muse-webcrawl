Paul de Chomedey, Sieur de Maisonneuve (15 February 1612 – 9 September 1676) was a French military officer and the founder of Fort Ville-Marie, which grew into the city of Montreal in New France.

Early life

Maisonneuve was born in Neuville-sur-Vannes, in the Champagne region of France, the son of Louis de Chomedey, seigneur of Chavannes. He entered military service as a young man and served in the European wars of the period, gaining experience that prepared him for his later role in Canada.

Founding of Montreal

In 1641, the Société Notre-Dame de Montréal, a group of devout laypeople and clergy inspired in part by Jeanne Mance and the visions of Jérôme le Royer de la Dauversière, recruited Maisonneuve to lead an expedition to found a mission colony on the Island of Montreal. He arrived in New France in the autumn of 1641 and wintered at Quebec. On 17 May 1642, Maisonneuve and his party landed at Pointe-à-Callière on the island and founded Ville-Marie, celebrating a mass at the site that became Montreal.

Governorship

Maisonneuve served as the first governor of Montreal for over two decades. He defended the small colony against attacks by the Iroquois (Haudenosaunee) during the Beaver Wars, organized its militia, and oversaw the construction of fortifications, a hospital (the Hôtel-Dieu, founded with Jeanne Mance), and the first church. In 1649 he famously dealt with the threat posed by flooding of the settlement, making a vow that led to the tradition of the illuminated cross on Mount Royal.

Later years and death

Recalled to France in 1665 amid political changes in the administration of New France, Maisonneuve lived in retirement in Paris, where he died on 9 September 1676. He never married and left his Canadian possessions to the colony.

Legacy

Maisonneuve is remembered as the founder of Montreal. The Maisonneuve Monument in Place d'Armes, unveiled in 1895, commemorates him, and his name is borne by boulevards, a park, and the Maisonneuve Regiment. His co-founder Jeanne Mance is commemorated alongside him.
