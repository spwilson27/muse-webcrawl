Unicode Consortium

The Unicode Consortium (legally Unicode, Inc.) is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization based in Mountain View, California, that maintains The Unicode Standard, the universal character-encoding scheme used for text in modern software and on the internet. Founded in 1991, the consortium coordinates the development of the standard with the ISO/IEC subcommittee responsible for ISO/IEC 10646, with which Unicode is synchronised.

History

The Unicode project began in 1987 with work by Joe Becker of Xerox and Lee Collins and Mark Davis of Apple. Unicode, Inc. was incorporated in 1991, and version 1.0 of the standard was published the same year. Full members have included most major technology companies — Apple, Google, Microsoft, IBM, Oracle and others — alongside government bodies and individual experts.

Work

Beyond the core standard, the consortium publishes the Unicode Character Database, the Common Locale Data Repository (CLDR) for software internationalisation, and technical standards for collation, segmentation, bidirectional text and security. Its Emoji Subcommittee reviews proposals for new emoji, whose annual releases attract widespread public attention. New characters and scripts are evaluated by the Script Encoding Initiative and the consortium's technical committee, with proposals open to the public.
