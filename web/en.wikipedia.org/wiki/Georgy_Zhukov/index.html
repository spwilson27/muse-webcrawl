Georgy Konstantinovich Zhukov (1 December 1896 – 18 June 1974) was a Soviet military commander and Marshal of the Soviet Union. He was the most decorated general officer in Soviet history and the Red Army's most prominent commander of the Second World War, overseeing the defence of Moscow, the victory at Stalingrad, the lifting of the siege of Leningrad and the capture of Berlin.

Early career

Born to a poor peasant family, Zhukov was apprenticed as a furrier before being conscripted into the Imperial Russian Army in 1915. He joined the Red Army and the Communist Party during the Russian Civil War. In 1939 he won a decisive victory over Japanese forces at the Battles of Khalkhin Gol in Mongolia, pioneering large-scale armoured operations.

Second World War

As Chief of the General Staff and then deputy commander-in-chief, Zhukov organised the defence of Leningrad and Moscow in 1941, co-planned the Stalingrad counteroffensive (Operation Uranus), and directed the battles of Kursk and Operation Bagration. In 1945 his 1st Belorussian Front captured Berlin, and he accepted the German Instrument of Surrender on behalf of the Soviet Union. He famously inspected the 1945 Moscow Victory Parade on a white horse.

Postwar

Suspicious of Zhukov's popularity, Stalin demoted him to provincial commands. After Stalin's death, Zhukov served as Minister of Defence (1955–1957) under Nikita Khrushchev, whom he helped consolidate power, before being dismissed and politically marginalised. His memoirs, published in 1969, were a bestseller, and he remains a revered figure in Russia, commemorated by an equestrian statue in Moscow's Manezhnaya Square.
