Fan law

The fan laws (also called fan affinity laws) are a group of expressions in fluid mechanics that govern how the performance of geometrically similar fans and pumps changes with impeller diameter, rotational speed, and fluid density. They are widely used in HVAC and process engineering to predict fan performance at conditions other than those tested.

The laws

For a given fan operating at a dynamically similar point: volumetric flow rate varies in direct proportion to speed; pressure varies with the square of speed; and shaft power varies with the cube of speed. Thus, doubling fan speed doubles airflow, quadruples pressure, and increases power demand eightfold — a relationship that makes modest speed reductions an effective energy-saving measure with variable-speed drives.

Limitations

The laws assume geometric similarity and unchanged efficiency, and they break down when compressibility, Reynolds number effects, or system resistance changes become significant. They apply at a single point on the fan curve; system curves must be considered for real installations.
