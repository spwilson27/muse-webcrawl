Isoxazole, systematically 1,2-oxazole, is an aromatic heterocyclic organic compound with the formula C₃H₃NO. It consists of a five-membered ring containing adjacent oxygen and nitrogen atoms, isomeric with oxazole (1,3-oxazole). Isoxazole itself is a colourless liquid with an odour resembling pyridine, and is weakly basic.

Synthesis and reactions

Isoxazoles are classically prepared by the 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition of nitrile oxides to alkynes, and many substituted derivatives are made by condensing hydroxylamine with 1,3-dicarbonyl compounds or their equivalents. The N–O bond is relatively weak: catalytic hydrogenation or other reductions cleave it, opening the ring to give β-enamino carbonyl compounds and related intermediates, which makes substituted isoxazoles useful synthetic precursors. The ring is comparatively resistant to electrophilic substitution because of the electron-withdrawing oxygen.

Occurrence and uses

The isoxazole ring appears in several pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals: the antibiotic oxacillin and related penicillins contain an isoxazolyl side chain; leflunomide and its active metabolite teriflunomide derive from an isoxazole precursor; the COX-2 inhibitor valdecoxib and the atypical antipsychotic risperidone (via a benzisoxazole ring) are further examples. The natural product ibotenic acid, found in Amanita muscaria, contains an isoxazole ring, as does its decarboxylation product muscimol.
