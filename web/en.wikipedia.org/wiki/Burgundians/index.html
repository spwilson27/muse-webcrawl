Burgundians

The Burgundians (Latin: Burgundiones) were an East Germanic people who migrated from Scandinavia — tradition links them to the island of Bornholm, "island of the Burgundians" — to the Vistula basin and later westward. During the Migration Period they established a kingdom on the middle Rhine and then in southeastern Gaul, giving their name to the modern French region of Burgundy.

Rhine kingdom

In 411 the Burgundians under King Gundahar (the historical Gunther) crossed the Rhine and established a kingdom around Worms in alliance with the usurper Jovinus. In 437 the kingdom was destroyed by Hunnic mercenaries acting for the Roman general Aetius — a catastrophe remembered in the medieval Nibelungenlied, where Gundahar becomes Gunther and the Burgundians' fall at Etzel's (Attila's) court forms the tragic core.

Kingdom in Gaul

Survivors were resettled by Aetius in Sapaudia (Savoy) in 443. From there the Burgundian kingdom expanded down the Rhône to Lyon and Provence under kings such as Gundioc and Gundobad. Gundobad issued the Lex Burgundionum, a law code for his Burgundian and Roman subjects, and the Burgundians — initially Arian Christians — gradually converted to Catholicism.

Fall and legacy

Frankish pressure culminated in conquest in 534, and the Burgundian kingdom was absorbed into the Merovingian realm. The Burgundian language died out, but the people's name survived in the region of Burgundy, later famous for its medieval duchy and wines.
