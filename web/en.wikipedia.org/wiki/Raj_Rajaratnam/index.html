Raj Rajaratnam (born June 15, 1957) is a Sri Lankan-American former hedge fund manager and founder of the Galleon Group, a New York-based hedge fund management firm. At its peak Galleon managed more than $7 billion.

In 2009 he was arrested by the FBI on insider trading charges, in what prosecutors described as the largest hedge fund insider-trading case in United States history. He was convicted in 2011 on 14 counts of conspiracy and securities fraud and sentenced to 11 years in prison — then the longest sentence for insider trading — and ordered to pay substantial fines and forfeiture. He was released to home confinement in 2019.

Early life and career

Rajaratnam was born in Colombo and educated in England, studying mechanical engineering at the University of Sussex before earning an MBA from Wharton in 1983. He worked as an analyst and banker, including at Needham & Co., where he became president, before founding Galleon in 1997. Galleon specialised in technology and healthcare stocks.

Insider trading case

The investigation, led by U.S. Attorney Preet Bharara, relied extensively on wiretaps of Rajaratnam's telephone conversations — an unusual technique in securities cases, more commonly associated with organised crime investigations under statutes such as RICO. Tipsters included executives and consultants at firms such as Intel, IBM, McKinsey and Goldman Sachs. Rajat Gupta, a former McKinsey chief and Goldman director, was also convicted in connection with the scheme.

Later life

After prison, Rajaratnam published a memoir, Uneven Justice (2021), arguing he was unfairly targeted.
