Hypochondroplasia

Specialty: Medical genetics
Cause: FGFR3 mutation (autosomal dominant)
Related: Achondroplasia, thanatophoric dysplasia

Hypochondroplasia is a developmental disorder caused by a genetic defect in the fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3) gene. It is a form of short-limbed dwarfism resembling a milder version of achondroplasia, which is caused by mutations in the same gene. The condition is inherited in an autosomal dominant manner, although many cases arise from new mutations.

Signs and symptoms

Individuals with hypochondroplasia typically have short stature, with disproportionately short arms and legs, a large head (macrocephaly) with mild frontal bossing, lumbar lordosis, and limited elbow extension. Shortening tends to be milder than in achondroplasia, and facial features are less pronounced; intelligence is usually normal, though mild learning difficulties are reported more often than in the general population. Features may not be recognisable until early childhood as growth falls away from standard curves.

Genetics

Roughly 60–70% of cases are caused by a single point mutation in FGFR3 (commonly p.Asn540Lys) that renders the receptor mildly overactive, restraining endochondral bone growth. Other FGFR3 variants account for additional cases. Genetic testing can confirm the diagnosis, which otherwise rests on clinical and radiographic findings such as shortening of the long bones and narrowing of the lumbar interpedicular distances.

Management

There is no cure; management is supportive and includes monitoring growth, spinal alignment, and joint function, physical therapy, and treatment of complications such as lumbar stenosis. Growth hormone therapy has variable and generally modest effects. Genetic counselling is offered regarding the 50% recurrence risk for offspring of an affected individual.
