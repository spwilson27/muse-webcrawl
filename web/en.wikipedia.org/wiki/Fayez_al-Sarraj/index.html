Fayez Mustafa al-Sarraj (Arabic: فائز مصطفى السراج; born 20 February 1960) is a Libyan politician who served as the Chairman of the Presidential Council of Libya and Prime Minister of the Government of National Accord (GNA) from 2016 to 2021. He took office under the Libyan Political Agreement signed at Skhirat, Morocco, in December 2015, and his government was recognised by the United Nations as Libya's legitimate government.

Early life and career

Al-Sarraj was born in Tripoli to a wealthy family of Turkish origin (his ancestor was an Ottoman-era notable). He trained as an architect and worked in housing and infrastructure during the Gaddafi era, including at the Housing Projects Authority, before running a private engineering practice. In 2014 he was elected to the General National Congress and then the House of Representatives for the Hay al-Andalus district of Tripoli.

Prime Minister

Appointed under the UN-brokered Skhirat agreement, al-Sarraj arrived in Tripoli by sea in March 2016 after rival factions contested his entry. His government fought Islamic State forces in Sirte (supported by US airstrikes in 2016) but struggled to assert authority over militias and the rival eastern administration of Khalifa Haftar. In April 2019 Haftar's Libyan National Army launched an offensive on Tripoli; with Turkish military support under a November 2019 security agreement, GNA forces repelled the assault and retook western Libya in mid-2020, leading to an October 2020 ceasefire.

Amid protests over living conditions, al-Sarraj announced in September 2020 his intention to resign; he ultimately handed power on 15 March 2021 to the Government of National Unity led by Abdul Hamid Dbeibah, chosen by the Libyan Political Dialogue Forum.
