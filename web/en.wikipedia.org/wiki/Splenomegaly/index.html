Splenomegaly

Splenomegaly is an enlargement of the spleen. A normal adult spleen weighs roughly 70–200 grams and measures up to about 11 cm in length; a spleen is generally considered enlarged when it exceeds about 12–13 cm. "Massive splenomegaly" is used when the spleen extends well into the lower abdomen or weighs over a kilogram. Splenomegaly is a sign of underlying disease rather than a diagnosis itself, and should be distinguished from hypersplenism, in which an enlarged spleen excessively removes blood cells causing cytopenias.

Causes

Causes are commonly grouped as infectious, congestive, hematologic, infiltrative, and autoimmune. Infections include infectious mononucleosis (EBV), malaria, visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar), endocarditis, and viral hepatitis. Congestive enlargement results from portal hypertension in cirrhosis or portal/splenic vein thrombosis. Hematologic causes include lymphomas, leukemias (notably hairy cell leukemia and chronic myeloid leukemia), myelofibrosis, and hemolytic anemias. Storage diseases such as Gaucher disease, amyloidosis, and sarcoidosis can infiltrate the spleen.

Diagnosis and treatment

Enlargement may be detected by palpation or percussion on physical examination and is confirmed and measured by ultrasound or CT imaging. Evaluation targets the underlying cause with blood counts, smear, cultures, serology, and sometimes bone marrow examination. Treatment is directed at the cause; complications include splenic rupture — a particular risk in mononucleosis, when contact sports are avoided — and hypersplenism. Splenectomy or splenic artery embolization is reserved for selected cases.
