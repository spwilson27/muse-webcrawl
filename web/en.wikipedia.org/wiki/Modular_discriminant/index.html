Modular discriminant

In mathematics, the modular discriminant Δ(z) is a modular form of weight 12 that plays a central role in the theory of elliptic functions and modular forms. It is defined on the upper half-plane and is intimately connected with the Weierstrass elliptic functions and the Dedekind eta function.

Definition

Let q = e2πiz for z in the upper half-plane. The modular discriminant is defined by the infinite product

Δ(z) = (2π)12 q ∏n≥1 (1 − qn)24,

equivalently Δ(z) = (2π)12 η(z)24, where η is the Dedekind eta function. Δ is a cusp form of weight 12 for the full modular group SL(2, ℤ), and the space of such cusp forms is one-dimensional, so Δ is uniquely determined (up to scale) by these properties. It vanishes nowhere on the upper half-plane, vanishing only at the cusp.

The tau function

The Fourier expansion of Δ defines Ramanujan's tau function τ(n):

Δ(z) = (2π)12 Σn≥1 τ(n) qn.

The values τ(1) = 1, τ(2) = −24, τ(3) = 252, … satisfy remarkable multiplicative and congruence properties first observed by Srinivasa Ramanujan, including the congruence τ(n) ≡ σ11(n) (mod 691). The Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture, that |τ(p)| ≤ 2p11/2 for prime p, was proved by Pierre Deligne in 1974 as a consequence of the Weil conjectures.

Relation to elliptic curves

For a lattice Λ in the complex plane, Δ(Λ) = g23 − 27g32, built from the Eisenstein series g2 and g3, is the discriminant of the corresponding Weierstrass cubic; it is nonzero precisely when the cubic defines a nonsingular elliptic curve. The quotient g23/Δ gives the j-invariant of the curve.
