Progeroid syndromes

Progeroid syndromes are a group of rare genetic disorders that cause aspects of premature aging. Affected individuals appear to age faster than normal, typically showing characteristics such as growth retardation, hair loss, skin changes, joint stiffness, and cardiovascular disease at an early age. The term progeroid means "resembling old age".

Classification

Not all progeroid syndromes affect every tissue equally; some involve only certain segments of the aging phenotype and are called "segmental" progerias. The best known include:

  • Hutchinson–Gilford progeria syndrome, caused by mutations in the LMNA gene affecting the nuclear envelope protein lamin A
  • Werner syndrome, caused by mutations in the WRN gene, a RecQ helicase involved in DNA repair
  • Cockayne syndrome, associated with defective nucleotide excision repair
  • Rothmund–Thomson syndrome and Bloom syndrome, also linked to genome instability

Mechanisms

Many progeroid syndromes arise from defects in DNA repair, nuclear architecture, or genome maintenance, leading researchers to study them for insight into normal human aging. No cure exists; treatment is supportive and directed at complications such as cardiovascular disease.
