Paul Graham

Paul Graham (born 13 November 1964) is an English-American computer scientist, essayist, entrepreneur and venture capitalist. He is known for his advocacy of the Lisp programming language, for co-founding the e-commerce company Viaweb, and for co-founding the startup accelerator Y Combinator.

Career

Graham studied at Cornell and earned a PhD in computer science from Harvard. In 1995 he and Robert Morris founded Viaweb, one of the first web-based applications, allowing users to build online stores; it was sold to Yahoo in 1998 and became Yahoo Store. In 2005, with Jessica Livingston, Trevor Blackwell and Robert Morris, he co-founded Y Combinator, which pioneered the model of funding batches of early-stage startups and backed companies including Airbnb, Dropbox, Stripe and Reddit.

Writing and Lisp

Graham's essays on his website, collected in Hackers and Painters (2004), are widely read on programming, startups and language design. He wrote books on Lisp (On Lisp, ANSI Common Lisp) and designed the dialects Arc and Bel. His essay "Great Hackers" and the concept of the "Blub paradox" about programming language power are well known in programmer culture.
