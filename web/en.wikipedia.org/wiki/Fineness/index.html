Fineness

The fineness of a precious metal object (coin, bar, jewellery) represents the weight of fine metal therein, in proportion to the total weight including alloying base metals and any impurities. Alloy metals are added to increase hardness and durability of coins, change colour, lower the cost per unit weight, or avoid the cost of high-purity refinement.

Expression

Fineness is traditionally expressed in parts per thousand (millesimal fineness). Pure metal is 1000 fine; sterling silver is 925 fine, meaning 92.5% silver by weight. For gold, fineness is also expressed in karats, with 24 karat being pure: 18-karat gold is 750 fine. Hallmarks stamped on precious-metal objects certify their fineness as verified by assay offices.

Historic standards

Bullion coins such as the American Gold Eagle are struck at 916.7 fine (22 karat), while modern investment bars are commonly 999.9 fine ("four nines"). Historically, debasement—reducing fineness while keeping face value—was a recurring fiscal practice of governments and a cause of inflationary pressure.
