Simo Häyhä

Born1905, Rautjärvi, Finland
Died2002, Hamina, Finland
Nickname"The White Death"

Simo Häyhä (1905–2002), often referred to by his nickname "The White Death", was a Finnish military sniper during the Winter War against the Soviet Union (1939–40). He is credited with around 500 kills of Soviet soldiers, the highest number of sniper kills in any major war.

Winter War service

Häyhä, a farmer and hunter before the war, served at the Battle of Kollaa. Dressed in white snow camouflage, he used an M/28-30 rifle — a Finnish variant of the Mosin–Nagant — with iron sights rather than a scope, to avoid glare and a raised profile, and packed snow in front of his muzzle to hide the blast. Temperatures fell as low as −40 °C.

Wounding and later life

On 6 March 1940 Häyhä was shot in the jaw by an explosive bullet and was found unconscious; he recovered consciousness a week later, the day peace was declared. He was promoted from corporal to second lieutenant, an exceptional jump. After the war he became a successful moose hunter and dog breeder, living quietly until his death in 2002.
