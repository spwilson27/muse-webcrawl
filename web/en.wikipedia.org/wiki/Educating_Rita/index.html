Educating Rita

Written byWilly Russell
CharactersFrank, Rita
Date premieredJune 1980
Place premieredDonmar Warehouse, London
Original languageEnglish
GenreComedy-drama

Educating Rita is a stage comedy by British playwright Willy Russell. It premiered in 1980 and was commissioned by the Royal Shakespeare Company. The play is a two-hander set entirely in the office of an Open University tutor.

Plot

The play follows Susan White, a 26-year-old working-class hairdresser from Liverpool who, dissatisfied with her life, enrols on an Open University course in English literature and takes the name "Rita" after the author Rita Mae Brown. Her tutor is Frank Bryant, a middle-aged, alcoholic university lecturer disillusioned with academia. Over the course of their tutorials, Rita gains confidence, knowledge and a new sense of identity, while Frank is forced to confront his own failures and emptiness.

As Rita grows more assured and begins to question the value of the world she has entered, the relationship between teacher and pupil reverses: Frank comes to depend on Rita emotionally, while she becomes increasingly independent of him. The play ends with Rita having passed her exams and facing a future of genuine choice, and Frank departing for a sabbatical in Australia.

Themes

The play explores class, education, freedom and personal transformation. Russell drew on his own experience as a working-class adult student and hairdresser. It has frequently been compared to George Bernard Shaw's Pygmalion, though Russell reverses the dynamic: it is the pupil who ultimately educates the teacher.

Film adaptation

The play was adapted into a 1983 film directed by Lewis Gilbert, starring Michael Caine as Frank and Julie Walters (who had originated the role of Rita on stage) as Rita. The film expanded the setting beyond Frank's office. Caine and Walters won BAFTA Awards for their performances, and the film received three Academy Award nominations.
