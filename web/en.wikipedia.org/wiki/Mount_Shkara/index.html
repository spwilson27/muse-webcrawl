Mount Shkhara
Elevation5,193 m (17,037 ft)
LocationGeorgia–Russia border, Svaneti
RangeGreater Caucasus

Mount Shkhara (Georgian: შხარა) is the highest point in Georgia and the third-highest peak in the Caucasus Mountains, at 5,193 metres (17,037 ft). It lies in the Svaneti region on the border with Russia, as the eastern anchor of the Bezengi Wall, a celebrated 12-kilometre ridge of 5,000-metre summits.

Climbing

Shkhara is a heavily glaciated massif with several sub-summits; its exact height has been variously measured, with recent surveys confirming it as Georgia's highest mountain. The peak was first climbed in 1888 by a British-Swiss party. Ascents are serious undertakings threatened by avalanches and crevasses, commonly approached from the village of Ushguli, one of Europe's highest permanently inhabited settlements and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Geography

The mountain rises above the Enguri River valley and forms part of the main watershed of the Greater Caucasus, separating Georgia's Svaneti from Russia's Kabardino-Balkaria.
