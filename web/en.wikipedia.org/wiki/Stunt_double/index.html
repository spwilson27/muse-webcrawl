A stunt double is a type of body double, specifically a skilled replacement used for dangerous sequences or other demanding work in film and television, usually for a particular actor. The double is cast to resemble the actor in build, height and hair, is made up and costumed identically, and is filmed in ways—distance, angle, motion—that conceal the substitution.

Role

Stunt doubles perform falls, fights, vehicle work, fire and water sequences, and other action that would risk injury to the principal actor or exceed the actor's skills. Many doubles specialise: precision drivers, gymnasts, martial artists, and high-fall experts. A long-standing star–double pairing is common; for example, some performers have doubled the same actor across decades of films. Stunt coordinators design and supervise the sequences and engage the doubles.

Distinction from other doubles

A body double substitutes for shots of the actor's body (including for nude scenes), a stand-in takes the actor's place while lighting and cameras are set up (and does not appear on film), and a photo double appears in stills or insert shots such as hands performing skilled tasks. Digital techniques—face replacement and CGI—now supplement or replace doubles for some sequences.

Recognition and safety

The profession has long campaigned for greater recognition, including Academy Award categories for stunt work; the Academy announced in 2025 that an Oscar for stunt design would be introduced for the 100th Academy Awards (honouring films of 2027). Stunt work remains hazardous, and fatal accidents have driven industry safety standards and the work of performers' associations and guild rosters such as the Taurus World Stunt Awards community.
