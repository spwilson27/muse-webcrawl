Youth detention center

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A youth detention center, also known as a juvenile detention center, juvenile detention home, or juvenile hall, is a prison for people under the age of majority, to which they have been sentenced and committed for a period of time, or detained on a short-term basis while awaiting trial or placement in a long-term care program. Juveniles go through a separate judicial process, the juvenile justice system, which sentences them to a particular detention center or program.

Overview

When minors violate the law, the court typically aims at rehabilitation rather than punishment. Juvenile detention centers hold young people who have been accused of committing a delinquent act and are deemed to require physically restrictive holding because they pose a risk to the community or to themselves, or because their parents or guardians are unable to exercise supervision. Detention is usually limited by statute to a maximum of 21 days.

Upon arrest, a juvenile offender is usually brought before a judge who determines whether detention is warranted. Youths may be detained for reasons including protection of the public, ensuring court appearance, and their own safety. Alternatives include home detention, shelter care, and release to a parent or guardian.

Conditions and criticism

Studies of youth detention facilities have often found conditions resembling those of adult prisons, with concerns about overcrowding, inadequate education, mental-health care, and the long-term effects of incarceration on adolescents. Research has associated juvenile incarceration with higher recidivism and worse educational outcomes, prompting reform movements favoring diversion programs, restorative justice, and community-based alternatives. In many jurisdictions the use of youth detention has declined substantially since the early 2000s.
