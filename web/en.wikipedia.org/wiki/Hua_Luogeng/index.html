Hua Luogeng
Born: 12 November 1910, Jintan, Jiangsu, Qing China
Died: 12 June 1985, Tokyo, Japan
Fields: Number theory, matrix geometry, functional analysis
Institutions: Tsinghua University, University of Cambridge, Chinese Academy of Sciences

Hua Luogeng (Chinese: 华罗庚; November 12, 1910 – June 12, 1985) was a Chinese mathematician and politician who made significant contributions to number theory, matrix geometry, economics and other fields. Largely self-taught, he became one of the most influential mathematicians of modern China.

Life

Born into a poor family in Jintan, Jiangsu, Hua left school to work in his father's shop, studying mathematics on his own. A paper he published correcting a solution by another mathematician brought him to the attention of Xiong Qinglai at Tsinghua University, where he rose from library assistant to lecturer. In 1936–38 he worked at Cambridge with G. H. Hardy, and he later taught in China and briefly in the United States.

Work

Hua is known for Hua's lemma and Hua's identity, fundamental results in analytic number theory and algebra, and for work on the additive theory of prime numbers connected with the Goldbach and Waring problems. After 1949 he helped build mathematics in the People's Republic, founded institutes, trained a generation of mathematicians, and popularised optimisation methods — the "overall planning method" and critical-path techniques — among industrial workers. He died of a heart attack while lecturing in Tokyo in 1985.
