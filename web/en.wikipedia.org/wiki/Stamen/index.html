Stamen

The stamen is the male reproductive organ of a flower. It typically consists of a slender stalk called the filament and a pollen-producing structure called the anther. Collectively, the stamens of a flower form the androecium.

Structure

The anther usually has two lobes containing four microsporangia (pollen sacs), in which pollen grains develop. When mature, the anther splits open (dehisces) to release pollen for pollination by wind, insects or other vectors.

Variation

Stamen number, fusion and arrangement vary widely among plant families and are important in plant taxonomy. In some flowers, sterile stamens called staminodes perform other roles, such as producing nectar or attracting pollinators.
