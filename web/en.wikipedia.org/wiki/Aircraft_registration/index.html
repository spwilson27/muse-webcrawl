Aircraft registration is the system by which every civil aircraft is assigned a unique identifying code by its state of registry, displayed on the airframe and recorded in a national register. Under Articles 17–21 of the 1944 Chicago Convention, an aircraft has the nationality of the state in which it is registered, may not be validly registered in more than one state, and must carry its nationality and registration marks. The marks consist of a nationality prefix allocated by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) followed by a registration suffix of letters or numbers assigned by the national authority.

Formats by country

In the United States the prefix is the single letter N (giving the familiar term "N-number"), followed by up to five characters administered by the Federal Aviation Administration. The United Kingdom uses G- followed by four letters (e.g., G-STBA); Germany uses D-; France F-; Japan JA followed by digits; Canada C-; Australia VH-; and China B-. Many registers allow personalised "out-of-sequence" or vanity registrations for a fee. Military aircraft instead carry serial numbers and national insignia rather than civil registrations, while gliders and ultralights may follow lighter regimes or historic exemptions — the United States, for example, allows suitably old aircraft to display smaller marks or period schemes.

Registration and leasing practice

Registration links an aircraft to a legal owner and jurisdiction, underpins airworthiness certification, insurance, and radio licensing, and must be transferred when an aircraft is sold across borders. Because of favorable corporate and legal regimes, certain registers are popular with leasing companies and business aviation: examples include Ireland, Aruba (P4-), Bermuda (VP-B/VQ-B), the Isle of Man (M-), San Marino (T7-), and Malta (9H-). An aircraft exported permanently must be de-registered from its old state before joining a new one, and a Certificate of Registration must be carried on board. Distinct from the registration is the mode S / ICAO 24-bit address, a unique electronic identifier used by transponders and flight-tracking networks.

History

Nationality marks were first allocated by the 1919 Paris Convention, whose allocations — such as K for the United Kingdom, later replaced by G — were revised after the Second World War under ICAO. The American "N" derives from the naval radio call-sign blocks assigned at international conferences in the 1910s–1920s.
