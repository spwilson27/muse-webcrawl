Dentures

Dentures (also known as false teeth) are prosthetic devices constructed to replace missing teeth, supported by the surrounding soft and hard tissues of the oral cavity. Conventional dentures are removable (removable partial dentures or complete dentures). However, many denture designs rely on bonding or clasping onto teeth or dental implants (fixed prosthodontics).

Types

  • Complete dentures replace all teeth in an arch and rest on the gums.
  • Partial dentures fill gaps where some natural teeth remain, often with metal or flexible frameworks and clasps.
  • Implant-supported dentures attach to dental implants for greatly improved stability.
  • Immediate dentures are fitted on the same day teeth are extracted.

Materials and construction

Modern dentures are usually made from acrylic resin, sometimes combined with metal frameworks of cobalt-chromium alloy; the artificial teeth are typically acrylic or porcelain. They are fabricated from impressions of the patient's mouth, with bite registration and trial fitting before final processing.

Care and issues

Dentures should be cleaned daily and removed at night. Over time, resorption of the alveolar ridge changes the fit, requiring relining or replacement. Poorly fitting dentures can cause sore spots, difficulty speaking and eating, and accelerated bone loss; denture adhesives can help stability modestly.
