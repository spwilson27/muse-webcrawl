Nonce word


Labelled placeholder: Jabberwocky text

A nonce word (also called an occasionalism) is a lexeme created for a single occasion to solve an immediate problem of communication. It was first used by James Murray, the principal editor of the Oxford English Dictionary, who included nonce-word as a headword. Some nonce words have a single known instance of use and may fade into obscurity, while others eventually enter common use; a famous example is the word "quark," coined by James Joyce in Finnegans Wake and later adopted by physicist Murray Gell-Mann for the subatomic particle.

Nonce words are frequently used in literature and children's books. In Jabberwocky, Lewis Carroll created many nonce words such as "galumphing" and "frabjous," some of which (like "chortle") have since become established English words. In modern studies of language, nonce words (sometimes called "wug words" after Jean Berko Gleason's Wug Test) are used in experiments on morphological productivity, especially in children.

Use in literature

Authors such as James Joyce, Lewis Carroll, Dr. Seuss and J. R. R. Tolkien are noted for inventive nonce formations. Joyce's Finnegans Wake contains hundreds of them; Dr. Seuss's books introduced words like "nerd" (first attested in If I Ran the Zoo, 1950), which later entered general usage.

In linguistics

Linguists construct novel nonce forms to test whether speakers apply morphological rules productively. The Wug Test (1958) showed that children who are shown a fictional creature called a "wug" will spontaneously say "two wugs," demonstrating knowledge of the plural rule.
