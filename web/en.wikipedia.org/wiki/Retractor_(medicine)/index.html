Retractor (medicine)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A retractor is a surgical instrument used to separate the edges of a surgical incision or wound, or to hold back underlying organs and tissues, so that body parts under the incision may be accessed during an operation. Retractors are fundamental instruments in nearly all branches of surgery. The broad term may also describe manually actuated tools such as hand retractors, as well as devices that hold themselves in place.

Types

Hand-held retractors are held by an assistant, nurse, or robotic surgical system throughout the procedure. Common examples, many named after their designers, include:

  • Senn retractor — a small, double-ended hand tool for superficial wounds.
  • Army–Navy retractor — a flat, double-ended blade used in minor procedures.
  • Richardson retractor — a right-angle retractor with a grip handle, used in deeper wounds.
  • Deaver retractor — a large, curved, hand-held blade used to retract organs such as the liver in abdominal surgery.
  • Rake retractors (such as the Volkman rake) — with sharp or blunt prongs for holding back superficial tissue.
  • Hohmann retractor — used in orthopaedic surgery around bones.

Self-retaining retractors hold tissues apart mechanically, usually with a ratchet, screw, spring, or frame, freeing the assistant's hands. Examples include the Weitlaner retractor (a small ratcheting tool with pronged tips), the Gelpi retractor, the Balfour retractor used in open abdominal surgery, table-mounted ring systems such as the Bookwalter retractor, rib spreaders (Finochietto) used in thoracic surgery, and speculum-like instruments used in neurosurgery and ophthalmology.

Considerations

Retractors must be applied with care: excessive or prolonged pressure can crush tissue, damage nerves, or compromise blood supply. Padding, intermittent release, and selection of an appropriately sized instrument reduce these risks. Traditional retractors are made of stainless steel; disposable plastic versions and illuminated retractors with integrated light sources are also available.
