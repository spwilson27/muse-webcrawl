Tylis (Greek: Τυλίς) was the capital of a short-lived Celtic (Galatian) kingdom established in eastern Thrace in the third century BC, following the great Galatian invasion of the Balkans in 279 BC. The kingdom maintained itself for several decades by exacting tribute from Greek cities of the region, including Byzantium, before it was destroyed by the Thracians around 212 BC.

The kingdom of Tylis lay astride the routes between Thrace and the Black Sea coast.

Foundation

In 279 BC Celtic tribes swept into Greece and Macedonia — one group sacked Delphi's environs before being repelled — while another, under the chief Comontorius, settled in Thrace and founded the kingdom of Tylis. The historian Polybius reports that the Galatians of Tylis grew powerful under kings such as Comontorius and Cavarus, minted coinage, and lived substantially by tribute levied on neighbouring peoples. Byzantium in particular paid escalating sums — reportedly rising to 80 talents of gold a year — to buy immunity from raiding, a burden that contributed to its dispute with Rhodes and Pergamon over Bosporus tolls.

Destruction

Around 212 BC (some sources give a slightly different date) the Thracian tribes rose against the Celts and destroyed Tylis, expelling or annihilating its population. Cavarus, its last known king, is described by Polybius as a cultivated ruler who mediated peace between Byzantium and its enemies shortly before the kingdom's end. The surviving Galatians of the invasion dispersed; the larger body of them had already crossed into Asia Minor, where they gave their name to Galatia.

Location

The site of Tylis itself has not been securely identified. It is generally placed in the vicinity of the Haemus (Balkan) Mountains in modern Bulgaria, and the name has been tentatively connected with the modern Bulgarian village of Tulovo.
