Carmel-by-the-Sea, California

Carmel-by-the-Sea, usually called simply Carmel, is a city in Monterey County, California, on the Monterey Peninsula. Known for its natural scenery, white-sand beach, fairytale cottages and long history as an artists' colony, it covers about one square mile and had a population of roughly 3,200 in 2020.

History

The area was home to the Rumsen Ohlone people. Spanish explorers named the bay and river for Our Lady of Mount Carmel; Mission San Carlos Borromeo, founded by Junípero Serra, stands nearby. After the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, writers and artists — including George Sterling, Mary Austin and Jack London's circle — made Carmel a bohemian colony.

Character and rules

Carmel is famous for quirks: no street addresses on houses historically, no street lights or sidewalks in residential areas, storybook architecture (notably Hugh Comstock's cottages), permits once required for high heels on uneven streets, and many art galleries, inns and dog-friendly businesses. Actor Clint Eastwood served as mayor 1986–1988.

Geography

Carmel Beach, Scenic Road, Point Lobos State Natural Reserve and nearby Pebble Beach and the 17-Mile Drive attract visitors. The climate is mild and often foggy. See Monterey Peninsula.
