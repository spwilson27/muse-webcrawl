Stephen the Great

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stephen III, commonly known as Stephen the Great (Romanian: Ștefan cel Mare; c. 1438/1439 – 2 July 1504), was Voivode (Prince) of Moldavia from 1457 to 1504, the longest-reigning and most celebrated ruler of the principality. He defended Moldavia's independence against the Ottoman Empire, Hungary, and Poland, and is revered in Romania and Moldova as a national hero; the Romanian Orthodox Church canonized him in 1992 as Saint Stephen the Great.

Accession

Stephen was the son of Bogdan II of Moldavia, who was murdered in 1451 by his brother Peter III Aron. After years of exile and struggle, Stephen, aided by Vlad the Impaler of Wallachia, defeated Peter Aron in 1457 and took the throne. He consolidated his rule, reformed the administration and the army, and strengthened the fortresses guarding Moldavia's frontiers.

Wars with the Ottomans

Stephen's refusal to continue paying tribute brought Moldavia into conflict with Sultan Mehmed II. In January 1475, at the Battle of Vaslui, his outnumbered army destroyed an Ottoman invasion force in one of the most celebrated victories of the era; Pope Sixtus IV hailed him as a "champion of Christ" (athleta Christi). Mehmed retaliated in person in 1476, defeating Stephen at Valea Albă, but failed to capture the key fortresses and withdrew. Stephen continued to resist, though after the loss of the Black Sea ports of Chilia and Cetatea Albă in 1484 he eventually accepted a tributary peace that preserved Moldavian autonomy.

Conflicts with Hungary and Poland

In 1467 Stephen defeated King Matthias Corvinus of Hungary at the Battle of Baia. In 1497 King John I Albert of Poland invaded Moldavia but was routed at the Battle of the Cosmin Forest, securing Stephen's northern frontier for the remainder of his reign.

Building and legacy

Tradition credits Stephen with founding a church or monastery after every victory — dozens in all — including the monasteries of Putna (his burial place), Voroneț (famed for its frescoes and later called the "Sistine Chapel of the East"), and Neamț. His image appears on Moldovan currency, and his statue stands in Chișinău and in towns across the region. He was succeeded by his son Bogdan III.
