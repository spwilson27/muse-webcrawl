Epistles of Wisdom

The Epistles of Wisdom (Arabic: رسائل الحكمة, Rasā'il al-Ḥikma) are a collection of 111 epistles that form the sacred scripture of the Druze faith, together with additional texts forming the Kitāb al-Ḥikma (Book of Wisdom). The epistles were written in the 11th century, principally by Ḥamza ibn ʿAlī ibn Aḥmad, the founder of the Druze movement, and by other early figures such as Ismāʿīl ibn Muḥammad al-Tamīmī and Bahā' al-Dīn al-Muqtanā.

Contents

The epistles are arranged in six books. They expound Druze theology, including strict monotheism (tawḥīd), the doctrine of the five cosmic principles or ḥudūd, reincarnation, and the esoteric interpretation of religious duties. The letters also contain directives to the community, refutations of opponents, and accounts of the movement's early history during the reign of the Fatimid caliph al-Ḥākim bi-Amr Allāh.

Significance

Access to the Epistles was traditionally restricted to initiated Druze (the ʿuqqāl), and the texts remain central to Druze religious life. Manuscripts are preserved in collections in Lebanon, Syria, and in Western libraries, and portions have been published and translated by scholars.
