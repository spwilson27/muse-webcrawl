Salsa20

Salsa20 is a stream cipher developed by Daniel J. Bernstein and published in 2005. It builds a pseudorandom keystream from a 256-bit key, a 64-bit nonce and a 64-bit block counter, and encrypts by XORing the keystream with the plaintext. Salsa20 was selected as a finalist in the eSTREAM project's software portfolio in 2008 and is in the public domain. Its reduced-round variants are named Salsa20/8 and Salsa20/12 according to the number of rounds.

Design

The Salsa20 core maps a 4×4 matrix of 32-bit words—containing the key, nonce, counter and the constants derived from the phrase "expand 32-byte k"—through repeated double rounds of an addition-rotation-XOR quarter-round function. The output is added word-wise to the input to produce each 64-byte keystream block. The design offers high speed in software, simple constant-time implementation, and random access to any keystream block via the counter.

Variants and adoption

Bernstein's later cipher ChaCha, a modification with improved diffusion per round, became far more widely deployed, including in TLS. Salsa20 itself is used in Bernstein's NaCl and in libsodium's default secret-stream constructions historically, and in the SUPERCOP benchmarking. No practical attack on the full 20-round Salsa20 is known; cryptanalysis has reached reduced-round versions, and the 8-round variant retains a comfortable security margin against known attacks.
