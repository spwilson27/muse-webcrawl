Jacobsen v. Katzer

Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

Decided: August 13, 2008

Citation: 535 F.3d 1373 (Fed. Cir. 2008)

Jacobsen v. Katzer, 535 F.3d 1373 (Fed. Cir. 2008), was a decision by the United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit concerning the enforceability of open-source software licenses under copyright law. The court held that the terms of the Artistic License are enforceable copyright conditions, not mere contractual covenants, so that a violation can support a claim of copyright infringement.

Background

Robert Jacobsen, a model railroad hobbyist, led the Java Model Railroad Interface (JMRI) project, which distributed open-source software for controlling model trains under the Artistic License. Matthew Katzer, through his company Kamind Associates, incorporated JMRI code into commercial products without the attribution and notices the license required. Jacobsen sued for copyright infringement and sought a preliminary injunction.

Decision

The district court initially denied the injunction, treating the license terms as contractual covenants whose breach would support only a contract claim. The Federal Circuit vacated and remanded, reasoning that the Artistic License's attribution and identification requirements are conditions of the license; exceeding the scope of the license is copyright infringement, with the remedies that follow from it.

Significance

The decision was widely regarded as a landmark for open-source software, confirming that open-source licensors can enforce their conditions through copyright law rather than relying solely on contract remedies.
