Archaeological geophysics

Archaeological geophysics is the application of geophysical survey methods — such as magnetometry, ground-penetrating radar, and electrical resistivity — to detect and map buried archaeological features without excavation. It is a form of remote sensing used in archaeological prospection and heritage management.

Field: Archaeology / Geophysics
Methods: Magnetometry, GPR, resistivity, LiDAR
Purpose: Non-invasive site prospection

Methods

Magnetometry measures small variations in the Earth's magnetic field caused by features such as hearths, ditches, and kilns. Ground-penetrating radar sends radar pulses into the soil to detect buried walls and voids. Electrical resistivity survey measures how readily the ground conducts current, revealing moisture differences associated with buried remains. Aerial photography, satellite imagery, and LiDAR complement ground-based techniques.

Applications

Geophysical survey is used to evaluate sites ahead of development, plan excavations, and investigate landscapes non-destructively, helping preserve fragile remains in situ.
