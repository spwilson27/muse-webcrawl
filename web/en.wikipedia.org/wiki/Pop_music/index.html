Pop music

Pop music is a genre of popular music that originated in its modern form during the mid-1950s in the United States and the United Kingdom. The term "pop" derives from "popular"; although the two are sometimes used interchangeably, pop music is distinguished by its aim at a general, mass audience and its emphasis on short, melodic songs with repeated choruses and hooks, simple structures, and polished production. Pop draws freely on other genres — rock, dance, R&B, country, electronic music, and hip hop — and functions as the commercial mainstream of the recording industry.

History

Pop emerged as rock and roll reshaped popular taste in the 1950s; by the 1960s, acts such as the Beatles and the Motown roster professionalised songwriting and production. The 1970s brought stadium rock-pop and disco, with groups like ABBA — propelled by their Eurovision win with "Waterloo" — demonstrating pop's global reach from studios such as Polar Studios in Stockholm. In the 1980s, Michael Jackson and Madonna defined the MTV-era superstar; the 1990s added boy bands, girl groups, and teen pop. The streaming era of the 2010s and 2020s globalised pop further, with K-pop acts achieving worldwide number-one singles and algorithms shaping song length and structure.

Characteristics

Musicologists note pop's "conservative" musical language — standard song forms, diatonic harmony, and danceable rhythms — combined with ever-shifting surface fashion. The songwriting factory model, from Tin Pan Alley and the Brill Building through Sweden's Cheiron Studios to contemporary writing camps, has been central to pop production. Critics have long debated pop's artistic status; modern pop criticism ("poptimism") treats mainstream success and craft as worthy of serious attention.

Global pop

Regional pop industries — J-pop, K-pop, Cantopop, Europop, Latin pop — developed their own star systems, and crossover collaborations are now routine. Pop remains the world's most commercially dominant popular genre by most measures of sales and streaming.
