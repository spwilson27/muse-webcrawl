Coole Park
LocationNear Gort, County Galway, Ireland
Famous forHome of Lady Gregory; centre of the Irish Literary Revival; Yeats's "Autograph Tree"
StatusNature reserve; house demolished (1941)

Coole Park is a nature reserve of approximately 405 hectares (1,000 acres) located a few miles west of the town of Gort, County Galway, Ireland. It is managed by the National Parks and Wildlife Service. The park contains extensive woodlands, a turlough (seasonal lake), and the remains of the Coole estate, once the home of Lady Augusta Gregory, dramatist, folklorist and co-founder of the Abbey Theatre.

The Gregory estate and the Literary Revival

Coole House was the home of the Gregory family; Lady Gregory inherited the estate after the death of her husband, Sir William Gregory, in 1892. From the late 1890s her home became a gathering place of the Irish Literary Revival: W. B. Yeats was a frequent summer guest, as were J. M. Synge, Seán O'Casey, George Bernard Shaw, John Masefield and others. Many visitors carved their initials into a great copper beech in the walled garden — the famous "Autograph Tree" — which survives today behind protective railings, bearing the signatures of Yeats, Shaw, Synge, Douglas Hyde and Augustus John among others.

Yeats and Coole

Yeats made the landscape famous in poems including "The Wild Swans at Coole" (1917), whose swans on Coole Lough became a meditation on change and permanence, and "Coole Park" and "Coole Park and Ballylee, 1931", elegies written as the house faced demolition. After Lady Gregory's death in 1932 the estate was sold to the Forestry Department; the house fell into ruin and was demolished in 1941. Yeats lived nearby for a time at Thoor Ballylee, a Norman tower house he restored.

The park today

The grounds — woodlands of ash, oak and beech, the walled garden, Coole Lough and its turlough system — are protected as a Special Area of Conservation and Special Protection Area and support whooper swans (the descendants in spirit of Yeats's swans), red squirrels and pine martens. A visitor centre in the former stables tells the story of Lady Gregory and the Revival.
