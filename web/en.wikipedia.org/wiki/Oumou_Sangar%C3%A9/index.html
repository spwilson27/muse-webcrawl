Oumou Sangaré

Born12 February 1968, Bamako, Mali
GenresWassoulou music, world music
OccupationSinger, songwriter, businesswoman, activist
Notable albumMoussolou (1990)

Oumou Sangaré (born 12 February 1968) is a Malian singer and songwriter, internationally celebrated as the "Songbird of Wassoulou". Performing in the Wassoulou style of southwestern Mali — built on the kamale ngoni harp and karinyan percussion — she sings in Bambara about women's lives, and has used her fame to campaign against forced marriage, polygamy, female genital cutting and child labour.

Career

Raised in poverty in Bamako, Sangaré sang at weddings and in a children's ensemble before recording her debut cassette, Moussolou ("Women", 1990), at the age of 21; it sold hundreds of thousands of copies in West Africa. Albums such as Ko Sira (1993), Worotan (1996), Oumou (2003), Mogoya (2017) and Acoustic (2020), and Timbuktu (2022) carried her music to world stages, with collaborations including work with artists in the wider world-music and pop scenes. She won a Grammy Award as a featured artist on Herbie Hancock's The Imagine Project (2010).

Activism and business

Sangaré is a prominent voice for women's rights in Mali, a Goodwill Ambassador for the UN Food and Agriculture Organization, and a successful entrepreneur — she has invested in a hotel and other businesses in Bamako, employing many women. Her uncompromising lyrics, unusual for a female performer in her society, made her a role model across West Africa.
