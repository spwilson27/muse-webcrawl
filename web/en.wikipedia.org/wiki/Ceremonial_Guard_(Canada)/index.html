Ceremonial Guard (Canada)

The Ceremonial Guard on Parliament Hill, Ottawa.

The Ceremonial Guard (French: Garde de cérémonie) is a unit of the Canadian Armed Forces that performs public duties in the National Capital Region, most notably the daily Changing of the Guard ceremony on Parliament Hill in Ottawa and sentry duty at Rideau Hall and the National War Memorial. The unit is formed each summer from personnel drawn from regiments across Canada, and its full-dress uniform of scarlet tunics and bearskin caps is modelled on the traditions of the Governor General's Foot Guards and the Canadian Grenadier Guards.

History

A public duties contingent had been maintained in Ottawa since the late 1950s, when the Governor General's Foot Guards and the Canadian Grenadier Guards began taking turns providing guards for the capital. In 1979, the Ceremonial Guard was formally established as a separate unit to consolidate these duties, drawing soldiers from both the Regular Force and the Primary Reserve. Since then, membership has been broadened to include personnel from across the Canadian Army, the Royal Canadian Navy and the Royal Canadian Air Force, as well as members of reserve regiments from every region of the country.

The Guard's most visible task is the Changing of the Guard at Parliament Hill, performed daily during the summer season, usually from late June to late August. The ceremony begins with the new guard marching from Cartier Square Drill Hall to the Hill behind the pipes and drums, where the old guard is relieved in a formal inspection and drill display. Guards also mount sentries at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier at the National War Memorial and at Rideau Hall, the residence of the Governor General and the monarch.

The Ceremonial Guard also provides guards of honour for visiting heads of state and other dignitaries, and participates in national commemorations such as Canada Day and Remembrance Day ceremonies in the capital.

Uniform

The full dress uniform consists of a scarlet tunic, dark blue trousers with a red stripe, and a tall black bearskin cap, closely following British foot guards tradition. Members wear the cap badge and buttons of their parent regiment, or of one of the two Ottawa guards regiments, reflecting the unit's composite nature. In winter or inclement weather, grey greatcoats may be worn on sentry duty.
