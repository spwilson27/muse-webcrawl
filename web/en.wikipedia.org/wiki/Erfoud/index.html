Erfoud (Arabic: أرفود) is an oasis town in the Drâa-Tafilalet region of eastern Morocco, situated in the Ziz Valley near the edge of the Sahara Desert. Built by the French in the early 20th century as an administrative and military outpost during the protectorate era, it serves today as a gateway for travellers heading to the Erg Chebbi dunes near Merzouga.

Geography

Erfoud lies at an elevation of about 807 metres in an arid desert landscape, surrounded by date palm groves. The town's modern grid layout dates from the French colonial period, when it was established as a garrison and market centre for the surrounding ksar villages of the Tafilalet.

Economy

The local economy rests on agriculture — especially dates from the Tafilalet oases — quarrying of fossil-bearing black marble, and tourism. The surrounding region is famous for its Devonian fossils, including trilobites, ammonites and goniatites, which are cut, polished and sold locally. Erfoud hosts an annual Date Festival celebrating the harvest.

Film location

The stark desert scenery around Erfoud has made it a popular filming location. Productions shot in the area include The Mummy (1999) and parts of Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time (2010).
