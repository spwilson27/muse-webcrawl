Caspar Olevianus

Caspar Olevianus (10 August 1536 – 15 March 1587) was a German Reformed theologian, best known as one of the authors, together with Zacharias Ursinus, of the Heidelberg Catechism (1563), one of the foundational confessional documents of the Reformed churches.

Life

Born in Trier, Olevianus studied law in Paris and Bourges, where a near-fatal accident prompted a vow to devote himself to theology and preaching the gospel in his native city. He studied theology in Geneva under John Calvin, as well as in Zurich and Lausanne. Returning to Trier in 1559, his Reformed preaching provoked conflict with the Catholic authorities, and he was imprisoned until released through the intervention of Elector Frederick III of the Palatinate.

Heidelberg

In Heidelberg, Olevianus became professor of theology and court preacher. Working with Ursinus, he helped draft the Heidelberg Catechism, contributing especially its emphasis on the believer's comfort in Christ and its covenantal framework. His major later work, De substantia foederis gratuiti (1585), was an important early statement of federal (covenant) theology.

Later career

After the Palatinate turned Lutheran under Ludwig VI, Olevianus moved to serve the counts of Nassau in Berleburg and later taught at the academy in Herborn, where he died in 1587.
