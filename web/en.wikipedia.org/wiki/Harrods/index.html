Harrods

Harrods is a luxury department store located on Brompton Road in Knightsbridge, London, England. It is owned by the state of Qatar through its sovereign wealth fund, the Qatar Investment Authority. The Harrods brand also applies to other enterprises undertaken by the Harrods group of companies, including Harrods Estates, Harrods Aviation, and Air Harrods.

Founded: 1834

Founder: Charles Henry Harrod

Headquarters: Knightsbridge, London

Owner: Qatar Investment Authority

Floor area: approx. 90,000 m² (970,000 sq ft)

Motto: Omnia Omnibus Ubique ("All things for all people, everywhere")

History

Charles Henry Harrod founded the business in 1834 as a wholesale grocery in Stepney, London. His son Charles Digby Harrod moved the store to Knightsbridge in 1849, where it grew from a single room into a grand emporium. A fire destroyed the store in 1883, but it was rebuilt on a larger scale; the present terracotta-clad building was completed in stages between 1894 and 1912. Harrods installed one of England's first escalators in 1898.

The store was acquired by Mohamed Al-Fayed in 1985 for £615 million. Under his ownership it became famous for its lavish Food Halls, the Egyptian Hall, its Christmas departments, and annual sales that drew celebrity openers. Al-Fayed sold Harrods to the Qatar Investment Authority in 2010 for a reported £1.5 billion.

The store

Occupying a 5-acre site, Harrods has around 90,000 square metres of selling space across more than 300 departments, making it one of the largest and most famous department stores in the world. Up to 100,000 bulbs illuminate its facade at night. The store historically offered an extraordinarily wide range of goods and services, and it held royal warrants from several members of the British royal family, which were withdrawn or not renewed in the early 2000s.

Within the store stood memorials to Diana, Princess of Wales, and Dodi Fayed, son of Mohamed Al-Fayed, who died in Paris in 1997; the memorials were removed in 2018 under new ownership.
