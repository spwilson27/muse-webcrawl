Holiday on Ice

TypeTouring ice show
First performanceDecember 1943, Toledo, Ohio, United States
GenreIce revue

Holiday on Ice is an ice show that has been running since 1943, touring internationally as one of the world's most successful ice revues. It was created in the United States and originated as a small set of performances at a hotel in Toledo, Ohio, over the Christmas holidays of 1943 — the source of the "Holiday" in its name. The production grew into a large touring spectacle featuring ensemble skating numbers, elaborate costumes and sets, and guest appearances by champion figure skaters.

History

The show was developed by Emery Gilbert, a Toledo engineer and builder of portable ice rinks, together with Morris Chalfen, who became its long-time owner and producer. The development of portable refrigeration equipment allowed Holiday on Ice to tour to arenas and venues without permanent ice, and it quickly expanded across the United States and then abroad. In the 1950s it became one of the first American entertainments to tour extensively in Latin America and Europe, and it famously performed in Mexico, South America, and behind the Iron Curtain during the Cold War.

Over the decades the production employed many famous skaters, including Olympic and world champions, and its tours were credited with popularizing figure skating in countries where the sport had little following. Ownership passed through several entertainment companies; the European operation later became part of the Stage Entertainment group, which continued to mount new productions under the Holiday on Ice name, particularly in Germany, France, and the Netherlands.

Productions

Holiday on Ice productions are typically themed revues combining precision ensemble routines, pair and adagio skating, aerial and acrobatic acts, and elaborate lighting and projection. Notable productions in the modern era have included Speed, Atlantis, and Showtime, a production celebrating the show's 75th anniversary. The company has claimed cumulative audiences in the hundreds of millions, making it one of the most attended live entertainment franchises in the world.
