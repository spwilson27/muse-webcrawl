Robert Southey (12 August 1774 – 21 March 1843) was an English poet of the Romantic school and Poet Laureate from 1813 until his death. One of the "Lake Poets" alongside William Wordsworth and Samuel Taylor Coleridge, he was among the most prolific writers of his age, producing epic poems, histories of Brazil and the Peninsular War, biographies — his Life of Nelson (1813) became a classic — and thousands of reviews and essays. To modern readers he is perhaps best known as the author of the original "Story of the Three Bears" (1837), the tale later attached to Goldilocks.

Life

Southey was born in Bristol and educated at Westminster School (from which he was expelled for an essay against flogging) and Balliol College, Oxford, which he left without a degree. In his youth he was a revolutionary sympathiser: with Coleridge he planned "Pantisocracy", a utopian community on the Susquehanna, and wrote the radical drama Wat Tyler (1794), whose unauthorised publication in 1817, when he had become a pillar of the establishment, caused a famous scandal. He married Edith Fricker in 1795 (Coleridge married her sister Sara), settled at Greta Hall, Keswick in 1803, and supported a large extended household by relentless writing. Appointed Poet Laureate in 1813, he became a hate figure for the younger Romantics; Byron savaged him in The Vision of Judgment (1822), a parody of Southey's own laureate poem on George III. After Edith's death in 1838 he married the poet Caroline Bowles; his mind failed in his last years, and he died at Keswick in 1843.

Works

Southey's long narrative poems — Joan of Arc (1796), Thalaba the Destroyer (1801), Madoc (1805), The Curse of Kehama (1810) and Roderick, the Last of the Goths (1814) — drew on Arabian, Hindu, Welsh and Spanish legend and enjoyed great contemporary fame, though his shorter lyrics, including "The Battle of Blenheim" with its refrain "But what good came of it at last?", proved more durable. His prose reputation rests on the Life of Nelson, the History of Brazil (1810–1819), the History of the Peninsular War (1823–1832), and The Doctor (1834–1847), the miscellany in which the Three Bears story first appeared.

Legacy

By the twentieth century Southey's poetry had fallen from fashion, eclipsed by the younger Romantics he outlived and antagonised, but scholars have revived interest in his prose, his reviewing, his translations from Spanish and Portuguese literature, and his vast correspondence.
