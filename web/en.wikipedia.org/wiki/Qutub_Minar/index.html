The Qutub Minar (also spelled Qutb Minar) is a minaret and victory tower in the Qutb complex at Mehrauli in Delhi, India. At 72.5 metres (238 feet) it is the tallest brick minaret in the world. Construction was begun around 1199 by Qutb al-Din Aibak, founder of the Delhi Sultanate, who completed only the base; his successor Iltutmish added three more storeys, and Firoz Shah Tughlaq added the fifth and final storey in the fourteenth century.

Architecture

The tower tapers from a diameter of about 14.3 metres at the base to 2.7 metres at the top and contains a spiral staircase of 379 steps. Its five distinct storeys are separated by balconies supported on muqarnas corbelling. The first three storeys are of red sandstone and the upper two of marble and sandstone. Alternating angular and rounded fluting, bands of Nagari and Arabic calligraphy, and intricate carving decorate the shaft. The minaret is thought to have been inspired by the Minaret of Jam in Afghanistan.

The Qutb complex

The surrounding complex includes the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, among the earliest surviving mosques in India; the famous Iron Pillar of Delhi, a fourth-century iron column notable for its resistance to corrosion; the Alai Darwaza gateway; and the tomb of Iltutmish. The complex was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993 and is one of Delhi's most visited monuments.
