Beta decay
Type: Wikipedia article (recreated from memory)

In nuclear physics, beta decay (β-decay) is a type of radioactive decay in which an atomic nucleus emits a beta particle (a fast energetic electron or positron), transforming a neutron into a proton (β⁻) or a proton into a neutron (β⁺), and thus changes the element while keeping the same mass number.

Beta decay is mediated by the weak nuclear force. The continuous energy spectrum of emitted electrons led Wolfgang Pauli in 1930 to postulate an unseen neutral particle — Enrico Fermi's 'neutrino' — incorporated into Fermi's 1933 theory of beta decay; the antineutrino was detected in 1956 (Cowan and Reines). Beta emitters include tritium, carbon-14 (used in dating), potassium-40 (a main source of natural internal radiation) and many fission products central to reactor physics and nuclear waste.
