Mahamuni Buddha Temple

The Mahamuni Buddha Temple (Burmese: မဟာမုနိဘုရားကြီး), also called the Mahamuni Pagoda, is a Buddhist temple and major pilgrimage site in Mandalay, Myanmar. It houses the Mahamuni Buddha image, a 3.8-metre bronze statue that is among the most revered Buddha images in the country. According to tradition it was cast in Arakan (Rakhine) during the Buddha's lifetime, and it was brought to Mandalay in 1784 after King Bodawpaya conquered the Arakanese kingdom of Mrauk U.

The Mahamuni image

Legend relates that the Buddha visited Arakan and agreed to have his likeness cast, breathing upon it so that it became a living representation of himself. Whatever its true age, the image became the palladium of the Arakanese kingdom before its removal to Mandalay. Devotees apply gold leaf to the statue daily, so that its body is now coated in a layer of gold some 15 cm thick, giving it a characteristically lumpy appearance; only men are permitted to approach and apply gold leaf. Each morning at about 4 a.m., monks wash the statue's face and brush its teeth in a ceremony attended by crowds of worshippers.

The temple complex

The complex includes a museum, ponds, and the celebrated set of Khmer bronze statues — including figures of Airavata, Hindu deities and lion guardians — taken from Angkor as war booty in earlier centuries and believed by devotees to have healing powers when rubbed. Fires damaged the complex in 1879 and 1884, after which it was rebuilt. The temple remains one of Myanmar's most important sites of devotion.
