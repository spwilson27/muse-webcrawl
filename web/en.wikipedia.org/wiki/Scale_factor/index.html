Scale factor

A scale factor is a number by which a quantity is multiplied, changing the scale or magnitude of the quantity. In geometry, the scale factor is the ratio of corresponding lengths in similar figures: if a shape is enlarged or reduced, each of its lengths is multiplied by the scale factor, its area by the square of the scale factor, and its volume by the cube.

Geometry

If two figures are similar, the scale factor from figure A to figure B is the ratio of any length in B to the corresponding length in A. A scale factor greater than 1 enlarges the figure; a factor between 0 and 1 reduces it; a factor of 1 leaves it congruent.

Cosmology

In cosmology, the term also refers to the scale factor a(t) of the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric, a dimensionless function of time that describes how distances in the expanding universe change relative to the present day; related is the concept of a parametric description of expansion.

Cartography

On maps, the scale factor relates distance on the map to distance on the ground, varying across a projection because a flat map cannot represent the curved Earth without distortion.
