Bias–variance tradeoff

[Image placeholder: Bias–variance bullseye diagrams]

In statistics and machine learning, the bias–variance tradeoff is the property of a model that the variance of the parameter estimated across samples can be reduced by increasing the bias in the estimated parameters. The expected prediction error of a learning algorithm can be decomposed into a bias term, a variance term, and irreducible noise.

Bias is the error from erroneous assumptions in the learning algorithm — high bias can cause underfitting. Variance is the error from sensitivity to small fluctuations in the training set — high variance can cause overfitting. Models of intermediate complexity usually generalize best.

Decomposition

For squared-error loss, E[(y − ŷ)²] = Bias(ŷ)² + Var(ŷ) + σ². The tradeoff motivates techniques such as cross-validation, regularization, bagging and model averaging. Recent work on overparameterized models and "double descent" has refined the classical picture, showing test error can fall again beyond the interpolation threshold.
