Roaming

A handset using a visited network outside its home coverage area

Roaming is a wireless telecommunication term typically used with mobile devices (such as mobile phones). It refers to the mobile phone being used outside the range of its native network and connecting to another available cell network. In more technical terms, roaming refers to the ability for a cellular customer to automatically make and receive voice calls, send and receive data, or access other services, including home data services, when travelling outside the geographical coverage area of the home network, by means of a visited network.

Process

When a mobile device is switched on in a visited network, it attempts to register using authentication credentials stored on the SIM card. The visited network contacts the subscriber's home location register to verify the subscriber's identity and authorizations. If successful, the visited network begins serving the device, and the home network is informed so that calls and messages can be routed correctly. Agreements between operators, coordinated through industry bodies such as the GSMA, govern the technical interconnection and the wholesale charges exchanged for this service.

Charges and regulation

Historically, roaming charges to end users were high, particularly for international travel and mobile data. The European Union progressively capped roaming surcharges from 2007 onwards and abolished them for travellers within the EU and EEA on 15 June 2017 under the "Roam Like at Home" rules, subject to fair-use limits. Other jurisdictions have introduced regional agreements or price caps of their own. Data roaming refers specifically to data services used while roaming; unexpected data charges gave rise to the term "bill shock" and to protective measures such as default spending cut-offs.

Types

Roaming may be domestic, within a subscriber's own country where the home operator lacks coverage, or international, across national borders. Inter-standard roaming allows movement between different radio technologies, and modern devices also roam between generations such as 4G and 5G.
