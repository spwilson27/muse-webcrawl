Gabriel (Hebrew: גַּבְרִיאֵל, Gaḇrīʾēl, "God is my strength"; Arabic: جِبْرِيل, Jibrīl) is an archangel and messenger of God in the Abrahamic religions. In the Hebrew Bible, Gabriel appears to the prophet Daniel to explain his visions. In the New Testament, he announces the births of John the Baptist and of Jesus — the latter event known as the Annunciation to the Virgin Mary.

In Islam, Gabriel (Jibril) is the angel who revealed the Quran to the prophet Muhammad over a period of 23 years, and is regarded as the chief of the angels.

In Judaism

Gabriel is named in the Book of Daniel, where he interprets Daniel's visions of the ram and the he-goat and delivers the prophecy of the seventy weeks. In later Jewish tradition he is one of the foremost archangels, associated with the element of fire and with the protection of Israel.

In Christianity

The Gospel of Luke recounts Gabriel's announcement to Zechariah that his wife Elizabeth would bear John the Baptist, and to Mary that she would conceive Jesus. Gabriel is venerated as a saint in Catholic, Orthodox and Anglican traditions and is the patron saint of messengers and communications workers. In some traditions he is the angel who will blow the trumpet announcing the Last Judgment.

In Islam

Jibril is believed to have dictated the Quran to Muhammad verse by verse, beginning with the first revelation in the cave of Hira in 610 CE. He is also identified with the "Holy Spirit" (Rūḥ al-Qudus) mentioned in the Quran. Tradition holds that Jibril accompanied Muhammad on the Night Journey (Isra and Mi'raj).
