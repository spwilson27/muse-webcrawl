Miꞌkmaq hieroglyphic writing

TypeLogographic / glyphic script
LanguagesMiꞌkmaq
Period17th–20th centuries
Also calledKomqwejwi'kasikl

Miꞌkmaq hieroglyphic writing (Miꞌkmaq: Komqwejwi'kasikl) is a writing system used by the Miꞌkmaq people of the Canadian Maritimes and the Gaspé Peninsula. It consists of glyphs traditionally inscribed on birch bark and other materials.

History

The French missionary Chrétien Le Clercq, working in the Gaspé in the 1670s, observed Miꞌkmaq children writing symbols on birch bark and adapted and expanded the glyphs into a system for teaching Catholic prayers and doctrine. In the 19th century the missionary Christian Kauder published Miꞌkmaq prayer books in the script in Vienna, and the system remained in use for religious texts into the 20th century.

Character of the script

Scholars debate to what extent the glyphs derive from pre-contact Indigenous symbols — petroglyphs in Nova Scotia include similar forms — and how much was systematised by missionaries. The script is largely logographic, with glyphs standing for words and concepts used to record prayers, hymns and the liturgical calendar.
