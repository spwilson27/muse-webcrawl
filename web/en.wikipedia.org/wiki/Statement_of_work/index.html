Statement of work

A typical statement of work document.

A statement of work (SOW) is a document routinely employed in the field of project management. It is the narrative description of a project's work requirement. It defines project-specific activities, deliverables and timelines for a vendor providing services to the client. The SOW is typically part of, or an annex to, a contract such as a master services agreement, and it forms a legally binding description of the work to be performed.

Contents

A statement of work usually includes:

  • Purpose and scope — the background of the project and a definition of the work in scope and out of scope
  • Deliverables — the specific products, reports, or results the vendor must produce
  • Schedule and milestones — deadlines for each deliverable and the overall period of performance
  • Standards and acceptance criteria — how the client will judge whether the work is complete and satisfactory
  • Place and period of performance — where the work is done and over what timeframe
  • Payment terms — pricing, invoicing schedule, and any assumptions on which pricing is based
  • Roles and responsibilities — obligations of the vendor and of the client, such as providing access, data, or approvals

Types

Three common types of SOW are recognized: the design/detail SOW, which tells the contractor exactly how to do the work; the level-of-effort/time-and-materials SOW, which engages effort over a period rather than a defined product; and the performance-based SOW, which states required outcomes and lets the contractor choose the methods. In U.S. government contracting, related documents include the statement of objectives (SOO) and the performance work statement (PWS), governed by the Federal Acquisition Regulation.

Importance

A well-written SOW minimizes disputes over scope creep, cost, and quality, because both parties agree in advance what will be delivered, when, and to what standard. Ambiguous statements of work are a frequent source of contract litigation.
