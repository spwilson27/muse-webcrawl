Samuel S. Wilks

Samuel Stanley Wilks
Born: June 17, 1906, Little Elm, Texas, U.S.
Died: March 7, 1964 (aged 57), Princeton, New Jersey
Known for: Wilks's lambda distribution; likelihood-ratio test asymptotics

Samuel Stanley Wilks (June 17, 1906 – March 7, 1964) was an American mathematician and academic who played an important role in the development of mathematical statistics, especially in regard to practical applications.

Biography

Wilks was born in Little Elm, Texas, and raised on a farm. He studied at North Texas State Teachers College and the University of Texas, and received his PhD from the University of Iowa in 1931 under Everett F. Lindquist. In 1933 he joined the faculty of Princeton University, where he remained for the rest of his career. He became editor of the Annals of Mathematical Statistics in 1938 and transformed it into a leading journal. During the Second World War he consulted for the Office of Naval Research and helped apply statistics to military problems.

Work

Wilks's research concerned multivariate statistics and likelihood methods. His name is attached to Wilks's lambda distribution, a probability distribution used in multivariate hypothesis testing, especially in relation to the likelihood-ratio test and multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA). He also proved that the likelihood ratio test statistic asymptotically follows a chi-squared distribution — a result widely used throughout applied statistics and sometimes called Wilks's theorem. At Princeton he built one of the strongest statistics groups in the United States and helped establish the field within American universities, mentoring figures such as John Tukey and Frederick Mosteller.

Legacy

The American Statistical Association's Wilks Memorial Award, established in his honour, is one of the discipline's distinguished prizes.
