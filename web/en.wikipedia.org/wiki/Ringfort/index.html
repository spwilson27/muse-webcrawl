Ringfort

Ringforts, also called ráth, lios, caiseal, cathair and dún in Irish sources, are circular fortified settlements built during the Bronze Age, Iron Age and especially the early medieval period in Ireland, Scotland, and Wales.

Description

A ringfort consists of a circular area, typically 20–60 metres across, enclosed by one or more earthen banks with external ditches (a rath) or by a stone wall (a cashel). Inside stood farm buildings and livestock enclosures. Over 45,000 sites have been identified in Ireland alone, making them the most common archaeological monument in the Irish landscape; most date from roughly 600–900 AD.

Function and folklore

Ringforts were primarily defended farmsteads of prosperous farmers rather than military fortresses. In Irish folklore they became known as "fairy forts", and superstitious respect for the sídhe is credited with preserving many of them from destruction. Comparable sites include raths in Wales and rounds in Cornwall.
