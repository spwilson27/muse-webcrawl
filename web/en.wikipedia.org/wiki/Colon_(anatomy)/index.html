Colon (anatomy)

Part ofLarge intestine
Length~1.5 m (adult human)
SectionsAscending, transverse, descending, sigmoid
PrecursorMidgut and hindgut
ArterySuperior and inferior mesenteric arteries

In human anatomy, the colon is the longest part of the large intestine, extending from the caecum to the rectum. Its main functions are to absorb water and electrolytes from indigestible food matter, to host the gut microbiota that ferment remaining material, and to compact and propel faeces toward the rectum. In an adult it is about 1.5 metres long.

Sections

The colon is conventionally divided into four sections. The ascending colon runs up the right side of the abdomen from the caecum; the transverse colon crosses the upper abdomen; the descending colon passes down the left side; and the S-shaped sigmoid colon leads to the rectum. The bends between sections are the hepatic (right colic) and splenic (left colic) flexures. Characteristic features include the taeniae coli (three longitudinal muscle bands), the haustra (sacculations) and fatty epiploic appendages.

Function and health

Each day the colon absorbs roughly a litre of water, together with sodium, potassium and short-chain fatty acids produced by bacterial fermentation of fibre. Disorders of the colon include colorectal cancer (among the commonest cancers worldwide), inflammatory bowel diseases (ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease), diverticular disease, irritable bowel syndrome and polyps; the organ is commonly examined by colonoscopy.
