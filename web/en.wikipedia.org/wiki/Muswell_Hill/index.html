Muswell Hill is a suburban district of north London, England, in the London Borough of Haringey, about 5.5 miles (8.9 km) north of Charing Cross. It is one of the most affluent suburbs in London, characterised by wide Edwardian streets, good schools and views over the city from its elevated position. The name is thought to derive from a "mossy well" — a spring or holy well once located in the area, near a medieval shrine of Our Lady of Muswell.

History

Muswell Hill remained rural until the late 19th century. In 1873 a railway station opened on the branch line from Highgate, and the opening of Alexandra Palace in the same year spurred development. Between 1896 and 1914 the area was transformed by planned Edwardian housing estates, giving Muswell Hill its distinctive red-brick character. The railway line closed to passengers in 1954, and much of its trackbed is now the Parkland Walk, a linear green trail between Finsbury Park and Alexandra Park.

Local features

Muswell Hill Broadway is the main shopping centre, noted for independent shops, cafés and the art-deco former Odeon cinema (now an Everyman). Nearby Alexandra Palace and its park host concerts, exhibitions and a farmers' market. The area is popular with families for its schools, including Fortismere School. The Kinks, the rock band formed by Muswell Hill brothers Ray and Dave Davies, commemorate the area in the album title Muswell Hillbillies (1971).

Transport

Muswell Hill has no Underground or National Rail station of its own; nearby stations include Highgate (Northern line), Bounds Green and Wood Green (Piccadilly line), and Alexandra Palace (National Rail). Numerous bus routes link the Broadway with central London and neighbouring districts. See also Haringey.
