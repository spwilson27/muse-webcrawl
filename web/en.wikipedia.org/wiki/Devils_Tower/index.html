Devils Tower is a butte, possibly laccolithic, composed of igneous rock in the Bear Lodge Ranger District of the Black Hills, near Hulett and Sundance in Crook County, northeastern Wyoming, above the Belle Fourche River. It rises dramatically 1,267 feet (386 m) above the river and is the first United States National Monument, established on 24 September 1906 by President Theodore Roosevelt.

Geology

The tower consists of striking columns of phonolite porphyry, formed when magma intruded into sedimentary rock some 50 million years ago and cooled. As the rock contracted, it fractured into mostly hexagonal columns — among the tallest and most regular examples of columnar jointing in the world. Erosion of the surrounding softer sedimentary layers has exposed the harder igneous core.

Indigenous significance and name

The tower is sacred to many Northern Plains tribes, including the Lakota, Cheyenne, Crow, Kiowa and Arapaho, whose names for it include "Bear's Lodge" and "Bear's Tipi". Tribal oral traditions commonly tell of girls fleeing a giant bear whose claws scored the tower's fluted sides. The name "Devils Tower" derives from an 1875 expedition interpreter's rendering of a native name as "Bad God's Tower", later shortened; proposals to rename it Bear Lodge have been advanced by tribal nations.

Climbing and popular culture

The tower was first climbed in 1893 by local ranchers using a wooden ladder, and is now a popular technical climbing destination, with a voluntary climbing closure in June out of respect for tribal ceremonies. It achieved worldwide fame as the mountain featured in Steven Spielberg's 1977 film Close Encounters of the Third Kind.
