Gagik I Artsruni (Armenian: Գագիկ Ա Արծրունի) was a prince and then the first king of the Armenian kingdom of Vaspurakan, south of Lake Van, ruling from 904 until his death in 937 (or 943 by some chronologies). A member of the Artsruni dynasty, he is remembered both for consolidating his kingdom between the rival powers of the Arabs, Byzantium, and the Bagratid kings of Ani, and for his celebrated building works.

Reign

Gagik succeeded his elder brother Ashot-Sargis, who died in 904. Having secured recognition from the Arab caliphate, he was crowned king in 908, a title granted through the Sajid emir Yusuf ibn Abi'l-Saj, even as Arab power in the region fluctuated. Gagik skilfully balanced relations with the Caliphate, the Byzantine Empire, and the Bagratid kingdom, and at the height of his power his influence extended over much of southern Armenia. He campaigned against rebellious vassals and Arab emirs of Azerbaijan, and was for a time the most powerful Armenian ruler.

Building works

Gagik's most famous commission is the Church of the Holy Cross on Aghtamar (Akdamar) Island in Lake Van, built between 915 and 921 by the architect-monk Manuel. The church is renowned for its elaborate exterior stone reliefs depicting biblical scenes, saints, and courtly figures — including a celebrated image of Gagik himself presenting a model of the church to Christ. He also founded the island city and palace complex on Aghtamar, described admiringly by the historian Thomas Artsruni, whose History of the House of the Artsrunik is the main source for the reign.
