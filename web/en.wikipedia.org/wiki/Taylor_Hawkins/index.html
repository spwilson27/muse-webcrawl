Oliver Taylor Hawkins (17 February 1972 – 25 March 2022) was an American musician, best known as the drummer of the rock band Foo Fighters, with whom he recorded eight studio albums. Before joining Foo Fighters in 1997, he toured with Alanis Morissette on her Jagged Little Pill tour. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with Foo Fighters in 2021.

Career

Raised in Laguna Beach, California, Hawkins played in local bands before joining singer Sass Jordan's band and then Morissette's touring band in 1995. In 1997 he replaced William Goldsmith in Foo Fighters, recruited by frontman Dave Grohl — himself a celebrated drummer — and formed one of rock's best-known frontman–drummer partnerships. Hawkins also sang lead on Foo Fighters songs including "Cold Day in the Sun", and led the side project Taylor Hawkins and the Coattail Riders, which released three albums. His drumming drew on influences including Roger Taylor of Queen, Stewart Copeland, and Neil Peart; he was voted "Best Rock Drummer" twice by Rhythm magazine readers.

Death and legacy

Hawkins died suddenly on 25 March 2022 in Bogotá, Colombia, where Foo Fighters were due to perform at the Estéreo Picnic Festival; he was 50. Colombian authorities reported that a toxicology screen found multiple substances, and no official cause of death was publicly announced. Tributes poured in from across the music world, and the band staged two all-star tribute concerts in London and Los Angeles in September 2022. He was succeeded in Foo Fighters by Josh Freese. Hawkins is remembered for his energetic, joyful style and powerful stage presence.
