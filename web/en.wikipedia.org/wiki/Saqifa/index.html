Saqifa

The Saqifa (Arabic: سقيفة بني ساعدة, Saqīfat Banī Sāʿida) was a courtyard or portico in Medina belonging to the Banu Sa'ida clan, famous as the place where, immediately after the death of the Islamic prophet Muhammad in 632 CE, a group of his companions pledged allegiance (bay'a) to Abu Bakr as the first caliph. The event is a central point of divergence between Sunni and Shia understandings of the succession to Muhammad.

The garden of the Saqifa in Medina, where Abu Bakr was acclaimed caliph in 632 CE.

The meeting

While Muhammad's close family, led by Ali ibn Abi Talib, were occupied with preparations for his burial, the Ansar (the Medinan helpers) gathered at the Saqifa to choose a leader of their own, proposing Sa'd ibn Ubada. Learning of the meeting, Abu Bakr, Umar, and Abu Ubayda hurried to the Saqifa. After heated debate — including proposals that there be two leaders, one from the Meccan emigrants (Muhajirun) and one from the Ansar — the assembly acclaimed Abu Bakr, arguing that the Arabs would accept leadership only from Muhammad's tribe, the Quraysh.

Aftermath and interpretations

A general pledge of allegiance followed the next day in the mosque. Sunni tradition regards the Saqifa as a legitimate consultative election that preserved the unity of the community. Shia tradition holds that Muhammad had designated Ali as his successor (notably at Ghadir Khumm) and views the Saqifa as a usurpation carried out while Ali and the Banu Hashim were absent; Ali is reported to have withheld his pledge for some months. The disagreement set the terms of disputes over the caliphate for centuries.
