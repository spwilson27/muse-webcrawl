Verdana

Verdana is a humanist sans-serif typeface designed by Matthew Carter for Microsoft Corporation, with hand-hinting done by Thomas Rickner. It was released in 1996 and has been included with Microsoft Windows and Office software, as well as being distributed as part of the "core fonts for the web" package.

Design

Verdana was designed specifically to be legible at small sizes on low-resolution computer screens. It features a large x-height, generous spacing between characters, wide proportions, and carefully distinguished letterforms — for example, the capital I is given serifs so it cannot be confused with the lowercase l or the numeral 1. The name derives from "verdant" (green), plus "Ana", the name of the designer's daughter.

Usage

Because of its wide availability on Windows and Macintosh systems, Verdana became one of the most commonly used fonts on websites. The Swedish furniture retailer IKEA drew widespread comment in 2009 when it changed its catalogue and logo typography from Futura to Verdana.
