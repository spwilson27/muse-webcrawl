Pamela Colman Smith

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pamela Colman Smith (16 February 1878 – 18 September 1951), nicknamed "Pixie", was a British artist, illustrator, writer, and occultist. She is best known as the illustrator of the Rider–Waite tarot deck (also called the Waite–Smith or Rider–Waite–Smith deck), the most popular tarot design in the world, created in collaboration with the mystic Arthur Edward Waite.

Early life

Smith was born in Pimlico, London, to American parents, and spent parts of her childhood in Manchester, Jamaica, and Brooklyn, New York. She studied art at the Pratt Institute in Brooklyn under Arthur Wesley Dow. Jamaica's folklore deeply influenced her; she published collections of Jamaican folk tales, Annancy Stories (1899), and performed them in costume. Her early illustration work included editions of works by W. B. Yeats and Bram Stoker.

Career

Back in London, Smith joined the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn in 1901, where she met Waite. In 1909 Waite commissioned her to illustrate a tarot deck; working in a matter of months for a modest fee, she produced 78 paintings whose fully illustrated Minor Arcana scenes were revolutionary — earlier decks showed only suit symbols on the pip cards. The deck, published by William Rider & Son in 1909, became the standard for English-language tarot, though Smith received little recognition or income from it during her lifetime.

Smith also co-edited the suffragist journal The Green Sheaf, ran a small press, campaigned for women's suffrage, and later converted to Catholicism. She spent her final years in Bude, Cornwall, and died in relative poverty in 1951.

Legacy

Modern reissues of the deck increasingly credit her as co-creator — the "Rider–Waite–Smith" deck — and her art nouveau-inflected imagery defines the visual language of tarot for most of the world.
