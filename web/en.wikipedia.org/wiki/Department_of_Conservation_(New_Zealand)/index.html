Department of Conservation (New Zealand)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Department of Conservation (DOC; Māori: Te Papa Atawhai) is the public service department of New Zealand charged with the conservation of New Zealand's natural and historical heritage. It was formed on 1 April 1987 under the Conservation Act 1987, bringing together functions from the former Forest Service, the Department of Lands and Survey, and the Wildlife Service.

Role

DOC administers most of New Zealand's public conservation land — roughly 8.7 million hectares, about a third of the country's land area — including 13 national parks, numerous conservation and forest parks, nature and scientific reserves, and over 40 marine reserves. Its work includes protecting native species, controlling introduced pests such as possums, stoats and rats, maintaining thousands of kilometres of tracks and more than 900 huts, and managing visitor facilities used by walkers on routes such as the Great Walks.

Species protection

The department runs recovery programmes for threatened species, including the kākāpō recovery programme, the protection of kiwi, whio (blue duck) and takahē, and pest-free offshore island sanctuaries. The Predator Free 2050 initiative, launched in 2016, aims to eliminate the most damaging introduced predators nationwide.

Structure and incidents

DOC's head office is in Wellington, with regional offices and ranger stations across the country. In 1995 the Cave Creek disaster — the collapse of a viewing platform built by the department in Paparoa National Park, killing 14 people — led to a commission of inquiry and significant changes to the department's systems and funding.
