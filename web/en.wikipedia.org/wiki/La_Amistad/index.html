La Amistad
Type: Spanish schooner
Event: Slave revolt, July 1839
US Supreme Court case: United States v. The Amistad (1841)

La Amistad was a 19th-century Spanish schooner famous for a revolt by enslaved Mende captives in July 1839 and the landmark legal case that followed in the United States.

Revolt

Fifty-three Africans, illegally kidnapped in Sierra Leone and shipped via Cuba, rose under Sengbe Pieh (Joseph Cinqué), killed the captain and cook, and ordered the surviving Spaniards to sail for Africa. Deceived, the ship zigzagged to Long Island, where the US Navy seized it.

Case

Abolitionists took up the Africans' defence; former president John Quincy Adams argued before the Supreme Court, which ruled in 1841 that the captives had been illegally enslaved and were free. Thirty-five survivors returned to Sierra Leone in 1842. The story was dramatised in Steven Spielberg's film Amistad (1997). See Slavery in the United States.
