Bombing of Dresden in World War II

Bombing of Dresden
Date: 13–15 February 1945
Location: Dresden, Germany
Result: Firestorm, destruction of city centre
Casualties: 22,700–25,000 killed

The bombing of Dresden was a joint British and American aerial bombing attack on Dresden, the capital of Saxony, during World War II. In four raids between 13 and 15 February 1945, 722 heavy bombers of the Royal Air Force and 527 of the United States Army Air Forces dropped more than 3,900 tons of high-explosive bombs and incendiary devices.

Background

Dresden was a major railway and communications centre and housed more than 100 factories and enterprises. By early 1945 Germany's military situation was desperate, with Soviet forces advancing from the east.

The raids

The first RAF raid created a firestorm that destroyed much of the historic city centre. Follow-up raids targeted railway facilities. Temperatures in the firestorm reached up to 1,500 °C.

Casualties and controversy

Estimates place deaths at 22,700 to 25,000. The bombing remains controversial, with debates over military necessity and whether it constituted a war crime. Nazi propaganda inflated figures, and the event was later used in Cold War politics.
