Association football culture

Supporters' displays are a central part of football culture

Association football culture is the set of social practices, rituals, identities and expressions that surround the sport of association football (soccer). With billions of followers, football is woven into local and national identity in much of the world, and its culture is expressed through fandom, chants and songs, clothing, literature, film, art and media coverage.

Supporter culture

Supporters organise around clubs and national teams, travelling to away matches, producing banners and coordinated displays known as tifo, and singing songs that are often adapted from popular music. Organised groups — called ultras in much of Europe and Latin America, barras bravas in Argentina, and torcidas in Brazil — emphasise visual spectacle and constant vocal support, but have sometimes been linked to violence, including the hooliganism that was prominent in England in the 1970s and 1980s. Rivalries, especially local derbies, concentrate these identities most intensely.

Football in the arts and media

Football has inspired novels such as Nick Hornby's Fever Pitch, films, music and visual art, and figures prominently in the work of writers who treated the game as a lens on class, nationhood and masculinity. Kits and casual fashion, sticker albums, fanzines, video games such as the FIFA/EA FC and Football Manager series, and global television audiences for events like the FIFA World Cup all extend the culture far beyond matchday.

Social role

Clubs frequently originated in churches, workplaces and immigrant communities, and football remains a vehicle for expressing regional, religious and political identities — as in the rivalry between Celtic and Rangers in Glasgow or El Clásico in Spain — as well as campaigns against racism and for social causes.
