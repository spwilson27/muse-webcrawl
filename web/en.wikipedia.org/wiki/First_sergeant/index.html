First sergeant

First sergeant is typically a senior non-commissioned officer rank, used in many countries. In the United States Army, first sergeant (abbreviated 1SG) is an E-8 rank above sergeant first class and below sergeant major. It is equal in grade to master sergeant, although the two ranks have different responsibilities.

A first sergeant is generally senior to the platoon leaders and is often referred to as the "first shirt" or "top". When a soldier is appointed to the rank of first sergeant, he or she is usually assigned to a company-sized unit and serves as the senior enlisted adviser to the company commander. The first sergeant is responsible for the morale, welfare, and conduct of all enlisted members of the company and is the chief adviser to the commander on enlisted matters.

United States

In the U.S. Army, a first sergeant's distinguishing insignia is a lozenge (colloquially known as a "diamond") between the chevrons and the rockers. In the United States Marine Corps, first sergeant is also an E-8 rank, distinguished by cross-rifles in the center of the chevrons, and serves as the senior enlisted advisor in a company or battery.

A first sergeant in the United States Space Force is known as a "first shirt" and performs similar duties.

Other countries

The rank of first sergeant, or equivalents, exists in the armed forces of many nations, including Singapore, Brazil (primeiro-sargento), and Israel. In some forces the title is an appointment rather than a rank, held by the senior non-commissioned officer of a sub-unit.
