Sun Ra (born Herman Poole Blount, 22 May 1914 – 30 May 1993) was an American jazz composer, bandleader, pianist, poet and philosopher known for his experimental music, "cosmic philosophy", prolific output and theatrical performances. He led The Arkestra, an ensemble with an ever-changing name and flexible line-up, for more than five decades, and claimed — as part of a carefully constructed mythology — to have been born on the planet Saturn and transported to Earth to preach peace through music.

Life and career

Blount grew up in Birmingham, Alabama, played in territory bands, and after a period as a conscientious objector during World War II settled in Chicago, where he worked as an arranger for Fletcher Henderson. In 1952 he legally changed his name to Le Sony'r Ra, shortened to Sun Ra. In Chicago he formed the Arkestra, studied Egyptian history and esoteric philosophy, and founded Saturn Records — one of the first artist-owned independent labels — issuing dozens of albums, often with hand-made covers.

Moving to New York in 1961 and then to Philadelphia in 1968, the Arkestra became a communal household that rehearsed daily and performed in elaborate Egyptian- and space-themed costumes. Sun Ra was among the first jazz musicians to embrace electronic keyboards and synthesizers, and his music ranged across the whole history of jazz — from ragtime, swing and bebop to free improvisation and dissonant "space chants" such as "Space Is the Place".

Legacy

Sun Ra recorded over one hundred albums and composed thousands of works. His blend of Afrofuturism, pageantry and musical freedom influenced generations of musicians from John Coltrane (whose saxophonist John Gilmore was a long-serving Arkestra member) to Funkadelic and contemporary experimental artists. After his death in 1993 — following a return "to Saturn", in the band's phrase — the Arkestra continued under saxophonist Marshall Allen.
