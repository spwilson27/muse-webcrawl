Humphrey (c. 1988 – March 2006) was a cat employed as the Chief Mouser to the Cabinet Office at 10 Downing Street, the official residence of the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, from October 1989 to 13 November 1997. He served under three prime ministers: Margaret Thatcher, John Major, and the first six months of Tony Blair's premiership.

Humphrey served as Chief Mouser at Downing Street for eight years.

Background

Humphrey was a long-haired black-and-white cat who arrived at Downing Street as a one-year-old stray, having been found by a Cabinet Office civil servant. He was named after Humphrey Appleby, the archetypal civil servant in the television comedies Yes Minister and Yes Prime Minister. He succeeded Wilberforce, who had died in 1988.

Career

Humphrey's duties as Chief Mouser were supported by an allowance of £100 a year from the Cabinet Office budget, spent mainly on food; it was often noted that this was far better value than the professional pest-controller retained by the Cabinet Office. In June 1995 Humphrey went missing and was presumed dead — his obituary was even prepared — but he was later found alive at the Royal Army Medical College, where he had apparently been taken in as a stray, and returned to Downing Street amid considerable press coverage.

Retirement and death

After the Blairs moved into Downing Street in 1997, reports circulated that Cherie Blair disliked the cat; photographs of her holding Humphrey were issued to counter the story. In November 1997 Humphrey was retired, officially on veterinary advice because of a kidney complaint, and went to live with a Cabinet Office official in the suburbs. His retirement was announced in the House of Commons. Humphrey died in March 2006, a fact disclosed by a Downing Street spokesman.
