Sergei Prokofiev

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Sergei Prokofiev at the piano in the 1920s: slicked hair, sharp suit, playing his own percussive music with steely fingers]

Sergei Sergeyevich Prokofiev (27 April 1891 – 5 March 1953) was a Russian composer and pianist. As the creator of many acknowledged masterpieces across numerous music genres, he is regarded as one of the major composers of the 20th century. His works include such widely heard pieces as the March from The Love for Three Oranges, the suite Lieutenant Kijé, the ballet Romeo and Juliet — from which 'Dance of the Knights' is taken — and Peter and the Wolf.

An enfant terrible of the St Petersburg Conservatory, Prokofiev spent 1918–36 in the United States and Paris, then returned permanently to the Soviet Union in one of music history's most debated decisions. There he produced the film scores for Eisenstein's Alexander Nevsky (1938) and Ivan the Terrible, the Fifth Symphony and the opera War and Peace — and, like Shostakovich, was condemned in 1948. He died on the same day as Stalin, his death eclipsed and his funeral flowers unavailable in Moscow.
