Wawel Dragon

TypeLegendary dragon
LocationWawel Hill, Kraków, Poland
AdversaryKing Krakus / the shoemaker Skuba

The Wawel Dragon (Polish: Smok Wawelski) is a famous dragon in Polish folklore, said to have lived in a cave beneath Wawel Hill on the banks of the Vistula River in Kraków.

The legend

According to the most popular version, recorded by Wincenty Kadłubek, the dragon terrorised the countryside during the reign of the legendary King Krakus, demanding offerings of cattle — and in later retellings, of young maidens. Knights failed to defeat it, but a clever shoemaker's apprentice named Skuba fed it a sheep stuffed with sulfur and tar. The dragon, burning with thirst, drank from the Vistula until it burst. Krakus rewarded the apprentice and, in some versions, gave him his daughter's hand in marriage.

Legacy

The Dragon's Den cave below Wawel Castle is a tourist attraction, and a metal statue of the dragon, designed by Bronisław Chromy in 1972, stands by the river and periodically breathes real fire. The dragon is one of the best-known symbols of Kraków.
