The Kingdom of the Isles, also known as the Kingdom of Man and the Isles (and in Norse sources as Suðreyjar, the "Southern Isles"), was a Norse-Gaelic kingdom encompassing the Hebrides, the Isle of Man, and at times parts of the western Scottish mainland, from the Viking Age until its cession to Scotland in 1266.

Origins

Viking raiders and settlers seized the Hebrides and Man in the 9th century; the islands formed part of the Scandinavian maritime world, nominally subject at various times to the kings of Norway or Dublin. Local dynasties of mixed Norse and Gaelic descent emerged, recorded in the Irish annals, the Orkneyinga saga, and the Chronicle of Mann. In the 11th century the kingdom was linked with the Kingdom of Dublin under rulers such as Echmarcach mac Ragnaill.

Godred Crovan and the Manx dynasty

After the Battle of Stamford Bridge (1066), the Norse-Gael Godred Crovan conquered Man (1079) and the Hebrides, founding a dynasty that ruled, with interruptions and Norwegian intervention—including Magnus Barelegs' expedition of 1098–1103—through the 12th century. In the 1150s–60s Somerled, a Gaelic-Norse lord, seized the southern Hebrides and Man, defeating Godred the Black at the Battle of Epiphany (1156); after Somerled's death (1164) his descendants (the Lords of the Isles) held the south while the Crovan dynasty kept Man and the northern isles.

End: Largs and the Treaty of Perth

King Haakon IV of Norway's expedition of 1263 to reassert control ended inconclusively at the Battle of Largs and with his death in Orkney. By the Treaty of Perth (1266), Norway ceded Man and the Hebrides to Alexander III of Scotland for a payment of 4,000 marks and an annuity. Magnus Olafsson, the last king of Man, submitted, and the kingdom was absorbed into Scotland, though the Lordship of the Isles preserved much of its Norse-Gaelic character for two more centuries.
