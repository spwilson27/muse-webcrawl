Varyag (aircraft carrier)

Career

Launched:25 November 1988
Renamed:Varyag (1990)

Varyag was a Soviet aircraft carrier of the Admiral Kuznetsov class, laid down at the Nikolayev South shipyard in Ukraine in 1985. Construction halted with the dissolution of the Soviet Union, when the ship was about 68 percent complete. After years of negotiation the unfinished hull was sold by Ukraine to China in 1998, ostensibly for conversion into a floating casino, and after a long and eventful tow to Dalian it was rebuilt by the People's Liberation Army Navy and commissioned in 2012 as the Liaoning, China's first aircraft carrier.

Construction and sale

Laid down as Riga and renamed Varyag in 1990, the carrier became Ukrainian property in 1992 after work stopped for lack of funds. In 1998 the Macau-based Chong Lot Travel Agency won the auction for the hull with a bid of $20 million, stating it would become a floating hotel and casino. The tow to China became an epic in itself: Turkey refused passage through the Bosphorus for over a year on safety grounds while the engineless hull circled the Black Sea, and the ship later broke free of its tugs in a storm in the Aegean, in which a sailor was killed, before finally being allowed through the straits in late 2001 and arriving at Dalian in March 2002.

Refit as Liaoning

Contrary to the casino plan, the ship was extensively refitted at Dalian Shipyard with new sensors, weapons and flight-deck systems. It began sea trials in August 2011 and was commissioned into the People's Liberation Army Navy on 25 September 2012 as Liaoning (hull number 16), serving primarily as a training and development carrier that paved the way for China's indigenously built carriers.
