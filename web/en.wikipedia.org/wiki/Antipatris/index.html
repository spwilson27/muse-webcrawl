Antipatris
LocationTel Afek, near Rosh HaAyin, Israel
Foundedc. 35 BC, by Herod the Great

Antipatris was an ancient city built by Herod the Great on the coastal plain of Judaea, at the site now known as Tel Afek, near the springs of the Yarkon River in modern Israel. Herod named it in honour of his father, Antipater the Idumaean.

History

Founded around 35 BC on the site of the earlier settlement of Aphek, Antipatris stood on the Via Maris trade route between Egypt and Syria. It is mentioned in the New Testament: the Apostle Paul was escorted by Roman soldiers as far as Antipatris on his journey from Jerusalem to Caesarea (Acts 23).

Archaeology

Excavations at Tel Afek have revealed the city's Roman street grid and later remains, including an Ottoman fortress. The site is now part of Yarkon National Park.
