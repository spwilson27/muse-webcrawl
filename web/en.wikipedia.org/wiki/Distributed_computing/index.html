Distributed computing

Distributed computing is a field of computer science that studies distributed systems, defined as systems whose components are located on networked computers, which communicate and coordinate their actions by passing messages. The components interact in order to achieve a common goal.

Distributed computing
Field: Computer science
Examples: Internet, cloud computing, peer-to-peer networks

Characteristics

Three significant challenges are concurrency of components, the lack of a global clock, and the independent failure of components (partial failure). Examples include telecommunications networks, the World Wide Web, multiplayer games, distributed databases, and volunteer computing projects such as SETI@home and Folding@home.

Architectures

  • Client–server
  • Peer-to-peer
  • Three-tier and n-tier
  • Clusters, grids and cloud computing
  • Microservices and edge computing

Key problems

Consensus (e.g. Paxos and Raft), distributed transactions, CAP theorem trade-offs between consistency, availability and partition tolerance, leader election, mutual exclusion, and Byzantine fault tolerance — central to blockchain systems.

History

The field grew from 1970s networking (ARPANET, Ethernet), through 1980s distributed operating systems and RPC, to web-scale systems at Google and Amazon in the 2000s, and modern cloud-native platforms orchestrated by systems such as Kubernetes.
