Kuomintang

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Kuomintang (KMT), also romanised as Guomindang, is a major political party in Taiwan and was the ruling party of the Republic of China on the mainland before 1949. Founded in its modern form by Sun Yat-sen in 1919 (from his earlier revolutionary organisations), it governed China through the Nanjing decade, the war against Japan and the resumed civil war, until its defeat by the Communists and retreat to Taiwan in 1949.

Under Chiang Kai-shek the KMT ruled Taiwan as a one-party authoritarian state under martial law (1949–1987), implementing land reform and export-led development while suppressing dissent in the White Terror. After democratisation, the KMT became one of Taiwan's two major parties, alternating in power with the Democratic Progressive Party; it generally favours closer economic ties with the mainland and the “1992 Consensus” formula on cross-strait relations, holding that there is one China with differing interpretations — a formula the DPP rejects in that form.

History

Sun Yat-sen's Three Principles of the People — nationalism, democracy and people's livelihood — remain the party's doctrine. The First United Front with the young CCP (1924–27) collapsed in the Shanghai massacre of 1927; the Northern Expedition (1926–28) nominally reunified China. Corruption, inflation and military failure in the civil war (1946–49) destroyed KMT rule on the mainland: an estimated one to two million soldiers, officials and refugees followed the government to Taiwan.

Organisation

The KMT is historically a Leninist-style party — Sun reorganised it in 1924 with Soviet advice — with a chairman, central committee and extensive local branches, and it long owned a large business empire whose assets have been subject to ill-gotten-party-assets legislation in Taiwan. Its pan-blue coalition identity contrasts with the pan-green camp around the DPP.
