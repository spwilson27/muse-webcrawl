Tōdai-ji (東大寺, "Great Eastern Temple") is a Buddhist temple complex in the city of Nara, Japan. Its Great Buddha Hall (Daibutsuden) houses the world's largest bronze statue of the Buddha Vairocana, known in Japanese as the Daibutsu.

History

Emperor Shōmu ordered the temple's construction amid epidemics and unrest, envisioning a network of provincial temples with Tōdai-ji at its head. The Great Buddha was dedicated in 752 in a lavish ceremony attended by monks from across Asia. The temple became the center of the Kegon school in Japan.

Architecture

The present Daibutsuden dates largely to a 1709 reconstruction and is about two-thirds the size of the original, yet remains one of the largest wooden structures in the world. The bronze Daibutsu stands some 15 metres tall. The precinct's Shōsōin storehouse preserves 8th-century imperial treasures from along the Silk Road.

Deer of Nara

The temple grounds adjoin Nara Park, home to hundreds of free-roaming sika deer regarded as sacred messengers of the gods.
