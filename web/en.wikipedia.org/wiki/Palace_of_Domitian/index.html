Palace of Domitian

The Palace of Domitian (Latin: Domus Augustana, with the adjacent Domus Flavia) was the imperial palace on the Palatine Hill in Rome, built for the emperor Domitian and substantially completed by 92 CE. Designed by the architect Rabirius, it became the official residence of the Roman emperors for more than three centuries; the word "palace" itself derives from the hill's name, Palatium.

Layout

The complex combined two functions. The Domus Flavia was the official, representational wing, organised around a vast peristyle and containing the throne room (Aula Regia), a basilica for legal audiences, the Lararium, and the enormous state dining room (Cenatio Iovis). The Domus Augustana was the emperor's private residence, a multi-storey complex built around sunken garden courtyards, its southern rooms curving around a lower peristyle garden. Adjacent stood a stadium-shaped garden, sometimes called the Hippodrome, and the Septizodium façade faced the Circus Maximus.

History

Domitian's palace replaced and buried earlier residences, including Nero's Domus Transitoria and parts of the Domus Aurea, as well as Republican-era houses still visible in excavation areas. Later emperors extended and remodelled the complex, down to Septimius Severus, who added baths and the Septizodium in 203 CE. The palace remained the emperors' Roman residence until late antiquity, and parts were occupied into the medieval period.

Excavation

Systematic excavation began in the 18th and 19th centuries; the ruins today form part of the open-air archaeological park of the Palatine, visited in combination with the Roman Forum and Colosseum.
