The University of Louisiana at Lafayette (UL Lafayette, ULL, or simply Louisiana) is a public research university in Lafayette, Louisiana, in the heart of the French-speaking Acadiana region. It is the largest campus of the University of Louisiana System and the second-largest university in the state. It achieved R1 "very high research activity" status in 2021.

Founded by an act of the Louisiana legislature in 1898 as the Southwestern Louisiana Industrial Institute, it opened in 1900 and became Southwestern Louisiana Institute, then the University of Southwestern Louisiana in 1960. It took its present name in 1999. The university was an early pioneer in computing — it established one of the first computer science master's programs in the United States in the 1960s and issued the first computer science PhD in the state.

Campus and culture

The main campus is noted for its live-oak trees, red-brick buildings, and Cypress Lake, a managed swamp habitat in the middle of campus inhabited by alligators, turtles, and herons. The university houses the Center for Louisiana Studies and the Archives of Cajun and Creole folklore, reflecting its location in Cajun country. Its athletic teams, the Ragin' Cajuns, compete in the Sun Belt Conference.
