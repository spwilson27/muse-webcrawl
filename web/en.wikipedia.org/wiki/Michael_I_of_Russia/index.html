Michael I (Russian: Михаил I; 22 July 1596 – 23 July 1645) was the first Russian tsar of the House of Romanov, reigning from 1613 until his death. Elected by the Zemsky Sobor at the age of sixteen to end the Time of Troubles, he founded the dynasty that ruled Russia until the February Revolution of 1917.

Election

Michael was the son of the boyar Fyodor Nikitich Romanov — later Patriarch Filaret — and a great-nephew by marriage of Ivan the Terrible's wife Anastasia Romanovna, the dynasty's original link to the throne. After the expulsion of Polish forces from Moscow in 1612, the national assembly (Zemsky Sobor) convened in early 1613 and, rejecting foreign candidates, elected Michael on 21 February 1613. A delegation famously found the reluctant youth living with his mother, the nun Martha, at the Ipatiev Monastery near Kostroma.

Reign

The first task was to end foreign wars: the Treaty of Stolbovo (1617) settled with Sweden, though Ingria was ceded; the Truce of Deulino (1618) ended the war with Poland-Lithuania, which retained Smolensk. Michael's father Filaret, released from Polish captivity in 1619 and made Patriarch, became effective co-ruler as "Great Sovereign" until his death in 1633. The reign saw restoration of central administration, tax reform, military modernisation with foreign officers, and a renewed but unsuccessful attempt to recover Smolensk in the Smolensk War of 1632–34. Russia expanded steadily into Siberia; Yakutsk was founded in 1632 and Russian explorers reached the Pacific coast by 1639.

Family and succession

After a first marriage to Maria Dolgorukova, who died in 1625, Michael married Eudoxia Streshneva in 1626; their son Alexis succeeded him. Michael died on the night of 12–13 July (O.S.) 1645, the day after his 49th birthday by the Julian reckoning used in the infobox above (23 July N.S.).
