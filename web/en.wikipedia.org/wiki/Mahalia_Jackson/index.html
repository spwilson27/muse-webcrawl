Mahalia Jackson (born Mahala Jackson; October 26, 1911 – January 27, 1972) was an American gospel singer, widely considered one of the most influential vocalists of the 20th century. With a career spanning 40 years, she was integral to the development and spread of gospel blues throughout black churches in the United States, and her powerful contralto voice earned her the title "The Queen of Gospel".

Life and career

Born into a poor family in New Orleans, Jackson began singing in church as a child and moved to Chicago in 1927 as part of the Great Migration. She sang with the Johnson Gospel Singers and first recorded in 1937, but success came after her 1947 recording of "Move On Up a Little Higher", which sold millions of copies and made her internationally known. She recorded about 30 albums, mostly for Columbia Records, and appeared in films and on television, including a performance before Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" speech at the 1963 March on Washington, where — at King's request — she sang "I've Been 'Buked and I've Been Scorned".

Legacy

Jackson influenced singers including Aretha Franklin, and her recordings were foundational to the popularisation of gospel music. She was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997 in the Early Influence category and received a posthumous Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2021. The Mahalia Jackson Theater in New Orleans is named in her honour.
