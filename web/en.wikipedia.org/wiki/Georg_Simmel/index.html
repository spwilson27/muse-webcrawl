Georg Simmel

Born: 1 March 1858, Berlin, Germany

Died: 26 September 1918, Strasbourg

Georg Simmel was a German sociologist and philosopher, a founding figure of sociology known for formal sociology — the study of recurring forms of social interaction (sociation) such as conflict, exchange, domination and sociability, regardless of content.

Works

The Philosophy of Money (1900) analysed money's effects on relationships, value and modern life. His essay "The Metropolis and Mental Life" (1903) described the blasé attitude of city dwellers bombarded by stimuli. Other famous essays treat the stranger, secrecy and secret societies, fashion, adventure, and the dyad and triad — showing how group size changes interaction.

Influence

Simmel influenced the Chicago School, social theory, network analysis and cultural sociology, despite a marginal academic position during his lifetime, partly due to antisemitism. His essayistic style influenced the sociology of art and aesthetics.
