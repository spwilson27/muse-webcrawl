Pomerania

Pomerania (Polish: Pomorze; German: Pommern; Kashubian: Pòmòrskô) is a historical region on the southern shore of the Baltic Sea in Central Europe, split between Poland and Germany. Its name derives from the Slavic po more, meaning "land by the sea." The region stretches roughly from the Recknitz and Peene rivers in the west, through the Oder estuary and Szczecin Lagoon, to the Vistula delta in the east. Major cities include Szczecin (Stettin), Gdańsk (Danzig), Koszalin (Köslin), Słupsk (Stolp), Greifswald, and Stralsund.

History

After earlier settlement by Germanic tribes, Slavic peoples settled the coast from the sixth century CE; the Pomeranians were among the West Slavic Lechitic tribes. From the late tenth century, the Polish Piast rulers sought to incorporate the region, while the mission of Otto of Bamberg in the 1120s Christianised it. The Duchy of Pomerania, ruled by the native Griffin dynasty from the twelfth century until 1637, became part of the Holy Roman Empire even as German settlement (the Ostsiedlung) transformed its towns and countryside. Ports such as Stralsund and Stettin joined the Hanseatic League.

After the Griffins died out during the Thirty Years' War, Pomerania was divided between Sweden (Swedish Pomerania) and Brandenburg-Prussia. Prussia gradually acquired the whole region, completing the process with the Congress of Vienna in 1815, and Pomerania became a province of Prussia and later of the German Empire. After the First World War, most of eastern Pomerania remained German, while the "Polish Corridor" gave Poland access to the sea; the Free City of Danzig stood between.

Following the Second World War, the Potsdam Agreement placed all Pomerania east of the Oder–Neisse line under Polish administration (confirmed as Polish territory in later treaties). The German population was expelled westward, and the region was repopulated by Poles, including many displaced from Poland's former eastern territories. Western Pomerania (Vorpommern) remained in Germany and today forms part of the state of Mecklenburg–Western Pomerania (Mecklenburg-Vorpommern), while the Polish part is divided among the West Pomeranian, Pomeranian, and (partly) Kuyavian-Pomeranian voivodeships.

Geography and culture

The landscape is a young glacial lowland of moraine hills, lakes (the Pomeranian Lake District), lagoons, and sandy Baltic beaches; the islands of Usedom, Wolin, and Rügen (just west of historical Pomerania) are noted resorts. The region's heritage includes brick Gothic churches and castles, amber working, and the Kashubian language, still spoken in the east. The Pomeranian dog breed (the Pom), a small spitz, takes its name from the region.
