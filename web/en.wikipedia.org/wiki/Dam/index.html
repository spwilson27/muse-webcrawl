Dam

[Image: A large concrete gravity dam holding back a reservoir]

Overview

A dam is a barrier that stops or restricts the flow of surface water or underground streams. Reservoirs created by dams not only suppress floods but also provide water for activities such as irrigation, human consumption, industrial use, aquaculture and navigability. Hydropower is often used in conjunction with dams to generate electricity.

Types and issues

Dams are classified by structure (gravity, arch, embankment, buttress) and purpose (storage, diversion, detention, tailings). The tallest dams exceed 300 m (Nurek, Tajikistan). Dams transform rivers — trapping sediment, blocking fish migration, displacing communities (the Three Gorges project displaced over a million people) — and can fail catastrophically (Banqiao, 1975; Vajont, 1963, overtopped by a landslide wave). An ageing-dam removal movement, mainly in North America and Europe, restores free-flowing rivers (the Elwha and Klamath removals).
