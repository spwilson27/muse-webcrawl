Interactive geometry software

Interactive geometry software (IGS), also called dynamic geometry software, allows users to create and manipulate geometric constructions interactively. Points, lines, circles, and other objects can be dragged with the mouse while the software maintains the geometric relationships — such as parallelism, perpendicularity, and incidence — that were used to construct them.

History

Early influential programs included The Geometer's Sketchpad and Cabri Geometry, developed in the 1980s and 1990s, which brought dynamic geometry into school mathematics. Later software such as GeoGebra combined geometry with algebra and calculus and spread widely in education, while Cinderella and the open-source Kig offered advanced features including automated theorem checking.

Features

Typical features include construction tools following compass-and-straightedge rules, measurement of lengths and angles, loci, transformations, macros for custom tools, and scripting. Because constructions update continuously as points move, students can explore conjectures and invariant properties experimentally.

Use in education

Interactive geometry is used from primary school through university mathematics and in teacher training, supporting inquiry-based learning and visualization of abstract concepts.
