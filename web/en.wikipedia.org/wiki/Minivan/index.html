Minivan

A minivan is a personal-use vehicle in an American car classification for vehicles built on the chassis of a small van or based on a passenger car platform, designed to maximise interior volume. Minivans typically have a one- or two-box design, seating for six to eight passengers in three rows, sliding rear doors and versatile fold-flat seating. In Europe similar vehicles are classified as MPVs (multi-purpose vehicles).

History

Precursors include the Volkswagen Type 2 and various station wagons, but the modern minivan segment was created by the 1984 Plymouth Voyager and Dodge Caravan, introduced by Chrysler under Lee Iacocca. Built on a front-wheel-drive car platform, they fit in a standard garage while carrying seven passengers, and they rapidly displaced the traditional station wagon as the North American family vehicle. The Renault Espace, launched the same year, pioneered the European MPV.

Decline and survivals

From the 2000s, minivans lost market share to SUVs and crossovers, and most manufacturers left the segment. Surviving models include the Chrysler Pacifica, Honda Odyssey, Toyota Sienna and Kia Carnival, often marketed with family features such as rear-seat entertainment, built-in vacuum cleaners and hybrid or all-wheel-drive options.
