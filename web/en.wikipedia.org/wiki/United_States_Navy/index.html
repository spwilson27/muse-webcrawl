United States Navy

The United States Navy (USN) is the maritime service branch of the United States Armed Forces and one of the eight uniformed services of the United States. It is the largest and most powerful navy in the world, with the estimated tonnage of its active battle fleet alone exceeding the next 13 navies combined, including 11 allies or partner nations of the United States. It is also the world's largest carrier fleet, with 11 in service and one under construction. The U.S. Navy has the world's largest aircraft carrier fleet and over 300,000 personnel on active duty and nearly 100,000 in the Ready Reserve.

History

Origins

The Continental Congress established the Continental Navy on 13 October 1775, purchasing two armed vessels to intercept British supply shipping during the American Revolutionary War. Captain John Paul Jones became one of its early heroes. After the war the young republic disbanded the navy, but attacks on American merchant shipping by Barbary corsairs led Congress to pass the Naval Act of 1794, authorising six frigates — including USS Constitution, which remains in commission today as the world's oldest commissioned naval vessel afloat.

19th century

The navy fought in the Quasi-War with France, the Barbary Wars, and the War of 1812, where American frigates won celebrated single-ship actions. During the American Civil War the navy blockaded the Confederacy and fought in the first clash of ironclads at Hampton Roads in 1862. Under Alfred Thayer Mahan's influential doctrine of sea power, the United States built a modern steel fleet, demonstrated by the Great White Fleet's circumnavigation of 1907–1909.

World Wars

In the Second World War the navy grew into a two-ocean force. After the attack on Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941), carrier aviation became decisive in the Pacific at the battles of Coral Sea, Midway, and Leyte Gulf, while amphibious forces supported landings from North Africa to Normandy and Okinawa. By 1945 the U.S. Navy was the largest navy in history.

Cold War and beyond

During the Cold War the navy pioneered nuclear propulsion under Admiral Hyman G. Rickover; USS Nautilus (1954) was the first nuclear-powered submarine, and ballistic-missile submarines became a leg of the nuclear triad. The navy has since maintained a forward presence worldwide, operating carrier strike groups and participating in conflicts from Korea and Vietnam to the Gulf War and operations in Afghanistan and Iraq.

Organisation

The navy is administered by the Department of the Navy, headed by the Secretary of the Navy; operational command runs through the Chief of Naval Operations, the service's senior officer. Major commands include the numbered fleets (Second through Seventh Fleets), Naval Air Forces, Naval Surface Forces, and Submarine Forces. Personnel include commissioned officers, chief petty officers, and enlisted sailors, organised by ratings (occupational specialties).

Equipment

Principal ship types include Nimitz- and Gerald R. Ford-class aircraft carriers, guided-missile cruisers and destroyers, attack and ballistic-missile submarines, amphibious assault ships, and littoral combat ships. Naval aviation operates aircraft such as the F/A-18E/F Super Hornet and the F-35C Lightning II. The navy's nuclear-powered fleet gives it effectively unlimited range and endurance, subject only to food and crew fatigue.
