The Southwest Conference (SWC) was an NCAA Division I college athletic conference in the United States that existed from 1914 to 1996. Composed predominantly of universities in Texas — with the University of Arkansas a long-time member and the University of Oklahoma an early one — it was one of the great college football conferences, producing national champions and Heisman Trophy winners, and its break-up helped trigger the modern era of conference realignment.

History

The conference was organised in 1914 at the Palace Hotel in Dallas, with founding members including Texas, Texas A&M, Baylor, Arkansas, Oklahoma, Oklahoma A&M, Southwestern and Rice. Over eight decades the SWC developed intense in-state rivalries — Texas vs. Texas A&M, the Baylor–TCU "Revivalry", SMU's "Pony Express" era — but was also plagued by recruiting scandals; the NCAA imposed the "death penalty" on SMU football in 1987.

Dissolution

Arkansas departed for the Southeastern Conference in 1991–92. In 1994 the Big Eight Conference invited Texas, Texas A&M, Baylor and Texas Tech to form the Big 12, and the remaining members (Houston, Rice, SMU, TCU) scattered to the Western Athletic Conference and Conference USA. The SWC played its final season in 1995–96 and formally disbanded on 31 May 1996, ending an 82-year history.
