Local municipality (South Africa)

Local municipalities
Country: South Africa
Category: Category B municipality
Number: 205 (as of 2016)
Governed by: Municipal council

In South Africa, a local municipality (Category B municipality) is the lowest tier of local government, below district municipalities (Category C) and metropolitan municipalities (Category A). Local municipalities share municipal executive and legislative authority with the district municipality in whose area they fall, and provide most day-to-day municipal services directly to residents.

Functions and structure

Local municipalities are responsible for services such as water reticulation and sanitation in many areas, local roads, refuse removal, municipal planning, local tourism, markets and community facilities. They are governed by elected municipal councils, headed by a mayor or an executive committee, and financed through property rates, service charges and transfers from national and provincial government under South Africa's intergovernmental fiscal system.

History

The current municipal structure was created by the Municipal Structures Act of 1998 and the demarcation process completed for the 2000 local elections, which replaced the fragmented apartheid-era arrangements with wall-to-wall municipalities covering the entire country. Boundary reorganisations, notably in 2016, reduced the number of local municipalities from 226 to 205.
