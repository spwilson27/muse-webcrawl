Fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups

The fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups, also known as the fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups in its finite case, is a central structure theorem of abstract algebra. It states that every finitely generated abelian group is isomorphic to a direct sum of a free abelian group and finitely many cyclic groups of prime-power order, and that this decomposition is unique up to the ordering of the summands.

Statement

Every finitely generated abelian group G is isomorphic to a group of the form

G ≅ ℤr ⊕ ℤ/n1ℤ ⊕ ℤ/n2ℤ ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ ℤ/nkℤ

where r ≥ 0 is the rank (the Betti number), and the invariant factors satisfy n1 | n2 | ⋯ | nk (each divides the next). Equivalently, in the primary decomposition, the torsion part splits into cyclic groups of prime-power order. For a finite abelian group, r = 0 and the theorem gives a complete classification up to isomorphism.

History and proof

The finite case was proved by Leopold Kronecker in 1870, developing work of Gauss on composition of quadratic forms; the general finitely generated version followed from work of Frobenius and Stickelberger (1878), with a modern treatment by Poincaré (1900). Standard proofs proceed by induction on the number of generators using the theory of matrices over the integers and reduction to Smith normal form. The theorem is the special case, for the ring ℤ, of the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain.
