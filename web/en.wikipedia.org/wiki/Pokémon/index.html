Pokémon (an abbreviation of the Japanese title Pocket Monsters) is a Japanese media franchise managed by The Pokémon Company, founded by Nintendo, Game Freak, and Creatures. Created by Satoshi Tajiri in 1996, the franchise is centred on fictional creatures called Pokémon, which humans known as Pokémon Trainers catch and train to battle each other for sport. It is the highest-grossing media franchise of all time, with revenue estimated in excess of US$100 billion.

Pikachu, the franchise's mascot, with the international Pokémon logo.

Video games

The franchise began with the role-playing video games Pokémon Red and Green for the Game Boy, developed by Game Freak and released in Japan in February 1996. The core series has continued through successive generations of Nintendo handheld and hybrid consoles, each introducing new species of Pokémon; more than 1,000 species exist as of the ninth generation (Pokémon Scarlet and Violet, 2022). Spin-offs include Pokémon Go (2016), an augmented-reality mobile game that became a global phenomenon.

Other media

The Pokémon anime television series, following Ash Ketchum and his partner Pikachu for 25 seasons, is one of the most successful video game adaptations ever made. The Pokémon Trading Card Game, launched in 1996, is one of the best-selling card games in the world. Numerous films, manga series, and vast merchandising lines have made Pokémon a fixture of global popular culture.
