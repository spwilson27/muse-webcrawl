Bochs

Bochs (pronounced "box") is a free and open-source IA-32 (x86) PC emulator written in C++. It includes emulation of the Intel x86 CPU, common I/O devices, and a custom BIOS. Bochs can emulate a wide variety of x86 processors, including the 386, 486, Pentium, Pentium Pro, AMD64, and later processors, though it is notably slower than virtualization solutions because it emulates every instruction rather than executing them natively.

History

Bochs began as a commercial product written by Kevin Lawton in 1994, sold for US$25. In March 2000, the company Mandrakesoft (later Mandriva) purchased Bochs and released it as free software under the GNU Lesser General Public License. Development has continued since under the Bochs Project, hosted on SourceForge.

Uses

Bochs can be compiled and used in a variety of modes, some of which are still in development. Its most typical use is x86 PC emulation, including the CPU, hardware devices, and BIOS. Because it emulates the entire machine, the host operating system is irrelevant, and guest operating systems — including MS-DOS, Windows, Linux, and BSD variants — can be run anywhere Bochs runs.

Bochs is widely used for debugging operating systems: its built-in debugger and extensive logging allow kernel developers to inspect every instruction executed. It is also used to run old software, and as a teaching tool in operating systems courses. Performance is roughly tens of millions of instructions per second, adequate for older software but far slower than hypervisors such as VirtualBox or VMware, which do not emulate the CPU.

Emulated hardware

Bochs emulates a full PC platform including VGA and SVGA graphics adapters (including Voodoo 3D accelerators), IDE and floppy disk controllers, USB controllers, serial and parallel ports, NE2000 and other network cards, and Sound Blaster compatible audio. It ships with its own BIOS derived from the SeaBIOS and earlier Bochs BIOS projects, and can also use third-party BIOS images.
