Ernesto de Martino

Ernesto de Martino
Born: 1 December 1908, Naples, Italy
Died: 9 May 1965, Rome, Italy
Occupation: Anthropologist, historian of religions, philosopher
Known for: Studies of Southern Italian magic and ritual

Ernesto de Martino (1908–1965) was an Italian anthropologist, philosopher and historian of religions. He is best known for his ethnographic studies of ritual practices in Southern Italy, including tarantism in Apulia, and for his theoretical work on magic, folklore and the "crisis of presence."

Life and work

De Martino studied under the philosopher Benedetto Croce at the University of Naples, graduating in 1934. Early in his career he joined the anti-fascist resistance. After the Second World War he became professor of the history of religions at the University of Cagliari. His major works include Naturalismo e storicismo nell'etnologia (1941), Il mondo magico (1948), Morte e pianto rituale (1958), Sud e magia (1959) and La terra del rimorso (1961).

Ideas

De Martino developed the concept of the "crisis of presence" (crisi della presenza), the risk that the individual subject loses its grip on being in the world, and argued that magic and religion function as cultural techniques for redeeming and stabilizing presence. His fieldwork on tarantism—the ritual dancing associated with the bite of the tarantula in Apulia—influenced both anthropology and the Italian folk music revival. He collaborated with ethnomusicologist Diego Carpitella on the recordings of his expeditions.

Legacy

De Martino is regarded as one of the founders of modern Italian ethnology, and his work anticipated later developments in the anthropology of religion and medical anthropology. His posthumous book La fine del mondo (1977) examined cultural apocalypses.
