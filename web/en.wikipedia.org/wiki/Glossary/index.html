Glossary

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Glossary
EtymologyFrom Greek glōssa ("tongue, word") via Latin glossarium
Also calledClavis (historically)

A glossary, also known historically as a clavis, is an alphabetical list of terms in a particular domain of knowledge with the definitions of those terms. Traditionally, a glossary appears at the end of a book and includes terms within that book that are either newly introduced, uncommon, or specialised. A bilingual glossary is a list of terms in one language defined in another, or glossed with synonyms (or near-synonyms) in another language.

Uses

Glossaries are common in technical manuals, academic textbooks, legal and regulatory documents, and works of fiction with invented or archaic vocabulary. In a general sense, a glossary contains explanations of concepts relevant to a certain field of study or action, helping readers who are new to the subject. Hyperlink-driven glossaries are widely used in e-learning and online documentation, where definitions can be displayed on demand. While a glossary is arranged alphabetically, it differs from a dictionary in being restricted to the terminology of a specific text or subject area, and from an index in providing definitions rather than page references.

History

The practice descends from the ancient and medieval glossae — explanatory notes written between the lines or in the margins of manuscripts — which were later collected into glossaries of difficult Latin, Greek and legal terms. Printed glossaries became standard apparatus in scholarly editions of the early modern period.
