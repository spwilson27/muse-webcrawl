Anxiety

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anxiety is an emotion characterised by apprehension and somatic symptoms of tension, directed at anticipated threat; it is a normal and adaptive response — sharpening attention and preparing action — that becomes a disorder when excessive, persistent and impairing. Anxiety disorders are the most common class of mental disorders worldwide, encompassing generalised anxiety disorder, panic disorder, phobias, social anxiety disorder and others, affecting on the order of 4% of the world's population at a given time.

Anxiety engages the brain's threat circuitry — the amygdala and its connections — and the autonomic stress response (racing heart, sweating, tremor, gut disturbance), mediated by adrenaline and cortisol, the same machinery that produces stress responses generally.

Treatment

First-line treatments are psychological — cognitive behavioural therapy, including exposure therapy for phobias and panic — and medicines such as SSRIs; benzodiazepines work acutely but carry dependence risk. Lifestyle factors (caffeine, sleep, exercise) are clinically relevant levers, and digital CBT has broadened access.

Development

Temperamental shyness in childhood predicts some adult anxiety; trauma and chronic stress are common roots. Anxiety commonly co-occurs with Depression — the two share genetics and circuitry.
