Tercio

The tercio was a military unit of the Spanish Army and an associated infantry formation during the era of Habsburg Spain, from the 1530s to the early 1700s. Combining pikemen, swordsmen, and arquebusiers (later musketeers) in large, deep formations, tercios dominated European battlefields for over a century.

Organisation and tactics

An administrative tercio might number about 3,000 men, deployed in the field as a great square of pikes flanked by sleeves (mangas) of shot. The formations were created by ordinances of 1534–1536 out of Spanish forces in Italy, units known as the tercios of Lombardy, Sicily, and Naples. Their discipline and combined-arms tactics made them exceptionally difficult to break.

Decline

The tercio's reputation declined after the Battle of Rocroi (1643) during the Franco-Spanish War, and improving firearms and linear tactics gradually made the deep square obsolete; Spain abolished the tercio system in favour of regiments in 1704.
