Matchmaking

Matchmakers have paired couples in many cultures for centuries.

Matchmaking is the process of matching two or more people together, usually for the purpose of marriage, but it is also used in the contexts of dating, business partnerships, and online video games, where players are matched in competitions. The person who arranges a match is a matchmaker, a role found in many cultures and often embedded in family and religious life.

Traditional matchmaking

In many societies marriages were historically arranged by families with the help of intermediaries. In Jewish tradition the matchmaker is the shadchan, a figure celebrated in the musical Fiddler on the Roof ("Matchmaker, Matchmaker"). In China, the mythical Old Man Under the Moon (Yue Lao) ties destined couples with a red thread, and professional matchmakers (meiren) long negotiated between families. In India, family networks, caste and community considerations, and horoscope matching traditionally shaped arranged marriages, and newspaper matrimonial advertisements became a major medium in the 20th century.

Modern matchmaking

The 20th century saw commercial dating services, personal advertisements, and video dating; from the 1990s, online dating platforms — beginning with sites such as Match.com (1995) and eHarmony (2000) — replaced many traditional functions with algorithmic matching based on questionnaires and behavioural data. Mobile apps such as Tinder (2012) shifted matching toward location-based, photograph-driven swiping. Professional matchmaking firms nevertheless persist, serving clients willing to pay premium fees for curated introductions.

Matchmaking in games and other domains

In multiplayer video games, matchmaking refers to the automated process of grouping players of comparable skill, commonly using rating systems derived from Elo or TrueSkill. The term is also applied to services that match donors with recipients, employers with jobseekers, and start-ups with investors.
