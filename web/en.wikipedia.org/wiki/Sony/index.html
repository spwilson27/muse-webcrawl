Sony Group Corporation (ソニーグループ株式会社), formerly known as Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo K.K. and Sony Corporation, is a Japanese multinational conglomerate headquartered in Minato, Tokyo. Its diversified business includes consumer and professional electronics, gaming and entertainment services, music, film production, and financial services, making it one of the largest manufacturers of consumer electronics and the world's largest video game console company.

History

Sony began in the aftermath of the Second World War. In 1946, Masaru Ibuka started an electronics shop in Shirokiya, a department store building in the Nihonbashi area of Tokyo. The company joined with Ibuka's colleague Akio Morita the following year under the name Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo ("Tokyo Telecommunications Engineering"). The company built Japan's first tape recorder, the Type-G, in 1950, and in 1955 launched Japan's first transistor radio, the TR-55. In 1958 the company adopted the name Sony, derived from the Latin sonus ("sound") and the American slang "sonny".

Sony became a global brand in consumer electronics with products including the Trinitron television (1968), the Walkman portable cassette player (1979), the Compact Disc (developed with Philips and launched in 1982), and the Handycam camcorder. In 1988 Sony acquired CBS Records (becoming Sony Music Entertainment), and in 1989 it bought Columbia Pictures, entering film production. In 1994, Sony Computer Entertainment launched the original PlayStation in Japan; the PlayStation brand became the best-selling line of home video game consoles in history, and gaming grew into the group's largest revenue segment.

Business segments

  • Game & Network Services — PlayStation consoles and software, PlayStation Network, and first-party studios under Sony Interactive Entertainment.
  • Music — Sony Music Entertainment, Sony Music Entertainment Japan and Sony Music Publishing, together among the world's largest music businesses.
  • Pictures — Sony Pictures Entertainment: Columbia Pictures, TriStar, Screen Gems, and television production.
  • Entertainment Technology & Services — televisions, cameras (including the Alpha mirrorless line), audio equipment and Xperia smartphones.
  • Imaging & Sensing Solutions — image sensors, in which Sony holds the largest global market share, supplying camera sensors for smartphones.
  • Financial Services — Sony Financial Group: life insurance, banking and related businesses in Japan.

Role in format wars

Sony has been a central combatant in several format wars. Its Betamax videocassette format lost to JVC's VHS in the 1970s–80s despite technical advantages, a defeat often cited as a lesson in licensing and marketing. The company later prevailed when its Blu-ray Disc defeated Toshiba's HD DVD in 2008, aided by the inclusion of a Blu-ray drive in the PlayStation 3. Sony also co-developed the Compact Disc and the DVD.
