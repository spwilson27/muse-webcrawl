Fedwire, formerly known as the Federal Reserve Wire Network, is a real-time gross settlement funds transfer system operated by the United States Federal Reserve Banks that allows financial institutions to electronically transfer funds between its more than 9,000 participants. Transfers are final and irrevocable once processed, and settlement occurs individually and in central bank money during operating hours.

Overview

The Fedwire Funds Service is used for large-value, time-critical payments, including interbank lending (federal funds transactions), corporate payments, real estate settlements, and the payment leg of securities transactions. In recent years the service has processed on the order of 190–200 million transfers annually, with a total value in the quadrillions of dollars — daily averages run to several trillion dollars.

The Federal Reserve also operates the Fedwire Securities Service, which provides for the safekeeping and transfer of securities issued by the U.S. Treasury, federal agencies, government-sponsored enterprises, and certain international organisations, against payment in Fedwire funds.

History

The Federal Reserve began moving funds by telegraph between Reserve Banks in 1915, using a Morse code system with dedicated leased lines. The network was progressively modernised through teletype, then computer-to-computer interfaces, and by the 1970s–1980s transfers were largely automated. In 2023 the Federal Reserve launched FedNow, a separate instant-payment service for smaller, round-the-clock retail transfers, complementing rather than replacing Fedwire, which remains the backbone of wholesale dollar settlement. Fedwire is the dollar leg of international arrangements such as the CLS system for foreign exchange settlement, and corresponds in function to systems such as TARGET2/T2 in the euro area and CHAPS in the United Kingdom.
