RAF Thorpe Abbotts

RAF Thorpe Abbotts
Location:Near Diss, Norfolk, England
Built:1942–43
In use:1943–1956
USAAF designation:Station 139

RAF Thorpe Abbotts was a Royal Air Force station in Norfolk, England, built during the Second World War and used by the United States Army Air Forces as Station 139. It is best known as the home of the 100th Bombardment Group (Heavy) of the Eighth Air Force — the "Bloody Hundredth" — which flew B-17 Flying Fortress bombers from the field from June 1943 until the end of the war in Europe.

History

The airfield was constructed in 1942–43 near the village of Thorpe Abbotts, south of Diss, with three concrete runways and dispersed accommodation for nearly 3,000 personnel. The 100th Bomb Group arrived in May–June 1943 and flew its first combat mission, against Bremen, on 25 June 1943.

The group earned its grim nickname from a series of costly missions, including the Regensburg shuttle mission of August 1943, the Berlin raids of March 1944, and the disastrous Münster mission of October 1943, in which only one of its thirteen B-17s returned to Thorpe Abbotts. The group flew its last combat mission in April 1945 and returned to the United States later that year.

Today

After RAF use ended in the 1950s, most of the airfield returned to agriculture. The control tower was restored and opened in 1977 as the 100th Bomb Group Memorial Museum, preserving the memory of the group and its men; several other wartime buildings survive. The 100th Bomb Group's story, including Thorpe Abbotts, was dramatised in the 2024 Apple TV+ series Masters of the Air.
