The Châteaux of the Loire Valley are part of the architectural heritage of the historic towns of Amboise, Angers, Blois, Chinon, Orléans, Saumur and Tours along the Loire River in France. They illustrate the ideals of the Renaissance and the Age of Enlightenment on the design of West European architecture and town planning, and number more than three hundred estates.

History

Many châteaux began as medieval fortresses guarding the Loire crossings. When the French court settled in the valley in the fifteenth century, nobles and financiers rebuilt them as pleasure residences in the Italian Renaissance style. Francis I built the vast Château de Chambord (from 1519), traditionally attributed in part to Leonardo da Vinci's influence; Catherine de' Medici extended Chenonceau across the river Cher. After the court returned to Paris and Versailles in the seventeenth century, building slowed, and many châteaux were later demolished or fell into ruin before restoration in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

Tourism

The central Loire Valley between Sully-sur-Loire and Chalonnes was inscribed by UNESCO as a World Heritage cultural landscape in 2000. Major châteaux open to visitors include Chambord, Chenonceau, Amboise with the Clos Lucé (Leonardo's last home), Blois, Cheverny, Villandry with its celebrated gardens, and Azay-le-Rideau.
