Handbook
Type: Reference work
Synonyms: Manual, vade mecum, enchiridion

A handbook is a type of reference work, or collection of instructions, intended to provide ready reference on a particular subject. Handbooks are concise, portable compendia designed to be consulted quickly in the field or workplace, as distinct from comprehensive treatises or textbooks.

History and forms

The tradition goes back to the ancient enchiridion — literally "that which is held in the hand" — such as the Enchiridion of Epictetus. Modern examples include employee handbooks, engineering handbooks (compiling tables, formulae and standards), field guides, style manuals, owners' manuals for machinery and software documentation.

Characteristics

Handbooks prioritise practical, organised information — checklists, tables, procedures — over narrative. Many professional disciplines maintain a standard handbook that is revised in successive editions as practice evolves.
