Cuba–United States relations

Cuba–United States relations are the bilateral relations between the Republic of Cuba and the United States of America. Relations date to the 19th century; the United States acquired strong economic and political influence over Cuba after the Spanish–American War of 1898. Modern relations have been deeply shaped by the Cold War, the Cuban Revolution of 1959, and the long-standing US embargo against Cuba.

History

Following independence from Spain under US occupation, Cuba became closely tied to the United States, whose 1903 Platt Amendment gave Washington a right to intervene and a naval base at Guantánamo Bay. After Fulgencio Batista was overthrown by Fidel Castro's revolution in 1959, relations deteriorated rapidly: Cuba nationalized US-owned assets, and the United States imposed escalating trade sanctions.

Cold War crises

The failed US-backed Bay of Pigs Invasion of April 1961 was followed by the Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962, when the discovery of Soviet nuclear missiles in Cuba brought the superpowers to the brink of nuclear war. Diplomatic relations were severed in 1961, and a full US trade embargo has remained in place since 1962, codified in law in the 1990s.

Thaw and reversals

In December 2014 Presidents Barack Obama and Raúl Castro announced the restoration of diplomatic relations (the "Cuban thaw"); embassies reopened in Havana and Washington in 2015 and Obama visited Cuba in 2016, the first sitting US president to do so since 1928. The Trump administration reversed many of these measures from 2017, tightened sanctions, and redesignated Cuba a state sponsor of terrorism in 2021, a status that remained a central point of dispute in subsequent years.

Migration and trade

Successive waves of Cuban emigration to the United States — after 1959, during the 1980 Mariel boatlift, and in the 2020s — have created a large Cuban-American community, especially in Florida. Despite the embargo, the United States is a significant exporter of food and agricultural goods to Cuba under a 2000 law.
