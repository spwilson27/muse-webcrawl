Jan Steen

Jan Havickszoon Steen (c. 1626 – buried 3 February 1679) was a Dutch Golden Age painter, one of the leading genre painters of the 17th century. His works are known for their lively, chaotic depictions of everyday life — taverns, households, festivals and schoolrooms — rich in humour, psychological insight and proverbial moralising. The Dutch expression "a Jan Steen household" (een huishouden van Jan Steen) still denotes a disorderly home.

Life

Steen was born in Leiden into a prosperous Catholic brewing family. He was educated at the Latin school and matriculated at the University of Leiden in 1646. His teachers are thought to have included the Utrecht painter Nicolaes Knüpfer and Adriaen van Ostade in Haarlem, and he became the son-in-law of the landscape painter Jan van Goyen, whose daughter Margriet he married in 1649 in The Hague. Steen was a founding member of Leiden's Guild of Saint Luke.

He moved frequently — living in The Hague, Leiden, Delft, Warmond and Haarlem — and painting alone did not support his family of eight children. Steen also worked as a brewer in Delft and later kept a tavern; in 1672 he obtained a licence to run an inn in Leiden. These experiences gave him first-hand material for the boisterous inn scenes that dominate his output of some 800 paintings.

Work

Steen's genre scenes are animated stage-like compositions crowded with figures laughing, drinking, dancing, squabbling and making music. Beneath the comedy lies a moralising intent drawn from Dutch proverbs and emblems: works such as Beware of Luxury (1663) show the consequences of vice, while The Feast of Saint Nicholas captures family joy with tender observation. He also painted portraits, religious and mythological subjects, and frequently inserted himself and his family as models. Though sometimes judged merely humorous by later critics, twentieth-century scholarship has recognised his technical virtuosity, subtle colour and acute social observation.

Legacy

Steen died in Leiden in 1679 and was buried in the family grave in the Pieterskerk. His paintings hang in the Rijksmuseum, the Mauritshuis, the National Gallery in London and the Louvre, and he remains, with Vermeer and Rembrandt, among the most popular of Dutch Golden Age painters.
