Aintree Racecourse is a racecourse in Aintree, Metropolitan Borough of Sefton, Merseyside, England, just north of Liverpool. The racecourse is best known for annually holding the famous steeplechase, the Grand National, which is held each April as the centrepiece of a three-day meeting.

History

Racing at Aintree dates to 1829, when the first meeting was organised by William Lynn, a Liverpool hotelier who leased land from Lord Sefton. The first Grand National — then called the Grand Liverpool Steeplechase — was run in 1836, and the race was run as the Grand National from 1839, when it was won by the appropriately named Lottery. The course was purchased by the Jockey Club in the 20th century after periods of financial uncertainty.

The Grand National course

The National course is a left-handed circuit of about 2 miles 2 furlongs, over which two circuits are run in the big race to make a distance of about 4 miles 2½ furlongs. Its 16 fences are notable for being dressed with spruce, and several have become famous in their own right, including Becher's Brook, the Canal Turn, Valentine's Brook and The Chair, the largest fence on the course. The race ends with a long run-in of 494 yards, including an elbow halfway.

Other events

Aintree also hosted motor racing: the Aintree Circuit, using parts of the racecourse grounds, staged the Formula One British Grand Prix five times between 1955 and 1962. The racecourse hosts concerts and other events outside the handful of race days each year.
