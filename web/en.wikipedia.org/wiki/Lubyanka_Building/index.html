The Lubyanka Building is a large yellow-brick building on Lubyanka Square in Moscow, Russia. It is the headquarters of the Federal Security Service (FSB) of Russia and was formerly the headquarters of the Soviet KGB and its predecessors. "The Lubyanka" became a byword for the Soviet secret police and its prison.

History

The building was constructed in 1897–1898 in a Neo-Baroque style as the headquarters of the All-Russia Insurance Company. After the October Revolution it was seized by the Bolsheviks and became the headquarters of the Cheka, the first Soviet secret police, under Felix Dzerzhinsky. Successive security organs — the GPU, NKVD, and KGB — occupied it, and its internal prison held many notable prisoners during the Great Terror. Between 1940 and 1948 the building was enlarged under the direction of architect Alexey Shchusev, giving it its present monumental appearance.

After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, the building passed to the Russian security services and has housed the FSB since 1995. A statue of Dzerzhinsky that stood in the square was toppled in August 1991 and later moved to an outdoor museum; the Solovetsky Stone, a memorial to victims of political repression, was placed in the square in 1990.
