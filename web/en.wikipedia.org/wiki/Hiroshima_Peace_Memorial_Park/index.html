Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park

Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park (広島平和記念公園) is a memorial park in the center of Hiroshima, Japan, dedicated to the legacy of the city as the first in the world to suffer a nuclear attack, on August 6, 1945, and to the memory of the bomb's victims. It occupies about 122,000 square metres on the former Nakajima district, near the hypocenter, and was designed by architect Kenzō Tange.

Features

The park contains the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum, which documents the bombing and its aftermath; the Cenotaph for the A-bomb Victims, an arched monument sheltering the stone chest with the registry of victims' names; the Flame of Peace, lit in 1964 and intended to burn until all nuclear weapons are abolished; the Children's Peace Monument, inspired by Sadako Sasaki and her paper cranes; and numerous other memorials, including the Peace Bell and the Memorial Tower to Students Mobilized for war work.

Genbaku Dome

Just across the Motoyasu River stands the Atomic Bomb Dome (Genbaku Dome), the skeletal ruin of the former Hiroshima Prefectural Industrial Promotion Hall, preserved as it stood after the blast and inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996.

Commemorations

Each August 6 the park hosts the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Ceremony, attended by survivors, officials, and foreign delegations, with a minute of silence at 8:15 a.m., the moment of detonation. The park receives well over a million visitors a year.
