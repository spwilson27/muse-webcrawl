Princess Mononoke

Princess Mononoke (Japanese: もののけ姫, Mononoke-hime) is a 1997 Japanese animated epic fantasy film written and directed by Hayao Miyazaki and produced by Studio Ghibli. Set in the late Muromachi period of Japan, it follows Ashitaka, a young Emishi prince cursed by a demon, who travels west and becomes entangled in a struggle between the forest gods and the human settlement of Irontown, led by Lady Eboshi, and meets San, a human girl raised by wolves.

Plot

After killing a boar god corrupted into a demon, Prince Ashitaka is cursed and told to seek a cure from the Great Forest Spirit, the Shishigami. At Irontown, a mining and iron-smelting settlement led by Lady Eboshi, he learns the settlers are clearing the forest and are at war with its animal gods. San, the adopted human daughter of the wolf goddess Moro, repeatedly attacks the town. Both sides converge at the forest pool when the Shishigami is beheaded by Eboshi at the instigation of the monk Jiko-bō; the unleashed death spreads across the land until Ashitaka and San return the head, lifting the curse and allowing the land to begin healing.

Production and themes

Miyazaki drew on the landscapes of Shirakami-Sanchi and Yakushima and on medieval Japanese history. The film explores environmental conflict without simple villains: Eboshi empowers women and lepers even as she destroys the forest. The score was composed by Joe Hisaishi.

Reception

Princess Mononoke was the highest-grossing film in Japan at the time of its release, earning about ¥19.3 billion domestically, and is widely regarded as one of the greatest animated films ever made. An English version, with a script by Neil Gaiman, was released by Miramax in 1999.
