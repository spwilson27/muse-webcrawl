Juan José Gerardi Conedera

Born27 December 1922, Guatemala City, Guatemala
Died26 April 1998, Guatemala City, Guatemala
OccupationRoman Catholic bishop, human rights advocate

Juan José Gerardi Conedera (1922–1998) was a Guatemalan Roman Catholic bishop and human rights defender. He was murdered two days after presenting a landmark report documenting atrocities committed during Guatemala's civil war.

Ministry

Ordained a priest in 1946, Gerardi served in several dioceses and became bishop of Verapaz and later of Santa Cruz del Quiché, regions heavily affected by the armed conflict. His defense of indigenous communities brought him into conflict with the military government, and he was forced into exile in 1980 after death threats and the closure of his diocese.

REMHI report

After returning to Guatemala, Gerardi directed the Recovery of Historical Memory Project (REMHI), sponsored by the Human Rights Office of the Archdiocese of Guatemala. On 24 April 1998 he presented its report, Guatemala: Nunca Más ("Never Again"), which documented tens of thousands of killings and attributed the great majority of violations to the military and allied forces.

Assassination and investigation

Two days later, on 26 April 1998, Gerardi was beaten to death in the garage of his parish house in Guatemala City. The investigation was marked by controversy, but in 2001 a court convicted several men, including military officers, of involvement in the murder; the convictions of the officers were later upheld on appeal.

Legacy

Gerardi is regarded internationally as a martyr for human rights, and his case remains a symbol of the struggle against impunity in Guatemala.
