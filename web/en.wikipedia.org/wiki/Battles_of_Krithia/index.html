Battles of Krithia

The Battles of Krithia were a series of three Allied attacks during the Gallipoli Campaign of the First World War, aimed at capturing the village of Krithia and the hill of Achi Baba on the Gallipoli peninsula in 1915. All three failed with heavy casualties, confirming that the Helles front had settled into the same trench warfare stalemate as the Western Front.

First battle, 28 April 1915

Three days after the landings at Cape Helles, exhausted British and French troops advanced toward Krithia in what resembled a manoeuvre from an earlier era of warfare — in line, in daylight, without adequate artillery preparation or reconnaissance of the ravines (dere) cutting the ground. The attack stalled well short of the village with around 3,000 casualties.

Second battle, 6–8 May 1915

Reinforced by ANZAC troops brought down from Anzac Cove, the Allies attacked again over three days in a series of piecemeal brigade advances. Gains were measured in hundreds of yards at a cost of some 6,500 casualties. The Australian 2nd Brigade's charge at Krithia on 8 May — advancing across open ground in full view of the Turkish trenches — cost it about a third of its strength in an afternoon.

Third battle, 4 June 1915

The third attack used improved methods — a heavier bombardment and staged objectives — and in the centre the British broke into the Turkish lines, but flanking units failed and counterattacks sealed off the gains. Casualties were again heavy on both sides. A further action at Krithia Vineyard in August likewise failed, and Achi Baba was never taken; the Helles beaches were evacuated in January 1916.
