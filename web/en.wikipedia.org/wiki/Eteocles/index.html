Eteocles

Eteocles (Ancient Greek: Ἐτεοκλῆς, "truly glorious") was a king of Thebes in Greek mythology, the son of Oedipus and Jocasta and the brother of Polynices, Antigone and Ismene. His conflict with Polynices over the Theban throne is the subject of Aeschylus's tragedy Seven Against Thebes and forms the background to Sophocles' Antigone.

Mythology

After Oedipus was driven into exile, Eteocles and Polynices agreed to share the kingship of Thebes, ruling in alternate years. When the first year ended, Eteocles refused to surrender the throne, and Polynices fled to Argos, where he married the daughter of King Adrastus and raised an army — the Seven against Thebes — to reclaim his share by force.

In Aeschylus's play, Eteocles organises the defence of the city's seven gates, assigning a champion to each; on learning that the seventh gate is attacked by Polynices himself, he insists on facing his brother despite the chorus's warnings and the curse of Oedipus that the brothers would divide their inheritance by the sword. The two brothers killed each other in single combat. Their uncle Creon, succeeding to the throne, ordered honourable burial for Eteocles as the city's defender but forbade burial of Polynices as a traitor — the decree whose defiance by Antigone drives Sophocles' tragedy.

In literature

Eteocles appears in Aeschylus's Seven Against Thebes, Euripides' Phoenician Women, and Statius's epic Thebaid. In Dante's Inferno, the flame that burns Ulysses and Diomedes is compared to the divided flame of the funeral pyre of Eteocles and Polynices, which split apart even in death.
