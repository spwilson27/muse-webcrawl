Thomas Wharton, 1st Baron Wharton (c. 1495 – 23 August 1568) was an English nobleman and a key military leader and administrator on the Anglo-Scottish border during the reigns of Henry VIII, Edward VI and Mary I. As Warden of the West March he was responsible for defending the western frontier against Scotland, and he achieved his greatest fame commanding the English forces that defeated a much larger Scottish army at the Battle of Solway Moss in 1542.

Early life

Wharton was born about 1495, the eldest son of Thomas Wharton of Wharton Hall in Westmorland and Agnes Warcup. The Whartons were an established gentry family of the north-west whose fortunes rose sharply through Thomas's service. He entered the service of the Earl of Cumberland and gained experience of border warfare from an early age.

Border service

Wharton was appointed Captain of Carlisle Castle and, in 1527, Deputy Warden of the West March. In the 1530s he administered the region during Henry VIII's campaigns of intimidation against Scotland, organised intelligence networks of spies and "assured Scots", and raided deep into southern Scotland. During the Pilgrimage of Grace (1536–37) he remained loyal to the Crown and held Carlisle against the rebels.

Solway Moss and after

On 24 November 1542, Wharton's small force intercepted a Scottish army of perhaps 15,000–18,000 men under Oliver Sinclair near Longtown, south of the River Esk. At the Battle of Solway Moss the Scots, disordered and demoralised, were routed with remarkably little English bloodshed; many Scottish nobles were captured, and King James V of Scotland died a few weeks later. In reward, Wharton was created Baron Wharton in 1544/45 and became Warden of the West March in full.

Wharton continued to govern the borders through the Rough Wooing and under Mary I, serving also as a Member of Parliament and a councillor of the north. He was a committed Catholic who conformed under successive regimes while retaining office. He retired in the early years of Elizabeth I and died on 23 August 1568. He was succeeded by his son, Thomas Wharton, 2nd Baron Wharton.

Legacy

Wharton's surviving correspondence is a major source for the history of the Tudor frontier. His name survives most famously in the border ballad tradition and in the Wharton family's rise — his descendants became Earls and Marquesses of Wharton and prominent Whig politicians of a later age.
