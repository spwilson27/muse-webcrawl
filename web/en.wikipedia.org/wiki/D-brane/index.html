D-brane

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In string theory, D-branes, short for Dirichlet membrane or Dirichlet-branes, are a class of extended objects upon which open strings can end with Dirichlet boundary conditions. D-branes were discovered by Dai, Leigh and Polchinski, and independently by Hořava, in 1989. In 1995, Polchinski showed that D-branes are the sources of Ramond–Ramond fields, demonstrating that they are non-perturbative objects essential to a complete formulation of string theory.

Background

In perturbative string theory, strings may be open (with endpoints) or closed (loops). The endpoints of open strings were originally required to satisfy Neumann boundary conditions, freely moving in spacetime. Imposing Dirichlet boundary conditions instead confines the endpoint to a hyperplane of spacetime; the dynamical object occupying that hyperplane is the D-brane. A Dp-brane has p spatial dimensions: a D0-brane is a point particle, a D1-brane a string, a D2-brane a membrane, and so on.

Properties

D-branes are dynamical: they can move, fluctuate, and carry gauge fields on their worldvolumes described at low energy by the Dirac–Born–Infeld action. They carry Ramond–Ramond charge and have tension scaling as the inverse of the string coupling, making them heavy at weak coupling and hence invisible in classical string perturbation theory. Stacks of coincident D-branes give rise to non-Abelian gauge theories, a fact central to gauge/gravity duality.

Applications

D-branes play a key role in the AdS/CFT correspondence, in Strominger and Vafa's microscopic counting of black hole entropy using D1–D5 systems, in brane-world cosmology, and in geometric constructions of particle physics models ("brane constructions").
