The World Sprint Speed Skating Championships are a series of speed skating competitions determined over two days, in which skaters race the 500 metres and 1000 metres twice each. The skater with the lowest combined time over the four races, calculated using the samalog points system, is declared world sprint champion. The championships are organised by the International Skating Union (ISU).

History

The first official World Sprint Championships were held in 1970 in West Allis, Wisconsin, although a precursor event, the ISU Sprint Championships, had been staged in 1969. For many years the event was held annually in late January or February. Since 2020, the sprint championships have been combined with the allround championships in a single event held every two years, alternating with the World Single Distances Championships in the Olympic cycle.

Format

Each skater competes over 500 m and 1000 m on the first day and repeats both distances on the second day, usually skating against a different opponent in each race. Times are converted into points, with the 500 m counting in full and the 1000 m halved, so that lower point totals are better.

Notable champions

Successful sprinters in the event's history include Eric Heiden, Igor Zhelezovski, Dan Jansen, Jeremy Wotherspoon, Shani Davis, Bonnie Blair, Monique Garbrecht-Enfeldt and Nao Kodaira. The Netherlands has historically been the most successful nation overall.
