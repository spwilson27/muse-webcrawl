Siegfried

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Siegfried (Middle High German: Sîvrit; Old Norse: Sigurd) is a legendary hero of Germanic heroic legend who became a central figure of the Nibelungenlied and the Norse Völsunga saga. He is best known for slaying the dragon Fafnir, bathing in its blood to become invulnerable, and for his treacherous murder by Hagen.

Legend

In the German tradition, Siegfried is a prince of Xanten on the Lower Rhine. Having killed the dragon and won the treasure of the Nibelungs, he comes to the Burgundian court at Worms and marries Kriemhild, sister of King Gunther, after helping Gunther win the superhumanly strong Icelandic queen Brünhild by means of a cloak of invisibility — a deception that breeds a fatal quarrel between the two queens. Hagen, Gunther's vassal, learns from Kriemhild of Siegfried's single vulnerable spot, where a linden leaf fell between his shoulders as he bathed in the dragon's blood, and murders him with a spear at a spring during a hunt.

In the Norse version, Sigurd, of the Völsung line, is raised by the smith Regin, reforges his father's sword Gram, kills Fafnir, understands the speech of birds after tasting the dragon's blood, and awakens the valkyrie Brynhildr. Tricked into marrying Gudrun and into wooing Brynhildr for Gunnar, he is murdered at Brynhildr's instigation.

Origins and legacy

The figure probably fuses several historical models, including the Merovingian king Sigebert I and memories of Arminius. Siegfried/Sigurd appears in eddic poetry, the Þiðrekssaga, Wagner's Ring cycle (where Siegfried is the third opera), Fritz Lang's film Die Nibelungen, and countless later adaptations.
