Game studies

Game studies, also known as ludology, is the study of games, the act of playing them, and the players and cultures surrounding them. It is a field of cultural studies that deals with all types of games throughout history. This field of research utilizes the tactics of, at least, anthropology, sociology and psychology (interdisciplinary). The field also interfaces with media studies more broadly, such as the study of video game culture, which includes all types of digital and non-digital games.

Within the larger and richer tradition of game studies, the discipline of video game studies has played an outsized role in the field and is often treated interchangeably with it. Since the early 2000s, scholars have studied video games as a cultural form, examining their design, their players, and their broader social and cultural effects.

Ludology versus narratology

Much of early game studies was framed by a debate between "ludologists", who emphasized the study of games as rule-based systems and the act of play itself, and "narratologists", who approached games through the lens of narrative theory. Scholars such as Espen Aarseth argued that games should be studied on their own terms as simulations and ergodic texts, rather than as a branch of preexisting media forms like literature or film. Later scholarship has generally treated the two approaches as complementary rather than opposed.

Approaches

  • Humanities: studying how games convey meaning, narrative, and representation.
  • Social science: studying the psychological and social effects of playing games, and the communities that form around them.
  • Design and industry: studying game design processes and the business of game development.
  • Technical: studying the platforms, algorithms, and computational structures underlying games.

Key texts and journals

Important works include Johan Huizinga's Homo Ludens (1938), Roger Caillois's Man, Play and Games (1958), Espen Aarseth's Cybertext (1997), and Jesper Juul's Half-Real (2005). Dedicated journals include Game Studies, founded in 2001, and Games and Culture.
