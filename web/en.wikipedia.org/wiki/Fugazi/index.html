Fugazi

Fugazi is an American post-hardcore band formed in Washington, D.C., in 1986 by Ian MacKaye, Joe Lally, and Brendan Canty, with guitarist and vocalist Guy Picciotto joining soon after. One of the most influential and defiantly independent bands in rock, Fugazi is known for its DIY ethics — self-released records on MacKaye's Dischord Records, low ticket prices, all-ages shows, and refusal of merchandising and major-label deals — as well as for a dynamic sound that fed into the development of post-hardcore and emo. The band has been on indefinite hiatus since 2003.

History

MacKaye, previously of Minor Threat and Embrace, formed Fugazi after the breakup of Embrace; the name is Vietnam-era military slang (an acronym for a phrase meaning a hopelessly messed-up situation) taken from a war memoir. Early releases — the Fugazi EP (1988), containing "Waiting Room" and "Suggestion", and Margin Walker (1989) — were combined on 13 Songs. The debut album Repeater (1990) and Steady Diet of Nothing (1991) consolidated a sound built on interlocking guitars, dub-influenced bass, and dramatic quiet–loud dynamics.

Later work and hiatus

In on the Kill Taker (1993), Red Medicine (1995), End Hits (1998), and The Argument (2001) showed increasing experimentation; The Argument is often considered the band's finest album. The documentary Instrument (1999), directed by Jem Cohen, chronicled the band's first decade. Fugazi played its last shows in 2002 and announced an indefinite hiatus in 2003, citing family commitments. Since 2011 the Fugazi Live Series has released recordings of nearly all of the band's roughly 1,000 concerts online.

Ethics and influence

Fugazi famously charged about five dollars for admission, refused to play venues without all-ages admission where possible, discouraged slam dancing and violence at shows, and turned down lucrative major-label offers. Critics regard the band as a model of artistic independence, and its members' stance influenced generations of punk and indie musicians.
