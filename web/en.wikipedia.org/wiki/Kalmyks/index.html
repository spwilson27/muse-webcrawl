Kalmyks

The Kalmyks (Kalmyk: Хальмгуд; Russian: Калмыки) are a Mongolic ethnic group living mainly in the Republic of Kalmykia in Southern Russia. They are the westernmost Mongolic people and descendants of the Oirat Mongols who migrated from Dzungaria (in present-day Xinjiang and western Mongolia) to the Lower Volga steppe in the early 17th century. Their language, Kalmyk, belongs to the Mongolic language family, and most Kalmyks practise Tibetan Buddhism.

History

Kalmyk nomads established the Kalmyk Khanate on the Volga steppe, acting as a military ally and frontier force of the Russian Empire. In 1771 a large part of the population undertook a return migration to Dzungaria under Ubashi Khan, a journey in which many perished. The remaining Kalmyks fought in Russian campaigns, including against Napoleon. In December 1943 the Kalmyks were deported en masse to Siberia on accusations of collaboration; thousands died before the exile ended in 1957 and they were permitted to return to their homeland.

Culture

Traditional Kalmyk culture is nomadic and pastoral, centred on livestock herding, the felt yurt (ger), tea with milk and salt, and the epic poem Jangar, performed by storytellers called jangarchi. Buddhism, adopted from Tibet in the 17th century, shapes religious life, with temples (khuruls) rebuilt after the Soviet period.
