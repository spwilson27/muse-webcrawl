Frigatebirds are a family, Fregatidae, of seabirds found across tropical and subtropical oceans. The five living species are all placed in the genus Fregata. They have long wings, deeply forked tails and long hooked bills; males display a distinctive scarlet throat pouch, or gular pouch, which they inflate during the breeding season to attract mates.

Behaviour

Frigatebirds have the largest wing-area-to-body-weight ratio of any bird and are essentially aerial, able to stay aloft for weeks at a time, soaring on thermals over the open ocean and even sleeping while flying. Because their plumage is not waterproof, they cannot land on water and instead snatch prey from the surface or steal food from other seabirds — a habit called kleptoparasitism that earned them the name "man-o'-war birds".

Species

The family comprises the magnificent frigatebird, great frigatebird, lesser frigatebird, Christmas Island frigatebird and Ascension frigatebird. The latter two are threatened, largely because of introduced predators on their breeding islands.
