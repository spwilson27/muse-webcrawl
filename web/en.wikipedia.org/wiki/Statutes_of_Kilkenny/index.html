Statutes of Kilkenny

The Statutes of Kilkenny were a series of thirty-five laws enacted at Kilkenny in 1366 by the Parliament of Ireland, under the leadership of Lionel, Duke of Clarence, son of Edward III of England. Their purpose was to halt the cultural assimilation of the Anglo-Norman settlers in Ireland to Gaelic Irish society.

Content

The statutes forbade the English in Ireland from marrying native Irish, fostering Irish children, using the Irish language, wearing Irish dress, riding horses in the Irish fashion, or adopting Irish law (the Brehon law) instead of English common law. Settlers who became "more Irish than the Irish themselves" — Hibernis ipsis Hiberniores — were the principal target.

Effect

The statutes were repeatedly re-enacted but proved largely unenforceable outside the Pale around Dublin. They remained formally in force until repealed in the seventeenth century and are often cited as an early example of legislated cultural segregation in Ireland.
