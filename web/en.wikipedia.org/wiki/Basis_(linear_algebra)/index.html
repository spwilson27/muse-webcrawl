Basis (linear algebra)

In mathematics, a basis of a vector space V over a field F is a set B of vectors in V such that every vector in V can be written in a unique way as a finite linear combination of elements of B. The coefficients of this linear combination are referred to as components or coordinates of the vector with respect to B.

Definition

Equivalently, a basis is a set that is both linearly independent and spanning (generating) the space. The standard basis of Fⁿ consists of the unit vectors e₁,…,eₙ with a single 1 and the rest 0. Every vector space has a basis (assuming the axiom of choice), and all bases of a given space have the same number of elements — its dimension.

Properties

Choosing a basis turns abstract vectors into coordinate tuples and linear maps into matrices; a change of basis conjugates the representing matrix by the change-of-basis matrix. A basis in which distinct vectors are mutually perpendicular and of unit length is an orthonormal basis, central to inner product spaces and obtained via the Gram–Schmidt process. In infinite dimensions one distinguishes Hamel bases (finite combinations only) from Schauder bases of Hilbert and Banach spaces, which allow infinite series.

Examples

The set {(1,0), (0,1)} is the standard basis of ℝ²; {(1,1), (1,−1)} is another basis of the same space. The monomials {1, x, x², …, xⁿ} form a basis of the space of polynomials of degree at most n.
