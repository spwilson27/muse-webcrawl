The World Chess Championship is played to determine the World Chess Champion in the game of chess. The current champion is Gukesh Dommaraju of India, who defeated Ding Liren of China in the 2024 championship held in Singapore.

History

The first event recognised as a world championship was the 1886 match between Wilhelm Steinitz and Johannes Zukertort, played in the United States. Before FIDE took control in 1948, the championship was arranged privately between the champion and a challenger, and the title changed hands through matches won by Emanuel Lasker, José Raúl Capablanca, Alexander Alekhine and Max Euwe.

After the death of Alekhine in 1946, FIDE organised a tournament in 1948 to fill the vacant title, won by Mikhail Botvinnik. From then until 1993 FIDE ran a three-year cycle of zonal and interzonal tournaments, a Candidates Tournament and a title match. Notable matches include the 1972 "Match of the Century" between Boris Spassky and Bobby Fischer in Reykjavík, and the marathon Karpov–Kasparov matches of the 1980s.

The split title (1993–2006)

In 1993 champion Garry Kasparov and challenger Nigel Short left FIDE and played their match under the Professional Chess Association. FIDE continued to stage its own championship, and two rival titles existed until a reunification match in 2006, won by Vladimir Kramnik over Veselin Topalov. Since then the championship has again been a single, unified title under FIDE.

Modern format

The championship is contested in a match between the defending champion and the winner of the eight-player Candidates Tournament. The match consists of 14 games with classical time controls; if the score is tied, rapid and then blitz playoffs decide the title. The championship now generally takes place every two years.

Champions since reunification

  • Vladimir Kramnik (2006–2007)
  • Viswanathan Anand (2007–2013)
  • Magnus Carlsen (2013–2023)
  • Ding Liren (2023–2024)
  • Gukesh Dommaraju (2024–present)
