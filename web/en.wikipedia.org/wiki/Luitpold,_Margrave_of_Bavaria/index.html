Luitpold (or Liutpold) (died 4 July 907) was a Bavarian nobleman of the late Carolingian period, margrave of Carinthia and Upper Pannonia from about 895 and margrave of Bavaria, and the founder of the Luitpolding dynasty, which ruled the Duchy of Bavaria for much of the 10th century. He is counted among the ancestors of the Bavarian ducal house and should not be confused with the much later Prince Regent Luitpold of Bavaria (1821–1912).

Life

Luitpold's origins are uncertain; he may have been related to the early Bavarian Huosi family and possibly to the Carolingians through his mother. He appears in the records as a loyal supporter of Emperor Arnulf of Carinthia, who entrusted him with the defence of the south-eastern frontier of East Francia, including the marches of Carinthia and Upper Pannonia. After Arnulf's death in 899, Luitpold remained a powerful figure at the court of the child king Louis the Child, and his influence in Bavaria grew to rival that of a duke.

Death at Pressburg

Luitpold died on 4 July 907 in the Battle of Pressburg (present-day Bratislava), one of the decisive battles of the Hungarian invasions of Europe, in which the Bavarian army was annihilated by the Magyars. His son Arnulf, called "the Bad", succeeded him as Duke of Bavaria.

Family

Luitpold married Kunigunda of Swabia, sister of the Swabian counts Erchanger and Berthold. Their sons Arnulf and Berthold both became dukes of Bavaria. Through the Luitpoldings and their descendants he is an ancestor connected to later Bavarian ruling families and, through dynastic tradition, claimed as a forebear of the House of Wittelsbach.
