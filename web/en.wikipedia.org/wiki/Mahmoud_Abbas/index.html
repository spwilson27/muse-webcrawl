Mahmoud Abbas (Arabic: مَحْمُود عَبَّاس, Maḥmūd ʿAbbās; born 15 November 1935), also known by the kunya Abu Mazen, is a Palestinian politician who has served as President of the State of Palestine and of the Palestinian National Authority since 2005. He has also been chairman of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) since 2004, succeeding Yasser Arafat.

Early life and education

Abbas was born in Safed in the Galilee, then part of Mandatory Palestine. His family fled to Syria during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. He later studied law at the University of Damascus and earned a PhD in history from Patrice Lumumba University in Moscow, where his doctoral dissertation dealt with the relations between Nazism and Zionism; the dissertation later attracted scholarly criticism.

Political career

Abbas was a founding member of Fatah in the late 1950s and became a senior figure in the PLO. He was a leading architect of the Oslo Accords of 1993 and 1995, having conducted back-channel negotiations with Israeli officials in Norway. In 2003 he served briefly as the first Prime Minister of the Palestinian National Authority under Arafat, resigning after a few months amid disputes over control of the security forces.

Presidency

Following Arafat's death in November 2004, Abbas won the Palestinian presidential election of January 2005. His four-year term was extended in 2008 by the PLO Central Council, and he has remained in office since, with no further presidential elections held. His tenure has been marked by the split between the Fatah-led administration in the West Bank and Hamas's control of the Gaza Strip since 2007, repeated rounds of reconciliation talks, and the Palestinian Authority's pursuit of international recognition, including Palestine's admission to UNESCO in 2011 and observer-state status at the United Nations in 2012.

Views and criticism

Abbas has long advocated a negotiated two-state solution and non-violent resistance, though critics, including within Palestinian politics, have accused him of authoritarian tendencies, particularly following the Palestinian Authority's postponement of elections and crackdowns on dissent. Israeli leaders have at various times described him as a partner for peace and, at other times, accused him of incitement — a charge his office denies.
