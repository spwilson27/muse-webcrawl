Choral scholar

A choral scholar is a student at a university or college, typically in the United Kingdom, who receives a scholarship or award in exchange for singing in the chapel choir or another official choir of the institution. Choral scholarships are a distinctive feature of the collegiate choral tradition at the universities of Oxford and Cambridge and at cathedral foundations.

Role

Choral scholars sing the daily services — Evensong on most days, and Eucharist on Sundays — alongside professional lay clerks or as the adult core of the choir, with boy or girl trebles singing the top line in cathedral and some college choirs. The award usually carries a modest stipend and often free singing lessons, and holders are expected to be full members of the college while studying any academic subject.

Recruitment

Candidates audition in the autumn before admission or during their first term, singing prepared pieces, sight-reading, and undergoing aural tests. Famous choirs offering choral scholarships include those of King's College and St John's College, Cambridge, and Magdalen, New College, and Christ Church, Oxford.

Related terms

Similar positions include the lay clerk (a professional adult singer) and the organ scholar, who assists the director of music at the organ.
