Erispoe (French: Erispoë; Latin: Herispogius; died 857) was King of Brittany from 851 until his assassination in 857. The son and successor of Nominoe, he consolidated Breton independence from the West Frankish kingdom of Charles the Bald through military victory and negotiated recognition.

Reign

On his father's death in 851, Erispoe took command of the Breton forces and on 22 August 851 decisively defeated Charles the Bald at the Battle of Jengland, near the Vilaine river. By the ensuing Treaty of Angers, Charles recognised Erispoe as king and ceded the counties of Rennes and Nantes and the Pays de Retz to Brittany, fixing an eastern frontier that endured for centuries. Erispoe subsequently received further recognition from Charles, in some accounts being granted royal insignia.

Death

Erispoe's rapprochement with the Franks and his favouring of the abbey of Redon alienated parts of the Breton aristocracy. In November 857 he was murdered at the altar of a church — traditionally at Talensac — by his cousin and successor Salomon, acting with another Breton noble, Almarchus. He was later venerated locally, and his daughter married Gurvand, a Breton leader.
