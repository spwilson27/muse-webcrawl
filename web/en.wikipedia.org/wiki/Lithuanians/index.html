Lithuanians (Lithuanian: lietuviai) are a Baltic ethnic group native to Lithuania, where they number about 2.4 million and form the large majority of the population. They speak Lithuanian, an Indo-European language of the Baltic branch, noted among living Indo-European languages for preserving many archaic features, including a pitch accent and a rich inflectional system. Most Lithuanians are Roman Catholic, a faith adopted in 1387 when Lithuania became the last pagan state in Europe to Christianise.

History

Baltic tribes ancestors of the Lithuanians have lived along the south-eastern Baltic shore for millennia. In the 13th century, Lithuanian tribes united under Mindaugas, crowned King of Lithuania in 1253, founding a state that expanded into the Grand Duchy of Lithuania — by the 15th century, under Vytautas the Great, one of the largest states in Europe, stretching from the Baltic to the Black Sea. In 1569 the Grand Duchy entered the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.

After the partitions of the Commonwealth (1772–1795), most Lithuanian lands fell under the Russian Empire. A national revival in the 19th century revived the Lithuanian language and culture despite a 40-year imperial ban on Lithuanian publications in the Latin alphabet (1864–1904), circumvented by book smugglers (knygnešiai). Lithuania declared independence in 1918, was occupied by the Soviet Union and Nazi Germany during and after the Second World War, and restored its independence in 1990–1991, the first Soviet republic to declare it.

Diaspora

Large Lithuanian communities formed abroad through emigration in the late 19th and early 20th centuries (to the United States, especially Chicago and Pennsylvania coal country), through wartime displacement after 1944, and through labour migration after Lithuania joined the European Union in 2004 — notably to the United Kingdom, Ireland, Germany and Norway.

Culture

Lithuanian culture includes the vast repertoire of folk songs (dainos) and the great song and dance festivals, recognised by UNESCO; the seasonal customs and Catholic calendar of the countryside; amber craft from the Baltic coast; and a celebrated basketball tradition — basketball is often called Lithuania's "second religion". The national dish is cepelinai, potato dumplings named for their zeppelin shape.
