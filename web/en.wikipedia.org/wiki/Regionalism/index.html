Regionalism

Regionalism may refer to several related concepts in politics, economics, art and literature.

In politics

In politics, regionalism is a political ideology that focuses on the interests and identity of a particular region or group of regions within a state. Regionalist movements may seek greater autonomy, devolution, or in some cases secession. Examples include movements in Catalonia, Scotland, Flanders and Quebec.

In international relations

In international relations, regionalism describes the expression of a common sense of identity and purpose combined with the creation of institutions that express that identity, often taking the form of regional organisations such as the European Union, the African Union or ASEAN.

In art

In art, Regionalism was an American realist modern art movement of the 1930s that depicted rural American life. Its best-known exponents were Grant Wood, Thomas Hart Benton and John Steuart Curry.

In literature

Regionalism in literature emphasises the specific features of a region — its dialect, customs, landscape and history — as seen in the local colour writing of authors such as Mark Twain, Sarah Orne Jewett and Thomas Hardy.
