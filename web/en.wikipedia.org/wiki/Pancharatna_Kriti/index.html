Pancharatna Kriti

The Pancharatna Kritis (Sanskrit: pañcaratna, "five gems") are a set of five kritis (songs) in Carnatic music composed by the saint-composer Tyagaraja (1767–1847). They are regarded as among the finest and most demanding works in the Carnatic repertoire, and are sung in unison by hundreds of musicians each year at the Tyagaraja Aradhana festival at Thiruvaiyaru, where the composer lived and died.

The five kritis

The five compositions, all in Telugu and set to Adi tala, are: Jagadanandakaraka (raga Nattai), Dudukugala (raga Gaula), Sadhinchene (raga Arabhi), Kanakana Ruchira (raga Varali), and Endaro Mahanubhavulu (raga Sri). The last, a salutation to all the great devotees of the world, is especially beloved and often learned by students as a milestone piece.

Performance

The group rendering of the Pancharatna Kritis became the centrepiece of the Aradhana commemorations in the twentieth century and is now mirrored by Tyagaraja festivals worldwide, notably the Cleveland Tyagaraja Festival in Ohio. The kritis' intricate sangatis (melodic variations) make them a standard test of a Carnatic musician's training.
