Fashion design

Fashion design is the art of applying design, aesthetics, clothing construction and natural beauty to clothing and its accessories. It is influenced by culture and different trends, and has varied over time and place. A fashion designer creates clothing, including dresses, suits, trousers, and skirts, and accessories for the range of clothing they are responsible for.

History

Fashion design is usually considered to have started in the 19th century with Charles Frederick Worth, the first designer to have his label sewn into the garments he created and to present collections on live models in his Paris house of fashion. Before Worth, dressmaking was largely anonymous and dictated by court fashion. In the 20th century, designers such as Coco Chanel, Christian Dior, Yves Saint Laurent and Cristóbal Balenciaga defined successive eras, while mass production made seasonal fashion available to a general market.

Types of fashion

Haute couture consists of exclusive, custom-fitted garments made to order in Paris under the rules of the Chambre Syndicale. Ready-to-wear (prêt-à-porter) is factory-made in standard sizes, and mass market fashion serves the widest audience at the lowest prices. Seasonal collections are traditionally shown at fashion weeks in Paris, Milan, New York and London.

The design process

Designers research trends, sketch designs, select fabrics and trims, and oversee the making of samples and patterns. Garments then pass to production, marketing and retail. Modern practice adds computer-aided design, 3D sampling and growing pressure for sustainability in materials and supply chains.

Fashion capitals

The traditional "big four" fashion capitals are Paris, Milan, New York City and London, with Tokyo, Berlin, and others also hosting significant industries.
