Prestige

Prestige is the level of regard, honour, or social status with which a person, occupation, institution, or thing is held in a society. In sociology it is one dimension of social stratification, distinct from wealth (economic class) and power. The word derives from the French prestige, originally meaning a delusion or illusion, from Latin praestigiae — the tricks of a juggler.

Occupational prestige

Sociologists have long ranked occupations by prestige, finding remarkably consistent orderings across countries and decades: physicians, judges, and professors rank highest; manual and unskilled service occupations rank lowest. Donald Treiman's comparative research showed a common international structure of occupational prestige correlated with education and income. Occupational prestige scales are used in measuring socioeconomic status.

Prestige in other fields

In sociolinguistics, a prestige dialect or variety is the form of a language associated with high-status speakers, such as Received Pronunciation in Britain; speakers may shift toward prestige forms in formal settings, while "covert prestige" attaches to non-standard forms that signal local identity and solidarity. In consumer behaviour, prestige brands and goods — whose appeal rests partly on exclusivity and signalling — are a recognized market category. In evolutionary anthropology, prestige (freely conferred deference to skilled or knowledgeable individuals) is contrasted with dominance as a route to social rank.
