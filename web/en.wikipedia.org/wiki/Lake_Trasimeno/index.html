Lake Trasimeno (Italian: Lago Trasimeno) is the largest lake in peninsular Italy, in the province of Perugia in Umbria, south of the Po valley. With a surface area of about 128 square kilometres it is the fourth-largest lake in Italy overall, but it is very shallow, with a maximum depth of only about six metres, and its level fluctuates considerably with rainfall.

History

The lake is famous as the site of the Battle of Lake Trasimene in 217 BC, during the Second Punic War, when Hannibal's Carthaginian army ambushed and destroyed a Roman army under the consul Gaius Flaminius on the lake's northern shore — one of the largest and most successful ambushes in military history. In the Middle Ages and later, efforts were made both to drain the lake and to preserve it as a fishery; the emissary channel and regulation of its outflow occupied engineers for centuries.

Geography and tourism

The lake has three islands: Isola Polvese, now a scientific-natural park; Isola Maggiore, the only inhabited island, with a small fishing village and associations with St. Francis of Assisi; and Isola Minore, privately owned. Towns such as Castiglione del Lago, Passignano sul Trasimeno, and Tuoro sul Trasimeno surround the lake, which is protected as a regional park and is a popular destination for sailing, windsurfing, and cycling.
