Insurance fraud

Insurance fraud is any act committed to defraud an insurance process. It occurs when a claimant attempts to obtain some benefit or advantage they are not entitled to, or when an insurer knowingly denies a benefit due. Fraudulent claims are a significant cost for insurers and policyholders worldwide.

Types

Hard fraud involves deliberate fabrication, such as staging a car accident, faking an injury, or setting fire to insured property. Soft fraud, or opportunistic fraud, involves exaggerating a legitimate claim or misrepresenting facts on an application to lower premiums. Fraud also occurs in health insurance billing, workers' compensation, and life insurance.

Detection and law

Insurers maintain special investigation units, use data analysis to flag suspicious patterns, and share information through industry databases. Many jurisdictions have specific insurance-fraud statutes and dedicated bureaus; convicted offenders may face fines, restitution and imprisonment. Industry bodies such as the Coalition Against Insurance Fraud in the United States campaign against the practice.
