Mark Gatiss

Mark Gatiss (born 17 October 1966) is an English actor, comedian, screenwriter, director, producer and novelist. He is best known as a member of the comedy team The League of Gentlemen together with Reece Shearsmith, Steve Pemberton and Jeremy Dyson, and as the co-creator, with Steven Moffat, of the BBC drama series Sherlock (2010–2017), in which he played Mycroft Holmes. He has written for and acted in Doctor Who, playing Professor Richard Lazarus and voicing other roles, and appeared as Tycho Nestoris in Game of Thrones.

Early life

Gatiss was born in Sedgefield, County Durham, and grew up opposite the Winterton Hospital, a psychiatric institution where his father worked as an engineer. A lifelong Doctor Who fan, he studied Theatre Arts at Bretton Hall College in Yorkshire, where he met his League of Gentlemen collaborators.

Career

The League of Gentlemen, a dark comedy set in the fictional town of Royston Vasey, won the Perrier Award at the Edinburgh Festival Fringe in 1997 and transferred to BBC radio and then television (1999–2002), winning a BAFTA. Gatiss played numerous characters and co-wrote the series and its 2005 film.

With Steven Moffat, Gatiss created Sherlock, a modern-day adaptation of Arthur Conan Doyle's stories starring Benedict Cumberbatch and Martin Freeman; the show won multiple Emmy and BAFTA awards, and Gatiss's portrayal of Mycroft Holmes became a recurring fan favourite. He also co-created the BBC/Netflix series Dracula (2020) with Moffat.

Gatiss has written numerous Doctor Who episodes and novels, presented BBC documentaries on horror cinema (including A History of Horror, 2010), and written the Lucifer Box series of spy novels. His stage work includes acclaimed performances in Coriolanus and The Motive and the Cue (2023), for which he won the Laurence Olivier Award for Best Actor for his portrayal of John Gielgud.
