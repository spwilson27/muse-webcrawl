SMART (database)

SMART (Simple Modular Architecture Research Tool) is a biological database and web resource for the identification and annotation of protein domains and the analysis of protein domain architectures. It is hosted at the European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL) in Heidelberg, Germany. SMART is widely used in genomics and proteomics to detect domains in protein sequences, combining manually curated hidden Markov models (HMMs) of domain families with tools for exploring combinations of domains within proteins.

Overview

SMART was introduced in 1998 by Jörg Schultz, Richard Copley, Chris Ponting, Peer Bork and colleagues. It initially focused on signalling domains abundant in metazoan proteins, a complement to the Pfam database's broader coverage. The underlying alignment collection has grown to include more than 1,300 domain families, each represented by a curated seed alignment and a profile hidden Markov model built with the HMMER software.

Modes of operation

SMART offers two modes. In Normal mode, searches run against a protein database including per-proteome predicted domain architectures, combined with other signals such as coiled-coil regions, transmembrane segments, low-complexity regions and signal peptides. Genomic mode uses the actual protein sequences of completely sequenced genomes, permitting analysis of domain evolution across species. Users can submit sequences for domain searches, browse domain architectures, view taxonomic distributions of domains, and explore proteins sharing a given architecture.

Applications

SMART is used to characterise newly sequenced proteins, classify protein families, investigate the evolution of multidomain proteins and signalling systems, and design experiments by identifying functional modules. Its manually annotated domain collection and architecture search tools make it a standard reference alongside Pfam, InterPro (of which SMART is a member database), PROSITE and the Conserved Domain Database.
