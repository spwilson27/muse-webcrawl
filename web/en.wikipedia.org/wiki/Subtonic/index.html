Subtonic

In music, the subtonic is the flattened seventh scale degree (♭VII) of a major scale — a whole step below the tonic. It contrasts with the leading tone, the natural seventh degree, which lies only a semitone below the tonic and strongly implies resolution upward to it. In C major, the subtonic is B♭, whereas the leading tone is the natural B.

Usage

The subtonic occurs naturally in the minor scale and in modes such as Mixolydian and Dorian. In popular music and rock, the ♭VII chord built on the subtonic is extremely common, appearing in cadences such as ♭VII–IV–I and the "double plagal" progression, and in the Andalusian cadence of flamenco and rock. Because it lacks the leading tone's pull, music based on the subtonic tends to feel modal and open-ended rather than classically resolved.
