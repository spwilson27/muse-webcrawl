Towel

A towel is a piece of absorbent fabric or paper used for drying or wiping a surface. Towels are ubiquitous in homes, hotels, gyms and hospitals, in forms ranging from hand towels and bath towels to disposable paper towels.

Materials and construction

Most cloth towels are woven from cotton, often in a terry-cloth construction whose uncut loops greatly increase the absorbent surface area. Microfibre and bamboo-fibre towels are modern alternatives; linen was historically common.

Types

  • Bath towel — large towel for drying the body after bathing
  • Hand towel and face towel — smaller sizes for sinks
  • Beach towel — oversized and often brightly patterned
  • Tea towel (dish towel) — for drying dishes in the kitchen
  • Paper towel — disposable absorbent sheet

History

Woven drying cloths are ancient, but the modern terry towel was industrialised in the nineteenth century, with the Turkish town of Bursa long famed for towel production.

In culture

Douglas Adams's The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy famously identifies a towel as the most useful item for an interstellar traveller, inspiring the annual Towel Day on 25 May.
