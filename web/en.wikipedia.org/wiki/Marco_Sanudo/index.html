Marco Sanudo

The Kastro of Naxos, founded by Sanudo

Marco Sanudo (c. 1153 – c. 1227) was a Venetian nobleman, nephew of the Doge Enrico Dandolo, and a participant in the Fourth Crusade. In 1207 he conquered the island of Naxos and several other Cyclades islands and founded the Duchy of the Archipelago (Duchy of Naxos), the most durable of the Latin states established in Greece after the crusade.

Fourth Crusade

Sanudo accompanied his uncle on the Fourth Crusade, which diverted to Constantinople and sacked the city in April 1204. He was present at the election of Baldwin of Flanders as Latin Emperor and served in negotiations and military operations in the Aegean. Dissatisfied with his prospects in the partition of the Byzantine Empire, he obtained permission to conquer islands on his own account.

Conquest of the Cyclades

In 1205–1207, with eight galleys fitted out at his own expense and a company of Venetian adventurers, Sanudo seized Naxos, defeating its Byzantine-Genoese garrison after a siege of the fortress of Apano Kastro. He went on to take or receive the submission of Paros, Antiparos, Milos, Kimolos, Ios, Amorgos, and other islands, distributing many of them as fiefs to his companions — the origins of the lesser lordships of the Cyclades.

Duchy of the Archipelago

Sanudo made the town of Naxos his capital, building its castle (Kastro) and harbour. Although a Venetian, he held his duchy as a vassal of the Latin Emperor rather than of Venice, a status that led to friction with the Republic. His rule was comparatively tolerant: the Orthodox Greek population retained its church hierarchy and many local institutions, and a dual Greek-Latin social order emerged that lasted for centuries. He died around 1227 and was succeeded by his son Angelo Sanudo.
