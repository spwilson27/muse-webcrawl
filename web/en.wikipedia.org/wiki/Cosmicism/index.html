FounderH. P. Lovecraft

Cosmicism is the literary and philosophical philosophy developed by the American writer H. P. Lovecraft (1890–1937) and embodied in his weird fiction and the Cthulhu Mythos. Cosmicism holds that there is no recognisable divine presence in the universe, that humanity is profoundly insignificant in the vastness of existence, and that the cosmos is fundamentally indifferent — or at best impenetrably alien — to human concerns.

Tenets

Lovecraft repeatedly stressed his philosophy of "cosmic indifferentism": the universe neither rewards nor punishes humanity because human values have no cosmic standing. In his stories, characters who glimpse the truth — that Earth was ruled by ancient, godlike extraterrestrial beings and that human civilisation is a recent accident — frequently suffer mental collapse, since the human mind cannot correlate the full contents of reality. Cosmic horror, the genre Lovecraft pioneered, derives its dread not from gore or malevolence but from this vertiginous insignificance and from truths too large to comprehend.

Relation to other philosophies

Cosmicism shares ground with nihilism and pessimism but differs in emphasis: Lovecraft held aesthetic and scientific curiosity to be humanity's best responses, and his materialism was avowedly mechanistic. Some commentators draw parallels with existentialism and with modern discussions of the "pale blue dot" perspective, though cosmicism predates them and is anchored in fiction rather than systematic philosophy.

Influence

Cosmicism shaped later writers of weird fiction and horror — including Thomas Ligotti, who explicitly developed a related pessimism — and permeates modern popular culture through games, film and literature inspired by the Cthulhu Mythos, such as works expanding on Cthulhu, Yog-Sothoth and the Great Old Ones.
