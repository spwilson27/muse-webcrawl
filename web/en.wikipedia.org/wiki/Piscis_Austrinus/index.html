Piscis Austrinus is a constellation in the Southern Celestial Hemisphere. The name is Latin for "the southern fish", in contrast with the larger constellation Pisces, which represents a pair of fishes. Before the 20th century it was also known as Piscis Notius. Its only bright star is Fomalhaut, the 18th-brightest star in the night sky and one of the closest stars to Earth.

History

Piscis Austrinus originated with Babylonian astronomy as the fish SIM.MAH's neighbour, and was one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy in his Almagest. In Greek mythology the fish was associated with the great fish that rescued Aphrodite and Eros from the monster Typhon, and the stream of water poured by nearby Aquarius was said to flow into its mouth.

Notable features

Fomalhaut (Alpha Piscis Austrini), about 25 light-years away, is a young white main-sequence star surrounded by a dusty debris disc; a candidate exoplanet, Fomalhaut b — once celebrated as one of the first directly imaged planets — is now thought to be an expanding dust cloud from a planetesimal collision. The constellation also contains Lacaille 9352, one of the nearest red dwarfs, and the barred spiral galaxy NGC 7314.
