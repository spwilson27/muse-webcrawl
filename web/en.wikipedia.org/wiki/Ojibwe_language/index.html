Ojibwe language

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Ojibwe is spoken around the Great Lakes and across the northern plains.

Ojibwe (also spelled Ojibwa, Ojibway, or Chippewa; native name Anishinaabemowin) is an Indigenous language of North America, belonging to the Algonquian language family. It is spoken by the Ojibwe people in a belt of communities from Quebec westward across Ontario, Manitoba and Saskatchewan in Canada, and south through Michigan, Wisconsin, Minnesota, North Dakota and Montana in the United States. With tens of thousands of speakers across its dialects, it is one of the most widely spoken Indigenous languages in Canada and the United States, though most dialects are endangered as intergenerational transmission declines.

Dialects

Ojibwe comprises a complex of related dialects sometimes treated as separate languages, including Southwestern Ojibwe (Chippewa) in Minnesota and Wisconsin, Northwestern Ojibwe, Eastern Ojibwe, Ottawa (Odawa), Algonquin, and the highly divergent Oji-Cree (Severn Ojibwe) in northern Ontario. Varieties differ in phonology — notably the syncope (vowel deletion) characteristic of Ottawa — and in vocabulary, but form a historically unified continuum.

Features

Ojibwe is a polysynthetic language with complex verb morphology: a single verb can encode subject, object, tense, direction and manner. Nouns are classified as animate or inanimate, a distinction of grammar rather than biology (some plants, stones and ceremonial objects are grammatically animate). The language distinguishes inclusive and exclusive "we", and has a fourth-person (obviative) category for a less topical third person. Word order is relatively free and discourse-driven.

Writing systems

Ojibwe has been written in several systems, including Canadian Aboriginal syllabics in northern communities. The most widely used Roman orthography is the double vowel system developed by Charles Fiero, which writes long vowels doubled (e.g. aa, ii, oo) and is now standard in much teaching and publishing. Language revitalisation efforts include immersion schools, master-apprentice programmes and university courses.
