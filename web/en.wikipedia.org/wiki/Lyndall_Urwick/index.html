Lyndall Urwick

Born3 March 1891, Malvern, Worcestershire, England
Died5 December 1983 (aged 92), Sydney, Australia
OccupationManagement consultant, writer

Lyndall Fownes Urwick MC (3 March 1891 – 5 December 1983) was a British management consultant and business thinker, one of the most influential British exponents of scientific management and administrative theory in the first half of the 20th century. He worked to integrate the ideas of earlier theorists such as Frederick Winslow Taylor and Henri Fayol into a comprehensive theory of management.

Career

Educated at Repton and New College, Oxford, Urwick served in the First World War, winning the Military Cross. After the war he worked at Rowntree's chocolate works in York, then became director of the International Management Institute in Geneva (1928–1933). Returning to Britain, he co-founded the consultancy Urwick, Orr and Partners, which became one of Britain's largest management consulting firms. He later emigrated to Australia.

Ideas

With Luther Gulick, Urwick edited the influential Papers on the Science of Administration (1937), which popularised concepts such as span of control and unity of command. He helped systematise Fayol's elements of management — forecasting, planning, organising, commanding, coordinating and controlling — and wrote extensively on management education and the biography of Taylor. His writing is noted for its strongly normative, principles-based approach.
