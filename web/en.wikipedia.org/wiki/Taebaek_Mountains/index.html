Taebaek Mountains

The Taebaek Mountains (Korean: 태백산맥) are a mountain range that stretches across North Korea and South Korea. They form the main ridge — the "Baekdudaegan" — of the Korean Peninsula, running along the eastern coast from Mount Kumgang in the north to the vicinity of Busan in the south, a distance of about 500 kilometres.

Geography

The range forms the watershed between the East Sea (Sea of Japan) and the rest of the peninsula: the eastern slopes are steep and short, while the western slopes descend more gently, giving rise to Korea's major rivers such as the Han and the Nakdong. Notable peaks include Mount Seorak (1,708 m), Mount Odae (1,563 m) and Mount Taebaek (1,567 m), the source of the Han and Nakdong rivers and a sacred site in Korean tradition. Several national parks — Seoraksan, Odaesan and Sobaeksan among them — protect parts of the range.

Geology and climate

The mountains are composed largely of Precambrian gneiss and Mesozoic granite. Because of their position along the east coast, the Taebaek range intercepts moist air from the East Sea, producing heavy snowfall in winter on the eastern slopes and a rain-shadow effect in the west. Ski resorts such as those around Pyeongchang, host of the 2018 Winter Olympics, lie within the range.
