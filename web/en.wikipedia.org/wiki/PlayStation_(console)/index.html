PlayStation (console)

The original PlayStation (1994)

The PlayStation (abbreviated PS, PS1 or PSX) is a home video game console developed and marketed by Sony Computer Entertainment. It was released in Japan on 3 December 1994, in North America in September 1995 and in Europe later that month, as the first entry in the PlayStation line of consoles.

History

The PlayStation originated in a deal between Sony engineer Ken Kutaragi and Nintendo to supply a sound chip for the Super NES, which expanded into a planned CD-ROM add-on. When Nintendo abruptly cancelled the partnership in 1991, Sony continued independently, producing a 32-bit console built around CD-ROM games, which were cheaper to manufacture than cartridges and could hold far more data. The console was powered by a 33 MHz MIPS-based CPU with a custom graphics processor.

Reception and legacy

The PlayStation was a major commercial success, becoming the first console to sell over 100 million units worldwide (about 102 million). Its library of more than 4,000 games included landmark titles such as Final Fantasy VII, Gran Turismo, Metal Gear Solid and Tomb Raider. Aggressive marketing toward young adults, strong third-party developer support, and the shift to 3D polygon graphics made it a defining system of the fifth generation of consoles. A smaller redesign, the PS One, followed in 2000, and the brand continued with the PlayStation 2.
