Douglas-fir

The Douglas-fir (Pseudotsuga menziesii) is an evergreen conifer species in the pine family, Pinaceae, native to western North America. Despite its common name it is not a true fir (Abies), nor a pine or hemlock; it belongs to its own genus, Pseudotsuga. The species is named for the Scottish botanist David Douglas, who introduced it into cultivation in Britain in 1827, and for Archibald Menzies, who first documented it at Nootka Sound in 1791.

Description

Douglas-fir is one of the world's tallest trees: coast Douglas-firs commonly reach 60–75 m and historical specimens exceeded 100 m, with diameters of several metres. The bark of old trees is thick, deeply furrowed and highly fire-resistant. The needles are soft, flat and spirally arranged, and the pendulous cones — 5–11 cm long — are distinctive for the three-pointed bracts protruding from between the scales, often described as resembling the hind legs and tail of a mouse diving into the cone.

Two principal varieties are recognised: coast Douglas-fir (var. menziesii) of the Pacific coastal forests, faster-growing and much larger, and Rocky Mountain Douglas-fir (var. glauca) of the interior, slower-growing and more cold-hardy.

Distribution and ecology

The species ranges from central British Columbia south through the Cascades and Sierra Nevada to Mexico, and east through the Rocky Mountains. It is a shade-intolerant pioneer that regenerates after fire, and old-growth Douglas-fir forests provide habitat for species including the northern spotted owl and marbled murrelet — a role that made Pacific Northwest old growth a focus of major conservation conflict in the late twentieth century.

Uses

Douglas-fir is among the most important timber trees in the world: its strong, stiff wood is the leading structural lumber in North America (often sold as "Douglas fir–larch" or under the older trade name Oregon pine) and is used for plywood, veneer, poles and heavy construction. It is widely planted for forestry in Europe, New Zealand and elsewhere, and is a popular Christmas tree.
