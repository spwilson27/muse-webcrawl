Arlie Russell Hochschild (born January 15, 1940) is an American professor emerita of sociology at the University of California, Berkeley. She is the author of ten books, including The Managed Heart: The Commercialization of Human Feeling (1983), in which she coined the term "emotional labor," and Strangers in Their Own Land: Anger and Mourning on the American Right (2016), a National Book Award finalist based on five years of fieldwork in Louisiana's Tea Party country.

Concepts

Hochschild's work examines the interplay of emotion, ideology, work and family. Key concepts she developed include emotional labor (the management of feeling to fulfill the emotional requirements of a job), the "second shift" (the labor performed at home after paid work, described in her 1989 book The Second Shift), the "time bind," global care chains, and the "deep story" — a feels-as-if narrative that political groups use to make sense of their world.
