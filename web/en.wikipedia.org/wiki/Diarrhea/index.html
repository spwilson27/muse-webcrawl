Oral rehydration therapy is the mainstay of treatment and has saved tens of millions of lives, especially among children.

Diarrhea (American English), also spelled diarrhoea, is the condition of having at least three loose, liquid, or watery bowel movements in a day. It often lasts for a few days and can result in dehydration due to fluid loss. Signs of dehydration often begin with loss of the normal stretchiness of the skin and irritable behaviour, and can progress to decreased urination, loss of skin color, a fast heart rate, and unresponsiveness.

Causes

The most common cause is an infection of the intestines due to a virus (such as rotavirus or norovirus), a bacterium (such as Campylobacter, Salmonella, or toxigenic E. coli), or a parasite. These conditions are often called gastroenteritis and are usually acquired from food or water contaminated with feces, or directly from another person. Other causes include irritable bowel syndrome, inflammatory bowel disease, malabsorption, lactose intolerance, hyperthyroidism, and medications such as antibiotics and metformin.

Classification

Diarrhea is classified as acute (lasting less than two weeks), persistent (two to four weeks), or chronic (more than four weeks). Mechanistically it may be secretory, osmotic, inflammatory, or due to altered intestinal motility. Dysentery is diarrhea containing visible blood.

Treatment and prevention

Management centres on preventing and correcting dehydration with oral rehydration solution — a mixture of clean water, salt, and sugar — supplemented by zinc in children. Most cases resolve without antibiotics. Prevention includes access to safe drinking water, improved sanitation, hand washing with soap, and rotavirus vaccination; diarrhea remains a leading cause of death in children under five in low-income countries, causing roughly half a million such deaths per year.
