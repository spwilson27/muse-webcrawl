Mehmed Talaat (September 1, 1874 – March 15, 1921), commonly known as Talaat Pasha, was an Ottoman politician and a leading figure of the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP). As Minister of the Interior from 1913 and Grand Vizier from 1917 to 1918, he was one of the "Three Pashas" (with Enver Pasha and Djemal Pasha) who effectively ruled the Ottoman Empire during the First World War. Talaat is regarded as the principal architect of the Armenian genocide, in which an estimated 600,000 to over one million Ottoman Armenians were killed through massacres and deportations ordered by his ministry.

Early life and rise

Born in Kırcaali (in present-day Bulgaria) to a family of modest means, Talaat worked as a telegraph clerk and teacher in Edirne and became an early activist of the Young Turk movement, for which he was imprisoned in the 1890s. After the Young Turk Revolution of 1908 he was elected deputy for Edirne. He rose through the CUP, serving as Minister of Posts and then Minister of the Interior, and after the 1913 coup — the Raid on the Sublime Porte — he became the party's dominant civilian leader.

First World War and the Armenian genocide

As Interior Minister, Talaat directed the wartime internal security apparatus. On April 24, 1915 the arrest of Armenian intellectuals and community leaders in Constantinople began mass deportations and killings across the empire, carried out under the Temporary Law of Deportation of May 1915 administered by his ministry; Talaat's orders and telegrams are central documentary evidence in scholarship on the genocide. He was also involved in measures against Ottoman Greeks and Assyrians. In February 1917 he became Grand Vizier, heading the government until resigning in October 1918 as defeat loomed.

Exile, assassination and trial

In November 1918 Talaat fled Constantinople aboard a German submarine to Berlin, where he lived under the alias Ali Sai. Ottoman courts-martial in 1919 convicted the CUP leaders in absentia and sentenced Talaat to death for the massacres. On March 15, 1921, he was shot dead in Berlin's Charlottenburg district by Soghomon Tehlirian, an Armenian genocide survivor, as part of the Armenian Revolutionary Federation's Operation Nemesis. Tehlirian was tried and acquitted by a German court after testimony about the massacres. Talaat's remains were returned to Istanbul in 1943, where he was reburied with honours at the Monument of Liberty — a commemoration that remains controversial.
