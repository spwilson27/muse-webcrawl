Regions of Sweden

Number: 21 regions (regioner)
Main responsibility: Healthcare
Correspond to: Counties of Sweden
Governed by: Elected regional councils

Sweden's regions (Swedish: regioner) are the self-governing regional authorities responsible principally for publicly funded healthcare. There are 21 regions, corresponding geographically to the country's 21 counties. Each is governed by an elected regional council (regionfullmäktige) and funded largely through regional income taxes.

Responsibilities

Healthcare accounts for the great majority of regional budgets: regions run hospitals, primary care and dental care for residents up to a certain age. Most regions also hold responsibility for regional public transport and regional development, often jointly with municipalities. Region Stockholm, Västra Götaland Region and Region Skåne are the largest by population.

History

The regional tier descends from the county councils (landsting) created in 1862. From the late 1990s, several county councils were reorganised and renamed "regions" with expanded development responsibilities; by 2020 all had adopted the name, when Region Gotland — a municipality with regional duties — completed the set.
