Armillaria mellea

Armillaria mellea, commonly known as the honey fungus, is a species of fungus in the family Physalacriaceae. It is a plant pathogen that causes Armillaria root rot in many trees and shrubs, and it is also edible, though it must be thoroughly cooked.

Description

The fruiting bodies grow in clusters at the bases of trees in autumn. The cap is honey-yellow to brown, 3–13 cm across, often covered with small dark scales near the centre; the gills are white, becoming spotted with age, and the ringed stem is pale above the ring. Black, bootlace-like rhizomorphs spread the fungus through the soil.

Ecology

The mycelium is bioluminescent. Members of the genus can become enormous, long-lived organisms: a related species in Oregon covers several square kilometres and is among the largest organisms on Earth. Control in gardens is difficult and relies on removing infected roots and stumps.
