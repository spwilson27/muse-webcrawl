Principality of Mingrelia

Capital: Zugdidi

Ruled by: House of Dadiani

Period: c. 1557–1867

Status: Principality in western Georgia; Russian protectorate from 1803

The Principality of Mingrelia (Georgian: სამეგრელოს სამთავრო, Samegrelos samtavro), also known as Odishi and Samegrelo, was a historical state in western Georgia, centred on the province of Mingrelia (Samegrelo) on the Black Sea coast. It was ruled by the House of Dadiani.

History

Mingrelia emerged as an autonomous principality as the Kingdom of Georgia fragmented in the 15th and 16th centuries. The Dadiani princes asserted independence from the kings of Imereti around 1557 under Levan I Dadiani. The principality was frequently at war with Imereti, Abkhazia and Guria, and resisted Ottoman pressure along the coast.

Russian protectorate and annexation

In 1803, Prince Grigol Dadiani placed Mingrelia under the protection of the Russian Empire, though the Dadiani retained internal autonomy. After the death of Prince David Dadiani in 1853, his widow Ekaterina governed as regent amid growing Russian interference. During the Crimean War the region was occupied by Ottoman forces. In 1867 Russia deposed the last prince, Niko Dadiani, and formally abolished the principality, incorporating it into the Kutaisi Governorate. The Dadiani palace at Zugdidi remains a landmark of the dynasty.
