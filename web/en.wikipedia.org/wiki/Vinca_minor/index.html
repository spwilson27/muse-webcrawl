Vinca minor

Vinca minor (common names lesser periwinkle and dwarf periwinkle) is a species of flowering plant in the dogbane family Apocynaceae, native to central and southern Europe, from Portugal and France north to the Netherlands and the Baltic States, and east to the Caucasus, and also southwestern Asia in Turkey.

Description

Vinca minor is an evergreen trailing subshrub that roots along its stems to form large colonies, invading woodland and hedgerows. The leaves are opposite, glossy dark green, 2–4.5 cm long, with a leathery texture. The flowers are solitary in the leaf axils, violet-purple (occasionally white in cultivated forms), 2–3 cm in diameter, with five petals, appearing mainly from early spring to mid summer.

Cultivation

The species is widely grown as a groundcover in gardens for its evergreen foliage and spring flowers, thriving in shade where little else grows. Numerous cultivars have been selected for flower colour (white, pale blue, deep purple, burgundy) and variegated foliage. It can be invasive in woodland, notably in parts of North America where it has naturalised extensively.

Chemistry

Like its relative Vinca major and the Madagascar periwinkle Catharanthus roseus, V. minor contains indole alkaloids; it is a source of vincamine, a compound used in some countries as a cerebral vasodilator.
