MicroSD

microSD
Type: Flash memory card
Dimensions: 15 × 11 × 1 mm
Introduced: 2005
Standard body: SD Association

microSD is a removable flash memory card format, the smallest member of the Secure Digital (SD) family. Originally launched by SanDisk as TransFlash in 2004 and adopted by the SD Association in 2005 as microSD, the cards measure just 15 × 11 × 1 mm — about a quarter the size of a standard SD card.

Capacities and variants

The original microSD (SDSC) cards held up to 2 GB. microSDHC cards extended capacity to 32 GB, microSDXC to 2 TB in specification, and microSDUC up to 128 TB. Speed classes (Class 10, UHS Speed Class, Video Speed Class and Application Performance Class A1/A2) describe minimum sequential write speeds and random performance for running apps.

Uses

microSD cards became the standard expandable storage for mobile phones, action cameras, drones, handheld game consoles and single-board computers such as the Raspberry Pi, which boots from one. An adapter allows a microSD card to be used in full-size SD slots.
