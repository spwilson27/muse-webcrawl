Blackpool is a seaside town and unitary authority on the Irish Sea coast of Lancashire, England. It is famous as one of Britain's most enduring holiday resorts, known for Blackpool Tower, its Pleasure Beach amusement park, its tramway, and the annual Blackpool Illuminations. The town's name derives from a historic drainage channel ("the black pool") that ran into the sea near the site of the settlement.

History

In the 18th century Blackpool was a small hamlet whose visitors came to bathe in the sea for their health. The arrival of the railway in 1846 transformed it into a mass resort for the factory workers of industrial Lancashire, especially during wakes weeks when mills closed and whole towns decamped to the coast. Piers were built in the 1860s and 1870s, and the promenade was developed to manage the crowds. In 1894 the 518-foot (158 m) Blackpool Tower, modelled on the Eiffel Tower, opened with its celebrated Tower Ballroom. Blackpool's electric tramway, opened in 1885, was among the first in the world, and its heritage trams still run.

Attractions

Blackpool Pleasure Beach, founded in 1896, remains one of the UK's most-visited tourist attractions; its rides include the Big One roller coaster. The Winter Gardens complex hosts conferences and the British National Dance Championships. Each autumn since 1879 the Illuminations light six miles of promenade. The town also has three piers (North, Central, and South), a zoo, and the Sandcastle Waterpark.

Economy and challenges

Cheap foreign package holidays from the 1960s onward eroded Blackpool's traditional market, and the town faces persistent socioeconomic challenges, ranking among England's most deprived areas on several measures. Tourism still dominates the economy, with roughly 19 million visitors a year before the COVID-19 pandemic, and regeneration efforts focus on the town centre, the Talbot Gateway development, and attracting conferences and events year-round.

Transport

Blackpool is served by Blackpool North and Blackpool South railway stations, with direct services to Manchester, Liverpool, and (since electrification in 2018) London via Preston. The M55 motorway links the town to the national network, and the tramway runs along the Fylde coast to Fleetwood.
