Liverpool Football Club is one of the most successful English clubs in European competition. The club first entered European competition in the 1964–65 European Cup, reaching the semi-finals, and went on to dominate continental football during the late 1970s and early 1980s under managers Bill Shankly, Bob Paisley and Joe Fagan.

History

Liverpool won their first European trophy, the UEFA Cup, in 1973, defeating Borussia Mönchengladbach in the final, and won the competition again in 1976. The club then won the European Cup four times in eight seasons: in 1977 and 1978 under Paisley, in 1981, and in 1984 under Fagan in Rome against Roma. A fifth European Cup followed in the famous 2005 Champions League final in Istanbul, when Liverpool recovered from 3–0 down against AC Milan to draw 3–3 and win on penalties.

The Heysel Stadium disaster of 1985, before the European Cup final against Juventus in Brussels, led to English clubs being banned from Europe for five years, with Liverpool banned for six. The club returned to European competition in 1991. A sixth European Cup arrived in 2019, when Jürgen Klopp's side beat Tottenham Hotspur 2–0 in Madrid, a season after losing the final to Real Madrid in Kyiv.
