Tatami

Tatami (畳) are types of mat used as flooring material in traditional Japanese-style rooms (washitsu). Tatami are made in standard sizes, twice as long as wide, about 0.9 m by 1.8 m depending on the region. In martial arts, tatami are used for training floors, and in modern judo and other competitions the word also denotes foam mats of a standard size.

Traditionally, tatami are made with a core of rice straw (in modern mass production often wood chip boards or polystyrene foam) covered with a surface of woven soft rush straw (igusa), and edged with a cloth or brocade border (heri). The surface gives off a distinctive grassy fragrance when new.

History and layout rules

Tatami developed during the Heian period as movable coverings used by the nobility; by the Muromachi period they covered whole floors in shoin-style rooms, and in the Edo period they became common in ordinary homes. Room sizes in Japanese architecture are still commonly expressed by the number of mats — for example a four-and-a-half mat tea room, or a six-mat bedroom. Auspicious and inauspicious layouts are traditionally observed: the mats should not be arranged so that four corners meet at a single point, an arrangement avoided in formal rooms.

Care and etiquette

Tatami are damaged by shoes and heavy furniture; one walks on them in socks or bare feet. They are cleaned by gentle vacuuming along the grain and are periodically aired, though they can attract mites and mould in humid weather. Because the mats standardize room dimensions, they have influenced Japanese modular construction for centuries.
