Demographics of Canada

The demographics of Canada are characterised by a large, diverse and heavily urbanised population concentrated near the United States border. The 2021 census recorded 36,991,981 people, and the population passed 40 million in 2023, driven mainly by high levels of immigration — among the highest per capita in the world.

Population distribution

More than 80% of Canadians live in urban areas, and the great majority live within 150 km of the US border, in the Windsor–Quebec City corridor and the metropolitan areas of Toronto, Montreal and Vancouver. Ontario is the most populous province (about 38–39% of the population), followed by Quebec, British Columbia and Alberta.

Immigration and ethnic origins

In the 2021 census, about 23% of residents were immigrants — the highest share since Confederation — and recent projections suggest the share could reach roughly a third by the 2040s. Canadians report more than 450 ethnic or cultural origins; the largest single reported origin is "Canadian", followed by English, Scottish, French, Irish, German and Chinese. South Asian, Chinese and Black Canadians are the largest visible-minority groups, and Indigenous peoples (First Nations, Métis and Inuit) made up about 5% of the population.

Languages and religion

English and French are the official languages; about 75% report English as a mother tongue and around 21–22% French, with French predominant in Quebec. Secularisation has been rapid: in 2021, about 53% identified as Christian (down from 67% a decade earlier), roughly 35% reported no religion, and Muslims formed the largest non-Christian group at about 5%.
