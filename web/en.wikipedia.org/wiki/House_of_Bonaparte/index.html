House of Bonaparte

The House of Bonaparte is a former imperial and royal European dynasty founded by Napoleon Bonaparte, originally of Corsican-Italian noble stock (Italian: Buonaparte). At the height of the Napoleonic Wars, members of the family ruled France, Spain, Italy, Holland, Westphalia and Naples.

History

Napoleon crowned himself Emperor of the French in 1804 and placed his siblings on European thrones: Joseph became King of Naples and then of Spain; Louis, King of Holland; Jérôme, King of Westphalia; and his sisters Elisa, Pauline and Caroline ruled Italian states. After Napoleon's fall in 1815 the family was exiled and its members barred from France.

The dynasty returned to power when Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte, son of Louis of Holland, was elected president of the Second Republic in 1848 and became Emperor Napoleon III in 1852, ruling until his capture in the Franco-Prussian War of 1870. Since then the head of the house, styled Prince Napoléon, has been a pretender to the French throne. The claim passed down through Napoléon-Joseph Charles (the Prince Napoléon) and his descendants; the current head is Jean-Christophe, Prince Napoléon.
