Dongola (Arabic: دنقلا, Dunqulā) is a town in northern Sudan, capital of the Northern State, lying on the east bank of the River Nile in the heart of Nubia. The modern town is a market and administrative centre for a fertile riverine strip prized for its date palms, wheat and beans, and it is connected by asphalt road bridges and highways to Khartoum, Wadi Halfa and Port Sudan. It is not to be confused with Old Dongola, the ruined medieval capital of the Christian kingdom of Makuria, which lies some 80 kilometres upstream (south) on the opposite bank of the Nile.

History

The Dongola Reach of the Nile — the comparatively fertile bend between the Third and Fourth Cataracts — has been settled since antiquity and formed part of the Kushite and later Christian Nubian worlds. Old Dongola, founded in the fifth or sixth century AD, became the capital of Makuria, famed for its churches and painted monasteries, and survived as a Christian royal city into the fourteenth century before passing under Muslim rule; a great mosque there began life as a throne hall in 1317. The modern town of Dongola grew under the Turco-Egyptian administration of the nineteenth century and figured in the Anglo-Egyptian reconquest of Sudan: in 1896 Kitchener's expedition advanced through the region, and the "Dongola Expedition" secured the town as the base for the campaign that led to Omdurman in 1898.

Geography and climate

Dongola sits amid one of the hottest, driest inhabited regions on Earth, with summer daytime temperatures regularly exceeding 45 °C, negligible rainfall, and agriculture entirely dependent on Nile irrigation. Palm groves line the riverbanks, while the town is a centre of the Sudanese date harvest. The surrounding desert contains archaeological sites of the Kerma culture and medieval Nubia, excavated by Sudanese and international (notably Polish) missions.

Transport

A modern road bridge crosses the Nile near the town, and paved highways link Dongola south to Khartoum and north toward the Egyptian border at Wadi Halfa; the town has a small airport. Historically the Nile steamers and the desert caravan routes made it the entrepôt of Nubian trade.
