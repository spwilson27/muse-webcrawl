Ski-Doo is the brand name of snowmobiles manufactured by Bombardier Recreational Products (BRP). The first Ski-Doo was launched in 1959 by Joseph-Armand Bombardier of Valcourt, Quebec, and is widely credited with creating the modern snowmobile industry and the sport of snowmobiling as a mass recreation.

History

Bombardier had worked on over-snow vehicles since the 1930s, producing large tracked carriers such as the B7 and B12. His ambition, however, was a lightweight individual machine. The 1959 Ski-Doo — originally to be called the "Ski-Dog," with the now-famous name resulting from a typographical error — paired a small air-cooled engine with a rubber endless track and skis, and became an immediate success for trappers, utility users, and winter recreation.

Development

Through the 1960s and 1970s Ski-Doo models such as the Olympique, TNT, and Blizzard racing sleds helped define the rapidly growing industry. Later innovations included the REV rider-forward chassis (2003), which repositioned the rider over the center of the sled and was widely imitated, and E-TEC direct-injection two-stroke engines.

Today

Ski-Doo remains one of the largest snowmobile brands in the world, competing with Polaris, Arctic Cat, and Yamaha, with model lines spanning mountain, trail, crossover, and utility segments, including the Summit, MXZ, Renegade, and Expedition families.
