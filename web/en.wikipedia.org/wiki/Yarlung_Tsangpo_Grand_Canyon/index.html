The Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon (Chinese: 雅鲁藏布大峡谷) is a canyon formed by the Yarlung Tsangpo River in the Tibet Autonomous Region of China. It is the deepest canyon in the world, and at slightly over 504 km long, it is also one of the longest. Because of its depth — reaching about 6,009 metres from the summit of Namcha Barwa to the river — it substantially exceeds the Grand Canyon in the United States.

Geography

The canyon begins near the town of Mainling and cuts through the eastern Himalaya where the Yarlung Tsangpo makes its Great Bend, swinging in a huge hairpin around the peaks of Namcha Barwa (7,782 m) and Gyala Peri before turning south into India, where the river becomes the Siang and then the Brahmaputra. The river drops some 2,000 metres within the gorge, descending through rapids and waterfalls in a remote, roadless section long known to Western geography only through survey reports and the accounts of explorers such as Frank Kingdon-Ward, who reached the area in 1924–25 searching for the legendary Falls of the Tsangpo.

Nature

Within a short horizontal distance the canyon passes from high alpine terrain to subtropical forest, and it is recognised as a major centre of biodiversity, sheltering Bengal tigers, clouded leopards, takin, red pandas, and many endemic plants. The region lies in Medog and Mainling counties; Medog was the last county in China to gain road access, in 2013.

Exploration and development

The first complete descent of the gorge by kayak expedition was achieved in 1998, and Chinese scientists formally surveyed and publicised its dimensions in the 1990s, establishing its claim as the world's deepest canyon. The lower reaches have been proposed for very large hydroelectric development; a mega-dam project on the lower Yarlung Tsangpo was approved by China in late 2024, with a planned capacity roughly three times that of the Three Gorges Dam, drawing attention and concern downstream in India and Bangladesh.
