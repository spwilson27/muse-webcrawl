Population genetics

Population genetics is the study of genetic variation within populations and of the forces that change allele frequencies: Mutation, natural selection, genetic drift and gene flow. It is a cornerstone of the modern synthesis of Evolution.

Foundations

Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium gives the null model: allele frequencies stay constant without evolutionary forces. R. A. Fisher, J. B. S. Haldane and Sewall Wright founded the field in the 1910s–1930s, reconciling Mendelian inheritance (Gregor Mendel, Punnett square) with continuous traits.

Key measures include heterozygosity, effective population size and linkage disequilibrium; the neutral theory (Motoo Kimura, 1968) proposes most molecular variation is neutral.

Applications

Population genetics informs conservation of endangered species, breeding, forensic genetics, and genome-wide association studies of human disease.
