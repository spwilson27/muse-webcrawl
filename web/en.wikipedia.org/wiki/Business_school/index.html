Business school

Business schools are university-level institutions offering degrees in business administration and management.

A business school is a university-level institution or a constituent part of a university that confers degrees in business administration and management. Such schools typically offer undergraduate programs (such as the Bachelor of Business Administration), master's degrees — most famously the Master of Business Administration (MBA) — and doctoral programs including the PhD and Doctor of Business Administration (DBA).

History

The first business schools appeared in Europe in the early 19th century; ESCP Business School in Paris, founded in 1819, is among the oldest. In the United States, the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania (1881) was the first collegiate business school, followed by the Tuck School at Dartmouth (1900) and Harvard Business School (1908), the latter pioneering the case method of teaching and the first MBA program. After the Second World War, reports by the Ford and Carnegie foundations prompted American schools to adopt a more rigorous, research-based curriculum drawing on economics, statistics and the social sciences.

Degrees and curriculum

Core subjects commonly include accounting, finance, marketing, organizational behaviour, operations management, business ethics, economics and strategy. Programs range from generalist MBAs to specialized master's degrees in finance, marketing, data analytics and other fields, as well as executive education for working managers.

Accreditation and rankings

Major accrediting bodies include AACSB, EQUIS and AMBA; schools holding all three are described as "triple accredited". Business schools are widely compared in published rankings by organizations such as the Financial Times, The Economist and U.S. News & World Report, which weigh factors such as graduate salaries, research output and international diversity.

Criticism

Critics have argued that business education can overemphasize shareholder value and short-term financial performance, that rankings distort institutional priorities, and that management theory taught in schools may be weakly connected to practice.
