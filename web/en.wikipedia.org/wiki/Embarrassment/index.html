Embarrassment

Embarrassment is an emotional state that is associated with moderate to high levels of discomfort, and which is thought to be a self-conscious emotion. It is often associated with shyness and shame, and its physiological manifestations include blushing. The word is derived from the French embarrasser, meaning to block or obstruct.

Psychology

Embarrassment is typically experienced when a person commits (or imagines committing) a socially awkward or frowned-upon act that is witnessed by, or revealed to, others. Usually some amount of loss of honour or dignity is involved, but how much depends on the embarrassment type and the embarrassing act. Embarrassment is similar to shame, except that shame may be felt without an audience being present.

The sociologist Erving Goffman studied embarrassment extensively, treating it as a central phenomenon of social interaction: the flustered individual has projected a definition of the situation that is suddenly contradicted by events, leaving them unsure of how to proceed.

Physiology

A common physical response to embarrassment is blushing, in which blood rushes to the face, neck and ears. This response is involuntary and is mediated by the sympathetic nervous system. Other responses include sweating, stammering, fidgeting and an urge to escape the situation. Charles Darwin discussed blushing in The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals (1872), noting that it seems unique to humans.

Types

Psychologists distinguish several types of embarrassment, including social embarrassment (resulting from a real social transgression), empathetic embarrassment (felt on behalf of another person, sometimes called vicarious embarrassment), and reflexive embarrassment caused merely by being the centre of attention.
