The Rugby World Cup is a men's rugby union tournament contested every four years between the top international teams, organised by World Rugby. The winners are awarded the Webb Ellis Cup, named after William Webb Ellis, the Rugby School pupil credited by legend with inventing the game. The tournament is one of the world's largest and most-watched sporting events.

The first tournament, co-hosted by Australia and New Zealand in 1987, was won by the All Blacks. South Africa's victory on home soil in 1995 — Nelson Mandela presenting the trophy to captain Francois Pienaar — became one of sport's defining images of post-apartheid reconciliation. England's extra-time win in Sydney in 2003, sealed by Jonny Wilkinson's drop goal, remains the only triumph by a northern-hemisphere team.

South Africa defended its title in France in 2023, beating New Zealand 12–11 in the final to become the first team to win four World Cups. The next edition is in Australia in 2027.
