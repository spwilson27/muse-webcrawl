Taba (Arabic: طابا) is a small town in Egypt, located near the northern tip of the Gulf of Aqaba on the Sinai Peninsula, immediately adjacent to the Israeli town of Eilat. It is best known as a resort destination and for the long-running territorial dispute between Egypt and Israel over its sovereignty.

History

Taba lay on the ancient caravan route between Egypt and the Levant. After the 1906 border demarcation between Egypt and Ottoman Palestine, and later under the British Mandate, the precise location of the boundary near Taba became a matter of contention. Israel occupied the Sinai, including Taba, after the Six-Day War of 1967.

Following the Egypt–Israel peace treaty of 1979, Israel withdrew from most of Sinai by 1982, but Taba remained disputed because the two sides disagreed about the location of several boundary pillars. The issue was submitted to international arbitration, and the tribunal ruled largely in Egypt's favour. Israel withdrew from Taba in 1989, and the area was returned to Egyptian control.

Tourism

Today Taba is a resort area with large hotels along the coast and in the nearby Taba Heights development. Its location makes it a gateway for excursions to Eilat, Aqaba in Jordan, and to sites in the Sinai interior such as the Coloured Canyon. The Taba Border Crossing is a major land crossing between Egypt and Israel.
