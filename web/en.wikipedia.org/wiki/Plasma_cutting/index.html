Plasma cutting

Plasma cutting is a process that cuts through electrically conductive materials by means of an accelerated jet of hot plasma. Typical materials cut with a plasma torch include steel, stainless steel, aluminium, brass, and copper. The process is widely used in fabrication, automotive repair, and industrial construction.

Process

A plasma cutter works by sending an electric arc through a gas (such as compressed air, oxygen, or nitrogen) passed through a constricted nozzle. This raises the temperature of the gas to the point where it enters the plasma state. The high-velocity jet melts the workpiece and blows the molten metal away, producing a cut with a narrow kerf. A pilot arc starts the process before the main cutting arc transfers to the workpiece.

Equipment

Systems range from handheld torches powered by inverter power supplies to large CNC cutting tables used in shipbuilding and structural steel fabrication. Plasma cutting is faster than oxy-fuel cutting on thin materials and, unlike oxy-fuel, can cut non-ferrous metals. Related processes include plasma etching and plasma spraying.

Safety

The process produces intense ultraviolet light, sparks, noise, and metal fumes, requiring eye protection, hearing protection, ventilation, and fire precautions.
