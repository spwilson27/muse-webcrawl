Gwich'in

RegionsNorthwest Territories, Yukon (Canada); Alaska (United States)
LanguageGwich'in (Athabaskan)
Traditional livelihoodCaribou hunting, fishing

The Gwich'in are an Indigenous people of the northern interior of Canada and Alaska, traditionally inhabiting the Mackenzie Delta region, the Yukon Flats, and the drainages of the Porcupine and Peel rivers. Their name means "people of the caribou flats" in their Athabaskan language.

Culture and livelihood

For millennia the Gwich'in have depended on the Porcupine caribou herd for food, clothing, and tools, and their culture and spirituality centre on the herd's calving grounds on the coastal plain of the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge, which they call "the sacred place where life begins".

Modern communities

Gwich'in communities in Canada include Inuvik, Aklavik, Fort McPherson, Tsiigehtchic, and Old Crow; in Alaska they include Arctic Village, Venetie, Fort Yukon, and Beaver. The Gwich'in Tribal Council and the Gwich'in Steering Committee represent their interests, including long-standing opposition to oil development on the calving grounds.
