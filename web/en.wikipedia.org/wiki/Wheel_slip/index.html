Wheel slip

Wheel slip (or wheelslip) occurs when the driving wheels of a locomotive or other rail vehicle rotate faster than corresponds to the vehicle's speed, because the applied tractive effort exceeds the available adhesion between wheel and rail. It is most common when starting a heavy train, on steep gradients, or when rails are contaminated by rain, ice, oil or crushed leaves.

Effects and control

Uncontrolled slipping wastes power, can stall the train, and may damage rails and wheel treads through heat build-up ("rail burns"). Drivers historically controlled slip by reducing power and applying sand to the railhead via sandbox equipment. Modern traction systems use electronic wheel-slip protection (WSP) that measures axle speeds and rapidly modulates power or braking, analogous to anti-lock braking in road vehicles. A related phenomenon, wheel slide, occurs under braking when a wheel locks and slides along the rail, producing flat spots on the tyre.
