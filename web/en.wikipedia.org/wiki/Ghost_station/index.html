Ghost station

A disused platform passed through by trains

A ghost station is a disused metro or railway station through which trains pass without stopping, often with the station still visible, dimly lit and abandoned, to passengers. Stations become ghost stations because of falling patronage, line closures, network reorganisation, war or political circumstances.

Notable examples

The best-known examples are the Geisterbahnhöfe of the Berlin U-Bahn and S-Bahn during the division of the city (1961–1989): stations in East Berlin on lines carrying West Berlin trains were closed, guarded and passed through without stopping, reopening after reunification. London Underground has many closed stations, such as Aldwych and Down Street, sometimes used for filming. The Paris Métro has several stations never opened or long closed, including Haxo and Croix-Rouge; New York's City Hall subway station, closed in 1945, is a celebrated ornate example.

Some ghost stations were never opened at all, having been built in anticipation of development that never came, while others have later been revived when lines were extended or demand returned.
