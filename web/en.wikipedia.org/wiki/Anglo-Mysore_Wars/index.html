Anglo-Mysore Wars

Date1767–1799
LocationSouth India
CombatantsSultanate of Mysore vs. British East India Company and allies
ResultBritish victory; Mysore partitioned and reduced

The Anglo-Mysore Wars were a series of four wars fought during the last three decades of the 18th century in India between the Sultanate of Mysore, ruled successively by Hyder Ali and his son Tipu Sultan, and the British East India Company, usually allied with the Maratha Empire and the Nizam of Hyderabad. The wars ended with Mysore's defeat and partial annexation.

The four wars

The First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–1769) ended with Hyder Ali dictating peace at the gates of Madras. In the Second War (1780–1784) Hyder inflicted major defeats on the British, notably at Pollilur, before the conflict closed with the Treaty of Mangalore restoring the status quo ante. The Third War (1790–1792), provoked by Tipu's attack on Travancore, ended with the Treaty of Seringapatam, by which Tipu ceded roughly half his territory and surrendered two of his sons as hostages. In the Fourth War (1798–1799), Governor-General Lord Wellesley invaded to pre-empt Tipu's alliance with Napoleonic France; Tipu was killed defending his capital, Seringapatam, on 4 May 1799.

Aftermath and legacy

Mysore was partitioned between the Company and the Nizam, while a reduced princely state was restored to the Wodeyar dynasty under British paramountcy. The wars are notable for Mysore's use of iron-cased rockets, which inspired the British Congreve rocket, and for Tipu's reputation as the "Tiger of Mysore", one of the most formidable Indian opponents of British expansion.
