Asymmetrical aircraft

Asymmetrical aircraft are aircraft whose components are not arranged symmetrically about a vertical plane through the fuselage. Designers have occasionally adopted asymmetric layouts to solve specific problems, such as improving pilot visibility, reducing torque effects, or accommodating unusual propulsion arrangements.

Examples

The best-known example is the German Blohm & Voss BV 141 reconnaissance aircraft of the Second World War, whose crew gondola was mounted on the starboard side of the wing while the engine and tail boom sat on the port side. Other examples include the Rutan Boomerang, a twin-engine general aviation design by Burt Rutan in which one engine is mounted in the fuselage and the other in an offset boom, intended to make engine failure handling safer.

Rationale

Asymmetry can counteract propeller torque and P-factor, give an unobstructed field of view or fire, or allow a sensor or weapon to be placed without interference, at the cost of more complex aerodynamics and structures.
