Carlisle is a cathedral city in Cumbria, England, close to the Scottish border. It is the county town of Cumbria and the largest settlement in the county, standing at the confluence of the rivers Eden, Caldew, and Petteril. Nicknamed the "Great Border City", Carlisle has been a strategic fortress and contested border town for two thousand years, and it is the main shopping, administrative, and transport centre for a large rural region reaching from the Lake District to the Scottish Borders.

History

The Romans founded Luguvalium around AD 72–73 near the western end of what became Hadrian's Wall, the great frontier fortification begun in AD 122, whose line passes just north of the city. After the Roman withdrawal the settlement persisted as a British and then Anglo-Saxon town before William II built Carlisle Castle in 1092. For centuries the city changed hands amid Anglo-Scottish wars; it was the centre of the Border Reivers' raiding country, and its castle held Mary, Queen of Scots in 1568. Carlisle was the last English city besieged in Britain, during the Jacobite rising of 1745, when Bonnie Prince Charlie's forces took the city before their defeat.

The modern city

In the industrial era Carlisle became a major railway junction — Carlisle Citadel station remains one of England's busiest non-London stations — and a centre for textiles, engineering, and biscuits (Carr's of Carlisle). Landmarks include the medieval cathedral, founded in 1133 and notable for its great east window, the castle keep, and the Tullie House museum. The city suffered severe flooding from Storm Desmond in December 2015, prompting major new flood defences. Carlisle United is the city's professional football club.
