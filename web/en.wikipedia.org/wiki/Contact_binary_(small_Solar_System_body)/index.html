Contact binary (small Solar System body)

A contact binary is a small Solar System body — an asteroid, comet nucleus or Kuiper belt object — consisting of two components that have come into physical contact, forming a single peanut- or snowman-shaped body.

Formation

Contact binaries are thought to form when two mutually orbiting bodies lose angular momentum (through collisions, outgassing or tidal effects) and gently merge at low velocity, preserving their individual identities as lobes joined by a narrow neck. Alternatively, a single rubble-pile object spinning up may deform into a bilobate shape.

Examples

  • 486958 Arrokoth — visited by New Horizons in 2019; the most pristine known example.
  • 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko — comet nucleus imaged by Rosetta; rubber-duck shaped.
  • 25143 Itokawa — asteroid sampled by Hayabusa.
  • 4179 Toutatis — near-Earth asteroid with a bilobed radar shape.
