Fubing system

The fubing system (Chinese: 府兵制; pinyin: fǔbīng zhì) was a military organization used in China from the Western Wei dynasty (c. 550s) through the Sui and into the early Tang dynasty. It relied on self-supporting farmer-soldiers organized in territorial militia units called fu, or garrisons.

Structure

Under the system, soldiers were drawn from registered households granted land under the equal-field system. In peacetime they farmed their plots; periodically they rotated to the capital for guard duty and training, and in wartime they were mobilized for campaigns, supplying much of their own equipment. At its height under the Tang, several hundred garrisons existed, concentrated near the capital region of Guanzhong.

Decline

The fubing system eroded in the 7th and 8th centuries as the equal-field land system broke down, campaigns grew longer and more distant, and service became an onerous burden that soldiers evaded. It was effectively replaced by recruited professional armies under military governors, a shift formalized around 749 when the militia registers ceased to be maintained.
