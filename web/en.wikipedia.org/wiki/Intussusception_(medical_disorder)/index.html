Intussusception (medical disorder)

Specialty: Paediatrics, gastroenterology
Symptoms: Abdominal pain, vomiting, "redcurrant jelly" stool
Usual onset: 6–18 months of age

Intussusception is a medical condition in which part of the intestine folds into the section immediately ahead of it, like a collapsing telescope. It typically involves the small bowel folding into the large bowel at the ileocaecal junction. The folding obstructs the bowel and can compromise its blood supply, making intussusception a paediatric emergency.

Signs and symptoms

Intussusception most often affects infants between six and eighteen months old. The classic presentation is sudden, intermittent, severe abdominal pain causing the child to draw up the knees and cry, separated by periods of calm. Vomiting is common, and later a characteristic stool mixed with blood and mucus, described as "redcurrant jelly", may appear. A sausage-shaped mass may be palpable in the abdomen.

Diagnosis and treatment

Ultrasound is the preferred diagnostic test and may show a "target" or "doughnut" sign in cross-section. In stable children, the intussusception can often be reduced non-operatively with an air or liquid enema under image guidance. Surgery is required if enema reduction fails, if the bowel has perforated, or if there are signs of peritonitis. In adults, intussusception is uncommon and usually has a pathological lead point, such as a tumour, which guides treatment.
