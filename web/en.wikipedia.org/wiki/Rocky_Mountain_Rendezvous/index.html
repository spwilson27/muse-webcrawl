Rocky Mountain Rendezvous

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Rocky Mountain Rendezvous was an annual gathering, held from 1825 to 1840, at various locations in the Rocky Mountains of the western United States. It was a trading fair where mountain men, trappers, fur trading companies, and Native Americans met to exchange beaver pelts and other furs for supplies, equipment, whisky, and manufactured goods.

History

The rendezvous system was devised by William Henry Ashley of the Rocky Mountain Fur Company, whose first rendezvous was held in 1825 on Henry's Fork of the Green River in present-day Wyoming. Rather than maintaining permanent trading posts, supply caravans from St. Louis brought goods to a prearranged location each summer, and trappers arrived with the season's furs. The system lowered overhead costs and dominated the Rocky Mountain fur trade for fifteen years.

Character and decline

Rendezvous were raucous occasions lasting days or weeks, marked by drinking, gambling, contests, and feasting, as well as serious trading. Attendees included celebrated mountain men such as Jedediah Smith, Jim Bridger, Kit Carson, and Thomas Fitzpatrick. The last rendezvous was held in 1840; the decline of the beaver population, falling demand as silk replaced beaver felt in hat-making, and the growth of the emigrant trade along the Oregon Trail ended the system.
