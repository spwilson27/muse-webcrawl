Gwalior is a major city in the central Indian state of Madhya Pradesh, about 343 km south of Delhi. It is dominated by its great hilltop fort and was the capital of the princely state ruled by the Scindia dynasty.

History

Legend derives the name from the sage Gwalipa, who is said to have cured a local chief of leprosy. Gwalior Fort, held in turn by Gurjara-Pratiharas, Kachchhapaghatas, Tomars, the Mughals and the Marathas, was famously described by Babur as "the pearl in the necklace of the forts of Hind". Under Man Singh Tomar (1486–1516) the fort's Man Mandir palace was built. In the 18th century the Scindias made Gwalior their capital; the city figured in the Revolt of 1857, when Rani Lakshmibai died fighting nearby in 1858.

Culture

Gwalior is a historic centre of Hindustani classical music: the Gwalior gharana is the oldest khayal gharana, and the court nurtured the legendary singer Tansen, whose tomb stands in the city. The annual Tansen Music Festival is held here each December.
