Wedding dress of Lady Diana Spencer

DesignersDavid and Elizabeth Emanuel
Worn byLady Diana Spencer (later Diana, Princess of Wales)
OccasionWedding of Prince Charles and Lady Diana Spencer, 29 July 1981, St Paul's Cathedral
MaterialsIvory silk taffeta and antique lace; 10,000 pearls and mother-of-pearl sequins

The wedding dress of Lady Diana Spencer was worn by Lady Diana Spencer at her wedding to Charles, Prince of Wales (now Charles III), on 29 July 1981 at St Paul's Cathedral, London. Designed by the young British designers David and Elizabeth Emanuel, it became one of the most famous and closely guarded dresses in fashion history, epitomising the voluminous, romantic bridal style of the early 1980s and influencing wedding fashion worldwide.

Design and creation

The gown was made of ivory silk taffeta with a fitted bodice, huge puff sleeves trimmed with bows, and a full skirt decorated with hand-embroidered lace, sequins and some 10,000 pearls and mother-of-pearl sequins. The lace included antique Carrickmacross lace that had belonged to Queen Mary. Its 25-foot (7.6 m) train was the longest yet seen at a British royal wedding. The veil of ivory silk tulle was held by the Spencer family tiara. Because the dress had to fit into the glass coach for the journey to St Paul's, it emerged visibly creased — a much-commented-upon detail of the day. Security was extreme: the Emanuels destroyed sketches, used a code name, and made a backup dress in case details leaked (the little-known spare, later sold at auction, differed in neckline detail).

Reception and legacy

Watched by a global television audience of around 750 million, the dress defined the "meringue" bridal silhouette of the 1980s and launched a generation of imitations. It has been exhibited many times, touring for years with the "Diana: A Celebration" exhibition, and remains a reference point in discussions of royal fashion; in 2021 it was displayed at Kensington Palace in the "Royal Style in the Making" exhibition, on loan from Diana's sons. The dress's value and fame are routinely cited alongside those of the wedding dresses of Grace Kelly and Catherine Middleton.
