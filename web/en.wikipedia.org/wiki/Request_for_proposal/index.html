Request for proposal

A request for proposal (RFP) is a document issued by an organisation — frequently a government agency or large company — soliciting bids from potential suppliers to provide a product or service. Unlike a simple price quotation, an RFP describes the buyer's needs, requirements and evaluation criteria, and invites vendors to propose how they would meet them, so that bids can be compared on quality, approach and experience as well as price. RFPs are a standard instrument of formal procurement.

Process

A typical RFP process involves defining requirements, issuing the document to selected vendors or publicly, holding a question-and-answer period, receiving written proposals by a deadline, scoring them against a weighted evaluation matrix, shortlisting, presentations or demonstrations, and contract negotiation. Related instruments include the request for quotation (RFQ), used where specifications are standardised and price dominates; the request for information (RFI), a preliminary market-sounding exercise; and the request for tender (RFT) or invitation to bid, common in construction and public works.

Contents

An RFP commonly specifies background on the buyer, scope of work, deliverables, timetable, technical and functional requirements, vendor qualifications, pricing format, terms and conditions, submission instructions, and the criteria and weighting by which proposals will be judged.

Criticism

RFPs are criticised for being costly and slow for both sides, for favouring incumbents or vendors skilled at proposal-writing over those best able to do the work, and for freezing requirements prematurely. Some sectors have moved towards lighter-weight competitive processes, pilots and framework agreements.
