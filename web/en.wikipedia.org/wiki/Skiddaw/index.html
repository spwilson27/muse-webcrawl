Skiddaw

Skiddaw
Elevation: 931 m (3,054 ft)
Prominence: 709 m
Location: Lake District, Cumbria, England
Range: Northern Fells

Skiddaw is a mountain in the Lake District National Park in Cumbria, England. At 931 metres (3,054 ft) it is the sixth-highest mountain in England, and the highest of the Northern Fells. Its large, smooth slopes dominate the skyline north of the town of Keswick, and it can be seen from as far away as the hills of southern Scotland on a clear day.

The mountain is composed of Skiddaw Slate (the Skiddaw Group of Ordovician mudstones and siltstones), which weathers into the rounded, scree-covered slopes characteristic of the massif. The main summit ridge runs roughly south to north, with subsidiary tops including Little Man, Carl Side, and Longside Edge. Ullock Pike and Bakestall are among the outlying fells of the group.

Ascents

Skiddaw is regarded as one of the most straightforward of the great Lakeland fells to climb: a well-graded bridleway, once used by pony tours from Keswick, leads up the southern slopes from the Latrigg car park to the summit, which is marked by a trig point and a toposcope. Alfred Wainwright described it as one of the finest viewpoints in the district.

The name is of uncertain origin; it may derive from Old Norse elements meaning a crag or archer's hill, or from Cumbric words for "shoulder."
