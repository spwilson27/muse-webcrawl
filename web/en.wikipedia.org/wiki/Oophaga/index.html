Oophaga

Oophaga is a genus of poison dart frogs (family Dendrobatidae) found in Central and South America, from Nicaragua through Colombia to Ecuador. The genus was established in 1994 by Grant and colleagues, after species formerly placed in Dendrobates were recognised as a distinct lineage. The name means "egg eater", referring to the tadpoles' diet.

Reproduction

Oophaga species are notable for obligate egg-feeding: the female carries each tadpole to a small water-filled bromeliad axil and returns periodically to lay unfertilised nutritive eggs, which are the tadpole's only food. The best-known species is the strawberry poison-dart frog, Oophaga pumilio, famous for its many colour morphs across the Bocas del Toro archipelago of Panama.

Species

Recognised species include O. pumilio, O. histrionica, O. speciosa, O. sylvatica, O. granulifera, O. arborea, O. lehmanni, O. occultator and O. vicentei. Several are threatened by habitat loss and collection for the pet trade.
