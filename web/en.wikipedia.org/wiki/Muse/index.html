Muse

In Greek mythology, the Muses (Ancient Greek: Μοῦσαι) are the inspirational goddesses of literature, science and the arts. They are traditionally nine sisters, daughters of Zeus and Mnemosyne: Calliope (epic poetry), Clio (history), Erato (lyric poetry), Euterpe (music), Melpomene (tragedy), Polyhymnia (hymns), Terpsichore (dance), Thalia (comedy) and Urania (astronomy).

Poets and artists from Homer and Hesiod onward invoked the Muses at the start of their works. Their homes were said to be Mount Olympus and Mount Helicon in Boeotia, and shrines to them gave rise to the word "museum".

Legacy

In modern usage, a muse is a person who inspires an artist. The Muses remain common figures in literature, art and popular culture.
