Siderography

Siderography is a 19th-century printing process for multiplying engraved images using steel. Developed by Jacob Perkins and refined by others in the 1820s, an engraving made on a soft steel plate is hardened, then transferred by pressure onto a soft steel roller or plate, which is in turn hardened and used to produce many identical printing plates.

Uses

The process revolutionised banknote and stamp printing, allowing a single master engraving to generate numerous plates without loss of detail — valuable both for production volume and for security against forgery, notably at firms such as Perkins, Bacon in London.

Decline

Siderography was gradually displaced by electrotyping and later photomechanical plate-making in the early 20th century.
