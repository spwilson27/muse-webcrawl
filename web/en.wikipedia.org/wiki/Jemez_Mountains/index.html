Jemez Mountains

Location: Northern New Mexico, United States
Highest point: Chicoma Mountain, 3,524 m (11,561 ft)
Geology: Volcanic field and calderas

The Jemez Mountains are a volcanic mountain range in Rio Arriba, Sandoval, Rio Grande and Los Alamos counties, New Mexico, in the southwestern United States. They form the southernmost extension of the Rocky Mountains in the state and are notable for the Valles Caldera, one of the world's largest and best-preserved volcanic calderas.

Geology

The range was built by volcanism associated with the Rio Grande rift over the past roughly 14 million years. Two enormous caldera-forming eruptions — about 1.6 and 1.25 million years ago — each ejected hundreds of cubic kilometres of material, forming the Toledo and Valles calderas; the younger eruption deposited the Upper Bandelier Tuff, in which the dwellings of Bandelier National Monument were later carved. Much of the Valles Caldera is now protected as the Valles Caldera National Preserve, managed by the National Park Service, and features resurgent domes, hot springs, and rich grazing valleys.

Human history and recreation

The mountains are the homeland of the Jemez (Walatowa) Pueblo people and contain numerous ancestral Pueblo sites, including the ruins at Jemez Historic Site and Bandelier. The town of Los Alamos and Los Alamos National Laboratory occupy the Pajarito Plateau on the range's eastern flank. The Jemez Mountains Trail is a National Scenic Byway, and the area offers hot springs, the Jemez Soda Dam, Battleship Rock, and ski and hiking terrain in the Santa Fe National Forest.
