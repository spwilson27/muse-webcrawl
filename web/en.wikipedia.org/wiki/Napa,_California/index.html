Napa is the largest city and county seat of Napa County, California, United States. It is the principal city of the Napa metropolitan area and a major gateway to the Napa Valley wine region. Its population was 79,246 at the 2020 census.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Patwin people. The city's name derives from a Patwin village called napato. Napa was founded in 1847 by Nathan Coombs, who laid out the town on land granted under the Rancho Entre Napa Mexican land grant, near where explorer George C. Yount had settled. It became the county seat and was incorporated in 1872.

In the later 19th century Napa prospered on silver mining at nearby Knoxville, leather tanning (the Sawyer Tanning Company), prune and walnut orchards, and riverboat trade via the Napa River. Prohibition closed the valley's wineries in 1920, but the city remained an agricultural and shipping centre. In the late 20th century Napa redeveloped its flood-prone downtown; the Napa River flood protection project, begun in 1998, created terraced parkland along the river, and the Oxbow Public Market (2008) and BottleRock Napa Valley music festival helped turn the city into a dining and tourism destination in its own right.

Geography and climate

Napa lies on the banks of the tidal Napa River, about 50 miles (80 km) north of San Francisco, in a Mediterranean climate of warm, dry summers and mild, wet winters. On 24 August 2014, the city was struck by a magnitude 6.0 earthquake, the strongest in the San Francisco Bay Area since 1989, which damaged numerous historic downtown buildings; most were repaired and retrofitted.

Economy and sights

Tourism and wine dominate the economy, though Napa is more residential than the up-valley resort towns. Attractions include the Napa Valley Opera House (opened 1880, restored 2003), the di Rosa Center for Contemporary Art, the Culinary Institute of America at Copia, and the Silverado Trail corridor of wineries.
