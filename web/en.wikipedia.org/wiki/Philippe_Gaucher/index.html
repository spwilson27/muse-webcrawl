Philippe Gaucher

Philippe Charles Ernest Gaucher
Born: 26 July 1854, Champigny, France
Died: 25 January 1918 (aged 63), Paris, France
Nationality: French
Occupation: Physician, dermatologist
Known for: First description of Gaucher's disease

Philippe Charles Ernest Gaucher (26 July 1854 – 25 January 1918) was a French physician and dermatologist. In 1882, while still a medical student, he published the first description of the disorder that now bears his name, Gaucher's disease.

Life and career

Gaucher was born in Champigny (Yonne) and studied medicine in Paris. In his doctoral thesis, De l'épithélioma primitif de la rate, hypertrophie idiopathique de la rate sans leucémie (1882), he described a 32-year-old woman with an enlarged spleen whose tissue contained large, distinctive cells — later known as Gaucher cells — and interpreted the condition as a primary epithelioma (cancer) of the spleen. Only decades later was the condition recognised as an inherited lysosomal storage disorder caused by deficiency of the enzyme glucocerebrosidase.

Gaucher pursued a career in dermatology and syphilology in Paris, becoming a professor and a leading figure in French dermatology; he founded the journal Annales des maladies vénériennes in 1906. He served as a medical officer during the First World War and died in Paris in 1918.

Legacy

Gaucher's name is permanently attached to Gaucher's disease, the most common lysosomal storage disorder, characterised by the accumulation of glucocerebroside in macrophages of the spleen, liver and bone marrow. His original case description remains a classic of medical literature, notable for the accuracy of its clinical and pathological observations despite its mistaken interpretation of the underlying cause.
