Jezebel

Jezebel was a Phoenician princess, the daughter of Ethbaal, king of Sidon, who became queen of the northern kingdom of Israel as the wife of King Ahab (9th century BCE), according to the Books of Kings in the Hebrew Bible. The biblical narrative portrays her as the driving force behind the introduction of the worship of Baal and Asherah into Israel and the persecution of the prophets of Yahweh, making her name a lasting byword for wickedness and idolatry.

Biblical account

Ahab's marriage to Jezebel was a diplomatic alliance with Phoenicia. Jezebel maintained hundreds of prophets of Baal and Asherah at her table and ordered the killing of the prophets of Yahweh, prompting the prophet Elijah's contest with the prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel. In the story of Naboth's vineyard, Jezebel arranged for Naboth to be falsely accused of blasphemy and stoned to death so that Ahab could seize his vineyard, drawing down Elijah's prophecy that dogs would eat Jezebel's flesh by the wall of Jezreel.

Death

After Ahab's death and the reigns of her sons Ahaziah and Jehoram, the usurper Jehu killed Jehoram and came to Jezreel. Jezebel, having painted her eyes and adorned her head, watched from a palace window and taunted him. Jehu ordered her eunuchs to throw her down; she was cast from the window, trampled by horses, and her body was largely devoured by dogs, so that only her skull, feet and palms remained for burial — fulfilling, in the narrator's view, Elijah's prophecy.

Legacy

In later tradition "Jezebel" became a generic term for an immoral or scheming woman, a usage echoed in the Book of Revelation, where a prophetess at Thyatira is condemned under the name. Modern scholarship reads the biblical account critically, as hostile propaganda of the Yahwistic editors; some historians note that Jezebel's patronage of her ancestral religion was unremarkable for an ancient queen, and feminist writers have reinterpreted her as a strong foreign queen demonised by the text. A seal bearing the name YZBL has sometimes been associated with her, though the identification is disputed.
