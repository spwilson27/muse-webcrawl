Transient-voltage-suppression diode

A transient-voltage-suppression (TVS) diode is an electronic component used to protect electronics from voltage spikes induced on connected wires. The device operates by shunting excess current when the induced voltage exceeds the avalanche breakdown potential. It is a clamping device that suppresses all overvoltages above its breakdown voltage. Like all clamping devices it automatically resets when the overvoltage dissipates, but absorbs much more of the transient energy internally than a similarly rated crowbar device.

Characteristics

A TVS diode may be either unidirectional or bidirectional. A unidirectional device operates as a rectifier in the forward direction like any other avalanche diode, but is made and tested to handle very large peak currents. A bidirectional TVS diode is represented by two mutually opposing avalanche diodes connected in series with one another and connected in parallel with the circuit to be protected. TVS diodes can respond to fast overvoltages within the picosecond range, though practical response is limited by package inductance.

Key parameters

  • Reverse standoff voltage (VRWM) — the maximum voltage at which the device conducts minimal leakage current.
  • Breakdown voltage (VBR) — the voltage at which the device begins to conduct significantly.
  • Clamping voltage (VC) — the voltage across the device at its rated peak pulse current.
  • Peak pulse power/current — the maximum transient the device can survive, commonly specified for the standard 10/1000 μs waveform.

Applications

TVS diodes are used to protect sensitive electronics against electrostatic discharge (ESD), lightning-induced surges, inductive load switching and other voltage transients, on data and signal lines, power supplies, automotive electronics, and industrial equipment.

Comparison with other devices

Compared with metal-oxide varistors (MOVs), TVS diodes clamp faster and at more precisely defined voltages, but handle less total energy. Gas discharge tubes handle higher energies but respond more slowly. The devices are often used together in staged protection schemes.
