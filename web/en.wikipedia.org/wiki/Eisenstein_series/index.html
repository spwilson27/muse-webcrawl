Eisenstein series

[Hero image placeholder: Eisenstein series domain colouring]

Eisenstein series, named after the German mathematician Gotthold Eisenstein, are particular modular forms with infinite series expansions that may be written down explicitly. Although they were originally defined for the modular group, Eisenstein series also appear in the theory of automorphic forms and in the spectral theory of automorphic representations, where they play a fundamental role.

Definition

Let τ be a complex number with strictly positive imaginary part. The holomorphic Eisenstein series G2k(τ) of weight 2k, where k ≥ 2 is an integer, is defined by the lattice sum

G2k(τ) = Σ(m,n) ≠ (0,0) 1 / (m + nτ)2k

The series converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of the upper half-plane and defines a modular form of weight 2k. Its Fourier expansion at the cusp is expressed in terms of the Bernoulli numbers and divisor sums:

G2k(τ) = 2ζ(2k) + (2πi)2k/(2k−1)! · Σn≥1 σ2k−1(n) qn,  q = e2πiτ

Examples

The lowest-weight cases involve ζ(4) = π4/90 and ζ(6) = π6/945. The normalized Eisenstein series E4 and E6 satisfy the relation E43 − E62 = 1728Δ, where Δ is the modular discriminant. The ring of modular forms for the full modular group is generated by E4 and E6.

The case of weight two

The series for k = 1, G2, converges only conditionally; with a suitable summation prescription it yields the quasimodular form E2, which is not modular but appears in the theory of elliptic functions, the Weierstrass ℘-function, and Ramanujan's identities for derivatives of Eisenstein series.

Generalizations

Eisenstein series have been generalized to other groups, notably in Langlands' theory of Eisenstein series for reductive groups, where they are built by summing induced representations over rational points and are central to the spectral decomposition of L2 spaces of automorphic forms.
