Laurus nobilis

Laurus nobilis
Common names: Bay laurel, bay tree, sweet bay
Family: Lauraceae
Origin: Mediterranean region

Laurus nobilis is an aromatic evergreen tree or large shrub of the laurel family, Lauraceae, native to the Mediterranean region. Its leaves — the culinary herb bay leaf — are used in cooking across many cuisines.

Leaves of the bay laurel.

Description

The tree grows 10–18 m tall, with smooth bark and lanceolate, glossy dark-green leaves 6–12 cm long. It is dioecious, bearing small pale-yellow flowers in spring on separate male and female plants, followed on females by small black berry-like fruits.

Cultural significance

In Greek mythology the laurel was sacred to Apollo, and in classical antiquity a wreath of laurel leaves crowned victors in athletic and poetic contests — the origin of terms such as "poet laureate" and "baccalaureate", and the expressions "to rest on one's laurels".
