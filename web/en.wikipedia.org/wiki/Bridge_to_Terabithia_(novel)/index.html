Bridge to Terabithia (novel)

Author: Katherine Paterson

Illustrator: Donna Diamond

Publisher: Thomas Y. Crowell Co.

Publication date: October 21, 1977

Pages: 208

Awards: Newbery Medal (1978)

Bridge to Terabithia is a children's novel by American author Katherine Paterson, published in 1977. It tells the story of the friendship between Jess Aarons, an artistic fifth-grade boy from a poor rural family, and Leslie Burke, the imaginative new girl at school, who together create the imaginary kingdom of Terabithia in the woods near their homes. The novel won the Newbery Medal in 1978 and is regarded as a classic of children's literature.

Plot

Jess Aarons trains all summer to be the fastest runner in fifth grade, but on the first day of school he is beaten by Leslie Burke, a newcomer from a well-off, intellectual family. Despite initial resentment, the two become close friends. Across a creek in the woods — reached by swinging on an old rope — they invent Terabithia, a magical kingdom where they reign as king and queen and escape their troubles at school and home.

One rainy morning, while Jess is away on a trip to Washington, D.C., with his teacher, Leslie attempts to cross the swollen creek alone; the rope breaks and she drowns. The remainder of the novel follows Jess's grief, guilt, and eventual acceptance, culminating in his building a bridge across the creek and introducing his younger sister May Belle to Terabithia.

Background

Paterson wrote the novel after her son David's best friend, an eight-year-old girl, was struck and killed by lightning. The book's frank treatment of death was unusual for children's fiction of its time. It has frequently appeared on lists of challenged and banned books, chiefly over its subject matter and language, yet remains widely taught in schools.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted as a PBS television film in 1985 and as a Walt Disney Pictures feature film in 2007, directed by Gábor Csupó and starring Josh Hutcherson and AnnaSophia Robb. A stage musical adaptation has also been produced.
