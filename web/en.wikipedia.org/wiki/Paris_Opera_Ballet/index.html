The Paris Opera Ballet (French: Ballet de l'Opéra national de Paris) is a French classical ballet company that is an integral part of the Paris Opera. It is the oldest national ballet company, founded in 1669 under Louis XIV—growing out of the Académie Royale de Danse of 1661—and many European and international ballet companies trace their origins to it. The company is based at the Palais Garnier and also performs at the Opéra Bastille.

History

Ballet itself developed at the French court from Italian Renaissance models; the Paris Opera stage professionalised it, at first with all-male casts (women joined in 1681). In the 19th century Paris was the capital of Romantic ballet, premiering La Sylphide (1832) and Giselle (1841). In the 20th century Serge Lifar revived the company, and Rudolf Nureyev's directorship (1983–1989) reshaped its hierarchy and repertoire. Directors since have included Patrick Dupond, Brigitte Lefèvre, Benjamin Millepied (2014–2016) and Aurélie Dupont (2016–2022); José Martínez became director of dance in 2022.

Hierarchy and school

Dancers are ranked—quadrilles, coryphées, sujets, premiers danseurs and, at the summit, étoiles, appointed by name. Most dancers graduate from the Paris Opera Ballet School (founded 1713, now at Nanterre), long known for housing its pupils—the petits rats of Degas's paintings and sculptures.
