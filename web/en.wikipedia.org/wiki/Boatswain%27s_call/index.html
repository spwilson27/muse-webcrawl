Boatswain's call

[Image placeholder: Boatswain's call]

A boatswain's call, also called a boatswain's pipe or bosun's whistle, is a pipe or non-diaphragm type whistle used on naval ships by a boatswain (bosun). It is a high-pitched instrument whose calls are used to pass commands and render honours aboard ship, since its shrill tone can be heard over the noise of the sea and weather.

History

The pipe has been used on English warships since at least the 15th century and became the badge of office of the boatswain. In the age of sail, calls signalled evolutions such as "hoist away", "away boats" and "pipe down"; the call also developed ceremonial uses, including piping senior officers aboard — the origin of the phrase "piping aboard".

Use

The instrument consists of a narrow tube (the gun), a spherical buoy, a flat keel and a shackle. Different notes and trills are produced by varying the hand position and breath. Calls are still used in navies including the Royal Navy, the Royal Canadian Navy and the United States Navy for ceremonies and routine orders.
