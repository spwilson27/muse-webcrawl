Henry Philibert Gaspard Darcy (10 June 1803 – 3 January 1858) was a French engineer who made several important contributions to fluid dynamics, including Darcy's law for flow in porous media and the Darcy–Weisbach equation for flow resistance in pipes.

Career

Darcy studied at the École Polytechnique and the École des Ponts et Chaussées in Paris. As chief engineer for the department of Côte-d'Or, he designed the famous water supply system of his native Dijon, delivering clean water through some 12 km of pressurised aqueduct — a project celebrated in his treatise Les fontaines publiques de la ville de Dijon (1856). In its appendix he described experiments on water flow through sand filters, from which Darcy's law was derived.

Legacy

The unit of permeability, the darcy, is named after him. His experimental work on pipe flow produced the friction-factor relationships generalised by Julius Weisbach in the Darcy–Weisbach equation.
