Resource sharing

Resource sharing is the practice of making resources available to multiple users, organisations or systems rather than dedicating them to a single user. The term is used in several fields, including library science, computing and economics.

In libraries

In library and information science, resource sharing refers to the cooperative arrangements through which libraries lend materials to one another, most notably through interlibrary loan. Consortia of libraries share catalogues, acquisitions and storage so that patrons of one library can access the collections of many. Union catalogues such as WorldCat record the holdings of thousands of libraries, enabling discovery and requesting of items across institutions. Resource sharing developed from nineteenth-century cooperative cataloguing and was greatly extended by computer networks from the 1960s onward.

In computing

In computing, resource sharing describes the allocation of hardware and software resources — processors, memory, storage, network bandwidth and peripherals — among multiple processes or users. Time-sharing systems of the 1960s allowed many users to work interactively on a single mainframe. In modern networks, file sharing, printer sharing and peer-to-peer systems all rely on the controlled distribution of shared resources, while cloud computing extends the model by pooling very large collections of servers.

In economics

In economics, the "sharing economy" refers to platforms that let individuals share underused assets such as rooms, vehicles and tools with others, typically for a fee arranged through an intermediary service.
