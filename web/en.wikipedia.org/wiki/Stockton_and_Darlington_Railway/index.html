Stockton and Darlington Railway

The Stockton and Darlington Railway (S&DR) was a railway company that operated in north-east England from 1825 to 1863. The world's first public railway to use steam locomotives, its first line connected collieries near Bishop Auckland to Darlington and Stockton-on-Tees.

Stockton and Darlington Railway
Opened: 27 September 1825
Engineer: George Stephenson
Locomotive: Locomotion No. 1
Gauge: 4 ft 8 in (later standard gauge)

Opening

Promoted by Edward Pease and engineered by George Stephenson, the line opened on 27 September 1825. Stephenson's Locomotion No. 1 hauled a train of coal wagons and a specially built passenger coach, Experiment, watched by huge crowds — an event celebrated as the birth of the modern railway.

Operations

The railway primarily carried coal to the Tees for shipment, but also developed passenger services. It pioneered many practices, employed Timothy Hackworth, and in 1863 merged into the North Eastern Railway.

Legacy

The 2025 bicentenary (Rail 200) celebrated its heritage; surviving sites include the Head of Steam museum in Darlington and the Skerne Bridge, depicted on the £5 note.
