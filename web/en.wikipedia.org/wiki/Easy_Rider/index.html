Easy Rider is a 1969 American independent road drama film directed by Dennis Hopper and starring Hopper and Peter Fonda, with a breakthrough performance by Jack Nicholson. A landmark of countercultural cinema and a touchstone of the New Hollywood era, it tells of two bikers who, after smuggling cocaine from Mexico to Los Angeles, ride across the American South and Southwest toward New Orleans for Mardi Gras, encountering the era's hopes and hostilities along the way.

Plot

Wyatt (Fonda) and Billy (Hopper) complete a cocaine deal and hide the proceeds in the fuel tank of Wyatt's stars-and-stripes Harley-Davidson chopper. Riding east, they visit a commune, are jailed in a small town, and are bailed out by George Hanson (Nicholson), a hard-drinking ACLU lawyer who joins them. George is beaten to death in the night by local men hostile to the long-haired travellers; Wyatt and Billy continue to New Orleans, visit a brothel, and take LSD in a cemetery. Heading for Florida, they are shot by two men in a pickup truck — the film ends with Wyatt's burning motorcycle, seen from the air. Wyatt's earlier remark, "We blew it," frames the journey as a failed search for freedom.

Production

The film was made independently for under $400,000, shot largely on location with a small crew by cinematographer László Kovács. Its soundtrack of contemporary rock — Steppenwolf's "Born to Be Wild" and "The Pusher", The Byrds, The Band, Jimi Hendrix and Roger McGuinn — was revolutionary: spending on music rights exceeded much of the rest of the budget, and the compilation soundtrack album became a bestseller. Easy Rider premiered at the Cannes Film Festival in May 1969, where Hopper won the prize for best first work.

Reception and legacy

An enormous commercial success, earning roughly $60 million worldwide, the film demonstrated that low-budget films aimed at young audiences could make money, helping to inaugurate the "New Hollywood" period in which studios gave directors greater creative control. It was nominated for Academy Awards for Best Supporting Actor (Nicholson) and Best Original Screenplay, and in 1998 was added to the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically and aesthetically significant. The "Captain America" chopper became one of the most famous motorcycles in the world; a surviving bike sold at auction in 2014 for $1.35 million.
