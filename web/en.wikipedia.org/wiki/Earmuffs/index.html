Earmuffs
Uses: Thermal warmth, hearing protection
Invented: 1873 (thermal, Chester Greenwood)

Earmuffs are accessories worn over the ears either for warmth in cold weather or for hearing protection against harmful noise. Thermal earmuffs consist of two insulated cups joined by a band; acoustic earmuffs use sound-absorbing foam in rigid cups to reduce noise exposure.

History

Thermal earmuffs were patented in 1873 by Chester Greenwood of Maine, then aged fifteen, who built them with wire, beaver fur and velvet with help from his grandmother and a local tinsmith. Greenwood's design was manufactured in Farmington, Maine for decades, including for American soldiers in the First World War.

Hearing protection

Acoustic earmuffs are rated by their noise reduction rating (NRR) and are widely used in industry, shooting and aviation. Electronic variants amplify quiet sounds while capping loud ones. Combined with earplugs they provide "double protection" for extremely loud environments.
