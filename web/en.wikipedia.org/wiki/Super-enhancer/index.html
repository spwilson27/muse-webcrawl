Super-enhancer

In genetics, a super-enhancer (SE) is a region of the mammalian genome comprising multiple enhancers that is collectively bound by an array of transcription factor proteins and drives expression of genes that define a cell's identity. Super-enhancers are much larger than typical enhancers — spanning roughly 8–20 kilobases or more — and are densely loaded with the Mediator complex, BRD4, histone acetylation (notably H3K27ac), and cell-type-specific "master" transcription factors.

Discovery and definition

The concept was introduced in 2013 by Richard A. Young's laboratory at the Whitehead Institute, in parallel papers analysing mouse embryonic stem cells and several other cell types. Ranking enhancers by their Mediator (or H3K27ac) ChIP-seq signal revealed a small number of exceptionally large, high-signal domains separated from typical enhancers by an inflection point in the rank plot. A few hundred super-enhancers per cell type were found, associated with key pluripotency genes such as Oct4, Sox2 and Nanog, and with lineage-defining genes in differentiated cells.

Function

Super-enhancers are thought to assemble high local concentrations of transcriptional machinery — in current models, phase-separated condensates (see syncytin-independent discussion under biomolecular condensates) — at genes governing cell state. They are unusually sensitive to perturbation: partial inhibition of general transcriptional coactivators such as BRD4 (by the BET inhibitor JQ1) or CDK7 preferentially suppresses super-enhancer-driven genes.

Disease relevance

Disease-associated genetic variants from genome-wide association studies are enriched in super-enhancers of disease-relevant cell types. In cancer, tumour cells acquire super-enhancers at oncogenes such as MYC; chromosomal rearrangements, focal amplifications and viral integration can create new super-enhancers, a phenomenon called super-enhancer hijacking. This dependency has made BET and CDK7 inhibitors candidates for cancer therapy. Whether super-enhancers are a genuinely distinct regulatory class, or simply the extreme of a continuum of enhancer clusters, remains debated.
