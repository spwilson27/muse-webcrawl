Earth-616 is the fictional universe in which most Marvel Comics titles take place, and the primary continuity within Marvel's larger multiverse. The vast majority of Marvel's superheroes — including Spider-Man, the X-Men, the Avengers and the Fantastic Four — live on Earth-616, a world closely resembling the real one but populated by superhumans, mutants, alien races and hidden civilisations.

Origin of the name

The designation first appeared in print in Marvel UK's The Daredevils #7 (1983), in a Captain Britain story written by Alan Moore; the term is commonly credited to Moore or to editor Dave Thorpe. Several explanations for the number have been offered, including that it was chosen as an arbitrary, unglamorous alternative to designations like DC Comics' "Earth-One", and a folk association with 616 as a variant of the Number of the Beast. The name was popularised in handbooks and reference works such as the Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe.

Within the multiverse

Alternate Marvel realities carry their own numbers: the Ultimate Marvel imprint was set on Earth-1610, and the Marvel Cinematic Universe films are designated Earth-199999 (though the MCU's own productions have used "616" internally for their main timeline). After the 2015 Secret Wars storyline destroyed and restored the multiverse, the main Marvel Universe was technically renumbered Earth-616 in reference works retained the familiar designation, with the reconstructed universe sometimes called the Eighth Cosmos.

Characteristics

Earth-616 mirrors real-world geography and history but diverges through the presence of superheroes since the Second World War, fictional locations such as Latveria, Wakanda and Genosha, and a "sliding timescale" that keeps characters' origins perpetually recent while their publication history stretches back to 1961 and beyond.
