Provençal cuisine

The cuisine of Provence, in south-eastern France, is a Mediterranean cooking tradition built around olive oil, garlic, tomatoes, herbs and vegetables, with seafood prominent along the coast. It differs from much of French cuisine in its generous use of olive oil in place of butter, its intense flavours and its relative lightness.

Characteristic dishes

Signature preparations include bouillabaisse, the saffron-scented fish stew of Marseille served with rouille; ratatouille, a stew of aubergine, courgette, peppers and tomato; aïoli, a garlic mayonnaise served with vegetables, fish and eggs; tapenade, a paste of olives, capers and anchovies; soupe au pistou, a vegetable and bean soup finished with basil-garlic pistou; and daube provençale, beef braised in red wine. Salade niçoise comes from Nice, at Provence's eastern edge.

Ingredients and flavours

Herbes de Provence — typically thyme, rosemary, oregano, savory and lavender — season grilled meats and stews. Olives and olive oil from Les Baux and Nyons, garlic, anchovies, saffron and the region's rosé wines are staples.

Recognition

Provençal cooking was popularised internationally in the 20th century by writers such as Elizabeth David and by cookbooks celebrating the Mediterranean diet, of which it is a classic expression.
