The Three-Body Problem (Chinese: 三体) is a hard science fiction novel by Chinese author Liu Cixin. It is the first book in the Remembrance of Earth's Past trilogy, followed by The Dark Forest (2008) and Death's End (2010). The title refers to the three-body problem of orbital mechanics. The English translation by Ken Liu won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 2015, the first translated novel to do so.

Plot summary

During China's Cultural Revolution, astrophysicist Ye Wenjie, disgraced after her father's persecution and death, is recruited to the secret Red Coast Base, which searches for extraterrestrial life. She makes contact with the planet Trisolaris, a world in a chaotic three-sun system whose inhabitants, facing extinction, resolve to invade Earth. In the present day, nanomaterials researcher Wang Miao is drawn into the investigation of a wave of suicides among scientists and into an immersive virtual-reality game, "Three Body," that simulates the Trisolaran world's unpredictable eras. The novel ends with humanity learning that the Trisolaran fleet is en route — a journey of some 450 years — and that the aliens have deployed "sophons," proton-sized supercomputers, to sabotage Earth's fundamental physics research.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted into a Chinese television series (2023) and Netflix's English-language series 3 Body Problem (2024), developed by David Benioff, D. B. Weiss, and Alexander Woo.
