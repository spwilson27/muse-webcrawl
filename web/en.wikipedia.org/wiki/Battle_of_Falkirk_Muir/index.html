Battle of Falkirk Muir

The Battle of Falkirk Muir, fought on 17 January 1746 near Falkirk, Scotland, was the last significant Jacobite victory of the Rising of 1745. The Jacobite army of Charles Edward Stuart defeated a government force under Lieutenant General Henry Hawley.

Background and battle

After retreating from England, the Jacobites besieged Stirling Castle. Hawley marched from Edinburgh to relieve it and encamped at Falkirk, underestimating his opponents. On 17 January the Jacobites advanced onto the high ground of Falkirk Muir. Hawley hastily deployed in worsening weather, with wind and rain driving into his men's faces. His dragoons charged and were repulsed; the Jacobite Highland charge then swept away much of the government infantry, though part of the government line stood firm and withdrew in order to Falkirk and then Edinburgh. Darkness and storm ended the pursuit.

Aftermath

The Jacobites failed to exploit the victory — Stirling Castle held out and the siege was abandoned as the Duke of Cumberland advanced north. The Jacobite army retreated into the Highlands, where it was destroyed three months later at the Battle of Culloden.
