Hafnian

In mathematics, the Hafnian of a symmetric matrix is a function that generalises the concept of the permanent. It was named by Eduardo R. Caianiello in honour of Hafnia, the Latin name for Copenhagen, where Niels Bohr worked.

Definition

For a 2n × 2n symmetric matrix A, the Hafnian is the sum over all perfect matchings (partitions of the index set into n unordered pairs) of the product of the corresponding matrix entries. For an odd-sized matrix the Hafnian is zero. Unlike the determinant, all terms have positive sign.

Applications

The Hafnian appears in combinatorics, in the theory of Pfaffians — to which it is closely related, since the square of the Pfaffian relates to the Hafnian for antisymmetric matrices — and in quantum optics. In Gaussian boson sampling, output probabilities of photonic quantum devices are proportional to Hafnians of submatrices, and computing Hafnians is #P-hard, which underpins claims of quantum computational advantage.
