The Battle of the Dunes, also known as the Battle of Dunkirk, was fought on 14 June 1658 (4 June in the Julian calendar then used in England) during the Franco-Spanish War and the Anglo-Spanish War. A French army under the Viscount Turenne, with a contingent of troops of the English Commonwealth under Sir William Lockhart, besieging Dunkirk, defeated a Spanish relief army commanded by Don Juan of Austria, in which the exiled English royalist force under the Duke of York (the future James II) also fought.

Background

By the Treaty of Paris (1657), Oliver Cromwell allied England with France against Spain, promising English troops and a fleet in return for the transfer of Dunkirk and Mardyck to England. In May 1658 Turenne besieged Dunkirk, and the Spanish army of Flanders advanced to relieve it, taking up positions among the sand dunes east of the town.

The battle

Turenne advanced at dawn, using the falling tide to move along the beach. On the allied left, English New Model Army regiments stormed a large dune held by Spanish tercios, fighting uphill in a fierce hand-to-hand action. The Spanish right was driven in, and when the French attacked elsewhere along the line the Spanish army collapsed. The royalist English regiments and their Spanish allies suffered heavy losses, while the Great Condé, commanding the Spanish cavalry, covered the retreat.

Aftermath

Dunkirk surrendered on 24 June and was handed over to the English Commonwealth, as promised. The battle helped bring the Franco-Spanish War to a close with the Treaty of the Pyrenees in 1659. Charles II sold Dunkirk back to France in 1662.
