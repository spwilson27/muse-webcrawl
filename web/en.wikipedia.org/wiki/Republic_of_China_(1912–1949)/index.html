The Republic of China (ROC) was a republican state in East Asia that governed mainland China from 1912, following the Xinhai Revolution and the overthrow of the Qing dynasty, until 1949, when its government retreated to Taiwan during the closing stages of the Chinese Civil War. The period is conventionally divided into the Beiyang government (1912–1928), the Nationalist government centred on Nanjing (1927–1949), and the wartime Chongqing government during the Second Sino-Japanese War.

History

Founding and the Beiyang era

The Republic was proclaimed on 1 January 1912 in Nanjing, with Sun Yat-sen as provisional president. Power soon passed to Yuan Shikai in Beijing; after Yuan's death in 1916 the country fragmented among rival military cliques during the Warlord Era.

Nanjing decade

The Kuomintang (KMT), reorganised by Sun and led after his death by Chiang Kai-shek, launched the Northern Expedition (1926–1928), nominally reunifying China under the Nationalist government at Nanjing. The ensuing "Nanjing decade" (1927–1937) saw administrative modernisation, industrial growth, and escalating conflict with the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and Japanese expansion.

War and civil war

Full-scale war with Japan began in 1937; the capital moved to Chongqing, and China fought as one of the Allies until Japan's defeat in 1945. War with the CCP resumed almost immediately. By 1949 the Communists had won control of the mainland and proclaimed the People's Republic of China in Beijing on 1 October 1949. The ROC government relocated to Taipei, Taiwan, where the Republic of China continues to function today.

Government and politics

The Republic's political theory rested on Sun Yat-sen's Three Principles of the People (nationalism, democracy, and people's livelihood) and a five-branch constitution modelled partly on Western separation of powers. In practice, KMT one-party "political tutelage" prevailed on the mainland.
