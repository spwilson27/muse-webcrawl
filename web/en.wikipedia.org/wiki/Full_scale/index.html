Full scale

Full scale is a term used in several related senses in engineering, instrumentation and measurement:

Instrumentation

In instrumentation, full scale (often abbreviated FS) refers to the maximum value that an instrument can measure or display — the upper limit of its measuring range. For an analogue meter it corresponds to full scale deflection (FSD), the point at which the pointer reaches the end of the scale. Quantities such as accuracy are frequently specified as a percentage of full scale: for example, an instrument with an accuracy of ±1% of full scale on a 0–100 V range may be in error by up to ±1 V at any point in the range. In analogue-to-digital conversion, full scale denotes the input level that produces the maximum digital output code, and signal levels are commonly expressed in dBFS (decibels relative to full scale) in digital audio.

Modelling and testing

A full-scale model or test article is one built at a scale of 1:1 — the actual size of the real object — as opposed to a reduced scale model. Full-scale testing, for example of aircraft structures, vehicles in crash tests, or prototypes in wind tunnels large enough to hold them, avoids the scaling effects (such as Reynolds number mismatch) that complicate the interpretation of small-scale experiments.

Other uses

  • In audio engineering, "full scale" is the reference maximum level (0 dBFS) in a digital system.
  • In drawings and cartography, a full-scale drawing reproduces the subject at actual size.
  • In military and planning contexts, "full-scale" is used figuratively, as in a "full-scale war" or "full-scale operation", meaning one conducted at maximum extent or intensity.
