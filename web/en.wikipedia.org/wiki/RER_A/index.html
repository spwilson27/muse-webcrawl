RER A

The RER A is one of five lines in the Réseau Express Régional (RER), a hybrid commuter rail and rapid transit system serving Paris and its Île-de-France suburbs. The line runs from east to west across the Paris region, connecting branches in the east at Marne-la-Vallée–Chessy (serving Disneyland Paris) and Boissy-Saint-Léger with western branches to Saint-Germain-en-Laye, Cergy-le-Haut, and Poissy. It is operated jointly by RATP and SNCF.

History

The central section of the line was built in stages beginning in the 1960s as the flagship of the RER project, with the full east–west crossing of Paris completed in 1977 when the Châtelet–Les Halles interchange opened. Extensions reached Marne-la-Vallée in 1980 and the Disneyland resort in 1992.

Usage

RER A is among the busiest urban rail lines in the world, carrying around a million passengers per weekday. Its central stations — Châtelet–Les Halles, Gare de Lyon, Auber, Nation, and Charles de Gaulle–Étoile — connect with numerous Métro lines. Chronic overcrowding prompted the introduction of double-deck trains (MI 09 stock) and continues to drive investment in capacity and the parallel relief offered by the westward extension of Métro Line 14 and the future RER E extension.

Stations

The line has 46 stations. Through central Paris trains run in a deep tunnel; in the suburbs they use existing surface alignments, with trains operated by RATP in the core and by SNCF on some outer branches.
