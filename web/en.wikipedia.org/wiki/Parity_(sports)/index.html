Parity (sports)

In sports, parity is the state in which competing teams have roughly equal strength, so that outcomes are unpredictable and many teams have a realistic chance of winning a championship. Leagues deliberately engineer parity in the belief that competitive balance sustains fan interest.

Mechanisms

Tools used to promote parity include salary caps, revenue sharing, luxury taxes, and draft systems that award the earliest picks to the weakest teams, as in the NFL Draft. The National Football League is frequently cited as a high-parity league, with frequent turnover among playoff teams.

Debate

Critics argue that excessive parity produces mediocrity and that dynasties can also drive interest; supporters counter that hope for all fan bases is the foundation of a healthy league. Parity is harder to achieve in leagues without caps, such as many European football leagues, where wealthy clubs dominate.
