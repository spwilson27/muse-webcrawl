False alarm

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A false alarm, also called a nuisance alarm, is the deceptive or erroneous report of an emergency, causing unnecessary panic or bringing resources (such as emergency services) to a place where they are not needed. False alarms may occur with residential and industrial burglar alarms, fire alarms, natural disaster warnings, and other devices such as personal SOS alarms.

False alarms can also signal a faulty system or device. Frequent false alarms may cause alarm fatigue and lead people to ignore real alarms, sometimes with tragic results. Related to this is the cry-wolf effect, in which repeated false alarms reduce confidence in warning systems.

Types

Fire alarms

False fire alarms are commonly caused by cooking fumes, steam, dust, aerosols, insects inside detectors, or deliberate misuse of manual call points. In many jurisdictions, knowingly raising a false fire alarm is a criminal offence.

Burglar alarms

Most activations of intruder alarms are false, often triggered by user error, animals, or poorly installed sensors. Many police forces have adopted verified-response policies or charge fees for repeated false callouts.

Emergency warning systems

On 13 January 2018, an emergency alert was erroneously issued in Hawaii warning of an incoming ballistic missile attack. The alert, sent during a shift change at the Hawaii Emergency Management Agency, caused widespread panic and took 38 minutes to correct. Investigations attributed the error to human mistake compounded by a confusing drill interface.

Consequences

False alarms waste the time and money of emergency services, delay responses to genuine emergencies, and can cause injury during unnecessary evacuations. In healthcare, excessive false clinical alarms contribute to alarm fatigue among staff, a recognised patient-safety hazard.
