Ratha Yatra

Ratha Yatra (Odia: ରଥଯାତ୍ରା), also rendered as Rath Yatra or the Car Festival, is a Hindu festival associated with the deity Jagannath (a form of Krishna). It is celebrated most famously at Puri in the Indian state of Odisha, where the images of Jagannath, his elder brother Balabhadra and his sister Subhadra are carried in procession from the Jagannath Temple to the Gundicha Temple on three huge, newly constructed wooden chariots (ratha), pulled through the streets by thousands of devotees.

The Puri festival

The festival falls in the Hindu month of Ashadha (June–July). The three chariots are built anew each year from specified woods and are decorated in distinctive colour schemes: Jagannath's chariot, Nandighosa, has sixteen wheels and red-and-yellow cloth; Balabhadra's Taladhwaja has fourteen wheels and red-and-green cloth; Subhadra's Darpadalana has twelve wheels and red-and-black cloth. The deities remain at the Gundicha Temple for about a week before the return journey, the Bahuda Yatra.

The festival is one of the oldest and largest Hindu festivals, described in medieval texts such as the Skanda Purana, and attracts well over a million pilgrims. During the procession the king of Puri ceremonially sweeps the road before the chariots with a golden broom, a ritual known as Chhera Panhara.

Other celebrations

Ratha Yatras are held at Jagannath temples throughout India — notably at Mahesh, Ahmedabad, Hyderabad and Dhamrai in Bangladesh — and, since the late 1960s, by the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON) in major cities worldwide, including London, New York, Los Angeles and Toronto. The immense scale of the Puri chariots gave English the word "juggernaut".
