Step response

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The step response of a system in a given initial state consists of the time evolution of its outputs when its control inputs are Heaviside step functions. In electronic engineering and control theory, the step response is the time behaviour of the outputs of a general system when its inputs change from zero to one in a very short time. The concept can be extended to the abstract mathematical notion of a dynamical system using an evolution parameter.

Characterisation

For a linear time-invariant system the step response reveals the essential dynamic behaviour: its final (steady-state) value, how quickly it approaches that value, and whether it overshoots or oscillates on the way. Standard performance measures include rise time, settling time, percentage overshoot and steady-state error. Second-order systems are classified as underdamped, critically damped or overdamped according to their damping ratio, ζ.

Relation to the impulse response

For linear systems, the step response is the time integral of the impulse response, and the impulse response is the derivative of the step response. Knowledge of either response, together with the transfer function, completely characterises the input–output behaviour of the system.
