The Snowdon Mountain Railway (Welsh: Rheilffordd yr Wyddfa) is a narrow gauge rack and pinion mountain railway in Gwynedd, north-west Wales. It is a tourist railway that travels for 4.7 miles (7.6 km) from Llanberis to the summit of Snowdon, the highest peak in Wales.

The SMR is the only public rack and pinion railway in the United Kingdom, and after more than 100 years of operation it remains a popular tourist attraction, carrying more than 130,000 passengers annually. The line is owned and operated by Heritage Great Britain, operators of several other tourist attractions in the United Kingdom.

History

The railway was constructed between December 1894, when the first sod was cut by Mrs Ellis — wife of the assistant estate manager — and February 1896, at a total cost of £63,800 (equivalent to £9.25 million in 2023). The first train ran on 6 April 1896, but a tragic accident on the opening day, in which one passenger died after jumping from a derailed carriage, led to the line's closure for a year.

Route

The line starts at Llanberis station, at the foot of Snowdon. Trains are propelled up the mountain by a single carriage pushed by a steam or diesel locomotive equipped with pinions that engage with the rack rail laid between the running rails. The journey passes stations at Waterfall, Hebron, Halfway, Rocky Valley and Clogwyn before reaching the summit station, beside the Hafod Eryri visitor centre, opened in 2009.

Locomotives

The railway operates a fleet of steam locomotives built by the Swiss Locomotive and Machine Works between 1895 and 1923, named after Welsh princes and local themes — L.A.D.A.S., Enid, Wyddfa, Snowdon Ranger, Moel Siabod, Padarn and Eryri — supplemented by diesel locomotives acquired from 1986 onwards.
