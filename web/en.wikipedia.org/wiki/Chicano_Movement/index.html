Chicano Movement

The Chicano Movement (El Movimiento) was a civil rights and cultural movement of the 1960s and 1970s through which Mexican Americans — redefining the once-pejorative term "Chicano" as a badge of pride — demanded farm workers' rights, bilingual education, voting and political power, land restitution, and an end to police brutality and discrimination. It was one of the major social movements of the postwar United States.

Strands of the movement

Farm workers

In 1965 César Chávez and Dolores Huerta's National Farm Workers Association (later the United Farm Workers) joined the Delano grape strike in California, launching a national boycott that won union contracts in 1970 and made the UFW's black eagle a national symbol.

Land and politics

In New Mexico, Reies Tijerina's Alianza Federal de Mercedes campaigned for the recovery of Spanish and Mexican land grants lost after the Mexican–American War, culminating in the 1967 Tierra Amarilla courthouse raid. In Texas, José Ángel Gutiérrez founded La Raza Unida Party (1970), which won local elections in Crystal City. In Denver, Rodolfo "Corky" Gonzales's Crusade for Justice hosted the 1969 National Chicano Liberation Youth Conference, which produced El Plan Espiritual de Aztlán, a manifesto of Chicano nationalism.

Education and the walkouts

Students demanded bilingual education, Chicano studies and an end to discriminatory schooling. The 1968 East Los Angeles walkouts ("blowouts") brought thousands of students into the streets, and the 1969 Plan de Santa Barbara led to Chicano studies programmes and MEChA chapters in universities across the Southwest.

Arts and legacy

The movement generated a flourishing of muralism, theatre (Luis Valdez's El Teatro Campesino), literature and the Chicano art movement. Although the organised movement waned by the late 1970s, it permanently reshaped Latino politics, education and identity in the United States, and the 1970 Chicano Moratorium against the Vietnam War in Los Angeles — where journalist Rubén Salazar was killed — remains a landmark event.
