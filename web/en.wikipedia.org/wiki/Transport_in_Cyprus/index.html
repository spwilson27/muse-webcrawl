Transport in Cyprus

Transport in Cyprus is dominated by road transport, since the island has no operating railways. Cyprus has a modern network of motorways linking its main cities, two international airports in the government-controlled area (Larnaca and Paphos), and major seaports at Limassol and Larnaca. Traffic drives on the left, a legacy of British rule.

Roads

The motorway network connects Nicosia with Limassol (A1), Larnaca (A2/A3), Paphos (A6), and the Ayia Napa resort area. Public transport is provided mainly by buses; urban and intercity bus services were reorganised in 2010 and again in subsequent years, and Cyprus has one of the highest rates of private car ownership in Europe. There are no toll roads in the Republic of Cyprus.

Air and sea

Larnaca International Airport is the island's principal gateway, with Paphos International Airport serving the west. Ercan International Airport in the north is recognised only by Turkey. The port of Limassol is the largest port and the main centre for passenger and cargo shipping; Cyprus is also a major ship-management and flag state, with one of the largest merchant fleets in the world.

Railways

The Cyprus Government Railway operated from 1905 to 1951 between Famagusta, Nicosia, and Morphou, but was closed for economic reasons; proposals to build a new railway or light rail have not been implemented.
