Order of the Chrysanthemum

Supreme Order of the Chrysanthemum
Japanese: 大勲位菊花大綬章
Established: 27 December 1876
Sovereign: Emperor of Japan
Classes: Collar; Grand Cordon

The Supreme Order of the Chrysanthemum (大勲位菊花大綬章, Daikun'i Kikkadai Jushō) is Japan's highest order. It was established by Emperor Meiji in 1876 and is awarded exceptionally rarely, principally to members of the Japanese imperial family, foreign heads of state, and a very small number of Japanese subjects for outstanding merit.

Classes and insignia

The order has two grades: the Collar (大勲位菊花章頸飾), the senior grade, and the Grand Cordon (大勲位菊花大綬章). The badge depicts a chrysanthemum flower in white enamel with rays of gold, suspended from a chrysanthemum leaf; the collar consists of linked chrysanthemum designs. The sash of the Grand Cordon is dark red (crimson).

Recipients

Posthumous awards to Japanese prime ministers have occasionally been made — notably to Shigeru Yoshida and Eisaku Satō — while most awards are to foreign monarchs and presidents on state occasions. Because the order is at the summit of the Japanese honours system, the number of living recipients outside royal families is very small.
