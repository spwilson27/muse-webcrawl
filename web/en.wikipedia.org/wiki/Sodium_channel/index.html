Sodium channel

Sodium channels are integral membrane proteins that form ion channels conducting sodium ions (Na+) through a cell's plasma membrane. They are classified by the trigger that opens them, most importantly voltage-gated sodium channels, which are essential for action potentials in neurons and muscle.

Voltage-gated channels

Voltage-gated sodium channels consist of a large alpha subunit that forms the pore, associated with auxiliary beta subunits. In humans, nine alpha-subunit isoforms (NaV1.1–NaV1.9) are encoded by SCN genes. Depolarisation opens the channel, allowing Na+ influx that drives the rising phase of the action potential, after which the channel rapidly inactivates.

Clinical significance

Mutations in sodium channel genes cause channelopathies including epilepsy syndromes, periodic paralysis, and pain disorders. Sodium channels are targets of local anaesthetics, some anticonvulsants, and toxins such as tetrodotoxin.
