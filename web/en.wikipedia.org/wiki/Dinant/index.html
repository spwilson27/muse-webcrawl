Dinant is a city and municipality in the Belgian province of Namur, in Wallonia. It stretches along the steep shores of the river Meuse beneath dramatic limestone cliffs, crowned by a citadel and the distinctive onion dome of the Collegiate Church of Notre Dame.

History

Dinant grew prosperous in the Middle Ages from its metalworking, especially brassware known as dinanderie. In 1466 Charles the Bold of Burgundy sacked the city, reportedly throwing 800 burghers into the Meuse. During the First World War, in August 1914, Dinant was the site of the largest massacre of Belgian civilians by German forces, in which 674 inhabitants were executed — an event still commemorated in the town. Dinant was also contested in the Second World War during the crossing of the Meuse in 1940 and the Battle of the Bulge in 1944.

Notable people and sights

Dinant is the birthplace of Adolphe Sax (1814–1894), inventor of the saxophone; colourful saxophone sculptures line the Charles de Gaulle bridge. The citadel, reached by cable car or 408 steps, overlooks the town. Nearby is the Bayard Rock, a tall limestone pinnacle split from the cliff, associated with the legend of the horse Bayard.
