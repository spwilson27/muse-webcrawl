Cervical dystonia

[Image: Abnormal head posture caused by cervical dystonia (torticollis)]

Cervical dystonia, also called spasmodic torticollis, is a painful chronic neurological movement disorder characterised by involuntary contractions of the neck muscles, causing the head to twist or turn to one side, tilt, or be pulled forwards or backwards. It is the most common form of focal dystonia in adults, typically beginning between the ages of 40 and 60, and affects women more often than men.

Symptoms and causes

The abnormal postures — rotation (torticollis), sideways tilt (laterocollis), forward flexion (antecollis) or backward extension (retrocollis) — are often accompanied by neck pain, shoulder elevation, head tremor and muscle hypertrophy. Many patients discover a "sensory trick" (geste antagoniste), such as touching the chin or cheek, that temporarily relieves the spasm. Most cases are idiopathic, arising from dysfunction in basal ganglia circuits; a minority are inherited or secondary to trauma, drugs or other neurological disease.

Treatment

The mainstay of treatment is regular injection of botulinum toxin into the affected muscles, which weakens the contractions for about three months at a time. Oral medications such as anticholinergics, physiotherapy, and — in severe refractory cases — deep brain stimulation of the globus pallidus or selective peripheral denervation surgery may be used.
