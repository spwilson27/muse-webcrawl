Bullet Cluster

The Bullet Cluster (1E 0657-558) consists of two colliding galaxy clusters. Strictly speaking, the name refers to the smaller subcluster, moving away from the larger cluster at a relative radial velocity of about 4,700 km/s. Gravitational lensing studies of the Bullet Cluster, announced in August 2006, provide some of the best evidence currently available for the existence of dark matter.

Collision and gas

X-ray images from the Chandra observatory show the hot intracluster gas of the smaller cluster shaped into a bow-shock "bullet", lagging behind its galaxies because the gas — the bulk of the ordinary (baryonic) matter — interacts electromagnetically and is slowed by ram pressure as the clusters pass through each other.

Evidence for dark matter

Weak gravitational lensing maps show the clusters' total mass concentrated not where the gas is, but with the collisionless galaxies. The natural explanation is that most of the mass is non-baryonic dark matter, which, like the galaxies, passes through the collision essentially without interacting. The observed separation is difficult to reproduce in modified-gravity theories such as MOND without additional unseen mass. A 2006 paper by Clowe and collaborators called the system "a direct empirical proof of the existence of dark matter"; later statistical analyses have debated how improbable the collision speed is within ΛCDM cosmology.
