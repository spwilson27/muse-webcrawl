Kelowna is a city on Okanagan Lake in the Okanagan Valley in the southern interior of British Columbia, Canada. It is the head office of the Regional District of Central Okanagan. The name Kelowna derives from the Okanagan language term kilawnaʔ, referring to a male grizzly bear.

Kelowna is the province's third-largest metropolitan area (after Vancouver and Victoria). It serves as a hub for the surrounding region, with a metropolitan population of over 220,000. Nearby communities include West Kelowna, Lake Country, and Peachland.

History

The Okanagan Valley has been home to the Syilx (Okanagan) peoples for thousands of years. European settlement began with Oblate missionaries, including Father Charles Pandosy, who established a mission in 1859. Kelowna was incorporated as a city on 4 May 1905. The arrival of the Canadian Pacific Railway sternwheelers on Okanagan Lake and later rail connections spurred growth in agriculture, particularly fruit growing, which became the valley's signature industry.

Economy and tourism

Kelowna's economy is driven by tourism, wine production, orchards, technology, and manufacturing. The surrounding Okanagan Valley is one of Canada's premier wine regions, with numerous wineries along the lake's shores. Kelowna International Airport (YLW) is one of the busiest airports in Canada by passenger traffic. Summer recreation on Okanagan Lake and winter skiing at nearby Big White Ski Resort make the city a year-round destination. The lake is also famous in local legend as the home of the Ogopogo, a purported lake monster.

Climate

Kelowna has a continental climate moderated by the lake, with hot, dry summers and relatively mild winters for its latitude. The semi-arid conditions and abundant sunshine support vineyards and orchards.
