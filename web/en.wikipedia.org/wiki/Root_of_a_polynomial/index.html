Root of a polynomial

In mathematics, a root of a polynomial P(x) is a value r such that P(r) = 0. In other words, a root of P is a solution of the polynomial equation P(x) = 0. Roots are also called zeros of the polynomial. For example, 2 and −2 are roots of x² − 4, since substituting either value gives zero.

Fundamental facts

The fundamental theorem of algebra states that every polynomial of degree n ≥ 1 with complex coefficients has exactly n complex roots, counted with multiplicity. A root r has multiplicity m if (x − r)m divides the polynomial but (x − r)m+1 does not. By the factor theorem, r is a root of P if and only if (x − r) is a factor of P; repeated polynomial division therefore factorises a polynomial completely over the complex numbers.

Finding roots

Formulas give the roots of polynomials of degree up to four: the quadratic formula for degree two, and the more elaborate cubic and quartic formulas discovered in 16th-century Italy by Tartaglia, Cardano and Ferrari. The Abel–Ruffini theorem (proved in the 1820s) shows that no general formula using only arithmetic operations and radicals exists for degree five or higher — a result that helped give rise to Galois theory, which characterises exactly which polynomials are solvable by radicals. In practice, roots of higher-degree polynomials are found numerically, using methods such as Newton's method, bisection, or eigenvalue algorithms applied to the companion matrix.

Properties

  • Viète's formulas relate the roots of a polynomial to its coefficients: for a quadratic x² + px + q, the roots sum to −p and their product is q.
  • The rational root theorem lists all possible rational roots of a polynomial with integer coefficients.
  • Descartes' rule of signs bounds the number of positive and negative real roots.
  • Non-real roots of a real polynomial occur in complex-conjugate pairs.
