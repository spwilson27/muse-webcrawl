Snowy Mountains Scheme

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Snowy Mountains Scheme, also known as the Snowy Mountains Hydro-electric Scheme, is a hydroelectricity and irrigation complex in south-eastern Australia. Constructed between 1949 and 1974, it diverts the waters of the Snowy River and other rivers westward through tunnels beneath the Great Dividing Range to irrigate inland areas and generate electricity.

Construction

The scheme was a landmark post-war nation-building project. At its peak it employed some 100,000 workers from more than thirty countries, many of them European migrants whose arrival transformed Australian society. More than 120 workers died during construction. The works comprise sixteen major dams, seven power stations, a pumping station and some 225 kilometres of tunnels, pipelines and aqueducts.

Operation

Water is stored in reservoirs such as Lake Eucumbene and released to the Murray and Murrumbidgee river systems for irrigation while generating peaking electrical power for New South Wales, Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory. The Snowy 2.0 expansion project adds pumped hydro storage linking Tantangara and Talbingo reservoirs.

Environmental impact

Diverting the Snowy River reduced its flow at Jindabyne to about one percent of natural levels; later environmental releases have partially restored flows, and the scheme remains an icon of Australian engineering.
