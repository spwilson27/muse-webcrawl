Philippines Campaign (1941–1942)
Date: 8 December 1941 – 8 May 1942
Location: Commonwealth of the Philippines
Result: Japanese victory; Japanese occupation of the Philippines
Belligerents: United States and Philippine Commonwealth vs. Empire of Japan
Commanders: Douglas MacArthur, Jonathan Wainwright; Masaharu Homma

The Philippines Campaign (Filipino: Kampanya sa Pilipinas), also known as the Battle of the Philippines, was the invasion of the Philippine Islands by the Empire of Japan and the defence of the islands by United States and Filipino forces during the Pacific War. It began on 8 December 1941, hours after the attack on Pearl Harbor, and ended with the surrender of Corregidor on 6 May 1942 (Mindanao forces capitulated by 8 May).

Background

The Philippines, an American Commonwealth under President Manuel L. Quezon, was garrisoned by US Army Forces in the Far East (USAFFE) under General Douglas MacArthur, comprising the Philippine Scouts, the Philippine Commonwealth Army and the US Asiatic Fleet. Japanese forces under General Masaharu Homma's Fourteenth Army were assigned to seize the islands to protect Japan's flank for the invasion of the Dutch East Indies.

The invasion

Japanese air attacks on 8 December destroyed much of the Far East Air Force on the ground at Clark and Iba fields despite hours of warning. Landings began on northern Luzon on 10 December, with the main invasion at Lingayen Gulf on 22 December and at Lamon Bay the next day. MacArthur withdrew his forces into the Bataan Peninsula under the pre-war War Plan Orange-3, evacuating Manila, which was declared an open city and occupied on 2 January 1942.

Bataan and Corregidor

On Bataan, American and Filipino troops held out for four months against Homma's army, hampered by disease and starvation rations. MacArthur was evacuated to Australia in March 1942, uttering the famous promise "I shall return". Bataan surrendered on 9 April 1942; some 75,000 prisoners were subjected to the Bataan Death March. Lieutenant General Jonathan Wainwright held Corregidor until 6 May 1942, when he surrendered all remaining US forces in the Philippines.

Aftermath

The fall of the Philippines was the largest surrender of US-led forces in American history. It began the Japanese occupation, which lasted until MacArthur's return in the Philippines campaign of 1944–45. The defence, though ultimately unsuccessful, delayed the Japanese timetable and became a potent symbol of resistance.
