Ariane (rocket family)

Ariane is a family of expendable launch systems developed by the European Space Agency (ESA) and operated by Arianespace, used to deliver payloads into geostationary transfer orbit (GTO) or low Earth orbit (LEO). The first Ariane rocket was launched on 24 December 1979 from the Guiana Space Centre at Kourou in French Guiana, where all Ariane launches take place, taking advantage of the site's proximity to the equator. The name comes from the French spelling of the mythological character Ariadne.

History

France was the third nation to launch its own satellite, but the collaborative Europa project to build a joint European launcher failed in the early 1970s. France proposed a new project, initially codenamed L3S, which became Ariane. The programme was managed by the French space agency CNES under ESA oversight. Ariane 1 flew successfully on its first attempt on Christmas Eve 1979, giving Europe independent access to space and a strong position in the commercial satellite launch market.

Versions

Ariane 1–4

Ariane 1 was a three-stage launcher able to place about 1,850 kg into GTO. Ariane 2 and 3 added stretched stages and solid boosters, and Ariane 4 — flown from 1988 to 2003 — became a major commercial success with six variants using different combinations of liquid and solid strap-on boosters, launching more than 100 times with a very high success rate.

Ariane 5

Ariane 5, introduced in 1996, was a much larger heavy-lift vehicle designed in part to launch the Hermes spaceplane (later cancelled) and capable of carrying two large communications satellites at once, with more than 10 tonnes to GTO. After an initial failure on its maiden flight, it became the workhorse of European launch services until its retirement in 2023 after 117 flights.

Ariane 6

Ariane 6 was developed to reduce costs and provide more flexible mission profiles, in two variants: the Ariane 62 with two solid boosters and the Ariane 64 with four. Its maiden flight took place on 9 July 2024 from Kourou.
