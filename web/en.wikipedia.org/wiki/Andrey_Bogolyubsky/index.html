Andrey Bogolyubsky (Russian: Андрей Боголюбский, c. 1111 – 29 June 1174) was Prince of Vladimir-Suzdal from 1157 until his death. The son of Yuri Dolgorukiy, founder of Moscow, he is regarded as one of the most powerful rulers of medieval Rus' and a key figure in the shift of political power from Kiev to the north-east.

Life

Andrey was born around 1111, probably in the Suzdal land. His nickname "Bogolyubsky" ("loved by God", or "of Bogolyubovo") derives from the residence he built at Bogolyubovo on the Nerl River, where according to tradition the Mother of God appeared to him in a vision.

In 1155 Andrey removed the revered icon of the Theotokos from Vyshgorod and carried it to Vladimir, an act later seen as a transfer of sacred authority. After his father's death in 1157 he became prince of Vladimir, Rostov and Suzdal, and made Vladimir his capital, building the golden-roofed Cathedral of the Dormition (1158–1160) and the Golden Gate.

Kiev campaign and later years

In 1169 Andrey's forces sacked Kiev, an unprecedented humiliation for the old capital, and he installed his brother Gleb as prince there while keeping his own seat in Vladimir — a signal that Kiev no longer dominated Rus'. His attempt to subdue Novgorod in 1170 failed, however, and his growing autocracy alienated the boyars.

On the night of 28–29 June 1174 Andrey was assassinated by a group of conspiring boyars at Bogolyubovo. He was later canonised by the Russian Orthodox Church. His building programme also produced the celebrated Church of the Intercession on the Nerl (1165).

Legacy

Andrey is remembered as the first ruler to attempt to unite Rus' under a single autocratic authority centred in the north-east, laying foundations for the later rise of Vladimir-Suzdal and ultimately Moscow.
