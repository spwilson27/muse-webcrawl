Lotte Lenya (born Karoline Wilhelmine Charlotte Blamauer; 18 October 1898 – 27 November 1981) was an Austrian singer and actress, best known as the wife of the composer Kurt Weill and the definitive interpreter of his songs. She created the role of Jenny in the world premiere of The Threepenny Opera (1928) and later introduced Weill's music to American audiences. To the general public she is also known for playing the villainous Rosa Klebb in the James Bond film From Russia with Love (1963).

Early life and career

Born into a working-class Roman Catholic family in Vienna, Blamauer studied ballet and acting in Zurich, where she took the stage name Lotte Lenya. In 1921 she moved to Berlin and was cast in productions associated with the circle around Weill, whom she married in 1926. Her smoky, untrained voice and sharp characterisation suited the new cabaret style of Bertolt Brecht and Weill: she appeared in Mahagonny and created Jenny in Die Dreigroschenoper (The Threepenny Opera) in 1928, as well as in Georg Wilhelm Pabst's 1931 film version, singing "Pirate Jenny" and "Mack the Knife".

Exile and America

Lenya and Weill divorced in 1933 as they fled Nazi Germany, settled in Paris and London, and remarried in 1937 after emigrating to the United States. In America she appeared in Weill's The Eternal Road and worked to establish his reputation. After Weill's death in 1950, she devoted herself to preserving and performing his work: her recordings for Columbia Records, including Lotte Lenya Sings Kurt Weill and the first complete recording of The Threepenny Opera in German, defined the postwar Weill revival. Louis Armstrong's and Bobby Darin's versions of "Mack the Knife" drew on her famous recording; the song "Seeräuberjenny" and her persona are celebrated in Bob Dylan's "When I Paint My Masterpiece" and in the standard repertoire of cabaret.

On stage she won a Tony Award for the 1956 off-Broadway production of The Threepenny Opera opposite Edward Asner, and originated the role of Fraulein Schneider in the Broadway premiere of Cabaret (1966), earning a Tony nomination. Her film roles included Rosa Klebb, the SPECTRE agent with a poisoned shoe-blade, in From Russia with Love (1963), and the Contessa in The Roman Spring of Mrs Stone (1961), for which she was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress. She died of cancer in New York in 1981 and is buried beside Weill in Haverstraw, New York.
