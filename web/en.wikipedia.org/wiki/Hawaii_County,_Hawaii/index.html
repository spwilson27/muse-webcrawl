Hawaii County is a county in the U.S. state of Hawaii, coterminous with the island of Hawaii, often called the "Big Island" to distinguish it from the state as a whole. Its county seat and largest community is Hilo, on the island's windward (eastern) coast.

Geography

The county is the largest in Hawaii by land area, containing about 63 percent of the state's total land. The island is built from five shield volcanoes, including Mauna Loa and Kilauea, two of the world's most active volcanoes, and Mauna Kea, whose summit hosts major astronomical observatories. Because the island continues to grow through volcanic activity, the county's land area slowly increases. Hawaii Volcanoes National Park covers a large part of the south-east of the island.

Government and economy

The county is governed by an elected mayor and a nine-member county council. The economy rests on tourism (centred especially on the Kona coast resorts), agriculture — including Kona coffee, macadamia nuts, and cattle ranching around Waimea — and astronomy and research associated with Mauna Kea and the University of Hawaii at Hilo.
