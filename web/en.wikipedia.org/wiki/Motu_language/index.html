Motu (sometimes called Pure Motu or True Motu to distinguish it from its pidgin form) is an Austronesian language spoken by the Motu people, indigenous inhabitants of the coastal region around present-day Port Moresby in Papua New Guinea's Central Province. It belongs to the Central Papuan Tip cluster of the Oceanic branch.

Hiri Motu

A simplified pidgin form, Hiri Motu, developed from the language of the hiri trading voyages in which Motu sailors exchanged clay pots for sago with the peoples of the Gulf of Papua. Hiri Motu spread widely as a lingua franca through its use by the colonial police force (the "Police Motu" variety) and administration, and it remains one of Papua New Guinea's most widely understood languages and an official language of the country, alongside English and Tok Pisin. Most speakers of Hiri Motu are not native Motu speakers, and the pidgin differs considerably in grammar and vocabulary from the pure language.

Characteristics

Pure Motu is a typical Oceanic language with subject–verb–object word order, a system of inclusive and exclusive first-person plural pronouns, and a vocabulary that has absorbed loanwords from neighbouring Papuan languages as well as, more recently, English. The number of first-language speakers of Pure Motu is comparatively small — on the order of tens of thousands — even as Hiri Motu is understood by several hundred thousand.
