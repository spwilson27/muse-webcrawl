Knot

A knot is an intentional complication in cordage which may be practical or decorative, or both. Practical knots are classified by function: hitches attach rope to an object, bends join two ropes together, loop knots form loops, and stopper knots prevent a rope end from fraying or slipping through a hole.

Terminology and properties

Knots are evaluated by their security, strength (knots weaken rope, often to 60–80% of its unknotted strength), ease of tying and untying, and resistance to jamming. Famous knots include the bowline, figure-eight knot, sheet bend, clove hitch and reef knot. The standard reference work is The Ashley Book of Knots (1944), which catalogues thousands of knots.

Units

"Knot" is also the unit of speed equal to one nautical mile per hour, derived from the historical practice of measuring a ship's speed with a knotted line and log.
