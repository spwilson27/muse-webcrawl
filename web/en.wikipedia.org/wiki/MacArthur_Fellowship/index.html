The MacArthur Fellowship, commonly called the "Genius Grant", is a prize awarded annually by the John D. and Catherine T. MacArthur Foundation to typically 20–30 individuals, working in any field, who have shown "extraordinary originality and dedication in their creative pursuits and a marked capacity for self-direction". It is one of the most prestigious unrestricted awards in the United States.

The award

Fellows receive a stipend — $800,000 as of the 2023 class — paid in quarterly instalments over five years, with no strings attached: the foundation imposes no requirements, reports or deliverables, intending the money to give recipients freedom to pursue their work. The prize is awarded to individuals, not projects or organisations, on the basis of their past achievement and future promise.

Selection

Candidates cannot apply. They are nominated by a rotating pool of anonymous nominators, reviewed by an anonymous selection committee, and approved by the foundation's board. Fellows are usually informed by a phone call shortly before the public announcement each autumn, and many describe the call as coming entirely out of the blue. Recipients must be citizens or residents of the United States.

Fellows

Since the first class in 1981, more than 1,100 fellows have been named, spanning the sciences, humanities, arts, social sciences, activism and entrepreneurship. Well-known fellows include Ta-Nehisi Coates, Lin-Manuel Miranda, Barbara Liskov, Errol Morris and Viet Thanh Nguyen.
