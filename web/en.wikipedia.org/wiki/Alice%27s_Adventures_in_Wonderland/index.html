Alice's Adventures in Wonderland (commonly shortened to Alice in Wonderland) is an 1865 English children's novel by Lewis Carroll, the pen name of Charles Lutwidge Dodgson. It tells of a young girl named Alice who falls through a rabbit hole into a fantasy world of anthropomorphic creatures.

Plot

Bored beside a riverbank, Alice follows a waistcoated White Rabbit down a rabbit hole and finds herself in Wonderland, where she changes size repeatedly and meets the Cheshire Cat, the Mad Hatter and March Hare at a tea party, the Caterpillar, the Duchess, and the Queen of Hearts, whose croquet game and trial of the Knave of Hearts frame the climax. Alice finally wakes to find it a dream.

Origins and legacy

The story grew from a tale Dodgson improvised during a boat trip on the Thames in 1862 for Alice Liddell and her sisters. Illustrated by John Tenniel, the book became one of the most famous works of Victorian literature, celebrated for its wordplay, logic puzzles, and satire. It has been translated into scores of languages and adapted countless times for stage, film, and other media.
