Johann Baptist Strauss I (14 March 1804 – 25 September 1849) was an Austrian composer of light music, particularly dance music and marches. He was the founder of a musical dynasty: his sons Johann Strauss II, Josef Strauss and Eduard Strauss all became composers of dance music, with Johann II surpassing his father's fame. Strauss I is credited, along with Joseph Lanner, with popularising the waltz in Vienna and beyond.

Life and career

Born in Vienna, Strauss was orphaned young and apprenticed to a bookbinder before joining a local dance orchestra as a viola player. He played in Joseph Lanner's ensemble and then formed his own orchestra, which by the late 1820s was the leading dance band in Vienna. His compositions — eventually more than 250 works, including waltzes, galops, quadrilles and marches — made him famous across Europe; he toured widely, including to Paris and London, and directed balls at the Imperial court.

Music

Strauss established the classic form of the Viennese waltz as a chain of linked waltz melodies with an introduction and coda. His best-known work is the Radetzky March, Op. 228 (1848), composed in honour of Field Marshal Joseph Radetzky von Radetz after his victories in Italy; it remains a fixture of New Year's concerts and military music. Other well-known waltzes include Täuberln-Walzer and Loreley-Rhein-Klänge.

Family conflict and death

Strauss opposed his sons' musical careers, wishing them to enter banking or the army, and famously feuded with Johann II once the son formed a rival orchestra. The two were later reconciled in public esteem if not fully in private. Strauss died of scarlet fever in Vienna in 1849, aged 45, and was buried beside Lanner in Döbling Cemetery; their remains were later moved to the Zentralfriedhof.
