Consumer confidence

Consumer confidence is an economic indicator that measures the degree of optimism consumers feel about the overall state of the economy and their own personal financial situation. Because consumer spending accounts for the majority of economic activity in most developed economies, confidence is watched as a leading indicator of spending, saving and borrowing.

Indices

The best-known measures are the Consumer Confidence Index published by the Conference Board and the Index of Consumer Sentiment published by the University of Michigan's Surveys of Consumers, both in the United States. Similar surveys are produced in many other countries, such as the GfK consumer climate index in Germany and European Commission consumer surveys. Respondents are typically asked about current business and employment conditions, expectations for the coming months, and their household finances.

Interpretation

Rising confidence suggests consumers will spend more and save less, supporting economic growth; falling confidence often precedes retrenchment in spending. Confidence is influenced by employment, wages, inflation, interest rates and major events, and tends to move with — and sometimes ahead of — the business cycle.
