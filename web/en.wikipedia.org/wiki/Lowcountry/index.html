South Carolina Lowcountry

The Lowcountry (sometimes Low Country) is a geographic and cultural region along the coast of South Carolina in the United States, centred on Charleston and the Sea Islands. It comprises the flat, low-lying tidal marshes, estuaries and barrier islands below the fall line, conventionally including Beaufort, Berkeley, Charleston, Colleton, Dorchester, Georgetown, Hampton and Jasper counties. Its marshy landscape of live oaks draped in Spanish moss, its plantation history, and its distinctive African American Gullah Geechee culture make it one of the most recognisable regions of the American South.

Geography

The Lowcountry lies on the Atlantic Coastal Plain at elevations typically below 50 feet (15 m), threaded by tidal rivers — the Ashley, Cooper, Edisto, Combahee, Waccamaw and Savannah — and by the Intracoastal Waterway. The Sea Islands, including Hilton Head, St. Helena, Kiawah, Edisto and Daufuskie, shelter vast salt marshes of smooth cordgrass (Spartina alterniflora). The region is exposed to hurricanes, most memorably Hugo in 1989, and to increasing tidal flooding associated with sea-level rise.

History and culture

From the late seventeenth century the Lowcountry became one of the richest plantation regions of British North America, growing first indigo and then, above all, rice in impounded tidal fields engineered and worked by enslaved West Africans whose knowledge of rice cultivation planters deliberately sought. The enslaved population long outnumbered whites, and its relative isolation on the Sea Islands preserved the Gullah language, a creole combining English with African languages, along with distinctive foodways, crafts such as sweetgrass basket weaving, and spiritual traditions.

Lowcountry cuisine reflects this heritage: dishes such as shrimp and grits, she-crab soup, Frogmore stew (Lowcountry boil), Hoppin' John and perloo draw on the seafood of the marshes and the rice of the plantations. Charleston, founded in 1670, preserves one of America's finest historic districts of pastel "Charleston single" houses and church steeples, and the region's economy today rests heavily on tourism, the Port of Charleston, military installations such as Parris Island, and retirement and resort development around Hilton Head and Beaufort.
