Cranford


Early edition title page

Cranford is an episodic novel by the English writer Elizabeth Gaskell. It first appeared in serial form in Charles Dickens's magazine Household Words between 1851 and 1853, and was published in book form in 1853. One of Gaskell's best-known works, it is a gently comic portrait of life in a small English town dominated by elderly ladies — the "Amazons" of Cranford — and their circle of visiting, gossip, genteel poverty, and "elegant economy".

Setting and characters

The fictional Cranford is modelled on Knutsford, Cheshire, where Gaskell grew up. The stories are narrated by Mary Smith, a younger visitor, and centre on the spinster sisters Deborah and Matilda ("Matty") Jenkyns, their friend Miss Pole, and the formidable Mrs Jamieson. Episodes include the arrival of the bluff Captain Brown, the visit of the conjuror Signor Brunoni, and the ruin and rescue of Matty after the failure of her bank.

Reception and adaptations

Cranford has been praised for its warmth, humour, and unsentimental observation of a vanishing pre-industrial society. It has been adapted several times for stage and television, most notably the BBC series Cranford (2007) with Judi Dench as Miss Matty, which incorporated material from other Gaskell stories.
