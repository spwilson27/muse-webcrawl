Sega CD

The Sega CD, known as the Mega-CD in most regions outside North America, is a CD-ROM accessory for the Sega Genesis video game console. It was released in Japan in December 1991, in North America in October 1992, and in Europe and Australia in 1993. It allowed the Genesis to play games distributed on compact discs, offering far greater storage than cartridges, enhanced audio, and an additional processor.

Hardware

The add-on contains its own Motorola 68000 processor running at 12.5 MHz, additional RAM, and custom graphics hardware supporting scaling and rotation, along with CD-quality (Red Book) audio. Two models were produced: an original motorised tray-loading unit that sat beneath the Genesis, and a cheaper top-loading model sold alongside the redesigned Genesis Model 2. Sega also licensed combination units such as the JVC X'Eye and its own CDX.

Games and controversy

The library of over 200 games is remembered for its many full-motion video titles, including Sewer Shark (bundled with the system), Night Trap, and Double Switch. Night Trap became a focus of the 1993 United States congressional hearings on video game violence, which contributed to the creation of the Entertainment Software Rating Board. Critically acclaimed titles included Sonic CD, Lunar: The Silver Star, Snatcher, and Final Fight CD.

Reception and legacy

The Sega CD sold approximately 2.24 million units worldwide. It was hampered by its high launch price of US$299, the limited colour palette it shared with the Genesis, and the mixed quality of its FMV-heavy library. Sega discontinued support in 1996 as it shifted focus to the Sega Saturn. The system is now regarded as an ambitious but flawed early step into optical media for consoles.
