Bob cut

[Image: A woman with a classic chin-length bob haircut]

A bob cut, also known as a bob, is a short to medium-length haircut, in which the hair is typically cut straight around the head at approximately jaw level, and often with a fringe (or "bangs") at the front. The standard bob is normally cut either between or just below the tips of the ears, and well above the shoulders.

History

Short hair for women became fashionable in the 1910s and 1920s, popularised by figures such as the dancer Irene Castle and the designer Coco Chanel, and was emblematic of the independent "flapper" woman. The hairdresser Antoine of Paris and, in London, Vidal Sassoon — whose geometric bobs of the 1960s, worn by Mary Quant and Grace Coddington, revived the style — are key names in its development.

Variations

Modern variants include the A-line bob, inverted bob, lob (long bob), asymmetrical bob, shaggy bob and the graduated bob, differing in length, layering and angle. The bob has repeatedly returned to fashion, including in the 1980s and 2010s.
