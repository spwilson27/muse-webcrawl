Niña, Pinta, and Santa María are the three ships used by Christopher Columbus on his first voyage across the Atlantic in 1492. The Santa María, a larger carrack, was Columbus's flagship; the Niña and Pinta were smaller, faster caravels captained by the Pinzón brothers, Martín Alonso and Vicente Yáñez.

The voyage

The fleet left Palos de la Frontera on 3 August 1492, refitted in the Canary Islands — where the Niña was re-rigged from lateen to square sails — and made landfall in the Bahamas on 12 October 1492. On Christmas Day 1492 the Santa María ran aground off Hispaniola and was abandoned, her timbers used to build the fort of La Navidad. Columbus returned to Spain aboard the Niña, briefly separated from the Pinta in a storm.

Legacy

The trio became an enduring symbol of the Age of Discovery; full-size replicas of all three ships have been built in Spain and the United States and are displayed in museums and ports.
