Thranduil is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. He first appears as the Elvenking in The Hobbit (1937), where he is the proud and wary ruler of the Elves of Mirkwood, and is later named and given a backstory in The Lord of the Rings and the posthumously published works edited by Christopher Tolkien.

Biography

Thranduil was a Sindarin Elf whose father Oropher founded the Woodland Realm in the forest later called Mirkwood. Oropher was slain in the Battle of Dagorlad at the end of the Second Age, and Thranduil returned north with barely a third of his father's host. As the shadow of Sauron grew in Dol Guldur, Thranduil withdrew his people to halls delved beneath the north-east of the forest.

In The Hobbit, Thranduil imprisons Thorin Oakenshield and his Dwarf company when they refuse to explain their errand — a mistrust rooted in an ancient quarrel over treasure — and Bilbo frees them in wine barrels down the Forest River. Thranduil marches to the Lonely Mountain to aid the men of Lake-town and claim a share of the dragon's hoard, but fights alongside Men and Dwarves in the Battle of the Five Armies. His son Legolas later joins the Fellowship of the Ring. In Peter Jackson's film adaptations of The Hobbit, Thranduil is played by Lee Pace.
