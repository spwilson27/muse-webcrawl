4th arrondissement of Paris

The Hôtel de Ville, seat of Paris's municipal government, stands in the 4th arrondissement.

The 4th arrondissement of Paris (IVe arrondissement) is one of the twenty arrondissements of the French capital. Located on the Right Bank of the Seine, it covers 1.60 km², includes the eastern part of the Île de la Cité and all of the Île Saint-Louis, and forms part of the administrative sector Paris Centre. It is governed from the Hôtel de Ville, Paris's city hall.

Quarters and landmarks

The arrondissement is divided into the quarters of Saint-Merri, Saint-Gervais, Arsenal, and Notre-Dame. Its northern half contains much of Le Marais, one of the city's best-preserved historic districts, with the Place des Vosges (the oldest planned square in Paris), the Hôtel de Sully, the Maison européenne de la photographie, and the Pletzl, the historic Jewish quarter. The west end includes part of the Centre Pompidou (Beaubourg) complex, shared with the 3rd arrondissement, while the Île de la Cité portion holds Notre-Dame Cathedral, the Sainte-Chapelle nearby in the 1st, the Préfecture de Police, and the Hôtel-Dieu hospital.

History and population

The district preserves some of the oldest streets in Paris; the Marais escaped Baron Haussmann's demolitions and retains many 17th-century aristocratic hôtels particuliers. Once densely populated, the arrondissement's population fell sharply in the 20th century as workshops and small dwellings gave way to offices, museums, and fashionable residences; roughly 28,000 people live there today, among the lowest of any Paris arrondissement.
