Dairy product

Dairy products (or milk products) are food products made from (or containing) milk. The most common dairy animals are cattle, water buffalo, goats, and sheep. Dairy products include milk itself, butter, cheese, yogurt, cream, ice cream, condensed and powdered milk, whey, and casein. A facility that produces dairy products is known as a dairy.

Types

Milk is sold fresh (pasteurised or UHT), condensed, evaporated, and dried. Fermented products such as yogurt, kefir, and cultured buttermilk are made by inoculating milk with lactic-acid bacteria. Cheese is produced by curdling milk, typically with rennet, and pressing the curds; hundreds of varieties exist. Butter is churned from cream, and its further-processed form, ghee, is used widely in South Asian cooking. Cream, sour cream, ice cream, and milk-derived ingredients such as whey protein round out the category.

Production and consumption

World milk production exceeds 900 million tonnes annually, with India the largest producer and the European Union, United States, and New Zealand major exporters. Dairy products are significant sources of calcium, protein, and vitamins B12 and D, though lactose intolerance in much of the world's adult population and concerns over saturated fat and the environmental footprint of dairy farming have spurred a large market in plant-based alternatives.
