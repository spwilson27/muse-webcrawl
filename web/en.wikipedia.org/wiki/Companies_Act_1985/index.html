Companies Act 1985

The 1985 Act consolidated UK company law and governed incorporation through Companies House.

Citation1985 c. 6
Royal assent11 March 1985
StatusLargely repealed and replaced by the Companies Act 2006

The Companies Act 1985 (c. 6) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland which enacted, in consolidated form, much of the law relating to companies. It replaced the Companies Act 1948 and subsequent amending legislation, and for over two decades was the central statute of United Kingdom company law, governing company formation, share capital, directors' duties (in partly statutory form), accounts and audit, and meetings and resolutions.

Overview

Together with the Company Securities (Insider Dealing) Act 1985 and, later, the Companies Act 1989, which amended it (notably on accounts and audit following European directives), the 1985 Act supplied the framework under which companies were incorporated and regulated in Great Britain; Northern Ireland had parallel legislation. The Act distinguished between private companies (Ltd) and public limited companies (plc), imposing stricter capital and reporting requirements on the latter, such as the minimum allotted share capital of £50,000.

Replacement

Following a comprehensive Company Law Review, the 1985 Act was progressively repealed and replaced by the Companies Act 2006 — one of the longest Acts ever passed by Parliament — which was brought fully into force on 1 October 2009. Certain provisions of the 1985 Act, notably Part XIV on investigations, survived in amended form for a time, and the 2006 Act's transitional provisions mean the 1985 Act still occasionally matters for events predating the changeover.
