Reception of Roman law

The reception of Roman law refers to the process by which the law of ancient Rome — above all Justinian's Corpus Juris Civilis — was adopted as the common law (ius commune) of much of continental Europe from the late Middle Ages onward.

History

Roman law was revived by the glossators at Bologna in the 11th and 12th centuries and spread through the universities. In the Holy Roman Empire, reception accelerated with the founding of the Reichskammergericht in 1495, whose judges applied the learned laws. Reception varied by region: stronger in Germany and Italy, weaker where codified local law, such as the Saxon Mirror, prevailed.

Legacy

The received Roman law, blended with canon law and local custom, formed the ius commune and underlies modern civil law systems and their codes.
