Euler's rotation theorem

Euler's rotation theorem, proved by Leonhard Euler in 1775, states that in three-dimensional space, any displacement of a rigid body with one point fixed is equivalent to a single rotation about some axis passing through that fixed point.

Statement

Equivalently, the composition of two rotations about different axes is itself a single rotation about a third axis (or, in degenerate cases, the identity). In matrix terms, every 3×3 rotation matrix has the real eigenvalue 1, whose eigenvector gives the rotation axis; the angle follows from the trace of the matrix.

Applications

The theorem is foundational in rigid-body mechanics, robotics, and computer graphics, where orientations are represented by a single axis–angle pair, and in the theory of quaternions, which encode such a rotation compactly. It complements Euler's angles, which decompose a rotation into three successive rotations about coordinate axes.
