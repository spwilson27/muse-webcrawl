Isabelle Anne Madeleine Huppert (born 16 March 1953) is a French actress. Described as one of the finest actors of her generation, she has appeared in more than 120 films since her debut in 1971 and is the most nominated actress for the César Award, with 16 nominations. She is known for her willingness to play morally ambiguous, emotionally distant characters, often in psychologically intense roles.

Career

Huppert rose to prominence with The Lacemaker (1977), winning a BAFTA for Most Promising Newcomer. She won the Best Actress award at Cannes for Violette Nozière (1978) and again for The Piano Teacher (2001), directed by Michael Haneke, with whom she also made Time of the Wolf (2003), Amour (2012) and Happy End (2017). She collaborated extensively with Claude Chabrol, including La Cérémonie (1995), for which she won the César Award for Best Actress.

International recognition

For her performance in Paul Verhoeven's Elle (2016), Huppert won the Golden Globe and received her first Academy Award nomination for Best Actress. She has also had a significant stage career in France and internationally, and has served as president of the jury at the Cannes Film Festival (2009).
