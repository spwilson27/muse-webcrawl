Universal Integrated Circuit Card

The Universal Integrated Circuit Card (UICC) is the smart card used in mobile terminals in GSM, UMTS, LTE, and 5G networks. In common usage, the UICC is what most consumers refer to as a "SIM card"; strictly, the SIM is an application running on the UICC.

Applications

A UICC can host several applications, depending on the network type:

  • SIM — for GSM networks;
  • USIM — for UMTS and later networks, providing stronger authentication;
  • ISIM — for IP Multimedia Subsystem services;
  • CSIM — for CDMA2000 networks.

Form factors

The UICC was originally produced in full-size (1FF) card format and successively miniaturised through mini-SIM (2FF), micro-SIM (3FF), and nano-SIM (4FF). The embedded UICC (eUICC, or eSIM) is soldered into the device and can be provisioned remotely with operator profiles, while the integrated iUICC is built into the device's system-on-chip.

Functions

The card securely stores subscriber identity material (such as the IMSI and authentication keys), carries network authentication by means of challenge-response protocols, and can store contacts and text messages.
