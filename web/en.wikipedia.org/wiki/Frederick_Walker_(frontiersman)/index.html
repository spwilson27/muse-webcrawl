Frederick Walker (frontiersman)

Frederick Walker (c. 1820 – 19 November 1866) was a British colonial official in Australia who founded and commanded the Native Police force in the Moreton Bay region and later in Queensland. His paramilitary command was central to the violent frontier conflicts between European settlers and Aboriginal Australians, and he played a role in the search for the lost Burke and Wills expedition.

Career

Walker arrived in New South Wales and in 1848 was appointed commandant of the newly established Native Police, a corps of Aboriginal troopers led by European officers. Operating across the northern frontier, the force conducted punitive expeditions that caused heavy Aboriginal casualties and made Walker a controversial figure in Australian history.

In 1861 Walker led a relief expedition searching for Burke and Wills and located traces of the party in the Gulf of Carpentaria region. He later became a police magistrate and commissioner of crown lands in Queensland, dying in 1866 at Floraville station.
