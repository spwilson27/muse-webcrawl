Jet propulsion is the propulsion of an object in one direction produced by a jet of fluid expelled in the opposite direction, an application of Newton's third law of motion. It encompasses jet engines used in aviation, rocket engines, and the natural locomotion of squid, octopuses, and jellyfish.

Jet engines

Air-breathing jet engines draw in air, compress it, burn fuel in it, and expel the hot exhaust at high velocity. The principal types are the turbojet, the turbofan (which drives a large fan for bypass thrust and dominates commercial aviation), the turboprop, and air-breathing engines without compressors such as the ramjet and scramjet.

Rocket propulsion

Rocket engines carry both fuel and oxidiser, working in vacuum; their performance is described by the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation and the concept of specific impulse.

History

The gas-turbine jet engine was developed independently in the 1930s by Frank Whittle in Britain and Hans von Ohain in Germany; the first jet aircraft, the Heinkel He 178, flew in 1939.
