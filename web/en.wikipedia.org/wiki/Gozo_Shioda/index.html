Gōzō Shioda

Gōzō Shioda (塩田 剛三, 9 September 1915 – 17 July 1994) was a Japanese aikido master and the founder of the Yoshinkan style of aikido. He was one of the most prominent students of aikido's founder, Morihei Ueshiba.

Shioda began training under Ueshiba in 1932 while a student at Takushoku University, spending about eight years as an uchi-deshi (live-in student) before the Second World War. After the war he worked to re-establish aikido in Japan, giving a celebrated public demonstration in 1954 that won first prize and led to sponsorship for a permanent dojo.

In 1955 he founded the Yoshinkan Aikido Foundation in Tokyo. Yoshinkan is known for its methodical teaching of basic movements and its use in training the Tokyo Metropolitan Riot Police. Shioda's book Dynamic Aikido introduced his style to English-speaking readers. He held the rank of 10th dan and was awarded the title meijin by the International Martial Arts Federation.
