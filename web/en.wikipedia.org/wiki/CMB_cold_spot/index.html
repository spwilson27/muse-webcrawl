CMB cold spot

The CMB Cold Spot or WMAP Cold Spot is a region of the sky in the constellation Eridanus that is unusually cold in the cosmic microwave background (CMB). Discovered in 2004 in data from the Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP), the spot spans about 5–10 degrees of the sky and is roughly 70 µK colder than the average CMB temperature — a deviation that is statistically unlikely in the standard model of cosmology.

Discovery

The spot was identified by Patricio Vielva and colleagues using wavelet analysis of WMAP's first-year data. The Planck satellite later confirmed that the feature is real and not an instrumental artefact.

Proposed explanations

One explanation invokes the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect from a huge underdensity of galaxies — a supervoid — along the line of sight; the Eridanus Supervoid, some 1.8 billion light-years across, has been proposed, though its measured effect appears too small to explain the whole signal. Other suggestions include cosmic textures, statistical flukes amplified by a posteriori selection, and more exotic possibilities such as a neighbouring universe. The mainstream view treats the cold spot as an unusual but not decisive anomaly.
