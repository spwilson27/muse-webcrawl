Ring-closing metathesis

Ring-closing metathesis (RCM) is a widely used variation of olefin metathesis in organic chemistry for the synthesis of cyclic alkenes. In this reaction, a diene undergoes an intramolecular metathesis reaction to produce a cycloalkene, with a small alkene byproduct (often ethylene) that is typically removed as a gas, driving the reaction forward.

Catalysts and mechanism

RCM is catalysed by metal alkylidene complexes, including Schrock catalysts based on molybdenum and the ruthenium-based Grubbs catalysts, which are tolerant of many functional groups. The mechanism proceeds through metallacyclobutane intermediates in a series of [2+2] cycloadditions and cycloreversions, as described by the Chauvin mechanism for olefin metathesis.

Applications

The method is used to form rings of five, six and seven members readily, and macrocycles under high-dilution conditions. It has been applied extensively in natural product total synthesis and pharmaceutical chemistry, for example in the synthesis of the hepatitis C drug simeprevir.
