BACS

Bacs Payment Schemes Limited, known simply as Bacs (formerly the Bankers' Automated Clearing System), is the United Kingdom's automated payment system for electronic funds transfers. Since 2018 it has been operated by Pay.UK. Bacs processes Direct Debits and Bacs Direct Credits, the mechanisms by which most British salaries, benefits, pensions, and household bills are paid.

History

Electronic funds transfer between British banks began in the late 1960s through the Inter-Bank Computer Bureau, set up in 1968. The Bankers' Automated Clearing Services was established in 1971 to run the growing system, later shortening its name to Bacs. Direct Debit, which allows a payee to collect varying amounts from a customer's account under an advance agreement and guarantee, became a mainstay of British bill payment.

Operation

Bacs payments run on a three-day cycle: the file is submitted on day one, processed by the banks on day two, and funds are debited and credited simultaneously on day three. Direct Credit is used for payroll and supplier payments, while Direct Debit is protected by the Direct Debit Guarantee, under which the payer's bank must refund any payment taken in error immediately. Bacs handles billions of transactions a year, with annual volumes exceeding six billion payments worth several trillion pounds.

Relation to other systems

Bacs is complemented by Faster Payments (introduced in 2008) for near-instant transfers, and by CHAPS for high-value same-day payments. Bacs itself remains the workhorse for bulk, non-urgent payments.
