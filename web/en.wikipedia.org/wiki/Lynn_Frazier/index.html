Lynn Frazier

Lynn Joseph Frazier (December 21, 1874 – January 11, 1947) was an American farmer and politician from North Dakota who served as the state's twelfth governor and as a United States senator. He is remembered as the first governor in United States history to be removed from office by a recall election.

Governor of North Dakota

A farmer with no previous political office, Frazier was drafted by the agrarian Nonpartisan League and elected governor in 1916, winning re-election in 1918 and 1920. His administrations created state-owned enterprises including the Bank of North Dakota and the State Mill and Elevator. Amid post-war agricultural distress and opposition from business-backed rivals, voters recalled Frazier, along with the attorney general and commissioner of agriculture, in October 1921.

Senate career

Frazier rebounded quickly, winning election to the U.S. Senate in 1922 as a Republican endorsed by the Nonpartisan League. He served from 1923 to 1941, supporting farm relief and New Deal-era measures as part of the Senate's progressive bloc, and was associated with the proposed Frazier–Lemke farm bankruptcy legislation. He lost in the 1940 primary after opposing aspects of the administration's foreign policy.
