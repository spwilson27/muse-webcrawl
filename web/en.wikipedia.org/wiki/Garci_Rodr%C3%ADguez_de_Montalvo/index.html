Garci Rodríguez de Montalvo

Garci Rodríguez de Montalvo (c. 1450 – 1504/1505) was a Spanish writer best known for Amadis of Gaul (Amadís de Gaula), the celebrated chivalric romance published in 1508. Montalvo reworked earlier, now-lost versions of the Amadís story into four books and added a fifth, Las Sergas de Esplandián (1510), introducing Queen Calafia and her island of California — the origin of the name of California.

Life

Little is known of Montalvo's life. He was a native of Medina del Campo in Castile, served as a regidor (councilman) of the town, and is thought to have fought in the Granada War. He died around 1504–1505, before his books appeared in print.

Works

Amadís de Gaula became the model for the Spanish romances of chivalry that flooded 16th-century Europe, and it is repeatedly referenced in Cervantes's Don Quixote, where it is the one romance spared from the bonfire. In Las Sergas de Esplandián, Montalvo described an island "on the right hand of the Indies" ruled by black Amazons under Queen Calafia — a passage that gave California its name when Spanish explorers applied it to the peninsula.
