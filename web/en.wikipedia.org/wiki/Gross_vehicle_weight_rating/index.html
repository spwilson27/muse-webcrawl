Gross vehicle weight rating

AbbreviationGVWR
DefinitionMaximum loaded weight specified by the manufacturer
IncludesVehicle, fluids, cargo, passengers, accessories
RelatedCurb weight, payload, GAWR, GCWR

The gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR), or gross vehicle mass (GVM), is the maximum operating weight/mass of a vehicle as specified by the manufacturer, including the vehicle's chassis, body, engine, engine fluids, fuel, accessories, driver, passengers, and cargo — but excluding that of any trailers.

Regulation

In the United States, the GVWR determines a truck's commercial classification and whether a commercial driver's license is required: vehicles over 26,000 lb (11,793 kg) GVWR generally require a CDL when used commercially. Federal regulations require the GVWR to be shown on the manufacturer's certification label, typically on the driver's door frame. Exceeding the rating can result in fines and liability, and can compromise braking and handling.

Related ratings

The gross axle weight rating (GAWR) limits the load on a single axle. Payload capacity equals GVWR minus curb weight. For a vehicle towing a trailer, the gross combination weight rating (GCWR) caps the total weight of vehicle and trailer together.

Weight classes

U.S. truck classes 1–8 are defined by GVWR ranges, from light-duty pickups under 6,000 lb to heavy trucks over 33,000 lb.
