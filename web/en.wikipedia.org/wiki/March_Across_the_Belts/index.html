March Across the Belts

The March Across the Belts (Swedish: Tåget över Bält) was a campaign of the Second Northern War, fought in the winter of 1657–1658, in which a Swedish army under King Charles X Gustav marched across the frozen Danish straits—the Little Belt and the Great Belt—to reach Zealand and Copenhagen.

Background

Denmark-Norway had declared war on Sweden in 1657 while Charles X Gustav was campaigning in Poland. The Swedish king rapidly withdrew westward through Germany and Jutland, defeating Danish forces on the peninsula by the autumn of 1657 but lacking the naval strength to cross to the Danish islands.

The crossing

The winter of 1657–1658 was one of the coldest of the Little Ice Age, and the straits froze solid. After reconnaissance of the ice, the Swedish army—roughly 9,000 cavalry and 3,000 infantry—crossed the Little Belt to Funen in late January 1658, defeating the Danish defenders there. In early February the army made the far more hazardous crossing of the Great Belt via Langeland and Lolland, a route chosen with the advice of engineer Erik Dahlbergh. During the crossing some squadrons and guns fell through the ice, but the bulk of the army reached Zealand intact.

Aftermath

With Swedish troops on Zealand and Copenhagen effectively indefensible, Denmark sued for peace. The resulting Treaty of Roskilde (26 February 1658) was one of the most punishing in Danish history: Denmark ceded Scania, Halland, Blekinge, and the island of Bornholm, as well as the Norwegian provinces of Bohuslän and Trøndelag. The march remains one of the most celebrated feats of arms in Swedish military history.
