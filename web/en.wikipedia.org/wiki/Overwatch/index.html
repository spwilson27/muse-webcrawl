Genre: Hero shooter / first-person shooter
Modes: Multiplayer (6v6 at launch)

Overwatch is a 2016 multiplayer first-person hero shooter video game developed and published by Blizzard Entertainment. Two teams of six players select from a roster of "heroes", each with unique abilities organized into damage, tank and support roles, and battle over objectives on maps set in a near-future Earth. The game arose from the cancelled Blizzard MMO project Titan, whose art and concepts were repurposed into a brightly optimistic vision of the future.

Gameplay

Matches centre on attacking or defending control points, escorting payloads, or hybrid combinations. Players can switch heroes mid-match to counter the enemy composition, and accumulate charge toward powerful "ultimate" abilities. Overwatch's emphasis on teamwork and distinct hero kits revitalized the hero shooter genre.

Reception and legacy

Overwatch was a critical and commercial success, selling tens of millions of copies, winning The Game Award for Game of the Year 2016, and anchoring the Overwatch League, a city-based franchised esports league launched in 2018. Its animated shorts and diverse cast were widely praised. In October 2022 the original game was shut down and replaced by the free-to-play sequel Overwatch 2, which shifted to 5v5 teams.
