The César Awards is the national film award of France, first given in 1976. The nominations are selected by the members of the twelve categories of the Académie des Arts et Techniques du Cinéma, and the winners are chosen by secret ballot. The Césars are considered the French equivalent of the American Academy Awards.

History

The awards were created by Georges Cravenne, who modelled them on the Oscars. The trophy, a compressed-metal sculpture, was designed by the sculptor César Baldaccini, whose name the awards bear. The first ceremony, held in Paris in April 1976, named Le Vieux Fusil as Best Film.

Categories and voting

More than twenty categories are awarded, including Best Film, Best Director, Best Actor and Actress, Best Original Screenplay, and awards for promising newcomers (révélations). Academy membership numbers several thousand film professionals, who vote in two rounds.
