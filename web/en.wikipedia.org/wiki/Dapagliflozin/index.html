Dapagliflozin, sold under the brand names Farxiga (US) and Forxiga (EU), is a medication used to treat type 2 diabetes. It is also used to treat adults with certain kinds of heart failure and chronic kidney disease. It was developed by Bristol-Myers Squibb in partnership with AstraZeneca.

Mechanism of action

Dapagliflozin is a selective inhibitor of the sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) in the proximal tubule of the kidney. By blocking SGLT2, it reduces the reabsorption of filtered glucose and promotes urinary glucose excretion, lowering blood glucose independently of insulin. The resulting osmotic diuresis and natriuresis are thought to contribute to its benefits in heart failure and kidney disease.

Medical uses

The landmark DAPA-HF trial showed that dapagliflozin reduced the combined risk of worsening heart failure or cardiovascular death in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, whether or not they had diabetes. The DAPA-CKD trial similarly demonstrated a reduction in the progression of chronic kidney disease. Common side effects include genital mycotic infections and urinary tract infections; rare but serious adverse effects include euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis and Fournier's gangrene.

History

Dapagliflozin was approved in the European Union in 2012 and by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in January 2014. It was the first SGLT2 inhibitor approved in Europe.
