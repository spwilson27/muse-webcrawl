South Lebanon conflict (1985–2000)

The South Lebanon conflict of 1985–2000 was a prolonged armed conflict in southern Lebanon between Hezbollah and other Lebanese guerrilla groups on one side, and Israel and its proxy, the South Lebanon Army (SLA), on the other. After Israel's 1982 invasion of Lebanon and its partial withdrawal in 1985, Israeli forces retained a "security zone" in the south, roughly a tenth of Lebanese territory, patrolled together with the SLA.

Course of the conflict

For fifteen years Hezbollah waged a guerrilla campaign of ambushes, roadside bombs, and rocket fire against Israeli and SLA positions, while Israel conducted periodic large operations, notably Operation Accountability (1993) and Operation Grapes of Wrath (1996), the latter marred by the Qana massacre, in which over 100 civilians sheltering at a UN compound were killed. Israeli public opinion turned against the occupation as casualties mounted, driven in part by the Four Mothers movement and the 1997 helicopter disaster that killed 73 soldiers.

Israeli withdrawal

In May 2000, under Prime Minister Ehud Barak, Israel withdrew unilaterally to the UN-demarcated Blue Line. The SLA collapsed almost immediately, many of its members fleeing to Israel, and Hezbollah declared victory, becoming the first Arab force credited with compelling an Israeli withdrawal without a negotiated settlement.
