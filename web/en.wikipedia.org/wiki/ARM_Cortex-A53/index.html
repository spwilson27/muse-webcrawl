ARM Cortex-A53

ARM Cortex-A53

Designer: ARM Holdings

Announced: 2012

Architecture: ARMv8-A (AArch64 and AArch32)

Pipeline: In-order, 8-stage, dual-issue

Role: Energy-efficient "LITTLE" core in big.LITTLE systems, or standalone

Predecessor: ARM Cortex-A7

The ARM Cortex-A53 is one of the first two CPU cores implementing the ARMv8-A 64-bit instruction set, designed by ARM Holdings, the other being the higher-performance Cortex-A57. Announced in 2012, the A53 is an in-order, dual-issue processor optimised for power efficiency: ARM positioned it as delivering performance comparable to the earlier Cortex-A9 while consuming substantially less power.

Design and use

The Cortex-A53 is most often used as the "LITTLE" cluster in big.LITTLE configurations, paired with Cortex-A57, A72, or A73 "big" cores, so operating systems can migrate tasks between performance and efficiency clusters. It is also used on its own in vast numbers of low-cost devices. Notable implementations include the Qualcomm Snapdragon 410/617/625 families, the Samsung Exynos 7 Octa, the MediaTek Helio series, and the Broadcom BCM2837 in the Raspberry Pi 3. Clock speeds in production chips typically run from about 1.2 to 2.0 GHz.

Impact

Because of its balance of 64-bit capability, small die area, and low power consumption, the Cortex-A53 became one of the most widely shipped CPU cores in history, appearing in billions of smartphones, tablets, set-top boxes, single-board computers, and embedded systems.
