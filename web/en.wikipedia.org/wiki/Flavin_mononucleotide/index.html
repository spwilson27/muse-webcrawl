Flavin mononucleotide

Other namesFMN, riboflavin-5′-phosphate
FormulaC17H21N4O9P
Derived fromRiboflavin (vitamin B2)
RoleProsthetic group of flavoproteins

Flavin mononucleotide (FMN), or riboflavin-5′-phosphate, is a biomolecule produced from riboflavin (vitamin B2) by the enzyme riboflavin kinase. It is the principal form in which riboflavin occurs in cells and tissues, and it functions as the prosthetic group of a large family of enzymes called flavoproteins.

Function

FMN is a stronger oxidising agent than NAD+ and can participate in both one-electron and two-electron transfers through the semiquinone radical intermediate FMNH•. This versatility makes it central to redox biochemistry. Its best-known role is as the first electron acceptor in Complex I (NADH dehydrogenase) of the mitochondrial respiratory chain, receiving electrons from NADH and passing them to iron–sulfur clusters. FMN is also the chromophore of blue-light photoreceptor proteins such as phototropin and cryptochrome, and a precursor of FAD (flavin adenine dinucleotide), formed by adenylation via FAD synthetase.

As a food additive

FMN is used as a yellow-orange food colouring (E number E101a, formerly E107) and in nutritional supplements, though its poor solubility in its free-acid form means the more soluble sodium salt is usually employed.
