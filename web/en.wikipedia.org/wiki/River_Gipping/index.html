The River Gipping is a river in Suffolk, England. It rises near Mendlesham Green and flows southwards for about 30 kilometres (19 mi) through Stowmarket, Needham Market, Bramford and Sproughton to Ipswich, where it becomes tidal and takes the name of the River Orwell.

Course and mills

The Gipping valley is notable for the number of watermills that once stood along its length — at one time as many as dozens of mills ground corn and, in some cases, drove machinery for the woollen and later fertiliser industries. Stowmarket grew into an industrial town on the river, with gun-cotton and explosives works established beside it in the 19th century.

Navigation

The Ipswich and Stowmarket Navigation, authorised in 1790 and opened in 1793, made the river navigable between Ipswich and Stowmarket by the construction of fifteen locks. The navigation carried coal, corn, malt and fertiliser, but trade declined after the arrival of the railway in 1846, and commercial use had effectively ceased by the early 20th century. The River Gipping Trust works to restore the navigation and to maintain the Gipping Valley River Path along the towpath.
