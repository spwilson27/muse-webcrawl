Social bond

The social bond is a concept in sociology describing the connections, attachments and shared identities that hold individuals together in a society. It is central to social control theory, particularly the work of Travis Hirschi.

Hirschi's social bond theory

In Causes of Delinquency (1969), Hirschi proposed that deviant behaviour occurs when an individual's bond to society is weakened, and identified four elements of the bond:

  • Attachment — emotional ties to conventional others (parents, teachers, peers).
  • Commitment — investment in conventional lines of action such as education and career, which would be risked by deviance.
  • Involvement — participation in conventional activities, leaving less time for delinquency.
  • Belief — acceptance of the moral validity of society's norms and laws.

Earlier roots

The idea descends from Émile Durkheim's work on solidarity and anomie: individuals integrated into a moral community are restrained by it, while weakened bonds produce normlessness. Durkheim applied the argument to suicide rates, showing they vary with the degree of social integration and regulation.
