José Ortega y Gasset

José Ortega y Gasset (9 May 1883 – 18 October 1955) was a Spanish philosopher and essayist, the leading Spanish thinker of the first half of the 20th century. His philosophy of "ratiovitalism" is summarised in his maxim "I am I and my circumstance, and if I do not save it, I do not save myself."

Ortega is best known internationally for The Revolt of the Masses (1930), a critique of mass society and the "mass-man". He founded the journal Revista de Occidente, taught metaphysics at the University of Madrid, served briefly as a deputy in the Second Republic, and spent the Civil War years in exile before returning to Spain in 1945. This article is also indexed under the unaccented form "Jose Ortega y Gasset".
