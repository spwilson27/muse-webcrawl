Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra

Image placeholder: the adjoint action, mapping each element to bracket with it

In mathematics, the adjoint representation (or adjoint action) of a Lie algebra is the representation of the algebra on itself given by the Lie bracket. For a Lie algebra 𝔤, each element x defines a linear map ad(x): 𝔤 → 𝔤 by ad(x)(y) = [x, y]. The map ad: 𝔤 → 𝔤𝔩(𝔤) is itself a Lie algebra homomorphism, which follows from the Jacobi identity.

Properties

Each operator ad(x) is a derivation of the algebra: ad(x)[y, z] = [ad(x)y, z] + [y, ad(x)z]. The kernel of the adjoint representation is the centre of 𝔤, so the representation is faithful exactly when the algebra is centreless — in particular, it is faithful for semisimple Lie algebras.

The trace form associated with the adjoint representation defines the Killing form, B(x, y) = tr(ad(x) ∘ ad(y)), a fundamental invariant used to characterise semisimplicity.

Relation to Lie groups

The adjoint representation of a Lie algebra is the differential at the identity of the adjoint representation of the corresponding Lie group, in which the group acts on its algebra by conjugation. Exponentiating ad(x) gives the corresponding group adjoint action.

Examples

For the algebra 𝔰𝔩(2, ℂ) of traceless 2×2 matrices, the adjoint representation is three-dimensional and isomorphic to the standard representation of SO(3) on rotations. More generally, for a matrix Lie algebra, ad(x)(y) is the commutator xy − yx.
