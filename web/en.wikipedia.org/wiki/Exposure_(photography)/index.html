Exposure (photography)

In photography, exposure is the amount of light per unit area reaching a frame of photographic film or the surface of an electronic image sensor, as determined by shutter speed, lens aperture, and scene luminance. The photographic response also depends on the sensitivity of the medium (film speed or ISO).

Exposure is governed by the "exposure triangle" of aperture, shutter speed, and ISO sensitivity, and is measured in exposure values (EV). Correct exposure is that which achieves the photographer's intended effect: underexposure loses shadow detail, while overexposure clips highlights. Cameras use light meters and exposure modes to estimate correct exposure, and photographers may apply exposure compensation or bracketing.

Reciprocity — the inverse relationship between intensity and duration — holds for most film and digital capture within normal ranges.
