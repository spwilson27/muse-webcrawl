Cerrejón

The Cerrejón open-pit coal mine in La Guajira, Colombia.

Cerrejón is a large open-pit coal mine in northern Colombia, in the department of La Guajira. It is one of the largest coal-mining operations in Latin America and among the largest open-pit coal mines in the world, covering tens of thousands of hectares in the basin of the Ranchería River. Since 2022 the mine has been wholly owned by the Swiss commodities company Glencore, which bought out the shares of its former partners BHP and Anglo American.

History

Coal deposits in the Cerrejón formation were known from the 19th century, but large-scale development came after an association contract in 1976 between the Colombian state company Carbocol and Intercor, a subsidiary of Exxon. Production began in the mid-1980s in the northern zone, and the consortium Carbones del Cerrejón was formed in 2000 when the operations were consolidated under the ownership of BHP, Anglo American and Glencore, each holding a one-third stake. In 2021 Glencore agreed to acquire the other two partners' shares, completing full ownership in January 2022.

Operations

Coal is extracted by truck-and-shovel and dragline methods from multiple pits, crushed, and transported by a dedicated 150-kilometre railway to Puerto Bolívar on the Caribbean coast, a deep-water port built for the mine where coal is loaded onto bulk carriers for export, mainly to markets in Europe, the Americas and Asia. Annual production has typically been in the range of 20–30 million tonnes of low-ash, low-sulphur thermal coal.

Controversies

The mine has long been the subject of disputes with local Wayuu indigenous communities and Afro-Colombian settlements over displacement, water use, dust and health impacts. The diversion of the Bruno stream (Arroyo Bruno) to expand a pit drew legal challenges, and Colombian courts have at times ordered suspensions or consultations. Communities such as Tabaco were relocated in the early 2000s under controversial circumstances. The operator maintains that it complies with Colombian law and international standards, while human-rights organizations continue to press claims on behalf of affected residents.
