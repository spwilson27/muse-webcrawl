MMR vaccine

MMR vaccine
TypeLive attenuated combination vaccine
TargetsMeasles, mumps, rubella
First licensed1971 (United States)

The MMR vaccine is a vaccine against measles, mumps and rubella (German measles). It is a mixture of live attenuated viruses of the three diseases, administered by injection. The combination vaccine was first licensed in the United States in 1971, combining the separate measles, mumps and rubella vaccines that had been developed in the 1960s, largely through the work of Maurice Hilleman and colleagues at Merck.

Use and effectiveness

The vaccine is typically given to children in two doses, the first around 12–15 months of age and the second before starting school (around 4–6 years in the United States; earlier in the United Kingdom schedule). Two doses are about 97% effective at preventing measles, about 88% effective against mumps, and at least 97% effective against rubella. Side effects are generally mild and include fever and a mild rash; serious adverse events are rare.

Autism claims

A 1998 paper by Andrew Wakefield falsely claimed a link between the MMR vaccine and autism. The paper was later shown to be fraudulent, was fully retracted by The Lancet in 2010, and Wakefield was struck off the UK medical register. Numerous large studies since have found no association between the MMR vaccine and autism. Nevertheless, the scare caused vaccination rates to fall in several countries and measles outbreaks followed.
