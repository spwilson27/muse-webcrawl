Culture of Armenia

A khachkar, the carved cross-stones emblematic of Armenian art.

The culture of Armenia encompasses the language, religion, art, music, cuisine and customs of Armenia and the worldwide Armenian diaspora. It is one of the oldest cultures of the Caucasus, shaped by the early adoption of Christianity (traditionally dated to 301 AD) and by a distinctive alphabet created in 405 AD by Mesrop Mashtots.

Language and literature

Armenian is an independent branch of the Indo-European language family with its own 39-letter alphabet. A rich literary tradition runs from medieval chroniclers and poets such as Gregory of Narek and Sayat-Nova to modern writers including Hovhannes Tumanyan and, in the diaspora, William Saroyan.

Art and architecture

Armenian churches are famed for their conical domes and stone carving; carved khachkars (cross-stones) are inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Heritage list. Medieval monasteries such as Geghard, Tatev and Haghpat anchor the landscape. Armenian illuminated manuscripts and the miniature-painting tradition flourished in Cilicia and Greater Armenia.

Music, dance and cuisine

The duduk, a double-reed woodwind made of apricot wood, is Armenia's signature instrument; its music is recognized by UNESCO. Composer Aram Khachaturian brought Armenian themes to world stages. Traditional dance is performed in circles at celebrations. The cuisine features lavash flatbread (also UNESCO-listed), khorovats (barbecued meats), dolma, harissa and ghapama, accompanied by Armenian brandy from the Ararat valley.
