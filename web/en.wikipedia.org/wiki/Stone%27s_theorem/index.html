Stone's theorem

Stone's theorem, proved by Marshall Harvey Stone in the early 1930s, establishes a one-to-one correspondence between strongly continuous one-parameter unitary groups on a Hilbert space and (possibly unbounded) self-adjoint operators.

Statement

If U(t) is a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group, there exists a unique self-adjoint operator A such that U(t) = e^(itA) for all real t; conversely every self-adjoint operator generates such a group via the functional calculus.

Significance

The theorem underpins quantum mechanics, where the Hamiltonian generates time evolution, and the theory of partial differential equations, where generators of unitary or contraction semigroups solve evolution equations (cf. the Hille–Yosida theorem).
