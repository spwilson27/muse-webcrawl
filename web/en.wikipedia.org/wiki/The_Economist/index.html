The Economist is a British weekly newspaper-format publication founded in September 1843 by James Wilson to campaign for free trade and the repeal of the Corn Laws. Written anonymously by tradition, it covers international politics, economics, business, finance, science and culture with a classical-liberal editorial line.

Style and anonymity

The Economist calls itself a newspaper, is printed in magazine format, and publishes no bylines — a policy dating to its founding, intended to give the paper a single collective voice. Its style book and dry wit are famous; so are its data features, from the Big Mac index (1986) to The World Ahead annual.

Ownership and reach

The Economist Group is owned by a mix of the Agnelli family's Exor and other shareholders, with the Rothschild family long prominent. Circulation is global and majority-American; its podcasts (The Intelligence) and Espresso briefings extend the brand.
