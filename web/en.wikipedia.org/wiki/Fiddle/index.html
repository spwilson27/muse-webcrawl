Fiddle

A fiddle is a bowed string musical instrument, most often a violin, used in folk music traditions. The words "violin" and "fiddle" refer to the same instrument; the distinction lies mainly in the style of music played and in setup details — "fiddle" is used for folk, country, bluegrass, Cajun, Irish traditional and old-time music, while "violin" is associated with classical and art music.

Style and technique

Fiddling styles vary widely by region and tradition, encompassing Irish, Scottish, English, Scandinavian, French, Cajun, old-time, bluegrass, country and western swing playing. Fiddlers often use different tunings (cross-tuning), rhythmic bowing patterns, drones and double stops that differ from classical technique. Fiddles may be set up with a flatter bridge to make double stops easier.

History

The modern fiddle descends from bowed instruments developed in Europe in the 16th century. It spread worldwide through trade and emigration, becoming central to many regional dance and folk traditions. Well-known repertoire includes reels, jigs, hornpipes and breakdowns performed at dances, sessions and fiddle contests.
