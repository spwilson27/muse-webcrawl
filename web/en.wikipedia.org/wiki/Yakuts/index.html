The Yakuts, who call themselves Sakha, are a Turkic ethnic group native to the Sakha Republic in north-eastern Siberia, Russia. They form the majority of the republic's population and are the northernmost Turkic-speaking people.

Origins and history

The ancestors of the Yakuts are thought to have migrated north from the Lake Baikal region between roughly the 10th and 13th centuries, bringing cattle and horse breeding into the subarctic Lena River valley. In the 17th century the region was incorporated into the Russian Empire. Under the Soviet Union, Yakut autonomy was formalised in 1922 with the creation of the Yakut ASSR; after 1991 it became the Sakha Republic within the Russian Federation.

Culture

Traditional Yakut life centred on horse and cattle herding, adapted to one of the coldest inhabited climates on Earth. Cultural symbols include the choron (a carved wooden kumis cup), the summer Yhyakh festival celebrating the solstice, and epic olonkho poetry, recognised by UNESCO. Shamanic traditions historically coexisted with Russian Orthodoxy, introduced in the imperial period.
