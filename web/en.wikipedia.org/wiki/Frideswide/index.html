Frideswide

Saint Frideswide (Old English: Friðuswīþ; c. 650 – 19 October 727) was an English abbess and the patron saint of Oxford and of the University of Oxford. She founded a monastery on the site of what is now Christ Church, Oxford, and her shrine there became one of the principal pilgrimage destinations in medieval England.

Life and legend

According to the medieval lives of the saint, Frideswide was the daughter of Dida of Eynsham, a Mercian prince or sub-king who ruled territory in the upper Thames valley, and his wife Safrida. She took a vow of virginity and founded a double monastery at Oxford, near a ford across the Thames, where she became abbess. The legend relates that a prince named Algar (Ælfgar) sought to marry her by force; fleeing his pursuit, she hid in the woods at Binsey and then in Oxford itself, and the prince was struck blind when he attempted to enter the city. He recovered his sight after repenting, washing his eyes in the water of the well associated with her at Binsey, which became a holy well.

Cult

Frideswide died on 19 October 727, the date kept as her feast day. Her monastery was destroyed by the Danes in 1002, then rebuilt as a community of secular canons and later of Augustinian canons regular, whose priory church became the present Christ Church Cathedral. Her relics were enshrined in 1180 and again in 1289. The shrine was destroyed during the Reformation in 1538, but fragments were recovered and the shrine was reconstructed in the twentieth century. Saint Frideswide's Well at Binsey and the church of St Margaret there remain associated with her cult.
