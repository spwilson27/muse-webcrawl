Sister group

A sister group or sister taxon is a phylogenetic term denoting the closest relative(s) of another given unit in an evolutionary tree. The expression is most easily illustrated by a cladogram: the sister group of a clade is the clade with which it shares an immediate common ancestor, and the two together form a more inclusive clade.

Definition and use

For example, on current evidence birds and crocodilians are sister groups, the two together comprising the clade Archosauria; the closest living relatives of humans are chimpanzees and bonobos, which together form our sister group within Hominini. Identifying sister groups is essential for comparative biology: because sister taxa have diverged from a single ancestor, comparisons between them (and with an outgroup) let researchers infer which traits are ancestral and which are derived.

Terminology

The term was popularised within phylogenetic systematics (cladistics) following Willi Hennig. When the unit in question is a single species, "sister species" is used. Two taxa that are each other's closest relatives are called sister taxa regardless of rank; their relationship is reciprocal. Sister group is sometimes confused with "basal" or "primitive" — both members of a sister pair have evolved for the same length of time since their common ancestor, so neither is more primitive than the other.
