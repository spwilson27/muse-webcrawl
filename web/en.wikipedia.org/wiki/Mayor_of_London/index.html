The Mayor of London is the chief executive of the Greater London Authority, the regional government of London, England. The role was created in 2000 after a referendum held the previous year established a devolved administration for the capital. The mayor is elected by Londoners for a fixed term of four years and, together with the 25-member London Assembly, forms the Greater London Authority (GLA).

Powers and functions

The mayor sets budgets and strategies for London-wide services: Transport for London, the Metropolitan Police (through the Mayor's Office for Policing and Crime), the London Fire Brigade, and strategic planning and housing. The mayor prepares statutory strategies on transport, environment, economic development and spatial development (the London Plan), and can direct development corporations and intervene in planning applications of potential strategic importance.

History

The GLA replaced the Greater London Council, which had been abolished in 1986. Ken Livingstone, formerly leader of the GLC, won the first mayoral election in 2000 as an independent and again in 2004 for Labour. Boris Johnson (Conservative) served two terms from 2008 to 2016, overseeing the London 2012 Olympic Games. Sadiq Khan (Labour) was elected in 2016 and re-elected in 2021 and 2024, becoming the first mayor to win a third term.

Distinction from the Lord Mayor

The Mayor of London should not be confused with the Lord Mayor of London, the ceremonial head of the City of London (the historic square mile), an office dating to the 12th century. The Mayor of London governs the whole of Greater London, an area of about 1,570 km² and more than 8.8 million residents.
