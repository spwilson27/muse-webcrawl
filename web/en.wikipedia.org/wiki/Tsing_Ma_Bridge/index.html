The Tsing Ma Bridge (Chinese: 青馬大橋) is a suspension bridge in Hong Kong, linking Tsing Yi Island with Ma Wan Island across the Ma Wan Channel. Named after the two islands it connects, it was the world's longest road-and-rail suspension bridge when it opened in 1997, and its 1,377-metre main span remains among the longest of any suspension bridge. It is the centrepiece of the Lantau Link, the road and rail connection to Hong Kong International Airport on Lantau Island.

Design

The bridge has two decks: the open upper deck carries three lanes of traffic in each direction, while the enclosed lower deck contains two railway tracks (used by the MTR Airport Express and Tung Chung lines) plus two sheltered single-lane carriageways for emergency and maintenance traffic, and for use during typhoons when the upper deck is closed to wind-sensitive vehicles. Two 206-metre concrete towers support the main cables, each made of thousands of parallel steel wires.

Construction

Construction began in 1992 as part of the Airport Core Programme, one of the largest infrastructure projects then undertaken anywhere. The bridge was opened by former British prime minister Margaret Thatcher on 27 April 1997, weeks before the handover of Hong Kong. Together with the Kap Shui Mun Bridge and the Ma Wan Viaduct, it completed the only road link to Lantau Island.
