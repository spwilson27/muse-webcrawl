The Mound is an artificial slope in central Edinburgh, Scotland, which connects Edinburgh's Old Town and New Town. It was formed by dumping approximately 1.5 million cartloads of earth excavated during the construction of the New Town into the drained Nor Loch, which previously occupied the valley between the two towns.

History

Construction of the earthen causeway began in 1781, following an idea attributed to the merchant George Boyd, and it grew progressively as building work in the New Town generated more spoil. The completed Mound provided a convenient route for pedestrians and vehicles between the Royal Mile on the Old Town ridge and Princes Street in the New Town, replacing earlier, steeper and less direct paths.

Landmarks

The Mound is flanked by some of Edinburgh's most prominent cultural institutions. On its eastern side stand the National Gallery of Scotland and the Royal Scottish Academy, both neoclassical buildings designed in the 19th century. At the top of the Mound lies Ramsay Gardens and Bank of Scotland's imposing headquarters building, built in the early 20th century. The Mound is also the site of Edinburgh's official Christmas tree, gifted annually by the people of Hordaland, Norway, and of the Floral Clock in the adjacent Princes Street Gardens, first created in 1903.

During the Edinburgh Festival Fringe, the Mound Precinct at the top of the slope is a popular venue for street performers.
