Northern Thrace

Northern Thrace (Bulgarian: Северна Тракия, Severna Trakiya) is the northern part of the historical region of Thrace, lying within modern Bulgaria. It is bounded by the Balkan Mountains (Stara Planina) to the north, the Rhodope Mountains to the south, the Black Sea to the east and the historical region of Macedonia to the west. Its core is the Upper Thracian Plain, drained by the Maritsa (Evros) River.

History

Anciently part of the Odrysian kingdom of the Thracians, the region came under Roman and then Byzantine rule. After the Ottoman conquest it formed the core of the province of Rumelia. In 1878 the Treaty of Berlin made Northern Thrace the autonomous Ottoman province of Eastern Rumelia, with its capital at Plovdiv; in 1885 Eastern Rumelia united with the Principality of Bulgaria, an act that helped precipitate the Serbo-Bulgarian War but was eventually recognised internationally.

Cities

Plovdiv, Bulgaria's second city and one of Europe's oldest continuously inhabited cities, is the region's chief centre. Other important cities include Stara Zagora, Sliven, Yambol, Haskovo, Pazardzhik and Asenovgrad. The plain is Bulgaria's most productive agricultural area, known for wheat, vegetables, fruit, wine and attar of roses from the adjoining Rose Valley.
