Achaean War

Date146 BC
LocationGreece
ResultRoman victory; dissolution of the Achaean League
BelligerentsRoman Republic vs. Achaean League

The Achaean War of 146 BC was the final conflict between the Roman Republic and the Greek Achaean League. It ended with the Roman victory at the Battle of Corinth, the sack and destruction of Corinth, and the establishment of direct Roman control over Greece.

Background

Relations between Rome and the League had deteriorated over the League's disputes with Sparta and its increasingly independent diplomacy. The Achaean assembly, influenced by leaders such as Critolaus and Diaeus, declared war on Sparta and defied Roman envoys, leading the Senate to declare war.

Course of the war

Roman forces under Quintus Caecilius Metellus defeated the Achaeans in Macedonia and central Greece, and the consul Lucius Mummius took command for the final campaign. At the Battle of Corinth (also called the Battle of Leucopetra), the Achaean army was destroyed; Mummius then sacked Corinth, executing or enslaving much of its population and shipping its artworks to Rome. The same year saw the destruction of Carthage at the end of the Third Punic War.

Aftermath

The Achaean League was dissolved, and Greece was placed under the oversight of the Roman governor of Macedonia, ending Greek political independence until Roman rule itself gave way to the Byzantine era.
