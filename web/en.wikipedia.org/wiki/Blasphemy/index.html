Blasphemy

Blasphemy is the act of insulting or showing contempt or lack of reverence to a deity, or to religious or holy persons or things, or toward something considered sacred or inviolable. The term derives from the Greek blasphēmía, "slander" or "evil speaking". Some religions regard blasphemy as a religious crime, and many countries have or have had laws punishing it.

In religions

In the Hebrew Bible, blaspheming the Name of God is a capital offence (Leviticus 24:16). In Christianity, blasphemy against the Holy Spirit is described in the Gospels as an "eternal sin" that will not be forgiven. In Islam, insulting God (sabb Allah) or the Prophet Muhammad (sabb al-rasul) is treated extremely seriously; the Quran prescribes no specific worldly penalty, but classical jurisprudence and some modern states' laws prescribe severe punishments, up to death.

Blasphemy laws

Many states retain laws against blasphemy or religious insult. Pakistan's blasphemy statutes, including a mandatory death penalty for insulting Muhammad, have drawn international criticism for their use in personal disputes and against minorities. Conversely, a series of countries — including Denmark (2017), Canada (2018), Ireland (by referendum, 2018), Greece (2019) and New Zealand (2019) — repealed their blasphemy laws. In England and Wales the common-law offences of blasphemy and blasphemous libel were abolished in 2008.

Famous cases

Well-known controversies include the fatwa issued against Salman Rushdie following The Satanic Verses (1988), the Danish Jyllands-Posten Muhammad cartoons affair (2005), and the 2015 attack on the French satirical magazine Charlie Hebdo. Such cases fuel continuing debate between freedom of expression and protection of religious sensibilities.
