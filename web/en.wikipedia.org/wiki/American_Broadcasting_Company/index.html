The American Broadcasting Company (ABC) is an American commercial broadcast television and radio network that serves as the flagship property of the Disney Entertainment division of the Walt Disney Company. Founded in 1943, ABC is one of the "Big Three" American television networks alongside CBS and NBC.

History

ABC traces its origins to the NBC Blue Network, a radio network that the Federal Communications Commission ordered NBC to divest. The network was bought by Edward J. Noble in 1943 and renamed the American Broadcasting Company. ABC began television broadcasts in 1948, initially lagging far behind CBS and NBC. In 1953 it merged with United Paramount Theatres under Leonard Goldenson, providing the capital that helped it compete.

ABC pioneered close ties with Hollywood studios: in 1954 it launched Disneyland, an anthology series produced by Walt Disney, beginning a relationship that culminated in Disney's acquisition of ABC's parent company, Capital Cities/ABC, in 1996. The network also built a strong sports division, ABC Sports, home of Monday Night Football and Wide World of Sports, under executive Roone Arledge, who also created ABC News programmes such as Nightline and World News Tonight.

Programming

Notable ABC programmes have included Happy Days, Roots, NYPD Blue, Lost, Grey's Anatomy, Modern Family, Dancing with the Stars and The Bachelor. Daytime programming includes Good Morning America and the soap opera General Hospital. Since 2001 the network has also broadcast the Academy Awards annually. ABC programming reaches viewers through more than 230 affiliated stations across the United States, plus eight owned-and-operated stations.
