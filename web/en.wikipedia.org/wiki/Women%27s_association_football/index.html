Women's association football

Women's association football, more commonly known as women's football or women's soccer, is the team sport of association football played by women. It is played at the professional level in multiple countries and at the international level by 176 national teams worldwide.

History

Women played football in the nineteenth century, and the sport drew large crowds in England during the First World War, famously through teams such as Dick, Kerr Ladies. In 1921 the Football Association banned women's matches at its members' grounds, a ban that stifled the game in England for fifty years until it was lifted in 1971. Similar restrictions existed in other countries.

Modern era

The first FIFA Women's World Cup was held in China in 1991, and women's football became an Olympic sport in 1996. The United States has been the most successful national team, while European club football, centred on the UEFA Women's Champions League, has professionalised rapidly since the 2010s. The 2023 World Cup in Australia and New Zealand set attendance records, reflecting the sport's steep growth in popularity and investment.
