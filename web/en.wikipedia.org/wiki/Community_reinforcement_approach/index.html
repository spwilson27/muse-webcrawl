Community reinforcement approach

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The community reinforcement approach (CRA) is a behavioural treatment programme for substance use disorders, developed in the 1970s by Nathan Azrin and colleagues. It is based on the operant-conditioning idea that substance use is maintained by its reinforcing consequences, and that treatment should rearrange a person's environment — family, work, social and recreational life — so that sober behaviour is more rewarding than drinking or drug use.

Components

CRA typically combines several elements, chosen according to the client's needs:

  • functional analysis of the triggers and consequences of substance use and of sober behaviour;
  • sober social-network building, including a "sobriety sampling" trial period of abstinence;
  • behavioural couples or family therapy, teaching partners to reinforce abstinence and communicate more effectively;
  • job-seeking and vocational skills training;
  • social and recreational counselling, helping the client develop enjoyable drug-free activities;
  • relapse-prevention and drink/drug-refusal skills.

A related variant, CRA plus vouchers, adds contingency management — vouchers exchangeable for goods and services awarded for objectively verified abstinence — and has been influential in the treatment of cocaine dependence. A version adapted for adolescents is called adolescent community reinforcement approach (A-CRA). The family-focused offshoot CRAFT (community reinforcement and family training) teaches concerned family members to encourage a reluctant loved one into treatment.

Evidence

CRA has accumulated substantial research support, beginning with Azrin's early studies in alcohol treatment, and is listed among evidence-based treatments for alcohol and drug use disorders in several national reviews. Meta-analyses generally find it effective, particularly when combined with contingency management.
