The 9/11 Commission Report, formally the Final Report of the National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States, is the official report of the events leading up to the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks. It was released on July 22, 2004, by the bipartisan 9/11 Commission, chaired by Thomas Kean with Lee H. Hamilton as vice chair.

Contents

Based on more than 1,200 interviews, a review of over 2.5 million pages of documents, and public hearings, the report recounts the planning of the attacks by al-Qaeda, the hijackings themselves, and the emergency response. It famously concluded that the attacks were "a shock, but they should not have come as a surprise," and identified failures of imagination, policy, capabilities, and management across U.S. government agencies.

Recommendations and reception

The report made 41 recommendations, including the creation of a Director of National Intelligence and a National Counterterrorism Center—both enacted by the Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act of 2004. Written in an unusually narrative style for a government report, it became a bestseller and was a finalist for the National Book Award. Critics, including some commissioners and family groups, later questioned whether all of its findings—particularly about funding of the plot—were fully supported.
