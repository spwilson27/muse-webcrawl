9/11 Commission
National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States
Established 2002; final report released 22 July 2004.

The 9/11 Commission, formally the National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States, was an independent bipartisan commission created by the United States Congress and President George W. Bush in 2002. It investigated the circumstances surrounding the September 11 attacks, including intelligence and law-enforcement failures, diplomacy, immigration and aviation security, and the emergency response in New York City and at the Pentagon.

Work and report

Chaired by former New Jersey governor Thomas Kean, with former congressman Lee H. Hamilton as vice chair, the commission interviewed more than 1,200 people, reviewed millions of pages of documents and held public hearings. Its 2004 9/11 Commission Report presented a detailed narrative of the plot and the government response. Written in an unusually readable style for an official report, it became a bestseller and was a finalist for the National Book Award.

Recommendations

The commission recommended a major reorganisation of the intelligence community, including creation of a Director of National Intelligence and a National Counterterrorism Center. It also urged stronger congressional oversight, improved information sharing, risk-based homeland-security funding, secure identification standards and foreign-policy measures aimed at countering extremism. Many recommendations were enacted in the Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act of 2004, though commissioners and families later criticised incomplete implementation.

Controversies

Debate surrounded the commission’s access to documents, initial funding, conflicts of interest and the role of executive director Philip Zelikow. Some victims’ families, who had campaigned for the commission’s creation, remained central public advocates and later formed groups to press for the recommendations. Despite disputes, the commission’s account remains the most widely cited official synthesis of the attacks.
