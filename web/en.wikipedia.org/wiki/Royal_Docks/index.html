Royal Docks

Named forTheir scale and royal associations

The Royal Docks are a group of three great enclosed docks in East London, in the London Borough of Newham: the Royal Victoria Dock (opened 1855), the Royal Albert Dock (1880) and the King George V Dock (1921). Together they formed the largest enclosed dock system in the world, with some 250 acres of water.

History

Built to accommodate the largest steamships of their day, the Royals were the last great downstream docks of the Port of London. They handled cargo from across the British Empire and employed thousands of dockers. Containerisation and the shift of shipping to Tilbury and Felixstowe led to their closure to commercial traffic in 1981.

Regeneration

Under the London Docklands Development Corporation, the area was redeveloped with the ExCeL London exhibition centre, London City Airport (opened 1987 on the King George V Dock site), the University of East London's Docklands campus and extensive housing. The docks remain a striking stretch of open water, used for rowing regattas, open-water swimming and events; the Royal Docks continue to be the focus of major regeneration plans for water-based housing and leisure.
