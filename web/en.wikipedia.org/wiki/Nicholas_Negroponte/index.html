Nicholas Negroponte

Nicholas Negroponte (born 1943) is a Greek-American architect and computer scientist, best known as the founder and chairman emeritus of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology's Media Lab and as the founder of the One Laptop per Child Association (OLPC).

Career

Trained as an architect, Negroponte joined the MIT faculty in 1966 and founded its Architecture Machine Group, a precursor of modern human–computer interaction research. In 1985 he co-founded the MIT Media Lab, which he directed for 15 years. His 1995 bestseller Being Digital popularised the contrast between "atoms" and "bits" and predicted the convergence of media and computing. He was also an early investor in Wired magazine and many technology start-ups.

One Laptop per Child

In 2005 Negroponte announced OLPC at the World Economic Forum, aiming to produce a laptop costing about $100 for children in developing countries. The resulting XO laptop, with its rugged design, mesh networking and open-source software, was distributed in the millions, though it never reached the original price and scale ambitions. Negroponte stepped down from day-to-day leadership in 2008.
