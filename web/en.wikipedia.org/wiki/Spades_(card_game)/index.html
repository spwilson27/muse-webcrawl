Spades (card game)

Spades

TypeTrick-taking, partnership
Players4 (usually 2 partnerships); variants for 2–6
DeckStandard 52 cards
OriginUnited States, 1930s–40s (Cincinnati, Ohio)

Spades is a trick-taking card game devised in the United States in the late 1930s. It is usually played by four players in two partnerships, although solo and two-player variants exist. The game is a descendant of the Whist family and is a relative of Bridge, Hearts, and Euchre.

Gameplay

All 52 cards are dealt. Each player bids the number of tricks they expect to win, and partners' bids are combined. Spades are always trump. A partnership scores 10 points per bid trick if it makes its contract, plus one point ("bag") per overtrick; accumulating ten bags costs 100 points. Failing to make the contract costs 10 points per trick bid. Special bids include nil (bid zero, worth 100 points) and blind nil. Games are typically played to 500 points.

History and popularity

Spades spread from Cincinnati in the 1940s, was carried widely by American soldiers during the Second World War, and became one of the most popular social card games in the United States, particularly in African-American communities and in prisons. It is widely played online and in tournaments.
