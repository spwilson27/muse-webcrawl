ACES II

ACES II is an ejection seat system produced by Collins Aerospace, a subsidiary of RTX Corporation (formerly Raytheon Technologies). The name is an acronym for Advanced Concept Ejection Seat, Model 2. It is used in a wide variety of United States Air Force aircraft, including the A-10 Thunderbolt II, the F-15 Eagle, the F-16 Fighting Falcon, the F-22 Raptor, the B-1B Lancer, and the B-2 Spirit.

The seat was originally developed by the McDonnell Douglas Corporation. It is a zero-zero ejection seat, meaning it can safely eject a crew member at zero altitude and zero airspeed. It is rocket-propelled, using a telescoping rocket motor to clear the aircraft, and incorporates an electronic sequencer that adjusts parachute deployment timing based on airspeed and altitude at ejection.

Design

The ACES II uses a single rocket catapult and a drogue gun to stabilize the seat after ejection. Its sequencer has three modes: a low-speed/low-altitude mode, a high-speed mode, and a high-altitude mode. The seat includes a survival kit and an emergency oxygen supply. A notable safety feature is that the ACES II was one of the first production seats designed to accommodate a wider range of pilot body sizes and weights, including female aircrew.

Operational history

ACES II seats have been credited with saving hundreds of lives since entering service in 1978. They were used in ejections during the Gulf War, the Kosovo War, and operations in Afghanistan and Iraq. Production was consolidated under Collins Aerospace after a series of corporate acquisitions.
