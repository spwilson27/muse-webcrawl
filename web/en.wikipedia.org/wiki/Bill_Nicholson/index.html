William Edward Nicholson OBE (26 January 1919 – 23 October 2004) was an English football player, coach, manager and scout who spent his entire professional life with Tottenham Hotspur. As manager from 1958 to 1974 he led Spurs to the most successful period in the club's history, highlighted by the League and FA Cup "Double" of 1960–61, the first double of the twentieth century in English football.

Playing career

Nicholson joined Tottenham's ground staff as a 16-year-old in 1936 and signed professional terms in 1938, playing as a wing half. His career was interrupted by the Second World War, during which he served as a physical education instructor. He was a member of the Spurs side that won the Second Division championship in 1949–50 and the First Division title in 1950–51 under Arthur Rowe's "push and run" team. He won a single cap for England in 1951, scoring with his first touch in international football against Portugal. He made 341 appearances for Tottenham before retiring in 1955.

Managerial career

After coaching at Tottenham and assisting the England national team, Nicholson was appointed Spurs manager in October 1958; his first match in charge ended in a 10–4 victory over Everton. Building around captain Danny Blanchflower and players such as Jimmy Greaves, Dave Mackay, and John White, Nicholson's team won the First Division title and the FA Cup in 1960–61, retaining the FA Cup in 1962 and winning the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1963 — the first European trophy won by a British club. Later honours included the FA Cup in 1967, League Cups in 1971 and 1973, and the UEFA Cup in 1972.

Later life and legacy

Nicholson resigned in 1974, disillusioned with the growing commercialisation of football, but returned to Tottenham as a scout and adviser, famously recommending Glenn Hoddle. He was appointed club president in 1991. His famous maxim — "It is better to fail aiming high than to succeed aiming low" — remains emblematic of the club's attacking philosophy. A statue of Nicholson stands outside Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, and the approach to the ground is named Bill Nicholson Way.
