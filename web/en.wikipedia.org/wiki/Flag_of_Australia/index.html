Flag of Australia

The flag of Australia is a defaced Blue Ensign: a blue field with the Union Jack in the canton, a large white seven-pointed star below it, and a representation of the Southern Cross constellation in the fly half. The seven-pointed Commonwealth Star has one point for each of the six states plus one for the territories. The Southern Cross appears as five white stars — one small five-pointed star and four seven-pointed stars.

History

The design resulted from a competition held at federation in 1901, won jointly by five entrants with similar designs. A simplified version was approved by King Edward VII in 1903, and the flag's specifications were formally adopted with passage of the Flags Act 1953. The present specification, with the Commonwealth Star given seven points, dates from 1908.

Symbolism

The Union Jack symbolizes Australia's history as a set of British colonies. The Commonwealth Star represents the federation of the states and territories. The Southern Cross is one of the most distinctive constellations visible in the Southern Hemisphere and has long been used to represent Australia. Other official flags include the Australian Red Ensign for merchant ships, the Aboriginal flag, and the Torres Strait Islander flag, the latter two recognized under the Flags Act in 1995.
