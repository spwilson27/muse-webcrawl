Free agent

In professional sports, a free agent is an athlete who is eligible to sign a contract with any team because he or she is not under contract to a specific team. The term also refers to a player whose contract with a team has expired or been terminated, or who was never drafted in leagues with an entry draft.

Types

Leagues distinguish between unrestricted free agents, who may sign anywhere with no compensation to their former team, and restricted free agents, whose former team retains rights — for example, the right to match an offer sheet — under rules set by the league's collective bargaining agreement. Players released mid-contract may pass through waivers, during which other teams can claim their existing contract before they become free agents.

History

Free agency emerged from the dismantling of the reserve system, under which clubs controlled players' rights indefinitely. Key milestones include the 1975 Messersmith–McNally arbitration ruling in Major League Baseball, which created modern baseball free agency, and court and union battles in other leagues; in European football, the 1995 Bosman ruling allowed out-of-contract players to move freely between clubs in the European Union. Free agency gives players bargaining leverage and mobility, and "free agent" signing periods are now major events in sports media calendars.

Outside sports

The term is sometimes used more broadly for any independent worker or unaffiliated person free to choose their next engagement.
