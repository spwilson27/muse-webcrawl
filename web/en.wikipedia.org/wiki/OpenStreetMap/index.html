OpenStreetMap (OSM) is a collaborative, free geographic database and world map built by a community of volunteer mappers who contribute and maintain data about roads, trails, cafés, railway stations, and much more, all over the world. It is often described as a "Wikipedia for maps".

History

OpenStreetMap was founded by Steve Coast in the United Kingdom in 2004, inspired by the success of Wikipedia and by the restrictions on freely available map data, which in the UK was dominated by the Ordnance Survey. The OpenStreetMap Foundation was established in 2006 to support the project. Mapping parties, the tracing of donated aerial imagery (notably from Yahoo! and later Bing), and imports of public-domain data such as the US Census Bureau's TIGER dataset drove rapid growth.

Data model and contribution

Contributors survey features using GPS devices and local knowledge, trace features from permitted aerial imagery, and edit the database through editors such as the web-based iD editor and the advanced desktop editor JOSM. Data is stored as nodes, ways, and relations annotated with free-form key–value tags. Registered users number in the millions, though a smaller core contributes most edits.

Uses

OSM data powers maps and routing in countless websites and apps, humanitarian mapping through the Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team (notably after the 2010 Haiti earthquake), and services from companies including Apple, Meta, Microsoft, and numerous national mapping and logistics users. Following a licence change in 2012, the database is available under the Open Database License.
