North Dakota is a state in the Upper Midwest of the United States. It is named after the Dakota Sioux tribe that historically inhabited the region. North Dakota is bordered by the Canadian provinces of Saskatchewan and Manitoba to the north, Minnesota to the east, South Dakota to the south, and Montana to the west. Its capital is Bismarck, and its most populous city is Fargo.

Geography

The state's eastern border is formed by the Red River of the North, flowing through the fertile Red River Valley. Central North Dakota is the Drift Prairie and Missouri Plateau; the southwest contains the rugged Badlands, where Theodore Roosevelt National Park preserves the eroded landscape. The geographic center of North America is near the town of Rugby. North Dakota has a continental climate with very cold winters and warm summers.

History

The territory was home to Mandan, Hidatsa, Sioux, Chippewa, and other peoples. Much of the land came to the United States through the Louisiana Purchase of 1803, and the Lewis and Clark Expedition wintered at Fort Mandan in 1804–05. Dakota Territory was created in 1861; North Dakota and South Dakota were admitted to the Union on 2 November 1889.

Economy

Agriculture — wheat, soybeans, corn, sunflowers, and cattle — has long dominated the economy. Since the 2000s, hydraulic fracturing of the Bakken Formation in the Williston Basin made North Dakota one of the largest oil-producing states and drove rapid population growth, particularly around Williston and Watford City. Wind energy is also significant.
