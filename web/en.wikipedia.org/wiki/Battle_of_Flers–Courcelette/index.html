Tanks were used in battle for the first time at Flers–Courcelette.

The Battle of Flers–Courcelette was fought from 15 to 22 September 1916 during the Battle of the Somme in the First World War. British Fourth Army and Reserve Army troops, with the Canadian Corps and the New Zealand Division prominent, attacked the German defences between Flers and Courcelette. The battle is famous as the first in which tanks were used.

Background

After two months of attrition on the Somme, General Haig planned a major push against the German third position. Forty-nine Mark I tanks were available to support the infantry, though mechanical unreliability reduced the number that reached the start line.

Battle

The attack began at dawn on 15 September behind a creeping barrage. The New Zealanders and British divisions took Flers, where a tank famously drove down the main street; the Canadians stormed Courcelette and held it against counter-attacks. The tank D1 and others supported the capture of objectives that had resisted earlier assaults, but many tanks broke down or were knocked out, and gains elsewhere were limited. Fighting continued until 22 September, advancing the line up to about 2,500 yards on a broad front.

Aftermath

Though not the breakthrough Haig hoped for, Flers–Courcelette captured important villages and demonstrated the potential of armour. The Germans were shaken by the new weapon, and tank development accelerated on both sides.
