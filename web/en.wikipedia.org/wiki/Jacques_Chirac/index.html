Jacques Chirac

Born29 November 1932, Paris
Died26 September 2019, Paris
OfficesPresident of France (1995–2007); Prime Minister (1974–76, 1986–88); Mayor of Paris (1977–95)

Jacques René Chirac (1932–2019) was a French politician who served as President of France from 1995 to 2007. A dominant Gaullist figure for four decades, he was previously prime minister under both Valéry Giscard d'Estaing and, in "cohabitation", under François Mitterrand, and mayor of Paris for 18 years.

Early career

An ENA graduate and protégé of Georges Pompidou, Chirac became a minister in the 1960s–70s, prime minister in 1974, founded the RPR party in 1976, and won the Paris mayoralty in 1977. He led the first cohabitation government (1986–88) and lost presidential elections in 1981 and 1988.

Presidency

Elected in 1995, Chirac ended nuclear testing after a controversial final series, acknowledged French state responsibility for the Vel d'Hiv deportations, abolished conscription, and professionalised the army. His early austerity sparked the huge 1995 strikes; a failed dissolution in 1997 brought socialist Lionel Jospin to government in a second cohabitation. In 2002 Chirac was re-elected with 82% against far-right candidate Jean-Marie Le Pen. He won wide praise in 2003 for opposing the US-led invasion of Iraq at the United Nations. His second term saw the 2005 riots and the rejected EU constitution referendum; presidential terms had been shortened to five years in 2000.

Later life

In 2011 Chirac was convicted (suspended sentence) over fictitious jobs at Paris city hall during his mayoralty. He died in 2019 and received national mourning and a state funeral ceremony at Les Invalides.
