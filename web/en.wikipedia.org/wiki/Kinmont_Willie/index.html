William Armstrong of Kinmont (c. 1540s – c. 1608), known as Kinmont Willie, was a famous Scottish border reiver of the Armstrong clan of Liddesdale. His rescue from Carlisle Castle in 1596 by Sir Walter Scott of Buccleuch is one of the most celebrated episodes of the Anglo-Scottish border and is commemorated in the Border ballad "Kinmont Willie".

Capture

The Armstrongs were among the most powerful and feared reiving families of the Scottish West March. On 17 March 1596—a truce day following a meeting of the wardens at Kershopefoot—Kinmont Willie was seized by English officers under Thomas Salkeld, deputy of the English warden Lord Scrope, while riding home through the Debateable Land. Captured in violation of the truce, he was carried to Carlisle Castle and held in irons pending execution.

The rescue

Walter Scott of Branxholme and Buccleuch, Keeper of Liddesdale, demanded Willie's release and, when Scrope refused, led a small party of about eighty horsemen to Carlisle on the night of 13 April 1596. In a storm, the raiders breached the castle's postern, overcame the guard, and carried Willie out—by some accounts with his chains still on, lowered down the wall. The daring raid humiliated the English March administration and caused a diplomatic crisis with Elizabeth I, who demanded Buccleuch be surrendered. Buccleuch eventually presented himself to the Queen, who is said to have remarked that with ten thousand such men James VI could shake any throne in Europe; he was released without punishment.

Legacy

The ballad of Kinmont Willie, collected by Sir Walter Scott (a descendant of Buccleuch) in the Minstrelsy of the Scottish Border (1802), gives Willie a defiant speech to Lord Scrope and immortalised the rescue as a symbol of border lawlessness and clan loyalty in the years just before the Union of the Crowns (1603) brought the reiving era to an end.
