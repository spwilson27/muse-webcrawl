Angular cheilitis (also called cheilosis, angular stomatitis, or perleche) is inflammation of one or both corners of the mouth (angles of the lips). It often presents as redness, cracking, scaling, bleeding, blistering and pain at the oral commissures.

Causes

The condition usually results from a combination of moisture accumulating in the folds at the mouth corners — which allows infection by the yeast Candida albicans or bacteria such as Staphylococcus aureus — and local irritation. Risk factors include ill-fitting dentures, drooling, lip licking, thumb sucking, and deficiencies of iron, vitamin B2 (riboflavin), vitamin B12 or folate.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is typically clinical, based on appearance. Swabs may be taken to identify the infecting organism, and blood tests can check for nutritional deficiencies or diabetes.

Treatment

Treatment addresses both the infection and its causes: antifungal or antibacterial creams, barrier ointments such as petroleum jelly to keep the area dry, correction of denture fit, and supplementation where a deficiency is found.
