Undetectable = untransmittable

Undetectable = untransmittable, commonly abbreviated U=U, is a public-health message stating that people living with HIV who take antiretroviral therapy (ART) and maintain an undetectable viral load cannot sexually transmit HIV to their partners.

Evidence

The claim rests on several large studies. HPTN 052 (2011) showed that early ART reduced transmission within serodifferent couples by over 90%, and the PARTNER, PARTNER2 and Opposites Attract studies, which followed couples having condomless sex when the partner with HIV was virally suppressed, observed zero linked transmissions across tens of thousands of sex acts. In 2016 the Prevention Access Campaign launched the U=U slogan to communicate these findings; the message has since been endorsed by the World Health Organization, UNAIDS and the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

Impact

Advocates credit U=U with reducing HIV stigma and encouraging testing and treatment adherence. The concept applies specifically to sexual transmission and requires sustained viral suppression, usually defined as below 200 copies of HIV RNA per millilitre of blood, confirmed by regular monitoring.
