Artist: Diego Velázquez

Year: 1634–35

Medium: Oil on canvas

Dimensions: 307 cm × 367 cm

Location: Museo del Prado, Madrid

The Surrender of Breda (Spanish: La rendición de Breda), also known as Las Lanzas ("The Lances"), is a painting by the Spanish Golden Age artist Diego Velázquez. It was painted between 1634 and 1635 and commemorates the surrender of the Dutch city of Breda on 2 June 1625, when the Dutch governor Justin of Nassau handed the keys of the city to the Genoese general Ambrogio Spinola, commander of the Spanish Army of Flanders.

Historical background

The siege of Breda (1624–25) was a major engagement of the Eighty Years' War between Spain and the Dutch Republic. After an eleven-month siege, the starving garrison capitulated on honourable terms. The painting was commissioned for the Hall of Realms (Salón de Reinos) of the Buen Retiro Palace in Madrid, as part of a cycle of twelve battle paintings celebrating Spanish victories.

Description

Velázquez depicts the moment of the surrender itself rather than the battle: Spinola, on the right, receives the keys from Justin of Nassau with a magnanimous gesture, placing a hand on the defeated commander's shoulder. Behind Spinola rises the famous forest of upright Spanish pikes that gives the canvas its alternative title. In the background the battlefield stretches toward the fortifications of Breda and the Scheldt estuary.

Art historians praise the canvas for its humane treatment of both sides, its atmospheric landscape, and the psychologically nuanced grouping of the figures, many of them portraits of identifiable officers.

Legacy

The painting hangs in the Museo del Prado. It is regarded as one of the finest history paintings of the Spanish Golden Age, and is notable for celebrating Spanish chivalry and clemency as much as Spanish arms.
