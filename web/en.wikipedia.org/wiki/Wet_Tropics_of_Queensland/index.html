Wet Tropics of Queensland

The Wet Tropics of Queensland is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the wet tropics bioregion of Queensland, Australia. Inscribed in 1988, it covers approximately 8,940 km² of tropical rainforest along the northeast coast, from Townsville to Cooktown, running largely parallel to the Great Barrier Reef.

Description

The site includes the Daintree Rainforest, one of the oldest continuously surviving tropical rainforests in the world, with origins in the ancient supercontinent of Gondwana. It preserves an extraordinary record of the evolution of Australia's marsupials, songbirds and primitive flowering plants, and contains a high proportion of species found nowhere else.

Notable areas include Daintree National Park, Wooroonooran National Park, Barron Gorge, Girringun and Paluma Range national parks.

Biodiversity

The Wet Tropics contain about 30% of Australia's marsupial species, 58% of its bat species, 29% of its frog species and 60% of its butterfly species, within less than 0.2% of the continent's land area. Endemic animals include the southern cassowary, musky rat-kangaroo and Lumholtz's tree-kangaroo.

Management

The area is managed by the Wet Tropics Management Authority. Aboriginal peoples, including the Eastern Kuku Yalanji, have lived in and hold cultural connections to the region for tens of thousands of years; in 2021 the Eastern Kuku Yalanji formally received ownership of parts of Daintree National Park.
