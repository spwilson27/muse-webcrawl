Rania Al Abdullah (Arabic: رانيا العبد الله; born Rania Al-Yassin, 31 August 1970) is the queen consort of Jordan as the wife of King Abdullah II. Born in Kuwait to Palestinian parents from Tulkarm, she was displaced by the Gulf War and settled in Amman, where she studied business administration at the American University in Cairo and worked in marketing and banking before meeting the then-Prince Abdullah.

Queen consort

She became queen on 22 March 1999, six weeks after her husband's accession. She has made education her central cause: she founded the Jordan River Foundation, the Jordan Education Initiative and the Teachers' Academy, and campaigns internationally for universal primary education, serving with figures such as UNICEF and as co-chair of the World Economic Forum's education initiative. She is also known for cross-cultural dialogue efforts, her widely followed social media presence, and her authorship of children's books including The Sandwich Swap.
