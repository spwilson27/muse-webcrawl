Muscovite

Muscovite (also known as common mica, isinglass, or potash mica) is a hydrated phyllosilicate mineral of aluminium and potassium with the chemical formula KAl2(AlSi3O10)(F,OH)2. It is the most common member of the mica group. The name derives from "Muscovy glass," because in medieval Russia large sheets of the mineral were used as a substitute for glass in windows.

Properties

Muscovite has a pearly to vitreous luster and a perfect basal cleavage, allowing it to split into very thin, flexible, elastic sheets. Its color ranges from colorless and silvery-white to pale shades of brown, green, or pink; it has a hardness of about 2–2.5 on the Mohs scale parallel to the sheet and a specific gravity around 2.8.

Occurrence

It is a common rock-forming mineral in granites, pegmatites, gneisses, and schists, and also occurs as fine-grained sericite in altered rocks and as detrital grains in sediments. Large sheet crystals are mined from pegmatites in India, Brazil, and elsewhere.

Uses

Because it is an excellent electrical insulator that withstands high temperatures, muscovite is used in electrical components, capacitors, and insulation, and ground mica serves as a filler and extender in paints, plastics, rubber, and cosmetics, where it provides shimmer in pearlescent pigments.
