David Allan Stewart (born 9 September 1952) is an English musician, songwriter and record producer, best known for Eurythmics, the rock duo he formed with Annie Lennox in 1980. He and Lennox were inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2020 and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2022. Stewart won the Grammy Award for Best Rock Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocals in 1987 for the Eurythmics song "Missionary Man".

Early life and career

Stewart was born in Sunderland in the northeast of England. While still in his teens he secured a record deal as part of the folk-rock band Longdancer, but the group soon folded. He then joined the band The Tourists with Peet Coombes and Annie Lennox, achieving moderate success including a hit cover of Dusty Springfield's "I Only Want to Be with You" in 1979. When The Tourists split in 1980, Stewart and Lennox formed Eurythmics.

Eurythmics

Eurythmics achieved worldwide success with their 1983 single "Sweet Dreams (Are Made of This)" and its accompanying video, which became iconic on MTV. The duo went on to sell an estimated 75 million records worldwide, with hit albums including Touch (1983), Be Yourself Tonight (1985), Revenge (1986) and We Too Are One (1989). Stewart co-wrote and produced nearly all of the group's material, pioneering a sound that blended synth-pop, soul, and rock. The duo reunited periodically for albums and performances, including the 1999 album Peace.

Solo work and production

Outside Eurythmics, Stewart formed Dave Stewart and the Spiritual Cowboys and released solo albums including The Blackbird Diaries (2011), The Ringmaster General (2012), and Spitballin' (2016). As a producer and songwriter he has collaborated with an extensive list of artists, including Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers (co-writing "Don't Come Around Here No More"), Bob Dylan, Mick Jagger (winning a Golden Globe for co-writing "Old Habits Die Hard" for the film Alfie), No Doubt, Stevie Nicks, and Joss Stone. He formed the supergroup SuperHeavy in 2011 with Mick Jagger, Joss Stone, Damian Marley and A. R. Rahman.

Other ventures

Stewart has worked extensively in film, television, and theatre, co-writing the musical Ghost: The Musical (2011) with Glen Ballard. He co-founded the media company Weapons of Mass Entertainment and has been involved in numerous publishing, technology and philanthropic projects. His memoir, Sweet Dreams Are Made of This: A Life in Music, was published in 2016.
