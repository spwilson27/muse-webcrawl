Hawker Siddeley

Hawker Siddeley was a group of British manufacturing companies engaged in aircraft production, heavy engineering and other activities. Formed in 1935 through the merger of the Hawker Aircraft company and the Armstrong Siddeley group, it became one of Britain's largest industrial groups. Its aviation division, Hawker Siddeley Aviation, produced famous aircraft including the Harrier jump jet, the Hawk trainer and the Nimrod maritime patrol aircraft. In 1977 the aviation businesses were nationalised and merged into British Aerospace; the remaining Hawker Siddeley Group was acquired by BTR in 1991.

The Harrier, Hawker Siddeley's most famous design, was the first operational VTOL combat aircraft.

History

The group grew out of Thomas Sopwith's aviation enterprises. During the Second World War its Hawker subsidiary produced the Hurricane fighter, while Armstrong Whitworth and Gloster built bombers and jet fighters such as the Meteor. After the war the group absorbed de Havilland, Blackburn and Folland in the rationalisation of the British aircraft industry around 1960. Hawker Siddeley diversified into diesel engines (Lister Petter, Brush), railway equipment, and electrical engineering, while subsidiaries in Canada (Avro Canada's successors) and Australia extended its reach.

Aircraft

The company's best-known products include the P.1127/Kestrel prototypes that led to the Harrier, the world's first operational vertical/short take-off and landing combat aircraft, which entered RAF service in 1969 and was adopted by the US Marine Corps as the AV-8. Other major types were the HS 748 turboprop airliner, the Trident jet airliner, the Buccaneer strike aircraft, the Nimrod, the HS 125 business jet, and the Hawk advanced trainer of 1974, flown by the Red Arrows display team. Hawker Siddeley Dynamics developed guided weapons including the Red Top missile.

Nationalisation and aftermath

Under the Aircraft and Shipbuilding Industries Act 1977, Hawker Siddeley Aviation and Hawker Siddeley Dynamics were nationalised and merged with British Aircraft Corporation and Scottish Aviation to form British Aerospace. The rump Hawker Siddeley Group plc, focused on industrial engineering, was the subject of a hostile takeover by BTR plc in 1991 and was broken up, ending the use of the name.
