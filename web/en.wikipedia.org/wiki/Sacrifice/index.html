Sacrifice
EtymologyLatin sacrificium, "to make sacred"
FormsAnimal sacrifice, libations, burnt offerings, votive offerings, human sacrifice (historical)
Found inMost historical and many contemporary religions

Sacrifice is the offering of material possessions or the lives of animals or humans to a deity as an act of propitiation or worship. The term derives from the Latin sacrificium, "to make sacred". Sacrificial practices are attested in most ancient religions and persist, in various forms, in many contemporary traditions.

An altar, the focal point of sacrificial ritual in many traditions.

Forms and purposes

Sacrifices may consist of animals, food, drink (libations), or manufactured goods, and in some historical societies also human beings. The offering is typically destroyed or transformed — by burning, pouring out, or consumption in a ritual meal — so that it passes from human to divine ownership. Proposed purposes include giving thanks, seeking favour or forgiveness, averting calamity, marking covenants, and sustaining the gods themselves. Major scriptural examples include the offerings of ancient Israel described in Leviticus, Vedic yajna rituals in India, and Greek and Roman animal sacrifice.

Theories

Scholars have interpreted sacrifice in many ways: Edward Tylor saw it as a gift intended to win divine favour; William Robertson Smith emphasised the communal meal shared by worshippers and god; Henri Hubert and Marcel Mauss analysed it as a rite of consecration mediating between sacred and profane worlds; René Girard argued that sacrifice originates in the channeling of communal violence onto a scapegoat. In Christianity, the crucifixion of Jesus is interpreted as a definitive sacrifice, commemorated in the Eucharist.

Modern practice

Animal sacrifice continues in traditions such as Islam's Eid al-Adha (Qurbani), Afro-Caribbean religions, and some Hindu and indigenous practices, and is regulated by animal-welfare law in many countries. Human sacrifice, practised historically in societies including the Aztec empire and Shang-dynasty China, has been universally abandoned and condemned.
