Corona Australis

Corona Australis is a constellation in the Southern Celestial Hemisphere. Its Latin name means "southern crown", and it is the southern counterpart of Corona Borealis, the northern crown. It is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy, and it remains one of the 88 modern constellations.

Features

The constellation forms a distinctive horseshoe or arc of stars south of Sagittarius. Its brightest star, Meridiana (Alpha Coronae Australis), is a white main-sequence star about 130 light-years away, notable for an excess of infrared emission indicating a debris disk. The Corona Australis molecular cloud, roughly 430 light-years distant, is one of the closest star-forming regions and contains young stellar objects and reflection nebulae such as NGC 6729. The globular cluster NGC 6541 lies within the constellation's boundaries.

Mythology

Corona Australis has been associated with a wreath or crown placed by Dionysus, or with the crown of the centaur Sagittarius. Some traditions link its stars to a turtle in Chinese astronomy, where they formed part of a river asterism.
