Chicago Auto Show

The Chicago Auto Show is an annual auto show held in Chicago, Illinois, United States. It is the largest auto show in North America by exhibition space and among the most attended in the world. First held in 1901, it is staged at the McCormick Place convention complex and is produced by the Chicago Automobile Trade Association (CATA), the nation's oldest and largest metropolitan dealer organisation.

History

The first official Chicago Auto Show was held in March 1901 at the Chicago Coliseum, with an admission price of 50 cents and exhibits of steam, electric and petrol-powered vehicles. The show survived the Great Depression and wartime interruptions; production-car displays were suspended during the Second World War, and the show returned in 1950 — the first postwar auto show in the United States — drawing record crowds eager to see new models after years of rationing.

The show moved to McCormick Place in 1961, and expanded into the rebuilt complex after the original building burned in 1967. With the opening of the North Building in 1997, exhibition space grew to more than one million square feet, making it the largest auto show in North America by floor area.

Features

The Chicago show is oriented toward consumers rather than industry debuts alone, though manufacturers regularly stage world and North American premieres there, particularly of trucks, SUVs and concept cars. Distinctive features include indoor test tracks — notably the Camp Jeep off-road course, introduced in 2005 — and ride-and-drive opportunities on the show floor. Special events include the First Look for Charity black-tie preview, which has raised tens of millions of dollars for local charities.

The 2021 show was rescheduled to July, in a special summer edition, because of the COVID-19 pandemic, before returning to its customary February dates.
