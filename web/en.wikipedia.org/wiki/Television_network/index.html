Television network

A television network (or TV network) is a distribution system for television programming in which a central operator supplies content to many affiliated or owned stations, which broadcast it to their local markets. Networks allow programmes — expensive to produce — to reach national or international audiences simultaneously, funded mainly by advertising or licence fees.

Structure

In the affiliate model, dominant in the United States, the network owns a small number of flagship stations (owned-and-operated stations) and contracts with independent local stations (affiliates) to carry its schedule. In return, affiliates receive programming and national advertising revenue shares while retaining local advertising slots and news operations. Public-service systems such as the BBC operate their own transmitters and regional centres directly.

History

Networks first grew out of radio: NBC and CBS linked stations by telephone lines in the 1920s and 1930s and carried the model into television after the Second World War, joined by ABC and the short-lived DuMont Network. Colour television, satellite distribution, and cable channels multiplied networks from the 1970s onward. The classic American "Big Three" (NBC, CBS, ABC) were later joined by Fox, The WB, UPN, and The CW, while cable and then streaming services fragmented the network audience from the 2000s.

Programming and scheduling

Networks commission or license series, news, sports, and events, and arrange them into a schedule designed to build audience flow across an evening ("prime time"). Affiliate relations, ratings measurement (historically by Nielsen), and advertising sales are core network functions. Flagship network events such as the Super Bowl and Olympic Games still assemble some of the largest simultaneous audiences in media.

International

Most countries developed national networks anchored by a public broadcaster and one or more commercial rivals: ITV and Channel 4 in Britain, ARD and ZDF in Germany, NHK in Japan, and CCTV in China. Global news networks such as CNN, BBC World News, and Al Jazeera distribute a single feed across many countries by satellite and cable.
