Palaeontology

Palaeontology (paleontology) is the scientific study of life that existed before the Holocene, based on fossils. It reconstructs extinct organisms, their environments and the history of life through the geologic time scale.

Scope and methods

Palaeontologists excavate and prepare fossils, date rocks with radiometric methods, and infer anatomy, behaviour and relationships (Phylogenetics). Subfields include vertebrate palaeontology (dinosaurs such as Triceratops), invertebrate palaeontology, palaeobotany and micropalaeontology.

Exceptional deposits like the Burgess Shale preserve soft tissues; trace fossils preserve tracks and burrows.

History

Georges Cuvier established extinction and comparative anatomy around 1800; Mary Anning's Jurassic coast finds transformed the field; Darwin used fossils as evidence for Evolution. Modern palaeontology uses CT scanning, geochemistry and statistical phylogenetics.
