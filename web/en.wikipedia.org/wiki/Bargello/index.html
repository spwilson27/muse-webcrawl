Bargello

The Bargello, also known as the Palazzo del Bargello or Museo Nazionale del Bargello, is a museum in Florence, Italy, housed in one of the city's oldest public buildings. Since 1865 it has served as a national museum devoted principally to Renaissance sculpture, holding masterpieces by Michelangelo, Donatello, Cellini, Giambologna, and the della Robbia family.

The building

The palace was begun in 1255 for the Capitano del Popolo, making it Florence's oldest surviving seat of civic government, older than the Palazzo Vecchio. Its name derives from the bargello, the chief of police who occupied the building from the later sixteenth century, when it also served as a prison; executions were carried out in its courtyard until capital punishment was abolished in Tuscany in 1786, the first European state to do so. The building's stern stone facade, battlements, and tall tower (the Volognana) make it an emblem of medieval Florence.

The museum

Established in 1865 during Florence's brief period as capital of Italy, the Bargello became Italy's first national museum of medieval and Renaissance art. Its treasures include Donatello's bronze and marble Davids, Michelangelo's Bacchus, Brutus, and the Pitti Tondo, Verrocchio's David, Cellini's bronze bust of Cosimo I, and Giambologna's Mercury, as well as the two bronze panels submitted in the famous 1401 competition for the Florence Baptistery doors by Brunelleschi and Ghiberti. The museum also holds collections of ceramics (maiolica), textiles, ivory, armor, and small bronzes.
