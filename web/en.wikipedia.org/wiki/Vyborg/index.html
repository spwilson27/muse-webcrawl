Vyborg (Russian: Выборг; Finnish: Viipuri; Swedish: Viborg) is a town in Leningrad Oblast, Russia, situated on the Karelian Isthmus near the head of Vyborg Bay, close to the Finnish border. It is famous for Vyborg Castle, founded by the Swedes in 1293 during the Third Swedish Crusade, around which the medieval town grew.

History

The area was inhabited by Karelians and was part of the Novgorod Republic's sphere before Swedish expansion. Vyborg Castle was founded in 1293 by Torkel Knutsson. The town received privileges in 1403. Ceded to Russia after the Great Northern War by the Treaty of Nystad (1721), it became part of the Grand Duchy of Finland in 1812. After Finnish independence in 1917, Viipuri was Finland's second-largest city and a major port. The Winter War (1939–40) and Continuation War saw the city ceded to the Soviet Union; its Finnish population was evacuated and resettled. Since 1944 it has been a Soviet, then Russian, town.

Geography and landmarks

Vyborg lies about 130 km northwest of St. Petersburg and 38 km from the Finnish border. Its old town has medieval street patterns, the Round Tower (1550s), the Clock Tower, and Monrepos Park, one of the finest English landscape parks in Eastern Europe, founded in the late 18th century.

Economy and transport

The town is a rail junction on the St. Petersburg–Helsinki line and a port on the Gulf of Finland. Industries include shipbuilding, food processing and manufacturing.
