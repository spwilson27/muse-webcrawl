Louis XII

Louis XII (27 June 1462 – 1 January 1515) was King of France from 1498 until his death in 1515, and King of Naples from 1501 to 1504. A member of the Valois-Orléans cadet branch of the House of Valois, he continued the Italian Wars begun by his predecessor Charles VIII, pressing claims to Milan and Naples. At home he was remembered favourably as "Father of the People" (Père du peuple), a title conferred by the Estates General in 1506 for his reduction of taxes and maintenance of peace and justice.

Early life

Louis was born at the Château de Blois, the son of Charles, Duke of Orléans, the poet-prince, and Marie of Cleves. He succeeded his father as Duke of Orléans in 1465. Louis XI forced him to marry the king's daughter Joan, who was reputed to be sterile and deformed, in an attempt to extinguish the Orléans line. During the Mad War (1485–1488) Louis rebelled against the regency of Anne de Beaujeu; he was defeated at Saint-Aubin-du-Cormier in 1488 and imprisoned for three years before being released and reconciled by Charles VIII, whom he accompanied to Italy in 1494.

Accession and Italian wars

Charles VIII died childless in 1498 and Louis, as his nearest male relative, succeeded without dispute, famously declaring that he would not avenge the wrongs done to the Duke of Orléans now that he was King of France. He had his marriage to Joan annulled by Pope Alexander VI and married Charles's widow, Anne of Brittany, in 1499, keeping Brittany attached to the crown.

Claiming the Duchy of Milan through his grandmother Valentina Visconti, Louis conquered Milan in 1499–1500, capturing Ludovico Sforza. He then joined Ferdinand II of Aragon in conquering Naples (1501), but the partners fell out and French forces were driven from Naples by Gonzalo Fernández de Córdoba in 1503–1504. Later phases saw France fight the League of Cambrai against Venice (victory at Agnadello, 1509) and then the Holy League of pope, Spain, England and others; the French won a brilliant but costly victory at Ravenna in 1512, where the young commander Gaston de Foix was killed, but were progressively expelled from Italy, losing Milan and suffering defeats at Novara (1513) and, at home, against the English at the Battle of the Spurs and the Swiss at Dijon.

Domestic reign and death

At home Louis's reign was prosperous and popular: he reduced the taille, reformed justice and coinage, and patronised building at Blois and Amboise in the early French Renaissance style. Anne of Brittany died in January 1514. In October 1514, to seal peace with England, the 52-year-old king married Mary Tudor, the 18-year-old sister of Henry VIII. He died less than three months later, on 1 January 1515, leaving no son; the throne passed to his cousin and son-in-law, Francis I.
