Breast reduction

Breast reduction, medically known as reduction mammoplasty, is a surgical procedure to reduce the size of large breasts by removing excess tissue, fat, and skin, and reshaping the remaining breast. It is performed both for cosmetic reasons and to relieve physical symptoms caused by macromastia, such as back, neck, and shoulder pain, and skin irritation.

Procedure

Common techniques include the inferior pedicle, vertical (short-scar), and free nipple graft methods. The surgeon makes incisions — often in an anchor, lollipop, or doughnut pattern — removes tissue, repositions the nipple-areola complex, and closes the incisions to achieve a smaller, lifted breast contour.

Risks and recovery

As with any surgery, risks include infection, scarring, changes in nipple sensation, asymmetry, and difficulty breastfeeding. Recovery typically takes several weeks, and studies consistently report high patient satisfaction and significant relief of physical symptoms.
