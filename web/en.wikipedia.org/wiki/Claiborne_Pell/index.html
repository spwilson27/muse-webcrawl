Claiborne Pell

Claiborne Pell, United States Senator from Rhode Island (1961–1997).

Claiborne de Borda Pell (November 22, 1918 – January 1, 2009) was an American politician and writer who served as a United States senator from Rhode Island for six terms from 1961 to 1997. He was the sponsor of the 1972 educational assistance law establishing the Basic Educational Opportunity Grant, which came to be widely known as the Pell Grant, a federal financial aid program for undergraduate students with financial need.

A member of the Democratic Party, Pell was chairman of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee from 1987 to 1995. He is the longest-serving senator in Rhode Island history, having served 36 years in the United States Senate.

Early life and education

Pell was born in New York City, the son of Matilda Bigelow and Herbert Claiborne Pell Jr., a former U.S. Representative and ambassador. He graduated from St. George's School in Middletown, Rhode Island, in 1936 and received his bachelor's degree in history from Princeton University in 1940. He later earned a master's degree in international relations from Columbia University in 1946.

Before Pearl Harbor, Pell enlisted in the U.S. Coast Guard. During World War II he served as a lieutenant, largely on convoy duty in the North Atlantic; he remained in the Coast Guard Reserve and retired as a captain in 1978. After the war he worked in the State Department and served as an aide in the United Nations Conference on International Organization in San Francisco.

United States Senate

Pell was first elected to the Senate in 1960, defeating former governor Dennis J. Roberts in the Democratic primary and Raoul Archambault in the general election. He was re-elected five times, generally with wide margins despite Rhode Island's closely watched races.

Pell was best known for the Pell Grant program, created under the Higher Education Act amendments of 1972, which provides need-based grants to low-income undergraduate students. He was also instrumental in creating the National Endowment for the Arts and the National Endowment for the Humanities in 1965, and was a strong advocate for mass transit funding, including support that helped establish Amtrak.

As chairman of the Foreign Relations Committee, Pell was a noted advocate of arms control and diplomacy. He supported the Senate's ratification of several arms control treaties and was known for an elegant, patrician style and an interest in parapsychology and unexplained phenomena.

Later life and death

Pell declined to seek re-election in 1996 and retired in January 1997; he was succeeded by Jack Reed. The Newport Bridge in Rhode Island was renamed the Claiborne Pell Newport Bridge in his honour. Pell died in Newport, Rhode Island, on January 1, 2009, at the age of 90, after suffering from Parkinson's disease.
