The Supremes were an American girl group and a premier act of Motown Records during the 1960s. Founded in Detroit as the Primettes in 1959, the group achieved success with the classic line-up of Florence Ballard, Mary Wilson and Diana Ross.

Success

Between 1964 and 1969 the Supremes had twelve number-one singles on the Billboard Hot 100, including "Where Did Our Love Go", "Baby Love", "Stop! In the Name of Love", "You Can't Hurry Love" and "You Keep Me Hangin' On", most written and produced by Holland–Dozier–Holland. They were among the first Black acts to achieve sustained success with white audiences, paving the way for later soul and R&B artists.

Later years

The group was renamed Diana Ross & the Supremes in 1967; Ross left for a solo career in 1970. The Supremes continued with Jean Terrell as lead singer, scoring hits such as "Up the Ladder to the Roof" and "Stoned Love", before disbanding in 1977. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1988.
