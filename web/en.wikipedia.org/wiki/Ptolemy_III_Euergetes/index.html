Ptolemy III Euergetes

Reign: 246 – 222 BC
Dynasty: Ptolemaic
Predecessor: Ptolemy II Philadelphus
Successor: Ptolemy IV Philopator
Spouse: Berenice II

Ptolemy III Euergetes ("the Benefactor"; c. 284 – 222 BC) was the third king of Ptolemaic Egypt, reigning from 246 to 222 BC. Under him the Ptolemaic kingdom reached the peak of its power and prosperity. He was married to Berenice II of Cyrene, whose dedication of a lock of her hair gave rise to the constellation Coma Berenices.

Third Syrian War

At his accession Ptolemy invaded Seleucid Syria in the Third Syrian War (246–241 BC), ostensibly to avenge his murdered sister Berenice Syra, advancing as far as Mesopotamia before a revolt recalled him to Egypt. The peace left the Ptolemies with extensive territories along the Levantine and Anatolian coasts and a huge indemnity of plunder, restored sacred objects earning him the epithet Euergetes from grateful Egyptian priests.

Domestic rule

The Canopus Decree (238 BC) records a synod of priests honouring the royal couple and attempted a leap-year calendar reform. Ptolemy III patronized the Library and Mouseion of Alexandria and built extensively in Egyptian temples, including work at Karnak and the temple of Khnum at Esna.
