Maceió

Maceió is the capital and largest city of the Brazilian state of Alagoas, on the country's northeastern Atlantic coast. With a population of around one million, it is a major tourist destination known for its urban beaches — Pajuçara, Ponta Verde, and Jatiúca — set between the ocean and the Mundaú and Manguaba lagoons, its emerald-green sea, and natural reef pools reached by raft. The name Maceió derives from the Tupi word maçayó or maçaio, referring to the springs and lagoons that periodically flood the coastal land.

History

Maceió grew around a sugar mill and plantation complex (engenho) in the colonial period and developed as a port exporting sugar, cotton, tobacco, and coconuts. It became the capital of Alagoas in 1839, succeeding the town of Alagoas (now Marechal Deodoro). The city is the birthplace of Brazil's first two presidents — Deodoro da Fonseca, who proclaimed the republic in 1889, and Floriano Peixoto — and of the writer Graciliano Ramos, who served as a local prefect and drew on the region in his novels. Since 2018, several neighbourhoods (Pinheiro, Mutange, Bebedouro and others) have been evacuated because of land subsidence and sinkhole risk caused by decades of rock-salt mining by the petrochemical company Braskem — one of Brazil's largest urban environmental disasters, displacing tens of thousands of residents.

Tourism and culture

Jangada rafts take bathers from Pajuçara beach to reef pools at low tide. The city's craft market (Mercado do Artesanato) and the Pavilhão do Artesanato sell lace (including filé embroidery), ceramics, and straw work. Regional cuisine features sururu (a local mussel), tapioca, and seafood stews. Nearby attractions include the beaches of Francês and Barra de São Miguel to the south and the historic town of Marechal Deodoro.
