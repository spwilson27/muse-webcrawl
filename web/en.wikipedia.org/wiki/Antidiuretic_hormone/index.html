Antidiuretic hormone

Antidiuretic hormone (ADH), also known as vasopressin or arginine vasopressin (AVP), is a peptide hormone synthesised in the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary gland. Its principal actions are to conserve body water by increasing water reabsorption in the kidneys and, at higher concentrations, to constrict blood vessels and raise blood pressure.

Physiology

ADH is produced in magnocellular neurons of the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei and transported to the posterior pituitary. Release is stimulated mainly by increased plasma osmolality, detected by hypothalamic osmoreceptors, and by reduced blood volume or pressure. In the kidney, ADH binds V2 receptors on collecting-duct principal cells, triggering insertion of aquaporin-2 water channels and concentrating the urine.

Clinical aspects

Deficiency of ADH (or renal resistance to it) causes diabetes insipidus, with large volumes of dilute urine and thirst. Excess inappropriate secretion (SIADH) causes water retention and hyponatraemia. The synthetic analogue desmopressin is used therapeutically in central diabetes insipidus, nocturnal enuresis and some bleeding disorders.
