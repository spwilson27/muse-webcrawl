Dalton Trumbo

James Dalton Trumbo (December 9, 1905 – September 10, 1976) was an American screenwriter and novelist who scripted many award-winning films, including Roman Holiday (1953), Spartacus (1960), Exodus (1960), and Papillon (1973). He was one of the "Hollywood Ten", a group of film professionals who were blacklisted by the Hollywood studios after refusing to testify about their political associations before the House Un-American Activities Committee (HUAC) in 1947.

Blacklist years

Cited for contempt of Congress, Trumbo served eleven months in federal prison in 1950–51 and then moved with his family to Mexico. During the blacklist he remained one of Hollywood’s most productive writers, working for low fees under pseudonyms and through "fronts". He won two Academy Awards under other names: for Roman Holiday, credited to his front Ian McLellan Hunter, and for The Brave One (1956), credited to the fictitious "Robert Rich" — an award no one stepped forward to claim at the ceremony.

End of the blacklist

The blacklist began to crack in 1960, when Kirk Douglas publicly credited Trumbo for Spartacus and director Otto Preminger announced that Trumbo had written Exodus. Trumbo thereafter worked openly again, writing Hawaii (1966), The Fixer (1968), and Papillon. He also directed the film of his own anti-war novel Johnny Got His Gun (1939; film 1971), which won prizes at Cannes. In 1993 he was posthumously awarded the Oscar for Roman Holiday, his widow Cleo accepting the statuette. His life was dramatised in the 2015 film Trumbo, starring Bryan Cranston.
