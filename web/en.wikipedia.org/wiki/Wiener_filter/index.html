Wiener filter

In signal processing, the Wiener filter is a filter used to produce an estimate of a desired or target random process by linear time-invariant (LTI) filtering of an observed noisy process. It was proposed by Norbert Wiener during the 1940s and published in 1949. The filter minimises the mean square error between the estimated random process and the desired process, assuming known spectral properties of the original signal and the noise.

The Wiener filter produces a least-mean-square estimate of the desired signal.

Approach

The Wiener filter's purpose is to reduce the amount of noise present in a signal by comparison with an estimation of the desired noiseless signal. Its solution — the Wiener–Hopf equations — depends on the power spectra of the signal and the noise. Where the signal dominates the noise, the filter passes the input largely unchanged; where noise dominates, the filter attenuates the output toward zero.

Applications

Wiener filtering is used extensively in:

  • Image restoration — deblurring and denoising images when the blur function and noise level are known.
  • Audio processing — removing hiss and background noise from recordings and hearing aids.
  • Channel equalisation — compensating for distortion in communication channels.
  • Prediction — extrapolating time series, a problem Wiener originally approached for anti-aircraft fire control.

Related techniques include the Kalman filter, which solves a similar estimation problem in the time domain for non-stationary processes, and adaptive filters that estimate the required statistics from the data.
