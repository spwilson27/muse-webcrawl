Hizb

A ḥizb (Arabic: حزب, plural aḥzāb, "part" or "party") is a division of the Quran used to facilitate its recitation over a set period. The Quran is divided into thirty roughly equal parts called juz' (ajzā'), and each juz' is subdivided into two aḥzāb, giving sixty aḥzāb in total. Each ḥizb is further divided into quarters, known as rub' al-ḥizb.

Use in recitation

The divisions allow a reader to complete the entire Quran in a structured period: reciting one juz' per day completes the text in a month, a practice especially common during Ramadan. A ḥizb corresponds to roughly half a day's portion in that scheme. Manuscripts and printed editions mark the start of each ḥizb with decorative marginal ornaments, and the rub' al-ḥizb symbol (۞) is a well-known marker in Quranic typography.

Other meanings

The word ḥizb literally means "party" or "faction", and in modern Arabic it commonly denotes a political party. In Sufi practice, a ḥizb is also a set litany or collection of prayers recited regularly by an order's adherents, such as the famous Ḥizb al-Baḥr ("Litany of the Sea") attributed to Abu al-Hasan al-Shadhili.
