Muscles of mastication

The muscles of mastication are a group of muscles that consist of the temporalis, masseter, medial pterygoid and lateral pterygoid muscles. They are attached to the mandible (jaw bone) and move it during chewing (mastication). The muscles of mastication are innervated by the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve (cranial nerve V3), consistent with their embryological origin from the first pharyngeal arch.

The muscles

Masseter

The masseter is a thick, somewhat quadrilateral muscle arising from the zygomatic arch and inserting into the angle and lateral surface of the ramus of the mandible. It elevates the mandible, closing the mouth, and is one of the strongest muscles in the body relative to its size.

Temporalis

The temporalis is a broad, fan-shaped muscle on the side of the skull that arises from the temporal fossa and inserts into the coronoid process of the mandible. It elevates and retracts the mandible.

Medial pterygoid

The medial pterygoid is a thick, quadrilateral muscle arising from the pterygoid process and inserting into the medial surface of the ramus and angle of the mandible. It assists in elevating the mandible and, acting with the lateral pterygoid of the opposite side, in side-to-side movements.

Lateral pterygoid

The lateral pterygoid is a short, thick muscle with two heads. It protrudes the mandible and depresses it (opening the mouth), and it is the main muscle responsible for opening the jaw.

Movements of the mandible

Together the muscles of mastication produce elevation (closing), depression (opening), protrusion, retraction, and lateral deviation of the mandible at the temporomandibular joint. The accessory muscles of mastication sometimes listed include the suprahyoid muscles, which assist in depressing the mandible.

Clinical significance

Disorders of the muscles of mastication include trismus (spasm, classically in tetanus), bruxism-related hypertrophy of the masseter, and myofascial pain associated with temporomandibular joint dysfunction.
