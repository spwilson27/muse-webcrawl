1974 FIFA World Cup

The 1974 FIFA World Cup was the tenth FIFA World Cup, a quadrennial tournament for men's senior national teams. It was held in West Germany (including West Berlin) from 13 June to 7 July 1974. The tournament marked the introduction of a new trophy, the FIFA World Cup Trophy, which replaced the Jules Rimet Trophy after Brazil had won it outright in 1970.

The final was held at the Olympiastadion in Munich, where hosts West Germany defeated the Netherlands 2–1 to win their second World Cup title. The Dutch side, led by Johan Cruyff and playing the innovative "Total Football" style, had taken the lead in the second minute through a Johan Neeskens penalty, but West Germany equalised through Paul Breitner and won with a goal from Gerd Müller.

Format

The 1974 tournament introduced a new format: after the first group stage, the eight surviving teams were divided into two further groups of four rather than proceeding directly to knockout rounds, with the group winners meeting in the final and the runners-up contesting third place. Sixteen teams qualified, including debutants East Germany, Haiti, Australia and Zaire.

Notable matches

The tournament is remembered for the first-round meeting between East Germany and West Germany in Hamburg, which East Germany won 1–0 in the only match ever played between the two German states at senior level. Poland emerged as a surprise force, with Grzegorz Lato finishing as top scorer, while defending champions Brazil finished fourth.

Final

In the final on 7 July 1974 at the Olympiastadion, Munich, the Netherlands scored before any West German player had touched the ball, Neeskens converting a penalty after Cruyff was fouled. Breitner equalised from another penalty in the 25th minute, and Müller scored the winner shortly before half-time. West Germany held on to win 2–1, becoming the first team to hold the World Cup and European Championship titles simultaneously.
