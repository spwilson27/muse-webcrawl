The President of Portugal (Portuguese: Presidente da República) is the head of state of the Portuguese Republic. Since the Carnation Revolution of 1974 and the Constitution of 1976, Portugal has been a semi-presidential republic in which the president is elected by universal suffrage and holds significant reserve powers, while day-to-day executive power is exercised by the prime minister and government.

Election and term

The president is elected by direct universal suffrage for a five-year term. A candidate must obtain an absolute majority of valid votes; if no candidate does so in the first round, a runoff is held between the top two candidates. The president may serve at most two consecutive terms. Candidates must be Portuguese citizens of at least 35 years of age.

Powers

The president appoints the prime minister, taking into account the results of legislative elections and consultations with the parties represented in the Assembly of the Republic, and appoints other members of the government on the prime minister's proposal. The president may dissolve the Assembly and call early elections, dismiss the government when necessary to ensure the proper functioning of the democratic institutions, veto legislation (a veto that parliament can override by absolute or, in some cases, two-thirds majority), refer laws to the Constitutional Court for review, grant pardons, and ratify international treaties. The president is also the supreme commander of the armed forces and chairs the Council of State, an advisory body.

History

The office dates to the proclamation of the republic in 1910; Manuel de Arriaga was the first elected president. The First Republic was marked by instability, with eight presidents between 1910 and 1926. Under the authoritarian Estado Novo, presidents such as Óscar Carmona and Américo Tomás were largely ceremonial figures subordinate to António de Oliveira Salazar and his successors. Following the 1974 revolution, presidents of the democratic Third Republic have included António Ramalho Eanes, Mário Soares, Jorge Sampaio, Aníbal Cavaco Silva, and Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa, who took office in 2016.
