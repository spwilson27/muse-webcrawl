Minutiae

Minutiae (singular minutia, from Latin minutiae, "smallness") are very small or trivial details. The term is used in several technical fields, most prominently in fingerprint analysis and biometrics, where minutiae are the specific ridge characteristics used to identify and match fingerprints.

Fingerprint minutiae

In fingerprint recognition, minutiae are major features of a fingerprint image. A fingerprint consists of ridges and valleys on the surface of a finger; the pattern of ridges is interrupted by points where a ridge ends, splits into two (a bifurcation), or forms other distinctive configurations. Commonly recorded minutiae types include:

  • Ridge ending — the point where a ridge terminates abruptly.
  • Bifurcation — the point where a single ridge divides into two ridges.
  • Lake (enclosure) — a ridge that splits and rejoins, enclosing a small valley.
  • Independent ridge — a short ridge entirely separate from its neighbours.
  • Dot — a very short ridge, about as long as it is wide.
  • Spur — a short ridge branching from a longer ridge.
  • Crossover — two ridges connected by a short bridging ridge.

Automated fingerprint identification systems (AFIS) typically extract 30–100 minutiae per fingerprint, recording each minutia's position, orientation and type. Matching algorithms compare the relative arrangement of minutiae between a probe print and stored templates; forensic standards in many jurisdictions have historically required a minimum number of matching minutiae points (often 12) to declare an identification, though modern practice increasingly relies on probabilistic assessment of the whole configuration.

Other uses

In general usage, "minutiae" denotes petty details or trivia, as in "the minutiae of the contract." In cellular biology and microscopy the term is occasionally applied to fine structural details. In all uses it derives from the Latin plural of minutia, a small thing.
