The Walton family is an American family whose collective fortune, derived from Walmart — the world's largest retailer, founded by brothers Sam and James "Bud" Walton — makes it among the richest families in the world, with a net worth estimated in the hundreds of billions of dollars.

Members

Sam Walton (1918–1992) founded Walmart in Rogers, Arkansas, in 1962 and Sam's Club in 1983. His children are Rob Walton (former Walmart chairman), John Walton (died in a 2005 plane crash), Jim Walton (chairman of Arvest Bank), and Alice Walton, founder of the Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art in Bentonville. The third generation includes Greg Penner, Sam's grandson-in-law and Walmart chairman since 2015, and investor Lukas Walton.

Wealth and philanthropy

The family holds roughly half of Walmart's shares, largely through Walton Enterprises and family trusts. The Walton Family Foundation, founded in 1987, gives heavily in K-12 education reform, environmental conservation (especially oceans and rivers) and its home region of northwest Arkansas. Alice Walton's Crystal Bridges (opened 2011) and the family's investments have transformed Bentonville into a cultural and mountain-biking destination.
