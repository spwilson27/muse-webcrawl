Tirupati Laddu, also known as Srivari Laddu, is the famous laddu sweet offered as prasadam to devotees of the Venkateswara Temple in Tirumala, Andhra Pradesh, India. It is one of the most recognisable temple offerings in the world; the Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams (TTD) produces several hundred thousand laddus daily for the tens of thousands of pilgrims visiting the shrine.

History and preparation

The offering of laddu at Tirumala is traced back several centuries, with the practice regulated in its modern form from the early 20th century. The laddus are prepared in the temple kitchen, the potu, by designated cooks (pachakas), following a strict recipe known as dittam that specifies the proportions of gram flour, sugar syrup, ghee, cashew nuts, raisins, and cardamom. Large "kalyanotsavam" laddus are prepared for special rituals, while the standard laddu weighs about 175 grams.

Geographical Indication

In 2009, the Tirupati Laddu received Geographical Indication (GI) status in India, meaning that only laddus made within the temple by the TTD may be sold under that name, protecting the offering from commercial imitation.

Controversies

In 2024, allegations by Andhra Pradesh's government that ghee used in the laddus during a previous administration had been adulterated sparked a major political and religious controversy and a court-ordered investigation, leading to changes in ghee procurement.
