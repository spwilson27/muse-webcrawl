Reticulated python

The reticulated python (Malayopython reticulatus) is a python species native to South and Southeast Asia. It is the world's longest snake, and the third heaviest behind the green anaconda and the Burmese python. It is listed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List because of its wide distribution.

Description

The reticulated python is the longest snake in the world; large adults regularly exceed 6.25 m (20.5 ft) in length. Its color pattern is a complex geometric pattern of irregular diamond shapes, typically in shades of tan, gold, brown, and black, which gives it its name from the Latin reticulatus, "net-like". Like all pythons, it is a non-venomous constrictor.

Distribution and habitat

The species is found throughout Southeast Asia, from the Nicobar Islands and Bangladesh through Myanmar, Thailand, Indochina, and the Malay Peninsula to Indonesia and the Philippines. It inhabits rainforests, woodland, and grassland, and is an excellent swimmer, often found near rivers and in areas near human settlement.

Relationship with humans

Reticulated pythons are hunted for their skin, for use in traditional medicine, and for the pet trade. Although generally not aggressive, large individuals are powerful and have occasionally killed, and in rare cases eaten, humans. Captive-bred dwarf populations from Indonesian islands are popular in the pet trade.
