Subtitles

Subtitles are text displayed at the bottom of a screen that transcribes or translates the dialogue (and sometimes other audio content) of a film, television program, video game, or other audiovisual work. They enable viewers who do not understand the spoken language, or who cannot hear the audio, to follow the work. Subtitles differ from a dub, which replaces the original voices with a new-language performance.

Forms

Subtitles may be intralingual (same language as the audio), serving deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers — often in the fuller form of closed captions, which also describe sound effects and identify speakers — or interlingual (translated), serving viewers of foreign-language media. They can be burned into the image ("hard" or open subtitles) or supplied as a separate, selectable data stream or file ("soft" subtitles), as in DVD/Blu-ray menus and streaming services. Common file formats include SubRip (.srt), WebVTT, and ASS/SSA.

Production

Creating subtitles involves transcription, translation, and "spotting" — timing each text block to appear and disappear with the speech — under constraints on reading speed (often around 12–21 characters per second for adults) and line length. Translators must condense dialogue while preserving meaning, and conventions vary: some countries (e.g. the Netherlands, Scandinavia, Portugal) predominantly subtitle foreign media, while others (e.g. Germany, France, Spain, Italy) predominantly dub it. Fansubbing — unofficial subtitles made by fans — played a notable role in spreading anime and other media internationally.

Uses and effects

Beyond accessibility and translation, subtitles support language learning, comprehension in noisy environments, and silent viewing on mobile devices; surveys in the streaming era show many younger viewers routinely watch with subtitles on even in their native language. Automatic speech recognition now generates real-time captions for live broadcasts and video calls, though accuracy remains an issue for names, accents, and technical vocabulary.
