Eye examination

An eye examination, commonly referred to as an eye test, is a series of tests performed to assess vision and the ability to focus on and discern objects, as well as other tests and examinations pertaining to the eyes. Eye examinations are primarily performed by an optometrist, ophthalmologist, or orthoptist.

Health care professionals often recommend regular eye examinations as part of routine primary care, especially for people over 40 and those with risk factors for glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, or macular degeneration. Examinations also detect general health problems such as diabetes and hypertension.

Components

A comprehensive examination typically includes a case history, measurement of visual acuity with a Snellen chart, refraction to determine a lens prescription, pupil function tests, assessment of eye movements and alignment, visual field testing, tonometry to measure intraocular pressure, slit-lamp examination of the anterior segment, and ophthalmoscopy of the retina, sometimes after pupil dilation.

Children

Screening in childhood aims to detect amblyopia (lazy eye), strabismus, and refractive errors early, when treatment is most effective. Many jurisdictions provide school-entry vision screening.
