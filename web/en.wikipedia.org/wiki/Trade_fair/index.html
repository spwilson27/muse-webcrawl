Trade fair
Also known asTrade show, trade exhibition, expo
ParticipantsCompanies in a specific industry, trade visitors
Notable venuesHannover Messe, Messe Frankfurt, Las Vegas Convention Center

A trade fair, also known as a trade show, trade exhibition, or trade exposition, is an exhibition organised so that companies in a specific industry can showcase and demonstrate their latest products and services, meet with industry partners and customers, study the activities of competitors, and examine recent market trends. In contrast to consumer fairs, most trade fairs are not open to the general public, or are open only on certain days.

History

Trade fairs have a long history: the Champagne fairs of medieval France were major commercial events of the 12th and 13th centuries. The modern trade fair developed in industrial Europe; Leipzig and Frankfurt became leading fair cities, and Germany remains the world's leading trade-fair nation, hosting events such as the Hannover Messe (industry) and the Frankfurt Book Fair. Large Asian venues such as the Canton Fair in Guangzhou (held since 1957) and North American shows such as CES in Las Vegas are among the biggest in their sectors.

Role in marketing

Trade fairs are a significant part of the business-to-business marketing mix, allowing face-to-face contact, product launches and lead generation. Exhibitors invest in booth design and staffing, while organisers sell floor space and sponsorships. The global exhibition industry suffered a severe contraction during the COVID-19 pandemic, with many events cancelled or moved online, before in-person shows recovered.
