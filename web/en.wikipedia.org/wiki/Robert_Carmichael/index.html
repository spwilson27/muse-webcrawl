Robert Daniel Carmichael

The best-known bearer of the name in mathematics is Robert Daniel Carmichael (March 1, 1879 – May 2, 1967), who earned his Ph.D. at Princeton University under G. D. Birkhoff in 1911 and taught for many years at the University of Illinois. Carmichael numbers—composite numbers that pass Fermat's primality test for all bases coprime to them—are named after him, as are the Carmichael function λ(n), which gives the exponent of the multiplicative group modulo n, and Carmichael's theorem generalizing Euler's theorem. He also wrote on the theory of relativity and on Diophantine analysis, and coined the term "Carmichael conjecture" for an open problem concerning his totient-related function.
