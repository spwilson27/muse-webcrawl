Carl Zeiss AG, branded as ZEISS, is a German manufacturer of optical systems and optoelectronics, founded in Jena in 1846 by optician Carl Zeiss. Together with Ernst Abbe and Otto Schott, Zeiss laid the foundations of modern scientific optics. The company is wholly owned by the Carl Zeiss Foundation (Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung), one of the oldest and largest science-promoting foundations in Germany.

History

Carl Zeiss opened his workshop for precision instruments in Jena in 1846, initially producing microscopes. The collaboration with physicist Ernst Abbe from 1866 placed microscope design on a scientific footing, and glass chemist Otto Schott's new optical glasses (from 1884, at the Jena glassworks) enabled high-performance lenses. Products expanded to include telescopes, planetariums, surveying instruments, binoculars (from 1894), and photographic lenses such as the Planar and Tessar designs.

After the Second World War the company was divided: the Jena works became the East German state enterprise VEB Carl Zeiss Jena, while managers and scientists who had been moved west founded Carl Zeiss in Oberkochen in West Germany. The two firms competed under the same brand until German reunification in 1990, after which they were reunited under the Oberkochen company and the Carl Zeiss Foundation.

Business areas

Zeiss operates in semiconductor manufacturing technology (its lithography optics are a critical component of ASML's EUV scanners), industrial quality and research (microscopy and metrology), medical technology (Carl Zeiss Meditec), and vision care and consumer products, including spectacle lenses, camera and cine lenses, binoculars, and planetarium projectors.
