List of special functions and eponyms

This is a list of many special functions in mathematics, together with the names (eponyms) of the people associated with them. Special functions arise as solutions of differential equations, integrals and series that recur throughout analysis, mathematical physics and engineering.

Selected functions

  • Gamma function — Euler; generalises the factorial to complex numbers.
  • Bessel functions — Friedrich Bessel; solutions of Bessel's differential equation, arising in wave propagation and heat conduction in cylindrical coordinates.
  • Legendre polynomials — Adrien-Marie Legendre; solutions of Legendre's equation, used in potential theory.
  • Hermite polynomials — Charles Hermite; used in probability and in the quantum harmonic oscillator.
  • Laguerre polynomials — Edmond Laguerre; appear in the radial part of the hydrogen atom wavefunction.
  • Chebyshev polynomials — Pafnuty Chebyshev; central to approximation theory.
  • Error function (erf) — related to the Gaussian integral and the normal distribution.
  • Riemann zeta function — Bernhard Riemann; central to analytic number theory.
  • Elliptic integrals and functions — associated with Legendre, Jacobi, Weierstrass and Abel.
  • Hypergeometric function — studied by Euler and Gauss; unifies many simpler functions.
  • Airy functions — George Biddell Airy; solutions of the Airy equation, used in optics and quantum mechanics.
  • Dirac delta function — Paul Dirac; a generalised function used in physics and engineering.
  • Heaviside step function — Oliver Heaviside.
  • Lambert W function — Johann Heinrich Lambert; inverse of f(w) = w·ew.
  • Digamma and polygamma functions — logarithmic derivatives of the gamma function.
  • Beta function — Euler; related to the gamma function.
  • Fresnel integrals — Augustin-Jean Fresnel; used in diffraction theory.
  • Whittaker, Hankel, Struve and Kelvin functions — families of cylinder functions.
  • Mathieu functions — Émile Léonard Mathieu; for vibrating elliptical membranes.
  • Spherical harmonics — associated with Laplace and Legendre.
