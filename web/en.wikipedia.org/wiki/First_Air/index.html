First Air

First Air was a Canadian airline that provided scheduled passenger and cargo services to communities in Canada's Arctic — Nunavut, Nunavik, and the Northwest Territories — as well as charter services. Headquartered in Kanata, Ontario, with hubs in Iqaluit, Yellowknife, and Kuujjuaq, it was wholly owned by the Inuit of Nunavik through the Makivik Corporation.

History

The airline was founded in 1946 as Bradley Air Services by Russell Bradley, operating charter flights from Ottawa. It adopted the name First Air in 1973 and grew into the principal air carrier of the eastern Arctic. In 2019, First Air merged with Canadian North under an agreement between Makivik Corporation and the Inuvialuit Development Corporation; the combined airline operates under the Canadian North name, and the First Air brand was retired in 2021.

Services and fleet

First Air operated Boeing 737 and ATR 42 aircraft, including gravel-kit-equipped 737-200 combis able to serve the unpaved runways of northern communities, carrying passengers, food, mail, and freight essential to remote settlements. It also operated the "air bridge" of scheduled services linking Arctic communities with Ottawa, Montreal, Edmonton, and Winnipeg.

Accidents

On 20 August 2011, First Air Flight 6560, a Boeing 737-200, crashed on approach to Resolute Bay, Nunavut, killing 12 of the 15 people on board.
