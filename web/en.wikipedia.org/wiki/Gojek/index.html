Gojek


A Gojek driver in Jakarta, the company's home market.

Gojek (stylised as gojek, formerly GO-JEK) is an Indonesian on-demand multi-service platform and digital payment technology group. Founded in Jakarta in 2010 as a call centre for booking ojek (motorcycle taxi) rides, it grew into Southeast Asia's first "super-app", and in 2021 merged with the e-commerce company Tokopedia to form the GoTo Group.

History

Gojek was founded by Nadiem Makarim, Kevin Aluwi and Michaelangelo Moran, initially connecting customers with informal ojek drivers by telephone. The launch of its mobile app in January 2015, offering transport (GoRide, GoCar), courier (GoSend) and food delivery (GoFood) services, triggered explosive growth and a fierce rivalry with Singapore-based Grab, which had entered Indonesia. Gojek expanded its services to include digital payments (GoPay), groceries, cleaning, massage, ticketing and many other services — over twenty in a single application — and internationalised into Vietnam, Thailand, Singapore and the Philippines. The company achieved decacorn status (valued above US$10 billion) and attracted investment from Google, Tencent, Temasek, Visa and others. Makarim resigned in 2019 to become Indonesia's Minister of Education and Culture.

GoTo merger and business

In May 2021 Gojek combined with Tokopedia to form PT GoTo Gojek Tokopedia, Indonesia's largest technology group, which listed on the Indonesia Stock Exchange in April 2022 in one of the country's largest-ever initial public offerings. GoTo's ecosystem combines e-commerce, on-demand services and financial technology, and reports hundreds of millions of users and millions of driver-partners and merchants. The platform is credited with formalising parts of Indonesia's informal economy, though, like its rivals, it has faced continuing debates over driver earnings, commissions and employment status.
