Soho House

Soho House is a museum and former home in Handsworth, Birmingham, England. It was the home of the industrialist Matthew Boulton from 1766 until his death in 1809, and a regular meeting place of the Lunar Society of Birmingham, the informal dining and discussion club whose members included James Watt, Erasmus Darwin, Joseph Priestley, and Josiah Wedgwood.

History

Boulton leased the site in 1761 and transformed a modest farmhouse into an elegant Georgian residence as his nearby Soho Manufactory grew. Members of the Lunar Society met at the house on the Monday nearest the full moon — so that the light would aid their journey home — to discuss science, engineering, and philosophy; the gatherings are considered one of the intellectual engines of the English Enlightenment and the Industrial Revolution.

After Boulton's death the house passed through various owners and uses, including a period as a police hostel. It was acquired by Birmingham City Council and opened as a museum in 1995, restored to its appearance in Boulton's time, with displays on his life, the Lunar Society, and the Soho enterprises. The museum is managed by Birmingham Museums Trust.
