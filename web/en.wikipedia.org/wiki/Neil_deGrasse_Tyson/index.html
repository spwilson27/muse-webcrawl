Neil deGrasse Tyson (born October 5, 1958) is an American astrophysicist, author, and science communicator. Since 1996 he has been the Frederick P. Rose Director of the Hayden Planetarium at the Rose Center for Earth and Space in New York City, part of the American Museum of Natural History, where he founded the Department of Astrophysics in 1997.

Early life and education

Tyson was born in Manhattan and raised in the Bronx. A visit to the Hayden Planetarium at age nine sparked his interest in astronomy. He attended the Bronx High School of Science, where he excelled at wrestling and astronomy, and earned his bachelor's degree in physics from Harvard University in 1980. After a master's degree at the University of Texas at Austin, he completed his PhD in astrophysics at Columbia University in 1991. His research has concerned stellar evolution, dwarf galaxies, galactic structure, and the formation of stars.

Career and public outreach

At the Hayden Planetarium, Tyson oversaw the facility's redesign; its classification of Pluto as part of the Kuiper belt rather than a planet anticipated the International Astronomical Union's 2006 reclassification and drew wide public attention. He has served on government commissions on the future of the US aerospace industry and NASA.

Tyson became one of the most prominent science popularizers in the United States through books such as Death by Black Hole (2007) and the bestselling Astrophysics for People in a Hurry (2017). He hosted the PBS series NOVA ScienceNow, co-founded and hosts the StarTalk podcast and radio show (begun in 2009), and in 2014 hosted Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey, the sequel to Carl Sagan's Cosmos, followed by Cosmos: Possible Worlds (2020).

Tyson has received numerous honors, including the NASA Distinguished Public Service Medal and election to the National Academy of Sciences. In 2018–2019 he was the subject of sexual misconduct allegations, which he denied; investigations by his employers concluded without findings of policy violations and he resumed his public roles.
