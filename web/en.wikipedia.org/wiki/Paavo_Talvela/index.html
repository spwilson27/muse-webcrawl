Paavo Talvela (born Paavo Juho Thorén; 19 February 1897 – 30 September 1973) was a Finnish soldier and general of infantry, one of Finland's most successful field commanders of the Second World War and one of the first Knights of the Mannerheim Cross. He is remembered especially for the victory at Tolvajärvi in the Winter War and for commanding Finnish forces in the conquest of East Karelia in 1941.

Early career

Talvela grew up in Helsinki and, like many young Finnish nationalists, secretly travelled to Germany in 1916 to receive military training with the Finnish Jäger troops of the Royal Prussian Jäger Battalion 27. Returning in February 1918, he fought for the Whites in the Finnish Civil War. In the interwar Finnish Army he rose steadily — commanding a regiment and studying in France — while also being active in the academic and veterans' movements; he served as an aide-de-camp to President Svinhufvud and was promoted to major general in 1939.

Winter War

In the Winter War (1939–40) Talvela commanded Group Talvela north of Lake Ladoga. His forces defeated two Soviet divisions at the Battle of Tolvajärvi (12–24 December 1939), the first major Finnish offensive victory of the war, relieving pressure on the main Karelian Isthmus front. In January–February 1940 his troops fought along the Aittojoki–Pitkäranta sector. The Tolvajärvi victory made him one of the best-known Finnish commanders.

Continuation War

In the Continuation War (1941–44) Talvela initially commanded VI Corps in Ladoga Karelia and then, as commander of the Aunus (Olonets) Group, directed the drive across East Karelia to the Svir River and toward Petrozavodsk in the autumn of 1941 — the deepest Finnish advance of the war. He was promoted to lieutenant general and awarded the Mannerheim Cross as one of its first recipients in August 1941. In 1942, after disagreements with headquarters, he spent a period as Finland's military representative in Berlin; he returned to command III Corps in the summer of 1944 during the great Soviet offensive, then Corps Talvela on the Karelian Isthmus in the war's final defensive battles.

Post-war life

Retiring after the war, Talvela worked in the paper industry and travelled in South America, returning to Finland in the 1960s. He published memoirs and remained an honored, if sometimes controversial, figure in Finnish military history — an aggressive, charismatic and demanding offensive commander whose style epitomized the Finnish sisu ethos of the era.
