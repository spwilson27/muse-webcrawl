Chinese five-spice powder

Chinese five-spice powder (Chinese: 五香粉; pinyin: wǔxiāngfěn) is a spice mixture of five or more spices used predominantly in Chinese cuisine, and also in other cuisines of Asia. The name alludes to five foundational flavours — sweet, bitter, sour, salty and umami — balanced in a single blend, though the exact composition varies widely by region and maker.

Composition

The classic blend most often comprises star anise, cloves, Chinese cinnamon (cassia), Sichuan pepper (or regular peppercorns) and fennel seeds. Regional variants may include ginger, nutmeg, cardamom, liquorice root, turmeric, mandarin-peel or galangal. Vietnamese and Taiwanese versions, and the related Vietnamese ngũ vị hương, differ in emphasis.

Culinary use

Five-spice powder is used to season roast pork, duck and chicken, red-braised dishes, marinades, dry rubs and Cantonese-style barbecue (char siu). It appears in snacks, sauces and desserts as well. A little goes a long way: the blend is potent, and a pinch is typically enough to scent a dish. In southern China it is one of the signature seasonings of Cantonese and Hakka cooking.
