Body mass index

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The body mass index (BMI) is a value derived from a person's mass and height — mass in kilograms divided by the square of height in metres (kg/m²) — used as a simple screening measure of underweight, healthy weight, overweight and Obesity. It was devised by Adolphe Quetelet in the 1830s and popularised under its present name in the 1970s.

Categories and use

For adults, WHO categories are: below 18.5 underweight; 18.5–24.9 healthy; 25–29.9 overweight; 30 and above obesity (with further classes). Public-health bodies use BMI to track populations and screen individuals because it is cheap and correlates broadly with body fat and with risks of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease and some cancers.

Limitations

BMI does not measure fat directly or where it is stored: muscular athletes score as overweight, older people can have high fat at 'normal' BMI, and risk thresholds differ by ancestry (lower cut-offs are used for many Asian populations). Clinicians therefore combine BMI with waist circumference and clinical assessment rather than treating it as a diagnosis.
