Lanark is a market town in the central belt of Scotland, in South Lanarkshire, on the River Clyde. It has been a royal burgh since the twelfth century and served as the county town of Lanarkshire. The town stands close to the Falls of Clyde and to New Lanark, the model industrial village founded by David Dale and developed by Robert Owen, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Lanark's origins lie in the early medieval kingdom of Strathclyde; King David I made it a royal burgh around 1140, and Scotland's first recorded parliament met in St Kentigern's Church in 978 by tradition, with later parliaments also held in the town. William Wallace is traditionally said to have begun his revolt against English rule at Lanark in 1297, killing the English sheriff William de Heselrig.

The town developed as a market and administrative centre for the Clyde valley, later known for its agricultural market — still among Scotland's largest livestock markets — and for the Lanimer Week celebrations each June, an ancient festival centred on checking the town's boundary marches.

Lanimers

The Lanark Lanimer Week, held annually in June, dates back several centuries and involves the ceremonial perambulation of the burgh boundaries, the crowning of a Lanimer Queen chosen from local schoolchildren, and a procession through the town.

Transport

Lanark railway station is the terminus of a branch from the West Coast Main Line and Argyle Line services to Glasgow.
