Jezero (crater)

Jezero is an impact crater on Mars, located in the Syrtis Major quadrangle at about 18.4°N, 77.5°E, and roughly 49 kilometres (30 miles) in diameter. More than 3.5 billion years ago the crater held a lake, fed by rivers that carved channels through its rim and deposited a prominent fan delta of sediments in its western side. The crater was selected as the landing site of NASA's Perseverance rover, which touched down on 18 February 2021.

Name and geology

The name Jezero was approved by the International Astronomical Union in 2007; it is the word for "lake" in several Slavic languages and is shared with a town in Bosnia and Herzegovina. Orbital observations by the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter showed that the crater floor and delta contain clay minerals and carbonates formed in water, making Jezero one of the most promising places to search for signs of ancient microbial life. An inlet channel breached the western rim, and an outlet channel on the eastern side indicates that water once flowed through the lake.

Exploration

Perseverance landed at a site later named Octavia E. Butler Landing, accompanied by the Ingenuity helicopter. The rover has explored the crater floor's volcanic rocks, then the delta front, collecting sealed tubes of rock and regolith samples intended for return to Earth by a future sample-return campaign. The rover's traverse continues towards the crater rim and the terrain beyond, sampling carbonate-bearing rocks along the way.
