Battle of Kohima

Date4 April – 22 June 1944
LocationKohima, Nagaland, British India
ResultAllied victory
BelligerentsBritish Empire (United Kingdom, British India, Nepal) vs. Empire of Japan
CommandersMontagu Stopford; John M. L. Grover vs. Kotoku Sato

The Battle of Kohima was a turning point of Japan's U-Go offensive into India during the Burma campaign of the Second World War. Fought from 4 April to 22 June 1944 around the town of Kohima in present-day Nagaland, it saw a small British and Indian garrison hold a vital road junction against a full Japanese division.

Background

In March 1944 the Japanese Fifteenth Army crossed the Chindwin River to destroy Allied forces around Imphal and cut the supply line to China. The Japanese 31st Division under Lieutenant General Kotoku Sato advanced on Kohima, whose ridge dominated the only road from the railhead at Dimapur to Imphal.

The siege

The Kohima garrison — roughly 1,500 men of the Royal West Kents, Assam Regiment, Assam Rifles and support troops — was besieged from 6 to 18 April, compressed into a perimeter only a few hundred metres across on Garrison Hill. Some of the closest fighting of the war took place across the deputy commissioner's tennis court. Relieved by the British 2nd Division, the Allies then fought for another month to clear the ridge feature by feature, while the starving Japanese, their supply lines broken, were ordered to withdraw at the end of May.

Aftermath

Together with the simultaneous Battle of Imphal, Kohima marked the furthest extent of the Japanese advance into India and the beginning of the Allied reconquest of Burma. The Kohima War Cemetery bears the famous Kohima Epitaph: "When you go home, tell them of us and say, for your tomorrow, we gave our today." In a 2013 poll by the National Army Museum, Kohima-Imphal was voted Britain's greatest battle.
