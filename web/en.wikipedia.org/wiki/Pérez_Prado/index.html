Dámaso Pérez Prado (11 December 1916 – 14 September 1989) was a Cuban bandleader, pianist, composer, and arranger who popularised the mambo in the 1950s and became known worldwide as the "King of the Mambo." His crisp arrangements, trademark grunt ("¡Ugh!"), and punchy brass writing made his orchestra one of the most successful Latin dance bands of all time.

Career

Born in Matanzas, Pérez Prado studied classical piano and played with local charangas and the Orquesta Casino de la Playa in Havana. He moved to Mexico City in 1949, where he formed his own orchestra and, with singer Benny Moré, ignited the mambo craze. His arrangement of "Qué rico el mambo" (released abroad as "Mambo No. 5") launched the numbered-mambo series that became his signature.

In 1955 his recording of the French song "Cherry Pink (and Apple Blossom White)," with trumpeter Billy Regis, spent ten weeks at number one in the United States. His 1958 composition "Patricia" also topped the charts and later became famous as the striptease anthem of Fellini's La Dolce Vita. Other hits included "Mambo No. 8," "Mambo Jambo," and "The Peanut Vendor."

Legacy

Pérez Prado helped turn Afro-Cuban dance music into a global pop phenomenon, influencing jazz, rock and roll, and later salsa. "Mambo No. 5" returned to the top of the charts in Lou Bega's 1999 adaptation. Prado died in Mexico City in 1989, having lived there for most of his later life.
