Biscay (Basque: Bizkaia; Spanish: Vizcaya) is a province of Spain and a historical territory of the Basque Country, located on the Bay of Biscay in the north of the Iberian Peninsula. Its capital and largest city is Bilbao.

Geography

Biscay is bordered by the Bay of Biscay to the north, Gipuzkoa to the east, Álava and Burgos to the south, and Cantabria to the west. The terrain is mountainous, cut by river valleys such as the Nervión, whose estuary — the Ría de Bilbao — hosts the province's metropolitan core and the Port of Bilbao, one of Spain's busiest.

History and economy

Historically governed under its own fueros (charters) by the Lordship of Biscay, the province industrialized rapidly in the nineteenth century around iron mining, steelmaking, and shipbuilding. After an industrial crisis in the late twentieth century, Bilbao reinvented itself as a service and cultural center, symbolized by the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao, opened in 1997. Biscay, together with the other Basque provinces, enjoys a high degree of fiscal autonomy under the Economic Agreement with the Spanish state.
