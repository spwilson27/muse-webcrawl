Lama

Lama (Tibetan: bla-ma, "chief" or "high priest") is a title for a teacher of the Dharma in Tibetan Buddhism. Originally used to translate the Sanskrit guru, the title denotes a respected spiritual teacher who has attained a high degree of spiritual development.

Lamas may be monks or lay practitioners. Some lineages recognise reincarnate lamas, or tulkus, believed to be the rebirth of a previous teacher; the best known are the Dalai Lama and the Panchen Lama of the Gelug school. Other major traditions — Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya and Jonang — also maintain lama lineages.

In the Vajrayana tradition the lama serves as the student's spiritual guide and a focus of guru yoga practice, embodying the qualities of the Buddha for the disciple.
