Trayvon Martin

Born: 5 February 1995, Miami, Florida, U.S.

Died: 26 February 2012 (aged 17), Sanford, Florida, U.S.

Cause of death: Gunshot wound

Trayvon Benjamin Martin (February 5, 1995 – February 26, 2012) was a 17-year-old African-American high school student from Miami Gardens, Florida. On the evening of February 26, 2012, he was fatally shot in Sanford, Florida, by George Zimmerman, a neighbourhood watch coordinator in the gated community where Martin was visiting relatives.

Background

Martin was visiting his father's fiancée at the Retreat at Twin Lakes in Sanford. Walking back from a convenience store, he was reported by Zimmerman to police as suspicious. Zimmerman followed him and, after a physical altercation, shot Martin once in the chest at close range. Martin was unarmed, carrying only a packet of Skittles and a can of iced tea.

Investigation and trial

Police initially released Zimmerman, citing Florida's stand-your-ground law and his claim of self-defence. After weeks of national protests and petitions, a special prosecutor charged Zimmerman with second-degree murder in April 2012. At trial in the summer of 2013, a jury acquitted him of both second-degree murder and manslaughter. The U.S. Department of Justice later declined to bring federal civil rights charges.

Legacy

Martin's death prompted national debate about racial profiling, gun laws and stand-your-ground statutes; the hoodie he wore became a symbol of protest. President Barack Obama remarked, "If I had a son, he'd look like Trayvon." The acquittal of Zimmerman was one of the events that gave rise to the Black Lives Matter movement, founded in 2013 by Alicia Garza, Patrisse Cullors and Opal Tometi.
