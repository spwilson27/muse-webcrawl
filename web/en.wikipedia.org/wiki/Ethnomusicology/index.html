Ethnomusicology

Ethnomusicology is the study of music from the cultural and social aspects of the people who make it. It encompasses distinct theoretical and methodological approaches that emphasize cultural, social, material, cognitive, and biological contexts of music, rather than only its isolated sound component.

History

The field emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as comparative musicology, studying non-Western music. Scholars such as Béla Bartók, Frances Densmore, and later Alan Lomax made extensive field recordings. After World War II the discipline was renamed ethnomusicology, and figures including Alan Merriam — who defined it as the study of music in culture — Bruno Nettl, and Mantle Hood broadened it to the anthropology of music.

Methods

Ethnomusicologists conduct prolonged fieldwork, participant observation, interviews, and recording, and often learn to perform the music they study — Hood's concept of bi-musicality. Related fields include musicology, anthropology, and folklore.

Organizations

The Society for Ethnomusicology, founded in 1955, publishes the journal Ethnomusicology.
