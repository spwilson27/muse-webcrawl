Inverter (electrical)

A power inverter is an electronic device that converts direct current (DC) electricity into alternating current (AC). Inverters allow DC sources such as batteries and solar panels to power equipment designed for mains AC electricity, and they are the complement of rectifiers, which convert AC to DC.

Operation

An inverter rapidly switches the DC input on and off — using transistors such as MOSFETs or IGBTs under the control of an oscillator — to produce an alternating waveform, which may then be filtered and stepped up by a transformer to the required voltage. The output waveform may be a square wave, a modified sine wave (stepped approximation), or, in higher-quality units, a nearly pure sine wave suited to sensitive electronics and motors.

Applications

Inverters are used in solar photovoltaic systems, uninterruptible power supplies, electric-vehicle and railway traction drives, variable-frequency motor drives, induction heating, and portable power for vehicles and boats. Grid-tie inverters synchronise their output with the utility grid and must include anti-islanding protection to shut down safely during outages.
