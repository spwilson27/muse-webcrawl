Needle and syringe programme

Programmes distribute sterile injecting equipment and safely collect used needles.

A needle and syringe programme (NSP), also known as a needle exchange programme, is a social service that allows people who inject drugs to obtain clean and unused needles and associated paraphernalia at little or no cost, and to dispose of used equipment safely. It is a core component of harm reduction policy, intended to reduce the transmission of blood-borne viruses such as HIV and hepatitis C.

History

Informal needle exchanges began in the Netherlands in the early 1980s, initially in response to hepatitis B among people who inject drugs in Amsterdam; the first official programme opened there in 1984. Exchanges spread through the United Kingdom, Australia and other countries from the mid-1980s as the HIV epidemic grew. In the United States, the first well-known programmes operated in Tacoma and San Francisco in 1988, despite legal prohibitions on drug paraphernalia in many jurisdictions; for many years a federal funding ban limited their expansion.

Services

Beyond needle and syringe exchange — whether strictly one-for-one or needs-based distribution — programmes commonly provide other injecting equipment (sterile water, filters, cookers), condoms, naloxone for overdose reversal, sharps containers, vaccinations, testing and treatment referrals for HIV and hepatitis, wound care, and referral to drug treatment and social services. Services may be provided from fixed sites, pharmacies, mobile units, vending machines, or peer outreach.

Evidence and debate

Reviews by bodies including the World Health Organization, the US National Academies, and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention have concluded that syringe services programmes reduce HIV transmission and do not increase drug use, drug-related litter, or crime, and that they serve as bridges to treatment. Programmes remain politically controversial in some places, where opponents argue they enable drug use; nevertheless hundreds of programmes operate worldwide.
