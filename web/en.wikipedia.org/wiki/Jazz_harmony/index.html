Jazz harmony

Jazz harmony is the theory and practice of how chords are used in jazz music. While jazz shares much of its harmonic vocabulary with other Western music, it is distinguished by its extensive use of extended chords, chord substitutions, and progressions such as the ii–V–I that underpin improvisation.

Characteristics

Jazz harmony typically employs seventh chords as basic building blocks, adding ninths, elevenths, and thirteenths as colour tones. Altered dominants, tritone substitutions, and modal interchange allow soloists a wide palette of notes over a given progression.

Common progressions

The ii–V–I progression is the most important cadence in jazz, appearing in major and minor keys throughout the standard repertoire. The twelve-bar blues and "rhythm changes" (based on George Gershwin's "I Got Rhythm") provide other frameworks for improvisation. See also jazz and chord progression.

Approaches

Players analyse harmony through chord-scale theory, guide tones, and voice leading. Pianists and guitarists realise progressions through voicings that emphasise the third and seventh of each chord.
