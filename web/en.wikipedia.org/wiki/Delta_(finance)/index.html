Delta (finance)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematical finance, delta (Δ) measures the rate of change of the theoretical value of an option with respect to changes in the price of the underlying asset. It is one of the Greeks, the quantities used to assess risk in options portfolios. Formally, delta is the partial derivative of the option price with respect to the underlying price:

Δ = ∂V / ∂S

For a call option, delta ranges between 0 and 1; for a put option, between −1 and 0. A call with a delta of 0.5 would be expected to gain roughly 50 cents in value for every $1 rise in the underlying, all else equal.

Interpretation

Delta has several related interpretations. It approximates the probability that the option will expire in the money (more precisely, it relates to the risk-neutral probability under certain assumptions). It also gives the hedge ratio: the number of shares of the underlying needed to hedge an option position. A market maker who sells a call option with delta 0.5 can hedge by buying half a share per option contract, and then adjusting the position as delta changes — a practice known as delta hedging.

Options deep in the money have deltas near ±1 and behave almost like the underlying stock itself. At-the-money options have deltas near ±0.5. Far out-of-the-money options have deltas near 0. Delta changes as the underlying price moves; the rate of that change is measured by another Greek, gamma.

Black–Scholes formula

Under the Black–Scholes model, the delta of a European call option on a non-dividend-paying stock is N(d1), where N is the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution and d1 combines the spot price, strike price, time to expiry, volatility, and the risk-free rate. The delta of the corresponding put is N(d1) − 1, a relationship that follows from put–call parity.

Portfolio delta

The delta of a portfolio of derivatives dependent on the same underlying is the sum of the deltas of the individual positions. Traders often express positions in "delta-adjusted" share equivalents, and portfolio managers monitor aggregate delta to control directional exposure to the market.
