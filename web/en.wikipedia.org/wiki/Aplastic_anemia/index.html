Aplastic anemia

SpecialtyHematology
SymptomsFatigue, pallor, frequent infections, easy bruising and bleeding
CausesAutoimmune attack on stem cells, drugs, radiation, viral infections, inherited syndromes
TreatmentImmunosuppression, eltrombopag, bone marrow transplantation

Aplastic anemia (or aplastic anaemia) is a rare, potentially life-threatening disorder in which the body's bone marrow fails to produce enough new blood cells. The condition leaves patients fatigued and at higher risk of infections and uncontrolled bleeding, because all three blood cell lines — red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets — are reduced, a state called pancytopenia.

Causes

In most acquired cases, aplastic anemia is believed to result from an autoimmune process in which the patient's own T lymphocytes attack hematopoietic stem cells. Triggers can include certain drugs (such as chloramphenicol), exposure to benzene and other chemicals, ionizing radiation, and viral infections including hepatitis viruses, Epstein–Barr virus, and parvovirus B19. Inherited forms include Fanconi anemia and dyskeratosis congenita.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis requires a bone marrow biopsy showing a markedly hypocellular marrow — typically less than 25% cellularity — without abnormal infiltration or fibrosis, together with low blood counts. Severity is graded by the Camitta criteria based on neutrophil, platelet, and reticulocyte counts.

Treatment

First-line treatment for younger patients with a matched sibling donor is hematopoietic stem cell transplantation. For others, immunosuppressive therapy with antithymocyte globulin and ciclosporin is standard, and the thrombopoietin receptor agonist eltrombopag has improved response rates when added to immunosuppression. Supportive care includes blood transfusions and infection prophylaxis.
