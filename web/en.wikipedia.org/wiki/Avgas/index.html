Avgas

Avgas (aviation gasoline), also known as aviation spirit in the UK, is an aviation fuel used in aircraft with spark-ignited internal combustion engines. It is distinguished from mogas (motor gasoline), which is the everyday gasoline used in motor vehicles and some light aircraft. Avgas is formulated for stability, a high octane rating and low vapour pressure at altitude.

Grades

The most common grade worldwide is 100LL (100 octane, low lead), dyed blue, which still contains the lead additive tetraethyllead to prevent engine knock in high-compression piston engines, though at lower levels than earlier grades. The older 100/130 grade was dyed green, and 80/87 red. Because of the environmental and health effects of lead, the industry and regulators in the United States and elsewhere have been working toward unleaded replacements under programmes such as PAFI and EAGLE, and unleaded grades such as UL94 are already available for lower-powered aircraft.

Properties and use

Avgas is used by general aviation piston-engine aircraft, which form the majority of the world's aircraft fleet by number, though they consume only a small fraction of aviation fuel compared with the jet fuel used by turbine aircraft. Its volatility characteristics are tightly controlled so that it vaporises reliably in the cold at altitude without causing vapour lock.
