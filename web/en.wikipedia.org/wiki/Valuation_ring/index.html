Valuation ring

In abstract algebra, a valuation ring is an integral domain R with fraction field K such that for every element x of K, at least one of x or x−1 belongs to R. Equivalently, the ideals of R are totally ordered by inclusion. Valuation rings are the algebraic counterpart of valuations and play a central role in algebraic geometry and number theory: they describe the local behaviour of rational functions at a point, and their study generalises that of discrete valuation rings.

The ideals of a valuation ring form a chain under inclusion.

Definitions and properties

A valuation ring is a local ring whose maximal ideal is principal in the discrete case; in general a domain R is a valuation ring if and only if its ideals are totally ordered. To every valuation ring corresponds a valuation v : K× → Γ, where Γ is a totally ordered abelian group (the value group), with R = { x ∈ K : v(x) ≥ 0 } ∪ {0}. Conversely, every valuation determines a valuation ring. Valuation rings are integrally closed, Bézout domains, and coherent; their prime ideals correspond to isolated subgroups of the value group.

Examples

  • The ring of p-adic integers Zp and any discrete valuation ring, such as the local ring of a smooth curve at a point.
  • The ring of formal power series k[[t]] over a field k.
  • Rings of germs of holomorphic functions, and the valuation rings arising from orderings of function fields in the Zariski–Riemann space.

Applications

Valuation rings are used to define the valuative criteria for separatedness and properness of morphisms of schemes, to construct models of curves over non-archimedean fields, and in the theory of Berkovich spaces and rigid analytic geometry.
