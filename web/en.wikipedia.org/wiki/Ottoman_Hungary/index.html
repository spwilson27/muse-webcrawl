Ottoman Hungary

Period1541 – 1699 (Treaty of Karlowitz)
CapitalBudin (Buda)
Main eyaletsBudin, Eğri, Kanije, Temeşvar, Varad

Ottoman Hungary (Hungarian: Török hódoltság, "Turkish subjection") was the southern and central part of the medieval Kingdom of Hungary ruled by the Ottoman Empire from 1541, after the capture of Buda, until the end of the 17th century, when the Habsburgs reconquered the territory and the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699) confirmed the transfer.

Tripartite Hungary in the late 16th century

History

After the Hungarian defeat at the Battle of Mohács (1526) and the civil war between rival kings, Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent occupied Buda in 1541 and annexed central Hungary. The kingdom split three ways: Ottoman eyalets in the centre and south, the Habsburg "Royal Hungary" in the west and north, and the vassal Principality of Transylvania in the east. A long frontier of fortresses saw near-constant raiding and the Long Turkish War (1593–1606).

Administration and society

The territory was organised into eyalets governed by pashas, with Buda's pasha the senior Ottoman official in Hungary. Ottoman rule brought mosques, Turkish baths (some still in use in Budapest), caravanserais and new crops, but also heavy taxation and warfare that devastated and depopulated much of the Great Hungarian Plain. Reformed Protestantism spread widely under Ottoman rule.

Reconquest

After the failed Ottoman siege of Vienna in 1683, the Holy League reconquered Buda in 1686 and won decisively at Zenta (1697); the Treaty of Karlowitz ceded most of Ottoman Hungary to the Habsburgs, ending roughly 150 years of Ottoman rule.
