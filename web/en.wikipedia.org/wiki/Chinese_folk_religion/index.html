Chinese folk religion

Chinese folk religion comprises the traditional religious practices of the Han Chinese, blending elements of ancestor veneration, worship of gods and spirits drawn from Chinese mythology, and ideas absorbed from Confucianism, Taoism, and Buddhism. It is sometimes described as the "Chinese traditional religion" and is practised by hundreds of millions of people in China, Taiwan, and the Chinese diaspora, usually without exclusive affiliation.

Practices

Central practices include venerating ancestors at household altars and graves (notably at the Qingming and Ghost Festivals), burning incense and paper offerings, consulting fortune tellers and feng shui masters, and worshipping deities such as the Jade Emperor, Guan Yu, Mazu, the City God, and the Earth God at local temples. The concept of shen (spirits or gods) and reverence for Heaven (Tian) underpin much of the tradition.

History

Folk religion developed from the shamanic and sacrificial cults of early China and was shaped over two millennia by interaction with the state cult and the three teachings. Suppressed during periods of the 20th century, temple worship and festivals have revived widely since the 1980s; some scholars describe the tradition as a single diffuse "Chinese religion" rather than a separate faith.
