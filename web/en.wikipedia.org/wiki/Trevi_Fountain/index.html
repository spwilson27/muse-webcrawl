The Trevi Fountain, the largest Baroque fountain in Rome.

The Trevi Fountain (Italian: Fontana di Trevi) is an 18th-century fountain in the Trevi district in Rome, Italy, designed by Italian architect Nicola Salvi and completed by Giuseppe Pannini in 1762. Standing 26.3 metres (86 ft) high and 49.15 metres (161.3 ft) wide, it is the largest Baroque fountain in the city and one of the most famous fountains in the world.

History

The fountain stands at the junction of three roads (tre vie), marking the terminal point of the Aqua Virgo aqueduct, built in 19 BC to supply the Baths of Agrippa. Pope Urban VIII commissioned a new fountain from Bernini in the 17th century, but the project lapsed. In 1730 Pope Clement XII held a competition, which Nicola Salvi eventually won; work began in 1732. Salvi died in 1751 with the work half finished; Giuseppe Pannini completed it in 1762.

Description

The backdrop is the Palazzo Poli, given a monumental facade of Corinthian pilasters. At the centre, under a triumphal arch, stands Oceanus (Neptune) in a shell-shaped chariot drawn by two hippocamps — sea horses — guided by Tritons, one struggling with a wild horse and the other with a calmer beast, symbolizing the contrasting moods of the sea. Statues representing Abundance and Salubrity flank the central figure. The fountain is made largely of travertine from Tivoli.

Coin throwing

Tradition holds that throwing a coin over one's shoulder into the fountain ensures a return to Rome; a second coin promises romance and a third marriage. An estimated 3,000 euros in coins are thrown daily; since 2006 the proceeds, collected by the city, have been donated to the charity Caritas. The fountain famously appeared in the films Roman Holiday, Three Coins in the Fountain and Federico Fellini's La Dolce Vita, in which Anita Ekberg waded into its waters.
