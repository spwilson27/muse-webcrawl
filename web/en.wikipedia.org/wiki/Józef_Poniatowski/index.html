Józef Poniatowski

Prince Józef Antoni Poniatowski (7 May 1763 – 19 October 1813) was a Polish general, minister of war, and Marshal of the French Empire during the Napoleonic Wars. A nephew of King Stanisław II Augustus of Poland, he is regarded as a national hero in Poland.

Poniatowski fought against Russia in the Polish–Russian War of 1792 and took part in the Kościuszko Uprising of 1794. After the partitions of Poland he rebuilt a Polish army in the Duchy of Warsaw and led Polish forces allied with Napoleon, distinguishing himself at Raszyn in 1809 and during the invasion of Russia in 1812.

Napoleon made him a Marshal of France during the Battle of Leipzig in October 1813 — the only foreigner to receive the honour. Days later, covering the French retreat, the wounded Poniatowski drowned in the White Elster river. His monument stands in Warsaw, and his name is inscribed on the Arc de Triomphe in Paris.
