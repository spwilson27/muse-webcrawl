Claudio Giovanni Antonio Monteverdi (15 May 1567 – 29 November 1643) was an Italian composer, choirmaster, and string player. He is regarded as a crucial transitional figure between the Renaissance and Baroque periods and as a pioneer of opera.

Life

Born in Cremona, Monteverdi served the Gonzaga court in Mantua from about 1590 to 1612, rising to maestro di cappella. Discharged after Duke Vincenzo's death, he became choirmaster at St Mark's Basilica, Venice, in 1613, remaining there until his death. He took holy orders in 1632.

Madrigals and the seconda pratica

His nine books of madrigals trace his journey from Renaissance polyphony to the expressive seconda pratica, in which text dominates music — a position he defended against Giovanni Artusi's attacks. The fifth book (1605) introduced basso continuo.

Operas

L'Orfeo (1607), the earliest opera still regularly performed, displays unprecedented dramatic power. The late Venetian operas Il ritorno d'Ulisse in patria (1640) and L'incoronazione di Poppea (1643) remain core repertory. His Vespro della Beata Vergine (1610) is a monument of sacred music.
