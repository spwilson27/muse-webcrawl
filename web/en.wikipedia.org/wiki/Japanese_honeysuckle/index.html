Japanese honeysuckle

Lonicera japonica
Family: Caprifoliaceae
Native to: East Asia
Type: Twining vine

Japanese honeysuckle (Lonicera japonica) is a species of honeysuckle native to eastern Asia, including China, Japan and Korea. It is a vigorous twining vine grown as an ornamental groundcover and widely naturalised elsewhere.

Description

The plant is semi-evergreen, with opposite oval leaves and very fragrant two-lipped flowers that open white and fade to yellow. The fruit is a small black berry containing several seeds.

Cultivation and invasiveness

Japanese honeysuckle tolerates shade and drought and is used for erosion control and screening. In North America, Australia, New Zealand and elsewhere it has become a serious invasive species, smothering shrubs and young trees in woodland edges and forest understories.

Uses

The flowers (Jinyinhua) are used in traditional Chinese medicine, and the plant has been studied for antiviral compounds.
