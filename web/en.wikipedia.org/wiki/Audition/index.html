Audition

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An audition is a sample performance by an actor, singer, musician, dancer or other performing artist. It is used in the casting process to demonstrate the individual's ability to carry out a particular role or to explore the suitability of the artist for an ensemble, orchestra, company or production. The word derives from the Latin audire, "to hear", reflecting the process's origins in musical selection.

A stage audition in progress.

Process

Auditions typically require prepared material: actors perform monologues or are given "sides" (excerpts of the script) to read, sometimes in cold readings; singers prepare arias or songs, often with an accompanist provided; instrumentalists perform set excerpts, frequently behind a screen in preliminary orchestral rounds to reduce bias ("blind auditions", whose introduction has been credited with increasing the proportion of women in major orchestras). Dance auditions often take the form of a class. A callback is a second, narrower audition for shortlisted candidates.

Forms

  • Open call / cattle call — open to all comers, common for musicals and reality television.
  • Appointment auditions — agent-submitted, scheduled slots.
  • Self-tape — recorded audition submitted digitally, now standard for first-round screen casting.
  • Chemistry read — paired reading to test interaction between potential co-stars.

History

The modern audition developed with the 19th-century theatre and concert industries; in early cinema, screen tests became standard under the studio system. Casting directors professionalised the process in the later 20th century, and performers' unions such as Equity regulate audition conditions.
