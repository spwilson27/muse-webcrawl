Álmos (Hungarian: [ˈaːlmoʃ]; c. 820 – c. 895) was — according to the medieval Hungarian chronicles — the first head of the confederation of the seven Magyar tribes. His life is recounted mainly in legendary terms in sources written centuries after his death, principally the Gesta Hungarorum of the anonymous notary of King Béla (Anonymus) and Simon of Kéza's chronicle. Most modern historians accept Álmos as a real historical person, the father of Árpád, who led the Magyars' conquest of the Carpathian Basin around 895.

Legendary origins

According to the chronicles, Álmos was descended from Ügyek and Emese. His birth was foretold in the "Turul dream": Emese dreamed that a turul — a mythical bird of prey — impregnated her and prophesied that her descendants would be glorious kings but would flourish in a foreign land. The child's name derives from the Hungarian word álom ("dream"). The chronicles state that the seven Magyar chieftains elected Álmos as their leader and swore the "blood oath" (vérszerződés) at Etelköz, mixing their blood in a vessel.

Historical role

The Byzantine emperor Constantine VII's De Administrando Imperio (c. 950) names Álmos as the father of Árpád and implies the Magyars had a single supreme leader in his time, though historians debate whether Álmos held the sacred title of kende or the military title of gyula. According to Anonymus, Álmos led the Magyars from Etelköz across the Carpathians in 895 but did not enter the new homeland: he was killed (or ritually sacrificed) in Transylvania, because — as the chronicle explains — he could not enter Pannonia, having slain the ruler of Cumania in an earlier episode of the legend. His son Árpád then led the conquest and founded the dynasty that ruled Hungary until 1301.
