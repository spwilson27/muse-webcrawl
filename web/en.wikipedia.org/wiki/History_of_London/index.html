History of London

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Thames has been the artery of London since Roman times.

The history of London, the capital of England and the United Kingdom, extends over some two thousand years, from the Roman foundation of Londinium around 47–50 CE to its present status as a global financial and cultural centre. London grew around the lowest bridging point on the River Thames and survived Viking attacks, the Norman Conquest, plague, fire and the Blitz to become the largest city in the world during the 19th century.

Roman and medieval London

Londinium became the capital of Roman Britain and a major port, walled and home to perhaps 60,000 people, before declining after the Roman withdrawal around 410. Anglo-Saxon Lundenwic revived trade from the 7th century; Alfred the Great reoccupied the Roman walls in 886. After 1066 William the Conqueror built the Tower of London and confirmed the city's privileges. Medieval London, governed from the Guildhall, grew rich on wool and trade, acquired the first stone London Bridge (1176–1209), and suffered the Peasants' Revolt (1381) and repeated plague, including the Black Death of 1348–49.

Tudor, Stuart and Georgian growth

Tudor London doubled in size; the dissolution of the monasteries reshaped its landholding. The Great Plague of 1665 and the Great Fire of 1666, which destroyed four-fifths of the City, led to rebuilding in brick and stone under Christopher Wren, including the new St Paul's Cathedral. In the 18th century London became the world's largest city, its growth driven by empire trade, coffeehouses, and the expansion of the West End squares and the Port of London.

Victorian metropolis to the present

Victorian London saw railways, the Underground (from 1863), Bazalgette's sewers after the Great Stink of 1858, and a population rising to over six million. Bombing in the Blitz (1940–41) and post-war planning produced the Green Belt, new towns and high-rise estates; dock decline was answered by the Canary Wharf financial district. The Greater London Authority and an elected mayor were created in 2000. London hosted the Olympic Games in 1908, 1948 and 2012.
