Frisch's

Frisch's Big Boy is a regional American restaurant chain headquartered in Cincinnati, Ohio, known for its Big Boy double-decker hamburger, tartar-sauce fish sandwiches, and hot fudge cake. Operating mainly in Ohio, Kentucky, and Indiana, it was for decades one of the principal franchisees of the Big Boy system, distinguished by its own variation of the Big Boy character in a red-and-white checked outfit.

History

Samuel Frisch opened a café in Cincinnati in 1905, and his son David took over the business, opening the Frisch's Mainliner drive-in near Cincinnati in 1939, one of the area's first drive-in restaurants. In 1947 David Frisch became a franchisee of Bob Wian's Big Boy and adapted the double-decker burger, adding tartar sauce — a Frisch's hallmark. The chain grew to well over 200 restaurants at its peak and was publicly traded for many years.

In 2015 Frisch's was acquired by an affiliate of NRD Capital, an Atlanta-based private equity firm, ending Frisch family ownership. In the years that followed, the chain shrank considerably, and in late 2024 Frisch's faced severe difficulties: dozens of locations closed after eviction proceedings brought by its landlord over unpaid rent, leaving only a reduced number of restaurants operating under new arrangements.

Menu and identity

Signature items include the Big Boy and Super Big Boy sandwiches, the Swiss Miss (Swiss cheese and tartar sauce), Buddie Boy sandwiches, onion rings, and the hot fudge cake. Frisch's statue depicts the Big Boy carrying a burger, and the chain long sponsored local institutions and events in the Cincinnati area.
