National Board of Medical Examiners


NBME
Founded: 1915
Headquarters: Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, US
Type: Nonprofit organization
Purpose: Medical assessment

The National Board of Medical Examiners (NBME) is a United States medical education and assessment organization founded in 1915. It develops and manages standardized examinations used in the assessment of health professionals, most notably as co-sponsor, with the Federation of State Medical Boards (FSMB), of the United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE).

Role

The NBME creates the content of the USMLE Step examinations, which medical students and graduates take as part of medical licensure in the United States. It also develops subject examinations ("shelf exams") used by medical schools during clinical clerkships, self-assessment products for students preparing for licensure examinations, and assessment services for other health professions and international medical education programs.

History

The NBME was established in 1915 to create a voluntary national certifying examination whose results state boards could accept for licensure, reducing the need for separate state examinations. Its examinations evolved over the twentieth century and were replaced in the 1990s by the USMLE sequence, developed jointly with the FSMB. In 2022, USMLE Step 1 reporting changed from a three-digit score to pass/fail, a change the NBME and FSMB announced in 2020.

Criticism

The organization has faced criticism over the cost of its examinations, the influence of its scores on residency selection, and broader debates about standardized testing in medical education, including calls for reform of the licensing process by student groups.
