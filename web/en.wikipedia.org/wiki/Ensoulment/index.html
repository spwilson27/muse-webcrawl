Ensoulment

Ensoulment is the moment at which a human being is believed to gain a soul. The concept features in religious and philosophical debates about the beginning of human personhood and hence in discussions of abortion, embryology and bioethics. Traditions have placed ensoulment at conception, at implantation, at various stages of foetal development, or at birth; traditions that affirm reincarnation may associate the soul's entry with still other points.

Ancient and medieval views

Aristotle proposed a succession of souls — vegetative, animal and rational — and held that the rational soul arrived later in development, traditionally reckoned at about 40 days for a male and 80–90 days for a female embryo, a view transmitted through medieval medicine. Following Aristotle, Thomas Aquinas taught delayed ensoulment (or "delayed hominization"): the early embryo was animated successively by vegetative and sensitive souls before God infused the rational soul once the body was suitably formed. Quickening — the mother's first perception of foetal movement — long served in law and theology as a practical marker.

In Catholic thought

Although penalties for abortion historically varied with the stage of development, the Catholic Church since 1869 (Pius IX's Apostolicae Sedis) has removed the distinction between the ensouled and unensouled fetus for purposes of excommunication, and modern teaching holds that life must be protected from conception while formally leaving the philosophical question of the precise moment of ensoulment open.

Other traditions

Many strands of Islam place ensoulment at 120 days after conception, based on a hadith describing the angel breathing the soul into the fetus; some scholars cite 40 days. Jewish views vary, with some texts treating the fetus as "mere water" for the first 40 days. In Hindu traditions the soul (atman) is generally held to enter at conception or in the early months. Contemporary bioethics continues to invoke or reject ensoulment in arguments about embryo research and the moral status of the fetus.
