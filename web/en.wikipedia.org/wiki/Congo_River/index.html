Congo River

The Congo River winding through dense rainforest

The Congo River, formerly also known as the Zaire River, is the second-longest river in Africa, after the Nile, and the second-largest river in the world by discharge volume, after the Amazon. It flows for about 4,700 kilometres through the Congo rainforest, crossing the equator twice. It is also the world's deepest recorded river, with measured depths exceeding 220 metres.

The Congo drains a basin of about 4 million square kilometres covering the Democratic Republic of the Congo and neighbouring countries. Its main tributaries include the Ubangi, Kasai and Lualaba. The capital cities Kinshasa and Brazzaville face each other across the river.

Navigation

Rapids interrupt navigation: the Boyoma (Stanley) Falls near Kisangani and the Livingstone Falls between Kinshasa and the Atlantic split the river into navigable sections that historically functioned as separate transport networks linked by portages and later railways. The Inga Falls, part of the Livingstone Falls, have one of the largest hydroelectric potentials of any site in the world.

History

The river gave its name to two modern states, the Republic of the Congo and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. In the late 19th century it was the centre of King Leopold II of Belgium's Congo Free State, notorious for abuses against the Congolese people.
