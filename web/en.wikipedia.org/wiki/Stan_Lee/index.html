Stan Lee (born Stanley Martin Lieber; December 28, 1922 – November 12, 2018) was an American comic book writer, editor, publisher, and producer. He rose through the ranks of a family-run business called Timely Comics which later became Marvel Comics. He was Marvel's primary creative leader for two decades, expanding it from a small publishing house to a multimedia corporation.

In collaboration with artists such as Jack Kirby and Steve Ditko, Lee co-created many iconic fictional characters, including the superhero teams the Fantastic Four, the X-Men, and the Avengers, and individual heroes such as Spider-Man, Iron Man, the Hulk, Thor, Black Panther, Daredevil, and Doctor Strange.

Early life

Lee was born in New York City to Romanian-born Jewish immigrant parents. He grew up in the Bronx and attended DeWitt Clinton High School. In 1939, with the help of his uncle, he became an assistant at the new Timely Comics division of pulp magazine publisher Martin Goodman.

Career

Lee made his comics-writing debut with a prose filler, "Captain America Foils the Traitor's Revenge", in Captain America Comics #3 (cover-dated May 1941), using the pen name "Stan Lee". He became interim editor of Timely at age 19. In the early 1960s, tasked with creating a superhero team to rival DC's Justice League, Lee and Kirby produced The Fantastic Four (1961), pioneering a more naturalistic, flawed-hero approach that revolutionized the medium. His writing method, in which he gave artists a plot synopsis and let them draw the story before he added dialogue, became known as the "Marvel Method". Lee also wrote newspaper strips, hosted television shows, and from 2000 onward made cameo appearances in nearly every Marvel film adaptation.

Later years and death

Lee became a public figurehead for Marvel, receiving the National Medal of Arts in 2008. He died on November 12, 2018, at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center in Los Angeles at the age of 95.

Legacy

Lee is credited with helping to transform comic books into a major American art form; his catchphrase was "Excelsior!" He was inducted into the Will Eisner Award Hall of Fame in 1994 and the Jack Kirby Hall of Fame in 1995.
