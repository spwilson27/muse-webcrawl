Directive (European Union)

A directive is a legal act of the European Union which requires member states to achieve a particular result without dictating the means of achieving it. Directives are defined in Article 288 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, and differ from regulations, which are directly applicable in all member states as law.

Member states must transpose a directive into national law by a deadline stated in the directive, choosing the form and methods themselves; failure to transpose on time can lead the European Commission to bring infringement proceedings before the Court of Justice, which since the Maastricht Treaty may impose fines. Directives are usually adopted jointly by the European Parliament and the Council under the ordinary legislative procedure.

Although directives are addressed to states rather than individuals, the Court of Justice has held that sufficiently clear and unconditional provisions may have vertical direct effect against the state after the transposition deadline (the Van Duyn and Ratti line of cases), and under the Francovich doctrine a state may be liable in damages to individuals harmed by its failure to implement a directive. Well-known directives include the Working Time Directive, the Habitats Directive, and the General Data Protection framework's predecessor, the Data Protection Directive 95/46/EC.
