Nassau, Bahamas

Nassau is the capital and largest city of the Commonwealth of The Bahamas. Located on the island of New Providence, it is home to roughly 275,000 people — about 70 percent of the country's population. Nassau is the centre of government, commerce and tourism for the Bahamas, and its harbour, dominated by cruise ship docks and the Paradise Island resorts opposite, is one of the busiest cruise ports in the Caribbean.

History

Nassau was founded by the English in the mid-17th century as Charles Town and renamed in 1695 in honour of William III of the House of Orange-Nassau. In the early 18th century its sheltered harbour and lawlessness made it the capital of the so-called Republic of Pirates, where buccaneers such as Blackbeard and Calico Jack operated until Governor Woodes Rogers arrived in 1718 with a royal pardon and restored British authority — an episode remembered in the Bahamian motto tradition of expelling pirates and restoring commerce. The town was attacked several times by Spain and briefly occupied by American forces during the Raid of Nassau in 1776, an early operation of the United States Navy and Marines.

Geography and landmarks

The city stretches along the northern coast of New Providence. Its colonial core centres on Bay Street and Parliament Square, with its pink government buildings, the Supreme Court and the statue of Queen Victoria. Historic defences include Fort Fincastle, Fort Charlotte and Fort Montagu. The Queen's Staircase, cut from limestone by enslaved workers in the 1790s, climbs to Fort Fincastle. Offshore lies Paradise Island, connected by bridges and dominated by the Atlantis resort. Nassau is also known for the Straw Market, Government House, and the annual Junkanoo festival parades held on Boxing Day and New Year's Day.

Economy and transport

Tourism and offshore financial services dominate the economy. Lynden Pindling International Airport, west of the city, is the country's main gateway. Nassau was the seat of the failed cryptocurrency exchange FTX, whose 2022 collapse brought the city unusual international attention. In September 2019 Hurricane Dorian devastated the northern Bahamas; Nassau escaped the worst of the storm but received thousands of evacuees from Abaco and Grand Bahama.
