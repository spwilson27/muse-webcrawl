Harees

Harees
RegionArabian Peninsula, Levant, Armenia, South Asia
TypePorridge / savoury pudding
Main ingredientsWheat or barley, meat (lamb or chicken), salt
UNESCOIntangible Cultural Heritage (2023)

Harees (Arabic: هريس, harīs) is a dish of boiled, cracked or coarsely ground wheat or barley mixed with meat, cooked slowly until it reaches the consistency of a smooth porridge. It is popular across the Arab world, especially during the holy month of Ramadan, and closely related dishes include Armenian harisa and Kashmiri hareesa.

Preparation

Wheat is soaked overnight, then simmered for hours with meat, salt and water until the grains disintegrate and the meat shreds; the mixture is then beaten or blended to a paste-like consistency and topped with melted ghee or olive oil. Preparation can take a full day, and in the Gulf states it is traditionally made in large communal pots.

Cultural significance

Harees is one of the oldest documented dishes in Arab cuisine, mentioned in medieval cookbooks such as the 10th-century Kitab al-Tabikh. In 2023, "Harees: know-how, skills and practices" was inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, nominated jointly by the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia and Oman.
