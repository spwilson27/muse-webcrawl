Warner Robins, Georgia

Warner Robins is a city in Houston and Peach counties in the U.S. state of Georgia.

Population: approx. 80,000 (2020)
Founded: 1942

Warner Robins is a city in the U.S. state of Georgia, located in Houston County with a small portion extending into Peach County, about 110 miles (177 km) south of Atlanta. It is part of the Macon–Warner Robins combined statistical area. The city is nicknamed "Georgia's International City".

History

The community was founded in 1942 as the town of Wellston, growing up around the new Warner Robins Army Air Depot — now Robins Air Force Base. The city was renamed and incorporated in 1943 in honour of Brigadier General Augustine Warner Robins, regarded as the "father of logistics" in the United States Air Force. The base remains the largest single industrial complex in Georgia and the economic anchor of the city.

Geography and demographics

Warner Robins lies in the coastal plain of central Georgia along U.S. Route 129/Georgia State Route 247 and near Interstate 75. The 2020 census counted roughly 80,000 residents, making it one of the larger cities in middle Georgia. The Museum of Aviation at Robins AFB, one of the largest aviation museums in the United States, is a major local attraction.
