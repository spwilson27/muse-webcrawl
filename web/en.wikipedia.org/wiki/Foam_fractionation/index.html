Foam fractionation

Foam fractionation is a chemical and separation process in which surface-active molecules are removed from a solution by adsorption at rising gas bubbles, collecting as foam at the surface; it is the operating principle of the protein skimmer.

Mechanism

Bubbles provide large surface area where amphiphilic compounds accumulate; the foam is skimmed off, carrying dissolved organic material with it, while cleaned liquid remains.

The process relates to froth flotation used in mineral processing.

Applications

Aquarists use foam fractionation in marine aquariums to export organic waste before it decomposes, and it is used industrially for protein recovery, wastewater treatment, and removing surfactants.
