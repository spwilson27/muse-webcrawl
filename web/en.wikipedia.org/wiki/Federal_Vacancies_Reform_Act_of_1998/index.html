Federal Vacancies Reform Act of 1998

The FVRA governs who may serve as an acting officer in US federal agencies.

The Federal Vacancies Reform Act of 1998 (FVRA) is a United States federal law, codified at 5 U.S.C. §§ 3345–3349d, that governs vacancies in federal executive agency positions that require Senate confirmation under the Appointments Clause. It establishes the exclusive means by which a person may temporarily perform the functions of a vacant advice-and-consent office, subject to limited exceptions.

Provisions

When a covered office becomes vacant through death, resignation, or other inability to serve, the Act provides three routes to an acting officer: (1) the first assistant to the office serves automatically; (2) the President may direct a person already confirmed by the Senate to another office to serve in an acting capacity; or (3) the President may direct a senior agency employee (generally GS-15 or above, who served in the agency for at least 90 days in the preceding year) to serve. Acting service is normally limited to 210 days, extendable while a nomination is pending and for limited periods after a nomination is rejected, withdrawn, or returned.

Enforcement and reporting

Agencies must report vacancies and acting designations to the Government Accountability Office (GAO) and Congress. Actions taken by a person serving in violation of the Act generally have no force or effect and cannot be ratified.

Controversies

The Act has figured in controversies over acting officials in several administrations, including disputes over acting attorneys general, department heads, and agency directors, and over the relationship between the FVRA and agency-specific succession statutes.
