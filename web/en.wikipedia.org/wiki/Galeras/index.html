Galeras (Urcunina among the indigenous people) is an Andean stratovolcano in the Colombian department of Nariño, near the departmental capital Pasto. Its summit rises 4,276 metres (14,029 ft) above sea level. It has erupted frequently since the Spanish conquest, with its first historical eruption recorded on 7 December 1580. A 1993 eruption killed nine people, including six scientists who had descended into the volcano's crater to sample gases. It is one of the most active volcanoes in Colombia and is designated a Decade Volcano.

Geology

Galeras is a complex volcano that has been active for more than a million years, building and partly collapsing in caldera-forming events. The present cone sits inside a horseshoe-shaped caldera open to the west. Its explosive andesitic eruptions generate pyroclastic flows, ash falls on Pasto — a city of some 450,000 people only about 9 km from the summit — and lahars, making careful monitoring by the Colombian Geological Service essential.

The 1993 disaster

On 14 January 1993, during an international volcanology workshop, Galeras erupted without recognised warning while a party led by geologist Stanley Williams was in the crater; six scientists and three tourists were killed by ballistic blocks and pyroclastic surges, and Williams was seriously injured. The event became a landmark case study — recounted in Williams's book Surviving Galeras — in volcano risk assessment and the ethics of fieldwork during unrest.

Recent activity

Reawakened in 1988 after 40 quiet years, Galeras erupted repeatedly through the 1990s and again in 2004–2014, prompting repeated evacuations of surrounding villages; an eruption in January 2010 led to the evacuation of some 8,000 people.
