Samuel Prescott

Born: 19 August 1751, Concord, Massachusetts

Died: c. 1777 (aged about 26), possibly Halifax, Nova Scotia

Occupation: Physician

Known for: Completing the "midnight ride" to Concord, 19 April 1775

Samuel Prescott (August 19, 1751 – c. 1777) was an American physician and a Patriot in the American Revolutionary War. He is best known for successfully completing the midnight ride to Concord, Massachusetts, on the night of April 18–19, 1775, after Paul Revere and William Dawes were stopped.

The midnight ride

Prescott, a Concord doctor returning home late from visiting his fiancée in Lexington, encountered Revere and Dawes on the road and joined their mission to warn the militia that British troops were marching to seize stores at Concord. When a British patrol intercepted the riders near Lincoln, Revere was captured and Dawes was thrown from his horse, but Prescott escaped across country — knowing the terrain well — and reached Concord around 1:30 a.m., raising the alarm that mobilised the militia before the Battles of Lexington and Concord.

Later life

Prescott served as a surgeon in the Continental Army. He was reportedly captured while serving aboard an American privateer and died in British captivity, possibly in Halifax, around 1777.

Legacy

His ride is commemorated in annual re-enactments along the Battle Road in Massachusetts, and he is honoured as the rider who actually carried the alarm to Concord.
