Kikunae Ikeda (池田 菊苗, 1864–1936) was a Japanese chemist and professor at Tokyo Imperial University who discovered umami, the savoury fifth basic taste.

Discovery of umami

In 1908, tasting dashi broth made from kombu seaweed, Ikeda identified glutamic acid as the source of its distinctive savoury flavour, distinct from sweet, sour, salty and bitter. He named it umami ("pleasant savoury taste").

MSG and Ajinomoto

Ikeda patented production of monosodium glutamate (MSG) and, with Saburosuke Suzuki, founded the Ajinomoto company in 1909 to market it as a seasoning. MSG became a global food ingredient.

Legacy

Umami was scientifically accepted as a basic taste only in the 1980s–2000s, when umami taste receptors were identified. Ikeda is counted among Japan's great inventors.
