Head-related transfer function

A head-related transfer function (HRTF) describes how an ear receives a sound from a point in space. As sound travels to the listener, the shape of the head, outer ears, ear canals, and torso modify the sound's spectrum and timing in ways that depend on the source direction, providing cues the auditory system uses for sound localization.

Measurement and use

HRTFs are measured with miniature microphones in a person's ears (or a mannequin head) in an anechoic chamber, recording responses to sounds from many directions. A pair of HRTFs for the two ears encodes interaural time differences, interaural level differences, and direction-dependent spectral filtering.

Applications

HRTFs are used in binaural audio, virtual and augmented reality, gaming, and hearing research to render spatialised sound over headphones. Because ear shapes differ between individuals, personalised HRTFs generally give more convincing externalisation than generic ones, and research continues into efficient individualisation methods.
