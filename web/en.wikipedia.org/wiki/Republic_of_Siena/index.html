Republic of Siena

The Republic of Siena (Italian: Repubblica di Siena) was a historic state consisting of the city of Siena and its surrounding territory in Tuscany, central Italy. It existed for over four centuries, from the late 12th century until 1555, when it was conquered by the rival Duchy of Florence and ultimately absorbed into the Grand Duchy of Tuscany.

History

Siena became a self-governing commune in the 12th century and grew rich on banking and the wool trade, becoming a major financial centre. Its government, famously the Council of Nine (1287–1355), commissioned enduring monuments including the Palazzo Pubblico and Ambrogio Lorenzetti's frescoes of Good and Bad Government. A fierce Ghibelline rival of Guelph Florence, Siena defeated the Florentines at the Battle of Montaperti in 1260, though it was itself defeated at Colle in 1269. The Black Death of 1348 devastated the city and halted work on its ambitious new cathedral.

Government

The republic was governed by a podestà and councils drawn from the city's factions, with regimes changing frequently — the governments of the Twelve, the Fifteen and others succeeded one another. Despite the instability, Siena maintained its independence for centuries through diplomacy and alliances.

Fall

After a heroic defence and a devastating siege in 1554–55, in which much of the population starved, Siena surrendered to Imperial-Spanish and Florentine forces in April 1555. Some Sienese exiles maintained a "Republic of Siena in exile" at Montalcino until 1559, when the Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis ended the claim and Siena passed definitively to Cosimo I de' Medici.
