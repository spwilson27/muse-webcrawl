Commissary

A commissary is a store for provisions, particularly in military, prison, or remote industrial settings. The word has several related senses, all deriving from the notion of goods "committed" to an official charged with their distribution.

Military commissaries

In the United States Armed Forces, a commissary is a grocery store on a military installation that sells food and household goods at cost plus a small surcharge to service members, retirees, and their families. The system is administered by the Defense Commissary Agency (DeCA), established in 1991, and operates hundreds of stores worldwide. Commissaries trace their origins to sales of provisions to soldiers dating to the early American republic; the modern benefit was standardized after the Civil War.

Prison commissaries

In prisons and jails, the commissary (or canteen) is a store where incarcerated people purchase food, toiletries, stationery, and other approved items using funds held in their accounts. Prison commissaries are significant to inmates' quality of life and have been studied and criticized for high prices and monopoly contracting.

Other uses

  • Mining and logging camps: company stores in remote settlements were often called commissaries.
  • Film and television: a commissary is the studio cafeteria or dining hall — the Warner Bros. and other studio commissaries were famous Hollywood institutions.
  • Commissary kitchen: a shared commercial kitchen used by food trucks and caterers to prepare food for sale elsewhere.
  • Soviet and diplomatic usage: "commissary" also translates offices and officials (commissars) responsible for supply in some states.
