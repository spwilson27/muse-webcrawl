Uco Valley

The Uco Valley (Spanish: Valle de Uco) is a viticultural region southwest of Mendoza, in Argentina. Situated along the Tunuyán River, the Uco Valley is widely considered one of the top wine regions in Mendoza, and all of Argentina. The annual average temperature is 14 °C (57 °F) and at an average elevation of about 900–1,200 metres (3,000–3,900 ft) above sea level, the region's combination of high elevation, alluvial soils, low rainfall and large diurnal temperature variation make it well suited to fine wine production.

Location: Mendoza Province, Argentina
Departments: Tunuyán, Tupungato, San Carlos
Elevation: ~900–1,700 m
Principal grape: Malbec

Geography and terroir

The valley lies in the foothills of the Andes, in the departments of Tunuyán, Tupungato and San Carlos. Vineyards, some above 1,500 m in sub-regions such as Gualtallary, receive abundant sunlight and cool nights, preserving acidity and deepening colour in red grapes. Soils are alluvial, stony and calcareous in places; irrigation comes from Andean snowmelt, delivered by canals and increasingly by drip systems drawing on groundwater.

Wines

The region is best known for Malbec of exceptional intensity and freshness, but also produces notable Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc, Merlot, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Torrontés. Since the 1990s, investment by Argentine and international producers (including estates such as Zuccardi, Catena, Salentein and Clos de los Siete) transformed the valley into the engine of Argentina's premium wine industry. Sub-appellations (Geographical Indications) such as Paraje Altamira, Gualtallary, Los Chacayes and San Pablo have been formally recognised, reflecting a shift toward terroir-specific bottlings.
