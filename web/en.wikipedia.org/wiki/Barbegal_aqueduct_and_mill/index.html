Barbegal aqueduct and mill

The Barbegal aqueduct and mill is a Roman watermill complex near Fontvieille in southern France, dating to the 2nd century AD. Fed by an aqueduct drawing water from the Alpilles hills, it comprised sixteen overshot waterwheels arranged in two parallel rows of eight, making it one of the largest known industrial installations of the ancient world.

LocationFontvieille, France
Built2nd century AD
Wheels16 overshot wheels
Output≈4.5 tonnes of flour per day

Description

Water descended a steep hillside through the two wheel rows, each wheel driving a pair of millstones via wooden gearing. Estimates suggest the mills could grind enough flour — around 4.5 tonnes daily — to supply bread for some 10,000 people, plausibly serving the nearby city of Arles.

Significance

Barbegal is frequently cited as evidence that Roman technology applied water power on a near-industrial scale, challenging older views that ancient economies relied almost entirely on muscle power. The ruins of the mill race and aqueduct bridges remain visible today.
