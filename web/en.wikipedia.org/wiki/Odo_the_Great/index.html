Odo the Great (also Eudes or Eudo, died c. 735) was Duke of Aquitaine and Gascony in the early 8th century, ruling a largely independent principality in southwestern Gaul between the Loire and the Pyrenees. He is remembered above all for his victory over an Umayyad army at the Battle of Toulouse in 721 — the first major check to Muslim expansion north of the Pyrenees — and for his subsequent alliance and conflict with Charles Martel.

Early rule

Odo's origins are obscure; he may have succeeded Duke Lupus and was possibly related to earlier Aquitanian rulers. By about 700 he controlled Aquitaine and held the loyalty of the Basques of Gascony. Taking advantage of civil war among the Franks, he asserted near-total independence from the Merovingian kings, campaigning as far as the Loire against the Neustrian mayor Charles Martel before a treaty in the late 710s recognised a fragile peace.

Battle of Toulouse, 721

After the Umayyad conquest of Visigothic Spain, Muslim forces crossed the Pyrenees, seized Narbonne and besieged Toulouse in 721. Odo, having gathered an army including Frankish contingents, fell on the besiegers on 9 June 721 and routed them, killing the governor al-Samh ibn Malik al-Khawlani. The victory was celebrated in Rome — Pope Gregory II had sent Odo consecrated gifts beforehand — and made Odo the leading Christian prince of the West for a time.

Diplomacy and conflict with the Umayyads

To secure his southern frontier, Odo allied with the Berber lord Munuza (Uthman ibn Naissa) in the Cerdanya, marrying his daughter (named Lampegia in later tradition) to him. When Munuza rebelled against the new emir Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi and was crushed, Abd al-Rahman invaded in 732, defeated Odo at the Battle of the River Garonne near Bordeaux — Odo's surviving forces and deserters reportedly suffering heavy losses — and sacked Bordeaux before marching north toward Tours.

Relations with Charles Martel

Faced with disaster, Odo appealed to his old rival Charles Martel, who defeated Abd al-Rahman's army at the Battle of Tours (Poitiers) in October 732, where Abd al-Rahman was killed. Chroniclers credit Odo's troops with attacking the Muslim camp during the battle. Afterward Odo submitted formally to Charles while retaining his duchy.

Death and succession

Odo died around 735 and was succeeded by his son Hunald I, who attempted to renew Aquitanian independence against Charles Martel and his heirs. Later medieval chroniclers, and the continuators of Fredegar, gave Odo an important but ambivalent place in the history of the Frankish south.
