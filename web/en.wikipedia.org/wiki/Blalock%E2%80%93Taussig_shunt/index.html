Blalock–Taussig shunt

The Blalock–Taussig shunt (also called the Blalock–Thomas–Taussig shunt) is a surgical procedure used in the palliative treatment of cyanotic congenital heart defects such as tetralogy of Fallot. It connects a systemic artery to the pulmonary artery to increase blood flow to the lungs.

History

The procedure was first performed in 1944 at Johns Hopkins Hospital by surgeon Alfred Blalock, cardiologist Helen B. Taussig, and laboratory technician Vivien Thomas, whose experimental work was essential to its development. The first patient was a 15-month-old girl, Eileen Saxon.

Modified shunt

In the modified Blalock–Taussig shunt, a synthetic Gore-Tex graft connects the subclavian artery to the pulmonary artery, preserving arm blood flow. The shunt is usually a temporary measure before complete repair of the heart defect.
