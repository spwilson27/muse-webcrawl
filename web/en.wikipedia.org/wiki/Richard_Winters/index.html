Richard Davis "Dick" Winters (January 21, 1918 – January 2, 2011) was an American military officer of the United States Army during World War II. He commanded Easy Company of the 2nd Battalion, 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment, part of the 101st Airborne Division, during the Normandy landings and subsequent fighting across north-west Europe. Winters was prominently featured in Stephen Ambrose's 1992 book Band of Brothers and was portrayed by Damian Lewis in the 2001 HBO miniseries of the same name.

Early life and training

Winters was born in Lancaster County, Pennsylvania, and graduated from Franklin and Marshall College in 1941 with a degree in economics. He enlisted in the Army that August, motivated partly by a wish to finish his service before the country entered the war. After Officer Candidate School he volunteered for the paratroopers and was assigned to Easy Company at Camp Toccoa, Georgia, under the famously demanding Captain Herbert Sobel, then trained at Camp Mackall and in England in preparation for the invasion of France.

World War II

Normandy

Winters jumped into Normandy in the early hours of D-Day, June 6, 1944, landing near Sainte-Mère-Église. With Easy Company's commander, Lieutenant Thomas Meehan, killed when his aircraft was shot down, Winters assumed command of the company. Later that day he led the celebrated assault on a battery of German 105 mm howitzers at Brécourt Manor that were firing on Utah Beach; the attack, in which a small force destroyed the guns and captured intelligence on German defences, is still studied at West Point as a model small-unit action. Winters received the Distinguished Service Cross for his leadership there.

Later campaigns

Winters led Easy Company through Operation Market Garden in the Netherlands, where he was promoted to executive officer of the 2nd Battalion, and commanded the battalion during parts of the Battle of the Bulge around Bastogne after its commander was wounded. In the spring of 1945 the battalion advanced into Germany, liberated the Kaufering concentration camp, and ended the war at Berchtesgaden and in Austria. Winters was promoted to major and was recommended for the Medal of Honor for Brécourt Manor, but received the Distinguished Service Cross instead because of a quota on awards within the division.

Later life

After the war Winters worked in business in New Jersey and Pennsylvania, including a stint working for his wartime comrade Lewis Nixon's family company, and later founded his own animal-feed supply business in Hershey, Pennsylvania. He was recalled to active duty during the Korean War to train infantry officers at Fort Dix but did not deploy. He lectured on leadership at West Point, and following the success of Band of Brothers became a sought-after speaker, though he remained characteristically modest; his own memoir, Beyond Band of Brothers, appeared in 2006. Winters died on January 2, 2011, in Palmyra, Pennsylvania, and was buried in Ephrata, Pennsylvania, beside his parents.
