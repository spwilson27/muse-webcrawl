Sun Lutang

Born22 November 1860, Wanping (Wan county), Hebei, China
Died16 December 1933 (aged 73)
StyleXingyiquan, baguazhang, tai chi (founder of Sun-style tai chi)
OccupationMartial artist, author

Sun Lutang (Chinese: 孫祿堂; 1860–1933), born Sun Fuquan, was a renowned Chinese martial artist who founded Sun-style tai chi and was among the first to publish systematic books on the internal martial arts. A scholar-practitioner, he mastered all three of the principal neijia arts — xingyiquan, baguazhang, and tai chi — a combination that earned him the nickname "Tiger-Head Sage" in martial arts circles.

Training

Sun studied xingyiquan first under Li Kuiyuan and then under the famous Guo Yunshen, one of the most celebrated fighters of the era. He subsequently learned baguazhang from Cheng Tinghua, the art's leading exponent in Beijing. In his fifties he studied Wu (Hao)-style tai chi under Hao Weizhen; according to the well-known account, when Hao fell ill while visiting Beijing, Sun cared for him, and Hao taught him his tai chi in gratitude.

Sun-style tai chi

Sun Lutang synthesised his three arts into a distinctive tai chi style characterised by an upright, agile stance, higher posture, compact steps that follow bagua footwork, and the principle that when one foot advances the other follows. The form is sometimes described as "opening and closing" tai chi (kai he huo bu), after its characteristic open-close hand movements and lively stepping. It is recognised as one of the five major family styles of tai chi, alongside Chen, Yang, Wu (Hao), and Wu styles.

Writings and legacy

Sun was unusually literary for a martial artist of his generation and wrote five influential books, including The Study of Xingyi Boxing (1915), The Study of Bagua Boxing, The True Meaning of Boxing, and The Study of Taiji Boxing (1921), which framed the internal arts in the language of the Yijing and Daoist and Confucian philosophy. He taught in Beijing and served as an instructor at martial arts institutes; his daughter Sun Jianyun carried on the family style after his death in 1933. His books remain standard references for the theory of the internal martial arts.
