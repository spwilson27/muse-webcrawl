Virgin Trains was a train operating company in the United Kingdom owned by Virgin Rail Group, a joint venture between the Virgin Group (51%) and Stagecoach (49%). It operated the InterCity West Coast franchise from March 1997 until December 2019, running long-distance services on the West Coast Main Line between London Euston and the West Midlands, North West England, North Wales, and Scotland. It also operated the CrossCountry franchise from 1997 to 2007.

History

Virgin won the West Coast franchise as part of the privatisation of British Rail, promising a major upgrade including new tilting trains and faster journey times. It introduced the Alstom-built Class 390 Pendolino electric trains and Bombardier Class 221 Super Voyager diesels in the early 2000s, coinciding with Network Rail's costly West Coast Main Line modernisation, which enabled 125 mph tilting operation and a much more frequent timetable from 2008 (the "Very High Frequency" timetable).

Franchise disputes

In 2012 the Department for Transport awarded the next West Coast franchise to FirstGroup; Virgin's legal challenge exposed serious flaws in the bidding process and the award was annulled, leading Virgin to continue on a management contract and then a direct-award franchise. In April 2019 the successor franchise was awarded to Avanti West Coast (FirstGroup/Trenitalia); Virgin's own bid had been disqualified over pension-risk terms, which Virgin disputed. Virgin Trains ran its final services on 7 December 2019, ending 22 years on the route.

Services and brand

The company was known for flamboyant branding under Richard Branson, distinctive red livery, and customer-service innovations; it also attracted criticism over fares and crowding. Passenger numbers roughly doubled over the franchise. Its related brands — Virgin Trains East Coast (2015–2018) and the US venture Virgin Trains USA (Brightline) — were separate operations.
