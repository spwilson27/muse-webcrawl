Ving Rhames

Irving Rameses "Ving" Rhames (born May 12, 1959) is an American actor. He is best known for his roles as crime boss Marsellus Wallace in Quentin Tarantino's Pulp Fiction (1994) and as IMF computer expert Luther Stickell in the Mission: Impossible film series, in which he is the only actor besides Tom Cruise to appear in all of the films.

Early life and education

Rhames was born and raised in Harlem, New York City. He attended the High School of Performing Arts and went on to study drama at SUNY Purchase before transferring to the Juilliard School, where he earned a Bachelor of Fine Arts in 1983. His stage name "Ving" is a shortening of Irving.

Career

After Broadway and television work in the 1980s, Rhames broke through in film with roles in Jacob's Ladder (1990) and Pulp Fiction. He won a Golden Globe for portraying boxing promoter Don King in the 1997 HBO film Don King: Only in America; in a widely remembered moment, he handed his award to fellow nominee Jack Lemmon during the ceremony. Other notable films include Con Air (1997), Out of Sight (1998), Entrapment (1999), Dawn of the Dead (2004), and the Mission: Impossible series beginning in 1996.
