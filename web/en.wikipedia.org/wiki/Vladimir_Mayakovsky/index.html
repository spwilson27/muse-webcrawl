Vladimir Mayakovsky

Vladimir Vladimirovich Mayakovsky (19 July [O.S. 7 July] 1893 – 14 April 1930) was a Russian and Soviet poet, playwright, artist and actor. He was among the foremost representatives of early-20th-century Russian Futurism and became the most celebrated poet of the revolutionary era, famous for works such as A Cloud in Trousers, 150,000,000 and the play The Bedbug.

Early life

Born in Baghdati, Georgia, to a Russian father (a forester of Cossack descent) and a Ukrainian mother, Mayakovsky moved to Moscow after his father's death in 1906. He joined the Bolshevik faction of the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party as a teenager, was arrested repeatedly, and began writing poetry in solitary confinement. Expelled from school, he studied art and met David Burliuk, becoming a leader of the Cubo-Futurist group that in 1912 issued the manifesto A Slap in the Face of Public Taste.

Career

His long poems A Cloud in Trousers (1915) and The Backbone Flute were partly inspired by his tumultuous love affair with Lilya Brik. After the October Revolution he put his talent at the service of the Soviet state: he wrote agitational posters for the ROSTA news agency, founded the journal LEF, and produced advertising verse of startling quality. His satirical plays The Bedbug (1929) and The Bathhouse (1930) attacked philistinism and bureaucracy.

Death and legacy

Disillusioned, attacked by orthodox critics and beset by personal troubles, Mayakovsky shot himself in Moscow on 14 April 1930. His suicide note quoted his unfinished poem: "the love boat has crashed against the everyday". After Lilya Brik appealed to Stalin, the leader declared Mayakovsky "the best, most talented poet of our Soviet epoch", canonising him — a fate Boris Pasternak called his "second death".
