Joseph Whitworth

Sir Joseph Whitworth, 1st Baronet (21 December 1803 - 22 January 1887) was an English engineer, inventor and entrepreneur. A pioneer of precision engineering, he standardized screw threads, built machine tools of unprecedented accuracy, and developed the Whitworth rifle, one of the first sniper rifles.

Early life and machine tools

Whitworth was born in Stockport and apprenticed as a mechanic, working for his uncle, a cotton spinner, and then in Manchester and London workshops, including Henry Maudslay's, where he absorbed the discipline of precision work. In 1833 he set up his own business in Manchester making lathes and other machine tools. His planing machines, true planes and measuring machines - capable of measuring differences of a millionth of an inch - set new standards for the engineering industry.

Standard screw thread

In 1841 Whitworth proposed a uniform system of screw threads based on a 55-degree thread angle and standard pitches for each diameter. The British Standard Whitworth (BSW) became the world's first national screw-thread standard and remained in use in Britain and the Commonwealth for more than a century.

Firearms and later life

During the Crimean War the British government asked Whitworth to investigate small arms. His hexagonal-bore rifle proved far more accurate and longer-ranged than the standard Enfield, and Whitworth rifles were prized by sharpshooters during the American Civil War. He also worked on breech-loading artillery and toughened ("fluid-compressed") steel. Whitworth endowed scholarships and educational institutions, including support for what became the University of Manchester's engineering tradition, and was created a baronet in 1869. He died at Monte Carlo in 1887.
