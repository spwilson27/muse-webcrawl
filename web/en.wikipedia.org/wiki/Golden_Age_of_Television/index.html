Golden Age of Television

The term Golden Age of Television is used for two eras: the first, from the late 1940s to about 1960, when live anthology drama flourished in the United States; and a second, from around 1999 onward, marked by acclaimed cable and streaming series.

Golden Age of Television
First era: c. 1947–1960
Landmarks: Kraft Television Theatre, I Love Lucy, Playhouse 90
Second era: c. 1999–present (The Sopranos onward)

First Golden Age

Live dramas from New York — Marty, Requiem for a Heavyweight — and anthology series like Studio One showcased writers such as Rod Serling and Paddy Chayefsky. Sitcoms like I Love Lucy pioneered filmed multi-camera production. The era faded as production moved to Hollywood and filmed series dominated.

Second Golden Age

Critics apply the label to the period beginning with The Sopranos (1999), encompassing The Wire, Breaking Bad and streaming-era prestige drama, characterised by serialised storytelling and cinematic production.
