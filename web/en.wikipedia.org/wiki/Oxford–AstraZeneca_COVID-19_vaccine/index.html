Oxford–AstraZeneca COVID-19 vaccine

The Oxford–AstraZeneca COVID-19 vaccine, sold under the brand names Covishield and Vaxzevria, is a viral vector vaccine against COVID-19 developed by the University of Oxford and AstraZeneca. It uses a modified chimpanzee adenovirus (ChAdOx1) carrying the gene for the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein.

Development and authorisation

The vaccine was developed at Oxford's Jenner Institute during 2020 and authorised for emergency use in the United Kingdom on 30 December 2020, followed by the European Union and many other countries. It was supplied on a not-for-profit basis during the pandemic and manufactured at scale by partners including the Serum Institute of India (as Covishield), making it the most widely used COVID-19 vaccine in low- and middle-income countries; more than 3 billion doses were delivered.

Safety

In early 2021 regulators identified a very rare side effect of thrombosis with thrombocytopenia (blood clots with low platelets), leading many countries to restrict its use by age. It requires only standard refrigeration, an important logistical advantage over mRNA vaccines.
