Hillman Imp

The Hillman Imp is a small economy car that was made by the Rootes Group and its successor Chrysler Europe from 1963 until 1976. It was the first British production car with the engine block and head cast in aluminium, and the first mass-produced British car with the engine in the back and an opening rear hatch.

Development

The Imp was conceived as Rootes' answer to the BMC Mini. Designed under the codename "Apex" by Michael Parkes and Tim Fry, it used an all-aluminium 875 cc overhead-camshaft inline-four derived from the Coventry Climax FWMA fire-pump engine, mounted at the rear and canted at 45 degrees. Production was located at a new government-backed factory at Linwood, Scotland, opened in 1963 by the Duke of Edinburgh.

Reception and legacy

Contemporary reviewers praised the Imp's handling, performance, and economy, but early cars suffered reliability problems, and the inexperienced Linwood workforce and persistent industrial disputes undermined production. Despite the car being a modest success — 440,032 were built, including Singer Chamois and Sunbeam variants and the Husky estate — the Linwood venture lost money and contributed to Rootes' takeover by Chrysler. The Imp has since gained a strong following in historic rallying and classic car circles.
