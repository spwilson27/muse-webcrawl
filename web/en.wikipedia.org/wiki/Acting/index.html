Acting

Acting is an activity in which a story is enacted by its actors, who adopt a character — in theatre, film, television, radio or another medium that makes use of the mimetic mode. Acting involves a broad range of skills, including a well-developed imagination, emotional facility, physical expressivity, vocal projection, clarity of speech and the ability to interpret drama.

History

Acting in the Western tradition originates in classical Greece; the word "actor" derives from the Greek hypokrites, "one who interprets". Thespis is traditionally credited as the first actor in a play. Acting developed through Roman theatre, medieval religious drama, the commedia dell'arte, and the professional stages of early modern Europe.

Techniques

Modern training is dominated by systems derived from Konstantin Stanislavski, whose "system" of emotional truth underlies method acting as taught by Lee Strasberg, and the contrasting approaches of Stella Adler and Sanford Meisner. Classical training emphasises voice, movement and text analysis, as in the work of Michael Chekhov and Uta Hagen.
