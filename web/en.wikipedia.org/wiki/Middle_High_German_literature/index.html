Middle High German literature

Middle High German literature refers to literature written in German between roughly 1050 and 1350, the age of Middle High German. It is dominated by the courtly culture of the Hohenstaufen era (c. 1170–1230), whose flowering produced the great Middle High German classics: the heroic epic Nibelungenlied, the courtly romances of Hartmann von Aue, Wolfram von Eschenbach and Gottfried von Straßburg, and the love lyrics of the Minnesang tradition, above all Walther von der Vogelweide.

Early period (c. 1050–1170)

Literacy after the near-silence of the late Old High German period was clerical: religious verse, biblical epics and didactic works such as the Ezzolied and the writings of the poetess Ava, the first named woman writer in German. By the mid-12th century, historical chronicle (the Kaiserchronik), heroic material and the earliest courtly love poetry (the Danubian Minnesang of Der von Kürenberg) heralded the classical age, while Heinrich von Veldeke's Eneasroman imported the romance form from France.

Classical period (c. 1170–1230)

Courtly romance

Hartmann von Aue adapted Chrétien de Troyes in Erec and Iwein and wrote the religious legend Gregorius and the verse novella Der arme Heinrich. Wolfram von Eschenbach's Parzival, the greatest German Arthurian romance, follows its hero's journey from folly to Grail kingship; Wolfram also wrote Titurel and Willehalm. Gottfried von Straßburg's Tristan (c. 1210), left unfinished, renders the adulterous love of Tristan and Isolde in dazzling rhetorical style.

Heroic epic

The Nibelungenlied (c. 1200) combines Burgundian legend with the fall of the Burgundian kingdom: Siegfried's murder by Hagen and Kriemhild's terrible revenge at the court of Etzel (Attila). Related heroic works include the Kudrun and the Dietrich cycle epics.

Minnesang

The lyric of courtly love flourished at courts from the Rhineland to Vienna. Walther von der Vogelweide (c. 1170–c. 1230) transcended the conventions of Minne with political Sprüche, crusading songs and the intimate dawn-song tradition; other major Minnesänger include Reinmar, Heinrich von Morungen, Wolfram and the didactic poet Freidank. The lavishly illustrated Codex Manesse (early 14th century) preserves the songs of 140 poets.

Late period (c. 1230–1350)

After 1230 courtly forms were imitated, parodied and transformed: Konrad von Würzburg refined the romance, Wernher der Gartenaere's Meier Helmbrecht offered biting social realism, and urban and didactic writing grew with the towns. By about 1350, Early New High German begins to replace Middle High German as the literary language.
