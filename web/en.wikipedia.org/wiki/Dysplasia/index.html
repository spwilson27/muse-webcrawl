Dysplasia

Dysplasia is an abnormal growth or development of cells or organs. The term is used in several distinct senses in medicine. In pathology, it most often refers to abnormal, disordered maturation of cells within a tissue — particularly epithelial dysplasia, a precancerous lesion in which cells show enlarged, irregular nuclei, increased nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio and loss of normal architectural organization. In developmental medicine, dysplasia denotes a malformation arising from abnormal organization of cells into tissue, as in hip dysplasia or the skeletal dysplasias.

Epithelial dysplasia

Epithelial dysplasia is graded as mild, moderate or severe according to how much of the epithelial thickness is involved by atypical cells. When the full thickness is affected, the lesion is classified as carcinoma in situ. Well-known examples include cervical intraepithelial neoplasia, detected by Pap smear screening; Barrett's oesophagus with dysplasia, a precursor of oesophageal adenocarcinoma; and dysplasia arising in chronic inflammatory conditions such as ulcerative colitis. Dysplasia is potentially reversible if the provoking cause is removed, but severe dysplasia carries a substantial risk of progression to invasive cancer, and is therefore often treated by local excision or ablation.

Screening

Because dysplasia precedes many cancers, screening programmes aim to detect and treat it before invasion occurs. Cervical cytology, colonoscopic surveillance in long-standing colitis, and endoscopic monitoring of Barrett's oesophagus are standard applications.

Developmental dysplasias

In a second sense, dysplasia describes congenital conditions of abnormal tissue development: developmental dysplasia of the hip, fibrous dysplasia of bone, and the large family of skeletal dysplasias (including achondroplasia) fall into this category, as do the myelodysplastic syndromes of the bone marrow.
