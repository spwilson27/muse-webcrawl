Burglary

Burglary, also called breaking and entering and sometimes housebreaking, is the act of illegally entering a building or other structure, often by force, with the intent to commit a criminal offence. Usually that offence is theft, robbery or murder, but most jurisdictions include others which fall within the ambit of burglary. To engage in the act of burglary is to burgle (in British English) or to burglarize (in North American English).

Common law definition

At common law, burglary was the breaking and entering of the dwelling house of another at night with the intent to commit a felony inside. Modern statutes in most jurisdictions have broadened the offence to cover entries into any building or structure, by day or night, and unlawful entry without actual force can suffice.

By jurisdiction

In England and Wales, burglary is defined by the Theft Act 1968, and aggravated burglary committed with a weapon carries heavier penalties. In the United States, burglary is typically graded by degree depending on whether the premises were a dwelling, whether the offence occurred at night, and whether the burglar was armed. Burglary is distinguished from robbery, which involves force or the threat of force against a person.
