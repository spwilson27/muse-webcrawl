Streptomyces


Phylum: Actinomycetota
Family: Streptomycetaceae
Type species: Streptomyces albus

Streptomyces is the largest genus of Actinomycetota and the type genus of the family Streptomycetaceae. Over 700 species of Streptomyces bacteria have been described. They are found predominantly in soil and decaying vegetation, are Gram-positive with high GC content genomes, and form branching filamentous mycelia with aerial hyphae that bear chains of spores — a fungus-like lifestyle unusual among bacteria.

Secondary metabolites

Streptomyces species produce over two-thirds of the clinically useful antibiotics of natural origin, among them streptomycin (from S. griseus, discovered by Albert Schatz and Selman Waksman in 1943), tetracyclines, erythromycin, chloramphenicol, and neomycin. They also produce antifungals (nystatin), antiparasitics (ivermectin from S. avermitilis), immunosuppressants, and anticancer agents. The volatile compound geosmin gives soil its characteristic earthy smell after rain.

Genomics and development

The model species S. coelicolor A3(2) has one of the largest known bacterial genomes (~8.7 Mb on a linear chromosome), rich in biosynthetic gene clusters for secondary metabolites, most of which are silent under laboratory conditions. The life cycle involves germination of spores, growth of a substrate mycelium, erection of aerial hyphae coated in a hydrophobic sheath, and septation into spore chains — a developmental programme controlled by the bld and whi genes.
