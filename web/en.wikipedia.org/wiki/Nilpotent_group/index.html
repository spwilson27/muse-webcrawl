Nilpotent group

In mathematics, specifically group theory, a nilpotent group is a group that is "almost abelian": it has a central series terminating at the identity, equivalently, its upper central series reaches the whole group. Nilpotent groups are named by analogy with nilpotent elements in ring theory and lie between abelian and solvable groups in complexity.

A nilpotent group's upper central series reaches the whole group.

Definition

A group G is nilpotent if its upper central series 1 = Z0 ≤ Z1 ≤ … reaches G in finitely many steps, where each Zi+1/Zi is the center of G/Zi. Equivalently, the lower central series, generated by iterated commutators, descends to the identity. The smallest number of steps is the nilpotency class; abelian groups are nilpotent of class at most 1.

Examples and properties

Every abelian group is nilpotent, as is every finite p-group. The Heisenberg group of upper-triangular 3×3 matrices with unit diagonal is the classic non-abelian example, of class 2. Finite groups are nilpotent if and only if they are direct products of their Sylow p-subgroups. Nilpotent groups are solvable, closed under subgroups, quotients and finite direct products, and every finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group embeds in a group of upper-triangular integer matrices.

Significance

Nilpotent groups underpin the theory of nilmanifolds in geometry, appear in Galois theory as groups with restricted ramification, and their Lie algebras counterpart — nilpotent Lie algebras — are fundamental through Engel's theorem.
