Fount of honour

The fount of honour (Latin: fons honorum) is a legal and heraldic concept referring to the person or body who, by virtue of sovereignty or delegated authority, is entitled to grant legitimate titles of nobility, orders of chivalry, coats of arms and other honours. In monarchies, this prerogative belongs to the sovereign as head of state.

Meaning and usage

In the United Kingdom, the monarch is the fount of honour and the source of all honours, dignities and privileges, acting usually on ministerial advice. Because the sovereign is the fount of honour, honours granted by pretenders to thrones, or by heads of dynastic orders no longer reigning, are contested in heraldic law; some authorities accept that a deposed monarch retains the ius honorum as a dynastic right, while others deny it. The concept extends to republics in modified form: presidents may confer state orders and decorations under constitutional or statutory authority.

Heraldic authority

As fount of honour, a sovereign may grant arms directly or through heraldic authorities such as the College of Arms in England or the Court of the Lord Lyon in Scotland, which exercise the prerogative by delegation.
