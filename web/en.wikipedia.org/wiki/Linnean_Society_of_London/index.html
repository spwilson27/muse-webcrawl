The Linnean Society of London is a learned society dedicated to the study and dissemination of information concerning natural history, evolution, and taxonomy. Founded in 1788, it is the world's oldest extant biological society.

History

The society was founded by the botanist Sir James Edward Smith, who in 1784 had purchased the collections and library of Carl Linnaeus from his widow; these collections — including Linnaeus's herbarium and the type specimens of thousands of species — remain the society's most famous possession. The society received its royal charter in 1802.

The society is celebrated as the venue for the first public presentation of the theory of evolution by natural selection: on 1 July 1858, papers by Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace were read to the society (neither author was present), announcing the theory jointly. The society awards the Darwin–Wallace Medal to commemorate the event, and its highest honour for botany and zoology is the Linnean Medal.

Activities

Based at Burlington House on Piccadilly since 1873, the society publishes the Biological Journal, Botanical Journal, and Zoological Journal of the Linnean Society, together with The Linnean magazine, holds regular meetings and lectures, and maintains a library and collections open to researchers. Fellows are elected and use the post-nominal FLS.
