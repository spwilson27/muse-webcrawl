A linear-feedback shift register (LFSR) is a shift register whose input bit is a linear function — in practice, the XOR — of its previous state. Starting from a non-zero seed, the register cycles through a sequence of states that appears random and is used to generate pseudorandom binary sequences.

Operation

The positions of the register that feed the XOR are called taps, and the tap pattern is described by a feedback polynomial over GF(2). In the Fibonacci configuration the XOR of the tapped bits feeds the input; in the Galois configuration the output bit is XORed with selected bits as they shift. The two forms generate equivalent sequences with different implementations. If the feedback polynomial is primitive, the LFSR attains its maximum period of 2n − 1 states before repeating.

Applications

LFSRs are cheap to implement in hardware and fast in software, so they appear in cyclic redundancy checks (CRCs), stream ciphers (such as the A5/1 and A5/2 ciphers formerly used in GSM, and the E0 cipher in Bluetooth), spread-spectrum and scrambling systems, built-in self-test of circuits, and as counters and pseudo-noise sequence generators in communications. Because their output is linear, a raw LFSR sequence is predictable — the Berlekamp–Massey algorithm can recover the polynomial from a short stretch of output — so cryptographic designs combine several registers or filter their outputs nonlinearly.
