United States Space Force

The United States Space Force (USSF) is the space service branch of the US Armed Forces, established on 20 December 2019 as the first new independent service since the Air Force in 1947. Its personnel — called Guardians — operate America's military satellites (GPS, missile warning, communications), run the launch ranges at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station and Vandenberg Space Force Base, and track every object in orbit.

The force grew from Air Force Space Command, inheriting its missions and much of its culture; its creation formalised space as a warfighting domain in US doctrine. Its delta-winged insignia and flag lean on heritage symbols — the central star and globe of the old space command — a lineage the service is fond of tracing to 1982 and, rhetorically, to the earliest rocket age.

In this crawl

The Space Force is the landlord of the American launch ranges chronicled here and operator of the GPS constellation (through its mission deltas), making it the quiet constant behind both military and civil spaceflight in the United States; its Space Operations Command also maintains the public catalogue of tracked Space debris used worldwide.
