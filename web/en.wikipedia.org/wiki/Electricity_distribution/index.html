Electricity distribution

Electricity distribution
Primary voltage: ~11–33 kV
Secondary voltage: 120/230–240 V
Key equipment: substations, feeders, pole transformers

Electricity distribution is the final stage in the delivery of electric power from generating stations to individual consumers. A distribution network carries electricity from the high-voltage transmission system and delivers it, stepped down through transformers, to homes, businesses, and industry.

Primary distribution

Power leaves a distribution substation at medium voltage, commonly between 4 and 35 kilovolts, along feeder circuits arranged in radial, loop, or network configurations. Reclosers, switches, and capacitor banks regulate voltage and protect the lines against faults.

Secondary distribution

Distribution transformers mounted on poles or pads reduce the voltage to the utilisation level — 230 volts in most of the world, or 120/240 volts split-phase in North America. Short secondary lines and service drops connect individual customers and their meters.

Modern developments

Smart grids add sensors, automated switching, and two-way communication to distribution networks, enabling faster fault isolation, integration of rooftop solar and electric vehicles, and demand response programmes.
