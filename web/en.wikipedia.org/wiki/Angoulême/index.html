Angoulême

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Angoulême (French pronunciation: [ɑ̃ɡulɛm]) is a commune and the prefecture of the Charente department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region of southwestern France. The town is built on a limestone plateau overlooking a bend in the Charente River. It is internationally known for the Angoulême International Comics Festival, the largest comics festival in Europe.

History

The site was a Roman settlement known as Iculisna. In the Middle Ages it became the seat of the counts of Angoulême; one of them, Isabella of Angoulême, married King John of England in 1200. The town's title later passed into the French royal family, and the county became a duchy held by members of the Valois line — Francis I, king of France from 1515, was born nearby at Cognac and had been count of Angoulême. Marguerite of Angoulême, his sister, was a notable Renaissance writer and patron.

Angoulême developed paper-making along the Charente, an industry that flourished from the seventeenth century, and later became known for foundries, printing, and animation studios. During the Second World War the town lay close to the demarcation line between occupied and Vichy France.

Geography

The old town occupies a flat-topped hill defended by ramparts, from which there are views over the Charente valley. The modern town spreads into suburbs on the opposite bank and along the valley floor. Angoulême lies roughly between Bordeaux to the southwest and Poitiers to the north, and is served by a TGV station on the high-speed line between Paris and Bordeaux.

Culture

The Angoulême International Comics Festival (Festival international de la bande dessinée d'Angoulême), founded in 1974, is held every January and draws artists, publishers, and around 200,000 visitors. The town hosts the Cité internationale de la bande dessinée et de l'image, a museum and archive devoted to comics, and painted comic-strip murals decorate many walls in the centre. Angoulême is also home to animation and video-game studios, and its cathedral of Saint-Pierre is a noted Romanesque church with a richly sculpted facade.
