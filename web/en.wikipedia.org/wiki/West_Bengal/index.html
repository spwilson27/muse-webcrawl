West Bengal

West Bengal is a state in the eastern region of India, along the Bay of Bengal. With a population of about 100 million, it is India's fourth-most populous state. Its capital and largest city is Kolkata. The state stretches from the Himalayas in the north (including the hill station of Darjeeling) to the mangrove forests of the Sundarbans in the south, and it shares borders with Bangladesh to the east, Nepal and Bhutan to the north, and the Indian states of Sikkim, Assam, Bihar, Jharkhand and Odisha.

History

The Bengal region was a centre of ancient kingdoms, of the Pala and Sena empires, and later a prosperous Mughal province famed for textiles and trade. Under British rule, Calcutta (now Kolkata) became the capital of British India until 1911. The Partition of Bengal in 1905 provoked widespread protest, and in 1947 Bengal was divided between India (West Bengal) and Pakistan (East Bengal, now Bangladesh). Post-independence, West Bengal was governed for 34 years (1977–2011) by the Left Front led by the Communist Party of India (Marxist), the world's longest-serving democratically elected communist government, followed by the Trinamool Congress under Mamata Banerjee.

Culture and economy

Bengali culture is celebrated for its literature (Rabindranath Tagore, the first non-European Nobel laureate in Literature), cinema (Satyajit Ray), music and festivals — above all Durga Puja in Kolkata, inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2021. The state is a major producer of rice, jute and Darjeeling tea, and Kolkata remains a significant port, industrial and educational centre.
