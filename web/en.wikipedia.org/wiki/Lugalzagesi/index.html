Lugalzagesi
Reignc. 2358–2334 BC (middle chronology)
CapitalUmma, then Uruk
PredecessorUru'inimgina (in Lagash)
SuccessorSargon of Akkad

Lugalzagesi (also spelled Lugal-Zage-Si) was the last Sumerian king to dominate Mesopotamia before the conquest of Sargon of Akkad. Ruling in the 24th century BC, he united the Sumerian city-states under his authority, creating the largest Mesopotamian realm yet seen — a short-lived empire that Sargon soon absorbed and surpassed.

Rise to power

Lugalzagesi began as ensi (governor-priest) of Umma. Taking advantage of the long-running border conflict between Umma and Lagash, he invaded and sacked Lagash, an act bitterly lamented in inscriptions of its reforming ruler Uru'inimgina. From this base he subdued the other Sumerian cities — Ur, Uruk, Larsa, Adab, and Nippur among them — and took the title "King of Uruk" and "King of the Land."

Claims of empire

In a celebrated inscription on stone vessels dedicated at Nippur, Lugalzagesi boasted that the god Enlil had given him kingship "from the Lower Sea (the Persian Gulf), along the Tigris and Euphrates, to the Upper Sea (the Mediterranean)," and that the lands lived in peace under his rule — the earliest known claim of an empire stretching from sea to sea, though the extent of his actual control in the north is debated.

Fall

Around 2334 BC, Sargon of Akkad attacked, defeated Lugalzagesi in a series of battles, and captured him. According to Sargon's inscriptions, the defeated king was led in a neck stock to the gate of Enlil at Nippur. His fall marks the end of the Early Dynastic period and the beginning of the Akkadian Empire.
