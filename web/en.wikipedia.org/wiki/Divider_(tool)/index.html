Divider (tool)

A divider, also known as a pair of dividers, is an instrument for measuring, transferring, or marking off distances, consisting of two adjustable legs hinged together, usually with sharp points on both ends. Dividers differ from compasses in that both legs end in points rather than one leg carrying a pencil or pen; they are used to "step off" equal distances repeatedly along a line or curve, to transfer measurements from a ruler or chart to a workpiece, and to scribe arcs and circles on material.

Types and use

Common forms include the spring divider, in which the legs are held apart by a spring and adjusted by a knurled nut for fine, precise settings; the firm-joint divider; and larger wing dividers used in carpentry and masonry. Navigators traditionally used dividers on nautical charts to measure distances against the chart's latitude scale, and machinists and woodworkers use them for layout work. In education and drafting, proportional dividers — with an adjustable pivot — are used to scale distances up or down by a fixed ratio.

Dividers are one of the classical instruments of geometry: together with the compass and straightedge they appear throughout Euclid's Elements-era construction techniques, and the divider remains a symbol of measurement, found on emblems and in iconography (for example, in the square and compasses of Freemasonry's related symbolism of precision).
