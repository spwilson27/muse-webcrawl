The Epic of Sundiata (also spelled Sunjata) is an epic poem of the Malinké people that narrates the life of Sundiata Keita (c. 1217 – c. 1255), the founder of the Mali Empire in West Africa. Preserved for centuries in oral form by griots (jeliw), hereditary historian-musicians, the epic blends history and legend: the sorcerer-king Sumanguru Kanté of Sosso, the prophecy of a lion-child born to the buffalo-woman Sogolon, Sundiata's childhood disability and miraculous first steps, his exile, and his triumphant return culminating in the Battle of Kirina (c. 1235).

Summary

The epic begins with the hunter's prophecy that Maghan Kon Fatta, king of the Mandé, must marry an ugly, hunchbacked woman, Sogolon Kedjou ("the buffalo woman"), who will bear a son destined to be a great king. Their child, Sundiata, cannot walk until, shamed by his mother's humiliation, he rises by bending an iron bar (or uproots a baobab, in some versions). After the king's death, the queen mother Sassouma Bérété places her own son on the throne, and Sundiata and Sogolon go into exile, wandering among neighbouring courts. Meanwhile Sumanguru, the smith-king of Sosso, conquers the Mandé. Recalled by his people, Sundiata gathers allies, learns the secret of Sumanguru's protective magic through his sister Nana Triban, and destroys him at Kirina. He then assembles the victorious allies at Kouroukan Fouga, where the epic tradition credits him with proclaiming a charter of rights and dividing the Mandé world among the clans.

Historical basis and versions

Written Arabic sources, notably Ibn Khaldun, confirm Sundiata (Mari Jata) as Mali's founder, though the epic is a literary and cultural monument rather than a literal chronicle. The best-known written version, published in 1960 by the Guinean historian D. T. Niane from the narration of the griot Djeli Mamoudou Kouyaté, made the epic a cornerstone of West African literature; many other griot versions exist, varying in detail. The Kouroukan Fouga charter associated with Sundiata's assembly was inscribed by UNESCO in 2009 on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
