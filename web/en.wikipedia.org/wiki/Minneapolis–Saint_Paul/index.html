Minneapolis–Saint Paul

Minneapolis–Saint Paul is a metropolitan area in the U.S. state of Minnesota, centred on the confluence of the Mississippi, Minnesota and St. Croix rivers. It is commonly known as the Twin Cities after its two largest cities, Minneapolis and Saint Paul, the state capital.

Geography and population

The area is Minnesota's most populous, home to roughly 3.7 million people in the wider metropolitan statistical area, which spans counties in Minnesota and western Wisconsin. Minneapolis is the larger of the two core cities; Saint Paul, downriver to the east, is the older settlement and the seat of state government.

History

Saint Paul developed as a trading post and river port in the 1840s, while Minneapolis grew around the milling power of Saint Anthony Falls. The rivalry and complementarity of the two cities shaped the region's railways, flour milling, and later its finance, medical device, retail and food industries.

Economy and culture

The Twin Cities host the headquarters of several major corporations, the University of Minnesota's main campus, a noted theatre and music scene, and extensive park systems along the rivers and lakes. The two downtowns are connected by light rail and a network of freeways.
