The Ridgeway is an ancient trackway and National Trail in southern England, often described as Britain's oldest road. The Trail runs for 87 miles (140 km) along the chalk ridge of the North Wessex Downs and the Chiltern Hills, from Overton Hill near Avebury in Wiltshire to Ivinghoe Beacon in Buckinghamshire.

History

The route follows a prehistoric trackway used for at least 5,000 years, part of a longer drovers' and trading route running from the Dorset coast to the Wash in Norfolk. Travellers preferred the dry, high chalk ridge above the marshy valleys. The western section passes an exceptional concentration of Neolithic and Bronze Age monuments, including Avebury stone circle, Silbury Hill, Wayland's Smithy long barrow and the Uffington White Horse.

The National Trail

The Ridgeway was designated one of England's National Trails in 1972 and opened in 1973. West of the Thames at Goring the trail is open to walkers, cyclists and horse riders; east of the Thames, in the Chilterns, it is a footpath only, passing through beech woodland and near the summit of Coombe Hill. The Thames Path provides an alternative continuation east of Goring.
