21st Century Communications and Video Accessibility Act of 2010

The Twenty-First Century Communications and Video Accessibility Act of 2010 (CVAA) is a United States federal law signed by President Barack Obama on 8 October 2010. The Act updates United States accessibility law — principally amendments to the Communications Act of 1934 — for the broadband and digital era, ensuring that people with disabilities can use modern communications technologies, including smartphones, internet video and advanced communications services.

CVAA

Long title: An Act to increase the access of persons with disabilities to modern communications
Public law: 111–260
Enacted: 8 October 2010
Signed by: President Barack Obama
Administered by: Federal Communications Commission

Provisions

The CVAA is divided into two titles. Title I covers communications access: it requires "advanced communications services" — such as text messaging, email and video communications — and the equipment used with them to be accessible to people with disabilities, unless not achievable; it extends disability access obligations to internet browsers on mobile phones; and it authorises funding for specialised equipment for people who are deafblind. Title II covers video programming: it reinstated the FCC's video description rules for television, required closed captions on certain internet-distributed video that had previously been shown on television with captions, required accessible on-screen programme guides and menus, and mandated accessibility features such as caption buttons on remote controls and televisions.

Implementation

The Federal Communications Commission implemented the Act through a series of rulemakings over the following decade, covering matters such as captioning of online video clips, user interface accessibility and real-time text as a successor to TTY services.
