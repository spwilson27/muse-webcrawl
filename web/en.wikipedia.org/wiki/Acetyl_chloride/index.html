Acetyl chloride

FormulaCH3COCl
Molar mass78.49 g/mol
AppearanceColourless liquid
Boiling point52 °C
Melting point−112 °C

Acetyl chloride (CH3COCl) is an acyl chloride derived from acetic acid. It is a colourless, corrosive, volatile liquid with a pungent odour, widely used in organic synthesis to introduce the acetyl group (CH3CO–) into molecules.

Preparation

Industrially, acetyl chloride is produced by the reaction of acetic acid or acetic anhydride with chlorinating agents; in the laboratory it is commonly prepared from acetic acid with thionyl chloride, phosphorus trichloride, or phosphorus pentachloride. A major industrial route is the carbonylation chemistry associated with acetic anhydride manufacture.

Reactions

Acetyl chloride reacts vigorously with water to give acetic acid and hydrogen chloride. It is a standard reagent for acetylation: with alcohols it forms acetate esters, with amines it forms acetamides, and with aromatic compounds under Friedel–Crafts conditions (with aluminium chloride) it gives aryl methyl ketones. It is used in the manufacture of pharmaceuticals, dyes, and agrochemicals.

Safety

Acetyl chloride is highly flammable, corrosive to skin and eyes, and a lachrymator; its fumes hydrolyse in moist air to hydrogen chloride.
