Akron, Ohio

Akron is a city in the U.S. state of Ohio and the county seat of Summit County. It is the fifth-most populous city in Ohio, with a population of about 190,000 at the 2020 census, and the centre of a metropolitan area of roughly 700,000 people. Located about 40 miles (64 km) south of Lake Erie in the northeastern part of the state, Akron grew from a canal town into the centre of the American rubber industry, earning the nickname "Rubber Capital of the World"; it remains a centre of polymer research.

History

Akron was laid out in 1825 by Simon Perkins and Paul Williams beside the under-construction Ohio and Erie Canal, at the summit (the highest point) of the canal between Lake Erie and the Ohio River — a position reflected in its name, from the Greek akron ("peak"). The canal brought rapid growth in milling and manufacturing; the later Pennsylvania and Ohio Canal and then railroads reinforced the city's role as a transport hub. In the nineteenth century Akron produced farm machinery (the birthplace of the mower and reaper industry) and hosted John Brown, who lived in the city for several years, and the abolitionist Sojourner Truth delivered her famous "Ain't I a Woman?" speech at a women's rights convention in Akron in 1851.

Rubber capital

In 1870 Benjamin Franklin Goodrich moved his small rubber company to Akron, attracted by its transport links and available capital. He was followed by the founders of Goodyear (1898, named for the inventor of vulcanization, Charles Goodyear), Firestone (1900), General Tire, and others. The automobile boom transformed Akron into one of America's fastest-growing cities — its population rose from about 69,000 in 1910 to over 255,000 by 1930 — drawing immigrant and migrant workers from Europe and the American South. Akron was also the centre of the rigid airship industry: Goodyear built the U.S. Navy airships Akron and Macon in the vast Akron Airdock, and the Goodyear blimps remain based in the area.

Tyre production largely left the city in the later twentieth century as the industry globalised, causing significant population decline. Akron reinvented itself around its polymer heritage: the University of Akron's College of Polymer Science and Polymer Engineering is among the world's leading such institutions, and the city hosts hundreds of polymer-related firms. Goodyear retains its global headquarters in Akron, and the city is home to the All-American Soap Box Derby, held there since 1935.

Geography and culture

Akron sits on the glaciated Allegheny Plateau, drained by the Cuyahoga and Little Cuyahoga rivers; the Cuyahoga Valley National Park lies between Akron and Cleveland to the north. Notable residents have included basketball player LeBron James, born in Akron in 1984, the rock band The Black Keys, singer Chrissie Hynde, and Alcoholics Anonymous co-founder Dr. Bob Smith, whose home is preserved as a museum.
