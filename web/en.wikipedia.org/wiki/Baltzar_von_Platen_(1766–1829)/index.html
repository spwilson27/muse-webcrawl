Count Baltzar Bogislaus von Platen (29 May 1766 – 6 December 1829) was a Swedish naval officer, statesman, and the principal promoter of the Göta Canal, which links Sweden's east and west coasts. He served as Governor-General of Norway from 1827 until his death.

Early life and naval career

Von Platen was born on the island of Rügen, then part of Swedish Pomerania, into a noble family of German origin. He entered the Swedish navy as a youth, was captured by the Russians during the war of 1788–1790, and rose to senior rank while studying canal engineering in England, where he met the Scottish engineer Thomas Telford.

The Göta Canal

Convinced that a waterway across Sweden would transform its trade and strategy, von Platen published a treatise on canals in 1806 and won parliamentary approval for the Göta Canal in 1810. As chairman of the canal company he directed construction from 1810 until his death, working with Telford. Tens of thousands of soldiers and labourers dug the 190-kilometre canal with its 58 locks; it was completed in 1832, three years after von Platen's death, and remains his chief monument. He also founded the engineering works at Motala Verkstad to supply the project.

Political career

Von Platen played a key role in the Swedish revolution of 1809 that deposed Gustav IV Adolf, helped negotiate the union with Norway in 1814, and was appointed Governor-General of Norway in 1827. His tenure in Christiania was contentious—he clashed with Norwegian opinion over the Constitution Day celebrations of 1829 (the "Battle of the Square")—and he died in office that December.
