Theodosius II (10 April 401 – 28 July 450), commonly known as Theodosius the Calligrapher, was the Eastern Roman emperor from 402 to 450. Proclaimed Augustus as an infant, he became sole emperor on the death of his father Arcadius in 408, and his reign — the longest of any Roman emperor — saw the construction of the Theodosian Walls of Constantinople, the promulgation of the Theodosian Code, and the foundation of the University of Constantinople.

Early reign and regency

For the first years of his sole reign, effective power lay with the praetorian prefect Anthemius, who oversaw the building of the great land walls that would defend Constantinople for a thousand years. From about 414, Theodosius's elder sister, the Augusta Pulcheria, assumed a guiding role at court, vowing virginity and shaping the devout character of the imperial household.

Achievements

In 425 Theodosius founded the University of Constantinople (the Pandidakterion), an imperial institution of higher learning with chairs in grammar, rhetoric, law and philosophy in both Latin and Greek. In 438 his great legal commission published the Codex Theodosianus, a compilation of imperial constitutions from Constantine I onward, which became a principal source of late Roman law and influenced the later Code of Justinian.

Theodosius's reign was marked by theological controversy: the Council of Ephesus (431), summoned by the emperor, condemned Nestorius and affirmed the title Theotokos for the Virgin Mary, while a second council at Ephesus in 449 — the notorious "Robber Council" — provoked the schism resolved only after his death at Chalcedon.

Foreign policy and death

Relations with the West saw the marriage of Theodosius's daughter Licinia Eudoxia to the western emperor Valentinian III, and eastern expeditions against the Vandals in Africa. The gravest threat of the reign was Attila the Hun, whose empire extorted ever larger annual payments of gold from Constantinople and devastated the Balkans in the 440s.

Theodosius died on 28 July 450 from injuries suffered in a riding accident near Constantinople. Having no son, he was succeeded by Marcian, who married Pulcheria. Theodosius is venerated as a saint in the Eastern Orthodox Church.
