Nightfall (Asimov short story)

"Nightfall" is a science fiction short story by the American writer Isaac Asimov, first published in the September 1941 issue of Astounding Science Fiction. It was voted the best short science fiction story of all time in 1964 by the Science Fiction Writers of America. In 1990 Asimov expanded the story into a novel of the same name in collaboration with Robert Silverberg.

Background

Editor John W. Campbell suggested the story to the 21-year-old Asimov, quoting Ralph Waldo Emerson: "If the stars should appear one night in a thousand years, how would men believe and adore, and preserve for many generations the remembrance of the city of God." Campbell inverted the thought: what if men went mad instead? Asimov wrote the story in the spring of 1941 and was paid a bonus rate for it; it became his breakthrough work.

Plot summary

The planet Lagash orbits in a system of six suns, so that at least one sun is always in the sky and true darkness never occurs. Astronomers at Saro University calculate that an unknown moon will eclipse the one sun then visible, bringing total darkness — and a view of the stars — for the first time in 2,049 years. A religious cult, the Apostles of Flame, predicts the event as an apocalypse described in their Book of Revelations, and archaeological evidence suggests civilisation on Lagash collapses in fire every 2,049 years. As the eclipse begins and the stars appear, the population, unprepared for darkness and the overwhelming sight of the Galaxy, descends into panic and madness, burning their cities to create light — restarting the cycle.

Reception and legacy

"Nightfall" is among the most anthologised science fiction stories ever written. The Science Fiction Writers of America voted it the best SF short story published before 1965, and it anchors the first volume of The Science Fiction Hall of Fame. The 1990 novel expansion, written with Robert Silverberg, adds material before and after the events of the story. A film adaptation, Nightfall, was released in 1988 to poor reviews.
