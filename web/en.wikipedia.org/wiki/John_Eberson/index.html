John Eberson

John Eberson (1875–1964) was an Austrian-American architect best known as the father of the "atmospheric theatre" — opulent movie palaces whose auditoriums simulated outdoor courtyards under a starry sky.

Career

Born in Czernowitz, then in Austria-Hungary, Eberson emigrated to the United States in 1901 and settled in Chicago. His Majestic Theatre in Houston (1923) was the first fully atmospheric theatre. With his son Drew Eberson he designed more than a hundred theatres, plus hundreds more theatres and other buildings across America.

Atmospheric style

Atmospheric auditoriums featured Mediterranean or Moorish facades, twinkling star ceilings created with small lights, projected drifting clouds, and lush ornament at a fraction of the cost of earlier palaces. Surviving examples include the Tampa Theatre, the Majestic Theatre in San Antonio and the Louisville Palace.
