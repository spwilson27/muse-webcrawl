Threefin blenny

Threefin blennies
Family: Tripterygiidae
Genera: about 30
Species: about 200

Threefin or triplefin blennies are blenniiform fishes of the family Tripterygiidae, found in tropical and temperate waters of the Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian Oceans. There are around 200 species in about 30 genera. The name derives from the three-part dorsal fin characteristic of the family (two spinous sections and one soft-rayed section).

Description

Triplefins are small, slender, bottom-dwelling fish, most species reaching only 2–6 cm in length; the largest, such as the yellow-and-black triplefin of New Zealand, may reach about 25 cm. They typically lack scales or have very small ones, have a blunt head with steep profile, and are often cryptically coloured to blend with rock, coral, and algae, though breeding males of many species develop bright colours. Like other blennies, they perch on the substrate, propped on their pelvic fins, and dart short distances to feed.

Habitat and behaviour

Threefin blennies live in shallow coastal waters, rockpools, and coral and rocky reefs, usually at depths of less than 30 metres, where they feed on tiny crustaceans and other invertebrates. Males defend small territories; eggs are laid on algae or rock and guarded by the male until hatching. New Zealand is a particular hotspot of diversity, with many endemic species.
