The Christus is a 3.4-metre marble statue of the resurrected Jesus Christ by the Danish sculptor Bertel Thorvaldsen (1770–1844). Completed in the 1830s, it stands in the Church of Our Lady in Copenhagen, Denmark, flanked by Thorvaldsen's statues of the Twelve Apostles. It is among the most widely copied sculptures of Christ in the world.

[Image: Thorvaldsen's Christus in the Church of Our Lady, Copenhagen]

History

Thorvaldsen was commissioned to produce statues of Christ and the apostles for the rebuilt Church of Our Lady, which had been destroyed during the British bombardment of Copenhagen in 1807. The plaster model of the Christus was completed in Rome in 1821; the marble version was executed in Thorvaldsen's workshop and installed in 1838. Unlike the triumphant or crucified Christ types common earlier, Thorvaldsen depicted a gentle, welcoming figure with arms outstretched and lowered, head bowed — an image of invitation echoing the words of Matthew 11:28, "Come unto me."

Replicas and influence

Copies of the statue stand in churches, cemeteries and museums worldwide. A full-size replica is displayed at the visitors' centre on Temple Square in Salt Lake City, Utah, and the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints adopted the Christus as an official symbol in 2020. The figure's softened classicism made it one of the most recognisable Christian images of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.
