Paris-Saclay University (French: Université Paris-Saclay) is a public research university in France located on the Saclay plateau south of Paris. It was created as part of a national project to build a research-intensive university cluster on the plateau, bringing together faculties and institutes formerly associated with the University of Paris-Sud (Paris XI), together with grandes écoles and national research organisations.

The university officially replaced Paris-Sud University on 1 January 2020. Its constituent and associate members include CentraleSupélec, ENS Paris-Saclay, AgroParisTech, the Institut d'Optique Graduate School, and associated universities including the University of Versailles Saint-Quentin-en-Yvelines and the University of Évry. Research organisations such as the CNRS, CEA, INRAE, Inria and INSERM participate in its laboratories.

Research and rankings

Paris-Saclay is especially known for mathematics and physics. In the Academic Ranking of World Universities (Shanghai Ranking) it has placed in the global top 20, and its mathematics programme has ranked first in the world in subject rankings. Several Fields Medalists, including Hugo Duminil-Copin, have been affiliated with the university and its laboratories. Research strengths include particle physics, astrophysics, materials science, life sciences, computer science and climate science; the SOLEIL synchrotron is located nearby on the plateau.

Campus

The university is spread across the Saclay plateau (Orsay, Gif-sur-Yvette, Palaiseau and Saclay) and other sites including Sceaux, Cachan, Villejuif and Versailles. Development of the Paris-Saclay cluster is part of the Grand Paris project, served by the future Grand Paris Express metro lines.
