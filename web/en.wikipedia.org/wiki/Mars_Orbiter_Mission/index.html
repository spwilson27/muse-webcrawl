The Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM), unofficially known as Mangalyaan ("Mars-craft", from Sanskrit maṅgala, "Mars", and yāna, "craft/vehicle"), was a space probe orbiting Mars from 24 September 2014 to 2022. It was launched on 5 November 2013 by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and was India's first interplanetary mission.

Artist's impression of the Mars Orbiter Mission spacecraft at Mars.

History

ISRO announced the mission in August 2012; it was developed in a record 15 months. The spacecraft was launched aboard a PSLV-XL rocket from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota. Because the PSLV lacked the power to place the probe directly on a Mars trajectory, the spacecraft spent about a month in Earth orbit, raising its orbit in a series of manoeuvres before a trans-Mars injection on 30 November 2013.

On 24 September 2014, Mangalyaan entered Mars orbit, making India the first nation to succeed on its maiden attempt to reach Mars, and ISRO the fourth space agency to reach Mars orbit, after Roscosmos, NASA, and the European Space Agency. The total cost, about US$74 million, made it the least-expensive Mars mission to date — frequently noted as costing less than the Hollywood film Gravity.

Payload

The orbiter carried five scientific instruments (15 kg total): the Mars Colour Camera (MCC), which returned over 1,000 full-disc images of Mars; the Methane Sensor for Mars (MSM); the Mars Exospheric Neutral Composition Analyser (MENCA); the Thermal Infrared Imaging Spectrometer (TIS); and the Lyman Alpha Photometer (LAP).

End of mission

Designed for a six-month life, the spacecraft operated for about eight years. In April 2022, following a long eclipse period, communication was lost and the spacecraft's propellant was deemed exhausted; ISRO formally declared the mission ended in October 2022. A follow-up mission, Mars Orbiter Mission 2 (Mangalyaan-2), has been under study.
