Dodge City, Kansas

Dodge City is a city in and the county seat of Ford County, Kansas, United States. As of the 2020 census, the population of the city was 27,788. It is named after Colonel Richard Irving Dodge. The city is known in American culture for its history as a wild frontier town of the Old West.

History

In 1847, Fort Mann was built in present-day Dodge City to provide protection for travellers on the Santa Fe Trail. Fort Dodge was established in 1865. In 1871 Henry J. Sitler built a sod house west of Fort Dodge, and in 1872 the town site was laid out just as the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway reached the area. Dodge City quickly became a trade centre for cattle drives from Texas along the Chisholm Trail and later the Great Western Trail.

Lawmen such as Bat Masterson, Wyatt Earp, Doc Holliday and the Dodge City Peace Commission were associated with the town, and the phrase "get out of Dodge" — referring to Dodge City as a place of lawlessness — entered American slang, reinforced by the radio and television series Gunsmoke, set in Dodge City.

Geography

Dodge City lies on the Arkansas River in the High Plains region of southwestern Kansas, about 151 miles (243 km) west of Wichita.

Economy

Meat packing is the primary industry; the city is home to large beef processing plants operated by Cargill and National Beef. Agriculture, wind energy and tourism related to its Western heritage are also important.
