John Dickson Carr (30 November 1906 – 27 February 1977) was an American author of detective stories, widely regarded as the master of the locked-room mystery — the "impossible crime" in which a murder appears to have been committed under impossible circumstances. Writing also as Carter Dickson, he created two celebrated detectives, Dr Gideon Fell and Sir Henry Merrivale, and was one of the few Americans admitted to London's Detection Club.

Life

Carr was born in Uniontown, Pennsylvania, and educated at Haverford College. A lifelong Anglophile fascinated by the seventeenth century and by tales of mystery and adventure, he moved to England in the 1930s and lived there for many years, marrying an Englishwoman, Clarice Cleaves. He wrote propaganda and radio drama for the BBC during the Second World War and later returned to the United States, settling in Greenville, South Carolina, where he died in 1977.

Works

Carr's first detective, the Parisian Henri Bencolin, debuted in It Walks By Night (1930). His best-known creation is the immense, Falstaffian lexicographer Dr Gideon Fell, modelled on G. K. Chesterton, introduced in Hag's Nook (1933). As Carter Dickson he wrote the Sir Henry Merrivale series beginning with The Plague Court Murders (1934).

The Three Coffins (1935; U.S. title The Hollow Man) is frequently voted the greatest locked-room mystery ever written; it contains Fell's famous "Locked-Room Lecture," a taxonomy of impossible crimes. Other classics include The Burning Court (1937), The Judas Window (1938), The Crooked Hinge (1938) and He Who Whispers (1946). Carr also wrote fine historical mysteries such as The Devil in Velvet (1951) and Fire, Burn! (1957), and a biography of Arthur Conan Doyle (1949).

Style and reputation

Carr combined airtight puzzle-plotting with atmosphere — Gothic country houses, apparent witchcraft and supernatural dread resolving into ingenious rational solutions. While his characterisation is often subordinated to the puzzle, connoisseurs of the Golden Age of detective fiction place him beside Agatha Christie and Ellery Queen; in 1970 the Mystery Writers of America gave him its Grand Master Award.
