The Ellipse viewed from the Washington Monument, with the White House.

The Ellipse is a 52-acre (21 ha) parklike area in Washington, D.C., located south of the White House fence and north of Constitution Avenue and the National Mall. Properly known as President's Park South, it is part of President's Park and is administered by the National Park Service. The park is ellipse-shaped, and a circular drive surrounds the open lawn.

History

The ground was set aside in Pierre L'Enfant's 1791 plan for the city. During the 19th century it was used for encampments, markets, and recreation; Union troops camped there during the Civil War. The ellipse shape was graded in the 1870s and the grounds landscaped in the following decades. Memorials around the park include the Zero Milestone (1923), the Boy Scout Memorial, the First Division Monument, and the Second Division Memorial.

Events

The Ellipse hosts the National Christmas Tree and the Pageant of Peace each December, a tradition begun in 1923 under President Calvin Coolidge. It is also a traditional site for demonstrations, rallies, and public gatherings, given its location directly south of the White House, and serves as the staging area for the annual National Cherry Blossom Festival parade nearby.
