Al-Kahf

Al-Kahf (Arabic: الكهف)
Classification: Meccan
Number: 18th surah
Verses: 110
Position: Juz' 15–16

Al-Kahf (Arabic: الكهف, al-kahf; meaning: "The Cave") is the 18th chapter (surah) of the Qur'an with 110 verses (ayat). A Meccan sura, it takes its name from the story of the Companions of the Cave (the Sleepers of Ephesus) told in verses 9–26. It is traditionally recited on Fridays, and a well-known hadith holds that whoever recites it on Friday will be protected from the Dajjal (the Antichrist figure in Islamic eschatology).

Principal narratives

The chapter contains four principal stories, each traditionally read as a trial: the Companions of the Cave, youths who fled persecution and slept in a cave for centuries (the trial of faith); the owner of two gardens, a rich man who grew arrogant and lost his wealth (the trial of wealth); Moses and al-Khidr, in which Moses learns patience before hidden wisdom (the trial of knowledge); and Dhu al-Qarnayn, the powerful ruler who built a barrier against Gog and Magog (the trial of power).

Themes

Al-Kahf addresses the transience of worldly life, the dangers of arrogance and attachment to wealth, and the difference between outward appearance and inner reality. Its central parable of the two gardens contrasts gratitude and humility with pride and disbelief.
