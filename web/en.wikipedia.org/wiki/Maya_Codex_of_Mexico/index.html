Maya Codex of Mexico

The Maya Codex of Mexico (Códice Maya de México), formerly known as the Grolier Codex, is a pre-Columbian Maya book and the oldest surviving book written in the Americas. It is an astronomical almanac centred on the movements of Venus, painted on bark paper (amate) in the early 13th century, around 1119–1221 CE by radiocarbon dating.

History

The codex reportedly surfaced in the 1960s, said to have been looted from a cave in Chiapas, Mexico, together with other objects. It was exhibited at the Grolier Club in New York in 1971 (hence its former name) and published by Michael Coe. Because it appeared on the antiquities market without provenance, many scholars long suspected forgery. The collector Josué Sáenz returned it to Mexico, where it is held by the Museo Nacional de Antropología and the Biblioteca Nacional de Antropología e Historia.

Authentication

A comprehensive re-examination led by Mexico's Instituto Nacional de Antropología e Historia concluded in 2018 that the codex is authentic: radiocarbon dating of the paper, analysis of pigments (including Maya blue and cochineal), the bark-paper manufacture, iconography and glyph style all proved consistent with a genuine pre-Hispanic work, and inconsistent with 20th-century materials and knowledge. It is thus the fourth known surviving Maya codex, alongside the Dresden, Madrid and Paris codices — and the oldest of them.

Contents

Only eleven pages survive of an original book of about twenty. The pages present Venus as the morning star, depicted as a deity figure seizing victims, with day-sign columns and numbers used to track the planet's 584-day synodic cycle — dangerous moments requiring ritual attention.
