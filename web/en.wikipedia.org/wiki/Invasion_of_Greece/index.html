Invasion of Greece


Date: 6–30 April 1941
Part of: Balkans campaign, Second World War

The invasion of Greece, also known as the Battle of Greece or Operation Marita, was the attack on Allied Greece by Italy and Germany during World War II. The Italian invasion in October 1940, which started the Greco-Italian War, was followed by the German invasion in April 1941. German landings on Crete (May 1941) came after Allied forces had been largely defeated in mainland Greece.

Background

Italy attacked Greece from Albania on 28 October 1940. Greek forces counter-attacked and drove the Italians back, prompting Hitler to intervene to secure Germany's southern flank before the invasion of the Soviet Union and to rescue his ally. On 6 April 1941 Germany simultaneously invaded Greece and Yugoslavia.

Course

The German Twelfth Army under Wilhelm List attacked through Bulgaria, outflanking the Metaxas Line and capturing Thessaloniki; Yugoslavia's collapse opened the Monastir gap, cutting off Greek forces in Albania. Athens fell on 27 April. A British and Commonwealth expeditionary force (W Force) and Greek troops were evacuated by sea, many to Crete, where the German airborne Battle of Crete followed in May.

Aftermath

Greece was partitioned into German, Italian, and Bulgarian occupation zones and suffered a harsh occupation and famine. The campaign's effect on the timing of Operation Barbarossa remains debated by historians.
