Front man

A front man (or frontwoman) is the member of a musical group who is the public face of the band and typically its lead singer. The front man is usually the member who interacts with the audience between songs, anchors live performances, and receives the most media attention.

Role

In rock and pop groups the role is usually filled by the lead vocalist, who may or may not play an instrument. Well-known front men are often cited for stagecraft and charisma as much as for musicianship; examples frequently given include Freddie Mercury of Queen, Mick Jagger of the Rolling Stones, and Robert Plant of Led Zeppelin. The term is also used more broadly for a figurehead: a person who represents an organization publicly while others exercise actual control, and in organized crime for someone who fronts a business used to conceal illegal activity.

Terminology

"Front man" is related to but distinct from "lead singer": a band may have a lead singer who is not its public leader, and some front men (such as Sid Vicious-era punk personalities or hype men in hip hop groups) contribute little lead singing. In hip hop, the equivalent energizing role is sometimes performed by a dedicated hype man.
