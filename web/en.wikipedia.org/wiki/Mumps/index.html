Mumps

Mumps
Specialty: Infectious disease
Cause: Mumps virus (MuV)
Symptoms: Swollen parotid glands, fever, headache, muscle aches
Prevention: MMR vaccine

Mumps is a viral disease caused by the mumps virus (MuV), a paramyxovirus. Its hallmark is painful swelling of the parotid salivary glands, giving the classic "hamster face" appearance, typically accompanied by fever, headache, and malaise after an incubation period of about 16–18 days.

Signs and complications

Up to a third of infections are asymptomatic. Most cases resolve within two weeks, but complications can include orchitis (inflammation of the testes) in post-pubertal males, oophoritis, pancreatitis, meningitis, encephalitis, and, rarely, permanent sensorineural deafness.

Prevention and treatment

There is no specific antiviral treatment; care is supportive. Mumps is vaccine-preventable: the live attenuated vaccine, developed by Maurice Hilleman in the 1960s from virus isolated from his daughter Jeryl Lynn, is given as part of the MMR vaccine (measles, mumps, rubella). Vaccination reduced incidence dramatically, though outbreaks still occur in close-contact settings such as universities, partly through waning immunity.
