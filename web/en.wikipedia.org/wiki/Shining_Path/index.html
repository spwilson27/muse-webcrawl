The Shining Path (Spanish: Sendero Luminoso), officially the Communist Party of Peru – Shining Path (PCP-SL), is a Maoist guerrilla movement in Peru. Founded around the university lecturer Abimael Guzmán, it launched a "protracted people's war" in 1980 that plunged Peru into an internal conflict lasting two decades. Its name derives from a phrase of the Peruvian Marxist José Carlos Mariátegui, whose ideas the movement claimed to follow along the "shining path" of Marxism.

Origins

Guzmán, a philosophy professor at the University of Huamanga in Ayacucho, built a faction of the Peruvian communist movement during the 1970s, recruiting among students and Quechua-speaking peasant communities in one of Peru's poorest regions. Rejecting electoral politics, the party prepared for armed struggle on the Maoist model of surrounding the cities from the countryside.

The internal conflict

The insurgency began on 17 May 1980 with the burning of ballot boxes in the village of Chuschi on the eve of Peru's first elections after military rule. Through the 1980s the Shining Path expanded its control in the countryside, assassinating local officials, landowners and rival leftists, and enforcing a harsh order in areas it controlled. The state's counter-insurgency, and abuses by the armed forces as well as the guerrillas, fell heavily on indigenous peasants. Peru's Truth and Reconciliation Commission later estimated some 69,000 deaths in the conflict, attributing the largest share to the Shining Path.

By the early 1990s the movement had brought its campaign into Lima with car bombings and assassinations, and the government of Alberto Fujimori waged an increasingly authoritarian counter-terror campaign. Guzmán was captured in Lima on 12 September 1992 by a police intelligence unit (GEIN) that had tracked him through surveillance of his associates; his arrest, followed by his televised appearance in a cage and his later call from prison for peace talks, shattered the organisation's leadership.

Remnants

Small remnant factions rejected the peace call and persisted in remote coca-growing regions, particularly the VRAEM (Valley of the Apurímac, Ene and Mantaro Rivers), where they became entangled with the cocaine trade and continued sporadic attacks on security forces. Guzmán died in prison in 2021.
