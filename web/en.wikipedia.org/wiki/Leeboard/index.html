Leeboard

A leeboard is a form of pivoting keel used by a sailboat largely and very often in lieu of a fixed keel. Typically mounted in pairs on each side of the hull, leeboards function like a centreboard, allowing shallow-draft vessels to sail upwind efficiently. Since only one leeboard is used at a time — the leeward one — the windward board can be raised, reducing drag.

History

Leeboards were used by Chinese junks for many centuries and were adopted in Europe by the Dutch in the 16th and 17th centuries, becoming characteristic of Dutch barges, spritsail barges on the Thames, and other flat-bottomed working craft that needed to navigate shallow coastal waters and canals while still sailing to windward.

Operation

Each board is mounted on a pivot bolt at the bow end and can be lowered or raised with a tackle. When tacking, the crew raises the old leeward board and lowers the new one. Because the board is on the outside of the hull, it does not intrude into the cargo space, an important advantage for working vessels, and it cannot jam with mud or stones in the way a centreboard in a case can.

Modern use

Leeboards remain in use on traditional sailing barges and on some modern small cruising boats and proas designed for very shallow water, where their simplicity and unobstructed interior are valued.
