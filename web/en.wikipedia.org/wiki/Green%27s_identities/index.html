Green's identities

In mathematics, Green's identities are a set of three identities in vector calculus relating the bulk with the boundary of a region on which differential operators are defined. They are named after the mathematician George Green, who discovered Green's theorem, and constitute special cases of the more general divergence theorem.

Green's first identity

The first identity is derived by applying the divergence theorem to the vector field F = ψ∇φ and states that the integral over a region U of (φ∇²ψ + ∇φ·∇ψ) equals the boundary integral of φ(∂ψ/∂n). It is essentially the higher-dimensional analogue of integration by parts, and is used extensively in the theory of partial differential equations, for example in proving the uniqueness of solutions to the Dirichlet problem.

Green's second identity

If φ and ψ are both twice continuously differentiable, the second identity relates the integral of (φ∇²ψ − ψ∇²φ) over U to an integral over the boundary involving the normal derivatives of both functions. It underlies Green's function methods and representation formulae for the Laplace and Helmholtz equations.

Green's third identity

The third identity is obtained by choosing φ to be a fundamental solution of Laplace's equation; it expresses the value of a harmonic function inside a region in terms of its values and normal derivative on the boundary — the starting point of boundary integral equation methods.
