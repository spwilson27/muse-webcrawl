Battle of Pliska

The Battle of Pliska (or Battle of the Vărbitsa Pass) on 26 July 811 was one of the worst defeats in Byzantine history. Khan Krum's Bulgarians ambushed and annihilated the invading army of Emperor Nikephoros I, who was killed — the first emperor to die in battle against a foreign enemy since Valens in 378. Krum famously had Nikephoros's skull lined with silver and used as a drinking cup.

Background

After Krum captured Serdica in 809, Nikephoros invaded in force in 811, brushed aside resistance, and sacked and burned the Bulgarian capital Pliska, refusing Krum's offers of peace.

Battle

On the march home through the Balkan passes, the Byzantines entered the Vărbitsa Pass, whose exits the Bulgarians blocked with wooden walls and ditches. Trapped in the defile, the army was attacked at dawn and destroyed piecemeal; Nikephoros was killed and his wounded son and successor Staurakios barely escaped, dying months later.

Aftermath

The disaster opened the road for Krum's counter-invasions, including victory at Versinikia in 813 and preparations to attack Constantinople, halted only by his sudden death in 814. It secured Bulgaria as a great power in the Balkans.
