The Brocken Railway (German: Brockenbahn) is a metre-gauge railway in the Harz mountains of Germany, forming part of the Harz Narrow Gauge Railways (Harzer Schmalspurbahnen, HSB) network. It runs from Drei Annen Hohne, on the Harz Railway between Wernigerode and Nordhausen, via Schierke to the summit of the Brocken (1,141 m), the highest peak in northern Germany. Trains are predominantly steam-hauled throughout the year, and the line is one of the most popular heritage railway operations in Germany.

History

The line was built by the Nordhausen-Wernigerode Railway Company (Nordhausen-Wernigeroder Eisenbahn-Gesellschaft) and opened in stages: the section from Drei Annen Hohne to Schierke opened on 20 June 1898, and trains first reached the Brocken summit on 27 March 1899. The railway carried tourists to the celebrated mountain — famous for its harsh weather, the Brocken spectre phenomenon and its literary associations with Goethe's Faust (the Walpurgis Night scene) — and took over supplying the summit's hotels and weather station.

After the Second World War the line lay in the Soviet occupation zone and then East Germany. The summit became a restricted military area, occupied by Soviet and East German listening posts watching the nearby inner German border; public trains were withdrawn in August 1961, when the border fortifications were built, although freight trains continued to supply the garrisons. Following German reunification the line was renovated and reopened to passengers on 15 September 1991, and in 1993 operations passed to the newly formed HSB, owned by local authorities along the line.

Route and operation

From Drei Annen Hohne (543 m) the railway climbs steadily through forest to Schierke (687 m), then ascends in long loops around the mountain, passing Goetheweg station, named for the path Goethe took to the summit in 1777, before reaching Brocken station at 1,125 m — the highest narrow-gauge station in Germany. Services run year-round, with steam locomotives (typically the 2-10-2 tank engines of class 99.23–24) hauling most trains; in severe winter weather, with the exposed summit section subject to storms and deep snow, trains may terminate at Schierke.
