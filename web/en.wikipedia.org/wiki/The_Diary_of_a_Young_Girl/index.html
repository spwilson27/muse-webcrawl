The Diary of a Young Girl

The Diary of a Young Girl, often published in English as The Diary of Anne Frank, is a book drawn from the wartime diary kept by the German-born Dutch Jewish teenager Anne Frank while she was in hiding with her family in Amsterdam during the Nazi occupation of the Netherlands. She received the diary for her thirteenth birthday in June 1942 and wrote in it until the hiding place was raided in August 1944.

Contents

The diary, addressed largely to an imaginary friend "Kitty," records life in the Secret Annex behind her father's business premises, where the Frank family and four others hid for just over two years. It combines candid accounts of adolescent life — quarrels with her mother, a budding romance with Peter van Pels, ambitions to be a writer — with reflections on fear, identity and humanity under persecution. After hearing a 1944 radio appeal for the preservation of wartime documents, Anne began revising the diary for publication.

Publication and significance

Anne died in the Bergen-Belsen concentration camp in early 1945. Her father Otto Frank, the annex's sole survivor, was given the diary by Miep Gies and others who had preserved it; he edited and published it in Dutch in 1947 as Het Achterhuis ("The House Behind"). The English translation followed in 1952. One of the most widely read books in the world, it has been translated into more than seventy languages and adapted for stage and screen; the 1995 "definitive edition" restored passages Otto had omitted. The diary is a central document of Holocaust literature and testimony.
