Isabella Augusta, Lady Gregory (née Persse; 15 March 1852 – 22 May 1932) was an Anglo-Irish dramatist, folklorist and theatre manager. With William Butler Yeats and Edward Martyn, she co-founded the Irish Literary Theatre and the Abbey Theatre, and wrote numerous short works for both companies. Lady Gregory produced a number of books of retellings of tales from Irish mythology, and her home at Coole Park in County Galway served as a focal point for the Irish Literary Revival.

Lady Gregory at Coole Park.

Life and work

Born into the landed Persse family, she married Sir William Henry Gregory, a former governor of Ceylon, in 1880. Widowed in 1892, she turned increasingly to writing. Her meeting with Yeats in the 1890s drew her into the revival movement; at Coole Park she hosted writers including Yeats, Synge, Seán O'Casey and George Bernard Shaw.

Her retellings Cuchulain of Muirthemne (1902) and Gods and Fighting Men (1904) made Irish heroic legend accessible in the "Kiltartan" dialect of her tenants. As a playwright she specialised in one-act comedies such as Spreading the News (1904) and The Rising of the Moon (1907). She also campaigned to keep the Lane collection of paintings in Dublin. Lady Gregory died of cancer in 1932.
