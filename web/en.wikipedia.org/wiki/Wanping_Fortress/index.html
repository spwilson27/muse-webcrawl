Wanping Fortress

Wanping Fortress (Chinese: 宛平城), also known as Wanping City, is a Ming dynasty walled town in Fengtai District, Beijing, on the eastern bank of the Yongding River near the Lugou Bridge (Marco Polo Bridge). It is historically significant as the site of the Marco Polo Bridge Incident of 7 July 1937, the clash that opened the full-scale Second Sino-Japanese War.

History

The fortress was constructed between 1638 and 1640, near the end of the Ming dynasty, to guard the western approach to Beijing and the strategic crossing of the Yongding River at the Lugou Bridge. Unusually, it was built from the start as a purely military fortress rather than a county town, with two gates, strong walls, and a garrison. During the Qing dynasty the town declined somewhat but retained its military role.

The 1937 incident

On the night of 7 July 1937, Japanese troops conducting exercises near the Lugou Bridge exchanged fire with the Chinese garrison of Wanping. Japanese forces then shelled the town, leaving shell marks that are still visible on its walls. The incident escalated into the full-scale invasion of China and the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945).

Today

Wanping is one of the best-preserved Ming fortress towns in China. Its main street retains historic shops and residences, and the town houses the Museum of the War of Chinese People's Resistance Against Japanese Aggression, opened in 1987. The fortress walls and gates are protected as a cultural heritage site.
