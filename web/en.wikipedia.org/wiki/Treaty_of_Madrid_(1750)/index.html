Treaty of Madrid (1750)

The Treaty of Madrid was an agreement concluded on 13 January 1750 between Spain and Portugal settling the boundaries of their colonies in South America, superseding the medieval Treaty of Tordesillas.

Terms

Instead of the papal meridian of 1494, which Portugal had long since overrun with its Brazilian settlements, the treaty adopted the principle of uti possidetis—possession on the ground. Portugal kept its extensive holdings in the Amazon basin and the interior of Brazil, while Spain's position in the Río de la Plata was adjusted: Portugal ceded Colónia do Sacramento on the Plata estuary to Spain, and in exchange Spain ceded the Seven Missions (Siete Pueblos de las Misiones), Jesuit mission territory east of the Uruguay River, to Portugal.

Guaraní War

The transfer of the Seven Missions required some 30,000 Guaraní inhabitants to abandon their towns and move west of the Uruguay. Their resistance, led by Sepé Tiaraju, produced the Guaraní War (1754–56), in which Spanish and Portuguese forces jointly crushed the missions' militia. The human cost and controversy contributed to the treaty's annulment by the Treaty of El Pardo (1761), though subsequent settlements—the Treaty of San Ildefonso (1777)—largely restored the Madrid boundaries, which broadly foreshadow modern Brazil's borders.
