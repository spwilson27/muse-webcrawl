Precipitation

In meteorology, precipitation is any product of the condensation of atmospheric water vapour that falls from clouds due to gravitational pull. The main forms of precipitation include drizzle, rain, sleet, snow, ice pellets, graupel, and hail. Precipitation occurs when a portion of the atmosphere becomes saturated with water vapour (reaching 100% relative humidity) so that water condenses and "precipitates" or falls. It is a major component of the water cycle and delivers most of the fresh water on the planet: on average, about 505,000 km³ of water falls as precipitation each year, the great majority over the oceans.

Formation

Precipitation forms within clouds when tiny droplets or ice crystals grow large enough for gravity to overcome updraughts. In warm clouds, droplets grow by collision and coalescence; in cold clouds, the Bergeron process dominates — because saturation vapour pressure over ice is lower than over supercooled water, ice crystals grow at the expense of droplets until they fall as snow or melt into rain. Air is lifted and cooled to saturation by frontal systems, convection, orographic lifting over mountains, and convergence in low-pressure systems.

Types and classification

Rain consists of drops larger than 0.5 mm; smaller drops are drizzle. Freezing rain supercools and freezes on contact; sleet (ice pellets) refreezes before reaching the ground; snow falls as aggregated ice crystals; hail forms in strong thunderstorm updraughts as layered balls of ice. Precipitation is also classified by the mechanism that generates it — stratiform, convective, cyclonic, or orographic — which strongly affects its intensity and distribution.

Measurement and distribution

Amounts are measured as the depth of liquid water in a gauge (millimetres, or inches), with snowfall converted to its water equivalent. Globally, precipitation is concentrated in the equatorial convergence zone and mid-latitude storm tracks, while subtropical highs maintain the great deserts. The wettest inhabited place on Earth, Mawsynram in India, averages about 11,870 mm per year, while parts of Chile's Atacama Desert average near 1 mm.
