Viktor Lvovich Korchnoi (23 March 1931 – 6 June 2016) was a Soviet (until 1976) and Swiss (from 1994) chess grandmaster and author. He is considered one of the strongest players never to have become World Chess Champion, contesting two championship matches against Anatoly Karpov, in 1978 and 1981. Nicknamed "Viktor the Terrible", he was known for his fierce counterattacking style, longevity, and a prickly, determined temperament.

Career

Korchnoi learned chess as a child in Leningrad, surviving the siege of the city during World War II. He won the USSR Chess Championship four times (1960, 1962, 1964, 1970) and was a member of six Soviet teams that won the Chess Olympiad. In Candidates competition he was a perennial contender, defeating players such as Tigran Petrosian, Boris Spassky and Robert Hübner in matches.

Defection and world championship matches

In 1976 Korchnoi defected to the West while playing in Amsterdam, an act that made him a prominent political figure and led to a Soviet boycott of events in which he played. As a stateless challenger he contested the 1978 World Championship in Baguio, Philippines — a bitter, controversy-filled match he lost 6–5 to Karpov — and the 1981 match in Merano, which he lost more decisively. His family remained in the Soviet Union for several years, and his son was imprisoned for refusing military service. Korchnoi later settled in Switzerland and represented it in Olympiads into his eighties, remaining an active competitor at a remarkable age.
