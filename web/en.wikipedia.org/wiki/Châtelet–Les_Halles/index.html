Châtelet–Les Halles (French pronunciation: [ʃɑtlɛ le al]) is a major train station in the centre of Paris, France, and one of the largest underground stations in the world. It is served by three RER commuter lines (A, B and D) and connects by underground passages to the Châtelet station of the Paris Métro (lines 1, 4, 7, 11 and 14), forming a combined complex beneath the Forum des Halles shopping centre.

History

The station was built on the site of the demolished Les Halles central food market, whose wholesale functions moved to Rungis in 1971. It opened on 9 December 1977 with the inauguration of the central section of RER A and the extension of RER B, realising the long-planned crossing of Paris by regional express lines beneath the historic centre.

Layout

The RER station lies deep below the Forum des Halles, with seven tracks in a single vast underground train hall. Its numerous corridors and connections — the walk to the far Métro lines at Châtelet can take fifteen minutes — are both praised as the beating heart of the Île-de-France transport network and criticised for their complexity. A major renovation in the 2010s, part of the redevelopment of the Halles district with its distinctive canopy (La Canopée), improved access, light and signage.

Usage

The Châtelet–Les Halles complex handles on the order of three-quarters of a million passengers a day, making it one of Europe's busiest underground interchanges and the central hub from which every part of the Paris region can be reached.
