Water tank

A water tank is a container for storing water for later use — drinking, irrigation, fire suppression, chemical processing, food preparation and many other purposes. Tanks range from small household cisterns of a few hundred litres to municipal reservoirs holding millions of litres.

Types

Elevated tanks (water towers) store water at height so that gravity provides pressure to a distribution system. Ground-level tanks may be made of steel, concrete, fibreglass or plastic, and are often paired with pumps. Flexible "bladder" tanks, underground cisterns and rooftop tanks serve buildings from farms to skyscrapers; in much of South Asia and Africa, plastic rooftop and ground tanks are a standard household defence against intermittent supply.

Design considerations

Materials must not leach contaminants into potable water; tanks for drinking water are covered to exclude light, animals and debris, and are periodically cleaned to control biofilm and sediment. Thermal insulation prevents freezing or excessive heating, and overflow and vent arrangements protect against pressure damage.
