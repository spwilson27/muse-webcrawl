The Lamar Hunt U.S. Open Cup, commonly known as the U.S. Open Cup (USOC), is a knockout cup competition in men's soccer in the United States. Run by the United States Soccer Federation, it is the oldest ongoing national soccer competition in the country, first held in 1913–14 as the National Challenge Cup.

Format and history

The tournament is open to all affiliated amateur and professional clubs in the U.S. soccer pyramid, from amateur leagues through Major League Soccer (MLS), giving it a rare "David vs. Goliath" character. The winner receives the Dewar Cup trophy, prize money, and a berth in the CONCACAF Champions Cup. In 1999 the competition was renamed in honour of Lamar Hunt, a founding investor of MLS and lifelong promoter of American soccer. Bethlehem Steel and Maccabee Los Angeles share the record with five titles; MLS clubs have dominated since the league's founding in 1996. The 1999 Rochester Rhinos remain the only non-MLS team to win in the MLS era.
