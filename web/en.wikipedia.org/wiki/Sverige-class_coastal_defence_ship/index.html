The Sverige class was a class of three coastal defence ships built for the Swedish Navy in the 1910s and early 1920s: HSwMS Sverige, HSwMS Drottning Victoria and HSwMS Gustav V. They were the largest and most powerful armoured ships ever operated by Sweden, and formed the core of the Swedish fleet through both World Wars.

Design

The ships displaced roughly 7,000 tonnes and were armed with four 283 mm (11.1 in) guns in two twin turrets and a secondary battery of eight 152 mm guns, an unusually heavy armament for their size, made possible by accepting modest speed (about 22–23 knots) and limited endurance, appropriate for operations in the Baltic Sea and among Sweden's coastal archipelagos.

History

The lead ship was funded after a famous public fundraising campaign, the Pansarbåtsinsamlingen, which raised money from the Swedish public for a new coastal defence ship in 1912. The three ships were modernized between the wars, receiving improved anti-aircraft armament and fire control; Gustav V remained in service as a training and harbour ship until 1970, and the class was finally struck in the 1970s.
