The IEEE Medal of Honor, the highest award of the IEEE.

The IEEE Medal of Honor is the highest recognition of the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE). Awarded annually since 1917, it honours an exceptional contribution or an extraordinary career in the IEEE fields of interest. The award consists of a gold medal, a bronze replica, a certificate, and a cash prize, and is presented at the IEEE Honors Ceremony.

History

The medal was established in 1917 as the Medal of Honor of the Institute of Radio Engineers (IRE), one of the IEEE's predecessor societies. Its first recipient was Edwin Howard Armstrong, inventor of the regenerative circuit and, later, of frequency modulation. When the IRE merged with the American Institute of Electrical Engineers in 1963 to form the IEEE, the award became the IEEE Medal of Honor, traditionally dated from the 1917 IRE award.

Recipients

Recipients span the foundational figures of electrical engineering, radio, information theory, and computing. They include Ernst Alexanderson (1919), Guglielmo Marconi (1920), Lee de Forest (1922), John R. Pierce (1963), Claude Shannon — honoured in 1966 for information theory — Charles K. Kao (1996, for fibre-optic communications), Robert Kahn (2024), and Mildred Dresselhaus (2015). In 2025 the prize accompanying the medal was increased to $2 million, endowing it as one of the most valuable engineering awards.
