Gamma Knife

Gamma Knife
Type: Stereotactic radiosurgery device
Manufacturer: Elekta
Inventor: Lars Leksell
Introduced: 1967 (first clinical unit, Stockholm)
Radiation source: Cobalt-60

Gamma Knife radiosurgery is a form of radiation therapy that uses precisely focused gamma rays to treat brain disorders. Despite its name, it involves no blade or incision: nearly 200 small beams of gamma radiation converge on a targeted area of tissue while sparing surrounding healthy brain.


A Gamma Knife unit

History

The technique was developed by Swedish neurosurgeon Lars Leksell, who defined stereotactic radiosurgery in 1951 and treated the first patient with the prototype Gamma Knife in Stockholm in 1967. Modern Leksell Gamma Knife units use 192 cobalt-60 sources arranged in a hemispherical array.

Applications

Gamma Knife treatment is used for benign and malignant brain tumours (including meningiomas, acoustic neuromas and metastases), arteriovenous malformations, and functional disorders such as trigeminal neuralgia. Treatment is usually delivered in a single outpatient session with the patient's head held in a stereotactic frame or mask.
