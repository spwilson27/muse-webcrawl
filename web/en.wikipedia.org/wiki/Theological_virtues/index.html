Theological virtues

Theological virtues
The three: Faith, Hope, Charity (Love)
Source: 1 Corinthians 13:13
Tradition: Christian theology
Contrasted with: the four cardinal virtues (prudence, justice, temperance, fortitude)
Together: the seven virtues

The theological virtues are the three virtues — faith, hope, and charity (love) — which Christian theology teaches are infused into the soul by God and directed toward God as their object. The name derives from Saint Paul's First Letter to the Corinthians: "And now these three remain: faith, hope and love. But the greatest of these is love" (1 Corinthians 13:13).

Distinction from the cardinal virtues

Whereas the four cardinal virtues (prudence, justice, temperance, and fortitude) can in principle be acquired by human effort and habit, the theological virtues are held to be gifts of grace that human beings cannot acquire by their own power. The medieval scholastics, above all Thomas Aquinas in the Summa Theologiae, systematised the distinction: the cardinal virtues perfect human faculties in relation to natural ends, while the theological virtues order the person toward the supernatural end of union with God.

The three virtues

Faith

Faith is the virtue by which the intellect assents to the truths revealed by God, on God's authority. In Catholic teaching it is the foundation of the spiritual life and is necessary, together with works of love, for salvation; Protestant traditions have classically stressed justification by faith.

Hope

Hope is the confident expectation of eternal life and of the graces needed to attain it, grounded not in one's own strength but in God's fidelity and mercy. Its opposite vices are traditionally despair and presumption.

Charity

Charity (Latin caritas, translating Greek agape) is love of God above all things and love of neighbour for God's sake. Paul calls it the greatest of the three, and scholastic theology describes it as the "form" of all the virtues, ordering them to their ultimate end. Charity is held to remain in the life to come, when faith gives way to sight and hope to possession.

In art

The theological virtues are frequently personified in Christian art as three women: Faith bearing a cross or chalice, Hope with an anchor, and Charity nursing or sheltering children. They appear throughout medieval and Renaissance sculpture, fresco, and funerary monuments, often paired against the cardinal virtues on the opposite side of a composition.
