The Sámi languages (/ˈsɑːmi/) are a group of Uralic languages spoken by the Sámi people in northern parts of Norway, Sweden, Finland, and the Kola Peninsula of Russia, a region collectively known as Sápmi. There are around ten living Sámi languages; they form a dialect continuum in which neighbouring varieties are mutually intelligible but the most distant ones are not.

Classification

The Sámi languages are divided into two main groups:

  • Western Sámi: Southern Sámi, Ume Sámi, Pite Sámi, Lule Sámi, and Northern Sámi.
  • Eastern Sámi: Inari Sámi, Skolt Sámi, Akkala Sámi (extinct since 2003), Kildin Sámi, and Ter Sámi.

Northern Sámi is by far the most widely spoken, with roughly 20,000 speakers, and is the language meant in most official contexts in Norway. At the other extreme, Ume and Pite Sámi each have only a few dozen fluent speakers and are critically endangered.

Status and recognition

The Sámi languages are recognised as official minority languages in Norway, Sweden and Finland, and Sámi language acts guarantee the right to use them in dealings with public authorities in designated administrative districts. Norway's Sámi Act of 1987 and the constitutions of Finland and Sweden provide varying degrees of protection. All Sámi languages are nevertheless considered endangered to some degree, a consequence of assimilation policies — including boarding-school systems in the 19th and 20th centuries that discouraged or forbade their use.

Writing systems

Most Sámi languages are written with extended Latin alphabets, whose special letters vary by language (for example á, č, đ, ŋ, š, ŧ, ž in Northern Sámi). Kildin Sámi, spoken in Russia, uses an extended Cyrillic alphabet. Standardised orthographies for most Western Sámi languages were established in the 20th century; the common Northern Sámi orthography dates to 1979.

Features

Sámi languages are agglutinative, with rich consonant gradation, a dual number in pronouns and verb conjugation alongside singular and plural, and no grammatical gender. Northern Sámi has seven cases. Vocabulary includes extensive terminology for snow, ice and reindeer husbandry, reflecting the traditional livelihoods of the Sámi.
