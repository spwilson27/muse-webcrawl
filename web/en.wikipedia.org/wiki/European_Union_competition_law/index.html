European Union competition law

European Union competition law is the system of antitrust and merger-control rules that promotes and maintains competition within the European single market. Its core provisions are Articles 101 and 102 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU).

Key provisions

Article 101 prohibits agreements between undertakings that restrict competition, such as cartels. Article 102 prohibits abuse of a dominant position by one or more undertakings. The EU Merger Regulation requires large concentrations with an EU dimension to be notified to and cleared by the European Commission. Articles 107–109 control state aid granted by member states.

Enforcement

The European Commission's Directorate-General for Competition investigates cartels, abuses of dominance and mergers, and can impose fines of up to 10% of worldwide turnover. National competition authorities and courts also enforce Articles 101 and 102 under Regulation 1/2003.

Notable cases

Landmark enforcement includes large fines against cartels and technology companies such as Google, and the development of the Digital Markets Act to regulate digital gatekeepers ex ante.
