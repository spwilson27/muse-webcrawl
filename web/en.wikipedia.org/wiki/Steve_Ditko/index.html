Steve Ditko

Stephen J. Ditko (November 2, 1927 – c. June 29, 2018) was an American comics artist best known as the co-creator, with Stan Lee, of the superheroes Spider-Man and Doctor Strange. His hallucinatory, surreal art style and his uncompromising philosophy made him one of the most distinctive and enigmatic figures in the medium.

Career at Marvel

Ditko studied under Jerry Robinson and began working in comics in the early 1950s, notably for Charlton Comics. At Atlas/Marvel he drew science-fiction and monster stories before being assigned, with writer-editor Stan Lee, to create a new teenage superhero. Spider-Man debuted in Amazing Fantasy #15 (1962); Ditko designed the costume, the web-shooters and the character's gangly, contorted visual identity, and plotted much of the series through issue #38 (1966). In 1963 he created Doctor Strange, whose psychedelic other-dimensional landscapes became his artistic signature. Creative and philosophical differences with Lee led Ditko to leave Marvel abruptly in 1966.

Later work and philosophy

At Charlton he created the superhero the Question and revived Captain Atom and Blue Beetle; at DC Comics he created the Creeper and Hawk and Dove. A committed adherent of Ayn Rand's Objectivism, Ditko expressed his moral absolutism most fully in his independent character Mr. A and in a long series of self-published works issued from his Manhattan studio. Famously reclusive, he refused almost all interviews and photographs for fifty years while continuing to produce new comics into his nineties. He died in New York in 2018, aged 90, and was posthumously recognised with induction into the industry's halls of fame, including the Eisner Awards Hall of Fame (1994) and designation as a Disney Legend (2024).
