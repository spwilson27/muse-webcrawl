A Stolperstein (German for "stumbling stone", plural Stolpersteine) is a commemorative brass plaque installed in the pavement in front of the last voluntarily chosen residence of a victim of Nazi persecution. The project was initiated by the German artist Gunter Demnig in 1992 and is considered the largest decentralised memorial in the world.

Description

Each Stolperstein is a concrete cube about 10 centimetres square, capped with a hand-inscribed brass plate. The inscription typically begins "Here lived…" followed by the victim's name, year of birth, and fate: deportation, murder, exile, or survival. The stones are set flush into the pavement or sidewalk outside the building where the person last lived by choice.

History and spread

Demnig installed the first stones in Cologne and Berlin in the 1990s, initially without permits. The project grew steadily through local sponsorships — a stone can be commissioned by relatives, schools, or local history groups. The 100,000th Stolperstein was installed in 2023, and stones can now be found in more than twenty European countries. Stolpersteine are also laid for Roma, Sinti, political prisoners, homosexuals, Jehovah's Witnesses, and other persecuted groups, as well as for survivors.

Reception

The project has been widely praised for restoring individual names and fates to everyday public space — Demnig cites the Talmudic saying that a person is only forgotten when their name is forgotten. It has also drawn some debate, including objections in a few cities to memorials placed underfoot.
