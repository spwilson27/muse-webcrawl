The Cathedral of Vasily the Blessed (Russian: Собор Василия Блаженного), commonly known as Saint Basil's Cathedral, is an Orthodox church in Red Square of Moscow, and is one of the most popular cultural symbols of Russia.

Construction

The building, now a museum, was built from 1555 to 1561 on orders from Ivan the Terrible to commemorate the capture of Kazan and Astrakhan. It is shaped like the flame of a bonfire rising into the sky, a design unique in Russian architecture, crowned by nine onion domes of vivid colours.

Architecture

The cathedral consists of nine chapels built around a central core, the Church of the Intercession. Its brightly coloured domes were added in the 1680s, and a popular legend holds that Ivan blinded the architects, Barma and Postnik, so they could never build anything more beautiful.

Later history

Confiscated by the Soviet state in 1928, the cathedral has operated as a division of the State Historical Museum. Together with the Kremlin and Red Square it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1990.
