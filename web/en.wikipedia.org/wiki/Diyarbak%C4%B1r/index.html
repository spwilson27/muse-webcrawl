Diyarbakır

Diyarbakır
Country: Turkey
Region: Southeastern Anatolia
Ancient name: Amida
Known for: Basalt city walls, Hevsel Gardens

Diyarbakır is a city in southeastern Turkey, situated on the banks of the Tigris River. Known in antiquity as Amida, it has been an important regional centre since the Hellenistic period and served as a capital of various Turkmen and Kurdish dynasties. The city is renowned for its massive black basalt walls, among the longest complete defensive walls in the world, and the Hevsel Gardens, inscribed with the fortress as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2015.

History

Amida was contested between Rome and Persia, and later passed through Arab, Seljuk and Ottoman hands. In the medieval period it was the capital of the Artuqid dynasty. The modern city is the largest in Turkey's predominantly Kurdish southeast and has been a focal point of the Kurdish–Turkish conflict.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Great Mosque of Diyarbakır, one of the oldest mosques in Anatolia, the Four-Footed Minaret, and the tenth-arch On Gözlü Bridge over the Tigris.
