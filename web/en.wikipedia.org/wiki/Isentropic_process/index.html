Isentropic process

An isentropic process is an idealized thermodynamic process that is both adiabatic and reversible. In such a process, the entropy of the system remains constant (ΔS = 0). The work transfers of the system are frictionless, and there is no net transfer of heat or matter.

Real processes involve friction, unrestrained expansion, and heat transfer, all of which increase entropy; the isentropic process is therefore an idealization used as a model and reference for real machines such as turbines, compressors, pumps, and nozzles. The concept of isentropic efficiency compares the performance of a real device with its isentropic ideal.

Isentropic relations

For a closed system undergoing an isentropic process with an ideal gas with constant specific heats, the temperature, pressure, and volume are related by power laws involving the heat capacity ratio k = cp/cv:

  • T2/T1 = (P2/P1)(k−1)/k
  • T2/T1 = (V1/V2)k−1
  • P2/P1 = (V1/V2)k

On a temperature–entropy (T-s) diagram, an isentropic process appears as a vertical line.

Applications

Isentropic analysis underlies the study of steady-flow devices in thermodynamics textbooks. Examples of approximately isentropic processes include pumping a liquid, compressing or expanding a gas in an insulated cylinder slowly and reversibly, and ideal gas flow through a well-designed nozzle.
