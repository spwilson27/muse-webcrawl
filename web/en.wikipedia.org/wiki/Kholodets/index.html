Kholodets

Kholodets (Russian: холодец), also called studen (студень), is a jellied meat dish of East Slavic cuisine, traditional in Russia, Ukraine and Belarus. It is a form of aspic made by slow-boiling meat rich in collagen — such as pork hocks, ears, tails or chicken — so that the broth sets into a jelly on cooling without any added gelatin. Kholodets is customarily served cold with horseradish or mustard, and is a staple of festive and New Year tables.

Preparation

Meat cuts high in connective tissue are simmered for many hours at a low temperature with onion, carrot, bay leaves and peppercorns. The cooked meat is picked from the bones, chopped or shredded, placed in moulds or dishes and covered with the strained broth, often with sliced garlic and sometimes a garnish of carrot or herbs; the dish is then chilled until set. A good kholodets is clear, firm but tender, and rich, with layers of meat suspended in the jelly.

Traditions

Because it keeps well in the cold and uses inexpensive cuts, kholodets was historically a winter and festive dish, prepared for Christmas, New Year and Easter feasts. Analogous jellied meats exist throughout Europe, including Polish galareta, Romanian piftie and German Sülze.
