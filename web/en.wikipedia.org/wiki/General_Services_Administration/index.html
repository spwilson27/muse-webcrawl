General Services Administration

The General Services Administration (GSA) is an independent agency of the United States government established in 1949 to help manage and support the basic functioning of federal agencies. GSA supplies products and communications for U.S. government offices, provides transportation and office space to federal employees, and supports government-wide cost-minimizing policies. It is often described as the government's landlord and purchasing agent.

History

President Harry S. Truman signed the Federal Property and Administrative Services Act in 1949, consolidating several wartime and postwar agencies into the new GSA. The agency took over functions including the management of federal buildings, stockpiling of strategic materials, and disposal of surplus property.

Organization and functions

GSA's principal components are the Public Buildings Service (PBS), which owns or leases more than 370 million square feet of workspace for over a million federal workers; the Federal Acquisition Service (FAS), which runs the GSA Schedule (Multiple Award Schedule) contracts, GSA Advantage!, and government-wide purchasing programs; and the Technology Transformation Services, home of teams such as 18F and Login.gov. GSA manages the federal vehicle fleet, fleet purchase cards, travel programs, and the .gov domain registry. The GSA Administrator also formally ascertains the apparent winner of presidential elections to release transition resources, a role that drew attention during the 2020 presidential transition.
