Continental drift is the hypothesis that the Earth's continents have moved over geological time relative to one another, so that they appear to have 'drifted' across the ocean bed. The German scientist Alfred Wegener published the idea in 1912, arguing that the continents had once formed a single supercontinent, Pangaea.

Evidence and reception

Wegener pointed to the matching coastlines of South America and Africa, identical fossil species such as Mesosaurus and Glossopteris on widely separated continents, and matching mountain ranges and glacial deposits. The hypothesis was largely rejected for decades because no plausible mechanism was known; it was vindicated in the 1960s when seafloor spreading and plate tectonics supplied the mechanism.
