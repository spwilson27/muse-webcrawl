In Greek mythology, Harmonia (Ancient Greek: Ἁρμονία, "harmony, agreement") is the immortal goddess of harmony and concord. She was in most accounts the daughter of Ares and Aphrodite, uniting in herself the opposite natures of war and love, and in Samothracian tradition a daughter of Zeus and the Pleiad Electra.

Marriage to Cadmus

Harmonia married Cadmus, the Phoenician founder of Thebes, in a wedding attended by all the Olympian gods — the first wedding of mortals so honoured. Among the gifts was a magnificent necklace, fashioned by Hephaestus, and a robe; the necklace of Harmonia brought misfortune to its later possessors and features in the tragedies of the Theban cycle, passing through the house of Oedipus to Eriphyle and the expedition of the Seven against Thebes.

Later life

Harmonia and Cadmus had several children, including Semele, Ino, Autonoë, Agave, Polydorus and Illyrius. In old age the couple migrated to Illyria, where they were transformed into serpents and eventually taken by Zeus to the Isles of the Blessed (Elysium).
