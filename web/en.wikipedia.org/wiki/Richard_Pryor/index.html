Richard Franklin Lennox Thomas Pryor (1 December 1940 – 10 December 2005) was an American stand-up comedian and actor. Widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential stand-up comedians of all time, Pryor revolutionised the form with his confessional, character-filled storytelling, his unflinching treatment of race and his own troubled life, and albums and concert films that remain landmark works of American comedy.

Early life

Pryor was born in Peoria, Illinois, and raised in his grandmother's brothel, where his mother worked; he endured a childhood marked by poverty and abuse. Expelled from school at fourteen, he worked a series of jobs and served in the U.S. Army in West Germany before beginning to perform in clubs in the early 1960s.

Career

Pryor first found success with material modelled on Bill Cosby's inoffensive style, appearing regularly on television variety shows. In 1967, in a famous on-stage epiphany in Las Vegas, he abandoned that persona; after moving among the counterculture of Berkeley and Los Angeles, he rebuilt his act around raw, autobiographical material and characters such as the wino philosopher Mudbone. His albums That Nigger's Crazy (1974), ...Is It Something I Said? (1975) and Bicentennial Nigger (1976) won Grammy Awards, and his concert films — Richard Pryor: Live in Concert (1979), Live on the Sunset Strip (1982) and Here and Now (1983) — set the standard for the genre. He co-wrote Blazing Saddles with Mel Brooks and starred in films including Silver Streak (1976), Stir Crazy (1980) and Brewster's Millions (1985), several paired with Gene Wilder. His own television variety show in 1977 was acclaimed but short-lived amid battles with network censors.

Personal life and later years

Pryor's life was shadowed by drug addiction; in 1980 he suffered severe burns in a freebasing incident that he later mined for material. In 1986 he was diagnosed with multiple sclerosis, which progressively limited his mobility and ended his performing career in the 1990s; his final film appearance was in Lost Highway (1997). He died of a heart attack in 2005.

Legacy

Pryor was the first recipient of the Kennedy Center's Mark Twain Prize for American Humor (1998), won an Emmy Award and five Grammy Awards, and was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame. Comedians including Eddie Murphy, Chris Rock, Dave Chappelle, Jerry Seinfeld and George Carlin have cited him as a defining influence; the Peabody Awards described him as a figure who "pioneered a new brand of stand-up comedy" rooted in the truth of his own experience.
