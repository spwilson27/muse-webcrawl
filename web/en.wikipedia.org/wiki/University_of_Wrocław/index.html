University of Wrocław

The University of Wrocław (Polish: Uniwersytet Wrocławski) is a public research university in Wrocław, Poland. Its roots go back to the Jesuit Leopoldina Academy founded in 1702 by Emperor Leopold I; the modern university was formed in 1811 as the Silesian Friedrich Wilhelm University in Breslau, and was re-established as a Polish university in 1945 after the city passed from Germany to Poland.

History

The Prussian university of 1811, created by merging the Leopoldina with the Viadrina from Frankfurt (Oder), became one of Germany's major universities; scholars associated with it include Alois Alzheimer, Max Born, and Otto Stern, and nine Nobel laureates studied or taught there. After 1945, Polish academics — many arriving from the lost university of Lwów (Lviv) — rebuilt the institution. Its baroque main building on the Oder riverbank contains the ornate Aula Leopoldina auditorium.

Today

The university has about 25,000 students across ten faculties, spanning humanities, law, natural sciences, and social sciences, and is one of Poland's leading research universities.
