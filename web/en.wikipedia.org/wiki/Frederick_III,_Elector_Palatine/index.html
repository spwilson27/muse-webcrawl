Frederick III, Elector Palatine

Frederick III of Simmern, the Pious (German: Friedrich III. von Simmern, der Fromme; 14 February 1515 – 26 October 1576) was Elector Palatine of the Rhine from 1559 to 1576. A committed Calvinist, he made the Electoral Palatinate the centre of the Reformed faith in Germany and commissioned the Heidelberg Catechism of 1563.

Reign

Frederick succeeded his childless cousin Otto Henry in 1559. Raised a Catholic and later sympathetic to Lutheranism, he came to embrace Calvinism and progressively reformed the Palatinate's church, removing images and altars and introducing Reformed worship, despite opposition from Lutheran princes of the Empire. At the Diet of Augsburg in 1566 he defended his confession before the Emperor, declaring that he acknowledged no head of the Church but Christ.

Legacy

Under Frederick, Heidelberg became a refuge for Protestant exiles from France, the Netherlands, and England, and its university a leading Reformed academy. The Heidelberg Catechism, prepared by Zacharias Ursinus and Caspar Olevianus, remains a foundational doctrinal standard of Reformed churches worldwide.
