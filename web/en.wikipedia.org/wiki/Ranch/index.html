Ranch

A ranch is an area of land, including various structures, given primarily to ranching: the practice of raising grazing livestock such as cattle and sheep. It is a subtype of farm, typically found in the western United States and Canada, as well as Australia (where it is called a station) and Mexico (where it is known as a rancho or estancia in parts of South America).

Ranching

Ranching developed on the open range of North America from Spanish colonial traditions brought by vaqueros. Herds graze on large tracts of native grassland, often public land leased from the government, and are worked by ranch hands — cowboys — traditionally on horseback. Seasonal roundups gather cattle for branding, veterinary care and sale. Many modern ranches supplement livestock income with hunting leases, guest ("dude ranch") tourism, or conservation easements. The largest ranches, such as the King Ranch in Texas, cover hundreds of thousands of acres.

Ranch houses

The word "ranch" is also used for the ranch-style house, a single-story, low-roofed domestic architectural style that originated in the American West in the 1920s and became dominant in suburban housing in the 1950s and 1960s.
