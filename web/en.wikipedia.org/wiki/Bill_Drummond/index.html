William Ernest Drummond (born 29 April 1953) is a Scottish artist, musician, writer, and record producer. He is best known as co-founder, with Jimmy Cauty, of the late-1980s and early-1990s electronic group The KLF, and for a series of provocative art actions — including burning one million pounds in cash — carried out under the name of the K Foundation.

Early career

Born in South Africa to a Scottish missionary family and raised in Scotland and England, Drummond worked in theatre design before entering music as a member of the Liverpool band Big in Japan. He went on to manage Echo & the Bunnymen and The Teardrop Explodes, and worked as an A&R man at Warner Bros. Records. In 1986 he resigned and released a solo album, The Man.

The KLF

With Jimmy Cauty, Drummond formed The Justified Ancients of Mu Mu (The JAMs), then The Timelords — whose novelty hit "Doctorin' the Tardis" reached number one in 1988 — and finally The KLF. The KLF became one of the best-selling singles acts in the world with stadium house hits such as "3 a.m. Eternal" and "Justified & Ancient." Their 1988 manual, The Manual (How to Have a Number One the Easy Way), later became a cult text. At the height of their success in 1992, the duo deleted their catalogue and exited the music business after a notorious BRIT Awards performance involving a machine gun firing blanks and a dead sheep.

The K Foundation and the burning of a million pounds

As the K Foundation, Drummond and Cauty staged an alternative art award in 1993, offering £40,000 for the "worst artist" of the year — twice the value of the Turner Prize — which was won by (and awarded to) Rachel Whiteread, winner of the Turner Prize itself. On 23 August 1994, on the island of Jura in Scotland, the two men burned £1 million in banknotes, an act filmed and later toured as Watch the K Foundation Burn a Million Quid. Drummond has repeatedly refused to give a single definitive explanation of the act, which remains one of the most debated gestures in modern art.

Later work

Drummond's later projects include the book 45 (2000), the choir project The17, the "No Music Day" observance, the Penkiln Burn publishing imprint, and long-running conceptual enterprises such as building beds under the brand of the Curfew Tower residency in Cushendall, Northern Ireland, and his World Tour of site-specific works. In 2017 Drummond and Cauty returned as The Justified Ancients of Mu Mu with the novel 2023.
