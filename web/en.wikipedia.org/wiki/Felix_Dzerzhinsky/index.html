Felix Edmundovich Dzerzhinsky (Polish: Feliks Dzierżyński; 11 September 1877 – 20 July 1926), nicknamed "Iron Felix", was a Polish-born Bolshevik revolutionary and Soviet statesman. In December 1917 he founded and headed the Cheka, the first Soviet secret police, and directed the Red Terror during the Russian Civil War. He remained at the head of Soviet state security — through its renamings as the GPU and OGPU — until his death.

Early life and revolution

Born to a Polish noble family in what is now Belarus, Dzerzhinsky became a professional revolutionary as a teenager, co-founding the Social Democracy of the Kingdom of Poland and Lithuania party. He spent some eleven years in tsarist prisons and Siberian exile, escaping repeatedly. Freed by the February Revolution of 1917, he joined Lenin's Bolsheviks, sat on the committee that organized the October Revolution, and on 20 December 1917 was appointed to lead the All-Russian Extraordinary Commission — the Cheka.

The Cheka and Red Terror

From its headquarters in the Lubyanka building in Moscow, the Cheka arrested, imprisoned, and executed real and suspected enemies of the regime, especially after the assassination attempts of August 1918 triggered the official Red Terror. Estimates of those executed under Dzerzhinsky's direction range from tens of thousands to considerably more. Simultaneously he held other major posts: People's Commissar for Internal Affairs (1919), head of transport (1921), and from 1924 chairman of the Supreme Council of National Economy (Vesenkha), where he supported the market-oriented New Economic Policy. He died of a heart attack in 1926 after a speech defending party unity against the opposition.

Legacy

Dzerzhinsky became an icon of Soviet state security; his statue ("Iron Felix") stood before the Lubyanka until it was toppled in August 1991 (it was restored to a Moscow park display and later re-erected in 2023). Streets, the city of Dzerzhinsk, and the KGB's successor insignia preserved his name, while in Poland and among victims' descendants he is remembered as an architect of terror.
