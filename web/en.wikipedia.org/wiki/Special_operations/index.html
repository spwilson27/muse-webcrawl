Special operations

Special operations or special ops are military activities conducted by specially designated, organized, selected, trained, and equipped forces, using unconventional tactics and techniques. They are typically undertaken by special forces units and may include reconnaissance, unconventional warfare, counter-terrorism, direct action, hostage rescue, and foreign internal defense. Special operations are generally characterized by small units operating with a high degree of autonomy, often behind enemy lines or in politically sensitive environments.

History

Although elite units have existed throughout military history, modern special operations trace their development to the Second World War, when units such as the British Special Air Service (SAS), Commandos, the American Office of Strategic Services (OSS), and German Brandenburgers conducted raids, sabotage, and intelligence missions. The success of these formations led most major militaries to maintain dedicated special forces after 1945.

United States

In the United States, special operations forces (SOF) are organized under United States Special Operations Command (USSOCOM), established in 1987 following the failure of the Iranian hostage rescue mission, Operation Eagle Claw. Component commands include Army Special Operations Command (home of the Green Berets, Rangers, and Delta Force), Naval Special Warfare Command (Navy SEALs), Air Force Special Operations Command, and Marine Corps Forces Special Operations Command (MARSOC).

Types of missions

Principal special operations missions include direct action (short strikes and raids), special reconnaissance, counter-terrorism, unconventional warfare, foreign internal defense, civil affairs, psychological operations, counter-proliferation of weapons of mass destruction, and hostage rescue. Operations are often joint and interagency, and may be classified; notable examples include the 2011 raid that killed Osama bin Laden, conducted by SEAL Team Six in Abbottabad, Pakistan.

Selection and training

Special operations personnel typically undergo exceptionally demanding selection courses with high attrition rates, followed by extended training in parachuting, diving, demolitions, languages, survival, and advanced marksmanship. Their ethos emphasizes quiet professionalism and the ability to operate in small teams far from support.
