Lambton Castle, near Chester-le-Street in County Durham, England, is a stately home and the ancestral seat of the Lambton family, Earls of Durham. The castle stands in a large park overlooking the River Wear, on land the Lambtons have held since the twelfth century.

The present house was built around the turn of the nineteenth century to designs begun by the architect Joseph Bonomi and continued by his son Ignatius Bonomi, replacing the earlier Harraton Hall. Coal mining subsidence forced the demolition of parts of the castle, including the great hall, in the mid-twentieth century.

History

The Lambton family rose to prominence in Durham over many centuries, and the estate gave rise to the famous legend of the Lambton Worm, in which John Lambton slays a monstrous serpent-like creature in the River Wear. In the nineteenth century John Lambton, 1st Earl of Durham, became a leading Whig statesman, Governor General of British North America and author of the Durham Report.

The castle estate later hosted an early safari park, Lambton Lion Park, in the 1970s, and the grounds have since been used for events and filming. The family retains the estate, which is not generally open to the public.

Architecture

The house is built in a castellated Gothic style from local sandstone, with later alterations following subsidence damage. Its parkland, designed in the landscape tradition, extends across the Wear valley.

The Lambton Worm

The castle is closely associated with the folk tale and song of the Lambton Worm, one of the best-known legends of north-east England, commemorated in a popular nineteenth-century music-hall song.
