FA Trophy

The Football Association Challenge Trophy, contested by semi-professional and amateur clubs.

The Football Association Challenge Trophy, commonly known as the FA Trophy, is an English football knockout cup competition organised by the Football Association (FA). It is contested by clubs playing in Steps 1–4 of the National League System, corresponding broadly to semi-professional and amateur teams below the English Football League. The final is held at Wembley Stadium, often as part of a "Non-League Finals Day" alongside the FA Vase final.

History

The competition was created in 1969 to afford semi-professional clubs, which were ineligible for the FA Amateur Cup, a chance to compete for a trophy culminating in a Wembley final. The first winners, in 1970, were Macclesfield Town, who defeated Telford United in the inaugural final. The FA Amateur Cup was abolished in 1974, after which the strongest amateur sides also entered the Trophy. Following the creation of the Alliance Premier League (now the National League) in 1979, the competition settled into its modern form for non-League clubs.

Format

The Trophy is a single-elimination tournament with rounds drawn regionally in the early stages. Entry is open to clubs in the National League, National League North and South, and the premier divisions and first divisions of the Isthmian, Northern Premier, and Southern Leagues. Ties are decided on the day, with replays largely abolished in favour of extra time and penalty shootouts.

Notable records

Woking, Scarborough, and Telford United are among the most successful clubs in the competition, each winning the Trophy three times. Scarborough's wins in the 1970s and the later successes of clubs such as Wycombe Wanderers and Colchester United, who won the Trophy shortly before achieving Football League status, illustrate the competition's place in the non-League game.
