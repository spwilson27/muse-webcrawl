Potosí is a city in southern Bolivia, capital of the department of the same name, famous as the site of the Cerro Rico, the mountain of silver whose mines financed much of the Spanish Empire. Founded in 1545 shortly after the discovery of silver ore, it grew with extraordinary speed: by the early seventeenth century, with around 160,000 inhabitants, it was one of the largest cities in the Americas and among the highest cities in the world at about 4,090 metres (13,420 ft) above sea level.

History

Silver from the Cerro Rico was extracted on a vast scale using indigenous labour drafted under the mita system, adapted from an Inca corvée, and refined — after the introduction of mercury amalgamation in the 1570s — in dozens of mills powered by water from artificial reservoirs. The Potosí mint produced the famous "pieces of eight" that became a global currency. The human cost was severe: generations of indigenous and African workers laboured in lethal conditions underground, and the mountain became a symbol of colonial exploitation. The Spanish expression valer un Potosí — "to be worth a Potosí" — entered the language as a synonym for immense wealth, and the city appears in Don Quixote.

Production declined through the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries as the richest ores were exhausted, and the city's population fell drastically. In the twentieth century tin replaced silver as the principal product, and the mines were nationalised after Bolivia's 1952 revolution. Today mining continues through cooperatives working the mountain's remaining silver, tin, and zinc in notoriously dangerous conditions; centuries of extraction have honeycombed the Cerro Rico and raised concerns about sinkholes and the deformation of its summit.

Heritage

The historic centre, with its colonial churches, the Casa de la Moneda (the former royal mint, now a museum), and miners' districts, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1987; in 2014 the site was placed on the list of World Heritage in Danger because of continuing mining damage to the mountain.
