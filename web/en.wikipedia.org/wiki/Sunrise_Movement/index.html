The Sunrise Movement is an American youth-led political movement that advocates political action on climate change. When launched in 2017, the movement's goal was to elect proponents of renewable energy in the 2018 midterm elections, first in the Democratic primaries and then in the general election, and has since expanded its program to promote a Green New Deal in cooperation with Justice Democrats and with New Consensus support.

Sunrise gained wide public attention after protesting alongside Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez at the office of then-outgoing Speaker Nancy Pelosi in November 2018, shortly after the midterm elections. The movement operates through a decentralized network of local chapters called "hubs" and a national team, and it has endorsed candidates at the federal, state, and local levels.

History

Sunrise was founded in 2017 by a group of young activists, several of whom had previously been involved with the fossil fuel divestment movement on college campuses and with organizations such as 350.org. Varshini Prakash, one of the co-founders, became the group's executive director. The movement built on earlier youth climate organizing, including the 2013 protests against the Keystone XL pipeline.

In November 2018, about 200 Sunrise activists occupied Nancy Pelosi's office to demand that the incoming Democratic House majority establish a select committee on a Green New Deal. Representative-elect Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez joined the sit-in, and images of the protest circulated widely. In February 2019, Sunrise activists confronted Senator Dianne Feinstein over her response to the Green New Deal resolution in a video that also drew national attention.

Green New Deal advocacy

The Green New Deal, a congressional resolution introduced by Ocasio-Cortez and Senator Ed Markey in February 2019, became the movement's central policy demand. Sunrise describes the framework as a ten-year mobilization to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions while creating millions of jobs and addressing racial and economic inequality. The movement has organized town halls, strikes, and lobbying campaigns in support of the resolution and related state and local measures.

Electoral work

Sunrise has endorsed and campaigned for progressive candidates, including Ed Markey in his 2020 Massachusetts Senate primary against Joseph Kennedy III, and Jamaal Bowman in New York. The movement's political arm has also been involved in campaigns around climate ballot measures and local Green New Deal proposals in cities such as Boston and New York.

Criticism

Sunrise has faced criticism from some commentators who argue that its confrontational tactics risk alienating potential allies, and from others who question the feasibility of the Green New Deal framework. Internal disputes over diversity and organizational direction also became public in 2021, when some staff members raised concerns about equity within the movement.
