The National Liberation Committee (Italian: Comitato di Liberazione Nazionale, CLN) was a political umbrella organisation and the main representative of the Italian resistance movement fighting against the German occupation of Italy in the aftermath of the armistice of Cassibile of 8 September 1943. It was founded in Rome on 9 September 1943 by the principal anti-fascist parties and functioned as a clandestine shadow government in occupied territory.

Composition

The CLN brought together six parties: the Italian Communist Party (PCI), the Italian Socialist Party (PSIUP), the Action Party (PdA), Christian Democracy (DC), the Italian Liberal Party (PLI), and the Labour Democratic Party (PDL). Ivanoe Bonomi, a veteran liberal statesman, served as its president. In the north, the CLN for Upper Italy (Comitato di Liberazione Nazionale Alta Italia, CLNAI), based in Milan, coordinated the large partisan formations of the occupied north and was recognised by the Allies as the effective governing authority there in the final phase of the war.

Role in the resistance

The CLN organised and legitimised the partisan war against German forces and the collaborationist Italian Social Republic. Its military arm, the Corpo Volontari della Libertà (CVL), unified partisan brigades of different political colours. In April 1945 the CLNAI ordered the general insurrection that liberated Turin, Milan, and Genoa ahead of the Allied advance; on 25 April 1945 — now celebrated as Liberation Day in Italy — the CLN proclaimed the uprising nationwide and assumed power in the liberated cities.

Postwar legacy

After liberation, CLN-backed governments under Ferruccio Parri and then Alcide De Gasperi led Italy's transition from monarchy to republic. The committee's member parties dominated the Constituent Assembly that drafted the Italian constitution, which entered into force in 1948, and the CLN dissolved as normal party politics resumed in 1947. The committee remains a central symbol of the democratic foundations of the Italian Republic.
