Bhikkhuni

[Image placeholder: Bhikkhunis in robes]

A bhikkhunī (Pali) or bhikṣuṇī (Sanskrit) is a fully ordained female monastic in Buddhism. Male monastics are called bhikkhus. Both bhikkhunis and bhikkhus live by the Patimokkha, the basic code of monastic discipline, which for bhikkhunis includes 311 rules in the Theravada tradition.

According to Buddhist scriptures, the order of bhikkhunis was first created by the Buddha at the request of his stepmother and aunt Mahapajapati Gotami, after mediation by his attendant Ananda. The first bhikkhuni was Mahapajapati herself.

Revival

The Theravada bhikkhuni lineage died out in Sri Lanka in the 11th century and was absent for centuries. Beginning in the late 20th century, women have been ordained again — in Sri Lanka from 1996/1998 onward using dual ordination with the help of East Asian (Dharmaguptaka) nuns — a revival that remains controversial among some monastic authorities. Tibetan Buddhism has likewise seen movements toward full ordination for women.
