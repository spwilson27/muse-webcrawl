Crocodilia

Crocodilia (or Crocodylia) is an order of mostly large, predatory, semiaquatic archosaurian reptiles, known as crocodilians. It includes true crocodiles, alligators and caimans, and the gharial and false gharial.

Crocodilia

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Reptilia
Clade: Archosauria

Characteristics

Crocodilians first appeared about 95 million years ago in the Late Cretaceous. They have elongated snouts, powerful jaws, conical teeth, short legs and long muscular tails, with armoured skin bearing osteoderms. They are ectothermic, lay eggs, and show parental care unusual among reptiles. They have a four-chambered heart.

Distribution and habitat

They live throughout the tropics in Africa, Asia, the Americas and Australia, mainly in freshwater rivers, lakes and wetlands, though the saltwater crocodile ranges widely at sea.

Families

  • Alligatoridae — alligators and caimans
  • Crocodylidae — true crocodiles
  • Gavialidae — gharial

Relation to humans

Some species are dangerous to humans; many are farmed or hunted for skin and meat, and several are endangered.
