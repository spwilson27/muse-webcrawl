Hertha BSC

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hertha, Berliner Sport-Club e. V., commonly known as Hertha BSC or Hertha Berlin, is a German professional football club based in the Charlottenburg locality of Berlin. Founded in 1892 as BFC Hertha 92 — named after a steamship with a blue-and-white funnel — the club is one of the oldest in German football and plays its home matches at the Olympiastadion, its home since 1963.

History

Hertha won consecutive German championships in 1930 and 1931 and was a founding member of the Bundesliga in 1963. Its location in West Berlin during the Cold War gave the club a special symbolic role. Hertha's most successful modern period came in the late 1990s under coach Jürgen Röber, qualifying for the UEFA Champions League in 1999. The club won the DFB-Ligapokal in 2001 and 2002 but has otherwise struggled, suffering several relegations between the Bundesliga and 2. Bundesliga, including in 2023.

Club identity

Hertha's colours are blue and white; its nickname is Die alte Dame ("The Old Lady"). Its main local rival is 1. FC Union Berlin, the club of the former East, against whom it contests the Berlin derby.
