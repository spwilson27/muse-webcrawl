White knight (business)

In business, a white knight is a friendly investor or company that acquires a corporation facing a hostile takeover bid, rescuing it from the unwanted acquirer — the "black knight". The target company invites the white knight's bid as a preferable alternative.

How it works

When a company is targeted by a hostile bidder, its board may search for a white knight willing to offer a higher price, better terms, or a promise to keep existing management and the company's identity intact. The white knight may be offered favourable terms — such as lock-up options on key assets or a break-up fee — to secure its bid against the original raider.

Related strategies

The white knight is one of several takeover defences. A white squire buys a large minority stake but stops short of full control, blocking the raider without acquiring the company. Management may also pursue a Pac-Man defence (counter-bidding for the raider), a poison pill, or a sale to a grey knight — a second, not fully friendly bidder. White-knight rescues were a hallmark of the merger waves of the 1980s and remain a standard boardroom response to hostile bids.
