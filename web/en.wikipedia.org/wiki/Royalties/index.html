Royalties

Royalties reward rights holders for use of their assets

Royalties are payments made by one party (the licensee or franchiser) to another (the licensor or franchisor) for the right to ongoing use of an asset, usually intellectual property. Royalties are typically agreed as a percentage of the gross or net revenues derived from the use of the asset, or as a fixed price per unit sold.

Types

Common forms include book royalties paid by publishers to authors (often 10–15% of the list price for hardbacks), music royalties paid to songwriters and performers for recordings, broadcasts and streaming, patent royalties for licensed technology, and trademark and franchise royalties for the use of a brand. In the extractive industries, landowners and governments receive royalties based on the value or volume of oil, gas or minerals removed.

Royalty agreements

A licence agreement normally specifies the territory, duration, exclusivity, the royalty rate and the basis of calculation, together with audit rights and minimum guarantees or advances, which are recoupable payments made before royalties are earned. Rates vary widely by industry and bargaining power.

Collection

Because individual monitoring is impractical, collecting societies such as performing rights organisations gather music royalties from broadcasters and venues and distribute them to members. In the United States, statutory mechanical royalty rates for recordings are set under copyright law.
