Darcy's law

Schematic of fluid flow through a porous medium.

Darcy's law is an equation that describes the flow of a fluid through a porous medium. The law was formulated by Henry Darcy based on results of experiments on the flow of water through beds of sand, forming the basis of hydrogeology, a branch of earth sciences. It is analogous to Ohm's law in electrostatics, linearly relating the volume flow rate of the fluid to the hydraulic head difference (which is often just related to the pressure difference) via the hydraulic conductivity.

Formulation

Darcy's law states that the total discharge Q is equal to the product of the intrinsic permeability of the medium k, the cross-sectional area A, and the pressure drop (pb − pa), divided by the dynamic viscosity μ and the length L over which the drop occurs:

Q = −kA (pb − pa) / (μL)

The negative sign follows the convention that fluid flows from high pressure to low pressure. The specific discharge q = Q/A is often called the Darcy flux or Darcy velocity, though it is not the true microscopic velocity of the fluid particles.

Applications

Darcy's law is fundamental in groundwater hydrology, petroleum engineering (where it underlies the calculation of oil and gas flow to wells), soil physics, and the design of filtration systems. It applies to laminar flow of incompressible fluids through saturated porous media; for high flow velocities, corrections such as the Forchheimer equation are used.
