Joachim du Bellay (c. 1522 – 1 January 1560) was a French poet, critic, and member of the Pléiade, the circle of Renaissance poets gathered around Pierre de Ronsard. He is best known for the manifesto Défense et illustration de la langue française (1549), which argued that French could rival Latin and Italian as a literary language, and for the sonnet sequence Les Regrets (1558).

Life

Born into minor nobility at Liré in Anjou, du Bellay studied law at Poitiers and then humanities in Paris, where he joined Ronsard and Jean-Antoine de Baïf. In 1553 he accompanied his cousin, Cardinal Jean du Bellay, to Rome as his secretary. The four years in Italy disappointed him — he found the papal court corrupt and grew homesick — but they produced his finest poetry: Les Regrets, a satirical and nostalgic sequence of 191 sonnets, and Les Antiquités de Rome, meditations on the ruins of the ancient city. He returned to France in 1557 in poor health and died in Paris in 1560.

Works

His first sonnet sequence, L'Olive (1549–1550), modelled on Petrarch, helped establish the French sonnet. The Défense urged French writers to enrich their language by imitating classical and Italian models and by reviving archaic and dialectal words. "Heureux qui, comme Ulysse, a fait un beau voyage," the opening sonnet of Les Regrets, is among the most famous poems in French literature.
