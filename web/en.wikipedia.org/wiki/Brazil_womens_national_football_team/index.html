Brazil, one of the leading teams in women's football

The Brazil women's national football team represents Brazil in international women's football and is governed by the Brazilian Football Confederation (CBF). One of the strongest teams in the world, Brazil has competed at every FIFA Women's World Cup, finishing as runners-up in 2007, when the team led by Marta lost the final to Germany. Brazil has won the Copa América Femenina a record number of times and has taken Olympic silver medals, including at Athens 2004, Beijing 2008 and Paris 2024.

History

Women's football was officially banned in Brazil from 1941 until 1979, and the national team was only formed in the 1980s, playing its first official match in 1986. Despite limited support, the team quickly became South America's dominant side. Its greatest era came in the 2000s behind Marta — widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time and a six-time FIFA World Player of the Year — along with Cristiane and Formiga, who played at seven World Cups. At the 2023 World Cup, however, Brazil was eliminated in the group stage for the first time since 1995.
