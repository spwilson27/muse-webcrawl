São Paulo/Guarulhos – Governor André Franco Montoro International Airport (IATA: GRU, ICAO: SBGR), commonly known as Guarulhos Airport or Cumbica Airport, is the primary international airport serving São Paulo, Brazil. Located in the municipality of Guarulhos, about 25 km northeast of downtown São Paulo, it is the busiest airport in Brazil and one of the busiest in Latin America, handling over 40 million passengers in peak years.

History

The airport was built on land adjoining the São Paulo Air Force Base (Base Aérea de São Paulo) at Cumbica and opened on 20 January 1985, gradually taking over international flights from the congested Congonhas Airport. A second terminal opened in 1993. In 2012 the airport was concessioned to a private consortium (GRU Airport, led by Invepar with Airports Company South Africa), which built the modern Terminal 3 for international flights, opened in May 2014 ahead of the FIFA World Cup.

Terminals

The airport has three passenger terminals. Terminal 1 serves domestic flights of smaller carriers; Terminal 2, the largest, handles domestic traffic and some international routes; Terminal 3, opened in 2014, is dedicated to international operations with roughly 190,000 m² of floor space. In 2018 the airport gained a direct rail link to central São Paulo with the opening of Line 13–Jade of the CPTM metropolitan train network, serving Aeroporto-Guarulhos station.

Traffic

Guarulhos is the main hub of LATAM Airlines Brasil and a major operating base for Gol and Azul. It offers nonstop flights to destinations across the Americas, Europe, Africa and the Middle East, and is Brazil's leading airport for international cargo.
