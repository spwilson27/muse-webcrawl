Balcony

A balcony (from Italian: balcone, "scaffold") is a platform projecting from the wall of a building, supported by columns or console brackets, and enclosed with a balustrade or railing, usually above the ground floor. Access is typically from an upper-floor window or door. Balconies may be cantilevered, hung from steel rods, or stacked on columns, and are usually small relative to the building's floor plan, though large examples shade into terraces and verandas.

Types

A French balcony (or Juliet balcony) is a shallow, largely decorative railing placed in front of a full-height opening window, with little or no usable floor. A loggia is recessed into the building rather than projecting from it. In theatres and concert halls, a balcony is a raised seating tier above the main floor. Maltese balconies are enclosed wooden galleries, brightly painted, projecting from the façades of townhouses in Valletta and other Maltese towns.

History

Projecting balconies appeared in medieval fortifications and Mediterranean houses, serving for display, surveillance, and outdoor living in warm climates. They became standard features of European urban architecture from the Renaissance onward, notably in the wrought-iron balconies of Parisian Haussmann buildings and the shuttered balconies of New Orleans and Spanish colonial cities. In the 20th century the balcony became a defining element of modernist apartment blocks and hotels, prized as private outdoor space in dense housing.

Functions and regulations

Balconies provide outdoor access, light, ventilation, and views; they also serve ceremonial purposes — monarchs and popes traditionally appear on balconies to greet crowds, as on the balcony of St. Peter's Basilica for the Urbi et Orbi blessing and at Buckingham Palace. Building codes govern railing heights, load capacity, and guard spacing, and structural failures of crowded or corroded balconies have led to stricter inspection regimes in several jurisdictions.

In literature

The balcony scene in Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet — in which Juliet appears above Romeo in the Capulets' orchard — made the balcony a lasting romantic symbol; the so-called Juliet's balcony in Verona, though assembled in the 20th century, remains a major tourist attraction.
