The S&P 500 is a stock market index tracking the stock performance of 500 of the largest companies listed on stock exchanges in the United States. It is one of the most commonly followed equity indices and is widely regarded as one of the best gauges of large-cap U.S. equities.

The index includes 503 common stocks issued by 500 large-cap companies, covering approximately 80% of the American equity market by capitalization. It is maintained by S&P Dow Jones Indices, and its constituents are selected by a committee.

History

Standard & Poor's introduced its first stock index in 1923; the S&P 500 in its current form was introduced on March 4, 1957. The index is calculated in real time and has spawned numerous index funds and exchange-traded funds, such as the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY).
