Federally funded research and development center

Federally funded research and development centers (FFRDCs) are special entities sponsored by agencies of the United States federal government to meet long-term research and development needs that cannot be met as effectively by existing government or contractor resources. They are administered by universities, non-profit organisations or corporations under long-term contracts, and operate in the public interest with objectivity and independence from commercial and political pressures.

Characteristics

FFRDCs have privileged access to government facilities, data and personnel, and in return are barred from competing for commercial work, manufacturing products, or taking work that would create conflicts of interest. Their work, governed by Federal Acquisition Regulation Part 35.017 and individual sponsoring agreements, includes operating national laboratories, conducting systems engineering and technical direction, and providing independent analysis and advice. There are roughly 40 FFRDCs in the United States.

Examples

Well-known FFRDCs include the Los Alamos and Lawrence Livermore national laboratories, the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (operated by Caltech for NASA), the MIT Lincoln Laboratory, the RAND Corporation's project centres, the MITRE Corporation's centres, and the Aerospace Corporation, which supports the US Space Force.

History

The model originated in the Second World War and its aftermath, when the government created contractor-operated laboratories and think tanks — RAND, founded in 1948, being the archetype — to retain scientific and analytic talent outside the civil service while keeping it devoted to public problems.
