Report on Manufactures

The Report on the Subject of Manufactures, generally known as the Report on Manufactures, was the third of four major reports on economic policy issued by Alexander Hamilton, the first United States Secretary of the Treasury. It was submitted to Congress on December 5, 1791.

Contents

The report argued that the United States should actively promote domestic manufacturing so as not to remain dependent on foreign, especially British, industry. Hamilton proposed a program of government support including protective tariffs, bounties (subsidies), premiums for innovation, patents, inspection standards for finished goods, and internal improvements such as roads and canals to facilitate transport.

Influence

Much of the report was not enacted during Hamilton's lifetime, but its infant-industry argument became foundational in American economic policy debates and influenced later protectionist thought, including the "American System" of Henry Clay, as well as Friedrich List's economic writings.
