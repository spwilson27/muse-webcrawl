Prince Amedeo, Duke of Aosta

Prince Amedeo, 3rd Duke of Aosta (Amedeo Umberto Isabella Luigi Filippo Maria Giuseppe Giovanni di Savoia; 21 October 1898 – 3 March 1942) was an Italian prince of the House of Savoy, a general of the Royal Italian Army, and Viceroy of Italian East Africa from 1937 to 1941. He is best remembered for commanding Italian forces in the East African campaign of the Second World War, culminating in the defence of Amba Alagi, after which he surrendered with full honours of war and died in British captivity in Nairobi.

Early life and career

Amedeo was the eldest son of Prince Emanuele Filiberto, Duke of Aosta, and Princess Hélène of Orléans. Educated partly in England at St Andrew's College, he volunteered for service in the First World War as an artillery officer. Between the wars he served in Italian Libya and Italian Somaliland, gaining a reputation as a capable colonial administrator and aviator.

Viceroy of Italian East Africa

In 1937 he succeeded Rodolfo Graziani as Viceroy of Italian East Africa and Governor-General of Ethiopia. He pursued a comparatively conciliatory administration and attempted to modernise the colony. When Italy entered the Second World War in June 1940, he became commander-in-chief of Italian forces in East Africa, overseeing the conquest of British Somaliland in August 1940.

East African campaign and death

Cut off from supplies, his forces were driven back by Commonwealth offensives in 1941. After the fall of Addis Ababa he withdrew to the mountain stronghold of Amba Alagi, surrendering on 19 May 1941; the British rendered the honours of war in recognition of the gallant defence. Held as a prisoner of war near Nairobi, he died there of complications from tuberculosis and malaria in March 1942.
