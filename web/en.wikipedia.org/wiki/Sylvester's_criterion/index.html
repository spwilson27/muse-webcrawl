Sylvester's criterion

In mathematics, Sylvester's criterion is a necessary and sufficient criterion to determine whether a Hermitian matrix is positive-definite. It is named after the British mathematician James Joseph Sylvester.

Statement

The theorem states that a Hermitian matrix M is positive-definite if and only if all of the following matrices have positive determinants:

  • the top-left 1 × 1 corner of M,
  • the top-left 2 × 2 corner of M,
  • …
  • M itself.

These determinants are the leading principal minors of M. Equivalently, a symmetric matrix with real entries is positive-definite if and only if all its leading principal minors are positive.

Related criteria

Sylvester's criterion also gives a test for positive semidefiniteness, but with an important refinement: a Hermitian matrix is positive-semidefinite if and only if all principal minors — not merely the leading ones — are nonnegative. Checking only leading principal minors is not sufficient in the semidefinite case.

There is an analogous criterion for negative-definite matrices: the signs of the leading principal minors must alternate, beginning with a negative first minor.

Applications

The criterion is used in optimisation to test whether the Hessian matrix of a function is positive-definite at a critical point (guaranteeing a local minimum), in control theory and linear algebra, and as a step in computing Cholesky factorisations, which exist precisely for positive-definite matrices.
