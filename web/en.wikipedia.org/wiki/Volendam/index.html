Volendam

Volendam is a town in North Holland, Netherlands, in the municipality of Edam-Volendam, on the shore of the Markermeer lake. Historically a fishing village attached to the port of Edam, it is famous for its traditional Dutch costumes, its harbour of old fishing boats, and a celebrated community of Dutch popular musicians — the Palingsound ("eel sound") named after the local eel trade.

History

Volendam arose in 1357 when the inhabitants of Edam dug a shorter canal to the Zuiderzee and dammed the old harbour mouth, creating a new settlement at the "filled dam" (Volle Dam). It remained a largely Catholic, close-knit fishing village even as the surrounding region became Protestant — a distinctiveness still reflected in its culture and very low crime and distinctive dialect. Artists discovered the picturesque village in the late 19th century, and the Hotel Spaander became a famous artists' colony. The closure of the Zuiderzee by the Afsluitdijk (1932) ended sea fishing, shifting the economy to tourism and eel farming.

Culture

The women's costume — high pointed lace cap, striped apron and coral necklace — is one of the best-known Dutch traditional outfits. Volendam produced an outsized share of Dutch pop music from the 1960s onward, including The Cats, BZN, Jan Smit and Nick & Simon, a phenomenon dubbed the Palingsound. Football club FC Volendam is nicknamed "the Eel Farmers".

2001 café fire

On New Year's Eve 2000–01, a fire in Café De Hemel killed 14 young people and injured hundreds, a national tragedy that led to stricter fire-safety enforcement across the Netherlands.
