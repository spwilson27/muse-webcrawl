Pentium III

The Pentium III is a sixth-generation x86 microprocessor developed by Intel and introduced in February 1999. Based on the P6 microarchitecture, it succeeded the Pentium II and introduced the SSE (Streaming SIMD Extensions) instruction set, adding 70 new instructions to accelerate floating-point and multimedia processing, along with a controversial Processor Serial Number embedded in each chip.

Successive versions — Katmai, Coppermine, and Tualatin — moved from a 250 nm to a 130 nm process, integrated the L2 cache on-die, and raised clock speeds from 450 MHz to 1.4 GHz. The Pentium III competed with AMD's Athlon during a period in which both companies raced to reach 1 GHz.

The P6 architecture underlying the Pentium III later proved influential: after being succeeded by the Pentium 4's NetBurst design, Intel returned to P6-derived designs for the Pentium M and the subsequent Core family.
