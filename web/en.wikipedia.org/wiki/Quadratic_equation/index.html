Quadratic equation

A quadratic equation is a polynomial equation of the second degree in a single variable x, with the general form

ax² + bx + c = 0, where a ≠ 0.

The values of x that satisfy the equation are called roots or solutions. A quadratic equation has at most two solutions, which may be real or complex.

Quadratic formula

The roots are given by the quadratic formula:

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a)

The discriminant b² − 4ac determines the nature of the roots: positive gives two distinct real roots, zero gives one repeated real root, and negative gives a pair of complex conjugate roots.

History

Babylonian mathematicians solved quadratic problems by geometry nearly 4,000 years ago. The method of completing the square was systematised by al-Khwarizmi in the 9th century, and the modern formula and notation developed in early modern Europe; René Descartes published the form familiar today in La Géométrie (1637).
