Mopsus

Mopsus (Greek: Μόψος, Mopsos) is the name of two famous seers in Greek mythology. The better known is the son of Manto, daughter of the prophet Tiresias, and either Rhacius or Apollo; the other is Mopsus son of Ampyx, a Lapith who sailed with the Argonauts. Sources ancient and modern sometimes conflate them.

Mopsus son of Manto

This Mopsus was a seer of Claros in Ionia and inherited his mother's prophetic gift. He famously defeated the great seer Calchas in a contest of divination at Claros after the Trojan War: Calchas could not guess the number of figs on a tree or the number of piglets a pregnant sow would bear, while Mopsus answered both exactly; Calchas died of grief at his defeat, fulfilling a prophecy that he would die when he met a better seer. Mopsus later travelled to Cilicia with Amphilochus, founded cities including Mopsuestia ("the hearth of Mopsus") and Mallos, and was eventually killed in a quarrel with Amphilochus over the kingship; both were buried in tombs that could not be seen from one another. His oracle at Mallos remained famous into Roman times.

Mopsus son of Ampyx

The Lapith Mopsus, from Thessaly, served as seer to the Argonauts in Apollonius Rhodius's Argonautica. He interpreted bird omens for Jason's crew but could not foresee his own death: in Libya he died from the bite of a snake born from a drop of Medusa's blood, and was buried by his companions in the sand near Lake Tritonis.

Legacy

The name Mopsus appears in Anatolian contexts — the Phoenician-Greek bilingual inscriptions from Cilicia mention a "House of Mopsus" (MPŠ in Phoenician), suggesting a historical or legendary dynastic founder behind the Greek tales. In later literature "Mopsus" became a stock name for a rustic shepherd-seer, used by Virgil in the Eclogues and by John Dryden.
