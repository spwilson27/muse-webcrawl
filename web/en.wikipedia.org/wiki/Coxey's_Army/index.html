Coxey's Army

Coxey's Army was a protest march by unemployed workers from the United States, led by Ohio businessman Jacob Coxey. They marched on Washington, D.C. in 1894, during the severe depression that followed the Panic of 1893. It was the second year of the depression and unemployment was extremely high. Coxey's Army was the first significant protest march on Washington, setting a precedent for later marches.

The march

Coxey proposed that the federal government issue $500 million in bonds to fund road construction, employing the jobless, and that local governments be able to fund public works with non-interest-bearing bonds. His "army" — the "Commonweal of Christ" — set out from Massillon, Ohio, on Easter Sunday, 25 March 1894, with about 100 men, and grew as it travelled; allied contingents marched from the West Coast and elsewhere, some famously commandeering trains. About 500 marchers reached Washington on 1 May 1894.

Outcome and legacy

Coxey was arrested for walking on the grass of the Capitol grounds and the movement dispersed without achieving its programme. Nevertheless, Coxey's Army entered American political vocabulary — "Enough food to feed Coxey's Army" remains a colloquialism — and the march pioneered the tactic of marching on the capital that later movements, from the Bonus Army of 1932 to the civil rights marches, would adopt. Coxey himself later served as mayor of Massillon and ran for president.
