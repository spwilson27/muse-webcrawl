The Howard Stern Show is an American radio show hosted by Howard Stern that gained wide recognition as the most popular morning programme in the United States during its terrestrial run from 1986 to 2005. Since January 2006 it has aired exclusively on Sirius XM Radio, where it remains one of the service's flagship programmes.

History

Stern developed his freewheeling style through stints in Detroit and Washington, D.C., before arriving at WNBC in New York in 1982. Fired in 1985, he moved to WXRK (K-Rock), where the show was syndicated nationally and by the early 1990s was broadcast in dozens of markets, frequently fined by the FCC for indecency. In 2004 Stern announced a move to satellite radio, escaping FCC content regulation; his Sirius debut on 9 January 2006 was a landmark in the medium's history.

Format

The show mixes long-form celebrity interviews — increasingly the focus of Stern's later years — with comedy sketches, staff antics, news read by co-host Robin Quivers, and contributions from the "Wack Pack" of eccentric regulars. Long-serving staff include writer and sound-effects man Fred Norris and producer Gary Dell'Abate ("Baba Booey").

Influence

Stern's success reshaped American radio, pioneered the "shock jock" format, and spawned the 1997 biographical film Private Parts, based on his bestselling memoir.
