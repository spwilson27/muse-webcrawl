The Guaraní are a group of culturally related Indigenous peoples of South America. They are distinguished from the related Tupí by their use of the Guaraní language. The traditional range of the Guaraní people is in what is now Paraguay between the Paraná River and the lower Paraguay River, the Misiones Province of Argentina, southern Brazil as far south as Rio Grande do Sul, and parts of Uruguay and Bolivia.

Although their demographic dominance of the region has been reduced by European colonisation and by the resulting demographic decrease of the Indigenous population, present-day "Paraguayans" include a large number of cultural and genetic descendants of the Guaraní. The Guaraní language, still widely spoken across the region, is one of the two official languages of Paraguay, where it is spoken by a majority of the population, including many people of non-Guaraní ancestry.

History

Guaraní groups practiced semi-sedentary agriculture centred on manioc and maize. During the colonial period, Jesuit missionaries gathered many Guaraní into missions (reducciones), most famously in the region of modern Paraguay, Argentina, and Brazil, where Guaraní militias later resisted Portuguese slave-raiders (bandeirantes). The Guaraní War of the 1750s followed the transfer of mission territory under the Treaty of Madrid. In the nineteenth century, Paraguay's population retained a strong Guaraní character, and Guaraní soldiers formed the backbone of its armies in the War of the Triple Alliance.

Culture and language

Guaraní culture emphasises reciprocal exchange and the concept of teko porã ("the good way of being"). Traditional religion centres on creator figures and shamanic practice. Paraguayan Guaraní has contributed vocabulary to Spanish and to other languages, and Guaraní-language media, literature, and education programmes continue to expand.

Subgroups

Major Guaraní subgroups include the Ava-Guaraní (Chiriguano), the Mbyá, the Pai Tavytera, the Guarani Ñandeva, and the Kaiowá of Mato Grosso do Sul. Land rights and deforestation remain pressing issues for Guaraní communities in Brazil and Paraguay.
