Mod (video games)

User-created modifications can add new maps, items or entire games

A mod (short for modification) is an alteration by players of a video game that changes one or more aspects of the game, such as how it looks or behaves. Mods range from small tweaks and new items to total conversions that use a game's engine to create an essentially new game. Modding is supported to widely varying degrees by developers; some games ship with official editing tools, while others can only be modified through reverse engineering.

History

Modding grew out of the hacker culture surrounding early computer games, and became mainstream in the 1990s with first-person shooters such as Doom and Quake, whose developers released level editors and scripting access. Several mods became commercially successful games in their own right: Counter-Strike began as a mod for Half-Life, Team Fortress as a Quake mod, and the MOBA genre originated with the Defense of the Ancients mod for Warcraft III.

Types

Common categories include graphical overhauls, new levels and quests, gameplay rebalances, user-interface changes, and "unofficial patches" that fix bugs or restore cut content. Distribution takes place through community sites and integrated services such as Steam Workshop and Nexus Mods.

Legal and commercial issues

Mods are usually distributed free of charge under end-user licence terms that leave the underlying intellectual property with the developer. Attempts to sell mods commercially, such as the paid-mods programme briefly introduced on Steam in 2015, have proved controversial. Some developers hire successful modders or acquire their creations.
