Solar Pons

Solar Pons is a fictional detective created by American author August Derleth as a deliberate pastiche of Sherlock Holmes. Pons operates from 7B Praed Street, London, assisted by his friend and chronicler Dr. Lyndon Parker, mirroring Holmes's Baker Street household. Derleth began the stories in the late 1920s, originally considering using Holmes himself before settling on an original homage character. The Solar Pons tales are among the best-known and most extensive examples of the Sherlockian pastiche genre.

Creation

Derleth, a prolific Wisconsin writer and founder of Arkham House publishers, was a devotee of Arthur Conan Doyle's stories. When Doyle declined permission to continue the Holmes series, Derleth invented Pons — the name is a joking bridge: pons is Latin for "bridge", and "solar" hints at a scholastic pun — and set the stories mainly in the 1920s and 1930s, a generation after Holmes. Pons admires Holmes as a real predecessor and occasionally comments on his cases.

Stories

Derleth wrote over seventy Solar Pons tales, published in collections including In Re: Sherlock Holmes (1945, later The Adventures of Solar Pons), The Memoirs of Solar Pons, The Return of Solar Pons, The Reminiscences of Solar Pons, The Casebook of Solar Pons, Praed Street Irregulars, The Chronicles of Solar Pons, and the novel Mr. Fairlie's Final Journey. After Derleth's death in 1971, Basil Copper continued the series with further volumes. The stories follow the Holmes formula: an eccentric consulting detective solves apparently impossible crimes through observation and deduction while his companion narrates.

Reception

Critics and Holmes enthusiasts have generally received the Pons stories warmly as affectionate and skillful imitations; they were praised by figures within the Sherlockian community and have remained in print through Arkham House and later publishers. The Praed Street setting and Pons's pipe, magnifying glass, and aphorisms deliberately echo 221B Baker Street.
