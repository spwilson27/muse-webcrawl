Cape Town water crisis

The Cape Town water crisis was a severe drought and water shortage in Cape Town, South Africa, that peaked in 2017 and 2018. After three years of below-average rainfall, the city's dams fell to critically low levels, and officials warned of "Day Zero", when municipal water supplies would largely be shut off.

Cape Town water crisis
Location: Cape Town, South Africa
Period: 2015–2018 (peak 2017–18)
Cause: Multi-year drought

Background

Cape Town relies mainly on surface water stored in dams fed by winter rainfall. From 2015, a persistent drought — linked in studies partly to climate change — depleted reservoirs such as Theewaterskloof, the city's largest dam.

Response

The city imposed escalating water restrictions, at their strictest limiting residents to 50 litres per person per day, raised tariffs, and invested in groundwater, desalination, and water reuse. Public campaigns cut consumption dramatically. Winter rains in 2018 raised dam levels and Day Zero was averted.
