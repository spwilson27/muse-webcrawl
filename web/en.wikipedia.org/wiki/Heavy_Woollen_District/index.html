Heavy Woollen District

The Heavy Woollen District is a region of West Yorkshire, England, historically centred on the production of heavy woollen cloth. It comprises the towns of Dewsbury, Batley, Heckmondwike, Cleckheaton, Liversedge, Mirfield, Ossett and surrounding settlements in the Spen Valley and the valley of the River Calder, lying between Leeds, Wakefield and Huddersfield.

Industry

The district became known in the 19th century for "shoddy" and "mungo" — woollen fibres recovered from recycled rags — which were blended with new wool to make heavy cloth, blankets and carpets. Rag merchants sorted imported rags, and local mills converted them into inexpensive heavy fabrics; Dewsbury and Batley became synonymous worldwide with the shoddy trade. The industry employed much of the local population and shaped the towns' mills, warehouses and terraced housing.

Rugby league

The name is also familiar in sport: the area is a heartland of rugby league, home to the Dewsbury Rams and Batley Bulldogs, and "Heavy Woollen" has been used for local representative teams and competitions, including a traditional Boxing Day friendly between the two clubs.

Today

Although large-scale textile production has declined, some specialist mills survive, and the Heavy Woollen District remains a recognised local identity, used by community organisations, local media and the heavy woollen planning area of Kirklees Council.
