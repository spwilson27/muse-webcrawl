Asser (died 908/909) was a Welsh monk from St Davids who became Bishop of Sherborne and is best known as the author of the Life of King Alfred (Latin: De rebus gestis Ælfredi regis), written in 893, the earliest biography of an English king and a principal source for the reign of Alfred the Great.

Life

Alfred recruited Asser from the monastery of St Davids in Dyfed around 885, as part of his programme of reviving learning by bringing scholars to his court. Asser divided his time between Wales and Wessex, was given the monasteries of Congresbury and Banwell and later Exeter, and was consecrated Bishop of Sherborne before 900. He helped the king in his studies and translation projects and acted as an intermediary with the Welsh rulers who submitted to Alfred.

The Life of King Alfred

Asser's biography combines a chronicle of events to 887, drawn in part from the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, with personal anecdotes about Alfred's character, piety, illnesses and efforts to learn Latin. It famously recounts how Alfred's mother offered a book of English poetry to whichever of her sons could first learn it, and how the king learned to read. The unique medieval manuscript (Cotton Otho A.xii) was destroyed in the Cotton library fire of 1731, but transcripts survive. Although its authenticity was once questioned, most historians now accept the work as genuine.
