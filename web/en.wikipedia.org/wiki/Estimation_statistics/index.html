Estimation statistics

Estimation statistics, or simply estimation, is a data analysis framework that uses a combination of effect sizes, confidence intervals, precision planning and meta-analysis to plan experiments, analyse data and interpret results. It is distinct from null hypothesis significance testing (NHST), which is considered to be less informative. Estimation statistics is sometimes referred to as the new statistics, a phrase coined by psychologist Geoff Cumming.

History

Although estimation methods have been used for many years, the approach gained new visibility in the early 2010s as concerns grew about the replication crisis in psychology and biomedical research. In 2011, Geoff Cumming published Understanding the New Statistics, and in 2013 the journal Psychological Science changed its author guidelines to encourage the use of estimation over significance testing. In 2019, the journal Nature published an influential comment, signed by more than 800 scientists, calling for the retirement of the concept of "statistical significance".

Methods

The central quantity in estimation statistics is the effect size — for example, the difference between two means — reported together with its confidence interval, which expresses the precision of the estimate. A widely used visualisation is the Gardner–Altman plot, in which the raw observations for two groups are shown beside a floating axis displaying the effect size and its bootstrapped confidence interval. Cumming plots display multiple effect sizes on a shared scale. Modern tools include resampling methods such as the bootstrap to construct confidence intervals without distributional assumptions.

Software

Free tools include the ESCI software accompanying Cumming's books and the DABEST packages for Python and R, which generate estimation plots automatically. Journals such as eNeuro and Nature have promoted estimation plots as replacements for traditional bar graphs with significance stars.
