Cool Britannia

Cool Britannia was a name given to a period of increased pride in British culture during the mid-to-late 1990s, inspired by the Swinging Sixties. It emphasized the resurgence of British music, fashion, art, and film, and coincided with the rise of Britpop, the Young British Artists, and the election of Tony Blair's New Labour government in 1997.

Origins

The phrase is a play on "Rule, Britannia!", the patriotic song. It had earlier been used as the title of a 1967 song by the Bonzo Dog Doo-Dah Band and as an ice-cream flavour name registered in 1996, but it entered wide circulation through magazine features — notably Newsweek's 1996 declaration that London was "the coolest city on the planet" and Vanity Fair's March 1997 "London Swings Again" issue featuring Liam Gallagher and Patsy Kensit.

Culture

The era was associated with Britpop bands such as Oasis and Blur, the Spice Girls and "girl power," designers like Alexander McQueen, artists such as Damien Hirst and Tracey Emin, and films including Trainspotting and Four Weddings and a Funeral. Noel Gallagher's visit to a Downing Street reception in 1997 became an emblem of the fusion of pop culture and politics.

Decline and legacy

By the early 2000s the label had faded, criticized as a marketing construct and overshadowed by political disillusionment. It remains a shorthand for 1990s British cultural optimism and is periodically revived in discussions of national branding.
