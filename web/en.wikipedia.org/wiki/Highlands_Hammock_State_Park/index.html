Highlands Hammock State Park

Highlands Hammock State Park is a 9,000-acre Florida State Park located near Sebring in Highlands County. Opened in 1931, it was one of Florida's first state parks — established four years before the formal creation of the state park system — and preserves one of the state's largest tracts of old-growth hardwood hammock.

Features

The park is best known for its ancient oak hammock, with live oaks over a thousand years old, including one often cited among the largest in Florida. Nine trails and an elevated boardwalk cross cypress swamps, pine flatwoods, scrub and bayheads. Wildlife includes alligators, white-tailed deer, bobcats, otters and the occasional Florida black bear or panther. The park's museum houses the Civilian Conservation Corps museum for Florida; CCC workers built much of the park's infrastructure in the 1930s, and the park is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Recreation

Visitors can camp, picnic, bicycle a paved loop road, ride equestrian trails and take ranger-led tram tours through areas closed to private vehicles.
