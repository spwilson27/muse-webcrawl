Constitution of Bolivia

The Constituent Assembly that drafted the 2009 constitution met in Sucre and Oruro amid intense political conflict.

The Constitution of Bolivia (Constitución Política del Estado) is the supreme law of the Plurinational State of Bolivia. The current constitution was drafted by an elected Constituent Assembly in 2006–2007, approved in a national referendum on 25 January 2009 with about 61% of the vote, and promulgated on 7 February 2009. It replaced the 1967 constitution (as amended in 1994 and 2004) and renamed the country from "Republic of Bolivia" to the "Plurinational State of Bolivia".

Content

The constitution, of 411 articles, redefines Bolivia as a unitary, plurinational, communitarian and decentralised state. It recognises 36 indigenous nations and their languages as official alongside Spanish, guarantees indigenous autonomies and customary justice, reserves state control over natural resources such as hydrocarbons and lithium, and establishes an extensive catalogue of rights, including to water, food, health, education and a healthy environment. Catholicism lost its former status as official religion, the state being declared independent of religion while respecting freedom of worship. Sucre remains the constitutional capital and seat of the judiciary, while La Paz is the seat of government and legislature. A controversial Constitutional Court ruling in 2017 held that term limits infringed political rights, effectively voiding the constitution's re-election limits — a decision at the centre of the 2019 political crisis.

Background

Bolivia has had numerous constitutions since its first in 1826, drafted under Simón Bolívar's influence. The 2009 text emerged from the "process of change" led by President Evo Morales and the MAS party, and from long-standing indigenous and social-movement demands following the 2000 Water War and 2003 Gas War. Debates over its provisions — especially regional autonomy for the eastern departments and the two-thirds rule in the Assembly — produced violent clashes in Sucre in 2007 before a negotiated text was passed by Congress in October 2008 and put to referendum.
