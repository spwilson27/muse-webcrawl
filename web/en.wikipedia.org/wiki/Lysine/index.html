Lysine

Lysine
Symbol: Lys, K
Formula: C6H14N2O2
Codons: AAA, AAG
Essential amino acid: Yes

Lysine (symbol Lys or K) is an α-amino acid used in the biosynthesis of proteins. It is an essential amino acid in humans, meaning it cannot be synthesized by the body and must be obtained from the diet. It contains an α-amino group, an α-carboxylic acid group, and a side chain lysyl group that classifies it as a basic, positively charged amino acid at physiological pH.

Biological roles

Lysine is important for protein synthesis, the production of carnitine, collagen cross-linking, and calcium absorption. It is the precursor of the ketogenic amino acid derivatives in its catabolic pathway. Post-translational modifications of lysine residues — acetylation, methylation, ubiquitination, and SUMOylation — are central to epigenetic regulation of histones.

Dietary sources and production

Good sources include meat, fish, dairy, eggs, and legumes. Cereals are typically low in lysine, making it the limiting amino acid in many plant-based diets. Industrial L-lysine is produced by fermentation with Corynebacterium glutamicum and is widely used as a feed additive for livestock.

History

Lysine was first isolated from casein in 1889 by the German chemist Edmund Drechsel, and its structure was established a few years later.
