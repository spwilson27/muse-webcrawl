Warren Hastings

Born6 December 1732, Churchill, Oxfordshire
Died22 August 1818, Daylesford, Gloucestershire
OfficeGovernor-General of India, 1773–1785

Warren Hastings (1732–1818) was a British colonial administrator who became the first Governor-General of India, serving from 1773 to 1785 under the East India Company. He laid the administrative foundations of the British Empire in India, centralising revenue collection, reforming the courts and codifying aspects of Hindu and Muslim law.

Career in India

Hastings arrived in Calcutta in 1750 as a clerk of the East India Company and rose through its service, becoming governor of Bengal in 1772 and, under the Regulating Act of 1773, the first Governor-General. His administration weathered the First Anglo-Maratha War and the Second Anglo-Mysore War, greatly extending Company influence, and he dealt with the Rohilla War and the fiscal crisis of Bengal. He was a patron of Oriental scholarship, encouraging the Asiatic Society founded by William Jones and the first English translation of the Bhagavad Gita.

Impeachment and legacy

After returning to England, Hastings was impeached by Parliament in 1788 on charges of corruption and misconduct, championed by Edmund Burke. The trial in Westminster Hall lasted seven years and ended in his acquittal in 1795. Historians continue to debate his legacy: he consolidated British rule in India and supported Indian learning, but his government was also extractive and implicated in wartime famine and coercive revenue demands.
