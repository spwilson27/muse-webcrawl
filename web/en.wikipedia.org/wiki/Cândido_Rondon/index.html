Cândido Rondon

Cândido Mariano da Silva Rondon
Born: 5 May 1865, Mimoso, Mato Grosso
Died: 19 January 1958, Rio de Janeiro
Rank: Marshal, Brazilian Army
Known for: Telegraph lines, Roosevelt–Rondon Expedition

Cândido Rondon (1865–1958) was a Brazilian military officer, explorer and indigenist who charted large parts of Brazil's interior, built telegraph lines across the Amazon basin, and founded Brazil's Indian Protection Service.

Career

Of Indigenous Bororo and Portuguese ancestry, Rondon led commissions (1907–1915) laying thousands of kilometres of telegraph line through Mato Grosso and Amazonas, mapping uncharted rivers. His motto — "Die if you must, but never kill" — governed peaceful contact with Indigenous peoples.

Roosevelt expedition

In 1913–14 he co-led the Roosevelt–Rondon Scientific Expedition with former US president Theodore Roosevelt down the River of Doubt, renamed Rio Roosevelt, a gruelling journey that nearly killed Roosevelt.

Legacy

The Brazilian state of Rondônia is named for him, as are numerous species. He was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize for his work protecting Indigenous peoples, though later historians note the assimilationist limits of his protection policies.
