Creative Scotland is the public body that supports the arts, screen and creative industries in Scotland.

Creative Scotland (Scottish Gaelic: Alba Chruthachail) is the development body for the arts and creative industries in Scotland. It is a non-departmental public body of the Scottish Government, established in 2010 under the Public Services Reform (Scotland) Act 2010, taking over the functions of the Scottish Arts Council and Scottish Screen.

Role

Creative Scotland distributes funding from the Scottish Government and the National Lottery to artists, cultural organisations, and screen productions across Scotland. Its stated purpose is to support an environment in which the arts, screen and creative industries can flourish, and it works toward long-term strategies covering funding, advocacy, and sector development.

Funding

The organisation administers several funding programmes, including multi-year funding for regularly funded organisations, open project funds for individuals and organisations, and targeted funds for film and television production. Its Screen Scotland partnership, launched in 2018, was created to drive growth in film and television production and skills in Scotland.

Criticism

Creative Scotland has periodically faced criticism from parts of the sector over funding decisions and administrative processes, notably in 2012, when an open letter signed by prominent Scottish artists prompted a review of the organisation's policies, and in later years over changes to multi-year funding rounds.
