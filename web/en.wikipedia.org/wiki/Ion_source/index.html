Ion source

Ion source
Used inParticle accelerators, mass spectrometers, ion implanters, ion thrusters
Common typesElectron impact, electrospray, plasma (ECR, Penning), surface ionisation, sputter

An ion source is a device that creates atomic and molecular ions. Ion sources are used to form ions for mass spectrometers, optical emission spectrometers, particle accelerators, ion implanters and ion engines. They work by removing electrons from, or adding electrons or ions to, neutral atoms or molecules, and extracting the resulting charged particles with electric fields into a beam or into an analyser.

Types

Electron ionisation (EI) bombards gas-phase molecules with electrons from a hot filament and is standard in gas chromatography–mass spectrometry. Electrospray ionisation (ESI), which earned John Fenn a share of the 2002 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, produces ions from large biomolecules in solution and revolutionised biological mass spectrometry. MALDI desorbs ions from a solid matrix using laser pulses. Plasma sources — including electron cyclotron resonance (ECR) sources, Penning sources and duoplasmatrons — generate intense beams for accelerators and can produce multiply charged ions. Caesium sputter sources produce negative ions, and liquid-metal ion sources provide the finely focused beams used in focused ion beam instruments.

Applications

Nearly every particle accelerator begins with an ion source: CERN's Linac4 uses a radio-frequency hydrogen source producing H− ions, while heavy-ion facilities use ECR sources. In the semiconductor industry, ion implanters dope wafers with boron, phosphorus and arsenic ions. Spacecraft ion thrusters, such as those on NASA's Dawn mission, use gridded electrostatic sources ionising xenon.
