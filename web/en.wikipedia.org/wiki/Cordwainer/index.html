Cordwainer

A cordwainer at work making fine shoes by hand.

A cordwainer is a shoemaker who makes new shoes from leather. The term is historically distinguished from a cobbler, who repairs shoes. The word derives from "cordwain" or "cordovan", the fine leather produced in Córdoba, Spain, originally from goatskin and later from horsehide, which was used for high-quality footwear.

History

In medieval and early modern England, cordwainers were organised in guilds — notably the Worshipful Company of Cordwainers in London — which regulated the trade and restricted who could make new shoes, while cobblers were limited to repairing old ones and working with old leather. Disputes between the two trades over their respective privileges were common and were the subject of legislation.

Modern use

Today the term survives in guild and company names, street names (such as Cordwainer Ward in the City of London), and among bespoke shoemakers who use "cordwainer" to emphasise that they make new shoes rather than merely repair them. The craft involves last-making, clicking (cutting out), closing, welting and finishing, traditionally done largely by hand.
