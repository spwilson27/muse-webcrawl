Binary relation

Binary relation
FieldMathematics (set theory, logic)
DefinitionA set of ordered pairs; a subset of a Cartesian product
Notationx R y when (x, y) ∈ R
Special typesFunctions, equivalence relations, orders

In mathematics, a binary relation associates elements of one set, called the domain, with elements of another set, called the codomain. Formally, a binary relation from a set X to a set Y is a subset of the Cartesian product X × Y — that is, a set of ordered pairs (x, y) with x in X and y in Y. When X = Y the relation is said to be a binary relation on X, which is the most commonly studied case. If (x, y) belongs to the relation R, one writes x R y, read "x is R-related to y."

A binary relation represented as a directed graph.

Examples

Everyday mathematical relations are binary relations: "less than" on the real numbers, "divides" on the integers, "is an element of" between sets, equality, congruence of geometric figures, and "is the parent of" among people. The relation of divisibility on the positive integers is reflexive, antisymmetric, and transitive, making it a partial order; the relation "is congruent modulo n" is an equivalence relation.

Properties

Relations on a single set are classified by properties including: reflexivity (every x is related to itself), symmetry (x R y implies y R x), transitivity (x R y and y R z imply x R z), antisymmetry, and totality or connectedness. Combinations of these yield the most important classes: equivalence relations (reflexive, symmetric, transitive), partial orders (reflexive, antisymmetric, transitive), and total orders. Other important notions include the domain and range of a relation, the inverse (converse) relation, and the composition of two relations.

Functions as relations

A function is a special kind of binary relation in which every element of the domain is related to exactly one element of the codomain. More generally, relations can be represented by Boolean matrices, by directed graphs, or — in database theory — as the tables of a relational database, in which an n-ary relation is the direct generalization of the binary case.
