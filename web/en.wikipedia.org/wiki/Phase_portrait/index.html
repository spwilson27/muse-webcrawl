Phase portrait

A phase portrait is a geometric representation of the trajectories of a dynamical system in the phase plane. Each set of initial conditions is represented by a different curve, or point.

Phase portraits are an invaluable tool in studying dynamical systems. They consist of a plot of typical trajectories in the state space. This reveals information such as whether an attractor, a repellor or a limit cycle is present for the chosen parameter value. The concept of topological equivalence is important in classifying the behaviour of systems by specifying when two different phase portraits represent the same qualitative dynamic behaviour.

Examples

A simple example is the phase portrait of a simple harmonic oscillator, which consists of concentric ellipses around the equilibrium point. Other classical examples include the pendulum, damped oscillations showing spirals toward a focus, and systems exhibiting saddle points.
