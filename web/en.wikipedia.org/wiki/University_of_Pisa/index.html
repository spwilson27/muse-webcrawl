University of Pisa

The University of Pisa (Italian: Università di Pisa) is a public research university in Pisa, Italy, formally established in 1343 by Pope Clement VI, though scholars taught there from the 11th century. It is among Europe's oldest universities.

History

Galileo Galilei studied and later taught mathematics at Pisa. The university was suppressed and revived several times, and under Napoleon was reorganized on French lines, giving rise to the Scuola Normale Superiore.

Today

It enrolls roughly 50,000 students across 20 departments, with strengths in physics, mathematics, computer science — Italy's first computer science degree was established at Pisa — medicine and engineering.
