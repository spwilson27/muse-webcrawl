Keyboard percussion instrument

A keyboard percussion instrument, also known as a bar or mallet percussion instrument, is a pitched percussion instrument which is arranged in a similar pattern to a piano keyboard and is played with a mallet. Although keyboard percussion instruments are notated on a staff, they are not truly keyboard instruments in the mechanical sense, since the player strikes the bars directly rather than pressing keys.

Instruments

Keyboard percussion instruments include:

  • Marimba
  • Xylophone
  • Vibraphone (also called vibraharp)
  • Glockenspiel (also called orchestral bells)
  • Crotales
  • Tubular bells (chimes)

Despite the name, instruments such as the celesta and toy piano are classified as keyboard instruments rather than keyboard percussion, because their keys mechanically actuate hammers rather than the player striking the sound-producing element directly.

Layout

The bars of keyboard percussion instruments are arranged in two rows, corresponding to the white and black keys of a piano keyboard. The "natural" notes are in the front row and the "accidental" notes (sharps and flats) in the raised back row. This layout allows the performer to read standard staff notation and to use techniques derived from piano geography, though the instruments are played with two or four mallets held in the hands rather than with the ten fingers.

Technique

Keyboard percussion instruments are typically played with two mallets, one in each hand, though advanced technique employs four mallets—two in each hand—using grips such as the Stevens grip or the traditional cross grip. Four-mallet technique allows the performer to play chords and independent melodic lines, and much of the modern solo repertoire for marimba and vibraphone depends upon it.
