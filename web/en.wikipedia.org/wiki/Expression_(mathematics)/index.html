Expression (mathematics)

In mathematics, an expression is a finite combination of symbols that is well-formed according to rules that depend on the context. Mathematical symbols can designate numbers (constants), variables, operations, functions, brackets, punctuation, and grouping to help determine order of operations and other aspects of logical syntax. Many authors distinguish an expression from a formula, with expressions denoting mathematical objects and formulas denoting statements about those objects.

Examples

Expressions include arithmetic expressions such as 8x − 5, algebraic expressions such as 3x² − 7xy + c, polynomials, rational expressions, and more complex constructs involving functions such as sin x or integrals such as ∫ x² dx. The evaluation of an expression depends on the values assigned to its free variables; an expression without free variables that can be evaluated to a specific number is sometimes called a closed-form expression.

Syntax and semantics

Expressions are commonly represented as trees, in which the leaves are variables and constants and the internal nodes are operators and function symbols. The study of expressions falls partly within algebra and partly within mathematical logic, where issues of well-formedness, substitution, and equivalence are treated formally. In computer science, expressions are central objects in programming languages and computer algebra systems.
