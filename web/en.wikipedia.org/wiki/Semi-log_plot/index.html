Semi-log plot

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A semi-log plot uses a logarithmic scale on one axis and a linear scale on the other.

In science and engineering, a semi-log plot (or semilogarithmic graph) is a way of visualizing data that are related according to an exponential relationship. One axis — conventionally the vertical axis — is scaled logarithmically, while the other remains linear. Such graphs are useful when one variable covers a very large range of values, or when the relationship being studied is exponential: on a semi-log plot an exponential function appears as a straight line whose slope gives the rate of growth or decay.

Equations

A function of the form y = a·bx becomes, after taking logarithms, log(y) = log(a) + x·log(b), which is the equation of a straight line in x. This makes semi-log paper a natural tool for checking whether data follow exponential growth, radioactive decay, or first-order chemical kinetics, and for reading off half-lives and doubling times.

Uses

Semi-log plots are used throughout the sciences: for plotting the magnitude response in Bode plots in control engineering, for epidemic curves showing case counts over time, for semiconductor device characteristics, and in seismology and finance. When both axes are logarithmic the result is a log–log plot, which instead linearizes power-law relationships.

Reading the axes

On the logarithmic axis, equal distances correspond to equal ratios rather than equal differences; the axis is divided into decades (factors of ten), and there is no zero on a logarithmic scale. Misreading these axes is a common source of error, so gridlines for intermediate values (2, 3, … 9 times each power of ten) are usually drawn on semi-log graph paper.
