Cadherin

Cadherins (named for "calcium-dependent adhesion") are cell adhesion molecules important in forming adherens junctions that let cells adhere to each other. Cadherins are a class of type-1 transmembrane proteins, and they depend on calcium (Ca2+) ions to function; hence their name. Cell-cell adhesion is mediated by extracellular cadherin domains, whereas the intracellular cytoplasmic tail associates with a large number of adaptors and signalling proteins, collectively referred to as the cadherin adhesome.

Structure and function

Classical cadherins have five extracellular cadherin repeats stiffened by bound calcium, a single transmembrane pass, and a conserved cytoplasmic domain that binds β-catenin and p120-catenin; β-catenin in turn links via α-catenin to the actin cytoskeleton. Cadherins on neighbouring cells bind homophilically — E-cadherin binds E-cadherin — a specificity that allows cell sorting during development.

Types

More than 100 cadherin superfamily members are known in humans, including classical cadherins (type I and II), desmosomal cadherins (desmogleins and desmocollins), protocadherins and atypical members such as FAT and flamingo/CELSR. E-cadherin (epithelial) and N-cadherin (neural) are the archetypes, first characterised in the 1970s–80s in the laboratories of Masatoshi Takeichi and others.

Clinical significance

Loss of E-cadherin function is a hallmark of epithelial–mesenchymal transition and tumour invasion; inherited mutations in CDH1 cause hereditary diffuse gastric cancer, leading many carriers to undergo prophylactic gastrectomy. The "cadherin switch" from E- to N-cadherin is associated with increased motility in several cancers.
