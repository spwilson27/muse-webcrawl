Tiridates I of Armenia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tiridates I (Armenian: Տրդատ Ա) was king of Armenia beginning in 53 AD and the founder of the Armenian Arsacid dynasty. A Parthian prince, brother of the Parthian king Vologases I, he was placed on the Armenian throne amid the long Roman–Parthian struggle over the kingdom.

Reign and the settlement with Rome

Roman forces under Corbulo campaigned in Armenia in the 50s AD, and the resulting Treaty of Rhandeia (63 AD) settled the question diplomatically: Tiridates, a Parthian Arsacid, would remain king of Armenia but would receive his crown from the Roman emperor. In 66 AD Tiridates travelled to Rome, where Nero crowned him in a lavish ceremony described by Suetonius and Dio Cassius. A Zoroastrian priest, he famously addressed Nero as a god, declaring that he had come to worship him as he worshipped Mithras. Nero gave him enormous sums of money and craftsmen to rebuild the Armenian capital Artaxata, which Tiridates renamed Neronia in the emperor's honour.

Legacy

The settlement kept the peace between Rome and Parthia over Armenia for some fifty years and established the Arsacid line that ruled Armenia for centuries. Tiridates is also credited by tradition with beginning Armenian interest in Greco-Roman culture, and his coronation journey remains one of the best-documented episodes in early Armenian history.
