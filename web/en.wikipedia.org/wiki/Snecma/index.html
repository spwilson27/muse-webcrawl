Snecma, now operating as Safran Aircraft Engines, is a French aerospace engine manufacturer headquartered in Courcouronnes. The name is an acronym of Société nationale d'études et de construction de moteurs d'aviation ("National Company for the Research and Construction of Aviation Engines").

Founded in 1945 from the nationalisation of Gnome et Rhône, Snecma developed military engines such as the Atar (powering the Mirage family) and the M53 and M88 (powering the Mirage 2000 and Rafale). In civil aviation its CFM International joint venture with GE Aerospace produces the CFM56 — among the best-selling jet engines in history — and its successor, the LEAP engine, which powers the Airbus A320neo family, Boeing 737 MAX and COMAC C919.

Space propulsion

Snecma's Vernon site develops rocket engines including the Vulcain 2 for the Ariane 5 and the Vinci upper-stage engine for Ariane 6.

History

In 2005 Snecma merged with Sagem to form the Safran group. In 2016 the engine business was rebranded Safran Aircraft Engines as part of a group-wide renaming.
