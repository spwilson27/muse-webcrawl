Impeachment of Park Geun-hye

The impeachment of Park Geun-hye, President of South Korea, was the culmination of a political scandal involving interventions by her close associate Choi Soon-sil in state affairs. On 9 December 2016, the National Assembly voted to impeach Park by 234 votes to 56, suspending her powers. On 10 March 2017, the Constitutional Court of Korea unanimously upheld the impeachment, removing Park from office — the first democratically elected South Korean president to be removed in this way.

Background

In October 2016, media investigations revealed that Choi Soon-sil, a private citizen with no official position and the daughter of cult leader Choi Tae-min, had received confidential government documents, edited presidential speeches, and used her influence to extort large donations from Korean conglomerates to foundations she controlled. Revelations triggered the candlelight protests, among the largest demonstrations in South Korean history, drawing millions of participants over successive weekends.

Proceedings

The National Assembly's impeachment motion charged Park with violating the Constitution and laws by allowing Choi to interfere in state affairs, abusing power in pressuring companies for donations, and failing in her duty to protect citizens' lives during the Sewol ferry disaster of 2014. Prime Minister Hwang Kyo-ahn served as acting president during the trial. The Constitutional Court's eight justices voted unanimously to remove Park, finding her actions a grave betrayal of the public trust.

Aftermath

Park was arrested in March 2017 and in 2018 was convicted of abuse of power, bribery and coercion, receiving a prison sentence of 24 years (later combined sentences totalling over 20 years after appeal proceedings). She was pardoned by President Moon Jae-in in December 2021 and released. The impeachment led to a snap presidential election in May 2017, won by Moon Jae-in.
