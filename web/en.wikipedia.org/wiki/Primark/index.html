Primark is an Irish multinational fast-fashion retailer headquartered in Dublin, Ireland, and a subsidiary of Associated British Foods. Founded by Arthur Ryan in 1969 as Penneys, the company operates stores across Europe and the United States, selling clothing, accessories, homeware and beauty products at low price points.

History

The first store opened on Mary Street, Dublin, in 1969 under the name Penneys — a name still used in Ireland. Expansion into the United Kingdom began in 1974; because the American retailer J. C. Penney owned the Penneys trademark outside Ireland, stores elsewhere trade as Primark. Growth accelerated in the 2000s, including the acquisition of sites from collapsed rivals, entry into Spain (2006), Germany and other European markets, and the first US store in Boston in 2015. By the mid-2020s Primark operated more than 400 stores in over a dozen countries.

Business model

Primark's model relies on very large stores, high volumes, minimal advertising and tight supply-chain costs to sustain low prices. Unusually for a major retailer, it long refused to sell online; it introduced click-and-collect trials in the UK in the 2020s while continuing to reject home delivery as incompatible with its price model.

Criticism and sustainability

Like other fast-fashion chains, Primark has faced criticism over labour conditions in its supply chain; it was a customer of factories in the Rana Plaza building that collapsed in Bangladesh in 2013, after which it paid compensation to victims. The company has responded with ethical trade programmes and sustainability commitments, including targets for more sustainably sourced cotton and recyclable packaging, though campaigners question the compatibility of ultra-low prices with environmental sustainability.
