Sexual attraction

Sexual attraction is attraction on the basis of sexual desire or the quality of arousing such interest. It may be experienced toward a person's physical or other traits, and may occur with or without an accompanying wish for emotional or romantic involvement. The pattern of a person's sexual attractions, together with behaviour and identity, forms part of their sexual orientation.

Factors

Research in evolutionary psychology, endocrinology and social psychology has identified many influencing factors, including:

  • Physical features such as facial symmetry, body shape and secondary sex characteristics.
  • Olfactory cues, including body odour and possible pheromone effects.
  • Voice pitch and other auditory cues.
  • Psychological and social factors: familiarity, similarity, reciprocity of liking, status and personality.
  • Hormonal state and context, including the effects of the menstrual cycle reported in some studies.

Components of orientation

Sexual attraction is distinguished from romantic attraction, aesthetic attraction and sensual attraction; people on the asexual spectrum may experience little or no sexual attraction while still experiencing other forms. Scales such as the Kinsey scale and the Klein Sexual Orientation Grid attempt to measure patterns of attraction, fantasy and behaviour separately.
