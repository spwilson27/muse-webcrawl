Civil unrest

Civil unrest is activity arising from a mass act of civil disobedience — such as a demonstration, riot, or general strike — in which the participants become hostile toward authority, and authorities incur difficulties in maintaining public safety and order, over the populace. It is the principal form of civil disorder examined by political scientists and security analysts studying political stability.

Forms

Civil unrest spans a spectrum from peaceful mass protest and occupation movements through strikes and boycotts to rioting, looting, and insurgency. Scholars distinguish unrest aimed at specific grievances from revolutionary unrest that seeks to overthrow the state. Economic shocks, food and fuel prices, unemployment, corruption scandals, and contested elections are among the most frequent precipitants identified in cross-national studies.

Measurement and study

Analysts track unrest using event databases, protest counts, and composite social-unrest indices produced by bodies such as the International Monetary Fund. Research links the likelihood of unrest to inequality, youth unemployment, weakening economic growth, and the speed of information spread via social media, which has lowered the cost of coordinating mass action.
