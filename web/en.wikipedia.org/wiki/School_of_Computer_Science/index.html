TypeAcademic unit within a university
FieldComputer science and related disciplines
FunctionsUndergraduate and postgraduate teaching, research

A school of computer science is an academic unit within a university dedicated to teaching and research in computer science. As the discipline grew out of mathematics and electrical engineering departments in the mid-twentieth century, many universities organised it first as a department and later — reflecting its size and breadth — as a school in its own right, grouping sub-fields such as artificial intelligence, software engineering, human–computer interaction, computer systems, and theoretical computer science.

History

The first dedicated computer science departments were founded in the 1960s, notably at Purdue University (1962) and Stanford (1965). In the United Kingdom and Commonwealth systems, where "school" commonly denotes a grouping of related subjects within a faculty, computer science units at universities such as Manchester, Birmingham, and Nottingham adopted the school model. Some institutions run several specialised schools — Carnegie Mellon University's School of Computer Science, founded in 1988 from its earlier department, is a prominent example, operating as a college-level unit with its own dean and multiple constituent departments and institutes.

Activities

Schools of computer science typically offer bachelor's and master's degrees in computer science and joint programmes with mathematics, engineering, and the sciences, as well as doctoral research. Their research spans algorithms, programming languages, machine learning, robotics, computer vision, cybersecurity, and data science, and they are often major recipients of industrial partnership and research council funding.
