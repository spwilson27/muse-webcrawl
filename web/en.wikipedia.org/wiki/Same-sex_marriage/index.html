Same-sex marriage, also known as gay marriage or marriage equality, is the marriage of two people of the same legal sex. As of the mid-2020s, marriage between same-sex couples is legally performed and recognised in more than 35 countries, home to roughly a fifth of the world's adult population, as well as in numerous subnational jurisdictions.

History

The Netherlands became the first country in the modern era to legalise same-sex marriage, in 2001, followed by Belgium (2003), Spain and Canada (2005), South Africa (2006) and others. In the United States, Massachusetts was the first state to issue licences in 2004, and the Supreme Court's decision in Obergefell v. Hodges (2015) established a nationwide constitutional right. Ireland became the first country to legalise it by popular referendum, in 2015; Taiwan became the first place in Asia to do so, in 2019.

Legal and social issues

Marriage typically confers hundreds of legal rights covering inheritance, hospital visitation, taxation, immigration and parenting. Debates have involved religious freedom, the relationship between marriage and procreation, and the welfare of children. Major medical and psychological associations state that there is no scientific basis for denying marriage to same-sex couples, and studies generally find no detriment to children raised by same-sex parents.

Public opinion

Support for same-sex marriage has risen steadily in most Western countries since the 1990s, with majority support now common in Western Europe, the Americas and parts of East Asia, while opposition remains strong in much of Africa, the Middle East and parts of Eastern Europe. Some jurisdictions instead offer civil unions or registered partnerships, and some that once did have since upgraded them to marriage.
