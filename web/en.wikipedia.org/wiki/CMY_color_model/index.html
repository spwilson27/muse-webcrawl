CMY and CMYK color models

The CMY color model is a subtractive color model in which cyan, magenta and yellow pigments are combined to make other colors. The CMYK color model adds a fourth, black ("key") ink and is the standard process for color printing. In subtractive mixing, inks absorb wavelengths from white light: cyan absorbs red, magenta absorbs green, and yellow absorbs blue.

Why black is added

In theory, equal parts of cyan, magenta and yellow produce black, but real inks give a muddy dark brown, use three times the ink, and slow drying. A separate black ink (K, for "key", since the black plate was often the key plate to which the other colors were registered) improves depth and shadow detail, sharpens text, and reduces cost. This replacement of CMY by black is known as undercolor removal or grey component replacement.

Conversion with RGB

CMYK has a smaller gamut than the additive RGB model used by displays, so converting screen images to print involves gamut mapping and color management with ICC profiles; vivid screen colors may appear duller in print.

Halftoning

Printing presses cannot vary ink density continuously, so CMYK images are printed as patterns of halftone dots of varying size, which the eye blends into continuous tones.
