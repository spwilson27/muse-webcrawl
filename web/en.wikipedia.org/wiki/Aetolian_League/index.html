The Aetolian League (Greek: Κοινὸν τῶν Αἰτωλῶν) was a federal state (koinon) of the tribal communities of Aetolia in central Greece, which rose to become one of the dominant powers of the Hellenistic world in the third and early second centuries BC.

Organisation

The League combined federal institutions with the autonomy of its member cities and tribes. A primary assembly of citizens met at Thermon (the Panaitolika) and at Delphi (the Pylaea), elected a strategos (general) who could not serve consecutive terms, a federal council (boule or synedrion), and a smaller standing committee of delegates (apokletoi). Its federal structure — combining direct democracy in assembly with proportional representation in council — is often cited by historians as an early model of federalism.

History

United for defence against Macedon, the Aetolians famously helped repulse the Galatian invasion of Greece in 279 BC, gaining control of Delphi and a seat on the Amphictyonic Council. In the third century the League expanded across central Greece and into the Peloponnese, rivalling Macedon and the Achaean League. Allied with Rome in the First and Second Macedonian Wars, it was disappointed by the peace settlement, turned against Rome, and was defeated in the Roman–Seleucid War; the peace of 189 BC reduced Aetolia to a Roman dependency, ending its role as an independent power.
