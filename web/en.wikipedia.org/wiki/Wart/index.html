Wart

A wart is a small, rough growth on the skin caused by human papillomavirus (HPV).

Cause: Human papillomavirus
Specialty: Dermatology

A wart is a small, rough, benign growth on the skin or mucous membranes caused by infection with human papillomavirus (HPV). Warts are very common, especially in children and young adults, and are spread by direct contact or through contaminated surfaces, particularly where the skin is broken or moist.

Types

Common warts (verruca vulgaris) usually appear on the hands and knees with a rough, cauliflower-like surface. Plantar warts grow on the soles of the feet and may be painful when walking; flat warts are smaller and smoother and often occur on the face; filiform warts grow around the mouth, nose or beard area; periungual warts form around the nails; and genital warts are a sexually transmitted form caused by particular HPV types.

Treatment

Many warts disappear without treatment within months or a couple of years as the immune system clears the virus. Treatments for persistent or troublesome warts include topical salicylic acid, cryotherapy (freezing with liquid nitrogen), curettage, laser treatment and immunotherapy. No treatment guarantees permanent removal, and warts can recur.
