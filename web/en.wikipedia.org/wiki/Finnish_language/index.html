Finnish language

Finnish (endonym: suomi or suomen kieli) is a Finnic language of the Uralic language family, spoken by the majority of the population of Finland and by ethnic Finns outside of Finland. It is one of the two official languages of Finland, alongside Swedish, and an official minority language in Sweden. Finnish is notable for its agglutinative morphology, extensive case system and vowel harmony.

History

Finnish descends from Proto-Finnic, spoken around the Gulf of Finland some two thousand years ago. The first written Finnish appeared in the 16th century, when Mikael Agricola, bishop of Turku, translated religious texts and created the first Finnish literary standard in his Abckiria (c. 1543). For centuries Finnish was subordinate to Swedish in administration; the 19th-century national-romantic movement, exemplified by Elias Lönnrot's compilation of the epic Kalevala (1835/1849), raised its status, and it became fully equal to Swedish in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Grammar

Finnish is an agglutinative language: grammatical relations are expressed chiefly by suffixes attached to stems. Nouns and adjectives decline in fifteen cases, including the nominative, genitive, partitive, inessive ("in"), elative ("out of"), illative ("into"), adessive ("on/at"), ablative ("from") and allative ("to"). There is no grammatical gender and no definite or indefinite article. Verbs conjugate for person and number, tense and mood, and the negative is expressed with a conjugated negative verb. Vowel harmony restricts which vowels may co-occur in a word, and consonant gradation alternates strong and weak consonant grades in inflection.

Phonology and orthography

Finnish has eight vowels, written a, e, i, o, u, y, ä, ö, and a relatively small consonant inventory. Length is phonemic for both vowels and consonants, and words are almost always stressed on the first syllable. The orthography is highly phonemic: each letter corresponds closely to one sound, and long sounds are written double.

Dialects and related languages

Finnish dialects divide broadly into western and eastern groups. Finnish is closely related to Karelian and Estonian; the latter belongs to the southern branch of Finnic. Meänkieli in Sweden and Kven in Norway are sometimes regarded as separate languages closely related to Finnish.
