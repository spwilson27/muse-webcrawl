The Horsehead Nebula (also known as Barnard 33 or B33) is a small dark nebula in the constellation Orion. The nebula is located just to the south of Alnitak, the easternmost star of Orion's Belt, and is part of the much larger Orion molecular cloud complex. It appears within the bright emission nebula IC 434, against which its dark silhouette is seen.

The nebula was first recorded by Scottish astronomer Williamina Fleming in 1888 on a photographic plate taken at Harvard College Observatory. It is one of the most identifiable nebulae because of its resemblance to a horse's head.

Structure

The dark cloud is composed of cold gas and dust that blocks the light of the ionised hydrogen behind it. The red glow of IC 434 originates from hydrogen gas ionised by the nearby bright star Sigma Orionis. Bright spots at the base of the Horsehead are young stars in the process of formation; evaporating streams of gas stream away from the dense pillar under the radiation of neighbouring massive stars. The nebula's distinctive "head" is a pillar roughly 3.5 light-years tall.

Observation

Despite its fame, the Horsehead is a difficult visual target: it requires a dark sky, a hydrogen-beta filter, and a moderate-aperture telescope, and is far easier to photograph than to see. It has been imaged at wavelengths from optical to infrared by the Hubble Space Telescope and other observatories; an infrared view for Hubble's 23rd anniversary in 2013 showed the nebula translucent and wispy rather than black.
