Vitrification

Vitrification is the transformation of a substance into a glass — an amorphous, non-crystalline solid — typically by rapid cooling of a melt so that crystals cannot form, or by heating materials such as clay until they fuse.

Applications

In cryobiology, vitrification is ultra-rapid cooling of cells or embryos in concentrated cryoprotectants so that water forms an ice-free glass, avoiding damaging ice crystals; it is now standard for oocyte and embryo cryopreservation. In the nuclear industry, high-level radioactive waste is vitrified — incorporated into borosilicate glass — for stable long-term storage. The term also describes the firing of ceramics to produce a dense, glassy, non-porous body, as in vitrified porcelain and clay pipes.

Physics

As a liquid is supercooled, its viscosity rises steeply until molecular motion effectively ceases at the glass transition temperature, freezing in the disordered liquid structure.
