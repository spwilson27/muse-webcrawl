Hispano-Suiza

Hispano-Suiza is a Spanish automotive and engineering company founded in Barcelona in 1904 by Damià Mateu, Marc Birkigt and others. The name means "Spanish-Swiss", reflecting Swiss engineer Birkigt's role. It became famous for luxury cars and aircraft engines.

History

Before World War II, Hispano-Suiza cars — built in Spain and at a French subsidiary in Paris — rivalled Rolls-Royce and Bugatti, famed for models such as the H6. Its V8 aero engines powered many Allied aircraft in World War I. After the Spanish Civil War and WWII the French arm moved into aerospace, later becoming part of Safran.

Legacy

The stork mascot and elegant coachbuilt cars remain icons of the classic era; the brand has been revived for limited hypercars in recent years.
