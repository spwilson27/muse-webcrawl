Tetsuya Theodore Fujita

Tetsuya Theodore Fujita (藤田 哲也, 23 October 1920 – 19 October 1998) was a Japanese-American meteorologist whose research at the University of Chicago revolutionised the study of severe storms. He is best known for developing the Fujita scale (F-scale) for rating tornado intensity from damage, and for discovering microbursts — concentrated downdraughts that are a hazard to aviation. His colleagues nicknamed him "Mr. Tornado".

Career

Fujita studied and taught in Japan, surveying the damage from the atomic bombings to understand blast patterns, before moving to the University of Chicago in 1953 at the invitation of Horace Byers. His analysis of aerial photographs of tornado damage, notably after the 1970 Lubbock tornado and the 1974 Super Outbreak, led him and Allen Pearson to introduce the Fujita scale in 1971.

Contributions

Fujita documented multiple-vortex tornadoes, mapped storm-scale airflows, and in the 1970s–80s demonstrated that microbursts had caused several airliner crashes, work that led to Doppler weather radar deployment at airports. His hand-drawn weather maps remain celebrated for their detail and artistry.
