Median nerve

The median nerve is a major peripheral nerve of the upper limb. It arises from the lateral and medial cords of the brachial plexus (roots C5–T1), runs down the arm alongside the brachial artery, passes through the cubital fossa and forearm, and enters the hand through the carpal tunnel.

Function

The median nerve supplies most of the flexor muscles of the forearm, the thenar muscles of the thumb and the first two lumbricals, and provides sensation to the palmar surface of the thumb, index, middle and half of the ring finger. Its recurrent branch controls fine thumb movements essential to grip.

Clinical significance

Compression of the nerve within the carpal tunnel causes carpal tunnel syndrome, the most common entrapment neuropathy, producing numbness, tingling and weakness in its distribution. Injury higher in the arm can produce the "hand of benediction" posture when attempting to make a fist.
