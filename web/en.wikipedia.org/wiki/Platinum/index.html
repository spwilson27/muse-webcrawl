Platinum is the chemical element with the symbol Pt and atomic number 78. It is a dense, silvery-white precious metal, extremely resistant to corrosion and with a very high melting point (1,768 °C), rarer and usually more valuable than gold.

Platinum is a superb catalyst — it sits at the heart of vehicle catalytic converters and fuel cells — and is used in fine jewellery, laboratory crucibles, electrical contacts and the chemotherapy drug cisplatin. The standard kilogram was long defined by a platinum–iridium cylinder.

History

Pre-Columbian peoples in South America worked platinum, and Spanish colonists dismissed it as 'platina', 'little silver', an impurity in gold. European scientists characterised it in the 18th century; Wollaston and Tennant discovered its sister metals palladium, rhodium, iridium and osmium in platinum ores in 1803–04.

Occurrence

Most platinum comes from South Africa's Bushveld Complex and from Russian deposits, often as a by-product of nickel and copper mining.
