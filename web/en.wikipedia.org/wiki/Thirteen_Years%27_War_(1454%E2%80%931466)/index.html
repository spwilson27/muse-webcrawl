Thirteen Years' War (1454–1466)


Date: 1454–1466
Location: Prussia, Pomerelia, Baltic coast
Parties: Prussian Confederation and Kingdom of Poland vs Teutonic Order
Result: Second Peace of Thorn (1466)

The Thirteen Years' War (1454–1466), also called the War of the Cities, was fought between the Prussian Confederation — allied with the Kingdom of Poland — and the State of the Teutonic Order over control of Prussia.

Course of the war

Prussian cities and nobles, resentful of the Order's taxes and monopolies, rose in 1454 and pledged allegiance to King Casimir IV of Poland. After the Polish defeat at Chojnice, the war became a grinding contest of sieges and mercenary armies, financed heavily by cities such as Gdańsk, Toruń and Elbląg. The Polish victory at Żarnowiec in 1462 turned the tide.

Outcome

Under the Second Peace of Thorn (1466), the western part of Prussia — Royal Prussia, including Gdańsk and Malbork — passed to Poland, while the Order retained eastern Prussia as a Polish fief. The settlement reshaped the Baltic balance of power.
