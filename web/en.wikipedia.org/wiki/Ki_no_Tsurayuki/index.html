Ki no Tsurayuki (Japanese: 紀 貫之, c. 866–945) was a Japanese author, poet, and courtier of the Heian period, best known as the chief compiler of the Kokin Wakashū, the first imperial anthology of Japanese poetry, and as the author of the Tosa Nikki, one of the earliest Japanese diaries. He is counted among the Thirty-six Poetry Immortals.

The Kokinshū and its preface

Commissioned by Emperor Daigo around 905, the Kokin Wakashū ("Collection of Japanese Poems of Ancient and Modern Times") gathered about 1,111 waka poems, mostly in the 31-syllable tanka form. Tsurayuki, as principal editor among four compilers, wrote its celebrated Japanese (kana) preface — the earliest work of poetry criticism in Japanese — which famously defines poetry as that which "takes the human heart as its seed and flourishes into the myriad leaves of words," moving heaven and earth without effort. The anthology set the standards of court poetry for a thousand years, and more than a hundred of Tsurayuki's own poems appear in it and later imperial collections.

Tosa Nikki

Appointed governor of Tosa Province in 930, Tsurayuki recorded his return voyage to Kyoto in 935 in the Tosa Nikki ("Tosa Diary"), written in kana and slyly presented as the work of a woman in his household. Blending travelogue, grief for a daughter who had died in Tosa, and more than fifty poems, it founded the tradition of Japanese poetic diaries and vernacular prose literature later taken up by women writers of the Heian court such as the author of The Pillow Book.
