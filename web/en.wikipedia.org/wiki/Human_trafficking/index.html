Human trafficking

Human trafficking is the trade of humans for the purpose of forced labour, sexual slavery, or commercial sexual exploitation. It is widely regarded as a form of modern-day slavery and is a serious crime and a grave violation of human rights. The United Nations defines human trafficking in the Protocol to Prevent, Suppress and Punish Trafficking in Persons, Especially Women and Children (the Palermo Protocol), adopted in 2000 as a supplement to the United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime.

Human trafficking affects virtually every country in the world.

Definition

The Palermo Protocol defines trafficking in persons as the recruitment, transportation, transfer, harbouring or receipt of persons, by means of the threat or use of force or other forms of coercion, of abduction, of fraud, of deception, of the abuse of power or of a position of vulnerability, or of the giving or receiving of payments or benefits to achieve the consent of a person having control over another person, for the purpose of exploitation. Exploitation includes, at a minimum, the exploitation of the prostitution of others or other forms of sexual exploitation, forced labour or services, slavery or practices similar to slavery, servitude, or the removal of organs.

Forms

Major forms of trafficking include sex trafficking, forced labour, bonded labour or debt bondage, domestic servitude, forced child labour, child soldiers, forced marriage, and organ trafficking. Victims may be men, women, and children of all ages and backgrounds, although women and girls form the majority of detected victims.

Scale

According to the International Labour Organization, forced labour alone generated an estimated US$236 billion in illegal profits annually in a 2024 estimate, and some 27.6 million people were in forced labour on any given day in 2021. The Global Estimates of Modern Slavery, produced jointly by the ILO, Walk Free, and the International Organization for Migration, estimated that 50 million people were living in modern slavery in 2021, including 22 million in forced marriage and 28 million in forced labour.

Causes

Root causes include poverty, lack of employment opportunities, gender inequality, political instability, armed conflict, demand for cheap labour and commercial sex, and corruption. Traffickers often use fraudulent job offers, false promises of education or marriage, and debt to control victims.

International response

In addition to the Palermo Protocol, international responses include the U.S. Trafficking Victims Protection Act of 2000, which requires an annual Trafficking in Persons Report ranking governments on their anti-trafficking efforts, and various regional conventions. Anti-trafficking strategies are commonly organized around the "three Ps": prevention, protection of victims, and prosecution of traffickers, with a fourth "P" — partnership — added later.
