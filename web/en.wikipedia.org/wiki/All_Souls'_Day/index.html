All Souls' Day

All Souls' Day, also called the Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed, is a day of prayer and remembrance for the souls of those who have died, observed on 2 November by Catholic and some other Christian churches, following All Saints' Day (1 November) and All Hallows' Eve.

Date2 November
ObservancesMasses for the dead, cemetery visits, candles, prayers for souls in purgatory
RelatedAll Saints' Day, Day of the Dead, Allhallowtide

History

St Odilo of Cluny instituted an annual commemoration for the dead at Cluny around 998, and the practice spread through the Western Church. In Catholic theology the day is linked to purgatory: the faithful departed undergoing purification benefit from the prayers and Masses of the living, and plenary indulgences may be gained for them.

Customs

Families visit and clean graves, leave flowers (especially chrysanthemums) and light candles. In Poland the day (Zaduszki) is a major public observance with cemeteries glowing with candles; in Mexico it overlaps with the Day of the Dead (Día de los Muertos). Some Anglican and Lutheran churches also observe the day, while Eastern Orthodox churches commemorate the departed on several Saturdays of Souls during the year.
