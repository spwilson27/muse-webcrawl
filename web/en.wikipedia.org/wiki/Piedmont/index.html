Piedmont (Italian: Piemonte, from Latin pedemontium, "at the foot of the mountains") is a region of northwest Italy, one of the country's 20 regions. It borders France, Switzerland and the Italian regions of Lombardy, Liguria, Emilia-Romagna and Aosta Valley. Its capital is Turin (Torino). Surrounded on three sides by the Alps and Apennines, it contains the source of the Po, Italy's longest river.

Geography

Piedmont's landscape ranges from high Alpine peaks — including Monte Rosa and Gran Paradiso massifs — through the vineyard hills of the Langhe, Roero and Monferrato (inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014) to the rice-growing plains of Vercelli and Novara, the largest rice-producing area in Europe. The region includes part of Lake Maggiore and Lake Orta.

History

In Roman times the area was inhabited by Celtic-Ligurian peoples such as the Taurini. From the 11th century it came progressively under the House of Savoy, whose core state it became; the Savoy monarchy moved its capital to Turin in 1563. Piedmont formed the political and military engine of Italian unification: the Kingdom of Sardinia, ruled from Turin by the Savoy kings, led the Risorgimento under Cavour, and Turin became the first capital of united Italy (1861–1865). Victor Emmanuel II, the first king of Italy, was a Savoy monarch.

Economy and culture

Piedmont is one of Italy's most industrialised and prosperous regions. Turin hosts the headquarters of Fiat (now part of Stellantis), and the region is known for automotive engineering, aerospace, textiles (Biella), and food and wine: the great red wines Barolo and Barbaresco, Asti sparkling wines, white truffles from Alba, hazelnuts, and chocolate from Turin. Juventus, one of Italy's most successful football clubs, is based in Turin, which also hosted the 2006 Winter Olympics. Regional languages include Piedmontese, Occitan, Franco-Provençal and Walser German.
