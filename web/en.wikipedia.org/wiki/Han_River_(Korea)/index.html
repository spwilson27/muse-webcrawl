The Han River (Korean: 한강, Hangang) is a major river in South Korea, formed by the confluence of the Namhan River (South Han) and the Bukhan River (North Han) east of Seoul. It flows westward through the capital and empties into the Yellow Sea near the border with North Korea, after a course of about 514 kilometres.

Geography

The river passes through the heart of Seoul, where it is unusually wide — around a kilometre in places — and is spanned by dozens of bridges. Islands in the river include Yeouido, home to the National Assembly and a major financial district, and Bamseom. Extensive Hangang Parks line its banks and are heavily used for cycling, picnics and festivals.

History

Control of the Han valley was contested by Korea's Three Kingdoms because of its strategic and agricultural importance. In modern usage, "the Miracle on the Han River" refers to South Korea's rapid post-war economic transformation. Since the division of Korea, the river's estuary lies near the Northern Limit Line and is a restricted area; the river's lowest reaches form part of a neutral zone at the mouth adjoining the Demilitarized Zone.
