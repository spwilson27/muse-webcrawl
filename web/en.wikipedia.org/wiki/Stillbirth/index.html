Stillbirth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stillbirth is typically defined as fetal death at or after 20 or 28 weeks of pregnancy, depending on the jurisdiction. It results in a baby born without signs of life. A stillbirth can often result in the feeling of guilt or grief in the mother. The term is in contrast to miscarriage, which is an early pregnancy loss, and sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS), where the baby dies a short time after being born alive.

Often, the cause is unknown. Causes may include pregnancy complications such as pre-eclampsia and birth complications, problems with the placenta or umbilical cord, birth defects, infections such as malaria and syphilis, and poor health in the mother. Risk factors include a mother's age over 35, smoking, drug use, use of assisted reproductive technology, and first pregnancy. Stillbirth may be suspected when no fetal movement is felt. Confirmation is by ultrasound.

Definition

Definitions of stillbirth vary by country and organization. The World Health Organization defines stillbirth as a baby born with no signs of life at or after 28 weeks' gestation, while the United States commonly uses 20 weeks as the threshold. In the United Kingdom, stillbirth is registered after 24 completed weeks of pregnancy.

Causes and risk factors

Known causes include:

  • Placental problems, including placental abruption and placenta praevia
  • Umbilical cord accidents
  • Congenital anomalies and genetic conditions
  • Infections, including syphilis, malaria, and listeriosis
  • Maternal conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, and pre-eclampsia
  • Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy

Women who are over 35 years old, who smoke, use drugs, or have had a previous stillbirth are at increased risk. About half of stillbirths worldwide occur during labor and delivery (intrapartum), particularly in low- and middle-income countries.

Diagnosis and management

Stillbirth is usually diagnosed by ultrasound after a mother reports reduced or absent fetal movement. Delivery is generally recommended shortly after diagnosis, most often by induction of labour. Vaginal birth is preferred in most cases, as caesarean section carries additional risks without benefit to a living child. Psychological support and bereavement care are important parts of management.

Epidemiology

Worldwide, there were an estimated 2 million stillbirths in 2019, a rate of about 13.9 per 1,000 total births, with wide variation between countries. Rates have declined significantly in high-income countries over recent decades, largely because of improvements in antenatal care, monitoring, and timely delivery.
