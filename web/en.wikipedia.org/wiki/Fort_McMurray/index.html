Fort McMurray is an urban service area in the Regional Municipality of Wood Buffalo in north-east Alberta, Canada. Situated in the boreal forest at the confluence of the Athabasca and Clearwater rivers, it is the hub of Canada's oil sands industry, one of the largest industrial developments in the world.

History

The area was long inhabited by Cree peoples. Explorer Peter Pond arrived in 1778, and in 1870 the Hudson's Bay Company established a trading post named for its chief factor, William McMurray. For a century the community remained a small fur-trade and transportation centre, linked south by rail from 1915. Development of the Athabasca oil sands transformed it from the 1960s onward, beginning with the Great Canadian Oil Sands plant in 1967 and the Syncrude consortium in 1978, producing boom-and-bust growth tied to world oil prices.

2016 wildfire

In May 2016 a wildfire swept into the city, forcing the evacuation of some 88,000 people — the largest wildfire evacuation in Canadian history — and destroying about 2,400 homes and buildings. The fire, nicknamed "the Beast", burned for months and shut down oil sands production. A further disaster struck in April 2020, when flooding of the Athabasca River forced another mass evacuation and caused extensive damage.

Economy

Oil sands extraction and upgrading dominate the economy, with major employers including Suncor, Syncrude, CNRL and Cenovus. A large fly-in/fly-out workforce supplements the resident population. The city is connected to Edmonton by Highway 63, twinned after years of advocacy over its accident record.
