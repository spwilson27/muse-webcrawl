March of Dimes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

March of Dimes
Founded: 1938, by Franklin D. Roosevelt
Original mission: Combat polio
Current focus: Health of mothers and babies

The March of Dimes is an American nonprofit organisation founded in 1938 by President Franklin D. Roosevelt — himself a polio survivor — as the National Foundation for Infantile Paralysis, to combat poliomyelitis (see poliomyelitis).

Its fundraising model was revolutionary: instead of seeking a few wealthy donors, it asked millions of ordinary Americans to contribute a dime each. Comedian Eddie Cantor coined the name "March of Dimes", playing on the newsreel title The March of Time. The foundation funded the research of Jonas Salk and the enormous 1954 vaccine field trial, then supported Sabin's oral vaccine as well.

With polio defeated in the United States, the organisation repurposed itself — first to birth defects and, since 2003, to preventing premature birth and improving the health of mothers and babies, its mission today.
