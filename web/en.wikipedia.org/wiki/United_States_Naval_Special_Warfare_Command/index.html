The United States Naval Special Warfare Command (NAVSPECWARCOM or WARCOM) is the naval component of United States Special Operations Command and the Navy's special operations force. Headquartered at Naval Amphibious Base Coronado in San Diego, California, it organizes, trains, and equips the Navy SEAL teams, SEAL Delivery Vehicle teams, and Special Warfare Combatant-craft Crewmen (SWCC) units.

History

Naval special warfare traces its origins to the Naval Combat Demolition Units and Underwater Demolition Teams of World War II, and to the Scouts and Raiders. The SEAL teams were established by President John F. Kennedy in 1962. Following the failed Operation Eagle Claw hostage rescue in 1980 and the subsequent creation of U.S. Special Operations Command in 1987, Naval Special Warfare Command was commissioned on 16 April 1987 to serve as the Navy component of the new joint command.

Organization

The command comprises several Naval Special Warfare Groups, including:

  • Group ONE (Coronado) — SEAL Teams 1, 3, 5, and 7
  • Group TWO (Little Creek, Virginia) — SEAL Teams 2, 4, 8, and 10
  • Group THREE — SEAL Delivery Vehicle teams and logistics support
  • Group FOUR — Special Boat Teams operating high-speed watercraft with SWCC crews
  • Group TEN and ELEVEN — support, intelligence, and reserve components

The Naval Special Warfare Center, also at Coronado, runs the famous Basic Underwater Demolition/SEAL (BUD/S) training pipeline, notorious for its "Hell Week." The Naval Special Warfare Development Group (DEVGRU, formerly SEAL Team Six) is administratively supported by the command but operationally assigned to Joint Special Operations Command.

Operations

Naval special warfare forces have operated in Vietnam, Grenada, Panama, the Persian Gulf tanker wars, Somalia, Afghanistan, and Iraq. In May 2011, operators of the Naval Special Warfare Development Group killed Osama bin Laden in Abbottabad, Pakistan, during Operation Neptune Spear. SEALs also carried out the 2009 rescue of Captain Richard Phillips from Somali pirates.
