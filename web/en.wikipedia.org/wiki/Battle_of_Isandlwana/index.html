The Battle of Isandlwana was fought on 22 January 1879 in the Anglo-Zulu War. A Zulu army of some 20,000 warriors under Ntshingwayo kaMahole overwhelmed and destroyed much of a British column camped near the distinctive hill of Isandlwana, in one of the worst defeats ever suffered by British forces against a technologically less-equipped enemy.

Background

Lord Chelmsford's central column invaded Zululand in January 1879 and camped at Isandlwana without laagering its wagons or entrenching. On 22 January Chelmsford took half the column away to pursue a reported Zulu force, leaving Lieutenant-Colonel Henry Pulleine to defend the camp. The main Zulu army, which had intended to attack the following day, was discovered by a British patrol and attacked at once.

Battle

The Zulus attacked in their classic "horns of the buffalo" formation. The British firing line, armed with Martini-Henry rifles, initially held the frontal assault, but the line was overextended and ammunition resupply faltered; when Colonel Durnford's men on the flank fell back, the horns closed around the camp. The fighting degenerated into hand-to-hand combat, and nearly the entire camp garrison was annihilated.

Aftermath

More than 1,300 British and allied troops were killed. That same evening a Zulu detachment was repulsed at Rorke's Drift. Isandlwana shocked the British public and government, prompting heavy reinforcements; the Zulu capital Ulundi was taken and burned in July 1879, ending the war.
