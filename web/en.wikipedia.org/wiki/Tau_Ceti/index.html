Tau Ceti, Latinised from τ Ceti, is a single star in the constellation Cetus that is spectrally similar to the Sun, although it has only about 78% of the Sun's mass. At a distance of just under 12 light-years from the Solar System, it is a relatively nearby star and the closest solitary G-class star. It is visible to the naked eye with an apparent magnitude of 3.5.

Properties

Tau Ceti is metal-poor compared with the Sun, with roughly one-third to one-half the solar abundance of iron, and it rotates slowly with a period of about 34 days, indicating magnetic quiescence and an age likely greater than the Sun's. Infrared observations with the Spitzer and Herschel space telescopes revealed a surrounding debris disc roughly ten times more massive than the Sun's Kuiper belt, implying frequent collisions and a dynamically active system.

Planetary system

Radial-velocity studies have reported a system of super-Earth-mass planets. Five candidate planets (b through f) were announced in 2012, with estimated masses between about 2 and 6.6 Earth masses; subsequent analyses (notably a 2017 study) strengthened the cases for four planets — e and f lie near the inner and outer edges of the star's habitable zone, though their larger-than-Earth masses and the dense debris disc complicate habitability prospects. None of the planets has been confirmed by transit or direct imaging, and some signals remain debated as possible stellar-activity artefacts.

SETI and culture

Because of its Sun-like nature and proximity, Tau Ceti was one of the two targets of Frank Drake's Project Ozma in 1960, the first modern search for extraterrestrial intelligence; no signals were found. The star's combination of nearness and relative familiarity has made it a fixture of science fiction, from Ursula K. Le Guin's and Kim Stanley Robinson's novels onward.
