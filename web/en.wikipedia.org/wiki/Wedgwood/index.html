Wedgwood

Founded1759, Burslem, Staffordshire, England
FounderJosiah Wedgwood
IndustryPottery, tableware, luxury goods
HeadquartersBarlaston, Stoke-on-Trent

Wedgwood is an English fine china, porcelain, and luxury accessories manufacturer founded on 1 May 1759 by potter and entrepreneur Josiah Wedgwood and his sons. Based in Stoke-on-Trent, Staffordshire, the company became one of the most influential names in British ceramics, known for creamware ("Queen's ware"), black basalt, and above all jasperware — a matte stoneware, most famously in pale blue with applied white classical reliefs — introduced in the 1770s.

History

Josiah Wedgwood's systematic experiments and marketing transformed pottery making: he supplied Queen Charlotte, Catherine the Great of Russia (the 944-piece Frog Service, 1773–74), and aristocratic customers across Europe, and his Etruria factory applied division of labour and early industrial methods. The firm also made the famous antislavery medallion "Am I Not a Man and a Brother" (1787). Wedgwood reproduced the Roman Portland Vase in jasper in 1790 after years of trials.

Later years

The company remained family-run into the 20th century, merged into Waterford Wedgwood in 1987, and passed through administration in 2009 before acquisition by the Finnish Fiskars Group in 2015. Production continues at Barlaston, alongside the Wedgwood Museum collection, one of the most important archives of British ceramics and industrial history.
