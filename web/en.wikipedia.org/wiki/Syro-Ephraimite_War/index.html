The Syro-Ephraimite War took place in the 8th century BCE (around 735–732 BCE), when the kingdoms of Aram-Damascus (Syria) and the northern Kingdom of Israel (Ephraim) formed an alliance and attacked the southern Kingdom of Judah. Their aim was to force Judah's king, Ahaz, into an anti-Assyrian coalition; when he refused, the allies sought to depose him and install a puppet king, the "son of Tabeal".

Background

The Neo-Assyrian Empire under Tiglath-Pileser III was expanding westward. King Rezin of Damascus and King Pekah of Israel organised a coalition of Levantine states to resist Assyria. Ahaz of Judah declined to join, prompting the invasion of Judah described in 2 Kings 16 and Isaiah 7–9.

The war

The allied armies inflicted heavy defeats on Judah; the biblical account reports large numbers of captives taken, though many were returned after the prophet Oded intervened. Jerusalem itself was besieged but not captured. Against the advice of the prophet Isaiah—who delivered the famous "Immanuel" prophecy during the crisis—Ahaz appealed to Tiglath-Pileser III for help, sending tribute from the Temple treasury.

Aftermath

Assyria responded by campaigning against Damascus in 732 BCE: the city fell, Rezin was killed, and Aram was annexed as an Assyrian province. Israel lost Galilee and Gilead, whose populations were deported; Pekah was assassinated and replaced by Hoshea. Judah survived but as an Assyrian vassal, a status that shaped its politics for the rest of the century.
