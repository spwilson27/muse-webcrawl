Silyl enol ether

In organosilicon chemistry, a silyl enol ether is an organic compound with the general structure R2C=CR–O–SiR3. They are important intermediates in organic synthesis, serving as protected, isolable equivalents of enols and enolates of ketones and aldehydes.

Preparation

Silyl enol ethers are generally prepared by reacting a carbonyl compound with a silyl chloride (such as trimethylsilyl chloride, TMSCl, or tert-butyldimethylsilyl chloride, TBSCl) in the presence of a base such as triethylamine, or by deprotonating the carbonyl with a strong base such as lithium diisopropylamide (LDA) and trapping the resulting enolate with the silyl chloride. The choice of conditions can control the regiochemistry of enolisation (kinetic versus thermodynamic product) and, for unsymmetrical ketones, which side of the carbonyl is functionalised.

Reactivity

Silyl enol ethers are nucleophiles at carbon. Their flagship reaction is the Mukaiyama aldol reaction, in which a silyl enol ether adds to an aldehyde or ketone activated by a Lewis acid. They also participate in Michael additions, halogenations, and cross-coupling reactions. Mild acid or fluoride ion cleaves the silicon–oxygen bond, regenerating the carbonyl or enolate.
