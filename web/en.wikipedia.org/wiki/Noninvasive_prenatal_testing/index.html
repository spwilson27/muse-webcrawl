Noninvasive prenatal testing
Other namesNIPT; cell-free DNA screening
PurposeScreening for fetal chromosomal abnormalities

Overview

Noninvasive prenatal testing (NIPT) is a method of determining the risk that a fetus will be born with certain genetic abnormalities. The test analyses small fragments of cell-free DNA circulating in the blood of a pregnant person, much of which originates from the placenta and reflects the genetic makeup of the fetus.

NIPT can be performed from around ten weeks of gestation and requires only a maternal blood sample, so it carries no risk of miscarriage, unlike invasive diagnostic procedures such as amniocentesis and chorionic villus sampling.

Applications

NIPT is most commonly used to screen for trisomy 21 (Down syndrome), trisomy 18 (Edwards syndrome), and trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome). It has a high detection rate and a low false-positive rate for these conditions. Some tests also screen for sex chromosome abnormalities and selected microdeletion syndromes.

Because NIPT is a screening test rather than a diagnostic test, positive results are usually followed by confirmatory diagnostic testing.

History

The presence of fetal DNA in maternal plasma was demonstrated in 1997 by Dennis Lo and colleagues. Commercial NIPT services became available in 2011, and the test has since been adopted widely in obstetric practice, reducing the number of invasive procedures performed.
