Treaty of Vereeniging

Signed31 May 1902
LocationMelrose House, Pretoria
PartiesBritish Empire, South African Republic, Orange Free State

The Treaty of Vereeniging was a peace treaty that ended the Second Boer War on 31 May 1902. It was signed between the British Empire and representatives of the South African Republic (Transvaal) and the Orange Free State. Although named after the town of Vereeniging in Transvaal, the treaty was actually negotiated and signed at Melrose House in Pretoria.

Terms

  • The Boer republics accepted British sovereignty, becoming the Transvaal Colony and the Orange River Colony;
  • All Boer combatants were to lay down their arms and take an oath of allegiance to Edward VII; returning burghers were guaranteed their liberty and property;
  • The British committed £3,000,000 for reconstruction and resettlement, and promised eventual self-government;
  • Dutch was permitted in schools and courts;
  • Critically, the question of native (Black) franchise was deferred until after the introduction of self-government — a provision that opened the way for white-minority rule and later apartheid.

Negotiations

Boer delegates debated the terms at Vereeniging in mid-May 1902; a committee including Jan Smuts, Louis Botha and Christiaan de Wet negotiated with Lord Kitchener and Lord Milner. The final vote among Boer delegates was 54 to 6 in favour of accepting the terms.

Aftermath

Responsible government was granted to the Transvaal in 1906 and the Orange River Colony in 1907, and in 1910 the colonies were united with the Cape Colony and Natal into the Union of South Africa, with former Boer generals Botha and Smuts among its first leaders.
