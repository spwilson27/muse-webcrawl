The Sultan Ahmed Mosque (Turkish: Sultan Ahmet Camii), popularly known as the Blue Mosque because of the blue tiles surrounding the walls of its interior, is an Ottoman-era historical imperial mosque located in Istanbul, Turkey. It was constructed between 1609 and 1616 during the rule of Ahmed I, whose tomb is located on the site. Hand-painted blue tiles adorn the mosque's interior walls, and at night the mosque is bathed in blue as lights frame its five main domes, six minarets and eight secondary domes.

The mosque sits next to the Hagia Sophia, the principal mosque of Istanbul until the Blue Mosque's construction, and another popular tourist site. It incorporates some Byzantine Christian elements of the neighbouring Hagia Sophia with traditional Islamic architecture and is considered to be the last great mosque of the classical period. The architect, Sedefkâr Mehmed Ağa, synthesized the ideas of his master Sinan, aiming for overwhelming size, majesty and splendour.

History

After the Peace of Zsitvatorok and the unsuccessful war with Persia, Sultan Ahmed I decided to build a large mosque in Istanbul to reassert Ottoman power. It would be the first imperial mosque built in forty years. Whereas his predecessors had paid for their mosques with the spoils of war, Ahmed I procured funds from the Treasury, because he had not gained remarkable victories. The construction started in 1609, under the supervision of the sultan himself. The mosque was built on the site of the palace of the Byzantine emperors, in front of the basilica Ayasofya and the hippodrome, a site of significant symbolic meaning.

The mosque was inaugurated in 1616, though the tiles were not fully installed until 1617. Ahmed I died shortly after its completion, aged only 27, and was buried in a mausoleum on the site.

Architecture

The Sultan Ahmed Mosque has five main domes, six minarets, and eight secondary domes. The design is the culmination of two centuries of Ottoman mosque development. The central dome is 23.5 metres (77 ft) in diameter and 43 metres (141 ft) high at its central point. The four semi-domes are flanked by four smaller domes, followed by yet smaller semi-domes in the corners.

The six minarets were an innovation at the time: only the mosque in Mecca had six minarets, and legend has it that the sultan ordered a seventh minaret built at Mecca to preserve its pre-eminence. The minarets have two or three balconies (şerefe) with stalactite supports.

Interior

The interior of the mosque is lined with more than 20,000 handmade ceramic tiles in more than fifty different tulip designs; the tiles at lower levels are traditional in design, while at gallery level their design becomes flamboyant with representations of flowers, fruit and cypresses. These were made under the supervision of the Iznik master potter Kasap Haci and Baris Efendi. The blue paint of the tiles has faded and dulled over time, and the light that enters through the 260 windows has diminished.
