Powder River (Wyoming and Montana)

Physical characteristics
Source:Bighorn Mountains, Wyoming
Mouth:Yellowstone River, Montana
Length:approx. 375 mi (604 km)
Basin:Powder River Basin

The Powder River is a tributary of the Yellowstone River, approximately 375 miles (604 km) long, flowing through north-eastern Wyoming and south-eastern Montana in the United States. It drains the Powder River Basin, a region lying between the Bighorn Mountains and the Black Hills, named for the powdery sand along its banks that early explorers likened to gunpowder.

Course

The river is formed by the confluence of several forks near Kaycee, Wyoming, draining the eastern slopes of the Bighorns, and flows generally northward past Broadus, Montana, joining the Yellowstone River near Terry, Montana. Its tributaries include the Little Powder River, Crazy Woman Creek, and Clear Creek.

History and resources

The Powder River country was contested ground in the wars between the United States and the Lakota, Cheyenne, and Arapaho in the 1860s and 1870s, including Red Cloud's War along the Bozeman Trail. The surrounding basin is rich in coal and coal-bed methane, and has become one of the largest coal-producing regions in the United States.
