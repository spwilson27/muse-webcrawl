Job satisfaction

Job satisfaction describes how content an individual is with their job — the extent to which they like or dislike aspects of their work. It is a central topic in industrial and organisational psychology and is linked to productivity, absenteeism, turnover and well-being.

Theories and measurement

Edwin Locke defined job satisfaction as a pleasurable emotional state resulting from the appraisal of one's job. Frederick Herzberg's two-factor theory distinguished hygiene factors (pay, conditions) from motivators (achievement, recognition). Common measures include the Job Descriptive Index and the Minnesota Satisfaction Questionnaire, assessing facets such as pay, promotion, supervision, co-workers and the work itself.

Correlates

Research finds modest positive relationships between job satisfaction and job performance and organisational citizenship behaviour, and negative relationships with withdrawal behaviours such as quitting and absence. Dispositional factors, job characteristics and perceived fairness all influence satisfaction levels.
