Alfonso IX (15 August 1171 – 24 September 1230) was King of León and Galicia from the death of his father Ferdinand II in 1188 until his own death. He is remembered for convening in 1188 the Cortes of León, which included representatives of the towns and is often cited as one of the earliest parliamentary bodies in Europe, and for his long, turbulent rivalry with his cousin Alfonso VIII of Castile.

Reign

Alfonso's reign was marked by conflict with Castile, which seized Leonese border fortresses, and by campaigns against the Almohads in al-Andalus. In 1195 he was defeated alongside Muslim allies at the Battle of Alarcos' aftermath politics; his alliance with the Almohads led to his excommunication by Pope Celestine III. He later joined the Christian victory at Las Navas de Tolosa in 1212 only indirectly, having exploited the campaign to recover territory from Castile. In his final years he conquered Cáceres (1229) and Mérida and Badajoz (1230), greatly extending León into Extremadura.

Marriages and succession

His first marriage, to Theresa of Portugal, was annulled for consanguinity; his second, to Berenguela of Castile, was likewise annulled, but their son Ferdinand was later recognised as legitimate. On Alfonso's death in 1230, Ferdinand inherited both León and Castile, permanently uniting the two crowns.
