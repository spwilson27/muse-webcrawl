Graphing calculator

A graphing calculator (also graphics calculator or graphic display calculator) is a handheld computer capable of plotting graphs, solving simultaneous equations, and performing other tasks with variables. Most graphing calculators are also programmable, allowing the user to write programs for science, engineering and education applications. Because they have large displays that can show several lines of text and calculations at once, they blur the line between calculator and handheld computer.

History

The first commercial graphing calculator was the Casio fx-7000G, released in 1985. Sharp and HP followed, HP's HP-28C (1987) adding symbolic computation (a computer algebra system, or CAS). Texas Instruments entered with the TI-81 in 1990, aimed at algebra and precalculus classes, and its successors — the TI-82, TI-83, TI-84 Plus family, and the TI-Nspire — came to dominate North American classrooms, while Casio and HP remained strong elsewhere. Later models added colour backlit screens (Casio Prizm, TI-84 Plus C, HP Prime), rechargeable batteries, exact arithmetic and touchpads.

Capabilities

Typical features include function, parametric and polar graphing with trace and zoom; tables of values; statistical plots and regression; matrix and list operations; numerical equation solving; calculus operations (derivatives and integrals, symbolic in CAS models); spreadsheets; data collection from external probes; and programming in a BASIC-like language, and on some models in C, assembly or Python. Connectivity by USB allows sharing programs and data, linking classrooms, and connecting to projectors.

In education

Graphing calculators became standard equipment in secondary mathematics in many countries. Examination policies vary: some tests permit them, others restrict them or require non-CAS "exam modes" that disable communication and certain functions. Their long dominance has been debated as smartphone apps and free web tools (such as Desmos) have offered comparable power, though the dedicated devices remain favoured in controlled exam settings.
