Child benefit

Child benefit or children's allowance is a social security payment which is distributed to the parents or guardians of children, teenagers and in some cases, young adults. A number of different countries operate different versions of the program.

Purpose

In most countries, child benefit is means-tested or subject to conditions such as school attendance, and the amount varies with the number of children, their ages, and household income. In some countries, such as the United Kingdom, the benefit is paid universally (though subject to a tax charge for higher earners). The United Kingdom introduced Child Benefit in 1977, replacing Family Allowances and child tax allowances. Canada provides the Canada Child Benefit, introduced in 2016, while the United States offers a Child Tax Credit rather than a direct allowance.

Rationale

Supporters argue that child benefits reduce child poverty, help families meet the costs of raising children, and provide income stability. Critics debate their cost, targeting, and effects on labor supply.
