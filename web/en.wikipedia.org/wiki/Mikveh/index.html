Mikveh

Mikveh
Hebrew: מִקְוֶה, "collection (of water)"
Religion: Judaism
Purpose: Ritual immersion for purification

A mikveh (also spelled mikvah) is a bath used for ritual immersion in Judaism to achieve ritual purity. Most forms of ritual impurity in traditional Jewish law can be purified by immersion in a mikveh containing naturally gathered water.

Requirements

Halakhic requirements specify that a mikveh hold at least 40 se'ah (roughly 575–1000 litres by various estimates) of water that has not been drawn in a vessel — traditionally rainwater collected and channelled into the pool. Modern mikva'ot typically combine a rainwater reservoir with a heated, filtered immersion pool connected by an aperture.

Uses

Immersion is practised by married women after menstruation and childbirth, by converts to Judaism, before the Sabbath and festivals by some men, and for the purification of new utensils (tevilat kelim). Mikva'ot from the Second Temple period survive archaeologically throughout Israel, including at Masada and in Jerusalem.
