Newcastle upon Tyne, commonly simply Newcastle, is a city in Tyne and Wear, England, situated on the north bank of the River Tyne. It is the most populous city in North East England and a major centre for business, culture and nightlife.

History

The Romans built the fort of Pons Aelius at the eastern end of Hadrian's Wall. The Norman castle built by Robert Curthose in 1080 gave the town its name. Newcastle grew rich on the coal trade — "carrying coals to Newcastle" became a proverb for a pointless task — and in the nineteenth century on shipbuilding and heavy engineering along the Tyne.

Landmarks

The city's bridges, including the Tyne Bridge (1928) and the Gateshead Millennium Bridge, link it to Gateshead across the river. Other landmarks include Newcastle Castle, St Nicholas Cathedral, Grey Street — often voted among Britain's finest streets — and St James' Park, home of Newcastle United F.C.
