Tactile hallucination

Formication — the feeling of insects crawling on the skin — is a common tactile hallucination

A tactile hallucination, also called a haptic hallucination, is a false perception of touch or of an object or event on or beneath the skin, occurring in the absence of any actual stimulus. The person experiences sensations such as being touched, pinched, burned, or having something move on or under the skin, although nothing is physically present.

Formication

The best-known form is formication, the sensation of small insects crawling on or under the skin (from the Latin formica, "ant"). Formication is a recognised symptom of stimulant intoxication — it is famously associated with cocaine use, giving rise to the slang term "coke bugs" — and of amphetamine use. It can lead sufferers to scratch and pick at their skin, causing lesions.

Causes

Tactile hallucinations occur in a range of conditions, including schizophrenia and other psychotic disorders, delirium tremens and alcohol withdrawal, stimulant and other drug intoxication or withdrawal, Parkinson's disease and Lewy body dementia, and certain neurological disorders. In delusional parasitosis (Ekbom syndrome), tactile hallucinations combine with the fixed false belief that the body is infested with parasites.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause — for example, antipsychotic medication for psychotic disorders, medically supervised withdrawal for substance-related causes, or treatment of an underlying neurological condition.
