Seudah shlishit

Seudah shlishit (Hebrew: סעודה שלישית, "third meal"), also known in Yiddish as shalosh seudos or shaleshudes, is the third of the three meals traditionally eaten by observant Jews on Shabbat (the Sabbath). It is eaten late on Saturday afternoon, before nightfall brings the Sabbath to a close.

Biblical and rabbinic basis

The custom of eating three meals on the Sabbath is derived by the Talmud from Exodus 16:25, in which Moses says of the manna, "Eat it today," with the word "today" mentioned three times, interpreted as referring to three meals. Unlike the Friday night and Saturday morning meals, seudah shlishit does not begin with Kiddush, although bread (or, in some customs, matzah or other mezonot foods) is eaten.

Customs

The meal is typically lighter than the other two and may consist of salads, fish, eggs, fruit, and pastries; in many Ashkenazi communities foods such as herring, egg salad, and chopped liver are traditional. Because it is eaten as daylight fades, the meal is associated in Hasidic tradition with a mood of yearning and spiritual intensity — the time is considered especially auspicious (the ra'ava d'ra'avin, "desire of desires") — and Hasidic Jews often gather around their rebbe's table (tish) singing slow, contemplative hymns (zemirot) as the Sabbath departs. Psalm 23 and other songs are commonly sung. It is customary to extend the meal into the night to prolong the Sabbath.

Following the meal

After nightfall, the Sabbath ends with the Havdalah ceremony, separating the holy day from the week that follows, often immediately preceded or followed in communal settings by a fourth meal, Melaveh Malkah.
