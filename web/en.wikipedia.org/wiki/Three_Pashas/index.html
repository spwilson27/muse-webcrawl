Three Pashas

The Three Pashas
Members: Enver Pasha, Talaat Pasha, Djemal Pasha
In power: 1913–1918
Party: Committee of Union and Progress
State: Ottoman Empire

The Three Pashas, also known as the Young Turk triumvirate, were the three leaders who effectively ruled the Ottoman Empire after the 1913 Ottoman coup d'état and during the First World War: Enver Pasha, Minister of War; Talaat Pasha, Minister of the Interior and later Grand Vizier; and Djemal Pasha, Minister of the Navy and military governor of Syria. All three were leading members of the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP).

Enver, Talaat, and Djemal, the triumvirate that governed the Ottoman Empire during the First World War.

Rule

The triumvirate came to power following the Raid on the Sublime Porte in January 1913. Their government pursued centralisation, Turkish nationalism, and closer ties with Germany, culminating in the Ottoman–German alliance and the empire's entry into the First World War in October–November 1914. Talaat, as Minister of the Interior, was the principal architect of the Armenian genocide of 1915–1917, in which Enver and Djemal were also implicated, as well as of deportations and massacres of Assyrian and Greek Christians.

Downfall and deaths

With the Ottoman defeat in 1918, the three fled the empire aboard a German vessel in November 1918. A Turkish court-martial tried them in absentia and sentenced them to death. Talaat was assassinated in Berlin in 1921 by Soghomon Tehlirian, an Armenian genocide survivor, as part of Operation Nemesis; Djemal was assassinated in Tiflis in 1922; Enver died the same year fighting Basmachi rebels alongside — and against — Bolshevik forces in Central Asia.
