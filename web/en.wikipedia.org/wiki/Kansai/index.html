Kansai region

The Kansai region (関西地方, Kansai-chihō) or the Kinki region (近畿地方, Kinki-chihō) lies in the southern-central region of Japan's main island Honshū. The region includes the prefectures of Nara, Wakayama, Kyoto, Osaka, Hyōgo and Shiga; often Mie is included as well. The metropolitan region of Osaka, Kobe and Kyoto (Keihanshin) is the second-most populous in Japan after Greater Tokyo.

History

Kansai is the historical heartland of Japan. The earliest Japanese states arose in the Yamato plain around Nara, and the imperial capital was located in the region — at Nara (710–784) and then Kyoto (794–1868) — for over a thousand years. Osaka developed as the nation's commercial hub and "kitchen of the land" during the Edo period.

Culture

The region is known for its distinctive Kansai dialect, its comedy tradition (manzai), and a food culture that favours lightly seasoned dishes such as udon in clear broth, takoyaki and okonomiyaki. Kansai is often contrasted culturally and commercially with the Kantō region around Tokyo. The region's electricity is supplied principally by the Kansai Electric Power Company.

Geography

Kansai contains Lake Biwa, Japan's largest lake, the Seto Inland Sea coast, and mountain ranges including the Kii Mountains, a UNESCO World Heritage pilgrimage landscape. Major cities include Osaka, Kobe, Kyoto, Nara, Sakai and Himeji.
