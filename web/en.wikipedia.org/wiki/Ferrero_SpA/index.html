Ferrero SpA, more commonly known as Ferrero Group, is an Italian multinational confectionery company headquartered in Alba, Piedmont. Founded by Pietro Ferrero in 1946, it is one of the world's largest chocolate and confectionery producers and remains under the control of the Ferrero family.

History

Pietro Ferrero, a confectioner from Alba, developed a hazelnut-and-cocoa paste in the 1940s when cocoa was rationed; the product, first sold as Giandujot and later as a spreadable cream, became the forerunner of Nutella, launched in its modern form by his son Michele Ferrero in 1964. Under Michele, the company expanded internationally and introduced Kinder Chocolate (1968), Tic Tac (1969), Ferrero Rocher (1982) and Mon Chéri.

After Michele Ferrero's death in 2015, his son Giovanni Ferrero led the group. Ferrero expanded through acquisitions, purchasing the British retailer Thorntons (2015), the American confectioner Fannie May (2017), Nestlé's United States confectionery business (2018), and Kellogg's cookie brands including Famous Amos and Keebler (2019).

Products

The group's best-known brands include Nutella hazelnut spread, the Kinder line (Kinder Surprise, Kinder Bueno, Kinder Joy), Ferrero Rocher and Raffaello pralines, Tic Tac mints, and Mon Chéri liqueur chocolates. Ferrero is one of the world's largest purchasers of hazelnuts, sourcing much of its supply from Turkey and its own plantations.

Corporate affairs

The Ferrero family is among Italy's wealthiest. The group employs tens of thousands of people in dozens of production plants worldwide and has historically been known for a secretive corporate culture and strong paternalistic ties to the town of Alba.
