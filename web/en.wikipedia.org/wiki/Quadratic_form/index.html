Quadratic form

In mathematics, a quadratic form is a polynomial with terms all of degree two. Examples include the squared Euclidean distance x²+y²+z² and the dot product expressed as xTAx for a symmetric matrix A.

Theory

Quadratic forms are central to number theory, geometry, and physics. Over the real numbers they are classified by Sylvester's law of inertia into positive definite, negative definite, and indefinite forms. Binary quadratic forms ax²+bxy+cy² were studied by Fermat, Euler, Lagrange, and Gauss, and connect to class groups and representations of integers.

Applications

They appear in optimisation (Hessians), statistics (chi-squared distributions), relativity (spacetime interval), and the theory of lattice packings.
