Mapp and Lucia

Mapp and Lucia is a collective name for a series of novels by E. F. Benson and, in British popular culture, for their two rival heroines: Elizabeth Mapp and Emmeline Lucas ("Lucia"). The series comprises six novels written between 1920 and 1939: Queen Lucia (1920), Miss Mapp (1922), Lucia in London (1927), Mapp and Lucia (1931), Lucia's Progress (1935), and Trouble for Lucia (1939).

Setting and characters

The first novels are set separately: Lucia reigns over the musical society of Riseholme, a Cotswold village modelled on Broadway, while Miss Mapp rules the social scene of Tilling, based on Rye, East Sussex, where Benson lived. In Mapp and Lucia (1931), Lucia, newly widowed, takes a summer lease on Miss Mapp's house in Tilling, and the two engage in a celebrated war of social one-upmanship involving bridge parties, garden fêtes, lobster recipes, and an attempted rescue during a flood on two floating kitchen tables and a plank.

Recurring characters include Lucia's devoted friend Georgie Pillson, the opera-loving Olga Bracely, the painter Irene Coles, the vicar's wife Evie Bartlett, and Major Benjy and his wife "Padre" and Susan Herbert.

Adaptations

Channel 4 adapted Mapp and Lucia in 1985–86, starring Geraldine McEwan as Lucia and Prunella Scales as Miss Mapp. The BBC produced a new three-part adaptation in 2014, written by Steve Pemberton and starring Miranda Richardson as Mapp and Anna Chancellor as Lucia.
