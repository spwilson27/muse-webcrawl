Timika

Timika
Country: Indonesia
Province: Central Papua
Regency: Mimika
Population: approx. 140,000 (metro area)

Timika is a town in the lowlands of Central Papua province, Indonesia, and the administrative centre of Mimika Regency. It lies near the southern coast of the island of New Guinea, close to the Arafura Sea, and serves as the principal service town for the vast Grasberg copper and gold mining complex operated by Freeport Indonesia in the mountains to the north.

Geography

Timika sits on a flat, swampy coastal plain drained by rivers flowing from the Sudirman Range. The climate is equatorial, hot and humid with heavy rainfall throughout the year. The town is connected by road to the port of Amamapare and by the Mozes Kilangin Airport to other Indonesian cities.

History

The town grew rapidly from the 1970s onward as the nearby Ertsberg and later Grasberg mines expanded. Its population is a mix of indigenous Papuan peoples, including the Amungme and Kamoro, and migrants from elsewhere in Indonesia drawn by mining employment.

Economy

Mining support services, government administration, trade and transport dominate the local economy. Timika is the logistics hub for one of the world's largest copper and gold mines.
