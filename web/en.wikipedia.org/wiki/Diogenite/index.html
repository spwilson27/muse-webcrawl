Diogenite

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Diogenites are a group of stony achondritic meteorites belonging to the HED meteorite clan (howardite–eucrite–diogenite). They are igneous rocks composed almost entirely of magnesium-rich orthopyroxene (hypersthene), with minor olivine, chromite, and other accessory minerals, and are thought to originate from the deep crust or upper mantle of the asteroid 4 Vesta.

Origin

Diogenites are believed to be cumulate rocks that crystallised in magma chambers deep within Vesta's crust, in contrast to the basaltic eucrites, which are surface lava flows. Together with howardites — breccias mixing eucritic and diogenitic material — they record the early differentiation of Vesta into core, mantle, and crust. NASA's Dawn spacecraft confirmed the link between Vesta and the HED meteorites by matching their spectral characteristics, and the giant south-pole impact basins on Vesta are the likely source from which HED fragments were excavated and eventually delivered to Earth.

Characteristics

Diogenites have a characteristically coarse-grained texture of large orthopyroxene crystals and often show brecciation from impacts. The group is named after the Greek philosopher Diogenes of Apollonia, in reference to his speculation that meteorites have a cosmic origin. Well-known diogenites include the Tatahouine meteorite, which fell in Tunisia in 1931, and Johnstown, which fell in Colorado in 1924.
