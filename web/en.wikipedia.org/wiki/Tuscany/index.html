Tuscany (Italian: Toscana) is a region in central Italy with an area of about 23,000 square kilometres and a population of about 3.8 million. Its capital is Florence. Tuscany is known for its landscapes, history, artistic legacy, and its influence on high culture; it is widely regarded as the birthplace of the Italian Renaissance and has been home to figures including Dante, Petrarch, Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, and Galileo.

Geography

The region stretches from the Apennine Mountains in the north and east to the Tyrrhenian Sea in the west, and includes the Tuscan Archipelago, whose largest island is Elba. Its terrain is mostly hilly, with the fertile Arno valley, the vineyard-covered Chianti hills, the marble mountains of the Apuan Alps, and the coastal Maremma plain.

History

Tuscany was the heartland of the Etruscan civilisation before Roman conquest. In the Middle Ages its city-states — Florence, Siena, Pisa, Lucca — grew rich on trade and banking. Under the Medici, Florence became the cradle of the Renaissance. The Grand Duchy of Tuscany later passed to the Habsburg-Lorraine dynasty and joined the Kingdom of Italy in 1860; Florence briefly served as Italy's capital (1865–1871).

Culture and economy

The region hosts several UNESCO World Heritage Sites, including the historic centres of Florence, Siena, San Gimignano, and Pienza, and the Val d'Orcia. Tuscan wines such as Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino, and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano are world-famous. The Tuscan dialect formed the basis of standard Italian. Tourism, agriculture, fashion, and marble quarrying at Carrara are major economic activities.
