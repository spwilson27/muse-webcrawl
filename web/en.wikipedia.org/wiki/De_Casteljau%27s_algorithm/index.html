De Casteljau's algorithm

In the mathematical field of numerical analysis, De Casteljau's algorithm is a recursive method to evaluate polynomials in Bernstein form or Bézier curves, named after its inventor Paul de Casteljau. The algorithm is a numerically stable way to compute points on a Bézier curve by repeated linear interpolation between its control points.

Description

Given a Bézier curve of degree n with control points P0, …, Pn and a parameter value t, the algorithm repeatedly interpolates between adjacent points of the current polygon:

P_i^(k) = (1 - t) * P_i^(k-1) + t * P_{i+1}^(k-1)

After n rounds, a single point remains: that point is the point B(t) on the curve. For a quadratic curve (three control points), one computes two interpolated points Q0 and Q1 along the control polygon, then interpolates between them to obtain the curve point. For a cubic, the process runs through three levels.

Subdivision and history

The intermediate points produced along the way are exactly the control points of two smaller Bézier curves that together make up the original curve split at B(t), which makes the algorithm the standard tool for subdividing curves in computer-aided design and rendering. De Casteljau developed the method in 1959 while working at the French car manufacturer Citroën, where he used it to design car body shapes; his work remained an unpublished company secret for years, while Pierre Bézier at Renault published a closely related formulation that gave the curves their name. The algorithm generalises to Bézier surfaces (via successive application along rows and columns) and to rational Bézier curves.
