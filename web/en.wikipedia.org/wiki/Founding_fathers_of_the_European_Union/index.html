Founding fathers of the European Union

Key figuresMonnet, Schuman, Adenauer, De Gasperi, Spaak
MilestoneSchuman Declaration, 9 May 1950
First communityEuropean Coal and Steel Community (1951)

The founding fathers of the European Union are the political leaders and thinkers credited with conceiving and launching European integration after the Second World War. The European Union officially recognizes eleven men as its founding fathers, including Jean Monnet, Robert Schuman, Konrad Adenauer, Alcide De Gasperi, and Paul-Henri Spaak.

The core figures

French statesman Robert Schuman proposed pooling French and German coal and steel production in his declaration of 9 May 1950. Civil servant and strategist Jean Monnet drafted the plan and became the first president of the High Authority of the European Coal and Steel Community. West German Chancellor Konrad Adenauer anchored the Federal Republic in the West, Italian Prime Minister Alcide De Gasperi championed European unity, and Belgian statesman Paul-Henri Spaak helped negotiate the Treaties of Rome.

Vision

The founders sought to make war between European states "not merely unthinkable, but materially impossible" by binding economies together, beginning with coal and steel and expanding step by step toward today's European Union.
