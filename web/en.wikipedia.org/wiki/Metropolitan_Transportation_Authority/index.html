The Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA) is a public-benefit corporation responsible for public transportation in the New York metropolitan area. It is the largest transportation network in North America, serving a population of more than 15 million people across New York City, Long Island, the lower Hudson Valley, and southwestern Connecticut. Its agencies carry several million passengers on an average weekday.

History

The MTA was chartered by New York State in 1965 as the Metropolitan Commuter Transportation Authority to oversee commuter railroads; in 1968 it took over the New York City Transit Authority (subways and buses) and the Triborough Bridge and Tunnel Authority, and took its present name. It has since been responsible for major programmes including the consolidation of bus services, the Second Avenue Subway, the East Side Access project bringing the Long Island Rail Road into Grand Central Madison (opened 2023), and the adoption of the OMNY contactless fare system to replace the MetroCard.

Operations and funding

Subsidiary agencies include MTA New York City Transit (subway and city buses), the Long Island Rail Road, Metro-North Railroad, MTA Bridges and Tunnels (whose toll surpluses subsidise transit), and MTA Bus Company. The MTA is governed by a board whose members are appointed by the Governor of New York and local officials. Its finances — a mix of fares, dedicated taxes, tolls, and state and federal aid — and its large capital debt have been recurring subjects of political debate; a congestion pricing programme for Manhattan, long planned, began tolling in January 2025.
