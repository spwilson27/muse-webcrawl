Symphony Hall is a concert hall located at 301 Massachusetts Avenue in Boston, Massachusetts, opened in 1900. Designed by the architectural firm McKim, Mead and White, it was built for the Boston Symphony Orchestra, which continues to make the hall its home. It is widely regarded as one of the top concert halls in the world acoustically.

History

The hall was commissioned after the orchestra's first home, the Boston Music Hall, was threatened by road and subway construction. Harvard physics professor Wallace Clement Sabine, the founder of architectural acoustics, advised on the design, making Symphony Hall one of the first auditoria designed in accordance with scientifically derived acoustical principles. Its shoebox shape, relatively narrow and high, contributes to its celebrated sound.

Interior

The interior is modeled on the second Gewandhaus concert hall in Leipzig. Sixteen replicas of Greek and Roman statues line the upper level, and the hall's name is inscribed in gold leaf on the proscenium. Beethoven's name is carved over the stage, the only musician so honored.

Organ

The hall contains a 4,800-pipe Aeolian-Skinner organ installed in 1949, designed by G. Donald Harrison, generally considered one of the finest concert organs in the United States.
