Pontiac GTO

The Pontiac GTO is an American automobile that was manufactured by General Motors' Pontiac division from 1964 to 1974 for the first generation, and by GM's Australian subsidiary Holden from 2004 to 2006 for a modern revival. Widely credited with popularising the muscle car, the GTO took its name from the Italian Gran Turismo Omologato ("grand tourer homologated"), borrowed from the Ferrari 250 GTO.

First generation (1964–1967)

The GTO began in 1964 as an option package for the Pontiac Tempest LeMans, devised by Pontiac engineers Russell Gee and Bill Collins and championed by division chief John DeLorean. It circumvented GM's ban on engines over 330 cubic inches in intermediate cars by offering the 389 cu in (6.4 L) V8 as part of an option rather than a standard engine. With 325 horsepower (or 348 hp with Tri-Power carburetion), bucket seats, and a base price of $2,491, the GTO sold over 32,000 units in its first year, far beyond expectations, and became a separate model in 1966 — the year sales peaked at nearly 97,000. The 1965 restyle brought stacked headlights, and 1967 added an optional 400 cu in engine.

Second and later generations (1968–1974)

Redesigned for 1968 with a flowing semi-fastback body and hidden headlights, the GTO won Motor Trend Car of the Year. The 1969 "Judge" package added bold stripes, a rear spoiler, and the Ram Air engine options and became an icon of the era. Muscle cars declined rapidly after 1970 under rising insurance rates, emissions rules, and the switch to unleaded fuel; the GTO reverted to an option package in 1972 and ended in 1974 as an option on the compact Ventura, with a 350 cu in engine.

Revival (2004–2006)

In 2004 Pontiac revived the GTO name for a rebadged Holden Monaro imported from Australia, powered by the Corvette's 5.7 L LS1 V8 (350 hp) and, in 2005–06, the 6.0 L LS2 (400 hp). Although praised for performance and build quality, its conservative styling drew criticism and sales fell short of targets; production ended in 2006 after about 41,000 cars.

Legacy

The GTO is remembered through songs such as Ronny & the Daytonas' 1964 hit "G.T.O." and its nicknames "Goat" and "The Great One." It is regarded as the car that launched the muscle-car era, inspiring the Chevrolet Chevelle SS, Ford Torino, Plymouth Road Runner, and many others.
