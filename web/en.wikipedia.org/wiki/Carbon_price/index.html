Carbon price

A carbon price is a cost applied to carbon emissions to encourage polluters to reduce the amount of greenhouse gases they emit into the atmosphere. Economists widely consider carbon pricing to be an effective and efficient way of reducing emissions.

Forms

Carbon prices can take two main forms. A carbon tax directly sets a price per tonne of carbon dioxide emitted, while an emissions trading system (cap and trade) sets a limit on total emissions and lets the market determine the price of allowances. Hybrid systems combine elements of both, such as price floors and ceilings within a trading scheme.

Rationale

Greenhouse gas emissions are a negative externality: emitters do not bear the full social cost of the damage caused by climate change. A carbon price internalises this cost, following the polluter pays principle. The social cost of carbon is an estimate of the economic damage caused by emitting one additional tonne of carbon.

Coverage

As of the 2020s, carbon pricing initiatives cover a substantial share of global emissions. The European Union Emissions Trading System, launched in 2005, is the largest carbon market, and national or subnational schemes operate in countries including Canada, China, Japan, and the United Kingdom.
