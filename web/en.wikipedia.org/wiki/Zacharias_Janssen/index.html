Zacharias Janssen

Zacharias Janssen (also Zacharias Jansen or Sacharias Jansen; c. 1585 – c. 1632) was a Dutch spectacle-maker from Middelburg associated with the invention of the first optical telescope and, sometimes, of the first compound microscope.

Biography

Janssen was born in The Hague, the son of Hans Janssen, a spectacle-maker. He grew up in Middelburg, at the time an important center of glass-making in the Netherlands. He worked as a spectacle-maker alongside his father and later alone.

Invention claims

Janssen is one of several claimants to the invention of the telescope around 1608, along with his neighbor and rival Hans Lipperhey and Jacob Metius. Another tradition credits Janssen's son, Johannes Zachariassen, with claiming that his father invented both the telescope and the compound microscope around 1590–1595. The claims were investigated by Cornelis Drebbel and others, and remain disputed by historians because the evidence rests largely on later testimony.
