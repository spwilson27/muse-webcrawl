Fernando Álvarez de Toledo y Pimentel, 3rd Duke of Alba (29 October 1507 – 11 December 1582), known as the Grand Duke of Alba and the "Iron Duke," was a Spanish noble, general, and diplomat in the service of Emperor Charles V and Philip II. He is remembered both as one of the most capable commanders of the 16th century and, in the Netherlands and in Protestant historiography, as a symbol of Spanish tyranny for his brutal governorship during the early Dutch Revolt.

Early career

Alba fought from youth under his grandfather the 2nd Duke, serving in the conquest of Navarre and the Italian Wars. He distinguished himself at the Battle of Pavia era campaigns, commanded Imperial forces against the Ottoman-backed siege of Vienna (1529), led the victorious army at the Battle of Mühlberg (1547) against the Schmalkaldic League, and served as viceroy of Naples and governor of Milan, fighting France in Italy in the 1550s.

Governor of the Netherlands

In 1567 Philip II sent Alba with an army of veterans—the famous march along the "Spanish Road"—to suppress unrest and iconoclasm in the Habsburg Netherlands. He established the Council of Troubles (popularly, the "Council of Blood"), which condemned thousands and executed the Counts of Egmont and Horn (1568). His heavy taxes, including the hated "Tenth Penny" sales tax, and military repression—such as the sack of Mechelen and sieges of Haarlem and Alkmaar—deepened resistance rather than ending it. The revolt under William of Orange continued, and Alba, ill and discredited, was recalled in 1573.

Final years

Out of favor for several years, Alba was recalled in 1580 to command the invasion that secured Portugal for Philip II after the Portuguese succession crisis, winning the Battle of Alcântara. He died in Lisbon in 1582.
