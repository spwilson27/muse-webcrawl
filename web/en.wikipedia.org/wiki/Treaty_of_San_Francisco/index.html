Treaty of San Francisco

Signed8 September 1951
LocationWar Memorial Opera House, San Francisco
In force28 April 1952
PartiesJapan and 48 Allied nations

The Treaty of San Francisco (サンフランシスコ講和条約), also called the Treaty of Peace with Japan, re-established peaceful relations between Japan and the Allied Powers on behalf of the United Nations to formally end the legal state of war between the two parties and brought the Allied occupation of Japan to an end. It was signed by 49 nations on 8 September 1951, in San Francisco, California, and entered into force on 28 April 1952.

Main provisions

  • Japan recognized the independence of Korea and renounced all rights to Taiwan (Formosa), the Pescadores, the Kurile Islands, South Sakhalin, and its League of Nations mandates — though the treaty did not specify to whom Taiwan would be transferred;
  • Japan accepted the judgments of the International Military Tribunal for the Far East;
  • Japan waived claims arising from its wartime actions, and the Allied Powers waived reparations claims, though Japan agreed to negotiate compensation with states it had occupied;
  • The United States retained administrative control over the Ryukyu Islands (including Okinawa) and the Bonin Islands, pending possible trusteeship proposals; Okinawa was returned to Japan in 1972.

Absences and disputes

Neither the Republic of China nor the People's Republic of China was invited, owing to the dispute over which government represented China; Japan later signed a separate peace treaty with the ROC (Treaty of Taipei, 1952) and normalized relations with the PRC in 1972. The Soviet Union attended but refused to sign, so Japan and the USSR only declared their state of war at an end with the Joint Declaration of 1956 — and the dispute over the southern Kurile Islands (the Northern Territories) continues to prevent a formal Russo-Japanese peace treaty to this day.
