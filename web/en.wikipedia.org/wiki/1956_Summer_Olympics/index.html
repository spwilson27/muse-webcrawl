The 1956 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XVI Olympiad, were held in Melbourne, Victoria, Australia from November 22 to December 8, 1956—the first Olympic Games held in the Southern Hemisphere and in Oceania. Because Australian quarantine laws barred the entry of foreign horses, the equestrian events were held separately in Stockholm, Sweden, in June 1956, making this the only edition of the Games split between two countries on two continents.

Context and boycotts

The Games took place amid Cold War tensions. Egypt, Iraq, and Lebanon boycotted over the Suez Crisis; the Netherlands, Spain, and Switzerland withdrew over the Soviet invasion of Hungary. The water polo match between Hungary and the Soviet Union, played days after the uprising was crushed, became notorious as the "Blood in the Water match," ending with Hungary winning 4–0 amid violence. The People's Republic of China withdrew over the recognition of the Republic of China (Taiwan).

Highlights

The Soviet Union topped the medal table for the first time. Australian swimmer Dawn Fraser began her Olympic career with gold medals, and the home team achieved its best result to that date. At the closing ceremony, following a suggestion by Melbourne schoolboy John Ian Wing, athletes entered the stadium mingled together rather than marching by nation—a tradition that endures. The flame was lit by Ron Clarke, and the Games became known as the "Friendly Games."

Medal table

The Soviet Union won 37 gold medals, ahead of the United States (32) and Australia (13). Seventeen-year-old Betty Cuthbert of Australia won three sprint golds, earning the nickname "Golden Girl."
