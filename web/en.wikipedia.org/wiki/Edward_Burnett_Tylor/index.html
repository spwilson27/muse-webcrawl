Edward Burnett Tylor

Born: 2 October 1832, London, England

Died: 2 January 1917, Wellington, Somerset

Sir Edward Burnett Tylor was an English anthropologist regarded as a founder of cultural anthropology. His theory of cultural evolution and his definition of culture shaped the new discipline.

Primitive Culture

In Primitive Culture (1871) Tylor defined culture as "that complex whole which includes knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom, and any other capabilities and habits acquired by man as a member of society", and proposed animism — belief in spiritual beings — as the earliest form of religion, developing through stages toward monotheism and science. His doctrine of "survivals" explained customs as remnants of earlier stages.

Career

Tylor became keeper of the University Museum at Oxford and, in 1896, Oxford's first professor of anthropology, following travels in Mexico that produced Anahuac (1861). Later anthropologists, including Clifford Geertz, criticised his unilineal evolutionism while retaining his broad concept of culture.
