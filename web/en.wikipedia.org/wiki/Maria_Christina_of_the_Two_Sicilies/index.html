Maria Christina of the Two Sicilies

Maria Christina (1806–1878), Regent of Spain

Maria Christina of the Two Sicilies (Italian: Maria Cristina Ferdinanda; 27 April 1806 – 22 August 1878) was Queen of Spain from 1829 to 1833 as the fourth wife of King Ferdinand VII, and Queen Regent of Spain from 1833 to 1840 during the minority of her daughter, Queen Isabella II.

Marriage and succession crisis

Born in Palermo, a daughter of King Francis I of the Two Sicilies and Maria Isabella of Spain, she married her uncle Ferdinand VII in 1829. The king, lacking a male heir, issued the Pragmatic Sanction of 1830 restoring female succession, setting aside the Salic law claim of his brother Don Carlos. When Ferdinand died in September 1833, the three-year-old Isabella was proclaimed queen and Maria Christina became regent; Don Carlos's supporters rose in arms, beginning the First Carlist War.

Regency

To sustain her daughter's throne against the Carlists, Maria Christina turned to the liberals, issuing an amnesty and accepting the Royal Statute of 1834 and later the more liberal Constitution of 1837. Her government, dominated by figures such as Martínez de la Rosa, Cea Bermúdez and Mendizábal (whose confiscation of church lands financed the war), faced mutinies, anticlerical violence, and the growing power of General Espartero. Her secret morganatic marriage in 1833 to Agustín Fernando Muñoz, a sergeant of the royal guard whom she made Duke of Riánsares, damaged her popularity when revealed.

Exile and later life

Forced out by Espartero's progressives in October 1840, Maria Christina went into exile in France, plotting against the regency until Espartero himself fell in 1843. She returned to Spain, exercised great influence at court during Isabella's reign, amassed a large fortune through railway and other concessions, and was expelled again after the 1854 revolution. She spent her final years in France and died at Le Havre in 1878.
