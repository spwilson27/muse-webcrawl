Galliformes

Galliformes
Order of heavy-bodied ground-feeding birds
Families include: Phasianidae, Numididae, Cracidae, Megapodiidae, Odontophoridae
Species: about 290

Galliformes is an order of heavy-bodied, ground-feeding birds that includes turkeys, chickens, grouse, pheasants, quail, peafowl, guineafowl, and chachalacas, among others. Common names for the order are gallinaceous birds or landfowl. The order contains about 290 living species in five families.

Characteristics

Galliformes are typically stout-bodied birds with strong legs adapted for scratching the ground for seeds and invertebrates, short rounded wings suited to brief bursts of flight, and, in many species, pronounced sexual dimorphism, with ornamented males. They are distributed worldwide except for the harshest deserts and perpetual ice. Together with the waterfowl (Anseriformes), they form the fowl clade Galloanserae, among the earliest-diverging lineages of modern birds, with a fossil record extending into the Late Cretaceous.

Relationship with humans

The order is of exceptional economic importance: the domestic chicken, descended from the red junglefowl, is the world's most numerous domesticated animal, and turkeys, guineafowl, and quail are also farmed. Many species are prized game birds, while others, such as the megapodes, are notable for incubating their eggs in mounds of decaying vegetation rather than with body heat.
