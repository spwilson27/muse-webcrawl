Tatev Monastery

The Tatev Monastery complex in Syunik Province, Armenia

The Tatev Monastery (Armenian: Տաթևի վանք) is a 9th-century Armenian Apostolic monastery located on a large basalt plateau near the village of Tatev in Syunik Province, southeastern Armenia, overlooking the deep gorge of the Vorotan River. It was one of the most important centres of learning in medieval Armenia.

History

Tradition holds that the site was sacred in pre-Christian times and that the monastery was named after Eustateus (Tatev), a disciple of St. Thaddeus the Apostle. The main church of Saints Paul and Peter was built between 895 and 906 under Bishop Hovhannes, with support from the princes of Syunik, after Tatev became the seat of the Syunik bishopric. The complex later included the Church of St. Gregory (848), a mausoleum, refectory, and defensive walls.

In the 14th and 15th centuries, Tatev hosted one of medieval Armenia's most important universities, where the philosophers Hovhan Vorotnetsi and Grigor Tatevatsi taught and produced illuminated manuscripts. The monastery was damaged by invasions and by the 1931 Zangezur earthquake, and was extensively restored in the late 20th and early 21st centuries.

The swinging pillar

A distinctive feature is the Gavazan, an octagonal swinging pillar erected in the 10th century, which tilts in response to earth tremors or movement and returns to rest — long regarded as an early seismic warning device and a marvel of medieval engineering.

Access

Since 2010 the monastery has been served by the Wings of Tatev aerial tramway across the Vorotan Gorge, which at 5.7 kilometres was recognised by Guinness World Records as the world's longest reversible aerial tramway. The monastery is a major tourist destination and is on Armenia's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status.
