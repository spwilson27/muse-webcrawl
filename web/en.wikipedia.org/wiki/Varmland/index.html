Varmland

Varmland is a common unaccented rendering of Värmland (Swedish pronunciation: [ˈvæ̌rmland]), a historical province (landskap) in west-central Sweden, bordering Norway to the west. Värmland County largely coincides with the province, whose largest city and county seat is Karlstad, situated on the northern shore of Lake Vänern, Sweden's largest lake.

Geography

Värmland is a land of forests, lakes, and rivers. The Klarälven river flows southward through the province to Lake Vänern, and the terrain rises in the west toward the Scandinavian Mountains along the Norwegian border. The Glaskogen nature reserve and numerous smaller lakes are popular for canoeing and outdoor recreation.

Culture

The province is celebrated in Swedish cultural life for its storytelling tradition, music, and folklore. The writer Selma Lagerlöf, the first woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, came from Mårbacka in Värmland, and her novel Gösta Berling's Saga is set in the region. The poet Gustaf Fröding was also born in Värmland. The area has a rich tradition of folk music and is associated in folklore with trolls and forest beings.

Economy and sport

Forestry, paper and pulp production, and ironworking historically formed the backbone of the provincial economy. Värmland is well known in sport as the home of the Swedish Rally, a round of the World Rally Championship held on snow-covered forest roads, and of the ice hockey club Färjestad BK of Karlstad.
