Clarissa; or, The History of a Young Lady is an epistolary novel by Samuel Richardson, published in 1748. It tells the story of the virtuous heroine Clarissa Harlowe, whose family pressures her to marry the odious Roger Solmes, and her subsequent abduction and persecution by the rake Robert Lovelace. At roughly one million words, it is among the longest novels in the English language.

Plot

The novel unfolds entirely in letters written by Clarissa, Lovelace, and their confidants. After Lovelace drugs and rapes her, Clarissa refuses his marriage proposal, escapes, and dies of grief and illness — a protracted, saintly death scene that made generations of readers weep. Lovelace is later killed in a duel by Clarissa's cousin, Colonel Morden.

Significance

Richardson's technique of "writing to the moment" — presenting consciousness through immediate correspondence — profoundly influenced the development of the novel, including Fielding, Austen, and the French epistolary tradition. The character of Lovelace gave English the term "lovelace" for a libertine.
