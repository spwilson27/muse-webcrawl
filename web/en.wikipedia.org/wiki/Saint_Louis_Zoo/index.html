Saint Louis Zoo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Saint Louis Zoo, officially the Saint Louis Zoological Park, is a zoo in Forest Park, St. Louis, Missouri, United States. Founded in 1910 and occupying about 90 acres, it is home to more than 16,000 animals representing some 500 species, and is recognised as a leader in animal management, research, conservation, and education. Admission is free, a distinction funded by the Metropolitan Zoological Park and Museum District; the zoo draws roughly three million visitors a year, making it one of the most visited zoos in the United States.

History

The zoo traces its origins to the 1904 Louisiana Purchase Exposition, after which the city acquired the fair's great walk-through bird cage — still a landmark exhibit today. The Zoological Society of St. Louis was founded in 1910, the zoo opened in Forest Park shortly afterward, and in 1971 St. Louis city and county voters approved a special tax district, allowing the zoo to offer free admission while expanding its programmes.

Exhibits

Major exhibits include River's Edge, the first major exhibit in North America themed around river ecosystems; the Fragile Forest ape habitat; Sea Lion Sound; Penguin & Puffin Coast; the Red Rocks area of big cats and giraffes; and the Emerson Children's Zoo. The zoo maintains the WildCare Institute, which supports field conservation in more than a dozen centres and programmes around the world, including work with hellbenders, Grevy's zebras, and Somali wild asses.

Conservation and research

The Saint Louis Zoo's Endangered Species Research Center and veterinary department contribute to captive breeding and reintroduction efforts, notably for the American burying beetle and Ozark hellbender. The zoo is accredited by the Association of Zoos and Aquariums (AZA) and participates in numerous Species Survival Plans.
