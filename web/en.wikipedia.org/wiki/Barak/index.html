Barak (Hebrew: בָּרָק, "lightning") was a ruler and military commander of ancient Israel. According to the Book of Judges (chapters 4 and 5), Barak was the son of Abinoam from Kedesh in Naphtali. Summoned by the prophetess and judge Deborah, he assembled an army of ten thousand men from the tribes of Naphtali and Zebulun at Mount Tabor and defeated the Canaanite forces of King Jabin of Hazor, commanded by Sisera, at the Kishon River.

Biblical account

Deborah prophesied that because Barak insisted she accompany him into battle, the honour of victory over Sisera would go to a woman. Sisera's army, including its 900 iron chariots, was thrown into confusion and routed; Sisera fled on foot and was later killed by Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, who drove a tent peg into his temple as he slept. The victory is celebrated in the Song of Deborah (Judges 5), one of the oldest passages in the Hebrew Bible.

Legacy

Barak is listed among the heroes of faith in the New Testament Epistle to the Hebrews (11:32). His name, meaning "lightning", is unrelated to the similar-sounding Arabic name meaning "blessing".
