Georg von Frundsberg

Born: 24 September 1473, Mindelheim, Bavaria

Died: 20 August 1528, Mindelheim

Known for: Commanding the Landsknechts in the Italian Wars

Georg von Frundsberg (24 September 1473 – 20 August 1528) was a German military commander and the foremost leader of the Landsknechts, the German mercenary pikemen who dominated European battlefields in the early 16th century. His devotion to his men earned him the affectionate reputation of a father to his soldiers.

Career

Frundsberg fought for the Habsburg emperors Maximilian I and Charles V throughout the Italian Wars. He distinguished himself at the Battle of Cerignola (1503) and was instrumental in the imperial victory at the Battle of Pavia (1525), where the French king Francis I was captured. He was a master of the pike square tactics that made the Landsknechts the counterpart of the Swiss mercenaries, whom he defeated on several occasions.

Sack of Rome and death

In 1526–27 Frundsberg raised an army in Germany, partly at his own expense, and marched over the Alps to reinforce imperial forces in Italy. When his unpaid, mutinous troops demanded to march on Rome in March 1527, Frundsberg tried to address them but collapsed with a stroke (apoplexy) during the confrontation. Leaderless, the army went on to perpetrate the notorious Sack of Rome in May 1527. Frundsberg never recovered and died at his castle in Mindelheim in 1528.

Legacy

Frundsberg remains a celebrated figure in German military tradition; several German and Austrian units and ships have borne his name, and he is a central character in popular Landsknecht lore and song.
