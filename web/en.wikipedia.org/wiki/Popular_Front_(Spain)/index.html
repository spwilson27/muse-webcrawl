The Popular Front (Spanish: Frente Popular) was an electoral alliance in Spain formed in January 1936 by republican and left-wing parties, including the Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE), the Republican Left, the Republican Union, the Communist Party of Spain (PCE) and several regionalist parties, with external support from the anarchist CNT. It won the general election of February 1936 and formed the government of the Second Spanish Republic in the months leading up to the Spanish Civil War.

Programme

The alliance's programme, signed on 15 January 1936, was comparatively moderate: it called for an amnesty for those imprisoned after the Revolution of 1934, the restoration of the Catalan statute of autonomy, reinstatement of workers dismissed for political activity, and a resumption of republican reforms in education and agriculture, while explicitly rejecting wholesale nationalisation and workers' control demanded by more radical sectors.

1936 election and government

In the election of 16 February 1936 the Popular Front won a narrow majority of the popular vote but, under Spain's electoral law, a commanding majority of seats in the Cortes. Manuel Azaña formed a government composed of republican parties; Azaña became President of the Republic in May, with Santiago Casares Quiroga as prime minister. The amnesty freed some 30,000 prisoners, and strikes, land occupations and political violence escalated through the spring and summer of 1936.

Civil War

The military uprising of 17–18 July 1936 against the Popular Front government began the Spanish Civil War. The coalition broadened during the war to include communists and, later, the socialist government of Juan Negrín, but internal tensions — including the Barcelona May Days of 1937 between communists and anarchists — weakened it. The Republic was defeated in 1939, and the Popular Front ceased to exist as an organised force.
