Problem of points

The problem of points, also called the problem of division of the stakes, is a classical problem in probability theory. It concerns a game of chance between two players who have equal chances of winning each round. The players contribute equally to a prize pot, and agree in advance that the first player to win a certain number of rounds wins the entire prize. The problem asks how the stakes should be divided if the game is interrupted partway through.


The problem of points concerns dividing stakes in an unfinished game of chance.

History

The problem was posed in Luca Pacioli's Summa de arithmetica (1494) and discussed by Tartaglia and Cardano, but it was famously solved in the correspondence of 1654 between Blaise Pascal and Pierre de Fermat — a founding moment of modern probability theory. Fermat's approach enumerated the equally likely ways the remaining rounds could play out; Pascal's approach used recursive reasoning about the value of a position, leading to arguments involving what is now known as Pascal's triangle and expected value.

Solution

The correct division depends not on the rounds already won, but on the probability that each player would eventually win if the game continued. For example, if the first player to win 6 rounds takes the pot, and play stops at 5–3, the trailing player must win three straight rounds; the leading player wins unless that happens, giving probabilities of 7/8 and 1/8, so the stakes divide in the ratio 7:1.
