Backpacking (travel)

Backpacking is a form of low-cost, independent travel, which often includes staying in inexpensive lodgings and carrying all necessary possessions in a backpack. It typically involves longer journeys than conventional holidays, frequently undertaken by young people during a gap year, though backpackers of all ages travel in this style. Backpacking grew out of the overland "hippie trail" journeys of the 1960s and 1970s and was popularised by budget guidebooks such as those of Lonely Planet, founded in 1973.

Characteristics

Backpackers favour public transport, budget accommodation such as hostels, and flexible itineraries over packaged tours. Well-worn circuits include the "Banana Pancake Trail" through Southeast Asia, interrailing around Europe, and routes through Australia, New Zealand and South America. The culture emphasises meeting other travellers and local people, and a degree of self-reliance and spontaneity.

Developments

The rise of low-cost airlines, online booking and smartphones has changed backpacking, giving rise to terms such as "flashpacking" for travellers with larger budgets and more electronics. The phenomenon has been studied by tourism researchers and sometimes criticised for the impact of enclave tourism on host communities, as well as celebrated as a formative, educational experience.
