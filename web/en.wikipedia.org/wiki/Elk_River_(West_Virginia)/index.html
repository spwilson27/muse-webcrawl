Elk River (West Virginia)

Elk River
Length: ~172 miles (277 km)
Source: Pocahontas County, West Virginia
Mouth: Kanawha River at Charleston
Major dam: Sutton Dam

The Elk River is a tributary of the Kanawha River in central West Virginia, about 172 miles (277 km) long. It rises in the Allegheny Mountains of Pocahontas County from the confluence of the Old Field Fork and Big Springs Fork, and flows generally westward to join the Kanawha at Charleston, the state capital.

Course

The river passes through Webster, Braxton, Clay, and Kanawha counties, with towns including Webster Springs, Sutton, Gassaway, Clay, and Elkview along its banks. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers' Sutton Dam, completed in 1961, impounds Sutton Lake for flood control and recreation.

Recreation

The Elk is known as one of West Virginia's finest trout and muskie streams, particularly the catch-and-release section below Sutton Dam, and sections of the Elk River Trail follow its valley on a former rail line.

2014 chemical spill

In January 2014, a storage tank operated by Freedom Industries leaked the coal-processing chemical MCHM into the river just upstream of Charleston's water intake, contaminating drinking water for about 300,000 residents and prompting a multi-day do-not-use order.
