Louisville Muhammad Ali International Airport (IATA: SDF, ICAO: KSDF), historically known as Louisville International Airport and Standiford Field, is a public and military airport in Louisville, Kentucky, United States. It is the home of Worldport, the worldwide air hub of UPS Airlines, and is consistently one of the busiest cargo airports in the world, while handling relatively modest passenger traffic.

History

The airport was built in 1941 on land named for Dr. Elisha David Standiford, and the IATA code SDF preserves the name Standiford Field. In 2019 the airport was renamed in honour of boxer and Louisville native Muhammad Ali.

UPS Worldport

UPS selected Louisville as its air hub in the early 1980s. Worldport, opened in stages and massively expanded in the 2000s, sorts millions of packages per day across a facility of over five million square feet, employing tens of thousands of people. The hub makes Louisville one of the top five cargo airports worldwide by traffic.

Facilities

The airport has three runways and a single passenger terminal with two concourses, served by airlines including American, Delta, Southwest, United and Allegiant. The Kentucky Air National Guard's 123d Airlift Wing is also based at the field.
