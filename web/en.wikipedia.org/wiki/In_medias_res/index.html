In medias res

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In medias res (Latin: "into the middle of things") is a narrative technique in which a story opens in the midst of the action rather than at its chronological beginning. The earlier events are later revealed through flashbacks, narration, or other devices. The term was coined by the Roman poet Horace in his Ars Poetica, where he praised Homer for plunging the listener "into the middle of things" rather than beginning the Trojan War "from the egg."

History

The technique is characteristic of the ancient epic. The Iliad opens near the end of the ten-year siege of Troy, and the Odyssey begins with Odysseus already long absent from Ithaca, his earlier wanderings recounted in flashback. Virgil's Aeneid and later epics such as Dante's Inferno — which begins "midway through the journey of our life" — follow the same model.

Usage

Opening in medias res lets a writer seize attention immediately with dramatic action and create narrative tension about how the characters arrived at that point. The technique is ubiquitous in modern fiction, film, and television, where a cold open in the middle of a crisis is a standard device. It is contrasted with ab ovo ("from the egg"), narration beginning at the very start of events.
