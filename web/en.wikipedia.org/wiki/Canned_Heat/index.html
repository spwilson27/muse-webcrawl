Canned Heat is an American blues rock band formed in Los Angeles in 1965. The group is known for its interpretations of blues material and for two worldwide hits — "On the Road Again" (1968) and "Going Up the Country" (1968), the latter becoming an unofficial anthem of the Woodstock festival, where the band performed in 1969.

History

Fronted by singer Bob Hite and guitarist/harmonica player and blues scholar Alan Wilson, the band took its name from Tommy Johnson's 1928 "Canned Heat Blues", referring to Sterno cooking fuel drunk as an alcohol substitute. Their early albums, including Boogie with Canned Heat (1968) and Living the Blues (1968), championed traditional blues; they also recorded with John Lee Hooker on the album Hooker 'n Heat (1971). Wilson died in 1970 and Hite in 1981, but drummer Fito de la Parra kept versions of the band touring and recording for decades afterwards.

Legacy

Canned Heat's boogie-based approach influenced later blues rock and their two hits remain staples of classic-rock radio and film soundtracks evoking the late 1960s.
