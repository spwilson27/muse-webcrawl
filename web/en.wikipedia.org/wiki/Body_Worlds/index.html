Body Worlds

Body Worlds (German: Körperwelten) is a series of travelling exhibitions of dissected human and animal bodies preserved through plastination, a technique developed by the anatomist Gunther von Hagens in the 1970s. First presented in Japan in 1995, the exhibitions have been seen by tens of millions of visitors worldwide, making them among the most successful travelling exhibitions of all time.

The exhibition

Whole-body plastinates are posed in lifelike positions — running, playing chess, riding a horse — to display the muscles, organs and nervous system. Specimens are supplemented with individual organs, some diseased, to promote health education. Von Hagens operates a body donation programme; earlier exhibitions attracted controversy over the provenance of some specimens and the ethics of displaying human remains.

Influence

Body Worlds spawned numerous imitators and several permanent exhibitions, and plastinates are now also produced in dedicated facilities for museum and educational display.
