Digital ink

Digital ink is the information captured when a user writes or draws with a stylus or finger on a digitiser or touchscreen. Rather than storing the result only as a picture, the system stores the strokes themselves — sequences of points with timing, pressure, and tilt data — which can be searched, recognised, replayed, or converted to text and shapes.

Formats and processing

Microsoft's Ink Serialized Format (ISF) and the W3C's InkML are standard representations of ink data. Handwriting recognition engines analyse stroke data to produce text, while "ink analysis" can segment writing from drawings and recognise shapes and diagrams.

Applications

Digital ink underpins note-taking applications, tablet PCs, smartpens, and signature capture. It features in products such as Windows Ink, Microsoft OneNote, and note-taking apps on tablet operating systems, where preserved stroke data enables features like ink-to-text conversion and shape recognition.
