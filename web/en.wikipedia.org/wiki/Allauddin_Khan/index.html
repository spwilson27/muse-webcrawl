Allauddin Khan (c. 1862 – 6 September 1972), universally known as Baba Allauddin Khan, was an Indian sarod player and multi-instrumentalist, composer and teacher of Hindustani classical music. He founded the Maihar gharana (school) and trained many of the most celebrated Indian musicians of the 20th century.

Life

Born in Shibpur village, Brahmanbaria, in present-day Bangladesh, Khan ran away from home as a boy to pursue music, studying with a succession of teachers in Calcutta and, most decisively, with the great beenkar Wazir Khan of Rampur, whose Senia tradition he inherited after years of discipleship. He eventually settled at the princely court of Maihar in central India, where he served as court musician and, in 1918, founded the Maihar Band, an orchestra of Indian instruments staffed partly by orphaned children he had adopted and trained.

Students and legacy

His disciples included his son Ali Akbar Khan, daughter Annapurna Devi, Ravi Shankar, Nikhil Banerjee, Pannalal Ghosh and Vasant Rai. A famously demanding teacher, he is credited with modernising sarod technique, creating ragas such as Madhukali and Hem-Behag, and broadening the Hindustani repertoire by absorbing Carnatic and folk elements. He received the Padma Bhushan in 1958 and the Padma Vibhushan in 1971.
