Mazu Daoyi (709–788; Chinese: 馬祖道一; Wade–Giles: Ma-tsu Tao-i) was an influential abbot of Chan Buddhism during the Tang dynasty. The earliest recorded use of "Chan" is from his discourse. Mazu is perhaps most famous for his teaching that "ordinary mind is the Way" and that "this very mind is Buddha." He founded the Hongzhou school of Chan, which dominated Chan teaching in the later Tang dynasty and profoundly influenced all later schools of Zen in China, Korea, and Japan.

Biography

Mazu was born in Chengdu in present-day Sichuan. He became a monk at a young age and studied under Nanyue Huairang, a disciple of the Sixth Patriarch Huineng, on Mount Heng. According to traditional accounts, Huairang famously polished a tile to illustrate to Mazu that one cannot become a Buddha merely by sitting in meditation. Mazu received dharma transmission from Huairang and later resided at Kaiyuan Temple in Hongzhou (Jiangxi), from which his school took its name; "Mazu" (Ancestor Ma) derives from his family name, Ma.

Teaching

Mazu's recorded teachings emphasise that enlightenment is not separate from ordinary experience. His sayings include "This very mind is Buddha" (jixin shi fo) and, paradoxically, "Neither mind nor Buddha," intended to free students from attachment to any fixed formulation. He was known for unconventional teaching methods, including shouts and unexpected actions, later characteristic of the Linji school. His disciples included Baizhang Huaihai, Nanquan Puyuan, and Damei Fachang.

Legacy

The Hongzhou school's approach became the dominant form of Chan in China. Mazu's recorded sayings were compiled by his disciples and remain a central text of Zen literature.
