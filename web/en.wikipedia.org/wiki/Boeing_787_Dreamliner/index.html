Boeing 787 Dreamliner

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Boeing 787 Dreamliner with its swept composite wing in flight above clouds]
A 787 in cruise.

The Boeing 787 Dreamliner is an American long-haul wide-body airliner developed and manufactured by Boeing, notable for its extensive use of composite materials (about 50% by weight), fuel-efficient engines and passenger-comfort features (larger windows with electronic dimming, lower cabin altitude, higher humidity). First flown in 2009 and entering service with ANA in 2011, the 787 family (-8, -9, -10) opened hundreds of new nonstop city pairs; over 1,100 have been delivered.

Innovation and troubles

Boeing outsourced major sections to partners worldwide (wings from Japan, fuselage barrels from Italy and South Carolina), which — with immature technology — caused three years of delay. In 2013, lithium-ion battery fires grounded the worldwide fleet for months until a redesigned containment system was certified.

Design and economics

Bleedless engines (GEnx or Trent 1000) power more-electric systems; raked wingtips and composite structure cut fuel burn ~20% versus the 767 it replaced. Airlines use its 7,000+ nmi range for routes like Perth–London nonstop (Qantas, 2018) — the first Australia–UK direct air link.

Production

Assembly consolidated in Charleston, South Carolina (2021). Quality escapes and fuselage-gap issues slowed deliveries in 2020–23, drawing FAA scrutiny; rates have since recovered with demand for long-haul twins strong.
