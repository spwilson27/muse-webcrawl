Focal dystonia

Focal dystonia
SpecialtyNeurology
SymptomsSustained or intermittent muscle contractions affecting one body region, often task-specific
Usual onsetAdulthood, typically 30s–50s
TreatmentBotulinum toxin injections, retraining, medication

Focal dystonia is a neurological movement disorder characterised by sustained or intermittent muscle contractions that affect a single part of the body. In many forms the abnormal movements are strikingly task-specific, appearing only during a particular skilled activity and not during other uses of the same muscles.

Writer's cramp is a classic focal, task-specific dystonia.

Types

  • Writer's cramp — hand and forearm contractions during writing
  • Musician's dystonia — loss of fine motor control in the hand, embouchure or other body part during playing; affects roughly 1% of professional musicians and has affected notable performers
  • Cervical dystonia (spasmodic torticollis) — involuntary twisting or tilting of the head and neck, the most common focal dystonia
  • Blepharospasm — excessive involuntary blinking and eyelid closure
  • Spasmodic dysphonia — contractions of the laryngeal muscles affecting speech
  • Oromandibular dystonia — affecting jaw, tongue and face; with blepharospasm it forms Meige syndrome

Causes

Most focal dystonias are idiopathic. They are thought to arise from dysfunction in the basal ganglia and sensorimotor circuits, with maladaptive plasticity: intensive, repetitive practice of fine motor skills may degrade the brain's sensory maps of the affected body part, and genetic susceptibility appears to contribute. Focal dystonia can also be secondary to injury, stroke, or other neurological disease.

Treatment

There is no cure, but symptoms can be managed. Botulinum toxin injections into the overactive muscles are the standard treatment for cervical dystonia and blepharospasm and are also used in limb dystonias. Other approaches include oral medications (such as anticholinergics), sensory-motor retraining and splinting for musician's and writer's dystonia, and, for severe cases, deep brain stimulation.
