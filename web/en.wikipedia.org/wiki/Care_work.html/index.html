Care work

Care work is the work of caring for others, including childcare, elder care, health care, and domestic work performed in the home. The term is used in economics, sociology, and feminist theory to describe labour — paid and unpaid — that involves nurturing, attending to the physical and emotional needs of others, and maintaining households.

Unpaid care work

A large share of care work worldwide is unpaid and performed within families, disproportionately by women and girls. The International Labour Organization has estimated that unpaid care and domestic work amounts to billions of hours per day globally, contributing substantially to economies while remaining largely invisible in national accounts and GDP measures.

Paid care work

Paid care workers include nurses, home health aides, childcare workers, and domestic workers. These occupations are often low-paid and have been the subject of labour organising and policy debates about wages, migration, and working conditions. Feminist economists argue that care work is systematically undervalued because it has historically been associated with women's unpaid labour in the home.
