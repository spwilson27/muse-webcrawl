An award in Australian labour law is a legally binding document made by an industrial tribunal that sets out the minimum terms and conditions of employment for employees in a particular industry or occupation, in addition to any statutory entitlements. Awards have been a central feature of the Australian industrial relations system since the early twentieth century, when compulsory conciliation and arbitration was adopted at the Commonwealth level following the Harvester Judgment of 1907.

History

The Commonwealth Court of Conciliation and Arbitration, established under the Conciliation and Arbitration Act 1904, was empowered to prevent and settle industrial disputes by making awards binding on the parties. Justice H. B. Higgins's 1907 Harvester decision established the concept of a "fair and reasonable" basic wage sufficient to support a man, a wife and three children. Over the following century, federal and state awards proliferated, eventually numbering in the thousands and covering most Australian employees.

Modern awards

Under the Fair Work Act 2009, the award system was rationalised into a set of modern awards, which commenced on 1 January 2010. The modernisation process, conducted by the Australian Industrial Relations Commission and later the Fair Work Commission, consolidated roughly 1,560 pre-modernisation instruments into 122 (later 121) industry and occupational awards.

Modern awards cover most Australian employees who are not covered by an enterprise agreement and who are not award-free. Together with the National Employment Standards, they form the safety net of minimum employment conditions. Awards typically deal with matters such as minimum wages and skill-based classifications, hours of work, overtime and penalty rates, allowances, leave loading, and dispute resolution procedures.

Interaction with enterprise agreements

Where an enterprise agreement applies to an employee, the modern award does not apply while the agreement is in operation, although the agreement must leave each employee "better off overall" against the relevant award under the BOOT (better off overall test) administered by the Fair Work Commission. Awards continue to underpin agreement-making as the benchmark for approval.

Annual wage review and award variations

The Fair Work Commission reviews modern award minimum wages each year in its Annual Wage Review, and may vary awards on its own initiative or on application, including through the periodic four-yearly (later absorbed into general) review process, which has addressed matters such as casual conversion, family and domestic violence leave, and penalty rates in the hospitality and retail awards.
