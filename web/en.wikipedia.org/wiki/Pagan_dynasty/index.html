The Pagan dynasty (Burmese: ပုဂံမင်းဆက်) was the first dynasty to unify the regions that would later constitute modern-day Myanmar. The dynasty ruled the Pagan Kingdom from its capital at Pagan (Bagan) for roughly 250 years, from the mid-11th to the late 13th century. Its rule ended in the aftermath of the Mongol invasions of Burma.

History

According to Burmese chronicles, the kingdom was founded in 849 CE by Pyinbya, though archaeological evidence suggests the Burmans, who had accompanied the Nanzhao raids of the 830s, began settling the region around the mid-to-late 9th century. The kingdom entered its imperial period with the accession of Anawrahta in 1044, who unified Upper and Lower Burma under one crown for the first time, conquered Thaton in 1057, and promoted Theravada Buddhism.

Under Anawrahta's successors Kyansittha and Alaungsithu, Pagan became a major regional power and an international centre of Buddhist scholarship. The kings and elites financed the construction of thousands of temples and stupas on the Pagan plain; over 2,000 survive today. Burmese replaced Pyu and Mon as the dominant written language of the court, and the Pagan period saw the earliest surviving Burmese inscriptions.

Decline and fall

From the early 13th century the kingdom weakened: ever-growing tax-free religious landholdings eroded the royal tax base, and the crown grew less able to maintain control over outlying regions. The Mongol invasions of 1277–1287, culminating in the Battle of Pagan in 1287, destroyed the kingdom's power. King Narathihapate fled the capital (earning him the epithet "the king who fled the Chinese") and was assassinated in 1289; the last nominal king, Kyawswa, was deposed in 1297, ending the dynasty.

Legacy

Pagan's unification laid the basis for Burma's modern cultural and administrative identity: the Burmese language and script, Theravada Buddhism, and the tradition of temple-building all took lasting form under the dynasty. The archaeological zone of Bagan was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019.
