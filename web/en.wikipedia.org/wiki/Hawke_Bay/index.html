Hawke Bay

Hawke Bay (Māori: Te Matau-a-Māui) is a large bay on the east coast of New Zealand's North Island. It stretches some 100 kilometres from the Mahia Peninsula in the northeast to Cape Kidnappers in the southwest, along the shoreline of the Hawke's Bay Region.

Geography and naming

The bay was named by Captain James Cook in honour of Admiral Sir Edward Hawke during Cook's voyage of 1769. The principal settlements on its shore are Napier and Hastings (inland), along with Wairoa in the north. Several rivers—the Wairoa, Mohaka, Tutaekuri, Ngaruroro and Tukituki—flow into the bay.

History

The Heretaunga Plains beside the bay were a centre of Māori settlement; European settlement followed in the 1850s. The 1931 Hawke's Bay earthquake raised parts of the bay's bed, draining the Ahuriri Lagoon and reshaping the coastline around Napier.
