Lisa Fonssagrives

Born17 May 1911, Gothenburg, Sweden
Died4 February 1992 (aged 80), New York City, US
OccupationModel, dancer, sculptor, photographer
SpousesFernand Fonssagrives (m. 1935; div. 1950); Irving Penn (m. 1950; died 1992)

Lisa Fonssagrives-Penn (born Lisa Birgitta Bernstone; 17 May 1911 – 4 February 1992) was a Swedish fashion model, dancer, sculptor and photographer. Generally credited as the world's first supermodel, she appeared on more than 200 covers of Vogue and worked with most of the leading fashion photographers of the 1930s, 1940s and 1950s, including Horst P. Horst, Man Ray, Erwin Blumenfeld, George Hoyningen-Huene and her second husband, Irving Penn.

Career

Trained as a dancer in Berlin in the school of Mary Wigman, Fonssagrives was discovered after a chance encounter in a Paris lift led to test photographs by her first husband, Fernand Fonssagrives. Her sculpted features, poise and self-possession made her the archetype of the elegant post-war couture model; a famous 1949 Penn photograph shows her in a Rochas mermaid dress. Time magazine featured her on its cover in 1949 in a story on fashion modelling.

Later life

After retiring from modelling in the late 1950s, she devoted herself to sculpture, working in bronze and marble, and exhibited in New York galleries. She died of pleurisy in New York in 1992; Irving Penn survived her by 17 years.
