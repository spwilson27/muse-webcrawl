Gordion

Gordion (Phrygian: Gordum; Greek: Γόρδιον) was the capital city of ancient Phrygia. It is located at the modern village of Yassıhüyük, about 70–80 km southwest of Ankara, in northwestern Turkey, near the confluence of the Sakarya and Porsuk rivers.

Gordion is best known as the home of King Midas and as the setting of the Gordian Knot legend. The site was occupied from the Bronze Age onward; it rose to prominence in the Early Iron Age as the political and commercial centre of the Phrygian kingdom, reaching its peak in the 8th century BC.

The site's many burial mounds (tumuli) include the great "Midas Mound" (Tumulus MM), one of the largest of its kind, which contained a rich royal burial with elaborate wooden furniture and bronze vessels. After the Cimmerian invasion and later Persian, Hellenistic, Roman, and Byzantine periods, the site declined and was eventually abandoned.

Major excavations were conducted by the University of Pennsylvania Museum beginning in 1950 under Rodney Young. Gordion was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2023.
