The Turtle Mountains are a plateau of rolling, forested hills rising above the surrounding prairie along the border between North Dakota in the United States and Manitoba in Canada. Dotted with hundreds of small lakes, wetlands, and aspen and oak woodland, the upland stands in sharp contrast to the flat glacial lake plain around it.

Geography

The plateau measures roughly 100 km east–west by 50 km north–south. Its hills consist largely of glacial deposits left at the margin of the Laurentide Ice Sheet, in places more than 300 feet above the surrounding plain. Butte Saint Paul in North Dakota is among the highest points.

Human geography

The Turtle Mountain Indian Reservation, home of the Turtle Mountain Band of Chippewa Indians, lies in the North Dakota portion. The International Peace Garden straddles the international border at the plateau's centre, and Turtle Mountain Provincial Park protects the Manitoba side. Timber, agriculture, and recreation are the principal land uses.
