Suez Canal

The Suez Canal is an artificial sea-level waterway in Egypt, connecting the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea through the Isthmus of Suez, dividing Africa and Asia. Opened on 17 November 1869 after ten years of construction under Ferdinand de Lesseps, it is about 193 km (120 mi) long and allows ships to travel between Europe and Asia without circumnavigating Africa.

The canal is one of the world's most important trade arteries — roughly a tenth or more of global trade by volume passes through it in normal years (figure approximate). Its history includes the Anglo-French condominium, Egypt's nationalisation under Nasser in 1956 (triggering the Suez Crisis invasion by Britain, France and Israel), closure after the 1967 war until 1975, expansion in 2015, the six-day blockage by the grounded container ship Ever Given in 2021, and shipping diversions caused by Red Sea attacks from 2023. Tolls are a major source of Egyptian revenue.
