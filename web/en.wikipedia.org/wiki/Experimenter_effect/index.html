Experimenter effect

The experimenter effect, also called experimenter bias or experimenter expectancy effect, is a phenomenon in experimental research in which the expectations or behavior of the researcher unconsciously influence participants' responses or the recording of results, biasing outcomes toward the expected finding.

Research

Robert Rosenthal extensively studied expectancy effects, including the classroom Pygmalion effect, and early demonstrations in which students' rats — randomly labelled maze-bright or maze-dull — performed according to the label. Related phenomena include demand characteristics described by Martin Orne, and the case of Clever Hans, a horse that appeared to calculate by reading its handler's cues.

Mitigation

Double-blind designs, automation, standardized protocols, and pre-registration reduce experimenter effects. See Demand characteristics, Observer-expectancy effect, and Blind experiment.
