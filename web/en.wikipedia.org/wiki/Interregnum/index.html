Interregnum

An interregnum (plural interregna or interregnums) is a period of discontinuity or gap in a government, organization or social order. Historically the term referred to the interval between the death or abdication of one monarch and the accession of the next, but it is also applied more broadly, for example to the period between the end of one presidential administration's authority and the start of another, or a vacancy in an elected office.

Historical examples

  • The Roman interregna, beginning with the gap after the death of Romulus, when the Senate appointed an interrex to hold office until a new king was chosen.
  • The English Interregnum (1649–1660), between the execution of Charles I and the restoration of Charles II, encompassing the Commonwealth of England under Oliver Cromwell.
  • The Ottoman Interregnum (1402–1413), a civil war among the sons of Bayezid I following his capture by Timur.
  • The Russian Time of Troubles (1598–1613), following the extinction of the Rurik dynasty.
  • The papal interregnum (sede vacante), the period between the death or resignation of a pope and the election of a successor.

Usage

Times of interregnum are often periods of instability, civil conflict or competing claims to power, since the normal line of succession or authority is interrupted. The concept also appears metaphorically in political theory, notably in Antonio Gramsci's description of crisis as an interregnum in which "the old is dying and the new cannot be born".
