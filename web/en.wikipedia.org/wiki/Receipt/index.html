Receipt

A receipt is a document acknowledging that a person has received money or property in payment following a sale or other transfer of goods or provision of a service. All receipts must have the date of purchase on them. If the recipient of the payment is legally required to collect sales tax or value-added tax (VAT) from the customer, the amount will usually be added to the receipt, and the collection will be deemed to have been on behalf of the relevant tax authority.

Details

A typical receipt itemises the goods or services sold, the quantity and unit price, any discounts, taxes charged, and the total amount paid, together with the date, the seller's identity and often the method of payment. Receipts serve as evidence of a transaction for returns and exchanges, warranty claims, expense reimbursement, bookkeeping and tax reporting. In many jurisdictions, businesses are required to issue receipts, and consumers may be entitled to demand one.

Forms

Receipts were traditionally handwritten or printed on paper, frequently using thermal paper, which can fade over time and often contains bisphenol A (BPA) coatings that have raised health concerns. Electronic receipts (e-receipts) delivered by email, text message or app are increasingly common and are used by retailers both for convenience and for marketing. Point-of-sale systems can generate itemised digital records that feed directly into accounting software.

In shipping and logistics, related documents such as bills of lading, delivery notes and dock receipts record the transfer of goods; in securities trading, a depositary receipt represents ownership of shares held abroad.
