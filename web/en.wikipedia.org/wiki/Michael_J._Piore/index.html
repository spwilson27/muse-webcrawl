Michael Joseph Piore (born 14 August 1940) is an American economist and professor of economics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. He is best known for his work on labor markets, internal labor markets, and the theory of the dual (segmented) labor market, developed with Peter B. Doeringer in the 1971 book Internal Labor Markets and Manpower Analysis.

Work

Piore argued that labor markets are divided into a primary sector of stable, well-paid jobs with advancement ladders and a secondary sector of low-wage, insecure work, and that this segmentation, rather than human capital differences alone, explains persistent inequality. With Charles Sabel he wrote The Second Industrial Divide (1984), which examined flexible specialization as an alternative to mass production. Later work addresses immigration, innovation systems, and employment regulation.
