Endostatin

SourceC-terminal fragment of collagen XVIII
FunctionAngiogenesis inhibitor
Size~20 kDa, 184 amino acids

Endostatin is a naturally occurring protein fragment, cleaved from the C-terminus of collagen type XVIII, that inhibits angiogenesis — the formation of new blood vessels. Because tumours depend on angiogenesis to grow, endostatin attracted intense interest as a potential anticancer agent.

Discovery

Endostatin was discovered in 1997 in the laboratory of Judah Folkman at Boston Children's Hospital, in work led by Michael O'Reilly. It was isolated from the conditioned medium of a murine haemangioendothelioma cell line and shown to suppress endothelial cell proliferation and shrink tumours in mice.

Mechanism and clinical development

Endostatin interferes with several pro-angiogenic signalling pathways, including those involving VEGF and integrins, and can induce apoptosis of proliferating endothelial cells. Despite dramatic early results in mice, clinical trials of recombinant human endostatin in the United States were disappointing. A modified version, Endostar, was approved in China in 2005 for use with chemotherapy in non-small-cell lung cancer.
