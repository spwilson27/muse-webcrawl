Saffir–Simpson scale

The Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale classifies hurricanes in the Atlantic and eastern Pacific into five categories based on their sustained wind speeds and potential for property damage. It was developed in the early 1970s by civil engineer Herbert Saffir and meteorologist Robert Simpson.

Use and limitations

The scale is used by the U.S. National Hurricane Center in public warnings. Storms of Category 3 and above are termed major hurricanes. The modern scale rates wind only: storm surge, flooding rainfall, and tornadoes — often the deadliest hazards — are not captured, a limitation highlighted after storms such as Katrina (2005), and earlier versions of the scale that included surge estimates were revised.
