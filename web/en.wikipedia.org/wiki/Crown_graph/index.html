Crown graph

In the mathematical field of graph theory, the crown graph S0n on 2n vertices is an undirected graph with two sets of vertices ui and vi, and edges between ui and vj whenever i ≠ j. It can equivalently be described as the complete bipartite graph Kn,n with a perfect matching removed, or as the bipartite Kneser graph and the incidence graph of a certain hypergraph.

Properties

The crown graph on 2n vertices is n-regular, with n(n − 1) edges. It has graph diameter 3 for n ≥ 3, and its automorphism group has order 2n!. Crown graphs are distance-transitive and are the bipartite double covers of the complete graphs. For n = 3, the crown graph is the utility graph K3,3 minus a matching, and is isomorphic to the graph of the vertices and edges of a hexagonal prism.

Applications

Crown graphs arise in the study of the bipartite matching problem and in parallel computing, where they model communication patterns. In combinatorics they provide extremal examples for several problems, including the determination of the crossing number and the study of graph colouring of bipartite graphs.
