Lineweaver–Burk plot

The Lineweaver–Burk plot, also known as the double reciprocal plot, is a graphical representation of the Michaelis–Menten equation of enzyme kinetics, published by Hans Lineweaver and Dean Burk in 1934. It plots the reciprocal of the reaction velocity (1/v) against the reciprocal of the substrate concentration (1/[S]).

Derivation

Taking reciprocals of the Michaelis–Menten equation v = Vmax[S] / (Km + [S]) gives a straight line: 1/v = (Km/Vmax)·(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax. The y-intercept equals 1/Vmax, the x-intercept equals −1/Km, and the slope is Km/Vmax, allowing both kinetic constants to be read from the graph.

Criticism and use

The double reciprocal transformation distorts experimental errors, giving undue weight to the least accurate measurements at low substrate concentrations. For this reason, non-linear regression on the untransformed equation is preferred for estimating Km and Vmax. The plot remains useful for distinguishing modes of enzyme inhibition: competitive, non-competitive and uncompetitive inhibitors produce characteristic patterns of intersecting lines.
