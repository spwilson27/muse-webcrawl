Classical Latin

Classical Latin is the form of the Latin language recognised as standard by writers of the late Roman Republic and early Roman Empire, roughly from 75 BC to the 2nd century AD, in contrast to earlier Old Latin and later Vulgar and Late Latin.

Classical Latin
Era: c. 75 BC – AD 200
Golden Age authors: Cicero, Caesar, Virgil, Horace, Livy
Silver Age: Tacitus, Juvenal, Pliny

Golden and Silver Ages

The Golden Age produced Cicero's oratory and philosophy, Caesar's commentaries, Virgil's Aeneid, Horace's odes and Ovid's poetry. Silver Age writers such as Tacitus, Seneca and Juvenal developed a more pointed style.

Features

Classical Latin has a highly inflected morphology: five noun declensions, six cases, and verbs conjugated for person, tense, mood and voice, with flexible word order used for emphasis. It differs from Vulgar Latin, the spoken register that evolved into the Romance languages.

Legacy

Classical Latin remained the model for Medieval and Renaissance Latin, education, law, science and the Roman Catholic liturgy, and is still the form taught in schools. See Latin.
