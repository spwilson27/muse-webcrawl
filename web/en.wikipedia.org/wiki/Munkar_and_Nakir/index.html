Munkar and Nakir

Munkar and Nakir (Arabic: منكر ونكير) are, in Islamic eschatology, the two angels who visit the dead in their graves in order to test their faith, in an event known as the "trial of the grave" (fitnat al-qabr). The names Munkar and Nakir mean roughly "the Denied" and "the Denier", though their names are not given in the Quran; they are named in hadith literature, including a tradition recorded by al-Tirmidhi.

The questioning

According to tradition, after burial the deceased is visited by the two angels, who raise the person to a sitting position and ask three questions: Who is your Lord? What is your religion? Who is your prophet? (or, in some versions, "What do you say about this man, Muhammad?"). The faithful believer answers correctly: my Lord is Allah, my religion is Islam, and my prophet is Muhammad. The grave of the believer is then widened and illuminated, and the person sleeps peacefully until the Day of Resurrection.

The unbeliever or hypocrite, by contrast, can only stammer "I do not know", and suffers punishment in the grave (ʿadhāb al-qabr) until the Day of Judgement. This interim state between death and resurrection is known as Barzakh.

Description

Hadith descriptions portray Munkar and Nakir as black angels with blue eyes, of fearsome appearance, who dig through the earth to reach the dead. They are distinguished from other angels with specific funerary roles: the recording angels Kiraman Katibin, the Angel of Death, and Ridwan, guardian of Paradise.
