Battle of Berestechko
Date28–30 June 1651
LocationBerestechko, Volhynia (present-day Ukraine)
ResultPolish–Lithuanian victory
BelligerentsPolish–Lithuanian Commonwealth vs Zaporozhian Cossacks and Crimean Khanate
CommandersJohn II Casimir, Jeremi Wiśniowiecki vs Bohdan Khmelnytsky, Islam III Giray

The Battle of Berestechko, fought from 28 to 30 June 1651 near the town of Berestechko in Volhynia, was one of the largest land battles of seventeenth-century Europe and a decisive engagement of the Khmelnytsky Uprising. The army of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, led in person by King John II Casimir, defeated the combined forces of the Zaporozhian Cossacks under Hetman Bohdan Khmelnytsky and their allies, the Crimean Tatars of Khan Islam III Giray.

Background

The uprising that began in 1648 had swept Ukrainian lands and inflicted severe defeats on the Commonwealth at Zhovti Vody, Korsun, and Pyliavtsi. After the indecisive fighting of 1649 and the uneasy Treaty of Zboriv, both sides prepared for a renewed campaign in 1651. The Commonwealth assembled one of the largest armies in its history, including the royal army, magnate retinues, and foreign infantry regiments, while Khmelnytsky gathered the Cossack host and secured the support of the Crimean Khanate.

The battle

The two armies met on the plain along the Styr River near Berestechko. The first two days saw cavalry skirmishing and a dramatic single combat tradition associated with the fighting. On 30 June, the decisive day, Polish cavalry under Prince Jeremi Wiśniowiecki struck the Cossack-Tatar army. The Tatar contingent of Islam III Giray broke and fled the field, and the khan, retreating, briefly detained Khmelnytsky himself, depriving the Cossacks of their commander at the critical moment. The Cossack wagon camp was besieged for ten days. Under the leadership of Ivan Bohun, part of the host escaped across the marshy Pliashivka River, but the camp fell on 10 July with heavy losses.

Aftermath

The defeat forced Khmelnytsky to accept the unfavorable Treaty of Bila Tserkva in September 1651, which sharply limited the Cossack register and restored Polish administration in much of Ukraine. Dissatisfied with these terms, Khmelnytsky turned increasingly toward Muscovy, a course that culminated in the Treaty of Pereiaslav in 1654 and a long Russo-Polish war.

Legacy

Berestechko is remembered in Ukrainian historical memory as a national tragedy and in Polish memory as a great royal victory. The battlefield near the village of Pliashivka is preserved as a memorial reserve, including the Church of St Michael, to which Cossack remains recovered from the field were transferred.
