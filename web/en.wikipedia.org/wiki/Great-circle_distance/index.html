Great-circle distance

The great-circle distance, orthodromic distance, or spherical distance is the distance along a great circle between two points on a sphere. It is the shortest distance between the two points on the surface of the sphere, and is measured in units of length. Because the Earth is approximately spherical, great-circle distances are used in navigation to find the shortest route between two points on the globe.

Formulae

Let the two points have longitude and latitude pairs (λ₁, φ₁) and (λ₂, φ₂). The central angle Δσ between them can be computed using the spherical law of cosines: Δσ = arccos(sin φ₁ sin φ₂ + cos φ₁ cos φ₂ cos Δλ). The arc length is then d = r Δσ, where r is the radius of the sphere. For numerical stability at small distances, the haversine formula is often preferred.

Applications

Airline routes between distant cities approximately follow great circles, which is why flights between North America and Asia often pass over or near the Arctic. Great-circle calculations are built into flight planning systems and geographic information systems.

Radius of the Earth

The Earth is not a perfect sphere but an oblate spheroid; calculations typically use a mean radius of about 6,371 kilometres. More precise calculations on the ellipsoid use methods such as Vincenty's formulae.
