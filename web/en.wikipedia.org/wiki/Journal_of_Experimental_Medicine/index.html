Journal of Experimental Medicine

The Journal of Experimental Medicine (JEM) is a peer-reviewed medical journal founded in 1896 by William Welch of Johns Hopkins University. It is published by Rockefeller University Press and covers research in experimental medicine, immunology, infectious disease, inflammation, cancer biology, stem cells, and related fields.

History

JEM began publication on January 1, 1896, at a time when experimental medicine was emerging as a discipline in the United States. In 1901 publication was taken over by the Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research (now Rockefeller University), which has published it ever since through its university press.

Scope and impact

The journal publishes original research articles, brief definitive reports, and reviews. Many landmark papers in immunology and microbiology have appeared in its pages, spanning the discovery of cytokines, mechanisms of immune tolerance, and the biology of viruses and tumors. JEM is among the most highly cited journals in immunology and experimental medicine.

Open access

Articles in JEM become freely available after an embargo period, and the journal's archive, extending back to 1896, is accessible through PubMed Central.
