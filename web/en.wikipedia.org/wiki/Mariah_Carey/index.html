Mariah Carey

Carey, "Queen of Christmas" and best-selling female artist in US history

Mariah Carey (born March 27, 1969) is an American singer, songwriter, record producer, and actress. Known for her five-octave vocal range, melismatic singing style, signature use of the whistle register, and songwriting, she is one of the best-selling music artists of all time, with more than 200 million records sold worldwide.

Early career

Born in Huntington, New York, to a mixed-race family, Carey began singing as a child, moved to New York City in 1988, and worked as a backing singer for Brenda K. Starr, whose demo tape reached Columbia Records executive Tommy Mottola. Her self-titled debut album (1990) produced four consecutive US number-one singles — "Vision of Love", "Love Takes Time", "Someday" and "I Don't Wanna Cry" — and won her two Grammy Awards.

Stardom and "All I Want for Christmas Is You"

Across the 1990s Carey dominated the charts with Music Box (1993), the five-times-platinum Daydream (1995), and Butterfly (1997), and she married Mottola in 1993, divorcing in 1998. Her 1994 holiday single "All I Want for Christmas Is You", co-written with Walter Afanasieff, became the best-selling Christmas song by a female artist and the best-selling single of all time by a solo artist, reaching US number one for the first time in 2019 and returning annually. She holds the record for the most number-one singles by a solo artist in the United States (19).

Later career

After a difficult period in 2001, including the poorly received film Glitter, Carey staged one of the most celebrated comebacks in pop with The Emancipation of Mimi (2005) and its single "We Belong Together", which topped the Billboard Hot 100 for fourteen weeks. She later served as a judge on American Idol, published the bestselling memoir The Meaning of Mariah Carey (2020), and continues to record and tour.
