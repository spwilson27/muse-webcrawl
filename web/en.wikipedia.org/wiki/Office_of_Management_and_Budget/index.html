The Office of Management and Budget (OMB) is the largest office within the Executive Office of the President of the United States. Its core mission is to assist the president in preparing the federal budget and to oversee the performance of federal agencies. The OMB director is a cabinet-level appointee confirmed by the Senate.

Functions

OMB assembles the president's annual budget request to Congress by reviewing agency submissions, holding hearings and issuing spending targets. It also coordinates the review of federal regulations through its Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA), which assesses the cost-benefit analyses of significant rules under executive orders. Other functions include legislative clearance (commenting on agency testimony and bills), management initiatives (financial management, IT, procurement and performance), and issuing circulars — such as Circular A-76 and Circular A-11 — that govern agency operations.

History

OMB descends from the Bureau of the Budget, created by the Budget and Accounting Act of 1921 and moved from the Treasury into the new Executive Office in 1939, where it became a powerful staff arm of the presidency under directors such as Harold Smith. President Richard Nixon reorganised it in 1970 (Reorganization Plan No. 2) as the Office of Management and Budget, adding a management remit. Its influence over domestic policy has waxed and waned with each administration; the director is sometimes nicknamed the "Budget Director" and is a central figure in fiscal politics.

Organisation

The office has roughly 500 staff, organised into Resource Management Offices aligned with agency groupings, plus statutory offices for information and regulatory affairs, federal procurement policy, financial management, and e-government. It is based mainly in the Eisenhower Executive Office Building and the New Executive Office Building in Washington, D.C.
