The Simien Mountains (Amharic: ስሜን ተራሮች) are a rugged mountain range in the Amhara Region of northern Ethiopia, forming part of the Ethiopian Highlands. Their dramatic escarpments — cliffs rising more than a kilometre above the surrounding plains — resulted from massive basaltic lava flows some 40–30 million years ago, subsequently eroded into jagged peaks, deep valleys, and precipices. The range includes Ras Dashen, at 4,550 metres the highest peak in Ethiopia.

Wildlife

The mountains are home to endemic species including the gelada baboon, the Ethiopian wolf, and the walia ibex. Much of the range is protected within the Simien Mountains National Park, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1978; the park was listed as endangered for many years owing to declines in walia ibex and other species, and to human settlement within its boundaries.
