Happy ending problem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The "happy ending problem" (so named because it led to the marriage of two of the mathematicians who worked on it) is the following result in discrete geometry: any set of five points in the plane in general position (no three collinear) has a subset of four points that form the vertices of a convex quadrilateral. More generally, for every positive integer n there is a smallest number of points that guarantees a convex n-gon among them.

History

The problem was posed by Esther Klein in the early 1930s and proved in full generality by Paul Erdős and George Szekeres in a famous 1935 paper, one of the founding works of Ramsey theory. Klein and Szekeres later married, giving the problem its name. Erdős and Szekeres conjectured that the exact number of points needed to force a convex n-gon is 2n−2 + 1; the conjecture is confirmed for small cases (for example, 5 points for quadrilaterals and 9 for pentagons) but remains open in general, though upper bounds have been substantially improved.
