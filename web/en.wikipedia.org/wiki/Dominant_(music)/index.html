Dominant (music)

In music, the dominant is the fifth scale degree of the diatonic scale, called "dominant" because it is the same distance above the tonic (a perfect fifth) as the subdominant is below it. The dominant chord is built on that degree and is typically represented by the Roman numeral V in major keys and v or V in minor keys.

Function

The dominant has a strong tendency to resolve to the tonic, especially in the dominant seventh chord (V7), whose tritone between the third and seventh drives the authentic cadence (V–I) that closes most phrases in common-practice harmony. Composers prolong or delay this resolution through devices such as the deceptive cadence (V–vi) and sequences of secondary dominants.

Related terms

The dominant also names the reciting tone in Gregorian chant psalmody, and the dominant key is the key of the fifth degree, the most common destination for modulation in sonata form.
