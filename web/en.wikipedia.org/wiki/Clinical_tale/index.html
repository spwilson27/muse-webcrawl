Clinical tale

A clinical tale is a case history written in narrative form, presenting a patient's symptoms, history, and treatment in the manner of a story. The genre is closely associated with psychoanalysis, in which the detailed narrative case study was a primary vehicle for presenting and arguing theory.

History

Sigmund Freud acknowledged that his case histories read like short stories, remarking in Studies on Hysteria (1895, with Josef Breuer) that the detailed presentation of mental processes he employed was unusual for a physician. His famous cases — including Dora, Little Hans, the Rat Man, the Wolf Man, and Schreber — were published as extended narratives and became central texts of psychoanalytic literature.

The neurologist and writer Oliver Sacks revived the clinical tale for a modern readership in books such as The Man Who Mistook His Wife for a Hat (1985) and An Anthropologist on Mars (1995), arguing for a "romantic science" that restores the patient as the subject of a biography rather than merely the bearer of a disease.
