Alan Stuart Edelman is an American mathematician and computer scientist. He is a professor of applied mathematics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) and a principal investigator at the MIT Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (CSAIL).

Research

Edelman's research spans numerical linear algebra, random matrix theory, and scientific computing. In random matrix theory he is known for work on the statistics of the smallest eigenvalues of random matrices, the condition numbers of random matrices, and the beta ensembles, which generalised the classical Gaussian ensembles to arbitrary inverse temperature β. He received the Chauvenet Prize in 1995 for his expository paper "How many zeros of a random polynomial are real?" written with Eric Kostlan.

Julia

Edelman is one of the creators of the Julia programming language, designed to combine the ease of use of dynamic languages with the speed of compiled languages for technical computing. He co-founded Julia Computing (later JuliaHub) to support the language's development and commercial use.
