Personnel selection

Personnel selection is the methodical process of selecting the best candidate for a job from a pool of applicants, using a combination of assessments to predict future job performance. It is a core function of human resource management and a major applied area of industrial and organisational psychology.

Process

Selection typically follows job analysis, which defines the knowledge, skills, abilities, and other characteristics (KSAOs) a role requires. Employers then screen applications, administer tests, conduct interviews, and check references or backgrounds before making an offer. Methods include cognitive ability tests, work sample tests, structured and unstructured interviews, personality inventories, situational judgement tests, assessment centres, and biodata.

Validity

Decades of meta-analytic research, notably by Frank Schmidt and John Hunter, find that general mental ability tests combined with structured interviews or work samples are among the strongest predictors of job performance, while unstructured interviews, years of experience, and graphology are weak predictors. Selection systems are evaluated for reliability, validity, utility, and fairness.

Legal and ethical considerations

In many countries selection procedures must comply with anti-discrimination law; in the United States, the Civil Rights Act and the Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures require that tests with adverse impact be demonstrably job-related. Modern concerns include algorithmic and AI-driven screening, candidate privacy, and the candidate experience.
