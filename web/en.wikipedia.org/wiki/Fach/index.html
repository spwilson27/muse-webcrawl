Fach

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Fach system (German: Fach, "subject/specialty"; plural Fächer) is a method of classifying singers, primarily opera singers, by the range, weight, and color of their voices. It is used worldwide, but principally in Europe, especially in German-speaking countries and in repertory opera houses, where each singer on the permanent ensemble is contracted to sing roles within an agreed Fach, with limited obligations outside it.

How the system works

A Fach is more specific than a general voice type such as soprano or tenor. Beyond range, it considers tessitura, vocal weight (the size of voice needed to carry over an orchestra in a given repertoire), timbre, and the typical dramatic character of roles. A singer engaged as a lyrischer Tenor (lyric tenor), for example, would not normally be asked to sing heldentenor roles, which demand a much heavier instrument.

Common Fächer

Examples include, for sopranos: Soubrette, lyric coloratura soprano, dramatic coloratura soprano, lyric soprano (lyrischer Sopran), young dramatic soprano (jugendlich dramatischer Sopran), and dramatic soprano (hochdramatischer Sopran). For tenors: Tenorbuffo (comic tenor), lyric tenor, jugendlicher Heldentenor, and Heldentenor. Lower voices include lyric baritone, cavalier baritone, character baritone, bass-baritone, character bass, and seriöser Bass (serious bass).

Purpose and criticism

The system protects singers' voices by preventing casting in unsuitable roles, and simplifies casting and contract negotiation in repertory houses. Critics argue it can be overly rigid, typecasting singers and discouraging the flexibility expected in freelance and American casting practice, where singers more often negotiate role by role.
