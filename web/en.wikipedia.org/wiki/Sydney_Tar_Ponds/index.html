Sydney Tar Ponds

The Sydney Tar Ponds were a hazardous waste site in Sydney, Nova Scotia, Canada, formed by a century of runoff from the coke ovens of the city's steel industry. Once described as the largest toxic waste site in Canada, the ponds were remediated in a major federal-provincial cleanup completed in 2013.

LocationSydney, Nova Scotia, Canada
CauseSteel and coke-oven waste in Muggah Creek
ContaminantsCoal tar, PAHs, metals, PCBs
Remediation completed2013 (opened as Open Hearth Park)

History

From 1901, steelmaking at Sydney discharged waste into the Muggah Creek estuary, creating tar ponds holding an estimated 700,000 tonnes of toxic sludge. Nearby residents, including the largely African Nova Scotian community of Whitney Pier, raised health concerns for decades.

Cleanup

After failed incineration plans, governments adopted a solidification and stabilisation approach: contaminants were mixed with cement-like agents and capped, the creek channel was rebuilt, and the site reopened in 2013 as Open Hearth Park with sports fields and trails.
