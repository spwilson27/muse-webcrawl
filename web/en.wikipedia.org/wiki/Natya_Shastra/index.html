Natya Shastra

The Natya Shastra (Sanskrit: Nāṭyaśāstra) is an ancient Indian treatise on the performing arts, traditionally attributed to the sage Bharata Muni. It is the foundational text of Indian theatre, dance and music, covering stagecraft, acting, dance movements, music, dramatic composition and aesthetics. The text is generally dated between roughly 200 BCE and 200 CE, though it likely accumulated over centuries.

Contents

Composed in Sanskrit verse across 36 or 37 chapters and some 6,000 verses, the treatise describes the mythical origin of drama as a "fifth Veda" created by the god Brahma to be accessible to all classes. It sets out detailed instructions on theatre construction, the preliminaries of performance, the training and movements of dancers, facial expressions and hand gestures (mudras), dramatic genres and plot construction, costumes and make-up, music and instruments, and the roles of the director (sutradhara) and the audience.

Rasa theory

The most influential part of the Natya Shastra is its theory of rasa — the aesthetic "flavour" or emotion savoured by the spectator. It identifies eight (later nine) rasas, including love (shringara), heroism (vira), wonder (adbhuta) and peace (shanta), each arising from durable emotional states (bhavas) conveyed through the performer's expression (abhinaya). The philosopher Abhinavagupta's 10th-century commentary, the Abhinavabharati, is the most famous interpretation and shaped Indian aesthetic theory for a millennium.

Influence

Classical Indian dance forms such as Bharatanatyam, Kathakali, Kathak, Odissi and Kuchipudi trace their technique and vocabulary to the Natya Shastra, as does traditional Indian theatre and much of Indian classical music theory.
