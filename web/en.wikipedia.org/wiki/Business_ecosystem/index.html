Business ecosystem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A business ecosystem is the network of organisations — including suppliers, distributors, customers, competitors, government agencies, and other stakeholders — involved in the delivery of a specific product or service through both competition and cooperation. The concept was popularised by James F. Moore in his 1993 Harvard Business Review article "Predators and Prey: A New Ecology of Competition".

Concept

Drawing an analogy with biological ecosystems, Moore argued that companies do not evolve in isolation but co-evolve within communities of interacting organisations. In this view, a successful strategy involves shaping the development of the whole ecosystem rather than merely beating direct competitors.

Structure

Business ecosystems are often described as having keystone players that create shared platforms or standards, niche players that specialise, and physical dominators that seek to integrate and control the network. In the digital economy, the idea underlies discussion of platform ecosystems built around products such as operating systems, app stores, and cloud services.

Criticism

Critics note that the biological metaphor can be stretched too far: firms, unlike organisms, act with foresight and intent, and membership in a business ecosystem is voluntary and fluid.
