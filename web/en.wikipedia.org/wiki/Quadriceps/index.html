The four muscles of the quadriceps femoris.

The quadriceps femoris (also called the quadriceps extensor, the quadriceps, or the quads) is a large muscle group that includes the four prevailing muscles on the front of the thigh. It is the great extensor muscle of the knee, forming a large fleshy mass which covers the front and sides of the femur. The name derives from Latin for "four-headed muscle of the femur".

Structure

The four muscles that make up the quadriceps are:

  • Rectus femoris — the only one of the four to cross the hip joint, arising from the ilium; it occupies the middle of the thigh.
  • Vastus lateralis — on the lateral (outer) side of the femur; the largest of the four.
  • Vastus medialis — on the medial (inner) side of the femur; its lowermost fibres form the "teardrop" above the knee.
  • Vastus intermedius — lying between the other vastus muscles, deep to the rectus femoris.

All four parts converge into the quadriceps tendon, which envelops the patella (kneecap) and continues as the patellar ligament to attach to the tibial tuberosity.

Function

The quadriceps is the primary extensor of the knee joint, essential for walking, running, jumping, kicking, and rising from a chair. The rectus femoris also flexes the thigh at the hip. The muscle group is supplied by the femoral nerve; the knee-jerk (patellar) reflex tests its spinal segments L2–L4.

Clinical significance

Contusions ("charley horses"), strains, and tendon ruptures are common sports injuries. Disuse or neurological damage can produce marked wasting, and strengthening the quadriceps is a mainstay of rehabilitation after knee surgery.
