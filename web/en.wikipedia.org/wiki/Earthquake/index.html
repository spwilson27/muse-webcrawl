Earthquake

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Cracked and buckled road after an earthquake (representative damage photograph)]

An earthquake is the shaking of the ground caused by a sudden movement of rock below Earth's surface, most often fault rupture driven by plate tectonics, and also by volcanic activity or human causes. Earthquakes are measured by magnitude (moment magnitude scale, successor to Richter) and by intensity of shaking (Modified Mercalli). Large subduction-zone quakes can generate tsunamis; the deadliest recorded earthquake, Shaanxi 1556, killed an estimated 800,000+ people, and the most powerful measured was Chile 1960 (magnitude 9.5).
