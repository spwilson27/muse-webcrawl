Battle of Breadfield

The Battle of Breadfield (Hungarian: Kenyérmezei csata; Romanian: Bătălia de pe Câmpul Pâinii) was fought on 13 October 1479 near the Mureș River in Transylvania, in present-day Romania, between the army of the Kingdom of Hungary and an Ottoman raiding army accompanied by Wallachian auxiliaries. It was one of the greatest Hungarian victories over the Ottomans, won largely through the timely intervention of the voivode of Transylvania, Stephen Báthory, and the heavy cavalry of Pál Kinizsi.

Background

In the autumn of 1479 a large Ottoman raiding force (akinji) crossed into Transylvania from Wallachia, plundering the Saxon towns of the Mureș valley and gathering enormous booty and captives. Transylvania's voivode, Stephen Báthory, assembled the county levies, Szeklers and Saxon troops, and, with Pál Kinizsi, captain of Timișoara, bringing reinforcements, moved to intercept the raiders as they withdrew.

Battle

The Hungarians caught the Ottomans on the Breadfield, a plain named — according to legend — because soldiers had once been fed there. The battle began badly: the Hungarian center and Báthory himself were driven back and the voivode was wounded and nearly killed. At the crisis of the battle Kinizsi's heavy cavalry charged into the Ottoman flank, collapsing their army. The raiders were slaughtered in large numbers and their booty and captives recovered. A famous, probably embroidered, tradition has Kinizsi dancing with a dead Turk in his teeth at the victory feast.

Significance

Breadfield was among the most celebrated Hungarian victories of the reign of Matthias Corvinus and ended major Ottoman raiding into Transylvania for several years. It remains a set-piece of Hungarian and Romanian regional memory.
