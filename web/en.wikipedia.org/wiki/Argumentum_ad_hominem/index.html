Ad hominem

Argumentum ad hominem (Latin for "argument to the person") is a term for several types of arguments, most of which are informal fallacies. Typically it refers to attacking the character, motive, or other attribute of the person making an argument rather than addressing the substance of the argument itself.

Types

Common forms include the abusive ad hominem (direct personal attack), the circumstantial ad hominem (suggesting a speaker's circumstances bias the claim), tu quoque ("you also", appealing to hypocrisy), and guilt by association.

Analysis

Philosophers note that not every reference to a person's character is fallacious: where credibility is itself at issue — for example, testimony — questions about a source's reliability can be relevant. The fallacy occurs when personal criticism is used as a substitute for engaging with the claim.
