Ford GPA

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Ford GPA ("Seep", for "sea jeep") was an amphibious version of the World War II Ford GPW jeep built by the Ford Motor Company. Some 12,778 were produced between 1942 and 1943. It was intended to give reconnaissance units the ability to cross rivers and make short water passages without a bridge.

Design

The GPA used the GPW's four-cylinder engine, four-wheel-drive running gear and transmission, fitted into a boat-like steel hull designed with input from the Sparkman & Stephens yacht firm. A propeller driven from the transfer case and a rudder steered by the front wheel gave about 5.5 knots in water; a bilge pump and a capstan winch were standard. On land it performed much like the standard jeep, though it was longer and heavier.

Service

Unlike the larger and highly successful DUKW amphibious truck, the GPA disappointed in service: it sat low in the water, could ship water easily in chop, was useless in surf, and could not climb steep or soft banks after swimming. Production was halted in 1943. Many GPAs were supplied under Lend-Lease to the Soviet Union, which valued them for its many river crossings and developed its own derivative, the GAZ-46. The United States used remaining vehicles in the Pacific late in the war.

Legacy

Surviving "Seeps" are prized by military vehicle collectors, and restored examples occasionally swim at shows — cautiously, and in calm water.
