Sola fide

Sola fide, Latin for "by faith alone", is a Christian theological doctrine held by most Protestant traditions — especially Lutheranism and the Reformed churches — which asserts that sinners are justified (declared righteous) before God by faith alone, apart from works of the law. Together with sola gratia (by grace alone) and sola scriptura (by Scripture alone), it is one of the classic "five solae" of the Protestant Reformation, and Martin Luther called justification by faith the article on which the church stands or falls (articulus stantis et cadentis ecclesiae).

Biblical basis

Defenders cite especially Paul's letters: "For we hold that one is justified by faith apart from works of the law" (Romans 3:28) and "by grace you have been saved through faith... not a result of works" (Ephesians 2:8–9). Luther, translating Romans into German, added the word allein ("alone") and defended the rendering as faithful to Paul's meaning. Protestants hold that faith is always accompanied by good works as its fruit, but that works contribute nothing to the act of justification itself.

Catholic response

The Roman Catholic Church, at the Council of Trent (1545–1563), rejected sola fide as Protestants formulated it, decreeing that justification involves faith working through love (Galatians 5:6), and anathematizing the claim that justifying faith is "nothing else but confidence in the divine mercy". Catholic theology teaches that justification is a process of sanctification and renewal, not only a forensic declaration. The Epistle of James — "a person is justified by works and not by faith alone" (James 2:24), the only place where the phrase "faith alone" appears in Scripture — is central to the Catholic reading.

Later developments

Methodists and many evangelicals affirm sola fide while differing from Lutherans on the possibility of apostasy and the role of sanctification. In 1999, the Lutheran World Federation and the Catholic Church signed the Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification, affirming a shared core: "By grace alone, in faith in Christ's saving work and not because of any merit on our part, we are accepted by God" — while acknowledging remaining differences in emphasis. Similar statements followed with Methodist, Anglican, and Reformed bodies.
