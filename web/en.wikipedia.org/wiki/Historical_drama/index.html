Historical drama

A historical drama (also known as a period drama, costume drama, or period piece) is a work of fiction—film, television, theatre, or literature—set in an earlier historical period. Historical dramas typically strive for visual authenticity in costume, set design, and language while telling stories that may be factual, fictionalized, or wholly invented within a real setting.

Characteristics and history

The genre ranges from Shakespeare's history plays to lavish film epics such as Lawrence of Arabia and television series like Downton Abbey, The Crown, and Rome. Korean sageuk, Japanese jidaigeki, and Chinese historical epics are prominent national traditions. Writers often balance fidelity to the record against dramatic licence, and the genre is frequently used to comment on present-day politics and society.

Criticism

Historical dramas are sometimes criticized for inaccuracies that reshape public memory of the past, though they also popularize history and drive interest in heritage sites and reading.
