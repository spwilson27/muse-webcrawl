Harwell Science and Innovation Campus

The Harwell Science and Innovation Campus is a 700-acre science and technology campus in Oxfordshire, England, near the villages of Harwell and Chilton. Originally established in 1946 as the Atomic Energy Research Establishment (AERE), the principal centre for nuclear research in the United Kingdom, the site has been redeveloped into a multi-disciplinary campus hosting national research facilities and more than 200 organisations employing some 6,000 people.

The Harwell campus, home to the Diamond Light Source and Rutherford Appleton Laboratory.

History

The site began as RAF Harwell, a wartime airfield. In 1946 it became the Atomic Energy Research Establishment under the direction of John Cockcroft, where Britain's first nuclear reactors, GLEEP (1947) and BEPO (1948), were built. Harwell played a central role in civil and military nuclear research until the 1990s, when the UK Atomic Energy Authority refocused on fusion research at Culham and began decommissioning and redeveloping the Harwell site.

Major facilities

The campus hosts the Rutherford Appleton Laboratory, home to the ISIS Neutron and Muon Source and the Central Laser Facility; the Diamond Light Source synchrotron; the Rosalind Franklin Institute; the UK Health Security Agency laboratories; and elements of the European Space Agency's ECSAT centre. Its organisations work across health, space, energy, quantum computing, and advanced materials. The campus is owned and managed as a joint venture between the UK Atomic Energy Authority, the Science and Technology Facilities Council, and private development partners.
