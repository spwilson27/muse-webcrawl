Carnitas (Spanish for "little meats") is a dish of Mexican cuisine originating in the state of Michoacán. It is made by braising or simmering pork — traditionally pork shoulder — in lard until tender, a method analogous to French confit, and then briefly roasting or frying the meat at higher heat to crisp the exterior. The result is tender, flavourful shredded meat with browned, crunchy edges.

Preparation

In the traditional method, chunks of pork are cooked slowly in a large copper pot (cazo) in rendered lard, often seasoned with salt, and sometimes with orange peel or juice, bay leaves, garlic, milk, or cola in regional and modern variations. Traditionally the whole pig may be prepared, with different cuts — ribs, skin (cueritos), snout, ears, and offal — sold by the cut. The towns of Quiroga and Santa Clara del Cobre in Michoacán are especially famous for their carnitas.

Serving

Carnitas are most commonly served in tacos with chopped onion, cilantro, salsa, and lime, but also appear in burritos, tortas, tamales, and as part of mixed platters. The dish is a staple of taquerías throughout Mexico and the United States.
