Blood status is a concept in J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter fictional universe concerning the magical ancestry of witches and wizards. Within the wizarding world, individuals are classified by the magical status of their parents and ancestors, and this classification carries strong social and political significance, functioning as a central allegory for prejudice in the series.

Categories

Pure-bloods are witches or wizards with exclusively magical ancestry, at least as far as can be traced. In practice no family is entirely free of non-magical ancestry, and "pure-blood" status is a social and political claim maintained by a small number of families, many of them listed in the fictional Pure-Blood Directory. Half-bloods have at least one magical parent and at least one non-magical (Muggle) parent or grandparent; most of the wizarding population falls into this category. Muggle-borns are witches and wizards born to two non-magical parents; the derogatory slur "Mudblood" is used against them by blood-supremacist characters. A Squib is the non-magical child of magical parents.

Role in the series

Belief in the superiority of pure blood is the ideology of Lord Voldemort and the Death Eaters, although Voldemort himself is a half-blood. Under the Death Eaters' regime in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows, the Ministry of Magic establishes a Muggle-Born Registration Commission that persecutes Muggle-borns. Rowling has described the blood-status hierarchy as an allegory of racism and eugenics, and the theme underlies much of the series' moral framework.
