Ligue 1 is the top division of the French football league system and one of Europe's "big five" leagues. Run by the Ligue de Football Professionnel, it was founded in 1932 as the professional National championship, became Division 1, and was renamed Ligue 1 in 2002. Eighteen clubs compete in a double round-robin season from August to May, with the bottom clubs relegated to Ligue 2.

History

Before professionalism, French football was amateur and regional. The first professional championship in 1932–33 was won by Olympique Lillois. Saint-Étienne dominated the 1960s and 1970s, winning a record-setting ten titles; Marseille won the European Cup in 1993, the only French club to do so for three decades. Lyon won seven consecutive titles from 2002 to 2008. Since the Qatari takeover of Paris Saint-Germain in 2011, PSG have dominated the league, surpassing Saint-Étienne's title record, powered by stars such as Zlatan Ibrahimović, Neymar, and Kylian Mbappé; PSG finally won the UEFA Champions League in 2025.

Format and European qualification

The champions and leading finishers qualify for the UEFA Champions League, with further places for the Europa League and Conference League. The league is renowned as a developer and exporter of young talent, supplying players to the other major European leagues. AS Monaco, based outside France, participates by long-standing agreement.
