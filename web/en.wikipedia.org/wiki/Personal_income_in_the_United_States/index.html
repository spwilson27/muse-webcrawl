Personal income in the United States

Personal income in the United States is measured by agencies including the U.S. Census Bureau and the Bureau of Economic Analysis. Personal income is an individual's total earnings from wages, investment interest, dividends, and other sources, and is a key indicator used alongside household income.

Patterns

Median personal income rises strongly with educational attainment: workers with professional and doctoral degrees earn substantially more than those with only a high school diploma. Income also varies by age, peaking in the 45–54 bracket, by occupation, by state — highest in states such as Massachusetts, Connecticut, and California — and by sex and race, gaps that are widely studied.

Measurement

The Census Bureau's Current Population Survey and American Community Survey provide annual estimates. Figures may be reported as mean or median, for individuals or households, and before or after taxes and transfers; comparisons across sources require care.
