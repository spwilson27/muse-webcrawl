Trooping the Colour

Trooping the Colour is a ceremony performed every year in London, and in Commonwealth realms, by regiments of the British Army. It has marked the official birthday of the British sovereign since 1748, and is held on a Saturday in June on Horse Guards Parade.

Origins

A regiment's "colours" — its consecrated flags — were once carried ("trooped") slowly through the ranks before battle so that every soldier could recognise his rallying point. The ceremony of trooping the colour of one battalion of the Foot Guards in the sovereign's presence became attached to the monarch's official birthday celebrations in the 18th century.

The ceremony

The sovereign travels down the Mall from Buckingham Palace and takes the salute. The escorted colour is then trooped through the ranks of the five regiments of Foot Guards — Grenadier, Coldstream, Scots, Irish, and Welsh Guards — accompanied by massed bands and the mounted troops of the Household Cavalry. Some 1,400 soldiers, 200 horses, and 400 musicians take part. The ceremony ends with a flypast by the Royal Air Force, watched from the Buckingham Palace balcony.
