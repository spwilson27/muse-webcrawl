Grappa

Grappa is an Italian pomace brandy: a fragrant, grape-based spirit made by distilling the pomace — the skins, pulp, seeds, and stems left over from winemaking. It typically contains 35–60% alcohol by volume and is protected as a denomination within the European Union, where "grappa" may be produced only in Italy (and the Italian part of Switzerland) from Italian-grown grapes.

Production and styles

Traditionally a peasant drink made to extract value from winery waste, grappa is now also produced as a premium spirit, often from a single grape variety (monovitigno) and sometimes aged in wooden casks, which gives it a golden colour and softer flavour. Young grappa (giovane) is clear and fiery; aged (invecchiata) and aromatic styles are smoother and more complex.

Serving

Grappa is usually served as a digestivo after meals, in small tulip-shaped glasses, and in north-eastern Italy is also added to espresso as a caffè corretto. The town of Bassano del Grappa in the Veneto is one of its historic centres of production.
