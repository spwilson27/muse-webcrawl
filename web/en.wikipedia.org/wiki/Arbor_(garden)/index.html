Arbor (garden)

An arbor (American English) or arbour (British English) is a vertical garden structure that can provide shelter, privacy, a transition space, or a focal point in a garden. Arbors are typically made of wood or metal latticework and are designed to support climbing plants, which over time grow over and through the frame, creating a shaded passageway or alcove.

Design

A classic arbor consists of two or four posts with an open, slatted or lattice roof, frequently arched, and often incorporates trellis sides and sometimes a built-in bench. It is usually placed over a gate, path, or patio entrance. Plants commonly grown over arbors include roses, wisteria, grapevines, jasmine, honeysuckle, and clematis.

Arbors are frequently confused with pergolas, which are generally larger, have flat roofs supported by columns, and cover a more extensive area such as a terrace; with gazebos, which are roofed, open-sided pavilions; and with trellises, which are flat panels used to support plants against a wall or as screens.

History

Shaded, vine-covered structures descend from the garden architecture of Egypt, Persia, and Rome, where grape arbors sheltered walks and dining spaces. In medieval and Renaissance gardens, the "herber" — a bower of woven branches and turf seats — was a standard feature of pleasure gardens, and the modern garden arbor is its descendant.
