Savannah is the oldest city in the U.S. state of Georgia and the county seat of Chatham County. Established in 1733 on the Savannah River by General James Oglethorpe, it became the colonial capital of the Province of Georgia and later the first state capital.

City plan

Savannah is famous for its original town plan, a grid of public squares laid out by Oglethorpe; 22 of the original 24 squares survive, shaded by live oaks draped with Spanish moss. The Savannah Historic District, one of the largest National Historic Landmark Districts in the United States, preserves hundreds of 18th- and 19th-century buildings.

History

The city grew rich as an Atlantic port shipping cotton; in 1793 Eli Whitney invented the cotton gin nearby. During the American Civil War, Savannah was presented to President Lincoln by General Sherman at the end of his March to the Sea in December 1864. Robert E. Lee's father, Henry "Light-Horse Harry" Lee, died and was buried in the area, and the city was the birthplace of Juliette Gordon Low, founder of the Girl Scouts of the USA.

Economy

The Port of Savannah is one of the busiest container ports in the United States. Tourism centred on the historic district, Forsyth Park, and River Street is a major industry, alongside manufacturing, the military, and the Savannah College of Art and Design.
