Bayreuth Festival

The Bayreuth Festival (German: Bayreuther Festspiele) is a music festival held annually in Bayreuth, Germany, at which performances of stage works by the 19th-century composer Richard Wagner are presented. Wagner himself conceived the festival and the purpose-built theatre, the Bayreuth Festspielhaus, to present his works — above all the four-opera cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen and Parsifal — under ideal conditions. The festival opened in 1876 with the first complete performance of the Ring.

History

Financed partly by King Ludwig II of Bavaria and by Wagner societies, the first festival of 1876 was attended by emperors, kings and composers including Tchaikovsky and Grieg. Wagner directed the second festival in 1882, premiering Parsifal, written expressly for the Festspielhaus and its hidden orchestra pit — the "mystic abyss". After Wagner's death his widow Cosima, then his son Siegfried, ran the festival. Under Winifred Wagner it became entangled with the Nazi regime; after the war, Wieland and Wolfgang Wagner's austere "New Bayreuth" productions from 1951 revolutionised opera staging.

The festival today

The festival runs for about a month each summer with around 30 performances of seven operas before some 60,000 attendees. Demand vastly exceeds supply — waiting lists for tickets have traditionally stretched to many years. Since 2008 the festival has been directed by members of the Wagner family, most recently Katharina Wagner, the composer's great-granddaughter.
