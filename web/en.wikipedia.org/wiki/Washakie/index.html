Washakie

Washakie (c. 1804–1900) was a prominent leader of the Eastern Shoshone people. Renowned as a warrior in his youth and as a diplomat in later life, he negotiated the treaties that established the Wind River Reservation in present-day Wyoming, where he spent his final decades.

Early life and leadership

Born around 1804, possibly of Shoshone and Flathead parentage, Washakie rose to leadership of the Eastern Shoshone through military skill against the Blackfeet, Crow and Sioux. He became known to American officials as a reliable ally, assisting emigrants on the Oregon Trail and serving as a scout for the U.S. Army.

Treaties and Wind River

Washakie signed the Treaty of Fort Bridger in 1863 and again in 1868, the latter creating a reservation of over 44 million acres for the Shoshone, later reduced by successive agreements. He consistently advocated accommodation with the United States as the best means of preserving his people's land and survival, while resisting the forced placement of the Arapaho on Shoshone land.

Later life

Washakie was baptised in the Episcopal Church in 1897 and was buried with full military honours at Fort Washakie in 1900, the only Native American leader to receive such honours at the time. The town of Washakie, Wyoming, and Camp Washakie are named for him.
