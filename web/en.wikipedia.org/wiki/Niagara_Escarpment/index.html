Niagara Escarpment

The Niagara Escarpment is a long, arc-shaped cuesta (escarpment) in North America running roughly 1,050 kilometres from western New York through Ontario, Michigan, Wisconsin, and Illinois. It is composed of resistant Silurian dolomite and limestone over softer shales, which erode to leave a steep cliff face.

Geology and features

The escarpment was formed by differential erosion of rock layers deposited in an ancient sea around 420–440 million years ago. The Niagara River plunges over it at Niagara Falls. In Ontario it rises more than 300 metres in places and carries the Bruce Trail, Canada's oldest long-distance footpath, for some 900 kilometres.

Protection

Ontario's Niagara Escarpment Plan (1985) was Canada's first large-scale environmental land-use plan, and the escarpment was designated a UNESCO World Biosphere Reserve in 1990. Its cliffs host ancient eastern white cedars, some more than a thousand years old, and support distinctive ecosystems and wine-growing regions below the escarpment's sheltered slopes.
