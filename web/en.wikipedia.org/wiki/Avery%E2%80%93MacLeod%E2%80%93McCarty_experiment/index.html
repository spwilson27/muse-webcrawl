Avery–MacLeod–McCarty experiment

The Avery–MacLeod–McCarty experiment, reported in 1944 by Oswald Avery, Colin MacLeod and Maclyn McCarty, demonstrated that DNA was the substance that caused bacterial transformation — in effect, that DNA, not protein, was the material of heredity.

Background

In 1928 Frederick Griffith had shown that a non-virulent "rough" strain of Streptococcus pneumoniae could be transformed into a virulent "smooth" strain by exposure to heat-killed smooth bacteria, but the identity of the "transforming principle" was unknown. Working at the Rockefeller Institute, Avery's team spent years purifying the active substance from the killed bacteria.

The experiment

Experiment summary

Published1944
AuthorsOswald Avery, Colin MacLeod, Maclyn McCarty

FindingDNA is the transforming principle
Confirmed byHershey–Chase experiment (1952)

The researchers fractionated the extract and tested which component carried transforming activity. The active fraction had the chemical properties of DNA: enzymes that digest proteins or RNA left it active, while the DNA-digesting enzyme deoxyribonuclease destroyed its activity. They concluded that DNA was the transforming principle, implying that genes are made of DNA.

Many scientists were initially sceptical, believing proteins — far more chemically varied than DNA — were likelier carriers of genetic information, and suspecting trace protein contamination. The 1952 Hershey–Chase experiment broadly confirmed the conclusion, and the experiment is now regarded as a landmark of molecular biology.
