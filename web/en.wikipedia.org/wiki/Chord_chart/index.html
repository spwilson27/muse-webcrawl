Chord chart
TypesFingering diagram; lead-sheet chord symbols
Used byGuitarists, ukulele players, accompanists
RelatedLead sheet, fake book, Nashville Number System

A chord chart is a compact written representation of musical chords. The term is used for two related tools: (1) a fingering diagram showing where to place the fingers on a fretted instrument such as guitar or ukulele, and (2) a chart of chord symbols for a song — essentially a lead sheet stripped of melody — giving the sequence of chords over the structure of the tune so that an accompanist can play along.

Fingering diagrams

A fingering chord chart draws a small grid representing the fretboard: vertical lines are strings, horizontal lines are frets, and dots mark finger positions. Symbols above the grid indicate open strings (O) and muted strings (X); a thick bar at the top represents the nut, or a number indicates the starting fret higher up the neck. Numbers inside or below the dots may show which finger to use. Such charts are a first reference for beginning guitarists learning open chords such as C, G, D, and Am.

Song charts and lead sheets

In the second sense, a chord chart arranges a song as a series of bars with chord symbols (e.g., Cmaj7, F#dim, Bb7) written above the bar lines, often with section labels (verse, chorus, bridge) and repeat signs rather than fully notated music. Rhythm-section players in jazz, pop, worship bands, and studio sessions routinely work from chord charts, improvising their parts around the given harmony. The Nashville Number System is a variant that writes chords as scale-degree numbers so a song can be transposed instantly to any key.
