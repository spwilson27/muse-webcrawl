Peter Carl Fabergé (born Karl Gustavovich Fabergé; 30 May 1846 – 24 September 1920) was a Russian jeweller best known for the famous Fabergé eggs made in the style of genuine Easter eggs, but using precious metals and gemstones rather than more mundane materials. He was one of the sons of the founder of the famous jewellery legacy House of Fabergé.

Life and career

Fabergé was born in Saint Petersburg to the Baltic German jeweller Gustav Fabergé and his Danish wife Charlotte Jungstedt. He trained as a goldsmith in Germany, France, Italy and England before taking over his father's firm in 1872. Under his direction the House of Fabergé became Russia's largest and most celebrated jewellery firm, with branches in Moscow, Odessa, Kiev and London.

Imperial Easter eggs

In 1885 Tsar Alexander III commissioned an Easter egg for his wife, Maria Feodorovna; the resulting "Hen Egg" so delighted the imperial family that the commission became an annual tradition. Fabergé's workshop produced about 50 Imperial eggs for Alexander III and Nicholas II, each an elaborate enamelled and jewelled object containing a miniature surprise. Following the Russian Revolution, the firm was nationalised and Fabergé fled abroad, dying in Switzerland in 1920.

Legacy

Fabergé's name became synonymous with luxury craftsmanship. The surviving Imperial eggs are held by museums and collectors worldwide, including the Fabergé Museum in Saint Petersburg, and are among the most famous decorative art objects ever made.
