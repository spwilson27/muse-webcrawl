Joan Jonas

Joan Jonas (born July 13, 1936) is an American visual artist and a pioneer of video and performance art, and one of the most important artists to emerge from the late 1960s and early 1970s. Her work combines drawing, sculpture, video, installation, and performance, often drawing on myth, literature, mirrors, masks, and ritual.

Early life and education

Born in New York City, Jonas studied art history at Mount Holyoke College, sculpture at the School of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, and received an MFA from Columbia University in 1965. Travels in Japan, Iceland, and elsewhere shaped her interest in landscape and storytelling.

Career

Early performances such as Mirror Pieces (1968–71) and the Organic Honey series (1972 onward) explored identity and the female persona through mirrors and early video monitors. Works including The Juniper Tree (1976), Lines in the Sand (2002), and Reading Dante (2007–10) layered live action with projected drawing.

She represented the United States at the 2015 Venice Biennale with They Come to Us without a Word, addressing oceans and climate. She has taught at MIT, serving as professor emerita. Major retrospectives were held at Tate Modern (2018) and the Museum of Modern Art, New York (2024).
