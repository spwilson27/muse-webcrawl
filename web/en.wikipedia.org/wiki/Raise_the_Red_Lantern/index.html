Raise the Red Lantern (大紅燈籠高高掛) is a 1991 Chinese period drama film directed by Zhang Yimou and starring Gong Li. It is an adaptation of Su Tong's 1990 novella Wives and Concubines. The film is widely regarded as one of the finest of the Fifth Generation of Chinese filmmakers and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.

Plot

In 1920s China, nineteen-year-old Songlian, whose father has died leaving the family bankrupt, gives up her university education and marries into the wealthy Chen household as the Fourth Mistress. The master of the house chooses each night which of his four wives to visit; red lanterns are lit outside her quarters, and she receives privileges including a foot massage and choosing the next day's menu. The wives — the aging First Mistress, the outwardly friendly but scheming Second Mistress, the opera-singer Third Mistress, and Songlian — compete bitterly for favor. Songlian feigns pregnancy to gain advantage, but is exposed by her maid Yan'er, who harbors her own fantasies. The intrigue culminates in tragedy: Yan'er is punished fatally for secretly lighting lanterns of her own, and the Third Mistress is murdered after being discovered with a lover. Songlian, traumatized, descends into madness as a new Fifth Mistress arrives.

Production and reception

The film was shot at the Qiao Family Compound in Shanxi province, whose grey courtyards contrast with the vivid red lanterns, in a striking color design characteristic of Zhang's work. It won the Silver Lion at the Venice Film Festival and was initially banned in China for a period before being released. Critics praised its visual composition, Gong Li's performance, and its critique of feudal patriarchy and ritualized oppression.
