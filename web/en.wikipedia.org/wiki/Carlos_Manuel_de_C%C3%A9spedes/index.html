Carlos Manuel de Céspedes

Carlos Manuel de Céspedes
Born: 18 April 1819, Bayamo, Cuba
Died: 27 February 1874, Sierra Maestra, Cuba
Known for: launching the Ten Years' War (1868); Grito de Yara
Title: Father of the Homeland (Padre de la Patria)
Office: President of the Republic of Cuba in Arms (1869–1873)

Carlos Manuel de Céspedes del Castillo (18 April 1819 – 27 February 1874) was a Cuban revolutionary leader and sugar planter who initiated the Ten Years' War, Cuba's first war of independence from Spain. On 10 October 1868, at his estate La Demajagua near Manzanillo, he freed his slaves and issued the Grito de Yara, a declaration of Cuban independence — an act commemorated as the birth of the Cuban nation.

Revolutionary leadership

Céspedes, a wealthy landowner educated in Havana and Spain, had become convinced that Spain would never grant reform. His manifesto called for independence, gradual abolition of slavery, and equality of all Cubans. In April 1869 the Guáimaro assembly elected him President of the Republic in Arms. His presidency was marked by friction with the civilian assembly and rival commanders, and political opponents deposed him in October 1873.

Death and legacy

Stripped of his escort, Céspedes was surprised by Spanish troops at San Lorenzo in the Sierra Maestra on 27 February 1874 and killed while resisting capture. Cubans honour him as the Padre de la Patria, the Father of the Homeland, and 10 October is a national holiday in Cuba. His son of the same name briefly served as President of Cuba in 1933.
