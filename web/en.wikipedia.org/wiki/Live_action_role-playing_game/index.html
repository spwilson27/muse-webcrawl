Live action role-playing game

A live action role-playing game (LARP) is a form of role-playing game where the participants physically portray their characters. The players pursue goals within a fictional setting represented by real-world environments while interacting with each other in character. The outcome of player actions may be mediated by game rules or decided by consensus among the players, and event arrangers called gamemasters decide the setting and mediate the game.

History

LARP grew out of tabletop role-playing games, historical reenactment and improvisational theatre in the late 1970s and 1980s. Groups such as the Society for Creative Anachronism (founded 1966) pioneered costumed role-play, while early dedicated LARP organisations included Dagorhir (1977) in the United States and Treasure Trap (1982) in the United Kingdom. The publication of Mind's Eye Theatre in the 1990s, adapting the Vampire: The Masquerade setting, popularised parlour-style LARPs based on social intrigue rather than combat.

Styles

Styles vary widely. "Boffer" LARPs simulate combat with padded foam weapons; Nordic larp emphasises immersion, emotion and artistic or political themes, often avoiding formal rules; parlour LARPs take place in a single indoor location over a few hours; campaign LARPs run recurring events in a persistent world; and large "fest" events, such as Denmark's Epic Empires or Germany's ConQuest of Mythodea, draw thousands of costumed participants battling with latex weapons.

Gameplay

Players create characters defined by abilities, background and goals, dress in costume appropriate to the setting — medieval fantasy is the most common genre, but horror, science fiction, post-apocalyptic and historical settings are also popular — and act out their characters' actions in real time. Combat may be resolved by physical mock fighting, by abstract mechanics such as rock-paper-scissors or card draws, or by negotiation.
