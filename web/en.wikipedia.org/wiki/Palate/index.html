Palate

The palate is the roof of the mouth in humans and other mammals. It separates the oral cavity from the nasal cavity. It consists of two parts: the bony hard palate at the front, formed by the maxilla and palatine bones, and the muscular soft palate (velum) at the back, which ends in the uvula.

Function

The palate is essential to sucking, swallowing, and speech. The soft palate rises to close off the nasopharynx during swallowing, preventing food and liquid from entering the nose; its position also shapes airflow and resonance in speech, distinguishing oral from nasal sounds. The hard palate's ridges (rugae) help the tongue manipulate food.

Development and disorders

The palate forms in the embryo by the fusion of palatal shelves; failure of fusion produces a cleft palate, one of the most common congenital conditions, usually repaired surgically in infancy. The word "palate" also gives us "palatable" and the sense of taste, since the palate was once thought the seat of flavour perception.
