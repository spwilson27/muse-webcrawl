Zvezda (ISS module)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zvezda (ISS module)

Launched12 July 2000 (Proton)
RoleRussian segment service module: life support, quarters, propulsion
Derived fromMir-class / Salyut design lineage
Zvezda in 2000.

Zvezda (Russian: Звезда, "star"), formally the DOS-8 Service Module, is the structural and functional heart of the International Space Station's Russian segment. Launched by a Proton rocket on 12 July 2000 and docked to the infant station a fortnight later, it provided the first full living quarters, life support, communications and propulsion — the module that turned two docked components into a habitable outpost and received the first resident crew that November.

Zvezda descends directly from the Salyut and Mir core-module lineage: a stepped cylinder with a work compartment, sleeping cabins for two, a toilet, galley table, and treadmill — plus the aft docking port where Progress and Soyuz vehicles arrive, and the engines long used to reboost the station.

Ageing heart

A quarter-century on, Zvezda is the station's most maintenance-hungry module: a slow air leak in its aft transfer tunnel, first noticed in 2019, has been hunted and patched for years and is managed by keeping the hatch closed except during cargo operations. Its systems' gradual handover — to the newer Nauka module and to the US segment — tracks the station's own ageing, ahead of its planned retirement around 2030.
