Ha'il

Ha'il (Arabic: حائل) is a city in north-western Saudi Arabia and the capital of Ha'il Province. Located in the Shammar Mountains region, it was historically a stop on pilgrimage and trade routes across the Arabian Peninsula and the seat of the Rashidi dynasty's Emirate of Jabal Shammar in the 19th and early 20th centuries.

History

Ha'il was ruled by the Al Rashid dynasty until 1921, when it was incorporated into Ibn Saud's kingdom. The region is known for rock art sites inscribed by UNESCO, including Jubbah and Shuwaymis, and for the poetry and hospitality associated with Hatim al-Tai, a legendary figure from the area.
