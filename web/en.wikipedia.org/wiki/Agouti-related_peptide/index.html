Agouti-related peptide

Agouti-related peptide (AgRP), also called agouti-related protein, is a neuropeptide produced in the arcuate nucleus of the hypothalamus by neurons that also express neuropeptide Y (NPY). AgRP is one of the most potent and long-lasting stimulators of appetite known.

Function

AgRP acts as an inverse agonist and antagonist at melanocortin receptors MC3R and MC4R, opposing the appetite-suppressing effects of α-MSH released by neighboring POMC neurons. Levels of AgRP rise during fasting — it is inhibited by leptin and stimulated by ghrelin — driving increased food intake and decreased energy expenditure. Its effects can persist for days after a single administration in animal experiments, in marked contrast to most other feeding signals.

Discovery and significance

AgRP was identified in 1997 through its sequence similarity to agouti signaling protein, which affects coat color in mice. Genetic variants affecting the melanocortin pathway that AgRP regulates are among the most common known causes of severe monogenic obesity, and the system is a target of anti-obesity drug development.
