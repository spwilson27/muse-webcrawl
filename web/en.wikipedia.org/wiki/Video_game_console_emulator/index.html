Video game console emulator

A video game console emulator is a type of emulator that allows a computing device to emulate a video game console's hardware and play its games on the emulating platform. Emulators are often used to play older games on modern hardware and for preservation, homebrew development and fan translation.

Methods

Low-level emulation reproduces hardware behaviour cycle-accurately, demanding more processing power; high-level emulation intercepts graphics and system calls, trading accuracy for speed. Modern emulators such as Dolphin (GameCube/Wii), PCSX2 (PlayStation 2) and Ryujinx/Yuzu-era Switch emulators demonstrate how far the field has advanced.

ROM images

Games are supplied as ROM images dumped from cartridges or discs. Downloading copyrighted ROMs is generally infringement in most jurisdictions, while dumping one's own media occupies a contested grey area.

Legality

Court cases including Sony v. Connectix (2000) and Sony v. Bleem! established in the United States that reverse-engineered emulation itself is lawful, though circumvention of copy protection and distribution of BIOS files remain legally fraught.
