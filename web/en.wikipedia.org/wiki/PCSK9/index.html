PCSK9

Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) is an enzyme encoded in humans by the PCSK9 gene on chromosome 1. It plays a central role in cholesterol homeostasis by regulating the number of low-density lipoprotein (LDL) receptors available on the surface of liver cells.

Function

PCSK9 binds to the LDL receptor on hepatocytes. When the PCSK9–receptor complex is internalised, the receptor is directed to the lysosome for degradation rather than being recycled back to the cell surface. Higher levels of PCSK9 therefore reduce LDL receptor numbers and raise circulating LDL cholesterol, while lower PCSK9 activity has the opposite effect.

Clinical significance

Gain-of-function mutations in PCSK9 were identified in 2003 as a cause of familial hypercholesterolaemia, while loss-of-function mutations are associated with low LDL cholesterol and a markedly reduced risk of coronary heart disease. These findings made PCSK9 an attractive drug target.

PCSK9 inhibitors

Monoclonal antibodies that block PCSK9, including alirocumab and evolocumab, were approved from 2015 onwards and can lower LDL cholesterol by around 60 percent. A small interfering RNA therapy, inclisiran, suppresses PCSK9 synthesis in the liver and is dosed twice yearly.
