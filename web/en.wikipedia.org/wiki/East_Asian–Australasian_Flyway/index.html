East Asian–Australasian Flyway

The East Asian–Australasian Flyway is one of the world's nine major migratory waterbird flyways. It stretches from breeding grounds in the Russian Far East and Alaska, through East and Southeast Asia, to non-breeding grounds in Australasia, including Australia and New Zealand. More than 50 million migratory waterbirds of over 250 populations use the flyway each year.

Geography

The flyway passes through some 22 countries, including Russia, Mongolia, China, Japan, the Korean Peninsula, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Papua New Guinea, the Philippines, Australia and New Zealand. Key stopover habitats include the tidal mudflats of the Yellow Sea, which are critical refuelling sites for shorebirds migrating between Siberia and Australia.

Species

Notable species include the bar-tailed godwit, famous for non-stop trans-Pacific flights between Alaska and New Zealand; the Far Eastern curlew; the red knot; and threatened species such as the spoon-billed sandpiper, Nordmann's greenshank and the Siberian crane. Around a fifth of the flyway's waterbird populations are considered threatened or near-threatened.

Conservation

Habitat loss — especially the reclamation of Yellow Sea intertidal flats in China and South Korea — is the principal threat, and several shorebird populations have declined steeply. The East Asian–Australasian Flyway Partnership (EAAFP), launched in 2006, coordinates conservation among governments, NGOs and site managers, and maintains a network of internationally important Flyway Network Sites. Parts of the Chinese Yellow Sea coast were inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage sites in 2019.
