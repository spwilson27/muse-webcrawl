Kingdom of Pontus

Period281 BC – AD 62 (annexed by Rome)

RegionSouth coast of the Black Sea, northeastern Anatolia

The Kingdom of Pontus (Greek: Βασιλεία τοῦ Πόντου) was a Hellenistic kingdom centered in the historical region of Pontus on the southern shore of the Black Sea in northeastern Anatolia (modern Turkey). Founded in 281 BC by Mithridates I Ktistes, it was ruled by the Mithridatic dynasty, of mixed Persian and Greek descent, until its conquest by the Roman Republic and final annexation under Nero.

Geography and culture

Pontus comprised fertile coastal plains and rugged mountains, with Greek colonies such as Sinope, Amisus, and Trapezus on the coast and the royal capital at Amaseia inland; the Pontic royal tombs carved into the cliffs at Amaseia still survive. The kingdom's culture blended Persian dynastic traditions, Anatolian peoples (Chalybes, Tibareni, and others), and Greek civic life, and its kings presented themselves as heirs of both the Achaemenids and Alexander the Great.

Mithridates VI and the wars with Rome

The kingdom reached its height under Mithridates VI Eupator (r. 120–63 BC), who annexed Colchis and the Bosporan Kingdom, bringing the Black Sea coasts under his control. His expansion into Roman Asia Minor triggered the three Mithridatic Wars (89–63 BC). In 88 BC he ordered the massacre of tens of thousands of Romans and Italians in Asia (the "Asiatic Vespers"). Though he won early successes against Sulla, he was ultimately crushed by Lucullus and Pompey; Pontus west of the Halys was made a Roman province (Bithynia et Pontus), and Mithridates fled to the Crimea, where he died in 63 BC.

Later history

A reduced client kingdom continued in eastern Pontus under rulers including Pharnaces II, Polemon I, and Queen Pythodorida, who governed into the reign of Tiberius. In AD 62–64 the emperor Nero deposed the last king, Polemon II, and annexed Pontus to the Roman Empire, organizing it within the province of Galatia and later Cappadocia.
