Premonstratensians

The Premonstratensians, formally the Order of Canons Regular of Prémontré (Latin: Candidus et Canonicus Ordo Praemonstratensis), are a religious order of the Roman Catholic Church founded by Saint Norbert of Xanten at Prémontré, near Laon in northern France, in 1120. Members, known for their white habits, are also called Norbertines or White Canons.

History

Norbert, a former courtier turned itinerant preacher, gathered a community at Prémontré in 1120; the order received papal approval from Honorius II in 1126. The canons follow the Rule of Saint Augustine, combining a communal, contemplative life with active pastoral ministry — preaching, teaching and parish work — a balance that distinguished them from purely monastic orders. By the end of the Middle Ages the order had hundreds of abbeys across Europe, including Strahov Abbey in Prague and many houses in England that were dissolved under Henry VIII.

Present day

Today Premonstratensian abbeys and priories exist in Europe, the Americas, Africa, India and Australia, engaged in parish ministry, education and retreats. The order also includes canonesses (Norbertine sisters) and lay tertiaries.
