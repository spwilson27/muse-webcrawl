Alpha compositing

In computer graphics, alpha compositing or alpha blending is the process of combining one image with a background to create the appearance of partial transparency. Each pixel carries, besides colour, an alpha value indicating its opacity or coverage, stored in an alpha channel alongside the red, green and blue channels.

The "over" operator

The foundational mathematics were set out by Thomas Porter and Tom Duff in 1984, building on work by Alvy Ray Smith. The most common operation, "over", places a foreground element over a background: the resulting colour is a weighted average governed by the foreground's alpha, Co = Caαa + Cb(1 − αa). Porter and Duff defined a family of compositing operators (over, in, out, atop, xor) using premultiplied alpha, in which colour values are pre-scaled by alpha to simplify the arithmetic.

Uses

Alpha compositing underlies image editors, window systems, video effects, web graphics formats such as PNG, and the rendering pipelines of games and user interfaces, allowing elements to be layered in a painter's-algorithm fashion.
