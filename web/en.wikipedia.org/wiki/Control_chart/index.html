Control charts are graphical tools used in statistical process control to determine whether a manufacturing or business process is in a state of statistical control. They were invented by Walter A. Shewhart at Bell Laboratories in 1924.

Overview

A control chart plots a quality characteristic over time. Points vary due to common causes (inherent noise) and special causes (assignable faults). Only special-cause variation should prompt intervention; reacting to common-cause noise makes a process worse.

Control limits

The upper and lower control limits are typically set at three standard deviations (3σ) from the process mean, distinct from specification limits set by customers.

Types

  • Variables charts: X̄-R, X̄-s, I-MR charts for measured data.
  • Attributes charts: p, np, c, u charts for counts and proportions.

Rules for signals

The Western Electric rules flag patterns such as a point beyond 3σ, two of three points beyond 2σ, or runs of points on one side of the centre line.
