Droseraceae

Droseraceae is a family of carnivorous flowering plants. It contains three extant genera: Drosera (the sundews), Dionaea (the Venus flytrap) and Aldrovanda (the waterwheel plant). The family belongs to the order Caryophyllales in the APG classification and is characterised by trap leaves adapted to capture and digest insects and other small prey, supplementing the mineral-poor, wet soils in which most of its members grow.

Genera and species

  • Drosera — the sundews, by far the largest genus with about 200 species, distributed on all continents except Antarctica. Sundews trap prey with sticky glandular tentacles that curl around the victim.
  • Dionaea — a single species, Dionaea muscipula, the Venus flytrap, native only to wetlands of the Carolinas in the United States. Its hinged leaves snap shut when trigger hairs are touched.
  • Aldrovanda — a single species, Aldrovanda vesiculosa, the waterwheel plant, a free-floating aquatic plant of the Old World whose small underwater traps work on the same snap mechanism as the flytrap's.

Evolution

Molecular evidence places the snap traps of Dionaea and Aldrovanda as an evolutionary development of the flypaper traps of sundew-like ancestors. Several fossil genera, including the pollen genus Droserapites and leaf fossils such as Droserapollis, have been attributed to the family, suggesting an origin in the Cretaceous where once classified as relatives within the Nepenthales; modern phylogenetics instead firmly nest the Droseraceae among the non-core Caryophyllales, alongside families such as Nepenthaceae and Drosophyllaceae.
