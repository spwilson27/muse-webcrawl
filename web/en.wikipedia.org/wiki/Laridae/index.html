Laridae is a family of seabirds in the order Charadriiformes that includes the gulls, terns, noddies, skimmers, and kittiwakes. It includes around 100 species arranged into 22 genera. They are an adaptable group of mostly aerial birds found worldwide.

Description

Larids are generally medium to large birds, typically grey or white, often with black markings on the head or wings. The bills are long and heavy in the gulls, and slimmer and more pointed in the terns, reflecting different feeding styles: gulls are generalist opportunists that scavenge as well as hunt, while terns feed chiefly by plunge-diving or dipping for fish and invertebrates. Skimmers have a unique bill with an elongated lower mandible used to skim food from the water surface.

Distribution and habitat

The family is distributed across all continents, including Antarctica in the case of some terns. Most species are coastal or pelagic, but several gulls have adapted successfully to inland habitats and to urban environments, exploiting human food waste. Colonies are typically located on islands, cliffs, or undisturbed beaches.

Behaviour

Larids are strong fliers and most are highly social, breeding in dense colonies of monogamous pairs. Both parents usually incubate the eggs and feed the chicks. Many species are long-distance migrants, notably the Arctic tern, which migrates between the Arctic and Antarctic.

Taxonomy

The family was introduced (as Laridia) by the French polymath Constantine Samuel Rafinesque in 1815. Molecular phylogenetic studies have reshaped the internal classification: some authorities merge the terns and skimmers into Laridae, while older arrangements placed them in their own families (Sternidae and Rynchopidae). The noddies, once thought related to terns, are now understood as an early-diverging lineage within the family.
