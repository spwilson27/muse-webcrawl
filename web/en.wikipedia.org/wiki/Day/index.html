Day

A day is the time period of a full rotation of the Earth with respect to the Sun. On average, this is 24 hours (86,400 seconds). As a day passes at a given location, it experiences morning, afternoon, evening, and night. The period of daylight may be followed by the period of darkness (night).

Definitions

The word "day" may refer to the interval of 24 hours, the period of daylight as opposed to night, or the time between one midnight and the next. In the International System of Units (SI), a day is defined as exactly 86,400 seconds, though the actual length of the solar day varies slightly and leap seconds are occasionally added to Coordinated Universal Time.

Sidereal and solar day

A sidereal day — Earth's rotation relative to distant stars — lasts about 23 hours, 56 minutes, and 4 seconds; the solar day is longer because Earth also moves along its orbit. Other planets have days of very different lengths; a day on Venus lasts longer than its year.

Cultural aspects

Calendars divide time into days, and conventions differ on when a day begins: midnight in most modern civil usage, sunset in Jewish and Islamic traditions.
