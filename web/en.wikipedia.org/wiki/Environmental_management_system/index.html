An environmental management system (EMS) is a set of processes and practices that enable an organisation to reduce its environmental impacts and increase its operating efficiency. It provides a systematic framework — typically based on the Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle — for setting an environmental policy, identifying environmental aspects and legal obligations, setting objectives, monitoring performance, and continually improving.

Standards

The most widely used EMS standard is ISO 14001, part of the ISO 14000 family published by the International Organization for Standardization; organisations can be certified against it by independent auditors. In the European Union, the Eco-Management and Audit Scheme (EMAS) builds on ISO 14001 with additional requirements for public reporting and employee involvement.

Elements and benefits

A typical EMS includes an environmental policy endorsed by top management, a register of environmental aspects and compliance obligations, objectives and programmes, operational controls, emergency preparedness, internal audits, management review, and corrective action. Benefits claimed include reduced waste and energy costs, better regulatory compliance, and improved reputation with customers and regulators.
