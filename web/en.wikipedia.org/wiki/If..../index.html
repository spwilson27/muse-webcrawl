if.... is a 1968 British satirical drama film produced and directed by Lindsay Anderson, starring Malcolm McDowell (in his film debut) as Mick Travis, a rebellious pupil at an English public school. Satirising traditional British education and the establishment, the film follows Travis and his friends as their resistance to the school's rigid hierarchy escalates to an armed uprising during Founders' Day.

Style and themes

The film deliberately mixes colour and black-and-white footage — partly for budgetary reasons on location shoots — and blends realism with surreal fantasy sequences, leaving it ambiguous where the boys' daydreams end and rebellion begins. It drew on co-writer David Sherwin's experiences at Tonbridge School and on Jean Vigo's Zéro de conduite.

Reception

The film won the Grand Prix (Palme d'Or) at the 1969 Cannes Film Festival and is regarded as one of the most significant British films of the 20th century. McDowell and Anderson continued Mick Travis's story in O Lucky Man! (1973) and Britannia Hospital (1982).
