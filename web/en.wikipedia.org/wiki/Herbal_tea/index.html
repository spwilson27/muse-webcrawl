Also called: Tisane
Made from: Herbs, spices, flowers, fruit, other than Camellia sinensis
Caffeine: Generally none

Herbal tea

An herbal tea, or tisane, is a beverage made from the infusion or decoction of herbs, spices, flowers, fruit, or other plant material in hot water. Unlike true tea, herbal teas do not contain leaves of the tea plant Camellia sinensis and are usually caffeine-free.

Varieties

Common herbal teas include chamomile, peppermint, ginger, rooibos (from the South African legume Aspalathus linearis), hibiscus, lemon balm, rosehip, and chrysanthemum. They may be brewed from a single ingredient or from blends, and are drunk both for flavour and for traditional medicinal purposes in herbal medicine traditions worldwide.

Terminology and safety

Because "tea" properly denotes Camellia sinensis, food-labelling rules in some jurisdictions prefer the term "tisane" or "infusion". Although generally safe as beverages, some herbs are pharmacologically active and certain tisanes should be avoided in pregnancy or with particular medications.

References: Heiss, M. L. & Heiss, R. J., "The Story of Tea" (2007); European Food Safety Authority assessments of botanical infusions.
