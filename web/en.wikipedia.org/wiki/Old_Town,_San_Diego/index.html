Old Town, San Diego

Old Town is a neighborhood of San Diego, California. It is the oldest settled area in the city and the first site of European settlement in present-day California, where the Presidio of San Diego and Mission San Diego de Alcalá were founded in 1769. Old Town is often called the "birthplace of California."

History

The Spanish established the presidio on a hill above the Kumeyaay village of Kosoy, and a small pueblo grew at the foot of the hill. After Mexican independence, the pueblo of San Diego flourished briefly in the 1820s–1840s around a central plaza; Richard Henry Dana described it in Two Years Before the Mast. Following the American conquest in 1846 and a devastating fire in 1872, the city's center of gravity shifted to "New Town" (present downtown), leaving Old Town in decline until 20th-century restoration efforts.

Old Town San Diego State Historic Park

Established in 1968, the state historic park preserves and reconstructs buildings from the 1820s to 1870s, including the Casa de Estudillo, the Whaley House, a blacksmith shop, and San Diego's first public schoolhouse. The neighborhood today is a major tourist destination known for its Mexican restaurants, museums, and shops.
