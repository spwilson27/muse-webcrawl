The iMac G3, originally released as the iMac, is a series of Macintosh personal computers that Apple Computer sold from 1998 to 2003. It was Apple's first major product launch after Steve Jobs returned to the company, and its translucent "Bondi blue" casing, designed by Jony Ive's team, became an industrial design landmark.

Design and features

The all-in-one machine integrated a 15-inch CRT display and dropped legacy ports and the floppy disk drive in favor of USB and a CD-ROM drive, betting on the Internet — the "i" in iMac. Later models added colors such as the five "fruity" flavors, slot-loading optical drives, FireWire, and faster processors, and patterns like Blue Dalmatian and Flower Power.

Reception

The iMac G3 was a commercial success, selling hundreds of thousands of units in its first months and returning Apple to profitability; it is widely credited with reviving the company.
