Akra–Bazzi method

In computer science, the Akra–Bazzi method, or Akra–Bazzi theorem, is used to analyse the asymptotic behaviour of recurrences that occur in the analysis of divide-and-conquer algorithms where the sub-problems have unequal sizes. It generalises the master theorem.

Formulation

For a recurrence of the form T(x) = g(x) + Σ aᵢ T(bᵢ x + hᵢ(x)) for x large enough, with constants aᵢ > 0 and 0 < bᵢ < 1, where g is a driving function and the hᵢ are bounded perturbations, let p be the unique real number such that Σ aᵢ bᵢᵖ = 1. Then T(x) = Θ( xᵖ ( 1 + ∫₁ˣ g(u)/uᵖ⁺¹ du ) ).

History and applications

The method was published by Mohamad Akra and Louay Bazzi in 1998. It applies to recurrences such as those arising from algorithms that split a problem into parts of differing proportions, where the classic master theorem's regularity conditions do not directly hold, and it tolerates floors, ceilings and other small perturbations in sub-problem sizes.
