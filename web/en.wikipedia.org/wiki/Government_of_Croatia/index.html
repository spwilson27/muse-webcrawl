Government of Croatia

The Government of Croatia (Croatian: Vlada Republike Hrvatske) is the main executive branch of government in Croatia. It is headed by the Prime Minister, consists of the deputy prime ministers and ministers, and is seated at Banski dvori in St. Mark's Square, Zagreb, opposite the Croatian Parliament (Sabor).

Under the Constitution of Croatia, the government proposes laws and the state budget, directs foreign and internal policy, and oversees the state administration. After parliamentary elections, the President of Croatia entrusts a mandate to form a government to the person who enjoys the confidence of a majority of deputies; the cabinet must then win a vote of confidence in the Sabor.

Croatia is a parliamentary republic: executive power is shared between the government, accountable to parliament, and the directly elected president, who has a more limited, largely representative role since the constitutional changes of 2000–2001. The government in its current form emerged with Croatia's independence in 1991.
