Ronson is a brand of lighters and lighter accessories best known for the pioneering automatic pocket lighters of the early 20th century. The business began in 1886 as The Art Metal Works in Newark, New Jersey, founded by Louis V. Aronson, a gifted metalworker and inventor. The company produced decorative metal goods before Aronson developed a superior lighter mechanism.

The automatic lighter

In 1926–1928 Ronson introduced the Banjo and then the famous De-Light lighter with the slogan "Press, it's lit; release, it's out" — the first fully automatic lighter, igniting with a single thumb press. The Ronson Touch-Tip table lighters and stylish Art Deco models became status objects, and the brand name became nearly generic for lighters. Ronson also popularized butane lighters such as the Varaflame line in the 1950s and sold Ronsonol lighter fuel, wicks, and flints.

Later history

The company diversified into small appliances and other products and passed through several owners amid declining smoking rates and competition from disposable lighters — though for many years its chief rival was Bradford, Pennsylvania's Zippo. In 2010 Zippo Manufacturing Company acquired the Ronson brand and related assets for the United States, Canada, and Mexico, while Ronson-branded products continue under other ownership in some markets.
