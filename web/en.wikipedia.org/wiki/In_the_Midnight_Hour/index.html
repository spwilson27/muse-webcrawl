"In the Midnight Hour" is a song originally performed by American soul singer Wilson Pickett in 1965 and released on his album of the same name. Written by Pickett and guitarist Steve Cropper at the Lorraine Motel in Memphis, it became one of the defining records of Southern soul.

Recording

Pickett recorded the song at Stax Studios in Memphis with the house band Booker T. & the M.G.'s and the Memphis Horns. Its signature groove is a delayed backbeat — drummer Al Jackson Jr. emphasising the beat slightly late — an innovation suggested by producer Jerry Wexler, who demonstrated the jerk dance step it resembled. Cropper's opening guitar figure and the horn punches made the record instantly recognisable.

Legacy

The song reached number one on the US R&B chart and has been covered by numerous artists including The Commitments, whose version featured in the 1991 film of the same name. It is ranked among Rolling Stone's greatest songs of all time, and Pickett's recording has been inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame.
