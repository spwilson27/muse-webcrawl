Entertainment Software Rating Board

The Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB) is the self-regulatory organisation that assigns age and content ratings to video games and apps in the United States, Canada and Mexico. Founded in 1994 by the Entertainment Software Association (then the Interactive Digital Software Association), the ESRB was created in response to congressional scrutiny of violent games such as Mortal Kombat and Night Trap, amid threats of federal regulation of the industry.

History

Senate hearings held in 1993–94, led prominently by Senator Joseph Lieberman, pressed the young games industry to regulate itself. At the time, Sega and Nintendo had rival, incompatible rating systems. In September 1994 the ESRB began rating games, headed by its founding president Patricia Vance, and major retailers agreed to stock only rated products and to enforce age restrictions on M- and AO-rated titles — an arrangement that forestalled legislation.

Ratings appear on game packaging together with content descriptors (such as "Blood and Gore", "Strong Language" or "Simulated Gambling") that explain what triggered the rating, and for digital products with notices about in-game purchases and user interaction. Ratings are assigned by trained raters reviewing publisher-submitted forms and video footage; controversial episodes — most famously the hidden "Hot Coffee" scenes that caused Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas to be re-rated AO in 2005 — have repeatedly tested the system. For apps and digital-only games, the ESRB participates in the International Age Rating Coalition (IARC), which generates local ratings for multiple regions from a single questionnaire. The ESRB also enforces industry advertising and marketing guidelines and offers privacy certification services.
