Impairment

Impairment is a general term for a loss, reduction or abnormality of psychological, physiological or anatomical structure or function. In the World Health Organization's classifications — the ICIDH (1980) and its successor, the International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF, 2001) — impairment is distinguished from disability (activity limitation) and handicap or participation restriction: impairment describes a problem in body function or structure, while disability is an umbrella term covering impairments, activity limitations and participation restrictions in interaction with the environment.

Categories

Impairments may be sensory (visual impairment, hearing impairment), physical or mobility-related, cognitive or intellectual, or related to mental health. They may be congenital or acquired through disease, injury or ageing, and may be temporary or permanent, visible or invisible.

Accounting usage

In accounting and finance, impairment has a distinct technical meaning: an asset is impaired when its carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount, requiring the entity to recognise an impairment loss reducing the asset's value on the balance sheet. Rules for impairment testing of assets, goodwill and financial instruments are set out in standards such as IAS 36 and IAS 39/IFRS 9, and in US GAAP. A related usage is "impairment" of capital or of a loan.

Other usages

The word is also used for functional impairment caused by alcohol or drugs ("impaired driving"), for impaired glucose tolerance in medicine, and in law for diminished capacity. The social model of disability argues that much of the disadvantage associated with impairment arises from social barriers rather than from the impairment itself.
