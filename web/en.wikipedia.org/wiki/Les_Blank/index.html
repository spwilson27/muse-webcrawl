Les Blank

Les Blank (27 November 1935 – 7 April 2013) was an American documentary filmmaker best known for warmly observed portraits of American regional cultures — especially music, food, and festivals — and for his long-running documentation of blues musician Lightnin' Hopkins and filmmaker Werner Herzog.

Career

Born in Tampa, Florida, Blank studied English literature and film at Tulane and the University of Southern California before founding his production company, Flower Films, in El Cerrito, California. His The Blues Accordin' to Lightnin' Hopkins (1968) launched a series of films on American roots music, including Cajun, zydeco, Tex-Mex, polka, and gospel subjects. Chulas Fronteras (1976), about norteño music on the Texas–Mexico border, was added to the National Film Registry.

Herzog films and GarliFest

Blank filmed Werner Herzog eating his shoe after losing a bet (Werner Herzog Eats His Shoe, 1980) and documented the fraught Amazon production of Fitzcarraldo in Burden of Dreams (1982), which won a BAFTA for best documentary. Other well-known works include Garlic Is as Good as Ten Mothers (1980) — screenings of which he sometimes accompanied with roasting garlic in the theatre — and In Heaven There Is No Beer? (1984), about polka culture. Blank died in Berkeley in 2013.
