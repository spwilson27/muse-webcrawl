Mobulidae

The Mobulidae (manta rays and devil rays) are a family of rays consisting of two genera, Manta and Mobula. Species in this family are among the largest rays: the giant oceanic manta ray (Mobula birostris, formerly Manta birostris) can reach a disc width of 7 metres and weigh over 2,000 kilograms. Genetic studies published in 2017 showed that mantas are nested within Mobula, and the genus Manta has since commonly been subsumed into Mobula, although some classifications retain it.

Biology

Mobulids are pelagic filter feeders that strain zooplankton, krill and small schooling fish from the water. The paired cephalic fins, which give devil rays their "horned" appearance, unroll to funnel water into the mouth, where modified gill rakers sieve out food. Unlike most other rays they lack a stinging spine. They are strong, continuous swimmers with enlarged pectoral fins that flap like wings, and some devil rays are famous for leaping clear of the water, sometimes in large aggregations. Mobulids are ovoviviparous, typically giving birth to a single pup after a long gestation — one reason populations recover slowly from fishing pressure.

Species and distribution

The family contains around ten recognised species, including the reef manta ray (Mobula alfredi), the giant oceanic manta (M. birostris), the spinetail devil ray (M. mobular), the Chilean and pygmy devil rays, found in tropical and subtropical oceans worldwide. Many mantas carry unique spot patterns on their undersides, allowing researchers to identify individuals photographically.

Conservation

Mobulids are targeted and taken as bycatch in fisheries, in part for their gill plates, which are traded for use in traditional medicine despite a lack of evidence of efficacy. Slow reproduction makes them highly vulnerable: the giant oceanic manta ray is listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List, and the family is included in Appendix II of CITES and in the appendices of the Convention on Migratory Species. Ecotourism based on manta encounters is, in many places, worth far more than the fisheries.
