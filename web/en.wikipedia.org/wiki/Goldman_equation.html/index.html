Goldman equation

The Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz voltage equation, more commonly known as the Goldman equation, is used in cell membrane physiology to determine the reversal (resting membrane) potential across a cell's membrane, taking into account all of the ions that are permeant through it.

Unlike the Nernst equation for a single ion, the Goldman equation weights each ion—principally potassium, sodium and chloride—by its membrane permeability and concentrations inside and outside the cell. It explains why the resting potential of most cells lies close to the potassium equilibrium potential but is shifted by sodium permeability.

The equation was derived using the constant-field assumption by David E. Goldman (1943) and developed by Alan Hodgkin and Bernard Katz (1949).
