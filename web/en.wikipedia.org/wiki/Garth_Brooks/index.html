Garth Brooks

Troyal Garth Brooks (born February 7, 1962) is an American country singer and songwriter. His integration of pop and rock elements into country music earned him immense popularity, particularly for spectacular live performances.

Early life

Brooks was born in Tulsa, Oklahoma, the youngest child of Troyal Raymond Brooks Jr., a draftsman, and Colleen McElroy Carroll, a 1950s-era country singer. He attended Oklahoma State University on a track scholarship, competing in the javelin, and graduated in 1984 with a degree in advertising.

Career

After moving to Nashville, Brooks released his self-titled debut album in 1989, reaching No. 2 on the country chart. No Fences (1990) included "Friends in Low Places" and "The Thunder Rolls" and became his best-selling album. Ropin' the Wind (1991) was the first country album to debut at No. 1 on both the Billboard 200 and country charts. Later albums include The Chase, In Pieces, Sevens and Scarecrow.

Brooks retired from recording and touring in 2001 to raise his daughters, returning with residencies in Las Vegas and a comeback tour in 2014–2017, one of the highest-grossing concert tours ever. In 1999 he recorded pop material as the fictional Chris Gaines.

Artistry and legacy

Brooks is one of the world's best-selling music artists, having sold more than 170 million records. He is the best-selling solo albums artist in the United States, ahead of Elvis Presley, and has received two Grammy Awards, 17 American Music Awards and the Library of Congress Gershwin Prize. He was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 2012. See also Country music and Trisha Yearwood, his wife since 2005.

Personal life

Brooks was married to Sandy Mahl (1986–2001), with whom he has three daughters, and married fellow country star Trisha Yearwood in 2005.
