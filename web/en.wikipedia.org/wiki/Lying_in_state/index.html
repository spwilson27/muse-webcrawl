Lying in state

A coffin lying in state, attended by a guard of honour.

Lying in state is the tradition in which the body of a deceased official, such as a head of state, is placed in a state building, either outside or inside a coffin, to allow the public to pay their respects. It traditionally takes place in a major government building of a country, state, or city. While it often follows a state funeral period of official mourning, lying in state is a distinct, preliminary ceremony in which the public may file past the coffin before the funeral itself.

Terminology

In the United States Capitol, when the deceased is a member of the military, the term is lying in state; for civilians in the Capitol rotunda the honour may be termed lying in honour, as was the case for civil rights leader Rosa Parks in 2005 and Capitol Police officer Brian Sicknick in 2021. In Canada the corresponding practice is called lying in state when held in the Centre Block of Parliament Hill. In the United Kingdom, lying in state customarily takes place in Westminster Hall, adjoining the Houses of Parliament.

Customs

The coffin typically rests on a raised platform called a catafalque, draped with a flag and sometimes with the insignia of orders or a crown. A guard of honour — often drawn from the armed forces, and in Britain including Gentlemen at Arms and Yeomen of the Guard — stands vigil at the corners of the catafalque, heads bowed. Members of the public queue, sometimes for many hours, to file past in silence. Lying in state may last from a few hours to several days.

Notable examples

In the United Kingdom, sovereigns and certain other figures have lain in state in Westminster Hall, including Edward VII, George V, George VI, Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother (2002), and Elizabeth II (2022), whose lying in state drew a queue stretching for miles along the Thames. In the United States, presidents including Abraham Lincoln, John F. Kennedy, Ronald Reagan, George H. W. Bush, and Jimmy Carter lay in state in the Capitol rotunda, alongside figures such as Senator John McCain and Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg — the first woman and first Jewish person to receive the honour there. In South Africa, Nelson Mandela lay in state at the Union Buildings in Pretoria in 2013.
