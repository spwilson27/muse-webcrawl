Migrant smuggling
Also called: People smuggling
Legal basis: UN Smuggling of Migrants Protocol (2000)

Migrant smuggling (also called people smuggling) is the facilitation, for financial or other material benefit, of the illegal entry of a person into a state of which that person is not a national or permanent resident. It is defined in the Protocol against the Smuggling of Migrants by Land, Sea and Air (2000), supplementing the UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime.

Distinction from trafficking

Smuggling is distinguished from human trafficking by consent and by its transnational character: a smuggling arrangement typically ends on arrival, whereas trafficking involves coercion and continuing exploitation. In practice the two can overlap when smuggled migrants are subjected to abuse, extortion or dangerous conditions en route.

Scale and risks

Smuggling networks operate on major migration routes — across the Mediterranean, the Mexico–United States border and elsewhere — charging fees that can run to thousands of dollars. Journeys in unseaworthy boats, sealed vehicles or desert crossings cause thousands of deaths each year, making smuggling a major focus of border enforcement, search-and-rescue operations and international policy debate.
