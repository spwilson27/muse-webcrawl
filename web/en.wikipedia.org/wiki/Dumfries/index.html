Dumfries

Dumfries (Scots: Dumfries; Scottish Gaelic: Dùn Phrìs) is a market town and former royal burgh in Dumfries and Galloway, Scotland, near the mouth of the River Nith on the Solway Firth. Sometimes known as the "Queen of the South," it is the traditional county town of Dumfriesshire and the administrative centre of the region, with a population of around 33,000.

History

Dumfries became a royal burgh in 1186. In 1306, Robert the Bruce murdered his rival John Comyn before the high altar of Greyfriars Kirk in the town, an act that sparked Bruce's bid for the Scottish crown. The poet Robert Burns spent his final years in Dumfries (1791–1796), writing many famous works including "Tam o' Shanter" (written while based nearby) and "Auld Lang Syne" versions; he is buried in St Michael's churchyard, and the town holds the Robert Burns Centre and Burns House museum.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Devorgilla Bridge (one of Scotland's oldest surviving bridges), the Midsteeple, Dumfries Museum and Camera Obscura, and the Globe Inn, Burns's favourite hostelry. The town's football club, Queen of the South F.C., takes its name from the town's nickname.
