Partition of Bengal (1905)

Date16 October 1905
ViceroyLord Curzon
New provinceEastern Bengal and Assam
Annulled1911

The first Partition of Bengal (1905) was a territorial reorganisation of the Bengal Presidency implemented by Viceroy Lord Curzon, dividing the province into "Bengal" (comprising western Bengal, Bihar and Orissa) and "Eastern Bengal and Assam", with its capital at Dhaka. The British justified the partition on administrative grounds, as Bengal was too large for a single administration, but Indians widely saw it as a "divide and rule" attempt to split Bengali unity along religious lines, creating a Muslim-majority eastern province.

Swadeshi movement

The partition provoked a massive nationalist reaction. The Swadeshi movement boycotted British goods, promoted indigenous industry and national education, and made the song "Vande Mataram" its anthem; Rabindranath Tagore composed "Amar Shonar Bangla" and promoted rakhi-tying between Hindu and Muslim Bengalis as a symbol of unity. The date of partition, 16 October 1905, was observed as a day of mourning.

Annulment

In the face of sustained agitation, King George V announced the annulment of the partition at the Delhi Durbar of 1911; Bengal was reunited, while Bihar and Orissa became a separate province. At the same time the capital of India was transferred from Calcutta to Delhi. A second, very different partition of Bengal accompanied the end of British rule in 1947.
