Local ring

In abstract algebra, a local ring is a ring that has a unique maximal ideal. Equivalently, the set of non-units of the ring forms an ideal. Local rings are central objects in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry: they represent the algebraic analogue of studying a geometric object in a small neighbourhood of a single point.

Examples

All fields are local rings, their unique maximal ideal being the zero ideal. A richer example is the ring of p-adic integers, whose unique maximal ideal is generated by the prime p. The ring of formal power series over a field is local, with maximal ideal consisting of series with zero constant term. For any commutative ring and any prime ideal, the localisation of the ring at that prime is a local ring whose maximal ideal corresponds to the chosen prime.

Properties

The quotient of a local ring by its maximal ideal is a field, called the residue field. Nakayama's lemma, a cornerstone result about finitely generated modules over local rings, has wide applications, for example in showing that minimal generating sets correspond to bases of the module modulo the maximal ideal. Noetherian local rings are studied through their completions and invariants such as depth, dimension, and multiplicity; a local ring in which every system of parameters generates an m-primary ideal of the expected size is called a Cohen–Macaulay ring.

Terminology

The term "local" comes from algebraic geometry: localising a ring of functions at a point retains exactly the information about behaviour near that point. Some authors additionally require local rings to be Noetherian; usage varies by text.
