E. W. Hornung

Ernest William Hornung
Born: 7 June 1866, Middlesbrough, England
Died: 22 March 1921, Saint-Jean-de-Luz, France
Known for: A. J. Raffles stories

Ernest William Hornung (7 June 1866 – 22 March 1921) was an English author and poet, best known for creating A. J. Raffles, the gentleman thief and amateur cricketer who appeared in short stories from 1898 onwards.

Life

Hornung was educated at Uppingham School and spent two years in Australia for his health. He married Constance Doyle, sister of Arthur Conan Doyle, in 1893. His only son was killed at Ypres in 1915, after which Hornung worked with the YMCA in France during the First World War.

Works

The Raffles stories, narrated by Bunny Manders, began with The Amateur Cracksman (1899) and continued in The Black Mask (1901), A Thief in the Night (1905) and the novel Mr Justice Raffles (1909). Hornung also wrote novels including The Bride from the Bush (1890), Peccavi (1900) and war poetry such as "Last Post" (1918).
