Men of Harlech

Harlech Castle, subject of the song

"Men of Harlech" (Welsh: Rhyfelgyrch Gwŷr Harlech, "March of the Men of Harlech") is a song and military march which is traditionally said to describe events during the seven-year siege of Harlech Castle in Wales between 1461 and 1468. It is Wales's best-known patriotic song and a regimental march of Welsh regiments of the British Army.

History

The tune was first published in 1794 in Edward Jones's Musical and Poetical Relicks of the Welsh Bards. The siege it commemorates was the longest in British history: Harlech Castle, held by the Lancastrian Dafydd ap Ieuan, resisted the Yorkist forces of Edward IV through much of the Wars of the Roses before finally surrendering in 1468. Welsh lyrics by the poet John Oxenford and others exist in several versions; the best-known English lyrics begin "Men of Harlech, march to glory."

Uses

The song is the regimental march of the Royal Welsh and is sung by supporters of Welsh sports teams, especially the national rugby union team. It featured prominently in the 1964 film Zulu, where the Welsh soldiers of B Company, 2nd Battalion, 24th Regiment of Foot sing it in answer to Zulu war chants before the Battle of Rorke's Drift. The tune is also used by schools and institutions in Wales and is played at military ceremonies and tattoos.
