Marnie (novel)

AuthorWinston Graham
Published1961
GenrePsychological thriller
SettingEngland

Marnie is a 1961 novel by English author Winston Graham, best known for the Poldark series. A psychological thriller told in the first person, it follows a young woman who steals from her employers and repeatedly changes her identity, and the publisher who, after catching her, blackmails her into marriage.

Plot

Marnie Elmer works in clerical jobs under false names, embezzles money from each employer in revenge for a childhood trauma she has repressed, and moves on before she is caught. When she steals from Rutland & Co., director Mark Rutland recognises her and, fascinated, pressures her into marriage rather than reporting her. Marnie's terror of men and of the colour red eventually leads, through a hunting accident and psychoanalysis, to her recovering the memory of the childhood event — the death of a sailor at her home — that shaped her pathology.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted by Alfred Hitchcock as the film Marnie (1964), starring Tippi Hedren and Sean Connery, which softened several elements of the book. Nico Muhly's opera Marnie, based on the novel, premiered at English National Opera in 2017 and was staged at the Metropolitan Opera in 2018.
