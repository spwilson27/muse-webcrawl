Syracuse is a city in and the county seat of Onondaga County, New York, United States. Located in Central New York on the southern shore of Lake Ontario's basin, it is the region's economic and educational hub and was historically famous for salt production, which earned it the nickname "The Salt City." The city sits on the traditional lands of the Onondaga Nation, keepers of the council fire of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy.

History

European settlement grew around salt springs discovered near Onondaga Lake in the late 18th century; the salt industry boomed after the Erie Canal reached the area in the 1820s, and Syracuse was incorporated as a city in 1848, merging the villages of Syracuse and Salina. Manufacturing — including typewriters, automobiles, and air conditioners from firms such as Carrier Corporation — powered the city through the mid-20th century before deindustrialisation shrank its population.

Education and culture

Syracuse University, founded in 1870, is a major employer and a centre of college sports; its teams are the Orange. The city hosts the Great New York State Fair, the oldest state fair in the United States. Landmarks include the Art Deco Niagara Mohawk Building and Clinton Square.

Climate

Syracuse is among the snowiest large cities in the United States, receiving heavy lake-effect snow from Lake Ontario, averaging around 120 inches (300 cm) per season.
