The Pistol Star is one of the most luminous and massive stars known in the Milky Way. It is a luminous blue variable in the Quintuplet Cluster near the Galactic Center, in the constellation Sagittarius. It takes its name from the shape of the surrounding Pistol Nebula, which it illuminates and partly ejected in past eruptions.

Discovery

The star is completely hidden from optical view by interstellar dust and was discovered in infrared images taken with the Hubble Space Telescope in the early 1990s by a team led by Don Figer. The surrounding nebula consists of gas expelled by the star in giant eruptions about 4,000–6,000 years ago, analogous to the eruptions of Eta Carinae.

Properties

The Pistol Star radiates several million times the power of the Sun and may have been born with a mass of around 200 solar masses, close to or above the classical Eddington limit. It expels material in a prodigious stellar wind — in six seconds it emits as much energy as the Sun does in a year. Like other luminous blue variables it is expected to end its short life (a few million years) in a supernova or hypernova.

Significance

Along with other very massive stars in the Quintuplet and Arches clusters, the Pistol Star has informed debates about the upper mass limit for star formation, suggesting that stars of at least 150 solar masses can form.
