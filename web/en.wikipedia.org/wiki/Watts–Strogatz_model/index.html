Watts–Strogatz model

FieldNetwork science
AuthorsDuncan J. Watts, Steven Strogatz
Published1998 (Nature)

The Watts–Strogatz model is a random graph generation model that produces graphs with small-world properties, including short average path lengths and high clustering. Proposed by Duncan J. Watts and Steven Strogatz in their 1998 article in Nature, it was the first model to explain how networks can simultaneously be highly clustered like regular lattices and have short paths like random graphs.

Algorithm

The model starts with a ring lattice of N nodes, each connected to its K nearest neighbours. Each edge is then rewired with probability β: one endpoint is replaced by a randomly chosen node. For β = 0 the graph remains a regular lattice; for β = 1 it becomes essentially random. At small intermediate values of β, the average path length collapses while the clustering coefficient stays high — the small-world regime.

Applications

The model helped launch modern network science and has been applied to neural networks, power grids, social networks and the spread of disease. Its main limitation is that it does not produce the heavy-tailed degree distributions seen in many real networks, a gap addressed by later preferential-attachment models.
