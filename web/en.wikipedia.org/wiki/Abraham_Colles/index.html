Abraham Colles (23 July 1773 – 16 November 1843) was an Irish surgeon and anatomist, professor of surgery at the Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland. He is best remembered for describing the fracture of the distal radius that bears his name — Colles' fracture — in 1814.

Life and career

Colles was born in Kilkenny and studied medicine in Dublin and Edinburgh, receiving his diploma from the Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland in 1795. He was elected professor of anatomy and surgery at the college in 1804 and served as its president on two occasions, refusing a baronetcy three times.

Contributions

In his 1814 paper "On the Fracture of the Carpal Extremity of the Radius", Colles described the common wrist fracture decades before radiography confirmed his clinical description. He also formulated "Colles' law" regarding congenital syphilis transmission, described the membranous layer of superficial fascia known as Colles' fascia, and identified the ligament of the groin later called Colles' ligament.
