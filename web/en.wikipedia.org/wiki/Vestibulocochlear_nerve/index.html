Vestibulocochlear nerve

Vestibulocochlear nerve
Cranial nerve: CN VIII
Components: Vestibular nerve; cochlear nerve
Innervates: Inner ear (balance and hearing)

The vestibulocochlear nerve, also known as the eighth cranial nerve (CN VIII), transmits sound and equilibrium (balance) information from the inner ear to the brain. It consists of two divisions: the cochlear nerve, which carries auditory signals, and the vestibular nerve, which carries information about head position and motion.

Anatomy

The nerve emerges from the pontomedullary junction of the brainstem and enters the internal acoustic meatus alongside the facial nerve. Its sensory neuron cell bodies lie in the spiral ganglion (for hearing) and the vestibular (Scarpa's) ganglion (for balance). Fibres project to the cochlear and vestibular nuclear complexes in the medulla and pons.

Function

Hair cells in the cochlea convert sound vibrations into neural signals carried by the cochlear division, while hair cells in the semicircular canals, utricle and saccule detect angular and linear acceleration for the vestibular division.

Clinical significance

Damage can cause sensorineural hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo and imbalance. A benign tumour of the nerve's Schwann cells, vestibular schwannoma (acoustic neuroma), typically presents with gradual unilateral hearing loss. See also cranial nerves.
