Musa acuminata is a species of banana native to Southeast Asia and one of the two principal wild ancestors of the modern edible banana, the other being Musa balbisiana. Most cultivated bananas are hybrids of these two species.

Description

The plant is a large herbaceous perennial with a pseudostem formed from tightly packed leaf sheaths. Its wild fruits contain numerous hard seeds and little pulp, unlike the seedless parthenocarpic fruits of cultivated varieties.

Cultivation

Triploid hybrids of M. acuminata (genome group AAA) include the Cavendish subgroup, which dominates the world export banana trade. The species is designated with the letter "A" in the Simmonds and Shepherd genome classification system.
