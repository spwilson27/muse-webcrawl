Philippines campaign (1944–1945)

Date20 October 1944 – 2 September 1945
LocationPhilippine Islands
ResultAllied victory
BelligerentsUnited States, Philippine Commonwealth, Australia vs. Empire of Japan

The Philippines campaign (1944–1945), known as the Liberation of the Philippines, was the American, Mexican, Australian and Filipino campaign to defeat and expel the Imperial Japanese forces occupying the Philippines during World War II. It began with the amphibious landings on Leyte on 20 October 1944 and continued until the end of the war in September 1945.

The campaign is regarded as the fulfilment of General Douglas MacArthur's famous pledge — "I shall return" — made after he was ordered to evacuate to Australia in March 1942. The Japanese had occupied the archipelago since 1942, following the fall of Bataan and Corregidor.

Background

By mid-1944, Allied forces advancing through the Southwest Pacific and Central Pacific had isolated the Japanese position in the Philippines. Debate within the American high command over whether to invade Formosa (Operation Causeway) or the Philippines was settled in favour of the Philippines, largely because of MacArthur's advocacy and the political importance of liberating the Commonwealth.

The Leyte landings

On 20 October 1944, the U.S. Sixth Army under Lieutenant General Walter Krueger landed on Leyte in the central Philippines, supported by the Seventh Fleet. MacArthur waded ashore at Palo, Leyte, and delivered his "I have returned" speech. The landings provoked the Imperial Japanese Navy into a desperate counterstroke, resulting in the Battle of Leyte Gulf (23–26 October 1944), widely considered the largest naval battle of the war, in which the Japanese fleet was effectively destroyed as an organised force. The battle also saw the first organised kamikaze attacks.

Luzon and Manila

After securing Leyte and landing on Mindoro, the Allies invaded Lingayen Gulf on Luzon on 9 January 1945. Filipino guerrillas and American forces liberated prisoners at Cabanatuan in a celebrated raid on 30 January. The Battle of Manila (February–March 1945) was one of the most destructive urban battles of the Pacific war; much of the city was destroyed and roughly 100,000 Filipino civilians died, many in massacres committed by retreating Japanese naval troops.

Southern Philippines

The Eighth Army under Robert Eichelberger conducted a series of landings across the Visayas and Mindanao through the spring and summer of 1945. General Tomoyuki Yamashita's forces held out in the mountains of northern Luzon until the formal Japanese surrender on 2 September 1945; Yamashita himself surrendered the following day.

Aftermath

The liberation restored the Philippine Commonwealth government under President Sergio Osmeña. The Philippines became independent the following year, on 4 July 1946. The campaign's cost was heavy: tens of thousands of Allied soldiers, perhaps a million Filipino civilians, and the great majority of the roughly 400,000 Japanese troops committed to the defence died during the campaign.
