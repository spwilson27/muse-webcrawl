Eastern Arc Mountains

The Eastern Arc Mountains are a chain of ancient mountain ranges in Kenya and Tanzania, renowned for their exceptional levels of biological endemism. The chain runs in an arc from the Taita Hills in Kenya south-westward through the Pare, Usambara, Uluguru, Udzungwa and Mahenge mountains of Tanzania. Formed from Precambrian crystalline rock perhaps 100 million years ago or more, they are far older than the volcanic peaks of Kilimanjaro and Mount Kenya.

Biodiversity

Moist winds from the Indian Ocean have kept the Eastern Arc forests wet for millions of years, even through the dry ice ages, allowing an extraordinary flora and fauna to evolve in isolation. The mountains are home to thousands of species found nowhere else, including African violets (Saintpaulia), many endemic frogs and chameleons, and birds such as the Udzungwa forest partridge, discovered by scientists only in 1991. The region is recognised as one of the world's most important biodiversity hotspots.

Conservation

The forests provide vital water catchments for Tanzanian cities including Dar es Salaam, but they have been heavily reduced by logging, agriculture and fire; only a fraction of the original forest cover remains. Parts of the range, including the Udzungwa Mountains National Park and the Amani Nature Reserve in the East Usambaras, are now protected, and the Tanzanian portion has been proposed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
