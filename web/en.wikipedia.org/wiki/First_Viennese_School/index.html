The First Viennese School is a name given to an era and group of composers of the Classical period who worked in Vienna in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. The principal figures are Joseph Haydn, Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart and Ludwig van Beethoven, with Franz Schubert frequently included as a later member. Their music defines the Classical style in its mature form.


The four principal figures: Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven and Schubert

Characteristics

The composers of the First Viennese School developed and perfected the main forms of Classical music: the symphony, the string quartet, the piano sonata and the concerto. Their style is characterised by clarity of structure, balance of phrases, the codification of sonata form, and a sophisticated use of thematic development and harmonic tension and release.

Relationships

Unusually among schools of composers, the members knew one another personally. Haydn and Mozart were close friends who played string quartets together, and each acknowledged the other's mastery. Beethoven studied briefly (and unhappily) with Haydn after arriving in Vienna in 1792 and also took lessons from Albrechtsberger. Schubert, a generation younger, revered Beethoven and served as a torchbearer at his funeral.

Name

The term "First Viennese School" was coined retrospectively, by analogy with the Second Viennese School of Arnold Schoenberg, Alban Berg and Anton Webern in the early 20th century. Unlike the Second School, the First was not a self-conscious movement with a single teacher, and some musicologists question whether "school" is the right word at all; the term remains in wide use for convenience.
