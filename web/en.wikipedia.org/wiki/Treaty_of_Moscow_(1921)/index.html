Treaty of Moscow (1921)

The Treaty of Moscow, officially the Treaty of Brotherhood, was signed on 16 March 1921 between the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic and the Grand National Assembly of Turkey, the government of Mustafa Kemal's national movement. It settled the two countries' northeastern frontier and cemented cooperation between Soviet Russia and Kemalist Turkey.

Terms

Turkey retained Kars and Ardahan, while Batum was returned to Soviet Georgia, with guarantees for its port and autonomy for its Muslim population. Both parties repudiated the Treaty of Sèvres and other agreements imposed on either side, agreed not to recognize international acts forced on the other, and confirmed Nakhichevan as an autonomous territory under Azerbaijan's protection. The Straits question was reserved for a future Black Sea conference.

Significance

The treaty was Soviet Russia's first agreement with a non-communist government on terms of equality, and it secured Turkey's eastern frontier while the Turkish War of Independence continued in the west. Its territorial arrangements were completed by the Treaty of Kars (October 1921) with the Soviet Transcaucasian republics, and the frontier drawn in 1921 remains essentially intact today.
