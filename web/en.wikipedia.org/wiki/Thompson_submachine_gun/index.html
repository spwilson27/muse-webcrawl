The Thompson submachine gun, nicknamed the "Tommy gun", "Chicago typewriter" and "trench broom", is a blowback-operated, selective-fire submachine gun invented by United States Army officer John T. Thompson in 1918. Chambered for the .45 ACP cartridge, it became famous during Prohibition as the weapon of choice of both gangsters and lawmen.

History

Developed by the Auto-Ordnance Corporation, the Thompson was marketed from 1921. Its association with organized crime in the 1920s and 1930s — including the St. Valentine's Day Massacre — made it notorious, though police and federal agents also used it widely.

Military service

The U.S. military adopted the simplified M1928 and later the wartime M1 and M1A1 Thompson, which replaced the drum magazine with stick magazines and removed refinements such as the compensator and finned barrel to speed production. More than 1.5 million military Thompsons were built during the Second World War, and the gun saw service from World War II through Korea and into the early Vietnam era.
