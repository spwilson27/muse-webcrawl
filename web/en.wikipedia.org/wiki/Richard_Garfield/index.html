Richard Channing Garfield (born June 26, 1963) is an American mathematician, inventor and game designer best known for creating Magic: The Gathering (1993), the first modern collectible card game. His design helped establish the trading card game genre and transformed the hobby games industry.

Early life

Garfield was born in Philadelphia and spent much of his childhood travelling with his family because of his father's work in architecture. A keen player of games including Dungeons & Dragons, he designed his first game at thirteen. He studied computer mathematics at the University of Pennsylvania and went on to earn a PhD in combinatorial mathematics there in 1993, planning an academic career.

Magic: The Gathering

While pitching a board game, RoboRally, to Wizards of the Coast, Garfield was asked by company president Peter Adkison for a portable game that could be played at conventions. Garfield developed Magic: The Gathering, combining his earlier designs with the collectible model of baseball cards: players buy randomised booster packs and build their own decks from a large pool of cards. Released in August 1993, the game was an immediate success and launched the trading card game industry.

Other work

Garfield designed or co-designed many other games, including the card games Netrunner, Jyhad (later Vampire: The Eternal Struggle), The Great Dalmuti, BattleTech (CCG), King of Tokyo, the board game RoboRally (eventually published in 1994), and the digital card game Artifact (2018) with Valve. He has also taught game design and worked on video games, and holds a patent (with Skaff Elias and others) on aspects of the trading card game concept. He later co-founded the studio Three Donkeys with Skaff Elias.
