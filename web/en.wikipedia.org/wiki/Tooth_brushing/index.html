Tooth brushing is the act of scrubbing teeth with a toothbrush equipped with toothpaste. It is the principal method of self-administered oral hygiene and, together with interdental cleaning such as flossing, is recommended to prevent tooth decay and gum disease by removing dental plaque and food debris.

Recommendations

Dental associations generally recommend brushing twice a day for two minutes with a fluoride toothpaste. A soft-bristled brush, manual or electric, is advised, held at about a 45-degree angle to the gum line and moved in short, gentle strokes (the Bass technique). Brushes should be replaced roughly every three to four months, or sooner if the bristles splay. Spitting rather than rinsing after brushing leaves fluoride in contact with the teeth for longer.

History

Tooth-cleaning implements long predate the modern toothbrush: chew sticks (such as the miswak), twigs, cloth and sponges were used in many cultures. Bristle brushes originated in China by the late 15th century, and the first mass-produced toothbrush is credited to William Addis in England around 1780, using a bone handle and animal bristles. Nylon-bristled brushes appeared in the 1930s, and electric toothbrushes became widely available from the 1960s.

Technique and considerations

  • Brush all surfaces: outer, inner and chewing surfaces, plus gentle brushing of the tongue.
  • Excessive force or hard bristles can cause gum recession and enamel abrasion.
  • Brushing immediately after acidic food or drink may soften enamel; waiting around 30 minutes is often advised.
  • Young children should be supervised and use age-appropriate amounts of fluoride toothpaste.
