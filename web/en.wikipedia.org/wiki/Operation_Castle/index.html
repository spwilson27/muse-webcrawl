Operation Castle was a United States series of high-yield nuclear tests conducted in 1954 at Bikini and Enewetak Atolls in the Marshall Islands. Its first shot, Castle Bravo, detonated on 1 March 1954, remains the largest nuclear device ever tested by the United States.

Castle Bravo

Bravo used a dry lithium-deuteride fusion fuel. An unanticipated contribution to the fusion reaction from lithium-7 made the yield about 15 megatons — two and a half times the predicted 6 megatons. The fallout contaminated a far larger area than expected, exposing Marshall Islanders on Rongelap and Utirik, US servicemen, and the crew of the Japanese fishing vessel Daigo Fukuryū Maru (Lucky Dragon No. 5), one of whose crew died. The incident became an international scandal and intensified worldwide pressure for a test ban.

Other shots

The series continued with Romeo, Koon, Union, Yankee, and Nectar, testing deliverable thermonuclear weapon designs. Castle's results fed directly into the deployment of the first operational hydrogen bombs, while the Bravo fallout controversy remains a landmark in the history of nuclear testing.
