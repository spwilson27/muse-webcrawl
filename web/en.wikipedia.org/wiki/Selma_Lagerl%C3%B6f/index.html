Selma Ottilia Lovisa Lagerlöf (20 November 1858 – 16 March 1940) was a Swedish writer, the first woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, which she received in 1909, and the first woman to be granted membership of the Swedish Academy, in 1914. She is one of Sweden's most widely read and translated authors.

Life and career

Lagerlöf was born at Mårbacka, an estate in Värmland, and trained as a teacher, working at a school in Landskrona from 1885. Her first novel, Gösta Berling's Saga (1891), a romantic, folkloric tale of Värmland, brought her recognition, and she was able to devote herself to writing full-time after receiving support from the Swedish royal family and the Swedish Academy. With the proceeds of her success she bought back Mårbacka, which had been sold after her father's death.

Works

Her best-known book internationally is The Wonderful Adventures of Nils (1906–07), commissioned as a geography reader for Swedish schoolchildren, in which a boy, Nils Holgersson, travels the length of Sweden on the back of a goose. Other major works include Jerusalem (1901–02), The Ring of the Löwenskölds trilogy, and the autobiographical Mårbacka books. During the Second World War she helped German intellectuals, including the poet Nelly Sachs, escape Nazi persecution.

Legacy

Lagerlöf's portrait appeared on the Swedish 20-kronor banknote. Mårbacka is preserved as a memorial estate, and the Selma Lagerlöf Prize is awarded annually to a Swedish-language author.
