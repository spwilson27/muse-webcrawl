The Third Wave is a 1980 book by Alvin Toffler. It is the second in a trilogy that begins with Future Shock (1970) and concludes with Powershift (1990). The book describes three great "waves" of human civilization, each largely superseding the cultures that came before it.

The three waves

The First Wave is the agricultural revolution, which spread over thousands of years and replaced hunter-gatherer cultures with settled farming societies. The Second Wave is the industrial revolution, built on mass production, mass education, mass media, standardization, and centralization. The Third Wave, which Toffler saw emerging from the mid-1950s onward, is a post-industrial, information-based civilization characterized by decentralization, de-massification of media and production, renewable energy, the "electronic cottage", and the rise of the "prosumer" who both produces and consumes.

Predictions and reception

Toffler anticipated developments such as home-based knowledge work, niche media, and flexible, customized manufacturing. The book was an international bestseller and was influential among politicians and business thinkers; it sold widely in China, where it was read by reform-era leaders. Commentators have credited it with foreseeing much of the information age, while critics noted its techno-optimism and broad-brush treatment of history.
