Takagi existence theorem

The existence theorem links abelian extensions to generalised ideal class groups

The Takagi existence theorem is a fundamental result of class field theory, proven by Teiji Takagi in work published around 1920. It states that for a number field K, the finite abelian extensions of K correspond exactly to generalised ideal class groups of K.

Statement

More precisely, given a modulus 𝔪 of K (a formal product of a finite ideal and certain real embeddings) and a subgroup of the group of fractional ideals coprime to 𝔪 lying between the ray modulo 𝔪 and the whole ideal group, there exists a unique finite abelian extension L/K whose associated norm group is that subgroup; its Galois group is canonically isomorphic to the corresponding generalised ideal class group. Conversely, every finite abelian extension arises this way, with its conductor dividing a suitable modulus.

Significance

Together with the companion results on norm groups and the Artin reciprocity law, the existence theorem completes the classification of abelian extensions of number fields, generalising Kronecker–Weber theory for ℚ. Takagi's work, presented in his 1920 paper on the theory of relative abelian fields, laid the foundations on which Emil Artin and others built modern class field theory. The correspondence is summarised by the fundamental inequality and the statement that the map from extensions to class groups is an inclusion-reversing bijection.
