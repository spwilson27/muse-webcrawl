Stelvio Pass

Elevation2,757 m (9,045 ft)
LocationSouth Tyrol / Lombardy, Italy
RangeEastern Alps

The Stelvio Pass (Italian: Passo dello Stelvio; German: Stilfser Joch) is a mountain pass in northern Italy bordering Switzerland at an elevation of 2,757 m (9,045 ft) above sea level. It is the highest paved mountain pass in the Eastern Alps, and the second highest in the Alps, after the Col de l'Iseran (2,764 m).

The road

The pass lies in the Ortler Alps between Stilfs in South Tyrol and Bormio in the province of Sondrio, Lombardy. The road, built in the 1820s by the Austrian Empire, is famous for its 48 hairpin turns on the northern ramp climbing from Prad, dramatically ascending the mountainside in a series of switchbacks frequently photographed and celebrated as one of the world's great driving roads. The road is generally open only from late May to November due to snow.

Sport

The Stelvio is a legendary climb of the Giro d'Italia, where the highest point of the race is marked by the Cima Coppi prize, and it is also used by motorcyclists, cyclists and skiers at the summer glacier ski area near the summit.
