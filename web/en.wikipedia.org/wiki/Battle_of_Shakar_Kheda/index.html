Battle of Shakar Kheda

Date11 October 1724
LocationShakar Kheda (now Fathkheda), Berar, Deccan, India
ResultVictory for Nizam-ul-Mulk; foundation of Hyderabad State
BelligerentsForces of Nizam-ul-Mulk vs. Mughal imperial forces under Mubariz Khan
CommandersNizam-ul-Mulk (Asaf Jah I) vs. Mubariz Khan (killed)

The Battle of Shakar Kheda was fought on 11 October 1724 at Shakar Kheda in Berar, in the Indian Deccan, between Nizam-ul-Mulk (later Asaf Jah I) and the Mughal imperial governor of the Deccan, Mubariz Khan. The Nizam's victory established his de facto independence from the Mughal Empire and founded the princely state of Hyderabad, which endured until 1948.

Background

Nizam-ul-Mulk, the empire's most capable noble and briefly its wazir, had withdrawn from the intrigues of the Delhi court to build a power base in the Deccan, where he had twice served as viceroy. The emperor Muhammad Shah, alarmed at his ambition, appointed Mubariz Khan governor of Hyderabad and ordered him to destroy the Nizam, promising him the viceroyalty of the Deccan.

The battle

The armies met at Shakar Kheda. Mubariz Khan had the larger force and attacked with confidence, but the Nizam's superior artillery and disciplined deployment prevailed; Maratha auxiliaries in the Nizam's service also played a part. In a hard-fought action Mubariz Khan was killed, his army routed, and the imperial cause in the Deccan collapsed. The village was renamed Fathkheda, "village of victory", in commemoration.

Aftermath

Emperor Muhammad Shah, unable to reverse the verdict of battle, confirmed the Nizam as viceroy of the Deccan with the title Asaf Jah. From his capital at Hyderabad, Asaf Jah I built a hereditary, effectively autonomous state while maintaining the forms of Mughal suzerainty — a landmark in the 18th-century transformation of the Mughal Empire into a constellation of successor states.
