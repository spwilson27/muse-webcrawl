Sinuiju Special Administrative Region

The Sinuiju Special Administrative Region was a special administrative region (SAR) established by North Korea in 2002 in and around the city of Sinuiju, on the Yalu River border with China. Modelled in part on the Hong Kong and Macau special administrative regions, it was intended to function as a market-oriented enclave with its own legal and economic system, largely separated from the rest of the North Korean state.

Establishment

The Basic Law of the SAR was adopted in September 2002. The region covered about 132 square kilometres, encompassing parts of Sinuiju and neighbouring counties. Yang Bin, a Chinese-Dutch businessman, was appointed its first governor, promising to develop the zone into a centre of trade, finance, manufacturing and tourism with its own courts, currency arrangements and visa-free access for many foreigners.

Collapse of the project

Weeks after his appointment, Yang Bin was arrested by Chinese authorities on charges including tax evasion and other financial crimes, and later sentenced to a lengthy prison term. Without its governor and Chinese support, the SAR project effectively collapsed before it had begun. Although the SAR's legal framework was never formally abolished for some years, it exercised no real autonomy, and Sinuiju continued to develop as a conventional North Korean border-trading city. Later proposals for special economic zones on Hwanggumpyong and Wihwa islands revived some of the same ambitions in a different form.
