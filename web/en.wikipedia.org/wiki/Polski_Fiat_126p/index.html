Polski Fiat 126p

The Polski Fiat 126p is a motor vehicle manufactured in Poland between 1973 and 2000. It was a licence-built version of the Italian Fiat 126, produced by Fabryka Samochodów Małolitrażowych (FSM) in Bielsko-Biała and Tychy. Affectionately nicknamed Maluch ("The Little One", "Toddler"), it became an iconic car of the Polish People's Republic and one of the most recognisable Polish automobiles.

History

In 1971, the Polish government purchased a licence from Fiat to produce the Fiat 126 in Poland. Production began on 18 July 1973 at the Bielsko-Biała plant. The car was intended to motorise Polish society, offering an affordable vehicle for ordinary families. A total of about 3.3 million units were produced in Poland, alongside roughly 1.35 million of the original in Italy.

Design

The 126p retained the rear-engined, rear-wheel-drive layout of the Fiat 500 and 126, with an air-cooled two-cylinder engine initially of 594 cc producing 23 horsepower, later enlarged to 652 cc. Its small size — under 3.1 metres long — and simple mechanics made it cheap to buy and maintain, though it was cramped and underpowered by Western standards.

Cultural significance

For many Poles, the Maluch was their first family car and a symbol of everyday life under communism, associated with long waiting lists, improvised repairs, and holiday trips with the whole family squeezed inside. It remains a beloved classic, celebrated at enthusiast rallies.
