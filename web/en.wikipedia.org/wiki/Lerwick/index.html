Lerwick

Lerwick
Shetland Islands, Scotland
Population: approx. 7,000
Principal town and port of Shetland

Lerwick (Old Norse: Leirvik, "muddy bay") is the main town and port of the Shetland archipelago, Scotland. It is the most northerly town of any size in the United Kingdom, lying over 200 miles (320 km) north of Aberdeen on the east coast of the Shetland mainland, and it is Shetland's only burgh.

History

Lerwick grew in the 17th century as a trading and fishing station serving Dutch herring fleets in Bressay Sound. It became the capital of Shetland in place of Scalloway in 1708. Fort Charlotte, guarding the harbour, was rebuilt during the Napoleonic era. The town expanded greatly with the North Sea oil boom of the 1970s, when the nearby Sullom Voe terminal transformed Shetland's economy.

Culture

On the last Tuesday of January, Lerwick hosts Up Helly Aa, Europe's largest fire festival, in which a squad of "guizers" led by the Guizer Jarl parades in Viking dress and burns a replica longship. The Shetland Museum and Archives stands on the waterfront at Hay's Dock. Lerwick is the ferry port for services to Aberdeen and Kirkwall and the home of Shetland's BBC Radio Shetland.

Climate

Lerwick has a subpolar oceanic climate with cool summers, mild but stormy winters, and some of the most changeable weather in Britain; its latitude near 60°N gives near-continuous daylight ("simmer dim") around midsummer.
