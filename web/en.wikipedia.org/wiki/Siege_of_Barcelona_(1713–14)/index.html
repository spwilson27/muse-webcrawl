Siege of Barcelona (1713–14)

Date: 25 July 1713 – 11 September 1714
Location: Barcelona, Catalonia
Result: Bourbon victory; fall of Barcelona

The siege of Barcelona (1713–14) was the final major episode of the War of the Spanish Succession in Spain. Bourbon forces of Philip V, commanded first by the Duke of Pópoli and later by the Duke of Berwick, blockaded and besieged Barcelona after Catalonia refused to accept the Peace of Utrecht and continued to support the Habsburg claimant, Archduke Charles.

Background

The Treaty of Utrecht (1713) recognised Philip V as king of Spain, and Imperial troops evacuated Catalonia. The Catalan governing bodies, however, voted in July 1713 to continue the war in defence of their constitutions. Barcelona, defended by Antonio de Villarroel and the city's militia, the Coronela, prepared for a siege.

The siege

The Bourbon blockade tightened through late 1713 and 1714. Berwick arrived in July 1714 with French reinforcements and artillery and opened formal siege trenches. After heavy bombardment, the final assault was launched in the early hours of 11 September 1714; after fierce street fighting, Villarroel, wounded, ordered the surrender.

Aftermath

The fall of Barcelona ended organised Catalan resistance. The Nueva Planta decrees abolished Catalonia's institutions and laws. The date, 11 September, is commemorated today as the National Day of Catalonia (the Diada).
