Scent hound

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Scent hounds (or scenthounds) are a type of hound that primarily hunts by scent rather than sight. These breeds are hunting dogs and are generally regarded as having some of the most sensitive noses of all dogs. Scent hounds specialize in following the scent of their quarry: they typically have long, drooping ears that help sweep scent up toward the nose, loose wrinkled skin, and long muzzles with large nasal cavities.

Characteristics

Scent hounds are built for endurance rather than speed, following trails for hours or days over rough terrain. They characteristically "give tongue"—baying or howling—while on a trail, allowing hunters to follow by sound. Their sense of smell may be tens of millions of times more sensitive than a human's; the bloodhound's trailing ability is so reliable that its evidence has been admitted in courts of law.

Breeds

Well-known scent hound breeds include:

  • Bloodhound
  • Beagle
  • Basset Hound
  • English Foxhound and American Foxhound
  • Coonhounds (Black and Tan, Bluetick, Redbone, Treeing Walker)
  • Dachshund
  • Harrier, Otterhound, and the continental chiens courants of France

Uses

Beyond hunting game such as fox, raccoon, deer, and wild boar, scent hounds are used by police and search-and-rescue services for tracking missing persons, fugitives, and disaster victims, and by conservationists to locate rare wildlife.
