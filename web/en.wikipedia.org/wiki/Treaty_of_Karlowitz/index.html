Treaty of Karlowitz

Treaty of Karlowitz
Signed: 26 January 1699, Karlowitz (Sremski Karlovci, Serbia)
Parties: Ottoman Empire vs Holy League (Austria, Poland, Venice, Russia)
Context: Great Turkish War

The Treaty of Karlowitz, concluded on 26 January 1699 at Karlowitz in modern Serbia, ended the Great Turkish War between the Ottoman Empire and the Holy League, marking the first major Ottoman territorial retreat in Europe and a turning point in Ottoman–European relations.

Terms

On the principle of uti possidetis, Austria received most of Hungary, Transylvania, Croatia and Slavonia; Poland recovered Podolia; Venice gained the Morea (Peloponnese) and parts of Dalmatia; Russia obtained Azov (confirmed separately in 1700). The Ottomans retained the Banat of Temesvár and Belgrade for the moment.

Significance

Negotiated with English and Dutch mediation, Karlowitz is often seen as the beginning of the Ottoman Empire's long decline in Europe and of Habsburg ascendancy in Central Europe. See Great Turkish War and Ottoman Empire.
