City hall

A city hall is the chief administrative building of a city or town, housing the offices of the mayor, the city council, and the municipal departments that run local services. In some regions the equivalent building is called a town hall, municipal building, or (in Britain, historically) a guildhall.

Functions

City halls typically contain the council chamber, offices for the city clerk and other officials, and public spaces for meetings, ceremonies and civil marriages. The building also serves as a symbol of civic identity, and many city halls — such as those of New York, Philadelphia, London and Paris (the Hôtel de Ville) — are architectural landmarks.

Metonymy

"City hall" is also used as a metonym for municipal government itself, as in the saying "you can't fight city hall", meaning that an individual cannot easily defeat a bureaucracy. Journalists likewise use "City Hall" to refer to the mayor's administration and local politics in general.
