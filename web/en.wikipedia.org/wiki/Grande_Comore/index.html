Grande Comore

Grande Comore (Comorian: Ngazidja) is the largest island of the Comoros archipelago in the Indian Ocean, off the coast of East Africa. It is an autonomous island within the Union of the Comoros and contains the national capital, Moroni.

Geography

The island is volcanic in origin and dominated by Mount Karthala, one of the world's largest active shield volcanoes, whose eruptions — most recently in 2005–2007 — have periodically forced evacuations. Unlike the other Comorian islands, Grande Comore has few permanent watercourses, and much of the coast is composed of dark volcanic rock.

History

Settled by African, Arab, and Malagasy peoples, the island was ruled by competing sultanates before coming under French control in the 19th century. The Comoros declared independence from France in 1975; Grande Comore has since been the seat of the federal government, though the islands' politics have been marked by coups and separatist tensions.

Economy

The economy is largely agricultural, producing vanilla, cloves, and ylang-ylang for export, alongside fishing and subsistence farming. Remittances from the Comorian diaspora are an important source of income.
