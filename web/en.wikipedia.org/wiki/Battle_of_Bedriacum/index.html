The Battles of Bedriacum were two engagements fought in northern Italy in AD 69 during the civil war known as the Year of the Four Emperors, near the village of Bedriacum (probably modern Calvatone) between Cremona and Mantua. Sometimes called the Battle of Cremona, the two battles decided the imperial succession twice in a single year.

First Battle of Bedriacum (14 April 69)

After the suicide of Nero and the brief reign of Galba, the throne was contested by Otho in Rome and Vitellius, commander of the legions of Germany. Vitellius's generals Caecina Alienus and Fabius Valens invaded Italy over the Alps. Otho marched north, but at Bedriacum his army — directed on the day by his brother Titianus and the proconsul Licinius Proculus, against the cautious advice of the experienced Suetonius Paulinus — was defeated after hard fighting around the Via Postumia. On hearing the news, Otho took his own life, and Vitellius became emperor.

Second Battle of Bedriacum (24 October 69)

Within months the eastern legions proclaimed their general Vespasian emperor. His advance commander, Marcus Antonius Primus, invaded Italy with the Danubian legions and met the Vitellian army near Bedriacum on 24 October. The battle began in the afternoon and raged through the night by moonlight; at dawn the Flavian legion III Gallica — long stationed in Syria — saluted the rising sun, and the Vitellians, believing Flavian reinforcements had arrived, broke and fled to Cremona, which was stormed and sacked. The victory opened the road to Rome; Vitellius was killed in December and Vespasian founded the Flavian dynasty.

Sources

The principal ancient account is the Histories of Tacitus, supplemented by Suetonius and Cassius Dio. Tacitus's vivid narrative of the night battle and the sack of Cremona is among the most celebrated passages of Latin historiography.
