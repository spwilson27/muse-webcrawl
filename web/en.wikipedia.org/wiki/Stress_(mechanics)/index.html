Stress (mechanics)

In continuum mechanics, stress is a physical quantity that describes forces present during deformation. It is defined as the internal force per unit area acting within a deformable body, and it is the intensity of the internal forces that neighbouring particles of a continuous material exert on each other. Stress is measured in pascals (Pa) in the SI system.

Components of the Cauchy stress tensor on a material element.

Definition

Stress at a point is described by the Cauchy stress tensor, a symmetric second-order tensor with nine components: three normal stresses (acting perpendicular to a face of an infinitesimal element) and six shear stresses (acting parallel to the faces). Principal stresses are the normal stresses on planes where shear stress vanishes. In simple cases, uniaxial stress is simply force divided by cross-sectional area, σ = F/A.

Types

  • Tensile stress — stretches the material (e.g. a cable in tension).
  • Compressive stress — squeezes the material (e.g. a column under load).
  • Shear stress — acts parallel to the surface (e.g. in a bolted joint).

Stress is related to strain (deformation) through constitutive laws such as Hooke's law for elastic materials. Engineering analysis of stress determines whether structures will withstand applied loads without yielding or fracturing; see strength of materials.
