Kalimpong is a town and municipality in the Indian state of West Bengal, situated on a ridge overlooking the Teesta River at an elevation of about 1,247 metres (4,091 ft). It is the headquarters of Kalimpong district and is known for its educational institutions, many dating to the British colonial period, its flower nurseries, and its trade history with Tibet.

History

Kalimpong was part of Sikkim and later Bhutan before being annexed by the British after the Anglo-Bhutan War of 1864–1865. Under British rule it developed as a hill station and a trading post for wool, furs and musk from Tibet, routed through the Jelep La pass. Scottish missionaries founded schools here, most famously Dr. Graham's Homes (1900), and the town's nurseries made it a centre of the Himalayan orchid and gladiolus trade.

Geography and culture

The town's population is largely of Nepali (Gorkha) origin alongside Lepcha, Bhutia, Tibetan and Bengali communities; Nepali is the predominant language. Since 2017 Kalimpong has been a separate district carved out of Darjeeling. Landmarks include the Deolo Hill viewpoint, Durpin Monastery (Zang Dhok Palri Phodang), MacFarlane Memorial Church, and the home where Rabindranath Tagore stayed, associated with his poem Janmadine.

Economy

Tourism, education, agriculture and floriculture underpin the local economy; Kalimpong's nurseries export orchids, cacti and other plants across India and abroad.
