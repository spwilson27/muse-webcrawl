Edward Stanley, 14th Earl of Derby

Edward George Geoffrey Smith-Stanley, 14th Earl of Derby (29 March 1799 – 23 October 1869), was a British statesman who served three times as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom (1852, 1858–1859 and 1866–1868). He was one of the longest-serving leaders of the Conservative Party and remains the party's longest-serving leader in its history.

Political career

Stanley entered Parliament in 1822 and served in Whig governments, notably as Chief Secretary for Ireland and as Secretary of State for War and the Colonies, where he piloted the Slavery Abolition Act 1833 through Parliament. He broke with the Whigs over Irish church policy and eventually led the protectionist Conservatives after the party split over the repeal of the Corn Laws in 1846. His second government passed the India Act 1858, transferring rule in India from the East India Company to the Crown. His third government, with Benjamin Disraeli as Chancellor of the Exchequer, passed the Reform Act 1867, which greatly extended the franchise in England and Wales.

Other interests

Derby was a distinguished classicist — his blank-verse translation of the Iliad was published in 1864 — and a celebrated racehorse owner whose family gave its name to the Derby at Epsom. He was Chancellor of the University of Oxford.
