Maxima (software)

Maxima is a free and open-source computer algebra system (CAS) for symbolic and numerical computation, including differentiation, integration, Taylor series, Laplace transforms, ordinary differential equations, systems of linear equations, polynomials, matrices and tensors. It is written in Common Lisp and released under the GNU General Public License.

History

Maxima descends from Macsyma, the legendary symbolic mathematics system developed at MIT from the late 1960s with funding from ARPA and other agencies, and later commercialised. In 1982, William Schelter began maintaining a version of Macsyma based on the Kyoto Common Lisp; with permission from MIT and the US Department of Energy (which had rights through its funding), he released this version under the GPL in 1998 under the name Maxima, maintaining it until his death in 2001. Development continues by a community of volunteers.

Features

Maxima manipulates symbolic expressions such as:

(%i1) diff(sin(x)*exp(x), x);
(%o1)          cos(x) %e^x + sin(x) %e^x

It supports arbitrary-precision arithmetic, rational functions, simplification, limits, summation, equation solving, plotting (via gnuplot), and programming in its own language as well as Lisp. Interfaces include the command line, the wxMaxima graphical interface, and embedding in systems such as SageMath and Jupyter (via a kernel). Maxima runs on Windows, macOS, Linux and Android.
