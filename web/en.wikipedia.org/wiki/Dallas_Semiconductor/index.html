Dallas Semiconductor was an American semiconductor company based in Dallas, Texas. In 2001 it was acquired by Maxim Integrated Products for about $2.5 billion; its products continued under the Maxim brand, and the Dallas name persisted in product lines for many years.

Products

The company was known for real-time clock chips (including the DS1287 found in IBM PC AT compatibles), battery-backed nonvolatile SRAM modules, temperature sensors, and the 1-Wire bus family, including the iButton, a stainless-steel-cased device used for identification, access control and data logging. It also made T/E carrier, battery management and microcontroller products.

History

Dallas Semiconductor was founded in 1984 by Vince McNamara and built a catalogue of thousands of mixed-signal and specialty ICs. Following the Maxim acquisition, its lines were integrated into Maxim, which itself was acquired by Analog Devices in 2021.
