Thomas Arne

Thomas Arne
Born: 12 March 1710, London
Died: 5 March 1778, London
Known for: "Rule, Britannia!", masques and operas

Thomas Augustine Arne (12 March 1710 – 5 March 1778) was an English composer and violinist, best known for his patriotic song "Rule, Britannia!". He was the leading British theatre composer of the 18th century, working at Drury Lane and Covent Garden, and composed many operas, masques, incidental music, and songs.

Life

Arne was born in Covent Garden, London, the son of an upholsterer. Educated at Eton, he studied music largely in secret, taking lessons from Michael Festing. His first opera, Rosamond (1733), launched his career; his sister Susannah Cibber became a celebrated singer and actress in his productions.

Works

His masque Alfred (1740), containing "Rule, Britannia!", was first performed at Cliveden for Frederick, Prince of Wales. The masque Comus (1738) and operas such as Artaxerxes (1762) — a successful English opera seria with the famous air "The Soldier Tir'd" — and the pastoral Love in a Village (1762) were long popular. He also wrote oratorios, concertos, symphonies, and many songs for the London pleasure gardens, including settings of Shakespeare.

Legacy

Arne received a doctorate in music from Oxford in 1759 and was buried in St Paul's, Covent Garden. Though overshadowed by Handel during his lifetime, his melodic gift and role in establishing English opera have been increasingly recognised.
