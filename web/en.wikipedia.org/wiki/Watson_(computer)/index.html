Watson (computer)

Watson is an IBM question-answering computer system capable of answering questions posed in natural language. Named after IBM's first CEO, Thomas J. Watson, it was developed as part of IBM's DeepQA project with the goal of competing on the quiz show Jeopardy!

Jeopardy! challenge

In February 2011, Watson competed against former Jeopardy! champions Brad Rutter and Ken Jennings in a televised exhibition match and won the $1 million first prize. The system answered clues using a combination of natural-language parsing, hypothesis generation across a large corpus, and statistical ranking of candidate answers with confidence scoring.

Technology

Watson ran on a cluster of IBM Power 750 servers, drawing on more than 200 million pages of content, including dictionaries, encyclopedias, news, and literature. Its DeepQA pipeline combined hundreds of algorithms for parsing, information retrieval, and evidence scoring.

Later use

After the Jeopardy! match, IBM adapted Watson technology for commercial applications, notably in healthcare (supporting oncology decision-making), finance, and customer service, under the Watson brand of cognitive computing products.
