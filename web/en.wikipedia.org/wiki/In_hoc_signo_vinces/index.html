In hoc signo vinces

In hoc signo vinces (Latin: "In this sign thou shalt conquer") is a Latin phrase traditionally attributed to the Roman emperor Constantine the Great, who, before the Battle of the Milvian Bridge in AD 312, is said to have seen a vision of a cross of light in the sky bearing the Greek words Ἐν Τούτῳ Νίκα (En toutōi nika, "In this, conquer").

Origin

The account is preserved by the church historians Lactantius and Eusebius of Caesarea, whose versions differ: Lactantius describes a dream in which Constantine was instructed to mark his soldiers' shields with the Chi-Rho, while Eusebius describes a midday vision of a cross above the sun seen by the whole army. Constantine went on to win the battle against Maxentius and subsequently favoured Christianity within the Roman Empire.

Later use

The phrase and the associated symbol were adopted as a motto by numerous institutions, military orders and noble families. It appears, in various forms, on the arms and regalia of organisations including the Knights Templar tradition, Greek and Serbian heraldry, and several universities and regiments. The Chi-Rho and the labarum standard became enduring emblems of Constantine's conversion.
