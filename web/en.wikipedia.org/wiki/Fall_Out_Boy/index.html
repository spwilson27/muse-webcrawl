Fall Out Boy is an American rock band formed in Wilmette, Illinois, a suburb of Chicago, in 2001. The band consists of lead vocalist and guitarist Patrick Stump, bassist and lyricist Pete Wentz, lead guitarist Joe Trohman, and drummer Andy Hurley. The band's name was suggested by an audience member at an early show, referencing the sidekick of Radioactive Man in The Simpsons.

History

After the independent album Take This to Your Grave (2003), the band achieved mainstream success with From Under the Cork Tree (2005), whose singles "Sugar, We're Goin Down" and "Dance, Dance" became hits. Infinity on High (2007) debuted at number one on the Billboard 200, driven by "This Ain't a Scene, It's an Arms Race". Following Folie à Deux (2008), the band went on hiatus in 2009.

Fall Out Boy returned in 2013 with Save Rock and Roll, followed by American Beauty/American Psycho (2015), which produced the hit "Centuries", Mania (2018), and So Much (for) Stardust (2023). The band is considered a defining act of 2000s pop-punk and emo pop.
