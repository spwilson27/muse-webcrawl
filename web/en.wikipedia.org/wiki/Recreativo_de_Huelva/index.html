Recreativo de Huelva

Real Club Recreativo de Huelva, S.A.D. is a Spanish football club based in Huelva, in the autonomous community of Andalusia. Founded on 23 December 1889, it is the oldest football club in Spain, and is nicknamed El Decano ("The Dean") of Spanish football.

History

The club was founded by British workers of the Rio Tinto mines, including Dr. William Alexander Mackay, and was originally called the Huelva Recreation Club. Football had been introduced to the region by British miners, and Recreativo's foundation predates every other Spanish club. For most of its history the club played in the second and third tiers of Spanish football.

Recreativo first reached La Liga in 1978, staying a single season. It returned to the top flight in 2002 and enjoyed its most famous moment in 2003, reaching the final of the Copa del Rey, where it lost to Mallorca. A further promotion came in 2006 under coach Marcelino García Toral, and the club achieved its best-ever league finish, eighth, in 2006–07. Financial difficulties later saw the club drop into the regionalized lower divisions, and in 2016 it narrowly avoided dissolution, being rescued by its supporters and new ownership.

Stadium

The club plays at the Estadio Nuevo Colombino, opened in 2001, with a capacity of about 21,670.
