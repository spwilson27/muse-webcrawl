Steamboat Rock State Park

Nearest cityElectric City, Washington
AreaApproximately 3,500 acres (1,400 ha)

Steamboat Rock, a basalt butte in the Grand Coulee, overlooks Banks Lake.

Steamboat Rock State Park is a public recreation area in Grant County, Washington, centred on Steamboat Rock, a prominent basalt butte rising about 800 feet (240 m) above the shore of Banks Lake at the northern end of the Grand Coulee, not far from Grand Coulee Dam.

Geology

The flat-topped rock is a remnant of the Columbia River basalt flows. During the Ice Age, catastrophic Missoula floods carved the Grand Coulee and left the butte standing as an erosional remnant between flood channels; from a distance its profile suggested a steamboat, giving it its name. Trails lead to the summit plateau, which covers more than 600 acres and offers views over the coulee, Banks Lake, and in spring, wildflower displays.

Recreation

The park offers camping (including full-hookup sites and cabins), a boat launch and moorage on Banks Lake, swimming, fishing for walleye and bass, picnicking, and hiking. It is one of the most popular state parks in eastern Washington during the summer season.
