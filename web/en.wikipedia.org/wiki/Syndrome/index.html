Syndrome

In medicine, a syndrome is a set of medical signs and symptoms that are correlated with each other and often associated with a particular disease or disorder. The word derives from the Greek σύνδρομον, meaning "concurrence" or "running together." Some syndromes, such as Down syndrome, have a known genetic cause; others are defined purely by a characteristic cluster of findings.

Usage

A syndrome differs from a disease in that the underlying cause may be unknown or multiple. When a cause is identified, the name often persists for historical reasons — for example, acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) kept its name after the HIV cause was found. Named syndromes are frequently eponymous, honoring the physician who first described the pattern, such as Guillain–Barré syndrome or Turner syndrome.

Genetics

In genetics, a syndrome denotes a recognizable pattern of anomalies thought to be pathogenetically related, as in contiguous gene deletion syndromes. Dysmorphology is the study of such patterns. A set of symptoms occurring together more often than chance is sometimes called a syndrome even in non-medical fields, a metaphorical usage common in psychology and sociology.
