Delhi Police

The Delhi Police is the law enforcement agency for the National Capital Territory of Delhi, India. Unlike most Indian state police forces, it does not report to the Government of Delhi but to the Union Ministry of Home Affairs through the Lieutenant Governor of Delhi. With roughly 90,000 personnel, it is one of the largest metropolitan police forces in the world.

History and structure

Policing in Delhi traces its origins to the kotwal system under the Mughals and early British rule; the modern force took shape after Indian independence, and the commissionerate system — with a Commissioner of Police holding magisterial powers — was introduced in 1978. The force is organized into districts (subdivided into police stations), along with specialized units including the Crime Branch, Economic Offences Wing, Traffic Police, Special Cell (counter-terrorism), and the SWAT-equipped Special Weapons and Tactics units.

Role

The Delhi Police is responsible for law and order, crime investigation, traffic management, and the security of the national capital, which hosts the President, Parliament, central government, and foreign embassies. Its prominence means its handling of major protests, riots (notably in 1984 and 2020), and high-profile criminal cases has frequently been the subject of national debate and scrutiny.
