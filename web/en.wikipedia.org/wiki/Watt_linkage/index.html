Watt linkage

TypeFour-bar linkage
InventorJames Watt
Patented1784

The Watt linkage is a type of mechanical linkage invented by James Watt in which the central moving point of the linkage traces an approximately straight line. It was described in Watt's patent of 1784 for the steam engine, and he considered it one of his most ingenious inventions.

Construction

The linkage consists of two equal-length horizontal bars pivoted at their outer ends, connected by a short vertical bar. The midpoint of the connecting bar moves in an approximate straight line over part of its travel, tracing a curve known as a lemniscate-like figure-eight, the Watt curve. In Watt's engines it guided the piston rod in a straight line while being driven by the arcing end of the beam.

Applications

Watt linkages are used in vehicle suspension systems to constrain an axle to move vertically without sideways displacement, and appear in some independent suspension designs. The geometry inspired later exact straight-line mechanisms such as the Peaucellier–Lipkin linkage.
