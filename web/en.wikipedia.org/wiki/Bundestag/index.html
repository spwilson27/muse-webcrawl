Bundestag

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Bundestag (German Federal Diet) is the lower house of the German federal parliament, the only federal organ directly elected by the German people. (The upper chamber, the Bundesrat, represents the Länder governments.) Elected for four years by a personalised proportional system (mixed-member proportional representation: voters cast a constituency vote and a party-list vote), the Bundestag elects the Chancellor, passes federal legislation jointly with the Bundesrat, approves the budget, and scrutinises the government.

Design lessons from Weimar and Bonn

The Basic Law (1949) designed the Bundestag against Weimar's instability: a 5 per cent threshold excludes splinter parties; the constructive vote of no confidence (the Bundestag may dismiss the Chancellor only by simultaneously electing a successor) prevents purely negative majorities; strong committees amend government bills substantially; and the chamber's electoral arithmetic usually requires coalition government negotiated in formal coalition agreements. Following decades of chamber growth through overhang and levelling seats, the 2023 electoral reform fixed the Bundestag at 630 seats (first applied in 2025) by dropping overhang compensation and subordinating constituency wins to second-vote coverage. The Bundestag sits in Berlin's Reichstag building, its glass dome (Foster, 1999) a deliberate emblem of transparency.
