Cauca River

The Cauca River (Spanish: Río Cauca) is a river in Colombia that lies between the Occidental and Central cordilleras of the Colombian Andes. It is the second most important river in the country after the Magdalena River, into which it flows.

Course

The Cauca rises in the Colombian Massif near the Laguna del Buey in the department of Cauca, at an elevation of about 3,200 metres. It flows northwards through the fertile inter-Andean Cauca Valley, passing the city of Cali, and continues through the departments of Valle del Cauca, Risaralda, Caldas and Antioquia before descending to the Caribbean lowlands and joining the Magdalena River near Mompós in the department of Bolívar.

Economy and environment

The Cauca Valley is one of Colombia's most important agricultural regions, dominated by sugarcane cultivation. The river supports hydroelectric projects, including the Salvajina dam and the large Hidroituango project on its lower course in Antioquia, whose construction crisis in 2018 forced the evacuation of downstream communities. Gold mining, both legal and illegal, has contributed mercury pollution to the river, and the Cauca is home to fisheries of local importance, including the bocachico.

Tributaries

Major tributaries include the Nechí, La Vieja, Otún, Palo and Desbaratado rivers.
