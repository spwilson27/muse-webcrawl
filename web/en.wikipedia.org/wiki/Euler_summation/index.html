Euler summation

Euler summation is a summability method for convergent and divergent series. Given a series Σan, the method computes the Euler transform of the series and, if that transform converges to a finite value, defines that value to be the Euler sum of the original series. The method is named after the Swiss mathematician Leonhard Euler, who used related transformation techniques extensively in his work on series.

Definition

The Euler transform of a sequence is defined through forward differences. For a series Σan, the Euler sum is given by

Σn=0∞ an = Σn=0∞ (1 / 2n+1) Σk=0n C(n, k) ak

where C(n, k) denotes the binomial coefficient. The transform is particularly effective for alternating series, for which it often greatly accelerates convergence.

Examples

Applied to the alternating series 1 − 1/2 + 1/3 − 1/4 + ⋯, Euler summation recovers the value ln 2. The method also assigns finite values to some divergent series, such as the Grandi series 1 − 1 + 1 − 1 + ⋯, to which it gives the value 1/2.

Relation to other methods

Euler summation is closely related to Borel summation and to Cesàro summation. Every series that is summable by the Euler method with a given parameter is also summable by certain other methods under appropriate conditions, and the Euler method is regular: it agrees with the ordinary sum whenever the series converges in the usual sense.
