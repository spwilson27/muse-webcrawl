Christian I (February 1426 – 21 May 1481) was a Scandinavian monarch under the Kalmar Union. He was King of Denmark (1448–1481), Norway (1450–1481) and Sweden (1457–1464). He was the first king of the House of Oldenburg, which went on to rule Denmark for more than four centuries.

Accession

Christian was the eldest son of Dietrich, Count of Oldenburg. After the death of King Christopher of Bavaria in 1448 without an heir, the Danish Council of the Realm offered the throne to Christian, who accepted the conditions of a charter limiting royal power. In 1449 he married Dorothea of Brandenburg, the widow of his predecessor. He was crowned king of Norway in Trondheim in 1450.

Sweden and the duchies

Christian was recognised as King of Sweden in 1457, but Swedish opposition led by the Sture family drove him out in 1464; his defeat at the Battle of Brunkeberg (1471) ended his attempts to regain Sweden. In 1460 he was proclaimed ruler of Schleswig and Holstein, uniting the duchies with the Danish crown under the Treaty of Ribe, whose motto up ewig ungedeelt ("forever undivided") shaped regional politics for centuries. In 1474 Emperor Frederick III raised Holstein to a duchy.

Later reign

In 1479 Christian founded the University of Copenhagen, Denmark's oldest university. Heavy taxation to service his debts made him unpopular. He died in 1481 and was succeeded by his son John.
