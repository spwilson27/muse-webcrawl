Havre de Grace is a city in Harford County, Maryland, United States, situated at the mouth of the Susquehanna River where it empties into the head of the Chesapeake Bay. The population was 14,807 at the 2020 census. Its French name — "Harbour of Grace" — is traditionally credited to the Marquis de Lafayette, who is said to have remarked that the sheltered harbour reminded him of Le Havre.

History

The town was laid out in the 1780s on the ferry route between Baltimore and points north. During the War of 1812, on 3 May 1813, a British squadron under Rear Admiral George Cockburn attacked and burned much of Havre de Grace; the Irish-born defender John O'Neill manned a cannon single-handedly at Concord Point and became a local hero, and his daughter Matilda later tended the Concord Point Light. By tradition, the town lost by one vote in the House of Representatives when Congress considered a new national capital after the war.

In the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries Havre de Grace prospered on fishing, oystering, duck hunting on the Susquehanna Flats, and the Susquehanna and Tidewater Canal. The Havre de Grace Racetrack ("The Graw", 1912–1950) drew horses such as Man o' War and Seabiscuit. Decoy carving became a signature local craft, and the town calls itself the "Decoy Capital of the World".

Geography and attractions

The city covers about 6.9 square miles, including waterfront along the bay. Attractions include the Concord Point Lighthouse (1827), the Havre de Grace Maritime Museum, the Decoy Museum, a riverfront promenade, and marinas. The Thomas J. Hatem Memorial Bridge and the Amtrak Susquehanna River Bridge cross the river nearby.
