Sanitary sewer

A sanitary sewer is an underground pipe or tunnel system for transporting sewage from houses and commercial buildings (but not stormwater) to a sewage treatment plant or disposal site. Sanitary sewers are a type of gravity sewer and are part of an overall system called a "sewage system" or sewerage.

Description

Sanitary sewers serving industrial areas also carry industrial wastewater. In municipalities served by sanitary sewers, separate storm drains may convey surface runoff directly to surface waters. Sanitary sewer systems are distinguished from combined sewers, which carry both sewage and storm runoff in one pipe. Keeping stormwater out of sanitary sewers is important because treatment plants are not designed to handle large storm flows, and excess inflow can cause sanitary sewer overflows.

Design and components

A sanitary sewer system typically consists of lateral sewers connecting buildings, collector (branch) sewers, trunk sewers, intercepting sewers, pumping stations, force mains, and manholes that provide access for inspection and cleaning. Pipes are commonly made of vitrified clay, PVC, cast iron, or reinforced concrete. Flow is usually by gravity, with pipes sized and sloped to maintain self-cleansing velocities that prevent solids from settling.

History

Some of the earliest sewers date to Roman times, including the Cloaca Maxima in Rome. Modern sanitary sewerage developed in the 19th century as cities recognized the link between contaminated water and diseases such as cholera and typhoid, leading to planned underground sewer networks in cities such as London and Paris.

Maintenance and problems

Common problems include blockages from fats, oils and grease ("fatbergs"), infiltration and inflow of groundwater and stormwater through cracked pipes and defective joints, root intrusion, and corrosion from hydrogen sulfide gas. Utilities manage these through inspection (often by closed-circuit television), cleaning, rehabilitation methods such as cured-in-place pipe lining, and replacement.
