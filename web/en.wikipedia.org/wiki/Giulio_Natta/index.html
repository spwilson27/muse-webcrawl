Giulio Natta

Died2 May 1979, Bergamo, Italy

Giulio Natta (1903–1979) was an Italian chemical engineer and Nobel laureate. He shared the 1963 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Karl Ziegler for their work on catalysts enabling the production of stereoregular polymers.

Work

Natta extended Ziegler's organometallic catalyst systems to the polymerization of propylene, discovering in 1954 that certain catalysts produced isotactic polypropylene — a crystalline, commercially valuable plastic — rather than a soft amorphous material. He coined terms such as isotactic, atactic, and syndiotactic to describe polymer stereochemistry.

Career

Natta studied chemical engineering at the Polytechnic University of Milan, where he later became professor and director of the Institute of Industrial Chemistry. His collaboration with the Montecatini company brought isotactic polypropylene into industrial production from 1957, transforming the plastics industry.
