Poincaré half-plane model

In non-Euclidean geometry, the Poincaré half-plane model is a model of two-dimensional hyperbolic geometry. It is named after Henri Poincaré, and represents the hyperbolic plane as the upper half of the complex plane, H = { (x, y) : y > 0 }, equipped with a metric that makes it conformal — angles measured in the model equal true hyperbolic angles.

Metric and geodesics

The metric is given by ds² = (dx² + dy²) / y², so that the distance element grows as one approaches the boundary line y = 0, which represents points at infinity. The geodesics ("lines") of the model are semicircles perpendicular to the real axis (with centres on the axis) together with vertical rays perpendicular to the axis. Distances along a geodesic can be computed with a cross-ratio formula in terms of the two ideal endpoints on the boundary.

Isometries

The isometries of the half-plane model are the Möbius transformations with real coefficients and positive determinant, forming the group PSL(2, ℝ), acting by z ↦ (az + b)/(cz + d). Reflections and other orientation-reversing isometries extend this to PGL(2, ℝ). The model is closely related to the Poincaré disk model via the Cayley transform, a conformal map between the upper half-plane and the unit disk.

Applications

The half-plane model is fundamental in the theory of modular forms and Fuchsian groups, since the quotient of H by the modular group SL(2, ℤ) and its subgroups gives rise to modular curves. It is also used in complex analysis, Teichmüller theory, and the study of hyperbolic manifolds.
