Tomb of the Unknown Soldier (Arlington)

LocationArlington National Cemetery, Arlington County, Virginia, United States
Established11 November 1921
DesignerLorimer Rich (architect); Thomas Hudson Jones (sculptor)
MaterialYule marble

The Tomb of the Unknown Soldier is a historic funerary monument dedicated to deceased U.S. service members whose remains have not been identified, located in Arlington National Cemetery in Arlington County, Virginia. Since 1948 it has been guarded continuously by sentinels of the United States Army's 3rd Infantry Regiment, "The Old Guard".

History

Following the First World War, Congress approved the burial of an unidentified American soldier in the plaza of the new Memorial Amphitheater. In October 1921 four unknown American dead were exhumed from cemeteries in France; Sergeant Edward F. Younger selected one by placing white roses on one of the caskets aboard the cruiser USS Olympia. The Unknown was interred on Armistice Day, 11 November 1921, beneath a simple marble slab.

The present marble sarcophagus, completed and unveiled in 1932, bears three carved Greek figures representing Peace, Victory and Valor, and the inscription: "Here rests in honored glory a comrade in arms known but to God." Crypts in front of the sarcophagus later received unknown soldiers of the Second World War and the Korean War (1958), and of the Vietnam War (1984). The Vietnam Unknown was identified in 1998 by DNA testing as Air Force First Lieutenant Michael Joseph Blassie, and the crypt was rededicated to "Honoring and Keeping Faith with America's Missing Servicemen".

Changing of the Guard

Tomb Guards perform the Changing of the Guard in a precisely choreographed ceremony, walking 21 steps in reference to the 21-gun salute, pausing 21 seconds, and repeating the pattern. The guard has been maintained around the clock, in all weather, since 1948.
