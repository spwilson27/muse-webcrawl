Beneš decrees

The Beneš decrees were a series of laws drafted by the Czechoslovak government-in-exile in the absence of the Czechoslovak parliament during the German occupation of Czechoslovakia in the Second World War and issued by President Edvard Beneš.

Content

The decrees dealt with restoring Czechoslovakia after the war. The best-known provided for the revocation of citizenship and confiscation of property of ethnic Germans and Hungarians who had collaborated or held German/Hungarian citizenship, and provided legal cover for the expulsion of ethnic Germans from Czechoslovakia after the war, during which some 3 million people were transferred to Germany and Austria.

Legacy

The decrees remain politically sensitive in relations between the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Germany, Austria and Hungary, and were debated during Czech accession to the European Union. Czech courts and governments have maintained that the decrees are historically exhausted but not repealed.
