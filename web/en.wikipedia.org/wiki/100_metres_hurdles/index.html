100 metres hurdles

The 100 metres hurdles is a track and field event run mainly by women (the male equivalent is the 110 metres hurdles). The race consists of ten hurdles at a height of 33 inches (83.8 cm) placed over a straight course of 100 metres. The first hurdle is placed 13 metres from the starting line, with 8.5 metres between hurdles and a run-in of 10.5 metres to the finish.

100 metres hurdles

Sport: Athletics

Category: Hurdles / sprint

Hurdles: 10, height 83.8 cm (33 in)

Olympic Games: Since 1972 (as 100 m); 80 m hurdles 1932–1968

World record: 12.12 s — Tobi Amusan (2022)

History

Women originally raced over 80 metres hurdles, an Olympic event from 1932 to 1968 with eight hurdles of 76.2 cm. In 1969 the distance was extended to 100 metres with ten higher hurdles, and the event made its Olympic debut in this form at the 1972 Munich Games, where East Germany's Annelie Ehrhardt won gold in a world record 12.59 seconds.

Technique

Elite hurdlers take three strides between hurdles, sprinting out of the blocks to the first barrier in eight strides. The lead leg is extended over the hurdle while the trail leg is pulled through sideways; arm action mirrors sprinting to maintain balance. Knocking down hurdles is not a disqualification, but deliberate knocking or trailing a leg around the side of a hurdle is.

Records and leading athletes

The world record of 12.12 seconds was set by Nigeria's Tobi Amusan in the semi-finals of the 2022 World Championships in Eugene, Oregon; she then ran 12.06 in the final with a tailwind above the allowable limit. The previous record of 12.20, set by Kendra Harrison in 2016, had broken the long-standing 12.21 of Yordanka Donkova from 1988. Other notable champions include Sally Pearson, Dawn Harper-Nelson, Brianna McNeal and Jasmine Camacho-Quinn, the 2020 Olympic champion.
