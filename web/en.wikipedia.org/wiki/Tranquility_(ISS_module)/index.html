Tranquility (ISS module)

Tranquility, also known as Node 3, is a module of the International Space Station (ISS). It contains environmental control and life support systems, a toilet, exercise equipment, and an observation cupola. It was built by the European Space Agency (ESA) in cooperation with the Italian Space Agency for NASA, and launched on STS-130 on 8 February 2010.

Contents

Tranquility houses the station's most advanced life support: the Water Recovery System, the Oxygen Generating System and a waste and hygiene compartment. It carries exercise devices including the Advanced Resistive Exercise Device and a treadmill. Four of its six ports are used to berth visiting vehicles or other modules, and its nadir port holds the Cupola, a seven-windowed observation module from which the station's robotic arm is operated.

Name

The module was named through a NASA public poll; comedian Stephen Colbert's campaign won the online vote, and NASA ultimately named the module Tranquility — honouring the Apollo 11 landing site, the Sea of Tranquility — while naming an exercise treadmill C.O.L.B.E.R.T. after Colbert.

Specifications

The pressurised cylinder is about 7.0 m long and 4.5 m in diameter, with a launch mass near 15.5 tonnes. Its shell is micrometeoroid-shielded aluminium built by Thales Alenia Space in Turin, Italy.
