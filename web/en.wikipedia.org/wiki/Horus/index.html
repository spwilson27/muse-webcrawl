Horus is one of the most significant ancient Egyptian gods, a sky deity depicted as a falcon or as a falcon-headed man, whose right eye was the sun and left eye the moon. He is best known as the son of Osiris and Isis, who avenged his father's murder and inherited kingship — a myth that made every living pharaoh a "Horus" and the deceased king an Osiris.

The contendings with Set

After Osiris's death, Isis raised Horus in hiding in the Delta marshes. Grown, he contested the throne with his uncle Set (god) in an eighty-year struggle of battles, races and legal pleadings before the gods — recorded in the New Kingdom tale The Contendings of Horus and Set — ending in Horus's vindication and coronation over a united Egypt. In the fighting Set tore out Horus's eye, which Thoth healed: the restored Eye of Horus (wedjat) became Egypt's most ubiquitous protective amulet, also used as a system of fractions.

Cult

Horus was worshipped in many local forms — Horus of Edfu, whose vast Ptolemaic temple, the best-preserved in Egypt, staged his annual sacred marriage to Hathor; Horus the Elder (Haroeris); and Harpocrates, the child Horus of popular devotion. The king's oldest title was his "Horus name", written in a serekh palace-facade — the falcon god stands behind Egyptian kingship from its very beginnings.
