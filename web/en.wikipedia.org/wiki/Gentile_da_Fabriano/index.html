Gentile da Fabriano

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gentile da Fabriano
Born: c. 1370, Fabriano, Papal States
Died: 1427, Rome
Movement: International Gothic
Notable work: Adoration of the Magi (1423)

Gentile da Fabriano (c. 1370 – 1427) was an Italian painter and the foremost exponent of the International Gothic style in Italy. Born Gentile di Niccolò di Giovanni di Massio in Fabriano in the Marche, he worked for courts and patrons across the peninsula — in Venice, Brescia, Florence, Siena, Orvieto and Rome — and his richly decorative, courtly art profoundly influenced contemporaries including Pisanello, who was his pupil or associate.

Career

Gentile's earliest documented work includes frescoes in his native Fabriano. Around 1408–1409 he painted in the Doge's Palace in Venice, where his lost fresco of the naval battle between the Venetians and Otto III was praised by contemporaries. For the banker Pandolfo Malatesta he frescoed a chapel in Brescia (c. 1414–1419), work now largely destroyed or detached.

The Adoration of the Magi

His masterpiece is the Adoration of the Magi altarpiece (1423), commissioned by the Florentine banker Palla Strozzi for the sacristy of Santa Trinita in Florence and now in the Uffizi. Signed and dated, it shows the Magi's procession winding through a landscape across the top of the panel into a glittering court scene before the Virgin and Child, sumptuously tooled in gold and lapis. The predella below includes one of the earliest true night scenes in Italian painting, the Nativity lit by divine light. Though painted in Florence at the moment the Renaissance was beginning, the panel is the supreme achievement of the Gothic courtly manner rather than of the new style.

Later work and death

Other works include the Quaratesi Polyptych (1425), now dispersed among several museums, and the Valle Romita Polyptych. In 1426 Gentile went to Rome to fresco the nave of St John Lateran for Pope Martin V; he died there in 1427 and the cycle was completed by Pisanello (it was destroyed in later rebuilding). His art, with its naturalistic details of animals, flowers and fabrics observed from life within a fairy-tale splendour, marks the high point of International Gothic painting in Italy.
