Tree of Life

The tree of life as a symbol of creation and interconnectedness.

The tree of life is a fundamental archetype in many of the world's mythological, religious, and philosophical traditions. It is closely related to the concept of the sacred tree. The tree of knowledge connecting to heaven and the underworld, as well as the tree of life — which connects all forms of creation — are both forms of the world tree or cosmic tree, and are portrayed in various religions and philosophies as the same tree.

In religion and mythology

In the Book of Genesis, the tree of life stands in the Garden of Eden alongside the tree of the knowledge of good and evil; its fruit grants immortality, and after the Fall it is guarded by cherubim with a flaming sword. In Norse mythology, the world tree Yggdrasil connects the nine worlds. Ancient Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Chinese and Mesoamerican cultures all feature sacred trees linking heaven, earth and the underworld. In Kabbalah, the Tree of Life is a diagram of ten sephirot through which the divine manifests.

In science

In biology, the "tree of life" is the metaphor Charles Darwin used for the branching descent of all species from common ancestors, sketched in On the Origin of Species (1859). Modern phylogenetic trees, including the molecular three-domain tree proposed by Carl Woese, depict evolutionary relationships across all known life.

In art and culture

The motif appears in Gustav Klimt's painting The Tree of Life (1905), in Baha'i and Mormon theology (where the tree of life vision of Lehi is central), and as the name of places, artworks and films, including Terrence Malick's 2011 film The Tree of Life.
