Pontiac Firebird

The Pontiac Firebird is an American car built and produced by Pontiac from 1967 to 2002 model years. Designed as a pony car to compete with the Ford Mustang, it was introduced on February 23, 1967, five months after GM's Chevrolet division's platform-sharing Camaro.

Generations

First generation (1967–1969) shared the F-body platform with the Camaro and featured Coke-bottle styling. Second generation (1970–1981) models became icons of 1970s American performance, especially the Trans Am, popularised by the film Smokey and the Bandit. Third generation (1982–1992) cars were lighter and more aerodynamic, and a black Trans Am starred as KITT in Knight Rider. Fourth generation (1993–2002) cars featured sleek styling and powerful V8 engines.

Trans Am

The Trans Am was the Firebird's high-performance variant, offered with handling packages, larger engines such as the 455 cu in V8 and later turbocharged and LS1-powered versions, and distinctive graphics including the large hood "screaming chicken" decal.

Discontinuation

Production ended in 2002 along with the Camaro, and the Pontiac brand itself was discontinued by General Motors in 2010.
