Plesiochronous digital hierarchy

The plesiochronous digital hierarchy (PDH) is a technology used in telecommunications networks to transport large quantities of data over digital transport equipment such as fibre optic and microwave radio systems. The term plesiochronous is derived from Greek plēsios, meaning near, and chronos, time, and refers to the fact that PDH networks run in a state where different parts of the network are nearly, but not quite perfectly, synchronised.

Structure

PDH multiplexes lower-rate streams bit-by-bit into higher-rate aggregates using bit stuffing (justification) to accommodate small differences in the clocks of the tributary signals. In North America the hierarchy is based on the 64 kbit/s DS0 channel and the 1.544 Mbit/s T1 signal (DS1), multiplexed to DS2 and DS3 (44.736 Mbit/s). In Europe the hierarchy is based on the 2.048 Mbit/s E1 signal, multiplexing up through E2/E3 to E4 at 139.264 Mbit/s.

Limitations

Because tributaries are not synchronised to a common clock, an individual low-rate channel cannot be extracted from a high-rate PDH stream without fully demultiplexing it ("back-to-back multiplexers"), and the hierarchy lacks standardised network management and protection features. These drawbacks led to its largely being superseded by the synchronous digital hierarchy (SDH/SONET).
