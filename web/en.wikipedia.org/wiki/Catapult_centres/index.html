Catapult centres are a network of technology and innovation centres established in the United Kingdom by Innovate UK (formerly the Technology Strategy Board) to bridge the gap between university research and commercial industry. The programme was inspired by Germany's Fraunhofer Institutes and similar applied-research organisations, and followed recommendations in reports by Hermann Hauser and Sir James Dyson on innovation and technology commercialisation.

History

The first Catapult, the High Value Manufacturing Catapult, opened in 2011 with a network of centres including the Advanced Manufacturing Research Centre and the Manufacturing Technology Centre. The government committed several hundred million pounds of core funding over the programme's first five years, with the intention that centres would also attract matched funding from industry contracts and collaborative research projects.

Centres

The network grew to include Catapults in areas such as:

  • High Value Manufacturing
  • Cell and Gene Therapy
  • Connected Places (formerly Future Cities and Transport Systems)
  • Digital
  • Energy Systems
  • Medicines Discovery
  • Offshore Renewable Energy
  • Satellite Applications

Funding model

Each Catapult is expected to operate on an approximate "thirds" model: one third core public grant, one third competitively won collaborative research funding, and one third commercial contract research income from businesses.
