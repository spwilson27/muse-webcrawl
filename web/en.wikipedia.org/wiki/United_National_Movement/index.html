The United National Movement (Georgian: ერთიანი ნაციონალური მოძრაობა, Ertiani Natsionaluri Modzraoba; UNM) is a political party in Georgia. Founded in 2001 by Mikheil Saakashvili, it led the 2003 Rose Revolution and governed Georgia from 2004 until 2012, pursuing sweeping market reforms, anti-corruption measures, and a strongly pro-Western foreign policy aimed at NATO and European Union membership.

History

Saakashvili formed the UNM after breaking with President Eduard Shevardnadze's Citizens' Union. Following disputed parliamentary elections in November 2003, mass protests led by the UNM and allied movements forced Shevardnadze's resignation—the Rose Revolution. Saakashvili won the January 2004 presidential election overwhelmingly, and the UNM gained a parliamentary majority.

In government, the party slashed bureaucracy, rebuilt infrastructure, reformed the police, and achieved rapid economic growth, but was criticized for concentrating power, pressuring media and business, and for the violent dispersal of protests in 2007. The August 2008 war with Russia over South Ossetia further strained its standing. In the 2012 parliamentary election the UNM lost to Bidzina Ivanishvili's Georgian Dream coalition in Georgia's first democratic transfer of power by election; Saakashvili conceded defeat and the UNM became the principal opposition party.

Opposition years

After 2012 many UNM figures faced prosecution, which the party denounced as political revenge; Saakashvili left Georgia and later served as governor of Ukraine's Odesa Oblast. The party split in 2017, with many MPs leaving to form European Georgia. The UNM remained the largest opposition force through the 2020 elections, while internal disputes and Saakashvili's imprisonment after his 2021 return to Georgia continued to shape its fortunes.
