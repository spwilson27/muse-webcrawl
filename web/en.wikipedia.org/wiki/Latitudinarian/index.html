Latitudinarian

Latitudinarians were a group of 17th-century English theologians and clergy in the Church of England who advocated a broad, tolerant approach to doctrine, emphasising reason, morality, and the essentials of faith over disputed ceremonies and dogmas. The name derives from their willingness to allow "latitude" in religious interpretation; contemporaries also called them the "men of latitude".

Origins

The movement grew out of the circle of the Cambridge Platonists and divines such as John Tillotson, Edward Stillingfleet, Simon Patrick, and Thomas Tenison, many of whom rose to bishoprics after the Glorious Revolution of 1688. In reaction to the religious conflicts of the Civil War era and Puritan dogmatism, latitudinarians preached in a plain style, stressed virtuous living, and sought comprehension of moderate dissenters within the national church.

Beliefs and influence

Latitudinarians held that Scripture should be read with reason, minimised differences over church government and liturgy, and were receptive to the new science of the Royal Society. Their approach shaped the comparatively tolerant character of the 18th-century Church of England and anticipated later "broad church" Anglicanism. Critics, including High Church opponents, accused them of reducing Christianity to mere moralism.
