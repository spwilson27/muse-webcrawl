Maurizio Gucci

Maurizio Gucci (26 September 1948 – 27 March 1995) was an Italian businessman who headed the luxury fashion house Gucci, founded by his grandfather Guccio Gucci. He was the last member of the family to control the company, and his murder in Milan in 1995 — ordered by his former wife, Patrizia Reggiani — became one of Italy's most notorious crimes.

Life and control of Gucci

The only child of actor Rodolfo Gucci, Maurizio inherited a 50 percent stake in the company on his father's death in 1983 and, after a bitter boardroom struggle, took control from his cousin Paolo and uncle Aldo Gucci in the late 1980s. In 1972 he had married Patrizia Reggiani, a Milanese socialite, with whom he had two daughters; the couple separated in 1985 and divorced in 1994. Maurizio's management saw mounting losses and debts, and in 1993 he sold his entire stake to the Bahrain-based investment group Investcorp for a reported $170 million, ending nearly seven decades of family ownership.

Assassination

On the morning of 27 March 1995, as he arrived at his office on Via Palestro in Milan, Maurizio Gucci was shot four times by a hitman and died on the steps of the building. Police initially considered business motives, but an investigation led, in 1997, to the arrest of Patrizia Reggiani, who had hired the gunman Benedetto Ceraulo through an intermediary, the socialite Giuseppina "Pina" Auriemma. In 1998 Reggiani was convicted of orchestrating the murder and sentenced to 29 years in prison (reduced on appeal and with early release in 2016). She reportedly described her motive as a mixture of financial claims after the divorce and resentment of Maurizio.

In popular culture

The story is told in Sara Gay Forden's book The House of Gucci (2001) and in Ridley Scott's film House of Gucci (2021), in which Adam Driver played Maurizio and Lady Gaga played Patrizia.
