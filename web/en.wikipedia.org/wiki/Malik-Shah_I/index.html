Malik-Shah I

Reign: 15 December 1072 – 19 November 1092

Predecessor: Alp Arslan

Successor: Mahmud I

Born: 16 August 1055

Died: 19 November 1092, Baghdad

Empire: Great Seljuk Empire

Malik-Shah I (Persian: ملک شاه; 1055–1092), full regnal name Jalāl al-Dawla Muʿizz al-Dīn Abu'l-Fatḥ Malik-Shāh, was the third sultan of the Great Seljuk Empire, reigning from 1072 to 1092. Under him the empire reached its greatest territorial extent, stretching from Anatolia and the Levant to Transoxiana and the borders of China.

Reign

Malik-Shah succeeded his father Alp Arslan, who died after being wounded by a captured fortress commander. His long-serving vizier, Nizam al-Mulk — author of the famous Siyasatnama (Book of Government) and founder of the Nizamiyya network of colleges — effectively administered the empire for much of the reign. Seljuk armies consolidated control in Syria, Palestine, and the Caucasus; in the east, campaigns secured Bukhara and Samarkand and pushed suzerainty toward Kashgar. In 1087 Malik-Shah took up residence in Baghdad, symbolically restoring the caliph's capital as an imperial centre, and married a daughter of the Abbasid caliph al-Muqtadi.

Culture and reforms

The reign is remembered as a golden age of Persianate culture. Malik-Shah commissioned the Jalali calendar, a solar reform computed by a team including Omar Khayyam, which proved more accurate than the Julian calendar; he built observatories, mosques — notably expanding the Jameh Mosque of Isfahan — roads, and caravanserais, and curbed the brigandage that plagued the pilgrimage routes.

Death and fragmentation

Nizam al-Mulk was assassinated by a Nizari Ismaili (Assassin) agent in October 1092; Malik-Shah died a month later in Baghdad, possibly by poison, amid disputes over the succession between his wife Terken Khatun and the vizier's supporters. His death triggered civil war among his sons and brothers, fragmenting the Great Seljuk Empire just as the First Crusade approached.
