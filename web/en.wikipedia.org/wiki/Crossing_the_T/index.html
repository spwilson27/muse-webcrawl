Crossing the T

Crossing the T or capping the T is a classic naval warfare tactic used from the late 19th to the mid-20th century. A line of warships crosses in front of a line of enemy ships, allowing the crossing line to bring all its guns to bear while receiving fire from only the forward guns of the enemy.

Advantages

The crossing fleet can fire full broadsides, each ship can aim at a separate target, and rangefinding is easier. The enemy's lead ships mask the fire of those behind and risk being disabled and blocking the line.

Examples

The tactic was famously achieved by Admiral Tōgō at the Battle of Tsushima (1905), by the British Grand Fleet crossing the German High Seas Fleet at Jutland (1916), and for the last time by US battleships at Surigao Strait (1944). Aircraft carriers, submarines and missiles made line-of-battle tactics obsolete.
