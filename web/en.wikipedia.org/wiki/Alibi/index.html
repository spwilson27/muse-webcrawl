Alibi

Alibi
Latin for "elsewhere"
Type: Criminal defence
Claim: The accused was elsewhere when the act took place

An alibi is a form of defence used in criminal procedure whereby a defendant attempts to prove that they were elsewhere when the crime in question was committed. The term comes from Latin alibi, meaning "elsewhere". In common usage outside law it can mean any excuse or account offered to avoid blame.

Legal use

An alibi defence asserts that the defendant could not physically have committed the offence because they were at another location at the relevant time. It is typically supported by witness testimony, documents, receipts, photographs, phone or location records and CCTV. In many jurisdictions the defence must give advance notice of an alibi — for example under notice-of-alibi rules in the United States and provisions of England and Wales's Criminal Procedure and Investigations Act 1996 — so the prosecution can investigate it; failure to give notice can restrict use of the evidence.

Agency alibi

An "agency alibi" is a term used by some investigators for a story provided by someone acting on the defendant's behalf to give a false alibi, or for intelligence-agency cover stories. Fabricating an alibi, or testifying falsely to one, can constitute perjury or obstruction of justice.

In popular culture

The alibi is a staple of detective fiction, where plots often turn on a "broken alibi" — apparently solid proof of presence elsewhere that detective work dismantles, a device used extensively in the works of Agatha Christie and in classic whodunits.
