Grand Lodge

A Grand Lodge (or Grand Orient) is the governing body of a jurisdiction of Freemasonry. It supervises the individual ("particular" or "constituent") lodges within its territory, grants their charters or warrants, and sets the standards — the so-called landmarks and constitutions — by which they work.

History

The first Grand Lodge was formed on 24 June 1717, when four London lodges met at the Goose and Gridiron alehouse and elected Anthony Sayer as Grand Master. In 1723 James Anderson's Constitutions gave the body its foundational rulebook. Rival grand lodges — the "Antients" and the "Moderns" in England — competed until they united in 1813 as the United Grand Lodge of England.

Organisation

Grand Lodges are typically organised by sovereign territory: each US state, for example, has its own Grand Lodge. They are presided over by a Grand Master and a body of Grand Officers. Grand Lodges decide which other jurisdictions to recognise as "regular", a system of mutual recognition that shapes international Freemasonry; disputes over regularity — for example concerning the Grand Orient of France after 1877 — have produced lasting divisions between "regular" and "liberal" (continental) traditions.
