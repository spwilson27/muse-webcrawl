Berkshire Hathaway Inc. is an American multinational conglomerate holding company headquartered in Omaha, Nebraska. Originally a textile manufacturer, it was taken over in 1965 by investor Warren Buffett, who — with his longtime partner Charlie Munger (who died in 2023) — transformed it into the vehicle for one of the most successful investment records in history. Berkshire wholly owns businesses including the insurer GEICO, the BNSF freight railroad, energy utilities, Duracell and dozens of manufacturers and retailers, and holds major minority stakes in public companies such as Apple (long its largest holding) and Bank of America. Its Class A shares are the most expensive of any public company, trading in the hundreds of thousands of dollars per share because Buffett has never split them.

History

Buffett began buying Berkshire stock in 1962 and took control in 1965; the textile mills closed by 1985, but the name survived as the holding company. Using the "float" from acquired insurers (National Indemnity in 1967, GEICO in full in 1996) to fund investments, Buffett and Munger bought undervalued businesses outright and large stock positions (Coca-Cola, American Express), compounding shareholder wealth at roughly 20 percent annually for decades. The annual shareholders' meeting in Omaha draws tens of thousands of investors and is nicknamed the "Woodstock for Capitalists".

Succession

In May 2025 Buffett announced he would step down as CEO at the end of 2025, with Greg Abel — vice chairman overseeing non-insurance businesses — as his successor; Buffett remains chairman. Berkshire's cash pile reached record levels above $300 billion in the mid-2020s as Buffett trimmed the Apple stake and struggled to find acquisitions large enough to matter.
