Kentucky Bourbon Trail

A distillery on the Kentucky Bourbon Trail

The Kentucky Bourbon Trail is the name of a program sponsored by the Kentucky Distillers' Association (KDA) designed to promote the bourbon whiskey industry in Kentucky. The trail connects visitors with distilleries across the state where they can tour production facilities, learn about the history and craft of bourbon making, and sample the products.

The KDA launched the program in 1999, modeled on wine trails in California and similar routes elsewhere. Visitors traditionally collect stamps in a "passport" at each distillery, receiving a gift upon completing the trail. The trail has grown from a handful of founding distilleries to dozens of stops, and the association later added a Craft Tour for smaller distilleries. Annual visits number well over a million, making bourbon tourism a significant part of Kentucky's economy.

Member distilleries

Participating distilleries have included Maker's Mark, Jim Beam, Wild Turkey, Woodford Reserve, Four Roses, Heaven Hill, Buffalo Trace, and Angel's Envy, among many others.

History

Bourbon production in Kentucky dates to the late 18th century, aided by the region's limestone-filtered water and corn-growing climate. The trail program capitalized on the bourbon revival of the 1990s and 2000s, when premium and small-batch bourbons gained national attention.
