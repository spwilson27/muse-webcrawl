Leverkusen (German pronunciation: [ˈleːvɐˌkuːzn̩]) is a city in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, on the eastern bank of the Rhine. To its south, Leverkusen borders the city of Cologne, and to the north the state capital, Düsseldorf. With about 164,000 inhabitants, Leverkusen is one of the smaller German cities. The city is known for the chemical company Bayer, founded there in 1863, and its associated sports club Bayer 04 Leverkusen.

History

The heart of what is now Leverkusen consists of Wiesdorf, a village first mentioned in the 12th century. The area belonged to the County (later Duchy) of Berg. The modern city was founded in 1930 by merging the towns of Wiesdorf, Schlebusch, Steinbüchel and Rheindorf, taking its name from the pharmacist Carl Leverkus, whose chemical factory had been established near Wiesdorf in the 1860s and later sold to Bayer. Bayer moved its headquarters to the site, and the settlement grew into a company town dominated by the pharmaceutical and chemical industries. In the 1975 municipal reform, Opladen and Bergisch Neukirchen were incorporated, roughly doubling the city's population.

Industry and landmarks

Leverkusen is dominated by Bayer AG's Chempark, one of Europe's largest chemical sites. The Bayer Cross — a 118-metre, illuminated advertising sign erected in 1958 and replaced by an LED version in 2009 — is one of the largest illuminated signs in the world and a symbol of the city. Other landmarks include Morsbroich Castle (Schloss Morsbroich), a baroque moated palace housing a modern art museum, and the Japanese Garden, laid out in 1913.

Sport

The city is home to Bayer 04 Leverkusen, whose football team plays at the BayArena and won its first Bundesliga title and the DFB-Pokal in the 2023–24 season, completing an unbeaten league campaign. The club grew out of Bayer's company sports association, founded in 1904. Leverkusen also has strong traditions in athletics, basketball (Bayer Giants) and women's handball.

Transport

Leverkusen lies on the Cologne–Düsseldorf–Ruhr rail corridor and the A1, A3 and A59 motorways; the Leverkusen Rhine bridge carries the A1 across the river. Leverkusen Mitte station is served by regional trains and the Rhine-Ruhr S-Bahn.
