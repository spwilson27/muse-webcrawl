Parliament of Italy

TypeBicameral
HousesSenate of the Republic; Chamber of Deputies
Seats200 senators; 400 deputies (since 2022)

The Italian Parliament is the national legislature of Italy. It is a bicameral legislature with 600 elected members: the 400 members of the Chamber of Deputies and the 200 elective members of the Senate of the Republic. Italy's system is one of "perfect bicameralism": both houses have equal powers and must approve legislation in identical terms.

Functions

Parliament elects the President of the Republic in joint session, grants confidence to the government, approves the budget, and can amend the constitution. Senators for life, including former presidents, sit in the Senate in addition to the elected members.

History

The modern parliament descends from the Parliament of the Kingdom of Italy established in 1861. The 1948 republican constitution created the current two chambers. A 2020 constitutional referendum cut the number of parliamentarians from 945 to 600, effective from the 2022 general election.
