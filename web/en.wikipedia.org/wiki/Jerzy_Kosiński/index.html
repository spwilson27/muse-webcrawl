Jerzy Kosiński

Born: Józef Lewinkopf, June 14, 1933, Łódź, Poland
Died: May 3, 1991, New York City, U.S.
Occupation: Novelist
Notable works: The Painted Bird (1965), Being There (1971)

Jerzy Kosiński (born Józef Lewinkopf; June 14, 1933 – May 3, 1991) was a Polish-American novelist and two-time president of the American chapter of PEN International. His best-known works are the novels The Painted Bird (1965), a harrowing account of a boy wandering through war-torn Eastern Europe, and Being There (1971), a satire about a simple gardener mistaken for a political sage, adapted into an acclaimed film starring Peter Sellers.

Life

Born in Łódź to Jewish parents, Kosiński survived the Second World War in hiding in Polish villages under the assumed Catholic identity "Jerzy Kosiński," separated for a time from his parents. After the war he studied history and sociology at the University of Łódź and worked as an academic before emigrating to the United States in 1957 on a foundation grant. He became a U.S. citizen in 1965 and taught at Wesleyan, Princeton, and Yale.

Works and controversies

The Painted Bird was initially presented with autobiographical implications; Kosiński later described it as fiction, though disputes over its sources, and later allegations in a 1982 Village Voice article about editorial assistance and plagiarism in his work, damaged his reputation. His other novels include Steps (1968), which won the National Book Award, The Devil Tree (1973), Cockpit (1975), and Passion Play (1979). He co-wrote the screenplay for Being There (1979) and acted in the film Reds (1981).

Suffering from ill health and heart disease, Kosiński died by suicide in New York City on May 3, 1991. Biographies, notably James Park Sloan's Jerzy Kosinski: A Biography (1996), re-examined the complex mixture of fact and fabrication in his life story.
