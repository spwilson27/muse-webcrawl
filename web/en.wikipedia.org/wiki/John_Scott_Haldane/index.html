John Scott Haldane

John Scott Haldane CH FRS (2 May 1860 – 14/15 March 1936) was a Scottish physiologist famous for his intrepid self-experimentation, which led to many important discoveries about the human body and the nature of gases. He investigated the effects of gases in mines, diving and high-altitude flight.

Work

Haldane studied the effects of carbon monoxide and other poisonous gases on miners, famously using canaries as sensitive indicators of gas in coal mines — a practice that continued for most of the twentieth century. He devised the Haldane apparatus for gas analysis, investigated blackdamp and afterdamp, and studied the physiology of respiration, showing how breathing is regulated by carbon dioxide levels in the blood.

Decompression

Commissioned by the Royal Navy, Haldane produced in 1908 the first decompression tables, based on the principle of staged ascent, which greatly reduced decompression sickness among divers. During the First World War he investigated poison gas attacks and helped develop respirators for troops.

Family

He was the father of the geneticist J. B. S. Haldane and the writer Naomi Mitchison. Haldane died in Oxford in 1936, reportedly hastening his end by continuing experiments on himself into old age.
