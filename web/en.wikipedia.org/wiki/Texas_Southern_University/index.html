Texas Southern University (TSU) is a public historically black university (HBCU) in Houston, Texas. Founded in 1927, it is one of the largest HBCUs in the United States and is a member school of the Thurgood Marshall College Fund. Its campus of about 150 acres lies in Houston's Third Ward, southeast of downtown.

History

The institution began in 1927 as the Houston Colored Junior College, operated by the Houston Independent School District at Jack Yates High School. In 1934 it became a four-year institution, Houston College for Negroes. In 1947, following the challenge of Heman Marion Sweatt to segregated legal education (leading to Sweatt v. Painter, 1950), the state purchased and designated the college as a state university — first named Texas State University for Negroes — partly in an attempt to preserve segregation by offering a separate law school. It was renamed Texas Southern University in 1951.

Academics

TSU comprises colleges and schools including the Thurgood Marshall School of Law, the Jesse H. Jones School of Business, the College of Pharmacy and Health Sciences, the Barbara Jordan–Mickey Leland School of Public Affairs, and colleges of science and technology, education, and liberal arts and behavioral sciences. The university is noted for its debate team, founded by Thomas Freeman and famously successful over decades, and for the Ocean of Soul marching band.

Athletics and alumni

The Tigers compete in the Southwestern Athletic Conference (SWAC). Notable alumni include congresswoman Barbara Jordan, congressman Mickey Leland, NFL Hall of Famer Michael Strahan, and rapper Megan Thee Stallion. In May 1967, during the civil-rights era, a confrontation known as the TSU riot followed police action on campus; one police officer died and five students (the "TSU Five") were charged, with charges later dropped.
