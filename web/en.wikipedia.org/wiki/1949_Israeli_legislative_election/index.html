1949 Israeli legislative election

The 1949 Israeli legislative election was held on 25 January 1949 to elect the Constituent Assembly of the newly established State of Israel. It was the first election in the country's history, conducted during the closing stages of the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. Mapai, led by David Ben-Gurion, emerged as the largest party with 46 of the 120 seats. The Constituent Assembly soon renamed itself the First Knesset.

Background

Israel had declared independence on 14 May 1948, and a provisional government led by Ben-Gurion administered the state during the war. Nearly half a million voters were eligible, including Arab citizens of the new state; voting proceeded under a nationwide proportional representation system with the whole country as one constituency and a 1% electoral threshold. Soldiers voted at the front, and polling was arranged so that the war effort was not disrupted.

Results

Mapai, the mainstream Labour Zionist party, won 35.7% of the vote and 46 seats. The left-wing Mapam won 19 seats, the United Religious Front 16, and Menachem Begin's Herut 14. Ben-Gurion formed a coalition government under the formula of excluding Herut and the Communists ("without Herut and Maki"), setting a pattern for Israeli coalition politics.

Aftermath

The Constituent Assembly convened in February 1949, elected Chaim Weizmann as President and adopted the Transition Law, converting itself into the First Knesset rather than completing a full written constitution. The First Knesset sat until 1951, passing foundational legislation including the Law of Return (1950).
