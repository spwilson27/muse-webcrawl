Shōnen manga (少年漫画) is a category of Japanese comics (manga) marketed primarily to adolescent boys, generally between the ages of 12 and 18. It is characterised by high-action plots, adventurous or martial-arts storylines, and themes of camaraderie, perseverance and self-improvement, though comedy, sports, science fiction and romance also feature widely. Along with shōjo manga (for girls), it is one of the most popular demographic categories of manga both in Japan and internationally.

Weekly Shōnen Jump, launched in 1968, is the best-selling shōnen magazine.

Characteristics

Shōnen series are typically serialised in weekly or monthly anthology magazines and later collected in tankōbon volumes. The archetypal shōnen hero is an energetic, often somewhat naive young male protagonist who grows stronger through training and rivalry. Weekly Shōnen Jump's editorial motto of "friendship, effort, victory" (yūjō, doryoku, shōri) famously codified the genre's values. Best-selling examples include Dragon Ball, One Piece — the best-selling manga series of all time — Naruto, Slam Dunk, Demon Slayer: Kimetsu no Yaiba and Jujutsu Kaisen.

History

Boys' magazines existed in Japan before the Second World War, but modern shōnen manga took shape in the postwar era under artists such as Osamu Tezuka. The launch of Weekly Shōnen Magazine and Weekly Shōnen Sunday in 1959, followed by Weekly Shōnen Jump in 1968, established the weekly anthology model. From the 1980s onward, shōnen titles became global cultural exports through anime adaptations, and the readership broadened well beyond the nominal target demographic.
