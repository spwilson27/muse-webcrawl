European Rugby Challenge Cup

The European Rugby Challenge Cup, currently known as the EPCR Challenge Cup, is an annual rugby union competition organised by European Professional Club Rugby (EPCR). It is the second-tier competition below the European Rugby Champions Cup, contested by clubs from the three major European leagues — the English Premiership, the French Top 14 and the United Rugby Championship — together with invited sides from other nations.

History

The competition began in the 1996–97 season as the European Conference, quickly renamed the European Shield and later the Parker Pen Challenge Cup and Amlin Challenge Cup, after European rugby's first tournament, the Heineken Cup, proved a success. French clubs dominated the early years; Bourgoin won the inaugural final in 1997. When EPCR replaced the old organisers in 2014, the tournament was relaunched as the European Rugby Challenge Cup with twenty teams. From the 2022–23 season, South African franchises entered European competition, and the Cheetahs and other invitational teams have also taken part.

Format

Teams are drawn into pools and play a small number of pool matches, with the best performers advancing to a knockout phase of round-of-16 ties, quarter-finals, semi-finals and a final held at a neutral venue, usually on the same weekend and in the same city as the Champions Cup final. Winners have included Harlequins, who in 2001 became the first English winners, Northampton Saints, Bath, Gloucester, Montpellier, Clermont Auvergne and Toulon, the latter winning in 2023 to complete a set of both European trophies.
