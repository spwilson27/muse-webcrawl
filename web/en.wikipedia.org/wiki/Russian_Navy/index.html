Russian Navy

The Russian Navy (Russian: Voyenno-morskoy flot Rossii) is the naval arm of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation. It traces its origins to the Imperial Russian Navy, traditionally dated to its founding by Peter the Great in 1696, and took its present form after the dissolution of the Soviet Navy in 1991–92.

Organisation

The Navy is organised into four fleets and one flotilla: the Northern Fleet (headquartered at Severomorsk), the Pacific Fleet (Vladivostok), the Baltic Fleet (Kaliningrad), the Black Sea Fleet (Sevastopol), and the Caspian Flotilla. Its principal branches include surface forces, submarine forces, naval aviation, coastal troops, and naval infantry.

Fleet and role

The Navy operates ballistic missile submarines forming the sea leg of Russia's nuclear triad, nuclear-powered attack and cruise-missile submarines, cruisers, destroyers, frigates, and corvettes. Its only aircraft carrier, Admiral Kuznetsov, has spent long periods in refit. After 1991 the fleet contracted sharply from Soviet-era size; a modernisation programme has since emphasised submarines and smaller missile-armed surface combatants.
