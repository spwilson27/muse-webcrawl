Corona discharge

Corona discharge
TypeElectrical discharge in a gas
CauseIonisation of fluid surrounding a conductor at high potential gradient
EffectsGlow, hissing, ozone production, power loss, radio interference

A corona discharge is an electrical discharge brought on by the ionisation of a fluid such as air surrounding a conductor carrying a high voltage. It occurs when the electric field is strong enough to ionise the air locally, but not strong enough — or the geometry not suited — to cause complete electrical breakdown or arcing to nearby objects. The discharge appears as a faint violet glow, often accompanied by a hissing or crackling sound, and produces ozone and oxides of nitrogen.

Mechanism

Around sharp points, edges and thin wires the electric field is greatly enhanced. Free electrons accelerated in this field collide with neutral molecules and ionise them, producing an avalanche confined to the region where the field exceeds the breakdown strength of air (about 30 kV/cm at standard conditions, described by Peek's law). In a positive corona, electrons are drawn into the conductor and positive ions drift away; in a negative corona, electrons are repelled outward and attach to molecules to form negative ions.

Applications

Corona discharge is exploited in electrostatic precipitators that clean exhaust gases, in photocopiers and laser printers (corona wires charge the photoconductor drum), in ozone generators, in ionisers and in the production of charged surfaces for materials processing. It is also the source of the ionic wind used in some experimental lifter devices.

Problems

On high-voltage transmission lines, corona wastes energy, generates audible noise and radio interference, corrodes conductors and produces ozone. Engineers minimise it with smooth, large-diameter or bundled conductors and corona rings. Corona can also degrade polymer insulation over time and is one of the phenomena associated with St. Elmo's fire on ships and aircraft.
