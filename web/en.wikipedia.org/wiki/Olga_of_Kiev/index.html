Olga of Kiev (Old East Slavic: Ольга; c. 890/925 – 969), also known as Saint Olga, was a regent of Kievan Rus' for her son Sviatoslav from 945 until 960. She is famed in the chronicles for her revenge against the Drevlians who had murdered her husband, Igor of Kiev, for administrative reforms of the tribute system, and for her conversion to Christianity, which paved the way for the Christianisation of Rus' under her grandson Vladimir the Great. She is venerated as a saint, with the title "Equal to the Apostles".

Regency and the Drevlian revenge

According to the Primary Chronicle, Igor was killed in 945 by the Drevlians while attempting to collect a second round of tribute. Olga, acting as regent for the infant Sviatoslav, exacted a legendary sequence of revenges: she had the Drevlian envoys who proposed she marry their prince Mal buried alive, burned a second embassy in a bathhouse, slaughtered Drevlians at a funeral feast, and finally burned their capital Iskorosten by tying burning material to birds that flew back to their nests in the town. Historians treat the details as partly legendary, but the subjugation of the Drevlians is historical.

Administrative reforms

Olga reorganised the collection of tribute, establishing fixed amounts and designated collection points (pogosty) with local officials, replacing the arbitrary itinerant collection (poliudie) that had led to Igor's death. These reforms are considered an important step in the formation of the Rus' state.

Conversion

Olga travelled to Constantinople — traditionally dated to 957 — where she was received by Emperor Constantine VII, whose De Ceremoniis describes the reception of a Rus' ruler "Elga". She was baptised, taking the Christian name Helena. The chronicle also tells that she outwitted the Patriarch and refused the Emperor's marriage proposal by pointing out that a Christian could not marry his own goddaughter (the Emperor having stood sponsor at her baptism). On returning to Kyiv she attempted, without success, to convert her son Sviatoslav, who remained pagan.

Death and legacy

Olga died in 969 during the Pecheneg siege of Kyiv, having summoned Sviatoslav back from Bulgaria to relieve the city. She was buried with Christian rites. Her grandson Vladimir officially Christianised Rus' in 988. Olga was canonised and is honoured as the first Rus' ruler to convert to Christianity and a forerunner of the nation's baptism; her feast day is 11 July (24 July in some calendars).
