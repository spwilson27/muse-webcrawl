Session (music)

A session (Irish: seisiún) in music is a social gathering of musicians who play together informally, most characteristically in Irish traditional music. Irish traditional music sessions usually take place in public houses (pubs) and involve musicians seated around a table or in a corner playing sets of dance tunes — reels, jigs, hornpipes and polkas — together, alongside songs and occasional solo pieces.

Characteristics

Unlike a performance, a session is played primarily for the musicians' own enjoyment; listeners are welcome but the music is not presented to an audience. There is no conductor or set list: a musician starts a tune, others who know it join in, and tunes are typically grouped into "sets" of two or three played without pause. Common instruments include the fiddle, wooden flute, tin whistle, uilleann pipes, button accordion, concertina, tenor banjo, guitar, bouzouki and the bodhrán frame drum. Etiquette generally discourages dominating the session, playing along loudly on tunes one does not know, or using non-traditional instruments without invitation.

Related traditions

Analogous gatherings exist in Scottish traditional music, in English folk clubs, in old-time and bluegrass jam sessions in North America, and in other folk traditions, though the Irish pub session — which flourished both in Ireland and in the Irish diaspora from the mid-20th century — is the form most widely known by the name.
