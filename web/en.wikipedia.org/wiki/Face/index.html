Face

The human face

The face is the front part of the head in humans and many other animals, featuring the eyes, nose and mouth, through which many senses are channelled and emotions expressed. In humans the face extends from the forehead to the chin and from ear to ear, and is central to personal identity: faces are the primary means by which individuals are recognised.

Anatomy

The face is supported by the bones of the facial skeleton (viscerocranium), including the maxilla, mandible, zygomatic bones and frontal bone. Overlying muscles of facial expression — more than forty in humans — are innervated by the facial nerve (cranial nerve VII) and allow the fine movements involved in speech, chewing and expression. The skin of the face contains the openings of the eyes (with eyelids and eyebrows), the external nose and the mouth with its lips and teeth.

Function and expression

Faces convey emotion through expressions such as happiness, anger, fear, disgust, sadness and surprise, which are broadly recognised across cultures. The psychologist Paul Ekman's work on basic emotions and the Facial Action Coding System systematised the description of these movements. Humans possess specialised brain regions, including the fusiform face area, devoted to face perception; damage to these areas can produce prosopagnosia, the inability to recognise familiar faces.

Social and cultural aspects

Facial attractiveness, symmetry and expression play major roles in social interaction, and practices such as cosmetics, tattooing and adornment of the face are found in cultures worldwide. The concept of "face" also has a metaphorical social meaning — dignity or prestige, as in "losing face" — important in many societies.
