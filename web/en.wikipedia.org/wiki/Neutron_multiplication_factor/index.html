Neutron multiplication factor

The neutron multiplication factor, symbol k, is a central quantity in nuclear reactor physics. It is defined as the ratio of the number of neutrons produced by fission in one generation to the number of neutrons produced in the preceding generation:

k = (neutrons in generation n+1) / (neutrons in generation n)

Criticality

If k = 1, the chain reaction is self-sustaining at a steady rate and the system is critical. If k < 1 the system is subcritical and the reaction dies away; if k > 1 the system is supercritical and the neutron population grows exponentially. Reactor control consists of adjusting k — with control rods, moderator conditions and fuel composition — to hold it at exactly 1 during steady power operation.

Related quantities

The effective multiplication factor (keff) applies to a finite system and accounts for neutrons leaking out of the core, while the infinite multiplication factor (k∞) describes an idealized infinite medium and is often computed with the four-factor formula. Reactivity is commonly expressed as ρ = (k − 1)/k.
