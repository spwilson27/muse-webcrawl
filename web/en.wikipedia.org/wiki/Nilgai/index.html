Nilgai

Nilgai
Scientific nameBoselaphus tragocamelus
FamilyBovidae
Native rangeIndian subcontinent

The nilgai (Boselaphus tragocamelus) is the largest antelope native to Asia. It is found across the northern Indian subcontinent, from Pakistan to Nepal and Bangladesh. The name means "blue bull" in Hindi, referring to the bluish-grey coat of adult males.

Description

Males stand 1.1–1.5 metres at the shoulder and are blue-grey, while females and juveniles are tawny orange-brown. Only males bear horns, which are short, smooth and slightly forward-curving. Both sexes have a white patch on the throat and dark markings on the legs.

Habitat and behaviour

Nilgai inhabit lightly wooded country, scrubland and agricultural margins, avoiding dense forest. They are diurnal grazers and browsers, living in small herds. Females typically give birth to twins after a gestation of about eight months.

Conservation and introductions

The species is listed as Least Concern. Large introduced populations exist in Texas, where nilgai were released on ranches in the 1920s–1940s and are hunted as game animals.
