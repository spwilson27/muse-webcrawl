The Libyan National Army (LNA; Arabic: الجيش الوطني الليبي), also known as the Libyan Arab Armed Forces, is a military coalition and de facto army of eastern Libya commanded by Field Marshal Khalifa Haftar. Nominally answerable to the eastern-based House of Representatives, in practice it is built around Haftar loyalist units supplemented by tribal and regional militias, Salafist (Madkhali) brigades, Sudanese and other foreign fighters, and — during 2019–2020 — Russian Wagner Group contractors.

History

Haftar, a former Gaddafi-era officer who had defected in the 1980s, launched "Operation Dignity" in May 2014 against Islamist militias in Benghazi, and the eastern parliament appointed him army commander in 2015. After a long, destructive battle, the LNA took Benghazi by 2017 and Derna in 2018, then seized the southwestern oil region of Fezzan and the Oil Crescent terminals in early 2019.

In April 2019 Haftar ordered an offensive on Tripoli to capture the capital from the UN-recognised Government of National Accord (GNA). The assault stalled in the suburbs; with Turkish military support the GNA pushed the LNA back from western Libya in mid-2020, and the front froze around Sirte and Jufra before an October 2020 ceasefire. The LNA remained in control of most of eastern and southern Libya and its oil infrastructure leverage, including periodic oil blockades. Haftar's sons have risen to senior commands, and since 2023–2024 the force has been reorganised as ground, air and naval commands under the family-led general command in Al-Rajma.

Organisation and support

The LNA combines regular brigades — notably the 106th Brigade and the Tariq Ben Zeyad Brigade — with affiliated militias, border guards and an air force operating MiG and Mirage aircraft dating largely from the Gaddafi era. It has received support from Egypt, the United Arab Emirates, Russia and (at times) France, while being accused by the UN and human-rights groups of war crimes, including summary executions, indiscriminate shelling and mass graves at Tarhuna, which the LNA denies.
