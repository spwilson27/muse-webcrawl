Blue Ocean Strategy

AuthorsW. Chan Kim, Renée Mauborgne
Published2005 (expanded edition 2015)
SubjectBusiness strategy

Blue Ocean Strategy is a business strategy book published in 2005 by W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne, professors at INSEAD. Based on a study of 150 strategic moves across more than thirty industries over a century, the book argues that companies succeed not by competing head-on in crowded markets — "red oceans" bloodied by competition — but by creating uncontested new market space, "blue oceans", in which competition is irrelevant.

Key concepts

Central to the strategy is value innovation: simultaneously pursuing differentiation and low cost, rather than accepting the conventional trade-off between them. The book provides analytical tools including the strategy canvas, which plots the factors an industry competes on, and the four actions framework — asking which factors should be eliminated, reduced, raised or created (the ERRC grid) — to reconstruct market boundaries.

The canonical case study is Cirque du Soleil, which eliminated animals and star performers, reduced danger and humour, raised the artistic and theatrical quality, and created a new form of entertainment combining circus and theatre, attracting adult theatre-goers rather than children. Other examples include the Nintendo Wii, Southwest Airlines and the wine brand [yellow tail].

Reception

The book became a bestseller, selling millions of copies and being translated into dozens of languages, and was followed by Blue Ocean Shift (2017). Critics have argued that the framework is descriptive in hindsight — that successful innovations are identified as "blue oceans" only after the fact — and that it understates how quickly new markets attract imitators.
