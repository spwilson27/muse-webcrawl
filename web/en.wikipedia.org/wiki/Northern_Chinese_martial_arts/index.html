Northern Chinese martial arts

Northern Chinese martial arts (Chinese: 北拳) is a collective term for the styles of kung fu that originated in northern China, roughly in the regions north of the Yangtze River. These styles are generally distinguished by their emphasys on extended, fluid movements, high kicks, wide stances, leaping techniques and rapid changes of posture, in contrast to the more compact, low-stance techniques often associated with southern styles.

Characteristics

Northern styles developed in the open plains and northern terrain, where lower, stronger stances and long-range techniques were favoured. They typically feature extensive use of the legs, sometimes summarized in the saying "Southern fists and Northern legs" (南拳北腿). Acrobatics such as flying kicks, tornado kicks, butterfly kicks and splits are common.

Major styles

Well-known northern systems include Changquan (Long Fist), Northern Shaolin, Wing Chun is southern, Baguazhang, Xingyiquan, Tongbeiquan, Praying Mantis, Eagle Claw, Mizongyi (Lost Track Fist), Piguazhang, Fanziquan (Tumbling Fist) and Moslem (Hui) styles such as Chaquan and Xinyiliuhequan.

History

Many northern styles trace legendary origins to the Shaolin Temple in Henan province. Over centuries of migration, trade routes and military influence, northern systems spread throughout the northern provinces and later influenced the development of modern wushu competition forms, which draw heavily on northern aesthetics.
