Aerosmith

Aerosmith is an American rock band formed in Boston in 1970. The classic line-up consists of Steven Tyler (vocals), Joe Perry and Brad Whitford (guitars), Tom Hamilton (bass), and Joey Kramer (drums). Sometimes called "the Bad Boys from Boston" and "America's Greatest Rock and Roll Band", they are the best-selling American hard rock band of all time.

Career

Signed to Columbia Records, Aerosmith broke through with the albums Toys in the Attic (1975) and Rocks (1976), featuring songs such as "Dream On", "Walk This Way", and "Sweet Emotion". Drug problems and internal conflict led to Perry's and Whitford's departures around 1980, but the original line-up reunited in 1984. A 1986 collaboration with Run-DMC on "Walk This Way" revived their career, followed by the multi-platinum albums Permanent Vacation, Pump, and Get a Grip, and the 1998 number-one single "I Don't Want to Miss a Thing".

Legacy

Aerosmith has sold more than 150 million records worldwide, won four Grammy Awards, and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2001. The band announced a farewell tour, "Peace Out", in 2023.
