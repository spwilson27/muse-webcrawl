Boeing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Boeing 747 in flight, its humped forward fuselage unmistakable]
A Boeing 747 in flight, its humped forward fuselage unmistakable

The Boeing Company is an American multinational corporation that designs, manufactures and sells airplanes, rotorcraft, rockets, satellites and missiles worldwide. Founded by William E. Boeing in Seattle in 1916, it is one of the largest global aerospace manufacturers and, historically, the largest US exporter by dollar value. Its commercial jets — the 707, 727, 737, 747, 777 and 787 — defined the jet age, in permanent rivalry with Europe's Airbus.

History

Boeing built mail planes and fighters between the wars, the B-17 and B-29 bombers in the Second World War, and bet the company on the jet airliner with the 707 (1958). The 747 'jumbo jet' (1970) democratised intercontinental travel; the 737 (1967) became the best-selling jetliner family in history. In 1997 Boeing absorbed McDonnell Douglas, inheriting a finance-driven culture critics later blamed for engineering decline.

737 MAX crisis and aftermath

Two crashes of the new Boeing 737 MAX (Lion Air 2018, Ethiopian Airlines 2019, 346 dead) traced to the MCAS flight-control system grounded the type worldwide for 20 months, cost tens of billions, and produced criminal and congressional scrutiny of Boeing and the FAA. A door-plug blowout on a 737 MAX 9 in January 2024 renewed the crisis: production caps, a mandated safety overhaul, a CEO change, a machinists' strike and a guilty-plea agreement followed; the Starliner spacecraft's troubled crewed flight (2024) deepened reputational damage in space.

Business

Boeing's divisions span commercial airplanes, defence and space (Fighter jets, the KC-46 tanker, NASA's Space Launch System core stage) and global services. Headquarters moved from Seattle to Chicago (2001) and then Arlington, Virginia (2022), while manufacturing remains concentrated in Puget Sound, Charleston and St. Louis.
