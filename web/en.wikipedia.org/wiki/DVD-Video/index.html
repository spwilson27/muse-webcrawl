DVD-Video

DVD-Video is a consumer video format used to store digital video on DVD discs. It was the dominant home video format throughout the 2000s, standardised by the DVD Forum in 1996–1997. DVD-Video discs use MPEG-2 video compression (with MPEG-1 also permitted), supporting standard-definition resolution, and audio in formats including uncompressed PCM, Dolby Digital (AC-3) and DTS.

Features

The format supports multiple audio tracks and subtitle streams, chapter markers, parental controls, multiple camera angles and interactive menus. Discs store their content in a VIDEO_TS directory as VOB (video object) files accompanied by IFO navigation files. Maximum video bitrate is about 9.8 Mbit/s, with resolution up to 720×480 (NTSC) or 720×576 (PAL).

Restrictions

Commercial DVD-Video discs are usually protected by the Content Scramble System (CSS) and carry a regional code (regions 1–8) restricting playback to players sold in a given geographic area, a practice widely criticised by consumers. Analogue outputs could also be encumbered by Macrovision copy protection.
