Wisdom tooth

This article is about the third molars. For removal surgery, see Wisdom tooth removal.

Wisdom tooth
Other name: Third molar
Eruption: typically ages 17–25
Number: up to four
Identifiers: teeth 1, 16, 17, 32 (Universal numbering)

The wisdom teeth are the third molars — the last set of molars to develop, one in each corner of the mouth. Most adults have four, though some have fewer or none. They usually erupt between the ages of 17 and 25, the period traditionally associated with the onset of maturity and "wisdom", which gives them their common name.

Variation and evolution

Third molars vary greatly between populations: agenesis (never forming) ranges from almost none in some groups to over a third of individuals in others, and rates appear to be increasing. Evolutionary explanations note that softer modern diets produce smaller jaws, leaving less room for the last molars, while tougher ancestral diets wore teeth down and made space. Because of this mismatch, wisdom teeth frequently become impacted — trapped in the jawbone or gum — erupting only partly or at an angle.

Clinical significance

Impacted wisdom teeth can cause pericoronitis (infection of the overlying gum flap), tooth decay, periodontal disease, damage to the adjacent second molar, cysts and pain. Surgical removal is one of the most common operations performed on young adults, typically under local or general anaesthesia. Routine prophylactic removal of symptom-free teeth is debated: guidance in the United Kingdom (NICE) advises against it, while removal of potentially problematic teeth remains common practice in the United States.
