Golf course

A golf course is the ground on which the sport of golf is played. It consists of a series of holes, each with a teeing ground, fairway, rough, and other hazards, and a green with a flagstick and hole (cup). A standard round of golf involves playing 18 holes, though many courses have 9 holes and some fewer or more.

Design and elements

Each hole is classified by its par — the expected number of strokes — typically par 3, 4, or 5. The fairway is closely mown grass leading to the putting green; hazards include sand bunkers and water. Course architecture balances strategy, aesthetics, and the natural landscape. Styles include links courses on coastal sandy land (the oldest form, as at St Andrews in Scotland), parkland courses, heathland, and desert courses.

Course rating and maintenance

Courses are rated for difficulty by course rating and slope rating used in handicapping. Maintenance requires intensive mowing, irrigation, and turf management, and has drawn criticism over water use and pesticide application, prompting efforts toward sustainability and reduced-input turf.

History

Golf originated on the links land of eastern Scotland; the Old Course at St Andrews is often called the "home of golf." From the nineteenth century, course design became a profession, with architects such as Old Tom Morris, Alister MacKenzie, Donald Ross, and A. W. Tillinghast shaping classic courses, and modern designers continuing the tradition worldwide.
