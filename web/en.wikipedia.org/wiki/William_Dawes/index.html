William Dawes

BornApril 6, 1745, Boston, Massachusetts Bay
DiedFebruary 25, 1799 (aged 53), Marlborough, Massachusetts
OccupationTanner, militia officer
Known forMidnight ride of April 18–19, 1775

William Dawes Jr. (April 6, 1745 – February 25, 1799) was an American tanner and militia officer who, on the night of April 18–19, 1775, rode from Boston to warn the countryside that British troops were marching to seize military stores at Concord. His ride took place alongside the more famous ride of Paul Revere.

The midnight ride

Dawes was selected by Dr. Joseph Warren to carry the alarm by the land route, crossing Boston Neck, while Revere rowed across the Charles River. Dawes slipped past the British sentry at the Neck and rode through Roxbury, Brookline, Brighton, Cambridge and Menotomy, warning households along the way. He reached Lexington and met Revere at the Hancock-Clarke House around midnight. The two then rode on toward Concord together, joined by Dr. Samuel Prescott. A British patrol captured Revere; Dawes escaped but was thrown from his horse and walked back to Lexington. It was Prescott who completed the ride to Concord.

Later life

Dawes served as a quartermaster in the Continental Army during the Revolutionary War. After the war he returned to his tanning trade. Long overshadowed by Longfellow's poem about Revere, Dawes has been recovered by modern historians as an equally daring rider whose less celebrated name simply lacked a poet.
