Macy's (originally R. H. Macy & Co.) is an American department store chain founded in 1858 by Rowland Hussey Macy. It is the flagship brand of Macy's, Inc., alongside Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury. Its flagship store at Herald Square in New York City, one of the largest department stores in the world, has been a landmark since 1902. Macy's is known for its red star logo and for sponsoring the annual Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, first held in 1924, and the Macy's 4th of July Fireworks.

History

Rowland Hussey Macy, a former Nantucket whaler turned dry-goods merchant, opened R. H. Macy & Co. on Sixth Avenue at 14th Street in Manhattan on October 28, 1858. The store was an early adopter of innovations including one-price cash-only sales, money-back guarantees, and colourful advertising. After Macy's death in 1877 the company continued under partners, and in 1895 it was acquired by brothers Isidor and Nathan Straus, who had earlier run the store's china department. The Strauses moved the store uptown to Herald Square in 1902; Isidor Straus and his wife Ida later died aboard the RMS Titanic in 1912.

Through the twentieth century Macy's expanded nationally, absorbing regional chains including Bamberger's, Davison's, and O'Connor, Moffat & Co. in San Francisco. In 1994 Macy's itself was acquired by Federated Department Stores, which in 2005 bought the rival May Department Stores Company and in 2006 converted most of May's regional nameplates — such as Marshall Field's in Chicago — to the Macy's banner, making it a nationwide chain. Federated renamed itself Macy's, Inc. in 2007. Like other department store chains, Macy's has since closed many stores amid the shift to online shopping.

Parade and cultural presence

The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, famous for its giant character balloons (introduced in 1927), travels through Manhattan each Thanksgiving morning and is broadcast nationally. Macy's Herald Square also figures centrally in the 1947 film Miracle on 34th Street.
