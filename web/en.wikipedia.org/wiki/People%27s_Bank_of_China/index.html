People's Bank of China

The People's Bank of China (PBC or PBOC) is the central bank of the People's Republic of China, responsible for carrying out monetary policy and regulating financial institutions in mainland China. Established on 1 December 1948, it is headquartered in Beijing and has held the largest stock of foreign exchange reserves of any central bank. Since 2023 its governor has been Pan Gongsheng.

History

The bank was formed in 1948 from the consolidation of banks in Communist-held areas and served as both central and commercial bank until reforms of the 1980s separated its functions, creating state commercial banks. The 1995 Law on the People's Bank of China formalised its status as central bank, operating under the direction of the State Council.

Functions

The PBC sets benchmark interest rates and reserve requirements, manages the exchange rate of the renminbi, issues currency, holds and manages state foreign-exchange and gold reserves through SAFE, and operates key payment systems. It has also been a pioneer in developing a central bank digital currency, the e-CNY (digital yuan).

Monetary policy tools

Policy is implemented through instruments including open market operations, the medium-term lending facility (MLF), reserve requirement ratios and window guidance to banks.
