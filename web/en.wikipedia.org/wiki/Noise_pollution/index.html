Noise pollution

Noise pollution, or sound pollution, is the propagation of noise or sound with potentially harmful impacts on the activity of human or animal life. Poorly planned urbanization, transportation systems, and industrial activity are the main sources. Outdoor noise worldwide comes mainly from machines, transport (especially motor vehicles, aircraft, and trains), and propagation systems.

Sources

Road traffic is the most widespread source of environmental noise, followed by air and rail traffic, industry, construction, and neighbourhood sources such as amplified music. In oceans, shipping and sonar contribute anthropogenic noise that interferes with marine animals that rely on sound for communication and navigation.

Health effects

Chronic exposure to high noise levels is associated with hearing loss, tinnitus, sleep disturbance, cardiovascular disease, elevated stress hormones, and impaired cognitive development in children. The World Health Organization has estimated that at least one million healthy life years are lost annually in western Europe alone from traffic-related noise.

Regulation

Governments regulate noise through zoning, building codes, vehicle and aircraft standards, and local noise ordinances. The United States Noise Control Act of 1972 established a national policy, though federal funding for the EPA's Office of Noise Abatement and Control was withdrawn in the early 1980s, leaving most regulation to state and local governments. The European Union's Environmental Noise Directive requires member states to produce strategic noise maps for major cities and transport corridors.
