The sabre is one of the three weapons used in the sport of fencing, alongside the foil and the épée. Descended from the cavalry sabre, it is a light cutting and thrusting weapon: hits may be scored with the edge of the blade as well as the point, and the valid target area is everything above the waist, including the head, arms and hands.

A sabre bout: the fastest of the three fencing weapons.

Rules

Sabre is governed by right-of-way (priority) rules similar to foil: when both fencers hit simultaneously, the point goes to the fencer who established priority, usually by starting the attack correctly. Bouts are fenced to 15 touches in direct elimination. Because cuts register with any part of the blade's edge, and the target includes the head, sabre is considered the fastest and most aggressive of the weapons, with exchanges often decided in fractions of a second.

History

Modern sabre fencing developed in the 19th century, principally in Hungary and Italy, from military sabre training. It was included in the first modern Olympic Games in 1896. Hungarian fencers dominated the event for decades; the women's individual sabre was added to the Olympics in 2004 and the team event in 2008.

Technique

Sabre technique emphasises explosive footwork — including the flèche and, since a rule change in the 2000s, the flunge — and cuts delivered with the fingers and forearm. Defensive actions include parries protecting the head and flanks, and counter-time actions exploiting the opponent's attack.
