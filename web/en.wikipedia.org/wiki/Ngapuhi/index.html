Ngāpuhi

Ngāpuhi is a Māori iwi (tribe) associated with the Northland region (Te Tai Tokerau) of New Zealand's North Island, centred on the Hokianga Harbour, the Bay of Islands, and Whangārei. It is the largest iwi by population, with well over 100,000 people affiliated in recent censuses.

History

Ngāpuhi trace descent from the ancestors Rahiri and others who settled the north. In the early 19th century, chiefs such as Hongi Hika acquired muskets and led campaigns in the Musket Wars. Missionaries established stations in Ngāpuhi territory, and the chiefs were prominent among the signatories of the Declaration of Independence (1835) and the Treaty of Waitangi (1840), first signed at Waitangi in the Bay of Islands. Disputes over the treaty's meaning and settler encroachment led to the Northern War (1845–46), involving Hōne Heke and Kawiti.

Organisation and claims

Ngāpuhi comprises numerous hapū, whose autonomy has complicated Treaty settlement negotiations with the Crown. Te Rūnanga-Ā-Iwi O Ngāpuhi is a mandated tribal authority, while the Tūhoronuku mandate proved controversial among hapū. The Waitangi Tribunal's Te Paparahi o Te Raki inquiry found that Ngāpuhi rangatira did not cede sovereignty in 1840.
