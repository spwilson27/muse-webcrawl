Unam sanctam

Unam sanctam is a papal bull issued by Pope Boniface VIII on 18 November 1302. It is one of the most extreme statements of papal supremacy in the Middle Ages, asserting the supremacy of the spiritual power over the temporal and concluding with the declaration that submission to the Roman Pontiff is necessary for salvation. It was issued during Boniface's conflict with King Philip IV of France.

Background

The bull grew out of a quarrel over Philip IV's taxation of the clergy, which Boniface had forbidden in Clericis laicos (1296), and escalated when Philip arrested the papal legate, Bishop Bernard Saisset, in 1301. Boniface responded with the bull Ausculta Fili, summoning the French bishops to Rome; Philip burned the bull and convened the Estates General against the pope. Unam sanctam was the pope's fullest answer.

Contents

The bull begins from the unity of the Church: "We are compelled in virtue of our faith to believe and maintain that there is only one holy Catholic Church, and that one is apostolic." Drawing on the allegory of the two swords of Luke 22:38, it argues that both the spiritual and the temporal sword are in the Church's power: the spiritual sword is wielded by the Church, the temporal sword for the Church, by kings under priestly direction. The temporal power is therefore subject to the spiritual, which may judge it if it errs, while the pope himself "is judged by no one". The bull closes with the famous definition: "Furthermore, we declare, we proclaim, we define that it is absolutely necessary for salvation that every human creature be subject to the Roman Pontiff."

Aftermath

Philip's ministers Guillaume de Nogaret and Sciarra Colonna answered with force: in September 1303 they seized Boniface at Anagni and briefly imprisoned him; he died weeks later. The papacy soon passed under French influence into the Avignon period. Later theologians debated the bull's authority and meaning; Dante echoed its language while placing Boniface among the simonists in the Inferno. The bull remains a key text in discussions of the doctrine of extra Ecclesiam nulla salus ("outside the Church there is no salvation").
