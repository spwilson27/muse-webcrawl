Leibniz formula for π

In mathematics, the Leibniz formula for π, named after Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, states that π/4 = 1 − 1/3 + 1/5 − 1/7 + 1/9 − ⋯, an alternating series. It is also called the Madhava–Leibniz series, as a special case of the Gregory series and of the Madhava series discovered earlier by the Indian mathematician Madhava of Sangamagrama and his school.

Convergence

The series converges extremely slowly: after 500,000 terms only five correct decimal places of π are obtained. Error bounds from the alternating series test show that about 10n terms are needed for n correct decimals, which makes the plain series useless for computation, although transformations such as Euler's series acceleration make it practical.

Derivation

The formula follows by integrating the geometric series for 1/(1 + x²) term by term to obtain the Taylor series of arctan x, and evaluating at x = 1, with some care needed to justify the endpoint value (for example via Abel's theorem).
