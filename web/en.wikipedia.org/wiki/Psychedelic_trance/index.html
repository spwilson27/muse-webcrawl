Psychedelic trance

Psychedelic trance
Also called: Psytrance, psy
Stylistic origins: Trance, acid house, Goa trance, techno
Cultural origins: Early 1990s, Goa (India), Israel, Europe
Typical tempo: 138–150 BPM

Psychedelic trance, or psytrance, is a subgenre of trance music characterised by fast tempos (usually 140–150 beats per minute), driving four-on-the-floor kick drums, rolling offbeat or triplet basslines, and layered, hypnotic synthesiser arpeggios and effects intended to create an intense, psychedelic dancefloor experience.

History

Psytrance developed directly from Goa trance, which emerged from the party scene of Goa, India, in the late 1980s and early 1990s, blending European electronic dance music with psychedelic rock and Indian musical and spiritual influences. As the sound commercialised and spread to Israel and Europe in the mid-1990s, the label "psychedelic trance" largely replaced "Goa trance", with artists such as Astral Projection, Hallucinogen, Infected Mushroom and Man With No Name defining the genre.

Styles and culture

Substyles include full-on, progressive psytrance, dark psytrance (darkpsy), forest, suomisaundi from Finland, psybreaks and psybient (a downtempo relative). Psytrance culture centres on dedicated festivals — among them Boom Festival in Portugal, Ozora in Hungary, and Universo Paralello in Brazil — featuring ultraviolet-reactive artwork, elaborate stage design, and an ethos influenced by psychedelic and neo-hippie spirituality.
