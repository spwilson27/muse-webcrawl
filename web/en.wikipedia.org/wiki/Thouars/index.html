Thouars (pronounced [twaʁ]) is a commune in the Deux-Sèvres department in western France, in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. It is situated on a cliff overlooking a meander of the River Thouet, a tributary of the Loire, and is dominated by its large château. On 1 January 2019, the neighbouring communes of Mauzé-Thouarsais, Missé and Sainte-Radegonde were merged into Thouars.

History

The site was occupied in Gallo-Roman times, and the town takes its name from a castle first attested in the early medieval period. The viscounts of Thouars were powerful lords in western France, frequently involved in the struggles between the kings of France and England; Geoffrey of Thouars fought alongside William the Conqueror at the Battle of Hastings in 1066. During the Hundred Years' War the town changed hands several times before passing definitively to France.

The present Château de Thouars was built in the 17th century for Marie de La Tour d'Auvergne, wife of Henri de La Trémoille, Duke of Thouars. With its long orange-tree-lined terrace overlooking the river, it is one of the great classical châteaux of the Loire region, sometimes compared to a smaller Versailles.

Main sights

Besides the château, sights include the church of Saint-Médard, with its distinctive twin towers; the church of Saint-Laon; the medieval bridge of Saint-André; and the old town's narrow streets. The town is also known for the Thouarsais vineyards and for melon production.

Transport

Thouars railway station has connections to Tours, Angers and La Roche-sur-Yon. Football club Thouars Foot 79 is based in the town.
