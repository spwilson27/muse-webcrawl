Cuboid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A cuboid is a hexahedron (a polyhedron with six faces) whose faces are all quadrilaterals. In the most common usage, a cuboid is a rectangular cuboid (or rectangular box): a convex polyhedron bounded by six rectangular faces, with all angles right angles and opposite faces congruent. A cube is the special case in which all three edge lengths are equal.

Rectangular cuboid

For a rectangular cuboid with length l, width w and height h:

  • Volume: V = lwh
  • Surface area: A = 2(lw + lh + wh)
  • Space diagonal: d = √(l2 + w2 + h2)

A rectangular cuboid has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices, and is also a right rectangular prism and a parallelepiped with rectangular faces. An Eulerian brick is a cuboid whose edges and face diagonals are all integers; a perfect cuboid would additionally have an integer space diagonal, and it remains an open problem whether any exist.

General cuboids

More generally, the term cuboid is sometimes used for a hexahedron with quadrilateral faces that need not be rectangles, topologically equivalent to a cube. In anatomy, the cuboid bone is one of the tarsal bones of the foot, so named for its roughly cube-like shape.
