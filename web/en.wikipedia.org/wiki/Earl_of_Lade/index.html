The Earls of Lade (Old Norse: Hlaðajarlar; Norwegian: Ladejarlene) were a dynasty of Norse jarls (earls) based at Lade, on the outskirts of modern Trondheim in Norway, who ruled the northern part of the country during the late Viking Age. For much of the period from the late 9th century to the early 11th century they were the dominant power in Norway, ruling sometimes independently and sometimes as allies, vassals or rivals of the kings of Denmark and the Fairhair kings of Norway.

History

According to the sagas, the earldom was established by Håkon Grjotgardsson, who allied with Harald Fairhair during the unification of Norway and received rule over Trøndelag. His son Sigurd Håkonsson continued the alliance but was killed by Harald Fairhair's sons.

The most famous holder was Sigurd's son, Håkon Sigurdsson (c. 937–995), who became the effective ruler of Norway after 970 with Danish support, governing as earl under the Danish king Harald Bluetooth. A staunch pagan, Håkon resisted the Christianisation pressed by Denmark, but his increasingly unpopular rule ended when he was killed by his own slave in 995, paving the way for Olaf Tryggvason.

Håkon's sons Eirik and Svein Håkonsson fought against Olaf Tryggvason at the Battle of Svolder (1000) and afterwards ruled Norway as earls under the Danish king Sweyn Forkbeard and Cnut the Great. Eirik later became Earl of Northumbria in England. The line lost power in Norway after the rise of Olaf II (St Olaf), and the last earl, Håkon Eiriksson, died in 1029/1030.

List of earls

  • Håkon Grjotgardsson (c. late 9th century)
  • Sigurd Håkonsson (died c. 962)
  • Håkon Sigurdsson (c. 970–995)
  • Eirik Håkonsson (1000–1012)
  • Svein Håkonsson (1000–1015)
  • Håkon Eiriksson (1015–1016, 1028–1029)
