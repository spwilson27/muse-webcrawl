Olympia (Manet)

ArtistÉdouard Manet
Year1863
MediumOil on canvas
LocationMusée d'Orsay, Paris

Olympia is a painting by Édouard Manet, first exhibited at the Paris Salon of 1865. It depicts a nude woman, a courtesan attended by a Black maid bearing flowers, reclining on a bed and staring directly at the viewer. The painting caused a scandal on its exhibition and is now regarded as a landmark of early modern art.

Subject and sources

The composition deliberately recalls Titian's Venus of Urbino and other Renaissance reclining nudes, but Manet replaced the idealized goddess with a contemporary Parisian sex worker, modelled by Victorine Meurent. Details such as the orchid in her hair, the black ribbon at her neck, and the black cat at the foot of the bed signal her profession.

Reception and legacy

Critics denounced the painting's flatness, harsh lighting, and perceived vulgarity, yet younger artists championed it. Olympia was purchased by public subscription, organized by Claude Monet, and donated to the French state in 1890; it now hangs in the Musée d'Orsay.
