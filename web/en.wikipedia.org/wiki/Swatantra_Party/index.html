The Swatantra Party was an Indian classical liberal political party that existed from 1959 to 1974. It was founded by C. Rajagopalachari in reaction to what he felt was the increasingly socialist and statist outlook of the Jawaharlal Nehru-led Indian National Congress. Prominent founding figures included K. M. Munshi, Minoo Masani and N. G. Ranga.

Ideology

The Swatantra Party favoured a market-based economy, opposed the Licence Raj of permits and quotas, defended private property, and demanded that the government adhere to the fundamental rights guaranteed in the Constitution of India. It was also socially conservative in other respects, supporting decentralization and the interests of landlords, princes and business. The word swatantra means "freedom" or "independence" in several Indian languages.

Electoral history

Swatantra won 18 seats in its first general election in 1962 and emerged as the single largest opposition party in the Lok Sabha in 1967 with 44 seats, also forming or joining governments in several states, including Rajasthan, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh and Odisha. The party declined after the 1971 election, in which it won only 8 seats, and in 1974 it merged into the Bharatiya Lok Dal led by Charan Singh. It remains India's most successful liberal and free-market party in electoral terms.
