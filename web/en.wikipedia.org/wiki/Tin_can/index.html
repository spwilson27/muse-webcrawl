Tin can

Tin can
Material: Tinplate steel (modern)
Use: Food and product packaging
Invented: early 19th century

A tin can (also called a tin, can, or canister) is a container for the distribution or storage of goods, composed of thin metal. Historically made from tinplate — steel coated with a thin layer of tin — most modern cans are made of aluminium or tin-free steel. Cans are widely used for preserving food and beverages because they are hermetic, durable and lightproof.

History

The tin can was patented in 1810 by British merchant Peter Durand, building on preservation experiments by Nicolas Appert in France. Early cans were laboriously hand-made and required a hammer and chisel to open; the first dedicated can openers appeared decades later. Mass production in the late 19th and 20th centuries made canned food a staple worldwide.

Manufacture and recycling

Modern food cans are usually drawn or welded cylinders with a double-seamed lid. Steel and aluminium cans are among the most recycled packaging materials, since metals can be remelted repeatedly without loss of quality.
