The March First Movement (Korean: 삼일운동, "Three-One Movement"), also known as the Manse Movement, was a series of protests against Japanese colonial rule of Korea that began on 1 March 1919 in Seoul and spread across the country. It was one of the earliest and largest public displays of Korean resistance, inspired in part by US President Woodrow Wilson's principle of self-determination.

Demonstrators in Seoul, March 1919, shouting "Manse!" (long live Korea)

Background

Korea had been annexed by Japan in 1910. Harsh military rule, land seizures and the sudden death in January 1919 of the former emperor Gojong — amid rumours of poisoning — inflamed public feeling. Thirty-three religious and cultural leaders signed a Korean Declaration of Independence.

The protests

On 1 March 1919 the signatories read the declaration in Seoul while student demonstrators proclaimed it at Pagoda Park, sparking mass marches. Over the following weeks protests spread to nearly every county; around two million Koreans are estimated to have taken part in more than 1,500 demonstrations. The movement was deliberately non-violent.

Suppression and aftermath

Japanese authorities suppressed the protests with considerable violence; Korean figures commonly cited record thousands killed and tens of thousands arrested. Among the remembered victims is Yu Gwan-sun, a teenage organiser who died in prison. Although independence was not achieved, the movement led Japan to replace military rule with a somewhat looser "cultural rule", and it galvanised the independence movement, including the Provisional Government of the Republic of Korea formed in Shanghai. March 1 is a national holiday in South Korea.
