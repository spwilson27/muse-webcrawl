Wallis Simpson

Wallis, Duchess of Windsor (born Bessie Wallis Warfield; 19 June 1896 – 24 April 1986), known as Wallis Simpson, was an American socialite whose intended marriage to the British king Edward VIII led to his abdication in 1936 — a constitutional crisis that reshaped the British monarchy. Time magazine named her its first Woman of the Year in 1936.

Early life and marriages

Born in Pennsylvania and raised in Baltimore after her father's early death, Wallis married naval pilot Earl Winfield Spencer Jr. in 1916; the marriage was unhappy and ended in divorce in 1927. She then married Ernest Simpson, an Anglo-American shipping executive, and settled in London, where she entered the social circle of Edward, Prince of Wales, becoming his mistress by 1934.

Abdication crisis

Edward became king in January 1936. His wish to marry Wallis, who was divorcing Simpson, collided with his role as head of the Church of England, which opposed remarriage after divorce. Prime Minister Stanley Baldwin informed him that the marriage was unacceptable to the government and Dominions. On 11 December 1936 Edward abdicated — the only British monarch to do so voluntarily — declaring he could not reign "without the help and support of the woman I love".

Later life

The couple married in France in June 1937; Edward became Duke of Windsor, but Wallis was denied the style "Royal Highness", a lasting grievance. Suspicions of Nazi sympathies dogged them — they visited Hitler in 1937 — and Edward was posted as Governor of the Bahamas during the war. Afterwards they lived in Paris as international society figures. Wallis outlived the Duke (d. 1972) by fourteen years and was buried beside him at Frogmore.
