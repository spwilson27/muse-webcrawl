West Yorkshire Combined Authority

The West Yorkshire Combined Authority is the combined authority for West Yorkshire in England, covering the five metropolitan districts of Leeds, Wakefield, Kirklees, Calderdale and Bradford. It was established on 1 April 2014 and is responsible for transport, economic development and regeneration across the Leeds City Region.

History

The authority succeeded earlier city-region bodies and took over the functions of the West Yorkshire Integrated Transport Authority. In March 2020 the West Yorkshire devolution deal was announced, transferring further powers and funding from central government. From May 2021 the authority has been led by a directly elected Mayor of West Yorkshire; the first mayor, Tracy Brabin, also assumed the functions of the West Yorkshire Police and Crime Commissioner.

Functions

The Combined Authority oversees public transport through the Metro brand, including bus franchising reforms, rail planning and cycling investment, and delivers skills, business support and housing programmes. It works alongside the Leeds City Region Enterprise Partnership.
