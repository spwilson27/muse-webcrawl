Lin Huiyin (Chinese: 林徽因; 10 June 1904 – 1 April 1955), also styled Phyllis Lin, was a Chinese architect, architectural historian, writer and poet. She is regarded as the first female architect in modern China, and with her husband Liang Sicheng she documented and worked to preserve China's ancient architecture.

Life and work

Born in Hangzhou and educated in Europe, Lin travelled with her father Lin Changmin and met the poet Xu Zhimo in London. Barred as a woman from the University of Pennsylvania's architecture degree program, she studied there in fine arts while taking architecture courses, then worked at Yale. In 1928 she married Liang Sicheng.

Through the 1930s the couple surveyed hundreds of historic sites across China, including in 1937 the Tang-dynasty Foguang Temple — then the oldest known timber building in China. Lin contributed to the design of the national emblem of the People's Republic of China and the Monument to the People's Heroes in Tiananmen Square, and campaigned, unsuccessfully, to preserve Beijing's city walls. She also wrote poetry, essays, fiction and drama. She died of tuberculosis in Beijing in 1955.

Legacy

Lin is remembered both for scholarship in Chinese architectural history and as a leading literary figure of the Crescent Moon society circle. Her Beijing residence with Liang is preserved as a memorial site.
