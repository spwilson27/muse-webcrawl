Selby

Selby is a market town and civil parish in North Yorkshire, England, situated on the River Ouse about 21 kilometres (13 miles) south of York. Historically part of the West Riding of Yorkshire, it had a population of around 19,000 at the 2021 census. The town is dominated by Selby Abbey, one of the largest parish churches in England.

History

Selby is traditionally the birthplace of Henry I of England in 1068 or 1069, the only English-born Norman king. Selby Abbey was founded in 1069 by Benedict of Auxerre under the patronage of William the Conqueror, and the town grew around the abbey's trade on the River Ouse, which was navigable to the sea. The abbey church survived the Dissolution of the Monasteries to become the parish church.

Geography and economy

The town stands on low-lying land near the confluence of the Ouse and the Selby Canal, which links it to the Aire and Calder Navigation. In the late twentieth century the surrounding Selby Coalfield was a major employer; its last pits closed in 2004. The nearby Drax power station remains a prominent feature of the local economy and landscape. Selby has a railway station on lines to York, Leeds, and Hull, and a swing bridge carries the A19 over the Ouse.

Landmarks

Selby Abbey is noted for its Gothic architecture, its Washington window bearing the arms of the Washington family, and a major restoration after a fire in 1906. The town also preserves a market cross and a number of Georgian buildings along the riverfront.
