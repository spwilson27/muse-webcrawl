The Köprülü family was a dynasty of grand viziers and statesmen of the Ottoman Empire, originating in the 17th century, whose members dominated the empire's government during the period often called the Köprülü era (1656–1683 and into the early 18th century).

Rise

The founder, Köprülü Mehmed Pasha, an Albanian-born official, became Grand Vizier in 1656 at an advanced age, accepting the office only with extraordinary powers. He ruthlessly restored state finances and discipline after a period of crisis. His son Köprülü Fazıl Ahmed Pasha (Grand Vizier 1661–1676) conquered Crete (1669) and parts of Ukraine, and patronised scholarship.

Later members

Their protégé Kara Mustafa Pasha led the failed siege of Vienna in 1683 and was executed. Köprülü Fazıl Mustafa Pasha (1689–1691) attempted reforms and died at the Battle of Slankamen. Amcazade Köprülü Hüseyin Pasha (1697–1702) negotiated the Treaty of Karlowitz. The family also produced provincial governors, scholars and the major library endowment of the Köprülü Library in Istanbul.
