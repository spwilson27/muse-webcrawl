Thiên Mụ Pagoda

Thiên Mụ Pagoda
Vietnamese: Chùa Thiên Mụ ("Pagoda of the Heavenly Lady")
Location: Hà Khê hill, Huế, Vietnam, overlooking the Perfume River
Founded: 1601, under Lord Nguyễn Hoàng
Landmark: Phước Duyên tower — seven storeys, 21 m, built 1844
Status: one of the oldest and most iconic religious sites in Vietnam

The Thiên Mụ Pagoda (Vietnamese: Chùa Thiên Mụ, "Pagoda of the Celestial Lady") is a historic Buddhist temple in the city of Huế in central Vietnam. Standing on Hà Khê hill on the northern bank of the Perfume River, about five kilometres from the Imperial City, it is one of the oldest pagodas in Vietnam and an enduring symbol of Huế and of the country itself.

Legend and founding

According to legend, an old woman — the "heavenly lady" — appeared on the hill dressed in red and blue, prophesied that a lord would come and build a pagoda on the site, and vanished into the clouds. When the Nguyễn lord Nguyễn Hoàng arrived in the region in 1601 and heard the story, he ordered a temple built there and named it after the apparition. The pagoda's name preserves the prophecy: Thiên Mụ means "Heavenly Lady".

Buildings

The site was repeatedly enlarged and rebuilt by successive Nguyễn lords and emperors. Its best-known structure is the Phước Duyên tower, an octagonal seven-storey brick tower 21 metres high, built in 1844 under Emperor Thiệu Trị; each storey holds a statue of the Buddha, and a golden Buddha image crowns the top. Behind it stand the main sanctuary housing a large statue of Maitreya, flanked by further shrines, stelae pavilions, and the monks' quarters, set among pine trees and gardens.

The grounds preserve notable treasures: a great bell cast in 1710 weighing over 2,000 kilograms, whose sound is said to carry ten kilometres, and a stone stele of 1715 recording the pagoda's history. A large marble turtle carries another stele near the entrance.

Modern history

The pagoda was a centre of Buddhist protest during the crisis of 1963 under the regime of Ngô Đình Diệm. The Austin car in which the monk Thích Quảng Đức travelled to Saigon, where he immolated himself in protest, is kept at the pagoda and has become a powerful memorial. Thiên Mụ remains an active monastery and one of Vietnam's most visited heritage sites.
