Royal and noble styles

SubjectForms of address and precedence
ExamplesMajesty, Royal Highness, Serene Highness, Excellency, Grace

Royal and noble styles are the forms of address and reference accorded to monarchs, members of royal families, nobles and certain office-holders. Styles mark rank within a hierarchy of precedence and traditionally derive from the grant or recognition of the sovereign.

Principal styles

At the summit stands Majesty, used by kings, queens, emperors and empresses (as Imperial Majesty). Below it come Royal Highness for princes and princesses, Imperial Highness for members of imperial houses, and Serene Highness, historically used by mediatised German princely houses and by the princes of Monaco and Liechtenstein. Grand Ducal Highness and plain Highness occupy intermediate ranks, while Excellency is accorded to ambassadors, governors and some senior officials. In Britain, dukes are addressed as "Your Grace", and the style Grace was historically used for kings before "Majesty" became standard.

Development

The elaborate ladder of styles largely crystallised in the courts of early modern Europe, where disputes over precedence between ambassadors were a serious matter of state. Many republics have abolished official styles, though some retain "Excellency" for heads of state and government.
