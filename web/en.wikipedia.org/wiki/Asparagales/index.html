Asparagales

Scientific name: Asparagales (Link)

Rank: Order

Group: Monocots

Families: 14 (APG IV)

Asparagales is an order of flowering plants in the monocots, named for the genus Asparagus. As circumscribed by the APG IV system (2016) it is the largest order of monocots, containing about 14 families, some 1,122 genera and roughly 36,000 species — including the orchids, whose family Orchidaceae is among the largest of all plant families.

Families

Families recognised in APG IV include Orchidaceae (orchids), Asparagaceae (asparagus, agave, hosta, hyacinth), Amaryllidaceae (daffodils, onions, garlic — including the subfamily Asphodelaceae-relative Allioideae), Iridaceae (iris, crocus, gladiolus), Asphodelaceae (aloes, daylilies), Xanthorrhoeaceae relatives now subsumed, Hypoxidaceae, Tecophilaeaceae and others.

Characteristics

Most Asparagales are herbaceous perennials with parallel-veined leaves; many form bulbs, corms or rhizomes. Diagnostic features include seeds with a black phytomelan crust, inferior ovaries in many families, and nectaries in the septa of the ovary. The order is overwhelmingly non-mycoheterotrophic except for some orchids.

Economic importance

The order supplies major foods (onion, garlic, asparagus), flavourings (vanilla, saffron), fibres (agave/sisal), medicinal plants (aloe), and a vast ornamental trade in orchids, irises, daffodils and hyacinths.
