Büyük Menderes River

The Büyük Menderes River (Turkish: Büyük Menderes Nehri, "Great Meander"), historically known as the Maeander or Meander, is a river in southwestern Turkey. It is famous for its winding course, from which the English word "meander" derives.

Course

The river rises near Dinar in Afyonkarahisar Province and flows westward through the provinces of Uşak, Denizli, and Aydın, passing the ancient cities of the Maeander valley, including Laodicea and Magnesia ad Maeandrum. It empties into the Aegean Sea near the ancient port of Miletus. Over antiquity its silt progressively filled the Latmian Gulf, turning the island of Lade into part of the mainland and leaving Miletus inland.

History and name

The Maeander was celebrated in Greek literature — Homer mentions it in the Iliad — and its serpentine course gave rise to the Maeander motif in classical art and to the modern geographical term for a winding river bend. The valley was a principal route between the Aegean coast and the Anatolian interior and remains an important agricultural region, producing figs, olives, and cotton; it is also exploited for geothermal energy around Denizli.
