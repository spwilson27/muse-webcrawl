The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring is a 2001 epic fantasy adventure film directed by Peter Jackson. It is the first instalment in The Lord of the Rings trilogy, based on the 1954 novel The Fellowship of the Ring, the first volume of J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings. The film was produced by New Line Cinema and WingNut Films.

Set in Middle-earth, the story tells of the Dark Lord Sauron, who seeks the One Ring, which contains much of his power. The Ring has fallen into the hands of the Hobbit Frodo Baggins. From his home in the Shire, Frodo and eight companions set out on a journey to Mount Doom, the only place where the Ring can be destroyed.

Plot

In the Second Age, the Dark Lord Sauron forges the One Ring to control the other Rings of Power and conquer Middle-earth. After Sauron's defeat, the Ring passes through several hands before coming to the Hobbit Bilbo Baggins of the Shire. Decades later, the wizard Gandalf the Grey identifies Bilbo's ring as the One Ring and tells Bilbo's heir, Frodo, that it must be destroyed in the fires of Mount Doom in Mordor, where it was forged.

Frodo sets out with his gardener Samwise Gamgee and cousins Merry and Pippin. Pursued by the Nazgûl, Sauron's ringwraiths, they reach Bree, where they meet the ranger Aragorn, who guides them to Rivendell. Frodo is wounded by a Morgul blade but recovers under the care of Elrond. At the Council of Elrond, a Fellowship of nine is formed: Frodo, Sam, Merry, Pippin, Gandalf, Aragorn, the Elf Legolas, the Dwarf Gimli, and Boromir of Gondor.

The Fellowship travels south, crosses the Misty Mountains through the Mines of Moria, where Gandalf falls battling a Balrog, and continues to Lothlórien, where the Elf-queen Galadriel gives them gifts. Travelling down the river Anduin, they are attacked by Uruk-hai sent by the corrupted wizard Saruman. Boromir is killed defending Merry and Pippin, who are captured. Frodo decides to continue to Mordor alone, but Sam insists on accompanying him, and the Fellowship is broken.

Production

Jackson began working on the trilogy in 1995, and the three films were shot simultaneously in New Zealand over roughly fifteen months of principal photography, with extensive use of miniatures, prosthetics, and computer-generated imagery developed by Weta Workshop and Weta Digital. An extended edition was released on DVD in 2002, adding about thirty minutes of footage.

Reception

The film was a major commercial and critical success. It grossed over $897 million worldwide and was nominated for thirteen Academy Awards, winning four: Best Cinematography, Best Makeup, Best Original Score, and Best Visual Effects. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential fantasy films ever made, and is credited with helping to popularise the fantasy genre in cinema. It was followed by The Two Towers (2002) and The Return of the King (2003).
