Conglomerate

Conglomerate may refer to several topics; this article covers both the geological and corporate senses in summary form.

[Image placeholder: conglomerate rock with rounded clasts]

Geology

In geology, a conglomerate is a coarse-grained sedimentary rock composed of rounded fragments (clasts) larger than 2 mm — pebbles, cobbles or boulders — set in a finer-grained matrix of sand, silt or natural cement such as silica, calcite or iron oxide. The rounding of the clasts distinguishes conglomerate from breccia, whose fragments are angular, indicating that conglomerate clasts were transported some distance by water or ice before deposition. Conglomerates form in high-energy environments such as river channels, alluvial fans and beaches, and their composition records the geology of the source terrain.

Business

In business, a conglomerate is a corporation made up of several different, sometimes unrelated, businesses operating in separate industries under one corporate group. Conglomerates grew rapidly in the 1960s through acquisition, and prominent examples include Berkshire Hathaway, General Electric (historically), and large Asian groups such as chaebol and keiretsu. Diversification can reduce risk and allow internal capital allocation, but conglomerates are often traded at a conglomerate discount because investors prefer focused firms whose performance is easier to evaluate.
