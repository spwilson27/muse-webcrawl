Indo-Guyanese

Indo-Guyanese or Indian-Guyanese are Guyanese nationals of Indian ancestry, the descendants of indentured labourers brought from India by the British to work on sugar plantations in British Guiana after the abolition of slavery. They form the largest ethnic group in Guyana, comprising roughly two-fifths of the population.

History

The first ship, the Hesperus, arrived in 1838 carrying Indian labourers; between then and the end of indenture in 1917–1920, roughly 240,000 Indians came to British Guiana. Most came from the Bhojpur region of present-day Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, with a significant minority from South India. Many remained after their contracts ended, becoming rice farmers, shopkeepers and professionals.

Culture

Indo-Guyanese culture blends Indian traditions with Caribbean influences. Hinduism is the largest religion, followed by Islam and Christianity. Festivals such as Diwali, Phagwah (Holi) and Eid are widely celebrated, and chutney music, tassa drumming and dishes such as roti and curry are integral to Guyanese national culture. Indo-Guyanese have played a central role in the country's politics.
