Underworld are a British electronic music group formed in Cardiff, Wales, in 1980 by Karl Hyde and Rick Smith. Initially a funk and synth-pop band, they reinvented themselves in the early 1990s as a techno and progressive house act, achieving international fame with the single "Born Slippy .NUXX", featured in the 1996 film Trainspotting. DJ Darren Emerson was a member from 1991 to 2000, during which the group released its best-known albums as a trio.

History

Hyde and Smith first worked together in the band Freur, which had a minor hit with "Doot-Doot" (1983). As Underworld they released two synth-pop albums, Underneath the Radar (1988) and Change the Weather (1989), before disbanding. After meeting Emerson, a young DJ, the reformed group developed a new sound mixing techno rhythms with Hyde's stream-of-consciousness lyrics, gathered from overheard conversations and his notebooks.

Breakthrough

The album dubnobasswithmyheadman (1994) was a critical success, and Second Toughest in the Infants (1996) and Beaucoup Fish (1999) consolidated their reputation as one of the leading live electronic acts. "Born Slippy .NUXX" reached number two in the UK charts. After Emerson's departure the duo continued with albums including A Hundred Days Off (2002), Barking (2010) and Barbara Barbara, We Face a Shining Future (2016). Smith was musical director for the opening ceremony of the 2012 London Olympic Games, directed by Danny Boyle, at which Underworld performed.
