Movses Khorenatsi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Movses Khorenatsi, revered as the father of Armenian historiography.

Movses Khorenatsi (Armenian: Մովսէս Խորենացի, Movsēs Khorenatsi; c. 410 – 490s AD) was a prominent Armenian historian from late antiquity and the author of the History of Armenia (Patmutiun Hayots), the earliest known work of Armenian historiography. He is credited with the earliest full-length account of Armenia from its origins up to his own time, and is traditionally honoured as the "father of Armenian history" (patmahayr).

Life

According to the account in his own work and in Armenian tradition, Khorenatsi was born in the village of Khoren (Khoronk) in the Taron region of Greater Armenia. He was a student of Mesrop Mashtots, the inventor of the Armenian alphabet, and of the Catholicos Sahak Partev. Sent abroad to complete his education, he studied in Edessa, Antioch, Jerusalem, and especially Alexandria, where he absorbed the learning of the Greek schools, before returning to Armenia around 440 AD. He later became a bishop and lived to an advanced age, dying in the 490s.

History of Armenia

The History of Armenia, written at the request of Prince Sahak Bagratuni, is divided into three books. The first traces the legendary origins of the Armenian people from Haik, the eponymous ancestor, through the early kings; the second covers the Arsacid period down to the death of Tiridates III; and the third brings the narrative to the fall of the Arsacid dynasty and the author's own era, ending with a lament on the misfortunes of Armenia. The work blends biblical genealogy, oral epic tradition, and classical learning, and preserves otherwise lost fragments of Armenian legend and song.

Dating controversy

Although tradition places Khorenatsi in the 5th century, some modern scholars have argued that the History in its surviving form contains later interpolations or was composed or revised as late as the 7th–9th centuries, citing apparent anachronisms in geography, vocabulary, and its references to sources. Others defend the traditional dating, attributing the disputed passages to later copying. The question remains one of the central debates in Armenian studies.

Legacy

Khorenatsi's History shaped Armenian national memory for more than a millennium and remains a foundational text. Streets, schools, and a state medal of the Republic of Armenia bear his name, and he is depicted on Armenian currency and commemorated as a saint by the Armenian Apostolic Church.
