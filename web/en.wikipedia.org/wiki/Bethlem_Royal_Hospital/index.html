Bethlem Royal Hospital, also known as St Mary Bethlehem, Bethlehem Hospital and Bedlam, is a psychiatric hospital in London. Founded in 1247, it is one of the oldest institutions in the world specialising in the treatment of mental illness, and its name became a byword — "bedlam" — for chaos and madness.

History

The institution began as the Priory of St Mary of Bethlehem in Bishopsgate, founded in 1247. By the early 15th century it was caring for mentally ill patients. In 1676 it moved to a grand new building at Moorfields designed by Robert Hooke, where visiting the patients became a popular public spectacle. In 1815 it moved again to Lambeth (the building whose dome now houses the Imperial War Museum), and in 1930 to its present site at Monks Orchard in Beckenham.

Historically, treatment at Bethlem reflected the harsh practices of its eras, though it also produced reforming physicians and superintendents. Today Bethlem is a modern mental health hospital forming part of the South London and Maudsley NHS Foundation Trust, providing specialist services including forensic psychiatry and national units for conditions such as obsessive–compulsive disorder. Its archives and the Bethlem Museum of the Mind document the long history of mental health care.
