Westmorland

Westmorland (spelled Westmoreland in some old sources) is a historic county in North West England. It formed an administrative county from 1889 to 1974, after which the whole county was merged into the new county of Cumbria. In April 2023 the name was revived in the unitary authority of Westmorland and Furness.

Geography

The county town was Appleby, though Kendal was the largest town and the administrative centre in later years. Westmorland included the eastern part of the Lake District, including most of Windermere's eastern shore, Helvellyn, and the upper Eden Valley, bordered by Cumberland to the north, Lancashire to the south, and Yorkshire to the east.

History

Recorded in the Domesday Book as part of Yorkshire and Lancashire holdings, Westmorland emerged as a county by the 13th century, taking its name from the "land of the people west of the moors" (the Pennines). It was historically divided into the baronies of Kendal and Westmorland (Appleby). The Clifford family, earls of Cumberland, were long dominant in the north of the county.
