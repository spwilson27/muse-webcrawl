American Beauty

American Beauty is a 1999 American black comedy-drama film written by Alan Ball and directed by Sam Mendes in his feature directorial debut. Kevin Spacey stars as Lester Burnham, a suburban advertising executive who, having a midlife crisis after becoming infatuated with his teenage daughter's best friend, decides to reinvent his life.

Plot

Lester Burnham narrates his life from beyond the grave: trapped in a loveless marriage with real-estate agent Carolyn and a strained relationship with his daughter Jane, he quits his job, starts working at a fast-food restaurant, buys the car of his dreams, and begins exercising after fantasising about Jane's friend Angela. Meanwhile Carolyn has an affair, Jane falls for the voyeuristic neighbour Ricky Fitts, and Ricky's repressive father Frank becomes convinced Lester is sexually involved with his son, with tragic consequences on the night Lester dies.

Reception and awards

Produced by DreamWorks, the film grossed over $356 million worldwide against a $15 million budget. It won five Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director for Mendes, Best Actor for Spacey, Best Original Screenplay, and Best Cinematography. Critics praised its satire of suburban American life and notions of beauty and fulfilment, though some later reassessments have been less favourable.

Themes

The film explores beauty, materialism, conformity, freedom, and redemption, symbolised by recurring images of rose petals and a plastic bag dancing in the wind, which Ricky films as "the most beautiful thing" he has ever captured. The title also refers to the American Beauty rose variety.
