High IQ society


IQ distribution and society cut-offs

A high IQ society is an organization that limits membership to people who have attained a specified score on an IQ test, or another standardized test of intelligence, usually in the top two percent of the population. The oldest and best-known such society is Mensa International, founded in England in 1946 by Roland Berrill and Lancelot Ware; membership requires a score at or above the 98th percentile on an approved intelligence test.

Other societies

More exclusive societies followed. The Triple Nine Society (founded 1978) requires the 99.9th percentile (about one in a thousand). The Prometheus Society (1982) and the Mega Society (1982) set still higher nominal thresholds, though the validity of tests at such extreme ranges is debated among psychometricians. Intertel, founded in 1966, admits the top one percent. Many of these societies publish journals, hold gatherings, and maintain special-interest groups.

Criticism

Critics question the reliability and cultural fairness of IQ testing at extreme score ranges, and note that membership criteria vary between tests and eras as norms are re-centered (the Flynn effect). Supporters view the societies chiefly as social and intellectual clubs for people with a shared statistical trait.
