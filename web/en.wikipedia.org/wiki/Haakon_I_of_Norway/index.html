Haakon I of Norway

Haakon Haraldsson (c. 920 – 961), known as Haakon the Good (Old Norse: Hákon góði), was Norway's third king, reigning from 934 to 961. He was the youngest son of Harald Fairhair and was fostered at the court of King Æthelstan of England, where he was raised as a Christian.

Reign

With English support Haakon returned to Norway and displaced his half-brother Eric Bloodaxe, who fled the country. Remembered for just rule and legal reforms, he is credited with establishing the Gulating and Frostating law assemblies in a more organised form and with creating the leiðangr naval levy system for coastal defence. His attempts to introduce Christianity met resistance from pagan chieftains, and he was obliged to compromise with the old religion.

Death

Haakon was mortally wounded at the Battle of Fitjar (c. 961) while defeating the sons of Eric Bloodaxe, and died of his wounds. He was succeeded by Harald Greycloak. According to the sagas, the skald Eyvindr Skáldaspillir composed the poem Hákonarmál in his honour, describing his reception in Valhalla.
