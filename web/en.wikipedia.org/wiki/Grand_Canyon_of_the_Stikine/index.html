The Grand Canyon of the Stikine is a steep-walled canyon carved by the Stikine River in northwestern British Columbia, Canada. Often described as the "Everest of rivers", the canyon's violent rapids, sheer basalt walls and remoteness make it one of the most challenging stretches of whitewater in the world.

Geography

The Stikine River forces its way through a narrow gorge in the basaltic lava plateau of the Mount Edziza complex. In places the canyon is so narrow that the river is spanned by natural rock bridges used as crossings by mountain goats, which give the landmark "Goat Bridge" its name.

Exploration

Long known to the Tahltan people, the canyon was descended by elite kayakers only in the 1980s and 1990s; the first successful full kayak descent was completed in 1992. The river was proposed for hydroelectric development in the late 1970s, but the project was abandoned, and the area remains largely wilderness.
