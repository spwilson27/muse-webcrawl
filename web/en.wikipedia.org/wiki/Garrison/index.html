Garrison

A garrison (from the French garnison, itself from the verb garnir, "to equip") is any body of troops stationed in a particular location, originally to guard it, but now often simply as a home base. The word now usually refers to:

  • the place where troops are stationed; e.g. garrison town or garrison community;
  • the troops stationed in such a place.

A garrison is usually in a city, town, fort, castle, or similar location. "Garrison town" is a common expression for any town that has a military base nearby.

History

Garrisons have existed since antiquity, when empires stationed troops in conquered or frontier settlements to secure their territory. In the Roman Empire, legions and auxiliary units were posted in fortresses along the frontiers (limes). Medieval castles and walled towns similarly held permanent garrisons of soldiers owed to a lord or monarch. In the early modern and colonial periods, garrison towns developed around permanent barracks and fortresses, often shaping the growth and economy of the surrounding community.

Modern usage

In modern armies, the term may refer to the collective units stationed at a base, or to the administrative headquarters responsible for a military installation. In the British Army, for example, garrisons such as Aldershot and Catterick are major concentrations of troops and facilities; in other countries, similar concentrations are called military stations, cantonments, or bases.
