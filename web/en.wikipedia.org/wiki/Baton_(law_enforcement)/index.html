Baton (law enforcement)

A baton (also known as a truncheon, nightstick, blackjack in some contexts, or billy club) is a roughly cylindrical club used by law enforcement officers as a compliance and defensive tool. Batons are among the oldest police weapons and are typically classified as impact weapons within use-of-force continuums.

Types

Traditional batons include the straight wooden nightstick carried by American patrol officers and the British police truncheon. The side-handle baton, derived from the Okinawan tonfa and popularised as the PR-24, offers additional blocking and control techniques. Modern forces widely use telescopic expandable batons, such as the ASP baton, which collapse to a short handle for carrying and extend with a flick of the wrist.

Use

Training emphasises strikes to large muscle groups of the arms and legs and discourages blows to the head, which can be lethal. Batons can also be used for blocking, joint locks, escort holds and breaking glass in emergencies.

Regulation

Possession by civilians is restricted in many jurisdictions; in the United Kingdom, telescopic truncheons are prohibited offensive weapons, while laws in the United States vary by state. The baton's use in crowd control and against protesters has repeatedly raised controversy over police violence.
