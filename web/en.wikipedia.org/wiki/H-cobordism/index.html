h-cobordism

In geometric topology, an h-cobordism is a cobordism W between two manifolds M and N such that the inclusion maps of both boundary components into W are homotopy equivalences. Explicitly, W is a compact (n+1)-manifold whose boundary is the disjoint union of M and N, and each of M, N is a deformation retract of W, or at least has the same homotopy type.

The h-cobordism theorem

The h-cobordism theorem, proved by Stephen Smale in 1961 as part of his work on the generalised Poincaré conjecture, states that if W is a simply connected h-cobordism of dimension at least 6 (so the boundary manifolds have dimension at least 5), then W is diffeomorphic to a product M × [0,1]; in particular M and N are diffeomorphic. The proof uses Morse theory and the "Whitney trick" to cancel pairs of critical points of a Morse function on W, showing that its handle decomposition can be simplified until no handles remain.

Refinements and generalisations

When the manifolds are not simply connected, an h-cobordism need not be a product; the finer s-cobordism theorem of Barry Mazur, John Stallings and Dennis Barden shows that an h-cobordism is a product if and only if its Whitehead torsion vanishes, an obstruction living in the Whitehead group of the fundamental group. h-cobordisms and the associated surgery theory became central tools in the classification of high-dimensional manifolds, and the theorem does not hold in dimension 4 in the smooth category, where exotic phenomena occur.
