Replevin

Goods detained pending an action in replevin

Replevin or claim and delivery is a legal action in which a plaintiff seeks the return of specific personal property that has been wrongfully taken or wrongfully detained, together with damages for its detention. Unlike an action in trover, which awarded the value of converted goods, replevin recovers the goods themselves.

History

Replevin originated in medieval English law as a remedy for tenants whose livestock or goods had been distrained (seized) by a landlord for rent or services in arrears. The plaintiff posted a bond, the sheriff returned the goods pending trial, and the legality of the distress was then litigated. Over time the action expanded to cover wrongful takings generally.

Modern practice

In modern United States practice, replevin (often called "claim and delivery") is governed by statute and court rule. A plaintiff typically files an affidavit identifying the property and its value, and a bond may be required before the sheriff seizes and delivers the property. Because pre-judgment seizure implicates due process, cases such as Fuentes v. Shevin (1972) imposed procedural safeguards, including notice and an opportunity for a hearing in many circumstances.
