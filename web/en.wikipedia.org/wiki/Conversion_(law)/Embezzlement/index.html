Embezzlement

Funds entrusted to an employee are the classic subject of embezzlement

Embezzlement is the act of withholding assets for the purpose of conversion (theft) of such assets, by one or more persons to whom the assets were entrusted, either to be held or to be used for specific purposes. Embezzlement is a type of financial fraud. For instance, a lawyer might embezzle funds from the trust accounts of their clients; a financial advisor might embezzle the funds of investors; and a husband or a wife might embezzle funds from a bank account jointly held with the spouse.

Elements

The defining feature of embezzlement, distinguishing it from ordinary larceny, is that the defendant initially obtained possession of the property lawfully, through a position of trust, and only afterwards converted it to their own use. Methods range from small-scale skimming of cash receipts to complex schemes involving falsified invoices and shell accounts. Ponzi schemes operated by trusted insiders are often prosecuted partly as embezzlement.

Penalties

Penalties vary by jurisdiction and by the amount taken. In the United States, embezzlement of federal funds is punishable under 18 U.S.C. § 641, and state statutes commonly scale the offence from misdemeanour to felony according to the value misappropriated. High-profile cases, such as the frauds perpetrated by Bernard Madoff, have drawn sentences measured in decades or more.
