Term sheet

A term sheet is a non-binding (with some exceptions) document setting out the basic terms and conditions under which an investment, loan or business transaction will be made. It serves as a template for lawyers to develop the detailed legal documents of a deal, and is used extensively in venture capital financing, mergers and acquisitions, and commercial lending.

Negotiating a term sheet

Contents

A venture capital term sheet typically includes:

  • Valuation — pre-money and post-money valuation of the company.
  • Investment amount and security type — usually convertible preferred stock.
  • Liquidation preference — the multiple and participation rights investors receive in an exit.
  • Anti-dilution provisions — protection against later down rounds.
  • Voting rights, board composition and protective provisions.
  • Founder vesting, pro-rata rights, drag-along and tag-along rights, and information rights.
  • Binding provisions — typically confidentiality, exclusivity ("no-shop"), and sometimes governing law and expense reimbursement.

Role in negotiation

Although most of a term sheet is legally non-binding, signing one is a pivotal step: it locks in the economics and control terms of the deal and signals serious commitment. The subsequent drafting of definitive agreements (such as a stock purchase agreement and amended charter) generally follows the term sheet. Standardised model documents, such as the NVCA model term sheet and Y Combinator's SAFE, have reduced the cost and variability of early-stage rounds.
