White Christmas (song)

"White Christmas" is an Irving Berlin song reminiscing about an old-fashioned Christmas setting. It was written by Berlin in 1940–1941 and introduced by Bing Crosby, whose 1942 recording is one of the best-selling singles of all time, with estimated sales in excess of 50 million copies worldwide for Crosby's version alone.

History

Crosby first sang the song publicly on his NBC radio show on Christmas Day 1941, weeks after the attack on Pearl Harbor, and performed it in the film Holiday Inn (1942), where it won the Academy Award for Best Original Song. Its wistful longing for home resonated strongly with American troops overseas during the Second World War.

Crosby re-recorded the song in 1947 after the original master wore out, and starred in the 1954 film White Christmas. The often-omitted introductory verse sets the scene in sunny Beverly Hills, contrasting with the snowy Christmas the narrator dreams of.

Legacy

The song has been covered by thousands of artists and remains a perennial holiday standard; Guinness World Records lists Crosby's version as the world's best-selling single.
