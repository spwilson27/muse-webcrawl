Winchester rifle is a term covering a series of lever-action repeating rifles manufactured by the Winchester Repeating Arms Company. Developed from the 1860 Henry rifle, Winchesters became iconic firearms of the American West, prized for their rapid fire from a tubular magazine.

History

Oliver Winchester's company first produced the Model 1866, nicknamed the "Yellowboy" for its brass receiver. The iron-framed Model 1873, chambered for the .44-40 Winchester cartridge shared with Colt revolvers, sold in the hundreds of thousands and was marketed as "the gun that won the West".

Later models

The Model 1892 and the John Browning-designed Model 1894 followed; the 1894, often chambered in .30-30 Winchester, became one of the best-selling sporting rifles in history, with over seven million produced. Winchester lever guns remained in production, with interruptions and under various owners of the brand, into the twenty-first century.

Cultural significance

The Winchester rifle is a staple of Western films and literature, and original nineteenth-century examples are highly collectible.
