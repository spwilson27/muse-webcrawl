The Battle of Sarikamish was an engagement between the Russian and Ottoman empires during the First World War, fought from 22 December 1914 to 17 January 1915 in the Caucasus. It ended in a catastrophic Ottoman defeat, in which the Third Army was effectively destroyed, largely by the winter conditions of the high mountains.

Background

After the Russian success at the Bergmann Offensive in November 1914, the Ottoman Minister of War, Enver Pasha, took personal command of the Third Army and planned an ambitious envelopment modelled on the German victory at Tannenberg: Ottoman corps would swing in wide arcs through the Allahuekber mountains to cut off and annihilate the Russian Sarikamish detachment. The plan ignored the realities of deep snow, extreme cold and almost non-existent supply lines at altitudes above 2,500 metres.

The battle

The offensive began on 22 December 1914. Ottoman columns lost cohesion in blizzards; units fired on one another, supply collapsed, and tens of thousands of inadequately clothed soldiers froze to death on the marches. Although advance elements briefly reached Sarikamish itself and fighting raged around the town, the Russian commander Nikolai Yudenich organised a stubborn defence and then counterattacked. By mid-January 1915 the surviving Ottoman formations were in full retreat, leaving the Third Army reduced to a fraction of its strength; of some 118,000 men who began the campaign, perhaps fewer than 20,000–40,000 remained effective, with total Ottoman losses often estimated at 60,000–90,000, most to frostbite and disease.

Aftermath

Sarikamish was one of the worst defeats in Ottoman history and opened the Caucasus front to Russian advances that culminated in the capture of Erzurum in 1916. Enver Pasha blamed the disaster on Armenian disloyalty — a claim historians reject — and the scapegoating of the Armenians fed into the persecutions that followed in 1915. The battle remains a central episode of Turkish military memory, commemorated annually at Sarıkamış.
