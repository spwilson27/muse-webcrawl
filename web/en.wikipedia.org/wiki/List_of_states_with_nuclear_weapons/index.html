List of states with nuclear weapons

[Image: World map colour-coded by nuclear-weapon status: NPT weapon states, other possessors, and states that formerly possessed weapons]

Nine states are generally considered to possess nuclear weapons. Five are recognised as nuclear-weapon states under the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT, 1968): the United States, Russia (as successor to the Soviet Union), the United Kingdom, France, and China. Four others possess weapons outside the NPT framework: India, Pakistan, North Korea, and Israel, the last of which maintains a policy of deliberate ambiguity and neither confirms nor denies possession.

Former possessors and programmes

South Africa built six nuclear devices in the 1980s and dismantled them before joining the NPT in 1991, the only state to develop and then renounce an indigenous arsenal. Belarus, Kazakhstan, and Ukraine inherited Soviet weapons after 1991 and returned them to Russia under the 1994 Budapest Memorandum. Several other states, including Iraq, Libya, and Syria, pursued clandestine programmes that were halted; Argentina, Brazil, South Korea, and Taiwan abandoned weapons-related work under international pressure.

Arsenals

Russia and the United States together hold the large majority of the world's warheads — on the order of five thousand each including retired weapons awaiting dismantlement, with roughly 1,500–1,700 strategically deployed apiece under counting rules associated with New START. Arsenals have fallen dramatically from Cold War peaks following agreements beginning with SALT, though modernisation programmes continue in all nine states.

Sharing and hosting

Under NATO nuclear sharing, US weapons are stationed in several allied countries but remain in US custody; Belarus has also hosted Russian weapons since 2023. Such arrangements are distinct from independent national arsenals.
