MV Cougar Ace is a Singapore-flagged car carrier built in 1993 and operated by Mitsui O.S.K. Lines. She became widely known in July 2006 when she developed a severe list in the North Pacific Ocean south of Alaska's Aleutian Islands while transporting nearly 5,000 new cars from Japan to North America, and was the subject of a high-profile salvage operation.

2006 incident

On the night of 23–24 July 2006, during a ballast water adjustment in the open Pacific, Cougar Ace took on too much water and developed a list of more than 30 degrees to port (later reported as up to about 60 degrees at times by some accounts; the list stabilised around 30 degrees). The ship was carrying 4,812 vehicles — including Mazda cars and Isuzu trucks and other vehicles — bound for ports including New Westminster, British Columbia, and Port Hueneme and Portland on the US West Coast.

The United States Coast Guard and Alaska Air National Guard conducted a rescue of the 23 crew members. One crew member died after being injured during the casualty; the others were airlifted off. The salvage company Titan Salvage boarded the vessel; a naval architect from the salvage team died in an accident during the operation. Cougar Ace was towed first toward Oregon, then to Portland, arriving in August 2006.

Aftermath

The cargo of vehicles was ultimately scrapped rather than sold, amid concerns over damage from salt water, immersion of electrical systems, and liability; Mazda alone destroyed over 4,700 nearly new vehicles. After repairs the ship returned to service, later renamed Grand Orion, and continued in car-carrying service before being scrapped in 2020.
