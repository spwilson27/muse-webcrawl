Leo Minor

Leo Minor
Abbreviation: LMi
Brightest star: Praecipua (46 LMi)
Area: 232 sq. deg.

Leo Minor is a small and faint constellation in the northern celestial hemisphere. Its name is Latin for "the smaller lion", in contrast to Leo, the larger lion immediately to its south. It was introduced by the Polish astronomer Johannes Hevelius in 1687 and is one of the 88 modern constellations.

Stars

Leo Minor contains no star brighter than fourth magnitude (with one exception at the borderline). Its brightest star, Praecipua (46 Leonis Minoris), is an orange giant of magnitude 3.8. Unusually, the constellation has no star designated Alpha: Hevelius did not assign Greek letters, and the star now called Praecipua received its name later; Beta Leonis Minoris is the only other lettered star of note. The constellation also contains HD 87883, one of the first stars found to host an exoplanet by radial velocity with a small telescope programme.

Deep-sky objects

Despite its small area, Leo Minor contains several notable galaxies, including the barred spiral NGC 3432, the lenticular galaxy NGC 3486, and Arp 107, a pair of interacting galaxies. The galaxy NGC 3003 and the extremely metal-poor galaxy first spotted in the Arecibo survey (the "Leo Minor" dwarf candidate AGC 198691, "Leoncinino") lie in its direction.
