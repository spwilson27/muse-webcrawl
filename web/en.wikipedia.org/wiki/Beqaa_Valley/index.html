Beqaa Valley

LocationEastern Lebanon
Length~120 km
Major townsZahlé, Baalbek, Anjar

The Beqaa Valley (Arabic: Wādī l-Biqāʿ) is a fertile valley in eastern Lebanon, lying between the Mount Lebanon range to the west and the Anti-Lebanon Mountains to the east, which form the border with Syria. About 120 kilometres long and averaging 16 kilometres wide, it is Lebanon's most important agricultural region and forms part of the northern Great Rift Valley system.

Geography and agriculture

The valley is drained in its southern and central parts by the Litani River, which flows south then west to the Mediterranean, and in the north by the Orontes (Asi), which flows into Syria. Despite relatively low rainfall in the rain shadow of Mount Lebanon, irrigation from the rivers and from the Qaraoun reservoir supports wheat, vegetables, fruit, and the vineyards of Lebanon's wine industry, centred near Zahlé and home to wineries such as Ksara, Kefraya, and Musar.

History

In Roman times the Beqaa, then part of Coele-Syria, was a granary of the empire; the great temple complex of Baalbek (Heliopolis), with its colossal Temple of Jupiter, stands in the northern valley. The Umayyad-era ruins of Anjar lie near the southern end. In recent decades the valley has been affected by the Lebanese Civil War, Syrian military presence (1976–2005), and conflict involving Hezbollah, whose strongholds include Baalbek; since 2011 it has also hosted large numbers of Syrian refugees in informal settlements.
