Sophie Germain prime

In number theory, a Sophie Germain prime is a prime number p such that 2p + 1 is also prime. The number 2p + 1 associated with a Sophie Germain prime is called a safe prime. For example, 11 is a Sophie Germain prime and 2 × 11 + 1 = 23 is its associated safe prime. Sophie Germain primes are named after French mathematician Sophie Germain, who used them in her investigations of Fermat's Last Theorem.

Examples

The first few Sophie Germain primes are 2, 3, 5, 11, 23, 29, 41, 83, 89, 113, 131, 173, 179, 191, 239, ... with corresponding safe primes 5, 7, 11, 23, 47, 59, 83, 167, 179, 227, 263, 347, 359, 383, 479, ...

Distribution

It is conjectured that there are infinitely many Sophie Germain primes, in analogy with the twin prime conjecture, but this remains unproven. A heuristic estimate based on a Hardy–Littlewood-type argument suggests their count below x is approximately 2C·x / (ln x)², with a constant similar to the twin prime constant. A chain of primes in which each is related to the next by the map p → 2p + 1 is called a Cunningham chain.

Applications

Safe primes — and hence Sophie Germain primes — are important in cryptography: the group of units modulo a safe prime has a large prime-order subgroup, which is desirable for Diffie–Hellman key exchange and related protocols in order to resist Pohlig–Hellman attacks. Sophie Germain primes also occur in primality testing (Pocklington's criterion).

Fermat's Last Theorem

Germain proved that if p is a Sophie Germain prime, then there are no solutions to Fermat's equation xp + yp = zp with p not dividing xyz (the "first case" of the theorem), an important early advance on the problem.
