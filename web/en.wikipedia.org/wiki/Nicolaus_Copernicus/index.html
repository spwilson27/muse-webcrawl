Nicolaus Copernicus

Nicolaus Copernicus
Born: 19 February 1473, Toruń, Royal Prussia
Died: 24 May 1543, Frombork, Poland
Known for: Heliocentrism, De revolutionibus orbium coelestium (1543)

Nicolaus Copernicus (1473–1543) was a Renaissance polymath, active as a mathematician, astronomer and Catholic canon, who formulated a model of the universe with the Sun, rather than Earth, at its centre — heliocentrism.

Life

Born Mikołaj Kopernik in Toruń, he studied at Kraków, Bologna, Padua and Ferrara, gaining a doctorate in canon law. As a canon at Frombork in Warmia he administered church lands, practised medicine, worked on monetary reform (formulating a principle later called Gresham's law and an early quantity theory of money), and developed his astronomy in private.

Heliocentric theory

His Commentariolus (c. 1514) sketched the heliocentric idea. His full work De revolutionibus orbium coelestium, published in Nuremberg in 1543, the year he died, placed the Sun near the centre, with Earth rotating daily and revolving yearly, explaining retrograde motion naturally. He retained circular orbits and epicycles, so the system was not yet simpler in prediction than Ptolemy's.

Legacy

The "Copernican Revolution" reshaped science and philosophy, paving the way for Kepler, Galileo and Newton, and displacing humanity from the centre of the cosmos in the Western imagination. The element copernicium is named for him.
