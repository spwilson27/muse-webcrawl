John of Berry or John the Magnificent (French: Jean de Berry, 30 November 1340 – 15 June 1416) was a French prince of the House of Valois, Duke of Berry and Auvergne and Count of Poitiers and Montpensier. He was the third son of King John II of France and a major patron of the arts.

John spent several years as a hostage in England under the Treaty of Brétigny in place of his father. As duke he governed extensive territories in central France, acted as a mediator between the rival Armagnac and Burgundian factions during the madness of Charles VI, and amassed one of the great princely collections of his age.

Patronage

The Duke is remembered above all as a patron: he commissioned illuminated manuscripts including the Très Riches Heures of the Limbourg brothers, the Belles Heures and the Grandes Heures, as well as tapestries, jewels and goldsmith work. His castles at Bourges, Mehun-sur-Yèvre and elsewhere were centres of International Gothic art.
