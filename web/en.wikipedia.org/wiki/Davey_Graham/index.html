Davey Graham

Davey Graham (born David Michael Gordon Graham, 26 November 1940 – 15 December 2008) was a British guitarist and one of the most influential figures in the 1960s British folk revival. A virtuoso fingerstyle player, he pioneered the fusion of folk, blues, jazz, and Middle Eastern music, and composed the instrumental "Anji," a rite of passage for acoustic guitarists.

Career

Born in Leicester to a Guyanese mother and Scottish father, Graham taught himself guitar, absorbing blues from records and technique from travelling and listening across Europe and North Africa. His 1961 recording of "Anji" (named for a girlfriend) appeared on the EP 3/4 AD (1962, with Alexis Korner) and was covered by Bert Jansch, Paul Simon (on Sounds of Silence), and many others. His albums Folk, Blues and Beyond (1964) and Folk Roots, New Routes (1964, with Shirley Collins) were landmarks of the British folk scene. Graham is credited with popularising the DADGAD tuning, adapted from the Moroccan oud, among folk guitarists.

Later life and legacy

Despite his influence on players including Jansch, John Renbourn, Martin Carthy, Jimmy Page, and Nick Drake, Graham's career was hampered by drug problems and long periods out of the public eye. A late revival of interest brought reissues and festival appearances before his death from lung cancer in 2008.
