Ratha Yatra

TypeHindu festival
Observed byHindus, especially Vaishnavas
Principal celebrationPuri, Odisha, India
DeitiesJagannath, Balabhadra, Subhadra

Ratha Yatra (Odia: ରଥଯାତ୍ରା), also called the Ratha Jatra or Jagannath Festival, is a Hindu festival in which the deities Jagannath, his elder brother Balabhadra and his sister Subhadra are taken out of their temple on enormous wooden chariots (ratha) and pulled through the streets by devotees, allowing those who cannot enter the temple to receive darshan (a glimpse of the divine). The most famous celebration takes place at Puri in Odisha, India, and the festival is also observed at Jagannath temples across India and around the world.

The Puri festival

At Puri, three new chariots are constructed each year from specified woods in front of the Jagannath Temple. Jagannath's chariot, Nandighosha, has 16 wheels and stands about 13 metres high; Balabhadra's Taladhwaja has 14 wheels, and Subhadra's Darpadalana has 12. On the festival day — the second day of the bright fortnight of the month of Ashadha (June–July) — the deities are carried out in a ceremonial procession (pahandi), and the Gajapati king of Puri ritually sweeps the chariots with a golden broom (chera pahara), symbolising that king and sweeper are equal before the god.

The chariots are drawn along the Grand Road (Bada Danda) to the Gundicha Temple, the deities' "aunt's house", where they stay for about a week before the return journey, the Bahuda Yatra. The Puri festival draws well over a million pilgrims in many years. The English word "juggernaut", denoting an unstoppable force, derives from Jagannath and early European accounts of this festival.

Other celebrations

Major Ratha Yatras are held in Ahmedabad, in Bengal (notably at Mahesh, Serampore, which dates to 1396), and abroad — most prominently those organised since the late 1960s by ISKCON in cities such as London, New York and Los Angeles, where A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada introduced the festival to the West.
