Left atrial appendage occlusion

[Image: Left atrial appendage occlusion device]

Left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO), also known as left atrial appendage closure, is a medical procedure used to reduce the risk of stroke in people with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (AF) who are at high risk of bleeding or otherwise unable to take long-term oral anticoagulant medication. In atrial fibrillation, blood can stagnate and form clots in the left atrial appendage — a small pouch attached to the left atrium — and these clots can travel to the brain and cause a stroke. Sealing off the appendage from the circulation prevents clots formed there from embolising.

Rationale

Studies of thrombus location in non-valvular AF suggest that the large majority of intracardiac clots form in the left atrial appendage. Lifelong anticoagulation with warfarin or a direct oral anticoagulant is the standard stroke-prevention therapy, but it carries a risk of major bleeding and is contraindicated or poorly tolerated in some patients. LAAO offers a mechanical alternative.

Procedure

The most common approach is percutaneous: a catheter is advanced from the femoral vein across the interatrial septum into the left atrium, and a self-expanding occluder device (such as the Watchman device or the Amplatzer Amulet) is deployed at the mouth of the appendage, where it is later covered by the heart's own tissue (endothelialisation). The procedure is performed under transoesophageal echocardiographic and fluoroscopic guidance. Surgical exclusion of the appendage can also be performed during other cardiac surgery, by clipping, stapling or excision; the LAAOS III trial showed that surgical occlusion in addition to anticoagulation further reduced stroke in patients undergoing cardiac surgery.

Evidence

Randomised trials including PROTECT AF and PREVAIL compared the Watchman device with warfarin and found the device non-inferior for stroke prevention, with lower rates of haemorrhagic stroke and bleeding over long-term follow-up. After implantation, patients typically receive a period of anticoagulant or dual antiplatelet therapy until the device surface has healed. Complications can include pericardial effusion, device embolisation, and device-related thrombus.
