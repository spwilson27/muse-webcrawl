The 18S rRNA forms the core of the eukaryotic 40S subunit

18S ribosomal RNA (abbreviated 18S rRNA) is the RNA component of the small subunit (40S) of eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes, and so one of the basic components of all eukaryotic cells. It is the eukaryotic homologue of the 16S ribosomal RNA of prokaryotes. The name refers to its sedimentation coefficient of 18 Svedberg units.

Structure and function

The human 18S rRNA is about 1,869 nucleotides long. It folds into a complex secondary structure of helices and loops that forms the structural scaffold of the 40S subunit and plays a direct role in decoding messenger RNA: the 18S rRNA monitors base-pairing between the mRNA codon and the transfer RNA anticodon at the ribosomal A site, contributing to the fidelity of translation.

Genes

The genes encoding 18S rRNA are arranged in tandemly repeated ribosomal DNA clusters in the nucleolus organiser regions of chromosomes. In humans, the 18S sequence is transcribed as part of a single 45S precursor RNA that also yields the 5.8S and 28S rRNAs, which is then processed into the mature species.

Use in phylogenetics

Because it is universally present, evolves slowly in its conserved regions yet contains hypervariable regions, the 18S rRNA gene is one of the most frequently used markers in phylogenetic studies of eukaryotes and in environmental DNA surveys, where it is used to identify and classify organisms from fungi and protists to animals.
