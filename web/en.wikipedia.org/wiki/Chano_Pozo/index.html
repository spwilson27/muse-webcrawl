Chano Pozo

Luciano Pozo González (January 7, 1915 – December 3, 1948), known professionally as Chano Pozo, was a Cuban jazz percussionist, singer, dancer and composer. A central figure in the founding of Latin jazz, he was the first Latin percussionist to be a featured member of a major American jazz orchestra, joining Dizzy Gillespie's big band in 1947.

Pozo brought Afro-Cuban conga rhythms into the heart of bebop.

Early life

Born into poverty in Havana, Pozo grew up immersed in the Afro-Cuban religious and musical traditions of the Abakuá society and Santería, mastering the sacred batá drums and the rumba's secular rhythms. He worked as a composer and performer in Cuba, writing hits such as "Blen Blen Blen," before emigrating to New York in 1947.

With Dizzy Gillespie

Introduced to Gillespie by Mario Bauzá, Pozo joined the trumpeter's big band and co-wrote (with Gillespie and arranger George Russell) the Afro-Cuban jazz landmarks "Manteca" and "Tin Tin Deo." "Manteca," recorded in December 1947, fused bebop harmony with Afro-Cuban rhythm and became one of the founding recordings of Latin jazz. Pozo's conga playing, chanting and dancing made him a sensation of the band's stage show.

Death and legacy

Pozo was shot and killed in a Harlem bar on December 3, 1948, following a dispute over a debt and drugs, at the height of his brief American fame. Despite a career of barely a year in the United States, he is credited with permanently transforming jazz rhythm and opening the door for generations of Latin percussionists.
