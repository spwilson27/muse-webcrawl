July Revolution

July Revolution
Date27–29 July 1830 ("Les Trois Glorieuses")
LocationParis, France
ResultAbdication of Charles X; Louis-Philippe proclaimed king; July Monarchy established

The July Revolution (French: Révolution de Juillet), also known as the Three Glorious Days (les Trois Glorieuses), was a French revolution in 1830 that overthrew King Charles X and brought his cousin Louis-Philippe, Duke of Orléans, to the throne, beginning the July Monarchy. It was the second French revolution of the post-Napoleonic Bourbon Restoration.

Causes

Charles X, who succeeded his brother Louis XVIII in 1824, pursued an ultra-royalist programme: compensating émigrés, restoring the influence of the Church and governing through conservative ministries. After liberal victories in the elections of March 1830, Charles responded on 25 July with the July Ordinances, issued under Article 14 of the Charter of 1814, which suspended the freedom of the press, dissolved the newly elected Chamber and restricted the franchise.

The Three Glorious Days

Paris rose on 27 July 1830. Journalists protested the ordinances; printers and workers, joined by students and the bourgeoisie, built barricades across the city. Over three days of fighting, royal troops under Marshal Marmont lost control of Paris; the Hôtel de Ville was seized and the tricolour raised again. Perhaps 800 insurgents and 200 soldiers were killed. Charles X, at Saint-Cloud, withdrew the ordinances too late and abdicated on 2 August in favour of his grandson, the Count of Chambord.

Outcome

Rather than a republic — which liberal deputies and bankers feared — the Chamber proclaimed Louis-Philippe "King of the French" on 9 August 1830, ruling under a revised, more liberal Charter. The July Monarchy that followed was a bourgeois constitutional monarchy, memorably captured by Delacroix's painting Liberty Leading the People. The revolution helped trigger the Belgian Revolution and the November Uprising in Poland, and is remembered as the moment the middle class displaced the old aristocratic order in France — though workers who had manned the barricades gained little, seeding the grievances that erupted in 1848.
