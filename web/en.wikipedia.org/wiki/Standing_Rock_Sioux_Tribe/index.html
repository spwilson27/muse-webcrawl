The Standing Rock Sioux Tribe is a federally recognised tribe of Yanktonai and Lakota Sioux, whose reservation spans the border of North Dakota and South Dakota along the Missouri River. Its capital is Fort Yates, North Dakota.

History

The Standing Rock Indian Reservation was established as part of the Great Sioux Reservation under the Fort Laramie Treaty of 1868 and reduced by later federal actions. Sitting Bull, the famed Hunkpapa Lakota leader, is buried near Fort Yates.

Dakota Access Pipeline protests

In 2016–2017, the tribe led protests against the Dakota Access Pipeline, whose route passed near the reservation's water supply. The camps at Standing Rock drew thousands of supporters and international attention to indigenous sovereignty and environmental protection.
