The Roman Missal (Latin: Missale Romanum) is the principal liturgical book of the Roman Rite of the Catholic Church. It contains the texts and rubrics for the celebration of the Mass: the Order of Mass, the proper prayers for Sundays, feasts and seasons, the prefaces, and the Eucharistic Prayers including the Roman Canon. Scripture readings themselves are contained in the companion Lectionary.

History

The Missal developed from the sacramentaries of the early Middle Ages, which gathered the celebrant's prayers, supplemented over time by other books for readings, chant and ceremonial directions. After the Council of Trent, Pope Pius V promulgated a standardised edition in 1570 with the bull Quo primum, imposing it across most of the Latin Church; this line of editions, revised periodically (notably in 1962), underlies the "Tridentine Mass".

Post-Vatican II editions

Following the Second Vatican Council's constitution Sacrosanctum Concilium (1963), Pope Paul VI promulgated a revised Missal in 1969–1970, the basis of the form of Mass most widely celebrated today, with an expanded lectionary cycle, concelebration, and permission for vernacular translation. A second typical edition followed in 1975 and a third in 2002. The English translation of the third edition, prepared under the norms of Liturgiam authenticam with more literal renderings (for example "and with your spirit" and "consubstantial with the Father"), came into use in most English-speaking countries in 2011.

Structure

The book opens with the General Instruction of the Roman Missal (GIRM), setting out the theology and practical norms of the celebration. The Missal proper follows the liturgical year: the Proper of Seasons (Advent, Christmas, Lent, Easter, Ordinary Time), the Order of Mass, the Proper of Saints, Common Masses, ritual and votive Masses, and Masses for the Dead, with musical notation for chanted parts in the altar edition.

Use of earlier editions

The 1962 edition may still be celebrated under conditions clarified by Pope Francis's motu proprio Traditionis custodes (2021), which made diocesan bishops responsible for regulating such celebrations and affirmed the post-conciliar books as the unique expression of the Roman Rite's lex orandi.
