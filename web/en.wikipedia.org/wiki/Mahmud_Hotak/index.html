Mahmud Hotak (Pashto: محمود هوتك; c. 1697 – 22 April 1725) was an Afghan ruler from Kandahar who founded the Hotak dynasty. The son of Mirwais Hotak, who had led the Ghilji Pashtuns in revolt against Safavid Persia, Mahmud succeeded his father and in 1722 invaded Persia itself, defeating the Safavid army at the Battle of Gulnabad and taking Isfahan after a prolonged siege.

Rise to power

Mahmud assumed leadership of the Ghilji Afghans of Kandahar after the death of his uncle Abdul Aziz in 1717. Consolidating control over the Kandahar region, he turned his ambitions toward the ailing Safavid Empire, which was weakened by court intrigues and rebellions on several fronts.

Conquest of Persia

In March 1722 Mahmud's army of roughly 20,000 men defeated a much larger Safavid force at Gulnabad, near Isfahan. The capital was besieged for about six months; famine inside the city forced Sultan Husayn to surrender in October 1722 and abdicate in Mahmud's favour. Mahmud was crowned Shah of Persia, nominally ruling an empire he controlled only in part.

Reign and death

Mahmud's reign in Isfahan was marked by paranoia and violence, including massacres of Persian nobles and members of the Safavid family. His mental state deteriorated markedly, and in early 1725 he was deposed by his cousin Ashraf Hotak, dying shortly afterwards, in April 1725, reportedly by smothering at Ashraf's orders or of natural causes worsened by his illness.
