Gennady Padalka

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gennady Padalka
Born21 June 1958, Krasnodar, Russian SFSR
OccupationCosmonaut, Air Force officer
RecordMost cumulative time in space: 878 days
Padalka aboard the ISS.

Gennady Ivanovich Padalka (born 1958) is a retired Russian cosmonaut who holds the record for the most total time spent in space by any human: 878 days, 11 hours and 29 minutes, accumulated over five missions between 1998 and 2015 — to Mir and four expeditions aboard the International Space Station, which he commanded three times.

A career air-force pilot selected in 1989, Padalka was a mainstay of the early ISS: he commanded Expedition 9 (2004), Expeditions 19/20 (2009 — the first six-person station crew), Expedition 31/32 (2012) and Expedition 43/44 (2015), during which he surpassed Sergei Krikalev's cumulative record.

The record

Padalka's 878 days equal nearly two and a half years off Earth; his record is unlikely to fall soon, since Peggy Whitson — the closest active challenger — stood near 695 days after 2025. He left the cosmonaut corps in 2017, reportedly frustrated at being passed over for a sixth flight that could have taken him past 1,000 days.
