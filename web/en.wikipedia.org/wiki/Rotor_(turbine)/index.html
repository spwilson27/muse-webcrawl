Rotor (turbine)

Rotor
ClassificationRotating machine component
Used inSteam turbines, gas turbines, wind turbines, generators
CounterpartStator

A rotor is the rotating component of a turbine, electric motor, generator or other rotating machine. In a turbine, the rotor consists of a shaft (or drum) carrying rows of blades; the working fluid (steam, gas, water or wind) imparts force to the blades, causing the rotor to spin and transmit mechanical power to a generator or driven machine.

Design

Turbine rotors must withstand large centrifugal stresses and, in steam and gas turbines, high temperatures and creep. They may be forged from a single piece of alloy steel (monobloc), built up from discs shrunk and keyed onto a shaft, or welded from drum sections. Rotor blades are attached by fir-tree or dovetail roots, and the final stage blades of large steam turbines can exceed a metre in length. Balancing and overspeed testing are critical, since a rotor burst releases enormous energy.

Rotordynamics

The study of rotor vibration — critical speeds, whirl and unbalance response — is central to turbine reliability. Rotors operate either as rigid bodies below their first critical speed or as flexible rotors passing through critical speeds during start-up. In wind turbines, "rotor" also denotes the whole hub-and-blade assembly whose diameter determines the swept area and power capture.
