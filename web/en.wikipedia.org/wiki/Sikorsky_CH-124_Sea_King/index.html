The Sikorsky CH-124 Sea King is a Canadian version of the American Sikorsky SH-3 Sea King ship-based anti-submarine warfare (ASW) helicopter, designed and produced by Sikorsky Aircraft. A total of 41 were assembled by United Aircraft of Canada in Longueuil, Quebec, and entered service with the Royal Canadian Navy in 1963 (later Canadian Forces Maritime Command and the Royal Canadian Air Force).

Service history

The Sea King operated from Canadian destroyers and frigates — pioneered by the Canadian-developed "Beartrap" haul-down system, which allowed operations in rough seas from small decks — as well as from HMCS Iroquois-class destroyers and Halifax-class frigates. Its roles included ASW, surveillance, search and rescue, and utility transport. CH-124s deployed on operations including the Gulf War (1991), off Somalia, and in the Arabian Sea after 2001.

Replacement

The Sea King's replacement was protracted: Canada selected the Sikorsky CH-148 Cyclone in 2004, but deliveries began only in 2015. The last CH-124s were retired in December 2018 after 55 years of service, among the longest-serving military aircraft in Canadian history.

Variants

  • CH-124 — standard ASW version (29 delivered).
  • CH-124A — upgraded with the Sea King Improvement Program (SKIP) avionics.
  • CH-124B — alternate configuration without dipping sonar, with additional troop seating; CH-124C — one aircraft used for testing.
