John Philip Kemble (February 1, 1757 – February 26, 1823) was a British actor and theatre manager, the leading tragedian of his generation and a member of the celebrated Kemble acting family. His sister was the actress Sarah Siddons.

Career

The son of strolling player-manager Roger Kemble, he was educated for the priesthood but chose the stage, making his first appearance in 1776. His London debut at Drury Lane in 1783, as Hamlet, established his reputation. Kemble became manager of Drury Lane and later of Covent Garden, where his introduction of a new pricing scheme in 1809 provoked the famous Old Price Riots. His acting style was formal and classical, celebrated in Shakespearean roles such as Hamlet, Coriolanus and Cato, and he further revised Shakespeare's texts for the stage in the manner of his predecessors.

Later years

Kemble retired in 1817 with a farewell performance as Coriolanus, and spent his last years partly in the south of France and Switzerland, dying at Lausanne in 1823.
