Governor Thomas E. Dewey

Thomas Edmund Dewey (March 24, 1902 – March 16, 1971) was an American lawyer and politician who served as the 47th governor of New York from 1943 to 1954. He was the Republican Party's nominee for president in 1944 and 1948, losing to Franklin D. Roosevelt and then, in a famous upset, to Harry S. Truman.

Crime-fighter

Dewey first won national fame in the 1930s as a special prosecutor and then Manhattan district attorney, successfully prosecuting organized-crime figures including Waxey Gordon, Dutch Schultz's organization, and Mafia boss Charles "Lucky" Luciano. His "racket-busting" made him a leading figure in the liberal, internationalist "Eastern establishment" wing of the Republican Party.

The 1948 election

In 1948 Dewey was the overwhelming frontrunner; polls and pundits predicted an easy victory, and the Chicago Daily Tribune famously printed the erroneous headline "Dewey Defeats Truman" on election night. Truman's energetic whistle-stop campaign won 303 electoral votes to Dewey's 189, in one of the greatest upsets in American political history.

Governor of New York

As governor, Dewey built the New York State Thruway, founded the State University of New York, signed the first state law against racial discrimination in employment, and ran an efficient, moderate administration that left the state with a budget surplus. He played a key role at the 1952 Republican convention in securing the nomination of Dwight D. Eisenhower over Robert A. Taft.
