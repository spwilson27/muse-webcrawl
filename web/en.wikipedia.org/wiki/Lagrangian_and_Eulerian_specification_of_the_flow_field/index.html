Lagrangian and Eulerian specification of the flow field

In classical field theory, the Lagrangian specification and the Eulerian specification of a flow field are two ways of looking at fluid motion. The Lagrangian specification follows an individual fluid parcel as it moves through space and time, while the Eulerian specification concentrates on specific locations in space through which the fluid flows as time passes.

Definitions

In the Lagrangian description, the position of a parcel initially at position x₀ at time t₀ is tracked: x = x(x₀, t). Each parcel is identified by its initial position or material coordinates. In the Eulerian description, the flow velocity field u(x, t) gives the velocity of the fluid at each fixed point in space and time, without following individual parcels.

Material derivative

The material (or substantive) derivative connects the two descriptions, expressing the rate of change of a quantity following a moving parcel: D/Dt = ∂/∂t + (u·∇). The Lagrangian viewpoint is natural for tracking particles and conservation of mass of a parcel, while the Eulerian viewpoint underlies the standard form of the Navier–Stokes equations and most computational fluid dynamics.
