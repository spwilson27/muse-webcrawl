English as a second or foreign language

English as a second or foreign language is the teaching and learning of the English language by non-native speakers. The field is commonly abbreviated with a cluster of related terms: ESL (English as a second language), EFL (English as a foreign language), ESOL (English for speakers of other languages), and TEFL/TESL for the teaching professions. A distinction is traditionally drawn between learning English in a country where it is widely spoken (second language) and learning it where it is mainly a school subject (foreign language), though modern usage often blurs the two.

Scale

English is the world's most studied language. Estimates of the number of learners run into the hundreds of millions; English is a compulsory or common subject in primary and secondary schools across most of Europe, East Asia, Latin America and the Middle East, and it is the dominant language of international business, science, aviation and the internet. Organisations such as the British Council and large private language-school chains employ tens of thousands of teachers worldwide.

Methods

Teaching approaches have changed markedly over time. The grammar–translation method, inherited from the teaching of Latin, emphasised reading and explicit grammar rules. The direct method and later audiolingual method stressed speaking, drills and habit formation. Since the 1970s the dominant paradigm has been communicative language teaching, which prioritises meaningful interaction, task-based activities and fluency alongside accuracy. More recent approaches include content and language integrated learning (CLIL), in which subjects such as science are taught through English.

Proficiency levels and testing

The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) divides proficiency into six levels, from A1 (beginner) to C2 (mastery), and is widely used by course providers and exam boards. Major international examinations include the TOEFL and IELTS tests for academic admission, and the Cambridge English qualifications. Scores on such tests are frequently required for university entry, employment and immigration in English-speaking countries.

Varieties and controversies

Scholars debate which variety of English should be taught: British or American standards, local varieties of World Englishes, or English as a lingua franca focused on international intelligibility. The large market for native-speaker teachers has also raised questions about "native speakerism" — the assumption that a native speaker is automatically the best teacher — and about the effects of global English on smaller languages.
