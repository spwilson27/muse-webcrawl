Forced prostitution

Forced prostitution, also known as involuntary prostitution or sexual slavery when it forms part of enslavement, is prostitution compelled by coercion, threats, deception, debt bondage or abuse of power. It is recognised internationally as a serious human rights violation and a form of human trafficking.

Legal framework

The United Nations Protocol to Prevent, Suppress and Punish Trafficking in Persons (the Palermo Protocol, 2000) obliges signatory states to criminalise trafficking for the purpose of sexual exploitation. Forced prostitution in armed conflict can constitute a war crime and a crime against humanity under the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court.

Contexts

Victims, who are disproportionately women and children, may be recruited through false promises of work, abducted, or sold by family members, and then controlled through violence, confiscation of documents and manufactured debts. Historically, forced prostitution has also occurred in wartime, including systems of military sexual slavery during the Second World War.

Responses

Responses include anti-trafficking legislation, victim support and rehabilitation services, and international cooperation against organised crime networks. Debates continue over how prostitution law in general affects the prevalence of coercion and trafficking.
