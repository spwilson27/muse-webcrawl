High Noon


Gary Cooper in High Noon (1952)

High Noon is a 1952 American Western film produced by Stanley Kramer, directed by Fred Zinnemann, and starring Gary Cooper and Grace Kelly. The plot, told in nearly real time, centres on Marshal Will Kane, who, on the day of his wedding and retirement, learns that the outlaw Frank Miller — whom Kane sent to prison — has been pardoned and is arriving on the noon train to kill him. Unable to recruit any of the townspeople to help, Kane must face Miller and his gang alone.

Production and themes

Carl Foreman's screenplay was widely read as an allegory of McCarthyism and the Hollywood blacklist: Foreman himself was blacklisted during production. The film's stark black-and-white cinematography by Floyd Crosby, its recurring clocks, and Dimitri Tiomkin's score — including the ballad "Do Not Forsake Me, Oh My Darlin'," sung by Tex Ritter — became iconic.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial success, winning four Academy Awards: Best Actor for Cooper, Best Editing, Best Score, and Best Song. It is regularly ranked among the greatest Westerns ever made, though it provoked controversy: John Wayne and Howard Hawks disliked its portrait of a marshal begging for help, and made Rio Bravo (1959) partly in response. High Noon was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 1989.
