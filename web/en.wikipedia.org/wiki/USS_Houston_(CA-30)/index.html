USS Houston (CA-30) was a Northampton-class heavy cruiser of the United States Navy. She was the second Navy vessel named for the city of Houston, Texas. Commissioned in 1930, she served for much of the 1930s as flagship of the Asiatic Fleet and made several presidential voyages carrying Franklin D. Roosevelt.

World War II

At the outbreak of the Pacific War, Houston was flagship of the Asiatic Fleet under Admiral Thomas C. Hart. She fought in the Dutch East Indies campaign, surviving numerous Japanese air attacks—including one in the Flores Sea on 4 February 1942 that destroyed her aft turret—and earned the nickname the "Galloping Ghost of the Java Coast" because Japanese reports repeatedly claimed, falsely, to have sunk her.

Loss

On the night of 28 February – 1 March 1942, Houston and the Australian light cruiser HMAS Perth encountered a Japanese invasion force in the Sunda Strait. In the ensuing Battle of Sunda Strait, heavily outnumbered, both Allied cruisers were sunk after inflicting damage on the invasion fleet. Of Houston's complement of about 1,060 men, only 368 survived; many of the survivors died later as prisoners of war, including on the Burma Railway. Her captain, Albert H. Rooks, received the Medal of Honor posthumously. The wreck was located by US Navy divers in 2014.

Honours

Houston received two battle stars and the Presidential Unit Citation for her service in the opening months of the war. A new cruiser, USS Houston (CL-81), was named in honour of the ship and her crew during the war.
