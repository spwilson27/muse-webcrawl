National Security Advisor (United States)

Formal title: Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs
Appointed by: The President (no Senate confirmation)
First holder: Robert Cutler (1953)
Reports to: The President

The Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs, commonly known as the National Security Advisor (NSA), is a senior aide in the Executive Office of the President, based in the West Wing of the White House. The advisor serves as the principal in-house adviser to the president on national security and foreign policy, and heads the staff of the National Security Council (NSC).

Role

Unlike the Secretaries of State and Defense, the National Security Advisor is appointed by the president alone, without Senate confirmation, and is not subject to congressional oversight of their advice. The role's influence has varied by administration: under some presidents the advisor acts mainly as an honest broker coordinating the policy process; under others — notably McGeorge Bundy, Henry Kissinger and Zbigniew Brzezinski — the advisor became a policy advocate and public figure in their own right. Kissinger held the post (1969–1975) while later also serving as Secretary of State.

History

The position was created in 1953 under President Dwight D. Eisenhower, with Robert Cutler as the first advisor. The NSC staff it heads grew from a handful of officials to several hundred. The Deputy National Security Advisor assists the advisor and chairs the Deputies Committee that prepares issues for the Principals Committee and the president.

Notable holders

Advisors have included Walt Rostow, Brent Scowcroft (twice, under Ford and George H. W. Bush), Samuel Berger, Condoleezza Rice, Stephen Hadley, James Jones, Tom Donilon, Susan Rice, Michael Flynn, H. R. McMaster, John Bolton, Robert O'Brien and Jake Sullivan.
