Norman Rockwell

Norman Percevel Rockwell (February 3, 1894 – November 8, 1978) was an American painter and illustrator whose works created an enduring visual record of everyday American life. He is famous for the cover illustrations he created for The Saturday Evening Post magazine over nearly five decades, and for paintings such as the Four Freedoms series and The Problem We All Live With.

Career

Rockwell sold his first Saturday Evening Post cover in 1916 and went on to paint 323 covers for the magazine through 1963. During the Second World War his Four Freedoms (1943), inspired by President Franklin D. Roosevelt's speech, toured the country and helped raise over $130 million in war bonds; his Rosie the Riveter became an iconic wartime image. After moving to Look magazine in 1964, he addressed civil rights directly in works such as The Problem We All Live With (1964), depicting young Ruby Bridges being escorted to school by US Marshals.

Style and legacy

Working from photographs and models, Rockwell combined meticulous realism with narrative humour and sentiment. Long dismissed by some critics as a mere illustrator, he has been reassessed as a major storyteller of 20th-century America. The Norman Rockwell Museum in Stockbridge, Massachusetts preserves his studio and largest collection of his work. In 1977 he received the Presidential Medal of Freedom.
