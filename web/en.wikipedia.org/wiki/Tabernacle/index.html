The Tabernacle (Hebrew: מִשְׁכַּן, mishkan, meaning "residence" or "dwelling place"), also known as the Tent of the Congregation or Tent of Meeting, was, according to the Hebrew Bible, the portable earthly dwelling place of Yahweh used by the Israelites from the Exodus until the conquest of Canaan. Its detailed construction is described in the Book of Exodus, where God instructs Moses to build it according to a pattern shown on Mount Sinai.

Description

The Tabernacle was a rectangular enclosure. At its centre stood a tent shrine divided by a veil into the Holy Place and the innermost Holy of Holies, which housed the Ark of the Covenant surmounted by the mercy seat and two cherubim. The Holy Place contained the golden lampstand (menorah), the table of showbread, and the altar of incense. In the courtyard stood the bronze altar of burnt offering and a laver for the priests.

Construction and transport

The structure was built by craftsmen led by Bezalel and Oholiab, using materials donated by the Israelites: gold, silver, bronze, dyed linen, goat hair, acacia wood and precious stones. Designed to be dismantled and carried, it was transported by the Levites during the wilderness wanderings and erected at each encampment, positioned at the centre of the camp with the tribes arranged around it.

Later history

After the conquest, the Tabernacle rested at sites including Shiloh, where it figures in the narrative of the prophet Samuel. When Solomon built the First Temple in Jerusalem, the Tabernacle's furnishings were transferred there, and the portable sanctuary passed out of use, though its design profoundly influenced the Temple's layout.
