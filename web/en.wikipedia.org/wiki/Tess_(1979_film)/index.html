Tess is a 1979 drama film directed by Roman Polanski, adapted from Thomas Hardy's 1891 novel Tess of the d'Urbervilles. It stars Nastassja Kinski in the title role, with Peter Firth as Angel Clare and Leigh Lawson as Alec d'Urberville. The film was a French-British co-production, shot largely in Normandy, which stood in for Hardy's Dorset ("Wessex").

Plot

In Victorian England, a poor country family, the Durbeyfields, learn they may be descended from the ancient aristocratic d'Urberville line. Their eldest daughter Tess is sent to "claim kin" with a wealthy family bearing the name, where she is seduced — in Hardy's telling, effectively raped — by Alec d'Urberville. She returns home, bears a child who dies, and later falls in love with Angel Clare, a parson's son learning farming. On their wedding night she confesses her past; Angel, unable to accept it, abandons her and leaves for Brazil. Destitute, Tess is forced back into Alec's keeping. When Angel returns, repentant, Tess murders Alec and flees with Angel; she is arrested at Stonehenge and hanged.

Production

Polanski dedicated the film to his late wife Sharon Tate, who had given him Hardy's novel shortly before her murder in 1969 and suggested it would make a good film. Principal photography ran for about nine months in 1978–79. Costumes by Anthony Powell and cinematography by Ghislain Cloquet and Geoffrey Unsworth (who died during production) were widely praised for their painterly evocation of rural nineteenth-century life.

Reception and awards

Tess was a commercial success and won three Academy Awards: Best Cinematography, Best Art Direction, and Best Costume Design. It also won the César Award for Best Film in France. Critics praised Kinski's performance and the film's fidelity to Hardy's tragic tone, though some found its three-hour length demanding.
