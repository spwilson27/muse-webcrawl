Qian Liu (錢鏐, 852–932), courtesy name Jumei, was the founder and first king of the kingdom of Wuyue during China's Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period. From his capital at Hangzhou he ruled the prosperous coastal region of modern Zhejiang for a quarter of a century, and he is remembered in the region for flood-control works and for the stability his reign brought.

Rise to power

Qian Liu began as a salt smuggler and militia soldier in the service of the warlord Dong Chang during the collapse of the Tang dynasty. Rising through campaigns against rebels, he seized Hangzhou in 893 and, after defeating and killing his former patron Dong Chang, controlled most of modern Zhejiang. When Tang fell in 907, the new Later Liang dynasty recognised him as King of Wuyue, a title he held while nominally submitting to successive northern dynasties — a policy of deference that kept his small kingdom safe.

Rule of Wuyue

Qian Liu invested heavily in hydraulic engineering, building seawalls against the famous tidal bore of the Qiantang River and dredging West Lake, works that made Hangzhou a wealthy port and silk-producing centre. He promoted Buddhism and overseas trade, and tradition credits him with an influential deathbed instruction to his descendants: serve the central Chinese dynasty faithfully and never abandon his people. His grandson's eventual peaceful surrender of Wuyue to the Song dynasty in 978 is often attributed to this maxim. The Qian family has remained prominent in China ever since, producing notables in many fields.
