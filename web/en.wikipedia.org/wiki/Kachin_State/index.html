Kachin State
Country: Myanmar
Capital: Myitkyina
Population: about 1.7 million (2014 census)
Principal groups: Kachin (Jingpo), Shan, Bamar and others

Kachin State is the northernmost state of Myanmar, bordered by China to the north and east, Shan State to the south and Sagaing Region and India to the west. Its capital is Myitkyina. The state is the homeland of the Kachin peoples and is rich in natural resources, including the famous jade mines of Hpakant.

Geography

The terrain ranges from the foothills of the Himalayas — including Hkakabo Razi, Southeast Asia's highest peak — to the fertile Hukawng Valley and the floodplains of the Ayeyarwady River, which is formed near Myitkyina by the confluence of the Mali and N'Mai rivers. Indawgyi Lake, Myanmar's largest inland lake, lies in the southwest of the state.

History

Historically organised in chiefdoms under duwas, the Kachin hills were incorporated into British Burma in the late 19th century. After independence, disputes over autonomy led to the formation of the Kachin Independence Army in 1961 and decades of intermittent conflict with the central government.

Economy

Jade, timber, gold and agriculture dominate the economy. The Myitsone Dam project on the Ayeyarwady, suspended in 2011, remains a prominent controversy involving China and local communities.
