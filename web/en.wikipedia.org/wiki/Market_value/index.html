Market value

Market value or open market valuation (OMV) is the price at which an asset would trade in a competitive auction setting. Market value is often used interchangeably with open market value, fair value or fair market value, although these terms have distinct definitions in different standards and differ in some situations.

Definition

The International Valuation Standards define market value as "the estimated amount for which an asset or liability should exchange on the valuation date between a willing buyer and a willing seller in an arm's length transaction, after proper marketing and where the parties had each acted knowledgeably, prudently and without compulsion." The concept implies a price negotiated in an open and competitive market, not a forced sale.

Uses

Market value is central to real-estate appraisal, where comparable recent sales of similar properties anchor valuations; to financial reporting, where instruments may be marked to market; and to taxation and expropriation, where compensation is generally based on market value. For a publicly traded company, market capitalisation — share price multiplied by shares outstanding — expresses the market value of its equity, and moves with the market trend.

Market value versus other values

Market value differs from book value (the carrying amount in accounts), from value in use to a specific owner, and from liquidation value in a distressed sale. In thin markets where few transactions occur, estimating market value is difficult and contested; the prices generated by market orders in such markets can be volatile and unrepresentative.
