Northumberland Strait

The Northumberland Strait is a strait in the southern part of the Gulf of St. Lawrence in eastern Canada, separating Prince Edward Island from the mainland provinces of New Brunswick and Nova Scotia. It extends roughly 225 km (140 mi) in length, narrowing to about 13 km at the Abegweit Passage, where it is crossed by the Confederation Bridge.

Geography

The strait's waters are among the warmest in eastern Canada in summer—reaching over 20 °C—supporting sandy beaches and a notable tourism industry, while in winter much of the strait freezes over. Its depths are generally shallow, less than 20 m on the western side and deeper to the east. Major communities on its shores include Summerside and Charlottetown approaches in Prince Edward Island, and Shediac, Pictou, and Pugwash on the mainland.

Fisheries and crossing

The strait supports important lobster, scallop, and oyster fisheries; the oyster beds of Malpeque Bay are famous. Historically the strait was served by ferries and, in winter, by iceboats. The 12.9 km Confederation Bridge, opened in 1997, provides a fixed road link between Borden-Carleton, Prince Edward Island, and Cape Jourimain, New Brunswick, while a ferry still operates between Wood Islands and Pictou.
