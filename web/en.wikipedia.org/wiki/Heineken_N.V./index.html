Heineken N.V. is a Dutch multinational brewing company, founded in 1864 by Gerard Adriaan Heineken in Amsterdam. It is one of the largest brewers in the world by volume and revenue — generally second only to Anheuser-Busch InBev — operating more than 160 breweries in over 70 countries and selling its products in around 190 markets.

History

Gerard Heineken, aged 22, bought the De Hooiberg brewery in Amsterdam in 1864. The company won prizes for its lager at the 1889 Paris World's Fair, adopted bottom-fermentation and its proprietary "Heineken A-yeast" (developed in 1886 in the laboratory of a Louis Pasteur student), and expanded internationally under his son Henry and grandson Alfred "Freddy" Heineken, who built the brand's global marketing — the green bottle, red star, and smiling "e" in the logo. Freddy was kidnapped in 1983 and released after a ransom, an episode later dramatised in film.

Brands and acquisitions

The flagship Heineken lager is one of the world's best-selling international premium beers. The group owns over 300 brands, including Amstel, Birra Moretti, Tiger, Sol, Dos Equis, Tecate, Desperados, Affligem, and the cider brand Strongbow. Major acquisitions include Scottish & Newcastle (2008, with Carlsberg), the beer business of Mexico's FEMSA (2010), and full control of Distell and Namibia Breweries (2023). The Heineken family retains control through a holding structure.

Sponsorship

Heineken has long been a sponsor of the UEFA Champions League (1994–2024), the Rugby World Cup, Formula One, and the James Bond film franchise, making its advertising among the most recognisable in the drinks industry.
