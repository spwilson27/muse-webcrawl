Hürrem Sultan

Hürrem Sultan
Born: c. 1504, probably Rohatyn
Died: 15 April 1558, Istanbul
Spouse: Suleiman the Magnificent
Known as: Roxelana in Europe

Hürrem Sultan (Ottoman Turkish: خرّم سلطان; c. 1504 – 15 April 1558), also known in Europe as Roxelana, was the chief consort and legal wife of the Ottoman sultan Suleiman the Magnificent. She became one of the most powerful women in Ottoman history and a prominent figure of the period known as the Sultanate of Women.

Origins and rise

She was probably born in Rohatyn, then in the Kingdom of Poland (now Ukraine), possibly the daughter of a Ruthenian Orthodox priest, and was captured in a Crimean Tatar raid and sold into the imperial harem in Istanbul. Her cheerful disposition earned her the name Hürrem, 'the laughing one'. Breaking two centuries of tradition, Suleiman freed and legally married her, and she accompanied him on campaign and corresponded with foreign rulers including Sigismund II Augustus of Poland.

Influence and works

Hürrem advised the sultan, was implicated by contemporaries and later historians in the falls of grand vizier Pargalı Ibrahim Pasha and of Şehzade Mustafa, and promoted her sons — Selim II succeeded. She founded charitable complexes including the Haseki Sultan Complex in Istanbul, with mosque, madrasa, hospital and soup kitchen, and the Haseki Sultan Imaret in Jerusalem.

Death and legacy

She died in 1558 and was buried in a domed mausoleum in the Süleymaniye Mosque complex. Her life inspired operas, novels, paintings and the Turkish television series Muhteşem Yüzyıl.
