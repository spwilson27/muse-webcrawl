USB

Universal Serial Bus (USB) is an industry standard for digital data transmission and power delivery between many types of electronics. It specifies the architecture, in particular the physical interfaces, and communication protocols to and from hosts, such as personal computers, to and from peripheral devices, e.g. displays, keyboards, and mass storage devices. USB has largely replaced earlier interfaces such as serial ports and parallel ports.

History

USB was developed in the mid-1990s by a group of companies including Intel, Microsoft, Compaq, DEC, IBM, NEC, and Nortel; Ajay Bhatt of Intel is widely credited as a co-inventor. The first specification, USB 1.0, was released in January 1996, offering data rates of 1.5 and 12 Mbit/s.

Versions

Successive versions raised speeds dramatically: USB 2.0 (2000) reached 480 Mbit/s ("Hi-Speed"); USB 3.0 (2008) reached 5 Gbit/s ("SuperSpeed"); and USB4 (2019), built on the Thunderbolt 3 protocol, supports up to 40 Gbit/s. Connector shapes evolved from the original Type-A and Type-B plugs through Mini and Micro variants to the reversible USB-C connector introduced in 2014, which also supports USB Power Delivery at up to 240 watts.

Architecture

USB is a host-controlled bus: a host controller polls devices in a tiered-star topology. Devices describe themselves through standard class definitions, allowing generic drivers for functions such as storage, audio, and human interface devices.
