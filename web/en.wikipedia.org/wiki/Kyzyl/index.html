Kyzyl (Russian: Кызыл; Tuvan: Кызыл, "red") is the capital city of the Tuva Republic, Russia. It lies in southern Siberia at the confluence of the Great (Bii-Khem) and Little (Kaa-Khem) Yenisei rivers, and claims to be near the geographical centre of Asia, marked by an obelisk "Centre of Asia" on the riverbank.

History

The city was founded in 1914 as Belotsarsk ("White Tsar's Town"). It was renamed Khem-Beldyr in 1918 and Kyzyl in 1926. It served as the capital of the Tuvan People's Republic (Tannu Tuva) from 1921 until Tuva's incorporation into the Soviet Union in 1944.

Geography and culture

Kyzyl has a strongly continental climate with very cold winters. It is a centre of Tuvan culture, including throat singing (khoomei), and hosts the National Museum of Tuva, which holds Scythian gold from the Arzhan burial mounds. Transport links are limited: the city has an airport and road connections, but historically lacked a railway.
