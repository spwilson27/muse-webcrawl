Stanley Harding Mortensen (26 May 1921 – 22 May 1991) was an English professional footballer, most famous for his part in the 1953 FA Cup Final between Blackpool and Bolton Wanderers. Although the match is remembered as the "Matthews Final" for Stanley Matthews' performance, Mortensen scored a hat-trick — still the only hat-trick in an FA Cup Final at Wembley — as Blackpool came from 3–1 down to win 4–3.

Stan Mortensen in his Blackpool years

Career

Born in South Shields, Mortensen joined Blackpool in 1941 and, after wartime service as a wireless operator in the RAF — surviving the crash of a Wellington bomber — became the club's leading centre forward in the post-war years, forming a celebrated forward line with Matthews. He scored prolifically for Blackpool through the late 1940s and 1950s and also played in the 1948 and 1951 FA Cup finals, both lost. He later played for Hull City, Southport and non-league Bath City and Lancaster City, and briefly managed Blackpool in the late 1960s.

International career

Mortensen won 25 caps for England, scoring 23 goals. He scored on his debut, played at the 1950 World Cup in Brazil, and famously scored four goals in a match against Portugal in 1947 and a hat-trick in the 1948 victory over Italy in Turin. He is also notable for having played once for Wales as a wartime guest when they were short of players.

Legacy

A statue of Mortensen stands outside Blackpool's Bloomfield Road ground, and a stand at the stadium bears his name. He is regarded as one of the finest English centre forwards of his generation.
