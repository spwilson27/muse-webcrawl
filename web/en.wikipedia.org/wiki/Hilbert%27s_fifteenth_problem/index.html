Hilbert's fifteenth problem

Hilbert's fifteenth problem is one of the 23 problems presented by the German mathematician David Hilbert in 1900. It asks for a rigorous foundation of Hermann Schubert's enumerative calculus.

Background

Schubert's calculus was a powerful method for counting geometric configurations satisfying given conditions, based on the principle of conservation of number. Nineteenth-century geometers used it to compute, for example, the number of lines tangent to given surfaces or the number of conics through five points.

Solutions

The problem was addressed through the development of intersection theory on homogeneous spaces, the construction of Chow rings, and the work of André Weil and others. Modern approaches use algebraic cycles, intersection products, and the cohomology of Grassmannians to put Schubert's counts on a rigorous footing.
