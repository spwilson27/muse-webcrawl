Espoo (Finnish: [ˈespoː]; Swedish: Esbo) is a city in the Uusimaa region of Finland, on the northern shore of the Gulf of Finland, immediately west of Helsinki. With a population of over 300,000, it is the second most populous city in Finland and forms part of the Helsinki metropolitan area. Unlike most cities, Espoo has no single historic centre; it is a polycentric network of districts, five of which function as urban centres: Leppävaara, Tapiola, Matinkylä, Espoon keskus and Espoonlahti.

History

The name Espoo derives, by tradition, from the aspen trees along its river (Swedish äspe). First mentioned in documents in the 1430s, it remained for centuries a rural parish of farms and manors; its stone church, Espoo Cathedral, dates to the 1480s. The municipality grew slowly until the post-war decades, when proximity to Helsinki drove rapid suburban development. Tapiola, built from the 1950s by the Asuntosäätiö housing foundation, became an internationally noted example of the modernist "garden city". Espoo gained city status in 1972.

Economy and education

Espoo is a major centre of Finnish high technology. The Keilaniemi district hosts the headquarters of companies including Nokia, KONE, Neste and Rovio Entertainment, and the Otaniemi district contains the main campus of Aalto University, formed in 2010 by merging Helsinki University of Technology with two other institutions, together with the VTT Technical Research Centre of Finland. The Länsimetro extension of the Helsinki Metro reached Espoo in 2017 and was extended to Kivenlahti in 2022.

Geography and nature

The city covers a landscape of lakes, forest and archipelago coastline; Nuuksio National Park, in north-western Espoo, preserves rugged lake-highland scenery within reach of the capital. Espoo has a significant Swedish-speaking minority (around 7% of residents) and one of the youngest and most international populations of any Finnish city.
