George Enescu

George Enescu
Born: August 19, 1881, Liveni, Kingdom of Romania
Died: May 4, 1955, Paris, France
Occupation: Composer, violinist, conductor, teacher

George Enescu (Romanian pronunciation: [ˈdʒe̯ordʒe eˈnesku]; August 19, 1881 – May 4, 1955), known in France as Georges Enesco, was a Romanian composer, violinist, pianist, conductor and teacher, and Romania's most celebrated musician. He was one of the most versatile musicians of the 20th century, famed as a virtuoso violinist and as the teacher of Yehudi Menuhin.

Life

A child prodigy, Enescu entered the Vienna Conservatory at age seven and later studied in Paris with Gabriel Fauré and Jules Massenet. His Poema română (1897) was premiered when he was 16, and his two Romanian Rhapsodies (1901) brought him early fame — renown he sometimes regretted, since it overshadowed his later, more ambitious works. He divided his career between Romania, France and international concert tours, conducted major orchestras, and during the Second World War remained in Romania; in 1946 he left for Paris, where he died in 1955.

Works

Enescu's masterpiece is the opera Œdipe (1936), on which he worked for over two decades. His orchestral music includes three symphonies and the Orchestral Suites; his chamber works — among them the Violin Sonata No. 3 "in Romanian folk character" (1926), the String Octet (1900) and the late Chamber Symphony (1954) — fuse Romanian folk idioms with rigorous classical form. He published only 33 opus numbers, leaving much music unfinished or in manuscript.

Legacy

The George Enescu Festival, founded in Bucharest in 1958, is one of Europe's major classical music festivals, and the Bucharest Philharmonic and the composer's museum in Bucharest bear his name. He appears on the Romanian 5-lei banknote.
