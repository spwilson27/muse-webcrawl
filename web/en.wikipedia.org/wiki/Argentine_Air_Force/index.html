Argentine Air Force

Aircraft of the Fuerza Aérea Argentina.

The Argentine Air Force (Spanish: Fuerza Aérea Argentina, FAA) is the air force of Argentina and one of the three branches of the Armed Forces of the Argentine Republic. It traces its origins to the Army's Military Aviation School founded in 1912 and became an independent service in 1945. At its peak in the early 1980s it was among the most capable air forces in Latin America, operating types such as the Mirage III, Dagger, A-4 Skyhawk and Canberra.

Falklands War

The FAA's most significant combat experience came in the Falklands War (Guerra de las Malvinas) of 1982 against the United Kingdom. Flying from mainland bases at extreme range, Argentine pilots attacked the British task force with bombs and Exocet missiles, sinking or damaging several warships, including HMS Sheffield, HMS Coventry and the container ship Atlantic Conveyor. The Air Force suffered heavy losses — roughly 55 aircraft and aircrew — and its performance in the war is commemorated in Argentina for its bravery under severe technical constraints, including bombs that often failed to explode after being released at very low level.

Modern era

Post-war embargoes and chronic underfunding eroded the force; supersonic interceptors were retired in 2015, leaving Argentina without a dedicated fighter for a period, and the fleet came to centre on A-4AR Fightinghawks, IA-63 Pampa trainers/light attack jets, transports and helicopters. In 2024 Argentina agreed to purchase second-hand F-16s from Denmark to restore a supersonic capability. The FAA also supports Antarctic operations and operates the Condor space and research activities in cooperation with national agencies.
