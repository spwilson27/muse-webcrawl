Jerk (cooking)

Jerk
Place of originJamaica
Main ingredientsMeat (chicken, pork), Scotch bonnet peppers, allspice (pimento)
Associated cuisineJamaican cuisine

Jerk is a style of cooking native to Jamaica, in which meat is dry-rubbed or wet-marinated with a hot spice mixture called Jamaican jerk spice, then slow-cooked over a smoky fire, traditionally over pimento wood. The principal ingredients of the marinade are allspice (called "pimento" in Jamaica) and Scotch bonnet peppers. Other ingredients may include cloves, cinnamon, scallions, nutmeg, thyme, garlic, brown sugar, ginger and salt.

History

Jerk originated with the indigenous Taíno people and was developed further by African Maroons — escaped slaves who fled into Jamaica's mountainous interior in the 17th century. The Maroons hunted wild hogs, seasoned the meat to preserve it, and cooked it slowly in pits or over smouldering fires in a way that produced little smoke, avoiding detection by the British. The word "jerk" is thought to derive from charqui, a Spanish term for dried meat (the source of the English word "jerky"), itself from the Quechua ch'arki.

Methods

Modern jerk is most often applied to chicken and pork, but also to fish, goat, beef, sausage, and even tofu and vegetables. In Jamaica, jerk is sold at roadside "jerk centres" and cooked on oil-drum grills. Boston Beach in Portland parish is famous as a centre of the tradition. The meat is typically served with festival (sweet fried dumplings), breadfruit, or hard-dough bread.

Spread

Jamaican migration carried jerk cooking to cities such as Toronto, London, New York and Miami, where jerk chicken is a staple of Caribbean restaurants and food festivals. Commercial jerk seasonings and sauces are sold worldwide.
