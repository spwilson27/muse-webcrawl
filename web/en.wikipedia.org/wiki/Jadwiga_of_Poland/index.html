Jadwiga of Poland

Reign1384–1399 (crowned as "king")
Bornc. 1373/74, Buda, Hungary
Died17 July 1399, Kraków
SpouseJogaila (Władysław II Jagiełło)
Canonised1997, by John Paul II

Jadwiga (c. 1373/74 – 1399), also known as Hedwig, was the first woman to be crowned monarch of the Kingdom of Poland — crowned as "king" in 1384, reigning until her death. A saint of the Catholic Church, she is remembered for her piety, charity and role in uniting Poland with Lithuania.

Accession and marriage

The younger daughter of Louis I of Hungary and Poland, Jadwiga was chosen by the Polish nobility after her father's death, breaking with her sister Mary. Crowned at Kraków aged about ten, she was betrothed to William of Austria, but the Polish lords arranged instead her marriage in 1386 to Jogaila, Grand Duke of Lithuania, who was baptised Władysław, was crowned co-monarch, and pledged the Christianisation of Lithuania — creating the Polish–Lithuanian union under the Union of Krewo.

Reign

Jadwiga was an active ruler: she led military expeditions to Red Ruthenia, negotiated with the Teutonic Knights, endowed churches and hospitals, promoted education, and in her will funded the restoration of Kraków Academy (the Jagiellonian University). She sold her jewels to finance the university's renewal.

Death and veneration

She died in 1399 shortly after childbirth, days after her infant daughter. Buried in Wawel Cathedral, she was venerated locally for centuries and canonised by Pope John Paul II in 1997, during his visit to Kraków.
