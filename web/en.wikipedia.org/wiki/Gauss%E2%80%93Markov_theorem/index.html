Gauss–Markov theorem

In statistics, the Gauss–Markov theorem states that the ordinary least squares (OLS) estimator has the lowest sampling variance within the class of linear unbiased estimators, provided the errors in the linear regression model are uncorrelated, have equal variances, and have an expectation value of zero. Estimators satisfying these properties are called BLUE: best linear unbiased estimators.

Assumptions

The classical assumptions are linearity in parameters, random sampling, no perfect multicollinearity, exogeneity (zero conditional mean of the errors), and homoskedasticity with no autocorrelation of the error terms. Notably, the theorem does not require the errors to be normally distributed.

Statement

Given the model y = Xβ + ε with E[ε|X] = 0 and Var(ε|X) = σ²I, the OLS estimator β̂ = (X′X)⁻¹X′y is the best linear unbiased estimator of β, meaning any other linear unbiased estimator has a variance at least as large in the matrix sense.

History

The result builds on work by Carl Friedrich Gauss on least squares in the early 19th century and was given its modern form through contributions by Andrey Markov, after whom the combined theorem is named.
