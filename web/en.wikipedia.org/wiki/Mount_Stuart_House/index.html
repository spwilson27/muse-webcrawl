Mount Stuart House

Mount Stuart House, on the east coast of the Isle of Bute, Scotland, is a Victorian Gothic palace and the ancestral home of the Stuart family, Marquesses of Bute. The present house was built after a fire destroyed the earlier Georgian mansion in 1877.

Architecture

Designed by architect Robert Rowand Anderson for the 3rd Marquess of Bute, the house is built of red sandstone and is noted for its opulent interiors: a marble hall with some eighty marble columns, a marble staircase, and stained glass. It was among the most technologically advanced houses of its era — reportedly the first Scottish home with an indoor heated swimming pool and among the first with electric lighting and a telephone system.

Gardens and estate

The house stands in about 300 acres of gardens and woodland policies within a larger estate, including a rockery, a pinetum, shoreline gardens, and an 18th-century landscaped park around the site of the original house. Mount Stuart is open to the public and hosts exhibitions and events; it is managed by the Mount Stuart Trust.
