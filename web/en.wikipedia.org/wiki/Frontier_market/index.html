Frontier market

A frontier market is a type of developing country that is considered more developed than a least developed country but too small, risky, or illiquid to be classified as an emerging market. The term was coined in 1992 by Farida Khambata of the International Finance Corporation. Frontier markets are sometimes called "pre-emerging markets."

Characteristics

Frontier markets typically have smaller stock market capitalisations, lower trading volumes, higher volatility, and greater political and currency risks than emerging markets, but may offer investors higher long-run growth potential and diversification benefits because their economies are less correlated with global markets. Index providers such as MSCI and FTSE Russell maintain frontier market indices; countries in the category have included Vietnam, Nigeria, Kenya, Morocco, Bangladesh, and Romania (some of which have since been promoted to emerging market status).

Investment

Investors access frontier markets through dedicated mutual funds, exchange-traded funds, and index products, though limited liquidity, capital controls, and weaker investor protections remain significant challenges.
