Gymnocephalus

Gymnocephalus is a genus of freshwater ray-finned fishes in the perch family Percidae, commonly known as ruffes. They are native to Europe and northern Asia, inhabiting rivers, lakes and brackish coastal waters. The best-known species, the ruffe (Gymnocephalus cernua), has been introduced to the Great Lakes of North America, where it is considered an invasive species.

Description and species

Members of the genus are small, perch-like fishes characterized by a connection between the spiny and soft parts of the dorsal fin, large eyes set high on the head, and channels of the lateral line system that open as conspicuous pits on the head — the feature referenced by the generic name, from Greek words meaning "naked head." They feed on benthic invertebrates and are most active at night.

Recognised species include the ruffe (G. cernua) of Europe and Siberia; the Donets ruffe (G. acerina) and Balon's ruffe (G. baloni) of the Danube and Dnieper basins; the striped ruffe (G. schraetser) of the Danube; and G. ambriaelacus of Lake Ammersee in Bavaria. The ruffe, introduced to Lake Superior in the 1980s, likely via ballast water, competes with native percids and has prompted control measures in the Great Lakes.
