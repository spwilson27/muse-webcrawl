Kamikōchi (上高地, "Upper Highlands") is a remote mountainous highland valley in the Hida Mountains of Nagano Prefecture, Japan, within the city of Matsumoto. Preserved in its natural state as part of Chūbu-Sangaku National Park, it is one of Japan's most celebrated scenic areas and a centre of Japanese mountain culture.

Geography

The valley stretches roughly 18 kilometres along the Azusa River at an elevation of about 1,500 metres, flanked by peaks including Mount Yake (2,455 m), an active volcano, and the Hotaka massif, whose highest summit, Mount Okuhotaka (3,190 m), is the third-highest peak in Japan. Landmarks include the Kappa Bridge over the Azusa, Taishō Pond — formed by a 1915 eruption of Mount Yake — and Myōjin Pond.

History and access

The area was opened to alpinism in the late 19th century, notably by the English missionary Walter Weston, whose writings popularized the "Japanese Alps". To protect the environment, private cars have been banned beyond the approach tunnels since the 1990s; visitors arrive by bus or taxi, and the valley is open seasonally from mid-April to mid-November, closing in winter.
