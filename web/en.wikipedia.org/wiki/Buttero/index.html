A buttero (plural butteri) is a herdsman or cowboy in the Maremma region of Tuscany and northern Lazio in Italy, traditionally mounted on a working horse of the native Maremmano breed. The butteri managed the semi-wild herds of Maremmana cattle and horses on the large estates of the marshy coastal plain, a way of life that persisted for centuries and is now maintained mainly as a cultural tradition.

Tradition

The buttero's equipment includes a distinctive broad-brimmed hat, heavy clothing suited to the thorny macchia scrub, a long staff called a mazzarella used to guide horses, and the pattada knife. Work involved rounding up, branding and breaking in young horses, celebrated today at festivals such as the May Day branding at Alberese in the Maremma Regional Park.

Buffalo Bill challenge

The butteri are famous for the episode of 1890, when Buffalo Bill's Wild West show toured Italy and its cowboys challenged local horsemen; at the show's stop near Rome, butteri led by Augusto Imperiali succeeded in riding and breaking wild horses that the Americans had failed to master, a feat celebrated in Italian popular memory as a point of national pride.
