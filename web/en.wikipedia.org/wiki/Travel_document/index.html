Travel document

A travel document is an identity document issued by a government or an international organization enabling its holder to travel internationally and to be identified at borders. The most common travel document is the passport, issued to citizens. Other forms serve people who cannot obtain a national passport, including refugees and stateless persons.

Types

  • Passport — issued to nationals; certifies identity and citizenship and requests passage and protection abroad.
  • Convention travel document — issued to refugees under the 1951 Refugee Convention, and to stateless persons under the 1954 Convention.
  • Laissez-passer — issued by the United Nations and some other bodies to staff traveling on official business.
  • Emergency travel document — a short-validity document issued to citizens who lose their passport abroad.
  • Identity cards and border passes — accepted for travel within certain regional arrangements, such as the European Union or between the United States and Canada for land travel.

Standards

The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) standardizes machine-readable travel documents in Doc 9303, specifying sizes, data layout, and, for modern e-passports, a contactless chip storing biometric data such as a facial image and fingerprints.

History

Safe-conduct documents date to antiquity; the modern passport system crystallized after the First World War, when the League of Nations standardized formats and the Nansen passport provided documents to displaced persons.
