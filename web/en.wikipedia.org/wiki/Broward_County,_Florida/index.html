Broward County, Florida

Broward County
County seat: Fort Lauderdale
State: Florida
Region: South Florida / Miami metropolitan area

Broward County is a county in southeastern Florida, between Miami-Dade County to the south and Palm Beach County to the north. Its county seat and largest city is Fort Lauderdale. With roughly two million residents, it is one of Florida's most populous counties and a core part of the Miami metropolitan area.

Geography

The county stretches from the Atlantic Ocean westward into the Everglades. Its eastern third is densely developed along canals, beaches, and the Intracoastal Waterway, while conservation areas protect much of the western portion. Fort Lauderdale–Hollywood International Airport and Port Everglades make the county a major transport and cruise hub.

History

Broward County was created in 1915 and named for Napoleon B. Broward, a former governor of Florida who promoted draining the Everglades. Land booms, tourism, suburban growth after the Second World War, and immigration—especially from the Caribbean and Latin America—transformed it into a diverse urban county.

Communities

Cities include Fort Lauderdale, Hollywood, Coral Springs, Pembroke Pines, Miramar, Pompano Beach, Plantation, Sunrise, Davie, and Weston. The county is known for beaches, boating canals, and as a centre for marine industries.
