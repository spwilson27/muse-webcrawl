Nunuku-whenua

Nunuku-whenua (fl. 16th century) was a Moriori chief (rangatira) of Rēkohu (the Chatham Islands) who is credited in Moriori tradition with establishing a covenant of peace — known as Nunuku's Law — renouncing warfare, killing, and cannibalism among his people.

The covenant of peace

According to Moriori oral tradition, after a period of inter-tribal conflict Nunuku declared that bloodshed must cease: disputes could be settled by ritual fighting with staffs (tūkari) limited to the first drawing of blood, but killing and the eating of human flesh were forbidden because they diminished the mana of the people and insulted the gods. The law is said to have governed Moriori society for centuries, and the Moriori maintained a strictly pacifist culture.

Legacy

Nunuku's covenant had tragic consequences in 1835, when two Māori iwi from Taranaki, Ngāti Mutunga and Ngāti Tama, invaded the Chatham Islands. Bound by their law of peace, the Moriori chose at a gathering at Te Awapatiki not to resist despite outnumbering the invaders, and many were killed or enslaved; the Moriori population collapsed. The covenant is nonetheless remembered as a foundational ethic of peace, commemorated in the Moriori renaissance of recent decades, in the carving and plantings at Hokotehi Moriori Trust sites, and in Waitangi Tribunal proceedings on the Chathams (Wai 262 and the Rekohu report).
