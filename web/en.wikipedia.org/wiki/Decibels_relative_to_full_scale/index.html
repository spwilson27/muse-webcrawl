Decibels relative to full scale (dBFS) is a unit of measurement for amplitude levels in digital systems, such as digital audio, which have a defined maximum peak level. The reference level, 0 dBFS, is assigned to the highest possible digital amplitude; all other levels are therefore negative numbers in dBFS.

Definition and conventions

For sampled data, 0 dBFS conventionally corresponds to the level of a full-scale sine wave or, in another convention, a full-scale square wave; the two conventions differ by about 3 dB and are a common source of confusion. Values above 0 dBFS can occur within floating-point processing but cannot be represented in fixed-point output without clipping.

Applications

dBFS is used on digital peak meters in recording studios and broadcast equipment. Broadcast loudness standards recommend average programme levels well below full scale — for example around −23 LUFS under EBU R 128 — leaving headroom measured in dBFS for peaks.
