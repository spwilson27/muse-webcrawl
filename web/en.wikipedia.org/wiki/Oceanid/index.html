In Greek mythology, the Oceanids (Greek: Ὠκεανίδες) are the nymph daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys. Each was the patroness of a particular spring, river, sea, lake or pond.

Mythology

Hesiod's Theogony states there are three thousand Oceanids, naming about forty-one of them, including Styx, Electra, Doris, Metis, Eurynome and Perse. Apollodorus gives a similar list. The Oceanids are elder cousins of the younger Nereids, daughters of Nereus, and are associated with fresh water as well as the sea.

Notable Oceanids

Metis was the first wife of Zeus and mother of Athena. Styx personified the river of the underworld by which gods swore oaths. Doris was mother of the fifty Nereids. Eurynome was mother of the Charites by Zeus. Clymene was mother of Phaethon. In Aeschylus's Prometheus Bound, a chorus of Oceanids visits the chained Prometheus.

In art

Oceanids appear frequently in classical art, vase painting and later European painting and sculpture as beautiful water nymphs accompanying sea gods and heroes.
