Murder in English law

Murder in English law is a common-law offence of unlawfully killing a reasonable person (a human being) under the King's peace, with malice aforethought, express or implied.

Malice aforethought means an intention to kill or an intention to cause grievous bodily harm. Murder carries a mandatory life sentence; judges set a minimum term before parole eligibility, with whole-life orders in the most serious cases.

Defences

Partial defences that reduce murder to manslaughter include loss of control and diminished responsibility under the Coroners and Justice Act 2009 and the Homicide Act 1957. Self-defence, where reasonable, is a complete defence. Infanticide is a separate offence for a mother who kills her child under 12 months while her mind is disturbed by childbirth.
