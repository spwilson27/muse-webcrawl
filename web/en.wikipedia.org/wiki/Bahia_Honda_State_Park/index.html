Bahia Honda State Park

Bahia Honda State Park is a Florida State Park located on Bahia Honda Key in the lower Florida Keys, at mile marker 37 of the Overseas Highway. The park covers more than 500 acres (200 hectares) of the island, most of which was originally owned by railroad magnate Henry Flagler, whose Florida East Coast Railway crossed the key on the Bahia Honda Rail Bridge. The park is renowned for its beaches, which are unusual among the rocky Florida Keys for their natural white sand.

Features

The park offers swimming, snorkelling, kayaking, fishing and camping, including a popular waterfront campground. Its Sandspur Beach and Calusa Beach face the Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf of Mexico respectively. The disused Bahia Honda Rail Bridge, its truss span partially removed, is a prominent landmark and provides views across the channel. Snorkelling trips run to the Looe Key National Marine Sanctuary offshore.

Natural history

The park protects rare plants including the yellow satinwood and the endangered silver palm, and its beaches are nesting habitat for sea turtles. It was named after the deep natural bay — bahía honda means "deep bay" in Spanish — beside which it sits.
