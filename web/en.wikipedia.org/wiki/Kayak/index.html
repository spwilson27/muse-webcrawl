Kayak

OriginArctic North America and Greenland
Traditional usersInuit, Yupik, Aleut
PropulsionDouble-bladed paddle
Traditional materialsDriftwood or whalebone frame, stitched sealskin cover

A kayak is a small, narrow human-powered watercraft traditionally propelled by means of a double-bladed paddle. The word kayak originates from the Greenlandic word qajaq. Kayaks were developed over thousands of years by the indigenous peoples of the Arctic regions of North America, Siberia, and Greenland, who used them for hunting and transport on coastal waters, rivers, and lakes.

History

The earliest kayaks were built from a lightweight wooden or whalebone frame covered with stretched, waterproofed animal skins, typically sealskin, with a small opening in the top for the paddler. The paddler sat low in the hull wearing a waterproof spray skirt that sealed the cockpit, allowing the craft to be righted after a capsize using a technique now known as the kayak roll. Different regional groups developed distinct hull forms suited to local conditions, from the long, narrow hunting kayaks of Greenland to the wider umiaks' smaller counterparts in Alaska.

Kayaks were introduced to Europe in the 19th century and became popular for sport in Germany and France in the early 20th century. Kayak racing became an Olympic sport at the 1936 Berlin Games, and whitewater kayaking developed after the Second World War with the advent of fibreglass and later rotomoulded plastic boats.

Design

Modern kayaks are generally made of polyethylene plastic, fibreglass, carbon fibre, or inflatable materials. Key design parameters include length, width (beam), hull cross-section, and rocker (the upward curve of the hull at the bow and stern). Longer kayaks track straighter and are faster; shorter kayaks are more manoeuvrable. Sea kayaks typically have sealed bulkheads for buoyancy and storage, while whitewater kayaks are short with rounded hulls for surfing river features.

Types and uses

  • Recreational kayaks — stable, wide boats for flat water.
  • Sea kayaks — long touring boats for open water expeditions (sea kayaking).
  • Whitewater kayaks — playboats, creek boats, and slalom boats for rivers.
  • Sprint and marathon kayaks — narrow racing shells used in canoe sprint.
  • Fishing kayaks — stable sit-on-top designs for angling.
  • Surf kayaks and surf skis — long, narrow open-deck boats for ocean racing and surf.
