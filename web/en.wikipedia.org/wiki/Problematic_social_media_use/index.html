Problematic social media use

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Problematic social media use — also discussed under labels such as social media addiction or excessive social media use — refers to a preoccupation with social media platforms that impairs daily functioning, including sleep, study, work and relationships. Researchers debate whether heavy use that causes harm is best characterised as an addiction, a compulsive behaviour, or a symptom of underlying conditions such as anxiety, depression or ADHD.

Signs and correlates

Proposed indicators include salience (social media dominating thought), tolerance and escalating use, withdrawal-like irritability when unable to check, unsuccessful attempts to cut down, conflict with family or school, and relapse. Studies find associations with poorer sleep quality, fear of missing out (FOMO), social comparison, low self-esteem, anxiety and depressive symptoms — especially among adolescents — though effect sizes are often small and causality is disputed.

Scientific debate

"Social media addiction" is not a formal diagnosis in the DSM-5 or ICD-11 (which does recognise gaming disorder). Critics warn against pathologising ordinary behaviour and note that commonly used screening scales can classify large proportions of normal users as "addicted". Others point to deliberately engaging design features — infinite scroll, pull-to-refresh, intermittent variable rewards and push notifications — as warranting concern.

Responses

Interventions studied include digital-wellbeing tools and screen-time limits, app timers, notification management, cognitive behavioural approaches for underlying distress, and school and family media plans. Several countries have debated or introduced age restrictions on social media accounts for minors.
