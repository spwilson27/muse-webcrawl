Substance abuse
SpecialtyPsychiatry, addiction medicine
Modern termSubstance use disorder (DSM-5)
Risk factorsGenetics, early exposure, mental illness, social environment

Substance abuse, also known as drug abuse, is the use of a psychoactive substance in amounts or by methods that are harmful to the individual or to others. It encompasses harmful use of alcohol, prescription medicines, and illicit drugs such as opioids, stimulants, and cannabis. In the DSM-5, the older diagnoses of substance abuse and substance dependence were merged into a single diagnosis, substance use disorder, graded mild, moderate, or severe.

Definitions and diagnosis

Diagnostic criteria include using larger amounts than intended, unsuccessful efforts to cut down, craving, failure to fulfil major obligations, continued use despite social or physical harm, tolerance, and withdrawal. The ICD-11 uses the categories "harmful pattern of use" and "dependence."

Causes

Addiction arises from interacting genetic vulnerability, developmental and environmental influences, and the pharmacological action of drugs on the brain's reward system, particularly dopaminergic pathways. Adverse childhood experiences and co-occurring mental disorders substantially increase risk.

Treatment and policy

Effective treatments include medications (such as methadone, buprenorphine, and naltrexone for opioid use disorder), behavioural therapies, motivational interviewing, and mutual-help groups. Public policy approaches range from prohibition and criminalisation to harm reduction measures — needle exchanges, supervised consumption sites, and naloxone distribution — and to regulated decriminalisation, as in Portugal's model since 2001.
