Blood sausage

Black pudding, the British and Irish blood sausage

A blood sausage is a sausage filled with blood that is cooked or dried and mixed with a filler until it is thick enough to solidify when cooled. Most commonly, the blood of pigs is used; blood from cattle, sheep, ducks, and other animals is also used in various cuisines. Typical fillers include meat, fat, suet, bread, cornmeal, onion, chestnuts, barley, rice, and oatmeal.

Varieties

Blood sausages are found in cuisines around the world. In Britain and Ireland, black pudding is made from pork blood and oatmeal and is a traditional part of a full cooked breakfast. France has boudin noir, often served with apples. Spain's morcilla is commonly seasoned with paprika and may include rice or onion. Germany's Blutwurst and Poland's kaszanka are similar preparations, while Finland's mustamakkara is a speciality of Tampere. In East Asia, Korean sundae is filled with blood, noodles, and vegetables, and blood is also used in curd form in Chinese and Vietnamese cooking.

History

Blood sausages are among the oldest forms of sausage, devised to use every part of a slaughtered animal. A blood sausage is mentioned in Homer's Odyssey, and the Roman cookery book Apicius contains recipes for them. Traditionally they are made at the autumn slaughter, when fresh blood is plentiful.

Preparation

Fresh blood is stirred to prevent clotting, mixed with the chosen fillers and seasonings, and piped into casings. The sausages are then gently poached until set. They may be eaten cold, grilled, fried, or baked, and are often reheated before serving.
