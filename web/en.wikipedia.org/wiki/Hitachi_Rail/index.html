Hitachi Rail
Type: Division of Hitachi
Industry: Rail transport
Headquarters: London, United Kingdom
Products: Rolling stock, signalling, rail systems

Hitachi Rail is the railway systems division of the Japanese industrial conglomerate Hitachi. It designs, manufactures and maintains rolling stock, signalling and traffic management systems, and turnkey railways, with operations in Japan, Europe, the Americas, Asia and Oceania.

History

Hitachi's rail business grew from its domestic Japanese works, which supplied locomotives and multiple units for Japanese railways and built trains for the Shinkansen network. Internationally, Hitachi broke into the British market with the Class 395 Javelin trains for Southeastern high-speed services (2009) and the Class 800/801/802 trains of the Intercity Express Programme, assembled at a new factory at Newton Aycliffe, County Durham, opened in 2015.

Expansion

In 2015 Hitachi acquired the Italian rolling-stock manufacturer AnsaldoBreda and a majority stake in the signalling company Ansaldo STS (completed in 2018–19), which became Hitachi Rail STS. The acquisitions made Hitachi a full-service global rail supplier. In 2024 Hitachi Rail completed the acquisition of Thales's Ground Transportation Systems business, significantly expanding its signalling operations.

Products and projects

Key products include A-train aluminium multiple units, the AT100–AT300 families in Britain, metros for cities such as Honolulu, Lima, Taipei and Copenhagen driverless lines (via STS), and ETCS and CBTC signalling. Hitachi Rail is part of the consortium building trains for HS2 in the United Kingdom with Alstom. Its headquarters are in London, with major sites in Japan, Italy, the United States and Australia.
