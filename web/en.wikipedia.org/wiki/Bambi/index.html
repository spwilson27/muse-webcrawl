Bambi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bambi is the title character of Felix Salten's 1923 novel Bambi, A Life in the Woods (German: Bambi. Eine Lebensgeschichte aus dem Walde) and of Walt Disney's 1942 animated film adaptation of the same name. In Salten's book Bambi is a roe deer; in the Disney film he is a white-tailed deer.

The novel

Salten, an Austrian writer and hunter, wrote the novel as a story of the perils of life in the forest for wild animals, centred on the threat of "He" — Man, the hunter. Bambi grows from a fawn under his mother's protection to a mature stag under the tutelage of the Old Prince, the eldest deer of the forest; along the way his mother is shot by a hunter, a formative episode in both book and film. The novel, which has darker and more philosophical content than the adaptation, was banned in Nazi Germany in 1936 as a supposed allegory of the treatment of Jews in Europe. An English translation by Whittaker Chambers appeared in 1928 and became a Book-of-the-Month Club success in the United States.

The Disney film

Walt Disney's Bambi, released on August 13, 1942, was the studio's fifth animated feature. It softened the novel's harshness but retained the death of Bambi's mother, one of the most famous and affecting scenes in animation. Disney's animators studied real deer and other animals to achieve an unprecedented naturalism, and supporting characters such as Thumper the rabbit and Flower the skunk were created or expanded for the film. A midquel, Bambi II, was released direct-to-video in 2006.

Cultural impact

"Bambi" has entered the language as a byword for wide-eyed innocence, and the "Bambi effect" describes public sympathy for charismatic animals and opposition to hunting them. The character remains one of Disney's most recognisable creations and is frequently cited among the most emotionally powerful figures in film history.
