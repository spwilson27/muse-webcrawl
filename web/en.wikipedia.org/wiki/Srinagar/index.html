Srinagar

Dal Lake, with its famous houseboats and shikaras.

Srinagar is a city in Indian-administered Jammu and Kashmir in the disputed Kashmir region. It is the largest city and the summer capital of Jammu and Kashmir, a union territory of India. It lies in the Kashmir Valley on the banks of the Jhelum River and on the shores of Dal Lake and Anchar Lake, between the Hari Parbat and Shankaracharya hills. The city is known for its natural environment, Persian-style gardens, waterfronts and houseboats, as well as for traditional Kashmiri handicrafts such as pashmina shawls and papier-mâché. According to the 2011 census, Srinagar had a population of about 1.18 million.

History

The city was founded in antiquity; tradition attributes its foundation to the Mauryan emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE, and it appears in Kalhana's 12th-century chronicle Rajatarangini. Srinagar was ruled in succession by Hindu and Buddhist kings, then from the 14th century by Muslim sultans, including the celebrated Zain-ul-Abidin. It passed to the Mughal Empire under Akbar in 1589, to Afghan Durrani rule in 1752, to the Sikhs in 1819, and to the Dogra dynasty under the Treaty of Amritsar in 1846. After 1947 it became the summer capital of the princely state's successor administration in India.

Geography and climate

Srinagar lies at an altitude of about 1,585 m (5,200 ft) in the Kashmir Valley, surrounded by the Pir Panjal range and the Zabarwan mountains. It has a humid subtropical climate bordering on a continental one, with cold winters — temperatures frequently fall below freezing and snowfall is common — and warm summers moderated by the elevation and lakes.

Tourism and gardens

The Mughal gardens of Nishat Bagh, Shalimar Bagh, and Chashme Shahi on the eastern shore of Dal Lake are among the city's best-known sights. Tourists have long stayed in carved wooden houseboats on Dal and Nigeen lakes and taken shikara rides to the floating gardens and markets. The old city (Shehar-e-Khaas) contains the Jamia Masjid, the shrine of Hazratbal, and historic wooden mosques such as the Khanqah-e-Moula.

Economy and transport

Tourism, handicrafts, horticulture (especially apples and saffron from the surrounding districts) and government services dominate the economy. Srinagar International Airport and National Highway 44 connect the city to Jammu and the rest of India; the Jammu–Baramulla railway is being extended toward the valley.
