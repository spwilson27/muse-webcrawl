Glomma

The Glomma, or Glåma, is the longest and largest river in Norway and in Scandinavia, with a total length of about 621 km (386 mi) and a drainage basin covering some 13% of Norway's land area. It flows southward from Lake Aursunden near Røros in Trøndelag, through the Østerdalen valley, past Elverum and Kongsvinger, and through the flat agricultural districts of Romerike and Østfold before emptying into the Oslofjord at Fredrikstad.

Course and features

Major tributaries include the Vorma, which drains Lake Mjøsa, Norway's largest lake, and joins the Glomma near Årnes. The river includes notable waterfalls and rapids such as Sarpsfossen at Sarpsborg, one of Europe's largest waterfalls by volume of water. The Glomma's mean discharge is roughly 700 m³/s, and its broad, slow lower reaches pass some of Norway's richest farmland.

Use and history

For centuries the Glomma was Norway's principal artery for timber floating, carrying logs from the forests of Østerdalen and Solør to the sawmills and paper mills at Sarpsborg and Fredrikstad; the floating ended in 1985 and is commemorated at the Glomma river museum at Fetsund Lenser, once one of the world's largest log-sorting stations. Today the river is dammed at several points for hydroelectric power and is valued for recreation and salmon and trout fishing. Serious floods struck the basin in 1789 (Storofsen) and 1995 (Vesleofsen), causing extensive damage in the valley towns.
