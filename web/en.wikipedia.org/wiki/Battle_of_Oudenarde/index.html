Battle of Oudenarde

The Battle of Oudenarde was fought on 11 July 1708 near the town of Oudenarde in the Spanish Netherlands (modern Belgium) during the War of the Spanish Succession. The Grand Alliance army under John Churchill, Duke of Marlborough, and Prince Eugene of Savoy defeated a French army commanded by the Duke of Vendôme and the Duke of Burgundy. It was one of Marlborough's four great victories in the war, alongside Blenheim, Ramillies, and Malplaquet.

Background

In 1708 the French went on the offensive in Flanders, seizing Ghent and Bruges. Marlborough force-marched to intercept them before they could besiege Oudenarde, crossing the River Scheldt by pontoon bridges on 11 July.

The battle

The battle developed piecemeal as both armies deployed from the march. Command friction between Vendôme and Burgundy hampered the French: Burgundy kept the French right on high ground out of the fight for much of the day, while Allied infantry and cavalry gradually enveloped the French left and centre. A sweeping Allied cavalry movement around the French flank completed the victory by nightfall.

Aftermath

The French army lost perhaps 15,000 men, including some 8,000 prisoners, against roughly 3,000 Allied casualties. The victory reopened the road into France and led to the Allied siege and capture of Lille later that year.
