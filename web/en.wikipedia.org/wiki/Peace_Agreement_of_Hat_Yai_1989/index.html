Peace Agreement of Hat Yai 1989

The Peace Agreement of Hat Yai 1989 was a peace treaty signed on 2 December 1989 at the Lee Gardens Plaza Hotel in Hat Yai, Songkhla Province, Thailand, between the Communist Party of Malaya (CPM) and the governments of Malaysia and Thailand. The agreement formally ended the CPM's armed struggle and brought to a close the second Malayan Emergency and the communist insurgency in Thailand's south.

Background

The Communist Party of Malaya had waged guerrilla war against the British and then the Malaysian government from 1948 to 1960 (the Malayan Emergency), and renewed its insurgency from bases along the Malaysian–Thai border from 1968. By the late 1980s the party was weakened by internal splits, the loss of its broadcasts from China, and the end of Cold War support for revolutionary movements. Negotiations mediated through Thai military contacts in the border town of Betong led to the Hat Yai settlement.

Terms

Under the agreement the CPM agreed to terminate its armed struggle, disband its armed units and destroy its weapons. In return, former CPM members were permitted to return to Malaysia on application and approval, or to settle in "peace villages" in southern Thailand set up for those who chose to remain. Malaysia's prime minister Mahathir Mohamad and the Thai authorities endorsed the settlement. The CPM's secretary-general Chin Peng signed for the party.

Aftermath

Some 1,188 CPM members laid down their arms. Chin Peng's applications to return to Malaysia were rejected by the Malaysian courts, and he died in exile in Bangkok in 2013; the return of his ashes in 2019 provoked public controversy in Malaysia. The agreement is regarded as the formal end of the communist insurgency that had shaped Malayan and Malaysian politics for four decades. See also Malayan Emergency and Communist Party of Malaya.
