Essential medicines

Essential medicines, as defined by the World Health Organization (WHO), are the medicines that "satisfy the priority health care needs of the population". Essential medicines should be available in a health system at all times, in adequate amounts, in the appropriate dosage forms, with assured quality, and at a price the individual and the community can afford.

WHO Model List

The WHO published the first Model List of Essential Medicines in 1977, containing 208 medicines. The list is updated every two years by an expert committee. The 2023 (23rd) list contains over 500 medicines. Since 2007, a separate list has been published for children up to 12 years of age, the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines for Children.

The list is divided into a core list, presenting a list of minimum medicine needs for a basic health-care system, and a complementary list, which contains medicines that require specialised diagnostic or monitoring facilities, specialist medical care, or specialist training.

Implementation

More than 150 countries have published national essential medicines lists based on the WHO model, and the concept has become a cornerstone of pharmaceutical policy, humanitarian drug donations and the selection of medicines for procurement by agencies such as UNICEF and Médecins Sans Frontières. Access to essential medicines is recognised as part of the right to health and is an explicit target of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 3.
