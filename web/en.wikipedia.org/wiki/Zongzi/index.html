Zongzi

Zongzi (Chinese: 粽子; pinyin: zòngzi) is a traditional Chinese food made of glutinous rice stuffed with different fillings and wrapped in bamboo, reed (Phragmites) or other large flat leaves. They are cooked by steaming or boiling. Zongzi is traditionally eaten during the Dragon Boat Festival (Duanwu), which falls on the fifth day of the fifth month of the Chinese lunar calendar. In Western cultures they are sometimes described as "Chinese tamales" or "rice dumplings".

Origins

Popular legend associates zongzi with the death of the poet and minister Qu Yuan (c. 340–278 BC) of the state of Chu. According to the legend, after Qu Yuan drowned himself in the Miluo River, local people threw rice into the river so that the fish would not eat his body, and beat drums and splashed with paddles to frighten them away — the legendary origins of both zongzi and dragon boat racing. Rice wrapped in leaves, however, predates the legend, and tube-cooked rice dishes are recorded from earlier periods.

Description

The glutinous rice may be seasoned or plain, and fillings vary widely by region. Northern-style zongzi are often sweet, filled with red bean paste, jujube (Chinese dates) or taro, while southern styles are typically savoury: pork belly, salted egg yolk, shiitake mushrooms, dried shrimp, chestnuts, peanuts, mung beans or Chinese sausage. "Alkaline" zongzi (jianshui zong) are made with rice treated with lye water, giving a translucent golden colour and bouncy texture, usually eaten plain or with sugar. Shapes range from tetrahedral pyramids (common in the south) to cones and elongated forms. Related leaf-wrapped rice dishes exist across East and Southeast Asia, including Japanese chimaki, Korean yeonipbap, and Vietnamese bánh ú tro.

Preparation

Leaves are soaked and cleaned; the rice is soaked (and sometimes stir-fried with seasonings in southern recipes). Each parcel is folded, filled, tied with string and then boiled or steamed for several hours until the rice becomes dense and sticky and absorbs the fragrance of the leaves.
