Rashi

Rashi
Born: c. 1040, Troyes, France
Died: 13 July 1105, Troyes
Occupation: Rabbi, commentator
Known for: Commentaries on the Tanakh and Talmud

Shlomo Yitzchaki (c. 1040 – 13 July 1105), generally known by the acronym Rashi, was a French rabbi who authored comprehensive commentaries on the Talmud and the Tanakh (Hebrew Bible). He is one of the most influential Jewish commentators of the Middle Ages, and his commentaries are considered indispensable to traditional Jewish study.

Life

Rashi was born in Troyes, in the Champagne region of France, and studied in the yeshivas of Worms and Mainz in the Rhineland. Returning to Troyes around 1070, he founded a yeshiva and earned his living as a vintner. He lived through the massacres of Jewish communities during the First Crusade (1096), which affected relatives and colleagues in the Rhineland.

Works

Rashi's commentary on the Torah explains the plain sense of the text (peshat) while incorporating midrashic readings, clarifying difficult Hebrew words — often with Old French glosses (laaz) that are a valuable source for historical linguistics. His Talmud commentary, covering nearly the whole Babylonian Talmud, makes the text intelligible to students and has been printed in virtually every edition since the first printed Talmuds. A distinctive semicursive Hebrew typeface, "Rashi script", is traditionally used to print his commentaries.

Legacy

Rashi's grandsons — Rashbam and Rabbeinu Tam — founded the Tosafist school. His commentaries were also studied by Christian Hebraists such as Nicholas of Lyra, influencing later Bible translation.
