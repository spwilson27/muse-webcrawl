Integrated Automated Fingerprint Identification System

DeveloperFBI Criminal Justice Information Services (CJIS)
LaunchedJuly 1999
SuccessorNext Generation Identification (NGI)

The Integrated Automated Fingerprint Identification System (IAFIS) was a national automated fingerprint identification and criminal-history system operated by the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI). It maintained the largest fingerprint database in the world, providing automated search capabilities and electronic storage of fingerprints and corresponding criminal histories for federal, state and local law-enforcement agencies.

Overview

IAFIS went live in July 1999, replacing a largely paper-based system of fingerprint cards. Its databases held tens of millions of criminal and civil fingerprint records — including those of arrestees, military personnel, and applicants for sensitive jobs — along with criminal histories, mug shots, scars and tattoo records, and physical descriptors. Authorized agencies could submit ten-print cards electronically and typically receive an identity response within hours for criminal searches and about a day for civil searches.

Latent prints

Beyond ten-print identification, IAFIS supported searches of latent fingerprints recovered from crime scenes against the national database, enabling investigators to generate candidate matches that fingerprint examiners then verified. Remote terminals allowed agencies around the country to query the system around the clock.

Successor

Beginning in 2011, the FBI phased in the Next Generation Identification (NGI) system, which added palm prints, iris recognition, facial recognition and other biometric modalities. NGI reached full operational capability in September 2014, at which point IAFIS was decommissioned and its functions absorbed into the new system.
