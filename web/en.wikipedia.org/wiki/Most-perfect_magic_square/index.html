Most-perfect magic square

An example most-perfect magic square of order 4

A most-perfect magic square of order n is a magic square containing the numbers 1 to n2 which has two additional properties:

1. Each 2×2 subsquare, including those formed by wrapping around the edges of the square (that is, the square is also pandiagonal), sums to the same total, 2(n2 + 1).

2. All pairs of integers distant n/2 along any diagonal (major or broken) are complementary, summing to n2 + 1.

Existence and examples

Most-perfect magic squares exist only for doubly even orders, that is, orders divisible by 4. The smallest example has order 4:

181312
141127
45169
151036

Here every row, column and diagonal sums to 34, every 2×2 block sums to 34, and complementary pairs are diametrically separated. There are 48 most-perfect squares of order 4 up to the usual symmetries, and 368,640 of order 8. A general construction exists for all doubly even orders, related to methods described by Kathleen Ollerenshaw and David Brée.
