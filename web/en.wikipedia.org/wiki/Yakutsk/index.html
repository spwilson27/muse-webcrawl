Yakutsk

Yakutsk (Russian: Якутск; Yakut: Дьокуускай, Cokuuskay) is the capital city of the Sakha Republic (Yakutia), Russia, located about 450 km (280 mi) south of the Arctic Circle. Founded in 1632 as a Cossack ostrog (fort) on the Lena River, it is one of the coldest major cities in the world and the largest city built on continuous permafrost.

History

The fort of Lensky ostrog was founded in 1632 by the Cossack Petr Beketov's party during the Russian expansion into Siberia; it was moved to its present site on the left bank of the Lena in the 1640s and renamed Yakutsk. It became the staging point for Russian expeditions to the Pacific, Kamchatka, Chukotka and Russian America. In the 19th and early 20th centuries Yakutsk served as a place of political exile. The city grew rapidly in the Soviet period with the development of the Sakha Republic's diamond, gold and coal industries.

Climate

Yakutsk has an intensely continental subarctic climate. Average January temperatures are around −38 °C, and readings below −50 °C occur; the lowest temperature recorded in the city is about −64 °C. In stark contrast, summers are warm and sometimes hot, with July averages near +20 °C and records above +38 °C — an annual temperature range among the largest of any city on Earth. The city lies in the zone of continuous permafrost, and buildings are raised on piles so their heat does not thaw the ground.

Economy and transport

Yakutsk is the administrative and cultural centre of a republic rich in diamonds (mined by Alrosa) and other minerals. The city has an airport and a river port on the Lena; for much of the year the frozen river serves as an ice road. The Amur–Yakutsk railway reached Nizhny Bestyakh, across the river, in the 2010s, and a long-planned road bridge across the Lena has been repeatedly re-scheduled.
