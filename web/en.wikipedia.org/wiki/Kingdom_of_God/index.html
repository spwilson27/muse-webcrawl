Kingdom of God

The Kingdom of God (or Kingdom of Heaven in Matthew's Gospel) is a central concept in the Abrahamic religions, denoting God's reign or sovereignty over creation. In the New Testament it is the heart of the preaching of Jesus, announced in parables and proclaimed as both a present spiritual reality and a future eschatological hope.

In Judaism

The Hebrew Bible speaks of God as king over Israel and the world, and later Jewish eschatology looked for a future age in which God's rule would be universally acknowledged, associated with the coming of the Messiah and the restoration of Israel.

In Christianity

Jesus's proclamation "the kingdom of God has come near" frames his ministry. Christian traditions differ on whether the kingdom is primarily future, present within the church, realised in believers' lives, or some combination of the "already" and the "not yet." The Lord's Prayer includes the petition "thy kingdom come."

In Islam

Islam affirms God's absolute sovereignty (mulk) over heaven and earth, though the phrase "kingdom of God" plays a less central role than in Christianity.
