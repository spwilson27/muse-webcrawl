United Airlines Flight 1175 was a scheduled passenger flight from San Francisco International Airport to Honolulu International Airport. On 13 February 2018, the Boeing 777-222 operating the flight suffered an uncontained failure of its right Pratt & Whitney PW4077 engine about 120 miles (190 km) from Honolulu. Fan blades separated and pieces of the engine inlet and fan cowl were lost over the Pacific Ocean. The flight crew declared an emergency, continued on the remaining engine, and landed safely in Honolulu with no fatalities.

Incident

Approximately 45 minutes before landing, passengers heard a loud bang and the aircraft began to vibrate. The number 2 (right) engine's fan section failed; the inlet cowl and fan cowls separated from the engine, leaving the core exposed. The crew shut down the engine, descended, and conducted a single-engine approach to Honolulu, landing about 40 minutes after the failure. One passenger sustained a minor injury. Images of the exposed engine, widely shared on social media, drew international attention.

Investigation

The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) investigated. Its final report, adopted in 2020, determined that the probable cause was a fatigue fracture of a hollow fan blade that had not been detected during overhaul inspections. The blade had been inspected at Pratt & Whitney's overhaul facility using thermal acoustic imaging, but a crack indication was mis-evaluated, in part because inspectors had not received formal training and the process lacked adequate standards. Contributing factors included the low probability of detection inherent to the inspection process at the time.

The NTSB recommended improved fan blade inspection training and record-keeping, and the redesign of the 777's fan cowl structure and inlet to better withstand a fan-blade-out event. The incident was frequently compared with the fatal Southwest Airlines Flight 1380 fan blade failure two months later, and with United Airlines Flight 328 in February 2021, another PW4000 fan blade failure on a 777, which prompted the temporary grounding of PW4000-powered 777s worldwide.
