Saints Cyril and Methodius were two Byzantine Christian theologian brothers from Thessalonica who became missionaries among the Slavic peoples of Great Moravia and Pannonia in the 9th century. Through their work they influenced the cultural development of all Slavs, for which they received the title "Apostles to the Slavs". They are credited with devising the Glagolitic alphabet, the first alphabet used to transcribe Old Church Slavonic. After their deaths, their pupils continued their missionary work among other Slavs, and the Cyrillic script — named in honour of Cyril — was developed from the Glagolitic.

Early life

The brothers were born in Thessalonica, in present-day Greece, then part of the Byzantine Empire. Their father Leo was a military officer (droungarios) and their mother Maria may have been of Slavic origin. Cyril, born Constantine, studied at the Imperial University of Constantinople, where he was taught by Photius, and became a librarian at the Hagia Sophia and a teacher of philosophy, earning the epithet "the Philosopher". Methodius, born Michael, served as an archon (governor) of a Slavic district before entering monastic life.

Missions

Around 860 the brothers were sent by Emperor Michael III and Patriarch Photius to the Khazars. In 862, Prince Rostislav of Great Moravia requested missionaries who could teach his people in the Slavonic language. In 863 Cyril and Methodius arrived in Moravia, having devised the Glagolitic alphabet and translated the Gospels and liturgical books into Slavonic. Their mission brought them into conflict with Frankish clergy who insisted on Latin alone.

Rome and later years

Summoned to Rome, the brothers defended the Slavonic liturgy before Pope Adrian II, who approved their work and ordained Methodius and several disciples. Cyril died in Rome in 869 and was buried in the Basilica of San Clemente. Methodius returned to Moravia as archbishop of Sirmium but was imprisoned by the Frankish bishops for two and a half years. After his death in 885, his disciples were expelled from Moravia and found refuge in the First Bulgarian Empire, where the Cyrillic script was developed at the Preslav and Ohrid literary schools.

Legacy

Cyril and Methodius are honoured as saints in both Eastern and Western Christianity. In 1980 Pope John Paul II declared them co-patrons of Europe. Their feast day, 24 May in the modern Bulgarian calendar convention, is celebrated as a national holiday in Bulgaria and North Macedonia honouring Slavic literacy and culture.
