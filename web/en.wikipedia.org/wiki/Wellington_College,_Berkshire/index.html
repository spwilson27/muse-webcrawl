Wellington College, Berkshire

Type: Public school; independent boarding and day school
Established: 1859
Location: Crowthorne, Berkshire, England
Enrolment: ~1,100
Houses: 18

Wellington College is a private (fee-charging) school (English public school) for pupils aged 13 to 18 in the village of Crowthorne, in the county of Berkshire, in England. It was founded in 1859 as a national memorial and educational institution in honour of Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington, and opened by Queen Victoria on 29 January 1859.

The college was built with funds subscribed by the nation and granted a royal charter to educate the sons of deceased officers of the British Army. Its first Master was Edward White Benson, later Archbishop of Canterbury. The campus, set in 400 acres of parkland, includes the chapel and Great School, designed by Sir Gilbert Scott.

Modern college

Today Wellington College is a co-educational boarding and day school of roughly 1,100 pupils organised into 18 houses. It admits pupils at 13+ and 16+, offers both A-levels and the International Baccalaureate Diploma, and is known for its emphasis on pastoral care, leadership and wellbeing education, including a purpose-built wellbeing curriculum. The college is the founding school of a family of affiliated schools overseas trading as Wellington College International, including campuses in China and Thailand, and a sister school, Eagle House, serves younger pupils.

Notable alumni

Old Wellingtonians include Field Marshal Sir Claude Auchinleck, writer Christopher Isherwood, architect Sir Denys Lasdun, and numerous senior military officers reflecting the college's origins.
