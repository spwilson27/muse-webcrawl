Zooxanthellae

Zooxanthellae (singular zooxanthella) are single-celled dinoflagellates that live in symbiosis with a wide range of marine invertebrates, including reef-building corals, sea anemones, jellyfish, giant clams and some sponges and flatworms. The term is a functional grouping rather than a single taxon: most zooxanthellae belong to the family Symbiodiniaceae (the genus Symbiodinium in the older sense), which modern molecular work has divided into several genera, including Symbiodinium, Breviolum, Cladocopium, Durusdinium and Fugacium.

Symbiosis

The algae live inside the host's cells (in gastrodermal cells of corals, within a host-derived vacuole called a symbiosome) and photosynthesise, transferring a large share of the fixed carbon — sugars, glycerol and amino acids — to the host. In return the host supplies inorganic nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus), carbon dioxide and shelter from grazers. This mutualism is the engine of coral reef productivity in nutrient-poor tropical waters: the recycled nutrients allow corals to grow and calcify rapidly enough to build reefs. Zooxanthellae are golden-brown in colour because of the pigment peridinin, and give corals much of their colour.

Coral bleaching

Under stress — especially sustained high water temperature, often combined with strong light — corals expel or digest their zooxanthellae. The loss of the pigmented cells leaves the white calcium carbonate skeleton visible through the transparent tissue: coral bleaching. Bleached corals are starved of most of their photosynthetic income and may die if conditions do not normalise in time. Mass bleaching events, which have become more frequent with ocean warming, are a major threat to reefs. Different symbiont types confer different tolerances; for example, Durusdinium trenchii is more heat-tolerant but may support slower coral growth.

Life cycle

Zooxanthellae alternate between a coccoid, non-motile stage (predominant inside the host) and a smaller flagellated, free-swimming mastigote stage. Hosts may acquire symbionts from the environment (horizontal transmission) or inherit them from the parent via eggs (vertical transmission).
