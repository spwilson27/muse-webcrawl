Oprah's Book Club

Oprah's Book Club selections have frequently become bestsellers.

Oprah's Book Club is a book discussion club segment of the American talk show The Oprah Winfrey Show, highlighting books chosen by host Oprah Winfrey. Winfrey started the book club in 1996, selecting a new novel for viewers to read and discuss each month. The club ran within the show until its conclusion in 2011, and was later revived in new formats, including a version for Apple TV+ launched in 2019.

The club is credited with popularizing book clubs generally and with dramatically boosting sales of chosen titles — an effect often called the "Oprah effect." Selected books routinely sold hundreds of thousands or millions of additional copies, and publishers printed special editions bearing the club's seal. Early selections included Jacquelyn Mitchard's The Deep End of the Ocean (the first pick, in September 1996) and Toni Morrison's Song of Solomon. Notable later picks included East of Eden by John Steinbeck, Anna Karenina by Leo Tolstoy, and Love in the Time of Cholera by Gabriel García Márquez.

Controversies

In 2005, Winfrey selected James Frey's memoir A Million Little Pieces; after reporting revealed that parts of the book were fabricated, Winfrey confronted Frey on air in a widely covered 2006 episode, and the publisher offered refunds. An earlier dispute occurred in 2001 when Jonathan Franzen expressed ambivalence about his novel The Corrections being selected, leading Winfrey to withdraw his invitation to appear on the show.

Later versions

In 2012, Winfrey launched Oprah's Book Club 2.0 in partnership with OWN and O, The Oprah Magazine, beginning with Cheryl Strayed's Wild. In 2019, a new iteration premiered on Apple TV+, whose first selection was The Water Dancer by Ta-Nehisi Coates.
