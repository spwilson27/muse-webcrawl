The Oaks Stakes, commonly known simply as The Oaks or the Epsom Oaks, is a Group 1 flat horse race in Great Britain open to three-year-old fillies. It is run at Epsom Downs over a distance of 1 mile, 4 furlongs and 6 yards (2,420 metres), and it is scheduled to take place each year in late May or early June. It is the second-oldest of the five British Classics, preceded only by the St Leger, and is the fourth of the five to be run each year, following the 2,000 Guineas, the 1,000 Guineas and the Derby.

History

The event is named after The Oaks, an estate located to the east of Epsom which was leased by the 12th Earl of Derby in the 18th century. The race was first run in 1779, one year before the first Derby, and was won by Bridget, owned by the Earl himself. The inaugural running took place during a house party at the estate, at which the companions also devised the Derby.

The Oaks forms the second leg of the English Fillies' Triple Crown, preceded by the 1,000 Guineas and followed by the St Leger. Few fillies attempt all three races; the last to win the fillies' Triple Crown was Oh So Sharp in 1985.

Course

The race is run over the same undulating, cambered course as the Derby, which tests a filly's balance, stamina and temperament. The first part of the race is uphill, followed by a sweeping left-hand descent around Tattenham Corner into the home straight.

Records

The most successful jockey in the race's history is Frank Buckle, with nine victories between 1797 and 1823. The leading trainer is Robert Robson, who prepared thirteen winners in the late 18th and early 19th centuries.
