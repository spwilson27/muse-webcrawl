Combinatorial design
FieldCombinatorics
Key structuresBlock designs, Steiner systems, Latin squares

Combinatorial design theory is the part of combinatorics concerned with the existence, construction, and properties of systems of finite sets whose arrangements satisfy specified balance or symmetry conditions. Such systems are called designs.

Block designs

A central example is the balanced incomplete block design, in which a set of v points is arranged into blocks of size k such that every pair of points occurs together in exactly λ blocks. The Fano plane is the smallest non-trivial example, with 7 points and 7 blocks of size 3.

History

The subject has roots in recreational problems such as Kirkman's schoolgirl problem (1850) and in experimental design in statistics, where R. A. Fisher and others used block designs to structure agricultural trials. It now has applications in coding theory, cryptography, and tournament scheduling.
