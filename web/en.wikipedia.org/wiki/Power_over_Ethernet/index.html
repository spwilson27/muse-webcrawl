Power over Ethernet

Power over Ethernet (PoE) describes standards that pass electric power along with data on twisted-pair Ethernet cabling, allowing a single cable to provide both a data connection and electrical power to devices such as wireless access points, IP cameras and VoIP telephones.

Standards

The original IEEE 802.3af standard (2003) delivers up to 15.4 W per port. IEEE 802.3at (PoE+, 2009) raised this to 30 W, and IEEE 802.3bt (PoE++, 2018) extended delivery to 60 W (Type 3) or 90 W (Type 4) using all four wire pairs, enabling power for devices such as PTZ cameras, video conferencing systems and thin clients.

How it works

Power sourcing equipment (PSE), usually a PoE-capable switch or a midspan injector, detects whether the connected device is PoE-compatible before applying power, and classifies its requirements. Power may be carried on the same pairs as data (phantom power) or, in 10/100 Mb/s networks, on the spare pairs.

Advantages

PoE simplifies installation because devices need no separate mains wiring or adapters, allows centralised backup power from the wiring closet, and makes it easy to relocate or remotely reset networked devices — benefits widely exploited in enterprise networking, security and building automation.
