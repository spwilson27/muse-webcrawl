Kim Dae-jung (Korean: 김대중; 6 January 1924 – 18 August 2009) was a South Korean politician, statesman and activist who served as the eighth president of South Korea from 1998 to 2003. A lifelong opponent of authoritarian rule who survived imprisonment, exile, a death sentence and several assassination attempts, he won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2000 for his work for democracy and human rights and for his "Sunshine Policy" of engagement with North Korea.

Opposition under authoritarian rule

Born on the island of Haui off Korea's south-west coast, Kim entered politics in the 1950s and was first elected to the National Assembly in 1961. As the opposition candidate in the 1971 presidential election, he won over 45 per cent of the vote against Park Chung-hee, alarming the regime. Months later he was seriously injured in a suspicious car crash. In 1973, agents of the Korean Central Intelligence Agency abducted him from a Tokyo hotel — an international scandal — intending, as later revealed, to kill him at sea before international pressure forced his release into house arrest. Under the Chun Doo-hwan regime, Kim was sentenced to death in 1980 on fabricated sedition charges connected with the Gwangju Uprising; American intervention helped commute the sentence, and he was released into exile in the United States in 1982. He returned to Korea in 1985 and, after democratisation in 1987, ran for president twice more, losing while building a liberal political base in the Honam region.

Presidency

Elected in December 1997 amid the Asian financial crisis, Kim took office as the first opposition candidate to win the presidency in South Korean history. His administration accepted an IMF bailout programme, pushed through sweeping reforms of the chaebol conglomerates, banking and labour markets, and achieved an unexpectedly rapid recovery, repaying the IMF loans ahead of schedule in 2001. His government also expanded the social safety net and presided over the spread of broadband internet and the co-hosting of the 2002 FIFA World Cup with Japan.

Sunshine Policy and Nobel Prize

Kim's signature foreign policy, the Sunshine Policy, sought reconciliation with North Korea through dialogue, trade and humanitarian aid, modelled partly on West Germany's Ostpolitik. It culminated in the June 2000 inter-Korean summit in Pyongyang — the first meeting of the two Koreas' leaders — where Kim met Kim Jong-il and signed the June 15th North–South Joint Declaration. In December 2000 he received the Nobel Peace Prize in Oslo. Critics later alleged that Hyundai had secretly transferred hundreds of millions of dollars to the North before the summit, a scandal that marred his legacy without charges against Kim himself.

Death and legacy

Kim died in Seoul on 18 August 2009 of pneumonia and multiple organ failure, and received a six-day state funeral. He is remembered as a founding figure of South Korean democracy, though assessments of the Sunshine Policy's results remain contested amid North Korea's continued nuclear development.
