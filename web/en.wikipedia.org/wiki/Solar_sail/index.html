Solar sail

Solar sails (also called light sails, light sail, and photon sails) are a method of spacecraft propulsion using radiation pressure exerted by sunlight on large surfaces.

Solar sail
PropellantNone (photons)
First demoIKAROS (2010)
MaterialMylar, Kapton, CP1

Principle

Photons carry momentum. When they reflect from a sail, they transfer momentum to the spacecraft, producing a small but continuous acceleration without fuel. Thrust falls with the inverse square of distance from the Sun.

History

The idea was suggested by Johannes Kepler and developed by Konstantin Tsiolkovsky and Fridrikh Tsander in the 1920s. JAXA's IKAROS (2010) was the first successful interplanetary solar sail, flying to Venus. The Planetary Society's LightSail 2 demonstrated controlled solar sailing in Earth orbit in 2019.

Design

Sails are very large, thin films — often tens to hundreds of metres across and only micrometres thick — deployed by booms or centrifugal force. Attitude is controlled by shifting mass, vanes, or electrochromic panels.

Applications

Proposed uses include station-keeping, interplanetary cargo, solar polar orbiters, and, with laser propulsion as in Breakthrough Starshot, interstellar probes.
