Mor lam

Mor lam (Lao: ໝໍລຳ; Thai: หมอลำ, mo lam) is a traditional form of song and performance in Laos and in the predominantly Lao-speaking Isan region of north-eastern Thailand. The term literally means "expert singer" or "expert song", and a performance centres on a singer (mor lam) improvising sung verse — humorous, topical, or drawn from folk tales and Buddhist jataka stories — accompanied by the khaen, a free-reed bamboo mouth organ.

Performance

In its traditional form (lam singing), a male and female singer may engage in a flirtatious sung dialogue or duel (lam phuen), trading improvised verses to the accompaniment of the khaen player (mor khaen). Melodies are built from a small set of stock tunes, and the verbal dexterity, wit, and memory of the singer are paramount. Themes range from courtship and village comedy to moral instruction and epic narratives such as the story of Sang Sinxay.

Modern forms

In the 20th century mor lam developed into large touring troupes (mor lam mu) with elaborate costumes, dance lines, comedy skits, and full folk-opera storylines. In Thailand it blended with luk thung and pop music to produce lam sing — faster, electrified, and often raunchy stage shows with bands combining khaen, phin lute, drums, and synthesisers — which became hugely popular in Isan from the 1980s onward. In Laos, mor lam remains a marker of national culture, and khaen music was inscribed by UNESCO on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2017.
