Selam (Australopithecus)

Selam (catalogue number DIK-1/1) is the fossilised skull and other skeletal remains of a three-year-old Australopithecus afarensis female, whose bones were first found in 2000 in Dikika, in the Afar region of Ethiopia. The child, popularly known as "Lucy's baby" or "Lucy's child", lived about 3.3 million years ago; in the Amharic language, selam means "peace".

Discovery

The fossil was discovered on 10 December 2000 by the Ethiopian palaeoanthropologist Zeresenay Alemseged and his team. The skull was found in several pieces embedded in a block of sandstone; extracting the bones took more than five years of meticulous preparation. The discovery was announced in the journal Nature in September 2006.

Description

Selam's skeleton is remarkably complete, including the whole skull with an impression of the brain, the torso, shoulder blades, and much of the legs and feet. The brain size was estimated at 275–335 cubic centimetres, similar to that of a three-year-old chimpanzee, and its slow growth suggested a prolonged, human-like childhood. Curved finger bones and gorilla-like shoulder blades hint that A. afarensis retained adaptations for climbing, while the knee and foot showed the species walked upright. The fossil preserved a hyoid bone, only the second such fossil in the hominin record, which resembled that of an ape.
