Mediator

Dispute resolution through a mediator is a structured, interactive process in which an impartial third party assists disputing parties in resolving conflict through the use of specialized communication and negotiation techniques.

Definition

A mediator is a neutral third party who helps two or more parties reach a voluntary agreement. Unlike an arbitrator or judge, a mediator does not impose a decision; the parties retain control over the outcome. Mediation is often described as assisted negotiation, and it is a common form of alternative dispute resolution (ADR).

The mediator's role is to facilitate communication, clarify issues, explore options, and help the parties craft a solution they can both accept. Confidentiality is central: discussions in mediation are generally private and cannot be used later in court if no agreement is reached.

The mediation process

Although models vary, mediation typically follows several stages:

  • Opening: the mediator explains the ground rules, confirms neutrality, and sets expectations.
  • Storytelling: each party describes the dispute and its impact, uninterrupted.
  • Issue identification: the mediator distills the dispute into a list of issues to be addressed.
  • Private sessions (caucuses): the mediator may meet separately with each party to explore interests and options confidentially.
  • Negotiation: the parties exchange proposals, often with the mediator shuttling between them.
  • Agreement: any settlement is written down and signed; if it is reached, it is usually enforceable as a contract.

Mediator roles and styles

Mediators may adopt different styles. A facilitative mediator focuses on process, asking questions and helping parties find their own solutions, while an evaluative mediator may offer opinions on the merits and the likely outcome in court. In practice most mediators blend the two approaches, and some traditions, such as transformative mediation, emphasize empowering the parties and improving their relationship rather than producing a settlement alone.

Applications

Mediation is used in family disputes, divorce, workplace conflict, commercial and construction disputes, community disagreements, and international diplomacy. Many courts now require or encourage mediation before trial, and some jurisdictions provide court-annexed mediation programs. It is also used in schools and in restorative justice programs.
