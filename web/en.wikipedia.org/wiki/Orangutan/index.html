Orangutan

Orangutans are great apes of the rainforests of Borneo and Sumatra — the world's largest tree-dwelling animals and the most solitary of the great apes. Three species are recognised, all Critically Endangered.

Life

Adults are covered in long reddish hair; males develop wide cheek pads (flanges). Orangutans move slowly through the canopy using all four limbs (‘quadrumanous scrambling’), eat mainly fruit (especially durian and figs), and build a new sleeping nest each night. They have the longest childhood dependency of any non-human animal — up to 8–9 years with their mother — and are renowned tool users and problem solvers.

Threats

Deforestation for oil-palm plantations, logging, fire and the pet trade have crashed populations; the Tapanuli orangutan, described only in 2017, numbers under 800. Rehabilitation centres raise orphaned youngsters for release. See Conservation biology.
