Thespis

Thespis
OriginIcaria, Attica (according to tradition)
Flourished6th century BC (c. 534 BC)
OccupationPoet, playwright, actor
Known forTraditionally credited as the first actor; first winner of a tragedy competition

Thespis was an Ancient Greek poet, traditionally regarded as the first person ever to appear on stage as an actor playing a character in a play, and thus the inventor of theatre as an art of impersonation. He is also credited as the first winner of a theatrical award, for tragedy at the Great Dionysia of Athens around 534 BC. From his name derives the word thespian, meaning an actor.

Tradition

According to ancient sources including the Parian Marble, Aristotle's Poetics and the later Byzantine encyclopedia the Suda, Thespis came from the Attic deme of Icaria, a place associated with the god Dionysus and the origins of both tragedy and comedy. Before Thespis, performances of dithyrambic chants were purely choral; Thespis is said to have stepped out of the chorus, put on a mask, and spoken as an individual character, engaging the chorus in dialogue — the decisive innovation from which drama developed.

Legend says Thespis toured his plays by wagon around Attica — the source of the expression "Thespis's cart". The lawgiver Solon is said to have objected to his performances as institutionalised lying. Only fragments and titles attributed to Thespis survive, and these are generally considered later forgeries; even his historicity, while widely accepted, rests on late sources.

Legacy

Thespis's innovation — the single actor separate from the chorus — was extended when Aeschylus added a second actor and Sophocles a third, creating the structure of classical tragedy. Actors in the English-speaking world are still called "thespians" in his honour, and the Thespis Award and the Thespians honour society for school theatre preserve his name.
