Chinese Eastern Railway

The Chinese Eastern Railway (CER; Russian: Китайско-Восточная железная дорога) was a railway through northeastern China (Manchuria) built by the Russian Empire between 1897 and 1903. It formed a shortcut for the Trans-Siberian Railway, running from the Siberian border near Manzhouli through Harbin to Vladivostok, with a southern branch to Dalian and Port Arthur (Lüshun). The city of Harbin itself was founded as the railway's construction and administration centre.

Construction

Under a secret 1896 treaty negotiated by Li Hongzhang, Qing China granted Russia a concession to build across Manchuria rather than around it. Construction proceeded from both ends and from Harbin simultaneously; the line opened to traffic in 1901 and was completed in 1903, including the difficult crossing of the Greater Khingan mountains. Russia administered a railway zone along the line with its own police, courts and troops.

Wars and transfers

The railway was a central prize of the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05); after Russia's defeat, the southern branch below Changchun passed to Japan and became the South Manchuria Railway. The remaining CER was contested during the Russian Civil War, jointly managed by the USSR and China from 1924, and briefly fought over in the Sino-Soviet conflict of 1929. In 1935 the Soviet Union sold its share to the Japanese puppet state of Manchukuo. After 1945 the line was jointly operated by China and the USSR until 1952, when the Soviet Union transferred it fully to the People's Republic of China; today it forms part of China Railway as the Harbin–Manzhouli and Harbin–Suifenhe lines.
