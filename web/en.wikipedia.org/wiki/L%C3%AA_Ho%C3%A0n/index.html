Lê Hoàn

Lê Hoàn (941–1005), posthumously known by his temple name Lê Đại Hành, was emperor of Đại Cồ Việt from 981 to 1005 and the founder of the Early Lê dynasty of Vietnam.

Rise to power

Lê Hoàn served as commander of the palace guard under Đinh Bộ Lĩnh, founder of the Đinh dynasty. After the emperor and his eldest son were assassinated in 979 and the child emperor Đinh Toàn faced the threat of invasion by Song dynasty China, the court and Empress Dowager Dương Vân Nga placed Lê Hoàn on the throne.

Reign

In 981, Lê Hoàn repelled the Song invasion, winning victories on land and at the Bạch Đằng River. He also conducted campaigns against Champa to the south, capturing and sacking its capital Indrapura in 982. His reign strengthened the young Vietnamese state, reformed administration and the army, and encouraged agriculture. He died in 1005; after a succession struggle among his sons, the throne passed in 1009 to Lý Công Uẩn, founder of the Lý dynasty.
