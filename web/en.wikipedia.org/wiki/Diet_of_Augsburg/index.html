Diet of Augsburg

The Diets of Augsburg were meetings of the Imperial Diet of the Holy Roman Empire held in the German city of Augsburg. Both an Imperial Diet and the historic evangelical church Congress of Augsburg took place there. The city, a Free Imperial City, had a unique position as a hub of trade and finance, and from 1530 to 1548 it was the site of several diets dealing with the growing Protestant Reformation.

Diet of 1530

The most famous Diet of Augsburg was held in 1530, summoned by Emperor Charles V in an attempt to halt the growing split within Western Christianity. At this diet the Lutheran princes and theologians presented the Augsburg Confession (Confessio Augustana), principally authored by Philipp Melanchthon with the approval of Martin Luther, on 25 June 1530. The emperor's Catholic theologians responded with the Confutatio Augustana, and Charles rejected the Lutheran confession.

Later diets

The Diet of 1547–48 produced the Augsburg Interim, a temporary religious settlement imposed by Charles after his victory in the Schmalkaldic War. The Diet of 1555 resulted in the Peace of Augsburg, which officially recognised Lutheranism and established the principle cuius regio, eius religio — rulers could determine the religion of their own territories.
