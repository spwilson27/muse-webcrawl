Hearing conservation program

A hearing conservation program is a structured set of workplace measures intended to prevent occupational hearing loss from exposure to hazardous noise. In the United States, such programs are required by the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) standard 29 CFR 1910.95 whenever workers are exposed to an 8-hour time-weighted average of 85 decibels or more.

Components

A compliant program includes noise monitoring, baseline and annual audiometric testing, hearing protection (earplugs and earmuffs) made available at no cost, annual worker training, and recordkeeping. Engineering and administrative controls—reducing noise at its source or limiting exposure time—are the preferred long-term measures.

Rationale

Noise-induced hearing loss is gradual, painless, and irreversible, making prevention programs essential in industries such as manufacturing, construction, mining, agriculture, and the military.
