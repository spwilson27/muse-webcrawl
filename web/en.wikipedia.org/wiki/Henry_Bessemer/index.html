Sir Henry Bessemer FRS (19 January 1813 – 15 March 1898) was an English inventor, whose steel-making process would become the most important technique for making steel in the nineteenth century for almost one hundred years. He also played a significant role in establishing the town of Sheffield, nicknamed "Steel City", as a major industrial centre.

Early life and inventions

Born in Charlton, Hertfordshire, Bessemer had little formal education but showed early mechanical talent in his father's type foundry. He moved to London and became a prolific inventor — registering more than 100 patents in his lifetime — in fields from gold powder manufacture and sugar refining to printing stamps and artillery.

The Bessemer process

In 1856 Bessemer patented a process for converting molten pig iron into steel by blowing air through it in a tilting vessel, the Bessemer converter. The air burned out excess carbon and impurities, dramatically cheapening steel production: the price of steel rails fell from around £50 to about £7 per ton. Cheap steel enabled the mass construction of railways, ships, bridges, and skyscrapers. The process was also developed independently in America by William Kelly, and patent disputes followed; Bessemer ultimately received a knighthood in 1879.

Later life

Bessemer built a large steel works in Sheffield, amassed a considerable fortune, and continued inventing into old age, including an (unsuccessful) stabilised "Bessemer saloon ship" intended to prevent seasickness. He died in London in 1898.
