The Socialist Party of America (SPA) was a socialist political party in the United States founded in 1901 by the merger of the Social Democratic Party of Eugene V. Debs and dissidents of the Socialist Labor Party. For its first two decades it was a significant force in American politics, electing two members of Congress, dozens of state legislators and more than a hundred mayors, including in Milwaukee, which had socialist mayors for much of the period 1910–1960.

Early success

The party combined Marxist theory with an American, democratic and often agrarian appeal; its newspaper, the Appeal to Reason, reached hundreds of thousands of readers in small-town America. Debs ran for president five times. In 1912 he won 6 per cent of the national vote — still the highest share ever for a Socialist Party candidate — while the party claimed some 113,000 dues-paying members and over a thousand elected officials across 33 states.

War, repression and decline

The party's opposition to American entry into the First World War brought federal repression: Debs was imprisoned under the Espionage Act for an anti-war speech in 1918 and ran his final campaign from prison in 1920, winning over 900,000 votes. In 1919 the party split over the Russian Revolution, its left wing departing to form communist parties, and membership collapsed. Under Norman Thomas, its presidential candidate from 1928 to 1948, the party revived modestly during the Great Depression but was undercut by Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal, which absorbed many of its proposals and voters.

Later years and legacy

After 1956 the party stopped running its own presidential candidates and pursued a "realignment" strategy of working within the Democratic Party; former members and sympathisers, including Michael Harrington (whose 1962 book The Other America influenced the War on Poverty), shaped liberal politics. In 1972–73 the organisation split into three successors: Social Democrats, USA; the Democratic Socialist Organizing Committee (a forerunner of the Democratic Socialists of America); and the Socialist Party USA, which continues to run candidates today. Many policies the SPA pioneered — social insurance, unemployment relief, minimum wages, the abolition of child labour — became law under other parties' banners.
