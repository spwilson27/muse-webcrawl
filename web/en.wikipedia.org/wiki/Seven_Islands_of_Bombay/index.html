Seven Islands of Bombay

The Seven Islands of Bombay were a group of islands off the west coast of India that formed the original territory of the city of Mumbai (formerly Bombay). Between the 18th and 19th centuries the islands were progressively joined together by land reclamation and causeways into a single landmass, today forming the southern part of Mumbai, bordered by the Arabian Sea to the west and Mumbai Harbour to the east.

The islands

The seven islands, from south to north, were:

  • Colaba (Kolabhat)
  • Old Woman's Island (Little Colaba, Alibag)
  • Bombay Island (the main island, site of Bombay Castle and the Fort)
  • Mazagaon
  • Parel
  • Worli
  • Mahim

History

The islands were inhabited from early times and came under successive Hindu, Muslim, and Portuguese rule; the Portuguese, who took them in 1534, named the harbour Bom Bahia ("good bay"). In 1661 the islands passed to the English Crown as part of the dowry of Catherine of Braganza on her marriage to Charles II, and in 1668 they were leased to the East India Company for £10 per year.

Reclamation

Under Governor William Hornby, the Hornby Vellard project (from 1782) built a causeway across the flats at Mahalaxmi, beginning the unification of the islands. Successive causeways at Sion, Mahim, and elsewhere, together with extensive reclamation of the tidal flats and the filling of the Worli and other creeks, had by about 1845 joined the seven islands into one. Later reclamations merged this landmass with Salsette Island to the north, creating modern Mumbai.
