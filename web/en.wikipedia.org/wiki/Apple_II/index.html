The Apple II (stylized as apple ][) is an 8-bit home computer designed primarily by Steve Wozniak and manufactured by Apple Computer. Introduced at the West Coast Computer Faire in April 1977 and released that June, it was one of the first highly successful mass-produced microcomputers, distinguished by its color graphics, built-in BASIC programming language, and eight internal expansion slots.

History

The original Apple II shipped with 4 KB of RAM and an interface for cassette storage; the Disk II floppy drive (1978) and the spreadsheet program VisiCalc (1979) transformed it into a serious business machine and drove sales dramatically. The Apple II series expanded to include the II Plus (1979), the IIe (1983) — the longest-lived model — the portable IIc (1984), and the 16-bit IIGS (1986). Apple II computers were dominant in American education throughout the 1980s, and the platform remained in production until 1993, with roughly five to six million units sold.

Legacy

The Apple II established Apple as a major company and helped launch the personal computer industry. Its open architecture, documented by Wozniak, fostered a large ecosystem of third-party hardware and software.
