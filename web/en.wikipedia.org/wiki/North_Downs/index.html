North Downs

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The North Downs are a ridge of chalk hills in south east England that stretch from Farnham in Surrey in the west to the White Cliffs of Dover in Kent in the east, a distance of about 153 km (95 mi). They form the northern rim of the Weald anticline and are mirrored to the south by the South Downs across the Weald.

Geography

The downs rise to a maximum elevation of 270 m at Botley Hill in Surrey. Famous viewpoints and summits include Box Hill, Leith Hill (just south of the main ridge), and the Hog's Back near Farnham. The ridge is cut by the river valleys of the Wey, Mole, Darent, Medway, and Stour. Much of the western part lies within the Surrey Hills Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.

Geology

The ridge consists of Cretaceous chalk of the Chalk Group, deposited in a shallow sea about 100 million years ago and uplifted during the Alpine orogeny. The steep south-facing scarp slope and gentler dip slope to the north are characteristic of cuesta landforms in the region.

Routes and history

The North Downs Way, a 246 km National Trail, follows the ridge from Farnham to Dover, partly along the ancient trackway known as the Pilgrims' Way, associated with pilgrims travelling to the shrine of Thomas Becket at Canterbury. During the Cold War and both World Wars, the downs' commanding position south of London made them a line of defensive positions and installations.
