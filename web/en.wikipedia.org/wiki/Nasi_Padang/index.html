Nasi Padang

Nasi Padang
Course: Main course
Place of origin: Indonesia (West Sumatra)
Region: Minangkabau highlands
Serving temperature: Room temperature or warm
Main ingredients: Steamed rice, curry dishes (gulai), rendang, sambal

Nasi Padang is a Minangkabau dish of steamed rice served with a variety of pre-cooked dishes originating from Padang, the capital of West Sumatra, Indonesia. It is one of the most famous exports of Minangkabau cuisine and is served in Padang restaurants (rumah makan Padang) throughout Indonesia and in neighboring Malaysia and Singapore.

Serving styles

There are two main ways Nasi Padang is served. In the pesan (order) method, used for single diners, the customer selects specific dishes from those displayed in the restaurant window, and they are plated with rice. In the hidang (serve) method, used for groups, the waiter brings a dozen or more dishes to the table at once—often carrying many plates stacked along both arms—and diners pay only for the dishes they actually eat.

Typical dishes

The dishes are known for their rich use of coconut milk and chili. Signature items include beef rendang (slow-cooked caramelized beef curry), various gulai (curries) of chicken, fish, offal, and vegetables, ayam pop (skinless fried chicken), dendeng balado (crispy beef with chili), sambal lado (chili relishes), boiled cassava leaves, and sayur nangka (young jackfruit curry). The food reflects the Minangkabau tradition of merantau (migration), in which dishes were designed to keep well on long journeys.

Cultural significance

Padang restaurants are among the most ubiquitous eateries in Indonesia, recognizable by their distinctive stepped-roof (gonjong) architecture modeled on the traditional rumah gadang and by their window displays of stacked plates. Nasi Padang is frequently cited in surveys among Indonesia's most beloved dishes.
