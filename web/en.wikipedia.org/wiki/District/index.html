Districts as administrative divisions

A district is a type of administrative division that in some countries is managed by the local government. Across the world, areas known as "districts" vary greatly in size, spanning regions or counties, several municipalities, subdivisions of municipalities, school district, or political district.

By country

Germany

In Germany, a district (Landkreis, literally "rural district", or kreisfreie Stadt for a city district) is an administrative unit between the Länder (states) and the local municipalities. Most of Germany's districts are rural districts of the first type.

India

In India, a district is an administrative division of a state or union territory, headed by a district collector (deputy commissioner). See district administration (India) and districts of Turkey for comparison.

United States

In the United States, the term district is used for a variety of purposes, including congressional districts, judicial districts, school districts, and special-purpose districts such as water and fire districts.

United Kingdom

In England, districts are a subdivision of counties and the lowest tier of two-tier local government, administered by district councils.
