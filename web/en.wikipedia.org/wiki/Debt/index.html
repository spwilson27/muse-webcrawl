Debt

Debt is an obligation that requires one party, the debtor, to pay money borrowed or withheld from another party, the creditor. Debt may be owed by a sovereign state or country, local government, company, or an individual. Commercial debt is generally subject to contractual terms regarding the amount and timing of repayments of principal and interest. Loans, bonds, notes, and mortgages are all types of debt. In financial accounting, debt is a type of financial transaction, as opposed to equity.

The term can also be used metaphorically to cover moral obligations and other interactions not based on a monetary value. For example, in Western cultures, a person who has been helped by a second person is sometimes said to owe a "debt of gratitude" to the second person.

Terms

Interest

Interest is the fee paid by the borrower to the lender, typically calculated as a percentage of the outstanding principal. Interest rates reflect the risk of default, inflation, and the time value of money. Historically, excessive interest — usury — was condemned by religious and legal codes.

Repayment and default

Debt instruments specify a repayment schedule. Failure to meet obligations is a default, which may lead to penalties, seizure of collateral, bankruptcy proceedings, or, for sovereign borrowers, a debt crisis and restructuring.

Types of debt

  • Secured debt is backed by collateral, such as a mortgage secured on property.
  • Unsecured debt, such as credit card balances, rests only on the borrower's promise to pay.
  • Public debt is owed by governments and is often issued as bonds.
  • Corporate debt includes bonds, loans and commercial paper issued by companies.

History

Debt predates coined money: records of loans in grain and silver survive from ancient Mesopotamia, and debt jubilees — periodic cancellations — are attested in the ancient Near East. Debt systems expanded with banking in medieval and early modern Europe and underpin modern credit economies. Critics, including the anthropologist David Graeber in Debt: The First 5000 Years (2011), have examined debt's moral and social dimensions.
