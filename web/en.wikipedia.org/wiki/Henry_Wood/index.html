Sir Henry Joseph Wood CH (3 March 1869 – 19 August 1944) was an English conductor best known for his association with London's annual series of promenade concerts, known as the Proms. He conducted them for nearly half a century, introducing a vast repertoire of music to British audiences, including works by many contemporary composers. Originally funded by the impresario Robert Newman, the Promenade Concerts were established in 1895 with the aim of reaching a wider audience by offering low ticket prices and an informal atmosphere in which the audience could walk about, eat, and smoke — though Wood's ambition was always to raise musical taste by mixing popular items with challenging new works.

Early life and career

Wood was born in Oxford Street, London, the son of a tradesman who was also a keen amateur musician. He showed early talent as a pianist, violinist, and organist, and studied at the Royal Academy of Music. He began his career as a singing teacher and conductor of amateur choral societies and opera companies, including the Carl Rosa Opera Company. In the early 1890s he conducted oratorio and symphony concerts before being engaged by Robert Newman in 1895 to conduct the new Queen's Hall Promenade Concerts.

The Proms

From 1895 until his death in 1944, Wood conducted the Proms, missing only a handful of concerts. He used the series to introduce British audiences to music by Wagner, Richard Strauss, Debussy, Rachmaninoff, Sibelius, and many others, and gave the British premieres of works by Mahler, Schoenberg, and Scriabin. He also championed British composers, programming works by Elgar, Vaughan Williams, Delius, and Bax.

Wood was an indefatigable worker and a strict trainer of orchestras, insisting on high standards of intonation and ensemble. He campaigned against the "deputy system" under which orchestral players could send substitutes to rehearsals, and he worked to improve conditions and pay for orchestral musicians. He was knighted in 1911, the first British conductor to receive such an honour for services to music.

Other work

Besides the Proms, Wood conducted festivals and concerts throughout Britain and abroad, directed the Queen's Hall Orchestra, and later worked with the London Symphony Orchestra and other ensembles. During the Second World War, after the Queen's Hall was destroyed by bombing in 1941, the Proms moved to the Royal Albert Hall, where they remain. Wood continued conducting until shortly before his death, despite failing eyesight and health; he died in Hitchin in August 1944, a few days after conducting his last Prom.

Legacy

Wood's most famous composition is the Fantasia on British Sea Songs (1905), traditionally performed at the Last Night of the Proms and including "Rule, Britannia!" and the hornpipe "Jack's the Lad". His name is commemorated in the Henry Wood Hall in London, and a bust of Wood, garlanded with flowers, presides over the Last Night of the Proms each year. His autobiography, My Life of Music, was published in 1938.
