The Cleveland Orchestra is an American orchestra based in Cleveland, Ohio. Founded in 1918 by music impresario Adella Prentiss Hughes, it is widely regarded as one of the finest orchestras in the world and is counted among the "Big Five" American orchestras.

History

The orchestra's first music director was the Russian-born conductor Nikolai Sokoloff (1918–1933), followed by Artur Rodziński and Erich Leinsdorf. It was under George Szell, music director from 1946 until his death in 1970, that the orchestra achieved its legendary precision and homogeneous sound; Szell rebuilt the ensemble, extended its touring, and made a celebrated series of recordings for Columbia Records.

Later music directors included Pierre Boulez (principal guest conductor), Lorin Maazel (1972–1982), Christoph von Dohnányi (1984–2002), and Franz Welser-Möst, who has led the orchestra since 2002.

Venues and activities

Since 1931 the orchestra's home has been Severance Hall in Cleveland's University Circle. Its summer home is the Blossom Music Center in Cuyahoga Falls, opened in 1968. The orchestra maintains residencies in Miami and Vienna, tours internationally, and runs extensive education programmes; its recordings under Szell and later conductors have won numerous Grammy Awards.
