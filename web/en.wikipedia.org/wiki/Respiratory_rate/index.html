Respiratory rate

The respiratory rate is the number of breaths a person takes per minute, usually measured at rest. It is one of the four main vital signs, along with blood pressure, pulse and temperature. In adults at rest the normal rate is generally 12 to 20 breaths per minute; rates are higher in children and highest in infants.

AgeTypical resting rate (breaths/min)
Newborn30–60
Infant (to 1 year)30–50
Child (1–5 years)20–30
Adult12–20

Abnormalities

An abnormally rapid rate is called tachypnoea and an abnormally slow rate bradypnoea; complete absence of breathing is apnoea. The respiratory rate rises with fever, exercise, pain, anxiety and many lung and metabolic diseases — notably it compensates for metabolic acidosis — and falls with sleep, sedative drugs and raised intracranial pressure. Characteristic abnormal patterns include Cheyne–Stokes respiration and Kussmaul breathing.

Measurement

The rate is usually counted by observing chest movements for 30 or 60 seconds, ideally without the patient being aware, since conscious attention alters breathing. It is a sensitive early warning sign of deterioration and forms part of early-warning scoring systems used in hospitals.
