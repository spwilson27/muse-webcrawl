Crates of Thebes

Crates of Thebes (Greek: Κράτης ὁ Θηβαῖος; c. 365 – c. 285 BC) was a Greek Cynic philosopher, the principal pupil of Diogenes of Sinope and the teacher of Zeno of Citium, the founder of Stoicism. Born into wealth, he gave away his fortune to live a life of voluntary poverty in Athens.

Life

According to Diogenes Laërtius, Crates renounced a large inheritance, gave his money to the citizens of Thebes, and went to Athens to follow Diogenes. He was nicknamed the "Door-Opener" because he entered houses uninvited to offer advice and reconcile quarrels, and was treated everywhere with respect. His marriage to Hipparchia — who adopted the Cynic life with him against her family's wishes — became famous as a model philosophical partnership; they had a son, Pasicles. Among his works, only fragments survive, including a parody of Homer describing the Cynic's ideal "city of the wallet" (Pera).

Influence

Through his pupil Zeno, Crates forms the bridge between Cynicism and Stoicism: Zeno's doctrine of self-sufficiency and indifference to externals developed directly from Crates' teaching and example.
