Arlington is a city in Tarrant County, Texas, United States, located between Dallas and Fort Worth in the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex. It is one of the largest cities in the United States without a comprehensive public transit system, and is home to the University of Texas at Arlington, major professional sports venues, and the original Six Flags theme park.

History

Arlington was founded in 1876 along the Texas and Pacific Railway and was named after Arlington, Virginia, the home of Robert E. Lee. It grew as an agricultural and cotton-ginning centre, then expanded rapidly after the Second World War with the arrival of a General Motors assembly plant in the 1950s and the growth of the metroplex.

Sports and entertainment

The city's entertainment district hosts AT&T Stadium, home of the Dallas Cowboys, and Globe Life Field, home of the Texas Rangers baseball team, whose earlier ballpark stood nearby. Six Flags Over Texas opened in 1961 as the first park in the Six Flags chain, alongside Hurricane Harbor water park.

Education and economy

The University of Texas at Arlington, founded in 1895, is a major research university. Major employers include General Motors, Texas Health Resources, and the entertainment and hospitality sector.
