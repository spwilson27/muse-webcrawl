Delfont Mackintosh Theatres is a theatre-owning and operating company in London's West End, founded by the producer Cameron Mackintosh and named in part after the impresario Bernard Delfont (Lord Delfont). The company owns and operates a portfolio of major West End playhouses and is one of the largest theatre landlords in London, alongside LW Theatres and the Ambassador Theatre Group.

History

Mackintosh, producer of long-running musicals including Les Misérables, The Phantom of the Opera, Cats and Miss Saigon, began acquiring freeholds of West End theatres in the 1990s to secure homes for his productions and to invest in the fabric of the buildings. The Delfont Mackintosh group grew to include the Prince Edward Theatre, the Prince of Wales Theatre, the Novello Theatre, the Gielgud Theatre, the Queen's Theatre, the Noël Coward Theatre, the Wyndham's Theatre and the Victoria Palace Theatre.

Restorations

The company is known for extensive restorations of its historic auditoriums. The Victoria Palace Theatre was extensively refurbished and reopened in 2017 as the London home of Hamilton. The Queen's Theatre — long the home of Les Misérables — was rebuilt internally and reopened in December 2019 renamed the Sondheim Theatre, in honour of Stephen Sondheim, with a new production of Les Misérables. The Gielgud, Novello and Prince of Wales theatres have also undergone major refurbishment programmes balancing heritage restoration with modern stage technology and improved seating.
