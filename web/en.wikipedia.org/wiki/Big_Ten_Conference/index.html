The Big Ten Conference (stylised as B1G) is the oldest NCAA Division I collegiate athletic conference in the United States. Founded in 1896 as the Intercollegiate Conference of Faculty Representatives (the Western Conference), its members are mostly large public research universities in the Midwest and, after expansion, the coasts.

Members and expansion

Long centred on schools such as Michigan, Ohio State, Wisconsin, Illinois, Purdue, Indiana, Iowa, Minnesota, Northwestern and Michigan State, the conference added Penn State (1990), Nebraska (2011), Maryland and Rutgers (2014), and in 2024 UCLA, USC, Oregon and Washington, bringing membership to 18 despite the historic name.

Sports and significance

The Big Ten sponsors 28 sports and is a "Power" conference in football, with the Ohio State–Michigan rivalry among the most famous in American sport. Its media deals are among the most lucrative in college athletics, and the Big Ten Network, launched in 2007, pioneered conference television channels.
