Cuisine of the United States

The hamburger is one of the most recognized American foods worldwide.

The cuisine of the United States comprises the cooking styles and traditions of the United States. It developed from Native American foodways combined with waves of immigration — British, German, African, Italian, Jewish, Chinese, Mexican and many others — producing both a national fast-food culture and a mosaic of strong regional cuisines.

Regional cuisines

New England is known for seafood, clam chowder and baked beans; the South for barbecue, fried chicken, soul food and low-country cooking; Louisiana for Creole and Cajun dishes such as gumbo and jambalaya; the Southwest and Texas for chili, Tex-Mex and smoked brisket; the Midwest for casseroles, corn and cheese-rich comfort food; and the Pacific Northwest for salmon and farm-to-table cooking. Cities such as New York developed their own icons, including New York-style pizza and the bagel.

Signature foods

Foods strongly associated with the United States include the hamburger, the hot dog, apple pie, macaroni and cheese, buffalo wings, the chocolate chip cookie, peanut butter, and Thanksgiving's roast turkey with stuffing and pumpkin pie. American Chinese, Italian-American and Tex-Mex cooking are hybrid traditions created by immigrant communities adapting Old World dishes to American ingredients.

Fast food and industry

The United States pioneered the modern fast-food industry, franchising and the drive-in restaurant, and American chains spread the hamburger-and-soft-drink format around the globe. In recent decades, farmers' markets, regional revivals and chef-driven movements have pushed back toward local and seasonal cooking.
