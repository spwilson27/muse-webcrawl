Makybe Diva (foaled 21 March 1999) is a champion Australian Thoroughbred racehorse who became the first horse to win the Melbourne Cup on three occasions, in 2003, 2004, and 2005. Retired after her third Cup victory with earnings of more than A$14.5 million, she was at the time the highest-earning racehorse in Australian history and remains one of the most celebrated. She was inducted into the Australian Racing Hall of Fame in 2006, and a statue of her stands at Flemington Racecourse.


Makybe Diva, ridden by Glen Boss, winning her third Melbourne Cup in 2005.

Background

Makybe Diva was bred in England by Emily Krstina (Aust) Pty Ltd and is by the Irish stallion Desert King out of Tugela, a daughter of Riverman. She was purchased as a yearling by South Australian tuna fisherman Tony Šantić, who named her by taking the first two letters of the names of five of his employees — Maureen, Kylie, Belinda, Diane, and Vanessa — to form "Makybe", with "Diva" appended. She began her career with trainer David Hall.

Racing career

After winning staying races including the Werribee Cup and Queen Elizabeth Stakes, Makybe Diva won the 2003 Melbourne Cup (3,200 m) carrying 51.5 kg, ridden by Glen Boss. Transferred to trainer Lee Freedman in 2004, she won the 2004 Cup under 55.5 kg in wet conditions. In 2005 she completed an extraordinary autumn–spring campaign: the Australian Cup and The BMW in the autumn, then in spring the Memsie Stakes, Turnbull Stakes, and the Cox Plate — Australasia's weight-for-age championship — before carrying 58 kg, the highest weight carried by a mare in the race, to win her third Melbourne Cup on 1 November 2005. Šantić announced her retirement in the winner's circle. She is one of only a handful of mares to win the race and the only horse to win it three times.

Breeding career and legacy

At stud, Makybe Diva produced foals by stallions including Galileo, Fusaichi Pegasus, and More Than Ready; none matched her own racing success. Her third Cup triumph is ingrained in Australian sporting culture, and commentator Greg Miles's call of the 2005 race is famous. In 2006 the Victoria Racing Club named the Makybe Diva Stakes, a Group 1 race at Flemington, in her honour.
