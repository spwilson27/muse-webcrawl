A deva (Sanskrit and Pali: देव, deva) in Buddhism is a divine being or god, one of the classes of beings who populate the Buddhist cosmos. Devas live in the heavens (devaloka), enjoy immensely long lives and great pleasure as the fruit of past good karma, but — crucially — they are not immortal, are not creators of the universe, and remain within saṃsāra: when their merit is exhausted they die and are reborn elsewhere, sometimes in lower realms. Buddhism thus accepts the gods of the Indian religious environment while subordinating them to the law of karma and to the Buddha, who is titled "teacher of gods and humans" (satthā devamanussānaṁ).

Cosmology

Buddhist cosmology arranges the heavens in tiers. Within the desire realm (kāmadhātu) are six heavens, including the Heaven of the Thirty-Three (Trāyastriṃśa, ruled by Śakra, the Buddhist form of Indra) and the Tuṣita heaven, where a bodhisattva dwells before his final human birth and where the future Buddha Maitreya is said to reside. Above these stand the progressively more refined heavens of the form realm (rūpadhātu), associated with the meditative absorptions (jhāna), including the Brahmā worlds, and the four formless realms. The great Brahmā of the form realm is depicted in early texts as mistakenly believing himself to be the creator of the world — a delusion the Buddha corrects.

Role in doctrine and practice

Devas appear throughout the scriptures as auditors of the Buddha's sermons, as protectors of the teaching, and as beings who rejoice at human virtue. Because their lives of pleasure can breed complacency, existence as a deva is not the goal of Buddhist practice; a human birth, with its mixture of pleasure and suffering, is considered the most favourable for awakening. In Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna traditions, certain celestial buddhas and bodhisattvas are sometimes loosely called devas in translation, though doctrinally they are distinguished from the saṃsāric devas proper. Merit-making rituals in Theravāda countries often include inviting the devas to witness and share in the merit.
