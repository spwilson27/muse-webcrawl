Overview

The Sicilian Mafia, or Cosa Nostra ("Our Thing"), is a criminal society and confederation of organised-crime families (cosche) originating in Sicily in the 19th century — in the citrus groves, sulfur mines and estates of the island's west, where mafiosi sold protection amid a weak state. It is the original "Mafia", from which the word generalised worldwide, though Italian law and scholarship distinguish Cosa Nostra from the Camorra (Campania), 'Ndrangheta (Calabria) and Sacra Corona Unita (Apulia).

Structure and wars

Initiated members ("men of honour") swear secrecy (omertà). Families control territories and answer to provincial commissions; the interprovincial "Cupola" was rebuilt under Totò Riina's Corleonesi, who won the Second Mafia War (1981–83) and then declared war on the state, murdering the prosecutors Giovanni Falcone and Paolo Borsellino in 1992 — atrocities that provoked a national backlash, mass arrests and the harsh 41-bis prison regime. Under Bernardo Provenzano the organisation turned to submersion (inabissamento); Matteo Messina Denaro, the last great fugitive boss, was captured in 2023 after thirty years and died in custody. Cosa Nostra remains entrenched in extortion (pizzo), public contracts, waste and drug trafficking, though weakened relative to the 'Ndrangheta.
