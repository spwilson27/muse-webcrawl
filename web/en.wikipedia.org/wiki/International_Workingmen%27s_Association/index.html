The International Workingmen's Association (IWA), often called the First International, was an international organisation founded in London in 1864 that aimed to unite working-class movements and socialist, communist and anarchist political groups across countries.

History

The IWA was founded at a meeting at St Martin's Hall, London, attended by workers' delegates from several European countries. Karl Marx drafted its Inaugural Address and provisional rules and became a leading figure on its General Council.

Conflicts and dissolution

Internal conflict between Marx's supporters and anarchists led by Mikhail Bakunin intensified after the suppression of the Paris Commune in 1871. The anarchists were expelled at the Hague Congress of 1872, the General Council was moved to New York, and the organisation was formally dissolved in Philadelphia in 1876.
