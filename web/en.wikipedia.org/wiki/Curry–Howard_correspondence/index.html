Curry–Howard correspondence

Curry–Howard correspondence
Propositions as types, proofs as programs.
FieldType theory, logic, computer science

In programming language theory and proof theory, the Curry–Howard correspondence (also known as the Curry–Howard isomorphism or propositions-as-types) is the observation that there is a direct relationship between programs and mathematical proofs: a proof of a proposition corresponds to a program whose type is that proposition, and checking a proof corresponds to type-checking a program. In its best-known form, intuitionistic proofs correspond to terms of the typed lambda calculus.

The correspondence was first noticed by Haskell Curry, who in 1934 observed that the types of combinators resemble axioms of implicational logic, and developed with Robert Feys in 1958. The general formulation is credited to William Alvin Howard, whose 1969 manuscript (published in 1980) extended the correspondence to full intuitionistic natural deduction and predicate logic. Nicolaas de Bruijn's AUTOMATH project developed similar ideas independently in the late 1960s.

The correspondence

Under this reading, a function that transforms proofs of A into proofs of B is the same thing as a program taking an input of type A and returning a result of type B. Simplification of proofs by cut elimination corresponds precisely to the evaluation of the associated program.

Examples

The identity proof of A → A corresponds to the identity function λx.x. A proof of A ∧ B → B ∧ A that swaps the conjuncts corresponds to a program that destructures a pair and rebuilds it with the components exchanged. Because a proposition with a proof must be inhabited by a term, an unprovable proposition corresponds to a type with no values; the empty type embodies falsity.

Extensions

The correspondence extends in many directions. Girard's System F corresponds to second-order logic; Thierry Coquand and Gérard Huet's calculus of constructions underlies the Coq proof assistant; control operators and continuations (Griffin's observation, 1990) extend the correspondence to classical logic, so that Peirce's law corresponds to the call/cc operator; and exceptions, state and other effects have logical readings as well. In homotopy type theory, types are read as spaces and equalities as paths, generalising the picture further.

Consequences

Practically, the correspondence is the foundation of interactive proof assistants such as Coq, Agda, Lean and Isabelle/HOL-style systems built on type theory: writing a proof is writing a program, and the kernel type-checker verifies proofs mechanically. It also shapes the design of functional programming languages, where richly typed programs can guarantee invariants — "if it type-checks, whole classes of errors are impossible" — and inspires the slogan that programs and proofs are the same thing viewed from two sides.
