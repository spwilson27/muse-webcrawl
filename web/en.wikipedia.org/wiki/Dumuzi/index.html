Dumuzi

Dumuzi (Sumerian: 𒌉𒍣, romanized as Dumuzid) was the ancient Mesopotamian god of shepherds and consort of the goddess Inanna (later Ishtar). He was later also referred to by the West Semitic name Tammuz. In Sumerian mythology his death and his annual return from the underworld echoed the cycle of the seasons.

Mythology

Dumuzi the shepherd is contrasted with Enkimdu, god of farming, in the myth of Inanna's courtship, in which Inanna ultimately chooses Dumuzi. In Inanna's Descent into the Underworld, when Inanna is released she finds Dumuzi has not mourned her, and she delivers him to the demons of the underworld in her place. In later versions Dumuzi's sister Geshtinanna agrees to take his place for half the year, so that he spends six months among the dead and six months in the world above — an explanation for the dry summer and the wet growing season.

Worship

The cult of Dumuzi/Tammuz spread widely; the month named for him, Tammuz, remains in the Hebrew calendar. Ritual lamentations for his death were held in summer, and he is mentioned in the Book of Ezekiel, where women at the temple gate are described as "weeping for Tammuz".
