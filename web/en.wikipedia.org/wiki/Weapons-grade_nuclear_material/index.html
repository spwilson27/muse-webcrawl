Weapons-grade nuclear material

Weapons-grade nuclear material is nuclear material of a purity and isotopic composition suitable for use in nuclear weapons. The two principal materials are highly enriched uranium (HEU) containing roughly 90% or more uranium-235, and weapons-grade plutonium containing at least about 93% plutonium-239. See also weapons-grade.

Uranium grades

Natural uranium contains 0.72% U-235. Enrichment processes such as gaseous diffusion and gas centrifuges raise the U-235 fraction: low-enriched uranium (LEU, below 20% U-235) fuels commercial reactors at typically 3–5%, while HEU above 20% is considered directly usable in principle, with weapons-grade material usually taken as ≥90%. The International Atomic Energy Agency defines a "significant quantity" of HEU as 25 kg of contained U-235.

Plutonium grades

Plutonium produced in reactors contains the fissile isotope Pu-239 together with Pu-240 and heavier isotopes that increase with fuel burnup. Weapons-grade plutonium (≥93% Pu-239) is made in production reactors with short irradiation times; reactor-grade plutonium from spent power-reactor fuel typically contains only 55–70% Pu-239 and more Pu-240, whose spontaneous neutron emission complicates weapon design. The IAEA significant quantity for plutonium is 8 kg.

Production, stockpiles, and safeguards

Five declared nuclear-weapon states accumulated large stockpiles during the Cold War; global inventories of HEU have been estimated around 1,300 tonnes and separated plutonium around 500 tonnes. Programs to reduce these stocks include down-blending HEU to LEU and disposition of surplus plutonium as mixed-oxide fuel. Because the same enrichment technology can produce both LEU and HEU, civilian enrichment and reprocessing plants are subject to international safeguards to detect diversion to weapons use.
