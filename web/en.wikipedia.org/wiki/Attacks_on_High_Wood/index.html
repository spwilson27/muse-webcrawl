Attacks on High Wood

Attacks on High Wood
Date14 July – 15 September 1916
LocationNear Bazentin-le-Petit, Somme, France
Part ofBattle of the Somme, First World War
ResultBritish capture of the wood

The attacks on High Wood were a series of British operations during the Battle of the Somme in 1916 to capture High Wood (known to the British as "High Wood" and in French as Bois des Fourcaux), a wood on high ground between Bazentin-le-Petit and Longueval in northern France. Fighting lasted from 14 July to 15 September 1916, making the wood one of the most contested pieces of ground on the Somme.

Background

High Wood crowned a ridge overlooking the surrounding battlefield, giving its German defenders observation over British lines. It was first attacked during the Battle of Bazentin Ridge on 14 July 1916, when cavalry — the Deccan Horse and 7th Dragoon Guards — made one of the few cavalry charges of the Western Front. British infantry briefly entered the wood but were driven back to its edges.

The fighting

For two months, repeated assaults by successive British divisions gained the wood trench by trench against determined German defence, at the cost of thousands of casualties on both sides. Artillery reduced the trees to splintered stumps. The wood was finally cleared on 15 September 1916 during the Battle of Flers–Courcelette, the attack in which tanks were used for the first time.

Commemoration

The site is marked by memorials, including to the London Scottish and the Black Watch, and several Commonwealth war cemeteries lie nearby. The poet Robert Graves was wounded in the fighting around the wood in July 1916.
