Yarkon River

Yarkon River
Hebrew: נחל הירקון (Nahal HaYarkon)
Country: Israel
Length: about 27.5 km
Source: Rosh HaAyin springs
Mouth: Mediterranean Sea at Tel Aviv

The Yarkon River (Hebrew: נחל הירקון, Nahal HaYarkon; also spelled Jarcon or Nahr al-Auja in Arabic) is a river in central Israel. At about 27.5 km long, it is the largest of Israel's coastal rivers. It rises at the springs of Rosh HaAyin near the Antipatris fortress, flows westward through Petah Tikva and the northern edge of Tel Aviv, and empties into the Mediterranean Sea at Tel Aviv Port.

History

The river's lower course marked the front line between Ottoman and British forces in 1917–18, crossed by General Allenby's troops in the Battle of Jaffa. In the 1950s much of the Yarkon's flow was diverted south through the Yarkon–Negev pipeline to irrigate the Negev desert, sharply reducing the river. The remaining flow was then heavily polluted by sewage and industrial effluent for decades; the dangers became notorious in 1997, when the Maccabiah bridge collapse plunged athletes into the toxic water, causing deaths from infection and poisoning.

Rehabilitation

The Yarkon River Authority, established in 1988, has overseen cleanup, sewage treatment, and restoration. Water quality has improved substantially, fish and birdlife have returned to stretches of the river, and its banks are lined by Yarkon Park (Ganei Yehoshua), Tel Aviv's largest urban park, with lawns, a boating lake, and sports facilities. The upper reaches at Yarkon Springs and Tel Afek form a national park around the Antipatris fortress.
