Integration of Hyderabad

Dates13–18 September 1948
LocationHyderabad State, India
ResultIndian victory; Hyderabad accedes to the Dominion of India
BelligerentsDominion of India vs. Hyderabad State and Razakars

The integration of Hyderabad, codenamed Operation Polo by the Indian military and referred to in India as a "police action", was a military operation in September 1948 in which the Indian Armed Forces invaded the princely state of Hyderabad and annexed it into the Dominion of India. Hyderabad, ruled by the Nizam Mir Osman Ali Khan, had declined to accede to either India or Pakistan at independence and had signed only a standstill agreement with India.

Background

Hyderabad was the largest and richest of the princely states, with a predominantly Hindu population ruled by a Muslim dynasty. Tensions rose through 1947–48 amid the activities of the Razakars, a paramilitary wing of the Majlis-e-Ittehadul Muslimeen under Qasim Razvi, reports of violence against Hindus and of communist-led peasant rebellion in Telangana, and the Nizam's appeal to the United Nations. The Indian government, with Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel as Home Minister, decided on military action.

The operation and aftermath

Indian forces under Major General J. N. Chaudhuri advanced from several directions on 13 September 1948. Hyderabad's forces and the Razakars offered limited resistance, and the state forces surrendered on 18 September. The Nizam withdrew his UN complaint and acceded to India; he was retained as Rajpramukh (ceremonial head) of the new Hyderabad State until 1956, when the state was divided on linguistic lines. The operation was accompanied and followed by widespread communal violence in which, according to the later Sunderlal Committee report, tens of thousands of Muslims were killed — a toll long suppressed in official accounts and still debated by historians.
