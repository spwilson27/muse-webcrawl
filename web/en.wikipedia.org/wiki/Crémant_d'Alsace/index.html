Crémant d'Alsace is a protected designation of origin (AOC) sparkling wine from the Alsace wine region of northeastern France. It is made by the traditional method — a second fermentation in the bottle, as with Champagne — and is the most popular crémant of France, accounting for a large share of all crémant production and roughly a fifth of Alsace's wine output.

Production

Grapes are harvested early and by hand, pressed gently as whole bunches, fermented into a still base wine, then bottled with yeast and sugar for the second fermentation. The wine ages on its lees for a minimum period before disgorgement. Pinot blanc is the workhorse grape, giving fresh, appley wines; rosé versions are made exclusively from Pinot noir.

History

Alsatian sparkling wine production dates to the turn of the 20th century, when producers such as Julien Dopff learned the Champagne method while working in Champagne. The AOC was formally created in 1976, among the first crémant appellations established in France.
