Oxygen–hemoglobin dissociation curve

The oxygen–hemoglobin dissociation curve, also called the oxygen dissociation curve, plots the proportion of hemoglobin in its saturated (oxygen-laden) form against the prevailing oxygen tension (partial pressure).

The sigmoid (S-shaped) curve reflects cooperative binding of oxygen to hemoglobin. Factors shifting the curve include pH (the Bohr effect), carbon dioxide, temperature, and 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate.
