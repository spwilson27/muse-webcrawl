Electronic warfare

Electronic warfare (EW) is any action involving the use of the electromagnetic spectrum (EM spectrum) or directed energy to control the spectrum, attack an enemy, or impede enemy operations. The purpose of electronic warfare is to deny the opponent the advantage of—and ensure friendly unimpeded access to—the EM spectrum. EW can be applied from air, sea, land, and/or space by crewed and uncrewed systems, and can target communication, radar, or other military and civilian assets.

Subdivisions

Electronic warfare is traditionally divided into three areas. Electronic attack (EA) includes jamming of enemy radar and communications, anti-radiation missiles that home on emitters, decoys, and directed-energy weapons. Electronic protection (EP) covers measures to defeat enemy attack, such as frequency hopping, emission control, and hardening. Electronic warfare support (ES) involves intercepting, identifying, and locating enemy emissions to support targeting and threat warning—the function historically called electronic support measures.

History

Electronic warfare began with radio in the early 20th century and matured during the Second World War, when both sides jammed radar and navigation beams in the "Battle of the Beams", used chaff (Window) to blind German air-defence radar, and hunted U-boats by direction-finding their transmissions. It grew through the Cold War—notably in suppressing surface-to-air missile radars in Vietnam and the 1982 Bekaa Valley operation—and remains central to modern combined-arms operations, as underscored by intensive GPS and communications jamming in the Russo-Ukrainian War.

Platforms

Dedicated EW aircraft include the EA-18G Growler and the retired EA-6B Prowler and EF-111 Raven; many combat aircraft carry self-protection jammers and radar-warning receivers, while ground and naval systems perform communications intelligence and jamming roles.
