Project Sunrise is a program by Australian airline Qantas to operate non-stop ultra-long-haul flights from the east coast of Australia (Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane) to London and New York — routes of up to around 19–20 hours that would be among the longest passenger flights in the world. The name alludes to the "Double Sunrise" Catalina flying boat service operated by Qantas between Perth and Ceylon during the Second World War, which saw two sunrises in a single flight.

Development

Qantas chief executive Alan Joyce publicly challenged Airbus and Boeing in 2017 to produce an aircraft capable of flying the routes non-stop with a full payload. In October and November 2019, Qantas operated three research flights using Boeing 787-9s — from New York and London to Sydney — studying crew and passenger wellbeing, including sleep, food and movement programs, in partnership with researchers.

In May 2022 Qantas ordered twelve specially configured Airbus A350-1000 aircraft with additional fuel capacity, reduced seating (238 seats in four classes), and a dedicated wellbeing zone. Entry into service was originally targeted for late 2025, with delays at Airbus pushing first deliveries and commercial launches to 2026–2027. See also Qantas and Ultra long-haul flight.
