Leeds City Council is the local authority for the City of Leeds, a metropolitan borough in West Yorkshire, England. It is one of the largest local authorities in the country, serving a population of over 800,000 people, and is responsible for services including education, social care, housing, planning, waste collection, libraries and highways.

History

Local government in Leeds dates to the borough's incorporation in the seventeenth century. The modern council was formed on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, replacing the former county borough of Leeds and merging it with surrounding districts including Morley, Pudsey, Horsforth, Otley, Rothwell and Garforth. It was initially a district council within the West Yorkshire metropolitan county; when the county council was abolished in 1986, Leeds City Council assumed most of its functions and operates today as a unitary-style metropolitan authority.

Governance

The council has 99 councillors elected from 33 wards, three per ward. It operates a leader and cabinet model: the Leader of the Council chairs an executive board of cabinet members, each holding a portfolio. The Labour Party has controlled the council for much of its modern history. The council is a constituent member of the West Yorkshire Combined Authority, formed in 2014, and works alongside the directly elected Mayor of West Yorkshire, a post created in 2021.

Services and budget

With an annual budget in excess of £1 billion and a workforce of well over ten thousand, the council is one of the largest employers in the city. Major responsibilities include adult and children's social care, council housing (managed through its housing service), the city's schools admissions and support, and cultural institutions such as Leeds Museums and Galleries and the Leeds Grand Theatre.
