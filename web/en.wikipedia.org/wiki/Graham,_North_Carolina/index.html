Graham is a city in Alamance County, North Carolina, United States, and its county seat. It is part of the Burlington metropolitan statistical area in the Piedmont region of the state. The population was 17,153 at the 2020 census.

History

Graham was laid out in 1849 when Alamance County was formed from Orange County, and the town was incorporated in 1851. It was named for William Alexander Graham, a United States senator, governor of North Carolina and later Secretary of the Navy. The Alamance County Courthouse, completed in 1924, anchors the town's courthouse square. In 1870 Graham was the scene of the lynching of Wyatt Outlaw, a Black town commissioner, by the Ku Klux Klan — an event that helped precipitate the Kirk-Holden War declared by Governor William Woods Holden.

Geography

The city lies just south of the Haw River and east of Burlington, along Interstate 40/85. According to the United States Census Bureau, it has a total area of about 9.7 square miles, nearly all of it land.

Demographics

At the 2020 census, Graham had 17,153 residents. The city has grown steadily in recent decades as part of the expanding Piedmont Triad and Research Triangle commuter belt.
