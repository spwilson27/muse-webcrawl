Wall

A wall is a vertical structure that defines an area, carries a load, or provides shelter or security. Walls have been built since the earliest settlements, and they range from the mud-brick walls of ancient houses to the curtain walls of modern skyscrapers and the fortification walls of cities and states.

Functions

Walls serve several overlapping functions. Load-bearing walls support the weight of floors and roofs above them and transfer it to the foundations. Non-load-bearing walls, including partition walls, divide interior space without carrying structural loads. Retaining walls hold back soil or water, while defensive walls protect settlements and borders from attack.

Materials and construction

Traditional walls were built of stone, mud brick, timber, or rammed earth. Brick became the dominant material in many regions after its spread from the ancient Near East. In modern construction, walls are commonly built of concrete block, poured concrete, steel framing with gypsum board, or glass panels. Cavity walls — two leaves of masonry separated by an air gap — improve insulation and weather resistance.

Types

  • Brick walls — laid in bonds such as stretcher, header, English, and Flemish bond.
  • Stone walls — dry stone walls are built without mortar and are common in upland farming regions.
  • Curtain walls — non-structural outer walls of glass and metal hung from a building's frame.
  • Party walls — shared walls between adjoining buildings, governed in many jurisdictions by specific legislation.
  • Retaining walls — engineered to resist lateral earth pressure, often with drainage and reinforcement.

Famous walls

Some of the most famous walls in history include the Great Wall of China, Hadrian's Wall in northern England, the Berlin Wall, the Western Wall in Jerusalem, and the walls of Constantinople. Each illustrates a different purpose: frontier defence, urban fortification, political division, and religious significance.

In culture

Walls feature prominently in idiom and metaphor — "hitting a brick wall", "the writing on the wall", "walls have ears" — and in literature and music, from the walls of Jericho in the Hebrew Bible to Pink Floyd's concept album The Wall (1979).
