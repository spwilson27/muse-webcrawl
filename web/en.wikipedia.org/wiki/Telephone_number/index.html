Telephone number

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A telephone number is a sequence of digits assigned to a fixed-line telephone subscriber station connected to a telephone line or to a wireless electronic telephony device, such as a mobile phone. Telephone numbers serve as addresses in the public switched telephone network (PSTN) and are administered under the international E.164 numbering plan by the ITU.

Structure

A complete international number consists of a country calling code (e.g., +1 for North America, +44 for the United Kingdom) followed by a national significant number, which may include an area code and a subscriber number, for a maximum of 15 digits. Within a country, numbers are often written in national formats, sometimes with a trunk prefix such as 0.

History

Telephone numbers were introduced in 1879 in Lowell, Massachusetts, replacing the practice of asking operators for subscribers by name. Early numbers were short; as networks grew, alphanumeric exchange names (e.g., "MUrray Hill 5") were used before all-number dialling became standard in the 1960s.

Special numbers

Numbering plans reserve ranges for emergency services (911, 112), toll-free calls, premium-rate services, and short codes. Number portability now allows subscribers in many countries to keep their number when changing providers.
