Amortization

Amortization (or amortisation) is a term used in finance and accounting with several related meanings. In lending, amortization is the process of paying off a debt over time through regular payments, so that each payment covers both interest and principal until the loan is fully repaid at maturity. In accounting, amortization refers to the practice of spreading the cost of an intangible asset over its useful life, analogous to depreciation for tangible assets.

An amortizing loan balance declines to zero at maturity.

Amortization of loans

An amortization schedule is a table detailing each periodic payment on an amortizing loan. Early payments consist mostly of interest, while later payments consist mostly of principal, because interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which falls over time. For a fixed-rate mortgage with principal P, periodic interest rate r, and n payments, the level payment is P · r(1+r)ⁿ / ((1+r)ⁿ − 1). Fully amortizing payments retire the debt exactly at the end of the term; partially amortizing loans leave a balloon payment.

Amortization in accounting

Under generally accepted accounting principles, the cost of intangible assets with finite lives — such as patents, copyrights, and capitalized software — is expensed over their useful lives, most often by the straight-line method. Goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles are not amortized under modern standards but are instead tested annually for impairment. Amortization expense reduces reported earnings but is a non-cash charge and is added back in the calculation of EBITDA.

Amortized analysis

In computer science, amortized analysis is a method for evaluating the average cost of operations in a data structure over a sequence, guaranteeing a bound on total cost even when some individual operations are expensive — for example, appending to a dynamically resizing array.
