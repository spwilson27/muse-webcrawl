Collectible card game

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Collectible card games combine card game play with trading card collecting.

A collectible card game (CCG), also called a trading card game (TCG), is a type of card game that mixes strategic deck building elements with trading card features. Typically players purchase cards in randomized booster packs and construct personalized decks from their own collections, which they use to compete against other players.

History

The genre was created by mathematics professor Richard Garfield and launched by Wizards of the Coast with Magic: The Gathering in 1993. Magic became a commercial phenomenon and spawned an industry; later successes included the Pokémon Trading Card Game (1996 in Japan, 1998 internationally) and Yu-Gi-Oh! (1999), which remain among the best-selling games of the type. The success of early CCGs led to a flood of imitators in the mid-1990s, most of which failed.

Gameplay

Most CCGs share common features: cards represent creatures, characters, items, effects, or resources; cards are graded by rarity (common, uncommon, rare, and higher tiers); and players build decks subject to construction rules such as minimum deck size and limits on copies of a card. Games are typically won by reducing an opponent's life total, defeating their characters, or achieving an alternate victory condition printed on specific cards.

Distribution and the secondary market

Cards are sold in starter decks and randomized booster packs. Because desirable rare cards are scarce, a robust secondary market exists in which individual cards can sell for large sums; early Magic cards such as Black Lotus have sold for hundreds of thousands of dollars. Tournament play, organized by publishers and local game stores, is a major part of the hobby.

Digital collectible card games

From the 2010s, digital CCGs such as Hearthstone (2014), Gwent, Magic: The Gathering Arena, and Marvel Snap brought the genre to computers and mobile devices, replacing physical scarcity with in-game currencies and crafting systems.
