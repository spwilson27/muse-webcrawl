Filopodia

Filopodia are slender cytoplasmic projections that extend beyond the leading edge of lamellipodia in migrating cells. Containing parallel bundles of actin filaments, they act as antennae with which a cell explores its environment, probing for chemical cues and points of adhesion.

Structure

A filopodium consists of a tight bundle of long actin filaments cross-linked by the protein fascin, enclosed in the cell membrane. The filaments elongate at the tip through actin polymerization regulated by formins and Ena/VASP proteins, and the whole structure is highly dynamic — filopodia extend, bend, and retract on timescales of seconds to minutes.

Functions

Filopodia sense guidance molecules in processes such as axon pathfinding, where growth cones steer toward targets; they initiate cell–cell contacts and adhesions; and they participate in wound healing, embryonic development, and the immune response, including macrophage surveillance. Excess filopodial activity is also associated with the invasion of cancer cells. Some bacteria and viruses exploit filopodia to move between cells.
