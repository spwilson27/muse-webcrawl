The Battle Creek Sanitarium was a world-renowned health resort and medical institution in Battle Creek, Michigan, United States. Founded in 1866 as the Western Health Reform Institute under Seventh-day Adventist auspices, it was renamed and greatly expanded under its longtime superintendent, Dr. John Harvey Kellogg, who took charge in 1876.

The Kellogg era

Kellogg promoted "biologic living": vegetarian diet, hydrotherapy, exercise, fresh air, and abstinence from alcohol, tobacco, and stimulants. The Sanitarium attracted thousands of wealthy and famous guests, including presidents, industrialists, and entertainers, and at its height employed a staff of more than a thousand. Its dietary experimentation led to the development of flaked breakfast cereals — John Harvey Kellogg's brother, W. K. Kellogg, went on to found the Kellogg Company, which together with C. W. Post's rival firm made Battle Creek the "Cereal City".

Decline

A lavish new main building with distinctive towers opened in 1928, but the Great Depression devastated the institution's finances. The Adventist church had already severed ties with Kellogg in 1907 over theological disputes. The property was sold to the U.S. Army in 1942 and became Percy Jones Army Hospital; today the main building serves as the Hart-Dole-Inouye Federal Center.
