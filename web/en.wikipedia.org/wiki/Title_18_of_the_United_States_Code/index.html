Title 18 of the United States Code

Title 18 of the United States Code is the main criminal code of the federal government of the United States, covering federal crimes and criminal procedure. It was enacted as positive law in 1948, consolidating earlier federal penal statutes.

Contents

Part I (Crimes) defines offences including treason, espionage, murder, robbery, fraud, racketeering (RICO, chapter 96), computer crimes and offences involving civil rights. Part II covers criminal procedure — arrest, trial, sentencing, imprisonment and parole — and Part III addresses federal prisons and correctional institutions. The Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure and the sentencing guidelines operate alongside the code.

Scope

Federal criminal jurisdiction is limited to matters within Congress's constitutional authority; most ordinary crime in the United States remains governed by state law. Title 18 also contains the federal death penalty provisions.
