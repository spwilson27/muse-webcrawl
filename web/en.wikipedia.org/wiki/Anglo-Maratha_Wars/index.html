Anglo-Maratha Wars

The Anglo-Maratha Wars were three wars fought between the British East India Company and the Maratha Confederacy in India between 1775 and 1819. They resulted in the dissolution of the Maratha Confederacy and British control over most of the Indian subcontinent.

First war (1775–1782)

The First Anglo-Maratha War began with the Treaty of Surat, in which the Bombay government backed the claimant Raghunathrao for Peshwa. After the Maratha victory at Wadgaon (1779) and campaigns by Mahadji Shinde, the war ended with the Treaty of Salbai (1782), which largely restored the status quo and gave Britain Salsette island.

Second war (1803–1805)

Renewed conflict followed the Treaty of Bassein (1802), by which the Peshwa Baji Rao II accepted a British subsidiary alliance. The forces of Arthur Wellesley (later Duke of Wellington) won at Assaye and Argaon, while Gerard Lake took Delhi and won at Laswari against the armies of Scindia and Bhonsle, who ceded extensive territories.

Third war (1817–1819)

The final war, coinciding with the Pindari War, saw the Peshwa, Bhonsle and Holkar rise against the British and be defeated in turn. The Peshwa's office was abolished, his territories annexed to the Bombay Presidency, and the Maratha Confederacy ceased to exist as a political power.
