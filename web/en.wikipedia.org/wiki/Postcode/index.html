Postcode

A postcode (known in the United States as a ZIP Code) is a series of letters and/or digits, sometimes including spaces or punctuation, included in a postal address for the purpose of sorting mail. Postcodes identify a specific geographic delivery area, which may be as large as a city district or as small as a single address or group of addresses.

History

Postal districts were introduced in large cities in the 19th century — London was divided into postal districts in 1857. The first modern national postcode system was introduced in Germany in 1941 with a two-digit system. The United Kingdom began introducing its alphanumeric postcodes in 1959, starting in Norwich, and completed nationwide coverage in 1974. The United States introduced ZIP Codes in 1963 and the expanded ZIP+4 in 1983.

Formats

Formats vary by country. UK postcodes are alphanumeric, such as "SW1A 1AA", with an outward code for the area and district and an inward code for a small group of addresses. German and French postcodes are five digits; Canadian postcodes alternate letters and digits ("K1A 0B1"). Many countries use four-digit numeric codes, including Australia and the Netherlands (the latter followed by two letters).

Uses beyond mail

Because they map neatly to geography, postcodes are widely used for statistics, insurance rating, retail catchment analysis, emergency services, and as input data for mapping and navigation software.
