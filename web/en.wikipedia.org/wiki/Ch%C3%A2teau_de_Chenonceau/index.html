Château de Chenonceau

The Château de Chenonceau is a French château spanning the river Cher, near the village of Chenonceaux in the Loire Valley, France. It is one of the best-known châteaux of the Loire and is often called the "Château des Dames" (Ladies' Château) for the succession of women who built, owned, and preserved it.

History

The château was built by Katherine Briçonnet in 1513–1515 and later became the beloved residence of Diane de Poitiers, mistress of King Henry II, who added the famous bridge across the Cher. After Henry's death, his queen, Catherine de' Medici, took the château from Diane and added the gallery over the bridge, creating its iconic appearance. Later owners included Louise Dupin, who saved it during the French Revolution, and the Menier family of chocolatiers, who own it today.

Architecture and gardens

The five-arch bridge carries a two-storey gallery 60 metres long, used as a ballroom and, during the First World War, a hospital ward; in the Second World War the river Cher marked the line between occupied and free France, and the gallery served as a passage between zones. Formal gardens named for Diane de Poitiers and Catherine de' Medici flank the approach.
