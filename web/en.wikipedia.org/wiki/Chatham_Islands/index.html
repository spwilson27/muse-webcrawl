The Chatham Islands are an archipelago in the Pacific Ocean about 800 kilometres (500 mi) east of New Zealand's South Island. The group consists of about ten islands within a radius of roughly 40 km, the largest being Chatham Island and Pitt Island. The islands are part of New Zealand and are administered by the Chatham Islands Council.

The indigenous Moriori people, who call the islands Rēkohu ("misty sun"), settled them centuries before Europeans arrived. The Māori name is Wharekauri. Unusually, the islands keep their own time, 45 minutes ahead of the rest of New Zealand.

Geography

Chatham Island is dominated by the large, shallow Te Whanga Lagoon, which covers much of its northern half. The terrain is generally low and windswept, with peat soils, lakes, and basalt outcrops in the south. Pitt Island, the only other inhabited island of the group, lies to the southeast. The surrounding ocean is rich in fish and shellfish, and the islands are an important site for endemic birds, including the endangered black robin, whose recovery from five individuals on Little Mangere Island is a celebrated conservation success.

History

Moriori developed a distinct, pacifist culture on the islands, governed by the law of Nunuku, which forbade killing. Europeans first reached the islands in 1791, when HMS Chatham, under William Broughton, arrived during the Vancouver Expedition; the islands take their English name from the ship. Sealers and whalers followed.

In 1835 two Māori iwi from the Wellington region, Ngāti Mutunga and Ngāti Tama, invaded the islands, killing and enslaving many Moriori — an event whose legacy remains significant in New Zealand law and in Moriori claims for redress, addressed in part by a Treaty of Waitangi settlement enacted in 2020.

Economy and life

The island economy rests on fishing (notably crayfish, pāua and blue cod), sheep and cattle farming, and a small tourism sector. Waitangi, on Chatham Island, is the main port and settlement. Air Chathams links the islands with Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch. Islanders are New Zealand citizens, vote in the Rongotai electorate (for Māori, the Te Tai Tonga electorate), and the islands are not part of any region of New Zealand, having their own council with near-regional powers.
