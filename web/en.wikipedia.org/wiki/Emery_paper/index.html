Emery paper

Emery paper is a type of coated abrasive that has emery, a hard rock consisting mainly of corundum, glued to a paper or cloth backing. It is similar to sandpaper and is used for hand polishing and finishing metal surfaces. Emery cloth, the cloth-backed form, is tougher and can be torn into strips for use on lathe work and curved surfaces.

Emery is a naturally occurring mixture of corundum and magnetite quarried historically in Turkey and on the Greek island of Naxos. The abrasive grains are dark, almost black, which gives emery paper its characteristic appearance compared with the lighter colour of garnet or aluminium oxide papers.

Grades and uses

Emery paper is graded by grit size from coarse to very fine. Coarse grades remove material quickly and are used for deburring and removing rust or paint; fine grades produce smooth, polished finishes on metal and are used by machinists, jewellers, and instrument makers. Very fine emery is used in the final stages of sharpening and in preparing metal surfaces for plating.

Because emery is somewhat less hard and less friable than modern synthetic abrasives such as silicon carbide, it tends to produce a softer finish on steel rather than deep scratches, which makes it well suited to fitting and finishing work. Emery sticks — wooden sticks wrapped in emery paper — are a traditional tool of gunsmiths and clockmakers.

Related abrasives

Emery powder is also used as the abrasive in emery boards for filing fingernails, though modern boards often substitute garnet or other minerals. Finely graded emery (sometimes sold as "flour emery") is used for polishing lenses and metal optics.
