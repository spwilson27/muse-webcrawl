Continuum mechanics is the branch of mechanics that models materials as continuous media, ignoring their atomic structure. Geometry is treated as smoothly deformable: mass is spread out over a region rather than concentrated in particles.

A material body deformed continuously under load.

Basic concepts

The motion of a body is described by the mapping from a reference configuration to a current configuration. Deformation gradient, strain tensors, and the Cauchy stress tensor quantify how the material stretches and how internal forces act.

Governing equations

Balance laws (mass, linear and angular momentum, energy) plus constitutive equations (e.g. Hooke's law for elasticity, Newtonian viscosity) close the system. The continuity equation supplies mass conservation; Cauchy's momentum equation supplies the force balance.

Branches

  • Solid mechanics: elasticity, plasticity, viscoelasticity.
  • Fluid mechanics: the Navier–Stokes equations for viscous flow, Euler equations for inviscid flow.

History

The field descends from Euler's fluid equations, Augustin Cauchy's stress theory, and the work of Navier and Stokes on viscous media in the 19th century.
