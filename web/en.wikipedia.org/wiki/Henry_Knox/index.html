Henry Knox

Henry Knox (July 25, 1750 – October 25, 1806) was an American military officer, bookseller, and statesman who served as the first United States Secretary of War from 1789 to 1794. A Boston bookseller with a passion for military history, he became George Washington's chief of artillery during the American Revolutionary War.

Revolutionary War

In the winter of 1775–76 Knox led the celebrated "noble train of artillery", hauling some 60 tons of cannon captured at Fort Ticonderoga by sled over roughly 300 miles of snow and ice to Boston, where the guns on Dorchester Heights forced the British evacuation in March 1776. As chief of artillery he served in nearly every major campaign, directed the guns at Yorktown, and founded the Pluckemin cantonment artillery school sometimes called the first American military academy. He succeeded Washington as overall commander of the army at the war's end.

Secretary of War

Under the Confederation and then President Washington, Knox administered military affairs, proposed the creation of a regular legion and coastal defences, and directed Native American policy, advocating negotiated purchase of land and "civilisation" programmes. He retired in 1795 to Thomaston, Maine, where he died in 1806. Fort Knox in Kentucky and the city of Knoxville, Tennessee, bear his name.
