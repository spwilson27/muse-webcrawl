Gytrash

Gytrash
Type: Folkloric spirit
Origin: Northern England (Yorkshire)
Forms: Large dog, horse, mule
Temperament: Benevolent or malevolent

The Gytrash (also Guytrash) is a legendary black dog or spirit of northern English folklore, particularly Yorkshire, said to haunt lonely roads in the form of a large dog, horse, mule, or cow.

Folklore

Unlike many black dog apparitions, the Gytrash could be benevolent, guiding lost travellers to the right path, though it could also frighten or mislead. It is related to the barghest and padfoot traditions.

In literature

Charlotte Brontë described the Gytrash in Jane Eyre (1847), where Rochester's dog Pilot is momentarily mistaken for the creature. The Gytrash also inspired later fantasy references, including in the Harry Potter series, where it is mentioned as a spirit-like dog.
