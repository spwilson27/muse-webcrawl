Four Thirds system

The Four Thirds system is a standard created by Olympus and Kodak for digital single-lens reflex camera (DSLR) design and development. The standard defines the size of the image sensor (4/3 inch type), the lens mount, and communication protocols, allowing lenses and bodies from different manufacturers to interoperate.

History

Announced in 2002 and launched with the Olympus E-1 in 2003, the system was the first DSLR standard designed from the ground up for digital photography rather than adapted from film cameras. Manufacturers producing Four Thirds equipment included Olympus, Panasonic (under the Lumix brand), Leica, Sigma, Fujifilm and Kodak.

Characteristics

The 4/3-type sensor measures about 18 × 13.5 mm, with a crop factor of 2.0 relative to 35 mm film. The smaller sensor permitted smaller, lighter lenses, while the standard's telecentric lens designs aimed to direct light perpendicular to the sensor.

Micro Four Thirds

In 2008 Olympus and Panasonic launched the mirrorless Micro Four Thirds system, using the same sensor size with a shorter flange distance. No new Four Thirds DSLR bodies followed, and the original standard was effectively succeeded by its mirrorless derivative.
