Bardet–Biedl syndrome

Bardet–Biedl syndrome (BBS) is a rare genetic disorder affecting multiple organ systems, classed among the ciliopathies because its genes encode proteins involved in the function of primary cilia. It is named after Georges Bardet and Arthur Biedl. Prevalence is roughly 1 in 100,000 in northern Europe, and higher in some isolated populations.

InheritanceAutosomal recessive
CategoryCiliopathy
Key featuresRetinal dystrophy, obesity, polydactyly, kidney disease

Clinical features

Primary features include rod–cone dystrophy leading to progressive vision loss, postaxial polydactyly (extra digits), truncal obesity, learning difficulties, genital anomalies in males, and kidney abnormalities, which are the main cause of premature death. Secondary features can include diabetes, hypertension, speech delay, and dental anomalies.

Genetics

Mutations in more than twenty BBS genes have been identified. Several of the encoded proteins form the "BBSome", a complex that traffics membrane proteins within cilia.

Management

There is no cure; treatment is supportive, including weight management, low-vision support, and monitoring of renal function. The appetite-suppressing drug setmelanotide has been approved in some jurisdictions for obesity associated with BBS.
