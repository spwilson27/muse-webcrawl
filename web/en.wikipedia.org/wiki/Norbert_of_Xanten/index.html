Overview

Saint Norbert of Xanten (c. 1075 – 6 June 1134), also known as Norbert Gennep, was a bishop of the Catholic Church, founder of the Premonstratensian order of canons regular, and archbishop of Magdeburg. He is venerated as a saint, and his feast day is 6 June.

Life

Norbert was born into a wealthy noble family connected to the imperial court and grew up at Xanten on the Lower Rhine. He received minor orders and obtained a canonry, living a largely worldly life at the court of Emperor Henry V. According to tradition, a near-fatal riding accident during a thunderstorm prompted his conversion to a life of strict religious observance around 1115.

After ordination, Norbert became an itinerant preacher in France and the Low Countries. In 1120, with the support of Pope Callixtus II, he founded a community in the isolated valley of Prémontré near Laon, which became the mother house of the Premonstratensian (Norbertine) order. In 1126 he was appointed archbishop of Magdeburg, where he worked to reform the clergy and extend Christianity among the Wends.

Legacy

Norbert's remains were transferred to Prague during the Thirty Years' War and rest in Strahov Monastery. He was canonised by Pope Gregory XIII in 1582. Norbert is regarded as a patron against childbirth complications and is often depicted holding a monstrance, reflecting his defence of belief in the real presence in the Eucharist.
