The dombra (Kazakh: домбыра, dombyra) is a long-necked, two-stringed lute central to the traditional music of the Kazakhs, Kyrgyz and related peoples of Central Asia. It is considered the national instrument of Kazakhstan.

Construction and playing

The dombra has a pear-shaped or trapezoid wooden body, a long thin neck with tied frets, and two strings traditionally of gut, now often nylon, tuned a fourth or fifth apart. It is played by strumming and plucking with the right hand alone, without a plectrum, allowing percussive rhythms alongside melody. Solo instrumental pieces called kui depict stories, battles and scenes from nature; the 19th-century composer Kurmangazy Sagyrbayuly is the most famous master of the form. The Mongolian variant is known as the tovshuur, and a three-stringed relative, the Kyrgyz komuz, is played similarly.

Cultural status

In 2014 UNESCO inscribed the traditional music of the dombra kui on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Kazakhstan celebrates a National Dombra Day on the first Sunday of July.
