Rice cooker

TypeKitchen appliance
First automatic model1955, Toshiba (Japan)
Power sourceElectricity
UsesCooking rice and other grains; steaming

A rice cooker or rice steamer is an automated kitchen appliance designed to boil or steam rice. It consists of a heat source, a cooking bowl and a thermostat; the bowl is filled with rice and water, and the appliance brings the water to a boil, then maintains a simmer until the water is absorbed, at which point the temperature rises and the thermostat switches the cooker to a "keep warm" mode.

Principle of operation

As long as liquid water remains in the bowl, its temperature cannot rise much above 100 °C (212 °F). Once the rice has absorbed the water, the temperature climbs; a temperature-sensitive switch — in early designs a bimetallic thermostat, in many cookers a magnet that loses its attraction at its Curie temperature — detects the rise and cuts the main heater, switching to a low-power warming element. This simple physical principle makes the classic rice cooker effectively automatic without electronics.

History

Primitive electric rice cookers appeared in Japan in the 1920s and 1930s but were unreliable. The breakthrough came in 1955 when Toshiba introduced the first commercially successful automatic electric rice cooker, developed with a double-pot design that solved the problem of burnt rice. Japanese manufacturers such as Zojirushi, Tiger and National (Panasonic) refined the appliance, which spread throughout East and Southeast Asia and then worldwide. Later innovations include microprocessor-controlled "fuzzy logic" cookers that adjust time and temperature for different kinds of rice, induction heating (IH) models that heat the whole pot evenly, and pressure cookers that cook at higher temperatures for softer texture.

Types and uses

Cookers range from inexpensive single-switch models to high-end induction pressure units. Beyond plain rice, they are used for porridge and congee, steamed vegetables and dumplings (often with a steaming tray), oatmeal, soups and even cakes. In much of Asia the rice cooker is considered an essential appliance; multi-cookers such as the Instant Pot have popularised combined pressure-and-rice cooking in North America and Europe.
