Henri de La Tour d'Auvergne, vicomte de Turenne (11 September 1611 – 27 July 1675), commonly known as Turenne, was a French general and one of only six marshals to have been promoted Marshal General of France. The most illustrious member of the La Tour d'Auvergne family, he achieved military fame and became one of the most important military commanders of early modern Europe, recognised as one of the greatest captains in military history alongside his rival, the Grand Condé.

Early life

Turenne was born at Sedan, the younger son of Henri de La Tour d'Auvergne, Duke of Bouillon, and Elisabeth of Nassau, daughter of William the Silent. Raised a Calvinist, he learned the art of war in the Netherlands under his uncles Maurice of Nassau and Frederick Henry, Prince of Orange, during the Dutch Revolt against Spain.

Career

Entering French service, Turenne distinguished himself in the Thirty Years' War, winning victories at Freiburg (1644) and Nördlingen (1645) alongside Condé. During the Fronde he briefly opposed the court before reconciling with Cardinal Mazarin. He later defeated Condé, then in Spanish service, at the Battle of the Dunes (1658), helping bring the Franco-Spanish War to a close with the Treaty of the Pyrenees.

Death and legacy

Turenne was killed by a cannonball at the Battle of Salzbach in 1675 during the Franco-Dutch War. Napoleon later ranked him among the greatest generals of history. His remains rest at Les Invalides in Paris.
