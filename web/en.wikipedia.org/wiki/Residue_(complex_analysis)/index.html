Residue (complex analysis)

In complex analysis, the residue of a meromorphic function at an isolated singularity is a complex number which describes the behaviour of line integrals along contours enclosing that singularity. Residues can be computed quite easily and, once known, allow the determination of general contour integrals via the residue theorem.

Definition

If f is holomorphic in a punctured neighbourhood of a point a, it has a Laurent series expansion there. The residue of f at a, written Res(f, a), is the coefficient of the (z − a)−1 term in that series. Equivalently, it is (1/2πi) times the integral of f around a small circle centred at a.

Calculating residues

  • At a simple pole, Res(f, a) = limz→a (z − a) f(z).
  • At a pole of order m, the residue is obtained from the (m−1)-th derivative of (z − a)m f(z), divided by (m−1)!.
  • For f = p/q with q having a simple zero at a, Res = p(a)/q′(a).
  • If f is holomorphic at a, the residue is zero. At an essential singularity the Laurent series must generally be computed directly.
