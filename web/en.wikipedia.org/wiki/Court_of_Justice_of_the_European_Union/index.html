Court of Justice of the European Union

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) is the judicial institution of the European Union, seated in Luxembourg. It comprises two courts: the Court of Justice proper (one judge per member state, assisted by advocates general) and the General Court, together ensuring that EU law is interpreted and applied uniformly across the member states.

Role and jurisprudence

The Court hears infringement actions by the Commission against member states, annulment actions against EU acts, and — its most consequential route — preliminary references from national courts on the interpretation or validity of EU law, which bind the referring court and effectively the whole Union. Through this dialogue the Court articulated the system's constitutional doctrines: direct effect (Van Gend en Loos, 1963), supremacy of EU law (Costa v ENEL, 1964), state liability for breaches (Francovich), and general principles including fundamental rights — constructing a legal order national courts now operate daily. Its Charter-era role extends this to rights review of EU and (within EU law's scope) national action, and its rulings on judicial independence in member states have made it a central actor in the rule-of-law crisis within the Union. Critics charge it with integrationist judicial activism; defenders answer that it merely holds governments to the law they made.
