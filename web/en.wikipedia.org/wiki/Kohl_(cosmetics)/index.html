Kohl (cosmetics)

Kohl is an ancient eye cosmetic, traditionally made by grinding stibnite (antimony sulfide) or galena (lead sulfide) into a fine powder. It is worn mainly by women in North Africa, the Middle East, South Asia, and parts of West Africa and the Horn of Africa, and also by some men and children.

History and use

Kohl has been used since the Bronze Age; in ancient Egypt it was applied by all classes to outline the eyes, believed to protect against eye infections, glare, and the evil eye. In South Asia it is known as kajal, surma, or kaajal and is applied even to infants' eyes in some traditions.

Health concerns

Traditional preparations based on lead sulfide have raised toxicity concerns, and several health authorities warn against lead-containing surma, especially for children. Modern commercial kohl and kajal products are generally formulated without lead compounds.
