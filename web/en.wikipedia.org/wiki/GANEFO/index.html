The Games of the New Emerging Forces (GANEFO) were an international multi-sport event established by Indonesian president Sukarno in 1963 as a counterweight to the Olympic Games, which he regarded as a tool of Western imperialism. The name was coined from the English phrase "Games of the New Emerging Forces", itself derived from Sukarno's political concept of the NEFOs ("New Emerging Forces") as opposed to the OLDEFOs ("Old Established Forces").

Background

The immediate trigger for GANEFO was the dispute surrounding the 1962 Asian Games in Jakarta, at which Indonesia refused entry visas to athletes from Israel and Taiwan. The International Olympic Committee (IOC) responded by suspending Indonesia, and Indonesia withdrew from the IOC in 1963. Sukarno announced that Indonesia would organise its own games, open to the "new emerging forces" of Asia, Africa, Latin America and the socialist world.

The 1963 games

The first GANEFO was held in Jakarta from 10 to 22 November 1963. Roughly 2,700 athletes from 51 nations took part, including strong delegations from China, North Korea, North Vietnam and the Soviet Union. China dominated the medal table. Competitions were held in some twenty sports, and the event was staged in the newly built Gelora Bung Karno sports complex, originally constructed for the 1962 Asian Games.

Aftermath

The IOC declared that athletes who competed at GANEFO would be barred from the 1964 Tokyo Olympics, a stance that deepened the split between Indonesia and the Olympic movement. A second, Asian edition of the games was held in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, in 1966, but after Sukarno's fall from power the movement faded, and Indonesia returned to the Olympic fold. GANEFO is remembered as one of the most prominent examples of sport being used as an instrument of Cold War-era geopolitics and of the Non-Aligned Movement.
