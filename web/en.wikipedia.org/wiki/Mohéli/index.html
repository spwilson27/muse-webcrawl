Mohéli, also known as Mwali, is the smallest of the three main islands of the Comoros archipelago in the Indian Ocean, off the south-eastern coast of Africa. It is an autonomous island within the Union of the Comoros. Its capital is Fomboni, and its population is roughly fifty thousand, the smallest of the three islands.

History

Like the other Comoro islands, Mohéli was settled by Austronesian and East African peoples and later influenced by Arab and Persian traders. In the 19th century it was ruled by sultans, notably Ramanataka, a Malagasy prince who took the name Abderremane, and later by Queen Djoumbé Soudi. The island came under French influence in the late 19th century and became part of the French Comoros colony. In 1975 Mohéli joined the independent Comoros state, and it briefly declared its own independence in 1997 before rejoining the union under the 2001 constitution, which granted the islands greater autonomy.

Environment

Mohéli is known for its relatively unspoiled natural environment. The Mohéli National Park, established in 2001 as the first protected area in the Comoros, covers much of the island and a large marine area. Its waters are home to sea turtles, dugongs, humpback whales, and the coelacanth, and the island's forests host the endemic Livingstone's fruit bat.
