Wari Empire

The Wari Empire (Spanish: Huari) was a Middle Horizon polity that flourished in the south-central Andes and coastal regions of modern-day Peru from about 500 to 1000 AD. Centred on the capital city of Huari, near present-day Ayacucho, it was the first pan-Andean state, preceding the Inca Empire by several centuries.

Expansion and administration

Wari expanded from its Ayacucho heartland to control much of the Peruvian highlands and coast, building planned provincial administrative centres — such as Pikillacta near Cusco and Viracochapampa in the north — connected by a road network that the Inca later expanded. Wari administration is associated with orthogonal planned architecture, terrace agriculture, and the spread of a distinctive art style featuring abstract designs derived in part from the earlier Nazca and Tiwanaku traditions. Wari textiles, ceramics and four-cornered hats were produced to exacting standards, possibly in state workshops.

Decline

Around 1000 AD the Wari state fragmented, perhaps under the pressure of prolonged drought, and its cities were abandoned; some sites show evidence of deliberate burning and sealing of doorways. Its legacy — roads, administrative planning, and the spread of Quechua and Andean statecraft — laid foundations on which the Inca later built.
