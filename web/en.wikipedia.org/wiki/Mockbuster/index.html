Mockbuster

A mockbuster (also called a knockbuster or a drafting opportunity) is a film created to exploit the publicity of another major film with a similar title or subject. Mockbusters are often produced on very low budgets and released direct-to-video or on streaming services to coincide with the theatrical release of the blockbuster they imitate.

Characteristics

Mockbusters typically mimic the title, poster art and premise of a popular film closely enough to attract attention from casual buyers and renters, while differing enough — their producers hope — to avoid legal liability for trademark infringement or consumer confusion. They are usually made by small independent studios and shot in a matter of weeks.

Notable producers

The best-known mockbuster studio is The Asylum, an American independent company whose titles include Transmorphers (echoing Transformers), Snakes on a Train (echoing Snakes on a Plane) and Atlantic Rim (echoing Pacific Rim). The Asylum later gained wider fame for the original Sharknado franchise. Other producers include GoodTimes Entertainment, whose animated films imitated Disney releases during the 1990s home-video boom, and various Brazilian and Italian exploitation studios of earlier decades.

Legal issues

Major studios have occasionally sued mockbuster producers. In 2012, Warner Bros. sued The Asylum over Age of the Hobbits, and a court blocked its release under that title; it was renamed Clash of the Empires. Disney obtained an injunction in the United Kingdom against Braver, an imitation of Pixar's Brave, requiring altered packaging.

History

The practice has roots in earlier exploitation cinema — for example, Italian films capitalising on the success of Jaws or Star Wars — but the term "mockbuster" became common in the 2000s with the growth of the direct-to-DVD market and video rental chains, where look-alike titles could be shelved beside the genuine blockbuster.
