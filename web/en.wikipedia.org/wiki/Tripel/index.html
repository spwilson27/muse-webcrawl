Tripel

Tripel is a style of strong pale ale originating in Belgium, most closely associated with Trappist and abbey breweries. The name derives from the Low Countries brewing tradition of marking beer strength with a series — enkel (single), dubbel (double), tripel (triple), and quadrupel — referring roughly to the quantity of malt and resulting alcohol.

History

The modern style was defined by the Trappist brewery of Westmalle, whose Westmalle Tripel (first brewed 1934, reformulated 1956) became the benchmark: a golden beer of about 9.5% ABV, highly carbonated, bottle-conditioned, with a complex spicy, fruity aroma from Belgian yeast and a dry, bitter finish despite its strength.

Characteristics

Tripels are pale gold, use pilsner malt with substantial additions of sugar to raise alcohol while keeping the body light, and are hopped with noble varieties such as Styrian Goldings and Saaz. Typical alcohol content ranges from 7.5% to 11.5% ABV. Well-known examples include Westmalle Tripel, Chimay White (Cinq Cents), and many abbey-style versions brewed worldwide.
