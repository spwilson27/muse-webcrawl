Indian Gorkha

Indian Gorkhas are an ethnic group native to India who speak Nepali as their mother tongue and trace their ancestry to the hills of Nepal and to Gorkha soldiers recruited into British and Indian service. They are concentrated in the Darjeeling and Kalimpong districts of West Bengal, the Dooars, Sikkim, Assam and other north-eastern states, Uttarakhand, and Himachal Pradesh.

History

Gorkha settlement in India expanded after the Anglo-Nepalese War (1814–16), when the British East India Company began raising Gorkha regiments and soldiers settled near cantonments such as Dehradun. Later migration followed employment in tea gardens in Darjeeling and Assam. After Indian independence in 1947, a tripartite agreement between India, Nepal, and Britain divided the Gorkha regiments between the British and Indian armies, and Gorkhas continued to serve in the Indian Army.

Identity and politics

Indian Gorkhas emphasise their Indian citizenship and distinguish themselves from citizens of Nepal, a distinction symbolised by the khukuri and by the slogan of the Gorkhaland movement. Since the 1980s, many in the Darjeeling hills have demanded a separate state of Gorkhaland within India; the agitation led to the creation of the Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council (1988) and later the Gorkhaland Territorial Administration (2011).

Culture

Nepali is a scheduled language of India and an official language in Sikkim and the Darjeeling region. Indian Gorkha culture shares festivals such as Dashain and Tihar with Nepal while incorporating local Indian traditions, and the community has produced notable literature, military figures, and sportsmen.
