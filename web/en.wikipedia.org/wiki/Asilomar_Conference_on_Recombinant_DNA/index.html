Asilomar Conference on Recombinant DNA

Date: 24–27 February 1975

Location: Asilomar Conference Grounds, Pacific Grove, California

Organisers: Paul Berg and colleagues

The Asilomar Conference on Recombinant DNA was a landmark conference organised by Paul Berg and held in February 1975 at the Asilomar Conference Grounds in Pacific Grove, California. About 140 professionals — mostly molecular biologists, with lawyers, physicians and journalists — met to discuss the potential biohazards of recombinant DNA technology and to establish voluntary guidelines for its safe use.

Background

New techniques in the early 1970s allowed scientists to join DNA from different organisms, raising fears that engineered bacteria might escape and cause disease or ecological harm. In 1974 Berg's committee published a letter in Science calling for a voluntary moratorium on certain experiments until the risks could be assessed — an unprecedented act of scientific self-restraint.

Outcome

The conference recommended that research proceed under a graded system of physical containment (laboratories rated P1–P4) and biological containment (enfeebled host organisms unable to survive outside the laboratory), matched to the risk of each experiment, and that some experiments — such as cloning genes for toxins or antibiotic resistance — be deferred entirely. The recommendations formed the basis of the NIH Guidelines issued in 1976 and influenced regulation worldwide.

Legacy

Asilomar became a byword for scientific self-governance, frequently invoked in later debates over gene editing, gain-of-function research and artificial intelligence.
