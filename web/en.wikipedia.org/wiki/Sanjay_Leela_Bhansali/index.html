Sanjay Leela Bhansali (born 24 February 1963) is an Indian filmmaker and music composer, who is known for his work in Hindi cinema. He is one of the most successful filmmakers in Indian cinema, with several of his films among the highest-grossing Indian productions, and the recipient of numerous accolades, including National Film Awards and Filmfare Awards. He was awarded the Padma Shri, India's fourth-highest civilian honour, in 2015.

Bhansali on set, known for his lavish period productions

Career

Bhansali began his career as an assistant to Vidhu Vinod Chopra, working on films such as Parinda (1989) and 1942: A Love Story (1994), the latter of which he co-wrote. His directorial debut, Khamoshi: The Musical (1996), was critically acclaimed. He achieved major commercial success with the romantic drama Hum Dil De Chuke Sanam (1999) and the period romance Devdas (2002), which was screened at the Cannes Film Festival and became one of the biggest Indian hits of its time.

His later films include Black (2005), widely regarded as one of the greatest Indian films; the historical epics Bajirao Mastani (2015) and Padmaavat (2018), the latter among the highest-grossing Indian films of all time; and the biographical crime drama Gangubai Kathiawadi (2022). Through his production company Bhansali Productions, he has also produced television series, and in 2024 he created the Netflix period drama series Heeramandi: The Diamond Bazaar. His work is characterised by opulent set design, operatic emotion, and music he frequently composes himself.
