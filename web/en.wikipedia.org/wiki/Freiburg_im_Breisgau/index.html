Freiburg im Breisgau is a city in Baden-Württemberg, Germany, at the western edge of the Black Forest, close to the borders with France and Switzerland. With a population of roughly 230,000, it is known for its well-preserved medieval old town, its Gothic minster, one of Germany's oldest universities, and its reputation as one of the country's sunniest and most environmentally progressive cities — a pioneer of solar energy and sustainable urban design.

History

Freiburg was founded in 1120 by the dukes of Zähringen as a free market town (its name means "free town/castle") and passed in 1368 to the Habsburgs, under whom it became a centre of the Further Austrian lands. In 1457 Archduke Albert VI founded the University of Freiburg, today one of Germany's leading research universities with around 25,000 students. The city was repeatedly contested between France and Austria in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries and joined the Grand Duchy of Baden in 1805. On 27 November 1944 a British bombing raid devastated the old town, killing some 2,800 people; the city was rebuilt largely on its medieval street plan. The Freiburg Minster, begun around 1200 and famous for its 116-metre open-work spire — called by the art historian Jacob Burckhardt "the most beautiful spire on earth" — survived the war substantially intact.

Geography and sights

The old town is threaded by the Bächle, small water channels fed by the Dreisam river that once supplied water and drained waste and are now a beloved emblem of the city. The Schlossberg hill overlooks the centre, and the Schauinsland mountain (1,284 m), reached by gondola, rises to the south. Freiburg is a gateway for tourism in the southern Black Forest and the Kaiserstuhl wine region.

Sustainability

Freiburg styles itself a "Green City": anti-nuclear protests at nearby Wyhl in the 1970s seeded a strong environmental movement; the Fraunhofer Institute for Solar Energy Systems is headquartered here; and districts such as Vauban — a car-reduced eco-district built from the 1990s on a former French barracks site — and Rieselfeld are internationally cited models of low-energy housing, district heating, cycling infrastructure, and citizen-owned renewable energy.
