Tarbela Dam

Location: Swabi and Haripur districts, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan
River: Indus River
Opened: 1976
Type: Earth-filled embankment dam
Height: 143.26 m (470 ft)
Installed capacity: 4,888 MW (with extensions)

Tarbela Dam is an earth-filled dam along the Indus River in Pakistan's Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province. Located principally in the Swabi district (with part in Haripur district), it is the largest earth-filled dam in the world by structural volume and stands about 143 metres (470 ft) high above the riverbed. Its reservoir, Tarbela Lake, has a surface area of roughly 250 square kilometres (97 sq mi).

History

The dam was built as part of the Indus Basin Project following the 1960 Indus Waters Treaty between Pakistan and India, which allocated the waters of the western rivers of the Indus system to Pakistan. Construction began in 1968 and the dam was completed in 1976, primarily for irrigation water storage and flood control, with hydroelectric generation added as a further benefit.

The project was designed and supervised by an international consortium and constructed with financing arranged through the World Bank and donor countries. Tarbela's reservoir stores monsoon and glacial-melt flows of the Indus for release during the dry winter season to irrigate farmland in the Indus plain.

Power generation

The Tarbela power station originally housed generating units supplied through tunnels through the dam's right abutment. Successive extensions have raised installed capacity: the fourth extension, completed in 2018, brought total capacity to about 4,888 MW, and a fifth extension (1,530 MW) was undertaken to raise capacity further, to roughly 6,418 MW. Tarbela is one of Pakistan's largest sources of electricity.

Sedimentation

The Indus carries an exceptionally heavy sediment load, and the reservoir has steadily lost storage capacity to siltation since filling began. Sediment management, including the possibility of flushing and the construction of upstream dams such as Diamer-Bhasha, has been a continuing concern for the dam's long-term operation.
