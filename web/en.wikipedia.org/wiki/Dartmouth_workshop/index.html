Dartmouth workshop

The Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence, held at Dartmouth College in Hanover, New Hampshire, in the summer of 1956, was an academic workshop widely considered the founding event of artificial intelligence (AI) as a field of research. It was organised by John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester and Claude Shannon, whose 1955 funding proposal to the Rockefeller Foundation coined the term "artificial intelligence".

The proposal

The 1955 proposal asserted that "every aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can in principle be so precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it", and predicted that significant progress could be made in two months by ten researchers. Topics listed included automatic computers, neuron nets, information theory, language, creativity and self-improvement — a remarkably prescient research agenda.

The workshop

Rather than a single conference, the event was a loosely structured summer of discussion and work in Dartmouth's mathematics department. Attendees came and went over the summer; among them were Newell and Simon, who demonstrated the Logic Theorist — arguably the first AI program — and researchers who would dominate the field for decades. Although the workshop fell short of its organisers' production goals, it established the name, community and optimism of the new field.

Legacy

The Dartmouth workshop is commemorated as AI's birthplace; a 50th-anniversary conference (AI@50) was held at Dartmouth in 2006, attended by five of the original participants. The founding optimism of 1956 — that human-level machine intelligence was a generation away — set expectations whose frustration contributed to later "AI winters".
