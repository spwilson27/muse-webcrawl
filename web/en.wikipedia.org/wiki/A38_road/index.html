A38 road

The A38, parts of which are known as the Devon Expressway, is a major A-class trunk road in England, running from Bodmin in Cornwall to Mansfield in Nottinghamshire. At about 292 miles (470 km), it is one of the longest A roads in the country and was formerly the main route from the West Midlands to the South West before being largely superseded by the M5 motorway.

Route

From Bodmin the road crosses Bodmin Moor to Liskeard and Plymouth, then follows the Devon Expressway through Ivybridge and Newton Abbot to Exeter, shadowing the M5 north-east through Taunton's outskirts' corridor (via Wellington and Bridgwater), on through Bristol, Gloucester, Worcester, Bromsgrove, and Birmingham. North of Birmingham it passes Lichfield, Burton upon Trent, and Derby before ending at Mansfield.

History

Much of the route has Roman and turnpike origins. The Devon Expressway section was progressively dualled in the 1960s–1980s, including the replacement of ferries by the Tamar Bridge (1961) between Plymouth and Saltash.
