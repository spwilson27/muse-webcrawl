Pyrazine

Pyrazine
Formula: C4H4N2
Molar mass: 80.09 g/mol
Melting point: 52 °C
Boiling point: 115 °C

Pyrazine is a heterocyclic aromatic organic compound with the molecular formula C4H4N2. Its ring consists of four carbon atoms and two nitrogen atoms in the 1,4-positions (opposite each other), making it a diazine; it is isomeric with pyridazine (1,2) and pyrimidine (1,3). Pyrazine is a colourless to pale-yellow crystalline solid with a characteristic odour.

Properties and occurrence

Pyrazine is a weaker base than pyridine. Derivatives of pyrazine are widespread in nature and food chemistry: alkylpyrazines formed in the Maillard reaction during roasting and cooking are responsible for much of the aroma of coffee, roasted nuts, cocoa, and baked goods. Important pyrazine derivatives include pyrazinamide, an antituberculosis drug, and the pigment and pharmaceutical intermediates built on the pyrazine ring.

Synthesis

Pyrazine can be prepared by the condensation of diamines with dicarbonyl compounds, or industrially from piperazine by catalytic dehydrogenation, and by reactions of ethylenediamine with glyoxal. Many substituted pyrazines are made by self-condensation of α-amino ketones followed by oxidation.
