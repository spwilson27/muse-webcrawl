The DMC DeLorean is a rear-engine two-door, two-seat sports car manufactured and marketed by John DeLorean's DeLorean Motor Company (DMC) for the American market from 1981 until 1983. It is most widely known for its distinctive gull-wing doors and brushed stainless-steel outer body panels, and for its appearance as the time machine in the Back to the Future film franchise.

History

The car was designed by Giorgetto Giugiaro of Italdesign and engineered with assistance from Lotus Cars. Production took place at a purpose-built factory in Dunmurry, a suburb of Belfast in Northern Ireland, financed largely by the British government. Just over 9,000 cars were built before the DeLorean Motor Company entered bankruptcy in late 1982, amid falling sales and John DeLorean's arrest on drug-trafficking charges (of which he was acquitted). The car's 2.85-litre PRV V6 produced about 130 horsepower in US specification, and contemporary reviews criticised its performance relative to its price.

Legacy

The DeLorean's selection as the time machine in Back to the Future (1985) gave it enduring cultural fame far beyond its production run. A large enthusiast community maintains surviving cars, and a Texas-based company acquired the DeLorean Motor Company name and remaining parts inventory, supplying parts and refurbished vehicles.
