Vendée Globe

The Vendée Globe is a single-handed (solo), non-stop, unassisted round-the-world yacht race. Founded in 1989 by French yachtsman Philippe Jeantot, it is held every four years and starts and finishes at Les Sables-d'Olonne in the Vendée department of France, from which the race takes its name. It is widely considered the most demanding event in ocean racing, and is nicknamed the "Everest of the Seas".

An IMOCA 60 yacht racing in the Southern Ocean during the Vendée Globe.

The race

Competitors sail alone around the world, passing south of the Cape of Good Hope, Cape Leeuwin, and Cape Horn, covering roughly 24,000 nautical miles (44,000 km). They may not stop, receive outside assistance, or have any physical contact with another person for the duration of the race. The yachts used are IMOCA 60 monohulls, 60 feet (18.3 m) long.

The course takes the fleet down the Atlantic, across the Southern Ocean around Antarctica — where icebergs, storms, and extreme isolation pose constant danger — and back up the Atlantic. Winning times have fallen from over 109 days in the first edition to around 74 days.

History

The first race, in 1989–90, was won by Titouan Lamazou. Later winners include Christophe Auguin, Michel Desjoyeaux (the only sailor to win twice), Vincent Riou, François Gabart, Armel Le Cléac'h, Yannick Bestaven, and Charlie Dalin. Ellen MacArthur's second-place finish in 2000–01 brought the race worldwide attention.

The race has seen dramatic rescues and tragedies, including the disappearance of Gerry Roufs in 1997 and the mid-ocean rescue of Kevin Escoffier in 2020, whose yacht broke apart and sank in the Southern Ocean.

Records

Armel Le Cléac'h set a record of 74 days, 3 hours in the 2016–17 edition. Charlie Dalin won the 2024–25 edition in a new record of about 64 days 19 hours, becoming the first sailor to complete the course in under 65 days.
