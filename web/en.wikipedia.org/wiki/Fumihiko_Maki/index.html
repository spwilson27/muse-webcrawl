Fumihiko Maki (Japanese: 槇 文彦, Maki Fumihiko, 6 September 1928 – 6 June 2024) was a Japanese architect. A graduate of the University of Tokyo, he studied in the United States at the Cranbrook Academy of Art and Harvard's Graduate School of Design, and worked for SOM and Sert Jackson and Associates before returning to Japan. In 1960 he was a founding member of the Metabolism movement, though his own work remained notably restrained and human-scaled.

Works

Maki founded Maki and Associates in 1965. His long-running Hillside Terrace complex in Daikanyama, Tokyo (built in phases from 1969 to 1992), is regarded as a model of contextual urban design. Other major works include the Spiral Building in Aoyama, Tokyo (1985), the Tokyo Metropolitan Gymnasium (1990), the MIT Media Lab extension in Cambridge, Massachusetts (2009), and Tower 4 of the new World Trade Center in New York (150 Greenwich Street, completed 2013).

Recognition

He received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 1993 and was celebrated for an architecture of lightness, transparency and meticulous detailing in metal and glass, combined with a lifelong theoretical interest in "collective form" and the changing city. He died in June 2024 at the age of 95.
