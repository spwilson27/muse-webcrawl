Anatoly Solovyev

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anatoly Solovyev
Born16 January 1948, Riga, Latvian SSR
OccupationCosmonaut, test pilot
RecordsMost spacewalks (16); most EVA time (82 hours)
Solovyev during one of his sixteen spacewalks.

Anatoly Yakovlevich Solovyev (born 1948) is a retired Russian cosmonaut who holds the world records for the most spacewalks performed by one person — sixteen — and the most time spent on EVA, about 82 hours, accumulated across five missions to Salyut 7, Mir and the early International Space Station era.

Solovyev was a specialist in the hard, unglamorous work of station repair: he flew the 1997 mission that carried out internal repairs after Mir's Progress collision, donning a suit to work inside the depressurised Spektr module, and his EVA experience made him the cosmonaut corps' acknowledged master of the spacewalk.

Career

Selected in 1976 after service as a fighter pilot, Solovyev first flew in 1988 on a short visiting mission to Mir. His long expeditions included the record-setting Mir flights of the 1990s; in 1995 he launched aboard the US Space Shuttle Atlantis for the first Shuttle–Mir docking. He retired from the cosmonaut corps in 1999.
