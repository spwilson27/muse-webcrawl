Chordae tendineae

The chordae tendineae (singular chorda tendinea), colloquially known as the heart strings, are inelastic cords of fibrous connective tissue (tendons) that connect the papillary muscles to the tricuspid valve and the mitral valve in the heart. They are composed mainly of collagen with some elastin.

Function

During ventricular contraction (systole), the papillary muscles contract and tension the chordae tendineae, preventing the leaflets of the atrioventricular valves from prolapsing (everting) into the atria under ventricular pressure. This ensures one-way blood flow and prevents regurgitation into the atria. The chordae are classified as primary (marginal), secondary (intermediate) and tertiary (basal) according to their insertion on the leaflets.

Clinical significance

Rupture of chordae tendineae — from infective endocarditis, myocardial infarction of a papillary muscle, myxomatous degeneration or trauma — can cause acute, severe mitral or tricuspid regurgitation and may require surgical valve repair, including artificial (ePTFE) chordal replacement. Chordal abnormalities are also seen in mitral valve prolapse.
