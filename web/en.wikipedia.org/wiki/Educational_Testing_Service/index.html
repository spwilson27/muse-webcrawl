Educational Testing Service (ETS), founded in 1947, is the world's largest private nonprofit educational testing and assessment organisation. Headquartered near Princeton, New Jersey, it develops and administers standardised tests used in admissions, certification and language assessment in more than 180 countries, and conducts research in psychometrics and educational measurement.

History

ETS was created by the American Council on Education, the Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching and the College Entrance Examination Board, which transferred their testing activities — including the SAT — to the new independent nonprofit. Henry Chauncey, a Harvard dean who had championed aptitude testing, was its first president and a driving force behind large-scale objective testing in American education.

Tests and services

ETS's best-known assessments include the TOEFL test of English proficiency (introduced 1964), the GRE for graduate admissions, the Praxis tests for teacher licensure, the TOEIC test of workplace English, and the AP and SAT programmes it long developed and administered for the College Board (the College Board later moved SAT administration in-house; ETS remains closely associated with the test's development era). ETS administers tens of millions of tests annually and provides scoring, research and assessment services to governments and institutions.

Criticism

As the dominant testing organisation in the United States, ETS has drawn persistent criticism: that standardised tests reflect and reinforce socioeconomic advantage, that its nonprofit status sits uneasily with large revenues and executive salaries, concerns about test security and cheating, and the broader test-optional movement in university admissions. ETS responds that its tests broaden access by giving applicants from unknown schools a common yardstick, and invests in fairness research and free preparation materials.
