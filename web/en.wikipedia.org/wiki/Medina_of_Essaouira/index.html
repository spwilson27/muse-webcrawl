Medina of Essaouira

The Medina of Essaouira, formerly known as Mogador, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the city of Essaouira on Morocco's Atlantic coast. Inscribed in 2001 as an outstanding example of a fortified port town of the late 18th century, it was built according to a regular plan in accordance with the principles of contemporary European military architecture adapted to a North African context.

History

Sultan Mohammed ben Abdallah founded the modern town in the 1760s, employing the French architect Théodore Cornut to lay out the harbour, ramparts and grid of streets. Mogador became Morocco's principal trading port, linking the Sahara caravan trade with Europe, and was home to a large Jewish community of royal merchants.

Features

Within the walls are the Skala de la Ville sea bastion with its row of bronze cannons, the harbour Skala, the mellah (Jewish quarter), mosques, synagogues and the Sidi Mohammed ben Abdallah Museum. The medina's whitewashed houses with blue shutters and its thuya-wood workshops remain a living commercial centre.
