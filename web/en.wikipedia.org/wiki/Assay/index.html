Assay

An assay is an investigative (analytic) procedure in laboratory medicine, mining, pharmacology, environmental biology and molecular biology for assessing or measuring the presence, amount, or functional activity of a target entity (the analyte). Assays range from simple chemical tests to complex automated platforms processing thousands of samples.

Types

In biochemistry and medicine, assays quantify substances such as enzymes, hormones, antibodies and drugs; well-known formats include the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), radioimmunoassay, and the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) for nucleic acids. Bioassays measure the effect of a substance on living cells or organisms. In mining and metallurgy, an assay determines the proportion of precious metal in ore or bullion — traditionally by fire assay — and assay offices test and hallmark precious-metal articles for purity.

Characteristics

A good assay is judged by sensitivity, specificity, precision, accuracy, dynamic range and robustness. Modern high-throughput screening in drug discovery uses miniaturised assays in microplates read by automated instruments.
