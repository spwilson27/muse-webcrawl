Prepositional case

The prepositional case (abbreviated PREP) is a grammatical case found in some languages, most notably Russian, whose use is restricted to contexts following certain prepositions. In Russian grammar it is called predlozhny padezh and is used after the prepositions v ("in"), na ("on"), o/ob ("about"), pri ("in the presence of / attached to") and po (in certain senses), typically expressing location or topic.

Russian

In Russian, the prepositional case is the only case that never appears without a preposition — hence its name. For most nouns its ending is -e (e.g. stol → na stole, "on the table"), though some nouns take a stressed -u in the related locative usage, and feminine nouns in -iya take -i. Because its main function is locative, some grammars call it the locative case; Russian retains remnants of a distinct older locative.

Other languages

The term is occasionally applied to case forms in other Slavic languages and to the "cas prépositionnel" of some grammatical traditions, but it is most standard as a label for the sixth case of Russian and closely related systems.
