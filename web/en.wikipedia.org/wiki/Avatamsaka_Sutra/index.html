Avatamsaka Sutra

The Avataṃsaka Sūtra (Sanskrit: Mahāvaipulya Buddhāvataṃsaka Sūtra, "The Great Expansive Buddha's Flower Garland Sutra") is one of the most influential Mahayana sutras of East Asian Buddhism. It describes a cosmos of infinite realms upon realms, mutually containing one another, and presents the vision of the Buddha Vairocana as the embodiment of the entire universe.

Contents

The sutra is a vast compilation, probably assembled in stages in South or Central Asia during the first centuries CE, and preserved most completely in Chinese translation as the Huayan jing. Its best-known sections circulate as independent texts: the Daśabhūmika Sūtra, describing the ten stages (bhūmis) of the bodhisattva path, and the Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra, which recounts the pilgrimage of the youth Sudhana to fifty-three teachers in quest of enlightenment. A central teaching is the interpenetration of all phenomena, illustrated by the metaphor of Indra's net, in which each jewel reflects all the others.

Influence

The text became the scriptural foundation of the Chinese Huayan school (Japanese: Kegon), whose patriarchs such as Fazang systematised its philosophy of totality and mutual interpenetration. It remains influential in Chinese, Korean, Japanese and Vietnamese Buddhism, and its imagery has inspired art and literature throughout East Asia.
