Chinese New Year, or the Spring Festival (Chinese: 春节; pinyin: Chūnjié), is a festival that celebrates the beginning of a new year on the traditional lunisolar Chinese calendar. It is one of the most important holidays in Chinese culture and is marked by family reunions, feasting, and wishes for prosperity and good fortune in the coming year.

Customs

Celebrations traditionally run from New Year's Eve, with the reunion dinner (年夜饭), to the Lantern Festival on the 15th day. Homes are decorated in red with couplets and the character 福 (fú, "fortune"); elders give children red envelopes (hongbao) containing money; and fireworks and firecrackers are set off, a custom rooted in the legend of the beast Nian, said to be frightened away by loud noises and the colour red.

Zodiac

Each year is associated with one of the twelve animals of the Chinese zodiac — Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig — in a repeating cycle, together with one of the five elements.

Public holiday and migration

The festival triggers the world's largest annual human migration (chunyun), as hundreds of millions of people in China travel to their hometowns for the holiday week. Major celebrations also occur in Chinatowns and diaspora communities worldwide.
