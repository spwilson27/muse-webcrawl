Borel subalgebra

In mathematics, specifically in the theory of Lie algebras, a Borel subalgebra is a maximal solvable subalgebra. The notion is named after Armand Borel, who introduced key results on the structure of such subalgebras in algebraic groups. Borel subalgebras are intimately connected with parabolic subalgebras and with the structure of Lie groups and algebraic groups.

Definition

Let g be a Lie algebra. A subalgebra b of g is called a Borel subalgebra if it is a maximal solvable subalgebra, that is, it is not properly contained in any larger solvable subalgebra. For a finite dimensional Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, Borel's theorems guarantee that a maximal solvable subalgebra exists and that all such subalgebras are conjugate under the adjoint group.

Properties

By Lie's theorem, if the ground field is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, a solvable Lie algebra of operators on a finite dimensional vector space admits a common eigenvector, and by induction the whole algebra can be put in upper triangular form. Consequently every Borel subalgebra of gl(n) is conjugate to the subalgebra of upper triangular matrices. More generally, in a semisimple Lie algebra, each Borel subalgebra contains a Cartan subalgebra, and the quotient of the Borel by its nilradical is abelian.

Relation to algebraic groups

For a connected reductive algebraic group G, a Borel subgroup is a maximal connected solvable algebraic subgroup; its Lie algebra is a Borel subalgebra. The quotient G/B is a projective variety known as the flag variety. Fixing a Borel subalgebra allows one to define the root system conventions of positive roots: the adjoint action of a maximal torus on the Borel determines a system of positive roots, and the Borel is the direct sum of the Cartan subalgebra and the positive root spaces.
