The Ironbridge Gorge is a deep gorge cut by the River Severn in Shropshire, England, formed at the end of the last ice age when meltwaters from Lake Lapworth overflowed and carved through the rock. It is celebrated as one of the birthplaces of the Industrial Revolution: here, in 1709, Abraham Darby I first smelted iron ore with coke at Coalbrookdale, and in 1779–81 the world's first major cast-iron bridge was erected over the river, giving the gorge and the town of Ironbridge their name. The gorge was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.

Industrial history

The gorge exposed seams of coal, ironstone, limestone and fireclay at the surface, while the Severn provided transport to Bristol and the sea. The Coalbrookdale ironworks of the Darby family pioneered coke smelting, cast-iron rails, steam-engine cylinders and the iron wheels and frames that fed the early industrial economy. In 1779 Abraham Darby III cast and erected The Iron Bridge, a 30-metre (100 ft) single span of interlocking cast-iron ribs assembled with carpentry-style joints and no bolts in its main structure; it opened on New Year's Day 1781 and became an instant international symbol of the new industrial age. Nearby settlements — Coalbrookdale, Coalport (with its china works and the Tar Tunnel), Jackfield (tile works) and Broseley (clay pipes and bricks) — formed a dense pioneering industrial landscape linked by inclined planes, canals and early railways such as the Coalport Canal and the Hay Inclined Plane.

The gorge today

After industry declined, the gorge was reclaimed by woodland and is now a noted landscape of industrial archaeology. The Ironbridge Gorge Museum Trust runs ten museum sites, including Blists Hill Victorian Town, the Coalbrookdale Museum of Iron, the Museum of the Gorge, Coalport China Museum, Jackfield Tile Museum and the Darby Houses. The Iron Bridge itself is in the care of English Heritage; a major conservation project in 2017–18 repaired cracks in its ribs. The gorge remains liable to landslips owing to its geology and mining history, monitored through the Ironbridge Gorge stabilisation programme.
