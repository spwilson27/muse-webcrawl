USNS Mercy

USNS Mercy (T-AH-19) is the lead ship of her class of hospital ships in the United States Navy. She is named for the virtue of mercy and is crewed by civilian mariners of the Military Sealift Command, with a naval medical staff embarked when activated. Converted from a San Clemente-class supertanker, she entered service as a hospital ship in 1986.

ClassMercy-class hospital ship

CapacityUp to 1,000 patient beds

Capabilities

Mercy provides rapid, mobile acute medical and surgical care in support of military operations and disaster relief. When fully staffed, her medical treatment facility can support up to 1,000 beds, twelve operating rooms, radiology and laboratory services, a blood bank, and intensive care units, with a medical staff that can exceed 1,200 personnel.

Service

The ship supported Operations Desert Shield and Desert Storm in 1990–91. She later deployed repeatedly for Pacific Partnership humanitarian missions in Southeast Asia and Oceania, responded to the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami relief effort, and in 2020 was deployed to Los Angeles to provide hospital capacity during the COVID-19 pandemic. Her sister ship is USNS Comfort, based on the East Coast.
