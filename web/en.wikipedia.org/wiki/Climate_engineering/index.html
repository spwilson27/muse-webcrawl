Climate engineering

Climate engineering (also called geoengineering) is the deliberate large-scale intervention in the Earth's climate system, intended to counteract human-caused climate change. The term encompasses two broad categories of approaches: solar radiation modification (SRM), which seeks to reflect a small fraction of sunlight back to space, and carbon dioxide removal (CDR), which seeks to remove carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.

Proposed methods

Solar radiation modification proposals include stratospheric aerosol injection, marine cloud brightening, and space-based reflectors. Carbon dioxide removal proposals include afforestation, bioenergy with carbon capture and storage (BECCS), direct air capture, ocean fertilization, and enhanced weathering.

Risks and governance

Climate engineering raises significant ethical, political, and environmental concerns. Solar radiation modification could alter regional precipitation patterns and would not address ocean acidification; if stopped abruptly, rapid "termination shock" warming could follow. The IPCC has assessed these approaches with caution, and there is no international governance framework specifically regulating deployment.
