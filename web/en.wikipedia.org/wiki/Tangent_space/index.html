Tangent space

In mathematics, the tangent space of a manifold is a vector space attached to each point of the manifold that generalises the notion of a tangent line to a curve or a tangent plane to a surface. If the manifold has dimension n, the tangent space at each point is an n-dimensional vector space. Its elements, called tangent vectors, represent the possible directions in which one can pass through the point along a curve lying in the manifold.

Definitions

There are several equivalent definitions. Informally, a tangent vector at a point p of a manifold M can be defined as the velocity vector of a smooth curve in M passing through p, with two curves identified if they have the same velocity at p in some (hence any) coordinate chart. More algebraically, a tangent vector is a derivation: a linear map from the algebra of smooth functions on M to the real numbers that obeys the Leibniz product rule and evaluates functions at p. In algebraic geometry, the tangent space at a point of a scheme is defined via the dual of the quotient 𝔪/𝔪² of the maximal ideal of the local ring.

Properties

The tangent spaces at all points of a manifold together form the tangent bundle, and vector fields are sections of this bundle. The derivative (differential) of a smooth map between manifolds is a linear map between the corresponding tangent spaces. The dual of the tangent space is the cotangent space, whose elements are covectors; the pairing between them underlies the definitions of differential forms and tensors.
