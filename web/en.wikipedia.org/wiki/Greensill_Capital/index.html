Greensill Capital

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Greensill Capital was a financial services company based in the United Kingdom and Australia that specialised in supply chain financing. Founded in 2011 by Lex Greensill, it grew rapidly, claiming to provide over $150 billion of financing to businesses worldwide, and was valued at around $7 billion at its peak.

Business model

Greensill bought receivables owed to suppliers by large buyers, paid suppliers early at a small discount, and packaged the debts into notes sold to investors, notably through funds managed by Credit Suisse totalling about $10 billion. Its largest client was Sanjeev Gupta's GFG Alliance steel and metals group.

Collapse

In March 2021, after its credit insurer declined to renew cover, Credit Suisse froze its Greensill-linked funds and Greensill filed for administration within days; its German bank was closed by BaFin. The collapse threatened thousands of jobs at GFG's Liberty Steel plants and left investors facing heavy losses.

Lobbying scandal

Greensill had employed former prime minister David Cameron as an adviser; his lobbying of ministers and officials on the firm's behalf in 2020 triggered a major scandal about lobbying and access in British politics, leading to official inquiries and reforms to lobbying rules.
