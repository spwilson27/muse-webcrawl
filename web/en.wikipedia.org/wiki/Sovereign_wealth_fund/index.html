Sovereign wealth fund

A sovereign wealth fund (SWF), also called a sovereign investment fund, is a state-owned investment fund that invests in real and financial assets such as stocks, bonds, real estate, precious metals, or alternative investments such as private equity and hedge funds. Sovereign wealth funds invest globally and are typically funded from a country's balance-of-payments surpluses, official foreign currency operations, proceeds of privatisations, government transfer payments, fiscal surpluses, or revenues from resource exports.

History

The term sovereign wealth fund was first used in 2005 by Andrew Rozanov. Some of the oldest funds date to the nineteenth century, but the modern era began with the Kuwait Investment Authority, founded in 1953 to invest surplus oil revenues, and the Kiribati Revenue Equalization Reserve Fund of 1956. The number and size of funds grew rapidly in the 2000s as oil and gas prices rose and Asian exporters accumulated foreign-exchange reserves.

Largest funds

By assets under management, the largest sovereign wealth funds include Norway's Government Pension Fund Global (the "Oil Fund"), built from petroleum revenues and holding roughly 1.5 per cent of all listed equities worldwide; the China Investment Corporation; funds of the United Arab Emirates, notably the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority; Kuwait's Future Generations Fund; and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund. Collectively, sovereign wealth funds manage well over US$11 trillion.

Objectives and concerns

Funds are created to stabilise government budgets, save resource wealth for future generations, and diversify national economies. Their size and state ownership have raised concerns about transparency, governance and political motives for investments abroad, leading to the 2008 Santiago Principles, a voluntary code of best practice adopted by the International Forum of Sovereign Wealth Funds.
