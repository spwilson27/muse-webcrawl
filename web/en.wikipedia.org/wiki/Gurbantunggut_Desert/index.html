Gurbantunggut Desert

The Gurbantunggut Desert (Chinese: 古尔班通古特沙漠) is a desert located in the Junggar Basin in northern Xinjiang, China. It covers an area of about 48,800 square kilometres (18,800 sq mi), making it the second-largest desert in China after the Taklamakan Desert.

Geography

The desert occupies the centre of the Junggar Basin, between the Altai Mountains to the north and the Tian Shan range to the south. Unlike many deserts, much of the Gurbantunggut consists of fixed and semi-fixed sand dunes stabilized by biological soil crusts and sparse vegetation, including saxaul (Haloxylon) shrubs and tamarisk. Annual precipitation is roughly 70–150 mm, much of it falling as snow in winter, which allows a surprising diversity of ephemeral spring plants.

Ecology

The desert supports desert-adapted wildlife including goitered gazelles, wild asses (khulan), and various rodents and reptiles. Parts of the desert fall within protected areas, and the peripheral oases and shrublands are used for seasonal grazing by Kazakh and Mongol herders.

Human activity

The Karamay oil fields lie near the desert's western margin, and oil and gas extraction, along with associated roads and pipelines, is the principal industrial activity in the region. National Highway 216 crosses the desert from north to south, linking Altay with Ürümqi.
