Sarnath is a locality in Varanasi district, Uttar Pradesh, India, about 10 kilometres northeast of the city of Varanasi. It is one of the four most important pilgrimage sites in Buddhism, revered as the place where Gautama Buddha delivered his first sermon, the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta ("Setting in Motion the Wheel of Dharma"), to his five former companions after his enlightenment at Bodh Gaya.

The Dhamek Stupa, marking the site of the Buddha's first sermon.

History

In ancient times Sarnath was known as Isipatana and as Migadaya, the Deer Park, where according to tradition the Buddha-to-be had in a previous life been a deer king. After the first sermon, the site grew into a major monastic centre. The emperor Ashoka built stupas and raised one of his inscribed pillars there in the 3rd century BC; the pillar's lion capital eventually became the national emblem of India, and its wheel, the Ashoka Chakra, appears on the Indian flag. Monasteries flourished under the Gupta dynasty, and the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang described a large and prosperous community in the 7th century AD. The complex was destroyed during the invasions of the 12th century and was excavated by British archaeologists in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Main sites

The principal monument is the Dhamek Stupa, a massive cylindrical tower marking the spot of the first sermon. Nearby stand the ruins of the Ashoka Pillar, the Dharmarajika Stupa, temples built by Buddhist communities from many countries, and the Sarnath Archaeological Museum, which houses the original Lion Capital of Ashoka.
