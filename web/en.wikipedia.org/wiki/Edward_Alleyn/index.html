Edward "Ned" Alleyn (1 September 1566 – 25 November 1626) was an English actor who was a major figure of the Elizabethan theatre and the founder of Dulwich College and Alleyn's School in Dulwich.

Career

Alleyn was the leading actor of the Admiral's Men, the great rival company to William Shakespeare's Lord Chamberlain's Men. He created the title roles in Christopher Marlowe's plays Tamburlaine the Great, Doctor Faustus, and The Jew of Malta, and starred in The Battle of Alcazar and Robert Greene's Orlando Furioso. Ben Jonson and Thomas Dekker praised his acting; Thomas Heywood called him "inimitable" and the best actor of his generation alongside Richard Burbage.

In 1592 Alleyn married Joan Woodward, stepdaughter of the theatre entrepreneur Philip Henslowe. With Henslowe he became a partner in lucrative entertainment ventures, including the Rose Theatre, the Fortune Theatre (built in 1600), bear-baiting arenas, and the Hope Theatre. His papers, together with Henslowe's diary, survive at Dulwich College and are the single most important source of information about the Elizabethan theatre business.

Later life and philanthropy

Alleyn retired from the stage around 1598, returned briefly, and finally left acting by about 1604, amassing a considerable fortune from his theatre interests. In 1605 he purchased the manor of Dulwich, and in 1619 he founded the College of God's Gift at Dulwich — now Dulwich College — endowing a school, almshouses, and a chapel. He died on 25 November 1626 and is commemorated in the college chapel he founded.
