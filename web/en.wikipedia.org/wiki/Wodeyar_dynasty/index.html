Wodeyar dynasty

The Wodeyar dynasty (also spelled Wadiyar or Odeyar) was a Hindu dynasty that ruled the Kingdom of Mysore in southern India from 1399 until 1950, with an interruption between 1761 and 1799 when Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan held power. The Wodeyars were noted patrons of art, music, and architecture, and their rule is associated with the transformation of Mysore into a major centre of Carnatic music and South Indian culture.

Origins

The dynasty was founded by Yaduraya (also called Vijaya), traditionally said to have come from Dwaraka in Gujarat and to have taken over Mysore in 1399 after rescuing the local royal family. The early Wodeyars ruled as feudatories of the Vijayanagara Empire. Raja Wodeyar I (1578–1617) greatly expanded the kingdom, captured Srirangapatna in 1610, and instituted the Dasara festival celebrations for which Mysore remains famous.

Interruption and restoration

In 1761 the military commander Haidar Ali seized effective power, and his son Tipu Sultan ruled until his death at the Siege of Seringapatam in 1799. The British then restored the Wodeyars, placing the five-year-old Krishnaraja Wodeyar III on the throne under a subsidiary alliance. Mysore was administered directly by British commissioners from 1831 to 1881, when the kingdom was formally rendered back to the dynasty in the "Rendition of Mysore".

Later rule

Krishnaraja Wodeyar IV (reigned 1894–1940) is regarded as one of the most enlightened Indian rulers; under him and his diwans, including Sir M. Visvesvaraya, Mysore pioneered hydroelectric power, modern education, and industry. His nephew Jayachamarajendra Wodeyar, the last maharaja, acceded to the Indian Union in 1947 and served as governor of Mysore and later Madras state. The palace at Mysore and the annual Dasara festivities remain enduring symbols of the dynasty.
