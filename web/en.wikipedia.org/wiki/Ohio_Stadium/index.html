Ohio Stadium, commonly referred to as the Horseshoe or the Shoe, is an American football stadium in Columbus, Ohio, on the campus of Ohio State University. It primarily serves as the home venue of the Ohio State Buckeyes football team and is one of the largest stadiums in the world, with an official capacity of 102,780.

History

Designed by architect Howard Dwight Smith in a horseshoe shape open at the south end, the stadium opened in 1922 at a cost of about $1.3 million, raised largely by public subscription. It pioneered the use of a double-decked horseshoe design and poured concrete construction. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1974. A major renovation completed in 2001 lowered the field, enclosed the south stands with permanent seating, and raised capacity above 100,000.

Events

Beyond Buckeyes football — including the annual rivalry game against Michigan — the stadium has hosted the Columbus Crew of Major League Soccer (1996–1998), NFL preseason games, major concerts, and the annual Ohio High School Athletic Association state football championships. Its record attendance exceeds 110,000.
