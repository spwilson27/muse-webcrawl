Mandapa

A mandapa (also spelled mantapa or mandap) is a pillared hall or pavilion in Indian architecture, especially Hindu temple architecture, used for public rituals, ceremonies, and gatherings. Mandapas form an essential part of the basic plan of a Hindu temple, serving as a porch or assembly space between the entrance and the inner sanctum (garbhagriha).

Temple architecture

In the classical temple plan, worshippers pass through one or more mandapas before reaching the sanctum. Types include the ardha-mandapa (entrance porch), the maha-mandapa (great assembly hall where devotees gather), and specialised halls such as the nritya-mandapa (for dance), ranga-mandapa, and kalyana-mandapa (for ceremonial marriages of deities). Large South Indian temples may include a "thousand-pillared hall" (sahasra stambha mandapa), richly carved with sculptures of deities, dancers, and mythological scenes.

Mandapas may be open, closed, or semi-open structures, usually square or rectangular, roofed and supported by rows of carved columns. Notable examples include the mandapas at Hampi, Madurai's Meenakshi Temple, and the Sun Temple at Konark.

Other uses

Outside temples, temporary mandaps (pandals) are erected for weddings, festivals such as Ganesh Chaturthi and Durga Puja, and other public celebrations across India and the diaspora. Jain and Buddhist architecture also use mandapa-type halls.
