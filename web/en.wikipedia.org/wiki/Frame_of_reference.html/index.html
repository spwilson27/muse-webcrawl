Frame of reference

In physics and astronomy, a frame of reference (or reference frame) is an abstract coordinate system whose origin, orientation, and scale are specified by a set of reference points whose relativistic positions are identified mathematically.

Inertial and non-inertial frames

An inertial frame of reference is one in which Newton's first law holds — objects free of forces move in straight lines at constant speed. Frames that accelerate or rotate are non-inertial and introduce fictitious forces such as the centrifugal and Coriolis forces.

In relativity

The concept is central to special and general relativity, where measurements of time and space depend on the observer's frame, while the laws of physics remain the same in all inertial frames.
