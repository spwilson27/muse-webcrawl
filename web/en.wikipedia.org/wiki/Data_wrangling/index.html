Data wrangling

Data wrangling, sometimes referred to as data munging, is the process of transforming and mapping data from one "raw" form into another format with the intent of making it more appropriate and valuable for a variety of downstream purposes such as analytics. It is a core part of the data preparation work performed by data scientists and analysts, and is often reported to consume the majority of the time in an analytics project.

Steps

Wrangling typically involves discovering and profiling the data; structuring it into tables; cleaning errors, duplicates and inconsistent values; enriching it by joining additional sources; validating the result against rules; and publishing it in a form suitable for analysis. Common operations include parsing dates and units, normalising category labels, reshaping wide data into long ("tidy") form, and converting between formats such as CSV, JSON and database tables.

Tools

Practitioners use general-purpose libraries such as pandas in Python and dplyr/tidyr in R, interactive visual wrangling tools, and dedicated data-preparation platforms. Small datasets are also wrangled in spreadsheet software, though this is harder to audit and reproduce.
