Milan–San Remo (Italian: Milano–Sanremo) is an annual one-day road cycling race between Milan and Sanremo in northwestern Italy. With a distance of around 298 kilometres it is the longest professional one-day race in modern cycling, and it traditionally opens the spring classics season in March.

History

The race was first held in 1907, inspired by a successful amateur event organized by the newspaper La Gazzetta dello Sport, and won by Lucien Petit-Breton. It became one of the five "Monuments" of cycling. Eddy Merckx holds the record with seven victories between 1966 and 1976.

Course

The route crosses Lombardy and Piedmont, climbs the Passo del Turchino to reach the Ligurian coast, and follows the Via Aurelia westward. Late climbs — the Cipressa (added 1982) and the Poggio (added 1960) — were introduced to keep sprinters from dominating, and the Poggio's descent into Sanremo frequently decides the race.

Character

Despite its flat reputation, the race's enormous length makes it a test of endurance nicknamed la Classicissima; its finishes alternate between solo attackers escaping on the Poggio and reduced-group sprints on the Via Roma.
