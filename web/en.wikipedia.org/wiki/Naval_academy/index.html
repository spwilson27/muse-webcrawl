Naval academy

A naval academy is a military educational institution that trains cadets (midshipmen) to become commissioned officers in a nation's navy. Programmes typically combine university-level academic study — engineering, navigation, mathematics, and the humanities — with seamanship, leadership, and practical training at sea, culminating in a degree and a commission.

Notable academies

Among the best known are the United States Naval Academy at Annapolis, Maryland (founded 1845); Britannia Royal Naval College at Dartmouth in the United Kingdom; the École navale in France; the German Naval Academy at Mürwik; the Italian Naval Academy at Livorno, one of the oldest (1881, with roots to earlier schools); the Imperial Japanese Naval Academy at Etajima; and China's Dalian Naval Academy. Many maritime nations maintain such institutions, and exchange programmes between allied academies are common.

Training

Cadet life is highly regimented, with uniforms, drill, physical training, and traditions such as ring ceremonies and graduation rituals. Training cruises aboard dedicated training ships provide practical experience in navigation and watchkeeping before graduates join the fleet as junior officers.
