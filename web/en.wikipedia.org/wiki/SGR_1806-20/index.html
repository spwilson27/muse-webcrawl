SGR 1806-20

SGR 1806−20
Type: Magnetar (soft gamma repeater)
Constellation: Sagittarius
Distance: ~42,000–50,000 light-years
Rotation period: ~7.5 s

SGR 1806−20 is a magnetar — a type of neutron star with an extremely powerful magnetic field — located in the constellation Sagittarius on the far side of the Milky Way. It is one of the best-known soft gamma repeaters, objects that emit repeated bursts of X-rays and gamma rays.

The 2004 giant flare

On 27 December 2004, SGR 1806−20 produced the largest gamma-ray flare ever recorded from an object outside the Solar System. In a tenth of a second the magnetar released more energy than the Sun emits in roughly 250,000 years; the radiation measurably ionised Earth's upper atmosphere and briefly saturated gamma-ray detectors on multiple spacecraft, despite the source being tens of thousands of light-years away. The flare is thought to have resulted from a catastrophic reconfiguration of the star's magnetic field.
