Guadalajara Cartel

The Guadalajara Cartel (Spanish: Cártel de Guadalajara) was a Mexican drug cartel formed in the 1970s and dominant in the 1980s, based in Guadalajara, Jalisco. It is considered the first major Mexican drug trafficking organization and the "mother" of the modern cartels that emerged after its breakup.

History

The cartel was founded by Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo, together with Rafael Caro Quintero and Ernesto Fonseca Carrillo ("Don Neto"). It consolidated marijuana and heroin trafficking from Sinaloa and pioneered cooperation with Colombian cocaine traffickers, moving cocaine through Mexico into the United States in exchange for a share of the product.

Downfall

The 1985 abduction and murder of U.S. DEA agent Enrique "Kiki" Camarena, ordered after a massive raid on the cartel's Rancho Búfalo marijuana plantation, triggered intense U.S. pressure. Caro Quintero and Fonseca were arrested in 1985 and Félix Gallardo in 1989. Afterward the organization fragmented into successor groups—including the Sinaloa, Tijuana, Juárez, and Gulf cartels—that have shaped Mexican organized crime ever since.
