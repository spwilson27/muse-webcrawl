Regulation and licensure in engineering

Engineering licensure
Regulated titlesProfessional Engineer (PE), Chartered Engineer (CEng), European Engineer (EUR ING)
PurposePublic safety, competence standards

Regulation and licensure in engineering is established by various jurisdictions of the world to encourage life, public safety, well-being, etc., and to ensure that the engineering expertise is well defined and trusted. Engineering professional bodies confer different types of degrees and qualifications, and jurisdictions have differing rules on who may practise engineering and use titles such as "engineer".

United States

In the United States, engineering licensure is regulated by the individual states. A Professional Engineer (PE) licence generally requires an accredited engineering degree, passing the Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) examination, several years of relevant experience under a licensed engineer, and passing the Principles and Practice of Engineering (PE) examination. Licensed engineers may seal and sign engineering documents submitted to public authorities. The "industrial exemption" means many engineers working in manufacturing are not required to be licensed.

Elsewhere

In the United Kingdom the title "Chartered Engineer" (CEng) is granted by the Engineering Council through licensed professional institutions, though the generic title "engineer" is not protected. Canada regulates practice through provincial bodies and reserves the title "engineer" to licence holders. Many countries recognise each other's qualifications through agreements such as the Washington Accord.
