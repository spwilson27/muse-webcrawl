Cross Fell

Elevation: 893 m (2,930 ft)
Location: Cumbria, England
Range: Pennines

Cross Fell is the highest mountain in the Pennines of Northern England and the highest point in England outside the Lake District. It is located in the North Pennines Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty in Cumbria. It rises to 893 metres (2,930 ft) and is traversed by the Pennine Way long-distance footpath.

Geography and weather

The summit is a broad, boggy plateau crowned by a cross-shaped cairn shelter. The mountain's summit area is a Site of Special Scientific Interest for its arctic-alpine flora, including rare plants such as the spring gentian in the surrounding Upper Teesdale area. Cross Fell is notable for the Helm Wind, a fierce north-easterly wind that roars down its south-western slopes — the only wind in Britain with its own name — accompanied by a stationary banner cloud, the Helm Bar.

History and folklore

The fell was formerly known as Fiends' Fell, and legend held it to be haunted by evil spirits; Saint Augustine is said to have blessed it and erected a cross, giving the mountain its present name. Lead mining was once extensive on its flanks, and the surrounding moorland is managed for grouse shooting.
