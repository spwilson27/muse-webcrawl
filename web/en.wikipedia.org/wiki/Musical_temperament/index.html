Musical temperament


Intervals in just intonation versus equal temperament.

In musical tuning, a temperament is a tuning system that slightly compromises the pure intervals of just intonation to meet other requirements. Most modern Western musical instruments are tuned in equal temperament, in which the octave is divided into twelve equal semitones.

Background

Pure intervals derive from simple frequency ratios (3:2 for the perfect fifth, 5:4 for the major third), but a closed circle of pure fifths cannot also yield pure octaves and thirds — the discrepancies are called commas, such as the Pythagorean comma and the syntonic comma. A temperament distributes ("tempers") these errors across the scale so that music can modulate between keys.

Historical systems

  • Pythagorean tuning — fifths pure, thirds wide; used in medieval music.
  • Meantone temperament — fifths narrowed to sweeten major thirds; dominant in the Renaissance and Baroque.
  • Well temperaments — irregular systems (Werckmeister, Vallotti) making all keys usable with distinct key colours; Bach's Well-Tempered Clavier was written for such a system.
  • Equal temperament — all semitones equal (ratio 21/12); standard since the 19th–20th centuries.
