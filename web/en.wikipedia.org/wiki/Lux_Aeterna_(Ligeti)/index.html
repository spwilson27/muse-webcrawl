Lux Aeterna (Ligeti)

ComposerGyörgy Ligeti
Composed1966
TextCommunion antiphon from the Requiem Mass
Scoring16 solo voices (SSAATTBB ×2), a cappella
Premiere1966, Stuttgart

Lux Aeterna is a work for 16 solo singers a cappella by the Hungarian composer György Ligeti, composed in 1966. It sets the Latin communion text from the Requiem Mass: Lux aeterna luceat eis, Domine, cum sanctis tuis in aeternum, quia pius es ("May eternal light shine upon them, O Lord, with your saints forever, for you are merciful").

Music

The piece is a classic example of Ligeti's micropolyphony: each of the 16 voices follows its own melodic line, entering in canon at different pitches and speeds, so that the individual parts fuse into slowly shifting harmonic clouds and clusters. The text is largely obscured by the dense texture. The work moves from dense, luminous sonorities to a close on a serene major chord, after which the voices fall silent one by one.

In popular culture

Lux Aeterna achieved wide fame when Stanley Kubrick used it, without initially seeking permission, in his 1968 film 2001: A Space Odyssey, during the sequence of the moon bus journey. Excerpts have since appeared in other films and have become emblematic of vast, otherworldly space imagery.
