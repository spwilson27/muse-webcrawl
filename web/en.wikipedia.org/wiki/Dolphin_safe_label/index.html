Dolphin safe label
Applies toCanned and packaged tuna products
US legal basisDolphin Protection Consumer Information Act (1990)
MonitoringNOAA Fisheries (US); Earth Island Institute internationally

Dolphin safe labels are used to mark tuna products whose fishing did not involve the deliberate chasing, netting or encirclement of dolphins, a practice once widespread in the eastern tropical Pacific, where tuna swim beneath dolphin herds and purse-seine nets set on dolphins killed millions of them.

Origins

Public outrage, fuelled by undercover footage from biologist Sam LaBudde in 1988, led major US tuna canners to adopt dolphin-safe policies in 1990, and the US Dolphin Protection Consumer Information Act set legal standards for the label. The Earth Island Institute monitors companies worldwide through an international observer programme, while NOAA administers the US standard under the Marine Mammal Protection Act.

Trade disputes

The labelling regime has been contested at the World Trade Organization, chiefly by Mexico, which argued the US rules discriminated against its fleet; WTO rulings in 2011–2018 criticised aspects of the scheme while accepting its conservation purpose, and the US amended its rules in response. Critics also note that some alternative fishing methods increase bycatch of sharks, rays and juvenile tuna.
