Via Domitia

A surviving stretch of the Via Domitia at Narbonne.

The Via Domitia was the first Roman road built in Gaul, to link Italy and Hispania through Gallia Narbonensis, across what is now southern France. It was constructed around 118 BC by the proconsul Gnaeus Domitius Ahenobarbus, from whom it takes its name, in the period when Rome was establishing the province of Gallia Narbonensis and founding the colony of Narbo Martius (Narbonne).

Route

From the Rhône crossing at Beaucaire (Ugernum), the road ran west through Nîmes (Nemausus), Montpellier, Béziers, and Narbonne, then southwest toward the Pyrenees, crossing at the Col de Panissars near the Trophy of Pompey and joining the Via Augusta in Hispania. The route was equipped with milestones and mansiones, and crossed the marshes of the lower Rhône delta on engineered causeways; the bridge of Saint-Thibéry and the Pont Ambroix carried it over the Vidourle and other rivers.

Remains

Many stretches survive. At Narbonne a section of the paved road is displayed in the Place de l'Hôtel de Ville. The Via Domitia is listed as a UNESCO-associated route of the Santiago de Compostela pilgrimage network in France, and its alignment is still followed by modern roads in Languedoc-Roussillon.
