Má vlast

Composer: Bedřich Smetana

Composed: 1874–1879

Movements: Six symphonic poems

Premiere (complete): 5 November 1882, Prague

Vyšehrad above the Vltava, the subjects of the cycle's first two poems.

Má vlast (Czech for "My Fatherland", or "My Country") is a cycle of six symphonic poems composed by Bedřich Smetana between 1874 and 1879. Each poem depicts an aspect of the landscape, history or legend of Bohemia, and together they form a cornerstone of Czech national music. Smetana wrote most of the cycle while completely deaf. Though each poem can stand alone, the six are usually performed together, and the complete cycle traditionally opens the Prague Spring Festival each year on 12 May, the anniversary of Smetana's death.

The six poems

  • Vyšehrad — the ancient castle on a cliff above Prague, evoked by the harp of the bard Lumír; its four-note motif recurs throughout the cycle.
  • Vltava (Die Moldau) — by far the most famous: the river's journey from two springs through forests, a peasant wedding, water nymphs, the St. John's Rapids and past Vyšehrad to the Elbe.
  • Šárka — the legendary warrior maiden who lures the warrior Ctirad and his men to their deaths.
  • Z českých luhů a hájů ("From Bohemia's Woods and Fields") — a celebration of the Czech countryside.
  • Tábor — the Hussite stronghold, built on the chorale "Ye Who Are Warriors of God".
  • Blaník — the mountain in which the Hussite warriors sleep, awaiting their nation's hour of greatest need; it closes with the chorale and the Vyšehrad motif in triumph.

The complete cycle was first performed in Prague on 5 November 1882. Vltava's main theme, long linked to the Moldau itself, closely resembles melodies found in several European traditions. See also Bedřich Smetana.
