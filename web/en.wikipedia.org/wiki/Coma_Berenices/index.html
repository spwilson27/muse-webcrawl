Coma Berenices

Abbreviation: Com
Genitive: Comae Berenices
Pronunciation: /ˈkoʊmə ˌbɛrəˈnaɪsiːz/
Symbolism: Berenice's Hair

Coma Berenices is an ancient asterism in the northern sky, which has been defined as one of the 88 modern constellations. It is in the direction of the fourth galactic quadrant, between Leo and Boötes, and is rich in galaxies because it contains the northern part of the Virgo Cluster and the Coma Cluster of galaxies.

Its brightest star is Beta Comae Berenices, a Sun-like star. The North Galactic Pole is located in Coma Berenices, making it a useful "window" out of the Milky Way for observing extragalactic objects.

Mythology

The constellation commemorates Queen Berenice II of Egypt, who, according to legend, vowed to sacrifice her long hair to the gods if her husband Ptolemy III returned safely from war. The hair was placed in a temple but disappeared; the court astronomer Conon of Samos explained that it had been carried to the heavens, where it could be seen as a cluster of stars.

Notable objects

  • Alpha Comae Berenices (Diadem) — a binary star representing the gem in Berenice's crown
  • Black Eye Galaxy (M64) — a spiral galaxy with a spectacular dark dust band
  • Needle Galaxy (NGC 4565) — an edge-on spiral galaxy
  • Coma Star Cluster (Melotte 111) — a large open cluster giving the constellation its "hair" appearance
  • Coma Cluster (Abell 1656) — a rich cluster of over 1,000 galaxies, central to Fritz Zwicky's discovery of dark matter
