A ritual is a sequence of activities involving gestures, words, actions, or revered objects, performed according to a set sequence. Rituals may be prescribed by the traditions of a community, including a religious community. Rituals are characterized, but not defined, by formalism, traditionalism, invariance, rule-governance, sacral symbolism, and performance.

Rituals are a feature of all known human societies. They include not only the worship rites and sacraments of organized religions and cults, but also rites of passage, atonement and purification rites, oaths of allegiance, dedication ceremonies, coronations and presidential inaugurations, marriages, funerals, and more. Even common actions such as hand-shaking and saying "hello" may be termed as rituals.

The field of ritual studies has seen a number of conflicting definitions of the term. One given by Kyriakidis is that a ritual is an outsider's or "etic" category for a set activity (or set of actions) that, to the outsider, seems irrational, non-contiguous, or illogical. The term can be used also by the insider or "emic" performer as an acknowledgement that this activity can be seen as such by the uninitiated onlooker.

Etymology

The English word ritual derives from the Latin ritualis, "that which pertains to rite (ritus)". In Roman juridical and religious usage, ritus was the proven way of doing something, or "correct performance, custom". The original concept of ritus may be related to the Sanskrit ṛtá ("visible order"), in Hinduism the "natural, lawful and regular order of the normal, and therefore proper, natural and true structure of cosmic, worldly, human and ritual events".

Characteristics

There are hardly any limits to the kind of actions that may be incorporated into a ritual. The rites of past and present societies have typically involved special gestures and words, recitation of fixed texts, performance of special music, songs or dances, processions, manipulation of certain objects, use of special dresses, consumption of special food, drink, or drugs, and much more.

Catherine Bell has provided a synthesis of the principal features of ritual, which she groups into six categories: ritualization, decorum, ceremony, magic, liturgy, and celebration. Scholars such as Arnold van Gennep and Victor Turner emphasized the structure of rituals, particularly the three-phase pattern of separation, liminality, and incorporation found in rites of passage.

Categories of ritual

Rituals fall into numerous categories, including:

  • Rites of passage — ceremonies marking transitions between life stages, such as birth, puberty, marriage, and death.
  • Calendrical and commemorative rites — rituals tied to the cycles of the year or to anniversaries of significant events.
  • Rites of exchange and communion — offerings, sacrifices, and communal meals.
  • Rites of affliction — rituals intended to heal illness or ward off misfortune.
  • Rites of feasting, fasting, and festivals.
  • Political rituals — ceremonies that construct and display political power, such as coronations and inaugurations.

Ritual and rite

In religious studies, the word rite often refers to a specific ritual act or to a family of rituals associated with a tradition, as in the Roman Rite of the Catholic Church. Ritual, by contrast, is the broader analytical category used by anthropologists and sociologists to compare ceremonial behaviour across cultures, whether religious or secular.

Social functions

Anthropologists have long studied the social functions of ritual. Émile Durkheim argued that rituals generate "collective effervescence" and reinforce social solidarity by uniting participants around shared sacred symbols. Rituals can legitimate authority, transmit cultural knowledge, mark group boundaries, and help individuals navigate major life transitions. They may also serve psychological functions, reducing anxiety in uncertain situations — a connection noted as early as Bronisław Malinowski's studies of Trobriand Islanders, who performed elaborate rituals before dangerous open-ocean fishing but not before safe lagoon fishing.
