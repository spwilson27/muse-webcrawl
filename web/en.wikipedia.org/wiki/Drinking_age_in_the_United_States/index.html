In the United States, the minimum legal age to purchase alcoholic beverages is 21 years of age in every state and territory except Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands, where it is 18. Although the minimum age for purchase is 21, this is not the minimum age for consumption in most states; many states allow underage consumption in specified circumstances, such as on private premises with parental consent, for religious ceremonies, or for medical purposes.

History

After the repeal of Prohibition in 1933, most states set the purchase age at 21, with some at 18. In the early 1970s, following the lowering of the voting age to 18 by the Twenty-sixth Amendment, 29 states lowered their drinking ages to 18, 19, or 20. Concern over drunk-driving fatalities among teenagers led to a reversal: the National Minimum Drinking Age Act of 1984 required states to raise the purchase and public possession age to 21 or lose 10 percent of federal highway funding. The Supreme Court upheld the law in South Dakota v. Dole (1987). By 1988 all states had complied; Louisiana was the last holdout for legal purchase. The US drinking age of 21 is among the highest in the world.

Enforcement and exceptions

States prohibit furnishing alcohol to minors, with exceptions that differ widely: 45 states have family/parental exceptions for consumption, around 30 allow consumption on private property, and most retain religious exceptions. Violations are typically misdemeanours. Fake-ID use and underage drinking on college campuses remain central enforcement issues.

Debate

Supporters cite reductions in alcohol-related traffic deaths among 18–20-year-olds. Critics — including groups such as the Amethyst Initiative of college presidents (2008) — argue the age promotes clandestine binge drinking and is inconsistent with other rights at 18. Research reviews generally associate the 21 age with lower youth consumption and crashes.
