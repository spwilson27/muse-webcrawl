Steel Pulse

Steel Pulse is a British reggae band from the Handsworth area of Birmingham, England. Formed in 1975 by school friends including David Hinds (lead vocals, guitar), Basil Gabbidon, and Ronald "Stepper" McQueen, the band — made up of musicians of Afro-Caribbean descent — became one of the most prominent Black British reggae acts, known for militant, politically conscious lyrics addressing racism and injustice, and for being the first non-Jamaican act to win the Grammy Award for Best Reggae Album.

Career

Steel Pulse emerged from Birmingham's sound-system culture and the punk-era music scene; their early single "Ku Klux Klan" (1978), a condemnation of racism, appeared on their debut album Handsworth Revolution (1978), widely regarded as a landmark of British reggae. Albums including Tribute to the Martyrs (1979), True Democracy (1982), and Earth Crisis (1984) combined roots reggae with polished production. Babylon the Bandit (1986) won the Grammy Award for Best Reggae Album in 1987.

The band toured extensively, sharing stages with Bob Marley and the Wailers, and remained a fixture of the international reggae circuit. Later albums, including African Holocaust (2004) and Mass Manipulation (2019), continued Hinds's themes of Pan-Africanism and social critique. Drummer Steve "Grizzly" Nisbett, a core member for decades, died in 2018.
