Reconnaissance

Reconnaissance (often shortened to recce in British English or recon in American English) is the military exploration of an area to gain information about enemy forces, terrain and other factors relevant to operations. It is a fundamental part of military intelligence, conducted before, during and after engagements to reduce uncertainty for commanders.

Types

Reconnaissance-in-force is a deliberate attack by a substantial force to provoke an enemy reaction that reveals its strength and dispositions. Route reconnaissance examines a specific road or corridor for movement, while zone and area reconnaissance gather detailed information on broader regions or specific locations. Special reconnaissance is conducted by special operations forces deep behind enemy lines.

Platforms and methods

Historically reconnaissance was the role of light cavalry scouts. The twentieth century added armoured reconnaissance vehicles, dedicated reconnaissance aircraft such as the U-2 and SR-71, and reconnaissance satellites, beginning with the Corona programme. Modern forces rely heavily on unmanned aerial vehicles (drones), signals intelligence, ground surveillance radar and cyber reconnaissance. Naval reconnaissance employs patrol aircraft, submarines and surface vessels.

Reconnaissance units are typically organised to "see without being seen," avoiding decisive engagement, and reporting by the fastest available means. The maxim attributed to cavalry tradition — that time spent on reconnaissance is seldom wasted — summarises its enduring value.
