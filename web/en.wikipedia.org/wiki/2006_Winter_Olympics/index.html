The 2006 Winter Olympics, officially the XX Olympic Winter Games, were held in Turin, Italy, from 10 to 26 February 2006. It was Italy's second Winter Games, fifty years after Cortina d'Ampezzo 1956, and the first Games held after the Salt Lake City bid scandal prompted the IOC's sweeping governance reforms. Eighty nations and about 2,500 athletes competed in 84 events; snowboard cross and team sprint cross-country skiing made their Olympic debuts.

Highlights

Germany topped the medal table with 29 medals, ahead of the United States (25) and Austria (23). Memorable performances included South Korea's dominance of short-track speed skating, Canada's Cindy Klassen winning five medals in speed skating, American teenager Shaun White and compatriot Hannah Teter winning the new halfpipe events, and Croatian Janica Kostelić adding to her family's legend. A doping raid on the Austrian cross-country and biathlon team's lodgings by Italian police — the Games were held under Italy's strict anti-doping laws — produced lifetime bans for several Austrian officials and athletes.

Organisation and legacy

Events were split between Turin itself (ice sports, at venues including the Palavela and the new Oval Lingotto) and mountain clusters in the Susa and Chisone valleys. Costs overruns and underused venues attracted criticism, though several Turin arenas found second lives in sport and concerts. The Games showcased Italy's design and cuisine to an estimated television audience of around two billion.
