Upper half-plane

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the upper half-plane, denoted ℍ, is the set of points (x, y) in the Cartesian plane with y > 0, or equivalently the set of complex numbers with positive imaginary part. It is one of the simplest examples of a Riemann surface and plays a central role in complex analysis, hyperbolic geometry and number theory.

Properties

The group SL(2,ℝ) acts on ℍ by Möbius transformations, making ℍ a homogeneous space and a model of the hyperbolic plane under the Poincaré half-plane metric. The quotient of ℍ by subgroups such as the modular group SL(2,ℤ) underlies the theory of modular forms and elliptic curves. The Riemann mapping theorem gives a conformal equivalence between ℍ and the open unit disk, realised for instance by the Cayley transform, so the two domains are interchangeable in complex analysis.
