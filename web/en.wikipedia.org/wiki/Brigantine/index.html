Brigantine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A brigantine combines a square-rigged foremast with a fore-and-aft mainmast (image placeholder)

A brigantine is a two-masted sailing vessel with a fully square-rigged foremast and at least two sails on the mainmast — a fore-and-aft mainsail and a square topsail above it. The rig combines the windward ability of fore-and-aft sails with the downwind power of square sails and needs a smaller crew than a full-rigged brig.

Development

The name derives from the Italian brigantino, originally a small vessel favoured by brigands and pirates in the Mediterranean. By the 17th century the term described a fast two-masted type in northern European and American waters; definitions shifted over time, and by the 18th century "brigantine" and "brig" were often confused, with the brigantine distinguished by its fore-and-aft mainsail in place of the brig's square main courses.

Use

Brigantines served as merchantmen, privateers, naval dispatch vessels and slavers, and remained common in coastal trades into the 19th century. Variants include the hermaphrodite brig (or brig-schooner), square-rigged only on the fore topmast. Examples surviving or replicated include sail-training brigantines such as Irving Johnson and Exy Johnson of Los Angeles.
