Asturias, officially the Principality of Asturias, is an autonomous community of Spain on the Bay of Biscay coast in the northwest of the country. Its capital is Oviedo and its largest city the port of Gijón. Asturias is famed for its green, mountainous landscape — part of "Green Spain" — its pre-Romanesque churches, cider culture and coal-mining heritage.

History

After the Muslim conquest of most of Iberia, the Visigothic noble Pelayo's victory at Covadonga (c. 718/722) founded the Kingdom of Asturias, remembered in Spanish tradition as the seed of the Reconquista. The kingdom's pre-Romanesque architecture around Oviedo is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The heir to the Spanish throne bears the title Prince (or Princess) of Asturias, and the Princess of Asturias Awards, among Spain's most prestigious prizes, are presented annually in Oviedo.

Geography and economy

The Cantabrian Mountains, including the Picos de Europa, separate Asturias from the Meseta. Coal mining and steel around Avilés and Gijón drove its industrial era; as mining declined, the region has shifted toward services, dairy farming and tourism. Asturian cuisine is known for fabada bean stew, Cabrales cheese and cider (sidra), poured from a height to aerate it.
