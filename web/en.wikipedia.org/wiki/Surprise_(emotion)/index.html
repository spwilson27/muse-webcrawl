Surprise (emotion)

Surprise is a brief mental and physiological state, a startle response experienced by animals and humans as the result of an unexpected event. In Paul Ekman's influential classification it is one of the basic emotions, characterised by a distinctive facial expression — raised eyebrows, widened eyes and a dropped jaw — that is recognised across cultures. Surprise is neutral in valence: it can be pleasant, unpleasant or both, and it typically resolves within seconds into another emotion such as joy, fear or anger as the surprising event is appraised.

Type: Basic emotion (Ekman)
Trigger: Unexpected event
Duration: Brief (under a few seconds)

Function and psychology

Researchers argue that surprise serves to interrupt ongoing activity and focus attention and cognitive resources on evaluating an unexpected stimulus, maximising readiness to respond. In predictive-processing accounts of the brain, surprise corresponds to prediction error — the mismatch between expectation and sensory input — which drives learning. Unexpectedness combined with appraisal of importance produces the intensity of experienced surprise; events inconsistent with a person's beliefs can also trigger curiosity or, when threatening, the startle reflex.

Expression and research

Charles Darwin described the expression of surprise in The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals (1872). Modern research using the Facial Action Coding System confirms its distinct muscular pattern, though some theorists dispute its status as a true emotion, regarding it instead as a cognitive state of expectancy violation. Surprise is central to humour, magic performance, narrative plot twists and product design.
