Battle of Marengo

The Battle of Marengo was fought on 14 June 1800 between French forces under First Consul Napoleon Bonaparte and an Austrian army commanded by Michael von Melas, near Alessandria in Piedmont, Italy. The dramatic French victory consolidated Napoleon's political position and drove Austria toward the Peace of Lunéville.

Course of the battle

Having crossed the Alps by the Great St Bernard Pass, Napoleon threatened the Austrian lines of communication but dispersed his forces and was surprised by Melas's attack. Through the morning the French were pushed back toward Marengo, and Melas, believing the battle won, handed command to a subordinate. Late in the afternoon the returning division of General Desaix counterattacked, supported by artillery under Marmont and a cavalry charge by Kellermann that shattered the Austrian column. Desaix was killed at the moment of victory.

Aftermath

The Convention of Alessandria granted France control of northern Italy west of the Mincio. Napoleon exploited the victory in propaganda to cement his regime as First Consul.

In culture

The battle gave its name to Chicken Marengo, reputedly improvised for Napoleon by his chef after the victory, and to the treasured warhorse Marengo.
