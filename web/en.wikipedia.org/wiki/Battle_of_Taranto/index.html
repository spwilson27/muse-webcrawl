The Battle of Taranto was a British naval air attack on the Italian fleet at Taranto on the night of 11–12 November 1940 during the Second World War. Twenty-one Fairey Swordfish torpedo bombers from the aircraft carrier HMS Illustrious crippled three Italian battleships in their defended harbour for the loss of two aircraft. It was the first all-aircraft naval attack in history and demonstrated the power of carrier aviation against capital ships in port.

Background

Admiral Andrew Cunningham's Mediterranean Fleet sought to neutralise the Italian Regia Marina, whose battle fleet at Taranto threatened British convoys to Malta and Egypt. Plans for a carrier strike dated from the 1930s. Photographic reconnaissance by RAF aircraft from Malta confirmed the fleet's disposition in the Mar Grande anchorage.

The attack

On the night of 11 November, two waves of Swordfish attacked, dropping flares and torpedoes fitted to run shallow under Italian anti-torpedo nets. The battleships Littorio (three hits), Conte di Cavour (sunk in shallow water, never fully returned to service) and Duilio (beached) were torpedoed, and a cruiser and destroyers were damaged by bombs. Only two Swordfish were shot down.

Aftermath

The Regia Marina withdrew its remaining battleships to Naples, and the balance of naval power in the Mediterranean shifted to Britain. Japanese planners studied Taranto closely when preparing the attack on Pearl Harbor a year later.
