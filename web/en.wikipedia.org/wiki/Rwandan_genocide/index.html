Rwandan genocide

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Rwandan genocide was a genocidal mass slaughter of the Tutsi in Rwanda by members of the Hutu majority government during the Rwandan Civil War, which began on 7 April 1994 and lasted for about 100 days, until mid-July 1994. It is estimated that between 500,000 and 1,000,000 Rwandans were killed — the figure of 800,000 is the most commonly cited — constituting perhaps 70 per cent of the Tutsi population. The genocide was organised by the ruling MRND party's elites (the akazu), executed by the army, the Interahamwe and Impuzamugambi militias and ordinary civilians mobilised by radio propaganda (RTLM), and triggered by the shooting down of President Habyarimana's plane on 6 April 1994.

International failure to prevent or stop the killing — UNAMIR's force was cut to a token presence under General Roméo Dallaire as the genocide began; the Security Council avoided the word “genocide” for weeks — became a defining scandal of post-Cold War humanitarian policy, driving US and UN “never again” reviews. The RPF under Paul Kagame ended the genocide by military victory; some two million Hutu refugees fled to Zaire, seeding the Congo Wars. Accountability followed through the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda (established November 1994; first international conviction for genocide, Akayesu, 1998), Rwanda's gacaca courts (which tried roughly 1.9 million cases) and universal-jurisdiction trials abroad. Annual commemoration (Kwibuka) begins on 7 April.
