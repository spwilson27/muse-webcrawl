Botox

Botox is a trade name for a drug made from botulinum toxin type A, a neurotoxic protein produced by the bacterium Clostridium botulinum. In small, carefully controlled doses, injections of the toxin temporarily paralyse muscle activity. Beyond its famous cosmetic use for reducing facial wrinkles such as glabellar lines, Botox is approved for a wide range of medical conditions.

Medical uses

Therapeutic applications include chronic migraine, severe underarm sweating (hyperhidrosis), overactive bladder, cervical dystonia, spasticity of the limbs, strabismus, and blepharospasm — the original indication, developed by ophthalmologist Alan Scott in the late 1970s. The effect of an injection typically appears within a few days and lasts three to six months, after which nerve terminals sprout and muscle function recovers.

Mechanism

Botulinum toxin cleaves components of the SNARE protein complex at neuromuscular junctions, preventing the release of the neurotransmitter acetylcholine and thereby blocking muscle contraction. It is one of the most potent toxins known; systematic spread of the effect can cause botulism-like symptoms, so dosing and injection technique are critical.

History and market

Allergan's product received its first approvals in 1989 and cosmetic approval in 2002, becoming a blockbuster drug and a cultural phenomenon. Competing formulations include Dysport, Xeomin, and Jeuveau.
