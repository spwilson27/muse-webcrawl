American Forests

American Forests is a non-profit conservation organization dedicated to protecting and restoring healthy forest ecosystems in the United States. Founded in 1875 as the American Forestry Association, it is the oldest national conservation organization in the country.

History

The organization was founded in Chicago in 1875 by physician and horticulturalist John Aston Warder and fellow citizens concerned about deforestation. It advocated for the creation of forest reserves and a national forestry policy, helping pave the way for the U.S. Forest Service (1905) and the national forest system. Its magazine, now called American Forests, has been published since the late nineteenth century and helped popularise Arbor Day and forest conservation.

Programs

American Forests is known for the National Register of Champion Trees, which since 1940 has catalogued the largest known specimens of U.S. tree species using a points system based on trunk circumference, height, and crown spread. Its Global ReLeaf program, launched in 1990, has planted tens of millions of trees in forest restoration projects across all 50 states and around the world. More recent work includes urban forestry and "Tree Equity" programs that map canopy cover to direct planting toward neighbourhoods with the fewest trees, and large-scale reforestation work in landscapes such as the Rio Grande and northern Rockies.
