Lorenzo di Piero de' Medici (1 January 1449 – 8 April 1492), known as Lorenzo the Magnificent (Italian: Lorenzo il Magnifico), was an Italian statesman, banker, de facto ruler of the Florentine Republic, and one of the most powerful and enthusiastic patrons of Renaissance culture in Italy.

Life

Lorenzo was the grandson of Cosimo de' Medici, the first Medici ruler of Florence. Educated by humanists such as Gentile de' Becchi, he displayed precocious diplomatic talent and was entrusted with missions abroad while still a teenager. On the death of his father Piero in 1469, Lorenzo assumed leadership of the republic together with his younger brother Giuliano.

In 1478 the Pazzi conspiracy, backed by Pope Sixtus IV, attempted to assassinate the brothers during Mass in Florence Cathedral. Giuliano was killed, but Lorenzo escaped, and the ensuing war ended in 1480 after Lorenzo's bold personal diplomacy with King Ferdinand I of Naples.

Patronage

Lorenzo's court became a centre of Renaissance learning and art. He supported artists including Sandro Botticelli, Leonardo da Vinci, and Michelangelo, and founded a sculpture garden where the young Michelangelo trained. He also wrote poetry in the Tuscan vernacular and encouraged the revival of Italian-language literature.

Death and legacy

Lorenzo died at Careggi on 8 April 1492, shortly before the invasion of Italy by Charles VIII of France. His death is often taken to mark the end of the Italian Renaissance's golden age in Florence. He was succeeded by his son Piero, who was driven from the city in 1494.
