Special reconnaissance

Special reconnaissance (SR) is conducted by special operations forces to obtain information of strategic or operational significance, employing reconnaissance and surveillance capabilities not normally found in conventional forces. SR is one of the core activities of United States special operations forces and comparable units worldwide.

Mission

Special reconnaissance supplements national and theater intelligence-collection systems by placing human observers in hostile, denied, or politically sensitive areas. Typical SR tasks include:

  • Environmental reconnaissance — weather, terrain, hydrographic, and radiological or chemical data
  • Target acquisition — locating, identifying, and tracking high-value targets
  • Threat reconnaissance — order of battle, capabilities, and disposition of enemy forces
  • Post-strike reconnaissance — bomb damage assessment of targets attacked by air or missiles
  • Area assessment — evaluating infrastructure, resources, and human terrain before the commitment of forces

Units

In the United States, SR is performed by elements of Army Special Forces, the 75th Ranger Regiment's Regimental Reconnaissance Company, Marine Raiders and Force Reconnaissance companies, Air Force Special Tactics teams, Navy SEALs, and units under the Joint Special Operations Command. The British equivalent tradition is associated with the Special Air Service and Special Reconnaissance Regiment, and the Australian SASR conducts similar missions.

Methods

SR teams insert by parachute (including military free fall), helicopter, small boat, submarine, or on foot, establish observation posts or caches, and report by burst satellite transmission to minimize detection. Unlike direct action missions, SR generally avoids contact with the enemy, though teams may designate targets for precision fires.
