Greenhouse gas emissions

Greenhouse gas emissions
Total (2019)≈59 Gt CO₂-equivalent
Main gasesCO₂, methane, nitrous oxide, fluorinated gases

Greenhouse gas emissions from human activities strengthen the greenhouse effect, causing climate change. Global emissions totalled about 59 billion tonnes of CO₂-equivalent in 2019. Most emissions are carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels — coal, oil, and natural gas — with significant contributions from methane, nitrous oxide, and fluorinated gases.

Sources by sector

Energy production and use dominate: electricity and heat generation, transport, industry, and buildings together account for the majority of emissions. Agriculture contributes methane from livestock and rice cultivation and nitrous oxide from fertilised soils, while deforestation releases stored carbon and reduces absorption. Since 1750 the world has emitted roughly 1.5 trillion tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and cement.

Sources by country

China is the largest current emitter (about 30% of the global total), followed by the United States, India, and the European Union; the United States remains the largest historical, cumulative emitter. Per-capita emissions vary enormously, from over 14 tonnes per person in the US to under 2 tonnes in India.

Reduction

Under the Paris Agreement (2015), countries pledged to limit warming to well below 2 °C. Pathways assessed by the IPCC require emissions to fall rapidly toward net zero around mid-century, through decarbonised electricity, electrification, efficiency, and protection of carbon sinks.
