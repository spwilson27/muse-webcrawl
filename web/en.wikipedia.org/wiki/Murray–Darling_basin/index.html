Murray–Darling basin

The Murray–Darling basin is the catchment of Australia's longest river system, formed by the Murray River (2,508 km) and its largest tributary, the Darling River (1,472 km), together with tributaries including the Murrumbidgee, Lachlan, Warrego, Barwon, Condamine and Goulburn rivers. The basin drains about 14 per cent of Australia's land area and supports more than two million people, as well as about half of the nation's irrigated land.

Governance

Because the basin crosses state borders, its management has required intergovernmental cooperation since the River Murray Waters Agreement of 1914. The Murray–Darling Basin Commission, and from 2008 the Murray–Darling Basin Authority, coordinate water sharing through the Murray–Darling Basin Agreement. The Basin Plan, legislated in 2012 under the Commonwealth Water Act 2007, set sustainable diversion limits that return roughly 2,750 gigalitres per year to the environment through entitlement buybacks and efficiency projects, an arrangement that remains politically contentious among farming communities.

Ecology

The basin contains 16 internationally significant Ramsar wetlands, vast river red gum forests along the Murray, and the Lower Lakes and Coorong near the Murray mouth in South Australia. Flow variability is extreme: the Darling can cease flowing in drought and run bank-full in flood. Native fish such as the Murray cod have declined through river regulation, cold-water pollution from dams and introduced carp. First Nations peoples, including the Barkindji and Wiradjuri, hold deep cultural ties to the rivers, increasingly recognised in water planning.
