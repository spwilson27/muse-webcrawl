Russo-Swedish War (1741–1743)
Date: 1741 – 1743
Location: Finland, Karelia
Result: Russian victory; Treaty of Åbo

The Russo-Swedish War of 1741–1743, known in Sweden as the Hats' War (Swedish: Hattarnas ryska krig), was fought between the Russian Empire and the Kingdom of Sweden. The Swedish "Hats" party, which had come to power in Stockholm, sought to reverse the losses Sweden had suffered in the Great Northern War and recover territory ceded to Russia by the Treaty of Nystad (1721). Encouraged and subsidised by France, Sweden declared war on Russia in the summer of 1741.

Course of the war

The Swedish army in Finland was poorly prepared and under-supplied. Russian forces under Field Marshal Peter Lacy defeated the Swedes at the Battle of Villmanstrand (Lappeenranta) in August 1741, early in the war. Political turmoil in St. Petersburg — the coup that brought Elizabeth of Russia to the throne in December 1741 — briefly raised Swedish hopes, and Elizabeth issued a manifesto promising Finnish autonomy in an attempt to win over the Finnish population.

In 1742 the Russian army advanced through southern Finland almost unopposed. Helsinki and most of the country were occupied, and the Swedish army capitulated at Helsinki in August 1742, leaving Finland under Russian occupation, a period later remembered in Finland as the "Lesser Wrath" (pikkuviha) — notably milder than the occupation of the Great Northern War.

Treaty of Åbo

The war ended with the Treaty of Åbo (Turku), signed in August 1743. Sweden ceded to Russia a strip of southeastern Finland east of the Kymi River, including the towns of Villmanstrand and Fredrikshamn (Hamina), shifting the border westward. Russia evacuated the rest of Finland, and Sweden agreed to elect Adolf Frederick of Holstein-Gottorp — a relative of Empress Elizabeth — as heir to the Swedish throne.
