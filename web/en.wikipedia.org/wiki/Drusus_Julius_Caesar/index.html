Drusus Julius Caesar (c. 8 BC – AD 33) was a member of the Julio-Claudian dynasty, the second son of the general Germanicus and Agrippina the Elder, and a grandson in the extended line of the emperors Augustus and Tiberius. He was a younger brother of the emperor Caligula and a nephew of Claudius. His eldest brother, Nero Julius Caesar, predeceased him; both were destroyed in the purges following the fall of Sejanus.

Life

Drusus grew up in the imperial household after his father's death in AD 19, which popular opinion blamed on poisoning. He received the toga virilis in AD 23, the year his cousin Drusus the Younger (Tiberius's son) died, which advanced Germanicus's sons in the succession. Drusus married Aemilia Lepida, a great-granddaughter of Augustus. The powerful praetorian prefect Sejanus cultivated Drusus and turned him against his elder brother Nero; according to Tacitus, Drusus, jealous and unstable, even helped inform on Nero, who was exiled in AD 29/30 and died soon after. Once Nero was gone, Sejanus turned on Drusus himself.

Imprisonment and death

In AD 30 Drusus was denounced, declared a public enemy by the Senate, and imprisoned in a cell beneath the imperial palace on the Palatine. He was held there for three years. After Sejanus's fall in AD 31, Drusus was briefly hopeful of release—he was even reportedly considered as a rallying point during the crisis—but Tiberius kept him confined. In AD 33 he starved to death in prison; Tacitus and Suetonius report that in his desperation he ate the stuffing of his mattress. Tiberius had his prison journal read to the Senate to justify the treatment. His mother Agrippina died in exile the same year.
