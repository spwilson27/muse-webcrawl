Liquid-crystal display

A liquid-crystal display (LCD) is a flat-panel display technology that uses the light-modulating properties of liquid crystals combined with polarizers. Liquid crystals do not emit light directly; instead, an LCD uses a backlight or reflector to produce images in colour or monochrome. LCDs are found in televisions, computer monitors, instrument panels, smartphones, calculators, watches and countless other devices.

How it works

Each picture element consists of a layer of liquid crystal material sandwiched between transparent electrodes and two polarizing filters whose axes are perpendicular. With no electric field, the twisted nematic crystal structure rotates the polarization of light passing through it, allowing light to pass. Applying a voltage untwists the molecules, blocking the light and making the pixel dark. Colour displays use red, green and blue subpixels with colour filters; modern panels switch each subpixel with its own thin-film transistor (an "active matrix"), enabling fast response and high resolution.

History

Liquid crystals were described by Friedrich Reinitzer in 1888, and their electro-optic effects studied through the 20th century. Practical display work began in the 1960s at RCA Laboratories with the dynamic scattering mode; the twisted nematic field effect was patented in 1970 by Schadt and Helfrich at Hoffmann-La Roche, and James Fergason independently developed similar technology in the United States. Sharp produced the first LCD pocket calculator in 1973. Active-matrix panels matured in the 1990s, and by the mid-2000s LCD televisions and monitors had overtaken cathode-ray tubes worldwide — although the backlit panel has itself since faced competition from emissive OLED displays.

Advantages and drawbacks

LCDs are thin, light, energy-efficient and free of geometric distortion, but traditionally suffer limited viewing angles, weaker black levels than emissive technologies (since the backlight is always on), and finite response times. Panel types such as IPS and VA address these weaknesses to different degrees.
