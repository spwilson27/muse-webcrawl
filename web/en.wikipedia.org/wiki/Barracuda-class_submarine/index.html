Barracuda-class submarine


Suffren, lead boat of the class

The Barracuda class (also known as the Suffren class) is a class of nuclear attack submarines (SSNs) built by Naval Group for the French Navy, intended to replace the Rubis-class submarines. Construction of the lead boat, Suffren, began in 2007; she was launched in 2019, commissioned in June 2022 and declared fully operational later that year. Six boats are planned.

Design

The class displaces about 4,765 tonnes surfaced and roughly 5,300 tonnes submerged, and is about 99.5 metres long. Unlike earlier French SSNs, the Barracuda uses a pump-jet propulsor and an X-configuration stern rudder, improving manoeuvrability and reducing its acoustic signature. Its reactor is derived from the pressurised water reactors used aboard the Triomphant-class ballistic missile submarines and the aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle, but uses low-enriched uranium fuel that can be refuelled roughly once a decade.

Armament is carried in four 533 mm torpedo tubes and includes F21 heavyweight torpedoes, Exocet SM39 anti-ship missiles and MdCN naval cruise missiles, giving the class a land-attack capability. The boats also have facilities for special forces, including a dry deck shelter for combat swimmers.

Boats

The six boats are named Suffren, Duguay-Trouin, Tourville, De Grasse, Rubis and Casabianca, after earlier French submarine and naval names. Duguay-Trouin entered service in 2024 and Tourville was delivered the same year.

Export variants

A conventionally powered derivative, the Shortfin Barracuda, was selected by Australia in 2016 under the SEA 1000 programme before the contract was cancelled in 2021 in favour of the AUKUS nuclear-powered arrangement. A further conventional variant was offered to the Netherlands for its Walrus-class replacement programme.
