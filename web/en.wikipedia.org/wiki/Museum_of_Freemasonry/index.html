Museum of Freemasonry

The Museum of Freemasonry is a museum, library and archive based at Freemasons' Hall, Great Queen Street, London, England, covering the history of Freemasonry and other fraternal orders. It is a registered charity and forms part of the collections of the United Grand Lodge of England. The museum is open to the public free of charge and contains an extensive collection of objects with Masonic decoration, including pottery, porcelain, glass, silver, furniture, clocks, regalia, jewels and prints, as well as items belonging to famous Freemasons such as Winston Churchill and several British monarchs.

History

The collections originated in the 19th century as the United Grand Lodge of England accumulated gifts and acquisitions, including the contents of the former library and museum of the old Grand Lodge. The current museum was established within Freemasons' Hall when the building — the third on the site and a memorial to Freemasons killed in the First World War — was completed in 1933. The Library and Museum Charitable Trust was formed in 1980 to manage the collections.

Collections and library

Notable exhibits include the large ceremonial throne chair made for the Prince Regent (later George IV), elaborate 18th-century tracing boards, and the museum's clock collection. The Library holds some 30,000 books and manuscripts on Freemasonry and related subjects and is a leading research resource for Masonic history. In 2019 the institution was renamed from the Library and Museum of Freemasonry to the Museum of Freemasonry.
