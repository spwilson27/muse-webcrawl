Slaughterhouse

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A modern industrial abattoir.

A slaughterhouse, also called an abattoir, is a facility where animals are slaughtered to provide food. Slaughterhouses supply meat, which then becomes the responsibility of packing plants for further processing and distribution. Modern industrial slaughterhouses can process very large numbers of animals per hour on production lines, while small local facilities serve regional markets. The industry is regulated in most countries for food safety, animal welfare, and occupational safety.

Process

In a typical large facility, livestock arrive by truck and are held in lairage pens. Animals are moved to a stunning area where they are rendered insensible — by captive bolt pistol (cattle), electrical stunning (pigs, poultry), or carbon dioxide (pigs) — then hoisted, exsanguinated, and passed through scalding, dehairing or skinning, evisceration, splitting, inspection, washing, and chilling stages. Carcasses are graded and sent to cutting rooms or shipped as halves or quarters. Poultry plants follow a similar sequence with shackling, electrical water-bath stunning, bleeding, scalding, plucking, and evisceration.

Regulation

In the United States, slaughter is governed principally by the Federal Meat Inspection Act and the Humane Methods of Slaughter Act, with inspectors of the USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service present in plants. The European Union regulates slaughter through hygiene rules and Council Regulation (EC) No 1099/2009 on the protection of animals at the time of killing. Religious slaughter for halal and kosher meat raises specific legal questions about stunning requirements, with rules varying by country.

History

Industrial slaughter began in the 19th century in centers such as Cincinnati and Chicago, where disassembly lines in the Union Stock Yards inspired Henry Ford's assembly lines. Upton Sinclair's 1906 novel The Jungle exposed conditions in Chicago packinghouses and helped spur the first federal meat inspection laws. Since the mid-20th century the industry has consolidated into a small number of large firms — in the US, Tyson Foods, JBS, Cargill, and Smithfield dominate beef and pork packing — and plants have moved from unionized urban stockyards to rural areas near livestock production.

Criticism and working conditions

Slaughterhouses are criticized by animal welfare and animal rights advocates over the treatment of animals, and by labor advocates over fast line speeds, repetitive-strain injuries, and the employment of vulnerable migrant workers. High-profile outbreaks of disease among workers, notably of COVID-19 in 2020, drew further attention to plant conditions. Environmental concerns include water use, effluent, and the disposal of by-products, which are otherwise rendered into tallow, gelatin, pet food, and other products.
