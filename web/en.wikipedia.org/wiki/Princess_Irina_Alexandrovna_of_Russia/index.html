Princess Irina Alexandrovna of Russia

Born: 15 July 1895, Peterhof, Russian Empire
Died: 26 February 1970, Paris, France
Spouse: Prince Felix Yusupov
Father: Grand Duke Alexander Mikhailovich
Mother: Grand Duchess Xenia Alexandrovna

Princess Irina Alexandrovna of Russia (15 July 1895 – 26 February 1970) was the only daughter of Grand Duke Alexander Mikhailovich and Grand Duchess Xenia Alexandrovna, and a niece of Tsar Nicholas II. She is best known as the wife of Prince Felix Yusupov, one of the men who murdered Grigori Rasputin in 1916.

Early life and marriage

Irina was raised partly at Ai-Todor, her father's estate in the Crimea. Despite initial family reservations about Yusupov's reputation, she married him in February 1914 in the last great Romanov wedding before the First World War. The couple had one daughter, also named Irina ("Bébé").

Revolution and exile

The Yusupovs were exiled to the Crimea after Rasputin's murder, which likely saved their lives: after the October Revolution they escaped aboard the British battleship HMS Marlborough in 1919 with other Romanov relatives. They settled in Paris, where Felix founded the short-lived fashion house Irfé.

Later life

In 1932 the Yusupovs won a libel suit against MGM over the film Rasputin and the Empress, a case that led studios to adopt the standard "all persons fictitious" disclaimer. Irina died in Paris in 1970 and was buried at Sainte-Geneviève-des-Bois Russian Cemetery beside her husband.
