SFR (formerly Société française du radiotéléphone) is a French telecommunications company and one of the four national mobile network operators in France, alongside Orange, Bouygues Telecom and Free Mobile. It provides mobile and fixed-line services, broadband internet and television to consumers and businesses, and is a subsidiary of Altice France.

History

SFR was founded in 1987 by the Compagnie Générale des Eaux (later Vivendi) to operate a mobile network in competition with France Télécom's Itineris. It launched its GSM network in the early 1990s and grew into France's second-largest operator. SFR absorbed the fixed-line operator Neuf Cegetel in 2008, becoming an integrated telecom provider.

In 2014 Vivendi sold SFR to Patrick Drahi's Altice and its French cable subsidiary Numericable, in a deal valued at around €17 billion, creating the Numericable-SFR group, later renamed SFR Group and then Altice France. The merger saddled the company with heavy debt, and in 2024–2025 Altice France reached agreements with creditors on a major debt restructuring involving a partial sale of the company.

Services

SFR markets mobile plans under the SFR and low-cost RED by SFR brands, and fixed broadband over its cable (FTTLA) and fibre networks, bundled with television services. It also operates sports and news media through related Altice brands, including RMC and BFM TV.
