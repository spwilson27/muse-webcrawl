The Missile Defense Agency (MDA) is a research, development and acquisition agency within the United States Department of Defense, responsible for developing the layered defense against ballistic missiles in all phases of flight — boost, midcourse and terminal.

History

The MDA traces its origins to the Strategic Defense Initiative Organization established in 1984 under President Ronald Reagan, which became the Ballistic Missile Defense Organization in 1993 and was renamed and elevated to agency status in 2002, following the United States' withdrawal from the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty.

Programs

The agency's portfolio includes the Ground-based Midcourse Defense system with interceptors in Alaska and California, the Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense System, the Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD) system, Patriot PAC-3, and a global network of sensors including upgraded early-warning radars and the Space Tracking and Surveillance System.
