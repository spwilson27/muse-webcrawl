Diamantinasaurus is an extinct genus of titanosaurian sauropod dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous Winton Formation of Queensland, Australia. The type and only species, Diamantinasaurus matildae, was described in 2009 by Scott Hocknull and colleagues, alongside the theropod Australovenator.

Discovery and description

The holotype specimen was found near Winton on Elderslie Station, in sediments of the Winton Formation dated to about 94 million years ago. The genus name refers to the nearby Diamantina River, while the species name honours "Matilda", after the song "Waltzing Matilda", written near Winton. Diamantinasaurus was a medium-sized, robustly built titanosaur about 15–16 metres long. Unusually for a sauropod, the first finger of the hand retained a thumb claw — a primitive feature that helped identify the Diamantinasauria, a Gondwanan clade uniting it with Savannasaurus and the South American Sarmientosaurus.

Palaeoecology

The Winton Formation preserves a lush floodplain environment of rivers, billabongs and forests. Diamantinasaurus shared it with the sauropods Wintonotitan and Savannasaurus, the megaraptoran predator Australovenator, and a rich fauna of turtles, crocodilians, lungfish and dinosaurs known from trackways.
