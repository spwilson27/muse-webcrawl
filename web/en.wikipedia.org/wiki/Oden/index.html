Oden (Japanese: おでん) is a type of Japanese one-pot dish (nabemono) consisting of several ingredients such as boiled eggs, daikon radish, konjac (konnyaku), and processed fishcakes stewed in a light, soy-flavoured dashi broth. It is a popular winter dish in Japan and is commonly sold at food stalls, convenience stores, and dedicated oden restaurants.

Ingredients

Common ingredients include hard-boiled eggs, sliced daikon, konnyaku, atsuage (fried tofu), chikuwa, satsuma-age and other fish paste cakes, hanpen, boiled octopus, potatoes, and skewered items such as tsukune and beef tendon. Regional variations exist: in Nagoya the broth may be flavoured with miso, while in Shizuoka a dark, strongly flavoured broth and toppings such as dried sardine powder are used. Karashi mustard is a typical condiment.

History

Oden derives from dengaku, grilled tofu with miso, a dish dating to the Muromachi period. The stewed form spread during the Edo period, and street stalls serving oden became widespread in the Meiji era. In the Kansai region a similar dish is called kanto-daki. Canned oden and convenience-store oden counters are fixtures of the Japanese winter.
