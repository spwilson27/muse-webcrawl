Düren is a town in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, lying between Aachen and Cologne on the river Rur at the northern edge of the Eifel hills. It is the capital of the district of the same name and has a population of around 91,000.

History

The site has Celtic and Roman origins (the Roman settlement was known as Marcodurum) and appears in Frankish records as a royal estate, Villa Duria. Düren received town rights in the 13th century and later belonged to the Duchy of Jülich. Its economy was transformed by paper-making, powered by the Rur's soft water, together with textile and metal industries; the town became known as a centre of the German paper industry.

Second World War and rebuilding

On 16 November 1944 Düren was almost completely destroyed in a major Allied bombing raid, one of the heaviest on any German town, and stood close to the front line during the fighting around the Rur in early 1945. The town was rebuilt in the 1950s in the style of that decade, giving its centre a distinctive post-war character.

Sights and culture

Landmarks include St Anne's Church, which holds the relic of the head of Saint Anne and draws an annual pilgrimage (Annafest/Annakirmes, one of the largest funfairs in the region), and the Leopold-Hoesch-Museum of modern art. The nearby Rur valley leads south to the Eifel and the Rursee lakes.
