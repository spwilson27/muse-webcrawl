SS Great Eastern was an iron sail-powered, paddle wheel and screw-propelled steamship designed by Isambard Kingdom Brunel and built by John Scott Russell on the River Thames at Millwall. At nearly 700 feet long and almost 19,000 gross register tons, she was by far the largest ship ever built at the time of her 1858 launch and remained the largest passenger ship in the world until 1899.

Design

Brunel conceived the ship to carry enough coal to sail from Britain to Australia and back without refuelling, making her effectively a floating coaling strategy as much as a liner. Uniquely, she combined three propulsion systems: two 56-foot paddle wheels, a four-bladed screw propeller, and six masts carrying sail. Her iron hull was double-skinned and divided into longitudinal and transverse bulkheads far in advance of contemporary practice.

Career

Construction and launch were beset by cost overruns and accidents that bankrupted her original owners; Brunel himself died in 1859, shortly after her first sea trials. Unsuited economically to the passenger routes for which she was sold, Great Eastern found her true role as a cable layer: in 1866 she laid the first successful transatlantic telegraph cable, and she went on to lay several more intercontinental cables — work for which her immense size was ideal. Later used as a floating advertisement and showboat at Liverpool, she was broken up in 1889–90, a job that reputedly took 18 months because of her double hull.
