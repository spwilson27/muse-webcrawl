The RSPB (Royal Society for the Protection of Birds) is a charitable organisation registered in England and Wales and in Scotland. It was founded in 1889 as the Society for the Protection of Birds, initially campaigning against the use of bird feathers in fashion. It is Europe's largest nature conservation charity and the United Kingdom's largest wildlife conservation organisation by membership, working to protect birds, other wildlife and the environment through research, habitat management, policy advocacy and public engagement. Its emblem is the avocet, a bird that returned to breed in Britain under the society's protection.

History

The society began in Manchester in 1889, founded by Emily Williamson at Didsbury, as the Plumage League, in protest at the plumage trade driving egrets and other birds toward extinction for ladies' hats. It merged with a London group and received its royal charter in 1904. The Importation of Plumage (Prohibition) Act 1921 was one of its early legislative successes. The society bought its first nature reserve, at Dungeness, in 1931.

Activities

The RSPB maintains more than 200 nature reserves across the UK covering roughly 143,000 hectares, including well-known sites such as Minsmere in Suffolk, Bempton Cliffs in Yorkshire, and its headquarters reserve at The Lodge, Sandy. It runs the Big Garden Birdwatch, a mass-participation survey held each January since 1979 and regarded as one of the world's largest wildlife surveys. Internationally, the RSPB is the UK partner of BirdLife International and works on issues such as illegal bird killing in the Mediterranean and the restoration of island ecosystems. It campaigns on climate change, farmland bird declines and marine protection, and was instrumental in the protection of species including the avocet, osprey and red kite.

Organisation

The society is governed by a council of trustees, publishes the members' magazine Birds (formerly Bird Notes and News), and operates through country offices in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. It does not receive direct government funding for its reserves and relies chiefly on membership subscriptions, donations, grants and legacies.
