Okavango Delta

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Okavango Delta
LocationNorthwestern Botswana
TypeInland (endorheic) alluvial delta
SizeRoughly 6,000–22,000 km² depending on seasonal flood
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (2014); Ramsar wetland

The Okavango Delta in Botswana is a vast inland river delta produced by seasonal flooding of the Okavango River, which drains summer rainfall from the Angola highlands 1,200 km away and arrives in the Kalahari during Botswana's dry winter (peaking around June–July). Unusually, the water never reaches the sea: it spreads over the flat delta fan, is soaked up by plants and evaporates, sinking into the sands — an endorheic system in one of the driest landscapes on Earth.

Wildlife and people

The flood pulse turns the delta into one of Africa's greatest wildlife concentrations: elephants (one of the largest remaining populations), lions (famous for swimming between islands), leopards, buffalo, hippos, red lechwe antelope and over 400 bird species. Permanent swamps anchor the system, centred on Chief's Island. Local communities, including the Wayeyi and Hambukushu peoples, navigate the channels in mokoro dugout canoes, now also the classic vehicle for safaris. The delta was inscribed as UNESCO's 1,000th World Heritage Site in 2014 and is protected in part by the Moremi Game Reserve; threats include upstream water abstraction in Angola and Namibia, fencing, and climate-driven changes in flood timing, tracked by conservation science of the kind summarised in the IUCN Red List.
