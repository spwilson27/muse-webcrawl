Bornite

General
Category: Sulfide mineral
Formula: Cu5FeS4
Mohs hardness: 3–3.25

Bornite, also known as peacock ore, is a sulfide mineral with chemical composition Cu5FeS4 that crystallizes in the orthorhombic system. It is an important copper ore, mined in many deposits worldwide. Fresh surfaces are copper-red to bronze-brown, but bornite rapidly tarnishes on exposure to air, developing iridescent blue, purple, and green hues that give rise to its popular name.

Occurrence

Bornite occurs in hydrothermal veins, contact metamorphic deposits, and porphyry copper deposits, commonly associated with chalcopyrite, chalcocite, and pyrite. Notable localities include Butte, Montana; Bristol, Connecticut; and deposits in Chile, Peru, Zambia, and Kazakhstan. The mineral was named in 1845 for the Austrian mineralogist Ignaz von Born.
