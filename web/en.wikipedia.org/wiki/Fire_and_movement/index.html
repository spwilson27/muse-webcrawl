Fire and movement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

One element fires while the other moves, then they swap roles.

Fire and movement, or fire and maneuver, is the basic modern military low-level unit tactic used to maneuver toward an enemy and destroy it. It consists of two mutually supporting elements: a firing element that suppresses the enemy with fire, and a moving element that advances while the enemy is suppressed. The two elements alternate roles, "leapfrogging" (bounding overwatch) toward the objective.

Principles

The tactic exploits the fact that a unit under accurate fire tends to keep its heads down and cannot shoot back effectively. Suppressive fire need not kill the enemy; it only needs to prevent effective return fire long enough for the moving element to reach a new position of advantage. Once the moving element is set, it takes over the firing role and the other element moves.

History

Fire and movement developed as infantry weapons became more lethal in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; it was refined during the First World War with infiltration tactics and became the foundation of squad and platoon tactics in the Second World War and all modern infantry doctrine.
