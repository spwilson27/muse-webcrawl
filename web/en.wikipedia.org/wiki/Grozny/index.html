Grozny
Capital city of the Chechen Republic, Russia
Founded: 1818 (as a fortress)

Grozny (Russian: Грозный; Chechen: Соьлжа-ГӀала) is the capital city of the Chechen Republic, Russia, located on the Sunzha River. It was founded in 1818 as a Russian military fortress during the Caucasian War and grew into a major centre of the oil industry in the North Caucasus.

History

The fortress of Groznaya ("the formidable") was established by General Aleksey Yermolov. Oil discovered in the area in the late 19th century made the city—renamed Grozny—an important industrial hub of the Soviet Union. The city was devastated during the First and Second Chechen Wars of the 1990s and 2000s; the battles of Grozny (1994–95 and 1999–2000) left much of it in ruins, and the United Nations described it as the most destroyed city on earth.

Reconstruction

Since the mid-2000s, Grozny has been extensively rebuilt under Ramzan Kadyrov, with new high-rise towers (Grozny-City), the Akhmad Kadyrov Mosque (the "Heart of Chechnya", one of Europe's largest mosques), and the Akhmat Arena stadium, home of FC Akhmat Grozny.
