The Baja 500 is an annual Mexican off-road desert race held on the Baja California Peninsula in northwestern Mexico. Typically run in late May or early June, it is one of the most prestigious events in desert racing and, together with the Baja 1000, one of the best-known off-road races in the world. The course is approximately 500 miles (800 km) long, usually forming a loop that starts and finishes in the port city of Ensenada.

History

The race was founded in 1969, two years after the first Baja 1000, and was originally organised by the National Off-Road Racing Association (NORRA). When SCORE International took over the Baja races in the mid-1970s, the 500 became a fixture of its desert series. Over the decades the event has attracted factory teams, movie stars, and amateur racers from around the world, and it became famous outside the sport through documentary coverage of its dust, silt beds, and three-foot-deep water crossings.

Course and vehicles

The route crosses mountain passes, dry lake beds, pine forests in the Sierra de Juárez and Sierra de San Pedro Mártir, and Pacific coastal sections, all on unpaved surfaces that range from smooth dirt to boulder fields and deep silt. Competitors race in classes including unlimited Trophy Trucks, Class 1 buggies, Baja Bugs (modified Volkswagen Beetles), production-based trucks and SUVs, UTVs, motorcycles, and ATVs. Motorcycles start before dawn; the fastest four-wheel vehicles typically finish the full course in under ten hours, while many entrants take more than twenty.

Spectators and safety

The race draws hundreds of thousands of spectators along the course, and crowd management and safety — for competitors and the public alike — have been persistent challenges, aggravated by spectators standing on the course and by unauthorised "booby traps" dug by locals to create spectacle. SCORE has responded with increased course marking, penalties, and security.
