Batting (cricket)

A batsman executing a drive

In cricket, batting is the act of hitting the ball with a bat in order to score runs and prevent the loss of one's wicket. The player who is batting is a batsman (also batter). Two batsmen are on the field at any time, one at each end of the pitch, facing the bowler's deliveries from the striker's end.

Technique

Batting technique combines stance, grip, backlift, footwork, and bat swing. Orthodox shots include the forward and back defensive, the drive, the cut, the pull and hook, the sweep, and the glance. Unorthodox shots such as the reverse sweep and the ramp have become common in limited-overs cricket. Batsmen wear protective equipment including pads, gloves, and a helmet.

Strategy

The aims of batting vary by format. In Test cricket, batsmen may occupy the crease for hours, accumulating runs while wearing down the bowling; in Twenty20, scoring rate dominates and batsmen attack from the outset. Running between the wickets converts struck balls into runs when the ball does not reach the boundary — four runs if it crosses after bouncing, six if it clears the rope on the full.

Records

The highest individual score in Test cricket is Brian Lara's 400 not out for the West Indies against England in 2004; Lara also holds the first-class record of 501 not out. Don Bradman's career Test batting average of 99.94 remains the most celebrated statistic in the sport.
