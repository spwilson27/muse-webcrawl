Cougar

Puma concolor. Conservation status: Least Concern (IUCN).

The cougar (Puma concolor), also known as the puma, mountain lion, catamount, or panther, is a large felid native to the Americas. Its range spans from the Canadian Yukon in the north to the southern Andes in the south, the widest geographic range of any large terrestrial mammal in the Western Hemisphere. The cougar holds the Guinness record for the animal with the greatest number of English names—over 40.

Description

The cougar is an ambush predator with a uniformly tan or grey-brown coat, a long cylindrical tail, and powerful hind legs proportionately the longest of any cat, adapted for leaping. Adults typically weigh between 29 and 90 kilograms, males being larger than females. Despite its size, the cougar is more closely related to smaller domestic cats than to the "big cats" of the genus Panthera, and it cannot roar.

Behaviour and ecology

Cougars are solitary, territorial animals active mainly at dawn and dusk. They prey principally on deer, but also take elk, moose, bighorn sheep, raccoons, and other mammals, typically by stalking and a short chase followed by a bite to the neck. They are capable of leaping as far as 5.5 metres horizontally and nearly as high vertically.

Distribution and conservation

An adaptable generalist, the cougar occupies habitats from desert to tropical forest. It was extirpated from much of eastern North America by the early 20th century, apart from the isolated Florida panther population, but has been recolonizing parts of its former range. Conflicts with humans, usually involving livestock depredation and rare attacks on people, occur where habitat is fragmented.
