Tuolumne River

The Tuolumne River is a 149-mile (240 km) river in Central California, flowing from the Sierra Nevada in Yosemite National Park westward through the San Joaquin Valley to join the San Joaquin River near Modesto. It is one of the largest rivers draining the western Sierra and a major source of water and hydroelectric power for San Francisco and Central Valley irrigation districts.

Course

The river begins at the confluence of the Lyell and Dana forks beneath Mount Lyell in Tuolumne Meadows. It descends through the Grand Canyon of the Tuolumne and is impounded by O'Shaughnessy Dam at Hetch Hetchy Reservoir, completed in 1923 amid a famous conservation battle lost by John Muir. Below, the river passes Don Pedro Reservoir and flows past Waterford and Modesto.

Use and ecology

The Hetch Hetchy system delivers drinking water to some 2.7 million people in the San Francisco Bay Area and generates hydroelectricity. The lower river supports fall-run Chinook salmon, and parts of the upper river are designated under the National Wild and Scenic Rivers System. Whitewater rafting on the Tuolumne is renowned for challenging rapids such as Clavey Falls.
