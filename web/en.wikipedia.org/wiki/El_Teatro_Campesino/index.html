El Teatro Campesino
Founded: 1965, Delano, California
Founder: Luis Valdez
Base: San Juan Bautista, California

El Teatro Campesino (Spanish for "The Farmworkers' Theatre") is a Chicano theatre company founded in 1965 by playwright and director Luis Valdez in support of the United Farm Workers' grape strike in Delano, California. It is regarded as the most influential Chicano theatre troupe in the United States and a foundational force in Latino theatre.

History

Valdez, working with organiser César Chávez, created short improvised skits called actos, performed by striking farmworkers themselves on picket lines and flatbed trucks. The actos used stock characters — the patroncito (grower), the campesino, the scab — and broad comedy drawn from Mexican carpa (tent show) traditions and commedia dell'arte to dramatise the strike and encourage union membership.

Later work

After 1967 the company gradually broadened its focus to the wider Chicano experience, developing the mito (exploring indigenous mythology) and the corridos (ballad plays). Valdez's full-length play Zoot Suit (1978), drawn from the 1942 Sleepy Lagoon trial and the Zoot Suit Riots, became a landmark of American theatre and was filmed in 1981; Valdez also wrote and directed the film La Bamba (1987). Since 1971 the company has been based in San Juan Bautista, California, where it continues to perform, including its celebrated Christmas pageants.
