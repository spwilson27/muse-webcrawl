Trip Hawkins

William M. "Trip" Hawkins III (born December 28, 1953) is an American entrepreneur and founder of the video game publisher Electronic Arts (EA), the 3DO Company, and Digital Chocolate.

Early career and Electronic Arts

Hawkins studied strategy and applied game theory at Harvard University and earned an MBA from Stanford. He was an early employee of Apple Inc., serving as director of strategy and marketing and helping plan the company's 1980 IPO. In 1982 he founded Electronic Arts, pioneering the treatment of game designers as "software artists," with album-style packaging and direct distribution to retailers. Under Hawkins, EA grew into one of the largest independent game publishers.

3DO and later ventures

In 1991 Hawkins left EA's management to head the 3DO Company, which licensed a 32-bit console design to manufacturers such as Panasonic; the 3DO Interactive Multiplayer launched in 1993 at a high price and struggled against cheaper rivals. He later founded the mobile and social game studio Digital Chocolate (2003) and the educational games company If You Can (2014), whose game If... aimed to teach empathy and social-emotional skills to children.
