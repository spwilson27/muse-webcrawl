Team New Zealand (Emirates Team New Zealand)
Country: New Zealand
Established: 1993
Yacht club: Royal New Zealand Yacht Squadron
Competition: America's Cup

Team New Zealand, competing as Emirates Team New Zealand, is a professional sailing team based in Auckland, New Zealand, representing the Royal New Zealand Yacht Squadron. It is best known for its campaigns in the America's Cup, the oldest trophy in international sport, and for repeatedly pioneering technical innovations in yacht design.

History

The team grew out of New Zealand's challenger campaigns of the 1980s and was formally established under the leadership of Sir Peter Blake. In 1995, skippered by Russell Coutts, Team New Zealand defeated Dennis Conner's Stars & Stripes 5–0 at San Diego to win the America's Cup, and successfully defended it in Auckland in 2000.

Later campaigns

After losing the Cup to Alinghi in 2003 and losing the 2013 final in San Francisco after leading 8–1 against Oracle Team USA, the team reclaimed the trophy in Bermuda in 2017, helmed by Peter Burling with Glenn Ashby as skipper. As defender it won again in Auckland in 2021 and in Barcelona in 2024. The team is also credited with driving the adoption of foiling catamarans and, later, foiling monohulls in the Cup, and with innovations such as the use of bicycle-style grinding stations ("cyclors") to generate hydraulic power.

Organisation

Long led by managing director Grant Dalton, Emirates Team New Zealand has been funded through a mixture of naming-rights sponsorship, commercial partners and government support, and defends the America's Cup on behalf of the Royal New Zealand Yacht Squadron.
