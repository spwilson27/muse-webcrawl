Erythritol tetranitrate

Erythritol tetranitrate (ETN) is an explosive organic compound, a nitrate ester of the sugar alcohol erythritol. Chemically similar to the better-known PETN, it is a white crystalline solid with a melting point of about 61 °C and a detonation velocity of roughly 8,100 m/s at high density.

Properties

ETN is more sensitive to friction than PETN and is appreciably soluble in some common organic solvents, and it can be melt-cast owing to its low melting point. It has a positive oxygen balance (about +5%), greater than that of most military explosives, which makes it attractive in theory as a mixture component; its sensitivity and relative instability have limited practical use.

Medical use

Like other organic nitrates such as nitroglycerin, erythritol tetranitrate was historically used in medicine as a vasodilator for the treatment of angina pectoris, marketed in tablet form in the mid-twentieth century, before falling out of common use.
