Pea

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The pea is most commonly the small spherical seed or the seed-pod of the pod fruit Pisum sativum. Each pod contains several peas, which can be green or yellow. Botanically, pea pods are fruit, since they contain seeds and develop from the ovary of a (pea) flower. The name is also used to describe other edible seeds from the Fabaceae family, such as the pigeon pea (Cajanus cajan), the cowpea (Vigna unguiculata), and the seeds from several species of Lathyrus.

Peas are annual plants, with a life cycle of one year. They are a cool-season crop grown in many parts of the world; planting can take place from winter to early summer depending on location. The average pea weighs between 0.1 and 0.36 grams. The immature peas (and in snow peas the tender pod as well) are used as a vegetable, fresh, frozen, or canned. The species is used as a model organism in genetics: Gregor Mendel's experiments with pea plants in the 1860s founded the science of heredity.

Description

The pea plant is a climbing or trailing annual with hollow stems that reach 0.5 to 2 metres in length when given support. The leaves are pinnate with one to three pairs of leaflets and end in branched tendrils used for climbing. The flowers are white, pink, or purple, about 1.5 to 3.5 cm across, and are followed by pods 4 to 15 cm long containing typically five to ten seeds.

History

The wild pea is native to the Mediterranean basin and the Near East, and peas are among the oldest cultivated crops. Archaeological evidence dates cultivation in the Fertile Crescent to around 9,000 years ago. Peas spread to Europe and Asia in antiquity and were a staple food through the Middle Ages, usually dried. Eating fresh immature peas became fashionable in 17th-century France, and by the 19th century Thomas Jefferson was growing more than 30 varieties at Monticello.

Cultivation

Peas thrive in cool weather and well-drained soil. Like other legumes, they form root nodules with symbiotic bacteria (Rhizobium leguminosarum) that fix atmospheric nitrogen, enriching the soil. Varieties include garden peas, field peas grown for drying, snow peas and sugar snap peas eaten pod and all, and marrowfat peas used in processed foods.

Nutrition and uses

Green peas are a good source of protein, dietary fibre, vitamin C, vitamin K, thiamine, folate, and several minerals. They are cooked as a side dish, added to soups, stews, salads, and rice dishes, and are a traditional accompaniment to fish and chips in Britain as mushy peas. Dried split peas are used in soups and dals. Pea protein is increasingly used in plant-based meat substitutes.

In science

Mendel's crossing experiments with seven contrasting traits of Pisum sativum — including seed shape and flower colour — led to the laws of segregation and independent assortment. The pea genome, published in 2019, spans about 4.45 billion base pairs.
