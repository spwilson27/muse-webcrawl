Woggle

A woggle is a device to fasten the neckerchief, or scarf, worn as part of a Scout or Girl Guides uniform. Woggles are typically rings of leather, cord, wood, metal or plastic through which the ends of the neckerchief are threaded, holding it in place around the neck without a knot.

History

The woggle is said to have been invented in the early 1920s by Bill Shankley, a member of the team running the Scout training centre at Gilwell Park in London. Inspired by slide fasteners he had seen used by cowboys with their neckerchiefs, he carved a ring from a branch, and the device came into widespread use. The leather "Gilwell woggle" awarded to those completing Wood Badge leader training courses is a distinctive form made of a two-strand Turk's head knot.

Variations

Woggles are produced in many national and commemorative designs, and at large jamborees they are traded between Scouts as souvenirs. Some groups craft their own woggles from rope, paracord, carved wood or 3D-printed plastic.
