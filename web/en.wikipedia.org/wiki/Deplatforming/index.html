Deplatforming
Also calledNo-platforming, debanning
DefinitionRemoval of a speaker or group from an online platform or venue
Related conceptsContent moderation, shadow banning, demonetization
[Image: Illustration of a user account being suspended from a social media platform]

Deplatforming, also known as no-platforming, is a form of political activism or content moderation in which a platform provider (such as a social media site, hosting service, payment processor or university) removes or denies a platform to a speaker or group, typically because their views or conduct violate the provider's policies. The term gained prominence in the 2010s as major technology platforms began suspending high-profile accounts accused of extremism, harassment or spreading misinformation.

History and notable cases

The "no platform" principle originated in British student politics in the 1970s, directed against far-right organisations. In the internet era, prominent instances include the removal of the neo-Nazi site The Daily Stormer by hosting and DNS providers in 2017, bans on conspiracy theorist Alex Jones by several platforms in 2018, and the suspension of U.S. President Donald Trump's accounts after the January 6 Capitol attack in 2021. Payment processors and app stores have also withdrawn services from platforms such as Gab and Parler.

Debate

Supporters argue that deplatforming reduces the reach and recruitment ability of extremist or harmful movements, and that private platforms have the right to set terms of service. Critics contend it amounts to censorship, can displace communities to less moderated spaces, and concentrates speech-policing power in a few corporations. Studies have found that deplatformed figures usually lose most of their audience, even when they migrate to alternative services.
