The Frick Collection is an art museum on the Upper East Side of Manhattan in New York City, housed in the former residence of the steel magnate Henry Clay Frick (1849–1919). The museum, which opened to the public in 1935, presents Frick's collection of Old Master paintings and fine decorative arts in the intimate setting of a Gilded Age mansion.

Adjacent to the museum is the Frick Art Research Library, founded in 1920 by Frick's daughter, Helen Clay Frick, one of the largest art history research libraries in the United States.

History

Henry Clay Frick made his fortune in coke and steel as chairman of the Carnegie Steel Company. He began collecting art seriously in the 1890s, advised by dealers such as Joseph Duveen. Between 1913 and 1914 he built a palatial residence at Fifth Avenue and 70th Street, designed by Thomas Hastings of Carrère and Hastings, intending from the start that the house and its contents would eventually become a public museum.

When Frick died in 1919, his will bequeathed the house, its furnishings and his art collection to the people of New York. After the death of his widow Adelaide in 1931, the building was adapted by the architect John Russell Pope, who enclosed the courtyard as a garden court and added new galleries. The museum opened on 16 December 1935.

Collection

The collection includes masterpieces of European painting from the Renaissance through the nineteenth century. Highlights include Bellini's St. Francis in the Desert, Vermeer's Officer and Laughing Girl and Mistress and Maid, three paintings by Rembrandt including the monumental Polish Rider, Turner's The Harbor of Dieppe, Whistler's portrait of the Comte Robert de Montesquiou, Goya's portrait of the Duke of Osuna, and Fragonard's Progress of Love series, which fills a room of the mansion. The decorative arts holdings include French furniture of the ancien régime, Sèvres and Meissen porcelain, Limoges enamels, clocks, and oriental rugs.

Expansion

From 2020 to 2025 the museum undertook a major renovation and enhancement project designed by Selldorf Architects, which restored the historic reception rooms, converted the family's second-floor bedrooms into galleries, and added a new auditorium and education spaces. During the closure, the collection was exhibited at the Breuer Building on Madison Avenue under the name Frick Madison. The museum reopened in its renovated home on 17 April 2025.
