Tom Jones is a 1963 British period comedy film directed by Tony Richardson and adapted by John Osborne from Henry Fielding's 1749 novel The History of Tom Jones, a Foundling. It stars Albert Finney as the foundling Tom Jones, a charming young rogue raised by Squire Allworthy who falls in love with the neighbouring squire's daughter, Sophie Western (Susannah York), and is sent away after a series of scandals.

Style and reception

The film is noted for its exuberant, irreverent style: silent-film-style opening sequences, asides to the camera, speeded-up chase scenes, and a famous wordless eating scene between Tom and Mrs Waters. It was both a critical and commercial success and is regarded as a landmark of the British New Wave.

Academy Awards

Tom Jones won four Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director for Richardson, Best Adapted Screenplay for Osborne, and Best Original Score for John Addison. It received ten nominations overall, with three of its actresses nominated for Best Supporting Actress in the same year.
