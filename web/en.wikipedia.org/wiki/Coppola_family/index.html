Coppola family

The Coppola family is an American family of Italian descent prominent in filmmaking and music across three generations. Its best-known members are director Francis Ford Coppola, his daughter Sofia Coppola, his sister Talia Shire, his father, composer Carmine Coppola, and his nephews Nicolas Cage and Jason Schwartzman. Members of the family have won numerous Academy Awards, and the family is frequently cited as one of the great dynasties of American cinema.

Origins

The family emigrated from Bernalda in Basilicata, southern Italy. Carmine Coppola (1910–1991), a flautist and composer who played under Arturo Toscanini at NBC, and his wife Italia Pennino raised three children in New York and Detroit: August Coppola, an academic; Francis Ford Coppola (born 1939); and Talia Coppola, who acted under the name Talia Shire.

Second and third generations

Francis Ford Coppola directed The Godfather trilogy, The Conversation, and Apocalypse Now, winning five Academy Awards, and co-founded American Zoetrope. His children include Gian-Carlos (died 1986), Roman Coppola, and Sofia Coppola, who won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay for Lost in Translation (2003) and directed The Virgin Suicides, Marie Antoinette, and Priscilla.

Talia Shire starred as Connie Corleone in the Godfather films and as Adrian in the Rocky series; her sons include actor-musician Jason Schwartzman and musician Robert Schwartzman. August Coppola's sons include Christopher Coppola and Nicolas Cage (born Nicolas Kim Coppola), who won the Academy Award for Best Actor for Leaving Las Vegas (1995). A fourth generation, including Gia Coppola, has also entered directing.

Other ventures

Beyond film, the family is associated with wine: Francis Ford Coppola's Inglenook estate in Napa Valley and the Francis Ford Coppola Winery in Sonoma County are substantial businesses, and literary ventures have included the magazine Zoetrope: All-Story.
