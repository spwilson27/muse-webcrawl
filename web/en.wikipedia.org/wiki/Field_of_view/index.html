The field of view (FOV) is the angular extent of the observable world that is seen at any given moment. In the case of optical instruments or sensors it is a solid angle through which a detector is sensitive to electromagnetic radiation. For the human eye it is the extent of the visual field.

In humans and animals

Humans have a slightly over 210-degree forward-facing horizontal arc of their visual field when including eye movements, with about 114 degrees of binocular overlap in which stereoscopic depth perception is possible. The vertical range is around 130–150 degrees. Prey animals such as rabbits have near-panoramic fields of view approaching 360 degrees, while predators typically have narrower, forward-facing fields with greater binocular overlap.

Photography and optics

In photography the field of view is usually expressed as the angle of view, determined by the focal length of the lens and the size of the image sensor: shorter focal lengths give wider fields of view. For a full-frame camera, a 50 mm lens gives a diagonal angle of view of about 47°, while a 24 mm wide-angle lens gives about 84°.

Other applications

Field of view is a critical specification in video games (where it affects perceived speed and motion sickness), virtual-reality headsets, astronomy (telescope and survey fields), remote sensing, and machine vision.
