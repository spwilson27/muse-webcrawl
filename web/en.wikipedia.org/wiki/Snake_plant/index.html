Snake plant

KingdomPlantae
OrderAsparagales
FamilyAsparagaceae
GenusDracaena
SpeciesD. trifasciata

Snake plant is the common name of Dracaena trifasciata (syn. Sansevieria trifasciata), a species of flowering plant in the family Asparagaceae, native to tropical West Africa from Nigeria east to the Congo. It is also known as mother-in-law's tongue, Saint George's sword, and viper's bowstring hemp.

It is an evergreen perennial plant forming dense stands, spreading by way of its creeping rhizome. Its stiff, vertical leaves grow up to 90 cm tall, dark green with light grey-green cross-banding. The species is grown widely as a houseplant because it tolerates low light and infrequent watering, and because it is effective at filtering indoor air—NASA's Clean Air Study found it removes several common volatile organic compounds.

Cultivation

Like other members of its genus, the snake plant thrives on neglect. It requires well-drained soil and is easily propagated by division or leaf cuttings. Excess water is the most common cause of death, as the rhizomes rot in saturated soil. In warmer climates it is grown outdoors in borders and rock gardens, and it has naturalised in parts of Florida, Australia and elsewhere.

Cultivars

Numerous cultivars have been selected, including 'Laurentii' with yellow leaf margins, 'Hahnii', a compact dwarf rosette form, and 'Moonshine' with pale silvery foliage.
