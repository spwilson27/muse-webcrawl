Hungarian diaspora

The Hungarian diaspora (Hungarian: magyar diaszpóra) comprises ethnic Hungarians and their descendants living outside present-day Hungary. It has two principal components: the indigenous Hungarian minorities in neighbouring countries of the Carpathian Basin, and emigrant communities overseas.

Carpathian Basin communities

Following the Treaty of Trianon (1920), Hungary lost two-thirds of its territory, leaving large Hungarian populations in Romania (especially Transylvania and Székely Land), Slovakia, Serbia (Vojvodina), Ukraine (Transcarpathia), Croatia and Austria. These communities number roughly two million and maintain Hungarian-language schools, churches and political parties.

Emigration waves

Overseas emigration began in earnest in the late nineteenth century, chiefly to the United States and Canada. Further waves followed the Second World War and the crushed Hungarian Revolution of 1956, when some 200,000 refugees fled west. Significant communities exist in the United States, Germany, Canada, Israel, Australia and the United Kingdom. Since 2010, Hungary has offered simplified naturalisation to ethnic Hungarians abroad, and diaspora culture is promoted through organisations and heritage programmes.
