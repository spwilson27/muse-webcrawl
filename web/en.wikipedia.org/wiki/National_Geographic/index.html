National Geographic (formerly The National Geographic Magazine) is the official magazine of the National Geographic Society. First published in October 1888, it is renowned for its photojournalism, distinctive yellow-bordered cover, and coverage of geography, natural science, history, exploration, and world cultures.

History

The magazine began as a scholarly journal, but under editor Gilbert H. Grosvenor it embraced popular appeal and photography, building a mass readership. Its June 1985 cover photograph of the Afghan refugee Sharbat Gula, the "Afghan Girl," became one of the most famous magazine covers in the world. The magazine is published in dozens of local-language editions internationally.

Media expansion

The National Geographic brand expanded into television, beginning with specials in the 1960s and the launch of the National Geographic Channel in 2001. In 2015 a joint venture, National Geographic Partners, was formed with 21st Century Fox; after Disney's acquisition of Fox in 2019, the Walt Disney Company became the majority owner, with the Society retaining a stake.
