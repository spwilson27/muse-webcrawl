Apiary

An apiary, also called a bee yard, is a location where beehives of honey bees are kept. A beekeeper who manages an apiary is an apiarist. Apiaries range from a few backyard hives to commercial operations with hundreds or thousands of colonies moved between sites for pollination and honey production.

Siting

Good apiary sites provide nearby forage, water, shelter from wind, sunlight and access for vehicles, while avoiding flooding and excessive disturbance. Bees may forage several kilometres from the hive, so the surrounding landscape strongly affects honey yield and colony health. Local rules often govern hive placement near neighbours and public paths.

Management

Apiarists inspect colonies for disease, parasites such as Varroa mites, queen condition and food stores. Hives in modern apiaries commonly use movable frames, allowing inspection and honey extraction without destroying the comb. Migratory beekeepers transport apiaries to follow flowering crops and to provide paid pollination services.
