Richards Bay

Country: South Africa
Province: KwaZulu-Natal
Municipality: King Cetshwayo District / City of uMhlathuze
Area: about 143 km²
Population (2011): 57,387

Richards Bay (Afrikaans: Richardsbaai) is a town in KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa, situated on a lagoon of the Mhlathuze River on the Indian Ocean coast. It is home to one of the country's largest harbours and its deepest natural port.

History

The bay is named after Rear Admiral Sir Frederick Richards of the Royal Navy, who surveyed the coast during the Anglo-Zulu War; a harbour board was proposed as early as 1900, but the modern deep-water port was only developed in the 1970s, opening in 1976 primarily as a coal export terminal.

Economy

The Richards Bay Coal Terminal is among the largest coal export facilities in the world. The town also hosts aluminium smelters, a titanium smelter (Richards Bay Minerals), fertiliser and paper plants, and is a gateway for tourists visiting the Hluhluwe–iMfolozi Park and iSimangaliso Wetland Park.
