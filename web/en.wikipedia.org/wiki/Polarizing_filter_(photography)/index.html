Polarizing filter (photography)

A polarizing filter is a photographic filter that attaches to the front of a camera lens to reduce reflections, darken skies, and increase colour saturation. It consists of a polarizer that transmits light of only one plane of polarization, cutting glare from non-metallic surfaces such as water and glass and suppressing scattered light from the atmosphere.

Types and use

Linear polarizers were used with film cameras; modern autofocus and metering systems generally require circular polarizers, which add a quarter-wave plate so that the light reaching the camera's sensors behaves unpolarized. The photographer rotates the filter to adjust the effect: skies become a deeper blue, clouds gain contrast, foliage colours intensify as surface reflections are removed, and reflections in windows or water can be reduced or eliminated. The angle of maximum effect on the sky is roughly 90° from the sun.

Limitations

Polarizers absorb one to two stops of light, can cause uneven sky darkening with wide-angle lenses, and do not remove reflections from bare metal. Unlike many photographic effects, polarization cannot be convincingly replicated in post-processing, so the filter remains a standard tool in landscape photography.
