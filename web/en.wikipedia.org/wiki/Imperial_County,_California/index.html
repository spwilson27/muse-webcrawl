Imperial County, California

Irrigated farmland in the Imperial Valley

Imperial County is a county in the southeast of the U.S. state of California. As of the 2020 census, the population was 179,702. The county seat is El Centro. Imperial County comprises the El Centro, California metropolitan statistical area and borders the Mexican state of Baja California to the south, Arizona to the east, Riverside County to the north, and San Diego County to the west.

History

Imperial County was formed in 1907 from the eastern half of San Diego County, making it the last county in California to be established. It takes its name from the Imperial Valley, itself named by the California Development Company, which diverted Colorado River water through canals to irrigate the desert valley. The catastrophic 1905–1907 Colorado River breach created the Salton Sea, which still lies partly within the county.

Geography and economy

The county is largely desert, including part of the Algodones Dunes, but the Imperial Valley is one of the most productive agricultural regions in the United States thanks to irrigation from the All-American Canal. Crops include lettuce, broccoli, alfalfa, and melons; the valley is a major winter vegetable supplier. Geothermal energy production around the Salton Sea and cross-border trade and services connected with Mexicali are also important to the economy. The county has a predominantly Latino population and one of the highest unemployment rates in California.

Communities

Cities include El Centro, Brawley, Calexico, Imperial, Holtville, Calipatria, and Westmorland. Calexico, directly across the border from Mexicali, is a major commercial gateway.
