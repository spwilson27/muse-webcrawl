Low-power broadcasting
ServicesLow-power FM (LPFM), low-power television (LPTV), Part 15
Regulator (US)Federal Communications Commission
Typical rangeA few kilometres

Low-power broadcasting is a form of broadcasting in which a radio or television station operates at a much lower transmitter power than full-service stations, serving a small local area. It enables community, educational, religious and niche programming that would not be viable at full power.

United States

In the United States, the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) licenses several low-power services. Low-power television (LPTV) dates from the early 1980s. Low-power FM (LPFM) was created in 2000 after campaigns for community and "microradio"; stations are limited to 100 watts (LP100) or 10 watts (LP10), are non-commercial, and are owned by local non-profit groups, schools and public bodies. The Local Community Radio Act of 2010 expanded LPFM availability in urban areas. Unlicensed very-low-power operation is permitted under Part 15 rules with ranges of only a few hundred feet.

Internationally

Many countries provide for community radio at low power, including the United Kingdom (Ofcom community radio licences and restricted service licences), Australia, New Zealand and Canada, where low-power stations serve remote and Indigenous communities.
