Sailor fuku

The sailor fuku (Japanese: セーラー服, sērā fuku, "sailor outfit") is a common style of school uniform worn by female middle and high school students in Japan. It is modelled on the sailor suits of naval personnel and is characterised by a blouse with a large square sailor collar, a pleated skirt, and a ribbon, scarf, or neckerchief tied at the front.

History

The sailor fuku was introduced in the 1920s. Fukuoka Jo Gakuin is commonly credited with adopting it in 1921 under principal Elizabeth Lee, who had encountered sailor-style dress while studying in Britain; a similar uniform was adopted at Heian Jogakuin in Kyoto around the same period. Replacing the earlier hakama-style uniform, the sailor fuku allowed greater freedom of movement and became widespread across Japan in the following decades.

Design

The traditional outfit consists of a long-sleeved blouse in navy, white, or black with a contrasting sailor collar bearing one to three stripes, a matching pleated skirt, knee socks or stockings, and loafers. Colours and details vary by school, and summer and winter versions differ in fabric weight and sleeve length. Since the 1980s many schools have switched to blazer-style uniforms, but the sailor fuku remains iconic.

In popular culture

The sailor fuku is a familiar visual symbol of Japanese school life and appears extensively in anime and manga, most famously as the transformed costume of the characters in Sailor Moon. The style has also influenced fashion and is worn in cosplay worldwide.
