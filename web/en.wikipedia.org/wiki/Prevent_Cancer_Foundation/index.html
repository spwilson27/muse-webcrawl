The Prevent Cancer Foundation is an American nonprofit organisation dedicated to cancer prevention and early detection through research, education, outreach and advocacy. Founded in 1985 and based in Alexandria, Virginia, it is one of the few national charities focused exclusively on preventing cancer rather than treating it, on the principle that a large share of cancer cases and deaths can be avoided through lifestyle change, vaccination, screening and early intervention.

History

The foundation was established by Carolyn R. ("Bo") Aldige after her father's death from cancer, at a time when prevention received only a small fraction of cancer funding. It was originally known as the Cancer Research Foundation of America, later the Cancer Research and Prevention Foundation, before adopting its present name in 2007. Aldige led the organisation for decades and became a prominent advocate for prevention funding in Congress.

Activities

The foundation funds research grants and fellowships for early-career scientists working on prevention and early detection, including work on biomarkers, chemoprevention and screening technologies. Its public education campaigns promote screening for colorectal, breast, cervical, lung, prostate and skin cancers, HPV vaccination, tobacco avoidance, sun safety and healthy diet. Signature events include an annual spring gala in Washington, D.C., one of the city's long-running charity galas, and the Dialogue for Action conference on cancer screening and prevention. The foundation also runs community grants programmes supporting screening and education in underserved communities, and convenes the National Colorectal Cancer Roundtable's 80% in Every Community efforts to raise screening rates.

Advocacy

The foundation advocates for federal research funding, insurance coverage of preventive services and screening, and policies on tobacco and vaccination. It has been a regular participant in congressional briefings and in campaigns to preserve preventive-care provisions in health legislation.
