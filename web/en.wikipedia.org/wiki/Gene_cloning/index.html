Gene cloning

Gene cloning
SynonymsMolecular cloning, DNA cloning
Key toolsRestriction enzymes, DNA ligase, plasmid vectors, host cells
First achieved1973 (Cohen, Boyer, Chang)
ApplicationsRecombinant proteins (e.g. insulin), research, gene therapy

Gene cloning (molecular cloning) is the process of making many identical copies of a specific gene or DNA fragment by inserting it into a self-replicating vector, usually a bacterial plasmid, and propagating it in a host organism. It is a foundational technique of molecular biology and genetic engineering, distinct from cloning a whole organism.

Procedure

  1. Isolation of the DNA fragment of interest, cut out with restriction enzymes or amplified by PCR.
  2. Ligation of the fragment into a vector cut with the same enzymes, using DNA ligase, forming recombinant DNA.
  3. Transformation of the recombinant vector into host cells, typically E. coli.
  4. Selection and screening for cells carrying the insert, using antibiotic-resistance markers and reporter systems such as blue-white screening.
  5. Amplification and analysis: the host multiplies the cloned DNA, which can be sequenced, expressed as protein, or further manipulated.

History

Stanley Cohen, Herbert Boyer and Annie Chang performed the first successful gene cloning experiments in 1973, joining antibiotic-resistance genes in plasmids and expressing them in bacteria; in 1977 Genentech produced the first human protein (somatostatin) in bacteria, followed by recombinant human insulin (Humulin, approved 1982), the first commercial product of the technology.

Applications

Cloning underlies DNA sequencing libraries, production of therapeutic proteins and vaccine antigens, creation of transgenic organisms, forensic DNA profiling and basic research into gene function. Related techniques include subcloning, site-directed mutagenesis and Gibson assembly.
