Stefan Starzyński

Stefan Starzyński (19 August 1893 – 21 or 22 December 1939) was a Polish politician, economist and writer who served as mayor of Warsaw from 1934 to 1939. He is remembered for his energetic modernisation of the capital and for his role in organising its defence during the German siege of September 1939.

Early life and career

Born in Warsaw, Starzyński studied economics and took part in the Polish independence movement, serving in the Polish Legions during the First World War. He later worked in state administration and wrote extensively on economic policy. In 1934 he was appointed acting (commissarial) mayor of Warsaw by the Sanation government.

Mayor of Warsaw

As mayor, Starzyński pushed through ambitious plans: expansion of the tram and road network, new schools and hospitals, the development of the Vistula boulevards, and a grandiose four-year plan for the city's growth. His administration also improved municipal finances.

Siege and death

During the siege of Warsaw in September 1939 he served as head of the Civil Defence and rallied the population with daily radio broadcasts. Arrested by the Gestapo in October 1939, he was murdered in December of that year, probably in Warsaw; the exact circumstances of his death remain uncertain.
