Super Pit gold mine

The Super Pit is a large open-pit gold mine in Kalgoorlie, Western Australia, formed by consolidating the historic underground mines of the "Golden Mile" into a single enormous excavation. Approximately 3.5 kilometres long, 1.5 kilometres wide and more than 600 metres deep, it was for many years Australia's largest open-pit gold mine. The mine is operated by Northern Star Resources, which acquired it after Kalgoorlie Consolidated Gold Mines (KCGM) — long a joint venture of Newmont and Barrick — was dissolved in 2019–2021.

History

Gold was discovered in the area in 1893 by Paddy Hannan, sparking a rush that created Kalgoorlie. Dozens of rich underground mines worked the Golden Mile for a century. In the late 1980s, businessman Alan Bond began buying up the leases to amalgamate them, and the Super Pit began operation in 1989 under KCGM, progressively swallowing the old workings.

Operations

Ore is blasted and hauled by a fleet of very large trucks, then processed at the Fimiston plant, which uses gravity concentration, flotation and carbon-in-leach recovery. The mine produces several hundred thousand ounces of gold per year and employs a substantial share of Kalgoorlie's workforce. A public lookout at the pit's edge is a major tourist attraction. Plans to extend the mine's depth and life have been under study, alongside exploration of the Golden Mile's deeper lodes.
