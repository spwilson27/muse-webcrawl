Beta-catenin

GeneCTNNB1 (human)
Chromosome3p22.1
Length781 amino acids
FunctionsCell adhesion; Wnt signalling transcription

Beta-catenin (β-catenin), encoded in humans by the CTNNB1 gene, is a multifunctional protein that serves both as a structural component of adherens junctions in cell–cell adhesion and as the central transcriptional effector of the canonical Wnt signalling pathway.

Structure

The protein consists of a central domain of twelve armadillo repeats flanked by flexible N- and C-terminal regions. The armadillo-repeat superhelix forms a binding groove that interacts with partners including E-cadherin, the tumour suppressor APC, axin, and the TCF/LEF family of transcription factors.

Role in adhesion

At the cell membrane, beta-catenin binds the cytoplasmic tail of cadherin molecules and, through alpha-catenin, links the junction to the actin cytoskeleton, helping to maintain tissue architecture.

Role in Wnt signalling

In the absence of a Wnt signal, cytosolic beta-catenin is captured by a destruction complex containing APC, axin, GSK3β and CK1, phosphorylated, ubiquitinated and degraded by the proteasome. When Wnt ligands engage Frizzled and LRP5/6 receptors, the destruction complex is inhibited; beta-catenin accumulates, enters the nucleus, and partners with TCF/LEF proteins to switch on target genes such as MYC and CCND1 that drive cell proliferation.

Clinical significance

Stabilising mutations in CTNNB1, or loss of APC, cause constitutive beta-catenin signalling and are among the most common drivers of colorectal cancer; mutations are also found in hepatocellular carcinoma, medulloblastoma and other tumours. Germline mutations can cause neurodevelopmental disorders.
