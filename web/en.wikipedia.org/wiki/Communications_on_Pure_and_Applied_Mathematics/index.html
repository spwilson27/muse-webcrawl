Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics (often abbreviated CPAM) is a monthly peer-reviewed scientific journal of mathematics published by Wiley on behalf of the Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences at New York University. Founded in 1948 as Communications on Applied Mathematics, it adopted its present title in 1951 and is among the most prestigious journals in analysis, partial differential equations, and mathematical physics.

History and scope

The journal grew out of the applied mathematics programme built at NYU by Richard Courant, whose institute became a world centre for the study of partial differential equations. Papers associated with the Courant school — including foundational work on hyperbolic equations, shock waves (Peter Lax), numerical analysis, and integrable systems — frequently appeared in its pages. The journal publishes original research across pure and applied mathematics, with a historic emphasis on PDEs, differential geometry, probability, and fluid mechanics.

Editors

Editors-in-chief have included figures associated with the Courant Institute; in recent decades the journal was long edited by S. R. Srinivasa Varadhan, with Charles M. Newman and others among its senior editors. The journal consistently ranks among the top mathematics journals by citation impact.
