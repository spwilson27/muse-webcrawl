Washington State Parks and Recreation Commission

The Washington State Parks and Recreation Commission is a government agency of the U.S. state of Washington that manages the Washington state park system. The commission operates more than 120 state parks and properties encompassing roughly 120,000 acres across the state, including marine parks, heritage sites, trails, and recreation areas.

History

The state parks system began in 1913 when the Washington legislature created the State Board of Park Commissioners; the first park, Larrabee State Park near Bellingham, was established in 1915 on land donated by businessman Charles Larrabee. The system grew steadily through donations and purchases, including major properties such as Deception Pass State Park, developed with Civilian Conservation Corps labour in the 1930s.

Discover Pass

Since 2011, vehicle access to state recreation lands has generally required a Discover Pass, a fee programme created by the legislature to support state parks and other agencies amid budget cuts. The pass system prompted debate about public access, and the agency also offers free days on which no pass is required.

Activities

State parks in Washington offer camping, boating, fishing, hiking, rock climbing, winter recreation, interpretive programmes, and historic sites such as forts and lighthouses.
