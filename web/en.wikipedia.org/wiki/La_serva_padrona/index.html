La serva padrona

La serva padrona (The Maid Turned Mistress) is an opera buffa in two short parts by Giovanni Battista Pergolesi, to an Italian libretto by Gennaro Antonio Federico, after a play by Jacopo Angelo Nelli. It was first performed as an intermezzo between the acts of Pergolesi's serious opera Il prigionier superbo at the Teatro San Bartolomeo in Naples on 28 August 1733. Despite its tiny scale — two singing roles, one mute role, and a small string orchestra — it became one of the most famous operas of the 18th century and the model of the comic intermezzo.

Synopsis

Uberto, an elderly bachelor, complains that his young servant Serpina has become tyrannical and refuses to serve him. Serpina, who intends to marry him and become mistress of the house, escalates the battle of wills. In the second part she presents the mute servant Vespone disguised as a ferocious soldier, "Captain Tempesta", supposedly demanding a huge dowry to marry her. Frightened at the alternative, and realising his affection for her, Uberto agrees to marry Serpina himself — the maid becomes mistress, as her famous aria "A Serpina penserete" and their closing duet celebrate.

Reception and influence

Performed across Europe in translation, La serva padrona was the work that ignited the Querelle des Bouffons in Paris in 1752, a pamphlet war between partisans of French tragédie lyrique and Italian opera buffa in which Jean-Jacques Rousseau championed the Italian style and answered with his own intermède, Le devin du village. Its naturalistic comedy, economical scoring and the integration of recitative and aria set the template for opera buffa and helped define Pergolesi's posthumous fame: he died in 1736, aged only 26.
