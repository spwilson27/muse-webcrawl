United Nations Security Council veto power

The United Nations Security Council veto power is the power of the five permanent members (P5) — China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom, and the United States — to veto any substantive Security Council resolution under Article 27 of the UN Charter. A negative vote by any permanent member blocks adoption, regardless of overall support.

HoldersChina, France, Russia, UK, US
BasisUN Charter, Article 27

Origins and use

The veto was the price of great-power participation agreed at Yalta and San Francisco in 1945. The Soviet Union/Russia has cast the most vetoes, followed by the United States — frequently on Middle East resolutions — while Britain and France have not vetoed since 1989. Abstention by a permanent member does not block a resolution, a practice established early and affirmed by the International Court of Justice.

Criticism and reform

Critics argue the veto paralyses action on mass atrocities and reflects 1945 power realities; reform proposals include enlarging the Council, restraint codes in atrocity cases (the French-Mexican initiative and ACT Code of Conduct), and the 2022 General Assembly requirement to debate after any veto.
