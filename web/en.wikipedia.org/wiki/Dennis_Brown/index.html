Dennis Emmanuel Brown CD (1 February 1957 – 1 July 1999) was a Jamaican reggae singer. During his prolific career, which began in the late 1960s when he was aged eleven, he recorded more than 75 albums and was one of the major stars of lovers rock, a subgenre of reggae. Bob Marley cited Brown as his favourite singer, dubbing him "The Crown Prince of Reggae", and Brown would prove influential on a generation of reggae singers.

Career

Brown began singing as a child and made his first recordings for producer Clement "Coxsone" Dodd at Studio One while still a schoolboy, releasing singles such as "No Man Is an Island". In the 1970s he worked with producers including Winston "Niney The Observer" Holness, Joe Gibbs and Prince Tony Robinson, recording signature songs such as "Money in My Pocket" — a major hit in 1979 — "Wolf and Leopards", "Cassandra" and "Westbound Train".

Signing to A&M Records in the early 1980s, Brown reached a wider international audience, and he remained a major live and recording artist through the 1980s and 1990s, recording extensively for Sly and Robbie's Taxi label and many other producers. His enormous catalogue, warm tenor voice and romantic style made him arguably the most beloved male vocalist in reggae after Marley.

Death and legacy

Brown's health declined in the 1990s amid long-standing substance problems, and he died in Kingston on 1 July 1999 of a collapsed lung / respiratory failure following pneumonia, aged 42. He was posthumously awarded the Order of Distinction by the Jamaican government. Brown's phrasing and repertoire continue to be standards of the genre, and "Money in My Pocket" and his Studio One recordings remain staples of reggae compilations.
