The Minnesota State Fair is the state fair of Minnesota, held each year in Falcon Heights, midway between Saint Paul and Minneapolis. Nicknamed "The Great Minnesota Get-Together", it is the largest state fair in the United States by average daily attendance (and second by total attendance, after the State Fair of Texas, which runs twice as long). The fair runs for twelve days from late August into early September, ending on Labor Day, and regularly draws close to two million visitors.

The fair attracts nearly two million visitors over twelve days.

History

A territorial fair was first held in 1859, the year after statehood, and the fair settled permanently on its current site — the former Ramsey County Poor Farm — in 1885. The grounds grew with landmark Works Progress Administration-era buildings. The fair was cancelled in 2020 because of the COVID-19 pandemic (a drive-through "Food Parade" was held instead), returning in 2021 with reduced attendance.

Attractions

The fair is famous for its food — hundreds of items, many served on a stick, from the Pronto Pup (a battered hot dog) to cheese curds, Sweet Martha's chocolate-chip cookies and deep-fried novelties announced each year. Agriculture remains central: livestock shows in the barns and Coliseum, the Miracle of Birth Center, butter sculptures of the Princess Kay of the Milky Way finalists carved in the Dairy Building, giant pumpkins, and crop-art (seed art) competitions. Other attractions include the Grandstand concert series, the Mighty Midway and Kidway, Machinery Hill's farm equipment displays, the Giant Slide, the SkyGlider and Skyride gondolas, and the Ye Old Mill tunnel-of-love boat ride dating to 1913.
