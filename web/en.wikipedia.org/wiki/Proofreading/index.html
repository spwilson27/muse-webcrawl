Proofreading

Proofreading is the process of reviewing the final draft of a text—traditionally a typeset "proof"—to detect and correct production errors such as misspellings, punctuation mistakes, and formatting inconsistencies before publication. Proofreading is distinct from copy editing, which works on an earlier draft and may involve substantive changes to wording and structure; the proofreader is normally expected to make the smallest changes necessary.

Methods

Traditional proofreading compares the proof against the edited manuscript, often with one person reading aloud while another marks corrections using standardized proofreaders' marks. Modern workflows use word processors and PDF annotation tools, and professional proofreaders may work "blind" against no copy when checking for surface errors only. Strategies for catching errors include reading slowly, reading aloud, and reading text in an unfamiliar format to defeat the brain's tendency to autocorrect.

In publishing

Proofreading is the final quality-control stage of the publishing process, after copy-editing and typesetting. Errors that survive proofreading may later be corrected in an erratum or a subsequent printing. See also proof (printing) and typographical error.
