The New Yorker is an American weekly magazine founded on 21 February 1925 by Harold Ross (with Jane Grant), published by Condé Nast, renowned for its reporting, profiles, cultural criticism, fiction, poetry and single-panel cartoons — and for the rigour of its fact-checking department.

Tradition

Ross's founding formula — a sophisticated metropolitan weekly 'not edited for the old lady in Dubuque' — was executed by editors William Shawn, Robert Gottlieb, Tina Brown and, since 1998, David Remnick. Its mascot Eustace Tilley, from Rea Irvin's first cover, reappears each anniversary issue.

Standing

The magazine's long-form journalism (from John Hersey's 'Hiroshima', which filled the whole issue of 31 August 1946, to modern investigative work) has won many Pulitzers and National Magazine Awards.
