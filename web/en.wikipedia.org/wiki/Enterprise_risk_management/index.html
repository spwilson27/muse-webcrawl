Enterprise risk management

Enterprise risk management (ERM) in business includes the methods and processes used by organizations to manage risks and seize opportunities related to achieving their objectives. ERM provides a framework for risk management, which typically involves identifying particular events or circumstances relevant to the organization's objectives (threats and opportunities), assessing them in terms of likelihood and magnitude of impact, determining a response strategy, and monitoring progress.

Frameworks

The most widely used frameworks are the COSO ERM framework (first released 2004, updated 2017 as "Integrating with Strategy and Performance") and the ISO 31000 standard for risk management. COSO organizes ERM around governance and culture, strategy and objective-setting, performance, review, and information/communication.

Risk categories

Typical categories include strategic, operational, financial, compliance, reputational, and hazard risks. Financial institutions additionally face credit, market and liquidity risk, regulated under frameworks such as Basel III.

Roles

Many organizations appoint a chief risk officer (CRO). Boards of directors bear oversight responsibility, sharpened after the 2007–08 financial crisis and corporate failures such as Enron.

Criticism

Critics argue ERM can become a compliance exercise, that models underestimate tail risks, and that quantification of strategic risk is unreliable.
