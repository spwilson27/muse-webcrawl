The 1999 İzmit earthquake (also known as the Kocaeli or Gölcük earthquake) occurred at 03:01 local time on August 17, 1999, in northwestern Turkey. The magnitude 7.6 shock ruptured a segment of the North Anatolian Fault near the city of İzmit, devastating the industrial heartland east of Istanbul.

Damage and casualties

The earthquake struck in the middle of the night, collapsing thousands of apartment buildings in İzmit, Gölcük, Yalova, Adapazarı and districts of Istanbul such as Avcılar. Official figures reported more than 17,000 deaths and roughly 43,000 injuries, with hundreds of thousands left homeless; unofficial estimates of the death toll were higher. A refinery fire at Tüpraş in Körfez burned for days, and a small tsunami was generated in the Gulf of İzmit. At the naval base in Gölcük, a building collapse killed numerous personnel.

Cause

The North Anatolian Fault is a right-lateral strike-slip fault along which the Anatolian Plate moves westward relative to Eurasia. The 1999 rupture extended about 150 km, and was followed three months later, on November 12, by the magnitude 7.2 Düzce earthquake on the adjoining segment to the east.

Response and aftermath

The scale of destruction overwhelmed local rescue services; international search-and-rescue teams arrived from many countries, including Greece — a response that helped thaw Greek–Turkish relations in a period dubbed "earthquake diplomacy." Investigations blamed shoddy construction and poor enforcement of building codes for much of the collapse toll, leading to reforms, a compulsory earthquake insurance scheme, and prosecutions of some contractors.
