The Cleveland Museum of Art (CMA) is an art museum in the University Circle district of Cleveland, Ohio, United States. Renowned for the quality and breadth of its collection of more than 60,000 works spanning 6,000 years, it is one of the most visited art museums in the United States and one of the wealthiest, with an endowment among the largest of any American art museum. General admission to its permanent collection is free to the public, in keeping with its charter "for the benefit of all the people forever."

History

The museum was founded in 1913 through the bequests of industrialists Hinman Hurlbut, John Huntington, and Horace Kelley, and opened its neoclassical marble building in Wade Park on June 6, 1916. Major expansions included the 1958 east wing, the 1971 north wing designed by Marcel Breuer, and a comprehensive renovation and expansion by architect Rafael Viñoly completed in 2013, which added east and west wings around a vast glass-covered atrium.

Collection

The collection is encyclopedic: strong holdings in Asian art (particularly Chinese painting), Egyptian and ancient art, medieval art, European painting from Caravaggio to the Impressionists and modernists, American painting, prints and drawings, photography, textiles, and decorative arts. Signature works include Caravaggio's The Crucifixion of Saint Andrew, Monet's Water Lilies triptych panels, and significant holdings of Poussin, Turner, and Picasso.

Innovation and access

In 2013 the museum opened Gallery One, an interactive gallery anchored by the 40-foot multi-touch "Collection Wall." In 2019 the CMA adopted an open access policy, releasing images and data for roughly 30,000 public-domain works under a Creative Commons CC0 license — one of the most extensive open-access initiatives by a major museum.
