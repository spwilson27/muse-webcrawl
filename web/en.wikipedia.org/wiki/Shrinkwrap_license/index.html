Shrinkwrap license

A shrinkwrap license (also spelled shrink-wrap licence) is a type of end-user license agreement that comes packaged with software or other goods, named after the clear plastic shrink wrap used to seal retail boxes. The licence terms are placed inside or under the sealed wrapping, and the packaging typically states that opening the wrap, or using the product, signifies agreement to the enclosed terms. See the main article at shrink wrap contract for the legal analysis.

Overview

Shrinkwrap licenses became common in the 1980s as personal computer software was sold in boxes at retail. Because a buyer could not inspect the full terms before purchase, vendors relied on "money-back if not in agreement with the terms" offers: the purchaser could read the licence after opening the box and return the product for a refund if the terms were unacceptable. Typical clauses forbid copying beyond a backup, reverse engineering, renting or resale of the software, and limit the vendor's liability. Critics note that such terms are a peculiar form of contract because the buyer is expected to agree to terms they cannot see at the time money changes hands; supporters respond that layered contracting is common and efficient, citing cases such as ProCD v. Zeidenberg.

Modern equivalents

As software distribution moved online, shrinkwrap licenses were largely displaced by clickwrap agreements presented during installation or account creation, and by app store terms. The underlying legal questions — notice, assent, and the status of terms delivered after payment — continue to be litigated in those newer settings.
