Mohammad Reza Pahlavi

Mohammad Reza Pahlavi (26 October 1919 – 27 July 1980) was the last Shah (king) of Iran, reigning from 1941 until his overthrow in the Iranian Revolution of 1979. The second ruler of the Pahlavi dynasty, he pursued ambitious modernisation and Westernisation — most notably the "White Revolution" of the 1960s — while ruling with increasingly authoritarian methods. He took the title Shahanshah ("King of Kings") and in 1971 staged lavish celebrations of 2,500 years of the Persian monarchy.

Early life and accession

Born in Tehran, the eldest son of the Cossack officer Reza Khan, who seized power and founded the Pahlavi dynasty, Mohammad Reza was educated in Switzerland at the Institut Le Rosey. He ascended the throne in September 1941 after the Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran forced his father's abdication. During the Second World War Iran served as a vital supply corridor to the Soviet Union, and in 1943 hosted the Tehran Conference of Allied leaders.

The 1953 coup

Conflict with Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh, who had nationalised the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company, climaxed in August 1953 when a coup organised by the American CIA and British intelligence (Operation Ajax) overthrew Mosaddegh and restored the Shah's authority after a brief flight abroad. Thereafter the Shah ruled as the dominant figure in Iranian politics, closely aligned with the United States.

White Revolution and late reign

In 1963 the Shah launched the White Revolution, a programme of land reform, women's suffrage, literacy campaigns, industrialisation and profit-sharing, which modernised Iran but alienated both the landed elite and the clerical establishment led by Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini, whose denunciations led to his exile in 1964. Surging oil revenues in the 1970s funded rapid growth but also inflation, corruption and conspicuous inequality; political repression by the SAVAK secret police deepened opposition. Mass protests throughout 1978 escalated into revolution; the Shah left Iran on 16 January 1979, and the monarchy collapsed weeks later.

Exile and death

Denied permanent refuge by former allies, the Shah travelled through Egypt, Morocco, the Bahamas, Mexico and the United States, where his admission for cancer treatment in October 1979 helped precipitate the Iran hostage crisis. He died in Cairo on 27 July 1980 and was buried in the Al-Rifa'i Mosque.
