Gaspar de Guzmán y Pimentel, Count-Duke of Olivares (6 January 1587 – 22 July 1645), was a Spanish royal favourite and minister who dominated the government of King Philip IV from 1622 until his fall in 1643. Born in Rome, where his father was Spanish ambassador, Olivares rose through the court and became the young king's principal adviser on Philip's accession in 1621. He combined the titles of Count of Olivares and Duke of Sanlúcar, hence his usual style of "Count-Duke".

Programme of reform

Olivares embarked on an ambitious programme to restore Spain's military power and prestige during the Thirty Years' War and the renewed conflict with the Dutch Republic. His most controversial scheme was the Union of Arms, an attempt to make all the kingdoms of the Spanish Monarchy contribute men and money to a common reserve army, in place of Castile bearing nearly the whole burden. The project met stiff resistance in the Crown of Aragon, the Basque provinces and Portugal.

War and revolt

Under Olivares, Spain achieved early successes, including the recapture of Breda in 1625 (immortalised in Velázquez's The Surrender of Breda), but the strain of simultaneous wars in Flanders, Germany, Italy and against France after 1635 proved ruinous. In 1640 the burden of war provoked the Catalan Revolt and the Portuguese Restoration War, which ended the Iberian Union. Olivares' prestige collapsed; he was dismissed in January 1643 and died in exile at Toro in 1645.

Legacy

Olivares was a major patron of the arts, employing Diego Velázquez as court painter and building the Buen Retiro Palace in Madrid. Historians debate his record: he is often seen as a far-sighted reformer defeated by Spain's structural weaknesses, or as an over-ambitious minister whose policies accelerated imperial decline.
