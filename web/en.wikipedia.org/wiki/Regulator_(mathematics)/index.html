Regulator (mathematics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In algebraic number theory, the regulator of an algebraic number field is a quantity measuring the density of the field's units. It is defined as the absolute value of the determinant of the matrix formed by the logarithms of the absolute values of a system of fundamental units under the field's embeddings into the real and complex numbers.

Definition

By Dirichlet's unit theorem, the group of units of the ring of integers of a number field K with r1 real embeddings and r2 pairs of complex embeddings has rank r = r1 + r2 − 1. Choosing fundamental units ε1, …, εr, the regulator RK is the absolute value of the determinant of the (r × r) matrix of values dj log |σj(εi)|, where the factor is 1 for real and 2 for complex embeddings. For an imaginary quadratic field the regulator is defined to be 1.

Significance

The regulator enters the analytic class number formula, which relates the residue of the Dedekind zeta function at s = 1 to the class number, regulator, number of roots of unity, and discriminant of the field. It also appears in the Stark conjectures and in algorithms for computing class groups. For a real quadratic field, the regulator is simply log ε, where ε > 1 is the fundamental unit.
