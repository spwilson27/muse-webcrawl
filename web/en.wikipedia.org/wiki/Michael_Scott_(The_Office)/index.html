Michael Gary Scott is a fictional character in NBC's The Office, portrayed by Steve Carell and based on David Brent in the British original. He is the regional manager of the Scranton, Pennsylvania branch of the Dunder Mifflin Paper Company for most of the series' run — a well-meaning but deeply inappropriate boss whose desperate need to be loved and admired by his employees drives much of the comedy.

Characterisation

Michael is characterised by cringe-inducing humour, misguided sensitivity training and diversity initiatives, catchphrases such as "That's what she said", and a mug reading "World's Best Boss" (which he bought himself). Beneath the incompetence, he is repeatedly shown to be a gifted salesman and, occasionally, a surprisingly loyal and kind friend; the series softens him over its run, contrasting with the darker Brent.

Story

Having started at Dunder Mifflin as a salesman in 1992, Michael rose to regional manager. Story arcs include his disastrous relationship with Jan Levinson, the founding of the Michael Scott Paper Company after resigning in season 5 (before returning with a buyout), and his romance with HR representative Holly Flax, whom he eventually marries. Carell left the series in season 7's episode "Goodbye, Michael" (2011), with Michael moving to Colorado; he made a surprise cameo in the 2013 series finale as Dwight Schrute's best man.

Reception

The character is widely cited among television's greatest comic creations; Carell won a Golden Globe (2006) and received six Emmy nominations for the role. "Scott's Tots", "Diversity Day" and "Dinner Party" are frequently listed among the show's most celebrated — and most excruciating — episodes.
