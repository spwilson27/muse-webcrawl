Valdez, Alaska

Valdez (Alutiiq: Suacit) is a city in the Chugach Census Area in the U.S. state of Alaska. Located on an arm of Prince William Sound, it is a fishing and shipping port and the southern terminus of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline System. The population was about 3,900 at recent censuses.

History

Port Valdez was named in 1790 by the Spanish explorer Salvador Fidalgo for the naval officer Antonio Valdés y Fernández Bazán. The town grew during the Klondike Gold Rush as a supply point and was the starting point of the Valdez Trail to the interior. The 1964 Good Friday earthquake destroyed the original townsite; residents rebuilt Valdez on stable ground about 6 km away in 1967.

Pipeline and oil spill

In the 1970s Valdez was chosen as the ice-free southern terminus of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline from Prudhoe Bay; the Valdez Marine Terminal loads crude oil onto tankers. On 24 March 1989 one of those tankers, the Exxon Valdez, ran aground on Bligh Reef and spilled roughly 11 million US gallons of crude oil into Prince William Sound — at the time the largest oil spill in U.S. waters, with lasting ecological and economic effects on the region.

Economy and geography

Valdez is one of the snowiest places in the United States, receiving around 300 inches (7.6 m) of snow a year, and is surrounded by the glaciers and peaks of the Chugach Mountains. Commercial fishing, the pipeline terminal, tourism and the state ferry system anchor the economy; the Richardson Highway connects the city to Fairbanks.
