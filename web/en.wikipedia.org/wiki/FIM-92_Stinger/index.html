The FIM-92 Stinger is an American man-portable air-defense system (MANPADS) that operates as an infrared-homing surface-to-air missile. Developed by General Dynamics and now produced by Raytheon, it entered US service in 1981 as the successor to the FIM-43 Redeye, giving infantry units an organic defence against low-flying aircraft and helicopters. The complete system — gripstock, launch tube and missile — weighs about 15 kg and is fired from the shoulder as a fire-and-forget weapon.

Design

The Stinger missile is 1.52 m long and 70 mm in diameter, propelled by a two-stage solid-fuel motor to roughly Mach 2.2. Its passive seeker homes on the infrared signature of an aircraft's exhaust; the Stinger-POST (passive optical seeker technique) and reprogrammable microprocessor (RMP) variants added a dual infrared/ultraviolet seeker and improved logic to reject flares and other countermeasures, together with an identification friend-or-foe (IFF) interrogator. The warhead is a high-explosive fragmentation charge of about 3 kg in the later penetrating hit-to-kill configuration. Airborne versions include the ATAS (Air-to-Air Stinger), carried by helicopters such as the OH-58 Kiowa and AH-64 Apache, and vehicle launchers such as the Avenger system.

Combat history

The Stinger's most famous employment came in the Soviet–Afghan War, where from 1986 the United States supplied Stingers to the Afghan mujahideen through Pakistan. The missiles sharply increased Soviet and Afghan government aircraft losses and are often credited — though historians debate the extent — with contributing to the Soviet decision to withdraw. The Stinger has since been exported to dozens of countries, used in conflicts from Angola and Chad to the Falklands-era debates, and supplied in large numbers to Ukraine following Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022, where it has been used against helicopters, jets and cruise-missile-era targets. Proliferation of MANPADS, and the risk of their use against civil aviation, has driven long-running buy-back and stockpile-security programmes.
