Estádio do Maracanã, officially the Estádio Jornalista Mário Filho, is a football stadium in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, and one of the most famous sporting venues in the world. Opened for the 1950 FIFA World Cup, it hosted the decisive match in which Uruguay defeated Brazil before a crowd officially recorded at 199,854 — the "Maracanazo", still the largest attendance in football history. The stadium takes its name from the Maracanã River and neighbourhood.

History

Construction began in 1948 as Brazil prepared to host the 1950 World Cup; the stadium was still unfinished when the tournament opened but was usable. Pelé scored his 1,000th career goal there in 1969. After Brazil won the right to host the 2014 World Cup, the Maracanã underwent a major reconstruction (2010–2013) that replaced the interior with a single-tier bowl and added a roof, reducing capacity to about 79,000. It hosted the 2014 World Cup final, in which Germany beat Argentina, and the opening and closing ceremonies and finals of the 2016 Olympic and Paralympic Games — one of only two stadiums to host both a World Cup final and the Olympic opening ceremony.

Use

The stadium is the home ground of Rio clubs Flamengo and Fluminense, whose Fla–Flu derby is among the most famous in world football, and regularly hosts Brazil national team matches and concerts.
