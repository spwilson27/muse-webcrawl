Ball-and-socket joint

Anatomical diagram of a ball-and-socket joint

The ball-and-socket joint (or spheroid joint) is a type of synovial joint in which the ball-shaped surface of one rounded bone fits into the cup-like depression of another bone. This structure allows movement in nearly all directions — flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, circumduction and rotation — making it the most mobile type of joint in the body.

Anatomy

In the human body, the two principal examples are the shoulder joint, where the head of the humerus fits into the glenoid cavity of the scapula, and the hip joint, where the head of the femur fits into the acetabulum of the pelvis. The shoulder sacrifices stability for range of motion and is therefore the most commonly dislocated major joint, whereas the hip's deeper socket makes it much more stable. Ball-and-socket joints are also standard mechanical components, used in vehicle suspensions, trailer couplings and adjustable fittings where pivoting in several planes is required.
