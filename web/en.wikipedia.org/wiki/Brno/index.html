Brno is the second-largest city in the Czech Republic and the historical capital of Moravia, situated at the confluence of the Svitava and Svratka rivers. It is the seat of the country's highest courts, a major university and technology centre, and home to about 380,000 inhabitants.

History

A settlement existed at the site from prehistory; Brno received city rights in 1243 and became a fortified royal city, resisting Hussite and Swedish sieges — most famously in 1645, when a small garrison held out against a much larger Swedish army. In the 19th century Brno industrialised as a textile centre, the "Moravian Manchester", and in the 1860s the Augustinian abbot Gregor Mendel conducted his pea experiments in the city, founding the science of genetics. After 1918 the city was part of Czechoslovakia; its large German population was expelled after the Second World War, including in the 1945 Brno death march. In the communist era and since 1989 Brno has developed as a judicial, academic and high-tech hub.

Landmarks

The skyline is dominated by the Cathedral of St Peter and Paul on Petrov Hill and by Špilberk Castle, once a notorious Habsburg prison. Brno's functionalist Villa Tugendhat (1930), designed by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Other sights include the Capuchin crypt, the old town hall with its "Brno dragon", the Mendel Museum, and the ossuary beneath St James's Church. The nearby Brno Circuit (Masaryk Circuit) long hosted the Czech Republic motorcycle Grand Prix.

Education and economy

Masaryk University, Brno University of Technology and Mendel University serve tens of thousands of students, giving Brno the largest student population relative to size in the country. The economy centres on IT, engineering, life sciences and the Brno Exhibition Centre's trade fairs.
