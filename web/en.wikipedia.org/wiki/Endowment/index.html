Endowment

Endowment may refer to several related concepts. In finance, a financial endowment is a legal structure for managing, and in many cases indefinitely perpetuating, a pool of financial, real estate, or other investments for a specific purpose according to the will of its founders and donors. Endowments are often structured so that the principal value is kept intact, while the investment income or a portion of the principal is available for use each year.

Universities, hospitals and foundations are commonly supported by endowments.

Financial endowments

Endowments are typically held by universities, hospitals, religious institutions and charitable foundations. Large university endowments, such as those of Harvard and Yale, rank among the largest institutional investment funds in the world. Donors may restrict the use of endowed funds to particular purposes, such as scholarships or endowed professorships (chairs).

Other uses

In economics, an endowment is the initial allocation of goods or resources in a model, as in the endowment effect or factor endowments in trade theory. In the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, the endowment is a temple ceremony. Historically, an endowment could also refer to a dowry or a widow's dower.
