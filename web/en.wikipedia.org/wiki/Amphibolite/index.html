TypeMetamorphic rock
CompositionChiefly hornblende (amphibole) and plagioclase feldspar

Amphibolite is a coarse-grained metamorphic rock composed mainly of the amphibole mineral hornblende and plagioclase feldspar, with little or no quartz. It is typically dark green to black and forms when mafic igneous rocks such as basalt and gabbro are metamorphosed under the moderate-to-high temperatures and pressures of the amphibolite facies.

Formation

Amphibolites are divided by origin into ortho-amphibolites, derived from igneous protoliths, and para-amphibolites, derived from calcareous sediments such as marl. Ortho-amphibolites record the metamorphism of oceanic crust and volcanic sequences and are common in ancient greenstone belts and Precambrian shield terrains. With increasing grade, amphibolite may pass into granulite; with retrograde alteration it can alter to greenschist or epidote-bearing assemblages.

Properties and uses

Amphibolite is hard and durable. It is quarried for crushed stone and aggregate, used as a dimension stone for cladding and paving, and was fashioned into polished axes and adzes by Neolithic peoples, who valued its toughness. Its composition and mineral assemblages are used by geologists as indicators of the pressure and temperature conditions of regional metamorphism.
