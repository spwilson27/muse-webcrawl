Adam Weishaupt

Johann Adam Weishaupt (6 February 1748 – 18 November 1830) was a German philosopher, professor of civil and canon law, and founder of the Illuminati, a secret society that sought to advance Enlightenment ideals of reason, secular education, and opposition to superstition and clerical influence.

Early life

Weishaupt was born in Ingolstadt in the Electorate of Bavaria. His father died when he was young, and he was raised by his godfather, Baron Johann Adam von Ickstatt, a professor of law who exposed him to Enlightenment and rationalist philosophy. Educated by Jesuits and then at the University of Ingolstadt, Weishaupt received a doctorate in law in 1768 and became professor of civil law in 1772, then professor of canon law in 1773 — the first layman to hold that chair, previously reserved for Jesuit clergy — a position that made him enemies among the ex-Jesuits who still influenced the university.

Founding of the Illuminati

On 1 May 1776, Weishaupt founded the Order of the Perfectibilists, soon renamed the Order of the Illuminati, with the aim of promoting rational thinking and moral improvement among elites through a secret network modeled partly on Freemasonry, which many members also joined. Taking the order name "Spartacus," Weishaupt devised an elaborate system of degrees and classical pseudonyms. The order attracted notables including Johann Wolfgang von Goethe and Johann Gottfried Herder, and grew especially after Adolph Freiherr Knigge joined and reorganized it, though the two men later quarreled.

Suppression and exile

The Bavarian government, alarmed by secret societies, banned them in 1784–1785 and specifically outlawed the Illuminati; papers seized from members were published and used to discredit the order. Weishaupt fled Bavaria and settled in Gotha under the protection of Duke Ernest II of Saxe-Gotha-Altenburg, where he wrote defenses of the order and works on philosophy and education until his death in 1830. Despite its brief existence, the Illuminati became a lasting focus of conspiracy theories alleging its secret survival and influence.
