Tap and die

Taps and dies used for cutting screw threads by hand

Taps and dies are tools used to create screw threads, in a process called threading. A tap cuts an internal (female) thread — the thread inside a hole, as in a nut — while a die cuts an external (male) thread on a rod or bolt blank. Together they allow machinists and repairers to make threaded fasteners and to repair damaged threads.

Taps

A tap resembles a bolt made of hardened steel with flutes ground along its length to form cutting edges and to provide channels for chips to escape. Hand taps are usually supplied in sets of three for each size: a taper tap, with a long gradual lead that starts easily; a plug (second) tap, with a shorter chamfer; and a bottoming (finishing) tap, which can cut threads nearly to the bottom of a blind hole. Taps are turned with a tap wrench and used with cutting fluid; the tap is typically advanced half a turn and backed off a quarter turn to break chips.

Dies

A die is a disc or block of hardened steel with a threaded central hole whose edges are cut away to form cutting faces. Round dies are held in a die stock (handle) and are started on the chamfered end of a rod. Die nuts (hexagonal re-threading dies) are used mainly to clean up existing threads rather than cut new ones.

Thread standards

Taps and dies are made in the thread systems in common use, principally ISO metric coarse and fine pitches, and in imperial systems such as UNC, UNF and British Standard Whitworth/BSF for older machinery. The tap drill size for a hole is chosen so that the drilled hole is slightly smaller than the thread's major diameter, leaving material for the tap to cut into full thread form.
