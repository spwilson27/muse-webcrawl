Islamic philosophy (falsafa) is the tradition of philosophical inquiry in the Islamic world, beginning with the Graeco-Arabic translation movement centred on Abbasid Baghdad's House of Wisdom. Thinkers such as al-Kindi, al-Farabi and Avicenna (Ibn Sina) developed Aristotelian and Neoplatonic systems of extraordinary sophistication; Al-Ghazali's theological critique and Averroes's rejoinder define its classic debate, and its transmission to Latin Europe transformed scholasticism.

Major themes

Central problems included the eternity versus creation of the world, the nature of the intellect and soul, prophecy, and God's knowledge of particulars. Avicenna's 'flying man' thought experiment (self-awareness without senses) and his essence–existence distinction influenced thinkers from Aquinas to Descartes. The tradition continued vigorously in Persia (Suhrawardi's Illuminationism, Mulla Sadra) even where it waned in the Arab west.
