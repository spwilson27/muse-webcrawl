Home rule

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Home rule is the government of a colony, dependent country or region by its own citizens — in British and Irish history, specifically the nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century movement for an Irish parliament within the United Kingdom, and by extension any devolved self-government arrangement, such as the modern assemblies in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

2. Wider usage

Irish home rule

Isaac Butt's Home Government Association (1870) and then Charles Stewart Parnell's disciplined Irish Parliamentary Party made home rule the central question of British politics: Gladstone's bills of 1886 (defeated by Liberal Unionist secession) and 1893 (passed the Commons, killed in the Lords), and Asquith's 1912 bill — passed under the Parliament Act but suspended for the Great War — faced Ulster unionist resistance armed and organised (the Ulster Volunteers, the Curragh incident) that prefigured civil war. The Easter Rising (1916) and Sinn Féin's 1918 victory overtook gradualism; the Government of Ireland Act 1920 partitioned the island into two home-rule states, the south becoming a dominion and then a republic.

Wider usage

The term now labels devolution settlements generally: Greenland's home rule (1979, extended to self-government 2009), US municipal home rule (cities' charter autonomy from state legislatures), and the District of Columbia's limited self-government (1973).
