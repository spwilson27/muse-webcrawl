Lesser evil

Concept"Choosing the lesser of two evils"
FieldEthics, politics
Related ideasPrinciple of double effect, dirty hands, harm reduction

The lesser evil — commonly expressed as "the lesser of two evils" — is an ethical and political principle according to which, when faced with two undesirable options, one should choose the one that is less harmful. The idea is ancient: analogous maxims appear in Aristotle and in later moral theology, and it underlies doctrines such as just-war reasoning and the principle of double effect.

In politics

In electoral politics, "lesser-evil voting" describes supporting a candidate one dislikes because the alternative is judged worse; it is a recurring theme in two-party systems and a frequent subject of debate, with critics arguing that repeated lesser-evil choices entrench bad options and depress ideal standards, and defenders arguing that outcomes, not intentions, determine real-world harm. The phrase also appears in discussions of wartime alliances and emergency measures justified as preventing something worse.

Criticism

Philosophers note the principle's ambiguity: it can serve genuine harm minimisation, but also rationalise complicity — the "banality" critique associated with Hannah Arendt's rejection of the lesser-evil defence by Nazi-era functionaries, who argued that participation itself legitimised the system. The concept remains a touchstone in debates over utilitarianism, dirty hands in politics and transitional justice.
