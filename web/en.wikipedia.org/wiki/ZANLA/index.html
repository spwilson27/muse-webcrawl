ZANLA

The Zimbabwe African National Liberation Army (ZANLA) was the military wing of the Zimbabwe African National Union (ZANU), a militant African nationalist organisation that fought against the government of Rhodesia in the Rhodesian Bush War (Second Chimurenga).

ZANLA
Active: 1965–1980
Country: Rhodesia / Zimbabwe
Allegiance: ZANU
Role: Guerrilla warfare
Disbanded: Integrated into Zimbabwe National Army, 1980–81

History

ZANLA was formed in the early 1960s after ZANU split from ZAPU. Operating from bases in Zambia and, after Mozambique's independence in 1975, from Mozambique, it waged an intensifying guerrilla campaign in eastern and northern Rhodesia, supported principally by China, while its rival ZIPRA was backed by the Soviet Union.

Operations

Notable episodes include the 1966 Chinhoyi battle, the 1972 Altena Farm attack that opened the war's decisive phase, and large-scale infiltration in the late 1970s. Figures such as Josiah Tongogara commanded the force.

After independence

Following the Lancaster House Agreement, ZANLA cadres assembled at designated points and were integrated with ZIPRA and former Rhodesian forces into the new Zimbabwe National Army; tensions in that integration led to the Entumbane clashes.
