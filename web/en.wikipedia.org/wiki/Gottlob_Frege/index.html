Gottlob Frege

Friedrich Ludwig Gottlob Frege (8 November 1848 – 26 July 1925) was a German philosopher, logician and mathematician, regarded as the father of analytic philosophy and modern mathematical logic.

Gottlob Frege
Born: 8 November 1848, Wismar, Germany
Died: 26 July 1925, Bad Kleinen
Works: Begriffsschrift (1879), The Foundations of Arithmetic (1884), "On Sense and Reference" (1892)

Logic and philosophy of language

Frege's Begriffsschrift (1879) invented quantificational logic. His distinction between sense (Sinn) and reference (Bedeutung), and his logicist project of reducing arithmetic to logic in Grundgesetze der Arithmetik, were shaken by Russell's paradox (1902), communicated by Bertrand Russell just as volume II went to press.

Legacy

Largely neglected in his lifetime, Frege was championed by Russell, Wittgenstein and Rudolf Carnap, and profoundly influenced analytic philosophy. His work forms a backdrop to later proof theory including that of Gerhard Gentzen, and to figures studied by comparative scholars of the same era.
