Acid–base homeostasis

Acid–base homeostasis is the homeostatic regulation of the pH of the body's extracellular fluid. The proper balance between acids and bases — the pH — in the extracellular fluid is crucial for the normal physiology of the body and for cellular metabolism. Arterial blood pH in humans is normally maintained between about 7.35 and 7.45.

Mechanisms

Three lines of defence keep pH within its narrow range. Chemical buffer systems, chiefly the bicarbonate buffer system, act within seconds to bind or release hydrogen ions. The respiratory system adjusts ventilation to alter the partial pressure of carbon dioxide within minutes. Finally, the kidneys excrete excess acid or base and regenerate bicarbonate over hours to days.

Disorders

A blood pH below 7.35 is called acidaemia and above 7.45 alkalaemia. Disorders are classified as respiratory or metabolic depending on whether the primary disturbance lies in carbon dioxide or bicarbonate. Disturbances are assessed clinically with arterial blood gas analysis, using the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation.
