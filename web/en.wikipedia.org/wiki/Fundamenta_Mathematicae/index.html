Fundamenta Mathematicae is a peer-reviewed scientific journal of mathematics, founded in Warsaw in 1920 by Zygmunt Janiszewski, Stefan Mazurkiewicz and Wacław Sierpiński. It was a landmark in the history of mathematical publishing: rather than covering all of mathematics, it specialised from the outset in the new fields of set theory, set-theoretic topology, real functions and related foundations — an early example of the specialised research journal.

History and content

The journal became the house organ of the Polish School of Mathematics, publishing foundational work by Sierpiński, Kazimierz Kuratowski, Bronisław Knaster, Alfred Tarski and others between the wars, when Warsaw was a world centre for set theory and topology. Publication was suspended during the Second World War — Janiszewski had already died in 1920, and Mazurkiewicz died in 1945 — and the journal was revived after the war under the Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences, where it continues today. Most papers now appear in English; a notable feature is that problems and questions posed in its pages have often spurred significant research.
