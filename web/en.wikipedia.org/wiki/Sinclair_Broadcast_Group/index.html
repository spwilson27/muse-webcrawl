Sinclair Broadcast Group, Inc. (SBG) is an American telecommunications conglomerate headquartered in the Baltimore suburb of Hunt Valley, Maryland, and the largest television station operator in the United States by number of stations and total coverage, operating or providing services to roughly 185 television stations in 86 markets affiliated with all major American broadcast networks.

History

The company's roots lie in the Chesapeake Television Corporation, founded by Julian Sinclair Smith, which put Baltimore's WBFF on the air in 1971. Smith's son David D. Smith founded Sinclair Broadcast Group in 1986 and expanded aggressively in the 1990s and 2000s as FCC ownership rules loosened, acquiring station groups including River City Broadcasting, Fisher Communications (2013), Allbritton Communications (2014), and stations from Tribune Media's portfolio. Its proposed 2017 acquisition of Tribune Media for $3.9 billion collapsed in 2018 amid FCC and Justice Department scrutiny and an administrative hearing designation over alleged misrepresentation. In 2019 Sinclair led the $10.6 billion purchase of the Fox regional sports networks (rebranded Bally Sports) through its Diamond Sports Group subsidiary, which filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2023 as cord-cutting eroded RSN economics.

Programming and criticism

Sinclair is known for centralized content mandates, including "must-run" news segments produced at corporate headquarters and aired across its stations, and for commentary segments by figures such as Mark Hyman and Boris Epshteyn. Critics and media studies scholars have described the company as promoting conservative viewpoints in local news; Sinclair maintains its reporting is balanced. A 2018 viral video compiling anchors at dozens of Sinclair stations reciting an identical corporate script about "false news" drew national attention to the practice of synchronized promotional announcements.

Beyond broadcasting, Sinclair owns the Tennis Channel, the streaming service Comet and other diginets (Charge!, TBD, and The Nest), and the wrestling promotion Ring of Honor (acquired 2011; sold to Tony Khan in 2022).
