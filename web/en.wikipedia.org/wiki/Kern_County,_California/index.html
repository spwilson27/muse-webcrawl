Kern County is a county located in the southern part of the San Joaquin Valley in the U.S. state of California. Its county seat and largest city is Bakersfield. With an area of about 8,163 square miles, it is the third-largest county in California by area, extending from the Sierra Nevada in the east across the southern San Joaquin Valley floor to the Temblor Range in the west and south into the Mojave Desert and Tehachapi Mountains.

Economy

Kern County is one of the most productive agricultural counties in the United States, growing almonds, pistachios, grapes, citrus, carrots, garlic and cotton, irrigated by the Kern River and the California Aqueduct. It is also California's leading oil-producing county: the Kern River, Midway-Sunset and other giant oil fields historically made it one of the top petroleum-producing regions in the nation, and the county remains a significant producer of oil, natural gas, wind and solar energy. Edwards Air Force Base, in the county's south-eastern desert, is a major aerospace testing centre.

History

The county was formed in 1866 from parts of Los Angeles and Tulare counties and named for the Kern River, itself named by explorer John C. Frémont for his cartographer Edward M. Kern. The discovery of gold along the Kern River in the 1850s and oil at the end of the 19th century drove settlement. During the Dust Bowl of the 1930s, Kern County received large numbers of "Okie" migrants, an experience immortalised in John Steinbeck's The Grapes of Wrath, whose fictional Weedpatch camp was based on a real federal camp near Arvin. Bakersfield also became famous for the "Bakersfield sound" in country music, associated with Buck Owens and Merle Haggard.
