William Le Baron Jenney

William Le Baron Jenney (September 25, 1832 – June 14, 1907) was an American architect and engineer, known as the father of the American skyscraper.

Born: September 25, 1832, Fairhaven, Massachusetts
Died: June 14, 1907, Los Angeles
Known for: Home Insurance Building, steel-frame skyscraper

Career

Trained in Paris at the École Centrale des Arts et Manufactures, Jenney served as a Union engineer in the Civil War, then opened a Chicago practice. His Home Insurance Building (1885) pioneered metal skeleton framing, enabling tall buildings — foundational to the Chicago school.

Works and influence

Other works include the Leiter Buildings and the Manhattan Building. He mentored Louis Sullivan, Daniel Burnham, and William Holabird. See Skyscraper and Chicago.
