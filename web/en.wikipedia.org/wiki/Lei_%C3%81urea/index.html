The Lei Áurea (Portuguese for "Golden Law"), officially Imperial Law No. 3,353, was adopted on 13 May 1888 and abolished slavery in Brazil. It was signed by Princess Isabel, acting as regent for her father, Emperor Pedro II. The law contained only two articles: the first declared slavery extinct in Brazil from the date of the law, and the second revoked all contrary provisions. It freed the remaining enslaved population, some 700,000 people, without compensation to slave owners.

Background

The law was the final step in a gradual abolition process that included the Law of the Free Womb (1871), freeing children born to enslaved women thereafter, and the Sexagenarian Law (1885), freeing enslaved people over sixty. Brazil was the last country in the Western Hemisphere to abolish slavery.

Consequences

Abolition without compensation alienated the planter class from the monarchy, contributing to the military coup that proclaimed the republic in 1889. The freed population received no land, education or integration programmes, a legacy linked to persistent racial and economic inequality in Brazil.
