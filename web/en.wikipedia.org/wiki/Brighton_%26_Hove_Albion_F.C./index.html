Brighton & Hove Albion Football Club, commonly referred to simply as Brighton, is a professional association football club based in the city of Brighton and Hove, East Sussex, England. The club competes in the Premier League, the top tier of English football.

History

Founded in 1901, Brighton spent much of its history in the lower divisions, with a first-division spell from 1979 to 1983 that included reaching the 1983 FA Cup Final, which they lost to Manchester United in a replay. Financial crisis in the mid-1990s forced the sale of their Goldstone Ground home and a two-year groundshare at Gillingham, just as the club came within one game of relegation from the Football League in 1997. After twelve years at the temporary Withdean Stadium, the club moved in 2011 to the Falmer Stadium, funded by chairman Tony Bloom.

Recent years

Brighton won promotion to the Premier League in 2017 under Chris Hughton. Coaches Graham Potter and Roberto De Zerbi subsequently established the club as an admired, data-driven top-flight side; a sixth-place finish in 2022–23 brought qualification for the UEFA Europa League, the club's first European campaign.
