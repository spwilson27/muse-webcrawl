Alberto Kenya Fujimori Inomoto (28 July 1938 – 11 September 2024) was a Peruvian politician, professor, and engineer who served as President of Peru from 1990 to 2000. A figure of enduring controversy, he is credited with defeating the Shining Path insurgency and restoring macroeconomic stability, and condemned for authoritarian rule, corruption, and human rights abuses for which he was imprisoned.

Rise to power

Born in Lima to Japanese immigrants, Fujimori trained as an agricultural engineer and became rector of the National Agrarian University. Running in 1990 as an outsider with the slogan "honesty, technology, and work", he defeated the writer Mario Vargas Llosa in a runoff. His "Fujishock" austerity programme ended hyperinflation, and sweeping market reforms followed.

Self-coup and counterinsurgency

On 5 April 1992, backed by the military, Fujimori dissolved Congress and intervened in the judiciary in a autogolpe ("self-coup"), ruling by decree until a new constitution in 1993 concentrated power in the presidency. In September 1992 intelligence police captured Abimael Guzmán, leader of the Shining Path, a turning point in the internal conflict — though the state's campaign, including the Grupo Colina death squad, committed grave abuses such as the Barrios Altos (1991) and La Cantuta (1992) massacres. Peru also fought the brief Cenepa War with Ecuador in 1995, later settled by treaty.

Fall, trial, and pardon

Re-elected in 1995 and again in a disputed 2000 election, Fujimori's government collapsed months later when videos showed his intelligence chief Vladimiro Montesinos bribing an opposition congressman. Fujimori fled to Japan, faxed his resignation, and lived in exile until his arrest in Chile in 2005. Extradited to Peru, he was convicted in 2009 of ordering killings and kidnappings and sentenced to 25 years, and received further sentences for corruption. President Pedro Pablo Kuczynski pardoned him in December 2017, provoking protests; courts annulled and reinstated aspects of the pardon before the Constitutional Court ordered his release in December 2023 under the 2017 pardon. He died in Lima on 11 September 2024, aged 86.

His daughter Keiko Fujimori has led the Fujimorist movement and run for president three times, keeping his legacy at the centre of Peruvian politics.
