Quillen–Suslin theorem
Field:Commutative algebra, algebraic geometry
Conjectured by:Jean-Pierre Serre (1955)
Proved by:Daniel Quillen, Andrei Suslin (1976)

The Quillen–Suslin theorem, also known as Serre's problem on projective modules, is a theorem in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry concerning the relationship between free modules and projective modules over polynomial rings. It states:

If R is a field (more generally a principal ideal domain), then every finitely generated projective module over the polynomial ring R[x1, ..., xn] is free.

History

Jean-Pierre Serre posed the problem in 1955, asking whether finitely generated projective modules over polynomial rings over fields are free. In geometric language, the question asked whether every algebraic vector bundle on affine space is trivial. The problem remained open for two decades and generated a large body of partial results.

In 1976, Daniel Quillen and Andrei Suslin independently and almost simultaneously proved the conjecture in full. Quillen's proof grew out of his work on higher algebraic K-theory; Suslin's proof used what came to be known as Suslin's monic polynomial lemma. Both received wide recognition — Quillen was awarded the Fields Medal in 1978, partly for this work.

Significance

The theorem resolved a fundamental question about the structure of modules over polynomial rings, showing that they behave as simply as possible, in contrast with the rich theory of projective modules over more general rings. It has applications in algebraic K-theory, systems theory, and the theory of unimodular rows and stably free modules. Generalisations include results for Laurent polynomial rings and for polynomial rings over more general coefficient rings.
