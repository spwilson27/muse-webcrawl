The Gallo-Romance branch of the Romance languages includes in the narrowest sense the langues d'oïl and Franco-Provençal. However, other definitions are far broader, variously encompassing the Occitan or Occitano-Romance, Gallo-Italic or Rhaeto-Romance languages. The name derives from Gallo-, referring to Gaul, whose Celtic-speaking inhabitants adopted Latin after the Roman conquest.

Characteristics

Gallo-Romance languages are characterised by strong influence from Celtic substrate and Germanic (Frankish) superstrate: extensive palatalisation, loss of final vowels other than /a/, front rounded vowels such as /y/ (written u in French), nasal vowels, and in French the loss of most final consonants and a pitch-accent shifted to stress on final syllables. The Oaths of Strasbourg (842) preserve one of the earliest written records of a Gallo-Romance vernacular.

Languages

The langues d'oïl include French and its close relatives: Picard, Walloon, Norman, Gallo, Lorrain, Champenois, Poitevin-Saintongeais, and Bourguignon, among others. Franco-Provençal (Arpitan) occupies the upper Rhône and western Alps in France, Romandy (Switzerland) and the Aosta Valley (Italy). Broad classifications (e.g. Ethnologue's older scheme and some Romance linguistics traditions) also group Catalan and Occitan (Occitano-Romance) and the Gallo-Italic tongues of northern Italy (Piedmontese, Lombard, Emilian-Romagnol, Ligurian, Venetian) with Gallo-Romance.
