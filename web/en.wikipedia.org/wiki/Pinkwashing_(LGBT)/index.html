Pinkwashing (LGBT)

Corporate Pride branding, a common target of pinkwashing criticism

In the context of LGBT rights, pinkwashing is the strategy of promoting LGBT friendliness by a corporation, organisation or state in order to distract from or soften other negative actions — presenting oneself as progressive and tolerant on gay rights while obscuring human-rights abuses, harmful business practices or political agendas. The word is modelled on "whitewash" and "greenwashing".

Corporate use

The term is often applied to businesses that market heavily to LGBTQ consumers during Pride events while simultaneously donating to anti-LGBT politicians, maintaining poor labour practices, or dropping inclusive policies under political pressure. Critics describe such behaviour as treating LGBTQ support as a branding exercise rather than a genuine commitment.

Use regarding Israel

The concept gained wide academic attention through scholar Jasbir Puar and activist Sarah Schulman, who argued in a 2011 op-ed that the Israeli government promoted the country's record on gay rights — for example marketing Tel Aviv as a gay tourism destination — as part of a public-relations strategy to improve Israel's image and deflect criticism of its treatment of Palestinians. Supporters of Israel reject the term, pointing to genuine legal protections for LGBTQ Israelis and the severe persecution of gay people in neighbouring territories.

Broader usage

Pinkwashing has since been applied to governments, universities, political campaigns and other institutions accused of using LGBTQ-friendly gestures to launder their reputations. It remains a contested label, since the same policies may be viewed either as substantive progress or as cynical public relations depending on the observer.
