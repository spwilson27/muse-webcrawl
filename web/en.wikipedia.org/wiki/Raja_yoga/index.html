Raja yoga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Raja yoga (Sanskrit: राज योग, "royal yoga") is a form of yoga centred on meditation and the control of the mind. In the modern sense the term is closely associated with Patanjali's Yoga Sutras and its eight-limbed (ashtanga) path, and was popularised in the West by Swami Vivekananda, whose 1896 book Raja Yoga presented it as a scientific, meditation-based path distinct from the physical postures of hatha yoga.

The eight limbs

Patanjali's path comprises eight limbs: yama (ethical restraints), niyama (observances), asana (steady posture for meditation), pranayama (breath regulation), pratyahara (withdrawal of the senses), dharana (concentration), dhyana (meditation), and samadhi (absorption). The final three together constitute samyama, the meditative core of the practice.

Historical usage

Before the modern period, "raja yoga" appeared in texts such as the Amanaska and later hatha yoga works, where it often denoted the state of samadhi itself, contrasted with the forceful methods of hatha. The medieval Dattatreya Yogashastra lists raja yoga as one of four approaches, teaching it through meditation on the deity. Vivekananda's reframing made raja yoga the umbrella term for meditation-based yoga in modern global usage.
