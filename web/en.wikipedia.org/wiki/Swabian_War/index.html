Swabian War

The Swabian War of 1499 (German: Schwabenkrieg, also called the Schweizerkrieg, "Swiss War") was the last major armed conflict between the Old Swiss Confederacy and the House of Habsburg. It ended with the Peace of Basel, which effectively confirmed the Swiss Confederacy's independence from the Holy Roman Empire, although formal recognition of Swiss sovereignty came only with the Peace of Westphalia in 1648.

Causes

The reforms of Emperor Maximilian I and the Imperial Diet of Worms (1495) — including the Common Penny tax and the Imperial Chamber Court — were rejected by the Swiss, who refused to submit to imperial courts or taxes. Tension also rose when Maximilian's Swabian League formed in 1488 as a counterweight in south Germany, and when disputes over jurisdiction in the Thurgau and the Lower Engadine brought Habsburg and Swiss forces into contact. A skirmish over the St. John's Day grazing dispute near Maienfeld in February 1499 escalated into open war.

Course of the war

Fighting ran from February to September 1499 along a front from Graubünden to the Sundgau. Swiss forces, fighting with pike squares of hardy infantry, won a string of battles — at Bruderholz, Schwaderloh, Frastanz, Calven, and decisively at Dornach on 22 July 1499. Imperial and Swabian League troops, hampered by poor coordination, unpaid soldiers, and Maximilian's distractions against France, failed to break the Confederacy. The war also coincided with the Italian Wars: Milanese and French diplomacy influenced both sides, and the Peace of Basel (22 September 1499) mediated by Ludovico Sforza's envoys exempted the Swiss from imperial taxes and courts.

Consequences

The peace settled the Confederacy's status: the Swiss cantons were de facto free of the Empire, and Basel and Schaffhausen joined the Confederacy in 1501. Swiss military prestige peaked — Swiss mercenaries became Europe's most sought-after infantry — and the war is often seen as the last great conflict of the late medieval style of Swiss warfare before gunpowder-era changes.
