Hijikata Toshizō

Hijikata Toshizō (土方 歳三, May 31, 1835 – June 20, 1869) was a Japanese swordsman of the Bakumatsu period and vice-commander of the Shinsengumi, the shogunate's special police force in Kyoto. Nicknamed the "Demon Vice-Commander" for his strict enforcement of the corps' code, he remained loyal to the Tokugawa shogunate to the end of the Boshin War.

Life

Born in Hino, Edo (now Tokyo) to a farming family, Hijikata worked as a medicine peddler while studying Tennen Rishin-ryū swordsmanship at Kondō Isami's Shieikan dojo. In 1863 he went to Kyoto with Kondō as part of the Rōshigumi, which became the Shinsengumi. As vice-commander he authored its ironclad regulations (the Kyokuchū Hatto), violations of which were punished by seppuku. He played a leading role in the Ikedaya incident of 1864 and in purging dissident factions within the corps.

Boshin War and death

After the shogunate's defeat at Toba–Fushimi in 1868, Hijikata fought at Kōshū-Katsunuma and Utsunomiya, then joined Enomoto Takeaki's fleet and the short-lived Republic of Ezo in Hokkaido. He was shot and killed on June 20, 1869, while leading troops at the Battle of Hakodate, two weeks before the republic's surrender. Hijikata is one of the most popular figures of the Bakumatsu in Japanese fiction, film and anime.
