British Nigerians

British Nigerians are British people of Nigerian descent, or Nigerians resident in the United Kingdom. Together with British Ghanaians, they form one of the two largest communities of African origin in Britain; the 2021 census recorded around 270,000 Nigerian-born residents in England and Wales, with the wider community including their British-born children considerably larger.

Peckham in south London is a centre of Nigerian British life.

History

Nigerians have been present in Britain since the 19th century as seamen, students and traders. Substantial migration began in the 1960s around Nigerian independence in 1960, when many came to study and intended to return; political instability, the Biafran war and economic difficulties in the 1980s and 1990s turned temporary stays into permanent settlement. A further wave of professional migration followed in the 2000s, including large numbers working in the National Health Service.

Community and culture

The community is concentrated in London — particularly Peckham, Woolwich, Thamesmead, Barking and Dagenham — and is among the most highly educated groups in Britain. Nigerian Pentecostal churches, such as the London branches of the Redeemed Christian Church of God, are central institutions, and Nigerian British writers, footballers and musicians have a high public profile. Languages spoken include Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa and Nigerian Pidgin English alongside English.
