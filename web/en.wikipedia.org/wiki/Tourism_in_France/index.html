Tourism in France

France has for decades been the world's most visited country, receiving on the order of 90 million international tourist arrivals in peak pre-pandemic years, and ranking among the top destinations by tourism receipts. Its attractions span cultural heritage, gastronomy, wine regions, beaches, mountains, and historic cities.

Paris and cultural tourism

Paris dominates, with landmarks including the Eiffel Tower, Notre-Dame Cathedral, the Louvre (the world's most visited museum), Sacré-Cœur, and the Champs-Élysées. Beyond the capital, major draws include the Loire Valley châteaux, Mont Saint-Michel, the Palace of Versailles, Chartres Cathedral, and the prehistoric caves of the Dordogne such as Lascaux.

Regions

The French Riviera (Côte d'Azur) around Nice, Cannes, and Monaco is a classic summer destination; Provence is known for lavender fields and Roman sites; Normandy for the D-Day landing beaches; Brittany for its coastline; and the French Alps — including Mont Blanc and resorts such as Chamonix and Courchevel — for skiing. Wine tourism is significant in Bordeaux, Burgundy, Champagne, and Alsace.

Economy

Tourism contributes a substantial share of French GDP and employment. France has one of the largest numbers of UNESCO World Heritage Sites of any country (over 50), and the sector is promoted by Atout France. Domestic tourism is also large, with French residents taking many holidays within the country, particularly in August.
