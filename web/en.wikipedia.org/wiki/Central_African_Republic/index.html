Central African Republic

The Central African Republic (CAR) is a landlocked country in Central Africa. It is bordered by Chad to the north, Sudan to the northeast, South Sudan to the east, the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the south, the Republic of the Congo to the southwest, and Cameroon to the west. The country covers an area of about 623,000 square kilometres (240,500 square miles) and had a population of around 5.5 million as of the early 2020s. Its capital and largest city is Bangui, on the Ubangi River.

History

The territory was colonised by France in the late 19th century as Ubangi-Shari, part of French Equatorial Africa, and marked by concessionary-company exploitation. It became independent on 13 August 1960 under David Dacko. Since independence the country has experienced repeated coups and instability, including the regime of Jean-Bédel Bokassa, who proclaimed himself emperor of the Central African Empire in 1976 before being overthrown in 1979. Civil wars from 2004 and especially from 2012–2013, when the Séléka coalition seized power, displaced hundreds of thousands and prompted international peacekeeping deployments, including the UN mission MINUSCA from 2014. Faustin-Archange Touadéra has been president since 2016.

Geography and economy

Most of the country consists of savanna plateau between the Congo and Chad basins, with rainforest in the southwest and the Ubangi River forming much of the southern border. Despite rich deposits of diamonds, gold, uranium, and timber, the Central African Republic is among the world's poorest countries, with an economy based largely on subsistence agriculture. It consistently ranks near the bottom of the Human Development Index, and armed-group control of mining areas has fuelled continued conflict.

Demographics and culture

The population comprises many ethnic groups, including the Baya (Gbaya), Banda, Mandjia, Sara, and Baka peoples. The official language is French, while Sango, a creole, serves as the national lingua franca and is co-official in practice. Christianity is the largest religion, with significant Muslim and indigenous-faith minorities. The Dzanga-Sangha protected areas in the southwest are noted for forest elephants and lowland gorillas.
