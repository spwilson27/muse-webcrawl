Evensong

Evensong is a church service traditionally held in the late afternoon or evening, centred on prayer, psalms, canticles and readings from the Bible. The term is used in several Christian traditions, but it is most closely associated with the Anglican service of Evening Prayer as set out in the Book of Common Prayer. When the service is sung by a choir it is often called Choral Evensong, a form especially cherished in English cathedrals and college chapels.

Origins

The Book of Common Prayer's Evening Prayer, compiled by Thomas Cranmer for the 1549 and 1552 prayer books, fused the medieval monastic offices of Vespers and Compline into a single service in the vernacular, intended for daily use by clergy and laity alike. Cranmer's aim was that the whole Bible would be read systematically over the course of a year, with the Psalms recited monthly.

Structure

A traditional service of Evensong includes:

  • An opening sentence of Scripture, exhortation, confession and absolution
  • The Preces (versicles and responses) and the Lord's Prayer
  • Psalm(s) appointed for the day, sung to Anglican chant
  • The First Lesson, from the Old Testament
  • The Magnificat (the Song of Mary, Luke 1:46–55)
  • The Second Lesson, from the New Testament
  • The Nunc dimittis (the Song of Simeon, Luke 2:29–32)
  • The Apostles' Creed
  • The Responses, including the Lord's Prayer and suffrages
  • Collects for the day, for peace, and for aid against all perils
  • An anthem, hymns, prayers, and a blessing

Music

Choral Evensong has generated a vast repertoire. Composers from Thomas Tallis, William Byrd and Orlando Gibbons in the Tudor and Stuart periods to Charles Villiers Stanford, Herbert Howells, William Walton and many contemporary composers have written settings of the Magnificat and Nunc dimittis (known as a "Service") and of the Preces and Responses. Cathedral and collegiate choirs — such as those of King's College, Cambridge, St Paul's Cathedral and Westminster Abbey — sing Evensong daily during term time. The BBC has broadcast Choral Evensong on Radio 3 since 1926, making it among the longest-running outside broadcasts in radio history.

Other traditions

In the Roman Catholic and Orthodox Churches, the corresponding evening office is Vespers (in Catholic usage also called Evening Prayer in the Liturgy of the Hours). Lutheran and Methodist traditions retain forms of evening prayer, and "evensong" is sometimes used generically for any evening service of sung prayer and praise.

Cultural significance

Evensong is often praised for its contemplative character, combining ancient texts, architecture and music. Attendance at cathedral evensong in Britain grew notably in the early 21st century, attracting people beyond regular churchgoers, and the service is widely regarded as a jewel of the Anglican choral tradition.
