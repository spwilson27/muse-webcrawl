Train protection system

A train protection system is a railway technical installation designed to ensure safe operation of trains by supervising their speed and movement, intervening automatically — typically by applying the brakes — if the driver fails to obey signals or exceeds permitted speeds. Such systems help prevent collisions and derailments caused by signals passed at danger.

Types

Systems range from simple mechanical train stops and warning systems, such as the British Automatic Warning System (AWS) and Train Protection & Warning System (TPWS), to continuous automatic train protection such as the German LZB and the European Train Control System (ETCS), which supervises a train's movement authority and speed profile at all times. In North America, Positive Train Control (PTC) provides similar functions using radio and GPS data.

Development

Train protection developed from early 20th-century experiments with intermittent inductive systems, such as the German Indusi, toward modern in-cab signalling in which lineside signals may be supplemented or replaced by cab displays.
