Greenlandic language

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Greenlandic (Greenlandic: kalaallisut; Danish: grønlandsk) is an Eskaleut language spoken by about 56,000 people, primarily Greenlandic Inuit in Greenland and Denmark. It is the only official language of Greenland since 2009.

Greenlandic is a polysynthetic language, in which very long words are built from a root plus many derivational and inflectional suffixes, so that a single word can express what requires a whole sentence in English. It is ergative–absolutive in alignment and has a complex system of four vowels and numerous consonant clusters.

Dialects

The principal dialects are West Greenlandic (kalaallisut proper), on which the standard written language is based; East Greenlandic (tunumiisut); and the northern dialect Inuktun (avanersuarmiutut) around Qaanaaq, which is closer to Canadian Inuktitut. East Greenlandic differs so strongly that mutual intelligibility with the standard is limited.

History and writing

The language arrived in Greenland with the Thule culture around the 13th century. Danish missionaries devised the first orthography; Samuel Kleinschmidt's system of 1851 was replaced by a reformed spelling in 1973 that brought writing closer to pronunciation. Greenlandic maintains high vitality, with nearly all ethnic Greenlanders speaking it, though Danish and English dominate some domains such as higher education.
