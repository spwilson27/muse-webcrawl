Picture hanging

Picture hanging is the practice of suspending framed pictures, mirrors, and other flat artworks on walls for display, using hardware such as hooks, nails, screws, wire, and picture rails.

Methods

A common method stretches picture wire between two D-rings or screw eyes on the back of the frame and hangs the wire over one or two wall hooks. The traditional rule of thumb for choosing wire length leaves the apex of the wire a few inches below the top of the frame. Heavier works require anchors rated for the load and fastened into studs or masonry.

Conventions

Museums and galleries often hang pictures so that the centre of the work is about 145–155 cm (57–61 inches) from the floor, roughly at the eye level of an average viewer. Grouped arrangements ("gallery walls" or salon-style hangs) vary this rule. Picture-rail systems, with mouldings near the ceiling and hanging chains or rods, were standard in nineteenth-century interiors and survive in historic houses.
