Dohyō-iri

The dohyō-iri (土俵入り, "ring-entering ceremony") is a ritual in professional sumo wrestling held before bouts in the top divisions. The wrestlers of the makuuchi and jūryō divisions enter the dohyō (the clay ring) in groups, wearing ornate embroidered aprons called keshō-mawashi, perform synchronized movements, and are introduced to the audience one by one.

The ceremony

Each division's wrestlers enter in two groups, East and West, led into the ring, where they form a circle facing outward, turn to face inward, clap their hands together, raise one hand, and briefly lift their aprons in unison — gestures symbolizing openness and the absence of weapons. The wrestlers' names, home prefectures (or countries), and stables are announced as they step forward.

Yokozuna dohyō-iri

The grand champion yokozuna performs a separate, individual ring-entering ceremony wearing a thick white rope (tsuna) around the waist, accompanied by a sword-bearer (tachimochi) and dew-sweeper (tsuyuharai). Two styles are performed: Unryū, with one arm held to the chest in a defensive posture, and Shiranui, with both arms extended, named after the 10th yokozuna Unryū Kyūkichi and the 11th yokozuna Shiranui Kōemon.
