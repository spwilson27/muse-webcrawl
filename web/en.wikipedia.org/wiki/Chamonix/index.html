Chamonix-Mont-Blanc, usually shortened to Chamonix, is a commune in the Haute-Savoie department in southeastern France, at the foot of Mont Blanc. A world-famous centre for mountaineering and skiing, it hosted the first Winter Olympic Games in 1924.

The valley's glaciers (Mer de Glace, Argentière) and granite spires made Chamonix the birthplace of alpinism: the first ascent of Mont Blanc was made from here in 1786, and its guides' company (1821) is the oldest in the world. The Aiguille du Midi cable car (1955) rises to 3,842 m, and the town is a hub for the Tour du Mont Blanc trek and UTMB trail races — as well as a front line for observing Alpine glacier retreat. Population is roughly 9,000, swollen manyfold by visitors (approximate).
