Chang'e

Chang'e (Chinese: 嫦娥) is the Chinese goddess of the Moon and a central figure of Chinese mythology. She is best known for stealing the elixir of immortality from her husband, the archer Yi (Hou Yi), and fleeing to the Moon, where she lives in the Moon Palace accompanied by the Jade Rabbit, which pounds the elixir of life with mortar and pestle.

The myth

In the best-known version, Hou Yi obtained the elixir after shooting down nine of the ten suns that scorched the earth. Chang'e, for reasons that vary by telling — to prevent a thief from taking it, or out of desire — swallowed the elixir (sometimes two pills, causing her to overflow with lightness) and floated up to the Moon, choosing it as her home because it was nearest the earth. Hou Yi, grieving, displayed her favourite foods on the anniversary of her ascent, a custom associated with the origins of the Mid-Autumn Festival, when Chang'e is worshipped and mooncakes are shared.

Cultural legacy

Chang'e is one of the most beloved figures of Chinese folklore and is celebrated across East and Southeast Asia at the Mid-Autumn Festival. China's lunar exploration programme, which has landed rovers on the Moon, is named the Chang'e programme in her honour; the Chang'e 4 mission's relay satellite was named Queqiao after the magpie bridge of another legend.
