Coal phase-out

Coal phase-out is an environmental policy intended to stop using coal and replace it with safer, cleaner, and renewable energy sources, or to capture its emissions. Its purpose is to mitigate climate change. Coal is the most carbon-intensive fossil fuel; phasing out coal power is considered essential to limiting global warming to 1.5 °C above pre-industrial levels, as targeted by the Paris Agreement.

Progress by country

The United Kingdom, historically the first country to use coal for electricity, closed its last coal-fired power station (Ratcliffe-on-Soar) in September 2024. Canada completed a phase-out of conventional coal power, Ontario having ended coal generation in 2014. Germany has legislated an exit by 2038 at the latest, with 2030 as an aspirational date. Several other European countries have already eliminated coal power or set near-term dates. Coal use nevertheless continues to grow in parts of Asia, with China and India accounting for most new coal capacity.

Mechanisms

Phase-out measures include carbon pricing, emissions standards, retirement schedules for power plants, and support for affected workers and regions under a "just transition". The Powering Past Coal Alliance, launched by Canada and the UK in 2017, coordinates government commitments.
