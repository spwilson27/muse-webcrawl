Giro

Giro may refer to several different things. In banking, a giro is a system of direct transfer of funds between accounts, widely used in Europe through postal giro systems in which payments are pushed from the payer's account to the payee's. The word derives from the Italian for "circulation" or "transfer".

In sport

"The Giro" most commonly refers to the Giro d'Italia, one of cycling's three Grand Tours, a multi-stage bicycle race held annually in Italy since 1909, traditionally in May and June. The race leader wears the pink jersey (maglia rosa).

Other uses

Giro is also the name of a manufacturer of helmets and sports gear, a character name in various works of fiction, and a term in finance for circulating payment orders. Postal giro systems, such as those once operated by national post offices, handled bill payments and transfers for customers without conventional bank accounts.

Banking systems

In a giro system, unlike cheque-based systems, the payer initiates the transfer, and funds move directly between accounts. Such systems were central to retail payments in countries including Germany, the Netherlands, and the United Kingdom, where the National Girobank operated from 1968.
