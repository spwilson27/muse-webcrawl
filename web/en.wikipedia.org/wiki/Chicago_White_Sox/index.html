The Chicago White Sox are an American professional baseball team based in Chicago, Illinois. They compete in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member club of the American League (AL) Central Division. The White Sox are one of two MLB teams based in Chicago, the other being the Chicago Cubs of the National League. The club is owned by Jerry Reinsdorf and plays its home games at Guaranteed Rate Field (known as Rate Field from 2025), located on the city's South Side in the Bridgeport neighborhood.

History

The franchise began as the Sioux City Cornhuskers in the minor Western League in 1894, moved to St. Paul, Minnesota, and then to Chicago in 1900 under owner Charles Comiskey. As the Chicago White Stockings — soon shortened to White Sox — the team was a charter member of the American League when it declared itself a major league in 1901, winning the first AL pennant that year.

The "Hitless Wonders" of 1906 upset the heavily favored Cubs in the only World Series meeting between the two Chicago clubs. The Sox won again in 1917, but the franchise was devastated by the Black Sox Scandal, in which eight players were accused of conspiring to throw the 1919 World Series against the Cincinnati Reds; all eight were banned from baseball for life, and the team did not win another pennant until the "Go-Go Sox" of 1959, who lost the World Series to the Los Angeles Dodgers.

After an 88-year championship drought, the 2005 White Sox, managed by Ozzie Guillén and led by Paul Konerko, Mark Buehrle, and Jermaine Dye, swept the Houston Astros to win the World Series. In 2024, the team set a modern MLB record with 121 losses in a season.

Ballparks

The White Sox played at Comiskey Park from 1910 to 1990, one of the classic steel-and-concrete ballparks of the era, famous for Bill Veeck's innovations including the exploding scoreboard. The current park opened across the street in 1991.

Rivalries

The Sox share the Crosstown Cup rivalry with the Cubs and have longstanding divisional rivalries with the Minnesota Twins, Cleveland Guardians, and Detroit Tigers.
