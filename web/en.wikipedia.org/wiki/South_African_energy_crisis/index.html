South African energy crisis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The South African energy crisis is an ongoing period of widespread national blackouts of electricity supply in South Africa, beginning in late 2007. The state utility Eskom implements rotational power cuts — known locally as load-shedding — when generation capacity fails to meet demand, in stages of increasing severity (Stage 1 removing roughly 1,000 MW up to Stage 8 and beyond).

Causes

The crisis arose from the aging of Eskom's predominantly coal-fired fleet, deferred maintenance, a shortage of generation reserve margin after years in which new capacity was not built, and management failures and corruption during the "state capture" era. The two giant new coal plants, Medupi and Kusile, were delivered years late and far over budget and suffered design defects, while units at older stations broke down at historically high rates. Coal supply problems and, at times, diesel shortages for peaking plants compounded the shortfalls.

Course of the crisis

Rolling blackouts first struck nationally in late 2007 and early 2008, forcing mines and smelters to close temporarily. After a period of relative stability, load-shedding returned in 2014–15, again in 2018–19, and intensified sharply from 2021; 2022 and 2023 set records, with cuts on most days of the year and stages reaching Stage 6, severely affecting businesses, hospitals, water supply, and daily life.

Response

Government responses have included restructuring Eskom into generation, transmission, and distribution businesses; creating a dedicated electricity ministry; licensing private and rooftop solar generation at scale, which expanded rapidly as households and firms installed their own systems; and procuring renewable energy from independent power producers. Generation performance improved in 2024, with extended periods without load-shedding, though officials cautioned that the system remained vulnerable.

Economic impact

Economists estimate the blackouts have cost South Africa several percent of GDP and hundreds of thousands of jobs, and the crisis has been a central political issue, contributing to the ruling party's declining electoral support.
