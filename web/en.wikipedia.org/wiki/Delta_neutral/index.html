Delta neutral

Delta neutral

FieldFinancial mathematics, derivatives trading
Key conceptDelta (Δ) — sensitivity of an option's price to the underlying asset
Used inOptions market making, volatility trading, hedging

In financial mathematics, a portfolio of securities is delta neutral when its value is not affected by small changes in the price of the underlying security. Such a portfolio typically contains options and their corresponding underlying assets, combined so that the positive and negative delta components offset, resulting in the portfolio's value being relatively insensitive to directional movements in the underlying market.

Delta and hedging

Delta measures the rate of change of an option's theoretical value with respect to changes in the underlying asset's price; it ranges from −1 to 0 for puts and 0 to +1 for calls. A trader holding a long call with a delta of +0.5, for example, can delta-hedge by shorting 0.5 units of the underlying per option, producing a position with approximately zero net delta. Because delta changes as the underlying price moves (an effect measured by gamma) and as time passes, a delta neutral portfolio must be rebalanced continuously or at intervals — a practice known as dynamic hedging, which underlies the risk-neutral replication argument of the Black–Scholes model.

Applications

Delta neutral strategies are used by options market makers, who hedge directional exposure in order to profit from spreads and volatility rather than price direction; by volatility traders using straddles and strangles, who seek to isolate exposure to implied volatility (vega); and by convertible-bond and equity arbitrageurs. A portfolio that is delta neutral is not risk-free: it remains exposed to changes in volatility, interest rates, time decay (theta) and large discontinuous price moves.

Related measures

Delta is one of the option "Greeks". Others include gamma (the rate of change of delta), theta (time decay), vega (sensitivity to volatility) and rho (sensitivity to interest rates). Portfolios can be constructed to be neutral with respect to several Greeks simultaneously, though perfect multi-dimensional neutrality is generally unattainable in practice because of discrete rebalancing and transaction costs.
