A krai (Russian: край, kray, literally "edge" or "frontier") is a type of federal subject of the Russian Federation, historically a vast peripheral territory of the Russian Empire and later the Soviet Union administered as a single unit. In the modern federation, krais are legally identical in status to oblasts; the distinction is traditional rather than constitutional.

History

In the Russian Empire, krais were large frontier regions — often newly annexed territories in the Caucasus, Siberia and the Far East — governed provisionally before being subdivided into oblasts and guberniyas. The Soviet Union formalised the krai as a major administrative unit, typically containing subordinate autonomous oblasts and okrugs; the RSFSR had several large krais such as Krasnoyarsk, Khabarovsk and Primorsky.

Modern krais

The Russian Federation currently has nine krais: Altai, Kamchatka, Khabarovsk, Krasnodar, Krasnoyarsk, Perm, Primorsky, Stavropol and Zabaykalsky. Several were formed or enlarged by mergers in the 2000s — Perm Krai (2005), Kamchatka Krai (2007) and Zabaykalsky Krai (2008) absorbed surrounding autonomous okrugs, a process that simplified Russia's federal structure.

The word is also used historically of regions in other Slavic contexts, and cognate administrative terms appear in the history of Ukraine (where krai simply means a land or region) and of the Czech lands (the modern Czech Republic is divided into kraje, regions).
