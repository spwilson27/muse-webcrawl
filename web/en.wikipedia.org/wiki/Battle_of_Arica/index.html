Battle of Arica

The Battle of Arica, also known as the Assault and Capture of El Morro Hill (Asalto y Toma del Morro de Arica), was a battle in the War of the Pacific fought on 7 June 1880 between Chilean forces and the Peruvian garrison defending the port of Arica, in present-day northern Chile.

Background and battle

After victory at Tacna, Chilean forces advanced on Arica, defended by a Peruvian garrison under Colonel Francisco Bolognesi, positioned on the Morro, a steep headland dominating the port, protected by minefields and strongpoints. Offered the chance to surrender, Bolognesi famously replied that he would fight "until the last cartridge has been burned" (hasta quemar el último cartucho). On 7 June, Chilean infantry stormed the Morro in a dawn assault lasting less than an hour, overwhelming the defenders. Bolognesi and many of his officers, including Alfonso Ugarte, were killed.

Aftermath

The fall of Arica gave Chile control of the southern Peruvian coast and is commemorated in Chile as the Día de las Glorias del Ejército, while Bolognesi is honoured in Peru as a national hero. The battle remains one of the emblematic episodes of the War of the Pacific.
