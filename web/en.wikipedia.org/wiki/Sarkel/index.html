Sarkel (Khazar for "white house") was a large limestone-and-brick fortress built by the Khazars on the left bank of the lower Don River in the 830s, at what is now the Rostov Oblast of Russia. It guarded the northwestern frontier of the Khazar Khaganate and the portage between the Don and the Volga.

Construction

The fortress was constructed around 833 with the assistance of engineers sent by the Byzantine emperor Theophilos, led by Petronas Kamateros, in exchange for Khazar support against common enemies. The Byzantine involvement reflects the close alliance between Constantinople and the Khazar court in this period.

History

Sarkel became an important trading station and garrison town. In 965 the Kievan Rus' prince Sviatoslav I conquered the fortress during his campaign that shattered Khazar power; the settlement continued under Rus' rule as Belaya Vezha ("white tower"). It was later abandoned following Cuman attacks in the twelfth century.

Archaeology

The site was excavated in the 1930s and early 1950s under Mikhail Artamonov, shortly before it was submerged by the creation of the Tsimlyansk Reservoir on the Don. The excavations remain a key source for the archaeology of the Khazars.
