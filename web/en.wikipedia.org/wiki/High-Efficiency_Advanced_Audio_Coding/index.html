High-Efficiency Advanced Audio Coding


Spectral band replication in HE-AAC

High-Efficiency Advanced Audio Coding (HE-AAC) is an audio coding format for lossy compression and encoding of digital audio, defined as an MPEG-4 Audio profile in ISO/IEC 14496-3. It is an extension of Low Complexity AAC (AAC-LC) optimized for low-bitrate applications such as streaming audio, satellite radio, and digital radio and television broadcasting.

Technology

HE-AAC version 1 adds spectral band replication (SBR), a technique that reconstructs the high-frequency portion of the signal from a small amount of side information, allowing the core coder to operate on a reduced bandwidth. HE-AAC version 2 further adds parametric stereo (PS), which encodes a stereo image from a mono signal plus parametric cues, enabling very low bitrates (as low as 16–24 kbit/s for stereo) with acceptable quality.

Usage

HE-AAC is used in Digital Radio Mondiale (DRM), DAB+ digital radio, XM Satellite Radio, terrestrial ISDB-Tsb broadcasts in Japan, and as one of the mandatory audio codecs in several mobile and streaming standards. It is also commonly used for internet radio streams and podcasts where bandwidth is constrained. Encoders and decoders are widely implemented; notable encoders include Fraunhofer's FDK AAC library.
