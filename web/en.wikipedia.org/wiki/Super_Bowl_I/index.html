The first AFL–NFL World Championship Game in professional American football, known retroactively as Super Bowl I and referred to in contemporaneous reports as the Super Bowl, was played on January 15, 1967, at the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum. The National Football League (NFL) champion Green Bay Packers defeated the American Football League (AFL) champion Kansas City Chiefs by a score of 35–10.

The game grew out of the merger agreement between the NFL and AFL announced in June 1966, which provided for a championship game between the two leagues beginning with the 1966 season and a full merger in 1970. At the time, many sportswriters and fans believed the AFL's best teams were inferior to the NFL's, and the Packers' comfortable win reinforced that view.

Game summary

Green Bay opened the scoring with a 37-yard touchdown pass from Bart Starr to Max McGee, who had spent much of the previous night out in Los Angeles and was playing because starter Boyd Dowler was injured early in the game. Kansas City answered with a touchdown pass from Len Dawson to Curtis McClinton, and trailed only 14–10 at halftime. In the second half the Packers dominated: Elijah Pitts and Jim Taylor ran for touchdowns, and McGee caught a second scoring pass to finish with seven receptions for 138 yards and two touchdowns. Starr was named the game's Most Valuable Player.

Broadcasting

Uniquely, the game was broadcast by both CBS (which held NFL rights) and NBC (which held AFL rights), each with its own announcers. No complete videotape of either broadcast was long thought to survive; a nearly complete recording of the CBS telecast was discovered in 2010 and later restored.

The halftime show featured trumpeter Al Hirt, marching bands from the University of Arizona and Grambling State University, and a demonstration of rocket belts. Tickets were priced at a maximum of $12, and the Coliseum was not sold out — the only Super Bowl that has failed to sell out.
