Chogyal (Tibetan: ཆོས་རྒྱལ, "Dharma King") was the title of the hereditary monarchs of the Kingdom of Sikkim in the eastern Himalayas, who ruled from 1642 until 1975. The Chogyals belonged to the Namgyal dynasty and combined spiritual and temporal authority in the manner of a Buddhist priest-king. The title derives from the Sanskrit dharmaraja and was also used by the kings of Bhutan in earlier centuries.

History

The first Chogyal, Phuntsog Namgyal, was consecrated in 1642 at Yuksom by three Tibetan lamas who had travelled to Sikkim. According to tradition, he was a descendant of Guru Tashi, a prince of the Mi-nyak house of eastern Tibet. His coronation established a theocratic monarchy in which the ruler was regarded as an incarnation of divine kingship and patron of Tibetan Buddhism.

Over the following centuries, Sikkim's territory shrank under pressure from Nepal, Bhutan, and British India. By treaties of the 19th century Sikkim became a British protectorate, and the Chogyal ruled under British, and later Indian, suzerainty. The palace was at Gangtok, the capital.

The last Chogyal

Palden Thondup Namgyal succeeded his father Tashi Namgyal in 1963. His marriage to the American Hope Cooke drew international attention. During the 1970s, democratic agitation among Sikkim's Nepali-speaking majority and friction with India culminated in a referendum in 1975, after which Sikkim became the 22nd state of India and the monarchy was abolished. Palden Thondup Namgyal died in 1982. His second son, Wangchuk Namgyal, is regarded by supporters as the titular thirteenth Chogyal, though the title has no official standing.
