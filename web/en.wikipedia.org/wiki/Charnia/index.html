Charnia

Fossil rangeEdiacaran, ~570–550 million years ago
Type localityCharnwood Forest, Leicestershire, England
GroupRangeomorpha

Charnia is a genus of frond-like Ediacaran organism with segmented, leaf-like ridges branching from a central stalk. It was among the first Precambrian macrofossils to be recognized as the remains of a complex organism, challenging the belief that such life began only in the Cambrian.

Discovery

The fossil was found in Charnwood Forest, Leicestershire, by schoolboy Roger Mason in 1957 and described by Trevor Ford, who named it after the forest. A related form, Charnia masoni, honours its discoverer.

Interpretation

Charnia grew attached to the deep seafloor by a holdfast and probably fed by absorbing nutrients from the water. It is classified among the rangeomorphs, a group of fractal-like Ediacaran organisms whose relationship to modern animals remains debated.
