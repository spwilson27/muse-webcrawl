Automotive industry in South Korea

The automotive industry in South Korea is the fifth largest in the world, measured by automobile unit production and also by automobile export volumes. In the 1980s the industry grew from assembling imported kits to a major export sector, driven by the Hyundai Motor Group and supported by government industrial policy. South Korean manufacturers today produce around 4 million vehicles a year, with roughly two-thirds exported, and the country is a leader in electric vehicles and battery production.

History

Beginnings

South Korea's motor industry began in the 1950s and 1960s with the assembly of imported complete knock-down kits. The government's Automobile Industry Promotion Policy of 1962 and subsequent plans pushed for local content and eventually indigenous models. Hyundai Motor Company, founded in 1967, first assembled Ford Cortinas under licence before launching its own Pony in 1975, designed with help from Mitsubishi and ItalDesign.

Export expansion

The Hyundai Excel's success in the United States from 1986 marked Korea's arrival as a volume exporter. Kia, Daewoo Motors and SsangYong also expanded. The 1997 Asian financial crisis forced restructuring: Daewoo collapsed and its car business was bought by General Motors (becoming GM Korea), Kia was absorbed by Hyundai, and Samsung Motors passed to Renault (later Renault Korea).

Recent developments

Hyundai and Kia have moved upmarket with the Genesis luxury brand and become major producers of electric vehicles such as the Hyundai Ioniq 5 and Kia EV6, supported by Korean battery makers LG Energy Solution, SK On and Samsung SDI. The industry faces competition from China and the costs of the transition to electrification.
