Hälsingland

Hälsingland is a historical province (landskap) in Norrland, central Sweden. It borders Gästrikland, Dalarna, Härjedalen, Medelpad and the Gulf of Bothnia, and largely coincides with Gävleborg County together with parts of neighbouring counties. The province is known for its large, richly decorated farmhouses and for the Ljusnan river valley.

Geography

Much of Hälsingland is forested and hilly, rising toward the northwest, with agriculture concentrated in the river valleys and along the coast. The Ljusnan, the province's longest river, flows southeast to the Bothnian coast at Ljusne.

Culture

Seven of the province's decorated farmhouses, the Hälsingegårdar, were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2012 as the "Decorated Farmhouses of Hälsingland", recognized for their large, ornately painted interiors built by prosperous independent farmers in the 18th and 19th centuries, largely on wealth from flax and timber.
