Holmenkollbakken

Holmenkollbakken, the ski jumping hill in Holmenkollen, Oslo

LocationHolmenkollen, Oslo, Norway
Opened1892 (current structure 2010)
SizeK-point 120 m; hill size 134 m
EventsFIS Ski Jumping World Cup; World Championships; 1952 Winter Olympics

Holmenkollbakken is a large ski jumping hill located at Holmenkollen in Oslo, Norway. It has a hill size of HS134, a construction point of K-120, and spectator capacity of 70,000. Holmenkollen has hosted the Holmenkollen Ski Festival since 1892, and ski jumping has taken place on the hill since that year. The venue has been the site of the 1952 Winter Olympics and several FIS Nordic World Ski Championships, most recently in 2011.

History

The first ski jumping competition at Holmenkollen was held on 31 January 1892, when the hill record was 21.5 metres. The hill was rebuilt and improved many times during the 20th century; a permanent concrete structure with a steel superstructure was raised for the 1952 Winter Olympics. The jump was repeatedly enlarged to allow longer flights as the sport developed.

The 2010 rebuild

The old hill was demolished in 2008 and replaced with an entirely new structure designed by the Danish architectural firm JDS Architects, which won an international competition. The new Holmenkollbakken, opened in March 2010, features a characteristic cantilevered steel design with a wind screen and a wrap of mesh around the in-run tower. The first official jump on the new hill was made by Anette Sagen. The hill is floodlit, and the top of the jump tower affords panoramic views over Oslo and the Oslofjord.

The area also includes the Holmenkollen Ski Museum, founded in 1923 and the world's oldest museum dedicated to skiing, located beneath the jump.
