A.L.A. Schechter Poultry Corp. v. United States

A.L.A. Schechter Poultry Corp. v. United States, 295 U.S. 495 (1935), nicknamed the 'Sick Chicken Case', was a unanimous US Supreme Court decision invalidating the National Industrial Recovery Act (NIRA), a centrepiece of the early New Deal.


Decided: 27 May 1935
Citation: 295 U.S. 495
Holding: NIRA codes unconstitutional

Facts and holding

The Schechter brothers' Brooklyn poultry business was convicted under the Live Poultry Code, which regulated wages, hours and trade practices. Chief Justice Charles Evans Hughes held that Congress had unconstitutionally delegated legislative power to the President and industry groups (the nondelegation doctrine) and that the defendants' intrastate activity affected interstate commerce too indirectly to regulate under the Commerce Clause.

Impact

Decided on 'Black Monday', the ruling crippled the National Recovery Administration and marked the high point of the Court's resistance to the New Deal before the 1937 'switch in time'.
