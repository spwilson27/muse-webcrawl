Australian Public Service

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Australian Public Service (APS) is the federal civil service of the Commonwealth of Australia responsible for the public administration, public policy, and public services of the departments and executive and statutory agencies of the Government of Australia. The APS employs around 170,000 people, concentrated in Canberra but with staff across the country and overseas.

History and structure

The service dates from Federation in 1901, when seven departments were established under the first Commonwealth government. Its modern legal basis is the Public Service Act 1999, which sets out APS Values and a Code of Conduct, establishes the role of the Australian Public Service Commissioner, and gives agency heads employer powers. The Secretary of the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet is the senior official and head of the service.

Employment

APS staff are classified from APS Level 1 to the Executive Levels and the Senior Executive Service (SES). Recruitment is merit-based. The service includes departments of state such as Treasury, Finance, Foreign Affairs and Trade, Defence and Home Affairs, as well as large delivery agencies such as Services Australia and the Australian Taxation Office.

Reform

The APS has been repeatedly reformed, notably following the Coombs Royal Commission of 1976, the devolution and managerialism of the 1980s–90s, and the 2019 Thodey review, which recommended a more unified, citizen-centred and digitally capable service. Periodic debates concern politicisation, capability loss through outsourcing, and the use of consultants.
