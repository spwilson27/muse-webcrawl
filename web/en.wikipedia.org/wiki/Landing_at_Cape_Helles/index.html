The landing at Cape Helles was the main Allied amphibious assault of the Gallipoli campaign, carried out on 25 April 1915 at the southwestern tip of the Gallipoli Peninsula in the Ottoman Empire. British and French forces of the Mediterranean Expeditionary Force under General Sir Ian Hamilton landed on five beaches around Cape Helles — designated S, V, W, X and Y from east to west — while the ANZAC corps landed separately further north at Anzac Cove and a French division made a diversionary landing at Kumkale on the Asiatic shore.

The landings

The principal assaults were at V Beach, before the fort and village of Sedd el Bahr, and W Beach ("Lancashire Landing"). At V Beach, troops disembarked from the converted collier SS River Clyde, which was run aground so men could pour from sally ports in her sides across lighters to the shore; Ottoman machine-gun and rifle fire from the castle and trenches above cut down the first waves, and the survivors were pinned until nightfall — five Victoria Crosses were awarded to men of the covering force and the ship's crew. At W Beach the Lancashire Fusiliers stormed through wire and fire in an action that won "six VCs before breakfast". X and Y beaches were taken almost unopposed, but the failures to exploit from Y Beach and to link up on the first day let the Ottoman defenders, under the overall direction of Liman von Sanders, concentrate reserves and contain the bridgehead.

Aftermath

By nightfall on 25 April the Allies held a shallow lodgement about half a mile deep. The objective, the hill of Achi Baba dominating the cape, was never taken: three battles of Krithia (April–June 1915) and months of trench warfare followed at heavy cost, and the Helles front was finally evacuated on the night of 8–9 January 1916, the last act of the Gallipoli campaign. Six Victoria Crosses in total were awarded for V Beach, and the Lancashire Fusiliers' honours at W Beach remain regimental legend.
