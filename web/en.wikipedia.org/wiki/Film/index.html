Film

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A classic film set: a large studio camera on a crane, klieg lights and a director's chair before a painted backdrop]

Film is a visual art form that uses moving images to tell stories, communicate ideas and create experiences. It emerged in the 1890s from experiments with sequential photography and projection — the Lumière brothers' first public screening took place in Paris in 1895 — and rapidly became the dominant popular entertainment medium of the 20th century.

The silent era developed film grammar through directors such as Georges Méliès, D. W. Griffith and Sergei Eisenstein; synchronised sound arrived commercially with The Jazz Singer (1927); colour (Technicolor), widescreen, and later computer-generated imagery and digital production successively transformed the medium.

Industry and art

Film is both a mass industry — centred on Hollywood, India's Bollywood and other national cinemas — and an art form explored by auteurs such as Alfred Hitchcock, Akira Kurosawa, Stanley Kubrick and Hayao Miyazaki. Major festivals include Cannes, Venice and Berlin; the Academy Awards (Oscars) honour American and international film annually.
