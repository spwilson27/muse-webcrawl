Northwest Airlines Flight 253 was an international passenger flight from Amsterdam to Detroit that was the target of a failed al-Qaeda bombing attempt on Christmas Day, 25 December 2009. Nigerian passenger Umar Farouk Abdulmutallab, later dubbed the "Underwear Bomber", attempted to detonate plastic explosive (PETN) sewn into his underwear as the Airbus A330 descended toward Detroit; the device caught fire rather than exploding, and he was subdued by passengers and crew.

The attack attempt

Abdulmutallab, 23, had boarded in Amsterdam after travelling from Lagos via Accra, paying cash for a ticket and checking no bags — facts that did not prevent boarding. About 20 minutes before landing he spent a long time in the lavatory, then returned to his seat (19A) and pulled a blanket over himself. Passengers heard popping sounds like firecrackers and smelled burning; flames and smoke came from his lap. Dutch passenger Jasper Schuringa climbed over seats and subdued him while flight attendants used fire extinguishers. The aircraft landed safely at Detroit Metropolitan Wayne County Airport, where Abdulmutallab, who suffered burns, was arrested.

Investigation and intelligence failures

Abdulmutallab's father, a prominent Nigerian banker, had warned the U.S. Embassy in Abuja weeks earlier that his son had become radicalised in Yemen; the report reached Washington, and his name was placed in the general TIDE database but not on the No Fly List or the Selectee list, and his U.S. visa was not revoked. President Barack Obama called the affair a "systemic failure" of intelligence sharing. Al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula (AQAP) claimed responsibility, saying the attack retaliated for U.S. support of Yemeni operations against the group; the bomb was attributed to AQAP bomb-maker Ibrahim al-Asiri.

Legal outcome

Abdulmutallab pleaded guilty in October 2011 to eight counts, including attempted use of a weapon of mass destruction and attempted murder. On 16 February 2012 he was sentenced to life imprisonment without parole (four concurrent life sentences plus additional terms) and is held at the ADX Florence supermax prison in Colorado.

Aftermath

The attempt triggered immediate global tightening of aviation security: extra pat-downs and carry-on restrictions on U.S.-bound flights, accelerated deployment of full-body (millimetre-wave and backscatter) scanners at U.S. airports, and reforms to watchlisting guidance so that more names from TIDE flow to the No Fly and Selectee lists.
