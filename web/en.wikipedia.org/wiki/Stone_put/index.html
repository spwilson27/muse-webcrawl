Stone put

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The stone put (Scottish Gaelic: clach air a chur) is a traditional Scottish heavy event at Highland games in which competitors throw a large stone as far as possible. It is one of the nine heavy events and is the historical ancestor of the modern Olympic shot put.

Styles

Two styles are contested. In the open stone, a stone of about 7.3–11.8 kg (16–26 lb) is thrown with one hand, and the thrower may use any reasonable approach, including a spin or glide similar to modern shot put technique. In the Braemar stone, a heavier stone of about 9–13 kg (20–28 lb) is put from a standing position with no run-up or movement of the feet, in the manner traditionally used at the Braemar Gathering. Women's stones are lighter in both events.

History

Throwing heavy stones for distance is an ancient test of strength in Scotland, mentioned in early accounts of Highland gatherings. The event was standardized in the 19th century as Highland games were formalized, and the shot put of modern athletics developed directly from it, replacing the irregular stone with a turned metal shot of fixed weight.
