The Battle of Kirkuk of October 2017 was a brief military operation in which Iraqi federal forces retook the disputed, oil-rich city and governorate of Kirkuk from the Peshmerga of the Kurdistan Regional Government. The operation followed the Kurdish independence referendum of 25 September 2017, which Baghdad had declared unconstitutional.

Background

Kurdish forces had controlled Kirkuk since June 2014, when the Iraqi army collapsed before the advance of the Islamic State and the Peshmerga moved in to secure the city and its oilfields. The September 2017 referendum, held in the Kurdistan Region and the disputed territories including Kirkuk, voted overwhelmingly for independence, provoking a crisis with the federal government of Prime Minister Haider al-Abadi, which demanded the annulment of the vote and the return of disputed areas to federal control.

The operation

On the night of 15–16 October 2017, Iraqi Counter Terrorism Service units, federal police, and Popular Mobilization Forces advanced on Kirkuk. After limited clashes, most Peshmerga units — particularly those of the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan, whose leaders were accused by their Kurdistan Democratic Party rivals of having reached an understanding with Baghdad — withdrew, and federal forces occupied the city, its airport, military bases, and the surrounding oilfields within about a day. Tens of thousands of Kurdish residents temporarily fled the city, though many returned in the following days and weeks.

Aftermath

Within days Iraqi forces also retook most of the other disputed territories, reducing the Kurdistan Region to its pre-2014 boundaries and stripping it of roughly half the oil production it had controlled. The referendum's fallout led Masoud Barzani to step down as president of the Kurdistan Region on 1 November 2017, and the independence bid was formally shelved.
