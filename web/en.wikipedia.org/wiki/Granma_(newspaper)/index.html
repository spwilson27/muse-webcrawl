Granma (newspaper)

Granma is the official newspaper of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of Cuba. It was founded on 3 October 1965, formed from the merger of two earlier papers, Hoy and Revolución. The newspaper is named after the yacht Granma, which carried Fidel Castro and 81 other rebels to Cuba in November 1956 to launch the Cuban Revolution.

Publication

The paper is published six days a week in Spanish, and weekly international editions are produced in English, French, Portuguese, Italian, and German. Granma Internacional, the international edition, was founded in 1966. The newspaper's website was launched in 1996, making it one of the first Cuban news organisations online.

As the official organ of the ruling party, Granma publishes government announcements, speeches by party leaders, and reports reflecting the party's positions. It also publishes the famous "Reflexiones del compañero Fidel" ("Reflections by comrade Fidel"), a column written by Fidel Castro after his retirement from the presidency.

Role and readership

With a circulation of several hundred thousand copies, Granma is the most widely read newspaper in Cuba. Its content is frequently cited by foreign media as an authoritative indication of Cuban government policy. See also Communist Party of Cuba and Granma (yacht).
