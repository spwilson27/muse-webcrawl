Orders, decorations, and medals of India

Indian honours system

Country: Republic of India

Highest civilian award: Bharat Ratna

Highest military award: Param Vir Chakra

The Indian honours system comprises the orders, decorations and medals of India, awarded by the Government of India to recognise individual and collective achievements in civilian life and military service. The system was established after independence and the adoption of the Constitution in 1950, replacing the British Indian orders of chivalry such as the Order of the Star of India and the Order of the Indian Empire.

Civilian awards

The highest civilian honour is the Bharat Ratna ("Jewel of India"), instituted in 1954 and awarded for exceptional service of the highest order in any field of human endeavour; recipients have included scientists, statesmen, artists and sportspeople. Below it stand the three Padma awards — Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan and Padma Shri — announced each year on the eve of Republic Day for distinguished service in fields such as art, education, industry, literature, science, social service, medicine, sports and public affairs. The awards are presented by the President of India at Rashtrapati Bhavan.

Military awards

The highest military decoration is the Param Vir Chakra, instituted in 1950 with retrospective effect to 1947, awarded for the most conspicuous bravery in the presence of the enemy; it has been awarded only 21 times. It is followed in wartime by the Maha Vir Chakra and Vir Chakra. Peacetime gallantry awards are headed by the Ashoka Chakra, Kirti Chakra and Shaurya Chakra. The system also includes distinguished-service, campaign, and long-service medals.

Order of wear and features

India maintains an official order of wearing for its decorations, in which the Bharat Ratna and Param Vir Chakra head the list. A distinctive constitutional feature shapes the system: Article 18 of the Indian Constitution abolishes titles, so although the awards may be held and displayed, recipients may not use them as prefixes or suffixes to their names — a provision upheld by the Supreme Court in the 1990s against challenges to the awards themselves.
