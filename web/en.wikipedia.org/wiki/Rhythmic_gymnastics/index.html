Rhythmic gymnastics is a sport in which gymnasts perform on a floor with an apparatus: hoop, ball, clubs, ribbon and rope. The sport combines elements of gymnastics, dance and calisthenics; gymnasts must be strong, flexible, agile, dexterous and coordinated. Rhythmic gymnastics is governed by World Gymnastics (formerly the Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique, FIG), which first recognized it as a sport in 1963.

History

Rhythmic gymnastics grew out of ideas and exercises developed in the 19th and early 20th centuries by figures such as François Delsarte, whose system of expression gymnastics influenced later movement systems, and by the schools of dance and physical education in Sweden, Denmark and Germany. The sport in its competitive form was developed in the Soviet Union in the 1940s, where "artistic gymnastics" combined ballet, apparatus work and expressive movement. The first Soviet championships were held in 1949, and the FIG recognized the discipline internationally in 1963. The first World Championships took place in Budapest that same year, won by Ludmila Savinkova of the Soviet Union.

Rhythmic gymnastics became an Olympic sport at the 1984 Los Angeles Games, where Canada's Lori Fung won the first individual all-around gold medal. Group competition was added to the Olympic programme at Atlanta 1996.

Apparatus

Senior gymnasts compete with four of the five apparatus in a given cycle; the rope was removed from the senior programme after 2010 but remains in junior competition. Each apparatus has prescribed technical requirements:

  • Rope — made of hemp or synthetic material, proportional to the gymnast's height; used for leaps, jumps, swings and throws.
  • Hoop — plastic or wood, 80–90 cm in diameter; rolls, rotations and throws.
  • Ball — rubber or synthetic, 18–20 cm in diameter; balanced and rolled over the body, bounced and thrown.
  • Clubs — two bottle-shaped implements, 40–50 cm long; mills, circles, throws and rhythmic tapping.
  • Ribbon — a satin ribbon 5–6 m long attached to a stick; spirals, snakes and figures of eight traced in the air.

Competition format

Competitions include an individual all-around, in which each gymnast performs one routine with each apparatus, apparatus finals, and a group competition in which five gymnasts perform two routines together — one with a single type of apparatus and one with a mixture. Routines last 75–90 seconds for individuals and 135–150 seconds for groups, performed to music on a 13 × 13 m carpet.

Scoring

Under the FIG Code of Points, scores combine a Difficulty (D) score, rewarding body difficulties (jumps and leaps, balances, rotations), apparatus handling and dynamic elements with rotation; an Artistry (A) score; and an Execution (E) score, from which faults in technique and apparatus loss are deducted. The Code is revised after each Olympic cycle.

Dominant nations

The sport has historically been dominated by Eastern European nations, particularly Russia, Ukraine, Belarus and Bulgaria, with strong programmes also in Italy, Spain, Israel and Azerbaijan. Notable gymnasts include Alina Kabaeva, Yevgeniya Kanayeva — the only rhythmic gymnast to win two Olympic all-around titles — Dina Averina and Linoy Ashram, the 2020 Olympic champion.
