Ofsted is the Office for Standards in Education, Children's Services and Skills, a non-ministerial department of the UK government responsible for inspecting and regulating services that care for children and young people, and services providing education and skills for learners of all ages in England. It reports directly to Parliament and is headed by His Majesty's Chief Inspector.

History

Ofsted was created in 1992 by the Education (Schools) Act under Prime Minister John Major, replacing the older system of Her Majesty's Inspectors (HMI), who had inspected schools since the nineteenth century. Its remit expanded in 2007 to include children's services and social care, nurseries and childminders, further education colleges, teacher training and some independent schools. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own inspectorates.

Inspections and judgements

Schools are inspected under the Education Inspection Framework (2019), which emphasises the quality of education ("intent, implementation and impact") alongside behaviour, personal development and leadership. Until reforms announced in 2024–2025, inspections concluded with a single overall grade — Outstanding, Good, Requires Improvement or Inadequate — a system criticised for its high-stakes consequences; following the death of headteacher Ruth Perry in 2023, which a coroner linked to an Ofsted inspection, the government moved to replace single-word judgements with report-card style assessments.

Criticism

Teaching unions and researchers have criticised Ofsted for workload, stress and inconsistency, while defenders credit it with raising standards and identifying failing schools. Debate about its methods and its effect on the profession is a recurring feature of English education politics.
