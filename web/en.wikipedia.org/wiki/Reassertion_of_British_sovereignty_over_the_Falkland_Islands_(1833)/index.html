Reassertion of British sovereignty over the Falkland Islands (1833)

The reassertion of British sovereignty over the Falkland Islands took place in January 1833, when British naval forces arrived at the islands and required the garrison of the United Provinces of the River Plate (part of present-day Argentina) to withdraw. The event is central to the long-standing sovereignty dispute over the Falkland Islands (Spanish: Islas Malvinas).

Background

Britain had established a settlement at Port Egmont in 1765 and, although it withdrew in 1774, never formally renounced its claim. Spain administered the islands from Buenos Aires until 1811. In 1820 the United Provinces formally took possession, and in 1829 appointed Louis Vernet as military and civil commander at Puerto Luis. After Vernet seized American sealing ships in 1831, the USS Lexington raided the settlement, and Buenos Aires sent a garrison under José María Pinedo in 1832.

Events of January 1833

On 2 January 1833 the British sloop HMS Clio, under Captain John James Onslow, arrived at Puerto Luis. Onslow informed Pinedo that he intended to reassert British sovereignty and required the Argentine flag to be lowered and the garrison to depart. Outmatched, Pinedo protested but complied, leaving on 5 January. Vernet's civilian settlers were encouraged to remain, and most stayed. Britain then administered the islands continuously thereafter, appointing a lieutenant-governor in 1842.

Legacy

Argentina maintains that the action was an illegal expulsion of its authorities and population and continues to claim the islands; the United Kingdom maintains its claim rested on earlier title and has pointed to islanders' self-determination. The dispute led to the Falklands War of 1982 and remains unresolved.
