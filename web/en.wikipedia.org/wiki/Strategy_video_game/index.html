Strategy video game

A strategy video game is a video game genre that emphasizes skillful thinking and planning to achieve victory. Players typically command armies, nations, or organizations from a top-down or god-like perspective, managing resources, economies, and units rather than directly controlling a single character's reflexes. The genre descends from tabletop wargames and strategy board games such as chess.

Subgenres

Real-time strategy (RTS)

In real-time strategy games, all players act simultaneously. Players gather resources, build bases, and command units in continuous time. Dune II (1992) established the genre template of harvesting, building, and attacking; Warcraft: Orcs & Humans (1994), Command & Conquer (1995), StarCraft (1998), and Age of Empires (1997) popularized it. StarCraft became a major esport, particularly in South Korea.

Turn-based strategy (TBS)

Turn-based strategy games divide play into discrete turns, allowing deliberate planning. Prominent examples include the Civilization series (1991–), Heroes of Might and Magic, and modern XCOM titles. The term 4X (explore, expand, exploit, exterminate) describes empire-building games such as Master of Orion and Civilization.

Other forms

Tower defense games task players with placing defensive structures against waves of enemies. Multiplayer online battle arenas (MOBAs) such as League of Legends and Dota 2 evolved from RTS modifications. Grand strategy games, notably Paradox Interactive's Europa Universalis and Crusader Kings, simulate centuries of statecraft on detailed maps. Auto battlers and real-time tactics games (which omit base-building and economics) are further offshoots.

Gameplay elements

Common mechanics include resource gathering, technology trees, unit production, fog of war, and counter systems in which unit types have rock-paper-scissors relationships. Strategy games reward long-term planning, economic efficiency, scouting, and adaptation to opponents' choices.

History

Early computer strategy games appeared on mainframes and home computers in the 1970s–80s, including Eastern Front (1941) and M.U.L.E. (1983). The 1990s brought the RTS boom; in the 2000s the genre's mainstream popularity declined relative to shooters and MOBAs, while turn-based and grand strategy found durable niches and mobile strategy games reached large audiences.
