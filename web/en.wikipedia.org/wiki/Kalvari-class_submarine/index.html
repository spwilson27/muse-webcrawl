Kalvari-class submarine

The Kalvari class is a class of diesel-electric attack submarines built for the Indian Navy under Project 75. Based on the French-designed Scorpène class, the six boats were constructed by Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders in Mumbai with technology transfer from the Naval Group (formerly DCNS), and are named after decommissioned Indian Foxtrot-class submarines of the 1960s.

Boats in class

  • INS Kalvari (S21) — commissioned 14 December 2017
  • INS Khanderi (S22) — commissioned 28 September 2019
  • INS Karanj (S23) — commissioned 10 March 2021
  • INS Vela (S24) — commissioned 25 November 2021
  • INS Vagir (S25) — commissioned 23 January 2023
  • INS Vagsheer (S26) — launched 2022; delivered in the mid-2020s

Design

The boats displace about 1,600–1,775 tonnes surfaced, measure roughly 67 m in length, and are powered by diesel-electric propulsion with a crew of about 35–43. They are armed with six 533 mm torpedo tubes capable of launching heavyweight torpedoes and Exocet SM39 anti-ship missiles, and can lay mines. The design's modular construction permits air-independent propulsion (AIP) to be added; India's Defence Research and Development Organisation developed a phosphoric-acid fuel-cell AIP module for retrofit into the class.

Programme history

Project 75 was approved in 2005 with a contract worth about ₹18,798 crore (roughly US$3.75 billion at the time). Construction suffered delays and cost overruns, but the programme gave India renewed experience in indigenous submarine construction after a long gap following the Shishumar (Type 209) boats. The Kalvari class forms the core of India's conventional submarine fleet alongside Sindhughosh (Kilo) and Shishumar-class boats, pending the follow-on Project 75I.
