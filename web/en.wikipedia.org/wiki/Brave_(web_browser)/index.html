Brave is a free and open-source web browser developed by Brave Software, Inc. It is based on the Chromium web browser. Brave is a privacy-focused browser: it blocks online advertisements and trackers by default through its "Shields" feature, and it integrates features such as built-in Tor private windows, an IPFS client, a cryptocurrency wallet, and its own search engine, Brave Search.

History

Brave Software was co-founded by Brendan Eich, creator of JavaScript and co-founder of Mozilla, and Brian Bondy. The browser was announced in January 2016 and version 1.0 was released in November 2019. The company also pioneered an attention-based advertising model: users who opt in to Brave Ads receive privacy-respecting advertisements and earn a share of revenue in the Basic Attention Token (BAT), a cryptocurrency, which they can use to tip or auto-contribute to websites and creators. Brave grew to tens of millions of monthly active users in the early 2020s.

Features

  • Brave Shields — default blocking of advertisements, cross-site trackers, fingerprinting, and third-party cookies, with automatic HTTPS upgrades.
  • Brave Search — an independent search engine, launched in 2021, which became the browser's default in major markets.
  • Private windows with Tor — optional routing through the Tor network.
  • Brave Rewards and BAT — opt-in advertising that pays users in Basic Attention Tokens.
  • Leo — an integrated AI assistant added in 2023.

Criticism

Brave has attracted controversy on occasion, including a 2020 incident in which affiliate referral codes were appended to some cryptocurrency exchange URLs typed into the address bar (promptly removed and apologised for), and debates about its advertising model and cryptocurrency components. The browser is nevertheless widely recommended by privacy advocates for its strong default settings.
