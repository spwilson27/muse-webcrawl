Cyclopentane

Cyclopentane (also called C pentane) is a flammable alicyclic hydrocarbon with the chemical formula C5H10 and CAS number 287-92-3, consisting of a ring of five carbon atoms each bonded with two hydrogen atoms above and below the plane. It occurs as a colorless liquid with a petrol-like odor. Its freezing point is −94 °C and its boiling point is 49 °C. Cyclopentane is in the class of cycloalkanes, being alkanes that have one or more carbon rings. It is formed by cracking cyclohexane in the presence of alumina at a high temperature and pressure.

Production

It was first prepared in 1893 by the German chemist Johannes Wislicenus and William Hentschel by reacting calcium adipate with dry distillation. Industrially, cyclopentane is produced from the C5 fraction of naphtha cracking and is purified by fractional distillation.

Uses

Cyclopentane is used in the manufacture of synthetic resins and rubber adhesives and also as a blowing agent in the manufacture of polyurethane insulating foam, as found in many domestic appliances such as refrigerators and freezers, replacing environmentally damaging alternatives such as the CFC-11 and HCFC-141b refrigerants. More advanced technologies, such as computer hard drives and outerspace equipment, employ multiply alkylated cyclopentane (MAC) lubricants because of their extremely low volatility.

Conformations

Cyclopentane is not planar; it adopts puckered "envelope" and "half-chair" conformations that reduce torsional strain, and these conformers interconvert rapidly (pseudorotation).

Safety

Cyclopentane is highly flammable and forms explosive mixtures with air. It is an asphyxiant and a narcotic at high concentrations, but is considered of relatively low toxicity.
