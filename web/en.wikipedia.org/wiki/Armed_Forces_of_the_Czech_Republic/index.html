The Armed Forces of the Czech Republic (Czech: Ozbrojené síly České republiky) are the military forces of the Czech Republic. They consist principally of the Czech Land Forces and the Czech Air Force, supported by joint and special components. The President of the Republic is commander-in-chief, and civilian control is exercised through the Ministry of Defence in Prague.

History

The modern armed forces were created on 1 January 1993, when the Czechoslovak People's Army was divided between the Czech Republic and Slovakia following the dissolution of Czechoslovakia. The Czech Republic joined NATO in March 1999 alongside Poland and Hungary, the alliance's first post–Cold War enlargement. Compulsory military service was abolished at the end of 2004, making the force fully professional.

Role and deployments

The armed forces defend the country's sovereignty and fulfil alliance commitments. Czech troops have served in the Balkans, Iraq, Afghanistan, Mali and the Baltic states, including chemical defence units that were in high demand after 2001 and a field hospital deployed to Kabul. The air force flies Saab JAS 39 Gripen fighters leased from Sweden and has ordered F-35 Lightning II aircraft; the land forces operate T-72M4CZ tanks and have acquired Leopard 2 tanks from Germany. Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the Czech Republic has substantially increased defence spending toward NATO targets and supplied heavy equipment to Ukraine.
