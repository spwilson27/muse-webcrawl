Paleoseismology

Paleoseismology is the study of ancient earthquakes through the geologic record. By identifying and dating evidence of prehistoric ground rupture, liquefaction and tsunami deposits, paleoseismologists extend the earthquake catalogue centuries to millennia beyond the written and instrumental records, which is essential for seismic hazard assessment on faults with long recurrence intervals.

Methods

The most common technique is trenching across an active fault: in the walls of the excavation, geologists map displaced and folded sediment layers, buried soils, fissure fills and colluvial wedges that record individual rupture events. Event ages are constrained by radiocarbon dating of organic material, optically stimulated luminescence dating of sediments, and stratigraphic correlation. Complementary evidence comes from offset landforms, uplifted marine terraces, submerged forests, turbidites and tsunami sand sheets.

Applications

Paleoseismic studies established, for example, that the Cascadia subduction zone produces great earthquakes — the most recent in 1700, identified from drowned forests and Japanese tsunami records — and have been used to estimate recurrence intervals and slip rates on faults such as the San Andreas and the Alpine Fault of New Zealand. Results feed directly into probabilistic seismic hazard models used in building codes and insurance.
