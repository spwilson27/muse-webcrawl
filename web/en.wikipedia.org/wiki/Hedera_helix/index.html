Hedera helix

Kingdom: Plantae

Hedera helix, the common ivy, English ivy, European ivy, or just ivy, is a species of flowering plant in the family Araliaceae, native to most of Europe and western Asia. A rampant, clinging evergreen vine, it is a familiar sight in gardens, waste spaces, and on the walls of buildings and tree trunks, where it climbs up to 20–30 m (66–98 ft) high. It is widely cultivated as an ornamental plant and has become naturalised in many parts of the world outside its native range, where it is often considered an invasive species.

Description

On suitable surfaces such as trees, rock faces and brickwork, Hedera helix climbs by means of aerial rootlets with matted pads that cling strongly to the substrate. The leaves are alternate, 50–100 mm long, with a 15–20 mm petiole. They are of two kinds: palmately five-lobed juvenile leaves on creeping and climbing stems, and unlobed cordate adult leaves on fertile flowering stems exposed to full sun. The flowers, produced from late summer until late autumn, are small, greenish-yellow, umbellate and rich in nectar, an important late-autumn food source for bees and other insects. The fruit are purple-black to orange-yellow berries 6–8 mm in diameter, ripening in late winter; they are poisonous to humans but eaten by many birds.

Distribution and habitat

The species ranges from Ireland and Norway in the north to the Mediterranean in the south, and eastward into Ukraine and Iran. It favours moist, shaded woodland but tolerates a wide range of soil conditions. Introduced to North America, Australia and New Zealand, it has escaped cultivation and forms dense monocultures on forest floors and tree canopies, where it is classed as a noxious weed in several jurisdictions.

Cultivation and uses

Numerous cultivars have been selected with variegated, curled or otherwise distinctive foliage. Ivy is used as a ground cover and as a climber to cover walls, though concerns exist that its rootlets can damage masonry. Extracts of the leaves have a long history in traditional medicine as an expectorant for coughs. The plant is also symbolically associated with fidelity and eternal life, and ivy berries and leaves feature in Christmas decorations.
