Omayra Sánchez Garzón (28 August 1972 – 16 November 1985) was a 13-year-old Colombian girl killed during the 1985 eruption of the Nevado del Ruiz volcano, whose lahars destroyed the town of Armero in the Armero tragedy, killing more than 23,000 people.

Death

Sánchez was trapped up to her neck in water, concrete, and debris for roughly 60 hours after a lahar destroyed her home. Rescue workers and journalists were unable to free her — her legs were pinned beneath a brick door and the body of her aunt — and lacked the pump needed to drain the water around her. She remained conscious and composed through much of the ordeal, speaking with rescuers and reporters, before dying, most likely of gangrene or hypothermia, on 16 November 1985.

Legacy

A photograph of Sánchez in her final hours, taken by photojournalist Frank Fournier, won the World Press Photo of the Year award and became one of the most famous news images of the decade. Her story is frequently cited as a symbol of the failures of the relief effort — the Colombian government was criticised for not evacuating Armero despite warnings about the volcano — and of the human cost of natural disasters.
