The Co-operative Commonwealth Federation (CCF) was a federal democratic socialist and social-democratic political party in Canada, founded in 1932 in Calgary by a coalition of farmer, labour and socialist groups during the Great Depression. Its founding programme, the Regina Manifesto of 1933, called for the replacement of capitalism with a planned, co-operative economy.

History

Led first by J. S. Woodsworth, the CCF won seats in the House of Commons from 1935 onward and became a major force in several provinces. Its greatest triumph came in Saskatchewan in 1944, when Tommy Douglas led the CCF to form the first socialist government in North America. That government introduced North America's first universal public hospital insurance and, in 1962 under Douglas's successor, universal medical care — the model for Canada's national Medicare.

Legacy

In 1961 the CCF joined with the Canadian Labour Congress to found the New Democratic Party (NDP), into which it merged. Many CCF policies — public health insurance, old-age pensions, unemployment insurance and public ownership of utilities — became enduring features of Canadian public life.
