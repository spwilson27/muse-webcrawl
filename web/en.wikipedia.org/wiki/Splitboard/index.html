Splitboard

A splitboard is a snowboard that can be separated lengthwise into two ski-like halves, allowing the rider to tour uphill using climbing skins in the manner of backcountry skiers, and then be reassembled into a solid snowboard for the descent. Splitboarding gives snowboarders access to backcountry terrain without the need for snowshoes on ascents.

Equipment

A splitboard system consists of the board itself, split-specific bindings that can rotate for touring mode or lock down for riding mode, collapsible poles, and adhesive climbing skins cut to fit each half. Clips and latches along the centre seam hold the two halves rigidly together when riding. Crampons are available for icy traverses, and, as with all backcountry travel, riders carry avalanche safety equipment including a transceiver, shovel, and probe.

History

Early homemade splitboards appeared in the late 1980s and early 1990s, when riders cut ordinary snowboards in half; Brett Kobernik is usually credited with producing the first DIY splitboard around 1990. Commercial kits from Voilé in the mid-1990s, which converted a standard board using a sawn-in-half insert pattern, made splitboarding practical, and purpose-built factory splitboards from manufacturers such as Voilé, Burton, Jones, and Karakoram followed.
