Snaefell (Manx: Sniaull) is the highest mountain on the Isle of Man, at 2,034 feet (620 m) above sea level. It is the island's only summit higher than 2,000 feet.

On a clear day it is said that from the summit one can see seven kingdoms: those of Mann, Scotland, England, Ireland, Wales, Heaven, and Manannán (the sea). The summit is crowned by a café and a communications mast, and is served by the Snaefell Mountain Railway, which has carried visitors from Laxey since 1895.

Geography

Snaefell rises in the northern part of the island's central mountain massif. The A18 Mountain Road, part of the Isle of Man TT course, passes close beneath the summit at the Bungalow, where the railway crosses the road. The name derives from Old Norse Snæfell, meaning "snow mountain".

Weather station and visibility

An automated weather station has operated at the summit since 1999. The summit is notorious for rapid weather changes, high winds and fog even when conditions at sea level are calm. The viewshed includes the Mourne Mountains in Ireland, the Galloway hills in Scotland, the Lake District in England and the mountains of Snowdonia in Wales.
