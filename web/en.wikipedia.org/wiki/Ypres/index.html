Ypres (Dutch: Ieper) is a Belgian city and municipality in the province of West Flanders. In the Middle Ages it was one of the great cloth-making cities of Flanders, and during the First World War it stood at the centre of the Ypres Salient, where a series of major battles devastated the city and surrounding countryside.

History

Ypres grew rich in the 12th and 13th centuries on the Flemish woollen cloth trade; its immense Cloth Hall (Lakenhalle), completed around 1304, symbolised that wealth. Decline followed as the cloth trade shifted to England and the countryside, and the city later passed through Spanish, French, and Austrian hands.

First World War

From 1914 to 1918 British, Commonwealth, French, and Belgian forces defended the salient around Ypres against repeated German offensives. The First (1914), Second (1915 — the first large-scale use of poison gas), and Third Battles of Ypres (1917, commonly called Passchendaele) caused hundreds of thousands of casualties, and artillery reduced the city to ruins. After the war Ypres was painstakingly rebuilt in its pre-war form, including the Cloth Hall and St Martin's Cathedral.

Remembrance

The Menin Gate Memorial to the Missing bears the names of more than 54,000 Commonwealth soldiers who died in the salient and have no known grave; the Last Post has been sounded beneath it every evening since 1928 (with an interruption during the Second World War). The In Flanders Fields Museum, named for John McCrae's famous poem, is housed in the Cloth Hall.
