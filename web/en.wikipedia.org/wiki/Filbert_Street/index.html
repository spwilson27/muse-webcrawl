Filbert Street was a football stadium in Leicester, England, which was the home of Leicester City F.C. from 1891 until 2002. Officially renamed the City Business Stadium in the 1990s, it was almost universally known by its street name.

History

Leicester City, then Leicester Fosse, moved to Filbert Street in 1891. The ground was gradually developed, with the Main Stand built in the 1920s and the distinctive double-decker North Stand added in 1993 under chairman Martin George. The stadium hosted the club through its First Division campaigns and cup runs of the twentieth century, and its record attendance of 47,298 was set for an FA Cup tie against Tottenham Hotspur in 1928.

Closure

Plans to expand the hemmed-in ground proved impractical, and the club built the new Walkers Stadium (later King Power Stadium) a short distance away on the banks of the River Soar. The last competitive match at Filbert Street was played in May 2002, and the stadium was demolished in 2003; the site was later redeveloped, partly as student accommodation, with Filbert Street itself retaining its name.
