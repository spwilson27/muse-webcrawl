Tatra National Park, Slovakia

LocationPrešov and Žilina Regions, Slovakia
Area738 km² (285 sq mi) plus protective zone
Established1 January 1949
Highest pointGerlachovský štít, 2,655 m

Tatra National Park, Slovakia (Slovak: Tatranský národný park; abbreviated TANAP) is one of the nine national parks in Slovakia. It is situated in north-central Slovakia in the Tatra Mountains. The park is important for the protection of a diverse range of flora and fauna species, with many endemic species, including the Tatra chamois. It was the first national park established in Slovakia.

Geography

The park covers the Slovak parts of the High Tatras and Western Tatras and the whole of the Belianske Tatras. Its highest summit, Gerlachovský štít (2,655 m), is the highest peak of the entire Carpathian range. The park contains more than a hundred mountain lakes (plesá), numerous waterfalls and the Belianska Cave, the only cave in the park open to the public. Together with the Polish Tatra National Park it forms a UNESCO biosphere reserve.

History

The park was established on 1 January 1949, making it the oldest national park in Slovakia; its regulations were significantly revised in later decades, and a large windstorm in November 2004 felled trees across thousands of hectares on the park's southern slopes, sparking debate about salvage logging and forest management.

Wildlife

Characteristic species include the Tatra chamois, alpine marmot, brown bear, Eurasian lynx, wolf, and golden eagle; the park also protects relict arctic-alpine plants and dense spruce and Swiss pine forests.
