Betamethasone is a potent corticosteroid medication of the glucocorticoid class. It is used, in tablet, cream, ointment and injectable forms, to treat a wide range of inflammatory and autoimmune conditions. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Betamethasone is used for skin disorders such as eczema and psoriasis (as topical preparations, often as the esters betamethasone dipropionate or valerate), for inflammatory joint and soft-tissue conditions by injection, and systemically for severe allergic reactions, asthma exacerbations and autoimmune disease. A major obstetric use is in pregnant women at risk of preterm delivery: a short course given to the mother crosses the placenta and accelerates fetal lung maturation, reducing the risk of respiratory distress syndrome in the newborn. In some countries it is also combined with antibiotics or antifungals in skin creams.

Pharmacology and adverse effects

Betamethasone is a long-acting glucocorticoid roughly 25–30 times as potent as hydrocortisone, with negligible mineralocorticoid activity; it differs from dexamethasone only in the orientation of one methyl group. Long-term or high-dose use can cause the characteristic corticosteroid adverse effects: suppression of the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis, increased blood sugar, fluid retention, thinning of the skin with topical use, and increased susceptibility to infection. Betamethasone is prohibited in sport as a performance-affecting glucocorticoid except by permitted routes.
