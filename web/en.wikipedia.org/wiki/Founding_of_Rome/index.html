Founding of Rome

The founding of Rome was a prehistoric event or process later greatly embellished by Roman historians and poets. Archaeological evidence indicates that the area of Rome was inhabited from the Bronze Age, and that the hilltop settlements gradually merged into a city during the early Iron Age. According to Roman tradition, the city was founded on 21 April 753 BC by Romulus, its first king.

The legend

According to tradition, Romulus and Remus were twin sons of Rhea Silvia and the god Mars, descendants of the Trojan hero Aeneas. Abandoned as infants and suckled by a she-wolf (the lupa Capitolina), they grew up to found a city. In a quarrel over the site and the omens — Remus favoured the Aventine, Romulus the Palatine — Remus was killed, and Romulus founded the city on the Palatine Hill, tracing its sacred boundary (the pomerium) with a plough. Romulus then populated the city, in part through the seizure of women from the neighbouring Sabines (the "Rape of the Sabine Women").

Archaeology

Excavations on the Palatine have uncovered Iron Age huts dating to the 8th century BC, consistent with the traditional date, and burials in the Forum Necropolis from the 10th century BC. Historians generally view the founding as a gradual process of synoecism — the unification of separate hilltop villages — rather than a single act, though the traditional date remains a reasonable approximation for the emergence of a unified settlement.
