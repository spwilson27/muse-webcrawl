Operation Barga

Operation Barga was a land reform programme implemented in the Indian state of West Bengal from 1978 by the Left Front government led by the Communist Party of India (Marxist). It aimed to protect and register sharecroppers, known in Bengali as bargadars, giving them legal recognition and security of tenure.

Under the programme, government officials and panchayat representatives held camps in villages to record sharecroppers in the land records. Registration gave the bargadar a hereditary right to cultivate the plot and a legally guaranteed share of the crop, commonly three-quarters if the sharecropper supplied the inputs, and protected them against arbitrary eviction by landowners.

By the mid-1980s well over a million sharecroppers had been recorded, covering a large share of West Bengal's cultivated land. Economists have frequently credited Operation Barga, together with tenancy reforms and panchayat decentralisation, with contributing to the marked growth of agricultural production in West Bengal during the 1980s.
