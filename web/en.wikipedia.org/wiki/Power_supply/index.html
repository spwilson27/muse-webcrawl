Power supply

A power supply is an electrical device that supplies electric power to an electrical load. The main purpose of a power supply is to convert electric current from a source to the correct voltage, current, and frequency to power the load. As a result, power supplies are sometimes referred to as electric power converters. Some power supplies are separate standalone pieces of equipment, while others are built into the load appliances that they power. Power supplies for electronic devices can be broadly divided into linear and switching types.

General classification

Power supplies may be classified in various ways, including by functional features. For example, a regulated power supply is one that maintains constant output voltage or current despite variations in load current or input voltage. Conversely a constant power supply adjusts its output voltage so that a constant power level is delivered. A programmable power supply allows remote control of its operation via an analog input or digital interface such as RS-232 or GPIB.

Types

DC power supplies

An AC-to-DC power supply operates on an input AC voltage and generates an output DC voltage. A rectifying circuit is used to convert the input AC to a pulsating DC, after which filtering smooths the output. Depending on the requirements, a voltage regulator may be used to stabilise the output.

Linear power supply

In a linear power supply, the input AC is first stepped down (or up) by a transformer, then rectified and filtered. Voltage regulation is achieved by dissipating excess power as heat in a series pass element, which makes linear supplies simple and quiet but relatively inefficient and heavy.

Switched-mode power supply

A switched-mode power supply (SMPS) incorporates a switching regulator that rapidly switches a transistor between on and off states, transferring energy through inductors, capacitors, or high-frequency transformers. Because the switching element dissipates little power, SMPS designs are compact, light, and efficient, and they dominate modern consumer electronics, including the ATX supplies used in desktop computers.

Uninterruptible power supply

An uninterruptible power supply (UPS) takes its input from two or more sources simultaneously, normally mains AC and a battery, so that the load continues to operate without interruption when the mains fails.

Applications

Power supplies are integral to computers, consumer electronics, industrial machinery, medical equipment, telecommunications, and laboratory test equipment. Bench power supplies used in electronics laboratories typically provide adjustable, current-limited DC outputs for prototyping and repair.

Protection

Most modern supplies include protection features such as overcurrent protection (OCP), overvoltage protection (OVP), undervoltage protection, short-circuit protection, and thermal shutdown, implemented with fuses, crowbar circuits, or supervisory integrated circuits.
