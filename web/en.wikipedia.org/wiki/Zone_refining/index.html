Zone refining

Zone refining
InventorWilliam Gardner Pfann
Year1951 (Bell Labs)
PrincipleSegregation of impurities at a moving melt interface
ApplicationsSemiconductor-grade silicon and germanium; ultrapure metals and organic compounds

Zone refining (also called zone melting or the floating-zone method) is a group of similar methods of purifying crystals, in which a narrow region of a crystal is melted, and this molten zone is moved along the crystal. The molten region melts impure solid at its forward edge and leaves a layer of purer material solidified behind it as it traverses the crystal. The impurities concentrate in the melt and are transported to one end of the ingot. The technique was invented by William Gardner Pfann at Bell Labs in the early 1950s to produce ultra-pure germanium and silicon for transistors.

Principle

Zone refining exploits the fact that most impurities are more soluble in the liquid phase than in the solid phase of a material. When a molten zone passes through a solid, the solid that freezes out behind the zone contains fewer impurities than the material ahead, while the impurities accumulate in the liquid and are carried along to the end of the charge. The degree of segregation is described by the distribution (segregation) coefficient k, the ratio of impurity concentration in the freezing solid to that in the liquid. Repeated passes of the zone can reduce impurity levels to parts per billion.

Applications

  • Semiconductors — purification of germanium and silicon; the floating-zone variant, in which a vertical rod is held without a crucible, produces very high-resistivity silicon for power devices and detectors.
  • Metals — production of ultra-pure metals for research and electronics.
  • Organic and inorganic compounds — purification of chemicals, pharmaceuticals and compounds for crystal growth.

Related methods

Zone levelling distributes a desired dopant uniformly through an ingot rather than removing impurities. The Czochralski process, by contrast, grows single crystals from a melt and is the dominant method for semiconductor wafers, often starting from zone-refined feedstock.
