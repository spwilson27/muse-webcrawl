Biblical judges

The biblical judges are described in the Hebrew Bible, primarily in the Book of Judges, as leaders of the Israelites during the period between the death of Joshua and the establishment of the Israelite monarchy. In Hebrew they are called shofetim (שֹׁפְטִים), a term that also encompasses magistrates and rulers more broadly. Unlike later kings, the judges were charismatic, often military leaders raised up, tradition holds, by God to deliver Israel from its enemies in times of crisis.

The judges led the Israelite tribes before the monarchy.

The cycle of the Book of Judges

The Book of Judges presents the era through a recurring theological cycle: the Israelites fall into idolatry, are oppressed by a neighbouring people, cry out for help, and are delivered by a judge raised up for the purpose; peace then lasts until the judge dies and the cycle begins again. This framework shapes the narratives of figures such as Othniel, Ehud, Deborah, Gideon, Jephthah and Samson.

Major and minor judges

Scholars conventionally distinguish the "major judges", whose deeds are narrated at length (Othniel, Ehud, Deborah, Gideon, Jephthah and Samson), from the "minor judges", who are mentioned only briefly (Shamgar, Tola, Jair, Ibzan, Elon and Abdon). Deborah, a prophetess who judged Israel at a palm tree between Ramah and Bethel, is the only female judge named. Samson, the last of the major judges, is remembered for his Nazirite vow and extraordinary strength.

Historical questions

The historicity and chronology of the period are debated. Some historians regard the judges as local heroes whose stories were later woven into a national narrative; others see the office as comparable to the suffetes of Carthage and other Phoenician cities, suggesting a genuine, pre-monarchic institution of tribal leadership.
