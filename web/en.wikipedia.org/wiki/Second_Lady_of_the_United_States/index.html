Second Lady of the United States

ResidenceNumber One Observatory Circle, Washington, D.C.
First holderAbigail Adams (as vice-presidential spouse, 1789)
Term usage"Second Lady" popularized in the 1980s

Second Lady of the United States (SLOTUS) is the informal title held by the wife of the vice president of the United States, concurrent with the vice president's term of office. The title is modeled on First Lady of the United States (FLOTUS). Since 2021 the male equivalent, "Second Gentleman," has also been used, when Doug Emhoff became the first husband of a vice president, serving alongside Vice President Kamala Harris.

History and role

Although the role is as old as the vice presidency — Abigail Adams was the first vice-presidential spouse — the "Second Lady" title only became common in the 1980s, partly through the visibility of Barbara Bush and Tipper Gore. The second spouse has no official duties but traditionally supports the vice president, hosts official visitors at the vice-presidential residence, Number One Observatory Circle, participates in ceremonial and charitable events, and often champions public causes: Tipper Gore campaigned on mental health and record labeling, Jill Biden on military families and community colleges, and Karen Pence on art therapy and military spouses.

Notable holders

Several second ladies later became first ladies, including Abigail Adams, Edith Roosevelt, and Lady Bird Johnson; Florence Harding and Bess Truman also held both roles. Letitia Tyler served as Second Lady for barely a month in 1841 before becoming First Lady. Modern second spouses maintain small staffs in the vice president's office and are protected by the Secret Service.

Second Gentleman

With the election of Kamala Harris in 2020, her husband Douglas Emhoff became the first "Second Gentleman of the United States," establishing the masculine form of the title and adapting the role's traditions to the first male spouse.
