Latin conjugation

Principal parts and endings of the regular conjugations

Latin conjugation is the system by which Latin verbs are inflected to express person, number, tense, mood, and voice. Latin verbs are traditionally divided into four conjugations, distinguished by the vowel of the infinitive ending: first (-āre), second (-ēre), third (-ere), and fourth (-īre). Each verb is learned with its four principal parts — for example amō, amāre, amāvī, amātum ("to love") — from which all other forms can be derived.

Tenses and moods

The indicative mood has six tenses: present, imperfect, and future (formed on the present stem), and perfect, pluperfect, and future perfect (formed on the perfect stem). The subjunctive has four tenses (present, imperfect, perfect, pluperfect), and the imperative is used for commands. Non-finite forms include infinitives, participles, the gerund, the gerundive, and the supine. Most tenses exist in both active and passive voice; deponent verbs have passive forms with active meanings.

Irregular verbs

Several common verbs are irregular, including esse ("to be"), posse ("to be able"), ferre ("to carry"), velle ("to want"), īre ("to go"), and fierī ("to become").
