Japanese numerals

[Image: Kanji numerals]

The Japanese numerals are the number words used in the Japanese language. In writing, they use both the native kanji characters and Arabic numerals, along with the traditional counting system inherited from Chinese. Japanese has two distinct sets of numerals: the native Japanese (yamato) words, used mainly for counting from one to ten, and the Sino-Japanese words, used for most larger numbers and in mathematics.

Large numbers

Large numbers group by ten thousand (万, man) rather than by a thousand, as in English. Thus 10,000 is ichiman, 100,000,000 (108) is oku (億), and 1012 is chō (兆). This system underlies the Japanese grouping of digits in sets of four.

Counters

Japanese uses a rich system of counters (助数詞, josūshi) that vary with the kind of object being counted: -hiki for small animals, -mai for flat objects, -hon for long cylindrical objects, and -nin for people, among hundreds of others.
