Highland games

Highland games (Scottish Gaelic: geamannan Gàidhealach) are events held in Scotland and in Scottish diaspora communities around the world to celebrate Scottish and Celtic culture, particularly that of the Scottish Highlands. Their distinctive features are heavy athletic events — most famously the caber toss — together with pipe band and solo piping competitions, Highland dancing, and clan gatherings.

Heavy events

  • Caber toss — the athlete lifts a long tapered log (the caber) and throws it so that it turns end over end, ideally landing in the "12 o'clock" position
  • Stone put — throwing a heavy stone, in open or "Braemar" style
  • Scottish hammer throw — a round metal hammer with a rigid handle, thrown with feet planted
  • Weight throw and weight for height — metal weights on a chain thrown for distance or over a bar
  • Sheaf toss — a burlap bag stuffed with straw thrown over a bar with a pitchfork

History and spread

Traditions of strength contests in the Highlands are old, but the modern games circuit dates largely from the 19th-century Highland revival — the Braemar Gathering, attended by Queen Victoria, remains among the most famous. Large games are also held in the United States (e.g. the Grandfather Mountain Highland Games in North Carolina), Canada, Australia, New Zealand and Norway.
