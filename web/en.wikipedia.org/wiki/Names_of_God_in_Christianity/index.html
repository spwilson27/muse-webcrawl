Names of God in Christianity

A number of traditions have lists of names of God, many of which enumerate the various qualities of a supreme being. In Christianity, the names of God derive from the Hebrew Bible and the New Testament. The most significant is the Tetragrammaton, YHWH, revealed to Moses in Exodus 3, often rendered "LORD" in English translations, and pronounced Jehovah or Yahweh. Its use in Christian worship varies by tradition.

Old Testament names

Names and epithets used in the Hebrew scriptures include El, Elohim, El Shaddai (God Almighty), Adonai (Lord), and compound forms such as YHWH Tzevaot (LORD of Hosts) and YHWH Yireh (the LORD will provide). Christians historically read these names as referring to the one God later revealed as Trinity.

New Testament

In the New Testament, Jesus addresses God as Abba (Father), and teaches his disciples to pray "Our Father". Jesus himself is given names and titles including Immanuel ("God with us"), the Word (Logos), Son of God, and Saviour. The Holy Spirit is named as Comforter or Advocate (Paraclete). Trinitarian doctrine holds that Father, Son and Holy Spirit together constitute the one God.

Devotional use

Lists of divine names compiled in devotion and theology served as aids to prayer and contemplation. In Catholic tradition, the Litany of the Holy Name of Jesus developed; Jehovah's Witnesses place particular emphasis on the name Jehovah as God's personal name.
