Chuvash language

Chuvash (Chuvash: Чӑваш чӗлхи, Chăvash chĕlhi) is a Turkic language spoken in European Russia, primarily in the Chuvash Republic and adjacent areas. It is the official language of Chuvashia together with Russian, and is spoken by roughly one million people. Chuvash is the only surviving member of the Oghuric branch of Turkic languages, whose other member, Bulgar, is extinct.

Classification

Chuvash is sharply distinct from the Common Turkic languages: its characteristic sound changes (rhotacism and lambdacism — for example, Chuvash hĕr "girl" versus Common Turkic qız) reflect the Oghuric branch spoken by the Volga Bulgars, from whom the Chuvash people are generally considered to descend.

Writing

The modern Chuvash alphabet, based on Cyrillic, was developed in the 1870s by the educator Ivan Yakovlev. It contains four additional letters (Ӑ, Ӗ, Ҫ, Ӳ) for sounds not found in Russian. Earlier periods used Arabic and various Cyrillic adaptations.

Status

Chuvash is classified as vulnerable by UNESCO, as transmission to younger generations weakens under the influence of Russian, which dominates education and media. It is nevertheless taught in schools in Chuvashia and used in regional media, literature, and the Chuvash Wikipedia.
