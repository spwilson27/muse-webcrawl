Nunavik

Nunavik (Inuktitut: ᓄᓇᕕᒃ) is the northernmost third of the province of Quebec, Canada, covering roughly 443,000 square kilometres of tundra and taiga north of the 55th parallel. It is the homeland of the Quebec Inuit, who constitute the large majority of its approximately 14,000 residents, living in fourteen coastal villages along Hudson Bay, Hudson Strait, and Ungava Bay. The regional centre is Kuujjuaq.

Governance

Rights and institutions in Nunavik derive from the James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement of 1975, the first modern land-claims treaty in Canada. The Kativik Regional Government provides municipal-type services to the region's northern villages, Makivik Corporation represents the Inuit beneficiaries and manages compensation funds, and health and education are delivered by dedicated Inuit-serving boards. Nunavik is not a separate territory like Nunavut; proposals for an autonomous Nunavik regional government have been debated for decades, and a self-government agreement remains an objective of Inuit leadership.

Geography and life

The region has no road connection to southern Quebec; communities depend on air transport and seasonal sealift. The landscape includes the crater lake Pingualuit, the Torngat Mountains on the Labrador border (partly protected as a national park), and extensive caribou habitat. Mining — notably the Raglan nickel mine — hydroelectric development on the region's rivers, and a growing tourism and film sector (the region was the setting of the Inuit film Atanarjuat: The Fast Runner's cultural milieu, and of the documentary tradition begun by Nanook of the North, filmed near Inukjuak) shape its economy alongside hunting, fishing, and Inuit art.
