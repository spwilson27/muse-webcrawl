United States visa

Issuing authorityU.S. Department of State
Administered byBureau of Consular Affairs
PurposeTravel to the United States
TypesImmigrant and nonimmigrant

A United States visa is a travel document issued by the U.S. Department of State through embassies and consulates abroad that permits the bearer to travel to a United States port of entry and request permission to enter the country. Possession of a visa does not guarantee admission; a Customs and Border Protection officer makes the final determination at the port of entry.

Visa categories

U.S. visas are broadly divided into immigrant visas, for persons intending to reside permanently in the United States, and nonimmigrant visas, for temporary stays such as tourism, business, study, and work. Well-known nonimmigrant categories include the B-1/B-2 visitor visa, the F-1 student visa, the J-1 exchange visitor visa, and the H-1B specialty occupation work visa.

Application process

Most applicants must complete the online DS-160 form, pay a machine-readable visa fee, schedule an interview at a U.S. embassy or consulate, and demonstrate strong ties to their home country sufficient to overcome the statutory presumption of immigrant intent. Citizens of countries participating in the Visa Waiver Program may instead travel for short business or tourist stays using an ESTA authorization.

History

The modern visa system developed from the Immigration Act of 1924 and was reshaped by the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1952 and the Immigration Act of 1965, which abolished the national-origins quota system.
