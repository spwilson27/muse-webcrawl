Tote board

A tote board is a large display board at a racecourse or other betting venue that shows the odds, pools, and results information for pari-mutuel wagering. The name derives from the totalisator (shortened to "tote"), the automated system that pools bets and calculates payouts.

Function

As bets are placed, the totalisator continuously updates the amounts wagered on each runner and the implied odds. The tote board displays this information to bettors in real time: win, place, and show pools in North American racing, or equivalent pool totals elsewhere. After a race, the board shows the official placings and the dividend (payout) for each winning combination.

History

Early totalisators were mechanical — the first automatic totalisator, invented by George Julius, was installed in New Zealand in 1913 and later at racecourses around the world. Electric and then electronic tote boards replaced hand-updated chalkboards, and modern systems feed the same data to television graphics and off-track betting terminals.

Related terms

The phrase "the tote" is commonly used in British and Australasian racing to refer to the pari-mutuel operator itself. In some jurisdictions the Tote is a specific betting organisation, such as the UK Tote.
