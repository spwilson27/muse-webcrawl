Metabolic engineering

Metabolic engineering is the practice of optimising genetic and regulatory processes within cells to increase the production of a desired substance by the cell. It combines principles of chemical engineering, molecular biology, and systems biology, and typically proceeds through repeated cycles of design, construction, testing, and analysis.

Approach

Engineers first analyse the cell's metabolic network to identify which reactions limit production of the target compound. Genetic modifications then increase the activity of enzymes along the desired pathway, knock out competing pathways, or introduce entirely new (heterologous) pathways from other organisms. Analytical tools include metabolic flux analysis, genome-scale metabolic models, and "omics" measurements of transcripts, proteins, and metabolites.

Applications

Classic successes include microbial production of the antimalarial precursor artemisinic acid in engineered yeast, the synthesis of 1,3-propanediol for polymers in E. coli, and increased yields of amino acids, vitamins, antibiotics, and biofuels such as ethanol and butanol. Engineered microbes are also used to produce insulin, industrial enzymes, flavours, and fragrances.
