Bruce Leslie McLaren (30 August 1937 – 2 June 1970) was a New Zealand racing driver, engineer and designer who founded the McLaren motor racing team. He won four Formula One Grands Prix, was runner-up in the 1960 World Championship, won the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 1966, and dominated the Can-Am sports car series with his own cars.

Career

McLaren began racing in New Zealand in an Austin 7 before winning a scholarship to race in Europe. He joined the Cooper works team in Formula One in 1959 and won the 1959 United States Grand Prix at Sebring, becoming at 22 the youngest Grand Prix winner up to that time, a record that stood for over four decades. In 1963 he formed Bruce McLaren Motor Racing Ltd. His team won its first Grand Prix at Spa in 1968, with McLaren himself driving, making him one of only a few driver-constructors to win a Formula One race in a car of his own design and name. With Denny Hulme, the "Bruce and Denny Show" dominated Can-Am racing from 1967 to 1971, and McLaren and Chris Amon won Le Mans in 1966 in a Ford GT40.

Death and legacy

McLaren was killed on 2 June 1970 while testing the McLaren M8D Can-Am car at the Goodwood Circuit in England, when the rear bodywork came adrift and the car spun into a marshal's post. The team he founded continued under Teddy Mayer and later Ron Dennis, becoming one of the most successful in Formula One history. The McLaren Technology Centre in Woking, England, preserves his legacy, and the Bruce McLaren Trust in New Zealand maintains his archive.
