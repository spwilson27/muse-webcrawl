Iron Gate (Danube)

Location: Danube, Serbia–Romania border
Length: ≈ 134 km of gorge system
Known for: Iron Gates hydroelectric dams

The Iron Gate (Serbian: Đerdap; Romanian: Porțile de Fier) is a gorge system on the river Danube forming part of the boundary between Serbia and Romania. For roughly 134 km the river cuts through the southern Carpathian Mountains, narrowing in the Kazan Gorge to about 150 metres between cliffs rising hundreds of metres. Historically its rapids, whirlpools, and reefs made it the most dangerous obstacle to navigation on the Danube.

History

The Roman emperor Trajan's engineers built a tow road along the gorge during the Dacian Wars, commemorated by the Tabula Traiana inscription, and a bridge downstream at Drobeta. In the 1890s a canal blasted at Sip bypassed the worst rapids. Archaeologically, the Serbian bank preserves the Mesolithic settlement of Lepenski Vir (c. 9500–6000 BC), famous for its trapezoidal houses and sculpted boulders.

Dams

Two joint Romanian–Yugoslav (now Serbian) hydroelectric and navigation projects transformed the gorge: Iron Gate I, completed in 1972, and Iron Gate II, completed in 1984. Their reservoirs raised the water level by about 35 metres, submerging the rapids, the island of Ada Kaleh, and several settlements, while their locks made the Danube navigable year-round. The surrounding areas are protected as Đerdap National Park in Serbia and Iron Gates Natural Park in Romania.
