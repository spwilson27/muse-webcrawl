Krishna is a major deity in Hinduism, worshipped as the eighth avatar of Vishnu and, in Krishna-centred traditions such as Gaudiya Vaishnavism, as the Supreme God in his own right. He is the god of protection, compassion, tenderness and love, and one of the most widely revered and popular figures in the Indian religious imagination.

Life in the texts

The sources — the Mahābhārata, the Harivaṃśa and above all the Bhāgavata Purāṇa — tell how Krishna was born in Mathura to Devaki and Vasudeva, was miraculously carried across the Yamuna to safety from the tyrant Kamsa, and was raised by cowherds in Vrindavan. His childhood exploits — stealing butter, subduing the serpent Kaliya, lifting Mount Govardhan — and his youthful love for Radha and the gopis became the heart of devotional poetry and art. As an adult he slew Kamsa, ruled in Dwarka, and in the Mahābhārata served as charioteer and counsellor to Arjuna, delivering the teaching of the Bhagavad Gita on the field of Kurukshetra.

Worship

Krishna's birthday, Janmashtami, is celebrated across India; the temples of Vrindavan, Mathura and Dwarka draw millions; and the Hare Krishna movement (ISKCON), founded in New York in 1966, carried his worship worldwide. The Rādhā-Krishna love story anchors an immense literature in Sanskrit and the regional languages, and Krishna's flute-playing, peacock feather and blue skin make him among the most recognisable images in world religion.
