Viewfinder

A viewfinder is a component of a camera that allows the photographer to see the field of view of the image that will be captured, in order to frame and focus the photograph before taking it. Viewfinders range from simple open frames to complex optical and electronic systems, and the type of viewfinder is one of the defining characteristics of camera design.

Types

  • Optical (direct) viewfinders — a simple window through or above the camera body, as on compact and rangefinder cameras; inexpensive but subject to parallax error at close distances
  • Waist-level and ground-glass finders — the image is viewed on a focusing screen, reversed left-to-right, as on twin-lens reflex and large-format cameras
  • Single-lens reflex (SLR) viewfinders — a mirror and pentaprism or pentamirror show exactly the image formed by the taking lens, with focusing aids and exposure information
  • Electronic viewfinders (EVF) — a small high-resolution display fed by the camera's sensor, standard on mirrorless cameras; can preview exposure, white balance, and depth of field in real time
  • LCD screens — the rear display used in live view, common on digital cameras and smartphones

Frame coverage (the percentage of the final image visible in the finder), magnification, and eye relief are key specifications. In cinematography, the director's viewfinder is a separate handheld optical device used to scout shots and select lenses before the camera is set up.
