Designer dog

A Labradoodle, among the best-known designer dogs

A designer dog is a dog that is the intentional cross of two recognised purebred dogs, typically produced and marketed as a companion animal. Designer crosses are usually given portmanteau names combining syllables of the parent breeds, such as the Labradoodle (Labrador Retriever × Poodle), the Cockapoo (Cocker Spaniel × Poodle), and the Goldendoodle (Golden Retriever × Poodle). Other popular crosses include the Puggle (Pug × Beagle) and the Schnoodle (Schnauzer × Poodle).

History

Deliberate crossbreeding is as old as dog breeding itself, but the designer dog phenomenon — naming, marketing and selling first-generation crosses at premium prices — grew rapidly from the 1980s and 1990s. The Labradoodle was first bred in Australia in 1989 by Wally Conron for a guide-dog organisation seeking a low-allergen guide dog; Conron later expressed regret at unleashing the fashion that followed.

Characteristics

Advocates claim designer dogs combine desirable traits of both parents — for example the Poodle's low-shedding coat with another breed's temperament — and may benefit from hybrid vigour. In practice, first-generation crosses vary considerably in coat, size and behaviour, and traits cannot be guaranteed. Poodle crosses in particular are frequently marketed as hypoallergenic, though no dog is completely allergen-free and shedding varies between individuals.

Controversy

Designer dogs are controversial within the dog world. Kennel clubs do not recognise them as breeds, and critics — including many breeders of the parent breeds — argue that high prices encourage irresponsible breeding without the health testing expected of pedigree breeders, and that similar dogs are available from shelters. Supporters respond that responsible designer-dog breeders do health-test and that demand reflects genuine preferences for size, coat and temperament. Some crosses have since been bred over multiple generations and are the subject of breed-development efforts and registries of their own.
