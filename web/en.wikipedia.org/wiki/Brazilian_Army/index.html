Brazilian Army

The Brazilian Army (Portuguese: Exército Brasileiro, EB) is the land branch of the Brazilian Armed Forces. It traces its origins to the colonial forces that fought the Dutch in the 17th century, and regards the First Battle of Guararapes (1648) as its foundational moment. With roughly 200,000 active personnel supported by large reserves, it is the largest land force in South America.

History

After independence in 1822 the army of the Empire of Brazil fought in the Cisplatine War and, alongside Argentina and Uruguay, in the Paraguayan War (1864–1870), the largest conflict in South American history. The 1889 proclamation of the republic was led by Marshal Deodoro da Fonseca. In the Second World War the Brazilian Expeditionary Force (FEB), some 25,000 troops, fought in Italy in 1944–45, taking Monte Castello and fighting at Montese. The Army was the central institution of the military government that ruled Brazil from 1964 to 1985, and since redemocratization it has concentrated on border security — especially in the Amazon — civic action, and peacekeeping, notably commanding the UN mission in Haiti (MINUSTAH, 2004–2017).

Organisation

The Army is organized into geographic military commands, including the Amazon Military Command at Manaus, and fields infantry, cavalry, artillery, engineering, communications, and aviation arms — its helicopters operated by Army Aviation Command at Taubaté. Elite formations include the Parachute Infantry Brigade in Rio de Janeiro and jungle warfare units trained at the Jungle Warfare Training Center (CIGS) in Manaus, considered a world reference in its field. Officers are trained at the Agulhas Negras Military Academy (AMAN) at Resende. Military service is compulsory for men at age 18, with a fraction of each cohort serving.

Equipment

The Army's armour includes Leopard 1A5 main battle tanks and the Brazilian-built Guarani 6×6 armoured personnel carrier family, alongside Cascavel and Urutu vehicles from the former Engesa. It fields ASTROS multiple rocket launchers produced by Avibras, widely exported.
