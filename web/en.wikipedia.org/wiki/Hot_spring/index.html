Hot spring

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A hot spring, hydrothermal spring, or geothermal spring is a spring produced by the emergence of geothermally heated groundwater onto the surface of the Earth. The groundwater is heated either by shallow bodies of magma (molten rock) or by circulation through faults to hot rock deep in the Earth's crust. Hot spring water often contains large amounts of dissolved minerals. Hot springs are found all over the Earth, even in deserts and on the ocean floor.

Definitions

There is no universally accepted definition of a hot spring. For example, one proposed definition is a spring with water temperatures above its surroundings; another is a spring whose water is above human body temperature, around 36.5–37.5 °C (97.7–99.5 °F). Because many hot springs are located in volcanic zones, their water can be heated to the boiling point, and some springs are dangerous to people and animals.

Sources of heat

The water issuing from a hot spring is heated geothermally, that is, with heat produced from the Earth's mantle. In volcanic areas, water may come into contact with very hot rock heated by magma chambers close to the surface, producing springs of extremely high temperature, sometimes including geysers and fumaroles. In non-volcanic areas, water can be heated by deep circulation through faults into rock warmed by the geothermal gradient.

Biology

Hot springs are often home to communities of thermophilic microorganisms, including cyanobacteria and other bacteria and archaea adapted to near-boiling water. The colourful microbial mats at springs such as those in Yellowstone National Park are a famous example; enzymes from hot-spring organisms, such as Taq polymerase from Thermus aquaticus, became essential tools in molecular biology.

Uses

Hot springs have been used for bathing and healing for thousands of years. In Japan, bathing in onsen is a major cultural tradition and tourist industry; Iceland, Hungary, New Zealand, and many other countries likewise have well-known spa towns built around geothermal waters.
