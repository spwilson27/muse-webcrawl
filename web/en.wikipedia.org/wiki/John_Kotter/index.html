John Kotter

John Paul Kotter (born 25 February 1947) is an American business professor and author. He is the Konosuke Matsushita Professor of Leadership, Emeritus, at Harvard Business School, and the founder of Kotter International, a management consulting firm. He is best known for his eight-step model of leading organizational change.

Education and career

Kotter graduated from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology with a degree in electrical engineering and completed his doctorate at Harvard Business School at the age of 24, joining its faculty in 1972. He became one of the youngest tenured full professors in Harvard's history.

Change management

In his 1996 bestseller Leading Change, Kotter proposed an eight-step process for managing organizational change: create urgency, build a guiding coalition, form a strategic vision, enlist a volunteer army, enable action by removing barriers, generate short-term wins, sustain acceleration, and institute change. His earlier fable Our Iceberg Is Melting (2006), co-authored with Holger Rathgeber, popularized the model.
