Shahrestan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shahrestan (Persian: شهرستان, romanised šahrestân, "county") is the second-level administrative division of Iran, below the province (ostân) and above the district (bakhsh) and rural district (dehestân). The word derives from shahr ("city") and the suffix -estân ("place of"). Each shahrestan is administered by a governor (farmândâr) appointed by the Ministry of the Interior, and is named after its principal city, which serves as its capital.

Structure

Iran's thirty-one provinces are divided into more than four hundred shahrestans. A shahrestan in turn comprises one or more districts (bakhsh), each consisting of cities and rural agglomerations. The number of counties has steadily increased over the decades as populous districts have been elevated to county status, often in response to local lobbying, since county status brings a larger share of state budgets and administrative offices.

Tehran and special cases

The shahrestans of Tehran Province include Tehran County itself, which contains the national capital. Some large cities, such as Tehran, Mashhad and Isfahan, form the core of their counties and dominate them administratively and demographically. Demographic and statistical data in Iran — census results, election returns and economic statistics — are commonly reported at the shahrestan level, making it the most widely used unit in Iranian statistical publications.
