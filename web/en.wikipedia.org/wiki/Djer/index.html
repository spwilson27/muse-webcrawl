Djer

Djer (or Zer or Sekhty) is considered the third pharaoh of the First Dynasty of ancient Egypt in current Egyptology. He lived around the mid-31st century BC and reigned for c. 40 years. A mummified forearm of Djer or his wife was discovered by Flinders Petrie, but was discarded by Émile Brugsch.

Reign

Evidence for Djer's reign comes from ivory and wooden labels found at Abydos and Saqqara, inscriptions, and later king lists. The Palermo Stone records events of his reign, including festivals, tax collection, and a possible military expedition. Manetho credits him with a reign of 57 years and attributes a medical treatise to him.

Tomb

Djer's tomb (Tomb O) is located at Umm el-Qa'ab in Abydos. In later periods it was reinterpreted as the tomb of the god Osiris. The tomb was surrounded by hundreds of subsidiary burials of retainers sacrificed to serve the king in the afterlife.
