Plant stem cell

The shoot apical meristem, a reservoir of plant stem cells that generates all aerial organs.

Plant stem cells are innately undifferentiated cells located in the meristems of plants. Plant stem cells serve as the origin of plant vitality and growth, since they maintain themselves while also supplying a continuous supply of precursor cells to form differentiated tissues and organs in plants. Unlike animal stem cells, plant stem cells are totipotent in many contexts and underpin the plant's ability to grow throughout its life and to regenerate organs after injury.

Meristems

Two primary regions maintain stem cells. The shoot apical meristem (SAM), at the tip of the shoot, produces leaves, stems and flowers. The root apical meristem (RAM), at the root tip, produces the root system. In each meristem a small group of slowly dividing cells forms the stem cell pool: in the SAM this is organised around an organising centre expressing WUSCHEL, with stem cells maintained by the CLAVATA signalling pathway; in the RAM the quiescent centre maintains surrounding initials, regulated by pathways involving WOX5 and the hormones auxin and cytokinin. Secondary meristems — the vascular cambium and cork cambium — contain stem cells responsible for secondary growth (thickening) in woody plants.

Properties

Plant stem cells divide asymmetrically or symmetrically to balance self-renewal and differentiation. Their activity is controlled by niches of surrounding cells, peptide signals, and phytohormones. Because differentiated plant cells can dedifferentiate and form callus tissue in culture, plant cells display a plasticity with few parallels in animals; whole plants can be regenerated from single somatic cells, which is the basis of plant tissue culture and micropropagation.

Applications

Cultured plant stem cells — such as cambial meristematic cells grown in bioreactors — are used commercially to produce cosmetic ingredients and plant secondary metabolites without harvesting wild plants. Plant stem cell research also informs agriculture, forestry, and efforts to improve regeneration in recalcitrant crop species.
