Recognition memory

Recognition memory, a subcategory of episodic memory and declarative memory, is the ability to recognize previously encountered events, objects, or people. When the previously experienced event is re-encountered, the content is matched against stored memory representations and a signal of familiarity is generated.

Components

Recognition memory is thought to depend on two distinct processes: recollection, a detailed retrieval of the item together with its context, and familiarity, a context-free sense of having encountered the item before. This dual-process view is supported by studies of patients with hippocampal damage, who often show impaired recollection but relatively preserved familiarity.

Neural basis

The medial temporal lobe, including the hippocampus and perirhinal cortex, is critical for recognition memory. Event-related potential studies associate familiarity with the FN400 component and recollection with a later parietal old/new effect.

Testing

Recognition is commonly tested by presenting a list of items at study and then asking participants to judge, for each test item, whether it is "old" or "new".
