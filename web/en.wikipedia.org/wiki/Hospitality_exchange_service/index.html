Hospitality exchange service

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A hospitality exchange service (also called a home stay network or hospitality network) is an organisation that connects travellers with local hosts who offer free accommodation in their homes. Hosts are not typically reimbursed in money; the exchange is based on reciprocity, cultural exchange, and the expectation that guests may one day host in return, though direct reciprocity with the same person is usually not required.

History

The earliest organised networks emerged after the Second World War. Servas International, founded in 1949, promoted peace through personal contact between travellers and hosts. Hospitality Club, launched in 2000, moved the model online; CouchSurfing, founded in 2004, popularised it worldwide with millions of members, before moving to a for-profit model in 2011 and introducing a paywall in 2020. Non-profit alternatives include BeWelcome and Trustroots, and Warm Showers serves touring cyclists.

How it works

Members create profiles, search for hosts in their destination, and send stay requests describing themselves and their trip. After a stay, both parties commonly leave references, which build trust within the community. Networks typically use verification systems, identity checks, and reference histories to improve safety, since stays take place in private homes between strangers.

Criticism and safety

Concerns include misuse of the system by people seeking free lodging without social exchange, "freeloading", and occasional reports of misconduct. Networks respond with moderation, reference systems, and safety guidelines, and advise members to communicate within the platform and review hosts' references before a stay.
