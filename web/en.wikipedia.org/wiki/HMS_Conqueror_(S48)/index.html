HMS Conqueror was a Churchill-class nuclear-powered fleet submarine of the Royal Navy. She is best known as the submarine that sank the Argentine cruiser ARA General Belgrano during the Falklands War in 1982 — the only nuclear-powered submarine ever to have engaged an enemy ship with torpedoes in wartime, and only the second submarine of any navy to sink a warship since the Second World War.

Sinking of the General Belgrano

Following the Argentine invasion of the Falkland Islands in April 1982, Conqueror, commanded by Commander Christopher Wreford-Brown, was deployed to the South Atlantic. Having shadowed the General Belgrano group, she received authorisation following a change to the rules of engagement and, on 2 May 1982, fired three Mark 8 torpedoes — a Second World War-era design chosen for reliability — of which two hit the cruiser. General Belgrano sank with the loss of 323 lives, nearly half of Argentina's total deaths in the war. After the attack, the Argentine Navy largely withdrew to coastal waters for the remainder of the conflict.

Later career

On returning to Faslane, Conqueror flew a "Jolly Roger" bearing symbols of her achievements, in the tradition of British submarines. A separate controversy followed reports that her logs for the period around the sinking could not be found when later sought by investigators. The submarine was decommissioned in 1990 and, like other retired British nuclear submarines, remained in storage at Devonport awaiting defuelling and disposal.
