Merovingian dynasty

The Merovingian dynasty was the ruling family of the Franks for nearly 300 years. The dynasty takes its name from Merovech (Latin: Meroveus or Merovius), the semi-legendary father of Childeric I. The Merovingians ruled the Frankish kingdom from the mid-5th century until 751, when Pepin the Short deposed Childeric III and founded the Carolingian dynasty.

Clovis and expansion

Clovis I, king of the Salian Franks from 481, united the Frankish tribes, defeated the Gallo-Roman ruler Syagrius, the Alemanni and the Visigoths, and founded the Merovingian realm. His conversion to Catholic Christianity — famously after his vow at the Battle of Tolbiac and his baptism by Saint Remigius — aligned the Franks with the Gallo-Roman population and the Church. Clovis's realm was divided among his four sons, establishing a recurring pattern of partition among heirs into sub-kingdoms such as Neustria, Austrasia and Burgundy.

Decline

From the late 7th century, royal power waned as the mayors of the palace, particularly the Pippinid (later Carolingian) family, accumulated real authority; later Merovingian kings were derided as rois fainéants ("do-nothing kings"). In 751 Pepin the Short, with papal approval, deposed Childeric III and had him tonsured into a monastery, ending the dynasty. The Merovingians are distinguished archaeologically by their long hair — a symbol of royal power — and by richly furnished burials such as that of Childeric, discovered at Tournai in 1653.
