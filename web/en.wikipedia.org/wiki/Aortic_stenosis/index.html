Aortic stenosis
SpecialtyCardiology
Key signEjection systolic (crescendo–decrescendo) murmur radiating to the carotids
Classic symptomsAngina, syncope, heart failure

Aortic stenosis (AS) is the narrowing of the opening of the aortic valve, which restricts blood flow from the left ventricle into the aorta. It is the most common valvular heart disease requiring intervention in developed countries, and its prevalence rises sharply with age.

Causes

The commonest cause is age-related calcific degeneration of a normal tricuspid valve. A congenitally bicuspid aortic valve calcifies earlier and is the leading cause in younger patients; rheumatic heart disease remains an important cause worldwide.

Signs and symptoms

Patients may be asymptomatic for decades. The classic triad of severe AS is exertional angina, syncope, and breathlessness from heart failure; once symptoms appear, prognosis without valve replacement is poor. Examination typically finds a slow-rising, low-volume pulse and an ejection systolic murmur loudest at the right upper sternal border, radiating to the carotid arteries.

Diagnosis and treatment

Echocardiography grades severity by valve area, peak jet velocity and mean pressure gradient. No medication reverses the narrowing; definitive treatment is aortic valve replacement, either surgical (SAVR) or transcatheter (TAVR), recommended once symptoms or left ventricular dysfunction develop.
