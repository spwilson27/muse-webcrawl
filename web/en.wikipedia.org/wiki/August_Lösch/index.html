August Lösch (October 15, 1906 – May 30, 1945) was a German economist, known as one of the founders of regional science and a pioneer of location theory. His main work, Die räumliche Ordnung der Wirtschaft ("The Economics of Location", 1940; English translation 1954), generalised central place theory into a comprehensive theory of market areas and regional systems.

Lösch's system of overlapping hexagonal market areas.

Life

Lösch studied economics at Freiburg, Kiel and Bonn, taking his doctorate under Walter Eucken and Joseph Schumpeter's influence. A Rockefeller fellowship took him to the United States in 1934–36, where he studied industrial location. From 1940 he worked at the Kiel Institute for the World Economy. His open opposition to Nazi economic planning — he insisted location decisions should follow economic rather than political criteria — blocked his academic advancement. He died of scarlet fever in May 1945, days after the end of the Second World War in Europe, aged 38.

Work

Where Johann Heinrich von Thünen had modelled agricultural location and Alfred Weber industrial least-cost location, and Walter Christaller had described hierarchies of settlements, Lösch built a general-equilibrium theory from the demand side: firms on a uniform plain generate hexagonal market areas, and the superposition of nets for different goods, rotated about a common metropolis, produces a rich spatial order of city-rich and city-poor sectors. His concepts of demand cones and spatial monopolistic competition anticipated later work in economic geography and the new economic geography of the 1990s.
