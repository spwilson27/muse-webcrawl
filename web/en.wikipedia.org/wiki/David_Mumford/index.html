David Bryant Mumford (born 11 June 1937) is an American mathematician known for distinguished work in algebraic geometry, and then for research into vision and pattern theory. He won the Fields Medal and was a MacArthur Fellow. In 2010 he was awarded the National Medal of Science. He is currently a University Professor Emeritus in the Division of Applied Mathematics at Brown University.

Early life and education

Mumford was born in Worth, West Sussex in England, of an English father and American mother. He grew up in the United States, and attended Phillips Exeter Academy, where he received a Westinghouse Science Talent Search prize. He then went to Harvard University, where he studied under Oscar Zariski, receiving his PhD in 1961 with a thesis entitled Existence of the moduli scheme for curves of any genus.

Work in algebraic geometry

Mumford's work in geometry combined traditional geometric insights with the latest algebraic techniques. He published on moduli spaces, on surfaces, and on theta functions. He was awarded the Fields Medal in 1974 at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Vancouver, largely for his work on moduli spaces and algebraic surfaces.

Work on vision and pattern theory

In the late 1980s Mumford shifted his research focus toward computer vision and pattern theory, collaborating with researchers in applied mathematics and neuroscience. With Jayant Shah he proposed the Mumford-Shah functional, a variational model for image segmentation that became highly influential.
