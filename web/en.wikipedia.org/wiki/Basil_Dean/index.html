Basil Dean

Basil Herbert Dean CBE (26 September 1888 – 22 April 1978) was an English theatre producer, director, and impresario who also played a major role in British cinema and in wartime entertainment for the armed forces.

Theatre career

Dean began as an actor before turning to management and production. He was associated with the Liverpool Repertory Theatre and later became prominent in London's West End, producing successful plays and musicals and working with leading playwrights and performers of the interwar years.

Film

In the late 1920s Dean moved into film production and directed a number of features. At Ealing Studios he produced films starring performers such as Gracie Fields and George Formby, which proved highly popular with British audiences during the 1930s.

ENSA

At the outbreak of the Second World War, Dean founded the Entertainments National Service Association (ENSA), which organized concerts and theatrical performances for British service personnel at home and overseas. For this work he was appointed CBE. ENSA's shows — sometimes jokingly said by troops to stand for "Every Night Something Awful" — nonetheless reached millions of servicemen and women.

Later life

After the war Dean continued in theatre management and wrote his memoirs. He died in London in 1978 at the age of 89.
