Geometrization conjecture

Thurston's geometrization conjecture (now a theorem) states that each of certain three-dimensional topological spaces has a certain nice geometric structure associated with it. It is an analogue of the uniformization theorem for two-dimensional surfaces.

Geometrization conjecture
Field: Geometric topology
Proposed: William Thurston, 1982
Proved: Grigori Perelman, 2003

Statement

Proposed by William Thurston in 1982, the conjecture says every closed orientable prime 3-manifold can be cut along tori into pieces, each of which admits one of eight model geometries: Euclidean, spherical, hyperbolic, S²×R, H²×R, the universal cover of SL(2,R), Nil and Sol.

Proof

Richard Hamilton developed Ricci flow as an approach; Grigori Perelman completed the proof in papers posted in 2002–2003, introducing Ricci flow with surgery. The result implies the Poincaré conjecture — a closed simply connected 3-manifold is a 3-sphere — which Perelman thereby also proved. Perelman declined the Fields Medal (2006) and the Clay Millennium Prize (2010). Verification was completed by several teams by 2006.
