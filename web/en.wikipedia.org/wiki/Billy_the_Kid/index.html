Billy the Kid

BornHenry McCarty, c. 1859, New York City, U.S.
DiedJuly 14, 1881, Fort Sumner, New Mexico Territory
Other namesWilliam H. Bonney, Henry Antrim
OccupationOutlaw, gunfighter, ranch hand
Known forLincoln County War; killed by Sheriff Pat Garrett

Billy the Kid (born Henry McCarty; c. 1859 – July 14, 1881), also known by the alias William H. Bonney, was an American outlaw and gunfighter of the Old West who became one of its most enduring legends. He participated in the Lincoln County War in New Mexico and was credited in folklore with killing 21 men — one for each year of his life — though historians believe the true figure was far lower, with Billy personally killing perhaps four men and participating in the deaths of several others.

Early life

Henry McCarty was born to Irish immigrant parents, most likely in New York City, around 1859. After his father's death, his mother Catherine moved the family west, eventually settling in Silver City, New Mexico, where she died of tuberculosis in 1874. Orphaned at 14, McCarty drifted into petty crime — his first arrest was for stealing food — and fled to Arizona after escaping jail. There he killed a blacksmith, Francis "Windy" Cahill, in 1877, in what he claimed was self-defence, and returned to New Mexico under the name William H. Bonney.

Lincoln County War

In New Mexico, Bonney joined the Regulators, a deputised posse fighting for rancher John Tunstall in the Lincoln County War (1878), a violent feud between rival merchant factions. After Tunstall's murder, the Regulators killed Sheriff William Brady and others, and Billy became a wanted man. Following the war he turned to cattle rustling. Governor Lew Wallace offered him amnesty in exchange for testimony, but the deal collapsed and Billy was convicted of Sheriff Brady's murder in 1881. He escaped from the Lincoln courthouse on April 28, 1881, killing two deputies in the process.

Death

Sheriff Pat Garrett tracked Billy to Fort Sumner and shot him dead on the night of July 14, 1881, in the darkened bedroom of Pete Maxwell. He was buried in the old Fort Sumner cemetery. Garrett's biography, published soon after, helped turn the Kid into a frontier legend; despite later claims by impostors such as "Brushy Bill" Roberts, historians accept that Garrett killed him in 1881.

Legacy

Billy the Kid has been portrayed in dozens of films, novels, songs, and Aaron Copland's 1938 ballet Billy the Kid. Only one photograph of him — a ferrotype taken around 1880 — is widely authenticated; it sold at auction in 2011 for $2.3 million.
