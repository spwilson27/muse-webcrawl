Sapindaceae
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Sapindales
Genera: ~140
Species: ~1,900
Common name: Soapberry family

The Sapindaceae, known as the soapberry family, is a family of flowering plants in the order Sapindales. It comprises about 140 genera and roughly 1,900 species of trees, shrubs, and lianas distributed worldwide, though mostly in tropical and subtropical regions.

Description

Members are typically trees or climbing plants with alternate, usually pinnately compound leaves and small, often unisexual flowers borne in panicles. Fruits take many forms — capsules, berries, drupes, samaras, and schizocarps. In the APG classification the family was expanded to include the former families Aceraceae (maples) and Hippocastanaceae (horse chestnuts), making maples (Acer) its largest temperate genus.

Economic importance

The family includes many valuable fruit trees: lychee, rambutan, longan, ackee, and guaraná. Soapberries (Sapindus) yield saponin-rich fruits used as a natural detergent, giving the family its name. Other important members include the sugar maple, source of maple syrup; the horse chestnut; golden rain trees and other ornamentals; and timber species.
