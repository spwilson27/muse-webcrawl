Zero-energy building

A net zero energy building (ZEB), also known as a zero net energy (ZNE) building, is a building with net zero energy consumption, meaning the total amount of energy used by the building on an annual basis is equal to the amount of renewable energy created on the site or supplied from off-site renewable sources.

Approach

Designs first minimize demand through passive measures — insulation, airtightness, heat-recovery ventilation, passive solar orientation — then meet the small remaining load with on-site generation such as rooftop photovoltaics, solar thermal or heat pumps. Definitions vary: some count only site energy, others source energy, energy costs, or carbon emissions, and grid-connected buildings typically export surplus in summer and import in winter.

Policy

The European Union's Energy Performance of Buildings Directive required all new buildings to be "nearly zero-energy" from 2021, and similar targets exist in California and elsewhere. Related standards include the German Passivhaus standard and the Living Building Challenge's net positive requirements.
