Philip V of Spain


Philip V (Spanish: Felipe V; 19 December 1683 – 9 July 1746) was King of Spain from 1700 to 1724 and again from 1724 to 1746. Founder of the Spanish Bourbon dynasty.

Philip V (19 December 1683 – 9 July 1746), born Philippe of France, Duke of Anjou, was the grandson of Louis XIV of France and the first Bourbon king of Spain. His accession on the death of the childless Charles II of Spain in 1700 triggered the War of the Spanish Succession (1701–1714).

Accession and the War of the Spanish Succession

Charles II bequeathed his entire inheritance to the Duke of Anjou, hoping that French support would preserve the Spanish Empire undivided. The prospect of a union of the French and Spanish crowns alarmed England, the Dutch Republic and the Austrian Habsburgs, who formed the Grand Alliance and backed the Habsburg Archduke Charles. The war ended with the Treaty of Utrecht (1713) and the Treaty of Rastatt (1714): Philip was recognised as king of Spain and the Spanish Indies, but renounced his place in the French succession, and Spain ceded Gibraltar and Minorca to Britain and its Italian and Flemish possessions to Austria and Savoy.

Reign

Philip modelled his government on the centralised administration of France. Through the Nueva Planta decrees (1707–1716) he abolished the traditional fueros of the kingdoms of the Crown of Aragon, bringing Spain closer to a unitary state. He founded institutions such as the Royal Spanish Academy (1713) and the Royal Library.

In foreign policy Philip, influenced first by his second wife Elisabeth Farnese and ministers such as Giulio Alberoni and José Patiño, sought to recover Spanish influence in Italy. Spanish forces temporarily occupied Sardinia and Sicily before the War of the Quadruple Alliance (1718–1720) forced a retreat; later, in the War of the Polish Succession, Spain recovered Naples and Sicily for Philip's son Charles (1734).

Abdication and death

Suffering from melancholy, Philip abdicated in January 1724 in favour of his son Louis I, but resumed the throne seven months later on Louis's death. He died in Madrid on 9 July 1746 and was succeeded by his son Ferdinand VI.
