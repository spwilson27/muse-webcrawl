Khazraj

The Banu Khazraj (Arabic: بنو خزرج) were one of the two main Arab tribes of Yathrib (later Medina) in the Hejaz, the other being the Banu Aws. Together the Aws and Khazraj were known in Islamic tradition as the Ansar ("the Helpers") after they welcomed and supported the Prophet Muhammad following his migration (Hijra) from Mecca in 622 CE. The Khazraj are entirely distinct from the Khazars, the Turkic people of the Eurasian steppe.

Origins

According to Arab genealogical tradition, the Khazraj and Aws descended from the South Arabian (Qahtanite) tribe of Azd, which migrated north from Yemen and settled in Yathrib several centuries before Islam. There they became date-palm cultivators and came into conflict and alliance with the oasis's Jewish tribes, including the Banu Qaynuqa, Banu Nadir and Banu Qurayza.

Role in early Islam

Long feuding between the Aws and Khazraj, culminating in the Battle of Bu'ath (c. 617), led both tribes to seek arbitration. Khazraj representatives met Muhammad at Mecca and made the pledges of al-Aqaba (621–622), inviting him to Medina as arbiter. The Khazraj supplied many of the Ansar who fought at Badr, Uhud and the Trench. Their chief Sa'd ibn Ubadah was a leading candidate for the caliphate at the Saqifa meeting after Muhammad's death in 632, but the Muhajirun candidate Abu Bakr prevailed.

One notable Khazraji, Abdullah ibn Ubayy, is remembered in Islamic sources as the leader of the munafiqun ("hypocrites") who nominally accepted Islam while undermining Muhammad. The poet Hassan ibn Thabit, Muhammad's court poet, was also of the Khazraj.
