Boll weevil

Anthonomus grandis
Family: Curculionidae (true weevils)
Order: Coleoptera
Native: Central Mexico

The boll weevil (Anthonomus grandis) is a beetle that feeds on cotton buds and flowers. Thought to be native to central Mexico, it spread to the United States in the 1890s and by the 1920s had infested all US cotton-growing areas, devastating the industry and Southern agriculture.

Life cycle

Adults, about 6 mm long with a long snout, lay eggs inside cotton bolls and buds; larvae develop inside, pupate, and emerge in 7–14 days. Adults overwinter in leaf litter.

Impact and control

The pest drove diversification of Southern farming — famously celebrated by the Boll Weevil Monument (1919) in Enterprise, Alabama. The Boll Weevil Eradication Program, using pheromone traps and targeted insecticide, largely eliminated it from US cotton by the early 2000s, though it remains a pest in South America.
