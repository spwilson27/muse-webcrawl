Protractor

A protractor is a measuring instrument, typically made of transparent plastic or glass, for measuring angles. Most protractors measure angles in degrees (°). Some are semicircular, covering 180°, while others are full circles of 360°.

Types

  • Semicircular protractors — the most common type, found in school geometry sets, usually with two opposed degree scales so angles can be read from either side.
  • Circular protractors — full 360° instruments used in drafting and navigation.
  • Bevel protractors — precision metal instruments with a vernier scale used in engineering and machining to measure angles to a fraction of a degree.
  • Navigation protractors — transparent plotting tools, such as the Douglas protractor, used on charts for course plotting.

Use

To measure an angle, the protractor's centre point is placed on the vertex of the angle and its baseline aligned with one side; the degree marking where the other side crosses the scale gives the angle's size. Protractors are used in mathematics education, geometry, technical drawing, carpentry, surveying and navigation.

History

Angle-measuring instruments descend from ancient devices such as the astrolabe. The term "protractor" was first used by Thomas Blundeville in his Exercises (1594), describing an instrument for laying down angles on paper. The familiar semicircular school protractor became widespread with mass-produced plastic geometry sets in the 20th century.
