Gouda (Dutch: Goudse kaas, "cheese from Gouda") is a sweet, creamy, yellow cow's-milk cheese originating from the Netherlands. It is named after the city of Gouda in South Holland — historically the market where the cheese was traded rather than where it was made — and it is one of the most popular cheeses in the world, accounting for roughly half or more of Dutch cheese production.

History

Cheese-making in the low countries around Gouda is documented from the twelfth century, and the name "Gouda" for the cheese appears in records by the late middle ages; the city's cheese market and weigh house (the Gouda Waag of 1668) regulated a trade that made the cheese famous across Europe. Gouda was traditionally made on farms throughout the western Netherlands during the summer milk season and brought to market in Gouda. Industrial production from the late nineteenth century spread "Gouda-style" cheese worldwide; because the name alone is not protected internationally, Gouda is now made in many countries, though "Gouda Holland" and the farmhouse cheese Boerenkaas carry EU protection.

Production

Gouda is a washed-curd cheese: after the curd is cut and part of the whey is drawn off, warm water is added to wash the curds, reducing lactose and yielding Gouda's characteristic mild sweetness (fewer sour notes develop during aging). The curd is pressed in round moulds, giving the cheese its traditional wheel shape, brined, and ripened. Wheels are usually coated with wax or plastic during aging — yellow or red for most Gouda, black for some aged versions.

Age categories and flavour

Dutch Gouda is sold by maturity, developing from mild and elastic to firm, crystalline, and caramel-sweet:

  • Jonge kaas (young) — aged about 4 weeks; soft, mild, buttery.
  • Jong belegen — 8–10 weeks; firmer, gently nutty.
  • Belegen — 16–18 weeks; pronounced flavour, smooth texture.
  • Extra belegen — 7–8 months; deeper, spicier.
  • Oude kaas (old) — 10–12 months or more; hard, dark golden, with crunchy tyrosine crystals and butterscotch/toffee notes.

Young Gouda pairs with lager and light wines; aged Gouda with dark beer, whisky, or full-bodied reds. Flavoured Goudas with cumin, mustard seed, herbs, or fenugreek are also traditional. Smoked Gouda, popular in North America, is a processed variant typically made from young cheese.

Cultural status

The Gouda cheese market — porters in guild colours carrying wheels on barrows to be weighed and haggled over — is re-enacted on the market square in front of the Waag on Thursday mornings in season, one of the Netherlands' signature tourist spectacles alongside the markets of Alkmaar and nearby Edam.
