Hotchkiss (automobile)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Hotchkiss luxury saloon of the 1930s

Hotchkiss was a French automobile manufacturer, a division of the armaments firm Hotchkiss et Cie founded by American gunsmith Benjamin B. Hotchkiss. Based at Saint-Denis near Paris, the company built cars from 1903 to 1955 and was noted for solidly engineered, conservative luxury vehicles favoured by the French professional classes.

History

Hotchkiss et Cie, famous for its machine guns and cannon, began building car engines for other makers before introducing complete cars in 1903, adopting a crossed-cannon-style badge in a circle that became one of the marque's identifiers. During the 1920s and 1930s the company produced successful models such as the AM and the 680 series, competed creditably in rallies — winning the Monte Carlo Rally in 1932, 1933, 1934, 1939, 1949, and 1950 — and experimented with front-wheel drive in the post-war Grégoire-based Hotchkiss-Grégoire. During the Second World War and after, it built the Hotchkiss-Brandt and the French Army's Hotchkiss M201 jeep under licence.

Punitive post-war taxation on large cars destroyed the market for French luxury marques. Hotchkiss merged with Delahaye in 1954, stopped making cars in 1955, and continued with trucks and military vehicles before passing through mergers into Brandt and eventually Thomson; the name disappeared from vehicle production by the late 1960s.
