Creech Air Force Base is a United States Air Force command and control facility in Clark County, Nevada, near the community of Indian Springs, about 35 miles (56 km) northwest of Las Vegas. It is the home of the 432nd Wing, which operates remotely piloted aircraft — principally the MQ-9 Reaper and formerly the MQ-1 Predator — supporting combat operations around the world through satellite links.

History

The airfield was established in 1942 as Indian Springs Army Airfield, a training field for aerial gunnery and later a support base for nuclear weapons tests at the Nevada Test Site. In 2005 it was renamed for General Wilbur L. "Bill" Creech, a former commander of Tactical Air Command. From the mid-2000s the base became the hub of the Air Force's rapidly expanding unmanned aircraft operations, with aircrew flying combat air patrols in theatres such as Afghanistan and Iraq from ground control stations in Nevada.

Units and role

The 432nd Wing and its expeditionary counterpart, the 432nd Air Expeditionary Wing, include operations, maintenance and mission-support groups and squadrons flying the MQ-9; the 556th Test and Evaluation Squadron and elements of the Nevada Air National Guard's 232nd Operations Squadron also work with remotely piloted aircraft there. The base also supports the USAF Thunderbirds' winter training and lies adjacent to the Nevada National Security Site and the Nevada Test and Training Range.

Protests and incidents

Creech has been a regular site of anti-drone protests by peace activists objecting to targeted killings conducted by remotely piloted aircraft, with periodic arrests of demonstrators at its gates.
