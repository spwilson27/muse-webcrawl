Jack St. Clair Kilby (November 8, 1923 – June 20, 2005) was an American electrical engineer who took part (along with Robert Noyce of Fairchild Semiconductor) in the realisation of the first integrated circuit while working at Texas Instruments (TI) in 1958. He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics on October 10, 2000. Kilby was also the co-inventor of the handheld calculator and the thermal printer.

Early life and education

Kilby was born in Jefferson City, Missouri, and grew up in Great Bend, Kansas, where his father ran a small electric utility company. His interest in electronics was sparked by amateur radio during a Kansas ice storm in 1938, when his father used radio to communicate with customers. He served in the U.S. Army during the Second World War, working on radio equipment in Burma with the Office of Strategic Services. He earned a bachelor's degree in electrical engineering from the University of Illinois in 1947 and a master's degree from the University of Wisconsin in 1950 while working at Centralab in Milwaukee.

The integrated circuit

Kilby joined Texas Instruments in Dallas in 1958. Because he was a new employee, he had not yet earned vacation time and worked alone in the lab during the summer shutdown. Reflecting on the "tyranny of numbers" problem — the escalating difficulty of wiring together ever more discrete components — he reasoned that all circuit elements could be made from a single block of semiconductor material. On September 12, 1958, he demonstrated the first working integrated circuit: a phase-shift oscillator made from a slice of germanium with gold wire connections. A patent application was filed in February 1959. Robert Noyce independently developed a practical version using silicon and planar processing at Fairchild a few months later, and the two men are jointly credited as inventors of the integrated circuit.

Later work

With Jerry Merryman and James Van Tassel, Kilby co-invented the first handheld electronic calculator, the "Cal Tech" prototype of 1967, and he led the TI team that developed the thermal printer. He held more than 60 patents. From 1978 to 1984 he was Distinguished Professor of Electrical Engineering at Texas A&M University. He received the National Medal of Science in 1969 and the IEEE Medal of Honor in 1986. In 2000 he shared one half of the Nobel Prize in Physics "for his part in the invention of the integrated circuit."
