Alcools is a collection of poems by the French poet Guillaume Apollinaire, published in 1913. Gathering work written between 1898 and 1913, it is regarded as one of the foundational works of modern French poetry and a landmark of literary modernism.

Contents and style

The collection opens with "Zone", a sweeping free-verse meditation on Paris and modern life, in which Apollinaire famously suppressed all punctuation — a feature he extended throughout the volume. Other celebrated poems include "Le Pont Mirabeau", with its refrain on the Seine flowing beneath the bridge, and "La Chanson du mal-aimé", a long elegy of unhappy love set in London and Paris. The poems range through lyric, narrative and experimental modes, mixing archaic and contemporary imagery, religious motifs and the shocks of the modern city.

Influence

Alcools helped define the avant-garde in the years before the First World War and influenced the surrealists, who admired Apollinaire as a precursor. Its portrait frontispiece by Pablo Picasso underscored Apollinaire's ties to the cubist painters whose work he championed as a critic.
