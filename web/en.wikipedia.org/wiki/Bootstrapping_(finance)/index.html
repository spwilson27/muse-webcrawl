Bootstrapping (finance)

Bootstrapping in finance is a method for constructing a fixed-income yield curve from the prices of a set of coupon-bearing products, e.g. bonds and swaps, by solving for zero-coupon rates sequentially.

Method

Starting with the shortest maturity instrument, its yield gives the first zero rate. Longer instruments' cash flows are discounted using already-derived rates for earlier dates, leaving one unknown rate to solve at each step, progressing up the curve.

Applications

The resulting zero curve is used to price interest-rate derivatives, value bonds, and derive forward rates. Modern practice often uses simultaneous curve fitting with swaps (OIS and LIBOR/SOFR curves).

General bootstrapping in business

The term also describes building a business without external capital, but this article's financial sense is the technical yield-curve method.
