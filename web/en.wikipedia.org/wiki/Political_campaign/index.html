Political campaign

A political campaign is an organised effort which seeks to influence the decision-making progress within a specific group. In democracies, political campaigns often refer to electoral campaigns, by which representatives are chosen or referendums are decided. A campaign typically involves a candidate, a campaign team, a message, fundraising, advertising and voter mobilisation efforts.

Elements

Modern campaigns are built around a message that summarises why the candidate should be elected, repeated through speeches, debates, advertisements and social media. Campaign organisations include managers, pollsters, media consultants, field organisers and volunteers who canvass voters door-to-door, run phone banks and organise get-out-the-vote operations on election day. Fundraising is often decisive: money buys advertising, polling and staff, and is regulated to varying degrees by campaign finance law.

Techniques and ethics

Campaigns employ positive messaging about their own candidate and negative campaigning against opponents. Microtargeting uses data to deliver tailored messages to small groups of voters. Critics raise concerns about misinformation, attack advertising, opaque funding and the "permanent campaign", in which governing becomes subordinate to continuous electioneering.
