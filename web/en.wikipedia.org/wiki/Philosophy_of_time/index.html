Philosophy of time

Philosophy of time, or philosophy of space and time, is the branch of philosophy concerned with the nature of time: whether it is real or an aspect of a more fundamental timeless reality, whether past and future exist as the present does, whether time flows, and how change and persistence are possible.

Major positions

J. M. E. McTaggart's 1908 paper "The Unreality of Time" distinguished the A-series (events as past, present, or future) from the B-series (events as earlier or later than one another) and argued that time is unreal. Contemporary debate divides between A-theorists, who take temporal passage and the distinction of past, present, and future to be fundamental — subdivided into presentists (only the present exists) and growing-block theorists — and B-theorists, who hold that all times exist equally (eternalism) and passage is perspectival, a view often seen as supported by special relativity's rejection of absolute simultaneity.

Other questions

Related topics include the direction or "arrow" of time and its relation to entropy; whether time had a beginning; time travel and its paradoxes; the endurance-versus-perdurance debate about persistence; and the phenomenology of temporal experience. Philosophers from Aristotle and Augustine ("What then is time? If no one asks me, I know; if I wish to explain it, I do not know") to Kant, Bergson, and Heidegger have placed time at the centre of their systems.
