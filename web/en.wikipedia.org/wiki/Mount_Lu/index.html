Mount Lu

Mount Lu (Chinese: 庐山; pinyin: Lúshān), also known as Lushan, is a mountain in Jiangxi Province, China, situated south of the Yangtze River near Jiujiang and Poyang Lake. Its highest peak, Dahanyang, reaches 1,474 metres. Renowned for its scenery, mists, and cultural heritage, it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of China's most famous mountains.

Culture and history

Mount Lu has been celebrated in Chinese poetry for over a thousand years; Li Bai wrote of its waterfall, and Su Shi composed a famous poem about the impossibility of seeing the mountain's "true face" from within it. The White Deer Grotto Academy on its slopes was one of the most important Confucian academies, revived by the philosopher Zhu Xi in the Song dynasty. The Pure Land school of Buddhism traces its origins to the Donglin Temple founded by Huiyuan at the mountain's foot in 402 CE.

Modern era

The hill station of Guling (Kuling), developed in the late 19th century by foreign missionaries and merchants, made Lushan a summer resort. In the 20th century it hosted major Chinese Communist Party conferences, including the 1959 Lushan Conference, at which Peng Dehuai was purged after criticizing the Great Leap Forward. Lushan National Park was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996 as a cultural landscape.
