Overview

Northern California (commonly shortened to NorCal) is the northern portion of the US state of California. It is generally taken to include the state's northern 48 counties, encompassing the San Francisco Bay Area, the Sacramento metropolitan area, the San Joaquin Valley's northern reaches, the Sierra Nevada, and the redwood coast. Its largest cities include San Jose, San Francisco, Sacramento, Oakland, and Fresno (the last sometimes treated as part of Central California).

Geography

The region contains some of California's most varied landscapes: the coast ranges and old-growth redwood forests of the North Coast, the fertile Sacramento Valley, the granite peaks of Yosemite and Lake Tahoe in the Sierra Nevada, and the volcanic Cascade peaks of Mount Shasta and Lassen Peak. The San Francisco Bay and the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta are defining hydrographic features.

Economy and culture

Northern California is a global centre of technology through Silicon Valley, a major wine-producing region in the Napa and Sonoma valleys, and the seat of state government at Sacramento. It is often contrasted culturally, politically, and climatically with Southern California, and the two regions are divided roughly at the Tehachapi Mountains and the Monterey–San Luis Obispo county line.
