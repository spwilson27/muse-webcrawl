Reichskommissariat Ostland (RKO) was the civilian occupation regime established by Nazi Germany in 1941 in the Baltic states (Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania) and the western part of the Byelorussian SSR during the Second World War. It was one of several Reichskommissariate planned for the conquered eastern territories and was administered by Reichskommissar Hinrich Lohse from its seat in Riga.

Administration

The territory was divided into four Generalbezirke (general districts): Estland, Lettland, Litauen and Weißruthenien, each under a Generalkommissar. The regime was formally dissolved as the Red Army reconquered the region in 1944–1945.

Occupation and crimes

Under the occupation the Jewish population of the Baltic states was almost entirely murdered in the Holocaust, with mass shootings carried out by Einsatzgruppen, police units and local auxiliaries, including the massacres at Ponary and Rumbula. German policy envisaged the eventual Germanisation and colonisation of the region in line with the Generalplan Ost. Partisan warfare intensified as the war turned against Germany, and by early 1945 the territory had been fully retaken by the Soviet Union.
