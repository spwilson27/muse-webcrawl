Congregational church

Congregational churches are Protestant churches practising congregationalist polity, in which each local congregation is autonomous and self-governing, affirming the priesthood of all believers and Christ as the sole head of the church. The tradition arises from the English Reformation and Puritanism.

History

Congregationalism emerged among English Puritans and Separatists such as the Pilgrims who founded Plymouth Colony in 1620. It became the established church of much of colonial New England and was influential in founding colleges such as Harvard and Yale. Key statements include the Savoy Declaration (1658) and the Cambridge Platform (1648).

Modern bodies

In the United States, many Congregational churches merged into the United Church of Christ (1957); others continue in the National Association of Congregational Christian Churches and the Conservative Congregational Christian Conference. In England and Wales, most joined the United Reformed Church (1972), with a continuing Congregational Federation.
