Jean Armour (27 February 1765 – 26 March 1834), also known as the "Belle of Mauchline", was the wife of Scottish poet Robert Burns. She inspired many of his poems and songs and, after his death, became the guardian of his manuscripts and reputation.

Life

Born in Mauchline, Ayrshire, the daughter of a master mason, Jean met Burns in 1785. Their relationship was stormy: her father disapproved and obtained a warrant against Burns, who briefly planned to emigrate to Jamaica. The couple had twins in 1786 and were eventually married in 1788, settling first at Ellisland Farm and then in Dumfries, where Burns worked as an exciseman.

Of their nine children, only three survived to adulthood. Jean outlived her husband by nearly 38 years, dying in Dumfries in 1834. She is buried beside Burns in the Burns Mausoleum at St Michael's Churchyard, Dumfries.

Legacy

Jean is the addressee of some of Burns's best-known lyrics, including "Of a' the airts the wind can blaw". Her preservation of his papers was crucial to the establishment of his posthumous fame.
