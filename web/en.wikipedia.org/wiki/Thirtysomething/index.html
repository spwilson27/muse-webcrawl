Thirtysomething is an American television drama series created by Edward Zwick and Marshall Herskovitz for ABC, running from 1987 to 1991. Set in Philadelphia, it followed a circle of baby-boomer friends — centred on advertising partners Michael Steadman (Ken Olin) and Elliot Weston (Timothy Busfield) and their families — as they negotiated careers, marriages, parenthood, and ageing parents.

Reception and influence

The series was praised for its realistic, dialogue-driven treatment of everyday adult life and won 13 Emmy Awards, including Outstanding Drama Series in 1988, along with two Golden Globes. The title entered the language as shorthand for the anxieties and self-absorption of affluent young professionals, and the show is frequently credited with reshaping American television drama toward character and relationships over plot. A sequel pilot, thirtysomething(else), centring on the original characters' children, was filmed for ABC in 2020 but not picked up.
