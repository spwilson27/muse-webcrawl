Multistage sampling

Multistage sampling is a sampling method in statistics in which selection takes place in successive stages, with smaller and smaller sampling units chosen at each stage. Large units, called primary sampling units, are sampled first; then smaller units are sampled within those, and so on.

Example

In a national household survey, researchers might first randomly select a sample of districts, then select villages or city blocks within those districts, then select households within the blocks, and finally interview individuals within the households. At each stage the selection may use simple random sampling, stratification or probability proportional to size.

Advantages and disadvantages

Multistage sampling greatly reduces the cost and logistical burden of surveying a widely dispersed population, since fieldwork is concentrated in selected areas. However, because sampled units within a cluster tend to resemble one another, the design is usually less statistically precise than a simple random sample of the same size; this is measured by the design effect.
