Archive of Our Own (AO3) is a nonprofit, open-source repository for fan fiction and other fanworks, run by the Organization for Transformative Works (OTW). Launched in beta in November 2009, it was created by fans — in reaction to the commercialisation and content-striking policies of services such as FanFiction.Net and LiveJournal (notably the 2007 "Strikethrough" incident) — to be an archive owned by fandom itself, committed to maximal inclusiveness of transformative works within the law.

Features

AO3 is distinguished by its folksonomic tagging system: users tag works freely, and volunteer "tag wranglers" link synonyms into a canonical, filterable taxonomy of fandoms, characters, relationships, ratings, warnings, and tropes. Works can be downloaded in EPUB, MOBI, PDF, and HTML; creators may post pseudonymously or orphan works to the archive. Its content policy permits adult material with mandatory ratings and archive warnings (such as "Major Character Death" and "Creator Chose Not To Use Archive Warnings"). The site's title alludes to Virginia Woolf's essay A Room of One's Own.

Scale and recognition

The archive passed one million works in 2014, five million in 2019, and continued to grow to well over thirteen million works in more than sixty thousand fandoms, with millions of registered users. In 2019 the site won the Hugo Award for Best Related Work, an honour shared, in the OTW's acceptance, with every fan creator on the platform. AO3 has weathered significant operational challenges, including a large distributed denial-of-service attack in July 2023 attributed to a hacktivist group, after which it strengthened its infrastructure and volunteer security practices.

Governance

The OTW funds AO3 entirely through donations, keeps the code (the "otwarchive" project) open source, and supports related projects including the Fanlore wiki and the academic journal Transformative Works and Cultures. Its legal committee advocates for the legality of fanworks as fair use.
