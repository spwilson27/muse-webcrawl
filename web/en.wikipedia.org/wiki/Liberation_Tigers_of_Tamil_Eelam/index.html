The Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE; Tamil: தமிழீழ விடுதலைப் புலிகள்), commonly known as the Tamil Tigers, was a militant separatist organisation in northern and eastern Sri Lanka that fought to create an independent Tamil state, Tamil Eelam. Founded in 1976 by Velupillai Prabhakaran, it waged a civil war against the Sri Lankan state for more than three decades until its military defeat in May 2009.

Origins and war

The LTTE emerged from Tamil grievances over discrimination by the Sinhalese-majority governments after independence, including the Sinhala Only Act and ethnic riots culminating in the 1983 "Black July" pogrom, which triggered full-scale civil war. The Tigers eliminated rival Tamil groups and built a conventional army, a navy (the Sea Tigers), an embryonic air wing, and a feared suicide-bombing unit, the Black Tigers. They pioneered the suicide vest as a weapon and assassinated numerous figures, including former Indian prime minister Rajiv Gandhi (1991) and Sri Lankan president Ranasinghe Premadasa (1993). At their height they administered a de facto state with courts, police, and a bank.

Defeat

A Norwegian-brokered ceasefire (2002) collapsed into renewed fighting in 2006. The Sri Lankan military's final offensive overran the LTTE's strongholds in the north; Prabhakaran was killed in May 2009 and the government declared victory. The end of the war was accompanied by mass civilian casualties in the Vanni, which remain the subject of international controversy and calls for accountability. The LTTE was designated a terrorist organisation by more than 30 countries, including India, the United States, and the European Union.
