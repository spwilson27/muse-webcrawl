Ali-Shir Nava'i

Born9 February 1441, Herat
Died3 January 1501, Herat
OccupationPoet, statesman, linguist, painter
LanguageChagatai Turkic, Persian

Ali-Shir Nava'i (1441–1501), also known as Nizām ad-Dīn ʿAlī Shīr Herawī, was a Timurid poet, writer, statesman and linguist. He is regarded as the greatest representative of Chagatai Turkic literature and a national hero in Uzbekistan.

Life and works

Nava'i served at the court of Sultan Husayn Bayqara in Herat, where he was a close friend and adviser, and a generous patron of scholars and artists. His works include the epic cycle Khamsa, modelled on Nizami's, and the Muhakamat al-Lughatayn ("The Judgment of the Two Languages"), which argued for the literary merits of Turkic over Persian.

Legacy

The city of Navoi in Uzbekistan and numerous institutions bear his name. His poetry remains central to the literary heritage of Central Asia.
