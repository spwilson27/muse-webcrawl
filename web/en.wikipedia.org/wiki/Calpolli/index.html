Calpolli

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A calpolli (from Nahuatl calpōlli, "large house") was the fundamental social and territorial unit of pre-Hispanic Aztec (Mexica) society. Calpolli organised families into corporate groups that held land in common, worshipped together, and supplied labour and soldiers to the state.

Organisation

Each calpolli held an allocation of arable land (calpolalli) that was divided among member families; rights to plots were maintained by cultivation and could revert to the group. The calpolli ran its own school (telpochcalli) for commoner youths, maintained a temple, elected its own headmen and officials, and delivered tribute and labour drafts as a unit. A city (altepetl) such as Tenochtitlan was composed of many calpolli grouped into larger quarters — Tenochtitlan's four great quarters contained dozens of them.

Debates

Scholars have debated whether calpolli were primarily kin-based clans or territorial wards. Early accounts emphasised clan organisation, while later research suggests they were largely neighbourhood and occupational groupings whose members were bound by residence, craft, and shared ritual as much as by common descent. Ethnic calpolli of particular crafts or of immigrant groups existed in the great cities.

After the conquest

Under Spanish rule the calpolli survived in altered form as the barrios and parcialidades of colonial towns, retaining communal landholding and internal governance into the colonial period.
