Federated identity

A federated identity in information technology is the means of linking a person's electronic identity and attributes, stored across multiple distinct identity management systems. Federated identity is related to single sign-on (SSO), in which a user's single authentication ticket, or token, is trusted across multiple IT systems or organisations.

How it works

In a federation, an identity provider (IdP) authenticates the user and issues signed assertions about them — for example a name, email address or group memberships. A service provider (SP), or relying party, trusts the IdP's assertions and grants access without holding the user's password. Trust between the organisations is established in advance through exchanged metadata, certificates and agreements. The user thus keeps a single account with their home organisation while accessing services across administrative domains.

Standards

Important standards and protocols include SAML 2.0, widely used in enterprise and academic federations such as the higher-education networks coordinated internationally by eduGAIN; OpenID Connect, built on the OAuth 2.0 authorisation framework and used for "social login" with providers such as Google and Microsoft; and Kerberos-based cross-realm trusts. Federated identity reduces password proliferation and centralises account lifecycle management, but makes the identity provider a critical point of trust and a high-value target for attackers.
