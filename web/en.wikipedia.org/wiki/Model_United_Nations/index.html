Model United Nations

Model United Nations (MUN) is an educational simulation in which students learn about diplomacy, international relations, and the United Nations. Participants — called delegates — are assigned countries or other political actors and committees, research their positions, and debate resolutions following procedures modeled on real UN bodies.

Format

Conferences range from small school events to large multi-day gatherings such as Harvard Model United Nations and the National Model United Nations in New York, which draw thousands of students. Committees may simulate the General Assembly, the Security Council, historical bodies, or crisis scenarios. Debate alternates between formal speeches, moderated caucuses for focused discussion, and unmoderated caucuses for negotiation and drafting.

Outcomes

Committees typically conclude by voting on draft resolutions, and many conferences give awards for outstanding delegates. MUN is credited with developing participants' public speaking, research, negotiation, and writing skills, and it is a popular extracurricular activity at high schools and universities worldwide.

History

The activity grew out of interwar simulations of the League of Nations in the 1920s and 1930s and expanded after the founding of the United Nations in 1945.
