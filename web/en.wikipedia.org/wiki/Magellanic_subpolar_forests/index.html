Magellanic subpolar forests

The Magellanic subpolar forests are a terrestrial ecoregion of southernmost South America, covering the western slopes of the southern Andes and the archipelagos of Patagonian Chile and Argentina around the Strait of Magellan and Tierra del Fuego. It is the world's southernmost forest ecosystem.

Ecology

Cool, hyper-humid and windy, the region receives up to 4,000 mm of rain in the west, grading into drier steppe in the east. Evergreen Magellanic beech (Nothofagus betuloides), Guaitecas cypress and Drimys dominate; extensive peat bogs and cushion-plant moorland occur where drainage is poor.

Conservation

Large areas are protected in reserves such as Cabo de Hornos and Alberto de Agostini national parks. The ecoregion's isolated biota is threatened by introduced beavers, which fell riparian forest on Tierra del Fuego.
