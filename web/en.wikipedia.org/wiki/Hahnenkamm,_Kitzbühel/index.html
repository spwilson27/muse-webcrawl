The Hahnenkamm is a mountain in the Kitzbühel Alps of Tyrol, Austria, rising to 1,712 metres (5,617 ft) immediately above the town of Kitzbühel. It is internationally famous for the Hahnenkamm Races (Hahnenkammrennen), held each January since 1931, the best-known event of which is the downhill race on the Streif course — widely considered the most demanding and dangerous downhill on the FIS Alpine Ski World Cup circuit.

The Streif

The Streif descends about 860 vertical metres over 3.3 kilometres from a starting gate near the summit. Its gradients reach 85 per cent (about 40°) at the Mausefalle ("mousetrap") jump near the top, and racers exceed 130 km/h through sections such as the Steilhang, the Brückenschuss and the final Zielschuss, landing enormous jumps at the Hausbergkante and the finish jump. Winning the Kitzbühel downhill — along with the slalom on the neighbouring Ganslernhang — is among the most prestigious achievements in alpine skiing, and winners' names are inscribed on the gondolas of the Hahnenkamm cable car, which has served the mountain since 1928.

The mountain

Beyond race week, the Hahnenkamm is a popular hiking and skiing area connected by gondola directly from Kitzbühel, with panoramic views over the Wilder Kaiser and the Hohe Tauern. A mountain museum near the summit station and the summer "Streif" hiking trail, which follows the race course past its famous sections, draw visitors year-round.
