Ovens River

Country: Australia (Victoria)

Length: 191 km

Source: Victorian Alps, near Mount Hotham

Mouth: Murray River at Lake Mulwala

Tributaries: Buckland, Buffalo, King, Morses Creek

The Ovens River is a perennial river of north-eastern Victoria, Australia, flowing 191 kilometres from the slopes of the Victorian Alps to join the Murray River at Lake Mulwala near Bundalong. It is a major tributary of the Murray–Darling Basin and one of the few substantial Victorian rivers without a large dam on its main stem.

Course

The river rises below Mount Hotham and the Great Dividing Range near Harrietville, flows north through Bright and Myrtleford in the broad Ovens Valley, and passes Wangaratta, where it is joined by the King River, before reaching the Murray floodplain. Its main tributaries are the Buckland, Buffalo and King rivers and Morses Creek.

History

Explorers Hamilton Hume and William Hovell crossed and named the river in 1824 after John Ovens, secretary to Governor Thomas Brisbane. The valley boomed with the 1850s gold rushes at Beechworth and Bright; dredging for gold around Myrtleford left lasting scars on the floodplain. Red gum (river red gum) forests line its lower reaches.

Ecology and use

The Ovens is regarded as one of Victoria's healthiest river systems, supporting native fish including Murray cod and trout cod, platypus and extensive river red gum floodplain. The valley is an important agricultural and tourism region, producing wine, hops, tobacco (historically), nuts and beef, and serving as the gateway to the Mount Buffalo and Alpine national parks.
