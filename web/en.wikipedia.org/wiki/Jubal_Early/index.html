Jubal Early

Jubal Anderson Early
Born: 3 November 1816, Franklin County, Virginia
Died: 2 March 1894, Lynchburg, Virginia
Allegiance: United States; Confederate States
Rank: Lieutenant general (Confederate)

Jubal Anderson Early (November 3, 1816 – March 2, 1894) was an American lawyer and Confederate general in the American Civil War. A West Point graduate who had fought in the Seminole Wars and the Mexican–American War and served in the Virginia legislature, he opposed secession but joined the Confederacy when Virginia left the Union.

Civil War service

Early fought at First Bull Run and in the Peninsula Campaign, and rose to divisional command in the Army of Northern Virginia, serving at Antietam, Fredericksburg, Chancellorsville and Gettysburg. In 1864 he commanded the Second Corps and led the Army of the Valley on an independent campaign down the Shenandoah Valley, winning at Monocacy and advancing to the outskirts of Washington, D.C., before being defeated by Philip Sheridan at Third Winchester, Fisher's Hill and Cedar Creek. His army was destroyed at Waynesboro in March 1865, after which Robert E. Lee relieved him of command.

Later life

After the war Early fled to Mexico and then Canada before returning to Virginia under amnesty. He became a prominent and unapologetic spokesman for the Confederate cause, helping to shape the "Lost Cause" interpretation of the war, and published a memoir, A Memoir of the Last Year of the War for Independence.
