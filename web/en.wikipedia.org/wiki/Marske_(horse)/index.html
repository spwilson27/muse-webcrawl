Marske (1750 – July 1779) was a British Thoroughbred racehorse and sire, remembered chiefly as the sire of the undefeated and hugely influential racehorse Eclipse.

Background

Marske was a brown stallion bred by John Hutton at Marske Hall in Yorkshire and later acquired by Prince William, Duke of Cumberland, the breeder of Herod. He was sired by Squirt, a grandson of the Darley Arabian, one of the three foundation sires of the Thoroughbred breed.

Racing career

Marske raced modestly successfully in the mid-1750s, winning a small number of races at Newmarket and elsewhere before being retired to stud.

Stud record

Initially standing for a low fee, Marske's reputation was transformed when his son Eclipse proved unbeaten in eighteen races in 1769–1770. Marske's stud fee rose dramatically, and he became one of the leading sires of his era, heading the British sires' list in the 1770s. Through Eclipse, who appears in the pedigree of the overwhelming majority of modern Thoroughbreds, Marske's sire line of the Darley Arabian became dominant worldwide. Marske died in July 1779.
