The Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health is the public health graduate school of Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore, Maryland. Founded in 1916, it was the first independent graduate school of public health in the United States and is consistently ranked among the top such schools in the world.

History

The school was established with funding from the Rockefeller Foundation as the School of Hygiene and Public Health, with William Henry Welch as its first dean. Its founding reflected the Hopkins model of uniting research, teaching and practice, and its faculty helped shape modern epidemiology, biostatistics, immunology and health policy. Among its landmark achievements were the development of the first polio epidemiology studies, pioneering work on water chlorination and tobacco's health effects, and contributions to the eradication of smallpox.

Renaming and growth

In 2001 the school was renamed in honour of alumnus and former New York City mayor Michael Bloomberg, the largest single benefactor in the university's history, whose gifts to the school have totalled hundreds of millions of dollars. The school enrols students from more than 80 countries across ten departments and offers MPH, DrPH and PhD programmes, including large-scale online degrees.

Research

Bloomberg School researchers work in more than 130 countries; the school gained wide public prominence during the COVID-19 pandemic, when its Coronavirus Resource Center and global case dashboard — launched in January 2020 by the Center for Systems Science and Engineering — became a principal worldwide reference for pandemic data.
