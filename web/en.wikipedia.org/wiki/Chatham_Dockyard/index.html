Chatham Dockyard was a Royal Navy Dockyard located on the River Medway in Chatham, Kent, England. Established in the mid-16th century, it expanded during the subsequent 200 years and was at its height during the 17th and 18th centuries, building some of the most famous warships of the sailing era. The yard closed as a naval base in 1984; the Georgian core survives as The Historic Dockyard Chatham, a heritage attraction and maritime museum.

History

Henry VIII ordered ships to be laid up at Gillingham Water, and Elizabeth I formally established a royal naval yard at Chatham in 1567. The Dutch raid on the Medway (1667) exposed its vulnerability, after which defences were strengthened and operations partly moved downstream to Sheerness. Through the Napoleonic Wars the yard built or refitted a large share of the fleet; the line-of-battle ship HMS Victory (launched 1765) was built at Chatham by shipwright Edward Hunt.

The yard adapted to the ironclad age, building steam warships in the 19th century — including the frigate Arethusa, the battleship Iron Duke, and later HMS Dreadnought (built at Portsmouth, but refitted at Chatham) — and remained a major construction and repair yard through both world wars, employing over 16,000 people at its wartime peak. After a government defence review it closed in 1984.

The Historic Dockyard today

The surviving 80-acre Georgian dockyard, with its covered building slips, anchor forge, saw mill, and the great Ropery — a quarter-mile-long ropery where rigging is still made by traditional methods — is maintained by the Chatham Historic Dockyard Trust. Vessels preserved on site include the Second World War destroyer HMS Cavalier, the sloop HMS Gannet, and the submarine HMS Ocelot. The site has also served as a filming location for period dramas including Call the Midwife and Les Misérables.
