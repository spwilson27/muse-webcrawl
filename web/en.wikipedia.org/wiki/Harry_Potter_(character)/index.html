Harry James Potter is a fictional character and the titular protagonist in J. K. Rowling's series of novels of the same name. Most of the books' plot covers seven years in the life of the orphan Harry, who, on his eleventh birthday, learns he is a wizard and the son of two famous magic-users murdered by the dark wizard Lord Voldemort. He attends Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry to practise magic under the guidance of the kindly headmaster Albus Dumbledore and other school professors, along with his best friends Ron Weasley and Hermione Granger.

Harry discovers that he is already famous throughout the wizarding world: as an infant he survived Voldemort's killing curse, which rebounded and destroyed the dark lord's body, leaving Harry with a lightning-shaped scar on his forehead. Across the series he repeatedly confronts Voldemort's attempts to return, learns that he himself is an unintentional Horcrux, and in the final battle at Hogwarts defeats Voldemort after willingly accepting death and returning.

Characterisation

Rowling has described Harry as a boy who "fights for what is right" with a conscience, temper, and strong capacity for love and loyalty. His defining traits include bravery, stubbornness, and skill as a Seeker in Quidditch and in Defence Against the Dark Arts. In the films he is portrayed by Daniel Radcliffe.

Family and later life

Harry is the only child of James and Lily Potter, raised after their deaths by his neglectful aunt and uncle, Petunia and Vernon Dursley. In the series' epilogue, set nineteen years after Voldemort's defeat, Harry is married to Ginny Weasley, has three children (James Sirius, Albus Severus, and Lily Luna), and works at the Ministry of Magic, eventually heading the Auror Office and then the Department of Magical Law Enforcement.

Reception

Harry is regarded as one of the most famous fictional characters of modern children's literature; the series has sold over 500 million copies worldwide, and the character regularly ranks among the greatest fictional heroes in polls.
