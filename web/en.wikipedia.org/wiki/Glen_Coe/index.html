Glen Coe (Scottish Gaelic: Gleann Comhann) is a glen of volcanic origins in the Highlands of Scotland, lying in the north of Argyll, close to the border with Lochaber. It is famous for its dramatic mountain scenery and for the Massacre of Glencoe of 1692.

Geography and geology

The glen runs roughly west to east for about 16 km, hemmed in by steep mountains including the Aonach Eagach ridge and the Three Sisters of Bidean nam Bian. Its rocks are the eroded remains of a supervolcano that erupted about 420 million years ago; Glen Coe is a classic site in the history of geology, among the first places where the concept of subsiding calderas was recognised. The glen is designated a National Scenic Area and a site of special scientific interest, and much of it is cared for by the National Trust for Scotland.

Massacre of Glencoe

On 13 February 1692, government soldiers of the Earl of Argyll's regiment, who had been billeted on the MacDonalds of Glencoe, turned on their hosts under orders signed by King William III. Thirty-eight MacDonalds, including the chief Alasdair MacIain, were killed, and more died of exposure after their homes were burned. The massacre became an enduring byword for treachery in Scottish memory.

Tourism

The A82 road runs through the glen, which is a major centre for hill walking and climbing and a popular filming location.
