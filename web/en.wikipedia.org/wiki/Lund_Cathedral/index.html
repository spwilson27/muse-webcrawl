Lund Cathedral (Swedish: Lunds domkyrka) is a Lutheran cathedral in Lund, Scania, Sweden, and the seat of the Bishop of Lund of the Church of Sweden. Built of sandstone in the Romanesque style in the first half of the 12th century, it is regarded as one of the finest Romanesque buildings in the Nordic countries. Its crypt, consecrated in 1123, is among the oldest stone buildings in Scandinavia still in use.

History

Lund was made an archbishopric for all Scandinavia in 1103–1104, and construction of the cathedral proceeded under Archbishop Ascer; the high altar was consecrated in 1145. The building suffered fires and repeated alterations: in the 16th century the astronomer Tycho Brahe's era saw changes, and a major 19th-century restoration by Helgo Zettervall remodelled the west front and towers. The cathedral became Lutheran at the Reformation, when Lund passed from Denmark to Sweden (1658).

Features

The cathedral is famed for its astronomical clock, the Horologium mirabile Lundense (c. 1425), which displays the movements of the sun and moon and plays at noon and 3 p.m. with moving figures of the Three Kings. The crypt contains the legendary giant Finn—said in folklore to have built the church—clutching a pillar, and the sarcophagus of Archbishop Birger Gunnersen. The sculpted apse, carved portals and the "Well of Finn" add to its artistic importance, and with more than 700,000 visitors a year it is one of Sweden's most visited churches.
