Melvil Dewey (born Melville Louis Kossuth Dewey; December 10, 1851 – December 26, 1931) was an American librarian and educator who invented the Dewey Decimal system of library classification. He was a founder of the American Library Association and a chief librarian at Columbia University. He also founded the Lake Placid Club, a health and education resort in New York.

Early life and education

Dewey was born in Adams Center, New York, the fifth and last child of Joel and Eliza Dewey. He attended local schools and briefly studied at Alfred University before enrolling at Amherst College, from which he graduated in 1874. While at Amherst he worked as an assistant in the college library and became convinced that library classification and management needed radical reform.

Career

After graduating, Dewey worked at Amherst as acting librarian. In 1876 he published A Classification and Subject Index for Cataloguing and Arranging the Books and Pamphlets of a Library, the first statement of the decimal classification that bears his name. That same year he helped found the American Library Association and became a founding editor of the Library Journal.

In 1883 Dewey became librarian at Columbia College (now Columbia University), where in 1887 he founded the School of Library Economy, the first school for librarians in the United States. In 1889 he moved to Albany, New York, to direct the New York State Library and to move his library school there; the school became the New York State Library School.

Spelling reform and other interests

Dewey was an ardent advocate of spelling reform and metric measurements. He shortened his own first name from Melville to Melvil and briefly spelled his surname "Dui." He was also a promoter of winter sports and helped bring the 1932 Winter Olympics to Lake Placid.

Later life

In his later years Dewey developed the Lake Placid Club in Florida. He died of a stroke on December 26, 1931. His classification system remains one of the most widely used library classification systems in the world.
