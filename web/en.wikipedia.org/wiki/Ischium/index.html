Ischium

The ischium (plural ischia) forms the lower and back part of the hip bone (os coxae). Situated below the ilium and behind the pubis, it is one of the three regions of the pelvis that fuse to form the hip bone. The ischial tuberosity — the "sitting bone" — bears the body's weight when seated.

Structure

The ischium consists of a body, which contributes to the acetabulum (the socket of the hip joint), and a ramus that joins the inferior ramus of the pubis, enclosing the obturator foramen. Landmarks include the ischial spine, which forms the boundary of the greater and lesser sciatic notches, and the ischial tuberosity, which gives origin to the hamstring muscles.

Clinical significance

Fractures of the ischium may occur in pelvic trauma and in avulsion injuries of the hamstring origin in young athletes. Ischial bursitis ("weaver's bottom") is inflammation of the bursa over the ischial tuberosity, caused by prolonged sitting on hard surfaces. The bone also gives the name of the dinosaur clade Saurischia ("lizard-hipped"), whose members possess a pubis pointing in a particular orientation relative to the ischium.
