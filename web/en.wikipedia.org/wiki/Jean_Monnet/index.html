Jean Monnet

Jean Omer Marie Gabriel Monnet (9 November 1888 – 16 March 1979) was a French civil servant, businessman, and political visionary widely regarded as a founding father of the European Union. Never elected to office, he worked behind the scenes to promote Franco-German reconciliation and European integration, drafting the Schuman Declaration of 1950 and becoming the first president of the High Authority of the European Coal and Steel Community.

Early career

Born into a family of cognac merchants in Cognac, Monnet travelled internationally for the family firm before the First World War. During the war he persuaded the French government to coordinate Allied purchasing, and after it he became Deputy Secretary-General of the new League of Nations (1919–1923). He later worked in international finance, helping stabilise currencies in Poland and Romania, and in banking in the United States and China.

Second World War and the Monnet Plan

In 1940 Monnet served on the British Supply Council in Washington, where he helped design the American "Victory Program" of war production; Keynes reportedly said Monnet's work may have shortened the war by a year. After joining de Gaulle's government-in-exile in Algiers, he argued France must recover by integrating with, not dominating, Germany. After the war he authored the Monnet Plan (1946) for French modernisation and became the first head of France's General Planning Commission.

European integration

In 1950, concerned that France's coal-and-steel policy toward Germany was failing, Monnet drafted the proposal announced by foreign minister Robert Schuman on 9 May 1950: pooling French and German coal and steel under a common High Authority — the nucleus of the European Coal and Steel Community (1951). Monnet presided over the High Authority in Luxembourg from 1952 to 1955. After the failure of the European Defence Community he resigned and founded the Action Committee for the United States of Europe, lobbying the parties and unions that helped produce the Treaties of Rome (1957).

Legacy

Monnet's "method" — achieving political union gradually through concrete economic steps and common institutions, later called functionalism or the engrenage (gearing) process — shaped the EU's development. He received the Charlemagne Prize and the Presidential Medal of Freedom, and in 1988 his remains were transferred to the Panthéon in Paris. His house at Houjarray, where much of the Schuman Plan was drafted, is now a museum.
