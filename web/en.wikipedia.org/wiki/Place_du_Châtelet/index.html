Place du Châtelet

Location: 1st and 4th arrondissements, Paris, France

The Place du Châtelet is a public square in Paris, on the right bank of the river Seine, on the borderline between the 1st and 4th arrondissements. It lies at the north end of the Pont au Change, and is the site of two Parisian theatres, the Théâtre du Châtelet and the Théâtre de la Ville. The square is named after the Grand Châtelet, a fortress demolished in the early 19th century.

History

The Grand Châtelet was a stronghold and prison built by order of Louis VI at the north end of the bridge that became the Pont au Change. It housed the offices of the prévôt de Paris and several prisons. After its demolition between 1802 and 1810 under Napoleon, the square was laid out in its place. The Fontaine du Palmier, designed by François-Jean Bralle and erected in 1806–1808 to celebrate Napoleon's victories, stands at the centre of the square; it is topped by a gilded statue of Victory.

Description

The square is a major transport hub: the Châtelet Métro station below is one of the largest in Paris. The two theatres facing each other across the square were both built in the 1860s under Baron Haussmann's renovation of Paris, to designs associated with Gabriel Davioud.
