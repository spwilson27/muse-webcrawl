Rogers Pass

Elevation1,330 m (4,360 ft)
LocationGlacier National Park, British Columbia, Canada
RangeSelkirk Mountains
Traversed byTrans-Canada Highway; Canadian Pacific Railway (Connaught and Mount Macdonald tunnels)

Rogers Pass is a high mountain pass through the Selkirk Mountains of British Columbia, Canada, used by the Canadian Pacific Railway and the Trans-Canada Highway. Located in Glacier National Park, it is a noted site of avalanche control and of the Canadian Pacific's historic route to the Pacific.

Rogers Pass in winter, amid some of the heaviest snowfall in Canada.

History

The pass was located in 1881 by Major Albert Bowman Rogers, a surveyor for the Canadian Pacific Railway, whose discovery of a route through the Selkirks fulfilled a condition of British Columbia's entry into Confederation. The railway crossed the pass from 1885, but severe avalanche danger — including the 1910 Rogers Pass avalanche, which killed 58 workers clearing a previous slide — led the CPR to bore the Connaught Tunnel (1916) beneath the pass, and later the Mount Macdonald Tunnel (1988), the longest railway tunnel in North America.

Avalanches

Rogers Pass receives some 10 metres of snow annually, and more than 130 avalanche paths threaten the highway and railway. The Royal Canadian Artillery operates the world's largest mobile avalanche-control programme from the pass, firing howitzer shells to trigger controlled slides. The Rogers Pass Discovery Centre interprets the pass's natural and railway history.
