The Saab 9-3 (pronounced "nine three") is a compact executive car produced by the Swedish manufacturer Saab Automobile. Launched in 1998 as a heavily reworked development of the Saab 900, it was marketed as the 9-3 to align with the larger 9-5. Two generations were built before Saab's bankruptcy in 2011, with a brief final production run under NEVS in 2013–14.

First generation (1998–2003)

The first 9-3 retained the hatchback body of the 900 but incorporated over a thousand changes, including revised suspension, improved crash structures and Saab's turbocharged engines. The high-performance Viggen model, named after the fighter aircraft, offered 230 hp from a 2.3-litre turbo and was noted for pronounced torque steer. A convertible was also offered.

Second generation (2002–2011)

The second-generation car, based on General Motors' Epsilon platform, abandoned the hatchback for a four-door sedan ("Sport Sedan"), joined by the SportCombi estate and a soft-top convertible. Turbocharged petrol engines predominated, alongside diesels from Fiat-GM Powertrain; the range-topping Turbo X of 2008 introduced Saab's XWD all-wheel-drive system with an electronic limited-slip differential. Distinctive Saab features included the ignition switch on the centre console, the "Night Panel" instrument-dimming function and fighter-jet-inspired cockpit ergonomics.

End of production

Production halted amid Saab's financial crisis in 2011. NEVS, which bought the Trollhättan assets, restarted limited 9-3 Aero production in late 2013 but ceased in 2014, ending the model's run.
