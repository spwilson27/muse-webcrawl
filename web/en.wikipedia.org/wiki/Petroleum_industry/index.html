Petroleum industry

Segments: Upstream, midstream, downstream

World output: approx. 100 million barrels/day (2023)

Major firms: Saudi Aramco, ExxonMobil, Shell, BP, Chevron

Trade body: OPEC

The petroleum industry, also known as the oil industry or the oil patch, comprises the global processes of exploration, extraction, refining, transportation and marketing of petroleum products. Its largest-volume products are fuel oil and petrol (gasoline), and petroleum is also the raw material for plastics, fertilisers, solvents, pharmaceuticals and other petrochemicals.

Segments

  • Upstream — searching for underground or underwater crude oil and natural gas fields, and drilling and operating wells.
  • Midstream — transportation by pipeline, tanker, rail and truck, plus storage and wholesale marketing of crude.
  • Downstream — refining crude into petrol, diesel, kerosene, liquefied petroleum gas and other products, and distributing them to consumers.

Structure and history

The modern industry dates from the 1859 Drake well at Titusville, Pennsylvania, and the empire built by John D. Rockefeller's Standard Oil. Today's market mixes state-owned "national oil companies" such as Saudi Aramco — which control most of the world's reserves — with investor-owned international majors descended from the "Seven Sisters". The cartel OPEC, founded in 1960, coordinates production among major exporting nations; crude prices are benchmarked to grades such as Brent and West Texas Intermediate.

The industry is among the world's largest by revenue, but faces structural challenge from the climate-policy-driven energy transition toward renewables and electric transport.
