Gougane Barra

Location: County Cork, Ireland
Coordinates: 51°50′N 9°19′W
Known for: St Finbarr's Oratory; forest park

Gougane Barra (Irish: Guagán Barra, meaning "the rock-cleft of Barra") is a settlement in County Cork, Ireland, west of Macroom. The name refers both to a lake and to the forest park and pilgrim site beside it, at the source of the River Lee. Gougane Barra is one of Ireland's most celebrated beauty spots, set in a mountain-ringed valley at the foot of the Shehy Mountains.

St Finbarr's hermitage

Tradition holds that Saint Finbarr, patron saint of Cork, founded a monastery on an island in the lake in the 6th century, before moving on to found the church that became the city of Cork at the mouth of the Lee. The ruins on the island include an early monastic enclosure, and a small church or oratory built in the late 19th and early 20th centuries — St Finbarr's Oratory — which is a popular venue for weddings and an iconic image of Ireland.

During Penal times, when Catholic worship was restricted, Mass was celebrated at a Mass rock in the area, and the pattern (pilgrimage) to Gougane Barra on the Sunday nearest the feast of St Finbarr (25 September) has continued for centuries. The priest and scholar Canon Peter O'Leary (Peadar Ua Laoghaire) is buried in the adjoining graveyard.

Forest park

Gougane Barra Forest Park, opened in 1966, was Ireland's first national forest park. Covering some 340 acres of the valley slopes, it offers waymarked trails through conifer plantations and native trees, with viewpoints over the lake and its island chapel. The River Lee rises in the valley and flows east through Ballingeary and onward to Cork city.
