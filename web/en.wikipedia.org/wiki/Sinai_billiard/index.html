Sinai billiard

FieldDynamical systems
Introduced byYakov Sinai, 1970
PropertiesErgodic, chaotic

The Sinai billiard, also known as the Lorentz gas, is a dynamical system consisting of a point particle moving in straight lines inside a square (or periodic array of squares) containing a circular obstacle, reflecting elastically off the walls and the obstacle. Introduced by Yakov Sinai in 1970, it is a canonical example of a chaotic Hamiltonian system.

Dynamics

Because the circular scatterer is convex (dispersing), nearby trajectories diverge exponentially, giving the system positive Lyapunov exponents and ergodicity. Sinai proved the system is ergodic, a landmark result in statistical mechanics because it rigorously justified chaotic behaviour in a physically motivated model.

Lorentz gas

In the periodic version, the particle moves among an infinite periodic array of circular obstacles; this is the Lorentz gas, originally proposed by Hendrik Lorentz to model electrons in a metal. The infinite-horizon and finite-horizon cases differ in their diffusion properties.

Significance

The Sinai billiard demonstrated that deterministic systems of hard spheres can exhibit the statistical behaviour assumed in thermodynamics, providing a foundation for the ergodic theory of billiards.
