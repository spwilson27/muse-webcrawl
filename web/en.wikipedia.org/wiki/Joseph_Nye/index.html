Joseph Nye

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Joseph Samuel Nye Jr. (19 January 1937 – 6 May 2025) was an American political scientist, co-founder (with Robert Keohane) of the neoliberal-institutionalist approach to international relations (Power and Interdependence, 1977) and originator of the concept of soft power — the ability to get what one wants through attraction rather than coercion or payment — developed in Bound to Lead (1990) and Soft Power (2004). He later combined the currencies into “smart power”, advising that effective foreign policy mixes hard and soft resources in context.

Nye served as Assistant Secretary of Defense and chair of the National Intelligence Council under President Clinton, and as dean of Harvard's Kennedy School (1995–2004), making him a leading scholar-practitioner of American foreign policy. His later work (The Future of Power, 2011; Is the American Century Over?, 2015; the memoir A Life in the American Century, 2024) argued that US power persists but must adapt to diffusion — power shifting to non-state actors and to Asia — and to the “Kindleberger trap” of a rising power's under-provision of public goods. He died in May 2025.
