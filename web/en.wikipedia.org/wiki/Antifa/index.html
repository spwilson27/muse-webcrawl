Antifa

Antifa is a left-wing anti-fascist and anti-racist political movement in the United States. It comprises autonomous groups and individuals that monitor and confront far-right extremists, seeking to prevent fascist and white supremacist movements from gaining a public platform through means ranging from research and public exposure to protests and, in some cases, physical confrontation. The movement is loosely organised, without a central leadership or formal membership.

Origins

The name derives from the German term Antifa, short for antifaschistisch (anti-fascist), echoing groups such as Antifaschistische Aktion founded in Weimar Germany in 1932. Modern American antifa descends in part from Anti-Racist Action, founded in the late 1980s to oppose neo-Nazi skinhead recruitment in the punk scene, and from broader anarchist and socialist anti-fascist traditions.

Activities and prominence

Antifa groups gained wide public attention during the presidency of Donald Trump, appearing at counter-demonstrations against alt-right and white nationalist rallies, notably around the 2017 Unite the Right rally in Charlottesville, Virginia. Participants often dress in black bloc attire. The movement has been criticised for property damage and violence at some protests, while supporters frame its tactics as community self-defence against fascist organising. Debates over its legal status have continued into the 2020s, including efforts in the United States to designate antifa as a terrorist organisation, despite official assessments describing it as a decentralised movement rather than a single organisation.
