Bow (gesture)

A bow is a gesture of deference, respect, greeting, or apology in which a person bends the torso and head forward. Bowing is characteristic of the cultures of East and Southeast Asia — including China, Japan, Korea, and Vietnam — but also appears in European court etiquette, religious practice, and the performing arts, where performers bow to acknowledge applause.

In East Asia

In Japan, bowing (ojigi) is an everyday social skill; depth and duration encode the degree of respect, gratitude, or apology, from a casual nod to a deep, prolonged bow, and kneeling bows (dogeza) express extreme apology or supplication. In Korea, deep bows are performed at weddings, funerals, and New Year (sebae) toward elders. Historically, the kowtow of imperial China involved kneeling and touching the forehead to the ground before the emperor.

In the West

European traditions include the court bow and the curtsy, and bows toward altars, monarchs, and flags. In theatre, dance, and music performance the bow (and its gendered forms) closes the exchange between performers and audience. Bowing also appears in martial arts, in liturgical practice, and in formal diplomatic protocol, where its equivalence to the handshake is often a point of etiquette discussion.
