IB Diploma Programme

The IB Diploma Programme (DP) is a two-year educational programme for students aged 16 to 19, administered by the International Baccalaureate (IB) and recognised by universities worldwide. Founded in 1968, it is taught in thousands of schools in over 150 countries.

Curriculum

Students study six subject groups — language and literature, language acquisition, individuals and societies, sciences, mathematics, and the arts — taking most subjects at either higher level (HL) or standard level (SL). All students complete the DP core: the Theory of Knowledge (TOK) course, an Extended Essay of up to 4,000 words, and Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS). Courses are graded 1–7, with up to three additional points for TOK and the Extended Essay, giving a maximum of 45 points; the diploma is awarded with a minimum of 24 points subject to conditions.

Assessment

Assessment combines external examinations held in May and November sessions with internally assessed coursework moderated by the IB. The programme is often noted for its academic breadth and rigour compared with single-system national qualifications.
