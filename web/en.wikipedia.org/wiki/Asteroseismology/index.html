Asteroseismology

Asteroseismology is the study of oscillations in stars. Stars have many resonant modes and frequencies, and the path of sound waves passing through a star depends on the local speed of sound, which in turn depends on local temperature and chemical composition. Because the resulting oscillation modes are sensitive to different parts of the star, they inform astronomers about the internal structure of the star, which is otherwise not directly possible from overall luminosity and surface temperature, for example.

Asteroseismology is closely related to helioseismology, the study of stellar pulsation specifically in the Sun. Though both are based on the same underlying physics, more and qualitatively different information is available for the Sun because its surface can be resolved.

Theory

Stars oscillate in a variety of modes. Pressure modes (p-modes) are essentially sound waves reflected many times within the star, while gravity modes (g-modes) are buoyancy waves confined to the deep interior. Measuring many mode frequencies allows inversion techniques, analogous to medical tomography, to map the sound-speed and density profile of the interior.

Observations

Space missions such as CoRoT, Kepler and TESS have provided ultra-precise photometry for tens of thousands of stars. Asteroseismic analysis yields stellar masses, radii and ages to a precision impossible by other means, and is a key tool for characterizing exoplanet host stars.
