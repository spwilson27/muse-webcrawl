Operation Fair Play was the code name of the military coup d'état conducted on 5 July 1977 by Pakistan Army chief General Muhammad Zia-ul-Haq, which overthrew the government of Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto of the Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP).

Background

The general election of March 1977 returned Bhutto's PPP to power, but the opposition Pakistan National Alliance (PNA) alleged systematic rigging and launched a campaign of street protests and agitation. Weeks of unrest, which left hundreds dead and brought parts of the economy to a halt, failed to produce a settlement. Negotiations between the government and the PNA were reportedly close to agreement on fresh elections when the army intervened.

The coup

In the early hours of 5 July 1977, troops detained Bhutto, his ministers and leading PNA politicians, suspended the constitution, dissolved the national and provincial assemblies and proclaimed martial law. Zia became Chief Martial Law Administrator and, in September 1978, President of Pakistan. He announced that elections would be held within 90 days and that the army would then return to barracks — a promise that was repeatedly postponed, with "Fair Play" presented as a neutral intervention to end the political deadlock.

Aftermath

Bhutto was released, then rearrested and tried for conspiring to murder a political opponent, Nawab Muhammad Ahmed Khan Kasuri. Convicted in a controversial trial, he was hanged on 4 April 1979. Zia ruled Pakistan for eleven years, pursuing a programme of Islamisation, supporting the Afghan mujahideen against the Soviet Union and amending the constitution to entrench military influence, until his death in an air crash in August 1988.
