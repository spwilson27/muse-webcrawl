Box lacrosse, also known as boxla or indoor lacrosse, is a version of lacrosse played indoors on a hockey rink floor or similar enclosed surface, between two teams of six players. Developed in Canada in the 1930s, it is the dominant form of the sport in Canada and is played professionally in the National Lacrosse League (NLL) in Canada and the United States.

History

The indoor game was created during the Great Depression, when owners of Canadian hockey arenas sought summer tenants and promoters adapted field lacrosse to the rink, using the boards to keep the ball in play. The smaller space, shot clock (adopted from the indoor rules) and physical play produced a fast, high-scoring game that quickly overtook field lacrosse in popularity in Canada. The Mann Cup, emblematic of the Canadian senior championship, has been contested under box rules since the 1930s.

Gameplay

Box lacrosse is played in three periods of 20 minutes (15 in some leagues). Teams have 30 seconds to shoot after gaining possession. Cross-checking and body contact are permitted, goalies wear extensive padding similar to ice hockey, and the enclosed boards make rebounds a central part of play. Penalties are served in a penalty box, producing man-up ("power play") and man-down situations.

International play

The World Box Lacrosse Championship, organised by World Lacrosse, has been held since 2003; Canada has won every edition, with the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Nationals) — representing the Indigenous creators of the game — perennially among the leading teams. The game remains especially rooted in Ontario and British Columbia and in Haudenosaunee communities in Canada and New York.
