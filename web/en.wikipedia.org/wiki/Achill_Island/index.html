Achill Island

Achill Island
Irish: Acaill, Oileán Acla
County: Mayo, Ireland
Area: 148 km²
Highest point: Croaghaun, 688 m
Population: about 2,600

Achill Island (Irish: Acaill) is the largest of the Irish offshore islands, lying off the west coast of County Mayo in the Atlantic Ocean. It is connected to the mainland by the Michael Davitt Bridge at Achill Sound. The island covers about 148 km² (57 sq mi) and is known for its dramatic sea cliffs, mountains — Slievemore (671 m) and Croaghaun (688 m), whose cliffs are among the highest sea cliffs in Europe — and Blue Flag beaches such as Keem Bay and Keel.

History

Achill has been inhabited since the Neolithic, about 5,000 years ago; megalithic tombs and promontory forts survive. In the medieval period it fell under the control of the O'Malley clan of Umhaill. The 19th century saw the Protestant mission colony founded by the Rev. Edward Nangle at Dugort (1834) — the "Achill Mission" — which became a center of controversy during the famine years. The island suffered heavily in the Great Famine and subsequent emigration. A notable landmark is the Deserted Village at the foot of Slievemore, the remains of some 80 stone cottages abandoned in the 19th century.

Culture and tourism

The German Nobel laureate Heinrich Böll wrote his Irish Journal (Irisches Tagebuch, 1957) partly from his time on Achill, where he had a cottage; artists Paul Henry and Robert Henri also painted on the island. Parts of Achill are within the Gaeltacht (Irish-speaking area). The island is a popular destination for surfing, hiking, and cycling — the Great Western Greenway rail trail ends at Achill Sound — and basking sharks are regularly seen in its waters.
