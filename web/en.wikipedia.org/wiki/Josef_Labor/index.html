Josef Labor

Josef Labor (29 June 1842 – 26 April 1924) was an Austrian pianist, organist, and composer. Blind from early childhood, he became one of the most respected musicians in Vienna, celebrated for his performances of Bach on the organ and for the chamber works he wrote for his patron and friend, the philosopher Ludwig Wittgenstein's musical family circle — in particular for the one-armed pianist Paul Wittgenstein.

Life

Labor was born in Hořovice, Bohemia (now in the Czech Republic). He lost his sight to smallpox as a small child. He studied at the Institute for the Blind in Vienna and at the Vienna Conservatory, and made successful concert tours as an organist and pianist, performing in France, England, Russia, and Scandinavia. He was appointed imperial court organist in Vienna and taught piano and theory; among the composers influenced by him were Arnold Schoenberg, who received early encouragement and counterpoint instruction, and Franz Schmidt, who studied piano with him.

Music

Labor's compositions are in a late-Romantic, classically disciplined idiom influenced by Brahms, whom he knew personally. His output includes a piano quintet, violin sonatas, a cello sonata, string quartets, organ works, and songs. He is especially remembered for the works written for Paul Wittgenstein, the pianist who lost his right arm in the First World War: Labor composed a series of chamber works for piano left-hand, violin, viola, and cello, which Wittgenstein premiered in the 1910s and 1920s — earlier models of the famous left-hand concerto commissions Wittgenstein later made to Ravel, Prokofiev, and Britten.
