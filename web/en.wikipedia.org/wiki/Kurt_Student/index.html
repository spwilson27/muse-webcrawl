Kurt Student

Kurt Arthur Benno Student (12 May 1890 – 1 July 1978) was a German general in the Luftwaffe during World War II who pioneered military parachute and airborne forces.

Career

A pilot in the First World War, Student was retained in the Reichswehr and, although glider and parachute troops initially fell under the army, he built the Luftwaffe's Fallschirmjäger into an elite force. His paratroopers spearheaded the invasions of Norway, Denmark, the Netherlands and Belgium in 1940 — including the audacious glider assault on Fort Eben-Emael — and captured Crete in May 1941, albeit with such heavy losses that Hitler forbade further large airborne operations.

Later war and trial

Student subsequently commanded airborne training and, from 1944, the First Parachute Army in the west, facing the Allied Operation Market Garden in the Netherlands. After the war he was tried by a British military court for war crimes connected with Crete and sentenced to five years' imprisonment, though he was released in 1948. He died in Lemgo, West Germany, in 1978.
