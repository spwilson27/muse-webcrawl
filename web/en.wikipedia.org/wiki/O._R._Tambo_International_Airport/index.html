O. R. Tambo International Airport

O. R. Tambo International Airport (IATA: JNB, ICAO: FAOR) is the primary international airport serving Johannesburg, South Africa. Located in Kempton Park, Ekurhuleni, Gauteng, it is Africa's busiest airport and the hub of South African Airways. The airport sits at an altitude of about 1,700 metres (5,560 feet), making it one of the world's highest major airports; the thin air requires unusually long runways, and its longest, at over 4,400 metres, is among the longest at any international airport.

History

The airport opened in 1952 as Jan Smuts Airport, named after the South African statesman and twice prime minister. It replaced Palmietfontein Airport as Johannesburg's main airport. In 1994, after the end of apartheid, it was neutrally renamed Johannesburg International Airport, and in 2006 it was renamed again in honour of Oliver Reginald Tambo, the anti-apartheid leader and former president of the African National Congress. A major expansion, including a new central terminal and the Gautrain rapid-rail station, was completed for the 2010 FIFA World Cup.

Facilities and traffic

The airport has two parallel runways and two international terminals (A and B) plus domestic terminals, handling more than 20 million passengers a year in peak pre-pandemic years. Because of the "hot and high" conditions, departing long-haul aircraft face payload restrictions, and some routes historically required technical stops. The Gautrain connects the airport to Sandton, Pretoria, and Johannesburg Park Station.
