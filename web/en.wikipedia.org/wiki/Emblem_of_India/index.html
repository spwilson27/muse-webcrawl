State Emblem of India

The State Emblem of India (ISO: Rāṣṭrīya Ciḥna Bhārata) is the national emblem of the Republic of India, adapted from the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath. It was adopted on 26 January 1950, the day India became a republic.

Description

The emblem depicts three Asiatic lions standing back to back (a fourth is hidden from view), mounted on an abacus with a frieze carrying sculpted figures of an elephant, a galloping horse, a bull, and a lion, separated by intervening wheels (chakras). Below the abacus is inscribed the motto Satyameva Jayate ("Truth alone triumphs") in Devanagari script, taken from the Mundaka Upanishad.

The original Lion Capital, dating to about 250 BCE, was erected by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka at Sarnath, the site of the Buddha's first sermon. The capital is preserved in the Sarnath Museum.

Use

The emblem is used on official documents, currency, passports, and government buildings. Its use is regulated by the State Emblem of India (Prohibition of Improper Use) Act, 2005, which restricts it to authorized government purposes.
