Valuation domain

In abstract algebra, a valuation domain is an integral domain R such that for every element x of its field of fractions, at least one of x or x−1 belongs to R. Equivalently, the ideals of a valuation domain are totally ordered by inclusion, and the principal ideals are likewise totally ordered.

Properties

Every valuation domain is a local ring and is integrally closed in its fraction field. A valuation domain is a Bézout domain: every finitely generated ideal is principal. A Noetherian valuation domain is either a field or a discrete valuation ring. Valuation domains arise naturally as the rings of valuations: given a valuation on a field with an ordered abelian value group, the set of elements of non-negative value forms a valuation domain, and every valuation domain arises this way.

Examples

Standard examples include the ring of p-adic integers for a prime p; the ring of formal power series over a field in one variable; and more generally the valuation rings associated with places of function fields in algebraic geometry. Valuation domains play a central role in the model theory of valued fields and in the Zariski–Riemann space of valuations of a function field.
