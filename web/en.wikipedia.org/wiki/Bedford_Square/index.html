Bedford Square is a garden square in Bloomsbury in the London Borough of Camden, built between 1775 and 1783 as an upper-middle-class residential development on the Bedford Estate. It is considered one of the best-preserved examples of Georgian architecture in London, with all of its original houses surviving, most of them Grade I listed.

Architecture

The square consists of Palladian-style terraced houses built of yellow London stock brick, with stucco detailing and characteristic features such as Coade stone ornament, central pedimented houses on each side, and original iron railings and lamp standards. The architect is often identified as Thomas Leverton, though the design was executed largely by the estate's builders.

Occupants

Notable residents have included Lord Eldon, Lord Chancellor, and the physician and author Henry Holland. The square long housed publishers and learned institutions; today it contains the Paul Mellon Centre for Studies in British Art, the London office of Yale University Press, the Architectural Association School of Architecture and other academic and cultural bodies. The central private garden retains its oval lawn and mature plane trees.
