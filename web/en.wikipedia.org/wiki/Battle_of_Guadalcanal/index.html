Battle of Guadalcanal

The Battle of Guadalcanal, also known as the Guadalcanal campaign and codenamed Operation Watchtower by American forces, was a military campaign fought between 7 August 1942 and 9 February 1943 on and around the island of Guadalcanal in the Pacific theatre of the Second World War. It was the first major land offensive by Allied forces against the Empire of Japan.

Background

Japanese forces had landed on Guadalcanal and begun building an airfield threatening Allied supply lines to Australia. On 7 August 1942 the U.S. 1st Marine Division landed and seized the nearly complete airfield, named Henderson Field. Control of the airfield — and the "Cactus Air Force" flying from it — became the focus of the campaign.

The campaign

Both sides fought a grinding six-month struggle on land, at sea and in the air. Major actions included the Battle of Savo Island, the Battle of Edson's Ridge, the Battle for Henderson Field, the Naval Battle of Guadalcanal (November 1942) and the Battle of Tassafaronga. Japanese supply runs by destroyer — the "Tokyo Express" — could not sustain their troops, who suffered terribly from starvation and disease. The Japanese battleship losses (Hiei and Kirishima) in November proved decisive at sea.

Outcome

Japan secretly evacuated its surviving troops in Operation Ke in early February 1943, and the island was declared secure on 9 February. Guadalcanal marked the strategic initiative passing to the Allies in the Pacific; from it, Allied forces advanced up the Solomon Islands chain in the "island hopping" strategy toward Rabaul and ultimately Japan.
