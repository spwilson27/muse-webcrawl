The FMA IA 63 Pampa is an advanced jet trainer with light combat capability, produced in Argentina by the Fábrica Argentina de Aviones (FAdeA), formerly Fábrica Militar de Aviones (FMA). Its design was influenced by the Dassault/Dornier Alpha Jet, with technical assistance from Dornier.

Development

Development began in the late 1970s to replace the Argentine Air Force's Morane-Saulnier MS.760 Paris trainers. The prototype first flew on 6 October 1984, and deliveries began in 1988. Production was interrupted by Argentina's economic difficulties, and only a small initial batch was completed.

Pampa II and III

Upgraded versions followed: the Pampa II introduced a more powerful Honeywell TFE731 engine and updated avionics, while the Pampa III, launched in the 2010s, added a glass cockpit, virtual training systems and modernised electronics. The type serves with the Argentine Air Force's IV Air Brigade at Mendoza for advanced training and light attack duties.

Characteristics

The Pampa is a single-engine, two-seat tandem aircraft with a maximum speed of about Mach 0.8 and a 30 mm DEFA cannon pod capability plus underwing stores stations.
