The Prime Minister of Portugal (Portuguese: Primeiro-Ministro) is the head of government of the Portuguese Republic. In Portugal's semi-presidential system, the prime minister leads the Government, directs executive policy, and is accountable to the Assembly of the Republic, while the President — head of state — appoints the prime minister after elections, taking into account the results and after hearing the parties represented in the Assembly. The prime minister's official residence is the São Bento Mansion, adjacent to the parliament in Lisbon.

History

Governments in Portugal have been led by a "president of the Council of Ministers" since 1834, a title used through the constitutional monarchy, the First Republic, and the authoritarian Estado Novo — whose longest-serving head of government was António de Oliveira Salazar (1932–68). The modern title Primeiro-Ministro dates from the 1976 Constitution adopted after the Carnation Revolution. Mário Soares became the first prime minister of democratic Portugal in 1976. Later holders include Aníbal Cavaco Silva (1985–95), António Guterres (1995–2002, later UN Secretary-General), José Manuel Barroso (2002–04, later President of the European Commission), José Sócrates, and Pedro Passos Coelho, who governed during the eurozone bailout.

Recent years

Socialist leader António Costa governed from 2015, first under a left-wing parliamentary arrangement (the "geringonça"), winning an absolute majority in 2022; he resigned in November 2023 amid a corruption investigation touching his office. Snap elections in March 2024 brought the centre-right Democratic Alliance to power, and Luís Montenegro became prime minister of a minority government on 2 April 2024.
