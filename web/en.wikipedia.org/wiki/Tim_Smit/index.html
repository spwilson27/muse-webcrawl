Tim Smit

Sir Timothy Bartel Smit (born 25 September 1954) is a Dutch-born British businessman and environmentalist best known as co-founder of the Eden Project in Cornwall, England, and for the restoration of the Lost Gardens of Heligan.

Early life and career

Smit was born in Scheveningen, Netherlands, and educated in England, studying anthropology and archaeology at Durham University. He worked first as an archaeologist, then entered the music business as a composer and producer.

Heligan and Eden

In 1990 Smit became involved in restoring the Lost Gardens of Heligan near Mevagissey, a project that became one of Britain's most celebrated garden restorations. He then conceived the Eden Project — vast geodesic biomes housing plants from around the world, built in a disused china clay pit near St Austell. Opened in 2001, Eden became a major visitor attraction and an educational charity promoting understanding of the natural world.

Honours

Smit was knighted in 2011 for services to public understanding of science. He has written about Eden and sustainability and advocates regenerative approaches to development.
