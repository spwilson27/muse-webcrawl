Lake Vänern (/ˈveɪnərn/ US also /ˈvɛnərn/; Swedish: [ˈvɛ̌ːnɛɳ]) is the largest lake in Sweden, the largest lake in the European Union, and the third-largest lake entirely in Europe after Ladoga and Onega in Russia. It is located in the provinces of Västergötland, Dalsland, and Värmland, in the southwest of the country. With its surface located at 44 metres (144 ft) above sea level and a maximum depth of 106 metres (348 ft), the lowest point of the Vänern basin is 62 metres (203 ft) below sea level.

The average depth is a more modest 27 metres (89 ft), which means that the average point of the lake floor is above sea level.

Geography

Vänern covers an area of 5,655 km² (2,183 sq mi) in, roughly, the southeast of the Scandinavian Mountains and was part of the ocean after the last ice age. The modern lake was formed after the Quaternary glaciation about 10,000 years ago; when the ice had melted, the entire width of Sweden was covered by water, creating a strait between Kattegat and the Gulf of Bothnia. Due to the fact that ensuing isostatic rebound surpassed concurrent sea level rise, lakes such as Vänern became cut off from the sea. Because of the post-glacial rebound, Vänern is still rising in the south but lowering in the north, at a rate of about 1 metre (3.3 ft) per century.

The lake drains to the southwest by the Göta River, which is part of the Göta Canal waterway, to the lake's outlet Vargön near Vänersborg, and downstream to Kattegat, an arm of the Atlantic Ocean. It is the largest lake in Sweden, with the Klarälven river as its biggest tributary entering in the north.

Environmental issues

Environmental monitoring studies are conducted annually. In a 2002 report, the data showed no marked decrease in overall water quality, but a slight decrease in visibility due to an increase of algae. An increasing level of nitrogen had been problematic during the 1970s through 1990s, but is now being regulated and is at a steady level. Some bays also have problems with eutrophication and have become overgrown with algae and plant plankton.

Fish

Vänern has many different species of fish. Locals and government officials try to enforce fishing preservation projects. The lake holds populations of Atlantic salmon, brown trout, zander, perch, pike, and roach. The lake's salmon, known as Vänern salmon, are landlocked and famous among anglers.
