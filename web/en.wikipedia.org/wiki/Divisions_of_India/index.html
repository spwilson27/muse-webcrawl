Divisions of India

The states and union territories of India are subdivided into divisions in some states — administrative groupings of districts headed by a divisional commissioner — while other states group districts differently or not at all. Divisions exist in states such as Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Punjab, Karnataka, and West Bengal.

Function

A divisional commissioner supervises the district magistrates (collectors) within the division, coordinating revenue administration, development programmes, and law and order between the state government and the districts. The office descends from the commissioners of divisions created under British rule in the 19th century.

Examples

Uttar Pradesh, the most populous state, has 18 divisions including Lucknow, Kanpur, Varanasi, Prayagraj, Gorakhpur, and Meerut. Maharashtra has six divisions (Konkan, Pune, Nashik, Aurangabad, Amravati, and Nagpur). Some states, notably in the south, use no divisional tier, dealing with districts directly.
