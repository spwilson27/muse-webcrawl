Wagashi

Wagashi (和菓子) are traditional Japanese confections typically made of plant-based ingredients — mochi (glutinous rice), anko (sweet red bean paste), agar (kanten), sugar and fruits — and often served with tea, especially in the tea ceremony.

History

Sweet-making in Japan was influenced by Chinese dim sum brought by envoys and monks, and later by Portuguese and Spanish confections (nanbangashi) in the 16th century, which introduced refined sugar and castella cake. During the Edo period, Kyoto court culture and the tea ceremony developed wagashi into a refined art, with designs reflecting seasons and nature.

Types

Categories include namagashi (fresh, highly perishable seasonal sweets), han namagashi, and higashi (dry sweets). Well-known examples are mochi, daifuku, dorayaki, yokan (jellied bean paste), monaka, kasutera, and nerikiri sculpted into flowers and seasonal motifs.

Cultural role

Wagashi aesthetics emphasize seasonality (shun), craftsmanship, and the five senses; sweet shops issue special designs for festivals such as Hanami and the New Year.
