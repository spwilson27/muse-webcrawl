Methyl orange

FormulaC14H14N3NaO3S
Molar mass327.33 g/mol
AppearanceOrange powder
pH transitionRed (pH < 3.1) to yellow (pH > 4.4)
pKa3.47

Methyl orange in acidic (left, red) and alkaline (right, yellow) solution

Methyl orange is a pH indicator frequently used in titration because of its clear and distinct color change over a moderate strength acidic range: it is red below pH 3.1, orange in between, and yellow above pH 4.4. Because it changes color at the pH of a mid-strength acid, it is commonly used for titrations of strong acids with weak or strong bases. It is an azo dye, the sodium salt of a sulfonated azo compound derived from dimethylaniline and sulfanilic acid.

Properties

Unlike a universal indicator, methyl orange does not display a full spectrum of color changes, but has a sharp end point. In a solution that becomes less acidic, methyl orange moves from red through orange to yellow; the process is reversed when acid is increased. The color change arises from protonation of the azo linkage, which alters the conjugated system of the molecule. Methyl orange is mutagenic under some conditions and is handled as an irritant; its use as a food dye is prohibited.
