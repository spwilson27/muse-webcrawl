James Devereux

James Patrick Sinnott Devereux (February 20, 1903 – August 5, 1988) was a United States Marine Corps general, Navy Cross recipient, and Republican congressman. He was the Marine officer in command during the defense of Wake Island in December 1941.

Early life and career

Devereux was born in Cabana, Cuba, where his father was an Army surgeon. He attended schools in the United States and Switzerland, and in 1923 was commissioned a second lieutenant in the Marine Corps. He served in Nicaragua, China and elsewhere, and qualified in coast artillery.

Wake Island

In October 1941 Devereux took command of the 1st Defense Battalion detachment on Wake Island. When Japanese forces attacked the atoll hours after Pearl Harbor, his Marines and civilian contractors resisted for fifteen days, sinking Japanese warships and repelling the first landing attempt on December 11 — the first Japanese amphibious repulse of the Pacific War. Wake fell on December 23, 1941, and Devereux spent the rest of the war as a prisoner of war in camps in China and Japan. His famous message, often misquoted as "Send us more Japs," became part of Marine Corps lore.

Later life

Devereux retired from the Marine Corps as a brigadier general in 1948. He represented Maryland in the US House of Representatives from 1951 to 1959 and later served as director of public safety for Baltimore County. He died in 1988 and is buried at Arlington National Cemetery.
