Electrokinetics

Electrokinetics is the study of the motion of charged particles and liquids under the influence of an electric field, and of the related phenomena occurring at charged interfaces. It is a branch of colloid and interface science, concerned with the electrical double layer that forms where a solid meets an electrolyte.

Phenomena

The principal electrokinetic phenomena are electrophoresis (motion of charged particles through a liquid under an electric field), electro-osmosis (motion of liquid past a charged surface), streaming potential (a voltage generated when liquid flows past a charged surface), and sedimentation potential. All are governed by the charge distribution in the diffuse part of the double layer, characterised by the zeta potential at the slipping plane.

Theory

The classical theory was developed by Helmholtz, Gouy, Chapman and Smoluchowski. The Smoluchowski equation relates electrophoretic mobility to zeta potential for particles large compared with the double-layer thickness, while the Hückel equation applies in the opposite limit. Measurements of electrokinetic behaviour are used to assess the stability of colloids, since a high zeta potential generally prevents aggregation.

Applications

Electrokinetic methods are used in capillary electrophoresis and microfluidic "lab-on-a-chip" pumping, soil decontamination, water treatment, paper manufacture and enhanced oil recovery.
