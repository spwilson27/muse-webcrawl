The Curry–Howard correspondence is a direct relationship between computer programs and mathematical proofs: propositions correspond to types, and proofs of a proposition correspond to programs (terms) of the corresponding type. It is often summarised as "proofs are programs, and propositions are types."

History

Haskell Curry observed in the 1930s–1950s that the types of combinators in combinatory logic matched the axioms of intuitionistic implicational logic. William Alvin Howard made the correspondence precise in a 1969 manuscript (published in 1980), showing that natural deduction proofs correspond to terms of the simply typed lambda calculus.

Correspondences

Under the correspondence, implication corresponds to function types, conjunction to product types, disjunction to sum types, and the empty type to falsity. Proof normalisation corresponds to program evaluation. Extensions link classical logic to control operators and continuations, and dependent types to full predicate logic.

Impact

The correspondence underlies modern type theory and interactive proof assistants such as Coq, Agda and Lean, in which writing a proof and writing a program are the same activity, and it has deeply influenced the design of typed functional programming languages.
