Gullfoss

Gullfoss ("Golden Falls") is a waterfall located in the canyon of the Hvítá river in southwest Iceland. It is one of the most popular tourist attractions in the country and forms part of the Golden Circle route, together with Þingvellir National Park and the geysers of Haukadalur.

Description

The Hvítá, fed by meltwater from the Langjökull glacier, plunges in two stages into a rugged canyon about 2.5 kilometres long. The first cascade drops 11 metres and the second 21 metres, at an angle that makes the falls appear to vanish into the earth when viewed from above. On sunny days a rainbow frequently forms in the spray.

History

In the early twentieth century the falls were threatened by plans to exploit the river for hydroelectric power, in which foreign investors were involved. Sigríður Tómasdóttir, the daughter of the farmer who owned the land, campaigned vigorously against the scheme, walking barefoot to Reykjavík to protest and threatening to throw herself into the falls; the project was eventually abandoned, and she is widely celebrated as having saved Gullfoss. A stone memorial to her stands above the falls. The site was designated a nature reserve in 1979 and is now state-owned and protected.
