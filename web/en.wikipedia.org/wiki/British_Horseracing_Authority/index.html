British Horseracing Authority

The British Horseracing Authority (BHA) is the regulatory and governing body for horse racing in Great Britain. It was formed on 31 July 2007 through the merger of the British Horseracing Board (BHB) and the Horseracing Regulatory Authority (HRA), the latter formerly part of the Jockey Club.

The BHA regulates racing at Britain's 59 licensed racecourses.

Responsibilities

The BHA is responsible for the governance, administration and regulation of racing in Britain. Its duties include compiling the fixture list and race programme, setting and enforcing the Rules of Racing, licensing participants — trainers, jockeys, owners and racecourses — protecting the integrity of the sport through anti-doping and anti-corruption work, and safeguarding horse welfare. It also oversees the registration of thoroughbreds through the Weatherbys stud book arrangements.

History

For over two centuries racing was governed by the Jockey Club, founded around 1750. Regulatory functions passed to the Horseracing Regulatory Authority in 2006 and then to the BHA the following year, leaving the Jockey Club as a commercial organisation owning racecourses and estates. The BHA's headquarters are at 75 High Holborn, London.
