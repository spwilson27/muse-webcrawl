Walter A. Shewhart

Walter Andrew Shewhart (March 18, 1891 – March 11, 1967) was an American physicist, engineer and statistician, known as the father of statistical quality control and creator of the Shewhart cycle, also known as the plan–do–check–act (PDCA) cycle.

Career

Shewhart received his bachelor's and master's degrees from the University of Illinois and his doctorate in physics from the University of California, Berkeley in 1917. After brief teaching appointments he joined the engineering department of Western Electric in 1918, moving to Bell Telephone Laboratories when it was established in 1925, where he remained until his retirement in 1956.

On May 16, 1924, Shewhart produced the first control chart, a graphical method for distinguishing between variation caused by assignable (special) causes and variation inherent in a process (common causes). This insight became the foundation of statistical process control. His ideas are set out in his 1931 book Economic Control of Quality of Manufactured Product and in the posthumous lectures published as Statistical Method from the Viewpoint of Quality Control (1939), edited by W. Edwards Deming.

Influence

Shewhart's work profoundly influenced Deming and Joseph M. Juran, who carried statistical quality methods to post-war Japan. The Shewhart cycle — plan, do, check (or study), act — became a cornerstone of continuous improvement and total quality management. The American Society for Quality awards the Shewhart Medal in his honour.
