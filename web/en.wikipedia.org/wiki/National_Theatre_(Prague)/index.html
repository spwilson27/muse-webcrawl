National Theatre (Prague)

Národní divadlo
Location: Prague, Czech Republic
Architect: Josef Zítek; Josef Schulz
Opened: 11 June 1881; reopened 18 November 1883

The National Theatre (Czech: Národní divadlo) in Prague is the principal stage of the Czech Republic, known as the alma mater of Czech opera and a national symbol of the Czech National Revival. The neo-Renaissance building, designed by Josef Zítek, stands on the bank of the Vltava River opposite Prague Castle.

History

The theatre was funded by donations collected across the Czech lands — its foundation stones, laid in 1868, came from historic Czech sites. It opened on 11 June 1881 with the premiere of Bedřich Smetana's opera Libuše, written for the occasion. Weeks later the nearly completed building was devastated by fire; amid national mourning, a further collection raised the money to rebuild it within two years. The reconstructed theatre, designed by Josef Schulz, reopened on 18 November 1883, again with Libuše.

The institution

The National Theatre company today comprises opera, drama, and ballet ensembles, performing at the historic building, the Estates Theatre, the State Opera, and the New Stage (Nová scéna), a glass building completed in 1983. The auditorium's curtain by Vojtěch Hynais and ceiling paintings by František Ženíšek depict the nation's artistic history; above the proscenium is inscribed the motto Národ sobě — "The Nation to Itself".
