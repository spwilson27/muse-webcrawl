Theresa May

Theresa Mary May, Baroness May of Maidenhead (born 1 October 1956) is a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom and Leader of the Conservative Party from 2016 to 2019. She was previously Home Secretary from 2010 to 2016 — one of the longest-serving holders of that office in modern times — and Member of Parliament for Maidenhead from 1997 to 2024.

Prime ministership

May became prime minister after David Cameron resigned following the 2016 referendum vote to leave the European Union. She triggered Article 50 in March 2017 to begin Brexit, and called a snap election that June in which the Conservatives lost their majority and governed with support from the Democratic Unionist Party. Her negotiated withdrawal agreement was rejected three times by Parliament — the first defeat, by 230 votes, being the largest in modern parliamentary history — amid deadlocked divisions over Brexit. Facing a loss of confidence, she announced her resignation in May 2019 and was succeeded by Boris Johnson.

Earlier career

The daughter of a Church of England vicar, May worked at the Bank of England and the Association for Payment Clearing Services before entering Parliament. As Home Secretary she pursued strict immigration policies, including the "hostile environment" policy, and later faced criticism over the Windrush scandal.
