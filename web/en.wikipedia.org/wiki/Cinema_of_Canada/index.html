Cinema of Canada

The cinema of Canada comprises the film industry based in Canada. Canada has a significant, well-developed film industry dating to the early twentieth century, centred in Toronto, Montreal, and Vancouver. Canadian film is strongly shaped by public institutions — above all the National Film Board of Canada (NFB, founded 1939) — and by the constant cultural and commercial pull of neighbouring Hollywood. Two broadly distinct industries exist: English-language cinema and the French-language cinema of Quebec.

History

Canada's first films were produced in the late 1890s, and early feature production flourished briefly before American distribution dominance took hold. The NFB, under commissioner John Grierson, made Canada a world leader in documentary and animation; Norman McLaren's pioneering animation at the NFB won international acclaim. In the 1960s, the NFB's French unit and cinéma vérité filmmakers such as Michel Brault helped spark a distinct Quebec cinema. The 1970s "tax shelter" years produced a flood of commercial features, while the 1980s saw the rise of internationally celebrated auteurs.

Quebec cinema

Quebec's French-language industry sustains its own star system and box office. Landmarks include Denys Arcand's The Decline of the American Empire (1986) and The Barbarian Invasions (2003), which won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. More recent international successes include Denis Villeneuve's Incendies (2010) and Xavier Dolan's films.

Industry today

Toronto and Vancouver are major production centres for American film and television as well as domestic work, and the Toronto International Film Festival (TIFF), founded in 1976, is one of the world's most influential festivals. Notable Canadian directors include David Cronenberg, James Cameron, Atom Egoyan, Deepa Mehta, and Sarah Polley.
