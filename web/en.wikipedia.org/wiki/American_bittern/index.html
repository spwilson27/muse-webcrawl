American bittern

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The American bittern (Botaurus lentiginosus) is a species of wading bird in the heron family. It has a Nearctic distribution, breeding in Canada and the northern and central parts of the United States, and wintering in the U.S. Gulf Coast states, all of Florida into the Everglades, the Caribbean islands and parts of Central America.

Scientific name: Botaurus lentiginosus
Family: Ardeidae (herons)
Length: 58–85 cm
Wingspan: 92–115 cm
Conservation status: Least Concern (IUCN)

Description

The American bittern is a large, chunky, brown bird, 58–85 cm (23–33 in) in length with a wingspan of 92–115 cm (36–45 in). It is heavily streaked in brown and white, with a black streak down the side of the neck. Its camouflage allows it to freeze with its bill pointed upward, swaying with the marsh vegetation to avoid detection.

Behaviour

It is a well-camouflaged, solitary brown bird that unobtrusively inhabits marshes and the coarse vegetation at the edges of lakes and ponds. The male's distinctive call is a series of deep, resonant "pump-er-lunk" sounds produced by gulping air into the oesophagus, famously described as a "water-belching" or stake-driving sound. Bitterns feed mainly on fish, frogs, insects, and small mammals.

Breeding

The female builds a bulky nest of reeds and cattails in dense emergent vegetation above shallow water, incubating three to five eggs. The species is polygynous; males do not take part in rearing the young.
