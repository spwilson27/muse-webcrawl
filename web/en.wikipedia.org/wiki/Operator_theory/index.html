Operator theory

Operator theory is the study of linear operators on function spaces, beginning with differential operators and integral operators. The operators may be presented abstractly by their characteristics, such as bounded linear operators or closed operators, and consideration may include nonlinear operators. The study, which depends heavily on the topology of function spaces, is a branch of functional analysis.

Scope

If a collection of operators forms an algebra over a field, then it is an operator algebra. The description of operator algebras is part of operator theory. The central objects include operators on Hilbert spaces and Banach spaces, their spectra, and the C*-algebras and von Neumann algebras they generate.

Single operator theory

Single operator theory deals with the properties and classification of individual operators. For example, the classification of normal operators up to unitary equivalence is given by the spectral theorem, which expresses such an operator as an integral over its spectrum. Related topics include invariant subspaces, the functional calculus, and the theory of compact operators, whose spectral behaviour closely resembles the finite-dimensional case.

History

The subject grew out of work on integral equations by Fredholm and Hilbert in the early twentieth century, and was transformed by John von Neumann's work on operators on Hilbert space in the late 1920s, motivated in part by the mathematical foundations of quantum mechanics.
