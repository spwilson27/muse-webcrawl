Cíbola

Cíbola
Type: legendary "Seven Cities of Gold"
Region claimed: present-day New Mexico / Arizona
Key expeditions: Marcos de Niza (1539); Francisco Vázquez de Coronado (1540–42)
Actual places: Zuni pueblos, including Hawikuh

The Seven Cities of Cíbola were a mythical group of fabulously wealthy cities that Spanish explorers of the 16th century believed existed in the deserts of northern New Spain. Reports originating with the survivors of the Narváez expedition — Álvar Núñez Cabeza de Vaca and the enslaved explorer Esteban — told of rich settlements to the north, fuelling expeditions in search of gold comparable to that of the Aztec and Inca empires.

The Coronado expedition

In 1539 the Franciscan friar Marcos de Niza, guided by Esteban, reported seeing a great city from a distance; Esteban was killed at the Zuni pueblo of Hawikuh. On the strength of these reports, Viceroy Antonio de Mendoza sent Francisco Vázquez de Coronado with a large expedition in 1540. Coronado's men stormed Hawikuh and found an ordinary farming pueblo with no gold. The expedition went on to explore much of the present-day American Southwest and reached central Kansas chasing a further myth, Quivira, before returning to Mexico City in 1542.

Legacy

Although the treasure was illusory, the search for Cíbola produced the first European explorations of the Southwest and the Grand Canyon. The name "Cibola" survives in place names such as Cibola County, New Mexico, and the Cibola National Forest.
