Grand tourer

A grand tourer (GT) is a type of car designed for high speed and long-distance driving, combining performance with comfort and refinement. The term comes from the Italian gran turismo, itself echoing the English "Grand Tour" — the extended journeys undertaken by young aristocrats.

Characteristics

Grand tourers are typically front-engined, rear-wheel-drive two-door coupés with a 2+2 seating layout, powerful engines, and interiors trimmed for comfort over long journeys rather than the minimalism of pure sports cars. Classic examples include the Aston Martin DB series, the Ferrari 250 GT, the Jaguar E-Type and the Maserati GranTurismo.

Racing

"GT" also designates classes of production-based sports car racing, including the former FIA GT Championship and today's GT3 and GT World Challenge categories, in which road-derived cars compete in endurance and sprint events.
