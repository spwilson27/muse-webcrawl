The Subjection of Women is an essay by the English philosopher John Stuart Mill, published in 1869, arguing for the legal and social equality of women. It is one of the foundational texts of liberal feminism. Mill acknowledged the profound influence of his wife, Harriet Taylor Mill, whose own essay The Enfranchisement of Women (1851) anticipated many of its arguments.

Arguments

Mill argued that the legal subordination of women was "wrong in itself" and one of the chief hindrances to human improvement. He compared the position of women to slavery, contending that claims about women's "nature" were worthless because women had never been allowed to develop freely: what was called the nature of women was "an eminently artificial thing". He advocated complete equality in law — in marriage, property, education and employment — and supported women's suffrage, which he had proposed as a Member of Parliament in 1867.

Reception

Published at the height of the early women's suffrage movement, the essay was widely translated and debated. It influenced suffragists in Britain, the United States and beyond, and remains a classic statement of the case for gender equality grounded in utilitarian and liberal principles.
