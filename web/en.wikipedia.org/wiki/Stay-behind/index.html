Stay-behind

In military operations, stay-behind refers to a strategy in which a state prepares clandestine forces, arms caches and intelligence networks in its own territory, to be activated if an enemy occupies the country. The stay-behind units would then conduct guerrilla warfare, sabotage and espionage behind enemy lines.

Cold War networks

The most famous stay-behind system was organized in Western Europe during the Cold War under NATO coordination, in anticipation of a possible Warsaw Pact invasion. These secret networks existed in numerous countries and became widely known in 1990 when the Italian network, Operation Gladio, was publicly acknowledged by Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti, prompting parliamentary inquiries across Europe.

Earlier examples

Stay-behind preparations were also made during the Second World War, including the British Auxiliary Units formed in 1940 to operate after a hypothetical German invasion, and various resistance-support arrangements made by the Special Operations Executive.
