Digital radio

Digital radio is the use of digital technology to transmit or receive across the radio spectrum. Digital transmission by radio waves includes digital broadcasting, and especially digital audio radio services. In digital broadcasting systems, the analog audio signal is digitized, compressed using an audio coding format such as AAC+ or MP2, and transmitted using a digital modulation scheme.

Systems

  • DAB and DAB+ (Digital Audio Broadcasting) — the dominant terrestrial digital radio standard in Europe and Australia, using COFDM in Band III and L-band.
  • HD Radio — the in-band on-channel system authorised in the United States, transmitting digital sidebands alongside analog AM/FM.
  • DRM (Digital Radio Mondiale) — for long-, medium- and short-wave bands.
  • ISDB-Tsb and DMB — used in Japan and South Korea respectively.
  • Satellite radio — such as Sirius XM in North America.

Advantages and drawbacks

Digital radio offers more stations in the same spectrum, consistent reception quality within coverage, and ancillary data such as programme information, text and images. Against this, reception tends to fail abruptly at the edge of coverage (the "digital cliff") rather than degrade gracefully; early DAB using MP2 audio was criticised for poor sound quality at low bit rates, a problem largely addressed by DAB+ with HE-AAC. Some countries, notably Norway (2017) and Switzerland (2024), have switched off national FM networks in favour of DAB+, while others maintain both.

History

DAB was developed under the European Eureka 147 project from the late 1980s; the BBC and other European broadcasters launched regular services in 1995. Adoption was initially slow due to receiver cost, but cheap receivers and car fitment drove growth in the 2010s.
