Château de Blois

The Royal Château de Blois (French: Château royal de Blois) is a château located in the city center of Blois, in the Loir-et-Cher department of the Loire Valley, France. The residence of several French kings, it is also the place where Joan of Arc went in 1429 to be blessed by the Archbishop of Reims before departing with her army to drive the English from Orléans.

History

The château effectively comprises several buildings constructed from the 13th to the 17th century around a main courtyard. Its most famous piece of architecture is the main staircase, a magnificent spiral structure in an octagonal tower on the François I wing, decorated with Renaissance carvings and the royal salamander emblem.

The medieval fortress of the counts of Blois was transformed over the centuries. Louis XII, born at Blois, rebuilt parts of it in the late Gothic style in the early 1500s. His successor François I added the celebrated Renaissance wing between 1515 and 1524. Catherine de' Medici died at the château in 1589, and it was here in 1588 that Henri I, Duke of Guise, was assassinated on the orders of King Henri III — one of the most dramatic episodes of the French Wars of Religion.

Architecture

The château is a textbook of French architecture: the 13th-century Salle des États (Hall of the Estates) survives from the medieval fortress; the Louis XII wing (1498–1503) is late Gothic with brick-and-stone patterning; the François I wing (1515–1524) is early French Renaissance; and the Gaston d'Orléans wing (1635–1638), designed by François Mansart, is an early example of French Classicism.

Museum

Today the château houses the Musée des Beaux-Arts de Blois, with collections of painting and sculpture from the 16th to the 19th century, and hosts a sound-and-light show recounting its history.
