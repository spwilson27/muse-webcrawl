Ubisoft Entertainment SA is a French video game publisher headquartered in Saint-Mandé. Founded in 1986 by the five Guillemot brothers, it grew from a distributor of imported games into one of the largest game publishers in the world, with development studios across the globe, most notably Ubisoft Montreal. The company is best known for the Assassin's Creed, Far Cry, Tom Clancy's (including Rainbow Six and Ghost Recon), Just Dance, Rayman, and Watch Dogs franchises.

Ubisoft's headquarters. The company is one of Europe's largest video game publishers.

History

The Guillemot family founded Ubisoft in 1986, initially selling games by mail order and distribution in France before moving into development and publishing. Early success came with Rayman (1995), designed by Michel Ancel. The company expanded internationally in the 1990s and 2000s, opening Ubisoft Montreal in 1997, which became its flagship studio. Licensing the Tom Clancy name and acquiring studios such as Red Storm Entertainment broadened its catalogue. In the 2010s it resisted a hostile takeover attempt by Vivendi, which ultimately sold its stake in 2018.

Operations and criticism

Ubisoft operates more than forty studios and subsidiaries on several continents and runs the Ubisoft+ subscription service. From 2020 the company faced widespread reporting of workplace misconduct at several studios, leading to executive departures and announced reforms. Critical discussion has also targeted the perceived formulaic design of its open-world games.
