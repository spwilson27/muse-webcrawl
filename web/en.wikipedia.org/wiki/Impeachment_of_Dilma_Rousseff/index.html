Impeachment of Dilma Rousseff

The Senate votes on the impeachment, August 2016

The impeachment of Dilma Rousseff, the 36th president of Brazil, began on 2 December 2015 with a petition for her impeachment being accepted by Eduardo Cunha, then president of the Chamber of Deputies, and culminated on 31 August 2016, when the Federal Senate voted 61–20 in favour of convicting her of crimes of responsibility and removing her from office.

Background

Rousseff, of the Workers' Party (PT), was re-elected in the close 2014 presidential election. Her second term was marked by a severe economic recession, the sprawling Petrobras corruption scandal (Operation Car Wash), and mass street protests. The impeachment charges centred on the practice known as pedaladas fiscais — the alleged manipulation of the federal budget by delaying Treasury transfers to state banks to make public accounts look healthier — and on issuing supplementary credit decrees without congressional approval, in breach of the Fiscal Responsibility Law.

Process

The Chamber of Deputies voted 367–137 on 17 April 2016 to authorise the Senate to try the charges. On 12 May the Senate voted to open the trial and suspend Rousseff from office for up to 180 days, with Vice President Michel Temer becoming acting president. Rousseff defended herself before the Senate on 29 August, and two days later the Senate convicted her. In a separate vote, senators declined to bar her from public office for eight years. Temer was sworn in as president the same day.

Reactions

Rousseff and her supporters described the impeachment as a parliamentary coup, arguing that the fiscal practices were used by previous presidents and did not constitute crimes of responsibility. Opponents maintained that the process followed the constitution and that she had lost the political conditions to govern. The episode deepened Brazil's political polarisation in the years that followed.
