Justin Drew Bieber (born 1 March 1994) is a Canadian singer and songwriter. Regarded as a prominent figure in contemporary popular music, he was discovered through YouTube videos in 2008 by talent manager Scooter Braun and signed to RBMG Records, releasing his debut EP My World in 2009.

Career

Bieber's debut studio album My World 2.0 (2010) spawned the international hit "Baby" and made him the youngest solo male artist to top the Billboard 200 in 47 years. Under the Mistletoe (2011) and Believe (2012) consolidated his teen-idol fame. After a period of public controversy, he re-emerged with Purpose (2015), whose singles "What Do You Mean?", "Sorry" and "Love Yourself" topped charts worldwide and shifted his sound toward electronic dance and tropical house. He collaborated on the record-breaking remix of "Despacito" (2017), and released Changes (2020) and Justice (2021), the latter featuring "Peaches" and "Stay" (with the Kid Laroi).

Artistry and impact

Bieber's music blends pop, R&B and EDM; his voice and image evolved from boyish teen pop to adult contemporary pop. He is among the best-selling music artists of all time, with estimated sales of over 150 million records, and has won two Grammy Awards, a Latin Grammy, and numerous American Music and Billboard Music Awards. In 2022 he revealed he had been diagnosed with Ramsay Hunt syndrome, which temporarily paralysed part of his face and led him to cancel touring.

Personal life

Bieber was raised in Stratford, Ontario. He married model Hailey Baldwin in 2018; their son was born in 2024. He is an outspoken Christian and one of the most-followed people on social media.
