Metropolitan District Railway

The Metropolitan District Railway, commonly known as the District Railway, was a passenger railway that served London from 1868 to 1933. It was created to complete, with the Metropolitan Railway, the "inner circle" (now the Circle line) linking London's main-line termini, and it built lines west and south-west of the capital that form the core of today's District line.

History

The company's first section opened on 24 December 1868 between South Kensington and Westminster, with trains operated by the Metropolitan Railway until the District acquired its own rolling stock. Relations between the two companies, dominated on the District side by chairman James Staats Forbes, were notoriously hostile even as they jointly completed the Circle in 1884. Extensions reached Hammersmith, Putney Bridge, Richmond, Ealing Broadway, Hounslow, Wimbledon, and eastward to Whitechapel and Upminster.

Electrification and the Underground Group

Financially weak and still running steam trains, the District was rescued by the American financier Charles Yerkes, whose Underground Electric Railways Company of London took control in 1901–02. The lines were electrified with a fourth-rail DC system and electric multiple units replaced steam in 1905. In 1933 the District passed to the London Passenger Transport Board, becoming part of London Underground.
