The IMSA SportsCar Championship, currently known as the IMSA WeatherTech SportsCar Championship for sponsorship reasons, is a sports car racing series based in the United States and Canada and organised by the International Motor Sports Association (IMSA). It was created in 2014 from the merger of the two rival North American sports car series, the American Le Mans Series and the Grand-Am Rolex Sports Car Series, unifying top-level endurance racing on the continent.

History

Following the 2012 announcement that NASCAR-owned Grand-Am would acquire the American Le Mans Series, the two series ran jointly in 2013 and merged for 2014 as the Tudor United SportsCar Championship, later renamed under WeatherTech sponsorship from 2016. The championship is anchored by the Rolex 24 at Daytona, the 12 Hours of Sebring, and Petit Le Mans at Road Atlanta, which together also form the Michelin Endurance Cup.

Classes

Cars compete in several classes simultaneously. The premier class since 2023 is GTP (Grand Touring Prototype), for LMDh hybrid prototypes — built by manufacturers including Porsche, Cadillac, Acura, BMW, and Lamborghini — which are eligible to race both in IMSA and in the FIA World Endurance Championship's Hypercar class, including at Le Mans. LMP2 is a pro-am prototype class using the global Oreca 07 chassis. Production-based GT cars race in GTD Pro (professional line-ups) and GTD (with amateur drivers), both using FIA GT3 machinery. An earlier top class, Daytona Prototype international (DPi), ran from 2017 to 2022.

Format

Races range from 100-minute sprint events to the 24 hours of Daytona. Most entries are shared by two or more drivers, and championships are awarded for drivers, teams, and manufacturers in each class. Balance of Performance adjustments are used to equalise the pace of different cars within a class.
