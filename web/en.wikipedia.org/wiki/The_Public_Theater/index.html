The Public Theater

Founded: 1954 (as the Shakespeare Workshop)
Founder: Joseph Papp
Location: Astor Place, New York City

The Public Theater is a nonprofit American theater company in New York City, founded by producer Joseph Papp. It is best known for producing Shakespeare in the Park, the free summer performances at the Delacorte Theater in Central Park, and for premiering landmark works of American musical theater.

History

Papp founded the Shakespeare Workshop in 1954 to make theater accessible to all. In 1967 the company took over the former Astor Library building on Lafayette Street, opening it as The Public Theater with the world premiere of the rock musical Hair. Under Papp and later artistic directors — JoAnne Akalaitis, George C. Wolfe, and Oskar Eustis (from 2005) — the Public developed a mission of producing new work by diverse writers.

Notable productions

Productions that began at the Public include A Chorus Line (1975), For Colored Girls Who Have Considered Suicide / When the Rainbow Is Enuf, The Normal Heart, Topdog/Underdog, Fun Home, and Hamilton (2015), many transferring to Broadway and winning Tony Awards and Pulitzer Prizes.

Shakespeare in the Park

Free Shakespeare at the open-air Delacorte Theater in Central Park has run since 1962 and remains one of New York's signature summer traditions.
