Jülich

Jülich (Dutch: Gulik) is a town in the district of Düren, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. It lies on the river Rur and has a population of roughly 33,000. Historically it was the capital of the Duchy of Jülich, one of the states of the Holy Roman Empire.

History

Jülich originated as a Roman settlement, Juliacum, on the road between Cologne and Boulogne. In the 16th century Duke William V rebuilt it as an ideal Renaissance fortress city, with a citadel designed in the Italian style that remains one of the best-preserved examples north of the Alps. The War of the Jülich Succession (1609–1614) over the inheritance of the united duchies was an early episode in the tensions leading to the Thirty Years' War.

Modern Jülich

The town was heavily damaged in the Second World War — on 16 November 1944 it was almost completely destroyed in an air raid — and rebuilt afterwards. Today Jülich is known for the Forschungszentrum Jülich, one of Europe's largest interdisciplinary research centres, and for its sugar industry.
