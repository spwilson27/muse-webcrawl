Andrei Rublev (c. 1360 – c. 1430) was a Russian monk and painter, regarded as the greatest master of medieval Russian icon painting. Almost nothing is documented of his life: he is first mentioned in 1405 painting the Cathedral of the Annunciation in the Moscow Kremlin alongside Theophanes the Greek and Prokhor of Gorodets, and he was a monk of the Trinity Lavra of St Sergius and later of the Andronikov Monastery in Moscow, where he died and was buried.

Works

His most famous work is the icon of the Holy Trinity (The Old Testament Trinity, c. 1411 or 1425–1427, now in the Tretyakov Gallery), painted for the Trinity Lavra: three angels seated about a table with a chalice, rendered in harmonious, luminous colour and universally considered a summit of Russian art. With his collaborator Daniil Cherny he painted the frescoes and iconostasis of the Dormition Cathedral in Vladimir (1408), and he is associated with the icons of the Zvenigorod Deesis tier, including the celebrated Saviour of Zvenigorod.

Legacy

Rublev's serene, contemplative style set the standard invoked by the Stoglav Council (1551) as the model for icon painters. He was canonised by the Russian Orthodox Church in 1988. Andrei Tarkovsky's 1966 film Andrei Rublev imagines his life against the turmoil of 15th-century Russia.
