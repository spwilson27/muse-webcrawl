Lathyrus sativus

Common names: Grass pea, chickling pea, Indian pea, kesari dal
Family: Fabaceae

Lathyrus sativus, also known as grass pea, is a legume grown for human consumption and livestock feed in Asia and East Africa. It is remarkably hardy, surviving drought and waterlogging that kill other crops, and fixes nitrogen in poor soils.

Cultivation and use

The seeds are eaten as dal, flour and flatbreads in India, Bangladesh, Ethiopia and the Mediterranean. Because it yields food in famine years when little else grows, it has been called as a kind of insurance crop, cultivated since Neolithic times.

Toxicity

The seeds contain the neurotoxin ODAP (β-N-oxalyl-L-α,β-diaminopropionic acid). Heavy, prolonged consumption—especially when grass pea dominates the diet during famine—causes neurolathyrism, an irreversible spastic paralysis of the legs. Breeding low-toxin varieties and processing methods such as soaking and parboiling reduce risk; some Indian states have restricted sale, though the crop remains vital for poor farmers.
