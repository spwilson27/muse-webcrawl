Institute for Justice

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Institute for Justice litigates public-interest cases, including before the U.S. Supreme Court.

The Institute for Justice (IJ) is a libertarian public-interest law firm in the United States. Founded in 1991 by Chip Mellor and Clint Bolick, it is headquartered in Arlington, Virginia, and litigates — typically free of charge for its clients — in four pillars: private property rights, economic liberty, free speech, and school choice.

Notable cases

IJ represented Susette Kelo in Kelo v. City of New London (2005), the eminent-domain case whose loss triggered nationwide reform legislation. It won Swedenburg v. Kelly (2005), striking down state bans on direct wine shipment, and Zelman v. Simmons-Harris (2002), upholding Cleveland's school voucher program. It has repeatedly challenged occupational-licensing rules (for casket sellers, hair braiders, and food-truck operators), campaigned against civil asset forfeiture, and litigated against fines-and-fees practices and "policing for profit."

Activities

Beyond litigation, IJ publishes empirical studies (such as its reports on forfeiture and licensing burdens), trains law students through its clinic partnerships and the Institute for Justice Clinic on Entrepreneurship at the University of Chicago, and runs legislative campaigns in statehouses. Its donor base includes foundations associated with free-market philanthropy.

Criticism

Critics on the left argue IJ's economic-liberty agenda echoes the discredited Lochner era and can harm consumers, while some conservatives have objected to particular positions; IJ describes its work as defending the rights of politically powerless individuals against government overreach regardless of ideology.
