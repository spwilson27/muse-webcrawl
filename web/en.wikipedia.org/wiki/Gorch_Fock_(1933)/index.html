Gorch Fock (1933)

Gorch Fock is a German Navy school ship, a three-masted barque launched in 1933. She was the first of the Gorch Fock-class sail training vessels, named after the German writer Gorch Fock (the pen name of Johann Wilhelm Kinau), who died at the Battle of Jutland in 1916.

Built by Blohm+Voss in Hamburg, the ship was launched on 3 May 1933. She served as a training ship for naval cadets of the Kriegsmarine. Near the end of the Second World War, on 1 May 1945, she was scuttled by her crew near Stralsund to prevent her capture by Soviet forces.

The wreck was raised by the Soviet Union in 1947 and, after repairs, entered service with the Soviet Navy in 1951 under the name Tovarishch (Russian for "Comrade"). After the dissolution of the Soviet Union she sailed under the Ukrainian flag. In 2003 she was acquired by a German association, returned to Stralsund, and renamed Gorch Fock again. She is now a museum ship moored in Stralsund harbour.

Her sister ships include the USCGC Eagle (ex-Horst Wessel), Sagres, Mircea, and the Gorch Fock of 1958, which still serves the modern German Navy.
