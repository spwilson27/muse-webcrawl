Anthraquinone process

Anthraquinone process
TypeIndustrial chemical process
ProductHydrogen peroxide

The anthraquinone process, also called the Riedl–Pfleiderer process, is the dominant industrial method for producing hydrogen peroxide, accounting for the great majority of world production.

Process

A 2-alkylanthraquinone "working compound" dissolved in a mixture of solvents is hydrogenated over a catalyst to the corresponding anthrahydroquinone. The solution is then oxidised with air, which regenerates the anthraquinone and releases hydrogen peroxide. The peroxide is extracted with water and concentrated by distillation, while the working solution is recycled.

Advantages

The process avoids directly combining hydrogen and oxygen, which would be explosive, and runs continuously with the quinone acting as a recyclable carrier.
