Loam

Loam is a soil composed of a roughly balanced mixture of sand, silt and clay particles — conventionally about 40% sand, 40% silt and 20% clay. Because it combines the drainage of sandy soils with the moisture and nutrient retention of clay, loam is widely considered the ideal medium for gardening and agriculture.

Properties

Loam is fertile, easy to work ("friable"), and holds moisture and nutrients without becoming waterlogged. Its balance of particle sizes gives it a crumbly structure that resists compaction and allows roots to penetrate readily. Soil science classifies loams more precisely on the soil texture triangle, with subtypes such as sandy loam, silt loam, clay loam and silty clay loam depending on which fraction predominates.

Uses

Beyond agriculture, loam has historically been used as a building material: mixed with straw it forms cob and adobe, and it has been used for plasters, daub and the moulds of foundries (loam moulding). The term is also applied to the rich dark soils of certain river valleys and glacial deposits.
