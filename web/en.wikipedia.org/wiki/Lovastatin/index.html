Lovastatin

Lovastatin

Trade name: Mevacor, others

Class: Statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor)

Use: Lowering cholesterol; preventing cardiovascular disease

Source: Originally isolated from Aspergillus terreus

Lovastatin, sold under the brand name Mevacor among others, is a statin medication, to treat high blood cholesterol and reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease. Its use is recommended together with dietary changes, exercise, and weight loss. It is taken by mouth.

History

Lovastatin was isolated from the fungus Aspergillus terreus and was the first statin approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, in 1987. It is a naturally occurring compound found in oyster mushrooms and red yeast rice. The semi-synthetic derivative simvastatin was developed from it. Lovastatin opened the era of statin therapy, which became one of the most widely prescribed classes of drugs in the world.

Mechanism of action

Lovastatin is administered as an inactive lactone prodrug and hydrolysed in the body to the active β-hydroxy acid, a potent competitive inhibitor of HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol biosynthesis in the liver. Reduced hepatic cholesterol synthesis upregulates LDL receptors, increasing clearance of LDL cholesterol from the blood.

Side effects

Common side effects include constipation, headaches, nausea and muscle pain. Serious side effects may include liver problems, muscle breakdown (rhabdomyolysis) and kidney failure. It should not be used in pregnancy. Grapefruit juice increases its blood levels by inhibiting its metabolism.
