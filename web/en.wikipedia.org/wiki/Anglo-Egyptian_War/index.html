LocationEgypt

The Anglo-Egyptian War (Arabic: الاحتلال البريطاني لمصر) occurred in 1882 between Egyptian and Sudanese forces under Ahmed Urabi and the United Kingdom. It ended a nationalist uprising against the Khedive Tewfik Pasha and began the British occupation of Egypt, which in various forms lasted until the 1950s.

Background

Egypt under Khedive Ismail had fallen heavily into debt to European creditors; after his deposition in 1879, Britain and France imposed financial control. The Urabi movement, led by Colonel Ahmed Urabi, demanded constitutional limits on the Khedive's power and an end to foreign domination. Anti-European riots in Alexandria in June 1882, in which dozens of Europeans were killed, precipitated intervention.

The war

On 11 July 1882 a British fleet under Admiral Sir Beauchamp Seymour bombarded Alexandria's forts. An expeditionary force under General Sir Garnet Wolseley landed in August, seized the Suez Canal zone, and on 13 September routed Urabi's army at the Battle of Tel el-Kebir in a dawn attack after a night march. Cairo was occupied the following day and Urabi was exiled to Ceylon. Though Britain repeatedly promised a swift withdrawal, its "temporary" occupation became permanent, formalised as a protectorate in 1914.
