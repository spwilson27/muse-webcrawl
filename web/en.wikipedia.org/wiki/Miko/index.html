Miko

A miko (巫女) is a young female attendant at a Shinto shrine in Japan. Historically, miko were shamans and spirit mediums who performed divination and channelled kami; today they primarily assist priests in ceremonies, perform sacred kagura dances, conduct rituals and sell omikuji fortunes and amulets at shrines, especially during New Year.

History

In ancient Japan, miko acted as mediums (yorimashi) delivering oracles while possessed by kami, and as priestesses at court and clan shrines; legend credits the shaman-queen Himiko of Yamatai. During the medieval period many miko became itinerant performers and diviners. The Meiji government banned spirit-medium practices in 1873 and regularised miko as shrine assistants under male priests (kannushi).

Modern role

Contemporary miko — often students working part-time — sweep grounds, staff shrine offices, assist at weddings and festivals, and perform miko-mai dances with bells, fans and scarves. They traditionally wear a white upper garment and scarlet hakama. Training may include kagura choreography and ritual etiquette at large shrines such as Ise and Fushimi Inari.

In popular culture

Miko appear widely in manga, anime and games — from shrine-maiden heroines to characters like Rei Hino in Sailor Moon — shaping the international image of the role.
