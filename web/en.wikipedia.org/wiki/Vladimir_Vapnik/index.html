Vladimir Naumovich Vapnik (born 6 December 1936) is a Russian-American computer scientist and statistician, one of the principal founders of statistical learning theory. With Alexey Chervonenkis he developed the Vapnik–Chervonenkis (VC) theory of empirical processes, including the VC dimension, and he is the principal inventor of the support-vector machine (SVM), one of the most influential machine learning algorithms of the 1990s and 2000s.

Career

Vapnik studied mathematics at Uzbek State University in Samarkand and completed his doctorate at the Institute of Control Sciences in Moscow, where he worked from 1961 to 1990, rising to head the Computer Science Research Department. There, with Chervonenkis, he formulated the theory of uniform convergence of empirical measures and the concept of VC dimension, which quantifies the capacity of a class of functions and bounds the generalisation error of learning algorithms.

In 1990 he emigrated to the United States and joined AT&T Bell Laboratories, where with Bernhard Boser and Isabelle Guyon he developed the support-vector machine, grounded in his principle of structural risk minimisation. His books, including The Nature of Statistical Learning Theory (1995), became standard references. He later worked at NEC Laboratories America, became a professor at Columbia University, and in 2014 joined Facebook's AI research group (FAIR). He has continued to develop extensions of learning theory, including the theory of learning using privileged information (LUPI).

Honours

Vapnik was elected to the US National Academy of Engineering and has received numerous awards for his contributions to learning theory, including the IEEE John von Neumann Medal (2017) and the Kampe de Feriet Award. The SVM and VC theory remain foundational in machine learning education and in fields from bioinformatics to computer vision.
