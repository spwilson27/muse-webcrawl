Icacinales

Icacinales is an order of flowering plants in the asterid clade of the eudicots. It was formally recognized in the APG IV classification system published in 2016, which established the order to accommodate two families whose relationships had previously been unresolved: the Icacinaceae and the Oncothecaceae.

Families

The order contains two families:

  • Icacinaceae — a family of mostly tropical trees, shrubs, and lianas, comprising roughly two dozen genera and around 150–200 species in modern circumscriptions (genera such as Icacina, Merrilliodendron, and Miquelia). Historically the family was much larger, but molecular studies showed it to be polyphyletic, and many former members were moved to other families and orders.
  • Oncothecaceae — a small family containing only the genus Oncotheca, with a few species endemic to New Caledonia.

Placement

Within the asterids, Icacinales is placed among the lamiid-grade lineages (the "basal lamiids"), near the orders Garryales, Metteniusales, and Vahliales, though the exact relationships among these small orders have been difficult to resolve. Plants of the order are largely woody species of tropical forests, of limited economic importance, though some Icacinaceae produce edible fruits or tubers locally.
