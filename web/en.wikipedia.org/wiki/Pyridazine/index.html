Pyridazine

Pyridazine
Formula: C4H4N2
Molar mass: 80.09 g/mol
Melting point: −8 °C
Boiling point: 208 °C

Pyridazine is a heterocyclic organic compound with the molecular formula C4H4N2. It is a six-membered aromatic ring containing two adjacent nitrogen atoms (the 1,2-positions), and is one of the three diazines, alongside pyrimidine (1,3) and pyrazine (1,4). Pure pyridazine is a colourless liquid with a pyridine-like odour.

Chemistry

Unlike pyridine, which is common, pyridazine itself is rare in nature, though the pyridazine scaffold appears in pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals — for example in the cardiotonic drug levosimendan, the antihistamine azelastine (a phthalazinone), hydralazine (a phthalazine), and in herbicidal compounds. The ring is markedly electron-deficient and participates in inverse-electron-demand Diels–Alder reactions ("click-release" chemistry) with strained alkenes.

Synthesis

Pyridazine is commonly prepared from maleic anhydride or fumaric acid derivatives: reaction with hydrazine gives maleic hydrazide, which is chlorinated to 3,6-dichloropyridazine and then hydrogenated to the parent compound. Condensation of hydrazine with 1,4-dicarbonyl compounds is another standard route to substituted pyridazines.
