Châtelet station

An entrance to Châtelet station near Place du Châtelet

Châtelet (French: [ʃɑtlɛ]) is a station of the Paris Métro and of the Paris Réseau Express Régional (RER), serving central Paris. Named after the Place du Châtelet, it is one of the largest underground stations in the world, and is connected by a long underground corridor to the Châtelet–Les Halles RER station, forming one of the largest metro stations anywhere.

Services

The Métro station serves lines 1, 4, 7, 11, and 14. Through the connecting corridor, passengers can transfer to RER lines A, B, and D at Châtelet–Les Halles. The complex lies beneath the Forum des Halles district in the 1st arrondissement, close to the Seine, the Île de la Cité, and the Hôtel de Ville.

History

The first platforms opened on August 6, 1900, shortly after the inauguration of Line 1. The station expanded as additional lines arrived through the early twentieth century, and the RER connection opened in 1977. The station is notorious among Parisians for its long transfer corridors, moving walkways, and labyrinthine signage. Extensive renovation works have modernised the complex in recent decades.
