Whiplash is a 2014 American psychological drama film written and directed by Damien Chazelle, starring Miles Teller as Andrew Neiman, a young jazz drummer enrolled at a fictional New York conservatory, and J. K. Simmons as Terence Fletcher, his abusive and perfectionist instructor. The film explores the psychological toll of Fletcher's relentless, manipulative teaching methods and Andrew's obsessive drive to succeed.

Plot

Andrew Neiman, a first-year student at the Shaffer Conservatory, is recruited by conductor Terence Fletcher into the school's top studio jazz band. Fletcher's methods include profanity-laced tirades, intimidation, and psychological games, including an incident in which he hurls a chair at Andrew for failing to keep tempo. After a competition performance in which Andrew, deliberately set up by Fletcher, is humiliated, he attacks Fletcher on stage and is expelled. Months later Andrew encounters Fletcher playing piano in a jazz club; Fletcher invites him to perform at a festival concert, only to sabotage him by giving the band an unfamiliar piece. Andrew recovers mid-performance and seizes control of the band, culminating in an extended drum solo that finally earns Fletcher's approval.

Reception and awards

The film premiered at the 2014 Sundance Film Festival, where it won the Grand Jury Prize and the Audience Award for drama. At the 87th Academy Awards it won three Oscars, including Best Supporting Actor for Simmons, Best Film Editing, and Best Sound Mixing. It is widely regarded as one of the finest films of the 2010s.
