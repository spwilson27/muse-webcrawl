Hasso Plattner (born 21 January 1944) is a German businessman and philanthropist, best known as a co-founder of the software company SAP SE. He served as SAP's co-chief executive and long-time supervisory board chairman, and was the driving force behind SAP HANA, the company's in-memory database platform.

Career

Plattner studied communications engineering at the University of Karlsruhe and began his career at IBM in Mannheim. In 1972 he left IBM with four colleagues — Dietmar Hopp, Claus Wellenreuther, Hans-Werner Hector and Klaus Tschira — to found the company that became SAP. He played a central technical role in the development of SAP's enterprise software, from the early R/2 system to the R/3 client-server suite.

Philanthropy and other ventures

In 1998 Plattner founded the Hasso Plattner Institute for software systems engineering at the University of Potsdam, and later supported a sister institute at Stanford University, home to its School of Design. His charitable foundation funded the Museum Barberini art museum in Potsdam, opened in 2017. Plattner is also the majority owner of the San Jose Sharks ice hockey team and a keen competitive sailor.
