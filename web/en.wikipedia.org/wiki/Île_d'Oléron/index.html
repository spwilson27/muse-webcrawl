Île d'Oléron

Île d'Oléron (French pronunciation: [il dɔleʁɔ̃]) is an island off the Atlantic coast of France, in the department of Charente-Maritime, Nouvelle-Aquitaine. At about 174 km², it is the second-largest island of metropolitan France, after Corsica. It is connected to the mainland by the Pont de l'île d'Oléron, one of the longest bridges in France when it opened in 1966.

Geography

The island lies off the west coast of France, just south of the Île d'Aix and the port of La Rochelle, and north of the Gironde estuary. Its western shore faces the open Atlantic, while its eastern side is sheltered by the mainland and the Pertuis d'Antioche strait. The terrain is low and flat, a mosaic of pine forest, vineyards, salt marshes and oyster beds. The island's northern tip is marked by the Chassiron lighthouse.

History

Oléron is famous in legal history for the Rolls of Oléron (Rôles d'Oléron), a celebrated code of maritime law compiled in the 12th century, traditionally associated with Eleanor of Aquitaine. The code, governing shipping, cargo and the rights of sailors, became a model for maritime law across northern Europe. During the Wars of Religion the island was contested between Catholic and Protestant forces, and its citadel at Le Château-d'Oléron was fortified under Cardinal Richelieu and later Vauban. In April 1945, near the end of the Second World War, Allied forces landed on the island in Operation Jupiter to liberate it from German occupation.

Economy

The island is renowned for its oysters, marketed as Marennes-Oléron, which are refined in the shallow clay ponds (claires) of the Seudre estuary and prized for their green-tinged gills and delicate flavour. Tourism is the other mainstay: beaches, cycling paths, fishing ports such as La Cotinière, and the salt marshes worked by sauniers (salt harvesters) draw large numbers of summer visitors.

Communes

The island comprises eight communes, including Saint-Pierre-d'Oléron, Saint-Georges-d'Oléron, Le Château-d'Oléron, Dolus-d'Oléron, Saint-Denis-d'Oléron and Saint-Trojan-les-Bains. The novelist Pierre Loti is buried in the garden of his family house at Saint-Pierre-d'Oléron.
