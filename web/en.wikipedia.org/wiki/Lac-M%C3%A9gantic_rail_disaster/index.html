Lac-Mégantic rail disaster
Date: 6 July 2013
Location: Lac-Mégantic, Quebec, Canada
Deaths: 47

The Lac-Mégantic rail disaster occurred in the town of Lac-Mégantic, Quebec, Canada, on 6 July 2013, when an unattended freight train carrying Bakken Formation crude oil rolled down a grade and derailed downtown. Multiple tank cars exploded and caught fire, killing 47 people and destroying much of the town centre. It is one of the deadliest rail accidents in Canadian history.

Causes

The Montreal, Maine and Atlantic Railway train had been left unattended overnight on the main line. A locomotive fire led to the shutdown of the engine supplying air to the brakes, and the insufficiently applied hand brakes could not hold the train. The Transportation Safety Board of Canada identified numerous safety deficiencies, and the disaster prompted major regulatory reforms in the transport of flammable liquids by rail in Canada and the United States.

Aftermath

The railway went bankrupt; the town's core was rebuilt after extensive decontamination, and a rail bypass around Lac-Mégantic was later approved.
