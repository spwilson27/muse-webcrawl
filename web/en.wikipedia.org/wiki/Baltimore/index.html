Baltimore

Baltimore is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Maryland, with a population of about 585,000 at the 2020 census. An independent city on the Patapsco River near Chesapeake Bay, it was founded in 1729 and named for Cecil Calvert, 2nd Baron Baltimore, proprietor of the Maryland colony.

History

Baltimore grew as a grain-exporting port. During the War of 1812, the British attack on Fort McHenry in 1814 inspired Francis Scott Key to write "The Star-Spangled Banner", later the U.S. national anthem. The city was a centre of shipbuilding (Baltimore clippers), railroading (the B&O Railroad, chartered 1827), and immigration. The Great Baltimore Fire of 1904 destroyed much of downtown, which was rebuilt to modern fire codes. Industrial decline and suburbanization drove population loss from a 1950 peak near 950,000, while the 1980s Inner Harbor redevelopment made tourism a pillar alongside health care and education.

Economy and culture

Major employers include Johns Hopkins University and Johns Hopkins Hospital. The Port of Baltimore is among the busiest U.S. ports for roll-on/roll-off cargo and automobiles; in March 2024 the Francis Scott Key Bridge collapsed after being struck by the container ship Dali, killing six workers and closing the channel for months. Cultural landmarks include the National Aquarium, the Walters Art Museum, and Edgar Allan Poe's grave. Sports teams include the NFL's Ravens and MLB's Orioles.

Neighbourhoods and demographics

The city comprises hundreds of distinctive neighbourhoods, from Federal Hill and Fells Point to Hampden and Charles Village. Baltimore has a majority Black population and a history of redlining extensively studied by historians. See also Washington, D.C., about 60 km southwest.
