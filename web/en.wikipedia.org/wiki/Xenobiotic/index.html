Xenobiotic

A xenobiotic is a chemical substance found within an organism that is not naturally produced by or normally present in that organism. The term covers synthetic compounds such as drugs, pesticides, industrial chemicals and food additives, as well as some natural compounds when they occur in an organism in which they are not ordinarily found. The word derives from the Greek xenos (stranger, foreign) and bios (life).

Metabolism

Organisms remove xenobiotics through a process called xenobiotic metabolism, concentrated in the liver. It is usually described in three phases: Phase I reactions (often performed by cytochrome P450 enzymes) modify the compound by oxidation, reduction or hydrolysis; Phase II reactions conjugate the product with polar molecules such as glucuronic acid, making it water-soluble; and Phase III transporters excrete the conjugates from cells. This detoxification is not always benign: some compounds, such as the carcinogen benzo[a]pyrene, are converted into more toxic or reactive metabolites.

Environmental significance

Xenobiotics in the environment include persistent pollutants such as polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs), dioxins, plasticisers and many pharmaceuticals that pass through wastewater treatment. Microorganisms capable of degrading such compounds are exploited in bioremediation. The prefix "xeno-" is used similarly in xenotransplantation and other fields to denote something originating outside the system in question.
