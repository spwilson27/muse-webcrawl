Basal body

A basal body (also called a kinetosome) is a barrel-shaped organelle found at the base of a eukaryotic cilium or flagellum. It serves as the nucleation site for the growth of the axoneme, the microtubule core of the cilium, and anchors the cilium to the cell. Basal bodies are structurally identical to centrioles, and in many cells a centriole converts into a basal body when the cell grows a cilium.

Structure

A basal body is about 200 nm in diameter and 400 nm long, consisting of nine triplet microtubules arranged in a cylinder, with a "cartwheel" structure at its proximal end. The two central microtubules of the axoneme begin just above the basal body, in the transition zone, which acts as a selective gate controlling which proteins enter the cilium. Accessory structures such as basal feet and rootlets orient and anchor the organelle.

Formation and function

Basal bodies arise either from existing centrioles or, in multiciliated cells such as those lining the airways, in large numbers from structures called deuterosomes. Defects in basal body and ciliary proteins cause a group of human diseases known as ciliopathies, affecting the kidneys, retina, skeleton, and brain. In the cell cycle, basal bodies return to the centrosome to act as centrioles during division.
