Cong (Irish: Conga, meaning "the narrow strait") is a village in County Mayo, Ireland, straddling the borders with County Galway and close to the north-eastern corner of Lough Corrib. It lies on an isthmus between Lough Mask and Lough Corrib, where the underground waters of the former rise to join the latter.

History

Cong was the seat of the last High King of Ireland, Rory O'Connor (Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair), who died in 1198 and was buried in the abbey church. The Augustinian Cong Abbey, founded in the early twelfth century, preserves fine Romanesque doorways and the famous Cross of Cong (now in the National Museum of Ireland). The village's stone circle and the "dry canal" carved to connect the two loughs are other antiquities.

The Quiet Man

Cong is internationally known as the location for John Ford's 1952 film The Quiet Man, starring John Wayne and Maureen O'Hara. The village hosts an annual Quiet Man fan club gathering, and locations from the film — the White O' Morn cottage, Pat Cohan's bar — remain tourist landmarks. The five-star Ashford Castle hotel, formerly the Guinness family's estate, stands on the lakeshore at Cong.
