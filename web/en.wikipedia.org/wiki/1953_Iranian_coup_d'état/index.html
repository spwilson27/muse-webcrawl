The 1953 Iranian coup d'état, known in Iran as the 28 Mordad coup d'état, was the overthrow of the democratically elected Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh on 19 August 1953. It was orchestrated by the United States Central Intelligence Agency (as Operation Ajax) and the United Kingdom's Secret Intelligence Service, following Mosaddegh's nationalisation of the Iranian oil industry, which had been controlled by the British-owned Anglo-Iranian Oil Company.

Background

In 1951 the Iranian parliament voted to nationalise the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company, and Mosaddegh became prime minister. Britain responded with a worldwide boycott of Iranian oil, an embargo that badly damaged Iran's economy, and began plotting Mosaddegh's removal. The Eisenhower administration, fearing that instability might bring the communist Tudeh Party to power, authorised the CIA to proceed in 1953.

The coup

The first attempt, on 15–16 August, failed: the Shah's decree dismissing Mosaddegh was delivered by Colonel Nematollah Nassiri, who was arrested, and the Shah fled to Baghdad and then Rome. CIA operative Kermit Roosevelt Jr. remained in Tehran and organised a second effort. On 19 August, paid crowds, army units and police loyal to General Fazlollah Zahedi attacked Mosaddegh's residence; after heavy fighting Mosaddegh surrendered. Zahedi became prime minister and the Shah returned in triumph.

Aftermath

The coup consolidated the Shah's authoritarian rule and led to a new oil agreement in 1954 giving Western companies a large share of Iranian production. The United States formally acknowledged its role in 2013 with the declassification of CIA documents. The coup remains a central grievance in Iranian attitudes toward the United States and is widely seen as a precursor to the 1979 Iranian Revolution.
