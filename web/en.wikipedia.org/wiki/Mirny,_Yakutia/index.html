Mirny (Russian: Мирный, "peaceful") is a town in the Sakha Republic (Yakutia) in eastern Siberia, Russia, famous as the home of the Mir diamond mine, one of the largest excavated holes in the world. It serves as the administrative centre of Mirninsky District and as a base for the diamond company Alrosa.

History

The town was founded in 1955 after geologists of the Amakinsk expedition, led by Yuri Khabardin, discovered the Mir kimberlite pipe — the first major diamond deposit found in the Soviet Union — following the famous coded telegram reporting that they had "lit the pipe of peace" (mir meaning both "peace" and "world"). A tent camp grew into a planned town built on piles above the permafrost.

The Mir mine

The open-pit Mir mine, adjacent to the town, is about 525 metres deep and 1.2 kilometres in diameter, with a spiral road descending its terraced walls. Open-pit mining ended in 2001, after which production moved underground; the underground mine suffered a major flooding accident in August 2017 in which eight miners died. The pit is so large that helicopters are prohibited from flying over it because of air currents.

Geography and climate

Mirny lies on the Irelyakh River in western Yakutia. It has an extreme subarctic climate, with winter temperatures regularly below −40 °C and buildings constructed on stilts to avoid melting the permafrost. The town is not connected to the rest of Russia by all-season roads; it depends on its airport and a seasonal winter road.
