Moët & Chandon, legally Moët et Chandon, is a French fine winery and part of the luxury group LVMH. Founded in 1743 by Claude Moët, it is one of the world's largest champagne producers and a prominent champagne house, based in Épernay in the Champagne region of France. Its best-known labels include the flagship Moët Impérial and the prestige cuvée Dom Pérignon.

History

Claude Moët founded the house in 1743 and began shipping wine from Champagne to Paris. His grandson Jean-Rémy Moët expanded the company and counted Napoleon Bonaparte among its admirers; the Impérial label is said to honour that connection.

In 1833 the company became Moët et Chandon when Pierre-Gabriel Chandon, a son-in-law of the family, joined as partner. The house introduced its first vintage champagne in 1842 and the Dom Pérignon prestige cuvée in 1921, released commercially in 1936.

Production and estates

Moët & Chandon owns about 1,190 hectares (2,900 acres) of vineyards and produces approximately 28.5 million bottles of champagne annually, more than any other house. Its cellars in Épernay extend some 28 kilometres beneath the town.

In 1971 the house merged with Hennessy cognac, and in 1987 that group merged with Louis Vuitton to form LVMH, the world's largest luxury conglomerate. The company has also established sparkling wine ventures abroad, including Chandon estates in California, Argentina, Australia, Brazil, China, and India.
