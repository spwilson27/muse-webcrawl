African forest elephant

The African forest elephant (Loxodonta cyclotis) is one of two extant species of African elephant, inhabiting the humid forests of West Africa and the Congo Basin. It is smaller than the African bush elephant, with rounder ears, straighter downward-pointing tusks, and a different skull shape.

Ecology

Forest elephants are important seed dispersers for many rainforest trees, some of which depend on passage through an elephant's gut to germinate. They gather at forest clearings called bais to feed on mineral-rich soil and water, and travel along traditional trail networks between fruiting areas.

Conservation

Long treated as a subspecies, it was recognised as a separate species on genetic evidence, and the IUCN listed it as Critically Endangered in 2021, estimating a decline of more than 80% over three generations, driven by ivory poaching and habitat loss. Its slow reproduction — females first calve in their twenties and at long intervals — makes recovery slow.
