Necktie

A necktie, or simply a tie, is a piece of cloth worn for decorative purposes around the neck, resting under the shirt collar and knotted at the throat, often paired with a dress shirt and suit. Typically made of silk or other woven fabric, modern ties are usually about 145 cm (57 in) long.

History

The necktie descends from the cravat worn by Croatian mercenaries in seventeenth-century France, whose name derives from Croates. Cravats evolved through the stocks and bow ties of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries into the long four-in-hand tie, whose modern shape was popularised in the 1920s by the New York tie maker Jesse Langsdorf, who cut the fabric on the bias.

Knots and styles

Common knots include the four-in-hand, half-Windsor and full Windsor. Ties may be solid, striped — regimental stripes traditionally indicate affiliation — or patterned, and width and length follow fashion cycles.
