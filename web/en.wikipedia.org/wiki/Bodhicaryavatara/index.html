Bodhicaryavatara

The Bodhicaryāvatāra ("A Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life"), here in its unaccented transliteration as Bodhicaryavatara, is a Mahāyāna Buddhist text written c. 700 AD in Sanskrit verse by Shantideva, a monk of Nālandā University in India. It is one of the most influential texts of Mahayana Buddhism. See also the accented entry at Bodhicaryāvatāra.

Contents

The work has ten chapters dedicated to the development of bodhicitta (the mind of enlightenment) through the practice of the six perfections (pāramitās): generosity, ethics, patience, effort, concentration and wisdom. The ninth chapter, on wisdom, contains an influential exposition of the Madhyamaka view of emptiness, while the tenth dedicates the merit of the work to all beings.

Influence

The Bodhicaryavatara has been studied continuously in Tibet, where it is a core text in monastic curricula, and it is one of the most frequently cited works of the 14th Dalai Lama. Numerous modern translations exist in English and other languages.
