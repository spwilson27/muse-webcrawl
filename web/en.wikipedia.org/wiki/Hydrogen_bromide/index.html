Hydrogen bromide

Hydrogen bromide is the inorganic compound with the formula HBr. It is a colorless, pungent gas that dissolves readily in water to form hydrobromic acid, a strong mineral acid. It is a diatomic molecule consisting of a hydrogen atom and a bromine atom joined by a single covalent bond.

Properties

Hydrogen bromide melts at −86.9 °C and boils at −66.8 °C. It fumes in moist air and is highly corrosive and irritating to the eyes, skin, and respiratory tract. Its aqueous solution, hydrobromic acid, is one of the strongest mineral acids, comparable to hydrochloric acid, and forms an azeotrope boiling at 124.3 °C containing about 47.6% HBr.

Preparation and uses

Industrially, HBr is produced by the direct combination of hydrogen and bromine, or as a byproduct of bromination reactions. In the laboratory it may be generated from sodium bromide and a non-oxidizing acid such as phosphoric acid. Hydrogen bromide adds to alkenes — ionically to give Markovnikov products, or radically in the presence of peroxides to give anti-Markovnikov products (the peroxide effect). Hydrobromic acid is used in the manufacture of inorganic bromides, alkyl bromides, and as a reagent and catalyst in organic synthesis.
