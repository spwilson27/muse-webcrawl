Phrasing

Phrase marks in a musical score indicate how notes are grouped and articulated.

In music theory, phrasing is the method of moulding a series of notes into a meaningful musical sentence. Much as a speaker groups words into phrases when speaking, a performer shapes notes into phrases, giving music its expressive contour through dynamics, articulation, timing and tone colour. Phrasing is one of the principal means by which a performer interprets a written score.

Musical phrase

A musical phrase is a unit of musical meter that has a complete musical sense of its own, ending with a cadence or other point of rest. In the practice of the Classical period, phrases are often four bars long, combining into antecedent ("question") and consequent ("answer") phrase pairs that form periods. Singers and wind players naturally phrase according to the breath, while string players phrase according to bowing.

Expressive phrasing

Techniques of expressive phrasing include the shaping of dynamics within a phrase (often growing toward a climax and relaxing toward its end), slight agogic accents or rubato, articulation such as legato and staccato, and the release of phrase endings. Skilled phrasing is central to jazz improvisation, where players such as saxophonists and singers develop highly individual ways of grouping and accenting notes.

The term "phrasing" is also used in other contexts: in speech it refers to the grouping of words in delivery, and in dance and figure skating to the matching of movements to musical phrases.
