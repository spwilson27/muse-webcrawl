T-Mobile US

T-Mobile US, Inc. is an American wireless network operator headquartered in Bellevue, Washington. Its largest shareholder is the German telecommunications company Deutsche Telekom. T-Mobile is the second-largest wireless carrier in the United States, with over 100 million subscribers.

History

The company traces its origins to VoiceStream Wireless, which Deutsche Telekom acquired in 2001 and rebranded under the T-Mobile name. In 2013 T-Mobile merged with MetroPCS Communications. Under chief executive John Legere, the company launched its "Un-carrier" marketing strategy, abolishing two-year contracts and other industry practices, and grew rapidly at the expense of its rivals.

On April 1, 2020, T-Mobile completed its merger with Sprint Corporation, in a deal valued at around $26 billion, making it a much closer competitor to Verizon and AT&T. The company subsequently shut down Sprint's legacy networks and integrated its spectrum, which gave T-Mobile a leading position in mid-band 5G deployment.

Products and services

T-Mobile provides wireless voice, messaging and data services under the T-Mobile and Metro by T-Mobile brands, along with home internet services delivered over its 5G network.
