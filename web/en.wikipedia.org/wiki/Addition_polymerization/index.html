Addition polymerization

In polymerization, addition polymerization occurs when the polymer is formed through addition reactions between monomers with no by-products, so the polymer's mass equals the sum of the monomer masses. Addition polymerization is a form of chain-growth polymerization in which unsaturated monomer molecules add onto the active site of a growing polymer chain one at a time.

Stages

The reaction proceeds in three stages. Initiation creates an active center, often a free radical generated from a peroxide initiator or by light. Propagation rapidly adds hundreds or thousands of monomers to the growing chain. Termination destroys the active center, typically by combination or disproportionation of two radicals.

Types

Depending on the active species, addition polymerization may be free-radical, cationic, anionic, or coordination polymerization. Ziegler–Natta coordination polymerization produces highly linear polyolefins with controlled stereochemistry, as in high-density polyethylene and isotactic polypropylene. Living anionic polymerization, lacking natural termination, enables the synthesis of block copolymers.
