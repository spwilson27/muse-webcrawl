Nat Adderley

Nathaniel Adderley (November 25, 1931 – January 2, 2000) was an American jazz cornet and trumpet player, best known for his long association with the band of his elder brother, alto saxophonist Julian "Cannonball" Adderley, and as the composer of the jazz standard "Work Song".

Life and career

Born in Tampa, Florida, Adderley developed his cornet playing through school and army bands — switching from trumpet to cornet while serving in the United States Army. He played with Lionel Hampton and J. J. Johnson in the mid-1950s, then joined his brother's quintet in 1959, remaining until Cannonball's death in 1975. The Cannonball Adderley Quintet became one of the most popular soul-jazz groups of the 1960s.

Adderley's best-known composition, "Work Song" (1960), with lyrics later added by Oscar Brown Jr., became a standard recorded by many artists; he also wrote "Jive Samba" and "Sack o' Woe" (with his brother). As a leader he recorded albums for Riverside, Atlantic, and other labels, including Work Song (1960) and Autobiography (1964–65).

After 1975 Adderley led his own groups, taught, and co-founded a jazz studies program; in later years he was artist-in-residence at Florida Southern College in Lakeland, Florida. He died in Lakeland in 2000 from complications of diabetes.
