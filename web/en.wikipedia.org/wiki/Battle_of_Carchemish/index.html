Battle of Carchemish

The Battle of Carchemish was a battle fought in 605 BC between a combined army of the Neo-Babylonian Empire and the Medes against the remaining forces of the Egyptian and Neo-Assyrian states. It took place at the city of Carchemish on the Euphrates River, in present-day Syria near the Turkish border. The Babylonian victory destroyed Egypt's military presence in the Levant and ended Assyria's last hope of restoration, marking one of the decisive battles of the ancient Near East.

Background

The Neo-Assyrian Empire, which had dominated the Near East for three centuries, collapsed in the late 7th century BC under pressure from the Babylonians under Nabopolassar and their Median allies. Nineveh fell in 612 BC, and the surviving Assyrian court fled west to Harran, where the last king, Ashur-uballit II, held out with Egyptian support. Pharaoh Necho II of Egypt marched north in 609 BC to aid the Assyrians; on the way he was delayed by King Josiah of Judah at Megiddo, where Josiah was killed. The Egyptians and Assyrians attempted to retake Harran but were defeated, and the Assyrian force effectively vanished from history. Egypt nonetheless occupied the Levant and Syria, including the strategic crossing point at Carchemish, and Babylonian campaigns in 606 BC failed to dislodge them.

The battle

In 605 BC the Babylonian crown prince Nebuchadnezzar, commanding the army of his father Nabopolassar, advanced on Carchemish and attacked the Egyptian garrison. The Babylonian Chronicle describes a violent battle in which the Babylonians stormed the city and defeated the Egyptian army so completely that, in the chronicler's words, not a single man escaped to his country. Archaeological excavations at Carchemish have uncovered evidence of destruction and of the intense fighting, including large numbers of arrowheads and sling stones. The Egyptian survivors fled south and were overtaken and destroyed again near Hamath, ending Egyptian power in Syria.

Aftermath

The victory at Carchemish gave Babylon control of the entire region west of the Euphrates down to the Egyptian border. Nebuchadnezzar was marching toward Egypt itself when news reached him that Nabopolassar had died; he hurried back to Babylon to secure the throne, becoming Nebuchadnezzar II. The battle is referenced in the Hebrew Bible, where the prophet Jeremiah (chapter 46) describes the Egyptian defeat at Carchemish, and the region's subjugation set the stage for Babylon's wars against Judah, including the sieges of Jerusalem in 597 and 586 BC.
