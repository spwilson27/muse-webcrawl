Battle of Anegawa

The Battle of Anegawa (Anegawa no Tatakai) was a battle fought on 9 August 1570 near Lake Biwa in Ōmi Province, Japan, between the combined forces of Oda Nobunaga and Tokugawa Ieyasu and the allied forces of the Azai and Asakura clans during the Sengoku period.

Background and battle

Nobunaga's invasion of Echizen in 1570 provoked his brother-in-law Azai Nagamasa to break his alliance with the Oda and attack. Nobunaga and his ally Tokugawa Ieyasu marched to meet the Azai–Asakura army along the Ane River. In the ensuing battle, the Oda engaged the Azai while the Tokugawa fought the Asakura. Notable fighting included the charge of Honda Tadakatsu and the struggle for the river crossing. After hard fighting, the Azai and Asakura forces were driven from the field with heavy losses.

Aftermath

The victory secured Nobunaga's southern flank and began the destruction of the Azai and Asakura, completed in 1573 with the sieges of Odani and Ichijōdani. The battle is remembered as one of the great set-piece engagements of the Sengoku period and for the exploits of warriors such as the Azai retainer Endō Naotsune, who was killed attempting to reach Nobunaga's headquarters.
