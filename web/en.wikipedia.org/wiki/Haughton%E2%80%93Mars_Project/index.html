Haughton–Mars Project

The Haughton–Mars Project (HMP) is an international interdisciplinary field research project centred on the scientific study of the Haughton impact crater and surrounding terrain on Devon Island in Canada's High Arctic, viewed as an analogue for the Moon and Mars. The project is led by the Mars Institute in collaboration with the SETI Institute and NASA, and has operated the Haughton-Mars Project Research Station since 1997.

Site

Haughton crater, about 23 kilometres in diameter and roughly 39 million years old, lies in a polar desert environment that shares many features with Mars: cold, dry, windy conditions, rocky terrain, and valleys thought to have been carved by water. Devon Island is the largest uninhabited island on Earth.

Research

The project studies the geology and biology of the site and uses it to test exploration technologies and operational concepts for human missions, including prototype spacesuits, rovers, drilling systems, and habitat designs. The Arthur Clarke Mars Greenhouse, installed in 2002, tests plant growth systems for life support research.
