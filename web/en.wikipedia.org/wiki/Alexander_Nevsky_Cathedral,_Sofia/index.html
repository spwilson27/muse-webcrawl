Alexander Nevsky Cathedral, Sofia

The St. Alexander Nevsky Cathedral (Bulgarian: Храм-паметник „Свети Александър Невски") is a Bulgarian Orthodox cathedral in Sofia, the capital of Bulgaria. Built in the Neo-Byzantine style, it serves as the cathedral church of the Patriarch of Bulgaria and is one of the largest Orthodox cathedrals in the world, as well as one of Sofia's primary symbols and tourist attractions.

History

The cathedral was built as a memorial to the Russian soldiers who died during the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878, which liberated Bulgaria from Ottoman rule. It is named after Saint Alexander Nevsky, the 13th-century Russian prince. The foundation stone was laid in 1882, but most construction took place between 1904 and 1912. The design, by the Russian architect Alexander Pomerantsev, follows the Neo-Byzantine style fashionable in Russia at the time.

Architecture

The cruciform cathedral covers an area of 3,170 square metres and can hold up to 5,000 people. Its bell tower rises 53 metres and contains 12 bells. The central dome is 45 metres high, gilded, and inscribed with the Lord's Prayer in Church Slavonic. The interior is richly decorated with Italian marble, Brazilian onyx, alabaster, and mosaics and murals by Bulgarian, Russian, and Czech artists. The crypt houses a museum of Bulgarian icons, part of the National Gallery, with one of the largest collections of Orthodox icons in Europe.

Changes of name

During the First World War, when Bulgaria and Russia were on opposing sides, the cathedral was briefly renamed in honour of Saints Cyril and Methodius (1916–1920), before the original name was restored.
