Dogra–Tibetan War

Date1841–1842
LocationTibet and Ladakh
ResultTreaty of Chushul; status quo ante

The Dogra–Tibetan War, also called the Sino-Sikh War, was fought from 1841 to 1842 between the forces of the Dogra Raja Gulab Singh of Jammu (a vassal of the Sikh Empire) under General Zorawar Singh, and Tibet, then under Qing Chinese overlordship.

Course

Zorawar Singh, who had already conquered Ladakh and Baltistan, invaded western Tibet in 1841, capturing Rudok and advancing as far as Taklakot. The expedition was destroyed by a Tibetan-Qing counteroffensive in the winter; Zorawar Singh was killed at the Battle of Toyo in December 1841. A Tibetan invasion of Ladakh the following year was defeated at Leh.

Outcome

The war ended with the Treaty of Chushul (1842), which restored the pre-war frontier between Ladakh and Tibet — a boundary settlement whose ambiguities later contributed to disputes over Aksai Chin.
