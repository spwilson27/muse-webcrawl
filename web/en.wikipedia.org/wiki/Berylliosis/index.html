Berylliosis, or chronic beryllium disease (CBD), is a chronic, allergic-type lung disease caused by exposure to beryllium in people who become immunologically sensitised to the metal. Inhaled beryllium particles provoke a cell-mediated immune response that produces granulomas — small collections of immune cells — in the lungs, closely resembling sarcoidosis. An acute chemical pneumonitis can follow heavy short-term exposure, but the chronic disease may develop after months or decades, even following relatively low exposures, in the roughly 2–10% of exposed workers who become sensitised.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms develop insidiously and include progressive shortness of breath on exertion, cough, fatigue, loss of appetite and weight, night sweats, and joint pains. As fibrosis advances, lung function declines and, in severe cases, cor pulmonale (right-sided heart failure) can develop. Beryllium exposure can also cause dermatitis and skin granulomas at sites where particles enter cuts.

Causes and diagnosis

Exposure occurs in beryllium mining and refining, aerospace and defence manufacturing, electronics, dental laboratories, and historically in the manufacture of fluorescent lamps. Genetic susceptibility is strongly associated with the HLA-DPB1 Glu69 variant. Diagnosis rests on documented exposure, a positive beryllium lymphocyte proliferation test (BeLPT) demonstrating sensitisation, and biopsy evidence of non-caseating granulomas with exclusion of other causes such as sarcoidosis.

Treatment and prevention

There is no cure; management consists of removal from further exposure, corticosteroids (such as prednisone) for symptomatic or progressive disease, and supplemental oxygen. Prevention relies on strict occupational exposure limits, ventilation, respiratory protection, and medical surveillance with BeLPT screening. Beryllium has been classified as a human carcinogen, and exposed workers are also monitored for lung cancer risk.
