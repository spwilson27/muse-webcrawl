Martha Maria Yeardley Smith (born July 3, 1964) is an American actress. She has voiced Lisa Simpson on the animated television series The Simpsons since 1987, first on The Tracey Ullman Show shorts and then on the long-running series from 1989 onward. Her distinctive voice is generally considered her natural speaking voice; Lisa is unusual among the show's characters in being voiced by only one actor throughout the series' run.

Early life and career

Smith was born in Paris, France, where her father worked for UPI, and was raised in Washington, D.C. She began acting professionally as a teenager and appeared on Broadway in Tom Stoppard's The Real Thing. In film she had roles in Heaven Help Us (1985), The Legend of Billie Jean (1985), Maximum Overdrive (1986), and later City Slickers (1991), As Good as It Gets (1997), and The Simpsons Movie (2007). On television she played Louise on Herman's Head (1991–1994) and had a recurring role on Dharma & Greg.

The Simpsons

Smith originally auditioned for Bart Simpson before being cast as his sister Lisa. She won a Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Voice-Over Performance in 1992. She has occasionally voiced other minor characters but has generally insisted on performing Lisa exclusively.

Other work

Smith wrote and performed the one-woman show More (2004) and the young-adult novel I, Lorelei (2009). Since 2017 she has co-hosted the true-crime podcast Small Town Dicks. She co-founded the production company Paperclip Ltd. and has been active in philanthropy and political advocacy.
