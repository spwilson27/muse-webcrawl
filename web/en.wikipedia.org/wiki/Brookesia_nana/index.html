Brookesia nana

Brookesia nana is among the smallest known reptiles in the world.

Brookesia nana, also known as the nano-chameleon, is a species of chameleon endemic to montane rainforests in northern Madagascar. It was described in 2021 and may be the smallest reptile in the world. The adult male holotype measures only about 21.6 mm (0.85 in) in total length, while females are somewhat larger at around 28.9 mm.

Description

Like other members of the genus Brookesia, the nano-chameleon is a miniaturised leaf-litter dweller. Its body is mottled brown, allowing it to blend into the forest floor. The male possesses relatively large hemipenes compared to its body size, a feature thought to compensate mechanically for the size difference between the sexes during mating.

Discovery and taxonomy

The species was formally described by a team led by Frank Glaw of the Bavarian State Collection of Zoology, based on specimens collected in the Sorata massif region of northern Madagascar. Its discovery drew attention to the remarkable diversity of miniaturised vertebrates on the island and to the conservation threats facing Madagascar's forests.

Habitat and conservation

Brookesia nana inhabits high-elevation rainforest. Its habitat is under pressure from deforestation, slash-and-burn agriculture and logging; the describing authors suggested that the species may qualify as endangered and recommended protective measures for the Sorata forest.
