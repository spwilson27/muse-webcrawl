Florian Ceynowa

Florian Stanisław Ceynowa, also Florian Cejnowa (4 May 1817 – 26 March 1881), was a doctor, soldier, Kashubian language activist and culturologist. He is regarded as the founder of the Kashubian national awakening movement and undertook the first attempts to develop a Kashubian literary language and orthography.

Life and work

Born in Sławoszyno near Puck, Ceynowa studied medicine at Breslau (Wrocław), where he came into contact with Slavic philology and national revival movements. He participated in the Polish revolutionary movement and was imprisoned by Prussian authorities for his role in the planned 1846 uprising. He later practised medicine in Bukowiec near Świecie.

His publications include Kashubian grammar sketches, collections of folk songs and tales, and texts written in his proposed Kashubian alphabet, which drew on Polish diacritics. Though criticised in his lifetime, including by Kashubians who saw their speech as a Polish dialect, Ceynowa is now commemorated as a foundational figure of Kashubian identity.
