Hamza ibn Ali

Hamza ibn Ali ibn Ahmad al-Zuzani (c. 985 – 1021 or later) was an 11th-century Persian Isma'ili missionary and scholar from Khorasan. He is considered the founder of the Druze faith, having proclaimed the divinity of the Fatimid caliph al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah in Cairo in 1017 (408 AH).

Life

Hamza was born in Zuzan in Khorasan, Persia, and may originally have been a felt-maker. He arrived in Cairo, the capital of the Fatimid Caliphate, around 1017, where he began preaching that the caliph al-Hakim was divine. His preaching, centred at the Raydan mosque, attracted followers but also provoked riots and opposition from the Sunni population and from rival Isma'ili figures such as al-Kirmani.

Doctrine and legacy

Hamza systematised the new doctrine in a series of epistles, later collected as part of the Druze canon, the Epistles of Wisdom (Rasa'il al-Hikma). In Druze theology Hamza occupies the highest rank among the five cosmic principles (hudud), identified with the Universal Intellect. After al-Hakim's disappearance in 1021, persecution forced Hamza into hiding; according to tradition he went into occultation and will return.
