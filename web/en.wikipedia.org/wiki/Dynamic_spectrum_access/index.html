Dynamic spectrum access

Dynamic spectrum access (DSA), also called dynamic spectrum management, is a set of techniques for using radio frequency spectrum more efficiently by allowing secondary users to transmit in licensed bands when the primary (licensed) users are not active, provided they cause no harmful interference. Measurements of actual spectrum use in many cities have repeatedly shown that large portions of assigned spectrum lie idle at any given time and place.

Approaches

Spectrum sensing

A cognitive radio senses its environment for unused channels ("white spaces") and vacates a channel when a primary user returns. Sensing techniques include energy detection, matched filtering and cyclostationary feature detection.

Geolocation databases

Devices consult an approved database of incumbent transmitters and available channels at their location, the approach adopted for television white space devices in the United States and other countries.

Spectrum sharing frameworks

Tiered systems, such as the Citizens Broadband Radio Service at 3.5 GHz, formalise priority levels between incumbents, licensed users and general authorised access, coordinated by an automated Spectrum Access System.

Regulation and deployment

Regulators including the FCC and Ofcom have authorised white-space and shared-access operation since the early 2010s. DSA principles underpin licence-assisted access in mobile networks and proposals for 6G spectrum sharing.
