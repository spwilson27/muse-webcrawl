Jürgen Habermas

Jürgen Habermas (born 18 June 1929) is a German philosopher and social theorist associated with the Frankfurt School of critical theory. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential philosophers of the postwar period, known for his work on the public sphere, communicative action, and deliberative democracy.

Major works

His early book The Structural Transformation of the Public Sphere (1962) analysed the emergence and decline of a bourgeois public realm of rational debate. His two-volume Theory of Communicative Action (1981) set out a comprehensive theory of society grounded in language and mutual understanding, distinguishing communicative action from strategic action. In Between Facts and Norms (1992) he developed a procedural theory of law and democracy.

Positions and influence

Habermas has been a prominent public intellectual in Germany, intervening in debates over historical memory (the Historikerstreit), European integration, religion in the public sphere, and the ethics of genetic engineering. His concepts — discourse ethics, constitutional patriotism, the ideal speech situation — remain central reference points in political theory and sociology.
