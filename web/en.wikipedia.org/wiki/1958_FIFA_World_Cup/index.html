The 1958 FIFA World Cup was the sixth edition of the FIFA World Cup, the quadrennial international football championship contested by the men's national teams of the member associations of FIFA. It was held in Sweden from 8 to 29 June 1958. It is the only World Cup in which all four United Kingdom teams — England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland — qualified and took part.

The Råsunda Stadium in Solna hosted the final between Brazil and Sweden.

The tournament was won by Brazil, who defeated hosts Sweden 5–2 in the final at the Råsunda Stadium in Solna. The tournament is chiefly remembered for the emergence of the 17-year-old Pelé, who scored a hat-trick in the semi-final against France and two goals in the final, announcing himself to the world.

Tournament summary

The sixteen teams were divided into four groups of four. Unusually, if teams finished level on points in the group stage, a play-off match rather than goal average was used to decide who advanced. France's Just Fontaine scored 13 goals during the tournament, a record for a single World Cup that still stands. Fontaine scored in every match France played, including four goals against West Germany in the third-place play-off, which France won 6–3.

Brazil's team, coached by Vicente Feola, combined the flair of Pelé and Garrincha with the goals of Vavá and the defensive organisation of captain Bellini. After being held to a goalless draw by England in the group stage — the first scoreless draw in World Cup history — Brazil beat Wales 1–0 in the quarter-finals before their celebrated 5–2 semi-final victory over France.

Final

Sweden took an early lead in the final through Nils Liedholm, but Brazil responded with goals from Vavá, Pelé and Mário Zagallo to win 5–2, beginning a period of Brazilian dominance in world football that would bring further titles in 1962 and 1970.
