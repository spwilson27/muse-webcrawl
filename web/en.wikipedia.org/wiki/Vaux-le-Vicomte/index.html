The Château de Vaux-le-Vicomte is a French Baroque château located in Maincy, near Melun, about 55 kilometres (34 mi) southeast of Paris, in the department of Seine-et-Marne. Built between 1658 and 1661 for Nicolas Fouquet, Marquis de Belle-Île, Viscount of Melun and Vaux, and superintendent of finances under King Louis XIV, the château was the work of three artists whom Fouquet had assembled: the architect Louis Le Vau, the landscape architect André Le Nôtre, and the painter-decorator Charles Le Brun. Their collaboration marked the beginning of the "Louis XIV style", in which architecture, interior decoration and landscape design were united for the first time on a grand scale.

History

Fouquet purchased the estate of Vaux in 1641 and began assembling the surrounding land. Construction of the present château began in 1658, employing some 18,000 workmen. The building is notable for its oval salon, a feature without precedent in French architecture, and for gardens laid out by Le Nôtre with parterres, grottoes, canals and fountains extending along a three-kilometre axis.

On 17 August 1661 Fouquet hosted a lavish fête at Vaux in honour of the king, with a play by Molière, music by Jean-Baptiste Lully and fireworks. The splendour of the celebration is often said to have provoked the king's jealousy. Three weeks later Fouquet was arrested by d'Artagnan on charges of embezzlement; he was tried and imprisoned for the rest of his life. Louis XIV seized the château's tapestries, statues and orange trees, and employed Le Vau, Le Nôtre and Le Brun to design the much larger Palace of Versailles, making Vaux-le-Vicomte the direct model for Versailles.

Later history

The estate passed through several owners. In 1875 it was bought at auction by Alfred Sommier, a sugar industrialist, who undertook a major restoration of the château and gardens. His descendants, the Vogüé family, still own the property, which is open to the public and is the largest privately owned château in France listed as a historic monument.
