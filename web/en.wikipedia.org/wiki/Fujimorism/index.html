Fujimorism (Spanish: Fujimorismo) is the political ideology and movement associated with former Peruvian president Alberto Fujimori and his family, particularly his daughter Keiko Fujimori. It has been one of the most durable political forces in Peru since the 1990s.

Origins and government

Alberto Fujimori, an agricultural engineer and university rector of Japanese descent, won the 1990 presidential election as an outsider. His government applied radical free-market "shock" reforms that ended hyperinflation, defeated the Shining Path insurgency after the capture of its leader Abimael Guzmán in 1992, and carried out the 1992 "self-coup" (autogolpe) in which Congress was dissolved and a new constitution adopted in 1993. Supporters credit Fujimorism with stabilising Peru; critics associate it with authoritarianism, corruption under intelligence chief Vladimiro Montesinos, and human rights abuses, including the forced sterilisation campaign of the late 1990s. Fujimori fled Peru in 2000 amid scandal, was later extradited from Chile, and was convicted of human rights abuses and corruption.

The movement after 2000

Under Keiko Fujimori, the movement reorganised through successive party vehicles, culminating in Fuerza Popular. Fujimorism retains a loyal base, especially among poorer voters who remember the defeat of terrorism and inflation, while remaining deeply polarising in Peruvian society. Scholars describe it in varying terms — populist, neoliberal, conservative and authoritarian — reflecting its combination of market economics, social conservatism and strongman politics.
