Music information retrieval

Music information retrieval (MIR) is the interdisciplinary science of retrieving information from music. It involves methods from musicology, cognitive science, signal processing, machine learning, and library science. MIR techniques underlie services such as music recommendation, automatic playlist generation, audio fingerprinting, and music transcription.

Applications

  • Recommendation systems — services like Spotify and Last.fm suggest music based on listening history and audio similarity.
  • Audio identification — fingerprinting systems such as Shazam identify recordings from short samples.
  • Automatic transcription — converting audio recordings into symbolic notation such as MIDI or sheet music.
  • Genre and mood classification — automatically tagging music by style or emotion.
  • Beat tracking and tempo estimation — used in DJ software and music production tools.
  • Source separation — isolating vocals or instruments from a mix.

Research community

The International Society for Music Information Retrieval (ISMIR) holds an annual conference, founded in 2000. The community evaluation campaign MIREX (Music Information Retrieval Evaluation eXchange) benchmarks algorithms on common tasks. Standard datasets include the Million Song Dataset and GTZAN genre collection.

Techniques

MIR systems commonly extract features from audio such as mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs), chroma features, spectral contrast, and onset envelopes. Symbolic representations (MIDI, MusicXML) allow analysis of pitch, rhythm, and harmony. Since the 2010s, deep learning methods—including convolutional and recurrent neural networks—have substantially improved performance on tasks like tagging, transcription, and audio event detection.
