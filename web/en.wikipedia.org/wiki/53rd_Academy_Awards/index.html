The 53rd Academy Awards ceremony, organised by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, honoured the best films of 1980 and took place on 31 March 1981 at the Dorothy Chandler Pavilion in Los Angeles. It was hosted by Johnny Carson.

Postponement

The ceremony was originally scheduled for 30 March but was postponed by one day following the assassination attempt on President Ronald Reagan by John Hinckley Jr. A videotaped greeting from Reagan, recorded before the shooting, was still shown.

Winners

Ordinary People, the directorial debut of Robert Redford, won Best Picture, Best Director, Best Supporting Actor (Timothy Hutton, then the youngest male acting winner) and Best Adapted Screenplay. Robert De Niro won Best Actor for Raging Bull; Sissy Spacek won Best Actress for Coal Miner's Daughter; Mary Steenburgen won Best Supporting Actress for Melvin and Howard; and Michael Gore and Dean Pitchford's "Fame" won Best Original Song. The decision of Ordinary People over Martin Scorsese's Raging Bull — now widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made — is often cited among the Academy's most debated results. Henry Fonda received an honorary award; the following year he won Best Actor for On Golden Pond.
