Vito Volterra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Vito Volterra (1860–1940), Italian mathematician and physicist.

Vito Volterra (3 May 1860 – 11 October 1940) was an Italian mathematician and physicist, known for his contributions to mathematical biology and integral equations, being one of the founders of functional analysis.

Biography

Born in Ancona, then part of the Papal States, Volterra showed early talent for mathematics and studied at the University of Pisa, where he became a professor of rational mechanics in 1883 at the age of 23. He later held chairs at Turin and at the University of Rome La Sapienza. During the First World War he served as an officer in the Italian Air Force and promoted the development of airships.

Volterra was one of only a handful of Italian academics who refused to take the oath of loyalty to the Fascist regime in 1931, and he was consequently forced to give up his university post and membership of Italian scientific academies.

Work

Volterra's early work on integral equations — the equations of Volterra type — and on functionals laid foundations for functional analysis. Later, inspired by statistics on Adriatic fish catches compiled by his son-in-law Umberto D'Ancona, he developed a mathematical model of predator–prey interaction, arriving independently at the equations also published by Alfred J. Lotka, now known as the Lotka–Volterra equations. His 1926 paper on the subject is regarded as a founding work of mathematical ecology.

He also made contributions to the theory of elasticity, including the theory of dislocations in crystals, celestial mechanics, and the mathematics of hereditary phenomena.
