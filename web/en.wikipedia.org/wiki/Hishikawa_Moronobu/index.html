Hishikawa Moronobu

Hishikawa Moronobu (菱川 師宣, died 1694) was a Japanese artist and printmaker, regarded as the founder of ukiyo-e, the popular woodblock print tradition of the Edo period. He transformed scattered book illustration into an independent art form aimed at the growing urban merchant class.

Life

Moronobu was born in Hota, Awa Province (modern Chiba), the son of an embroiderer, and moved to Edo (Tokyo) where he trained in painting styles influenced by the Tosa and Kanō schools before turning to book design. From the 1670s he produced illustrations for dozens of popular books — guides to the pleasure quarters, storybooks, and picture albums.

Work

Around 1673 Moronobu began issuing single-sheet prints (ichimai-e) in bold black ink, sometimes hand-colored, depicting beauties, actors, erotic scenes (shunga), and scenes of Edo life. His monumental composition "Beauty Looking Back" (Mikaeri bijin), a hanging scroll painting, is among the most famous images in Japanese art. His vigorous line, large figures, and unified compositions set the template for ukiyo-e for a century.

Legacy

Moronobu's students and imitators formed the Hishikawa school, and his fusion of painting and popular publishing established the commercial basis on which later masters such as Hokusai and Hiroshige built.
