Hong Kong Monetary Authority


The HKMA offices at Two International Finance Centre

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the central banking institution of Hong Kong. Established on 1 April 1993, it is a government authority responsible for maintaining monetary and banking stability in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region. Its head office is in Two International Finance Centre in Central, Hong Kong.

Responsibilities

The HKMA's primary functions are to maintain currency stability within the framework of the Linked Exchange Rate System, under which the Hong Kong dollar is pegged to the US dollar at a rate of HK$7.80 to US$1; to promote the stability and integrity of the financial system, including the banking system; to help maintain Hong Kong's status as an international financial centre; and to manage the Exchange Fund, Hong Kong's official reserves.

History

The HKMA was formed by merging the Office of the Exchange Fund with the Office of the Commissioner of Banking. The linked exchange rate system had been introduced in October 1983 to restore confidence in the Hong Kong dollar during negotiations over the territory's future. During the Asian financial crisis of 1998, the HKMA intervened directly in the stock and futures markets in August 1998 to counter speculative attacks; the shares acquired were later disposed of through the Tracker Fund of Hong Kong.

Organisation

The HKMA is headed by a Chief Executive, appointed by the Financial Secretary. As of 2019 the Chief Executive is Eddie Yue, who succeeded Norman Chan. The HKMA also supervises authorized institutions, operates the Faster Payment System, issues banknotes through three note-issuing banks in denominations not covered by government notes, and oversees Hong Kong's deposit protection and financial infrastructure.
