The River Leven is a river in Fife, Scotland. It flows from Loch Leven near Kinross eastwards for about 26 kilometres (16 mi), passing through Glenrothes, Markinch and Leslie, before entering the Firth of Forth at the town of Leven. It should not be confused with the other Scottish rivers of the same name, including the River Leven flowing from Loch Lomond.

Course and industry

The river leaves Loch Leven through sluices that regulate the loch's level — works first constructed in the 19th century partly to supply water power to mills along the river. In its lower course the Leven powered paper mills at Markinch and Leslie, flax and linen works, and other industries in the Howe of Fife. The towns of Glenrothes, a post-war New Town, grew up along its middle reaches.

Environment

Industrial pollution once seriously degraded the river, but water quality has improved markedly since the late 20th century with the decline of heavy industry and investment in sewage treatment; salmon and sea trout have returned to parts of the river. The River Leven valley includes the Riverside Park in Glenrothes and forms part of a network of local paths and wildlife corridors.
