Fanny pack

Fanny pack
Other namesBum bag (UK, Australia), belt bag, waist bag, hip pack
TypeSmall fabric pouch with waist strap
Peak popularityLate 1980s–1990s; revived 2010s

A fanny pack is a small fabric pouch secured with a strap and worn around the waist or, in more recent fashion, slung across the chest or back. It is known as a bum bag in British and Australian English (the word "fanny" being vulgar slang in those varieties), and also as a belt bag, waist bag, or hip pack. In France it is called a banane. The bag is typically closed with a zip and one or more buckles, and is used to carry small items such as a wallet, phone, keys and passport while keeping the hands free.

History

Belt-worn pouches have ancient precedents — Ötzi the Iceman carried a belt pouch around 3400 BC, and medieval and Native American belt pouches served similar purposes. The modern nylon fanny pack emerged in the 1960s–70s, with Australian Melba Stone often credited for a 1962 design inspired by the kangaroo's pouch; leather versions were marketed to skiers as early as 1954. The accessory reached mass popularity in the late 1980s and early 1990s, when it was ubiquitous among tourists and casual wearers.

Fashion cycles

By the late 1990s and 2000s the fanny pack had become a byword for unfashionable tourist attire. From about 2017, however, luxury houses and streetwear brands revived it as the "belt bag", worn cross-body, and it returned to mainstream fashion in the late 2010s and 2020s, helped by its practicality for travel, festivals and running.
