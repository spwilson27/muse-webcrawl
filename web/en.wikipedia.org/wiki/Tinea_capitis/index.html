Tinea capitis


Tinea capitis causes scaly patches and hair loss

Tinea capitis (also known as "herpes tonsurans", "ringworm of the hair", and "scalp ringworm") is a cutaneous fungal infection (dermatophytosis) of the scalp. The disease is primarily caused by dermatophytes in the genera Trichophyton and Microsporum that invade the hair shaft. It predominantly affects preadolescent children and is one of the most common dermatophyte infections worldwide.

Signs and symptoms

Tinea capitis typically appears as itchy, scaly patches of hair loss, sometimes with "black dots" where hairs have broken off at the scalp surface. Inflammatory forms can produce a kerion — a boggy, tender swelling that may ooze and scar. Posterior cervical lymphadenopathy is common. Spread occurs through direct contact and shared combs, hats, and bedding, and infected pets can be a source.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis may be aided by a Wood's lamp examination, dermoscopy, potassium hydroxide microscopy, and fungal culture or molecular identification of the organism. Because topically applied antifungals do not penetrate the hair shaft effectively, treatment requires oral antifungal therapy, commonly terbinafine or griseofulvin for several weeks, sometimes supplemented by antifungal shampoos to reduce transmission.
