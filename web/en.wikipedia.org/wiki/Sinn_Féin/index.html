Sinn Féin (Irish: [ʃɪn̠ʲ fʲeːnʲ]; literally "We Ourselves") is an Irish republican and democratic socialist political party in both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland. Founded in 1905 by Arthur Griffith, it has the goal of a united Ireland as a republic, and operates on an all-Ireland basis, contesting elections in both jurisdictions.

History

The original Sinn Féin organisation was founded on 28 November 1905, drawing together earlier nationalist groups around Griffith's policy of Irish self-reliance and abstention from the Westminster parliament. After the Easter Rising of 1916 — with which it was popularly (though inaccurately) associated — the party was reorganised under Éamon de Valera and won a landslide in the 1918 general election, establishing the First Dáil in Dublin. Following the Irish Civil War and further splits (which produced Cumann na nGaedheal, later Fine Gael, and Fianna Fáil), Sinn Féin dwindled.

The modern party emerged from the 1970 split over abstentionism and, during the Troubles, was closely associated in public perception with the Provisional IRA. Under Gerry Adams's leadership from 1983, the party moved toward electoral politics, supported the peace process culminating in the 1998 Good Friday Agreement, and entered government in Northern Ireland in 2007 alongside the Democratic Unionist Party.

Recent electoral performance

At the 2020 Irish general election, Sinn Féin — led by Mary Lou McDonald, who succeeded Adams in 2018 — won the largest share of first-preference votes and 37 seats, though it remained in opposition. At the 2022 Northern Ireland Assembly election the party became the largest party in Northern Ireland for the first time, and Michelle O'Neill became First Minister in February 2024, the first nationalist to hold the post.

Policies

The party advocates Irish reunification, large-scale public housebuilding, expanded public healthcare, and left-of-centre economic policies, while historically practising abstentionism at Westminster: its MPs, returned for Northern Ireland constituencies, do not take their seats in the House of Commons.
