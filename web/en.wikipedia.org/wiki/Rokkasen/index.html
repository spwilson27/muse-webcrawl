Meaning"Six Immortals of Poetry" (Six Poetry Immortals)
PeriodEarly Heian period, 9th century
SourcePreface to the Kokin Wakashū (c. 905) by Ki no Tsurayuki
MembersAriwara no Narihira, Ono no Komachi, Bishop Henjō, Fun'ya no Yasuhide, Kisen Hōshi, Ōtomo no Kuronushi

The Rokkasen (六歌仙, "Six Immortals of Poetry" or "Six Poetry Immortals") are six Japanese poets of the early Heian period singled out for praise — mixed with pointed criticism — in the kana and mana prefaces that Ki no Tsurayuki wrote for the imperial poetry anthology Kokin Wakashū (c. 905). They are regarded as the supreme waka poets of the ninth century and as predecessors to the later, larger canon of the Thirty-Six Immortals of Poetry.

The six poets

  • Ariwara no Narihira (825–880) — courtier and lover-poet, hero of The Tales of Ise; Tsurayuki praised his feeling but found his verse sometimes over-rich, "like a faded flower whose fragrance lingers".
  • Ono no Komachi (c. 825 – c. 900) — the most famous woman poet of the age, renowned for beauty and passionate love poems; Tsurayuki likened her style to "a beautiful woman suffering from an ailment".
  • Bishop Henjō (816–890) — born Yoshimine no Munesada, a courtier who became a Buddhist monk; praised for technical skill but judged to lack sincerity of heart.
  • Fun'ya no Yasuhide (d. c. 885) — a minor official; Tsurayuki admired his verbal skill but compared the effect to "a pedlar dressed up in court costume".
  • Kisen Hōshi (early 9th century) — a semi-legendary monk-poet of Mount Uji, whose few surviving poems Tsurayuki found promising but obscure.
  • Ōtomo no Kuronushi (late 9th century) — a provincial landowner-poet of Ōmi, whose rustic style Tsurayuki compared to "a woodcutter resting under a tree with flowers in his arms".

Legacy

The group was retrospectively named by combining the six portraits in Tsurayuki's prefaces, and together they dominate the early books of the Kokinshū. Their images became a popular subject in Japanese painting, prints and Noh drama — Komachi alone is the heroine of several famous plays — and the conception of "poetry immortals" (kasen) later expanded into the Thirty-Six Immortals of Poetry chosen in the eleventh century.
