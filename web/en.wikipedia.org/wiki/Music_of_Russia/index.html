Music of Russia

The music of Russia encompasses the folk traditions of its many peoples, the liturgical chant of the Orthodox Church, and one of the world's major classical traditions. From the 19th century, Russian composers such as Tchaikovsky, Rachmaninoff and Stravinsky achieved worldwide prominence, and Russian performers and conservatoires in Moscow and Saint Petersburg remain central to classical music.

Folk and sacred music

Russian folk music features lyrical "drawn-out" songs (protyazhnaya), dance songs, chastushki ditties and instruments including the balalaika, domra, gusli, garmon (accordion) and spoons. Epic byliny were sung in the north. Orthodox liturgical music developed from Byzantine chant into znamenny chant and, from the 17th century, rich polyphonic choral singing, which produced the beloved sacred concertos of Bortniansky and later Rachmaninoff's All-Night Vigil.

Classical music

Mikhail Glinka's operas A Life for the Tsar (1836) and Ruslan and Lyudmila founded a national school, followed by "The Five" (Balakirev, Cui, Mussorgsky, Rimsky-Korsakov and Borodin), who sought a distinctly Russian idiom. Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky combined this with Western forms in symphonies, concertos and the ballets Swan Lake, The Sleeping Beauty and The Nutcracker. In the 20th century, Scriabin, Rachmaninoff, Stravinsky, Prokofiev and Shostakovich carried Russian music through revolution and Soviet cultural politics; after 1991 composers and performers continued to tour worldwide despite new political pressures.

Popular music

The Soviet era produced the state-sponsored VIA ensembles, bard songwriters such as Vladimir Vysotsky and Bulat Okudzhava, and, from the 1980s, Russian rock led by Kino's Viktor Tsoi and Aquarium. Post-Soviet pop (estrada), hip-hop and electronic music thrive, and Russia competed in the Eurovision Song Contest from 1994 to 2021, winning in 2008 with Dima Bilan.
