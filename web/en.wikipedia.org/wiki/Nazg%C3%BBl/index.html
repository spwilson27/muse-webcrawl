The Nazgûl (from the Black Speech nazg, "ring", and gûl, "wraith"), also called Ringwraiths, the Nine Riders, or simply the Nine, are fictional characters in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. They were once great kings and sorcerers of Men who were given nine Rings of Power by the Dark Lord Sauron; the rings prolonged their lives but gradually consumed them, turning them into invisible wraiths bound to Sauron's will and to the fate of the One Ring.

History in the legendarium

The Nazgûl first appeared around 2251 of the Second Age. Their chief, the Witch-king of Angmar, waged war against the northern kingdom of Arnor and later commanded Sauron's armies in the assault on Gondor during the War of the Ring. In The Fellowship of the Ring the Nine hunt Frodo Baggins across Eriador, wounding him on Weathertop. By the Third Age they rode black horses and later winged fell beasts. During the Battle of the Pelennor Fields the Witch-king was slain by Éowyn and Merry Brandybuck, fulfilling the prophecy that he would not fall "by the hand of man". The remaining eight perished when the One Ring was destroyed in Mount Doom.

Characteristics

Invisible to ordinary sight, the Nazgûl wear black cloaks to give themselves form. Their presence induces terror (the "Black Breath"), and they perceive the world of the unseen. They are among the most iconic villains of modern fantasy literature and appear in adaptations including Peter Jackson's film trilogies.
