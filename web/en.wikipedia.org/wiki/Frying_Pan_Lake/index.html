Frying Pan Lake

Frying Pan Lake (renamed Waimangu Cauldron in 1917) is the world's largest hot spring. It is located in the Echo Crater of the Waimangu Volcanic Rift Valley, New Zealand, and its acidic water maintains a temperature of about 50–60 °C (122–140 °F).


Frying Pan Lake, Waimangu

Geography

The lake covers 38,000 square metres (9.4 acres) and occupies part of the floor of Echo Crater, which was formed by the 1886 eruption of Mount Tarawera. The lake is shallow, averaging about 6 metres (20 ft) deep, and its outflow feeds Inferno Crater Lake through a series of small streams. Despite its name suggesting boiling, the water does not boil; the apparent "frying" is caused by carbon dioxide and hydrogen sulphide gases bubbling to the surface, and steam continually rises from the lake.

History

The lake formed following the 1886 Tarawera eruption, which reshaped the region's hydrothermal system. The Waimangu system developed further after a major eruption in 1903. The lake was renamed Waimangu Cauldron in 1917, although the older name Frying Pan Lake remains widely used.
