Camillo Paolo Filippo Giulio Benso, Count of Cavour (1810–1861) was an Italian statesman and the chief architect of Italian unification (the Risorgimento). As prime minister of the Kingdom of Piedmont-Sardinia from 1852, he combined diplomacy, war, and reform to bring most of Italy under one crown.

Policy

Cavour modernised Piedmont's economy with railways, free trade, and banking reform. He secured a French alliance at Plombières in 1858, provoked war with Austria in 1859, and allied with Garibaldi's conquests in the south.

Legacy

He became the first prime minister of the Kingdom of Italy in March 1861 but died three months later. Alongside Garibaldi, Mazzini, and Victor Emmanuel II, he is remembered as one of the "fathers of the fatherland."
