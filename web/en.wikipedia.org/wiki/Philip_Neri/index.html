Philip Neri

Saint Philip Neri
Born: 21/22 July 1515, Florence, Republic of Florence
Died: 26 May 1595 (aged 79), Rome, Papal States
Canonized: 12 March 1622, by Pope Gregory XV
Feast day: 26 May
Known for: Founding the Congregation of the Oratory

Philip Romolo Neri (Italian: Filippo Romolo Neri; 21/22 July 1515 – 26 May 1595), known as the "Third Apostle of Rome" after Saints Peter and Paul, was an Italian Catholic priest who founded the Congregation of the Oratory and was renowned for his humility, cheerfulness and work among the people of Rome.

Life

Born in Florence, Neri was educated by the Dominicans at San Marco. In his late teens he moved to San Germano (now Cassino) to work in business, but in 1533 he went to Rome, where he lived as a tutor and spent some seventeen years as a layman devoted to prayer and apostolic work among pilgrims, bankers' clerks and the sick. Ordained a priest in 1551, he joined the community at San Girolamo della Carità, where his informal afternoon meetings of prayer, music, discussion and works of charity developed into the Oratory.

The Oratory

In 1575 Pope Gregory XIII formally recognised Neri's community as the Congregation of the Oratory, giving it the church of Santa Maria in Vallicella, rebuilt as the Chiesa Nuova. The Oratory's musical meetings gave their name to the oratorio form of sacred music drama. Neri's circle included Cesare Baronius, the church historian, and the composer Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina was among his penitents. Famous for playful humility — he wore odd clothes and joked to avoid being taken too seriously — Neri counselled popes and cardinals yet called himself a "fool for Christ".

Death and legacy

Neri died on 26 May 1595, the day after Corpus Christi, and was canonised in 1622 alongside Ignatius of Loyola, Francis Xavier, Teresa of Ávila and Isidore the Farmer. His body lies in the Chiesa Nuova in Rome. Oratories inspired by his congregation later spread worldwide, including the Birmingham Oratory founded by John Henry Newman.
