Eurasia, the largest continental landmass on Earth.

Eurasia is the largest continental area on Earth, comprising all of Europe and Asia. It covers about 55 million square kilometres (21 million square miles), or around 36.2% of the Earth's total land area, and is home to the great majority of the world's population. The landmass is bounded by the Atlantic Ocean to the west, the Pacific Ocean to the east, the Arctic Ocean to the north, and by Africa, the Mediterranean Sea, and the Indian Ocean to the south.

The Europe–Asia boundary

Europe and Asia are conventionally treated as separate continents for historical and cultural reasons, although they form a single continuous landmass. The commonly accepted boundary runs along the Ural Mountains, the Ural River, the Caspian Sea, the Caucasus, the Black Sea, and the Turkish Straits. Some geographers instead treat Eurasia as a single continent, making it one of the largest of the world's continents.

Geology

Eurasia was assembled from several ancient cratons and terranes — including Baltica, Siberia, Laurentia (partly), and numerous fragments accreted during the closure of ancient oceans. Major mountain systems such as the Himalayas and the Alps were raised by the continuing collision of the Indian and Arabian plates with the Eurasian Plate. The Eurasia of today emerged in recognisable form during the Cenozoic era.

History and geopolitics

Eurasia has been the principal theatre of world history: the Silk Road linked China, Central Asia, Persia, and Europe for over a millennium, and the Mongol Empire created the largest contiguous land empire in history across it. In geopolitics, Halford Mackinder's "Heartland" theory treated control of Eurasia's interior as the key to world power, and the term remains central to discussion of transcontinental trade corridors, railways, and the Eurasian Economic Union.
