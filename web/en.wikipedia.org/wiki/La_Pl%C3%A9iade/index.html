La Pléiade

La Pléiade was a group of 16th-century French Renaissance poets, principally Pierre de Ronsard and Joachim du Bellay, who sought to elevate French into a literary language rivalling Latin and Greek. The name, borrowed from the Alexandrian Pleiad of seven poets, was also later adopted for the prestigious Gallimard book series.

The poets

The core members were Ronsard, du Bellay, and Jean-Antoine de Baïf, together with Étienne Jodelle, Rémy Belleau, Jacques Peletier du Mans, Jean de la Péruse, and Pontus de Tyard. Du Bellay's manifesto La Défense et illustration de la langue française (1549) set out the group's programme: enrich the French language through coinages, borrowings, and imitation of classical models.

Legacy

The Pléiade reshaped French poetry, introducing the sonnet, the ode, and the epic to French literature. Ronsard became known as the "prince of poets."

Bibliothèque de la Pléiade

The name was revived in 1931 by Jacques Schiffrin for a series of deluxe editions of classic authors published by Gallimard, printed on bible paper and bound in leather; admission to the series is considered a major honour for an author.
