SAIC Motor Corporation Limited (SAIC, formerly Shanghai Automotive Industry Corporation) is a Chinese state-owned automobile manufacturer headquartered in Shanghai. It has consistently been the largest automaker in China by sales volume, selling several million vehicles annually, and ranks among the largest automotive companies in the world.

History

SAIC's origins trace to Shanghai's early automotive industry in the mid-20th century. It became a pioneer of China's modern car industry through its joint venture with Volkswagen, established in 1984 as Shanghai Volkswagen, which produced the hugely successful Santana sedan. A second major joint venture, Shanghai General Motors, followed in 1997. In the 2000s SAIC acquired rights and assets connected with the British Rover marque, launching its own Roewe brand, and later acquired the MG brand, which it relaunched internationally with considerable success in markets including Europe and Australia.

Operations

SAIC operates through joint ventures including SAIC Volkswagen, SAIC General Motors, and SAIC-GM-Wuling (SGMW), the last known for the best-selling Wuling Hongguang Mini EV. Its own brands include MG, Roewe, Maxus (LDV), and the premium electric brands IM Motors (a venture with Alibaba and Zhangjiang Hi-Tech) and Rising Auto. The company also has substantial parts, finance, and mobility-service businesses, and is a major investor in electric vehicles, batteries, and autonomous driving technology.

International expansion

Under the MG badge, SAIC has become one of the largest exporters of Chinese-built cars, with MG models among the best-selling Chinese-brand vehicles in Europe. The company operates manufacturing and assembly facilities in countries including Thailand, India, and Indonesia.
