Flight dynamics (spacecraft)

Spacecraft flight dynamics is the application of mechanics — principally Newton's laws of motion and orbital mechanics — to the motion of spacecraft. It encompasses both orbital dynamics, the motion of the spacecraft's center of mass along its trajectory, and attitude dynamics, the rotation of the spacecraft about its center of mass — the domain of attitude determination and control.

Orbital dynamics

The translational motion of a spacecraft is governed primarily by gravity. In the two-body approximation, orbits are conic sections described by Kepler's laws and specified by six orbital elements. Real trajectories are perturbed by the non-spherical gravity field of the central body, atmospheric drag, third-body gravity, and solar radiation pressure, and mission design uses maneuvers such as the Hohmann transfer and gravity assists to change orbit with minimal propellant, budgeted as delta-v.

Attitude dynamics

Rotational motion is described by Euler's equations for rigid-body motion, using moments of inertia about the spacecraft's principal axes. The torque-free motion of an asymmetric body can be unstable about its intermediate axis (the Dzhanibekov effect), a fact of practical importance for spin-stabilized spacecraft. Attitude is measured relative to inertial or orbital reference frames, commonly parameterized by Euler angles, direction-cosine matrices, or quaternions (the latter preferred computationally because they avoid gimbal-lock singularities).

Flight dynamics operations

In mission operations, "flight dynamics" is also the name of the team and function responsible for orbit determination from tracking data, maneuver planning, trajectory prediction, and conjunction assessment. Flight dynamics officers (FDOs) in crewed spaceflight are flight controllers responsible for the vehicle's trajectory.
