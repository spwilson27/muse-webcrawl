SuperphylumDeuterostomia
PhylumChordata (Bateson, 1885)
SubgroupsCephalochordata, Tunicata, Vertebrata

A chordate is an animal belonging to the phylum Chordata. Chordates possess, at some stage of their development, five key anatomical features: a notochord, a dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, an endostyle or thyroid, and a post-anal tail. The phylum includes the vertebrates (fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals), the lancelets, and the tunicates (sea squirts and salps).

Anatomy

The notochord is a flexible, rod-shaped body found in the embryos of all chordates, composed of cells derived from the mesoderm. In most vertebrates it is replaced during development by the vertebral column. The dorsal hollow nerve cord develops into the brain and spinal cord in vertebrates. Pharyngeal slits are openings in the pharynx that allow water to exit the body; in tetrapods they appear only in embryos. The post-anal tail extends beyond the anus and contains skeletal elements and muscles.

Classification

The phylum Chordata is divided into three subphyla. The Cephalochordata (lancelets, such as Branchiostoma) are small, fish-like marine animals that retain all five chordate features as adults. The Tunicata (Urochordata) include sea squirts, whose free-swimming larvae show chordate features that are largely lost in the sessile adult. The Vertebrata are the largest group, with more than 65,000 described species characterised by a backbone of vertebrae. Molecular studies group tunicates and vertebrates together in the clade Olfactores, with cephalochordates as the earliest-diverging chordate lineage.

Evolution

Chordates first appear in the fossil record of the Cambrian explosion, over 500 million years ago. Early chordate fossils from the Chengjiang biota of China include Haikouichthys and Myllokunmingia, among the oldest known vertebrates, and the enigmatic Pikaia from the Burgess Shale. Together with echinoderms and hemichordates, chordates form the deuterostomes, animals in which the anus develops before the mouth in embryonic development.
