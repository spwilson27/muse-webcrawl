A Rakat (Arabic: ركعة rakʿah, plural: ركعات rakʿāt) is a single unit of Islamic prayer (salah). Each daily prayer is composed of a different number of obligatory rakats, and each rakat consists of a prescribed sequence of movements and recitations.

Description of a rakat

A single rakat generally proceeds as follows:

  1. The worshipper stands (qiyam) and, after the opening takbir, recites the opening chapter of the Quran, Al-Fatiha, followed by another short passage of the Quran.
  2. The worshipper bows from the waist (ruku), hands on knees, glorifying God.
  3. The worshipper rises back to a standing position.
  4. The worshipper prostrates (sujud), placing forehead, nose, hands, knees and toes on the ground, glorifying God.
  5. The worshipper sits briefly (jalsah).
  6. A second prostration is performed.

This completes one rakat. Depending on the prayer, the worshipper then either stands for the next rakat or, in the final rakat, sits for the tashahhud (testimony of faith) and concludes the prayer with the taslim, turning the head to the right and left with the greeting of peace.

Number of rakats in each prayer

The five daily prayers consist of the following numbers of obligatory (fard) rakats:

  • Fajr (dawn) — 2 rakats
  • Dhuhr (midday) — 4 rakats
  • Asr (afternoon) — 4 rakats
  • Maghrib (sunset) — 3 rakats
  • Isha (night) — 4 rakats

In addition, voluntary (sunnah and nafl) rakats are commonly performed before or after the obligatory ones, and the Witr prayer, usually of an odd number of rakats, is performed after Isha. The Friday congregational prayer (Jumu'ah) consists of 2 obligatory rakats preceded by a sermon.
