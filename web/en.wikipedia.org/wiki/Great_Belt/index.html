The Great Belt (Danish: Storebælt) is a strait between the major Danish islands of Zealand (Sjælland) and Funen (Fyn). It is one of the three Danish Straits that connect the Baltic Sea to the North Sea via the Kattegat and Skagerrak. Since 1998 the islands have been linked by the Great Belt Fixed Link, a combined bridge and tunnel system.

Geography

The Great Belt is about 60 km long and 16–32 km wide. It contains several small islands, including Sprogø in the middle. The main channel is an important shipping route, providing the deepest and most navigable passage for large vessels between the Baltic and the open sea.

Great Belt Fixed Link

The fixed link consists of a road suspension bridge between Zealand and Sprogø, a road and rail box-girder bridge from Sprogø to Funen, and a bored railway tunnel under the eastern channel. The East Bridge, with a main span of 1,624 metres, was among the longest suspension bridges in the world when it opened.

History

Historically the strait froze in severe winters, allowing armies to cross on foot — most famously the Swedish army's March across the Belts in 1658. Ferry services crossed the Belt for over a century before the fixed link replaced them in the 1990s.
