ATSIC

The Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Commission (ATSIC) was the Australian Government body through which Aboriginal Australians and Torres Strait Islanders were formally involved in the processes of government affecting their lives. Established by the Hawke government in 1990, it combined a national elected representative structure with responsibility for administering Indigenous programs and funding. It was abolished by the Howard government in 2005.

Structure

ATSIC consisted of an elected arm — regional councils across Australia and a national Board of Commissioners elected by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander voters — and an administrative arm responsible for programs in areas such as housing, health, employment (including the Community Development Employment Projects scheme) and community infrastructure. Its first chairperson was Lowitja O'Donoghue.

Abolition

The Commission was dogged by controversy, including allegations of financial mismanagement and disputes surrounding its long-serving chairman Geoff Clark. Following a government review, the Howard government announced its abolition in 2004; legislation passed in 2005 wound up the elected structure, and its programs were transferred to mainstream government departments under a policy of "mainstreaming" Indigenous services. The abolition remains debated in discussions of Indigenous representative bodies, a topic later revisited by the Uluru Statement from the Heart and the 2023 Voice referendum.
