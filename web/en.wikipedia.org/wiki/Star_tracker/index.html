Star tracker

A star tracker is an optical device that measures the positions of stars using photocells or a camera. As the positions of many stars have been measured by astronomers to a high degree of accuracy, a star tracker on a satellite or spacecraft may be used to determine the orientation (attitude) of the spacecraft with respect to those stars.

Operation

The tracker photographs a field of stars, extracts their centroids, and matches the pattern of observed stars to an onboard star catalog. From the matched stars, the instrument computes the spacecraft's orientation as a quaternion or rotation matrix, typically with an accuracy of a few arc-seconds. A star tracker can also operate in a tracking mode, following individual stars to provide high-rate attitude updates.

Uses

Star trackers were used in early ballistic missiles and are now standard on most satellites, interplanetary probes, and many aircraft. They form part of the attitude determination and control system alongside sun sensors, gyroscopes, and magnetometers. In astronomy, small star trackers (guide cameras) keep telescopes aligned during long exposures, and amateur astrophotographers use similar devices for polar alignment.
