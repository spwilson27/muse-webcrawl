Giro d'Italia

Giro d'Italia
First edition: 1909
Editions: annually (as of 2024, 107)
Region: Italy (occasionally neighbouring countries)
General classification jersey: Pink (maglia rosa)

The Giro d'Italia is an annual multiple-stage bicycle race primarily held in Italy, though it sometimes starts or passes through other countries. First organized in 1909 to promote the newspaper La Gazzetta dello Sport, it is one of cycling's three Grand Tours, alongside the Tour de France and the Vuelta a España, and is part of the UCI World Tour.

Format

The race, usually held in May, lasts about three weeks and covers roughly 3,500 km across mountain stages in the Alps and Dolomites, flat sprinter stages, and individual and team time trials. The leader of the general classification wears the pink jersey (maglia rosa), coloured after the pink paper on which La Gazzetta is printed. Other classifications include the points classification (maglia ciclamino), mountains classification (maglia azzurra), and young rider classification (maglia bianca).

History and records

The race was suspended during both World Wars. Alfredo Binda, Fausto Coppi, and Eddy Merckx share the record of five overall victories each. Famous mountain passes associated with the Giro include the Passo dello Stelvio, the Mortirolo, and the Passo Gavia; the highest point of each edition is designated the Cima Coppi.
