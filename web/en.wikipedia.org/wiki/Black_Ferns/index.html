The Black Ferns are New Zealand's women's national rugby union team, so named for the colour of their uniform and the silver fern, a national symbol. The team is one of the most successful in the history of women's rugby, having won the Women's Rugby World Cup six times. The Black Ferns perform a haka before matches and are administered by New Zealand Rugby.

History

Women's rugby in New Zealand grew through the 1980s, and a national team was first selected for the 1990 RugbyFest in Christchurch. The Black Ferns played at the first Women's Rugby World Cup in 1991, reaching the semi-finals, and went on to dominate the professional era: they won four consecutive World Cups from 1998 to 2010 under coaches including Darryl Suasau and Jed Rowlands, featuring legendary players such as Farah Palmer, Anna Richards, and Emma Jensen.

Recent era

After a shock pool-stage exit in 2014, the Black Ferns returned to win the 2017 World Cup in Ireland, defeating England in the final. In 2022, hosting the postponed 2021 tournament, they won a dramatic final against England 34–31 before a record crowd at Eden Park in Auckland, with stars including Portia Woodman, Ruby Tui, and Stacey Waaka; Woodman became the leading try-scorer in Women's World Cup history. The team also competes annually in the Pacific Four Series and is central to the growth of women's rugby in New Zealand, which moved towards professional contracts from 2018.

Honours

  • Women's Rugby World Cup champions: 1998, 2002, 2006, 2010, 2017, 2021
  • Numerous World Rugby Women's Team of the Year awards
  • Home of multiple World Rugby Women's Player of the Year winners
