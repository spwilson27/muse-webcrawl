Royal Australian Air Force

The Royal Australian Air Force (RAAF) is the principal air and space force of Australia, a part of the Australian Defence Force along with the Royal Australian Navy and the Australian Army. Formed on 31 March 1921, it is the second-oldest independent air force in the world after Britain's RAF.

History

Australian airmen served in the First World War in the Australian Flying Corps. In the Second World War the RAAF grew to over 150,000 personnel, fighting in Europe, the Mediterranean and the Pacific. It has since served in Korea, Vietnam, the Gulf War, Afghanistan and Iraq.

Modern force

Today the RAAF has roughly 17,000 permanent personnel and operates F-35A Lightning II fighters, F/A-18F Super Hornets, EA-18G Growlers, C-17 Globemaster and KC-30A transports, P-8A Poseidon maritime patrol aircraft and E-7A Wedgetail airborne early warning aircraft from bases across Australia, including Tindal, Williamtown and Amberley. Its motto is Per ardua ad astra.
