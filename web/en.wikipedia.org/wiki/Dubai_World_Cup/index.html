Dubai World Cup

The Dubai World Cup is run at Meydan Racecourse under lights

The Dubai World Cup (Arabic: كأس دبي العالمي) is a Thoroughbred horse race held annually since 1996 at Meydan Racecourse in Dubai, United Arab Emirates. Run over 2,000 metres (about 1¼ miles) on dirt, it is the feature race of Dubai World Cup Night, one of the richest meetings in world racing, and carried a purse of US$12 million from 2019, making it for a time the world's richest horse race until surpassed by the Saudi Cup.

History

The race was created by Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum, ruler of Dubai and founder of the Godolphin racing operation, to showcase Dubai as a centre of international racing. The first running, in 1996 at Nad Al Sheba Racecourse, was won by the American champion Cigar, whose victory helped establish the event's prestige. From 2010 the meeting moved to the newly built Meydan Racecourse, whose grandstand is over a mile long; between 2010 and 2014 the race was run on a synthetic Tapeta surface before reverting to dirt in 2015.

Notable winners

Notable winners include Cigar (1996), Dubai Millennium (2000), Street Cry (2002), Curlin (2008), Animal Kingdom (2013), California Chrome (2016), and Arrogate (2017), whose come-from-last victory is considered one of the race's greatest performances. Thunder Snow became the first horse to win the race twice, in 2018 and 2019. Ushba Tesoro (2023) and Laurel River (2024) are among the recent winners.

Dubai World Cup Night

The supporting card on the same evening includes the Dubai Sheema Classic and Dubai Turf (both Group 1 races on grass), the Dubai Golden Shaheen sprint, the Al Quoz Sprint, the UAE Derby and the Dubai Gold Cup, making the meeting a major international fixture held each year on the last Saturday in March.
