Splitting field


Roots of a polynomial inside its splitting field.

In abstract algebra, a splitting field of a polynomial with coefficients in a field is the smallest field extension in which the polynomial splits, or decomposes into linear factors. That is, a splitting field is an extension generated by adjoining all the roots of the polynomial to the base field, so that over the extension the polynomial factors completely as a product of terms (x − ri).

Construction and uniqueness

Given a polynomial p(x) over a field F, one can construct a splitting field by successively adjoining roots using quotient rings F[x]/(irreducible factor). A fundamental theorem states that splitting fields always exist and are unique up to isomorphism over F: any two splitting fields of the same polynomial are isomorphic as extensions of F.

Properties

A splitting field is a finite extension, and its degree divides n! where n is the degree of the polynomial. Splitting fields are normal extensions, and when the polynomial is separable the extension is also Galois, connecting splitting fields to the Galois group of the polynomial. For example, the splitting field of x2 − 2 over the rationals is ℚ(√2), while the splitting field of x3 − 2 requires adjoining both a real cube root and a primitive cube root of unity.
