Sneakers

Sneakers (also called athletic shoes, trainers in British English, or runners) are shoes primarily designed for sports or other forms of physical exercise, but which are also widely used for everyday casual wear.

The shoes generally have a flexible rubber or synthetic sole and an upper of leather, canvas or engineered mesh. The name "sneakers" is attributed to the quiet rubber soles, which allowed the wearer to "sneak" up on someone; American advertising agent Henry Nelson McKinney is often credited with popularising the term around 1917.

History

Rubber-soled "plimsolls" appeared in the 1870s. In the early 20th century the U.S. Rubber Company introduced Keds (1916) and Converse launched its All Star basketball shoe (1917). Adidas and Puma, founded by the brothers Adolf and Rudolf Dassler after the Second World War, drove technical innovation, while Nike (founded as Blue Ribbon Sports in 1964) and its Air Jordan line from 1985 turned sneakers into a global fashion and collecting phenomenon.

Design

Modern sneakers incorporate cushioning foams, air or gel units, carbon-fibre plates in racing shoes, and sport-specific geometries for running, basketball, tennis, skateboarding and cross-training. Pronation control, stability features and energy-return foams are major axes of design.

Culture

"Sneakerhead" collecting culture, limited-edition drops, celebrity collaborations and a large resale market have made sneakers a significant cultural and economic force, influencing street fashion worldwide.
