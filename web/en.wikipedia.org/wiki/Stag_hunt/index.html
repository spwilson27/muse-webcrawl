Stag hunt

In game theory, the stag hunt is a dilemma that describes a conflict between safety and social cooperation. The story involves hunters who can cooperate to catch a stag, which requires mutual trust, or individually hunt a hare, which is safer but less rewarding.

Formalisation

The stag hunt is a game with two pure-strategy Nash equilibria: the payoff-dominant equilibrium in which both hunters choose stag, and the risk-dominant equilibrium in which both choose hare. Unlike the prisoner's dilemma, mutual cooperation is itself an equilibrium.

Philosophical origins

Jean-Jacques Rousseau described the scenario in his Discourse on Inequality (1755) as a parable about the foundations of civil society. The game has since been used to study trust, coordination and the evolution of cooperation.
