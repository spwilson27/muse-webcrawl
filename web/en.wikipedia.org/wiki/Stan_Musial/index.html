Stan Musial

Stanley Frank Musial (/ˈmjuːziəl/; born Stanisław Franciszek Musiał; November 21, 1920 – January 19, 2013), nicknamed "Stan the Man", was an American baseball outfielder and first baseman. He spent his entire 22-season Major League Baseball career (1941–1963, missing 1945 for military service) with the St. Louis Cardinals, and is widely considered one of the greatest hitters in the history of the game.

Career

Musial was signed by the Cardinals as a pitcher but converted to the outfield after injuring his shoulder. He broke into the majors in September 1941 and quickly became a star, winning the first of his three National League Most Valuable Player awards in 1943 and helping St. Louis win the World Series in 1942, 1944 and 1946. He served in the U.S. Navy in 1945.

Famous for his unusual coiled, peek-a-boo batting stance, Musial won seven National League batting titles and led the league in nearly every major offensive category at various times. He retired with 3,630 hits — at the time second only to Ty Cobb — a figure notable for its symmetry: exactly 1,815 hits at home and 1,815 on the road. He was selected to 24 All-Star Games (including both games in the years when two were played) and was named to Major League Baseball's All-Century Team in 1999.

The nickname "Stan the Man" was bestowed by Brooklyn Dodgers fans in 1946, groaning in admiration as Musial repeatedly punished their team at Ebbets Field. He was elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1969 on the first ballot. After his playing career he served briefly as the Cardinals' general manager, winning the 1967 World Series in that role, and ran successful business ventures in St. Louis, where he remained a beloved civic figure. In 2011 President Barack Obama awarded him the Presidential Medal of Freedom. A statue of Musial stands outside Busch Stadium in St. Louis.
