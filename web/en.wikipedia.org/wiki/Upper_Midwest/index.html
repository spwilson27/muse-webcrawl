Upper Midwest

The Upper Midwest, centred on the western Great Lakes

The Upper Midwest is a region of the United States generally considered to comprise the northern tier of the Midwestern states, including Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan, Iowa, and the Dakotas; definitions sometimes also include Illinois and the northwestern parts of neighbouring states. The region is sometimes called the "North Country" and is associated with the western Great Lakes and the headwaters of the Mississippi River.

Geography and climate

The region is characterised by glacial lakes, northern forests, and prairie, with a humid continental climate featuring cold winters and warm summers. Major metropolitan areas include Minneapolis–Saint Paul, Milwaukee, Detroit, and Des Moines.

Culture

The Upper Midwest was settled heavily by Scandinavian and German immigrants in the 19th century, an inheritance still visible in its foodways, place names, and political traditions. The region is known for a distinctive dialect (featured in the film Fargo), an ethos of civic engagement, and historically strong support for progressive and farmer-labour movements.
