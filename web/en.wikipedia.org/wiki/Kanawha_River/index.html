The Kanawha River is a tributary of the Ohio River, approximately 97 miles (156 km) long, in the U.S. state of West Virginia. It is formed at Gauley Bridge by the confluence of the New River and the Gauley River, flows northwest through the state capital, Charleston, and joins the Ohio at Point Pleasant. Including the New River, the Kanawha system drains the largest watershed in West Virginia.

History and industry

The valley was an early centre of the American salt industry, and its coal, oil, natural gas and brine later supported a major chemical industry; towns such as South Charleston and Nitro grew around chemical plants in the twentieth century, earning the valley the name "Chemical Valley". Locks and dams built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers make the river navigable for barge traffic carrying coal and chemicals.

Events

In January 2014 a spill of crude MCHM from a Freedom Industries facility on the Elk River, a tributary, contaminated the water supply of some 300,000 people in the Charleston area, one of the largest drinking-water emergencies in recent U.S. history.
