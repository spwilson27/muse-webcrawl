Udon

Udon (うどん) is a thick noodle made of wheat flour used in Japanese cuisine. It is usually served hot in a mild, dashi-based broth (kake udon), or chilled with a dipping sauce.

History

Noodles were introduced to Japan from China; a predecessor of udon, made from a wheat-flour dumpling dough, is described in sources from the Nara and Heian periods. Udon became widespread in the Edo period. Kagawa Prefecture on Shikoku is famous for its firm Sanuki udon.

Dishes

Common udon dishes include kitsune udon (topped with sweetened fried tofu), tempura udon, nabeyaki udon (served in a hot pot), yaki udon (stir-fried), and zaru udon (chilled on a bamboo tray). Udon is also used in regional hot pots such as hōtō from Yamanashi.

Production

Udon dough is made from wheat flour, water and salt, kneaded (traditionally by foot), rested, rolled and cut into thick strands. Fresh, dried and frozen udon are sold commercially.
