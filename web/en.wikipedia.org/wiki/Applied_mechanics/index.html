Applied mechanics

Applied mechanics is the application of the principles of mechanics — the branch of physics concerned with the motion of bodies and the forces acting on them — to practical engineering problems. It bridges theoretical mechanics and fields such as civil, mechanical, aerospace, and biomedical engineering.

Discipline: Engineering / Physics
Subfields: Statics, dynamics, solid mechanics, fluid mechanics, biomechanics

Branches

Applied mechanics includes statics (bodies at rest), dynamics (bodies in motion), strength of materials and solid mechanics (deformation and fracture of solids), fluid mechanics (liquids and gases), and specialized areas such as fracture mechanics, contact mechanics, and biomechanics.

Methods

Practitioners use analytical mathematics, experimental testing, and computational methods such as the finite element method to model stresses, strains, vibrations, and flows in structures and machines, from bridges and aircraft to prosthetic devices.
