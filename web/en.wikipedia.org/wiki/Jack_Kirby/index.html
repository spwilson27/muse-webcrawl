Jack Kirby (born Jacob Kurtzberg; August 28, 1917 – February 6, 1994) was an American comic book artist, writer, and editor, widely regarded as one of the medium's major innovators and one of its most prolific and influential creators. He grew up in New York City and learned to draw cartoon figures by tracing characters from comic strips and editorial cartoons. He entered the nascent comics industry in the 1930s, drawing various comics features under different pen names, eventually settling on Jack Kirby.

In 1940, he and writer-editor Joe Simon created the highly successful Captain America for Timely Comics, the predecessor of Marvel Comics. During the 1940s Kirby regularly teamed with Simon — creating numerous characters for that company and for National Comics Publications, later DC Comics — and helped pioneer the romance comics genre.

Marvel years

After the super-hero genre declined in the 1950s, Kirby returned to Marvel in the late 1950s. With writer Stan Lee he co-created much of the Marvel Universe in a burst of creativity beginning with the Fantastic Four (1961) and continuing through the Hulk, Thor, Iron Man, the X-Men, the Avengers, the Black Panther (the first Black superhero in mainstream American comics), the Inhumans, the Silver Surfer, and Galactus. Kirby's kinetic artwork — extreme foreshortening, cosmic machinery, and the energy texture known as "Kirby crackle" — and his plotting under the "Marvel Method" made him the visual architect of the Marvel Age.

DC and the Fourth World

Unhappy with his treatment at Marvel, Kirby moved to DC Comics in 1970 and created the Fourth World saga across titles including The New Gods, Mister Miracle, and The Forever People, introducing Darkseid, Orion, and the planet Apokolips. He later created Kamandi and The Eternals (on a brief return to Marvel), and worked in animation in the 1980s.

Legacy and recognition

Kirby spent his later years in a celebrated dispute with Marvel over credit and the return of his original artwork, much of which was eventually returned. He was posthumously inducted into the industry's halls of fame, and the Jack Kirby Awards and Kirby Awards bear his name. In 2017 the Kurtzberg family reached a settlement with Marvel days before the U.S. Supreme Court was to consider their copyright-termination case (the parties had earlier settled in 2014). Retrospective assessments widely credit Kirby as co-author of the characters he drew, and modern Marvel films and comics constantly draw on his designs.
