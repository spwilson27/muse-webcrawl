Equal Employment Opportunity Commission

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) is a United States federal government agency that was established via the Civil Rights Act of 1964 to administer and enforce civil rights laws against workplace discrimination. It is headquartered in Washington, D.C., with field offices across the country.

Jurisdiction

The EEOC enforces federal laws prohibiting employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex (including pregnancy, sexual orientation, and gender identity), national origin, age (40 or older), disability, and genetic information. These laws include Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Equal Pay Act of 1963, the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967, and Titles I and V of the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990. Most employers with at least 15 employees (20 for age discrimination) are covered.

Process

Individuals generally must file a charge of discrimination with the EEOC before suing an employer in court. The agency investigates charges, may mediate or conciliate, and can file suit on behalf of complainants. If it declines to act, it issues a "right to sue" notice. The Commission consists of five members appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate.
