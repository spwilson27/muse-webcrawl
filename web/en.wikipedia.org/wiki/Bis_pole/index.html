Bis pole

A bis pole (bisj, mbis) is a ritual wooden pole sculpted by the Asmat people of New Guinea, in the southwestern part of the Indonesian province of South Papua. Bis poles are among the most celebrated examples of Oceanic art and can reach heights of more than 7 metres (23 feet).

Purpose

Bis poles were erected as memorials to the dead and as the centrepiece of a bis feast, a ritual cycle connected with headhunting and the avenging of deaths believed to have been caused by enemy action or sorcery. The feast restored spiritual balance between the living and the ancestors. After the ceremony the poles were traditionally left to decay in sago palm groves, returning fertility to the land.

Design

A bis pole is carved from a single mangrove tree placed root-side up. Stacked openwork figures represent ancestors and recently deceased community members, along with symbols such as the cemen (phalliform motifs) and representations of animals. The American collector Michael C. Rockefeller disappeared in 1961 while collecting Asmat art in the region.
