Fuzhou

Fuzhou (Chinese: 福州; pinyin: Fúzhōu) is the capital and one of the largest cities in Fujian province, China. Located on the western shore of the Taiwan Strait at the estuary of the Min River, it has been an important port for over two thousand years.

History

Founded in the Han dynasty era, Fuzhou was one of the five treaty ports opened after the First Opium War in 1842 and became a centre of the tea trade. The Foochow Arsenal, established in the 1860s, was a major early Chinese naval shipyard.

Geography and climate

The city sits in a basin surrounded by mountains, with a humid subtropical climate, hot humid summers and mild winters. It is famed for its many banyan trees, giving it the nickname "Banyan City".

Culture

Fuzhou is known for Min cuisine, Fuzhou dialect (a prestige variety of Eastern Min), lacquerware, and the historic Three Lanes and Seven Alleys district.
