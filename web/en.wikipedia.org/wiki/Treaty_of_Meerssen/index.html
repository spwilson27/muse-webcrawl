Treaty of Meerssen

Signed8 August 870
LocationMeerssen, near Maastricht
PartiesCharles the Bald (West Francia); Louis the German (East Francia)
EffectPartition of Lotharingia

The partition of Lotharingia between the West and East Frankish kingdoms in 870.

The Treaty of Meerssen, concluded on 8 August 870 at Meerssen (near Maastricht in the present-day Netherlands), divided the Middle Frankish kingdom of Lotharingia between the West Frankish king Charles the Bald and the East Frankish king Louis the German.

Background

The Treaty of Verdun (843) had divided Charlemagne's empire among his three grandsons: Charles the Bald received West Francia, Louis the German East Francia, and Lothair I the central strip known as Middle Francia. When Lothair's son Lothair II died childless in 869, his uncles Charles and Louis moved to divide his kingdom of Lotharingia between them, disregarding the claims of Lothair II's brother, Emperor Louis II of Italy.

Terms

The partition line ran roughly along the rivers Meuse, Ourthe and Moselle. Louis the German received the larger eastern portion, including Aachen, much of the Rhineland and the cities of Cologne and Metz's eastern hinterland, while Charles the Bald received the western part of Lotharingia, including parts of Flanders and the lands west of the Meuse. The division was resented by Louis II and by the papacy, but it effectively extinguished Lotharingia as an independent kingdom for a generation.

Aftermath

The Treaty of Ribemont (880) later reassigned the whole of Lotharingia to East Francia after Charles the Bald's death. Lotharingia remained a disputed borderland between France and Germany for centuries, a legacy that echoes in the later history of Alsace-Lorraine.
