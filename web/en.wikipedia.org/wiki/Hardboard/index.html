Hardboard

Hardboard, also called high-density fiberboard (HDF), is a type of fiberboard, an engineered wood product. It is made by compressing exploded wood fibres under heat and pressure; unlike particleboard it uses the natural lignin in wood as its main binder.

Manufacture

In the Masonite process, patented by William H. Mason in 1924, wood chips are steam-exploded into fibres, formed into mats and hot-pressed into dense, smooth sheets. Tempered hardboard is further treated with oils and heat for moisture resistance.

Uses

Hardboard is used in furniture backs, door skins, flooring underlayment, packaging, and as the painting support known as Masonite board used by artists. Pegboard is a perforated form of tempered hardboard.
