Expected shortfall

Expected shortfall (ES) is a risk measure — a concept used in the field of financial risk measurement to evaluate the market risk or credit risk of a portfolio. The "expected shortfall at q% level" is the expected return on the portfolio in the worst q% of cases. ES is an alternative to value at risk (VaR) that is more sensitive to the shape of the tail of the loss distribution.

Expected shortfall is the average loss in the tail beyond the VaR threshold.

Definition

Expected shortfall is defined as the conditional expectation of loss given that the loss exceeds the VaR at the same confidence level. Unlike VaR, it satisfies all four axioms of a coherent risk measure — in particular subadditivity, meaning diversification never increases measured risk — which VaR can violate.

Regulatory use

Following the 2007–2008 financial crisis, the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision moved market-risk capital requirements from VaR to expected shortfall in the Fundamental Review of the Trading Book (FRTB), specifying ES at the 97.5% confidence level, calibrated to be broadly comparable to the previous 99% VaR standard for normal distributions while capturing tail risk more effectively.

Estimation

ES can be estimated by historical simulation (averaging the worst observed losses), parametric methods, or Monte Carlo simulation. Because it depends on rare tail events, estimates are noisy, and backtesting ES is statistically harder than backtesting VaR — a long-standing practical objection partly answered by joint elicitability results for the (VaR, ES) pair.
