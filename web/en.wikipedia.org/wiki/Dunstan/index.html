Dunstan

Saint Dunstan
Born: c. 909, Baltonsborough, Wessex
Died: 19 May 988, Canterbury
Archbishop of Canterbury: 959–988
Feast day: 19 May

Dunstan (c. 909 – 19 May 988) was an English Benedictine monk, abbot, and Archbishop of Canterbury, who later was canonised as a saint. A central figure in the tenth-century English Benedictine Reform, he served as chief minister to several kings and was for centuries among the most popular saints in England.

Life

Born near Glastonbury, Dunstan was educated by Irish scholars and served at the court of King Æthelstan before becoming a monk. Appointed abbot of Glastonbury around 943 by King Edmund, he rebuilt the abbey and made it a centre of learning. Exiled under King Eadwig, he spent time in Flanders observing continental monastic practice; recalled by King Edgar, he was made Archbishop of Canterbury in 959.

Working with Æthelwold of Winchester and Oswald of Worcester, Dunstan promoted strict Benedictine observance, education, and church reform, and guided the drafting of the Regularis Concordia. He was also renowned as a craftsman — metalworker, jeweller, and illuminator — and as a musician. Folklore remembers him tweaking the Devil's nose with red-hot tongs.

Veneration

Dunstan is the patron saint of goldsmiths, silversmiths, blacksmiths, jewellers, musicians, and bell founders. His shrine at Canterbury was among the most visited in medieval England until its destruction in the Reformation.
