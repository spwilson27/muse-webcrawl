Violeta Parra

Violeta del Carmen Parra Sandoval (4 October 1917 – 5 February 1967) was a Chilean singer-songwriter, folklorist, ethnomusicologist and visual artist. She was a foundational figure of the Nueva canción chilena (New Chilean Song) movement, which revived and transformed Chilean folk music and influenced socially committed song across Latin America.

Life and work

Born in San Carlos (or San Fabián de Alico) in the Ñuble region to a schoolteacher father and a peasant mother, Parra grew up in poverty and began singing and playing guitar as a child. After performing in bars, circuses and on radio, she devoted herself from the early 1950s to travelling Chile recording traditional singers, rescuing thousands of folk songs, dances, proverbs and recipes from oblivion. Her radio programme and her own compositions — including "Volver a los 17", "Run Run se fue pa'l norte" and the celebrated "Gracias a la vida" — fused this research with original, often politically charged songwriting.

In the 1960s Parra lived in Paris, where in 1964 she became the first Latin American artist to have a solo exhibition at the Louvre, showing her arpilleras (embroidered tapestries) and oil paintings. Back in Chile she founded the Carpa de La Reina, a cultural tent in Santiago, which drew small audiences despite her growing international reputation. Suffering from depression, she took her own life in 1967.

Legacy

Parra's children Isabel and Ángel Parra became leading figures of the Nueva canción, and her work inspired artists including Mercedes Sosa, Víctor Jara and Joan Baez. The Museo Violeta Parra opened in Santiago in 2015, and her centenary was celebrated throughout Chile in 2017. "Gracias a la vida" is among the most covered Spanish-language songs in the world.
