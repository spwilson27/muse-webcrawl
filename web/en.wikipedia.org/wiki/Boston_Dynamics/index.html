Boston Dynamics is an American engineering and robotics design company founded in 1992 as a spin-off from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. It is best known for developing highly mobile robots, including BigDog, Spot, and the humanoid Atlas.

History

Founder Marc Raibert's research at MIT's Leg Lab focused on dynamically balancing legged robots. The company initially worked on simulation software and DARPA-funded military robots such as BigDog, a quadruped intended to carry equipment for soldiers over rough terrain. Google X acquired the company in 2013; it was sold to SoftBank in 2017, and a controlling stake was acquired by Hyundai Motor Group in 2021.

Robots

Spot, a four-legged robot with an optional articulated arm, became the company's first commercial product in 2019 and is used for inspection and data collection in industry and construction. Stretch is a warehouse robot for truck unloading. Atlas, the bipedal humanoid research platform, demonstrated parkour, gymnastics, and dancing in viral videos; an all-electric Atlas was unveiled in 2024.

Reception

Boston Dynamics' demonstration videos have attracted enormous public attention, while also prompting discussion about the future military and workplace roles of agile robots. The company maintains a policy against weaponizing its general-purpose robots.
