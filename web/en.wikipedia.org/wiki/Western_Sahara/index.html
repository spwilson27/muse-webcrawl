Western Sahara is a disputed territory in the Maghreb region of North and West Africa, bordered by Morocco to the north, Algeria to the northeast, Mauritania to the east and south, and the Atlantic Ocean to the west. A former Spanish colony, it is one of the most sparsely populated territories in the world, consisting mainly of desert flatlands.

History

Spain established a protectorate in the region in 1884 and later organized it as Spanish Sahara. Under pressure from Morocco, Mauritania and the United Nations, Spain withdrew in 1975–76 under the Madrid Accords, which divided administration between Morocco and Mauritania. The Polisario Front, representing the indigenous Sahrawi people, proclaimed the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic and fought a guerrilla war against both countries. Mauritania withdrew in 1979, and Morocco annexed most of the territory.

A UN-brokered ceasefire in 1991 ended the fighting and promised a referendum on independence that has never been held. Morocco controls the territory west of the sand wall known as the Berm, including Laayoune and the phosphate-rich region around Bou Craa, while the Polisario Front controls the sparsely populated "Free Zone" to the east. A new ceasefire collapse in 2020 resumed low-level conflict.
