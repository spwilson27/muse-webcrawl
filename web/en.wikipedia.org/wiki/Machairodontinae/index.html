Machairodontinae

Machairodontinae is an extinct subfamily of carnivoran mammals of the cat family Felidae, known commonly as sabre-toothed cats. Living from the Miocene to the end of the Pleistocene (about 23 million to 10,000 years ago), machairodonts were found on all continents except Australia and Antarctica.

Characteristics

The group is defined by elongated, flattened upper canine teeth, often with serrated edges, accompanied by powerful forelimbs and shortened tails. Two broad body plans are recognised: the dirk-toothed forms, such as Smilodon and Homotherium (the latter more "scimitar-toothed"), built for wrestling large prey, and more gracile forms. Sabre teeth evolved independently in several other mammalian lineages, making the machairodonts a classic example of convergent evolution.

Extinction

The last genera, Smilodon in the Americas and Homotherium in Eurasia and North America, disappeared around 10,000 years ago during the Quaternary extinction event, which also wiped out much of the megafauna on which they preyed.
