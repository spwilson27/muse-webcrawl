Ray-Ban

Ray-Ban is an American brand of sunglasses and eyeglasses created in 1936 by the optical company Bausch & Lomb. It is famous for the Aviator and Wayfarer styles, among the most enduring eyewear designs of the 20th century. The brand has been owned by the Italian-French group EssilorLuxottica since 1999.

History

The Aviator was developed in the 1930s for U.S. Army Air Corps pilots, who needed glare protection at altitude; its large teardrop lenses and green G-15 lenses became an icon, popularized by figures such as General Douglas MacArthur. The Wayfarer, launched in 1952 with moulded plastic frames, broke with metal-frame tradition and became a cultural staple through films such as Risky Business and The Blues Brothers.

Ownership and revival

After declining sales in the 1990s, Bausch & Lomb sold Ray-Ban to the Italian Luxottica Group in 1999 for a reported $640 million. Luxottica repositioned the brand upmarket, tightened distribution, and restored it to global leadership in premium eyewear; Ray-Ban has since extended into smart glasses collaborations, including the Ray-Ban Meta line.
