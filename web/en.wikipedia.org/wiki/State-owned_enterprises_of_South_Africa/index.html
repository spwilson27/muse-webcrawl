State-owned enterprises of South Africa

Major state-owned enterprises dominate electricity, transport and logistics in South Africa.

State-owned enterprises (SOEs) in South Africa are companies owned wholly or partly by the national government. They play a central role in the economy, particularly in electricity generation, freight and passenger transport, ports, pipelines, defence manufacturing, and broadcasting. A dedicated Department of Public Enterprises historically exercised shareholder oversight over the largest of them (the department was wound up in 2024, with oversight moved to the Presidency).

Major enterprises

  • Eskom — the national electricity utility, generating the great majority of South Africa's power.
  • Transnet — freight rail, ports, and pipelines.
  • PRASA — passenger rail services.
  • South African Airways — the flag carrier, restructured after business rescue in 2019–2021.
  • Denel — armaments and aerospace manufacturer.
  • SABC — the public broadcaster.
  • PetroSA — the state petroleum company.
  • South African Post Office and Postbank — postal and banking services.
  • Industrial Development Corporation — a development finance institution.

Governance and challenges

South Africa has hundreds of SOEs at national, provincial and municipal level, scheduled under the Public Finance Management Act. Many have struggled with debt, operational failures and governance breakdowns. The Zondo Commission into state capture documented extensive corruption and political interference at companies including Eskom and Transnet during the 2010s, and several large SOEs required government bailouts or debt relief. Rolling electricity blackouts (load shedding), linked to Eskom's ageing coal fleet, became a major national economic constraint, easing after reforms that opened generation to private producers.
