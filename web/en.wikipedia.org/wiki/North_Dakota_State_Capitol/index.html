North Dakota State Capitol

[Image: North Dakota State Capitol]

The North Dakota State Capitol is the house of government of the U.S. state of North Dakota, located in Bismarck. The 19-story Art Deco tower, completed in 1934, is the tallest habitable building in the state at 241 feet 8 inches (73.7 m), and is nicknamed "the Skyscraper on the Prairie".

History

The first territorial and state capitol on the site burned on 28 December 1930, in the depths of the Great Depression. Designed by architects Joseph Bell DeRemer of Grand Forks and W. F. Kurke of Fargo, the replacement was built economically in stripped Art Deco style for about $2 million, reusing Indiana limestone facing. A statue of Sakakawea (Sacagawea) by Leonard Crunelle stands on the grounds, alongside the Pioneer Family statue and the All Veterans Memorial.

The building and grounds

The capitol houses the chambers of the North Dakota Legislative Assembly, the governor's office, and the state Supreme Court (in the judicial wing added in 1981). Inside, Memorial Hall features bronze and marble finishes and chandeliers. The 160-acre grounds, laid out as an arboretum-like campus in the northern part of Bismarck, also contain the State Library, the North Dakota Heritage Center, and the governor's residence.

Observation

An observation area on the top floor offers views over Bismarck and the Missouri River valley. The building is listed on the National Register of Historic Places as part of the capitol grounds district.
