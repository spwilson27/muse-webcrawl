Kings of Athens

The kings of Athens were the rulers of the ancient Greek city-state of Athens in its earliest, largely legendary period, before the establishment of the archonship and, later, Athenian democracy.

Legendary kings

According to Athenian tradition, the first king was Cecrops, a mythical half-man, half-serpent figure said to have founded the city and judged the contest between Athena and Poseidon for its patronage. His successors in myth include Erechtheus, Aegeus, and Theseus, the hero credited with the synoikismos — the political unification of Attica under Athens — and with feats including the slaying of the Minotaur on Crete.

Last kings and the archons

The last king is traditionally said to be Codrus, who, legend held, sacrificed himself in a war against Dorian invaders. After his death, the Athenians supposedly abolished the kingship in his honour, replacing it first with lifelong archons, then with archons serving ten-year terms, and from 683/2 BC with the annual board of nine archons. One archon retained the title archon basileus ("king archon") and inherited the king's religious functions.
