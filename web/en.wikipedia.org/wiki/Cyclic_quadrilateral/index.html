Cyclic quadrilateral

A cyclic quadrilateral with its circumcircle.

In Euclidean geometry, a cyclic quadrilateral or inscribed quadrilateral is a quadrilateral whose vertices all lie on a single circle. This circle is called the circumcircle or circumscribed circle, and the vertices are said to be concyclic. Every triangle is cyclic, but not every quadrilateral is: a quadrilateral is cyclic if and only if its opposite angles are supplementary, summing to 180 degrees.

Properties

Ptolemy's theorem states that for a cyclic quadrilateral, the product of the diagonals equals the sum of the products of opposite sides. Brahmagupta's formula gives the area of a cyclic quadrilateral in terms of its side lengths and is the maximum area attainable by any quadrilateral with those sides. The perpendicular bisectors of the four sides meet at the centre of the circumcircle.

Special cases

Squares, rectangles, and isosceles trapezoids are always cyclic. A kite is cyclic exactly when it has two right angles. Quadrilaterals with additional structure, such as Brahmagupta quadrilaterals with integer sides, diagonals, and area, are studied in number theory and geometry.
