Trireme

A trireme (from Latin trirēmis, "with three banks of oars"; Greek: τριήρης, triērēs) was an ancient vessel and a type of galley used by the sea-faring civilisations of the Mediterranean from the 7th to the 4th centuries BC, including the Phoenicians, Greeks, and Romans.

Design

The trireme was about 37 metres long with a beam of about 5.5 metres. Its roughly 170 oars were arranged in three levels — thranites (top), zygites (middle), and thalamites (bottom) — one rower per oar, pulling through an outrigger (parexeiresia) that gave the top bank its leverage. A bronze-sheathed ram at the waterline was the principal weapon; tactics centred on ramming manoeuvres such as the diekplous and periplous. A single square sail was used for cruising but lowered before battle.

History

Triremes formed the backbone of the Athenian navy that defeated Persia at Salamis (480 BC) and fought through the Peloponnesian War. They were eventually superseded by larger polyremes such as quinqueremes. In the 1980s the Hellenic Navy and the Trireme Trust built the full-scale reconstruction Olympias, whose sea trials confirmed the ancient sources' performance claims, including sprint speeds near 9 knots.
