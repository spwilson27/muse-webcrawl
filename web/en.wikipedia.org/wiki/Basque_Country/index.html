The Basque Country (Basque: Euskal Herria) is the name given to the home of the Basque people in the western Pyrenees, straddling the border between France and Spain on the coast of the Bay of Biscay. It comprises the autonomous communities of the Basque Country and Navarre in Spain and the Northern Basque Country in France.

Language and culture

The Basque language, Euskara, is a language isolate unrelated to any other known language, and is one of the oldest languages still spoken in Europe. Basque culture is noted for its rural sports, distinctive cuisine, festivals, and the bertsolari tradition of improvised sung verse. Industrialization in the 19th and 20th centuries made the Bilbao area one of Spain's economic centres.

Politics

Basque nationalism emerged in the late 19th century with Sabino Arana, founder of the Basque Nationalist Party (PNV). The region experienced repression under Franco's dictatorship, and the armed group ETA waged a campaign for independence from 1959 until its dissolution in 2018. The Spanish Basque Country has enjoyed broad autonomy, including its own police force (Ertzaintza) and tax-raising powers, since the 1979 Statute of Autonomy.
