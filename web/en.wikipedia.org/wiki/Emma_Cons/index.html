Emma Cons (4 March 1838 – 24 July 1912) was a British social reformer, educationalist and theatre manager. She is best remembered as the founder of the Old Vic theatre in Lambeth, London, which she ran as a temperance music hall offering affordable, alcohol-free entertainment and lectures to working-class audiences.

Early life and reform work

Born in St Pancras, London, Cons trained as an artist and worked for the firm of John Ruskin's associates before becoming involved in social causes. She worked with Octavia Hill on improving housing for the poor, managing tenement buildings and promoting tenants' welfare, and campaigned for women's employment and against the sweated trades.

The Old Vic

In 1880 Cons took over the Royal Victoria Coffee and Music Hall—later the Old Vic—in Waterloo Road, turning a rowdy music hall into a "coffee palace" venue offering opera, Shakespeare excerpts, concerts and popular science lectures at low prices. After her death in 1912, her niece Lilian Baylis continued and greatly expanded her work at the Old Vic and Sadler's Wells.

Public life

Cons was among the first women elected to the London County Council in 1889 (though initially prevented from taking her seat, she served as an alderman). She also founded the Morley Memorial College for Working Men and Women in 1889, named after the philanthropist Samuel Morley.
