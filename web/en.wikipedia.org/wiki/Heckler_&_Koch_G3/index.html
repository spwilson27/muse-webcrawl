Heckler & Koch G3

G3
Type: Battle rifle
Place of origin: West Germany
Designer: Heckler & Koch
Cartridge: 7.62×51mm NATO
Action: Roller-delayed blowback
Feed: 20-round detachable box magazine

The Heckler & Koch G3 (German: Gewehr 3) is a select-fire battle rifle chambered in 7.62×51mm NATO, developed in the 1950s by the German armament manufacturer Heckler & Koch in collaboration with the Spanish state-owned design and development agency CETME.

History

The G3's origins lie in the CETME rifle, itself derived from wartime German designs. Adopted by the Bundeswehr in 1959 as its standard service rifle, it remained in German service until replaced by the G36 in the 1990s. It was exported widely and produced under licence in many countries.

Design details

The G3 uses a roller-delayed blowback operating system. It features a free-floating barrel, a fluted chamber to ease extraction, and could mount telescopic sights, bayonets, bipods and rifle grenades. Variants include the G3A3 with fixed stock, the retracting-stock G3A4, and the accurised G3SG/1.

Users

The G3 and its derivatives have been used by the armed forces of more than 70 countries, and licence-built versions such as the Portuguese m/961 and Iranian DIO production served for decades.
