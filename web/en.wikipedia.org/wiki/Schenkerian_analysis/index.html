Schenkerian analysis

Schenkerian analysis is a method of analyzing tonal music that interprets a composition as an elaboration — a "prolongation" — of a simple underlying structure. It was developed by the Austrian music theorist Heinrich Schenker (1868–1935), whose ideas were presented in works such as Der freie Satz (Free Composition, 1935). Schenkerian analysis is especially influential in North American music theory.

Basic concepts

The deepest level of a piece, the background (Ursatz), is a fundamental structure consisting of a stepwise descending melodic line (the Urlinie, or fundamental line) supported by a bass arpeggiation from tonic to dominant and back (the Bassbrechung). Through successive levels — the middleground and foreground — this structure is elaborated with passing tones, neighbour notes, arpeggiations, and other diminutions until it becomes the actual surface of the piece. Analyses are presented in a distinctive graphic notation combining elements of scores, graphs, and prose commentary.

Reception

Schenker restricted his method to the masterworks of the German tonal tradition, and both his exclusions and his dogmatic tone have drawn criticism. Nevertheless, his concepts of structural levels and prolongation have influenced analysts of a wide range of music, including later applications to popular music and jazz.
