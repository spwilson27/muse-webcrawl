Curtain


Image placeholder: Curtain

A curtain is a piece of cloth or other material intended to block or obscure light, air drafts, or views, or to protect against weather. Curtains are hung in doorways, over windows, and around beds and showers, and are a movable, usually pleated, hanging screen.

Uses

Domestic curtains provide privacy, insulation, and light control; heavier drapes with linings such as blackout fabric are common in bedrooms. Shower curtains keep water inside a bath or shower stall. In theatres, the curtain separates the stage from the auditorium and is raised or parted to begin a performance.

History and materials

Evidence of early curtains survives from ancient Egypt, Greece, and Rome, where cloth hangings divided rooms and doorways. Modern curtains are made from cotton, linen, polyester, velvet, and sheer fabrics such as voile, and are operated by hand, cords, tracks, or motorised systems.
