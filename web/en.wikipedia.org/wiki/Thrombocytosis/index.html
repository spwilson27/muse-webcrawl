Thrombocytosis

Thrombocytosis
Specialty: Hematology
Definition: Platelet count above 450 × 109/L
Types: Primary (essential), secondary (reactive)
ICD-10: D47.3, D75.8

Thrombocytosis is a disorder in which the body produces too many platelets. It is defined by a platelet count exceeding the normal range — usually above 450,000 platelets per microlitre of blood (450 × 109/L). Thrombocytosis is classified as primary, when caused by a disorder of the bone marrow itself, or secondary (reactive), when it arises as a response to another condition.

A blood smear with an elevated platelet count.

Primary thrombocytosis

Primary or essential thrombocythemia is a myeloproliferative neoplasm in which the bone marrow overproduces platelets, often associated with mutations in the JAK2, CALR, or MPL genes. It most often affects older adults. Patients may be asymptomatic or suffer complications from abnormal clotting (thrombosis) or, paradoxically, bleeding, as well as burning pain and redness of the hands and feet (erythromelalgia), headaches, and visual disturbances.

Secondary thrombocytosis

Reactive thrombocytosis is far more common and is triggered by conditions such as acute infection or inflammation, iron-deficiency anaemia, blood loss, cancer, or removal of the spleen (splenectomy). Platelet counts typically normalise when the underlying cause resolves, and the risk of thrombosis is usually low.

Diagnosis and treatment

Evaluation includes a complete blood count, blood smear, iron studies, inflammatory markers, and testing for JAK2, CALR and MPL mutations; bone-marrow biopsy may be needed to confirm a primary disorder. Secondary thrombocytosis requires no platelet-directed treatment. In essential thrombocythemia, low-dose aspirin is used for many patients, and cytoreductive drugs such as hydroxyurea or anagrelide are reserved for those at high risk of thrombosis.
