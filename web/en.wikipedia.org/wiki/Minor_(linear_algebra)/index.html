Minor (linear algebra)

In linear algebra, a minor of a matrix A is the determinant of a smaller square matrix obtained from A by deleting one or more of its rows and columns. More precisely, for certain index sets I (rows) and J (columns) of equal size k, the minor corresponding to (I, J) is the determinant of the k × k submatrix formed by the entries lying in those rows and columns.

First minors and cofactors

First minors (for a square matrix of size n) are the minors obtained by removing exactly one row and one column. The first minor of the entry aij, denoted Mij, is the determinant of the (n−1) × (n−1) matrix that remains after deleting row i and column j. The cofactor of aij is defined as Cij = (−1)i+j Mij.

Applications

Minors and cofactors are central to several classical constructions:

  • Laplace expansion: the determinant of a square matrix can be computed by expanding along any row or column as a sum of entries times their cofactors.
  • Adjugate matrix: the transpose of the cofactor matrix; the inverse of an invertible matrix equals its adjugate divided by its determinant.
  • Cramer's rule: solutions of linear systems expressed as ratios of determinants.
  • Rank: the rank of a matrix equals the largest order of any non-zero minor.
  • Cauchy–Binet formula: expresses minors of a product of matrices as sums of products of minors of the factors.

A principal minor is a minor whose row and column index sets are the same (I = J); leading principal minors are used in tests such as Sylvester's criterion for positive definiteness of symmetric matrices.
