Gravity Probe A

Gravity Probe A lifts off from Wallops Island, Virginia, in 1976.

Gravity Probe A (GP-A) was a space-based experiment to test the equivalence principle, a feature of Einstein's theory of general relativity. Carried out jointly by NASA and the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory, it was launched on 18 June 1976 from Wallops Island, Virginia, aboard a Scout rocket on a suborbital flight lasting just under two hours.

Experiment

The probe carried a hydrogen maser atomic clock to an altitude of about 10,000 kilometres (6,200 miles). According to general relativity, a clock higher in a gravitational field — where gravity is weaker — should tick faster than an identical clock on Earth's surface, an effect called gravitational redshift. Signals from the spaceborne maser were continuously compared with those of an identical maser kept on the ground, with a two-way microwave link used to cancel the much larger first-order Doppler shift caused by the rocket's motion.

Results

The experiment confirmed the gravitational redshift prediction to an accuracy of about 0.007% (70 parts per million), the most precise direct measurement of the effect of its era. Gravity Probe A remains a landmark test of the Einstein equivalence principle and of the behaviour of clocks in curved spacetime. Its companion experiment, Gravity Probe B, launched in 2004, measured the geodetic effect and frame-dragging using gyroscopes in Earth orbit.
