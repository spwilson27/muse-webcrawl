Cantarell Field

The Cantarell Field is a giant offshore oil field in the Bay of Campeche, Gulf of Mexico, Mexico. Discovered in 1976, it was once the world's second most productive oil field and the backbone of Mexican production; it is operated by Pemex.

Discovery

Fisherman Rudesindo Cantarell noticed oil seeps in the 1970s; exploration confirmed a huge reservoir in brecciated limestone associated with the Chicxulub impact crater's ejecta. The field complex includes Akal, Nohoch, Chac and Kutz.

Production and decline

Output peaked in 2004 at about 2.1 million barrels per day. A massive nitrogen injection programme — the world's largest — maintained pressure for a time. Production then declined steeply, a classic case in peak-oil discussions, falling below 200,000 bbl/day by the late 2010s and affecting Mexican public finances.

Significance

Cantarell financed a large share of Mexico's federal budget for decades. Its decline spurred energy reforms and development of fields such as Ku-Maloob-Zaap. See Pemex.
