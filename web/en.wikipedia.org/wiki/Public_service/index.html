Public service

A public service is any service intended to address the needs of a community, typically provided by government or under government responsibility. The term also refers collectively to the body of government employees — the civil service — who deliver such services.

Scope

Public services include policing and fire protection, public education, healthcare, public transportation, water supply and sanitation, waste management, road and infrastructure maintenance, social welfare, and public broadcasting. Some are provided directly by the state; others are delivered by publicly owned corporations or regulated private providers.

Rationale

Public provision is commonly justified by market failure: many public services are public goods (non-excludable and non-rival) or natural monopolies, or are considered essential entitlements of citizenship that should be available regardless of ability to pay. Economists also cite positive externalities, as with vaccination and education.

Organization and reform

Public services are financed mainly through taxation and user charges, and their management has been a recurring subject of reform — from traditional public administration to New Public Management in the 1980s–90s, which introduced market-style mechanisms, and more recent emphases on digital service delivery and citizen participation.
