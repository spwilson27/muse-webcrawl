Year of the Four Emperors

The Year of the Four Emperors, AD 69, was the first civil war of the Roman Empire, during which four emperors ruled in succession: Galba, Otho, Vitellius, and Vespasian. It is also known as the Year of the Four Caesars.

Year of the Four Emperors
Date: AD 69
Emperors: Galba, Otho, Vitellius, Vespasian
Preceded by: Nero, Julio-Claudian dynasty
Followed by: Flavian dynasty

Background

After Nero's suicide in June 68, Galba, governor of Hispania, took power but alienated the army and Praetorian Guard. He was murdered in January 69 and replaced by Otho.

Civil war

Vitellius, proclaimed by the Rhine legions, defeated Otho at the First Battle of Bedriacum; Otho committed suicide. Vitellius's rule in Rome degenerated, while legions in the East proclaimed Vespasian in July. Vespasian's forces under Antonius Primus won the Second Battle of Bedriacum; Vitellius was killed in December 69.

Outcome

Vespasian founded the Flavian dynasty. Tacitus, Suetonius, and Josephus are the main sources; the year exposed, in Tacitus's phrase, the secret of empire — that an emperor could be made elsewhere than at Rome.
