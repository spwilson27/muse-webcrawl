Pedro Páramo


Author: Juan Rulfo
Language: Spanish
Genre: Novel; precursor of magical realism
Published: 1955 (Fondo de Cultura Económica)
Pages: ~124 (first edition)

Pedro Páramo is a novel by Mexican writer Juan Rulfo, first published in 1955. Widely considered one of the masterpieces of twentieth-century Spanish-language literature, it is a foundational text of magical realism and had a decisive influence on Gabriel García Márquez and the Latin American Boom.

Plot

The narrator, Juan Preciado, travels to the town of Comala to fulfil his dying mother's wish that he find his father, Pedro Páramo, a ruthless landowner (cacique). He discovers Comala to be a ghost town inhabited by the murmuring spirits of the dead. Midway through the novel it emerges that Juan himself has died, and much of the narrative unfolds in fragmented flashbacks that piece together Pedro Páramo's rise, his obsessive love for Susana San Juan, his cruelties, and the town's slow death as he lets its lands lie fallow in mourning.

Style and themes

The novel is noted for its fragmented chronology, shifting voices, sparse dialogue, and fusion of the living and the dead. Its themes include power and corruption, death, guilt, unfulfilled love, and the devastation of rural Mexico after the Revolution and the Cristero War.

Reception and adaptations

Initially modestly received, the novel's reputation grew steadily; it is routinely ranked among the greatest novels in Spanish. It has been adapted for film several times, including a 1967 Mexican film and a 2024 Netflix adaptation directed by Rodrigo Prieto.
