Ono no Komachi (Japanese: 小野 小町; c. 825 – c. 900) was a Japanese waka poet, counted among the Rokkasen — the six best waka poets of the early Heian period — and the only woman on that list. She was renowned in later tradition for her beauty, and her name became a byword for a beautiful woman in Japanese.

Life and legend

Almost nothing is known for certain about Komachi's life. She is thought to have served at the imperial court, possibly under Emperor Ninmyō. Around her sparse biography grew a rich body of legend: tales tell of her many suitors, most famously Fukakusa no Shōshō, who was promised her hand if he visited her home for one hundred consecutive nights, only to die on the final night. Other stories depict her old age in poverty and obscurity, a meditation on the transience of beauty.

Poetry

Komachi's surviving poems are included in imperial anthologies, notably the Kokin Wakashū (c. 905), whose preface by Ki no Tsurayuki praises her style as having "an air of gentleness and pathos". Her verse is known for passionate love poems, verbal complexity, and wordplay. Her most famous poem, on the fading of cherry blossoms and of her own beauty, plays on the double meaning of furu ("to fall" and "to grow old").

Legacy

Komachi is the subject of several Noh plays, including Sotoba Komachi, attributed to Kan'ami, in which her aged ghost debates with monks. The Akita Shinkansen high-speed train service is named Komachi after her, as Akita Prefecture claims her as a native daughter.
