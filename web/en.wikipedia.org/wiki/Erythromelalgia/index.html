Erythromelalgia

SpecialtyNeurology, dermatology
SymptomsBurning pain, redness and heat in the extremities
CausesPrimary (genetic, SCN9A mutations) or secondary to other disorders
Named afterGreek: erythros (red), melos (limb), algos (pain)

Erythromelalgia or Mitchell's disease (after Silas Weir Mitchell) is a rare vascular peripheral pain disorder in which blood vessels in the lower extremities or hands are episodically blocked, then become hyperaemic and inflamed. There is severe burning pain (in the small fibre sensory nerves) and skin redness. The attacks are periodic and are commonly triggered by heat, pressure, mild activity, insomnia or stress.

Causes

Primary erythromelalgia may be inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern due to gain-of-function mutations in the SCN9A gene, which encodes the voltage-gated sodium channel Nav1.7 in pain-sensing neurons; these mutations lower the threshold for firing and produce hyperexcitability. Secondary erythromelalgia is associated with myeloproliferative disorders such as polycythaemia vera and essential thrombocythaemia, as well as with diabetes, autoimmune diseases, and certain medications.

Management

There is no cure. Management focuses on avoiding triggers, cooling the affected limbs (with care, since aggressive immersion in cold water can cause skin damage), and medications — in secondary cases aspirin can be strikingly effective when a platelet-mediated mechanism is involved; other agents tried include gabapentinoids, tricyclic antidepressants, and sodium channel blockers such as mexiletine and lidocaine.

See also: NaV1.7, Polycythemia vera, Raynaud syndrome.
