The Judgement of Paris is a story from Greek mythology that was one of the events leading to the Trojan War. Eris, goddess of strife, uninvited to the wedding of Peleus and Thetis, threw into the feast a golden apple inscribed "for the fairest". Three goddesses claimed it — Hera, Athena and Aphrodite — and Zeus, unwilling to decide, appointed the Trojan prince Paris, then herding sheep on Mount Ida, as judge.

The bribes

Each goddess offered Paris a gift. Hera offered dominion over Europe and Asia; Athena offered victory in battle and wisdom; Aphrodite offered the love of the most beautiful woman in the world, Helen of Sparta. Paris awarded the apple to Aphrodite. In granting him Helen, who was already married to Menelaus, Aphrodite set in motion the expedition of the Greek kings against Troy; Hera and Athena, spurned, became implacable enemies of the Trojans throughout the war — a partisan division repeatedly invoked in the Iliad.

In art

The Judgement was one of the most frequently depicted myths in Western art, offering painters a sanctioned subject of three female nudes: Lucas Cranach the Elder painted it more than twenty times; Rubens, Watteau and Cézanne all returned to it. The phrase "apple of discord" survives as an idiom for an object or issue that provokes contention.
