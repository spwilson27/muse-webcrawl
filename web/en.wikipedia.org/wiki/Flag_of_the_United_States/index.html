Flag of the United States

Use: National flag and ensign
Proportion: 10:19
Adopted: June 14, 1777 (original 13-star version); July 4, 1960 (current 50-star version)
Design: Thirteen horizontal red and white stripes with a blue canton bearing fifty white stars

The national flag of the United States, often referred to as the American flag or the Stars and Stripes, consists of thirteen horizontal stripes, alternating red and white, with a blue rectangle in the canton (referred to specifically as the "union") bearing fifty small, white, five-pointed stars arranged in nine offset horizontal rows.

The 13 stripes represent the original Thirteen Colonies that declared independence from the Kingdom of Great Britain and became the first states of the Union. The 50 stars represent the 50 states. Nicknames for the flag include the Stars and Stripes, Old Glory, and the Star-Spangled Banner (the name of the national anthem, written during the War of 1812 about the flag flying over Fort McHenry).

History

The Continental Congress passed the first Flag Act on June 14, 1777, resolving that the flag consist of thirteen stripes and thirteen stars "representing a new constellation". The Flag Act of 1794 added stripes and stars for Vermont and Kentucky, making fifteen stripes; the Flag Act of 1818 fixed the number of stripes at thirteen permanently and required a new star to be added on the July 4 following each state's admission. The current 50-star design, adopted after Hawaii's statehood, was drawn by high-school student Robert G. Heft in 1958 and selected by President Dwight D. Eisenhower.

Symbolism and protocol

Official symbolism is limited to the colours as used in the Great Seal: white signifies purity and innocence, red hardiness and valour, and blue vigilance, perseverance and justice. The United States Flag Code prescribes etiquette for display and handling, including that the flag should never touch the ground and should be retired by burning when worn.
