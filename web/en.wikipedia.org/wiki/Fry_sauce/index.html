Fry sauce

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fry sauce served as a dipping sauce for French fries.

Fry sauce is a condiment consisting of a mixture of ketchup and mayonnaise, served primarily as a dipping sauce for French fries and other fried foods. It is especially associated with the U.S. state of Utah, where it is a staple at fast-food restaurants and burger stands, though similar sauces exist under other names in many parts of the world.

History

The sauce is generally credited to chef Don Carlos Edwards, who served a ketchup-and-mayonnaise mixture at his Don Carlos' Barbecue restaurant in Salt Lake City in the 1940s. Edwards went on to found the Arctic Circle fast-food chain, which popularised the condiment across Utah and the Intermountain West under the name "pink sauce" before "fry sauce" became standard. Arctic Circle claims to have served its version since the chain's early years and still guards its exact recipe.

Fry sauce became so identified with Utah that it was among the foods featured during the 2002 Winter Olympics in Salt Lake City, where souvenir pins depicting fry sauce were produced.

Preparation and variations

The basic recipe is roughly one part ketchup to two parts mayonnaise, though ratios vary widely. Common additions include pickle juice or finely chopped pickles, lemon juice, vinegar, garlic or onion powder, paprika, and hot sauce. Some versions substitute barbecue sauce, Thousand Island dressing, or ranch seasoning.

Similar sauces elsewhere

  • Argentina and Uruguay: salsa golf, a mayonnaise-and-ketchup sauce said to have been invented by Luis Federico Leloir, is nearly identical.
  • Germany: Rot-Weiß ("red-white") fries are served with ketchup and mayonnaise side by side.
  • United Kingdom: "burger sauce" and Marie Rose sauce (used for prawn cocktail) are close relatives.
  • United States (elsewhere): similar mixtures appear as "comeback sauce" in Mississippi and as house "special sauces" at burger chains.
