Blasket Islands

The Blasket Islands (Irish: Na Blascaodaí) are a group of islands off the west coast of the Dingle Peninsula in County Kerry, Ireland. The largest, Great Blasket Island, supported a small Irish-speaking fishing community until its evacuation in 1953. The islands are celebrated for their contribution to Irish-language literature.

History

The islanders lived by fishing, farming and weaving, speaking Irish exclusively. The population peaked at 175 in 1916 but declined through emigration and hardship. During the winter of 1947 a young islander died after storms prevented a doctor or priest reaching the island, an event that sharpened demands for evacuation. The last 22 residents were moved to the mainland on 17 November 1953.

Literature

Despite its size, the community produced a remarkable body of Irish-language writing, encouraged by visiting scholars such as Carl Marstrander and George Thomson. Tomás Ó Criomhthain's An tOileánach (The Islandman, 1929) is a classic autobiography; Peig Sayers' Peig (1936) became a fixture (and for many students a notorious one) of the Irish school curriculum; and Muiris Ó Súilleabháin's Fiche Bliain ag Fás (Twenty Years A-Growing, 1933) gives a vivid account of island youth.

Today

The islands are now uninhabited except for seasonal caretakers and visitors. Great Blasket's ruined village is preserved, and the Blasket Centre (Ionad an Bhlascaoid Mhóir) at Dunquin on the mainland interprets the islanders' heritage. Inishvickillane was owned by former Taoiseach Charles Haughey. The waters and islands support grey seals, seabird colonies and basking sharks.
