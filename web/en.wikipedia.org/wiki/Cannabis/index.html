Cannabis is a genus of flowering plants in the family Cannabaceae. Indigenous to Central Asia and South Asia, cannabis has been cultivated for millennia for its fibre (hemp), seeds and seed oils, for medicinal purposes, and as a recreational drug. The plant's chemical compounds include at least 113 distinct cannabinoids, most notably tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), the principal psychoactive constituent, and cannabidiol (CBD).

Taxonomy and description

Botanists disagree whether cannabis constitutes a single highly variable species, Cannabis sativa, or several species including C. indica and C. ruderalis. The plants are annual, dioecious (with separate male and female individuals), and wind-pollinated, with palmate compound leaves of serrated leaflets. Female plants produce resinous flower clusters rich in cannabinoids and terpenes.

History

Cannabis is among humanity's oldest cultivated plants. Archaeological evidence from East and Central Asia shows use of hemp fibre more than 8,000 years ago, and Chinese pharmacopoeias attributed medicinal uses to it by the first millennium BCE. Herodotus described Scythians inhaling cannabis vapour in ritual baths. In the 19th century, cannabis tinctures entered Western medicine; in the early 20th century, prohibition spread internationally, culminating in the 1961 UN Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs. Since the 1990s–2010s, many jurisdictions have legalized medical cannabis, and a growing number — beginning with Uruguay (2013) and Canada (2018) — have legalized recreational use.

Uses

Hemp, low-THC cannabis, is grown for fibre, hurd, seed and oil, used in textiles, paper, bioplastics, construction materials and food. Medical cannabis is prescribed or used for chemotherapy-induced nausea, chronic pain, multiple sclerosis spasticity and certain severe epilepsies; a purified CBD medicine (Epidiolex) is approved for rare seizure disorders. Recreational cannabis (marijuana) is consumed by smoking, vaporizing or eating, producing euphoria, altered perception and increased appetite.

Effects and health

Short-term effects include relaxation, impaired short-term memory and coordination, dry mouth, and, at high doses or in susceptible people, anxiety or transient psychotic symptoms. Long-term heavy use is associated with cannabis use disorder (affecting roughly one in ten users), respiratory harm when smoked, and an elevated risk of psychosis in predisposed individuals. Driving while intoxicated increases accident risk. Cannabis has a far lower acute toxicity than alcohol or opioids; no fatal overdose from cannabis alone is documented.

Legality

Legal status varies widely: full recreational legalization in Canada, Uruguay, Germany (partial, 2024) and numerous US states; decriminalization in many other countries; and severe penalties in parts of Asia and the Middle East. In 2020 the UN Commission on Narcotic Drugs removed cannabis from Schedule IV of the Single Convention, recognizing its medical utility while keeping it controlled.
