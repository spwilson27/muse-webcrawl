Yangtze Patrol

Active1854–1949 (US Navy presence)
BranchUnited States Navy
TypeRiver gunboat flotilla
AreaYangtze River, China

The Yangtze Patrol, also known as the Yangtze River Patrol Force, was a United States Navy formation that patrolled the Yangtze River in China for nearly a century, protecting American commercial interests, missionaries, and nationals under the treaty-port system.

History

American warships first operated on the Yangtze in the 1850s following the treaties that opened Chinese ports to foreign trade. The patrol was formally organised in the 1860s as part of the Asiatic Squadron and later the Asiatic Fleet. Its shallow-draft gunboats — famous "YangPat" vessels such as USS Palos, Monocacy, and later purpose-built river gunboats of the 1920s — were based at Shanghai and ranged upriver to Chongqing.

The patrol's work included suppressing piracy, escorting merchant traffic, and evacuating foreigners during unrest, notably during the rise of the Nationalist and Communist movements. USS Panay, bombed by Japanese aircraft in December 1937, was a patrol vessel. The force dwindled after 1941 as war made the river untenable, and the last American gunboats withdrew in 1949 as Communist forces took control of the river valley.
