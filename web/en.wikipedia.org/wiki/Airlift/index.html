An airlift is the organized delivery of supplies or personnel primarily via military transport aircraft. Airlifting consists of two distinct types: strategic airlift and tactical airlift. Typically, strategic airlifting involves moving material long distances (such as across or off an ocean), whereas tactical airlifting focuses on deploying resources and material into a specific location with high precision.

Such deliveries can be made to places or regions that are otherwise unreachable by land or sea, to besieged areas, or as a rapid means of reinforcing forces during emergencies. Airlifts became a defining feature of military logistics in the twentieth century and remain central to humanitarian relief operations.

Strategic airlift

Strategic airlift is the transportation of cargo and personnel over long distances, typically between continents or theatres of war, using large transport aircraft such as the C-17 Globemaster III, C-5 Galaxy, Antonov An-124 and Ilyushin Il-76. It provides the rapid global deployment capability on which modern expeditionary forces depend.

Tactical airlift

Tactical airlift operates within a theatre, moving troops and materiel over shorter distances, often into austere or unprepared airstrips. Aircraft such as the C-130 Hercules are typical. Tactical delivery methods include airdrops by parachute, low-altitude extraction, and short take-off and landing operations.

History

The first practical airlifts took place during the First World War, but the technique matured in the Second World War. The Allied effort to supply China over the Himalayas — known as "The Hump" — carried hundreds of thousands of tons of cargo between 1942 and 1945.

The most famous airlift was the Berlin Airlift of 1948–49, in which Western Allied aircraft supplied West Berlin during a Soviet blockade, delivering over 2.3 million tons of food and fuel. Later major airlifts include Operation Nickel Grass (1973), which resupplied Israel during the Yom Kippur War, and numerous humanitarian operations following earthquakes, famines and conflicts.
