Weierstrass factorization theorem

In mathematics, and particularly in the field of complex analysis, the Weierstrass factorization theorem asserts that every entire function can be represented as a (possibly infinite) product involving its zeroes. The theorem may be viewed as an extension of the fundamental theorem of algebra, which asserts that every polynomial may be factored into linear factors, one for each root.

Elementary factors

To make an infinite product over the zeros {an} converge, Weierstrass introduced elementary factors Ep(z): E0(z) = 1 − z, and for p ≥ 1, Ep(z) = (1 − z) exp(z + z²/2 + … + zp/p). Choosing the integers pn large enough guarantees convergence of the product over all nonzero zeros of f.

Forms of the theorem

The theorem states that an entire function f with a zero of order m at the origin and other zeros {an} can be written f(z) = zm eg(z) ∏ Epn(z/an), where g is an entire function. Conversely, any such product defines an entire function with the prescribed zeros. The Hadamard factorization theorem refines this for functions of finite order, bounding the genus of the product and the degree of g. A classic application gives the product representation of the sine function; the theorem is complemented by the Mittag-Leffler theorem, its analogue for prescribing poles of meromorphic functions.
