Seudat mitzvah

A seudat mitzvah (Hebrew: סעודת מצוה, "meal of commandment" or "obligatory festive meal") is, in Jewish tradition, a celebratory meal held in fulfilment of, or in conjunction with, the performance of a mitzvah (commandment). Unlike an optional repast (seudat reshut), a seudat mitzvah has religious significance, and partaking of it is itself considered meritorious.

Occasions

Classic occasions for a seudat mitzvah include:

  • The meal following a brit milah (ritual circumcision).
  • The feast at a pidyon haben (redemption of the firstborn son).
  • The wedding feast (seudat nissuin) and the seven days of sheva brachot meals after a wedding.
  • A siyum meal celebrating the completion of a tractate of Talmud or another major unit of Torah study.
  • A bar mitzvah or bat mitzvah meal.
  • The meal eaten on the eve of Passover by firstborn males after a siyum, which by custom exempts them from the Fast of the Firstborn; similarly, meals that break or precede certain fasts in celebratory contexts.
  • Meals of the Sabbath and festivals, which some authorities class as seudot mitzvah, including the third Sabbath meal, seudah shlishit.

Customs

It is customary to speak words of Torah (divrei Torah) at the table, to sing zemirot (table hymns) on the Sabbath, and to include the poor and scholars among the guests. At a siyum, the person completing the study recites the concluding passage and the Hadran prayer, followed by Kaddish. The festive character of the meal — including meat and wine where customary — is itself part of the celebration of the mitzvah.
