Omrides

The Omrides (Hebrew: בית עמרי, Beit Omri, "House of Omri") were the ruling dynasty of the northern Kingdom of Israel in the 9th century BCE, founded by Omri (reigned c. 885–874 BCE). The dynasty is known both from the Hebrew Bible, which portrays its kings predominantly negatively, and from Assyrian and Moabite inscriptions, which attest to Israel's regional power under them.

History

Omri, a military commander, seized the throne after civil war and founded a new capital at Samaria. His son Ahab (c. 874–853 BCE) expanded the kingdom, fought at the Battle of Qarqar against Shalmaneser III of Assyria (853 BCE), and — through marriage to Jezebel of Sidon — became notorious in biblical tradition for promoting Baal worship, provoking conflict with the prophet Elijah. Later Omrides, Ahaziah and Jehoram, allied with Judah through marriage to Athaliah. The dynasty was destroyed in the coup of Jehu (c. 841 BCE), who exterminated the royal house; Assyrian records long continued to call Israel "the land of the house of Omri."

Archaeology

The Mesha Stele, the Kurkh Monographs, and the Black Obelisk provide extrabiblical evidence for the dynasty. Excavations at Samaria, Megiddo, and Hazor reveal major Iron Age building works attributed to the Omride period, though their dating remains debated among archaeologists.
