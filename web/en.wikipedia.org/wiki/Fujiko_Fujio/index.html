Fujiko Fujio

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Doraemon, the most famous creation of Fujiko Fujio, first appeared in 1969.

Fujiko Fujio (藤子 不二雄) was the pen name of a Japanese manga duo: Fujimoto Hiroshi (藤本 弘, 1933–1996) and Abiko Motoo (安孫子 素雄, 1934–2022). The pair are best known as the creators of Doraemon (1969), one of the most widely recognised and commercially successful manga and anime franchises in the world.

Biography

The two boys met in elementary school in Takaoka, Toyama Prefecture, and began drawing manga together. After moving to Tokyo in 1954, they joined the Tokiwa-sō apartment, a legendary lodging house for young manga artists, alongside Osamu Tezuka and others. They published under various joint names before settling on Fujiko Fujio in 1954, initially as "Ashizuka Fujio".

Major works

  • Doraemon (1969–1996) — the adventures of a robot cat from the 22nd century and the schoolboy Nobita Nobi.
  • Ninja Hattori-kun (1964) — a young ninja living in modern Japan.
  • Perman (1966) — an ordinary boy given superhero powers by a birdman.
  • Kaibutsu-kun (1965) — a young monster prince befriending human children.
  • Obake no Q-Tarō (1964) — a mischievous ghost who moves in with a human family.

Split

In 1987 the duo dissolved their partnership over creative differences. Fujimoto continued as Fujiko F. Fujio (藤子・F・不二雄) and kept Doraemon, while Abiko became Fujiko Fujio A (藤子不二雄A), working on works such as The Laughing Salesman and Manga Michi. Fujimoto died in 1996; Abiko died in 2022.

Legacy

Doraemon alone has sold over 100 million manga copies, spawned long-running television anime (1973, 1979, 2005) and dozens of films, and was named Japan's first "anime ambassador" by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in 2008. A museum dedicated to Fujiko F. Fujio opened in Kawasaki in 2011.
