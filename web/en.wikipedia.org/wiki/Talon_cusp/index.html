Talon cusp

SpecialtyDentistry
First describedMitchell, 1892 (as an eagle's talon-like process)
Most affectedMaxillary lateral incisors

A talon cusp is a dental anomaly in which an extra cusp, resembling an eagle's talon in shape, projects from the lingual (or occasionally facial) surface of an anterior tooth, usually a maxillary lateral incisor. It is composed of enamel and dentine and may contain an extension of the pulp.

Classification

Hattab and colleagues classified talon cusps into three types: a true talon extending at least half the distance from the cementoenamel junction to the incisal edge; a semi-talon; and a trace talon, a slightly enlarged cingulum.

Clinical significance

Talon cusps can interfere with occlusion, irritate the tongue, trap plaque, and complicate aesthetics. Management ranges from simple monitoring and gradual grinding with fluoride protection to, in severe cases, endodontic treatment. The anomaly may occur alone or in association with syndromes such as Rubinstein–Taybi syndrome and Mohr syndrome.
