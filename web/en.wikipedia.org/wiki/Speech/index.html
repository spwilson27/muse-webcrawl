Speech

Speech is the expression of thoughts and feelings by articulate sounds. It is the most used mode of human language and the primary form in which natural languages are transmitted. The production of speech involves coordinated activity of the respiratory system, the larynx, and the articulating structures of the mouth and nasal cavity, under cortical control in regions classically associated with Broca's area and Wernicke's area.

Production

In speech production, air expelled from the lungs passes through the vocal folds of the larynx, whose vibration (phonation) provides a voiced sound source that is then filtered by the vocal tract. The positions of the tongue, lips, jaw, soft palate, and other articulators shape this source into the speech sounds, or phones, of a language. The study of speech sounds is phonetics, divided into articulatory, acoustic, and auditory branches; the abstract sound system of a language is the subject of phonology.

Perception and development

Listeners recover linguistic structure from the acoustic signal with remarkable robustness, a process studied as speech perception. Infants are born with broad sensitivity to phonetic contrasts, narrow their perception to the contrasts of their native language within the first year, and progress from cooing and babbling to first words around twelve months and fluent sentences in early childhood.

Disorders and technology

Speech can be impaired by disorders of voice, articulation, fluency (such as stuttering), or language itself (aphasia), treated within speech–language pathology. Technologies that synthesize and recognize speech, from early vocoders to modern neural text-to-speech and automatic speech recognition systems, model the production and perception of speech computationally.
