Fangorn Forest is a fictional forest in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth, featured prominently in The Two Towers, the second volume of The Lord of the Rings. Located at the south-eastern end of the Misty Mountains near Isengard, it is a remnant of the vast primeval woodland that once covered much of Eriador and Calenardhon. "Fangorn" is Sindarin for "Treebeard", and the forest takes its name from its most famous inhabitant, the oldest of the Ents.

Description

Tolkien describes Fangorn as dark, ancient, and brooding, with huge gnarled trees, hanging moss, and a heavy, watchful atmosphere. The forest is home to the Ents, the shepherds of the trees, and to the Huorns, trees that have become partly "entish" — mobile and dangerous. The Wellinghall of Treebeard, the Entmoot meeting place at Derndingle, and the hill of Amon Hen's counterpart landscapes lie within or near it.

Role in the story

In The Two Towers, the hobbits Merry and Pippin escape from Orcs into Fangorn and meet Treebeard. Their account of Saruman's destruction of trees prompts the Ents to hold an Entmoot, which decides on the Last March of the Ents against Isengard; the Ents, with the Huorns later defeating the Orcs at Helm's Deep, destroy Saruman's fortress. Aragorn, Legolas, and Gimli also enter the forest in search of the hobbits, and Gandalf the White meets them among the trees.

Origins

Tolkien drew on his love of trees and his grief at their industrial destruction; Fangorn and Treebeard express his environmental themes. The forest's name and atmosphere echo Old English and Germanic woodland lore, and commentators have compared it with the Forest of Dean and Puzzlewood, near places Tolkien visited, though he did not confirm a specific model.
