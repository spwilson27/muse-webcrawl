Lulach mac Gille Coemgáin (died 17 March 1058), nicknamed Tairbith, "the Unfortunate", and Fatuus, "the Foolish", was King of Alba (Scotland) from 15 August 1057 until his death on 17 March 1058, a reign of only seven months. He was the stepson of Macbeth, whom he succeeded, and is generally regarded as a weak king.

Life

Lulach was the son of Gille Coemgáin, Mormaer of Moray, and of Gruoch, granddaughter of King Kenneth III. After Gille Coemgáin was killed in 1032, Gruoch married Macbeth, who thereby became Lulach's stepfather. When Macbeth was killed by the forces of Malcolm Canmore at the Battle of Lumphanan on 15 August 1057, Lulach was crowned king at Scone, probably on 8 September 1057.

He was the last king of the House of Moray. Malcolm's supporters continued their campaign against him, and Lulach was killed at Essie in Strathbogie on 17 March 1058. He was succeeded by Malcolm III.

Family

Lulach had two known children: a son, Máel Snechtai, who became Mormaer of Moray and died in 1085, and a daughter whose son, Óengus of Moray, claimed the kingship in 1130 and was killed in battle against David I.

In fiction

Lulach appears as a character in Dorothy Dunnett's historical novel King Hereafter (1982), about Macbeth, and as a minor figure in Shakespeare-influenced retellings of the Macbeth story.
