Employment contract

An employment contract or contract of employment is a kind of contract used in labour law to attribute rights and responsibilities between parties to a bargain — the employee and the employer. It governs matters such as wages, hours, duties, holidays, notice and termination, and may be written, oral or implied by conduct, though many jurisdictions require written particulars.

Contents

Typical terms cover job title and description, place of work, pay and benefits, working time, probationary periods, confidentiality and restrictive covenants. Some terms are implied by law, custom or the duty of mutual trust and confidence; statutory minimums, such as minimum wage and dismissal protections, usually override less favourable contract terms.

Types

Contracts may be permanent (open-ended) or fixed-term, full-time or part-time, and include zero-hours and agency arrangements. The distinction between an employee under a contract of service and an independent contractor under a contract for services determines rights to benefits, tax treatment and protections such as those against wrongful dismissal.

Termination

Employment usually ends by resignation, expiry of a fixed term, redundancy or dismissal, subject to notice periods and statutory procedures. Collective agreements negotiated by unions may supplement individual contracts, particularly in Europe where bodies such as the European works council operate.
