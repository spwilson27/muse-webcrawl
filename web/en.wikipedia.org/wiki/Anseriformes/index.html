Anseriformes

Anseriformes is an order of birds commonly known as waterfowl. It includes ducks, geese and swans in the family Anatidae, the magpie goose in Anseranatidae, and the screamers of South America in Anhimidae. Most species are adapted to aquatic habitats, although some graze extensively on land.

Characteristics

Living anseriforms typically have broad bills with lamellae for filtering or grazing, webbed feet, dense waterproof plumage and strong flight. Their young are precocial and can swim and feed soon after hatching. Many species undertake long migrations between breeding wetlands in northern regions and milder wintering areas.

Evolution and human relations

The order has a fossil record extending to the Late Cretaceous or early Paleogene depending on interpretation, with forms such as Vegavis and Presbyornis informing debate about early waterfowl evolution. Humans have domesticated ducks and geese, hunted many species for food and sport, and conserved wetlands that support migratory populations.
