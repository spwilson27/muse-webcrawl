Bitstream Vera is a family of digital typefaces released in 2003 by Bitstream Inc. under a permissive license, developed primarily for the GNOME desktop environment. The family comprises a humanist sans-serif (Vera Sans), a serif companion (Vera Serif), and a monospaced face (Vera Sans Mono), each in several weights and styles, designed by Jim Lyles on the basis of Bitstream's Prima design.

License and derivatives

The Vera license permits redistribution and modification, with conditions that derivative fonts not be sold by themselves and not use the "Vera" name. This liberal licensing made Vera hugely influential in free software typography. The DejaVu fonts project extended Vera with far broader character coverage (including many scripts and symbols) while retaining its proportions, becoming a default on many Linux distributions. Other derivatives include the Arev fonts and, more distantly, fonts inspired by Vera's metrics.

Design

Vera Sans has a large x-height, open apertures, and clearly distinguished similar characters (such as capital I, lowercase l, and digit 1), features chosen for legibility at small sizes on screens of its era.
