Sneaker resale

Sneaker resale is the practice of purchasing limited-edition or in-demand sneakers at retail and reselling them, typically at a markup, on secondary marketplaces. The resale market grew rapidly in the 2010s alongside the rise of sneaker culture, celebrity collaborations, and online platforms dedicated to buying and selling authenticated footwear.

History

Resale has long existed around rare collectible shoes, but the market industrialised with the emergence of platforms such as StockX, GOAT, and eBay's authenticity programmes, which standardised pricing and authentication. Limited releases from brands such as Nike and Adidas, often distributed through raffles, created scarcity that fuelled secondary demand.

Market

Analysts have estimated the global sneaker resale market in the billions of dollars annually. Prices are driven by rarity, condition, size, and cultural relevance, with some pairs trading at many multiples of their retail price.

Criticism

The resale market has been criticised for the use of automated purchasing bots, which allow resellers to buy up stock before ordinary consumers, and for encouraging speculation in consumer goods.
