Bandwidth (signal processing)

Bandwidth is the difference between the upper and lower frequencies in a continuous band of frequencies. It is typically measured in hertz (Hz), and depending on context may specifically refer to passband bandwidth (the width of a filter's passband) or baseband bandwidth.

Definitions

For a bandpass filter or a resonant system, bandwidth is conventionally measured between the "half-power" or −3 dB points, where the signal power falls to half its peak value. The bandwidth of a low-pass signal is simply its highest significant frequency. In radio communications, bandwidth describes the width of spectrum occupied by a modulated signal — for example, an FM broadcast channel is about 200 kHz wide, while an AM channel is about 10 kHz.

Relation to data rate

Bandwidth limits the information a channel can carry. The Nyquist rate gives the maximum symbol rate for a noiseless channel of bandwidth B, and the Shannon–Hartley theorem gives the maximum data rate as a function of bandwidth and signal-to-noise ratio. This connection is why "bandwidth" in everyday networking language refers to data transfer capacity, usually measured in bits per second, even though strictly it is a frequency width.

Fractional bandwidth

Antenna and filter engineers also use fractional bandwidth — the bandwidth divided by the centre frequency — to compare devices operating at different frequencies, and the related quality factor Q, defined as the centre frequency divided by the bandwidth.
