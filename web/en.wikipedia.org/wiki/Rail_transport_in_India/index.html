Rail transport in India

Rail transport in India is provided almost entirely by the state-owned Indian Railways, one of the largest railway networks in the world, with over 68,000 kilometres of route and more than 7,300 stations. Railways are the principal mode of long-distance land transport in the country, carrying billions of passengers and over a billion tonnes of freight each year.

History

The first railway in India opened in 1853 between Bombay (Mumbai) and Thane, a distance of 34 km. Under British rule the network expanded rapidly, built by private companies under government guarantee and later by the state. After independence in 1947 the various systems were nationalised and merged into Indian Railways in 1951. The network historically used three gauges — broad (1,676 mm), metre and narrow — and Project Unigauge has since converted most routes to broad gauge.

Operations

Indian Railways is organised into zones and runs everything from suburban trains in Mumbai and Kolkata to long-distance expresses such as the Rajdhani and Shatabdi services and the semi-high-speed Vande Bharat trains. It is one of the world's largest employers. Electrification now covers almost the entire broad-gauge network, and steam locomotives survive mainly on heritage and mountain railways, three of which are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Freight and development

Freight traffic is dominated by coal, ores, cement and containers, and dedicated freight corridors have been built to relieve congested mixed-traffic routes. Urban rail has expanded with metro systems in many major cities.
