Microchip implant (human)

A human microchip implant is typically an identifying integrated circuit device or radio-frequency identification (RFID) transponder encased in silicate glass and implanted in the body of a human being, usually in the hand.

Uses

Implants have been used for identification, access control, contactless payment experiments and storing medical or contact information readable by NFC-capable devices. The VeriChip was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2004 for medical identification.

Concerns

Debate surrounds privacy, security, bodily autonomy and potential health risks. Several US states have enacted laws prohibiting mandatory human implantation. Critics note that readings can potentially be cloned and that long-term effects remain debated.
