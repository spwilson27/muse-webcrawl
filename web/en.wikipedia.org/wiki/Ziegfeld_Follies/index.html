Ziegfeld Follies

Ziegfeld Follies
Producer: Florenz Ziegfeld Jr.
First edition: 1907
Editions: 1907-1931 (plus revivals)
Venue: New Amsterdam Theatre and others, Broadway

The Ziegfeld Follies was a series of elaborate theatrical revue productions on Broadway in New York City from 1907 to 1931, created by impresario Florenz Ziegfeld Jr. Modelled in part on the Folies Bergère of Paris, the Follies combined musical numbers, comedy sketches, lavish scenery and costumes, and lines of showgirls — the "Ziegfeld Girls" — into a glamorous spectacle that defined an era of American popular theatre.

Productions

The first Follies opened in 1907 at the Jardin de Paris rooftop theatre. The productions moved to larger houses, above all the New Amsterdam Theatre, and grew in scale and expense, with sets and costumes by leading designers and songs by composers such as Irving Berlin, Jerome Kern and George Gershwin. Companion series included the more risqué Midnight Frolic and the touring editions. The final original edition appeared in 1931, shortly before Ziegfeld's death in 1932; the name was later revived for editions in the 1930s, 1940s and 1950s.

Performers

The Follies launched or showcased many stars, including Fanny Brice, W. C. Fields, Will Rogers, Eddie Cantor, Ruth Etting, Bert Williams — one of the first Black performers to star in a major Broadway production — and dancers such as Marilyn Miller. Song standards introduced in the Follies include "Shine On, Harvest Moon" and "By the Light of the Silvery Moon."

Legacy

The Follies' blend of beauty, comedy and spectacle set the template for the American revue and influenced stage and film musicals. MGM's film The Great Ziegfeld (1936) won the Academy Award for Best Picture, and Ziegfeld Girl (1941) and Ziegfeld Follies (1945) continued the brand on screen. The 1971 Stephen Sondheim musical Follies evokes the era in its portrait of ageing showgirls at a reunion.
