Amazon MGM Studios is an American film and television production and distribution studio owned by Amazon. Founded in 2010 as Amazon Studios, it was among the first of the streaming-era studios, developing original series for Prime Video through a novel crowdsourced script-submission model before shifting to conventional commissioning. In March 2022 Amazon completed its $8.45 billion acquisition of Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer (MGM), the historic Hollywood studio founded in 1924, and in 2023 the company was renamed Amazon MGM Studios, combining the streaming originals operation with MGM's film and television units and its library of some 4,000 films and 17,000 television episodes.

The studio's notable productions include the series Transparent, The Marvelous Mrs. Maisel, The Boys, The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power and Fallout, and films including Manchester by the Sea (2016), the first streaming-service film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture, and The Big Sick. Through MGM it controls the James Bond franchise (shared with Eon Productions), the Rocky/Creed series and the MGM library.

History

Amazon Studios launched in 2010 inviting the public to submit scripts and "test movies", producing its first pilots in 2013 and letting viewers vote on them. Under Roy Price and later Jennifer Salke, it grew into a major buyer of independent film and a producer of prestige television, winning multiple Emmy and Academy Awards. The MGM acquisition, announced in May 2021 and closed on 17 March 2022 after regulatory review, made Amazon a major theatrical studio overnight.

Film and television production

Following the merger, Amazon MGM releases a slate of theatrical films under the MGM and Orion banners alongside direct-to-streaming originals for Prime Video, and produces series both for Prime Video and for MGM+ (the former Epix). Its television hits include the MGM-produced Wednesday (for Netflix) and The Handmaid's Tale (for Hulu).

Distribution

The studio took over MGM's distribution arrangements, releasing films theatrically in North America and internationally, and in 2025 assumed creative control of the James Bond franchise from long-time producers Barbara Broccoli and Michael G. Wilson, who formed a joint venture with the studio.
