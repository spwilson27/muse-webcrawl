Mausoleum of Augustus

The Mausoleum of Augustus (Italian: Mausoleo di Augusto) is a large circular tomb in the Campus Martius in Rome, built by the emperor Augustus in 28 BCE as a dynastic burial monument for himself and the imperial family. With a diameter of about 87 metres (285 feet), it was one of the largest tombs of the ancient world, and for centuries dominated the northern skyline of the city.

Description

The structure stood on a high podium and consisted of concentric walls rising in terraces, planted with cypress trees and crowned by a colossal bronze statue of Augustus. The entrance, flanked by two pink granite obelisks (later moved to the Quirinal and Esquiline hills), led to a circular burial chamber where golden urns held the ashes of the imperial family. Bronze pillars at the entrance displayed the Res Gestae Divi Augusti, Augustus's account of his own achievements. Among those buried there were Marcellus, Agrippa, Octavia, Drusus, Gaius and Lucius Caesar, Augustus himself (14 CE), Livia, Tiberius, and later emperors down to Nerva; the deified Julius Caesar's ashes were also sometimes associated with the monument.

Later history

After antiquity the mausoleum was successively a fortified stronghold of the Colonna family, a quarry, a hanging garden, an amphitheatre for bullfights and fireworks (the Corea amphitheatre), and finally, from 1908, a concert hall — the Augusteo — until it was demolished and the monument "liberated" under Mussolini's urban programme in the 1930s, which surrounded it with the Fascist-era Piazza Augusto Imperatore. Long neglected thereafter, the mausoleum underwent a major restoration funded by the city of Rome and the Fondazione TIM, reopening to visitors in March 2021.
