Samuel Richardson

Samuel Richardson (baptised 19 August 1689 – 4 July 1761) was an English writer and printer, best known for three epistolary novels: Pamela; or, Virtue Rewarded (1740), Clarissa: Or the History of a Young Lady (1748), and The History of Sir Charles Grandison (1753). He is regarded as one of the founders of the modern novel in English.

Life

Richardson was born in Mackworth, Derbyshire, the son of a joiner. Apprenticed to a London printer, he built a successful printing business, becoming printer to the House of Commons and master of the Stationers' Company. He came to fiction late: in 1739 he was asked to produce a volume of model letters for country readers, and out of that project grew Pamela, the story of a servant girl who resists her master's advances and is rewarded by marriage.

Works

Pamela (1740)

Pamela was an immediate sensation and a landmark of the epistolary novel — told entirely in letters written "to the moment". It also provoked a wave of parodies and counter-works, most famously Henry Fielding's Shamela and Joseph Andrews.

Clarissa (1748)

Clarissa, Richardson's masterpiece and one of the longest novels in English, traces the persecution of its heroine by the rake Lovelace and her family. Its psychological depth and tragic ending were widely admired across Europe.

Sir Charles Grandison (1753)

His final novel portrays an exemplary male hero and was a favourite of Jane Austen.

Legacy

Richardson, alongside Henry Fielding and Daniel Defoe, is credited with establishing the novel as the dominant literary form in English. His minute, inward exploration of consciousness profoundly influenced the development of the psychological novel.
