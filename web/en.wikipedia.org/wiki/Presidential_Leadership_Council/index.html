The Presidential Leadership Council (Arabic: مجلس القيادة الرئاسي) is the executive body that exercises the functions of head of state of the Republic of Yemen. It was created on 7 April 2022 when President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi, in a decree issued from Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, transferred his powers to the new council and dismissed Vice President Ali Mohsen al-Ahmar. The move followed consultations among Yemen's anti-Houthi factions hosted by the Gulf Cooperation Council.

Composition

The council consists of a chairman, Rashad al-Alimi, a former interior minister, and seven deputy chairmen drawn from the main armed and political components of the anti-Houthi camp. They include Aidarus al-Zoubaidi, head of the Southern Transitional Council; Tariq Saleh, commander of the National Resistance on the Red Sea coast; Sultan al-Arada, governor of Marib; Abdullah al-Alimi; Othman Mujalli; Abed al-Rahman Abu Zara'a; and Faraj al-Bahsani. The body was designed to unify rival forces that had sometimes fought one another, notably in Aden in 2019.

Role and mandate

The council holds the powers of the president and vice president under the Yemeni constitution, including supreme command of the armed forces, appointment of governors and senior officials, and ratification of agreements. Its founding declaration charged it with managing the state politically, militarily and economically during a transitional period, and with negotiating with the Houthi movement (Ansar Allah) for a ceasefire and final political settlement of the civil war that began in 2014–15.

Activities

The council is based in Aden, Yemen's interim capital, though its members frequently meet in Riyadh. It has overseen the internationally recognised government during a UN-brokered truce that began in April 2022, and has faced persistent challenges: tension between its southern separatist and unionist members, an economy fragmented between rival central banks and currencies, and Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping from late 2023 that drew the recognised government into wider regional escalation.
