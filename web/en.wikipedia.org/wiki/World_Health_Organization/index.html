The World Health Organization (WHO) is a specialized agency of the United Nations responsible for international public health. Established on 7 April 1948 — a date now marked each year as World Health Day — it is headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland, and works through six regional offices and around 150 country offices worldwide.

The WHO's stated objective, set out in its constitution, is 'the attainment by all peoples of the highest possible level of health', with health defined not merely as the absence of disease but as 'a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being'. Its work includes coordinating responses to health emergencies, setting international health standards and guidelines, monitoring disease outbreaks, supporting vaccination, and helping countries strengthen their health systems.

The organisation is governed by the World Health Assembly, attended by delegations from its 194 member states, which approves the programme and budget and elects the Director-General. The WHO played a leading role in the eradication of smallpox, declared in 1980, the near-eradication of polio, and the global response to the COVID-19 pandemic, which it declared a pandemic on 11 March 2020.

History

The WHO succeeded earlier international health bodies including the League of Nations Health Organisation and the Office International d'Hygiène Publique. Its constitution was signed in New York in 1946 and came into force on 7 April 1948. Early priorities included malaria, tuberculosis, venereal diseases, maternal and child health, and environmental sanitation. In 1978 the Alma-Ata Declaration identified primary health care as the key to 'Health for All'.

Structure

The Secretariat, headed by the Director-General, is based at headquarters in Geneva. Six regional offices — for Africa, the Americas (PAHO), South-East Asia, Europe, the Eastern Mediterranean and the Western Pacific — enjoy considerable autonomy, a structure that distinguishes the WHO from more centralised UN agencies. The Executive Board, made up of 34 member-state representatives, prepares the Assembly's work.

Work and achievements

Achievements associated with the WHO include the global eradication of smallpox (1980), the near-eradication of poliomyelitis through the Global Polio Eradication Initiative, control programmes for onchocerciasis ('river blindness'), the Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (2003), and the International Health Regulations governing outbreak notification. The WHO also publishes the ICD classification of diseases and an influential World Health Report / World Health Statistics series.

The organisation has also faced criticism — over its response timelines to the West African Ebola outbreak of 2014–2016 and to COVID-19, and over funding dependence: most of its budget comes from voluntary contributions earmarked for specific programmes rather than assessed member dues.
