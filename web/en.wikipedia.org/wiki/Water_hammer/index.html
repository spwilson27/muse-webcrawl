Water hammer

Water hammer (or hydraulic shock) is a pressure surge or wave caused when a fluid in motion is forced to stop or change direction suddenly, as when a valve closes quickly. The resulting spike can damage pipes, fittings and appliances.

Water hammer
CauseSudden change in fluid velocity
Governing relationJoukowsky equation ΔP = ρ·a·Δv
MitigationArrestors, air chambers, slow-closing valves, surge tanks

Mechanism

When flow is arrested, the kinetic energy of the moving column converts into a pressure wave that propagates through the pipe at the speed of sound in the fluid–pipe system (often over 1,000 m/s in water mains). The Joukowsky equation gives the pressure rise; repeated oscillations can cause the banging noises heard in domestic plumbing, and in severe cases column separation and collapse pressures on the rebound.

Prevention

Engineers control water hammer by closing valves slowly, fitting water-hammer arrestors or standpipes, using flywheels on pumps, and modelling transients in large pipelines and hydroelectric penstocks.
