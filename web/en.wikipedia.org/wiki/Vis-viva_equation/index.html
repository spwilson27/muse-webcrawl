Vis-viva equation


Orbital speed as a function of position on an elliptical orbit

In astrodynamics, the vis-viva equation is one of the equations that model the motion of orbiting bodies. It is the direct result of the principle of conservation of mechanical energy, which applies when the only force acting on the system is the object's own weight, that is, its gravity.

Equation

For any Keplerian orbit, the vis-viva equation states:

v² = μ (2/r − 1/a)

where v is the relative speed of the two bodies, r is the distance between them, a is the length of the semi-major axis, and μ is the standard gravitational parameter, equal to the product of the gravitational constant and the mass of the central body.

Applications

Given the current distance and speed of an orbiting body, the equation yields its semi-major axis and hence its orbital period and the apsis distances of its orbit. It is routinely used in mission design to compute the velocity changes required for orbital manoeuvres.

History

The equation derives its name from the older concept of vis viva ("living force"), an early formulation of kinetic energy associated with Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz.
