Dreamland was an amusement park at Coney Island in Brooklyn, New York, that operated from 1904 until its destruction by fire in 1911. Created by former state senator William H. Reynolds and built to outdo the neighbouring Luna Park, it was the largest and most elaborately decorated of Coney Island's three great early amusement parks, alongside Luna Park and Steeplechase Park.

Attractions

Dreamland was designed in a grand, white-and-gold classical style around a central lagoon and a 375-foot beacon tower, lit at night by roughly a million electric light bulbs. Attractions included recreations of the canals of Venice, a Swiss alpine village, a Japanese garden, the Lilliputian Village inhabited by some 300 little people, an infant incubator exhibit displaying premature babies — then a genuine and influential medical attraction — a ballroom, railways to "Hell Gate", and spectacular disaster re-enactments and animal shows featuring Captain Jack Bonavita's lions.

Fire of 1911

On the night of 26–27 May 1911, on the eve of the new season, a fire broke out in the Hell Gate attraction during last-minute repairs: tar being heated for roof work ignited, and the park's highly flammable lath-and-stucco construction burned catastrophically. Panic in the animal menagerie saw lions roaming the streets before being shot by police; some of the incubator babies were rescued by staff. Dreamland was never rebuilt — a public aquarium and later other uses occupied parts of the site; the modern New York Aquarium stands nearby — and its destruction, following Luna Park's survival, closed the first golden age of Coney Island parks just as Steeplechase alone endured.

Legacy

Dreamland's name has been revived several times at Coney Island, including a small amusement area opened in 2009, and the original park remains a standard reference in histories of American leisure, spectacular architecture and mass entertainment.
