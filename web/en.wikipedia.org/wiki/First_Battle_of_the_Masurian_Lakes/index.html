The First Battle of the Masurian Lakes was a German offensive in the early stages of the First World War, fought from 7 to 14 September 1914. It followed the German victory at the Battle of Tannenberg in late August and pushed the Russian First Army, commanded by Paul von Rennenkampf, out of East Prussia.

Background

After destroying the Russian Second Army at Tannenberg, the German Eighth Army under Paul von Hindenburg and Erich Ludendorff turned north against Rennenkampf's First Army, which had advanced cautiously into East Prussia and taken up positions along the Masurian Lakes. Reinforced by troops transferred from France, the Germans attacked the Russian left flank and rolled the line back.

Outcome

The Russians retreated in reasonable order across the border, losing perhaps 125,000 men including prisoners, but avoiding the annihilation suffered by the Second Army. The battle ended the first Russian invasion of East Prussia and cemented Hindenburg's reputation; a second battle in the same region followed in February 1915.
