Environmental humanities

The environmental humanities are an interdisciplinary field of scholarship that draws on history, literature, philosophy, anthropology, cultural studies and the arts to examine the relationships between human cultures and the natural world. Emerging under that name in the 2000s and 2010s, the field consolidated older areas such as environmental history, ecocriticism and environmental philosophy.

Scope and themes

Environmental humanists study how environments are imagined, represented and valued — in texts, images, memory and law — and how those representations shape material outcomes such as conservation, extraction and climate policy. Recurring themes include the Anthropocene and the human transformation of the Earth system; environmental justice and the unequal distribution of ecological harms; indigenous knowledge and decolonial approaches; multispecies relations and the ethics of care for nonhumans; and the emotional dimensions of ecological loss, sometimes termed solastalgia.

Institutions

The field is supported by journals such as Environmental Humanities (founded 2012) and ISLE, by research centres including the Rachel Carson Center in Munich and the KTH Environmental Humanities Laboratory in Stockholm, and by degree programmes at universities in Australia, Europe and North America.

Debates

Proponents argue technical and scientific knowledge alone cannot resolve ecological crises rooted in values and narratives; critics within and outside the field debate its practical leverage, its theoretical jargon, and how it should relate to activism and the natural sciences.
