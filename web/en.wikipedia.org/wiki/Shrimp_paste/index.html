Shrimp paste

Shrimp paste or shrimp sauce is a fermented condiment commonly used in Southeast Asian and coastal Chinese cuisines. It is made from finely crushed shrimp or krill mixed with salt, then fermented for several weeks.

RegionSoutheast Asia, southern China
Main ingredientsShrimp or krill, salt
VariationsBelacan, terasi, kapi, ngapi, bagoong alamang

Varieties

In Malaysia and Singapore it is belacan; in Indonesia terasi (trassi in Dutch); in Thailand kapi; in Myanmar ngapi; in the Philippines liquid or wet bagoong alamang; in Vietnam mắm tôm; in Hong Kong and Guangdong fine shrimp sauce. Colour ranges from pale pink to dark brown, texture from soft sauce to hard dried blocks.

Use

Despite a pungent aroma when raw, shrimp paste mellows and adds deep umami when cooked. It is essential in sambals (sambal belacan), curries, laksa, fried rice, kangkung belacan (stir-fried water spinach), and dipping sauces. Only small quantities are used.

Production

Tiny shrimp are salted, sun-dried, ground, fermented, then dried and pressed into blocks. Traditional production continues in fishing villages such as those in Malaysia's coast and Indonesia's Java coast.
