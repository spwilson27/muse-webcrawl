Hyde Park, Chicago

Hyde Park is a community area on the South Side of Chicago, Illinois, located on the shore of Lake Michigan seven miles (11 km) south of the Loop. It is home to the University of Chicago, the Griffin Museum of Science and Industry, and was the site of the World's Columbian Exposition of 1893.

History

Hyde Park was founded by Paul Cornell in 1853 as a suburban resort community around a hotel on Lake Michigan. It was annexed to Chicago in 1889. In 1893, neighbouring Jackson Park hosted the World's Columbian Exposition, whose "White City" and Midway Plaisance transformed the area; the Palace of Fine Arts survived as the Museum of Science and Industry. The University of Chicago, founded in 1890, became the neighbourhood's dominant institution.

Landmarks and culture

Notable sites include the Robie House (1909) by Frank Lloyd Wright, the Midway Plaisance, Promontory Point, the Oriental Institute, and the DuSable Black History Museum. Hyde Park was home to Barack Obama before and after his presidency. The neighbourhood is known for its racial diversity, bookstores, and the Hyde Park Jazz Festival.

Education

Besides the University of Chicago, Hyde Park contains several theological seminaries, the University of Chicago Laboratory Schools, and Kenwood Academy.
