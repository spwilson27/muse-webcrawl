Pole bending

Pole bending is a rodeo and gymkhana event in which a horse and rider run a serpentine pattern around a line of six poles, timed for speed. It is one of the standard timed events in high school and youth rodeo, at National High School Rodeo Association and many local competitions, and is also contested in gymkhana and playday shows.

Pattern

Six poles are set in a straight line, each 21 feet (about 6.4 m) apart, with the first pole 21 feet from the starting line; the poles are typically about 6 feet (1.8 m) tall, mounted on heavy bases so they tip rather than injure the horse if struck. The horse and rider cross the start line at a run, race to the far end of the line, weave down through the poles in a serpentine, turn around the first pole, weave back up through the poles, turn around the end pole, and sprint straight down the line to the finish.

Penalties and technique

Knocking over a pole typically adds a five-second penalty (in some associations it disqualifies the run), and breaking the pattern results in no time. Winning runs at national-level youth competition are commonly around 20 seconds or faster. Success demands a horse that rates (checks its speed) for each turn, changes leads cleanly, and can accelerate out of each bend; riders use leg pressure and slight rein cues while keeping their hands quiet so as not to unbalance the horse.

History

The event developed from informal California gymkhana games in the mid-twentieth century and spread through western riding clubs and school rodeo programmes across the United States, prizing the agility and responsiveness valued in ranch work.
