Cabu

BornJean Cabut, 13 January 1938, Châlons-sur-Marne
Died7 January 2015, Paris
OccupationCartoonist, caricaturist
Known forHara-Kiri, Charlie Hebdo, Le Canard enchaîné

Jean Cabut (13 January 1938 – 7 January 2015), known as Cabu, was a French comic strip artist and caricaturist. He was one of France's most popular cartoonists, a pillar of the satirical magazines Hara-Kiri and Charlie Hebdo, and a long-time contributor to Le Canard enchaîné.

Cabu was killed on 7 January 2015 in the terrorist attack on the offices of Charlie Hebdo in Paris, along with eleven others.

Career

Cabu began publishing drawings in the 1950s and, after military service in Algeria — which made him a lifelong anti-militarist — joined Hara-Kiri in the 1960s. He created recurring characters such as Le Grand Duduche and Mon Beauf, a caricature of the vulgar, reactionary Frenchman.

Television

He reached a wide audience through television programmes such as Droit de réponse and the children's show Récré A2, where he drew live alongside Dorothée.

Legacy

Cabu's deceptively gentle line carried fierce satire of politicians, the military, and religious extremism. His death prompted national mourning in France; he was posthumously honoured, and his work remains emblematic of press freedom.
