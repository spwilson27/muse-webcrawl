Seollal (Korean: 설날; RR: Seollal) is a Korean traditional festival and national holiday marking the first day of the lunisolar calendar. It is one of the most important traditional holidays in Korea, typically celebrated over three days: the day before, the day itself, and the day after. Seollal generally coincides with the period of the Lunar New Year celebrations held across East and Southeast Asia.

Customs

Many Koreans travel to their hometowns to visit families, in one of the largest annual migrations in the country. Central customs include charye, an ancestral memorial rite performed at home on the morning of Seollal, in which food offerings are placed before a memorial table for deceased ancestors; sebae, a deep traditional bow performed by children and younger relatives to elders, who in return give sebaetdon, money in silk pouches; and eating tteokguk, a soup of sliced rice cakes traditionally said to add a year to one's age.

Clothing and games

Many people wear colourful traditional clothing called hanbok. Traditional games played during the holiday include yunnori (a board game played with throwing sticks), neolttwigi (a standing seesaw game for girls and women), kite flying, and jegichagi (kicking a shuttlecock-like object).
