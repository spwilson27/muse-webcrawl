Planet Rock

Artist: Afrika Bambaataa and the Soulsonic Force
Released: April 1982
Label: Tommy Boy
Genre: Electro, hip hop
Producers: Arthur Baker, John Robie

"Planet Rock" is a song by American hip hop artist Afrika Bambaataa and the Soulsonic Force, released as a single in April 1982 on Tommy Boy Records. Produced by Arthur Baker and John Robie, it is one of the foundational records of electro-funk and one of the most influential singles in the history of hip hop and electronic dance music.

Composition

The track was built around the Roland TR-808 drum machine, whose deep electronic kick and handclaps drive the record, and borrowed two elements from the German electronic group Kraftwerk: the melody of "Trans-Europe Express" (1977) and the rhythm and vocoder refrain of "Numbers" (1981), with Kraftwerk later receiving songwriting credit after legal pressure. Over this futuristic backing, Bambaataa and the Soulsonic Force (including Mr. Biggs, Pow Wow, and G.L.O.B.E.) trade raps and chants calling listeners to "rock it, don't stop it" on a universal dance floor.

Impact

"Planet Rock" reached number four on the Billboard soul chart and reshaped Black dance music in the United States, launching the electro movement that produced freestyle, Miami bass, and much early techno and house. Its fusion of rap with European electronic music pointed hip hop beyond looped disco breaks, and the record is consistently ranked among the greatest hip hop songs ever recorded. Rolling Stone placed it among the 500 greatest songs of all time, and its 808 patterns remain a template for electronic production.
