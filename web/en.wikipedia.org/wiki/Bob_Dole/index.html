Robert Joseph Dole
Born: July 22, 1923, Russell, Kansas, U.S.
Died: December 5, 2021 (aged 98), Washington, D.C.
U.S. Senator from Kansas: 1969–1996
Senate Majority Leader: 1985–1987, 1995–1996
Bob Dole, Republican leader in the U.S. Senate

Robert Joseph Dole (July 22, 1923 – December 5, 2021) was an American politician and attorney from Kansas who represented the state in the United States Senate from 1969 to 1996. He was the Republican Leader of the Senate during the final 11 years of his tenure, including three non-consecutive years as Senate Majority Leader. Dole was the Republican presidential nominee in the 1996 election and the vice presidential nominee in the 1976 election.

Early life and war service

Dole was born in Russell, Kansas, and attended the University of Kansas before enlisting in the U.S. Army in 1943. Serving with the 10th Mountain Division in Italy, he was gravely wounded by German fire near Castel d'Aiano in April 1945. The wounds left his right arm permanently disabled and he underwent years of rehabilitation; he compensated by learning to write with his left hand and famously carried a pen clasped in his right hand in public.

Congressional career

After earning a law degree, Dole was elected to the Kansas House of Representatives and then to the U.S. House in 1960, moving to the Senate in 1968. He chaired the Republican National Committee under Richard Nixon and was Gerald Ford's running mate in 1976. As Senate leader he was a pragmatic deal-maker, instrumental in the 1983 Social Security rescue, the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990, and food stamp legislation — a reflection of his Depression-era upbringing and interest in nutrition programs.

1996 presidential campaign

Dole resigned from the Senate in June 1996 to campaign full-time against President Bill Clinton, with Jack Kemp as his running mate. Hampered by a large fundraising and charisma gap, he lost the election, winning 40.7 percent of the popular vote and 159 electoral votes.

Later life

In retirement Dole remained an elder statesman: he chaired the fund-raising campaign for the National World War II Memorial, co-chaired commissions on veterans' care, wrote memoirs, and appeared in advertising and television comedy. He was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1997 and the Congressional Gold Medal in 2018. Dole died in 2021 at age 98 and lay in state in the U.S. Capitol.
