William Wilberforce (24 August 1759 – 29 July 1833) was a British politician, philanthropist, and a leader of the movement to abolish the slave trade. A native of Kingston upon Hull, Yorkshire, he began his political career in 1780 and became an independent Member of Parliament. In 1785 he underwent a profound evangelical conversion, after which he devoted his career to reform. In 1787 he came into contact with Thomas Clarkson and a group of abolitionists, and he soon became the leading English abolitionist in Parliament.

Early life

Wilberforce was born into a wealthy merchant family in Hull. He studied at St John's College, Cambridge, where he formed a lifelong friendship with William Pitt the Younger. Elected MP for Hull in 1780 while still a student, he later sat for Yorkshire, then the largest county constituency in England.

Abolition of the slave trade

From 1789 Wilberforce repeatedly introduced abolition bills. Supported by the research of Clarkson and the evidence gathered by the Society for Effecting the Abolition of the Slave Trade, he finally saw the Slave Trade Act 1807 pass, outlawing the transatlantic slave trade throughout the British Empire. He then campaigned against slavery itself, lending his support to the Anti-Slavery Society founded in 1823.

Other reforms and later life

Wilberforce was a co-founder of the Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (later the RSPCA) and supported missions, education, and moral reform. Failing health forced his retirement from Parliament in 1825. He died on 29 July 1833, just days after the Slavery Abolition Act passed the Commons, ending slavery in most of the British Empire. He was buried in Westminster Abbey.

Legacy

Wilberforce is remembered as one of Britain's foremost humanitarians; Wilberforce University in Ohio, the first college owned and operated by African Americans, is named for him, and his story was dramatized in the 2006 film Amazing Grace.
