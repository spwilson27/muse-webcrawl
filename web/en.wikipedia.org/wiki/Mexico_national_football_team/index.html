The Mexico national football team (Spanish: Selección de fútbol de México) represents Mexico in men's international football and is governed by the Federación Mexicana de Fútbol. Known by the nickname El Tri, the team plays most of its home matches at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City and traditionally wears green shirts, white shorts and red socks, echoing the national flag.

History

Football was introduced to Mexico by British immigrants and miners in the early twentieth century, and an amateur national side represented the country at the 1928 Olympic Games. Mexico played in the inaugural FIFA World Cup in 1930 and has since become one of the most consistent qualifiers in world football, appearing at the tournament more times than any nation outside Europe and South America's traditional powers. Mexico hosted the World Cup in 1970 and 1986, reaching the quarter-finals on both occasions — its best finishes — and is set to co-host the 2026 tournament with the United States and Canada.

Between 1994 and 2018 Mexico reached the round of 16 at seven consecutive World Cups, a run that ended at the group stage in Qatar in 2022. Within its confederation, Mexico is the most successful team in the CONCACAF Gold Cup, winning the tournament a record number of times, and it won the FIFA Confederations Cup on home soil in 1999, defeating Brazil in the final.

Rivalries

Mexico's principal rivalry is with the United States, a fixture that has grown in intensity since the 1990s and is famous for the "dos a cero" (2–0) scorelines that long favoured the Americans in Columbus, Ohio. Long-standing regional rivalries also exist with Costa Rica and Honduras.

Stadium

The Estadio Azteca, opened in 1966, is one of the most iconic grounds in world football and the only stadium to have staged two World Cup finals, in 1970 and 1986. It is scheduled to host the opening match of the 2026 World Cup.

Honours

Mexico's major honours include the 1999 FIFA Confederations Cup, multiple CONCACAF Gold Cup titles (beginning in 1993 and most recently in 2023 and 2025), the gold medal at the 2012 London Olympics (won by the under-23 team), and two FIFA U-17 World Cup titles, in 2005 and 2011.

Notable players

Andrés Guardado is among the most-capped players in the team's history, while Javier "Chicharito" Hernández is the all-time leading scorer. Other celebrated figures include Hugo Sánchez, Rafael Márquez, Cuauhtémoc Blanco and goalkeepers Jorge Campos and Guillermo Ochoa.
