Tabi

White tabi, worn with formal kimono

Tabi (足袋) are traditional Japanese socks worn with thonged footwear such as zōri and geta. They are ankle-high and feature a distinctive split between the big toe and the other toes, allowing them to be worn with the thong strap of traditional sandals.

Description

Tabi are usually made of cloth, traditionally white cotton for formal wear, and are fastened along the back of the ankle with small metal clasps called kohaze. Unlike ordinary socks, traditional tabi are sewn from cut fabric rather than knitted, with a flat sole of heavier cloth. White tabi are worn on formal occasions; coloured and patterned tabi are used for more casual dress, and black tabi have traditionally been worn by men and by workers.

Jika-tabi

A related form, jika-tabi (地下足袋, "tabi that contact the ground"), are heavy-duty split-toe footwear with rubber soles worn directly as shoes. Developed in the early 20th century, they are still popular with construction workers, farmers, gardeners, and festival participants in Japan, who value the ground feel and grip of the split-toe design.

Influence

The split-toe silhouette of tabi inspired modern fashion designs, most famously the "Tabi" boots introduced by Maison Martin Margiela in 1988, which became an iconic avant-garde fashion item.
