Risk

Risk is the possibility of something bad happening. More precisely, risk involves uncertainty about the effects or implications of an activity with respect to something that humans value—such as health, well-being, wealth, property, or the environment—often focusing on negative, undesirable consequences. Many different definitions have been proposed. A widely used international standard definition is "the effect of uncertainty on objectives".

The understanding of risk, the methods of assessment and management, and the descriptions of risk differ across application areas, including business and economics, environment, finance, information technology, health, insurance, safety, security, and privacy. Risk can be quantified in probabilistic terms when outcomes and their probabilities can be estimated; in other settings it is described qualitatively, or treated through concepts such as uncertainty, vulnerability, and hazard.

Definitions

Definitions of risk vary. Knightian analysis distinguishes risk, where probabilities are known or measurable, from uncertainty, where they are not. In decision theory and finance, risk is often identified with variability of returns or the probability of loss. In safety engineering, risk is commonly expressed as the combination of the probability of a harmful event and the severity of its consequences.

Risk assessment and management

Risk assessment is the systematic process of identifying hazards, analysing the likelihood and consequence of unwanted events, and evaluating whether the risk is tolerable. Risk management extends this to selecting and implementing measures—avoidance, reduction, sharing or transfer (for example through insurance), or retention—to modify risk. Frameworks such as ISO 31000 provide generic guidelines for managing risk in organizations.

Perception of risk

Research in psychology shows that people's perception of risk often deviates from statistical estimates. Judged riskiness is influenced by factors such as dread, familiarity, controllability, and catastrophic potential, as described in the psychometric paradigm. Risk perception shapes individual behaviour and public policy, and communication about risk is therefore a distinct discipline.

Risk in finance

In finance, risk refers to the degree of uncertainty and potential financial loss inherent in an investment decision. Types include market risk, credit risk, liquidity risk, and operational risk. Measures such as volatility (standard deviation of returns), beta, and value at risk are used to quantify it, and diversification and hedging are standard mitigation strategies.
