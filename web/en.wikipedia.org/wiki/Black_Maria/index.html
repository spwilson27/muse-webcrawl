Black Maria

"Black Maria" is a term with several meanings, most famously a slang name for a police vehicle used to transport prisoners.

Police van

Black Maria (pronounced "mah-RYE-ah") is a slang term for a police patrol wagon or van used for transporting prisoners, widely used in Britain, the United States and other English-speaking countries since the nineteenth century. The term is applied today to windowless or caged police vans and prison-transfer vehicles.

Origin

The origin of the phrase is uncertain. A popular folk etymology connects it with Maria Lee, a Black woman in nineteenth-century Boston said to have been of such formidable strength that police called for her help with violent prisoners — "send for the Black Maria" — though the story is not documented. Another explanation derives it from the name of a famous black racing mare of the 1830s. The term appears in print for horse-drawn police vans by the mid-nineteenth century.

Other meanings

  • Card games: "Black Maria" is a name for the queen of spades in Hearts and for a British card game related to Hearts in which the queen of spades, ace of hearts and other cards carry penalty points.
  • Film: The Black Maria was Thomas Edison's film studio in West Orange, New Jersey, a tar-papered, rotating building completed in 1893 and named by its staff after the police vans it resembled.
  • Military: British soldiers in the First World War nicknamed a German heavy artillery shell "Black Maria" after the dark smoke of its explosion.
  • Signal: A black flag or light historically shown at ports and stations to indicate a death or a vessel with disease.
