Joker (playing card)

The Joker is a special playing card found in most modern decks of French-suited playing cards, in addition to the standard 52 cards. Usually printed with the image of a jester or clown, the Joker is not part of any suit and functions in many games as a wild card.

History

The Joker was introduced by American card manufacturers in the 1860s. It appears to have originated as a special trump card — the "best bower" — for the game of Euchre, which already used the two bowers (the Jacks of the trump suit and the same-coloured suit). The card's name is thought to derive from a variant of "Euchre" pronunciation, or from the German word Jucker associated with the game; the jester imagery was applied somewhat later. From the 1880s the Joker became a standard inclusion in American decks, and it subsequently spread worldwide, with manufacturers adding their own trademark jester designs and sometimes a guarantee or brand information printed on the card.

Use in games

Many card games do not use the Joker at all, and it is simply set aside. In games that do, its role varies: it may be a fully wild card (as in many forms of rummy and in Canasta variants and video poker), the highest trump (Euchre and some trick-taking games), a penalty card (as in Old Maid-style games where it may substitute for the unmatched card), or a unique special card, as in some versions of poker. Decks commonly include two Jokers, sometimes distinguishable as "red" and "black" or full-colour and limited-colour printings.

Cultural significance

The Joker's image — the unpredictable jester outside the normal order of the suits — has made it a potent cultural symbol of wildness, trickery, and chaos, most famously embodied by the Batman villain of the same name.
