The Office of Economic Opportunity (OEO) was the agency responsible for administering most of the War on Poverty programmes created as part of United States President Lyndon B. Johnson's Great Society legislative agenda. It was established in 1964 under the Economic Opportunity Act and headed by Sargent Shriver, who had previously directed the Peace Corps.

Programmes

The OEO launched many of the most recognisable anti-poverty programmes in American history. Head Start provided early childhood education, health and nutrition services to low-income children. The Job Corps offered residential education and vocational training to disadvantaged young people. VISTA (Volunteers in Service to America) placed volunteers with community organisations fighting poverty, and the Community Action Program funded local Community Action Agencies designed to involve the poor themselves — under the statutory principle of "maximum feasible participation" — in designing services. The OEO also created the federal Legal Services programme, giving poor Americans access to civil legal aid.

Controversy and decline

Community Action proved politically controversial: mayors and local officials complained that federally funded agencies bypassed and sometimes challenged local government. The Nixon administration moved many OEO programmes to other departments; in 1973 Howard Phillips, appointed acting director, attempted to dismantle the agency, an effort courts partly blocked. In 1975 the OEO was renamed the Community Services Administration, which President Ronald Reagan abolished in 1981. Several of its creations — Head Start, Job Corps and Legal Services among them — survive today under other agencies.

Legacy

Historians debate the OEO's effectiveness. The official poverty rate in the United States fell sharply during its early years, from about 19 percent in 1964 to about 11 percent in 1973, though scholars argue over how much of that decline the War on Poverty itself caused. The agency remains a landmark of 1960s liberal governance and of the idea that the federal government could attack poverty directly.
