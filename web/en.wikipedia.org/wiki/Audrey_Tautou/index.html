Audrey Tautou

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Audrey Justine Tautou (born 9 August 1976) is a French actress known for playing wide-eyed ingenues, with her first major role being in Venus Beauty Institute (1999), for which she won a César Award for Most Promising Actress. She rose to international prominence for her lead role in the 2001 film Amélie, which met with critical acclaim and was a major box-office success.

Tautou achieved international recognition for her lead role in the 2006 mystery thriller film The Da Vinci Code, alongside Tom Hanks. She has also portrayed fashion designer Coco Chanel in the biographical drama Coco Before Chanel (2009), earning a BAFTA nomination for Best Actress in a Leading Role, and has starred in a number of arthouse films.

Early life

Tautou was born in Beaumont, in the Puy-de-Dôme department of Auvergne, and was raised in Montluçon, in nearby Allier, Auvergne. Her father is a dental surgeon and her mother is a schoolteacher. She showed an interest in comedy from an early age and started her acting lessons at the Cours Florent drama school in Paris. She has said that Audrey Hepburn, for whom she was named, was an early inspiration.

Career

After small roles in French television, Tautou won the César for Most Promising Actress for Venus Beauty Institute (1999). Director Jean-Pierre Jeunet then cast her, after Emily Watson withdrew, as the whimsical Parisian waitress in Amélie (2001). The film became one of the most successful French films ever released internationally and made Tautou a star. She reunited with Jeunet for the romantic war drama A Very Long Engagement (2004).

Her English-language roles include Dirty Pretty Things (2002), directed by Stephen Frears, and The Da Vinci Code (2006), in which she played cryptologist Sophie Neveu. Subsequent French films include Priceless (2006), Coco Before Chanel (2009), Thérèse Desqueyroux (2012) and Michel Gondry's Mood Indigo (2013). She served on the jury of the 2015 Berlin International Film Festival and has been a prominent ambassador for French cinema abroad.
