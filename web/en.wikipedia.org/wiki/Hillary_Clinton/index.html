Hillary Clinton

Hillary Diane Rodham Clinton (born October 26, 1947) is an American politician and diplomat who served as the 67th United States secretary of state from 2009 to 2013 under President Barack Obama. Previously a US senator from New York (2001–2009) and first lady of the United States (1993–2001) as the wife of President Bill Clinton, she was the Democratic Party's nominee for president in the 2016 election — the first woman nominated for president by a major US party.

Early life and career

Rodham grew up in Park Ridge, a suburb of Chicago. She graduated from Wellesley College in 1969 — her commencement speech drew national attention — and from Yale Law School in 1973. She worked as a congressional legal counsel during the Watergate inquiry and moved to Arkansas, where she co-founded Arkansas Advocates for Children and Families and became the first female partner at the Rose Law Firm. As first lady of Arkansas and then of the United States, she led the Clinton administration's unsuccessful 1993–94 health care reform effort and championed the Children's Health Insurance Program.

Senate and 2008 campaign

In 2000 Clinton was elected to the US Senate from New York, the first former first lady to hold elected office. She supported the Iraq War authorization in 2002 and sat on the Armed Services Committee. In the 2008 Democratic presidential primaries she narrowly lost the nomination to Barack Obama.

Secretary of State

As secretary of state, Clinton advocated a "pivot to Asia," managed the response to the Arab Spring, and oversaw the diplomacy surrounding the raid that killed Osama bin Laden. Her tenure ended amid controversy over the 2012 attack on the US consulate in Benghazi, Libya, and scrutiny of her use of a private email server for official business.

2016 presidential campaign

Clinton won the 2016 Democratic nomination and the national popular vote by nearly three million votes but lost the Electoral College to Donald Trump. She later authored the memoir What Happened (2017) and has remained active in public life, including serving as chancellor of Queen's University Belfast.
