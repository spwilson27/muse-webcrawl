Gallaudet University is a private, federally chartered university in Washington, D.C., for the education of deaf and hard of hearing students. It was the first school for the advanced education of the deaf in the world and remains the world's only university in which all programmes and services are specifically designed to accommodate deaf and hard of hearing students. Instruction is bilingual in American Sign Language (ASL) and English.

History

The institution grew from a school for deaf and blind children founded in 1857 by Amos Kendall, who hired Edward Miner Gallaudet as superintendent. In 1864 President Abraham Lincoln signed the act authorising the Columbia Institution for the Instruction of the Deaf and Dumb and the Blind to confer college degrees. It was renamed Gallaudet College in 1894 in honour of Thomas Hopkins Gallaudet, the founder of deaf education in the United States and father of Edward Miner Gallaudet, and became Gallaudet University in 1986.

Deaf President Now

In March 1988, the appointment of a hearing president over deaf finalists sparked the "Deaf President Now" protest, in which students shut down the campus. The board reversed its decision and appointed I. King Jordan, the university's first deaf president — a landmark moment in deaf civil rights. Roberta Cordano became the university's first deaf woman president in 2016.

Campus and programmes

The historic campus near Capitol Hill, with buildings including Chapel Hall (1870–71), is a centre of deaf culture and ASL scholarship. The university enrolls roughly a thousand undergraduate and several hundred graduate students, and also operates the Laurent Clerc National Deaf Education Center with its demonstration elementary and secondary schools.
