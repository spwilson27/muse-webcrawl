National Capital Region (Canada)

The National Capital Region (French: Région de la capitale nationale) is an official federal designation for the Canadian capital of Ottawa, Ontario, the neighbouring city of Gatineau, Quebec, and the surrounding urban and rural communities on both sides of the Ottawa River. Covering about 4,715 square kilometres, it is home to roughly 1.4 million people. The region is administered in part by the National Capital Commission (NCC), a federal Crown corporation responsible for planning, parks and federal lands in the capital.

Definition and geography

Ottawa was chosen as capital by Queen Victoria in 1857, partly for its position on the border between English-speaking Ontario and French-speaking Quebec and its defensible distance from the American border. The National Capital Region straddles the provincial boundary: Ottawa on the south bank of the Ottawa River, and Gatineau — formed from Hull and neighbouring municipalities — on the north bank in Quebec. Major features include Parliament Hill, the Rideau Canal (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), Gatineau Park, and the Greenbelt encircling Ottawa's inner suburbs.

Governance

Unlike some federal capitals, the region is not a separate federal district; its residents vote in Ontario and Quebec provincial elections and are served by ordinary municipal governments. The National Capital Commission, created in 1959 from the earlier Federal District Commission, owns and manages about 11 percent of the land in the region, including parkways, Gatineau Park, official residences and major festival sites, and must approve designs for federal buildings and lands. The bilingual, two-province character of the region makes transit, planning and language policy perennial subjects of intergovernmental cooperation.

Economy and life

The federal public service is the region's largest employer, supplemented by a significant technology sector in Kanata and elsewhere ("Silicon Valley North"). Cultural institutions include the National Gallery of Canada, the Canadian Museum of History in Gatineau, and the National Arts Centre. Winterlude, held each February, and Canada Day celebrations on Parliament Hill are the region's signature events.
