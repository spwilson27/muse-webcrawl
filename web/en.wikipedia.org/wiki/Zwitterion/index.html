Zwitterion

In chemistry, a zwitterion (from German Zwitter, "hybrid"), also called an inner salt, is a molecule that contains an equal number of positively and negatively charged functional groups, making it electrically neutral overall despite bearing separated formal charges.

Amino acids

The classic examples are amino acids: in aqueous solution the carboxyl group deprotonates and the amino group protonates, giving a form with both –COO− and –NH3+ groups. The pH at which the zwitterion predominates is the isoelectric point. Zwitterionic character explains amino acids' high melting points, solubility in water, and amphoteric behaviour.

Other examples

Zwitterions also include sulfobetaines and other betaines, molecules such as phosphatidylcholine headgroups in cell membranes, and certain psychoactive compounds such as psilocybin. Zwitterionic buffers like HEPES and MOPS are widely used in biochemistry.
