Qabus-nama

The Qabus-nama (Persian: قابوس‌نامه, Qābūs-nāma), also rendered Kabusnameh, is a major work of Persian literature, written in 1082–83 CE by Kay Kavus ibn Iskandar, the Ziyarid ruler of Tabaristan in northern Iran. It is named after the author's grandfather, Qabus ibn Wushmagir.

Content

The book is a "mirror for princes": a father's advice to his son, Gilanshah, on how to live well and rule wisely. Its 44 chapters range over etiquette, education, friendship, love, warfare, trade, religion and the conduct of kings, blending practical counsel with anecdotes and case studies from history.

Influence

The Qabus-nama circulated widely for centuries, was translated into Turkish, Arabic and several European languages, and remains a classic of Persian prose and of the advice-literature genre.
