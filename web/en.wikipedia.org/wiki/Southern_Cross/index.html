Southern Cross (Crux)

Crux is a constellation of the southern sky that is centred on four bright stars in a cross-shaped asterism commonly known as the Southern Cross. It lies on the southern end of the Milky Way's visible band. It is the smallest of the 88 modern constellations, but also one of the most distinctive and culturally significant.

The asterism

The Southern Cross consists of five prominent stars: Acrux (α), Mimosa (β), Gacrux (γ), Imai (δ), and the fainter Ginan (ε). The long axis of the cross, from Gacrux through Acrux, points roughly toward the south celestial pole, extended about four and a half times, and navigators have used it for centuries to find south. Two bright stars, Alpha and Beta Centauri, serve as "pointers" toward the cross. Nearby lies the Coalsack Nebula, a prominent dark nebula silhouetted against the Milky Way, and the Jewel Box open cluster near Mimosa.

History and symbolism

The stars of Crux were visible from the Mediterranean in antiquity and were catalogued by Ptolemy as part of Centaurus; precession gradually carried them below the horizon for northern latitudes. European explorers of the Age of Discovery adopted the cross as an emblem of the southern seas. It features prominently on the flags of Australia, New Zealand, Brazil, Papua New Guinea, and Samoa, and in numerous coats of arms, songs, and regional symbols across the Southern Hemisphere.

In the Indigenous traditions of Australia, the cross and the Coalsack figure in Dreaming stories; in Andean cultures, the constellation was significant to Inca astronomy, and the dark nebula was seen as part of a celestial llama.
