Garrya elliptica, commonly called the coast silk-tassel or wavyleaf silktassel, is an evergreen shrub in the family Garryaceae, native to the coastal ranges of California and southern Oregon in the western United States.

Description

The plant is a dense, upright shrub typically growing 2 to 5 metres tall. Its leaves are leathery, glossy dark green above and pale and woolly beneath, with distinctly wavy margins. Garrya elliptica is dioecious: male and female flowers occur on separate plants. The male flowers are borne in long, pendulous, silvery-grey catkins that can reach 20 to 30 centimetres in length and appear in midwinter, giving the plant its ornamental appeal. Female catkins are shorter and followed by clusters of purple-black berries.

Distribution and habitat

The species is native to coastal scrub, chaparral and the margins of oak woodland from the San Francisco Bay Area north into Oregon. It tolerates drought, wind and poor soils once established, and is valued in coastal gardens.

Cultivation

Several cultivars are grown as ornamental shrubs, including 'James Roof' with exceptionally long male catkins and 'Evie', a compact female form. Plants prefer full sun to part shade and free-draining soil, and are hardy in Mediterranean-type climates.
