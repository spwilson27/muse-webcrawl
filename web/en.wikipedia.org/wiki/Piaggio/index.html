Piaggio & C. SpA is an Italian motor vehicle manufacturer which produces a range of two-wheeled motor vehicles and compact commercial vehicles under several brands. Founded by Rinaldo Piaggio in 1884, the company initially produced locomotives and railway carriages, later moved into aircraft manufacturing, and after the Second World War became famous for the Vespa scooter, launched in 1946.

History

Rinaldo Piaggio founded the company in Sestri Ponente in 1884, initially fitting out luxury ships before moving into rolling stock. During the First World War Piaggio turned to aeronautics, producing aircraft and engines at Pontedera in Tuscany. The Pontedera plant was heavily bombed during the Second World War because of its military aircraft production.

After the war, Enrico Piaggio, son of the founder, decided to leave the aeronautical field in favour of affordable personal transport for a country in need of mobility. Working with aeronautical engineer Corradino D'Ascanio, the company designed a scooter with a body enclosing the mechanical parts, a leg shield, and small wheels — the Vespa ("wasp" in Italian), patented in 1946. The Vespa became an icon of post-war Italian design and was famously featured in the 1953 film Roman Holiday. In 1948 Piaggio launched the Ape, a three-wheeled light commercial vehicle based on the Vespa.

Brands and acquisitions

Piaggio acquired the motorcycle manufacturer Gilera in 1969, the Spanish Derbi in 2001, and in 2004 bought the Italian motorcycle makers Aprilia and Moto Guzzi, becoming one of the world's largest two-wheeler manufacturers and Europe's largest scooter producer. In 2006 the company was listed on the Milan Stock Exchange. Piaggio also produces the MP3, a three-wheeled scooter with two front wheels, and operates in India, Vietnam and China through subsidiaries such as Piaggio Vehicles Private Limited.

Piaggio Aerospace

The aircraft business was separated as Piaggio Aero Industries (later Piaggio Aerospace), known for the P.180 Avanti twin-turboprop business aircraft.
