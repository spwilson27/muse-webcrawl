The Football Association (known by its abbreviation the FA) is the governing body of association football in England and the Crown Dependencies of Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man. Formed in 1863, it is the oldest football association in the world and is responsible for overseeing all aspects of the amateur and professional game in its territory.

The FA sanctions the running of the English football league system and the national football teams of England, and organises the FA Cup, the oldest national football competition in the world, first played in the 1871–72 season.

History

The FA was founded on 26 October 1863 at the Freemasons' Tavern in Great Queen Street, London, where representatives of a dozen London and suburban clubs met to agree a common set of rules. Ebenezer Cobb Morley, a solicitor and captain of Barnes Football Club, drew up the first Laws of the Game. The meeting famously led to a split with the clubs that favoured handling the ball, who went on to found the Rugby Football Union in 1871.

Because it was the first association, it has no distinguishing prefix in its name. The FA was a founding member of the International Football Association Board (IFAB), which maintains the Laws of the Game, and rejoined FIFA permanently in 1946 after earlier disputes over professionalism and relations with Germany.

Responsibilities

The FA's responsibilities include:

  • Governance and regulation of football in England, including discipline and refereeing.
  • The England national teams (men's, women's, and youth teams), based at St George's Park, Burton upon Trent.
  • Competitions: the FA Cup, FA Trophy, FA Vase, Women's FA Cup and FA Youth Cup.
  • County football associations and grassroots development.

Wembley Stadium

The FA owns Wembley Stadium through its subsidiary Wembley National Stadium Ltd. The original stadium of 1923 was demolished and replaced by the current 90,000-capacity stadium, opened in 2007, which hosts the FA Cup final and England home internationals.
