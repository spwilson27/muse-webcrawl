Réseau express métropolitain

The Réseau express métropolitain (REM) is an automated light metro system serving the Greater Montreal area in Quebec, Canada. The network is centred on Montreal's Gare Centrale (Central Station) and uses the Mount Royal Tunnel, with branches extending to the South Shore at Brossard, to the West Island and Montréal–Trudeau International Airport, to Deux-Montagnes in the northwest, and along the corridor toward Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue.

History

The project was announced in 2016 by CDPQ Infra, a subsidiary of the Caisse de dépôt et placement du Québec, which developed, financed, and built the system — an unusual model for public transit in Canada. It incorporated the existing Deux-Montagnes commuter rail line, whose services were suspended for conversion. Construction began in 2018. The first segment, between Brossard and Gare Centrale across the Samuel De Champlain Bridge, opened on July 31, 2023, with further branches opening in subsequent stages.

System

At full build-out the REM comprises about 67 kilometres of track and 26 stations. Trains are fully automated (driverless) Alstom Metropolis vehicles, operating at high frequency — as often as every few minutes on the central trunk — with platform screen doors at stations. The REM is integrated into the region's OPUS fare card system and operated under contract for CDPQ Infra.
