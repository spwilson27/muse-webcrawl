Myricaceae

Myricaceae is a small family of flowering plants in the order Fagales, commonly called the bayberry family or wax-myrtle family. The family consists of shrubs and small trees with simple, often aromatic leaves dotted with resin glands. As currently circumscribed in the APG IV system it contains four genera and about 55 species, distributed mainly in temperate and subtropical regions, with outposts in tropical mountains and southern Africa.

Genera

  • Myrica — about two species, including Myrica gale (bog myrtle or sweetgale) of northern wetlands.
  • Morella — the largest genus, about 45 species, including wax myrtles and bayberries such as Morella cerifera and Morella rubra (the East Asian yangmei, grown for its fruit).
  • Comptonia — a single species, Comptonia peregrina (sweetfern) of eastern North America.
  • Canacomyrica — a single species endemic to New Caledonia.

The older, broadly defined Myrica was split in modern treatments, with most species transferred to Morella.

Description and ecology

Plants are usually dioecious or monoecious, with inconspicuous wind-pollinated flowers in catkins and small drupes often coated with wax. Leaves and fruits are aromatic. Many species form root nodules with the nitrogen-fixing actinobacterium Frankia, enabling them to colonise poor, sandy or waterlogged soils, dunes and bogs.

Uses

The waxy coating of bayberries (Morella cerifera, M. pensylvanica) was traditionally rendered for candle-making, giving a fragrant, clean-burning wax. Bog myrtle (Myrica gale) was used to flavour beer before hops and remains an ingredient of traditional ales and gin; its essential oils repel insects. Yangmei (Morella rubra) is an important fruit crop in China. Several species are planted as ornamentals and for land reclamation.
