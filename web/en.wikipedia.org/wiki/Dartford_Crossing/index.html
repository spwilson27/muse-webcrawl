Dartford Crossing

The Dartford–Thurrock River Crossing, commonly known as the Dartford Crossing, is a major road crossing of the River Thames in England, carrying the A282 road between Dartford in Kent (south) and Thurrock in Essex (north). It comprises two bored tunnels (opened 1963 and 1980) carrying southbound traffic and the Queen Elizabeth II Bridge, a cable-stayed bridge opened in 1991, carrying northbound traffic.

It is the only fixed road crossing of the Thames east of Greater London and one of the busiest estuarial crossings in the United Kingdom, used by around 130,000–160,000 vehicles on busy days. A road user charge, the "Dart Charge", has applied since toll booths were removed in 2014.

History

A tunnel at Dartford was first proposed in the 1920s; a pilot tunnel was dug in the 1930s before work was halted by war. The first full tunnel opened on 18 November 1963, followed by a second bore in 1980 as traffic grew. The Queen Elizabeth II Bridge, opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 30 October 1991, was at the time the largest cable-supported bridge in Europe. The crossing forms part of the M25 London orbital route, although the A282 section is technically an all-purpose road rather than motorway, allowing non-motorway traffic to cross.

Charging

Originally built under toll concessions intended to end once construction costs were repaid, charges were retained by the government after 2003. In November 2014 the toll booths — a notorious bottleneck — were removed and replaced by free-flow automatic number-plate recognition under the Dart Charge scheme, with payment required online, by phone or at retail outlets by midnight the following day.

Future

Chronic congestion has driven plans for the Lower Thames Crossing, a new tunnel further east between Gravesend and Tilbury, which received development consent in 2025.
