Dangdut

Stylistic origins: Orkes Melayu, Indian film music, Arabic music, Western rock
Cultural origins: 1960s–1970s, Jakarta, Indonesia
Key figure: Rhoma Irama, the "King of Dangdut"

Dangdut is a genre of Indonesian folk and popular music that developed from the 1960s onward, blending Malay orkes music with Indian (especially Bollywood) film music, Arabic influences and later Western rock and pop. The name is onomatopoeic, imitating the characteristic sounds of the tabla-style drum: dang and dut.

History

Emerging from the orkes Melayu ensembles of Jakarta, dangdut became the music of Indonesia's urban working class. Rhoma Irama revolutionised the genre in the 1970s with his band Soneta, adding electric guitars and Islamic moral messages, and starring in numerous dangdut films. Elvy Sukaesih, the "Queen of Dangdut", was his counterpart and sometime rival.

Modern dangdut

Regional variants such as dangdut koplo in East Java feature faster tempos and exuberant stage performance. In the 2000s singer Inul Daratista sparked national controversy — and debates about morality and censorship — with her suggestive goyang ngebor ("drilling") dance. Today dangdut remains Indonesia's most popular domestic genre, increasingly streamed and exported to Malaysia.
