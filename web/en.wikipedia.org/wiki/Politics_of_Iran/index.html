Politics of Iran

The politics of Iran operate within the framework of the Islamic Republic established after the 1979 revolution — a theocratic-republican hybrid in which elected institutions coexist with clerical bodies holding overriding authority.

Structure of power

The Supreme Leader, chosen by the Assembly of Experts, is commander-in-chief and appoints the heads of the judiciary, state broadcasting and the armed forces. Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini held the post until 1989 and was succeeded by Ali Khamenei. The President, elected for four-year terms, heads the executive branch of government but is subordinate to the Supreme Leader.

The unicameral Islamic Consultative Assembly (Majlis) legislates, but all laws and all candidates for election are vetted by the Guardian Council, twelve jurists half appointed by the Supreme Leader. The Expediency Council arbitrates disputes between the Majlis and the Guardian Council. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) is a powerful military and economic actor alongside the regular armed forces.

Political currents

Iranian politics are contested between "principlist" (conservative) and reformist camps, with all candidates pre-screened for loyalty to the system. Mass protests — notably the 2009 Green Movement after a disputed presidential election and the 2022 protests following the death of Mahsa Amini — have repeatedly challenged the establishment and been met with repression.
