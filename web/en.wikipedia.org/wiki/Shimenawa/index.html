Shimenawa

Shimenawa (注連縄) are lengths of laid rice straw or hemp rope used for ritual purification in the Shinto religion of Japan. They mark the boundaries of sacred space and indicate the presence of kami, and are often hung with shide—zigzag paper streamers.

Uses

Shimenawa are hung across the entrances of Shinto shrines, around sacred trees and rocks known as yorishiro, at torii gates, and around objects or places believed to be inhabited by spirits. A particularly large shimenawa hangs at the Izumo-taisha shrine in Shimane Prefecture, one of the largest in Japan. During New Year celebrations, related straw decorations called shimekazari are hung on homes to welcome the gods and ward off evil.

In sumo wrestling, the rope belt worn by a yokozuna, the highest-ranking sumo wrestler, is a shimenawa-style rope called a tsuna, signifying the wrestler's sacred status.

Tradition

The custom is associated with the legend of the sun goddess Amaterasu, who was lured from a cave and prevented from retreating back into it by a rope stretched across the entrance. Shimenawa are traditionally made from rice straw left after the harvest, and shrines renew them at set times of the year.
