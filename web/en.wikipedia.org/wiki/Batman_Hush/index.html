Batman: Hush

"Hush" is a 2002–2003 Batman comic book story arc published by DC Comics. It ran in the Batman monthly series issues #608–619, written by Jeph Loeb and drawn by Jim Lee, with inks by Scott Williams and colours by Alex Sinclair. The story depicts a mysterious stalker known as Hush who manipulates Batman's enemies and allies against him, while Batman's relationship with Catwoman deepens and his childhood friend, surgeon Thomas Elliot, re-enters his life. The arc was a major commercial success and helped revitalise the Batman title, ranking among the best-selling comics of its period.

Plot

While rescuing a kidnapped boy from Killer Croc, Batman's batrope is cut and he suffers a fractured skull in the fall; he is saved by Huntress and treated by Thomas Elliot, a brilliant neurosurgeon and Bruce Wayne's childhood friend. As Batman pursues the people behind the ransom money — including Poison Ivy, who briefly controls Superman with kryptonite-laced lipstick — he draws closer to Catwoman. A shadowy bandaged figure, Hush, coordinates the villains from behind the scenes. The conspiracy climaxes with the apparent resurrection of Jason Todd, an attack by a re-formed Harvey Dent, and the revelation that the Riddler masterminded the scheme using a Lazarus Pit, and that Elliot — having faked his own death with the help of Clayface — is Hush. Dent, restored to sanity, shoots Elliot, whose body falls into the river and is not recovered.

Reception and legacy

The storyline was praised for Jim Lee's artwork and for showcasing Batman's entire rogues' gallery and supporting cast, though some critics considered the plot overly reliant on guest appearances and twists. It was followed by "Heart of Hush" by Paul Dini, and Hush returned in several later storylines. An animated film adaptation, Batman: Hush, was released in 2019 as part of the DC Animated Movie Universe, with changes to the identity behind the conspiracy. The arc remains one of the most frequently reprinted modern Batman stories and is often recommended as an entry point for new readers.
