Autonomism

Autonomism, also known as autonomist Marxism, is an anti-authoritarian left-wing political and social movement and theory. It grew out of the Italian operaismo (workerism) movement of the 1960s and spread internationally, influencing movements in Germany (the Autonomen), France and the Americas.

Theory

Autonomist theory holds that the working class is an active, autonomous force in the development of capitalism rather than a passive victim of it: capital restructures production in response to workers' struggles, not the other way round. Key ideas include the refusal of work, self-valorisation, and the analysis of the "social factory", in which capitalist relations extend beyond the workplace into society as a whole.

History

Autonomism

The movement's theorists included Mario Tronti, whose Workers and Capital (1966) framed the "strategy of refusal", and Antonio Negri, who with Michael Hardt later developed autonomist ideas for a global audience in Empire (2000). In Italy the movement peaked in the mass strikes and occupations of the "Hot Autumn" of 1969 and the movement of 1977, before heavy state repression in the late 1970s. German Autonomen became known for squatting and black bloc tactics from the 1980s onward.
