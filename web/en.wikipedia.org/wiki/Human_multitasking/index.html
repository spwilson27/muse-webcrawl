Human multitasking

Human multitasking is the concept of a person performing two or more tasks at the same time, such as operating a vehicle while talking on a phone. Research in cognitive psychology indicates that, for most cognitively demanding tasks, apparent multitasking is actually rapid task switching: the brain shifts attention serially between tasks, incurring switch costs in time and accuracy.

FieldCognitive psychology

Research findings

Experiments show that switching between tasks increases reaction times and error rates — the "switch cost". Heavy media multitaskers have been found in some studies to be more susceptible to distraction. Driving while using a mobile phone significantly increases crash risk, comparable in some measures to alcohol impairment, and many jurisdictions have banned handheld phone use while driving.

Task combinations

Tasks that are highly practised and automatic (such as walking) can be combined with other activities with little interference. Interference is greatest when two tasks draw on the same sensory modality or response systems, a pattern explained by dual-task paradigms and the psychological refractory period.

"Supertaskers"

A small minority of individuals — estimated around 2.5% in one widely cited study — appear able to dual-task (e.g., driving simulation plus an auditory memory task) without measurable performance loss. For the overwhelming majority, performance on at least one task degrades.
