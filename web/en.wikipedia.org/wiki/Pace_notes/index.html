Pacenotes

Pacenotes are a system of shorthand notes used in rallying to describe the route ahead in detail. During reconnaissance ("recce") runs before an event, the driver describes the road and the co-driver writes the notes; during the competitive stage the co-driver reads them back over the car's intercom so the driver can anticipate corners, crests, and hazards at speed on roads they cannot see around.

Content of the notes

A typical note encodes the severity and direction of a corner, its length, the distance to the next feature, and additional information such as jumps, crests, surface changes, and whether a corner tightens or opens. Systems vary between crews: many use a numeric scale (for example 1 for a hairpin to 6 for a fast kink) or a descriptive scale, with modifiers such as "long," "tightens," "don't cut," "caution," and "over crest."

A call such as "100, five-left long over crest, into three-right don't cut, 50, hairpin left" tells the driver what is coming and in what sequence, allowing the car to be positioned and braked before the feature is visible.

History and use

Detailed route notes began to be supplied by organizers in some events in the 1950s and 1960s. Their use became widespread in the World Rally Championship as speeds rose, and in most top-level events crews now write their own notes during limited-speed reconnaissance passes, sometimes adapting organizer-supplied base notes. Because the vocabulary is personal, a co-driver's notes are usually intelligible only to the crew that wrote them.

In popular culture

The rhythm and compression of pacenote calls have become a recognizable feature of rally broadcasting and simulation games, where players can select a co-driver voice to call notes during stages.
