Pétanque


Origin: La Ciotat, Provence, France (1907). Equipment: metal boules, wooden cochonnet.

Pétanque (French pronunciation: [petɑ̃k]) is a French boules game in which players throw hollow metal balls (boules) as close as possible to a small wooden target ball called the cochonnet ("little piglet"), while standing with both feet anchored inside a circle. It is one of the family of boules sports that also includes bocce and lawn bowls.

History

Pétanque was invented in 1907 in La Ciotat, near Marseille, when Jules Lenoir, a champion of the older running-throw game jeu provençal who could no longer run because of rheumatism, began playing with his feet planted — pieds tanqués, "feet anchored," in Provençal. The first tournament was held in La Ciotat in 1910.

Gameplay

The game is played between two teams of one, two, or three players. A player throws the cochonnet 6–10 metres, then teams alternate throwing boules, either pointing (rolling or lobbing close to the jack) or shooting (knocking an opponent's boule away). When all boules are thrown, the team with the closest boule scores one point for each of its boules nearer than the opponent's best. Games are usually played to 13 points.

Popularity

Pétanque is played worldwide, with an international federation (FIPJP) founded in 1958, world championships, and strong followings in France, Thailand, and across francophone Africa and Southeast Asia. It is a fixture of village squares throughout Provence and southern France.
