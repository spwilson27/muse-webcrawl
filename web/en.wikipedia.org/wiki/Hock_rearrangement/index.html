Hock rearrangement

The Hock rearrangement is an acid-catalysed rearrangement reaction in organic chemistry in which an allylic or benzylic hydroperoxide is converted into a carbonyl compound and an alcohol (in the allylic case), or, in its best-known form, cumene hydroperoxide is converted into phenol and acetone.

Mechanism

Under acidic conditions the hydroperoxide oxygen is protonated and water departs, generating an electron-deficient oxygen centre. A neighbouring group — in the cumene case a phenyl group — migrates from carbon to oxygen, producing a carbocation that is trapped by water; hydrolysis of the resulting hemiacetal-like intermediate then yields the final products.

Industrial importance

The rearrangement of cumene hydroperoxide is the key step of the cumene process (Hock process), the dominant industrial route to phenol, which also co-produces acetone. The reaction is named after the German chemist Heinrich Hock, who reported it in 1944.
