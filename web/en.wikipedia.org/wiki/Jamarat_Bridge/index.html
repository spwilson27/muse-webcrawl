Jamarat Bridge

The Jamarat Bridge (Arabic: جسر الجمرات) is a multi-level pedestrian bridge in Mina, near Mecca, Saudi Arabia, used by Muslim pilgrims during the Hajj for the ritual of the Stoning of the Devil (ramy al-jamarat), in which pilgrims throw pebbles at three walls (formerly pillars) called jamarāt.

History and reconstruction

The original bridge was built in the 1960s to allow pilgrims to throw stones from above as well as from ground level. The ritual site was historically the scene of deadly crowd crushes, including the 1990, 1994, 1998, 2001, 2004, and 2006 disasters, prompting a complete redesign. The old bridge was demolished after the 2006 Hajj and replaced by a vast new structure, completed in stages between 2007 and 2009, with five levels, eleven entrances and twelve exits, escalators, and air conditioning, designed to move over 300,000 pilgrims per hour.

The three pillars were also replaced by long elliptical walls some 26 metres across to give pilgrims a larger target and reduce crowding. Despite the improvements, a catastrophic crowd crush occurred in Mina in 2015 — not on the bridge itself but at a nearby street intersection — killing over two thousand pilgrims by some estimates.

Design

The current bridge is about 950 metres long and 80 metres wide, with capacity planned for expansion to five million pilgrims. It incorporates crowd-management features such as one-way flows, separation of entry and exit routes, and shaded access tunnels, and has been described as one of the largest crowd-engineering projects in the world.
