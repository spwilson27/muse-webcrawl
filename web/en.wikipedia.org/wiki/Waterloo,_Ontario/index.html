Waterloo is a city in the Regional Municipality of Waterloo in southern Ontario, Canada. It is the smallest of the three cities in the Region of Waterloo, and is adjacent to the larger city of Kitchener, with which it forms the contiguous urban area often called Kitchener–Waterloo. The city had a population of about 121,000 in the 2021 census; the region as a whole exceeds 580,000.

History

The area was first settled by Mennonite families from Pennsylvania, beginning around 1804; Abraham Erb, one of the first settlers, founded the village of Waterloo in 1806. The town was renamed Waterloo in 1816, after the Battle of Waterloo. The coming of the railway in the 1850s and its incorporation as a town in 1857 and as a city in 1948 fueled steady growth.

Education and economy

Waterloo is home to the University of Waterloo, known for its mathematics, engineering and co-operative education programs, and Wilfrid Laurier University. The city is a major Canadian technology hub — often nicknamed "Canada's Silicon Valley" — anchored by the Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics and by companies such as OpenText and the former Research In Motion (BlackBerry), founded in Waterloo in 1984. The city's economy also spans insurance (Manulife, Sun Life), manufacturing and brewing, and it hosts the annual Waterloo Busker Carnival and Oktoberfest celebrations shared with Kitchener.
