Marble is a metamorphic rock formed when limestone or dolomite is subjected to heat and pressure, recrystallising into an interlocking mosaic of calcite or dolomite crystals. It has been prized since antiquity as a material for sculpture and architecture for its relative softness when quarried, its translucency, and its capacity to take a fine polish. The word derives from the Ancient Greek mármaros, "crystalline rock, shining stone". (The small glass or stone balls used in children's games, also called marbles, are a separate topic.)

Formation and properties

During metamorphism the original carbonate sediments recrystallise and sedimentary structures and fossils are typically obliterated. Pure marble is white; impurities such as clay, silt, iron oxides or chert produce the swirls and veins — grey, pink, green, red or black — that characterise decorative varieties. Marble is relatively soft (Mohs hardness about 3), which makes it easy to carve but vulnerable to acid attack and weathering outdoors, a major conservation problem for monuments in polluted cities. Geologists restrict the term to metamorphosed carbonate rock, while the stone trade uses "marble" more loosely for any polishable limestone.

Quarries and varieties

Famous sources include the quarries of Carrara in Tuscany, Italy, which supplied Michelangelo and have operated since Roman times; the fine-grained Pentelic marble of Mount Pentelicus used for the Parthenon; Parian marble from the island of Paros, favoured for classical Greek sculpture; and Makrana marble in Rajasthan, used for the Taj Mahal. Other major producers include Turkey, China, Spain and the United States (Vermont, Georgia, Colorado). The Yule marble of Colorado was used for the Lincoln Memorial and the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier.

Uses

Marble is used for sculpture, building cladding and columns, flooring, staircases, countertops and monuments. Finely ground marble is used as a filler and pigment (whiting) in paints, plastics, paper and cosmetics, and as a source of calcium in animal feed and pharmaceuticals.
