An aerial lift, also called an aerial work platform (AWP) or mobile elevating work platform (MEWP), is a mechanical device used to provide temporary access for people and equipment to elevated work areas. Unlike a crane, an aerial lift is designed to carry personnel in a guarded platform or bucket, and it is widely used in construction, building maintenance, warehousing, utilities, orchard work, and film production.

Types

Boom lifts use a hinged or telescoping arm to position a platform; articulating ("knuckle") booms can reach up and over obstacles, while telescopic booms offer maximum horizontal outreach, with the largest models exceeding 55 metres of working height. The familiar vehicle-mounted "cherry picker" — originally developed for orchards and now standard for utility line work — is a boom lift on a truck chassis. Scissor lifts raise a larger deck vertically on crossed pantograph arms and are common in indoor work. Other forms include mast lifts, personnel lifts, towable lifts, and spider lifts on tracked chassis for confined spaces.

Safety

The principal hazards are falls from the platform, tip-overs, electrocution from overhead power lines, and crushing against overhead structures. Modern standards require guardrails, fall-arrest anchor points on boom lifts, load and tilt sensing, and operator training and familiarisation; international standards include the ANSI A92 series in North America and ISO 16368. Major manufacturers include JLG, Genie (Terex), Haulotte, and Skyjack.
