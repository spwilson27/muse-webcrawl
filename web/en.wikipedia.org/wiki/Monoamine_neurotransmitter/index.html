Monoamine neurotransmitter

Monoamine neurotransmitters are neurotransmitters and neuromodulators that contain one amino group connected to an aromatic ring by a two-carbon chain. They are derived from aromatic amino acids such as phenylalanine, tyrosine, and tryptophan. The principal monoamine neurotransmitters are serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine), dopamine, norepinephrine, epinephrine, melatonin, and histamine.

The catecholamines — dopamine, norepinephrine, and epinephrine — are synthesized from tyrosine, while serotonin is synthesized from tryptophan. Monoamine systems in the brain originate in small clusters of neurons in the brainstem and project widely; they regulate mood, arousal, reward, attention, sleep, and appetite. Their signaling is terminated mainly by reuptake through plasma membrane transporters (such as the serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine transporters) and by enzymatic breakdown by monoamine oxidase.

Disorders of monoamine signaling have been implicated in depression, schizophrenia, Parkinson's disease, and ADHD, and many major classes of psychiatric and neurological drugs — including SSRIs, SNRIs, MAO inhibitors, and most antipsychotics — act on monoamine systems.
