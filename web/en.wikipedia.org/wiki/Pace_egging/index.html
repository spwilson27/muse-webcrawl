Pace egging

Pace egging is a traditional folk custom practised at Easter in parts of northern England, particularly Lancashire, Yorkshire, and Cumbria. Pace derives from Pascha, relating to Easter. The custom has two related parts: the giving or rolling of decorated pace eggs, and the performance of a pace egging play (a form of mummers' play) by groups who went from house to house or performed in public places in return for money, food, or eggs.

Pace eggs

Pace eggs are hard-boiled eggs dyed or decorated — traditionally with onion skins, gorse flowers, or other natural dyes, sometimes leaving patterned imprints — and given to children or used in egg rolling and egg tapping games at Easter. Egg rolling down hillsides remains popular in parts of the North West, notably at Avenham Park in Preston on Easter Monday.

Pace egging plays

The plays are short folk dramas featuring stock characters such as Saint George, Bold Slasher, the Doctor, Old Tosspot, and Beelzebub, involving a combat in which a character is slain and then revived by the Doctor. Songs such as the "Pace Egging Song" accompany the performances. Traditions survive in places such as Middleton, Heptonstall, and the Calder Valley, where local teams have revived the plays since the 20th century.
