Fortune-telling

Fortune-telling is the practice of predicting information about a person's life, often through supernatural or intuitive means. The scope of fortune-telling is in principle identical with the practice of divination. The difference is that divination is the term used for predictions considered part of a religious ritual, invoking deities or spirits, while the term fortune-telling implies a less serious or formal setting, even one of popular culture, where belief in occult workings behind the prediction is less prominent than the concept of suggestion, spiritual or practical advisory or affirmation.

History

Fortune-telling has existed in nearly every culture and era, from the oracles of ancient Greece and the haruspices of Rome to the astrologers of imperial China and India. In the West, fortune-telling became a popular commercial entertainment during the 19th and 20th centuries, associated with fairs, carnivals, and travelling Romani communities, whose members were stereotypically portrayed as palm readers and crystal-gazers.

Methods

Common methods of fortune-telling include astrology, palmistry (chiromancy), cartomancy with tarot cards or ordinary playing cards, crystal gazing (crystallomancy), numerology, dream interpretation, tea-leaf reading (tasseography), and the casting of lots or runes. In East Asia, methods such as the I Ching, face reading, and kau cim (fortune sticks) are widely practised.

Criticism

Sceptics regard fortune-telling as pseudoscience and attribute its apparent successes to cold reading, the Barnum effect, confirmation bias, and vagueness of predictions. Many jurisdictions historically regulated or prohibited paid fortune-telling as a form of fraud, though enforcement varies and the practice remains legal as entertainment in many places.
