The Havana Conference of December 1946 was a historic meeting of the American Mafia and the Jewish Mob in Havana, Cuba. Organised by Meyer Lansky and attended by leading organised-crime figures from across the United States, it is considered one of the most important summits in the history of the American underworld, comparable to the later Apalachin meeting of 1957.

Background

Charles "Lucky" Luciano, the imprisoned boss credited with structuring the American Mafia's Commission, had been paroled and deported to Italy in 1946 in return for his wartime cooperation with US naval intelligence. In October 1946 he secretly travelled to Havana, where Lansky had extensive casino and hotel interests under the regime of Fulgencio Batista. The conference, held at the Hotel Nacional from 20 to 22 December, was disguised as a celebration featuring a performance by Frank Sinatra, who flew in with members of the Fischetti family.

Agenda and decisions

The delegates discussed the distribution of narcotics trafficking, the division of Cuban casino interests, and the organisation's operations in Las Vegas. The most consequential item concerned Bugsy Siegel, whose Flamingo Hotel project in Las Vegas had run far over budget amid suspicions he was skimming construction funds. The conference is widely reported to have confirmed a death sentence on Siegel, to be carried out once the Flamingo was completed; Siegel was murdered in June 1947, minutes after the Flamingo's relaunch, and Mob figures took over the property.

Luciano also attempted to have himself reinstated as the pre-eminent boss of the American Mafia, but Vito Genovese's ambitions and pressure from US authorities — who detected Luciano's presence and forced Cuba to deport him in February 1947 — ended the plan.

Legacy

The conference, and Sinatra's attendance, attracted lasting attention from the FBI and the press and features in works such as Mario Puzo's The Godfather Part II storyline analogue (the Hyman Roth birthday gathering in Havana) and numerous Mafia histories.
