16:9 aspect ratio

16:9 (also written 1.78:1) is an aspect ratio with a width of 16 units and a height of 9 units. It became the international standard format for HDTV, non-HD digital television, and analogue widescreen television, and is now the most common aspect ratio for computer monitors and television screens.

History

The ratio was proposed in the 1980s by Kerns H. Powers, a member of the SMPTE working group on high-definition electronic production. Powers cut out rectangles with equal areas shaped to match all popular aspect ratios of the time and found that 16:9 was close to the geometric mean of the extremes, making it an efficient compromise format. It was adopted for the HD-MAC and later ATSC and DVB standards.

Common resolutions

Common 16:9 resolutions include 1280×720 (720p HD), 1920×1080 (1080p Full HD), 2560×1440 (1440p QHD), and 3840×2160 (4K UHD). Lower resolutions such as 640×360 and 854×480 also occur in streaming video.

Use in film and streaming

Although theatrical films commonly use wider ratios such as 1.85:1 or 2.39:1, much streaming and broadcast content is framed for 16:9 displays, and the format became the de facto standard for online video platforms.
