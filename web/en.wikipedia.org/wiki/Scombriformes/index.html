OrderScombriformes

Scombriformes is an order of bony fishes containing the mackerels, tunas and their relatives. In the classification of Fishes of the World (5th edition, 2016), the order comprises a single family, the Scombridae. Other classifications based on molecular phylogenetics have expanded the order to include additional families of pelagic fishes — such as barracudas (Sphyraenidae), cutlassfishes (Trichiuridae), the escolar and snake mackerels (Gempylidae), medusafishes and driftfishes — grouping them in a broader scombriform assemblage.

Mackerels exemplify the streamlined scombriform body plan.

Characteristics

Members in the traditional sense are fast-swimming, epipelagic predators with elongated, fusiform bodies, deeply forked tails and (in scombrids) finlets behind the dorsal and anal fins. The expanded molecular grouping is united more by phylogenetic evidence than by a single obvious anatomical trait.

Classification history

The group was long placed within the order Perciformes as the suborder Scombroidei. Phylogenetic studies in the 2010s led several authorities, including the Catalog of Fishes and Betancur-R. and colleagues, to recognise Scombriformes as an order in its own right within the Percomorpha.
