Safari (web browser)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Safari (web browser)
Released7 January 2003
DeveloperApple Inc.
EngineWebKit
PlatformsmacOS, iOS, iPadOS
[Image: The Safari logo: a silver compass with a red and white needle]

Safari is a graphical web browser developed by Apple. It is built into Apple's operating systems since Mac OS X Panther (2003) and is the default browser on Macs, iPhones and iPads — which makes it, thanks to the iPhone, the second most-used browser family in the world.

Safari's rendering engine, WebKit, was forked by Apple from the open-source KHTML engine of the KDE project; WebKit later became the ancestor of Chrome's original engine, and even after Google forked it into Blink, WebKit remained the required engine for every browser on iOS. Steve Jobs announced Safari at Macworld 2003 as Apple's answer to the ageing Internet Explorer for Mac, touting its speed; a Windows version appeared in 2007 and was discontinued in 2012.

Privacy has been Safari's signature battleground: its Intelligent Tracking Prevention (from 2017) blocks cross-site tracking cookies by default, forcing the advertising industry to adapt, and its privacy reports show users what was blocked. Web developers periodically debate Apple's pace on new web features, making Safari the target of an 'is Safari the new IE?' argument that resurfaces every few years.
