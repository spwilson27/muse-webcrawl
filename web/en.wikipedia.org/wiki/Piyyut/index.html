Piyyut

A piyyut (plural piyyutim) is a Jewish liturgical poem, usually designated to be sung, chanted, or recited during religious services. Piyyutim have been written since Temple times; most were written in Hebrew or Aramaic. The poets who composed them are known as paytanim.

History

The classical era of piyyut flourished in Byzantine Palestine from about the 5th to the 8th centuries, with poets such as Yose ben Yose, Yannai, and Eleazar ben Killir, whose elaborate compositions — involving acrostics, biblical allusion, and newly coined words — were embedded in the statutory prayers for Sabbaths and festivals. Later centres of production arose in Babylonia, Italy, Spain (where poets like Solomon ibn Gabirol wrote in a purer biblical Hebrew), and Ashkenaz.

Forms

Piyyutim take many forms, including the kerovah (a cycle woven through the Amidah), yotzer poems for the morning blessings, selichot penitential poems, and kinot elegies for Tisha B'Av. Many piyyutim, such as "Adon Olam" and "Yigdal", became universally known parts of the prayer book, while thousands of others survive only in manuscripts recovered from the Cairo Geniza.
