Post-it Note

A Post-it Note (or sticky note) is a small piece of paper with a strip of re-adherable adhesive on its back, made for temporarily attaching notes to documents and other surfaces. Post-it is a brand name of 3M, and the notes' iconic canary yellow colour is a 3M trademark.

Invention

In 1968, 3M scientist Spencer Silver developed a weak, pressure-sensitive adhesive based on tiny acrylic spheres. His colleague Art Fry, who sang in a church choir and was frustrated by bookmarks falling out of his hymnal, realized in 1974 that the adhesive could make a reusable bookmark. After an internal "Bootlegging" development period and an initially lukewarm 1977 launch as the "Press 'n Peel," the product was relaunched in 1980 following the "Boise Blitz" free-sample campaign in Boise, Idaho, and became a major success.

Products and culture

Post-it Notes are sold in many sizes, colours, and shapes, and are widely used for brainstorming, project planning (including agile and design-thinking workshops), and office organization. Competing "sticky notes" proliferated after 3M's patents expired in the 1990s. The notes have become a cultural icon, appearing in artworks and public installations.
