Virgin Group Ltd is a British multinational venture capital conglomerate founded by Richard Branson and Nik Powell in February 1970. Its trading name is "Virgin," and it licenses the Virgin brand to a wide variety of companies across sectors including travel, telecommunications, health, banking, music, and leisure. Virgin Group companies operate in more than 35 countries and employ roughly 60,000–70,000 people worldwide across the licensed businesses.

History

Virgin began as a mail-order record retailer in 1970 and a record shop on London's Oxford Street in 1971. Virgin Records, launched in 1972 with a residential recording studio at The Manor in Oxfordshire, signed Mike Oldfield, whose Tubular Bells (1973) was the label's first release and a major success. The label went on to sign the Sex Pistols, Culture Club, the Rolling Stones, and Janet Jackson. In 1992, Branson sold Virgin Records to Thorn EMI for about £510 million to fund Virgin Atlantic.

Virgin Atlantic Airways was founded in 1984 and grew into a major long-haul carrier. Other ventures followed: Virgin Megastores, Virgin Trains (1997–2019), Virgin Mobile (launched 1999 as the world's first mobile virtual network operator with T-Mobile), Virgin Money, Virgin Media (created from the rebranding of NTL:Telewest, later sold to Liberty Global), the Virgin Racing/ Virgin-branded Formula E involvement, and Virgin Galactic, founded in 2004 to develop commercial suborbital spaceflight.

Business model

Unlike a conventional conglomerate, Virgin Group largely acts as an investment holding company and brand licensor: many "Virgin" companies are separate legal entities, partly or wholly independent, licensing the brand. The group emphasizes entering markets with a challenger, consumer-focused identity, with Branson's personal publicity often promoting launches.

Notable companies

Current and former Virgin companies include Virgin Atlantic, Virgin Australia (founded as Virgin Blue; Virgin Group retains a stake following the airline's 2020 sale), Virgin Galactic, Virgin Voyages (cruise line launched in 2021), Virgin Hotels, Virgin Active gyms, Virgin Money UK, Virgin Red (group loyalty programme), Virgin Radio, and Virgin Orbit (satellite launch company, which ceased operations in 2023).

Criticism

The group and Branson have faced criticism over tax arrangements in the UK—Branson has lived on Necker Island in the British Virgin Islands since the 1970s—and over public subsidies sought or received by Virgin companies, including Virgin Care's contracts with the National Health Service and Virgin Trains' rail franchises. The group disputes characterizations of its tax position and points to jobs and investment created by its companies.
