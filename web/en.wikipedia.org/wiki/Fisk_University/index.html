Fisk University

Fisk University is a private historically black university (HBCU) in Nashville, Tennessee. Founded in 1866, shortly after the end of the American Civil War, Fisk is the oldest institution of higher learning in Nashville. It was founded by John Ogden, the Reverend Erastus Milo Cravath, and the Reverend Edward Parmelee Smith of the American Missionary Association, and named for General Clinton B. Fisk, who provided the school with its first facilities in former Union Army barracks.

History

The school opened on January 9, 1866, serving formerly enslaved people of all ages. In 1871 the university faced financial crisis, and its treasurer George L. White formed a group of student singers, the Fisk Jubilee Singers, to tour and raise money. Their performances of African-American spirituals across the United States and Europe saved the institution, and the funds raised built Jubilee Hall (1876), now a National Historic Landmark and one of the oldest structures associated with African-American education in the South.

Academics

Fisk offers undergraduate degrees in the liberal arts and sciences and graduate programs including master's degrees and a bridge program with Vanderbilt University. It is a member of the United Negro College Fund and is accredited by the Southern Association of Colleges and Schools. Enrolment is around 1,000 students. Notable figures associated with Fisk include W. E. B. Du Bois (class of 1888), sociologist Charles S. Johnson, historian John Hope Franklin, poet Nikki Giovanni, and congressman John Lewis.

Campus and collections

The university holds significant art collections, including the Alfred Stieglitz Collection of Modern Art, donated in 1949 by Georgia O'Keeffe, featuring works by Picasso, Renoir, Cézanne and others.
