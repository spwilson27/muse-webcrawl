Van Fortress and Lake Van

Van (Kurdish: Wan; Armenian: Վան) is a city in eastern Turkey, on the eastern shore of Lake Van, Turkey's largest lake. It is the capital of Van Province and one of the largest cities of the Eastern Anatolia Region. The modern population is predominantly Kurdish, and the city is a regional centre for trade, education (Yüzüncü Yıl University) and transport.

History

Van is among the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the region. As Tushpa, it was the capital of the Iron Age kingdom of Urartu in the 9th–6th centuries BCE; the massive Van Fortress (Van Kalesi), built on a rock outcrop by the lake, carries cuneiform inscriptions of the Urartian kings, including a trilingual-style inscription of the Persian king Xerxes I. The city later passed through Armenian, Byzantine, Seljuk, Mongol and Ottoman hands, and developed a substantial Armenian population and cultural life — the nearby island church of Akdamar (Church of the Holy Cross, 10th century) is a masterpiece of Armenian architecture.

During the First World War the city was the scene of fierce fighting in 1915 — the Armenian resistance in Van against massacres, Russia's occupation, and the destruction of the old city — after which the Armenian population was eliminated through massacre and deportation as part of the Armenian genocide. The city was rebuilt on a new site a few kilometres away. In October–November 2011 the Van region suffered two major earthquakes that killed more than 600 people.

Culture and landmarks

Van is famous within Turkey for its lavish communal breakfasts (Van kahvaltısı), for the swimming Van cat — a white breed with heterochromatic eyes, one of the city's symbols — and for Lake Van's alkaline waters, ferry crossings and the "Van Lake Monster" legend. The Van Museum displays major Urartian collections.
