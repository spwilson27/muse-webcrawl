Jack mackerel

Jack mackerel is a common name applied to several species of schooling pelagic fish in the jack family Carangidae, particularly those of the genus Trachurus. Major commercial species include the Chilean jack mackerel (Trachurus murphyi) of the South Pacific, the Japanese jack mackerel (Trachurus japonicus), the Atlantic horse mackerel (Trachurus trachurus), and the Pacific jack mackerel (Trachurus symmetricus) off the west coast of North America.

Biology

Jack mackerels are streamlined, silvery fish with a distinctive lateral line bearing enlarged scutes. They form large schools in coastal and oceanic waters, feed on small crustaceans, squid, and small fish, and are themselves prey for larger predators including tuna, seabirds, and marine mammals.

Fisheries

Jack mackerels support some of the largest fisheries in the world by volume. The Chilean jack mackerel fishery in the southeastern Pacific grew enormously in the late twentieth century, with catches used for fishmeal, canning, and direct human consumption in many countries. Heavy exploitation led to sharp declines, prompting international management through the South Pacific Regional Fisheries Management Organisation (SPRFMO). Jack mackerel is marketed canned and frozen, and is an affordable source of protein in West Africa, Latin America, and elsewhere.
