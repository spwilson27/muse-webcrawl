Altissimo

Altissimo
Altissimo playing on the saxophone
InstrumentsWoodwinds, especially saxophone and clarinet
TechniqueOverblowing harmonics with specialised fingerings and voicing

Altissimo (Italian for "very high") is the uppermost register of woodwind instruments, above the high register and extending beyond the instrument's normal written range. For saxophones, the normal range ends around high F or F♯, and altissimo notes lie above that; clarinettists similarly speak of an altissimo register above the clarion register.

Technique

Altissimo notes are produced by overblowing — using the air column's upper harmonics with the help of special "false" fingerings, altered embouchure, and precise control of the tongue and throat ("voicing"). Because intonation and tone are difficult to stabilise, altissimo playing is considered an advanced technique. Sigurd Raschèr, a pioneer of the classical saxophone, systematised altissimo fingerings in his 1941 method Top-Tones for the Saxophone.

Use

Although once regarded mainly as a novelty or special effect, the altissimo register became a routine part of jazz saxophone vocabulary in the hands of players such as John Coltrane and later Michael Brecker, and contemporary classical saxophone and clarinet repertoire frequently writes into the register. Similar extensions exist on flute (whistle tones and high harmonics) and oboe.
