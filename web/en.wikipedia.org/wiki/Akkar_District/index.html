Akkar District

The hills and villages of Akkar in northern Lebanon

Akkar District (Arabic: قضاء عكار) is the sole district of Akkar Governorate in northern Lebanon. Its capital is Halba. Extending from the Mediterranean coast in the west to the Syrian border in the north and the mountains toward the Beqaa in the east, it covers about 788 square kilometres and is one of the most rural and agriculturally oriented districts in the country.

Geography

The district combines a narrow coastal plain along the Mediterranean with rolling foothills rising eastward toward the northern end of the Mount Lebanon range. The Nahr al-Kabir river forms part of the border with Syria. The terrain supports olives, citrus, tobacco, cereals and vegetables, and farming remains the main economic activity for many of its communities.

Demographics and society

Akkar's population is religiously mixed, with Sunni Muslims forming the majority, alongside Greek Orthodox and Maronite Christians, Alawites and others concentrated in different sub-regions. The district has historically been among Lebanon's most underdeveloped, with limited infrastructure and public services, and since 2011 it has hosted a large number of Syrian refugees in towns and informal settlements.
