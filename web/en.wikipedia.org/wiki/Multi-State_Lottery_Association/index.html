The Multi-State Lottery Association (MUSL) is a nonprofit, government-benefit association owned and operated by its 34 member lotteries in the United States. Headquartered in Urbandale, Iowa, MUSL coordinates multi-jurisdictional lottery games — most famously Powerball — allowing member lotteries to offer the giant jackpots that no single state could support alone.

History

MUSL was formed in 1987 by seven state lotteries (Iowa, Illinois, Kansas, Missouri, Oregon, Rhode Island and West Virginia, plus the District of Columbia) to operate the joint game Lotto America, first drawn in 1988. Lotto America was replaced in 1992 by Powerball, which grew to become the world's best-known jackpot game, producing record prizes including a $2.04 billion jackpot in November 2022. MUSL's membership expanded steadily, especially after 2009–2010 when cross-selling agreements let MUSL states also offer Mega Millions and vice versa.

Games and operations

Besides Powerball, MUSL administers or facilitates games such as Lotto America, 2by2, and various linked progressive and raffle promotions among its members. It maintains drawing facilities — Powerball drawings are held at the Florida Lottery's Tallahassee studio — as well as central random number generation, auditing and security standards for its members, and manages the consortium's shared trademarks and game rules. All profits from MUSL games flow to the member lotteries and their state beneficiaries.
