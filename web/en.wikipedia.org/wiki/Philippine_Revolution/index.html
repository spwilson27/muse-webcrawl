Philippine Revolution
Date: August 1896 – August 13, 1898
Location: Philippines, Spanish East Indies
Result: Philippine victory; Philippine Declaration of Independence; outbreak of the Spanish–American War; Treaty of Paris (1898)
Belligerents: Katipunan / Revolutionary Government vs. Spanish Empire
Key figures: Andrés Bonifacio; Emilio Aguinaldo; José Rizal (inspiration)

The Philippine Revolution (Filipino: Himagsikang Pilipino), also known as the Tagalog War, was a revolution and conflict between the people of the Philippines and the Spanish colonial authorities. It began in August 1896 and led to the Philippine Declaration of Independence on 12 June 1898, ending more than three centuries of Spanish rule, before Spain ceded the islands to the United States in the Treaty of Paris.

Origins

Filipino resentment of Spanish rule, the abuses of the friar orders, and the execution of the priests Gomburza in 1872 fed a growing nationalist movement. The Propaganda Movement of ilustrados such as José Rizal, Marcelo H. del Pilar and Graciano López Jaena campaigned for reforms, while in 1892 Andrés Bonifacio founded the secret society Katipunan (KKK), dedicated to independence through armed revolt.

Course of the revolution

Discovered by the Spanish in August 1896, the Katipunan launched the uprising with the Cry of Pugad Lawin and won early battles before being driven from Manila. Leadership passed from Bonifacio to Emilio Aguinaldo, whose forces in Cavite defeated Spanish troops at Imus and Binakayan; Bonifacio was later tried and executed by Aguinaldo's government in 1897. The Pact of Biak-na-Bato (December 1897) sent Aguinaldo into exile in Hong Kong, but fighting continued.

When the Spanish–American War began in 1898, Aguinaldo returned with American cooperation, proclaimed independence at Kawit, Cavite on 12 June 1898, and established the First Philippine Republic at Malolos. Spain nevertheless sold the Philippines to the United States, leading to the Philippine–American War in 1899.

Legacy

The Revolution is celebrated as the birth of the Filipino nation; 12 June is observed as Independence Day. Its heroes — Bonifacio, Aguinaldo, Rizal and others — remain central figures of Philippine national memory.
