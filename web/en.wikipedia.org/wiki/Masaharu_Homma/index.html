Masaharu Homma

Masaharu Homma (Japanese: 本間 雅晴, 27 November 1887 – 3 April 1946) was a lieutenant general in the Imperial Japanese Army during World War II. He commanded the 14th Army in the invasion of the Philippines, and was later tried and executed for war crimes in connection with the Bataan Death March.

Early career

Homma was born on Sado Island, Niigata Prefecture, and graduated from the Imperial Japanese Army Academy in 1907. An Anglophile noted for his Western tastes and love of English literature, he served as a military observer with the British Army in France in World War I, being awarded the Military Cross, and later as military attaché in London and India.

Philippines campaign and Bataan

In December 1941 Homma led the invasion of Luzon. After American and Filipino forces surrendered on Bataan in April 1942, tens of thousands of prisoners were force-marched to Camp O'Donnell; thousands died of starvation, disease, and brutality in the Bataan Death March. Homma was relieved of command in August 1942, his insistence on humane treatment of Manila's population and delays in taking Bataan having displeased Tokyo; he spent the rest of the war in semi-retirement.

Trial and execution

After the war, Homma was tried by an American military commission in Manila on command-responsibility grounds for atrocities against prisoners. His defence that he neither ordered nor knew of the march's crimes was rejected; he was convicted and executed by firing squad at Los Baños on 3 April 1946. His trial remains a leading case in debates over command responsibility, comparable to that of General Yamashita.
