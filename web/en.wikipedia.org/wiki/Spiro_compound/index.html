Spiro compound

A spiro compound is an organic compound in which two rings are connected through a single shared atom, called the spiro atom, which is typically a quaternary carbon. Because the two rings lie in perpendicular planes when the spiro atom is tetrahedral, spiro compounds have distinctive three-dimensional shapes and may exhibit axial chirality even without a stereogenic centre in the usual sense.

Nomenclature

In IUPAC nomenclature, spiro compounds are named with the prefix "spiro" followed by brackets giving the number of atoms in the smaller ring, then the larger ring (excluding the spiro atom), and the name of the corresponding open-chain hydrocarbon; numbering starts next to the spiro atom in the smaller ring. For example, spiro[4.5]decane has a five-membered and a six-membered ring sharing one carbon atom, ten carbons in total.

Chirality

When the two rings bear suitable substituents, the perpendicular arrangement of the rings makes the molecule chiral without any atom bearing four different groups in the classic sense; such compounds are examples of axial chirality and can exist as enantiomers that are not superimposable.

Occurrence and uses

Spiro motifs occur in many natural products and pharmaceuticals, where the rigid, three-dimensional scaffold is valued in drug design for improving selectivity and escaping flat aromatic chemistry. The polymer and materials field uses spiro compounds such as spiro-OMeTAD, an organic semiconductor widely used as a hole-transport material in perovskite solar cells.
