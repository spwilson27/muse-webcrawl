Judiciary of New York

EstablishedUnder the New York State Constitution
Highest courtNew York Court of Appeals
Chief JudgeChief Judge of the State of New York

The judiciary of New York is the judicial branch of the government of the U.S. state of New York, organized as the Unified Court System. It is considered one of the most complex court systems in the United States, with numerous trial courts of overlapping jurisdiction.

Structure

At the top stands the Court of Appeals, the state's highest court, composed of a Chief Judge and six Associate Judges appointed by the governor. Below it, the Appellate Division of the Supreme Court hears appeals in four geographic departments.

Trial courts

Unusually, the Supreme Court is a trial court of general jurisdiction rather than the highest court. Other courts include the Court of Claims, County Courts, Family Courts, Surrogate's Courts, and the Civil and Criminal Courts of New York City, as well as town and village justice courts.

Administration

The system is administered by the Chief Judge and the Office of Court Administration. Judges reach the bench by a mixture of election and appointment depending on the court. The judiciary's opinions are published in official reports, and the New York Law Journal covers its daily proceedings.
