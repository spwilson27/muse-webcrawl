University of Waterloo

The University of Waterloo (UWaterloo) is a public research university in Waterloo, Ontario, Canada. Founded in 1957, it has about 42,000 students and is renowned for operating the world's largest post-secondary co-operative education programme, in which students alternate academic terms with paid work terms.

History and strengths

The university grew out of Waterloo College's associate faculties of engineering and was built around applied teaching and research links with industry. It is especially strong in computer science, mathematics, and engineering; its Faculty of Mathematics is the largest concentration of mathematical and computing researchers in Canada. The university's intellectual property policy, which lets creators own their inventions, helped seed Waterloo Region's technology sector, dubbed "Silicon Valley North".

Research

Affiliated institutes include the Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics and the Institute for Quantum Computing. Physicist Donna Strickland, a Waterloo professor, shared the 2018 Nobel Prize in Physics for chirped pulse amplification. Waterloo also houses one of Canada's leading schools of optometry and a prominent co-op employer network of thousands of companies.
