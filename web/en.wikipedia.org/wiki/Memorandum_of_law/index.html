Memorandum of law

Memorandum of law
TypeLegal document
PurposeAnalysis or advocacy on a legal question
Common formsOffice memorandum; memorandum in support of a motion

A memorandum of law, also called a legal memorandum or memo, is a document that analyses a legal question, typically summarising the relevant facts, identifying the legal issues, discussing the applicable statutes and case law, and predicting or arguing how a court would resolve the matter.

Types

An office memorandum is an internal, objective document prepared for a supervising attorney or client, candidly assessing both favourable and unfavourable authority. A memorandum of law in support (or in opposition) is a persuasive document filed with a court alongside a motion, arguing for a particular outcome; it is closely related to a trial or appellate brief.

Structure

A typical memorandum includes a heading, a statement of the questions presented, a brief answer, a statement of facts, a discussion applying the law to those facts (often organised by the IRAC method: issue, rule, analysis, conclusion), and a conclusion.
