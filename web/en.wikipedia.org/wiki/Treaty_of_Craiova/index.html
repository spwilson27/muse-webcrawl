Treaty of Craiova

Signed7 September 1940
LocationCraiova, Kingdom of Romania
PartiesKingdom of Romania; Kingdom of Bulgaria
EffectReturn of Southern Dobruja to Bulgaria

The Treaty of Craiova was signed on 7 September 1940 between the Kingdom of Bulgaria and the Kingdom of Romania. Under its terms, Romania ceded to Bulgaria the region of Southern Dobruja (the Cadrilater), which Romania had gained after the Second Balkan War in 1913, and the two countries agreed to a compulsory population exchange of their ethnic minorities in the affected regions.

Background

The treaty was concluded under heavy Axis pressure in the summer of 1940, as Romania, abandoned by its Western guarantors, was forced to make territorial concessions to its neighbours: Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina to the Soviet Union (June 1940) and Northern Transylvania to Hungary under the Second Vienna Award (August 1940). Negotiations with Bulgaria, backed by Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy, took place at Craiova in late August and early September 1940.

Terms

Southern Dobruja, an area of roughly 7,500 square kilometres with a population of about 407,000, was returned to Bulgaria, roughly restoring the border of 1878–1913. Some 110,000 Romanians and Aromanians living in Southern Dobruja were compulsorily resettled in Romania, while about 77,000 Bulgarians from Northern Dobruja were moved to Bulgaria. Romania also paid Bulgaria financial compensation for Bulgarian property left behind.

Aftermath

Uniquely among Romania's 1940 territorial losses, the Treaty of Craiova was confirmed after the Second World War by the Paris Peace Treaties of 1947, and the border it established between Romania and Bulgaria remains in force today. In Bulgaria the recovery of Southern Dobruja is commemorated as a national achievement; Dobrich and Silistra are its main towns.
