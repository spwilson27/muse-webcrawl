Dumbarton (Scots Gaelic: Dùn Breatann) is a town in West Dunbartonshire, Scotland, on the north bank of the River Clyde where the River Leven joins it, about 13 miles (21 km) northwest of Glasgow. The town grew up around Dumbarton Rock, a volcanic plug crowned by Dumbarton Castle.

History

The rock was the stronghold of Alt Clut, capital of the Brythonic Kingdom of Strathclyde, which endured repeated Viking sieges, including a famous four-month siege in 870 after which much of the population was carried off to Dublin. Dumbarton became a royal burgh in 1222 and its castle later served as a refuge for the young Mary, Queen of Scots, who sailed from there to France in 1548. In the 19th century the town became a centre of shipbuilding—William Denny and Brothers launched many notable vessels, including the clipper Cutty Sark, built in Dumbarton in 1869—and of whisky distilling and glassmaking.

Present day

Shipbuilding and heavy industry have largely gone, and Dumbarton now functions partly as a commuter town for Glasgow, with rail links from Dumbarton Central and Dumbarton East stations. Dumbarton Castle, maintained by Historic Environment Scotland, occupies the rock with defences spanning many centuries. The town's football club, Dumbarton F.C., founded in 1872, was one of the early powers of Scottish football and joint winner of the first Scottish league championship.
