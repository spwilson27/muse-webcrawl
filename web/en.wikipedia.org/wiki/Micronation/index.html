A micronation is a political entity whose members claim that it is an independent nation or sovereign state, but which lacks legal recognition by world governments or major international organizations. Micronations are distinct from microstates such as Monaco or Liechtenstein, which are small but internationally recognized. Most micronations claim very small territories — a house, a farm, an offshore platform, or an uninhabited patch of land — and many exist largely or entirely online.

Motivations

Founders' motives vary widely: political protest, libertarian experimentation, tax avoidance, art projects, social experiments, personal amusement, or the promotion of a cause. Some, such as the Principality of Hutt River in Australia (1970–2020), grew out of genuine disputes with governments; others, like Kevin Baugh's Republic of Molossia in Nevada, are openly hobbyist projects.

Notable examples

  • Sealand — claimed on HM Fort Roughs, a sea fort off the English coast, since 1967.
  • Principality of Hutt River — an Australian farm that claimed secession from 1970 until its dissolution in 2020.
  • Liberland — proclaimed in 2015 on disputed land between Croatia and Serbia.
  • Conch Republic — a tongue-in-cheek 1982 "secession" of Key West, Florida, now a tourism brand.
  • Christiania — a freetown in Copenhagen sometimes described as a micronation.

Legal status

Under the Montevideo Convention criteria — a permanent population, defined territory, government, and capacity to enter relations with other states — micronations generally fail on recognition and effective control, and established states typically ignore or administratively absorb their claims. Nevertheless, micronationalism is a persistent cultural phenomenon, with summits such as MicroCon bringing micronationalists together since 2015.
