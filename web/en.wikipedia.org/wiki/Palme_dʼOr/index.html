The Palme d'Or (French pronunciation: [palm dɔʁ]; English: Golden Palm) is the highest prize awarded at the Cannes Film Festival. It is presented by the festival's jury to the best film screened in the Official Competition. Introduced in 1955, it is widely regarded as one of the most prestigious awards in the film industry, alongside the Golden Lion of the Venice Film Festival and the Golden Bear of the Berlin International Film Festival.

History

From the festival's first edition in 1939 (held in 1946 after wartime interruption) until 1954, the top prize was the Grand Prix du Festival International du Film. In 1955 the organising committee introduced the Palme d'Or, its design — a palm frond, echoing the palm trees of the Croisette and the coat of arms of Cannes — created with input from artists including Jean Cocteau. The first winner was Marty, directed by Delbert Mann, which also went on to win the Academy Award for Best Picture.

Between 1964 and 1974 the Palme was temporarily replaced once again by the Grand Prix, owing in part to copyright difficulties concerning the design. It was permanently reinstated in 1975, and in 1997, for the festival's fiftieth anniversary, a special one-off Palme des Palmes was awarded to Ingmar Bergman. Since 2000 the trophy has been produced by the Swiss jeweller Chopard, with the golden palm resting on a hand-cut rock-crystal cushion; no two crystals are identical.

Selection

The Palme d'Or is chosen by the festival's Competition jury, composed each year of film-makers, actors, and other figures from world cinema, headed by a jury president. The jury may, exceptionally, award the prize jointly to two films, and may attach special mentions or combine the Palme with other jury honours. Rules prevent the Palme winner from also receiving certain other main awards, though acting prizes have occasionally been combined with it.

Notable winners

Landmark recipients include The Wages of Fear (1953, under the Grand Prix), Federico Fellini's La Dolce Vita (1960), Michelangelo Antonioni's Blowup (1966), Francis Ford Coppola's Apocalypse Now (1979, shared), Martin Scorsese's Taxi Driver (1976), Quentin Tarantino's Pulp Fiction (1994), Jane Campion's The Piano (1993) — Campion being the first woman to win — Michael Haneke's The White Ribbon (2009) and Amour (2012), and Bong Joon-ho's Parasite (2019), the first South Korean winner, which subsequently won the Academy Award for Best Picture.

Only a handful of directors have won the Palme twice, among them Alf Sjöberg, Francis Ford Coppola, Bille August, Emir Kusturica, Shohei Imamura, the Dardenne brothers, Michael Haneke, Ken Loach, and Ruben Östlund. In 2013 the jury took the unusual step of awarding the Palme to the two lead actresses of Blue Is the Warmest Colour alongside its director, Abdellatif Kechiche.

Honorary Palme

Separately from the competitive prize, the festival has intermittently presented an honorary Palme d'Or for lifetime achievement, with recipients including Woody Allen, Bernardo Bertolucci, Jane Fonda, Agnès Varda, Jean-Pierre Léaud, Jeffrey Katzenberg, Tom Cruise, and Harrison Ford.
