Hit to lead

Hit to lead (H2L), also known as lead generation, is a stage in early drug discovery in which small molecule hits from a high-throughput screen (HTS) are evaluated and optimised into lead compounds. It follows hit confirmation and precedes the more intensive lead optimization phase.

Process

During high-throughput screening, large libraries of compounds are tested against a biological target, producing "hits" that show desired activity. In hit to lead, chemists confirm that the activity is genuine and reproducible, resynthesise the hit compounds to verify their identity and purity, and test them in secondary and orthogonal assays to eliminate false positives, including promiscuous inhibitors and pan-assay interference compounds (PAINS).

Evaluation criteria

Candidate series are assessed for potency, selectivity against related targets, and early ADMET properties (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion and toxicity), as well as physicochemical characteristics such as solubility, lipophilicity (often measured as logP or logD) and chemical tractability. Rules of thumb such as Lipinski's rule of five and ligand efficiency metrics guide selection. A tractable structure–activity relationship (SAR) — systematic changes in structure producing systematic changes in activity — is a key sign that a series can be optimised.

Outcome

The output of a successful hit to lead phase is one or more lead series: compounds with confirmed activity, acceptable preliminary drug-like properties and a clear path for medicinal chemistry optimisation. Lead optimization then refines these into preclinical development candidates. Hit to lead typically takes place over months and bridges the gap between screening biology and full medicinal chemistry programmes.
