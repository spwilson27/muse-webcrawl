Sholes and Glidden typewriter

InventorsChristopher Latham Sholes, Carlos Glidden, Samuel W. Soulé
Introduced1873–1874 (manufactured by E. Remington and Sons)
Notable featureFirst commercially successful typewriter; QWERTY keyboard

The Sholes and Glidden typewriter, also marketed as the Remington No. 1, was the first commercially successful typewriter. Designed in Milwaukee by Christopher Latham Sholes with Carlos Glidden and Samuel W. Soulé, it was manufactured from 1873 by the firearms and sewing-machine maker E. Remington and Sons of Ilion, New York.

Design

The machine used typebars striking upward against a platen, so the operator could not see the line being typed. It typed only capital letters, used a foot treadle for carriage return in early form, and was often mounted on a sewing-machine stand with elaborate floral decoration. Crucially, Sholes's patents introduced the QWERTY keyboard layout, arranged — according to the traditional account — to separate frequently used letter pairs and reduce typebar clashes.

Impact

Though expensive and initially slow to sell, the Sholes and Glidden machine created the template for the typewriter industry: Remington and competitors refined it into the standard office typewriter, and QWERTY became the dominant keyboard layout in the English-speaking world. A famous early adopter, Mark Twain, was among the first authors to submit a typewritten manuscript to a publisher.
