Personal informatics
Also known asQuantified self, self-tracking, lifelogging
DomainHuman–computer interaction
Key modelStage-based model (Li, Dey & Forlizzi, 2010)

Personal informatics is a class of tools and practices in which people collect and reflect on data about themselves — such as physical activity, sleep, mood, diet, finances or productivity — in order to gain self-knowledge and change behaviour. The term was popularised in human–computer interaction research around 2010 and overlaps with the Quantified Self movement, whose motto is "self-knowledge through numbers".

Stage-based model

Li, Dey and Forlizzi (2010) described personal informatics as a five-stage process: preparation (choosing what and how to track), collection, integration (preparing and combining data), reflection, and action (deciding what to change). Later research emphasised that self-tracking is often episodic and "lived" — people lapse and resume tracking as goals change, rather than following a linear pipeline.

Tools

Early self-trackers used spreadsheets and paper journals; the field expanded with wearable devices such as Fitbit trackers, smartwatches, continuous glucose monitors and sleep trackers, and with apps for mood, food and time logging. Passive sensing has reduced collection effort, shifting the burden to interpretation and meaningful feedback.

Challenges

Research identifies barriers including the time cost of manual tracking, data fragmentation across platforms, difficulty interpreting correlations, privacy risks from sensitive data held by commercial services, and the risk that metrics displace intrinsic motivation or cause anxiety.
