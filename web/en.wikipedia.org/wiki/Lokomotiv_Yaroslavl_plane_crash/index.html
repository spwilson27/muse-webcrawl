Lokomotiv Yaroslavl plane crash

Accident summary
Date: 7 September 2011
Site: near Yaroslavl, Russia
Aircraft: Yakovlev Yak-42D
Operator: Yak-Service
Occupants: 45
Fatalities: 44
Survivors: 1

On 7 September 2011, a Yakovlev Yak-42D passenger aircraft operated by Yak-Service as Flight 9633, carrying players and coaching staff of the Lokomotiv Yaroslavl professional ice hockey team, crashed during takeoff near Yaroslavl, Russia. Of the 45 people on board, 44 died; one, flight engineer Alexander Sizov, survived.

The aircraft ran off the runway at Tunoshna Airport before briefly lifting off, striking an antenna mast, catching fire and crashing on the bank of the Volga River. The team was travelling to Minsk, Belarus, to play their opening game of the 2011–12 Kontinental Hockey League (KHL) season against Dinamo Minsk. The entire main roster was on the flight.

Background

Lokomotiv Yaroslavl was one of the leading teams in the KHL. Its roster included former NHL players and international stars such as Pavol Demitra, Ruslan Salei, Kārlis Skrastiņš, Josef Vašíček, Karel Rachůnek and Stefan Liv, as well as head coach Brad McCrimmon, a former NHL defenceman and assistant coach.

Investigation

The Interstate Aviation Committee investigation concluded that the crash was caused by pilot error: one of the pilots inadvertently applied the brakes during the takeoff roll, preventing the aircraft from reaching rotation speed in the available distance, so the jet lifted off beyond the runway end at an excessive pitch angle and stalled.

Aftermath

The KHL suspended its season for several days, and the opening game was rescheduled as a memorial ceremony. Lokomotiv withdrew from the 2011–12 KHL season, fielding a team in the lower-level VHL instead, and returned to the KHL the following season with a rebuilt roster. The 7th of September has since been marked by the league as a day of remembrance, with no regular-season games scheduled. The disaster is among the deadliest accidents involving a professional sports team.
