County Armagh (Irish: Contae Ard Mhacha, named after the goddess Macha) is one of the thirty-two traditional counties of Ireland and one of the six counties of Northern Ireland. Its county town is the city of Armagh, the ecclesiastical capital of Ireland, seat of both the Roman Catholic and Church of Ireland Archbishops of Armagh, primates of all Ireland.

Geography

The county stretches from the southern shore of Lough Neagh — the largest freshwater lake in Ireland and Britain — south to the Ring of Gullion, a ring of hills around the ancient volcanic peak of Slieve Gullion, and the border with County Louth. The north of the county is rich fruit-growing country, especially around Loughgall, earning Armagh its nickname "the Orchard County" for its apple orchards. The main towns are Armagh, Craigavon (including Lurgan and Portadown), and Crossmaglen in the south.

History

Armagh has been a religious centre since Saint Patrick founded his church there in the 5th century; nearby Navan Fort (Emain Macha) was the ancient royal seat of the kings of Ulster. During the Plantation of Ulster the county was settled by English and Scottish colonists. In the 20th century the county, particularly South Armagh, was heavily affected by the Troubles, when the area's strong republican tradition and border position made it a focus of IRA activity and British Army operations.

Culture

The county is a stronghold of Gaelic games; Armagh won the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship in 2002 and again in 2024. The Bramley apple, strongly associated with the county's orchards, has EU protected status. Armagh Observatory and Planetarium continue a scientific tradition dating to 1790.
