Hanif Kureishi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hanif Kureishi CBE (born 5 December 1954) is a British novelist, screenwriter, playwright and filmmaker of Pakistani and English descent. He is best known for the film My Beautiful Laundrette (1985) and the novel The Buddha of Suburbia (1990), works that reshaped portrayals of race, class and sexuality in modern Britain.

Early life

Kureishi was born in Bromley, south London, to a Pakistani father, Rafiushan Kureishi, who came from a wealthy Madras family and worked at the Pakistan Embassy, and an English mother, Audrey. He studied philosophy at King's College London and began writing plays while working at the Riverside Studios.

Career

His screenplay for My Beautiful Laundrette, directed by Stephen Frears, was nominated for an Academy Award and became a landmark of 1980s British cinema. The Buddha of Suburbia won the Whitbread Award for best first novel and was adapted as a BBC television series with a soundtrack by David Bowie. Other works include the films Sammy and Rosie Get Laid (1987), London Kills Me (1991) and The Mother (2003), and the novels The Black Album (1995), Intimacy (1998), Something to Tell You (2008), The Last Word (2014) and The Nothing (2017). His memoir My Ear at His Heart (2004) explores his father's life and unrealised literary ambitions.

Later life

In December 2022 Kureishi suffered a fall in Rome that left him with serious spinal injuries and unable to walk or use his hands. From hospital he began publishing dispatches about his condition and recovery in an online newsletter, later collected in the memoir Shattered (2024). He was appointed CBE in 2008 for services to literature and drama.
