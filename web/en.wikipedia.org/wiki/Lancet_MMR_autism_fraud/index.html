Lancet MMR autism fraud

The Lancet MMR autism fraud refers to a fraudulent 1998 research paper published in The Lancet by Andrew Wakefield and co-authors, which falsely claimed a link between the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine, bowel disease and autism.

Investigative reporting by journalist Brian Deer in The Sunday Times and the BMJ showed that data had been falsified, that children had undergone invasive procedures without proper ethical approval, and that Wakefield had undisclosed financial conflicts of interest, including funding from lawyers preparing litigation against vaccine manufacturers and a patent for a rival vaccine.

The Lancet partially retracted the paper in 2004 and fully retracted it in 2010; Wakefield was struck off the UK medical register. Extensive subsequent studies found no link between MMR and autism, but vaccination rates fell for years and measles outbreaks followed, making the episode a landmark case of scientific fraud and its public-health consequences.
