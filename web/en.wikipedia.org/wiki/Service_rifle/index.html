Service rifle

A service rifle (or standard-issue rifle) is a rifle a military issues to its regular infantry. In modern forces, the service rifle is typically a versatile and rugged select-fire assault rifle or battle rifle suitable for use in nearly all environments and effective in most combat situations.

History

Originally, armies issued muskets and later rifled long guns that were produced in large numbers to a standard pattern. The adoption of metallic cartridges and magazine-fed bolt-action rifles in the late 19th century — typified by designs such as the Mauser Gewehr 98, the Lee–Enfield, and the Mosin–Nagant — created the modern concept of a standard infantry rifle. Semi-automatic rifles such as the American M1 Garand and Soviet SVT-40 saw widespread issue during the Second World War.

After 1945, the service rifle bifurcated into battle rifles chambered for full-power cartridges (such as the FN FAL, Heckler & Koch G3, and M14) and assault rifles chambered for intermediate cartridges (such as the AK-47 and M16 families). By the late 20th century, assault rifles had become nearly universal as service rifles, and many forces adopted bullpup designs such as the Steyr AUG, FAMAS, and SA80.

Characteristics

Modern service rifles are generally chambered in NATO-standard calibres such as 5.56×45mm or 7.62×51mm, or their counterparts such as 5.45×39mm and 5.8×42mm. They feature detachable box magazines, selective fire or burst modes, accessory rails for optics and lights, and furniture of polymer or alloy for durability and ease of maintenance. Programmes such as the United States' Next Generation Squad Weapon have sought replacements emphasising greater range and lethality.
