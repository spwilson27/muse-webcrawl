Electric power

Electric power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred by an electric circuit, measured in watts (W). For a component absorbing power, it is the product of the voltage across it and the current through it. Electric power is generated at power stations, transmitted over electrical grids and used for lighting, heating, motors and electronics.

P = V · I

Direct and alternating current

In DC circuits, power is simply P = VI, and for a resistor P = I²R = V²/R. In AC circuits, voltage and current vary sinusoidally; the average (real) power depends on the phase difference between them, expressed through the power factor. Apparent power is measured in volt-amperes (VA) and reactive power in var.

Generation and delivery

Most power is generated as three-phase AC, stepped up by transformers to high voltages for efficient long-distance transmission, then stepped down for distribution to consumers. Grid operators balance generation and load continuously to maintain frequency.

Units

Domestic consumption is billed in kilowatt-hours; large generators are rated in megawatts or gigawatts. One horsepower is about 746 W.
