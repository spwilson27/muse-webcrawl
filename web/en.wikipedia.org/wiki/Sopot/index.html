Sopot

Sopot (Polish: [ˈsɔpɔt]; German: Zoppot) is a seaside resort city in northern Poland, in Pomeranian Voivodeship, on the southern coast of the Baltic Sea. Together with Gdańsk and Gdynia it forms the Tricity metropolitan area. Sopot is known for its health spas, sandy beaches, and the longest wooden pier in Europe, and for the Sopot International Song Festival.

History

The area was settled by Pomeranian tribes in the early Middle Ages and later belonged to the Teutonic Order, the Kingdom of Poland, Prussia, and the Free City of Danzig. In the 19th century Sopot developed into a fashionable spa and bathing resort; the first spa house was established by Jean Georg Haffner in the 1820s. After the Second World War it became part of Poland.

Sights

The Sopot Pier (Molo), at 511.5 metres, is the longest wooden pier in Europe. Other attractions include the crooked house (Krzywy Domek) on Monte Cassino Street, the Forest Opera (Opera Leśna) amphitheatre, the Grand Hotel, and the lighthouse. The Sopot International Song Festival, once the largest music festival in Europe alongside Eurovision, is held at the Forest Opera.

Transport

Sopot is served by the Tricity SKM commuter rail and lies close to Gdańsk Lech Wałęsa Airport.
