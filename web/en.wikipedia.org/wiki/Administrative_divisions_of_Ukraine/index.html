Administrative divisions of Ukraine

Image placeholder: Ukraine's oblasts and major cities

The administrative divisions of Ukraine form a three-tier system of local government. At the first level are 24 oblasts (provinces), the Autonomous Republic of Crimea, and two cities with special status, Kyiv and Sevastopol. Below them are raions (districts), and at the base level are hromadas — amalgamated territorial communities created by Ukraine's decentralisation reform.

First-level divisions

Oblasts are named after their administrative centres: for example Lviv Oblast, Kharkiv Oblast, Odesa Oblast and Dnipropetrovsk Oblast. Each oblast has a state administration whose head is appointed by the President of Ukraine, alongside an elected oblast council. Crimea enjoys autonomous-republic status in the Ukrainian constitution, though it has been administered by Russia since the annexation of 2014, which Ukraine and most states do not recognise.

Raions

A major reform in July 2020 reduced the number of raions from 490 to 136 enlarged districts, each governed by a raion council and state administration. Cities that were formerly "of oblast significance" were folded into the new raions.

Hromadas

The decentralisation programme begun in 2014 merged thousands of villages and towns into about 1,470 self-governing hromadas, which received wider powers and a direct share of tax revenues. Elections to the new community councils were held across the country in October 2020.
