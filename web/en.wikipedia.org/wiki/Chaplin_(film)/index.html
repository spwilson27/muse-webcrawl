Chaplin (film)

Robert Downey Jr. as Charlie Chaplin

Chaplin is a 1992 biographical comedy-drama film directed by Richard Attenborough, based on the life of silent film star Charlie Chaplin. Robert Downey Jr. stars in the title role, in a performance widely praised for its uncanny recreation of Chaplin's moves and mannerisms; he was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actor.

Plot

The film is framed as the elderly Chaplin recounting his life to his editor while preparing his autobiography. It follows him from a childhood of poverty in London through his music-hall career, rise to fame in Hollywood as the Tramp, his marriages and scandals, his exile from the United States during the McCarthy era, and his eventual return to receive an honorary Oscar in 1972.

Cast

The ensemble includes Dan Aykroyd as Mack Sennett, Kevin Kline as Douglas Fairbanks, Anthony Hopkins as editor George Hayden, and Geraldine Chaplin—Chaplin's real daughter—playing her own grandmother, Hannah Chaplin.

Reception

The film received mixed reviews for its script but near-universal acclaim for Downey's performance and John Barry's score. It has since been reassessed as an affectionate, if conventional, tribute.
