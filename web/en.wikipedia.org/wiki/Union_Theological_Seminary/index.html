Union Theological Seminary

Union Theological Seminary in the City of New York (commonly shortened to Union Theological Seminary or UTS) is a private, ecumenical, liberal Christian seminary in Morningside Heights, Manhattan, New York City. It was founded in 1836 by members of the Presbyterian Church but is now open to students of many denominations and faiths. Since 2010, Union has served as the divinity school of Columbia University, although it remains administratively independent.

The seminary's Gothic Revival buildings on Broadway at 120th Street.

History

Union was founded in 1836 in response to a desire for theological education in New York City. It moved to its present Morningside Heights campus in 1910, in buildings designed in the English Gothic style around a quadrangle. During the 20th century it became one of the most influential liberal seminaries in the United States.

Notable people

Union's faculty and community have included theologians Reinhold Niebuhr and Paul Tillich, both of whom taught there for many years. Dietrich Bonhoeffer spent a fellowship year at Union in 1930–31 and returned briefly in 1939. James H. Cone, a founder of Black liberation theology, was a long-time professor. Other noted figures associated with Union include Henry Sloane Coffin, who served as president.

Burke Library

Union is home to the Burke Library, one of the largest theological libraries in the Western Hemisphere, now operated as part of the Columbia University library system.

Academics

Union offers master's degrees including the Master of Divinity, Master of Arts, and Master of Sacred Theology, together with the Doctor of Philosophy in conjunction with Columbia. It is known for emphases on social justice, interreligious engagement, and the study of religion in public life.
