Paul Tibbets

Paul Warfield Tibbets Jr. (23 February 1915 – 1 November 2007) was a brigadier general in the United States Air Force, best known as the pilot who flew the B-29 Enola Gay when it dropped the atomic bomb "Little Boy" on Hiroshima on 6 August 1945.

Career

Tibbets flew combat missions in Europe and North Africa early in the war, including leading the first US daylight bombing raid over occupied Europe in 1942. Chosen in 1944 to command the 509th Composite Group, he supervised its secret training for the atomic mission at Wendover, Utah, and on Tinian, naming the aircraft after his mother.

Later life

After the war Tibbets served in various Air Force posts and retired in 1966. He consistently stated he had no regrets about the mission, believing it shortened the war. He died in Ohio in 2007, having asked for no funeral or headstone to avoid protests at his grave.
