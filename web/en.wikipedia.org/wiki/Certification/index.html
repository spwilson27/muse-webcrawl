Certification

Certification is the formal attestation, usually by an independent third party, that a person, product, process, or organization conforms to specified requirements or standards. Certification differs from licensing, which is a legal permission granted by a government authority, and from accreditation, which recognizes the competence of the bodies that perform certification.

Professional certification

Professional or personnel certification demonstrates that an individual has met predetermined qualifications in an occupation, typically through education, experience, and examination. Examples include the Certified Public Accountant (CPA) credential in accounting, the Project Management Professional (PMP) credential, charterholder status from the CFA Institute, and vendor certifications in information technology. Certifications may be time-limited and require continuing education for renewal.

Product and management system certification

Product certification verifies that goods meet safety, quality, or performance standards — for example the CE marking in Europe, Underwriters Laboratories (UL) listing for electrical equipment, or organic certification for food. Management system certification assesses an organization's systems against standards such as ISO 9001 (quality), ISO 14001 (environment), and ISO/IEC 27001 (information security). Certification bodies are themselves accredited by national accreditation bodies under international standards such as ISO/IEC 17065 and ISO/IEC 17021, within a global framework overseen by the International Accreditation Forum.

Criticism

Critics note that certification can become a barrier to entry that protects incumbents, that fee-driven certification markets create conflicts of interest, and that possession of a certificate does not guarantee competence or ethical conduct. Nonetheless, certification remains a principal mechanism for signalling quality and compliance in complex markets.
