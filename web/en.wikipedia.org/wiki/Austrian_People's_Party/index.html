The Austrian People's Party (German: Österreichische Volkspartei, ÖVP) is a centre-right Christian-democratic and conservative political party in Austria. Founded in 1945, it is the successor — though not a strict legal continuation — of the Christian Social Party, and has been one of the two dominant parties of the Second Republic alongside the Social Democratic Party of Austria (SPÖ).

History

The ÖVP was founded on 17 April 1945 in Vienna by figures including Leopold Figl, who became Austria's first postwar Chancellor. The party won an absolute majority in the first free elections of November 1945 and governed alone or in "grand coalition" with the SPÖ for much of the postwar period. From 1970 to 1983 it sat in opposition to Bruno Kreisky's SPÖ governments.

Under Wolfgang Schüssel the ÖVP formed a controversial coalition with the far-right Freedom Party in 2000, provoking diplomatic sanctions from EU partners. Sebastian Kurz took the leadership in 2017, rebranding the party in turquoise as "the new People's Party" and winning the 2017 and 2019 elections; his government fell in the wake of the Ibiza affair of 2019, and Kurz himself resigned in 2021 amid corruption investigations. The party continued in government in coalition with the Greens.

Organisation

The ÖVP is structured around powerful constituent leagues (Bünde) representing farmers, businesses, workers and employees, women, youth, and seniors, giving interest groups a formal role unusual among European parties. It is traditionally strong in rural areas and in the western and southern Länder.
