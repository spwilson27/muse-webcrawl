Internal validity

Internal validity is the extent to which a piece of evidence supports a claim about cause and effect within the context of a particular study. It is one of the most important properties of scientific studies and is an important concept in reasoning about evidence more generally.

Description

A study has high internal validity when the relationships observed can be confidently attributed to the manipulated variables rather than to confounding factors. Random assignment of participants to conditions, as in a randomized controlled trial, is a primary means of achieving it. Internal validity is contrasted with external validity, which concerns how far results generalize beyond the study.

Threats to internal validity

Classic threats identified by Campbell and Stanley include history, maturation, testing effects, instrumentation changes, statistical regression, selection bias, experimental mortality (attrition), and interactions among these factors.
