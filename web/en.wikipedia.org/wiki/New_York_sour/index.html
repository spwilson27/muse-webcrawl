New York sour

The New York sour is a cocktail in the sour family: a whiskey sour topped with a float of dry red wine. It is typically made with rye or bourbon whiskey, lemon juice and simple syrup, shaken with ice, strained, and finished by carefully pouring red wine over the back of a spoon so that it floats on the surface.

History

Wine floats on sours date to the 19th century, when drinks such as the "Continental sour" and "Southern whiskey sour" were known. The drink acquired the New York name by the early 20th century, though some accounts place its creation in Chicago in the 1880s. The wine float — traditionally a Claret-style red, today often Malbec or Shiraz — gives the drink its distinctive layered red-over-amber appearance and adds fruity acidity.

Preparation

A common recipe is 60 ml rye whiskey, 22.5 ml lemon juice and 15 ml simple syrup, shaken, strained over ice, and topped with about 15 ml dry red wine. Some versions include egg white for texture.
