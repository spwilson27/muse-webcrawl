BritNed is a 1,000-megawatt high-voltage direct current (HVDC) submarine power cable between the Isle of Grain in Kent, England, and Maasvlakte, near Rotterdam, in the Netherlands. It runs for about 260 kilometres (160 miles) beneath the North Sea and links the British electricity transmission grid, operated by National Grid, with the Dutch grid operated by TenneT.

History

The project was developed as a joint venture between National Grid and TenneT. Construction began in 2009, with the cable manufactured and laid by specialist contractors, and the link entered commercial operation on 1 April 2011. The converter stations are located at Grain in England and at Maasvlakte in the Netherlands.

Technical description

BritNed is a bipole HVDC system operating at ±450 kV with a transmission capacity of 1,000 MW in either direction. The link allows electricity to flow between the British and Dutch markets, supporting price convergence, competition and security of supply, and helping both countries balance variable renewable generation such as offshore wind.

Outages

In March 2021 a fire at the converter station led to a prolonged reduction in capacity while repairs were carried out; the link returned to full service later that year. The interconnector, along with the other links between Great Britain and continental Europe, forms part of the wider European network of interconnectors that also includes IFA-2 and the IFA link to France and Viking Link to Denmark.
