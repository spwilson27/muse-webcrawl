Ottoman–Hungarian Wars

Datec. 1360s – 1526 (first phase); conflicts continued under Habsburg rule to 1699
LocationBalkans, Hungary, Croatia, Wallachia
BelligerentsOttoman Empire; Kingdom of Hungary and allies
Key battlesNicopolis (1396), Varna (1444), Belgrade (1456), Mohács (1526)

The Ottoman–Hungarian Wars were a long series of conflicts between the expanding Ottoman Empire and the Kingdom of Hungary spanning roughly two centuries, from the first Ottoman raids into Hungarian-held Balkan territories in the later fourteenth century to the catastrophic Hungarian defeat at Mohács in 1526, after which central Hungary passed under Ottoman rule for some 150 years.

Early confrontations

Hungary under Louis the Great campaigned against Ottoman forces in the Balkans in the 1360s–1370s, and the frontier hardened after the Ottoman victory over Serbia at Kosovo in 1389. Sigismund of Hungary led a great international crusade that the Ottomans crushed at Nicopolis in 1396. Thereafter Hungary maintained a defensive system of border fortresses (the végvár line) along the Danube and Sava, anchored on Belgrade.

Hunyadi era

The voivode John Hunyadi became the era's great anti-Ottoman commander, winning a series of campaigns in the 1440s, though the Crusade of Varna ended in disaster in 1444 with the death of King Władysław III. Hunyadi's finest hour came in 1456, when, with the Franciscan preacher John of Capistrano and a largely peasant army, he broke Mehmed II's siege of Belgrade — a victory still commemorated by the noon bell rung in Catholic churches. His son, Matthias Corvinus (r. 1458–1490), professionalised Hungarian defence with the standing Black Army and retook territory in Bosnia and Serbia, though his main ambitions lay in the west.

Collapse and Mohács

After Matthias's death the magnates dismantled much of his military system, and the frontier forts decayed under the weak Jagiellonian kings. Belgrade fell to Suleiman the Magnificent in 1521. On 29 August 1526, at Mohács, Suleiman annihilated the army of King Louis II, who died fleeing the field. Hungary was partitioned: the centre became an Ottoman province centred on Buda (1541), the east became the tributary Principality of Transylvania, and a western and northern strip passed to the Habsburgs as "Royal Hungary". Fighting between Ottomans and Habsburgs across Hungary continued through the Long Turkish War (1593–1606) and the Great Turkish War, ending with the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699), which restored most of Hungary to Habsburg rule.
