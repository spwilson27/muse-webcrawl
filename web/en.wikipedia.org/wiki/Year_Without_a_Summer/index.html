The Year Without a Summer was 1816, when severe climate abnormalities caused global temperatures to drop, destroying harvests across the Northern Hemisphere. The anomaly resulted mainly from the April 1815 eruption of Mount Tambora in the Dutch East Indies (now Indonesia) — the largest volcanic eruption in recorded history — which injected vast quantities of sulphate aerosols into the stratosphere. A second, unidentified eruption around 1808/09 and the eruption of Mayon in 1814 compounded the cooling, as did low solar activity during the Dalton Minimum.

Effects

In North America, frosts struck New England and Canada in every summer month of 1816 — snow fell in Albany and Maine in June — killing crops and earning the year the nickname "Eighteen Hundred and Froze to Death". Maize prices soared and many farming families migrated westward. Europe, still recovering from the Napoleonic Wars, suffered its last great subsistence crisis: grain prices multiplied, typhus and famine spread, food riots broke out, and the price of oats drove some to slaughter horses. In Asia, the monsoon was disrupted; flooding in China and famine in Yunnan coincided with crop failures in India and the spread of a cholera outbreak from Bengal.

Cultural consequences

The gloomy, rain-sodden summer of 1816 confined Lord Byron, Percy Bysshe Shelley, Mary Shelley and John Polidori to the Villa Diodati near Lake Geneva, where their ghost-story contest produced Mary Shelley's Frankenstein and Polidori's The Vampyre. Byron's poem "Darkness" was written that July. The spectacular sunsets caused by volcanic aerosols are often linked to the vivid skies in J. M. W. Turner's paintings. Economic distress also spurred emigration from Europe, and the high price of oats has been credited with inspiring Karl Drais's 1817 invention of the Laufmaschine, a forerunner of the bicycle.

Scale

Average global temperatures fell by about 0.4–0.7 °C (0.7–1.3 °F). The event is the classic historical example of a volcanic winter and is studied as an analogue for the climatic effects of large eruptions and nuclear war.
