H.D. (born Hilda Doolittle; September 10, 1886 – September 27, 1961) was an American poet, novelist and memoirist, associated with the early 20th-century avant-garde Imagist group of poets. She published under the pen name H.D., a signature given to her by Ezra Pound.

Life and career

Born in Bethlehem, Pennsylvania, Doolittle moved to London in 1911, where her work — spare, classically inflected free verse such as "Oread" and "Heat" — became central to the Imagist movement launched by Pound, Richard Aldington (whom she married in 1913) and others. Her first collection, Sea Garden, appeared in 1916.

After the First World War, which cost her a brother and shattered her marriage, H.D. formed a lifelong partnership with the writer Bryher (Winifred Ellerman). She underwent psychoanalysis with Sigmund Freud in Vienna in 1933–34, an experience recorded in Tribute to Freud. During the Second World War she remained in London through the Blitz and wrote Trilogy (1944–46), now regarded as a major modernist long poem. Her late epic Helen in Egypt was published shortly after her death in 1961.

Legacy

Long overshadowed by her male modernist contemporaries, H.D. was recovered by feminist scholarship from the 1970s onward and is now recognised as a major figure in modernist poetry, notable for her reworkings of Greek myth, her interest in psychoanalysis and esotericism, and her experiments in prose, including the novels Palimpsest and the autobiographical HERmione.
