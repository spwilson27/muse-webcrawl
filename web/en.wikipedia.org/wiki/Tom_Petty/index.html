Tom Petty

Tom Petty
Born: 20 October 1950, Gainesville, Florida
Died: 2 October 2017, Santa Monica, California
Bands: Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers; Traveling Wilburys

Thomas Earl Petty (October 20, 1950 – October 2, 2017) was an American singer, songwriter, and guitarist. He was the leader of the rock band Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers and a member of the supergroup the Traveling Wilburys, alongside Bob Dylan, George Harrison, Roy Orbison, and Jeff Lynne.

Career

Formed in Gainesville, Florida, the Heartbreakers broke through with Damn the Torpedoes (1979), featuring "Refugee" and "Don't Do Me Like That". Petty's solo album Full Moon Fever (1989) produced "Free Fallin'", "I Won't Back Down", and "Runnin' Down a Dream". With more than 80 million records sold worldwide, his catalogue also includes "American Girl", "Learning to Fly", and "Mary Jane's Last Dance".

Legacy

Petty was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2002 with the Heartbreakers. Remembered for his jangling Rickenbacker sound and his battles with record labels over pricing and artistic control, he died in October 2017, shortly after completing a 40th-anniversary tour, from an accidental overdose of prescribed pain medication.
