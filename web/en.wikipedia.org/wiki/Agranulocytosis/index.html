Agranulocytosis is an acute, severe reduction in granulocytes — principally neutrophils — in the blood, usually defined as an absolute neutrophil count below 0.5 × 10⁹ per litre. Because neutrophils are the body's main defence against bacterial and fungal infection, agranulocytosis leaves patients highly vulnerable to life-threatening infections and is a medical emergency. It is the most severe end of the spectrum of neutropenia.

Causes

Most cases are drug-induced: clozapine, antithyroid drugs (such as carbimazole and propylthiouracil), certain antibiotics, and cytotoxic chemotherapy are well-known triggers, acting either through direct marrow toxicity or immune destruction of neutrophils and their precursors. Other causes include autoimmune disorders, aplastic anaemia and other marrow failure states, overwhelming infection, and rare congenital conditions.

Presentation and management

Patients typically present with fever, sore throat, mouth ulcers and infections that progress rapidly; the combination of fever with severe neutropenia (febrile neutropenia) demands immediate broad-spectrum antibiotics. Management includes stopping the suspect drug, protective isolation measures, treatment of infection, and granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) to speed marrow recovery. Because of its clozapine association, patients on that drug undergo mandatory regular blood monitoring in many countries.
