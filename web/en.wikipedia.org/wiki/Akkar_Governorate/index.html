Akkar Governorate

The Akkar countryside in Lebanon's far north

Akkar Governorate (Arabic: محافظة عكار) is the northernmost governorate of Lebanon. It was established by law in 2003, when it was separated from the North Governorate, though it was only fully implemented in 2014 with the appointment of its first governor. The governorate consists of a single district, Akkar District, and its capital is Halba.

Geography

Akkar borders Syria to the north and east, the North Governorate to the south, and the Mediterranean Sea to the west. Its landscape ranges from a fertile coastal plain through rolling hills to upland areas reaching the northern Mount Lebanon foothills. The Nahr al-Kabir al-Janoubi river runs along much of the Syrian frontier.

Population and economy

The governorate has a mixed population of Sunni Muslims, Christians (mainly Greek Orthodox and Maronite), and Alawites. Agriculture — olives, citrus fruit, tobacco and vegetables — is the backbone of the local economy. Akkar is frequently cited as Lebanon's poorest region, and development agencies have long highlighted its deficient roads, water networks and schools. Since the outbreak of the Syrian civil war, Akkar has received tens of thousands of Syrian refugees, adding pressure to local services.
