Mina Harker

Mina Harker, whose journal forms a large part of the narrative of Dracula.

Wilhelmina "Mina" Harker (née Murray) is a fictional character in Bram Stoker's 1897 Gothic horror novel Dracula. A schoolmistress and later wife of Jonathan Harker, she is one of the novel's principal narrators: much of the story is told through her journal, letters, and the typewritten dossier she assembles for the group hunting Count Dracula.

Role in the novel

Mina begins the novel in Whitby with her friend Lucy Westenra, witnessing the arrival of the storm-wrecked ship Demeter. After marrying the traumatized Jonathan, she joins Dr. John Seward, Dr. Abraham Van Helsing, Arthur Holmwood, and Quincey Morris in the pursuit of Dracula. The Count attacks Mina in revenge, forcing her to drink his blood and forging a psychic link between them — a link Van Helsing turns to the party's advantage, hypnotizing Mina to track Dracula's movements as he flees back to Transylvania, where he is destroyed. Mina's intelligence, shorthand, typing, and mastery of railway timetables make her, in a real sense, the organizational center of the hunt.

Interpretation and adaptations

Critics often read Mina as a version of the Victorian "New Woman": professional, technologically adept, and intellectually formidable, yet devoted to traditional marriage. She has been portrayed on screen by many actresses, including Helen Chandler in Dracula (1931) and Winona Ryder in Bram Stoker's Dracula (1992), the latter adding a reincarnated-love storyline absent from the novel.
