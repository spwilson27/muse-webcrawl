Te Moananui-a-Kiwa Ngarimu

Te Moananui-a-Kiwa Ngarimu VC
Born: 7 April 1919, Whareponga, New Zealand
Died: 27 March 1943 (aged 23), Tunisia
Unit: Māori Battalion
Award: Victoria Cross (posthumous)

Te Moananui-a-Kiwa Ngarimu VC (7 April 1919 – 27 March 1943) was a New Zealand soldier of Ngāti Porou and Te Whānau-a-Apanui descent who served with the Māori Battalion during the Second World War. He was posthumously awarded the Victoria Cross, the highest award for gallantry in the face of the enemy that can be awarded to British and Commonwealth forces, for his actions at the Tebaga Gap in Tunisia.

Early life

Ngarimu was born at Whareponga, near Ruatoria on New Zealand's East Coast, on 7 April 1919. He was educated at Te Aute College, where he excelled at rugby and studies, and was working as a shepherd when war broke out. He enlisted in February 1940, joining C Company of the Māori Battalion, which was recruited largely from Ngāti Porou.

Action at Tebaga Gap

During the assault on Point 209 at the Tebaga Gap on 26–27 March 1943, Second Lieutenant Ngarimu led his platoon up the hill feature, personally destroying German machine-gun positions and holding the summit through repeated counter-attacks despite being wounded twice. He was killed on the morning of 27 March while beating off another attack. His leadership and example were central to the capture and retention of the position.

Legacy

Ngarimu's Victoria Cross — the first awarded to a Māori soldier — was presented to his parents by the Governor-General, Lord Freyberg, at a ceremony at Ruatoria in October 1943 attended by thousands. A scholarship for Māori university students, the Ngarimu VC and 28 (Māori) Battalion Memorial Scholarship, commemorates his name.
