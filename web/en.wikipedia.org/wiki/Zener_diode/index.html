Zener diode

A Zener diode is a type of diode that allows current to flow not only in the forward direction, like a normal diode, but also in the reverse direction when the voltage exceeds a well-defined breakdown value known as the Zener voltage. It is named after American physicist Clarence Melvin Zener, who first described the underlying quantum tunnelling effect in 1934.

Operating principle

In heavily doped diodes with breakdown voltages below about 5 volts, reverse breakdown occurs mainly by quantum mechanical tunnelling of electrons across the narrow depletion region — the true Zener effect. In diodes with higher breakdown voltages, impact ionization (avalanche breakdown) dominates, but such devices are still commonly sold as "Zener" diodes. Around 5.6 V the two mechanisms' temperature coefficients cancel, making such diodes useful as stable voltage references.

Applications

Zener diodes are widely used as shunt voltage regulators: connected in reverse across a load with a series resistor, they hold the output near their breakdown voltage despite variations in supply or load. They also serve in voltage reference circuits, overvoltage protection, clipping and clamping circuits, and random noise generation.
