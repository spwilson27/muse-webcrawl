Adrenaline

Adrenaline (epinephrine) is a hormone and Neurotransmitter secreted by the adrenal medulla (Adrenal gland) in the fight-or-flight response. It is also one of medicine's most important emergency drugs.

Actions

Within seconds adrenaline raises heart rate and contractility, widens airways, diverts blood to muscles, dilates pupils and mobilises glucose — preparing the body for action. It works through alpha and beta adrenergic receptors; noradrenaline is its close chemical and functional sibling.

Medical uses

Adrenaline is the first-line treatment for Anaphylaxis (by auto-injector or injection), a core drug in cardiac-arrest resuscitation, and an additive to local anaesthetics to prolong their effect. Side effects — racing heart, tremor, anxiety — mimic a natural adrenaline surge.
