Microcrystalline wax
TypePetroleum wax
Melting point60–93 °C (140–200 °F)
AppearanceWhite to dark brown, odourless solid
SourceDe-oiling of petrolatum / residual petroleum fractions

Microcrystalline waxes are a type of wax produced by de-oiling petrolatum, as part of the petroleum refining process. In contrast to the more familiar paraffin wax, which contains mostly unbranched alkanes, microcrystalline wax contains a higher percentage of isoparaffinic (branched) hydrocarbons and naphthenic hydrocarbons.

Properties

Microcrystalline wax is characterized by the fineness of its crystals — much smaller than those of paraffin wax — which gives it greater flexibility, elasticity, and adhesive strength. Its molecular weight is higher than paraffin wax, and its melting point is correspondingly higher, typically between 60 and 93 °C. It is more viscous when molten and binds oils, solvents, and other waxes effectively, preventing the sweating out of components in blended products.

Production

Microcrystalline wax is obtained from the residual fractions of crude oil refining. Petrolatum is de-oiled, and the resulting wax may be further refined by hydrotreating or clay treatment to remove colour and odour.

Uses

Microcrystalline wax is a valuable ingredient in cosmetics and personal care products (as the base for lipsticks and creams), in chewing gum base, in candles, in tyre and rubber manufacture (where it protects against ozone cracking), in precision casting (lost-wax processes), in adhesives and laminating, and in protective coatings for foods such as cheese. It is also used in sports equipment including skis, snowboards, and surfboards.
