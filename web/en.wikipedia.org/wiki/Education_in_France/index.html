Education in France

Education in France is centralised and organised by the Ministry of National Education, one of the largest public employers in the country. Schooling is compulsory from age 3 to 16 (instruction is obligatory to 18 in some form since reforms of 2019–2020), and public education is free and secular (laïque).

Stages

  • École maternelle (nursery school): ages 3–6, compulsory since 2019.
  • École élémentaire (primary school): ages 6–11, five years (CP to CM2).
  • Collège (lower secondary): ages 11–15, four years (sixième to troisième), ending with the diplôme national du brevet.
  • Lycée (upper secondary): ages 15–18, three years (seconde, première, terminale), ending with the baccalauréat, which grants access to higher education. Lycées offer general, technological and vocational (lycée professionnel) tracks.

Higher education

France has a dual higher education system: open-enrolment public universities, and the selective grandes écoles (such as École Polytechnique, HEC Paris and the Écoles Normales Supérieures), entered through competitive examinations (concours) after two years of preparatory classes (classes préparatoires). Degrees follow the LMD (licence–master–doctorat) structure of the Bologna Process.

History

The system was shaped by Napoleon, who created the lycées and the Imperial University, and by the Jules Ferry laws of 1881–82, which made primary education free, compulsory and secular. The 1905 law on separation of church and state entrenched laïcité in public schools, a principle that still governs rules on religious symbols in classrooms.
