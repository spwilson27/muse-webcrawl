Foo Fighters is an American rock band formed in Seattle in 1994. Founded as a one-man project by former Nirvana drummer Dave Grohl, the lineup now consists of Grohl (lead vocals, guitar), Nate Mendel (bass), Pat Smear (guitar), Chris Shiflett (guitar) and Rami Jaffee (keyboards). Drummer Taylor Hawkins joined in 1997 and remained until his death in 2022; Josh Freese served as drummer from 2023 to 2025.

Grohl created the band as his own project after the dissolution of Nirvana and the death of Kurt Cobain in 1994. The band's name refers to the foo fighter phenomenon reported by Allied aircraft pilots in World War II.

History

The self-titled debut album (1995), written and performed almost entirely by Grohl, was followed by a full touring band. The Colour and the Shape (1997) produced hits including "Everlong" and "My Hero". Subsequent albums include There Is Nothing Left to Lose (1999), One by One (2002), the double album In Your Honor (2005), Echoes, Silence, Patience & Grace (2007), Wasting Light (2011) and Medicine at Midnight (2021). In 2023 the band released But Here We Are, its first album following Hawkins's death.

Style and legacy

The band's sound mixes melodic elements with heavier ones, often building from quiet verses to loud, anthemic choruses. Foo Fighters have won numerous Grammy Awards, including for Best Rock Album, and were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2021, their first year of eligibility.
