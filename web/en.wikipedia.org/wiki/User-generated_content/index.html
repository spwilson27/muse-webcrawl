User-generated content (UGC), alternatively known as user-created content (UCC), is any form of content — text, images, video, audio, reviews or interactive media — publicly posted by users of an online system or service rather than by the service's owners. It became the defining feature of "Web 2.0" in the 2000s, when platforms such as YouTube, Facebook, Wikipedia and blogging services turned audiences into creators.

History

Precursors include Usenet newsgroups, bulletin-board systems, fan fiction and open-source software. The mass adoption of broadband, cheap cameras and smartphones in the 2000s removed the technical barriers to publishing; Time magazine marked the shift by naming "You" its 2006 Person of the Year. In the 2010s and 2020s, short-video platforms and the "creator economy" professionalised UGC, with some creators earning substantial advertising and sponsorship income.

Characteristics and issues

The OECD has identified three hallmarks of UGC: it is publicly available, reflects creative effort, and is created outside professional routines and practices. UGC offers diversity and immediacy but raises persistent problems: copyright infringement, misinformation, harassment, and the legal liability of platforms hosting it. Laws such as Section 230 in the United States and the EU Digital Services Act allocate responsibility between users and platforms, and large platforms employ extensive content-moderation systems. Marketers, meanwhile, prize UGC for its authenticity and routinely solicit it in campaigns.
