Morgan Freeman

Morgan Freeman (born June 1, 1937, in Memphis, Tennessee) is an American actor, producer, and narrator, widely regarded as one of the finest actors of his generation and celebrated for his distinctive, authoritative voice. Over a career spanning more than five decades he has received an Academy Award, a Golden Globe Award, and a Screen Actors Guild Award, among many honours.

Career

Freeman worked in theatre and on the children's television series The Electric Company in the 1970s before a film breakthrough in Street Smart (1987). He became a leading star with Driving Miss Daisy and Glory (1989), Robin Hood: Prince of Thieves (1991), Unforgiven (1992), The Shawshank Redemption (1994) — now among the most beloved films ever made — and Se7en (1995). He won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for Million Dollar Baby (2004), played God in Bruce Almighty (2003), Lucius Fox in Christopher Nolan's Dark Knight trilogy, and Nelson Mandela in Invictus (2009).

Narration and other work

Freeman's narration has become a genre of its own: he narrated the documentaries The Long Way Home, March of the Penguins (2005), and the science series Through the Wormhole, and the story of his career is intertwined with public causes including African-American history education and bee conservation on his Mississippi ranch.
