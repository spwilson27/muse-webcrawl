ONPG

ONPG (ortho-nitrophenyl-β-D-galactopyranoside) is a synthetic, colourless substrate for the enzyme β-galactosidase. When the enzyme cleaves ONPG, it releases galactose and ortho-nitrophenol, a bright yellow compound that absorbs strongly at about 420 nm. This colour change makes ONPG a classic tool for detecting and measuring β-galactosidase activity.

Uses

In microbiology, the ONPG test distinguishes bacteria that possess β-galactosidase from those that do not. It is particularly useful for identifying late lactose fermenters: organisms that carry the enzyme but lack the membrane permease needed to import lactose quickly, so they ferment lactose on conventional media only slowly or not at all. Because ONPG can enter such cells without the permease, these organisms test ONPG-positive despite appearing lactose-negative. The test is used, among other things, to help separate Shigella sonnei (ONPG-positive) and certain Salmonella subspecies from other enteric bacteria.

In molecular biology and biochemistry, ONPG hydrolysis is a simple quantitative assay for β-galactosidase activity — for example, in studies of the lac operon and in reporter-gene experiments where the lacZ gene reports on promoter activity (Miller assays). The related compound X-gal is preferred when a blue precipitate in colonies on agar plates is desired instead of a soluble yellow product.
