Awards and Decorations of the United States Armed Forces

The awards and decorations of the United States Armed Forces include various medals, ribbons, and badges awarded by the United States Department of Defense and the individual service branches to recognize heroism, meritorious service, achievement, and participation in campaigns. They are displayed on uniforms in a prescribed order of precedence.

Personal decorations

The highest military decoration is the Medal of Honor, awarded for conspicuous gallantry above and beyond the call of duty. Below it stand the service crosses — the Distinguished Service Cross (Army), Navy Cross, and Air Force Cross — followed by the Distinguished Service Medals, the Silver Star for gallantry in action, the Legion of Merit, the Distinguished Flying Cross, and the Bronze Star Medal. The Purple Heart is awarded to those wounded or killed in action and is one of the oldest American military decorations, tracing its lineage to George Washington's Badge of Military Merit of 1782.

Service and campaign awards

Service medals recognize participation in particular campaigns and theatres, such as the World War II Victory Medal, the Korea Service Medal, the Vietnam Service Medal, and the Iraq and Afghanistan Campaign Medals. Unit awards, including the Presidential Unit Citation and the Meritorious Unit Commendation, are granted to entire units for collective performance. Marksmanship, good conduct, and reserve service are recognized by their own awards.

Order of precedence and devices

Each service publishes an order of precedence governing how ribbons are arranged. Additional awards of the same medal are denoted by devices such as oak leaf clusters (Army and Air Force) and gold or silver stars (Navy and Marine Corps); the "V" device denotes valor in combat. Ribbons are worn on a rack on the chest, and full-size medals are worn on dress uniforms on ceremonial occasions.
