Field: Mathematics

Constant (mathematics)

In mathematics, the word constant conveys multiple meanings. As an adjective, it refers to non-variance: a constant function always returns the same value regardless of its argument. As a noun, a constant is a fixed, well-defined number, such as π, e, or the golden ratio, whose value does not change within a given context.

Mathematical constants

Celebrated constants include π ≈ 3.14159 (the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter), e ≈ 2.71828 (the base of natural logarithms), the imaginary unit i, and Feigenbaum's constant from chaos theory. Some constants arise as solutions to equations, others as limits of processes, and a few remain mysterious as to whether they are even irrational.

Constants in expressions

In algebra, a constant is a term that does not contain variables, in contrast to coefficients and unknowns. In calculus, differentiating a constant yields zero, and an indefinite integral introduces an arbitrary constant of integration. The distinction between constants and variables is context-dependent: a letter such as c may denote a specific constant in one discussion and a parameter in another, in the same spirit as the care needed to parse formal statements generally.
