Nepali language

Nepali (नेपाली, Nepālī) is an Indo-Aryan language native to Nepal and parts of India, and the official language of Nepal. It is spoken by roughly 16 million people as a first language and by many more as a second language, including large communities in the Indian states of Sikkim (where it is an official language), West Bengal (Darjeeling), and Assam, and in the Nepali diaspora worldwide. Nepali is written in the Devanagari script.

History

Nepali belongs to the Eastern Pahari group of Indo-Aryan languages and developed from the Khas language (Khas kurā) spoken in the Karnali region of western Nepal. Inscriptions in early forms of the language date from the medieval period. As the Gorkha kingdom expanded in the 18th century and unified Nepal, the language spread eastward and became the lingua franca of the new state; it was earlier called Gorkhali before being renamed Nepali in the 20th century.

Grammar and features

Nepali is a subject–object–verb language with postpositions, split ergativity in perfective transitive clauses, and an elaborate system of honorifics distinguishing levels of respect and formality. It has two grammatical genders, a rich set of conjunct verbs, and vocabulary drawn heavily from Sanskrit, with later loans from Persian, Arabic, Hindi, and English.

Literature

Bhanubhakta Acharya (1814–1868), who translated the Ramayana into Nepali, is honoured as the language's first poet (Ādi Kavi). Modern Nepali literature flourished in the 20th century with writers such as Laxmi Prasad Devkota, often regarded as Nepal's greatest poet. Nepali-language media, cinema, and music are centred in Kathmandu.
