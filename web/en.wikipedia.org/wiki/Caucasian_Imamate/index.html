Caucasian Imamate

The Caucasian Imamate (1828–1859) was a state established by the imams of Dagestan and Chechnya in the North Caucasus to resist the expanding Russian Empire during the Caucasian War. It united numerous peoples under Sharia law and Naqshbandi Sufi organisation.

Imams

The first imam was Ghazi Muhammad (1828–1832), followed by Hamzat Bek (1832–1834), and then Imam Shamil (1834–1859), the state's most capable leader, who built a regular administration with districts governed by naibs, taxation, and a standing force.

Fall

After the Crimean War Russia concentrated overwhelming forces under Prince Baryatinsky. Shamil surrendered at Gunib in August 1859, ending the Imamate, though resistance in the western Caucasus continued until 1864.
