Ammonium lauryl sulfate (ALS), also called ammonium dodecyl sulfate, is an anionic surfactant widely used in personal care and cleaning products. It is the ammonium salt of the sulfate ester of lauryl (dodecyl) alcohol. Commercial material is typically a mixture of alkyl chain lengths dominated by the C12 homologue, derived from coconut or palm kernel oil fatty alcohols.

Properties and uses

As a surfactant, ALS lowers the surface tension of water, producing abundant foam and effective wetting and cleansing, which makes it a common primary detergent in shampoos, body washes, bubble baths, and hand soaps. It is chemically analogous to sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS) but is somewhat milder and more soluble at low temperatures, and its ammonium counterion gives slightly different foaming and viscosity behaviour in formulations.

Safety

Like other alkyl sulfates, ALS can irritate skin and eyes at high concentrations, but it is considered safe for use in rinse-off cosmetic products at typical levels by regulatory review bodies. It is biodegradable under aerobic conditions.
