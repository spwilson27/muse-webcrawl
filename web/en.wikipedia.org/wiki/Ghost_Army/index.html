Ghost Army

The Ghost Army was the nickname of the United States Army's 23rd Headquarters Special Troops, a tactical deception unit of about 1,100 men that operated in Europe during the Second World War from 1944 to 1945. Its mission was to impersonate other Allied units in order to deceive German forces, using inflatable tanks and aircraft, sound effects, fake radio traffic and theatrical impersonation. The unit staged more than twenty battlefield deceptions, and its existence remained classified for decades after the war.

Recruitment and organisation

The unit was activated in January 1944 under the inspiration of British deception successes and advocates such as Ralph Ingersoll. Recruits were drawn heavily from art schools, advertising agencies and architecture programmes; among its members were the fashion designer Bill Blass, painter Ellsworth Kelly and photographer Art Kane. It comprised a headquarters company, the 603rd Engineer Camouflage Battalion (visual deception), the Signal Company Special (spoof radio), the 3132nd Signal Service Company (sonic deception) and the 406th Engineer Combat Company (security and construction).

Methods

The Ghost Army deployed rubber dummy tanks, artillery and trucks that could be inflated in minutes, powerful loudspeaker systems that projected recorded sounds of armoured columns and construction up to 15 miles, actors wearing the insignia of the units being impersonated who frequented cafés behind the lines, and operators who mimicked the "fist" (keying style) of real radio operators while broadcasting scripted traffic. Combined, these created "traveling road shows" that could suggest a force of 30,000 men.

Operations

From Normandy in June 1944 to the Rhine crossing in March 1945 (Operation Viersen), the unit carried out deceptions that drew German attention away from real troop movements, notably during the Brittany campaign, the siege of Brest, the defence of Luxembourg after the Battle of the Bulge, and Patton's crossing of the Rhine. Its work is credited with saving thousands of Allied lives.

Recognition

Declassified in 1996, the Ghost Army's story became widely known through books and a 2013 PBS documentary. In 2022 the United States Congress awarded its veterans the Congressional Gold Medal, formally recognising the unit's contribution; surviving members received the medal in a ceremony in 2024.
