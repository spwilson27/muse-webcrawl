Yama (Yoga)

In yoga philosophy, the yamas (Sanskrit: यम, "restraints") are ethical disciplines governing one's conduct toward others. In Patanjali's Yoga Sutras they form the first of the eight limbs (ashtanga) of yoga, preceding the niyamas (observances), asana, and meditation. Patanjali calls them the "great vow" (mahāvrata), universal and unrestricted by birth, place, time, or circumstance.

The five yamas

  1. Ahimsa — non-violence toward all beings, in thought, word, and deed.
  2. Satya — truthfulness.
  3. Asteya — non-stealing.
  4. Brahmacharya — right use of energy, traditionally continence or fidelity.
  5. Aparigraha — non-possessiveness or non-grasping.

Other lists and traditions

Other texts give longer lists: the Shandilya Upanishad enumerates ten yamas, adding compassion (daya), patience, fortitude, and moderation in diet. Jainism's five vows closely parallel the yamas, reflecting shared roots in ancient Indian ascetic traditions. In modern yoga teaching the yamas are frequently presented as the ethical foundation that makes physical and meditative practice meaningful.
