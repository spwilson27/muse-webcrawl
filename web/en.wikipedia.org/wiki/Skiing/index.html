Skiing

Skiing
Equipment: Skis, bindings, boots, poles
Olympic: Since 1924 (Nordic), 1936 (Alpine)
Governing body: FIS

Skiing is the use of skis to glide on snow. Long a practical means of transport in snowy regions, it is now a major recreational activity and competitive sport worldwide. Skiing is broadly divided into Alpine (downhill), Nordic (cross-country and ski jumping), and freestyle disciplines, alongside variants such as telemark skiing, ski touring, and adaptive skiing.

History

Skis date back thousands of years; rock carvings and preserved skis from Scandinavia and Russia are among the oldest, with finds such as the Rødøy carving (c. 3200 BCE) and the Kalvträsk ski (c. 3200 BCE). Skiing was essential for hunting and travel in northern Eurasia and was militarily important for centuries. In the 19th century, Sondre Norheim of Telemark, Norway, pioneered modern techniques and equipment, and Fridtjof Nansen's 1888 crossing of Greenland popularised the sport. Alpine skiing developed in the Alps in the early 20th century under figures such as Hannes Schneider, whose Arlberg technique spread internationally.

Techniques and equipment

Modern skis are composite constructions with steel edges; boots lock into bindings that release under dangerous loads. Alpine skiers control speed with carved turns; cross-country skiers use classic or skate techniques on narrower skis with free-heel bindings. Resorts use lifts — gondolas, chairlifts, and surface lifts — and groomed pistes rated by difficulty.

Competition

Nordic events featured at the first Winter Olympics in 1924; Alpine racing joined in 1936. The FIS organises World Cup circuits in Alpine, cross-country, jumping, freestyle, and snowboarding. Freestyle disciplines such as moguls, aerials, halfpipe, and slopestyle have expanded the Olympic programme since the 1990s.

Safety

Common risks include knee injuries (especially ACL tears), collisions, and avalanche exposure off-piste. Helmets are now widely worn, and backcountry skiers carry transceivers, shovels, and probes.
