Predictor@home

Predictor@home was a distributed computing project for protein structure prediction, operated by The Scripps Research Institute in La Jolla, California. Launched in 2004, it used the BOINC platform to harness volunteers' idle home computers to test algorithms for predicting the three-dimensional structure of proteins from their amino-acid sequences — a central problem in computational biology, with applications in understanding disease and designing drugs.

Overview

Predictor@home was among the first biomedical projects built on BOINC, the middleware developed at Berkeley for volunteer computing that also powered SETI@home. Participants downloaded small work units describing a target protein sequence; their computers ran structure-prediction algorithms — including methods developed at Scripps for the CASP (Critical Assessment of Structure Prediction) experiments — and returned candidate structures for scoring and analysis.

Significance and end

The project demonstrated that large-scale volunteer computing could contribute to the protein-folding problem and helped validate Scripps' prediction methods against known structures. After several years of operation and participation by tens of thousands of volunteers, the project wound down as the research group moved on; the protein-prediction field was later transformed by deep-learning methods such as AlphaFold. Predictor@home is remembered alongside projects such as Folding@home, Rosetta@home and the World Community Grid as part of the volunteer-computing era of structural biology.
