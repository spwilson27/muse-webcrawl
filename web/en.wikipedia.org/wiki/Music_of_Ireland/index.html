Music of Ireland

The music of Ireland encompasses a rich tradition of folk music — instrumental dance music and song, in both Irish and English — as well as classical composition and internationally successful popular music. Irish traditional music has remained vibrant through sessions, competitions and the diaspora, and has influenced folk music worldwide.

Traditional music

The core repertoire consists of dance tunes — reels, jigs, hornpipes and polkas — played on fiddle, wooden flute, tin whistle, uilleann pipes, button accordion, concertina, banjo, harp, and accompanied by guitar, bouzouki and the bodhrán frame drum. Musicians gather in informal pub sessions; organised group playing developed through the céilí bands of the 20th century. The harp is Ireland's oldest emblematic instrument, associated with itinerant harpers such as Turlough O'Carolan (1670–1738). The slow, ornamented singing style sean-nós ("old style") is the pinnacle of the Irish-language song tradition.

Comhaltas Ceoltóirí Éireann, founded in 1951, organises the annual Fleadh Cheoil competitions that underpin the tradition's transmission. The folk revival of the 1960s, led by groups such as The Chieftains, The Dubliners and Planxty, carried Irish music to global audiences.

Popular music

Ireland's showbands dominated domestic popular music in the 1950s–60s. From the 1970s, Irish rock and pop produced Van Morrison, Thin Lizzy, Rory Gallagher, U2 — one of the world's best-selling acts — Sinéad O'Connor, The Cranberries, Enya, Hozier and Fontaines D.C. Ireland is the most successful country in the Eurovision Song Contest, with seven victories, and the stage show Riverdance (1994) globalised Irish dance music.
