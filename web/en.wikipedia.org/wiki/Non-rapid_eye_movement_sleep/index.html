Non-rapid eye movement sleep

Non-rapid eye movement sleep (NREM sleep), also known as quiescent sleep, is, collectively, sleep stages 1–3, previously divided into stages 1–4. It is distinguished from rapid eye movement (REM) sleep by the absence of rapid eye movements, a lower metabolic rate, and characteristic patterns of brain activity on electroencephalography.

Stages

Under the American Academy of Sleep Medicine classification, NREM sleep comprises three stages. N1 is the light transitional stage between wakefulness and sleep, with theta waves replacing alpha rhythms. N2 features sleep spindles and K-complexes, and occupies roughly half of a typical night's sleep. N3, or slow-wave sleep (deep sleep), is dominated by high-amplitude delta waves; it is the stage from which a person is hardest to awaken and is associated with physical restoration, growth-hormone release and declarative memory consolidation.

Physiology

During NREM sleep, muscle tone is reduced but present (unlike the atonia of REM), heart rate and blood pressure fall, and many neurons of the brainstem and cortex fire in synchronised oscillations. Sleepwalking, night terrors and confusional arousals arise from N3, particularly in children. Through the night, cycles of NREM and REM alternate roughly every 90 minutes, with deep N3 concentrated in the first part of the night and REM periods lengthening toward morning.
