The Green Heart (Dutch: Groene Hart) is a relatively sparsely populated, largely agricultural area in the centre of the Randstad, the urban agglomeration of the western Netherlands. It is ringed by the cities of Amsterdam, Rotterdam, The Hague, Utrecht, Leiden, Haarlem, Gouda and Alphen aan den Rijn, and covers roughly 1,800 square kilometres of peat meadows, polders, lakes and small historic towns such as Woerden, Schoonhoven, Oudewater and Nieuwkoop.

Landscape

The Green Heart lies mostly below sea level and consists of reclaimed peat bogs divided into long, narrow plots by drainage ditches — the classic Dutch polder landscape. Characteristic features include grazing meadows, willow-lined canals, windmills, fortified towns of the old Dutch Waterline, and the shallow lake districts of Nieuwkoop, Vinkeveen and the Loosdrechtse Plassen, created by peat digging.

Planning policy

Since the mid-20th century the Green Heart has been a central concept in Dutch spatial planning. National policy has generally sought to keep the area open — as a green counterweight and "lung" for the surrounding cities — by restricting large-scale housing and industrial development within it and concentrating urban growth in and around the Randstad ring. The policy has been regularly debated, as population pressure, farming decline and infrastructure projects continue to erode the contrast between city and countryside.
