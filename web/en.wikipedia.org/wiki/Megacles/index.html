Megacles (Greek: Μεγακλῆς, c. 594 BC – after 510 BC) was an Athenian statesman of the aristocratic Alcmaeonid family, best known as the chief political rival of the tyrant Peisistratus and as the father of Cleisthenes, the reformer credited with founding Athenian democracy.

Family and early life

Megacles was the son of Alcmaeon, a wealthy Athenian who, according to Herodotus, had enriched himself through gifts from Croesus of Lydia. The Alcmaeonids carried a hereditary religious curse said to stem from the killing of the supporters of Cylon at the altars of the gods in about 632 BC, an event associated with Megacles' grandfather, also named Megacles, who had been archon at the time.

Political career

After the reforms of Solon, Athens was divided among regional factions. Megacles led the faction of the coast (the Paralioi), in competition with Lycurgus, leader of the men of the plain, and Peisistratus, leader of the men of the hills. Megacles at first allied with Peisistratus: he helped drive him into exile's opponents and gave him his daughter Coesyra in marriage. When Peisistratus refused to consummate the marriage in the normal way for fear of the Alcmaeonid curse, Megacles broke with him and joined Lycurgus to expel him a second time.

Peisistratus eventually returned with foreign support and ruled Athens until his death. His sons, Hippias and Hipparchus, later fell out with the Alcmaeonids, who went into exile and, with Spartan help under Cleomenes I, finally expelled Hippias in 510 BC.

Descendants

Megacles was the father of Cleisthenes, who in 508/507 BC carried through the constitutional reforms that reorganised the Athenian citizen body into ten tribes and laid the foundations of democracy. Through his daughter Agariste, he was also the grandfather of Pericles: Agariste married Xanthippus, and their son Pericles became Athens' leading statesman of the fifth century BC.

Namesakes

Several other members of the Alcmaeonid family bore the name Megacles, including the archon of 632 BC blamed for the Cylonian pollution, and a later Megacles, son of Hippocrates, who was ostracised in 486 BC.
