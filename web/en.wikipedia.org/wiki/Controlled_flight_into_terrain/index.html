Controlled flight into terrain
AbbreviationCFIT
DefinitionAn airworthy aircraft under pilot control is unintentionally flown into terrain, water, or an obstacle
Key mitigationsGPWS / EGPWS (TAWS), MSAW, crew training

Controlled flight into terrain (CFIT, usually pronounced cee-fit) is an aviation accident category in which an airworthy aircraft under the full control of the pilot is unintentionally flown into the ground, a mountain, a body of water, or another obstacle. In a typical CFIT scenario, the crew is unaware of the impending disaster until impact; there is usually no prior indication of mechanical failure.

CFIT accidents most often occur during approach in poor visibility near high terrain.

Causes

Contributing factors commonly include pilot error and loss of situational awareness, fatigue, poor visibility and adverse weather, non-precision approaches, mis-set altimeters, navigational errors, inadequate terrain awareness, and communication problems with air traffic control. CFIT most frequently occurs during the approach and landing phases of flight, often in instrument meteorological conditions.

Prevention

The introduction of the Ground Proximity Warning System (GPWS) in the 1970s — pioneered by engineer C. Donald Bateman at Honeywell — dramatically reduced CFIT accidents. Enhanced GPWS (EGPWS), marketed as part of the Terrain Awareness and Warning System (TAWS), adds a worldwide terrain database and GPS positioning, providing earlier and more accurate warnings. Following FAA mandates from 1974 (GPWS) and requirements in the 2000s for TAWS on turbine aircraft, CFIT fatality rates fell sharply, though the category remains a leading cause of hull-loss accidents in general aviation and in regions with less advanced equipage.

Notable accidents

Examples include American Airlines Flight 965 (Boeing 757, near Cali, Colombia, 1995), Korean Air Flight 801 (Guam, 1997), Air China Flight 129 (Busan, 2002), and the 2010 Smolensk air disaster involving a Polish Air Force Tu-154. The Flight Safety Foundation's CFIT reduction task forces have been influential in mitigation campaigns.
