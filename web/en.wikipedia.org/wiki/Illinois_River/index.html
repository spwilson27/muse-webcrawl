Illinois River

Length273 miles (439 km)
SourceConfluence of the Des Plaines and Kankakee Rivers, Illinois
MouthMississippi River near Grafton, Illinois
Basin sizeAbout 29,000 sq mi (75,000 km²)

The Illinois River is a principal tributary of the Mississippi River in the U.S. state of Illinois, about 273 miles (439 km) long. It begins at the confluence of the Des Plaines and Kankakee Rivers southwest of Chicago and flows generally southwestward through Peoria to join the Mississippi near Grafton.

Course and waterway

The river forms the core of the Illinois Waterway, a navigable system of canals, locks and dredged channels linking the Great Lakes and Chicago to the Mississippi River and the Gulf of Mexico. Major locks and dams maintain a 9-foot navigation channel for barge traffic carrying grain, coal and other bulk goods.

Ecology

The broad floodplain once supported rich wetlands and backwater lakes that were premier habitat for fish and migratory waterfowl. In the mid-twentieth century, water quality declined severely from municipal and industrial pollution; cleanup efforts since the 1970s have improved conditions, though invasive species such as silver and bighead carp now pose major ecological challenges.

History

The valley was a heartland of the Illinois Confederacy and an early corridor for French exploration. In prehistoric times it hosted Mississippian and Woodland cultures, and the river's importance for trade and transport continues today.
