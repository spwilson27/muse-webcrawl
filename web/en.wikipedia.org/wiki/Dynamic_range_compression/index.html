Dynamic range compression
Device: compressor
Key parameters: threshold, ratio, attack, release, knee

Dynamic range compression (DRC), or simply compression, is an audio signal processing operation that reduces the volume of loud sounds or amplifies quiet sounds, thus reducing or compressing an audio signal's dynamic range. Compression is commonly used in sound recording, live sound reinforcement, and broadcasting to make audio more consistent in level.

Operation

A compressor reduces gain whenever the input exceeds a threshold, by an amount set by the ratio control (e.g. 4:1 means a 4 dB excess is reduced to 1 dB). Attack and release times govern how quickly gain reduction begins and recovers; the knee shapes the transition. Limiting is compression with a very high ratio used to prevent clipping. Dedicated hardware units, guitar pedals, and software plug-ins all implement the process.

Uses and criticism

Compression evens out vocals, sustains electric guitar, shapes drum transients, and keeps broadcast levels consistent. Its heavy use in mastering — maximising average loudness at the expense of dynamics — fuelled the "loudness war" criticism of recordings from the 1990s onward, later mitigated by loudness normalisation on streaming services.
