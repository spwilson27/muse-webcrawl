Ignacio Zaragoza

Ignacio Zaragoza Seguín (March 24, 1829 – September 8, 1862) was a Mexican general and politician best known for defeating the invading French army at the Battle of Puebla on May 5, 1862 — the victory commemorated by the Cinco de Mayo holiday. Born in Spanish Texas at Bahía del Espíritu Santo (present-day Goliad, Texas), he became a leading liberal commander during the Reform War and briefly served as Minister of War under President Benito Juárez.

Early life and career

Zaragoza was born in Texas when it was still part of Mexico; after Texas independence, his family moved to Matamoros and later Monterrey. He joined the Nuevo León national guard in 1853 and supported the liberal Plan of Ayutla against the dictatorship of Santa Anna. During the Reform War (1858–1861) he rose rapidly through the liberal ranks, distinguishing himself in campaigns in northern Mexico and at the decisive battle of Calpulalpan. Juárez appointed him Minister of War in April 1861, a post he left in December to take command of the Army of the East facing the European intervention.

Battle of Puebla

After France, angered by Mexico's suspension of foreign debt payments, invaded in force in 1862, Zaragoza fell back to Puebla and fortified the hills of Loreto and Guadalupe. On May 5, 1862, his roughly 4,000–4,500 troops repelled repeated assaults by the larger, better-equipped French expeditionary force of General Charles de Lorencez, then regarded as among the finest armies in the world. His telegram to Juárez reported: "The arms of the nation have been covered with glory." The victory became a powerful symbol of Mexican resistance, though the French returned the following year with reinforcements and captured Puebla and Mexico City.

Death and legacy

Zaragoza did not live to see those defeats: he died of typhoid fever in Puebla on September 8, 1862, aged 33. Juárez declared national mourning, and the city of Puebla was renamed Puebla de Zaragoza in his honor. His remains rest in the San Fernando Cemetery in Mexico City. Cinco de Mayo celebrations in Mexico center on Puebla, while in the United States the date has grown into a broad celebration of Mexican-American culture. A statue of Zaragoza stands in his birthplace of Goliad, Texas.
