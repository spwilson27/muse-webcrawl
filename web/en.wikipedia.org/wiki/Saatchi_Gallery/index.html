The Saatchi Gallery is a London gallery for contemporary art, opened by advertising executive and collector Charles Saatchi in 1985. It has exerted an outsized influence on the British art world, most famously by promoting the Young British Artists (YBAs) in the 1990s.

History

Saatchi first opened the gallery in 1985 in a converted paint factory on Boundary Road, St John's Wood, showing American and European minimalists and artists such as Donald Judd, Cy Twombly, Anselm Kiefer and Richard Serra; its vast white spaces set a template for contemporary galleries worldwide. In the early 1990s Saatchi shifted to buying the work of young British artists en masse — Damien Hirst, Rachel Whiteread, the Chapman brothers, Tracey Emin among them — culminating in the era-defining Sensation exhibition at the Royal Academy in 1997, which caused a media storm and drew record crowds.

The gallery moved to County Hall on the South Bank in 2003, then in 2008 to the Duke of York's Headquarters on the King's Road in Chelsea, a 70,000 sq ft neoclassical building where admission to most exhibitions is free. In 2019 it registered as a charity, the Saatchi Gallery Foundation. Later exhibitions have ranged from "The New Cool" and Korean contemporary art to "Tutankhamun: Treasures of the Golden Pharaoh" (2019–20) and shows devoted to black British artists and street photography.

Influence

Critics have debated Saatchi's market power — his purchases could make careers and his sales break them — but the gallery is widely credited with turning London into a centre of contemporary art and for popularizing a spectacle-driven, media-savvy mode of exhibition-making.
