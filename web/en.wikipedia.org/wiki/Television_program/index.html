Television program

A television program (British spelling: programme) is a segment of content broadcast on television, ranging from a one-off special to a recurring series of episodes. Programs are the basic unit of television schedules and include dramas, comedies, news bulletins, documentaries, game shows, talk shows, sports events, and children's programming.

Formats

Programs are organized by genre and format. Scripted formats include the single play or television film, the episodic series, the continuing serial or soap opera, and the limited series (miniseries). Unscripted formats include news and current affairs, reality television, variety shows, and sports broadcasts. Episode lengths follow scheduling conventions — roughly 22 minutes for a half-hour slot or 42–45 minutes for an hour slot on commercial television, leaving room for advertising.

Production

Most programs are produced by independent production companies commissioned by broadcasters or streaming platforms, or in-house by networks. Production typically passes through development, scripting, casting, filming (single-camera or multi-camera), post-production, and delivery. A showrunner usually oversees a scripted series' creative direction and production.

Distribution

Beyond first-run broadcast, programs earn revenue through syndication, international format sales (selling the concept for local remakes), home video, and streaming licences. The shift to streaming in the 2010s popularized full-season "box set" releases and binge viewing, changing how programs are written and scheduled.

Ratings and reception

Audience measurement services estimate viewership, and ratings strongly influence renewal and cancellation on advertising-funded television. Programs are also honoured by awards such as the Emmys, BAFTAs, and Peabodys, and the most successful enter the cultural canon — series such as I Love Lucy, The Simpsons, and Breaking Bad are routinely cited among the greatest ever made.
