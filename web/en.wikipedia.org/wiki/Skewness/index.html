Skewness
Notationγ1
TypeStandardised third central moment

In probability theory and statistics, skewness is a measure of the asymmetry of the probability distribution of a real-valued random variable about its mean. The skewness value can be positive, zero, negative, or undefined. For a unimodal distribution, negative skew commonly indicates that the tail is on the left side of the distribution, and positive skew indicates that the tail is on the right.

Definition

The skewness of a random variable X is the third standardised moment, defined as γ1 = E[(X − μ)3] / σ3, where μ is the mean and σ is the standard deviation. A symmetric distribution such as the normal distribution has skewness zero. The sample version of the formula, with bias corrections, is known as the Fisher–Pearson coefficient of skewness.

Negative skew (left tail) and positive skew (right tail)

Interpretation

In positively skewed distributions the mean is usually greater than the median, which in turn exceeds the mode; the relationship reverses for negative skew, though these rules of thumb have exceptions. Skewness matters in finance, where asset returns often show negative skewness and fat tails, and in quality control and data transformation, where strongly skewed data are often logged or otherwise normalised before analysis.
