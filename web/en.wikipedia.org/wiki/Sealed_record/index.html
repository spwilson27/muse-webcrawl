Sealed record

A sealed record is a legal or official record — such as a court file, criminal record or adoption record — that a court or authority has ordered closed to public inspection. Sealing restricts access to the parties and authorised officials; the record continues to exist but is hidden from ordinary searches, and it may be unsealed only by court order.

Sealing criminal records

Many jurisdictions allow people to petition to seal records of arrests that did not lead to conviction, of charges that were dismissed, or, after waiting periods, of minor convictions, in order to reduce barriers to employment and housing. Sealing differs from expungement, which generally destroys or erases the record: a sealed record can typically be reopened for law-enforcement purposes or in later proceedings, whereas an expunged one is treated as though it never occurred.

Other contexts

Courts also seal documents in ongoing litigation (filings under seal), settlement agreements, juvenile proceedings, and adoption files, balancing public access to judicial records against privacy, safety and fair-trial interests.
