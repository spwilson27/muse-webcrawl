Economic equilibrium

In economics, economic equilibrium is a situation in which economic forces such as supply and demand are balanced and, in the absence of external influences, the values of economic variables will not change.

Equilibrium
Types: Partial, general, competitive
Key figures: Walras, Arrow, Debreu

Partial equilibrium

Partial equilibrium analyzes a single market, where price adjusts until quantity supplied equals quantity demanded. Alfred Marshall popularized this approach.

General equilibrium

General equilibrium, developed by Léon Walras and formalized by Kenneth Arrow and Gérard Debreu (1954), considers all markets simultaneously. The Arrow–Debreu model proved existence of competitive equilibrium under certain conditions and underlies the fundamental theorems of welfare economics.

Properties

A competitive equilibrium is Pareto efficient under the first welfare theorem. Disequilibrium can arise from price rigidity, shocks, or rationing. Dynamic and stochastic extensions (DSGE) are used in macroeconomics.
