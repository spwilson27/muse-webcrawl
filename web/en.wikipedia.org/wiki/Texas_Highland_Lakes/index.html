Texas Highland Lakes

The Texas Highland Lakes are a chain of six freshwater reservoirs on the Colorado River in Central Texas, northwest of Austin. Created and managed by the Lower Colorado River Authority (LCRA) between the 1930s and 1950s, the lakes provide hydroelectric power, flood control, and water supply for the region, and support a major recreation and tourism area in the Texas Hill Country.

The lakes

From upstream to downstream, the lakes are Lake Buchanan, Inks Lake, Lake Lyndon B. Johnson (Lake LBJ), Lake Marble Falls, Lake Travis, and Lake Austin. Together they extend for roughly 85 miles (137 km). Lake Travis, impounded by Mansfield Dam, is the largest and serves as the principal flood-control reservoir protecting Austin.

History

The dams were built after devastating Colorado River floods repeatedly inundated Austin, culminating in the flood of 1935. The LCRA, created by the Texas Legislature in 1934, completed Buchanan Dam in 1937 and the remaining dams over the following two decades.

Recreation and management

The lakes are popular for boating, fishing, and lakeside communities such as Marble Falls, Kingsland, and Lago Vista. Water levels in the constant-level lakes (Inks, LBJ, Marble Falls, and Austin) are held steady, while Lakes Buchanan and Travis fluctuate as storage reservoirs, drawing attention during Texas droughts.
