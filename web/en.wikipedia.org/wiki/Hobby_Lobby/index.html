Industry: Retail (arts and crafts)

Founder: David Green
Headquarters: Oklahoma City, Oklahoma
Stores: over 1,000

Hobby Lobby Stores, Inc., formerly Hobby Lobby Creative Centers, is an American retail chain of arts and crafts stores. Founded in 1972 by David Green in Oklahoma City, it has grown into the largest privately owned arts-and-crafts retailer in the United States, with more than 1,000 stores nationwide and tens of thousands of employees. The company is known for its conservative Christian corporate culture: stores close on Sundays, and Christian music plays in-store.

History

David Green opened the first Hobby Lobby in 1972, growing out of a miniature picture-frame business he ran from his garage with a $600 loan. The chain expanded steadily across the Midwest and South, and by the 2010s operated in nearly every state. The Green family also funds the Museum of the Bible in Washington, D.C., which opened in 2017.

Legal cases and controversies

Hobby Lobby was the named plaintiff in Burwell v. Hobby Lobby Stores, Inc. (2014), in which the U.S. Supreme Court held 5–4 that closely held for-profit corporations could claim religious exemptions from the Affordable Care Act's contraceptive mandate. In 2017, the company agreed to pay a $3 million fine and forfeit thousands of ancient Iraqi artefacts (cuneiform tablets and cylinder seals) that federal prosecutors said had been smuggled into the United States with false customs declarations.
