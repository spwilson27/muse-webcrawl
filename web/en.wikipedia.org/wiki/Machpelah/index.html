Machpelah

Machpelah (Hebrew: מערת המכפלה, "Cave of the Double Tombs") is a site in Hebron, in the West Bank, traditionally identified as the burial place of the biblical patriarchs and matriarchs. The cave and the field containing it were purchased by Abraham, according to the Book of Genesis, as a family burial plot. The large Herodian enclosure built over the cave is known as the Cave of the Patriarchs and is sacred in Judaism, Christianity and Islam.

Biblical account

Genesis 23 describes Abraham buying the cave of Machpelah and the surrounding field from Ephron the Hittite for four hundred shekels of silver, in order to bury his wife Sarah. Abraham himself, Isaac, Rebekah, Jacob and Leah were later buried there; Rachel, by tradition, was buried near Bethlehem.

The structure

Above the cave stands a massive rectangular enclosure built by Herod the Great in the first century BCE, with walls of huge drafted stone blocks. Inside, later additions include Byzantine, Muslim and Crusader-era features: the site has served as a church and as a mosque, and today contains the Ibrahimi Mosque. Cenotaphs mark the traditional tombs of the patriarchs and matriarchs.

Significance

Machpelah is considered the second holiest site in Judaism after the Temple Mount in Jerusalem, and Hebron is one of the four holy cities of Judaism. The cave itself lies beneath the prayer halls and is generally inaccessible.
