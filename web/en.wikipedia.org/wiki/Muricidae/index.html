Muricidae

Muricidae is a large and diverse taxonomic family of predatory sea snails, marine gastropod molluscs, commonly known as murex snails or rock snails. The family contains roughly 1,600 living species, making it one of the largest families of marine gastropods.

Description

Muricids are noted for their ornate shells, which often bear spines, fronds, varices and sculptured ridges. Many species are carnivores that prey on bivalves and other molluscs by boring holes through their shells with the radula aided by acid secretions; others scavenge or feed on barnacles and worms.

Tyrian purple

Species such as Bolinus brandaris and Hexaplex trunculus were the source of Tyrian purple, the costly purple dye of antiquity produced from a secretion of the snail's hypobranchial gland. The dye was a major industry of the Phoenician cities of the Mediterranean.
