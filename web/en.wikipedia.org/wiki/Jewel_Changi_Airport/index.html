Jewel Changi Airport

Location: Changi Airport, Singapore
Opened: April 17, 2019
Architect: Moshe Safdie (Safdie Architects)
Floor area: approx. 135,700 m²
Key feature: Rain Vortex, the world's tallest indoor waterfall
Cost: approx. S$1.7 billion

Jewel Changi Airport, commonly known as Jewel, is a mixed-use entertainment and retail complex at Changi Airport in Singapore. Designed by architect Moshe Safdie, it opened on 17 April 2019 and links Terminals 1, 2, and 3. Its centerpiece is the Rain Vortex, a 40-metre (131 ft) indoor waterfall — the tallest in the world — falling through the oculus of a vast glass-and-steel toroidal dome, surrounded by a terraced indoor forest, the Shiseido Forest Valley.

Design and attractions

The dome contains ten storeys (five above ground and five basement levels) housing some 280 shops and restaurants, a hotel, cinema, and attractions concentrated on the top-level Canopy Park, which includes hedge and mirror mazes, a canopy bridge with a glass floor, sky nets, discovery slides, and seasonal floral displays. Rainwater is harvested for the Rain Vortex and building cooling. At night the waterfall becomes the canvas for a light-and-sound show. The airport's Skytrain passes through the dome, giving transiting passengers a view of the Vortex.

Development and reception

Jewel was built on the site of Terminal 1's former open-air car park as a joint venture between Changi Airport Group and CapitaLand, at a cost of about S$1.7 billion, to strengthen Changi's status as a destination in itself and boost Singapore's appeal for stopovers. It drew some 50 million visitors in its first year and has won international design awards, including the Prix Versailles for its architecture.
