Precision agriculture

Precision agriculture (PA) is a farming management concept based on observing, measuring and responding to variability within fields and crops. Its goal is to optimise returns on inputs — seed, fertiliser, water, pesticides — while reducing waste and environmental impact, by applying the right input, in the right amount, at the right place and time, rather than treating whole fields uniformly.

Technologies

Precision agriculture relies on a stack of technologies: satellite positioning (GNSS/GPS) and guidance systems that steer machinery with centimetre accuracy, often using RTK corrections; yield monitors on harvesters that generate yield maps; soil sampling and electrical-conductivity mapping; remote sensing from satellites and drones (including NDVI vegetation indices); and variable-rate technology (VRT) that adjusts application rates on the move according to prescription maps. Farm management software and telematics integrate the data for decision-making, and autonomous machinery is an emerging frontier.

History and adoption

The concept emerged in the 1980s and 1990s as GPS became available for civilian use and yield monitors were commercialised, first for large grain farms in North America, Europe and Australia. Adoption has since spread to vineyards, orchards and livestock operations (precision livestock farming), though costs, data interoperability and skills remain barriers for smaller farms.
