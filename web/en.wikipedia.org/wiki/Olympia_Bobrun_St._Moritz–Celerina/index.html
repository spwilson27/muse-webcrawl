Olympia Bobrun St. Moritz–Celerina

The Olympia Bobrun St. Moritz–Celerina is a bobsleigh and skeleton track located in St. Moritz, Switzerland. It is the oldest bobsleigh track in the world still in use, and the only remaining track made of natural ice, rebuilt by hand each winter from snow and ice rather than being artificially refrigerated. The track runs from St. Moritz down to the neighbouring village of Celerina in the Engadin valley.

History

The track was built in 1903 and opened on 1 January 1904, following the founding of the Saint Moritz Bobsleigh Club in 1897. It hosted the bobsleigh events of the 1928 and 1948 Winter Olympics, both held in St. Moritz, and has hosted numerous FIBT World Championships. The Cresta Run, a separate tobogganing track, is located nearby.

Track

The track is approximately 1,722 metres long with a vertical drop of about 130 metres and 19 curves, including the famous Horseshoe curve and the "Shuttlecock" region near the finish. Because the track is sculpted from natural snow each season, its exact profile varies slightly from year to year. In addition to World Cup bobsleigh and skeleton races, the bob run is known for its taxi rides, in which guests ride between a professional pilot and brakeman.
