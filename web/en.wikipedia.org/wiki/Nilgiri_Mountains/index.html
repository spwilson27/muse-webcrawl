Nilgiri Mountains

LocationTamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka, India
Highest peakDoddabetta, 2,637 m (8,652 ft)
Part ofWestern Ghats

The Nilgiri Mountains (literally "Blue Mountains") are a range of mountains forming part of the Western Ghats in western Tamil Nadu, southern Karnataka, and eastern Kerala in southern India. At least 24 peaks rise above 2,000 metres, the highest being Doddabetta at 2,637 m near the hill station of Udhagamandalam (Ooty).

Geography and climate

The Nilgiris lie at the junction of the Western and Eastern Ghats and cover about 2,479 km². The range receives rain from both the southwest and northeast monsoons, giving it a cool, moist climate unusual for the tropics and supporting a mosaic of montane grassland and shola forest.

Biodiversity

The Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, designated by UNESCO in 1986, protects elephants, tigers, leopards, gaur, and the endangered Nilgiri tahr and lion-tailed macaque. Much of the native grassland has been converted to tea estates and plantations of eucalyptus, wattle, and other exotic trees introduced in the colonial period. The region is the traditional homeland of indigenous peoples including the Toda, Kota, Badaga, and Kurumba.

Tourism and transport

Ooty, Coonoor, and Kotagiri are popular hill stations. The Nilgiri Mountain Railway, a metre-gauge rack railway from Mettupalayam to Ooty opened in 1908, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Mountain Railways of India.
