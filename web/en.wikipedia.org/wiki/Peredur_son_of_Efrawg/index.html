Peredur son of Efrawg

Peredur son of Efrawg is one of the Three Welsh Romances associated with the Mabinogion. It tells the story of the hero's education and his glimpsing of a mysterious procession involving a bleeding lance and a severed head. Its parallels with Chrétien de Troyes' unfinished romance Perceval, the Story of the Grail have made it central to debates about the origins of the Grail legend.

Plot

Peredur is raised in the wilderness by his mother, who has lost her husband and six sons in battle and tries to keep him ignorant of arms. Encountering knights, he travels to Arthur's court, where he is mocked but vows revenge on the knight who insulted him. After training, he rescues a lady's lands, visits his uncle's castle — where he witnesses a strange procession of a youth bearing a bleeding lance and two maidens carrying a salver with a man's head — and later learns that the head was his cousin's and that the events relate to the vengeance he must take. In the Welsh version, unlike Chrétien's, there is no Grail; the story ends with Peredur avenging his cousin by killing the witches of Gloucester, who had caused the death.

Sources and parallels

The tale survives in four manuscripts, including the White Book of Rhydderch and the Red Book of Hergest. It draws on Welsh tradition about Peredur, a northern British hero, combined with themes from continental Arthurian romance. Scholars debate whether the Welsh tale derives from Chrétien, from a common source, or developed largely independently.
