Southwestern Athletic Conference

The Southwestern Athletic Conference (SWAC) is a collegiate athletic conference in the United States, founded in 1920, whose member institutions are historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) in the southern states. Its twelve members compete in NCAA Division I, with football in the Football Championship Subdivision (FCS).

Members and sports

Members include Alabama State, Alcorn State, Grambling State, Jackson State, Prairie View A&M, Southern, Texas Southern, Florida A&M and Bethune-Cookman. The SWAC sponsors championships in sports including football, basketball, baseball and track and field. Its football championship game and the Celebration Bowl berth, together with classics such as the Bayou Classic between Grambling and Southern, draw large crowds.

Culture

SWAC events are renowned for their marching bands — including Southern's "Human Jukebox" and Jackson State's "Sonic Boom of the South" — whose halftime shows and "fifth quarter" battles are central to the spectacle. The conference has produced notable athletes and coaches, including Walter Payton (Jackson State), Jerry Rice (Mississippi Valley State) and coach Eddie Robinson of Grambling, long the winningest coach in college football.
