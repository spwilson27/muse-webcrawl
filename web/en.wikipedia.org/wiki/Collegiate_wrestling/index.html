Collegiate wrestling

Collegiate wrestling, also called folkstyle wrestling or scholastic wrestling, is the form of amateur wrestling practised at the college and university level in the United States, and in modified form in American high schools and middle schools. Unlike the international styles of freestyle and Greco-Roman wrestling, the collegiate style emphasises control of one's opponent and rewards riding time and escapes, in addition to takedowns and near falls.

Competition

College matches consist of three periods totalling seven minutes (3–2–2). Points are scored for takedowns, escapes, reversals and near falls; a wrestler who accumulates more than a minute of net control time ("riding time") earns an additional point. A fall (pin) ends the match immediately. In dual meets, team points also reflect the margin of victory.

Governing bodies and championships

The NCAA sponsors national championships in three divisions, first held in 1928. Traditionally dominant programmes include Iowa, Oklahoma State, Penn State and Iowa State. The NCAA Division I championships are among the best-attended collegiate championships in the United States. Women's collegiate wrestling, long contested under freestyle rules, has grown rapidly and gained NCAA championship status.

Weight classes

NCAA men's competition uses ten weight classes from 125 pounds (57 kg) to the heavyweight class of up to 285 pounds (129 kg). Rapid weight cutting has been curtailed by NCAA weight-management rules introduced after wrestler deaths in 1997.
