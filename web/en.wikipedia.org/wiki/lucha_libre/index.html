Lucha libre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lucha libre (Spanish for "free wrestling" or "free fight") is the term for the style of professional wrestling originating in Mexico. It is characterized by the colourful masks worn by many performers (luchadores), rapid sequences of holds and manoeuvres, and high-flying aerial techniques, including dives out of the ring known as topes and planchas.

History

Professional wrestling was introduced to Mexico in the early 20th century, but lucha libre took its modern form in 1933 when Salvador Lutteroth founded the Empresa Mexicana de Lucha Libre (now CMLL, Consejo Mundial de Lucha Libre), the oldest professional wrestling promotion still in operation. Masked wrestlers such as El Santo, Blue Demon, and Mil Máscaras became national folk heroes in the 1940s–1960s, starring in films as well as in the ring.

Masks and apuestas

The mask (máscara) is sacred in lucha libre: losing it in a wager match (lucha de apuestas) is a profound dishonour, and unmasked wrestlers may never wear a mask again under the same identity. Famous apuestas matches have included mask vs. mask, mask vs. hair, and hair vs. hair contests. Wrestlers are traditionally divided into técnicos (fan favourites who follow the rules) and rudos (villains who bend them).

Style and rules

Matches frequently feature trios (tríos) contests contested under two-out-of-three-falls rules with team captains. The style emphasizes acrobatics, springboard attacks and intricate submission holds (llaves). Lucha libre has influenced wrestling worldwide, especially through promotions such as AAA (Lucha Libre AAA Worldwide) and the migration of luchadores to Japan and the United States.

Cultural status

In 2018, Mexico City declared lucha libre an intangible cultural heritage of the city. Its imagery — especially the mask of El Santo — is iconic in Mexican popular culture.
