Vasily Zaitsev (1915–1991)
Biography
Born:23 March 1915, Yeleninskoye, Russian Empire
Died:15 December 1991, Kiev, Ukraine
Allegiance:Soviet Union
Rank:Captain
Awards:Hero of the Soviet Union

Vasily Grigoryevich Zaitsev (23 March 1915 – 15 December 1991) was a Soviet sniper during World War II, credited with 225 kills from 10 October to 17 December 1942 during the Battle of Stalingrad, including 11 enemy snipers. Prior to Stalingrad he served as a clerk in the Soviet Navy, volunteering for front-line duty after witnessing the destruction inflicted on the city.

Stalingrad

Zaitsev, who had learned marksmanship hunting deer and wolves in the Ural Mountains, was assigned to the 1047th Rifle Regiment of the 284th Rifle Division. His skill with the standard-issue Mosin–Nagant rifle quickly drew attention; the saying attributed to him, that for the Soviet troops "there is no land beyond the Volga," became a rallying slogan. He helped organize a sniper school in the ruins of the city, training dozens of marksmen—his pupils, nicknamed zaichata ("leverets", or baby hares, after his surname from zayats, hare)—who were credited with several thousand enemy kills.

In January 1943 Zaitsev was blinded by a mortar round. His eyesight was restored by the famous ophthalmologist Vladimir Filatov, and he returned to the front, ending the war as a captain on the Seversky Donets and at the Dniester; he wrote two textbooks on sniping that influenced Soviet doctrine.

In popular culture

Zaitsev's story was popularized by William Craig's 1973 book Enemy at the Gates and the 2001 film of the same name, starring Jude Law, which dramatizes a duel in Stalingrad between Zaitsev and a German master sniper, Major König—a duel whose historicity historians have questioned. His scoped Mosin–Nagant rifle is preserved at the Battle of Stalingrad museum in Volgograd. He died in Kiev in 1991, days before the dissolution of the Soviet Union; in 2006 his remains were reburied, as he had wished, at Mamayev Kurgan in Volgograd.
