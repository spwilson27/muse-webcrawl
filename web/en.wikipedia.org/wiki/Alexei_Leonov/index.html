Alexei Leonov

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Leonov during the first EVA, 18 March 1965.

Alexei Arkhipovich Leonov (30 May 1934 – 11 October 2019) was a Soviet and Russian cosmonaut, Air Force general and artist. On 18 March 1965 he became the first human to conduct a spacewalk, exiting the Voskhod 2 spacecraft and floating in space for about twelve minutes at the end of a 5-metre tether.

The spacewalk nearly ended in disaster: Leonov's spacesuit ballooned in vacuum until he could not fit back through the airlock, and he had to bleed off pressure to squeeze inside. The mission's dangers continued — the automatic landing system failed and the crew landed manually, far off course in deep snow in the Urals, spending a night in the forest before rescue. A decade later Leonov commanded the Soviet half of the Apollo–Soyuz Test Project, the first joint US–Soviet mission, shaking hands in orbit with Thomas Stafford in July 1975.

Art and later life

Leonov was also an accomplished artist whose paintings of space, some created in orbit, were widely exhibited. He had been selected as the Soviet cosmonaut most likely to make the first crewed lunar flight before the Soviet Moon programme was cancelled.
