Quilombo

A quilombo is a Brazilian hinterland settlement founded by people of African descent who escaped from slavery, known as maroons. The term comes from the Kimbundu word kilombo. Quilombos ranged from small hiding places to large, organized communities with thousands of inhabitants, agriculture, trade, and their own political structures.

Quilombos were settlements of escaped enslaved Africans and their descendants in colonial Brazil.

Palmares

The most famous quilombo was Palmares, a federation of settlements in the captaincy of Pernambuco that endured for most of the seventeenth century and may have housed tens of thousands of people. Its last leader, Zumbi, was killed on 20 November 1695 when Portuguese forces finally destroyed the settlement; the date is now commemorated in Brazil as Black Consciousness Day.

Modern quilombola communities

Descendants of quilombo residents, called quilombolas, still live in thousands of communities across Brazil. The 1988 Constitution of Brazil recognized the land rights of quilombola communities, granting them entitlement to title over their traditional lands. The certification and titling process is administered by the Palmares Cultural Foundation and the land reform agency INCRA, though many communities still await full title. Quilombola culture, land rights, and political representation remain significant issues in contemporary Brazil.
