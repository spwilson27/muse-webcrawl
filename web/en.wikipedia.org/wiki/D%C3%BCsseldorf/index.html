Düsseldorf

Düsseldorf is the capital city of the German state of North Rhine-Westphalia and a major centre of business, fashion, and trade fairs. Located on the east bank of the Rhine at the mouth of the Düssel river, it has a population of roughly 630,000 and forms part of the Rhine-Ruhr metropolitan region, one of Europe's largest urban areas.

History

Düsseldorf was founded where the Düssel meets the Rhine and received its town charter in 1288 following the Battle of Worringen. It became the capital of the Duchy of Berg and, under Elector Palatine Johann Wilhelm in the late 17th and early 18th centuries, a flourishing baroque court city. Industrialisation in the 19th century made it an administrative centre of the Ruhr industries — the "desk of the Ruhr". Heavily bombed in the Second World War, it was rebuilt and became capital of the newly created state of North Rhine-Westphalia in 1946.

Culture and economy

The city is famous for the Königsallee ("Kö"), a luxury shopping boulevard along a landscaped canal; the Altstadt (Old Town), nicknamed "the longest bar in the world" for its hundreds of pubs serving the local Altbier; and its Rhenish Carnival. Düsseldorf hosts major trade fairs through Messe Düsseldorf, a large Japanese community — the largest in Germany — and headquarters of companies in telecommunications, advertising, fashion, and industry. The electronic-music pioneers Kraftwerk formed in the city, and the Kunstakademie Düsseldorf has shaped modern art from Joseph Beuys to Andreas Gursky.
