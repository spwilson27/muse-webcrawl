Neuralgia

Neuralgia (Greek neuron, "nerve" + algos, "pain") is pain in the distribution of a nerve or nerves, as in intercostal neuralgia, trigeminal neuralgia and postherpetic neuralgia. It may arise from irritation or damage to a nerve, sometimes without an identifiable lesion, and is typically sharp, shooting or burning in quality.

Types

Trigeminal neuralgia causes brief, electric-shock-like facial pain, often triggered by light touch, and is frequently associated with vascular compression of the trigeminal nerve root. Postherpetic neuralgia follows shingles (herpes zoster) in the same dermatome. Occipital neuralgia affects the back of the head; glossopharyngeal neuralgia affects the throat and ear.

Treatment

Management includes anticonvulsants such as carbamazepine (first-line for trigeminal neuralgia), gabapentinoids, tricyclic antidepressants, topical agents, nerve blocks and, for refractory cases, surgery such as microvascular decompression.
