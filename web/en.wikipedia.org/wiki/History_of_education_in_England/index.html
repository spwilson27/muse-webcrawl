History of education in England

The history of education in England stretches from medieval cathedral and grammar schools through church-run charity schools to a universal state system. For centuries, schooling was provided mainly by the Church and endowed grammar schools, reaching only a minority of children.

State involvement

The Elementary Education Act 1870 (Forster's Act) created school boards to fill gaps in provision, and education became compulsory in 1880 and free in 1891. The 1902 Act established local education authorities and expanded secondary schooling.

Butler Act and comprehensives

The Education Act 1944 ("Butler Act") provided free secondary education for all, organised in a tripartite system selected by the eleven-plus examination. From the 1960s, most areas adopted comprehensive schools. The Education Reform Act 1988 introduced a National Curriculum and national testing.

Recent developments

Recent decades have seen academies, free schools, tuition fees in higher education, and repeated curriculum reform, with education remaining a central political issue. See also Education in England.
