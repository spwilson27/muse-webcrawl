Majewski osteodysplastic primordial dwarfism type II (MOPD II) is a rare genetic disorder characterized by severe intrauterine and postnatal growth retardation, microcephaly and distinctive skeletal abnormalities. It is the most common form of microcephalic primordial dwarfism. Affected individuals are among the smallest people in the world; adult height is typically around 100 cm (about 3 ft 3 in).

Signs and symptoms

Features of MOPD II include low birth weight, progressive microcephaly, a high-pitched squeaky voice, distinctive facial features such as a prominent nose with a wide root, dental abnormalities including small or missing teeth, sparse hair, and pigmentary changes of the skin. Skeletal findings include short limbs, coxa vara and dislocated joints. Intelligence is usually mildly to moderately impaired. Individuals are at increased risk of cerebrovascular disease, including moyamoya disease and intracranial aneurysms, as well as insulin resistance and early-onset type 2 diabetes.

Genetics

MOPD II is caused by loss-of-function mutations in the PCNT gene on chromosome 21, which encodes pericentrin, a protein of the centrosome involved in cell division and the DNA damage response. The condition is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. Pericentrin deficiency disrupts mitotic spindle function, reducing cell proliferation and thereby body size.

Notable cases

The condition has been documented in a number of well-publicized individuals, including entertainers who performed as some of the shortest adults recorded.
