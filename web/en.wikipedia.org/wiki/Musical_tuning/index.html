Musical tuning

In music, musical tuning is the process of adjusting the pitch of one or many tones from musical instruments to establish typical intervals between these tones. Tuning is usually based on a fixed reference, such as A = 440 Hz. The term "out of tune" refers to a pitch or tone that is either too high (sharp) or too low (flat) in relation to a given reference pitch.

While an instrument might be in tune relative to its own range of notes, it may not be considered "in tune" if it does not match the chosen reference pitch. Some instruments, such as bells, have harmonics that deviate from the harmonic series, so their tuning involves compromises between partials.

Tuning systems

A tuning system is the system used to define which tones, or pitches, to use when playing music. In other words, it is the choice and number of pitch values which are used. Throughout history, numerous systems have been used:

  • Pythagorean tuning — a system in which all intervals are generated from the ratio 3:2 (the perfect fifth).
  • Just intonation — tuning based on simple whole-number ratios found in the harmonic series.
  • Meantone temperament — a family of temperaments that temper the syntonic comma to make certain thirds more consonant.
  • Well temperament — irregular temperaments in which all keys are usable but each has a distinct character.
  • Equal temperament — the dominant system in Western music since the 19th century; the octave is divided into 12 equal semitone steps.

Reference pitches

The international standard reference is A440, in which the A above middle C is tuned to 440 hertz. This was standardized in the 20th century, although historically pitch levels varied widely: Baroque performance commonly uses A = 415 Hz, and some modern orchestras tune as high as A = 443 or 445 Hz.

String instruments are typically tuned in perfect fifths (violins, violas, cellos, mandolins), in fourths (double basses, and mostly guitars), or in combinations of fourths and thirds. Tuning open strings to intervals other than fifths or fourths is called scordatura.

Altered tunings

Musicians frequently use tunings other than the standard for their instruments. Guitarists, for example, use open tunings and drop tunings (such as drop D). Composers have also specified unusual tunings for effect, as in some of the music of Béla Bartók.
