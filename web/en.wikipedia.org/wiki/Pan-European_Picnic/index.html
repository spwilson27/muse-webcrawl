Pan-European Picnic

Date19 August 1989
LocationNear Sopron, on the Austrian–Hungarian border
OutcomeOver 600 East Germans crossed into Austria; a key event in the fall of the Iron Curtain

The Pan-European Picnic (German: Paneuropäisches Picknick; Hungarian: Páneurópai piknik) was a peace demonstration held on the Austrian–Hungarian border near Sopron, Hungary, on 19 August 1989. The event was a pivotal moment in the chain of events that led to the fall of the Iron Curtain and the reunification of Germany: during a symbolic three-hour opening of a border gate, more than 600 East German citizens, who had come to Hungary on "holiday", fled across the border into Austria — the largest mass exodus from East Germany since the building of the Berlin Wall in 1961.

Background and planning

The picnic grew out of an idea discussed in June 1989 between Otto von Habsburg, president of the Pan-European Union, and Ferenc Mészáros of the Hungarian Democratic Forum. Local organisation in Sopron was led by the town's opposition groups; the picnic's patrons included Habsburg and the Hungarian reform-communist minister of state Imre Pozsgay. The plan was to open the old Bratislava road border gate symbolically for three hours and hold a small celebration on the frontier — a test, in Habsburg's later words, of how far Moscow's non-intervention would go.

Word of the event spread among the thousands of East Germans then vacationing in Hungary, spread by leaflets and word of mouth. The Soviet leadership did not intervene, and Hungarian border guards had instructions not to use force.

The crossing

On the afternoon of 19 August, as the gate was opened for the ceremony, hundreds of East Germans rushed through to Austria, pushing past the surprised guards; the Hungarian commander on the spot, Lt. Col. Árpád Bella, chose not to order his men to fire. By the end of the day, over 600 people had crossed. The exodus made world news, encouraged further escapes through Hungary in the following weeks, and on 11 September Hungary formally opened its border, allowing tens of thousands of East Germans to leave — a decisive step toward the fall of the Berlin Wall on 9 November 1989.

Legacy

The site is now marked by a memorial park with monuments and a piece of the border fence. Anniversaries of the picnic have been attended by German and Hungarian leaders, including Chancellor Angela Merkel at the 30th anniversary in 2019, who called it a milestone on the road to a united Europe.
