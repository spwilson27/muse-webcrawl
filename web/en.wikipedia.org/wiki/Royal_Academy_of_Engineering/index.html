The Royal Academy of Engineering (RAEng) is the United Kingdom's national academy for engineering and technology. It brings together Britain's most eminent engineers from all disciplines to promote excellence in engineering, advise government, and champion the profession's contribution to society. Fellows are elected and use the post-nominal letters FREng.

History

The Academy was founded in 1976 as the Fellowship of Engineering, largely through the drive of Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, who served as its Senior Fellow, together with leading industrialists and engineers who felt engineering lacked a national institution comparable to the Royal Society. It received a Royal Charter in 1983 and adopted its present name in 1992. Since 2012 it has been based at Prince Philip House, 3 Carlton House Terrace, London.

Activities

The Academy publishes policy advice on issues from infrastructure and energy to digital technology and engineering skills; runs programmes to support engineering education, diversity in the profession, and enterprise — including the Enterprise Hub for engineering entrepreneurs. It awards significant prizes: the MacRobert Award (since 1969, Britain's premier prize for engineering innovation, originally run with the institutions), the Queen Elizabeth Prize for Engineering (launched in 2011, a £500,000 biennial global prize administered by a separate foundation), and medals recognising research and public engagement.

As one of the UK's four national academies — alongside the Royal Society, the British Academy, and the Academy of Medical Sciences — it works with the engineering institutions grouped in the Engineering Council and with the National Engineering Policy Centre partnership.
