Automatic identification system

The automatic identification system (AIS) is an automatic tracking system that uses transceivers on ships and is used by vessel traffic services. AIS information supplements marine radar, which remains the primary method of collision avoidance for water transport.

When satellites are used to receive AIS signatures, the term Satellite-AIS (S-AIS) is used. AIS information includes a unique identification, position, course, and speed, and can be displayed on a screen or an electronic chart display and information system (ECDIS).

Overview

AIS is intended to help a vessel's watchstanding officers and allow maritime authorities to track and monitor vessel movements. AIS integrates a standardized VHF transceiver with a positioning system such as a GPS receiver, with other electronic navigation sensors, such as a gyrocompass or rate of turn indicator. Vessels fitted with AIS transceivers can be tracked by AIS base stations located along coastlines or, when out of range of terrestrial networks, through satellites fitted with special AIS receivers.

Requirements

Channels161.975 and 162.025 MHz
RequiredShips ≥300 gross tonnage (international voyages); all passenger ships
DataMMSI, position, course, speed, destination
RangeAbout 20–40 nautical miles (terrestrial)

The International Maritime Organization's SOLAS convention requires AIS to be fitted aboard international voyaging ships with 300 or more gross tonnage, and all passenger ships regardless of size. Ships broadcast static information (name, call sign, dimensions, type), dynamic information (position, speed over ground, course, heading, rate of turn) and voyage-related information (destination, estimated time of arrival, draught).

Uses and misuse

Shore authorities use AIS for traffic management, search and rescue, accident investigation and monitoring of fishing activity. Public websites aggregate AIS data into live maps of world shipping. Because AIS is unencrypted, vessels engaged in illegal fishing or sanctions evasion sometimes switch it off or broadcast false identities, a practice known as "going dark" or identity spoofing.
