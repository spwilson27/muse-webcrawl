Freeminer

A freeminer is a customary miner of the Forest of Dean in Gloucestershire, England, who by ancient right may mine coal, iron ore, and stone within the Forest. The rights, traditionally said to have been granted by Edward I in recognition of the miners' service at the siege of Berwick, were confirmed by statute in the Dean Forest (Mines) Act 1838 and are still exercised today.

The custom

By custom, a person qualifies as a freeminer if born and bred within the Hundred of St Briavels, aged at least 21, and having worked for a year and a day in a coal or iron mine within the Hundred. Eligible miners register with the Deputy Gaveller, the Crown's officer, and may be granted a "gale" — an area of minerals to work — paying a rent and royalties. The freeminers' distinctive brass, bearing the arms of the freeminers, is awarded on registration.

History

Dean's iron and coal workings date to Roman times and before. Freeminers tunnelled for the Crown, including famously undermining Berwick Castle in 1296. Although large collieries dominated production in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, small freeminer pits continued, and a small number of working gales remain active, preserving one of Britain's oldest industrial customs.
