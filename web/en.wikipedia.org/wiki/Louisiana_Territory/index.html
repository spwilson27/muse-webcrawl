The Territory of Louisiana or Louisiana Territory was an organized incorporated territory of the United States that existed from July 4, 1805, until June 4, 1812, when it was renamed the Missouri Territory. It was formed from the District of Louisiana, which consisted of the portion of the Louisiana Purchase north of the 33rd parallel (the northern boundary of the modern state of Arkansas).

History

The Louisiana Purchase of 1803 transferred a vast territory from France to the United States. Congress initially divided it at the 33rd parallel: the south became the Territory of Orleans and the north the District of Louisiana, temporarily attached to the Indiana Territory. On March 3, 1805, Congress passed legislation organizing the district as the Territory of Louisiana, effective July 4, 1805, with its seat of government at St. Louis.

The first territorial governor was James Wilkinson, who was succeeded by Meriwether Lewis (1807), the explorer, who died in 1809, and then by Benjamin Howard and William Clark. The territory encompassed most of the modern states of Missouri, Arkansas (in part), Iowa, Minnesota, Kansas, Nebraska, the Dakotas, Montana, Wyoming, Colorado (in part) and Oklahoma (in part).

Renaming

When the Territory of Orleans was admitted to the Union as the State of Louisiana on April 30, 1812, the remaining territory was renamed the Missouri Territory on June 4, 1812, to avoid confusion with the new state.
