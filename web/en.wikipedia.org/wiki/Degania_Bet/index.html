Degania Bet (Hebrew: דְּגַנְיָה ב׳) is a kibbutz in northern Israel, located just south of the Sea of Galilee near the Jordan River, in the jurisdiction of the Emek HaYarden Regional Council. It was founded in 1920 by pioneers of the Second Aliyah, many of them from the town of Romny in Ukraine, who had first worked at neighbouring Degania Alef — the original kvutza founded in 1910 and regarded as the first kibbutz.

History

The settlers of Degania Bet drained and farmed the fertile Jordan Valley land, planting bananas, citrus, and field crops in the hot, humid climate. During the 1948 Arab–Israeli War the Deganias stood in the path of the Syrian advance into the valley; Syrian tanks were stopped at Degania Alef in May 1948, one of the emblematic defensive battles of the war. A destroyed Syrian tank remains as a memorial at the kibbutz gate there.

Economy and life

Like many kibbutzim, Degania Bet has moved from pure agriculture toward industry, tourism, and services, and has adopted reforms differentiating wages and privatising some services while retaining communal institutions. Its economy has included agriculture, a factory producing agricultural sprayers (Degania Sprayers), and guest facilities serving visitors to the Sea of Galilee region.

Notable residents

The kibbutz was the lifelong home of Michael Kolganov's predecessors in the Degania tradition of communal education; among its best-known members was the songwriter and cultural figure associated with the Jordan Valley settlements. The kibbutz cemetery and the nearby Beit Gordon natural history museum in Degania Alef preserve the history of the valley's settlement.
