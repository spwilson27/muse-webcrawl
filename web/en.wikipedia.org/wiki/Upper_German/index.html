Upper German

Upper German (German: Oberdeutsch) is a family of High German dialects spoken in the southern parts of the German-speaking area, including southern Germany (Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg), Austria, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, and northern Italy (South Tyrol). Together with Central German, it constitutes the High German group, defined by the effects of the High German consonant shift.

Classification

Upper German is conventionally divided into two main groups: Alemannic, spoken in southwestern Germany, Switzerland (Swiss German), Alsace, Liechtenstein, and western Austria (Vorarlberg); and Bavarian (Austro-Bavarian), spoken in Bavaria, most of Austria, and South Tyrol. East Franconian, spoken around Nuremberg and Würzburg, is a transitional group between Upper and Central German and is sometimes classified with either. The nearly extinct Lombardic language and the Cimbrian and Mòcheno enclaves in Italy are also Upper German varieties.

Features

Upper German dialects carried the High German consonant shift to its fullest extent: the voiceless stops /p, t, k/ were shifted in virtually all positions (compare Standard German Pfund, essen, machen). The Benrath line (the machen/maken isogloss) separates High German from Low German, while the Speyer line separates Upper from Central German. Many Upper German varieties use the diminutive suffixes -lein, -le, -erl, or -li and have distinctive vowel systems preserved from Middle High German.
