A unified combatant command, also referred to as a combatant command (CCMD), is a joint military command of the United States Department of Defense composed of forces from at least two military departments — Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Space Force and Coast Guard — with a broad and continuing mission. Its commander, a four-star officer, exercises combatant command (COCOM) authority over assigned forces.

Organisation

Commands are either geographic, with an area of responsibility covering part of the globe — Northern, Southern, European, Indo-Pacific, Central, Africa and Space Commands — or functional, organised around a capability: Strategic, Transportation, Special Operations and Cyber Commands. The structure is set out in the Unified Command Plan, periodically reviewed and signed by the President.

History

The first unified commands were created after the Second World War under the National Security Act of 1947. The system was greatly strengthened by the Goldwater–Nichols Act of 1986, which clarified that the chain of command runs from the President through the Secretary of Defense to the combatant commanders, and enhanced the authority of the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. The newest command, United States Space Command, was re-established in 2019.
