Versine

Notationversin θ, vers θ
Definitionversin θ = 1 − cos θ
RelatedHaversine, coversine, exsecant

The versine or versed sine is a trigonometric function found in some of the earliest trigonometric tables. It is defined as versin(θ) = 1 − cos(θ), equal to twice the square of the sine of the half-angle and to the sagitta — the height of a circular segment — for a unit circle.

History and uses

The versine (from the Latin sinus versus, "flipped sine") was one of a family of "secondary" trigonometric functions, along with the coversine (1 − sin θ), the haversine (half the versine) and the exsecant, that were tabulated extensively because they simplified hand computation. The haversine in particular became central to navigation through the haversine formula for great-circle distances. In civil engineering, the versine is still used in railway track surveying to measure the deviation of a curve (its "versine") from a straight chord when checking track alignment.

Decline

With the spread of electronic calculators and computers, the versine and its relatives fell out of general use, since 1 − cos θ can be computed directly. They survive mainly in historical tables, surveying practice, and the haversine formula embedded in many geographic software libraries.
