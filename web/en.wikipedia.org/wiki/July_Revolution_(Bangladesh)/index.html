The July Revolution, also known as the July Mass Uprising or the Student–People's Uprising, was a mass protest movement in Bangladesh in 2024 that culminated in the overthrow of Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina and her Awami League government. What began in early July as a student movement demanding reform of quotas in government jobs escalated, after a violent state crackdown, into a nationwide uprising whose single demand became the resignation of the government. On 5 August 2024, after weeks of protests in which hundreds were killed, Hasina resigned and fled to India, and an interim government headed by Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus took office three days later. Background

Sheikh Hasina's Awami League had governed Bangladesh since 2009, winning elections in 2014, 2018 and January 2024 that the opposition largely boycotted or denounced as rigged. Critics accused the government of authoritarian drift, enforced disappearances, suppression of the press, and corruption, while inflation and youth unemployment fuelled discontent. In June 2024 the High Court reinstated a quota system reserving 30 per cent of civil service jobs for descendants of freedom fighters of the 1971 Liberation War — a system the government had abolished after student protests in 2018.

Quota reform protests

Students, coordinated under the banner Students Against Discrimination, began demonstrations on 1 July 2024 demanding that quotas be replaced largely by merit-based recruitment. Protests spread across universities and cities in mid-July; after Hasina appeared to dismiss the protesters as "Razakars" — a term for collaborators in 1971 — demonstrations intensified.

Crackdown and escalation

From 16 July, police, the Rapid Action Battalion, Border Guard Bangladesh, and ruling-party activists attacked the protesters. Student Abu Sayed was shot dead by police in Rangpur on 16 July, an image that became an emblem of the movement. The government imposed curfews, shut down the internet, and deployed the army. A United Nations investigation later estimated that up to 1,400 people may have been killed in the unrest, the great majority by state security forces. The Supreme Court scaled back the quotas on 21 July, but by then the movement's demands had expanded to justice for the killings and the government's resignation.

One-point movement and fall of the government

In early August the movement consolidated around a "one-point" demand: Hasina's resignation. On 4 August renewed clashes left scores dead. On 5 August, huge crowds marched on Dhaka in the "Long March to Dhaka"; the army chief, General Waker-uz-Zaman, announced that Hasina had resigned, and she fled by helicopter to India. Celebrating crowds occupied the prime minister's residence and parliament. The president dissolved parliament, and on 8 August an interim government was sworn in with Muhammad Yunus as Chief Adviser.

Aftermath

The interim government pledged constitutional and institutional reform, established reform commissions, and promised fresh elections. Hasina, in exile in India, faced charges including crimes against humanity before Bangladesh's International Crimes Tribunal in connection with the crackdown. In November 2025 the tribunal sentenced her to death in absentia for crimes against humanity — a verdict she rejected as politically motivated. The events are commemorated in Bangladesh as the July Revolution, and the movement's charter proposals — often called the "July Charter" — became central to debates over the country's political future.
