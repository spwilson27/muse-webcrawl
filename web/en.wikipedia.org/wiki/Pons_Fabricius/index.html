The Pons Fabricius (Italian: Ponte Fabricio), also known as the Ponte dei Quattro Capi, is the oldest Roman bridge in Rome, Italy, still existing in its original state. Built in 62 BC, it spans half of the Tiber River, from the Campus Martius on the east side to Tiber Island in the middle. The Pons Cestius is situated on the western side of the island.

History

According to the historian Cassius Dio, the bridge was built in 62 BC to replace an earlier wooden bridge destroyed by fire. It was commissioned by Lucius Fabricius, the curator viarum (superintendent of the roads) that year, whose name is inscribed several times on the bridge in Latin: L · FABRICIVS · C · F · CVR · VIAR | FACIVNDVM · COERAVIT ("Lucius Fabricius, son of Gaius, superintendent of the roads, took care of its construction").

An inscription added later records a restoration under the Emperor Augustus in 21 BC, carried out by the consuls Quintus Lepidus and Marcus Lollius, after a flood.

Description

The bridge is 62 metres long and 5.5 metres wide. It is constructed from cores of tuff faced with travertine, with two wide arches supported by a central pier. Its parapet bears four-faced herms of Janus, which give the bridge its Italian name Ponte dei Quattro Capi ("bridge of the four heads"). The bridge has remained in continuous use for more than two thousand years and today carries pedestrians only.
