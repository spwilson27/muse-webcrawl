Mifepristone

Mifepristone
Trade names: Mifeprex, Mifegyne
Other names: RU-486
Type: Progesterone receptor antagonist
Routes: By mouth

Mifepristone, sold under the brand names Mifeprex and Mifegyne among others, and also known by its developmental code name RU-486, is a medication typically used in combination with misoprostol to bring about a medical abortion during pregnancy. This combination is effective in more than 95% of cases during the first 63 days of pregnancy. It is also effective in the second trimester and, at lower doses, as an emergency contraceptive. At higher doses it is used to treat high blood sugar in people with Cushing's syndrome.

Mechanism

Mifepristone is a steroidal antiprogestogen: it competitively blocks the progesterone receptor, causing the breakdown of the uterine lining, softening and dilation of the cervix, and detachment of the embryo. It also acts as an antiglucocorticoid, blocking cortisol receptors, which accounts for its use in Cushing's syndrome.

History

The compound was synthesized in 1980 at the French pharmaceutical company Roussel-Uclaf by a team led by Georges Teutsch, and its abortion-inducing properties were investigated by endocrinologist Étienne-Émile Baulieu. France approved it in 1988; it was approved in the United States in 2000. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Society and legal status

Mifepristone has been the subject of sustained political and legal controversy since its introduction because of its use in abortion. Its availability, prescription rules and distribution vary widely by country and, in the United States, have been contested in litigation over the authority of the Food and Drug Administration.
