Trochotron

A trochotron is a type of beam-switching vacuum tube used as a decade counter and selector in early electronic equipment. Developed in Sweden in the 1940s by Fritz H. H. Lindberg and manufactured commercially by LM Ericsson and, in the United States, by Burroughs (as the Beam-X switch), it directs an electron beam onto one of ten targets arranged in a circle.

Operation

Inside the tube, a cylindrical magnet and shaped electrodes create crossed electric and magnetic fields. The electron beam travels in a trochoidal path and locks onto one of ten "spade" electrodes. Input pulses advance the beam step by step to successive targets, so the tube counts pulses in decimal and provides ten outputs, one per digit. Unlike the dekatron, a gas-filled counter tube, the trochotron is a high-vacuum device and can switch at much higher speeds, up to the megahertz range.

Use

Trochotrons were used in telephone exchanges, frequency counters, and early computing and instrumentation equipment before being displaced by transistor circuits in the 1960s.
