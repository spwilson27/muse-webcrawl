Sir John Soane's Museum

LocationLincoln's Inn Fields, London
FounderSir John Soane
Established1837
TypeHouse museum

Sir John Soane's Museum is a house museum in Lincoln's Inn Fields, London, occupying the former home of the architect Sir John Soane (1753-1837). It preserves Soane's eclectic collection of antiquities, paintings, sculpture and architectural models, arranged much as he left them under an Act of Parliament of 1833.

Collection

Highlights include the sarcophagus of the Egyptian pharaoh Seti I, Hogarth's series A Rake's Progress and An Election, paintings by Canaletto and Turner, and the Picture Room, whose movable wall planes open to reveal layers of pictures. The basement 'Monk's Parlour' and catacombs display Soane's taste for dramatic, top-lit interiors.

The house

Soane rebuilt the houses at Nos. 12, 13 and 14 Lincoln's Inn Fields over his career, using mirrors, domes and skylights to manipulate light and space in small rooms. No. 13 is the principal museum building.

History

Soane negotiated an Act of Parliament in 1833 to preserve the house and collection for the nation, disinheriting his son in the process. The museum opened after his death in 1837 and remains free to visit.
