Israeli citizenship law

Israeli citizenship law details the conditions by which a person holds citizenship of Israel. The primary law governing these requirements is the Nationality Law of 1952, together with the Law of Return of 1950, which grants Jews worldwide the right to immigrate to Israel and acquire citizenship.

Acquisition

Citizenship may be acquired by return (under the Law of Return), by residence, by birth to an Israeli citizen parent (jus sanguinis), or by naturalisation. Israel has no general jus soli rule: birth in the territory alone does not confer citizenship. Naturalisation requires a period of residence, knowledge of Hebrew, and renunciation of prior citizenship in most cases, though exceptions exist.

Law of Return

The Law of Return, amended in 1970, extends its benefits to the children, grandchildren, and spouses of Jews. It was enacted in the wake of the Holocaust and is considered a foundational expression of Israel as a Jewish state.

Loss and revocation

Citizenship may be renounced with ministerial approval, and may be revoked in cases of fraud in its acquisition or, under later amendments, for certain acts of disloyalty such as terrorism or treason.
