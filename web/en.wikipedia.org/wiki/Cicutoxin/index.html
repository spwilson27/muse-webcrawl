Cicutoxin is a highly potent neurotoxin found in water hemlock (Cicuta species), among the most poisonous plants native to North America and Europe. It is a long-chain polyacetylenic alcohol concentrated mainly in the plant's roots and rootstock, and ingestion can cause violent convulsions and death.

Chemistry

Cicutoxin is a C17 polyacetylene bearing three conjugated double bonds, two triple bonds and two hydroxyl groups. Closely related compounds, including cicutol and cicudiol, occur alongside it in Cicuta species, and similar toxins (such as oenanthotoxin) are found in related Apiaceae genera.

Mechanism of toxicity

Cicutoxin acts as a noncompetitive antagonist of the GABAA receptor, blocking the main inhibitory neurotransmitter system of the central nervous system. The resulting uncontrolled neuronal excitation produces the characteristic symptoms of poisoning: nausea, salivation, trismus and severe generalized seizures (status epilepticus), which may lead to rhabdomyolysis, metabolic acidosis and death from respiratory failure.

Poisoning

Human poisoning usually follows mistaken identity: the roots of water hemlock have been confused with wild parsnip, artichoke or other edible plants. Livestock are also frequently poisoned. There is no specific antidote; treatment is supportive, with control of seizures using benzodiazepines or barbiturates and airway management being central.
