Poverty in the United States

Poverty in the United States refers to people in the United States who lack sufficient income or material possessions for their needs. The federal government measures poverty with the official poverty measure (OPM), developed in the 1960s, and the Supplemental Poverty Measure (SPM), introduced in 2011, which accounts for taxes, transfers, and regional costs. In recent years the official poverty rate has hovered around 11–12 percent of the population — roughly 37 million people — though it fell sharply in 2021 under pandemic relief programmes before rising again as they expired.

Measurement

The official poverty thresholds were originally based on the cost of a minimum food diet in 1963, multiplied by three to cover other expenses, and have since been updated only for inflation. Critics argue the measure is outdated because food is now a much smaller share of household budgets than housing, childcare, and medical care. The Supplemental Poverty Measure addresses this by including government benefits such as SNAP (food stamps) and tax credits, and subtracting necessary expenses.

Demographics and geography

Poverty rates are higher among children than adults, and highest among households headed by single mothers. Black and Hispanic Americans experience poverty at roughly twice the rate of non-Hispanic white Americans, and Native American communities have among the highest rates of all. Regionally, rates are highest in the South and parts of the Southwest and Appalachia, and in many inner-city and rural counties where they can exceed 25 percent.

Causes and policy

Debated causes include wage stagnation, deindustrialization, educational inequality, housing costs, medical debt, and family structure. Major federal anti-poverty programmes include SNAP, Medicaid, the Earned Income Tax Credit, Supplemental Security Income, and housing assistance. The 2021 expansion of the Child Tax Credit temporarily cut measured child poverty nearly in half, an episode widely cited in policy debates about cash transfers.
