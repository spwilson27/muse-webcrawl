South Bank Parklands

The South Bank Parklands are a heritage-listed series of parklands on the southern bank of the Brisbane River in South Brisbane, Queensland, Australia. The parklands, which were established after the site hosted World Expo 88, opened to the public on 20 June 1992. They cover about 17.5 hectares and attract millions of visitors each year, forming Brisbane's premier lifestyle and cultural destination alongside the adjoining Queensland Cultural Centre.

History

Before European settlement the area was a traditional meeting place for Aboriginal peoples. The site was redeveloped for World Expo 88, whose success led the Queensland Government to preserve the riverside as public parkland rather than sell it for commercial development; the present parklands were designed by Denton Corker Marshall and opened in 1992. The parklands were damaged in the 2010–11 Queensland floods but were restored and reopened progressively during 2011.

Attractions

Major features include Streets Beach, a man-made lagoon beach with white sand and lifeguards; the Grand Arbour, a kilometre-long arbour of steel columns covered in bougainvillea; the Wheel of Brisbane, a 60-metre Ferris wheel; the Nepalese Peace Pagoda, retained from Expo 88; the Parklands markets, restaurants, and cafes along Little Stanley Street and Grey Street; and rainforest and picnic areas linked by the Clem Jones Promenade along the river.

Management

The parklands are managed by the South Bank Corporation (now within the Queensland Government's city planning structures) and are connected to the city centre by the Victoria Bridge and the Goodwill Bridge, and to Kangaroo Point by the Neville Bonner Bridge.
