Joseph Smith Jr. (December 23, 1805 – June 27, 1844) was an American religious leader and the founder of Mormonism and the Latter Day Saint movement. When he was 24, Smith published the Book of Mormon, which he said was an English translation of golden plates given to him by an angel. During the next fourteen years he attracted thousands of followers, founded cities and temples, and articulated doctrines — including plural marriage and a new priesthood — that set his movement apart. Smith and his brother Hyrum were killed by a mob in 1844, an event believers regard as martyrdom.

Early life and First Vision

Smith was born in Sharon, Vermont, and grew up near Palmyra, New York, in the "burned-over district" of revivalist fervor. According to his later account, in 1820 he was visited by God the Father and Jesus Christ in what Latter-day Saints call the First Vision, and in 1823 the angel Moroni directed him to golden plates buried in a hill near his home. He said he received the plates in 1827 and dictated their translation, largely with the help of scribes including Oliver Cowdery, publishing the Book of Mormon in March 1830.

Church leadership

Smith organized the Church of Christ on April 6, 1830. Converts gathered first at Kirtland, Ohio — where they built a temple and endured a bank failure in 1837 — and in Missouri, where violent conflict with settlers culminated in Governor Lilburn Boggs's 1838 "extermination order" driving the Saints from the state. In Illinois, Smith founded the city of Nauvoo, which by 1844 rivaled Chicago in population; he served as its mayor, commanded its militia, the Nauvoo Legion, and announced his candidacy for President of the United States. He published further scripture (Doctrine and Covenants, Pearl of Great Price) and introduced temple ordinances and, privately, plural marriage.

Death and succession

In June 1844, after Smith ordered the destruction of the dissenting Nauvoo Expositor press, he was arrested and jailed in Carthage, Illinois. On June 27, an armed mob with blackened faces stormed the jail and shot Joseph and Hyrum Smith to death. His death triggered a succession crisis: the largest faction followed Brigham Young west to Utah, becoming The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, while others followed Smith's son Joseph Smith III in what became the Reorganized Church (now Community of Christ).

Legacy

Smith is revered by Latter-day Saints as a prophet, seer, and revelator comparable to Moses; critics have variously viewed him as a charismatic fraud or a religious genius. The movement he founded now counts tens of millions of adherents worldwide.
