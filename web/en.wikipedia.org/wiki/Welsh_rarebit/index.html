Welsh rarebit

Welsh rarebit (also Welsh rabbit) is a dish of hot cheese sauce served on toasted bread. The cheese sauce is typically made from sharp Cheddar melted with beer or milk, mustard, Worcestershire sauce and seasonings, poured over toast and grilled until bubbling and browned. Variants include a poached egg on top ("buck rarebit") or tomato ("tomato rarebit").

Name

The earliest reference, in 1725, is to "Welsh rabbit" — probably jocular: the Welsh were stereotypically too poor for meat, so cheese was their "rabbit". "Rarebit", a later corruption possibly devised to make the name respectable, appears from the mid-18th century; Hannah Glasse's The Art of Cookery (1747) gives recipes for Welsh, Scotch and English rabbit. Despite the folk etymology, no rabbit is involved.

Preparation and culture

A classic sauce uses grated mature Cheddar, ale, butter, flour, English mustard, cayenne and Worcestershire sauce, sometimes with an egg yolk to enrich and stabilise it. Cheese on toast, its simpler cousin, lacks the sauce. Rarebit became a staple of Victorian and Edwardian savoury courses and of railway and club menus; in Wales it is celebrated as a national dish, with 3 September sometimes marked as Welsh Rarebit Day. A related dish, "blushing bunny", uses tomato soup. See also cheese on toast.
