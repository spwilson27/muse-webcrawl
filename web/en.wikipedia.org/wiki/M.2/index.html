M.2

M.2, pronounced "M dot two", is a specification for internally mounted computer expansion cards and associated connectors. Formerly known as the Next Generation Form Factor (NGFF), it supersedes the mSATA standard and is widely used for solid-state drives (SSDs).

M.2
Type: Internal expansion connector
Predecessor: mSATA
Common use: NVMe and SATA SSDs
Lengths: 2230, 2242, 2260, 2280, 22110

Overview

M.2 modules are rectangular boards with widths of 12, 16, 22 or 30 mm and lengths from 16 to 110 mm. The most common size for desktop and laptop SSDs is the 22 mm wide, 80 mm long "2280" format.

Interfaces and keying

M.2 connectors support multiple host interfaces, including PCI Express (used by NVMe drives), SATA and USB. Different keying notches (such as B-key and M-key) prevent modules from being inserted into incompatible sockets.
