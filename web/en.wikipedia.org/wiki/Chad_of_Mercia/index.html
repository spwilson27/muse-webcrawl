Chad of Mercia

Chad (Old English: Ceadda; c. 634 – 2 March 672) was an Anglo-Saxon saint, Abbot of Lastingham and Bishop of Mercia and Lindsey. He is best known for his missionary work in Mercia and Northumbria in the 7th century.

Chad was one of four brothers, including Cedd, who were educated at Lindisfarne under Aidan. After serving as abbot of Lastingham in Yorkshire, he was appointed Bishop of York in 664, but his consecration was later questioned. Theodore of Tarsus, Archbishop of Canterbury, regularised his position and appointed him Bishop of the Mercians in 669, with his see at Lichfield.

Life

Chad established the diocese of Lichfield and undertook extensive pastoral journeys on foot throughout Mercia. Bede praised his humility, prayer and dedication to preaching. He died of plague on 2 March 672 and was buried at Lichfield.

Veneration

His feast day is 2 March. St Chad's Cathedral in Birmingham and many churches bear his dedication. His shrine at Lichfield Cathedral was a major pilgrimage site in the Middle Ages.
