Podocarpaceae

The Podocarpaceae (podocarps) are a large family of conifers in the order Pinales, comprising roughly 19 genera and 170–200 species of evergreen trees and shrubs. They are the second-largest conifer family and are distributed mainly in the Southern Hemisphere, especially Australasia, Southeast Asia, and South America, with some genera extending into Japan, China, Mexico, and the Caribbean.

Characteristics

Unlike most conifers, podocarps generally do not bear their seeds in woody cones. Their seeds are often partially or wholly enclosed in a fleshy, brightly coloured structure — a modified cone scale or a swollen receptacle (the podocarpium, from which the family name derives) — adapted for dispersal by birds and mammals. Leaves vary from needle-like to broad and flattened, sometimes arranged to mimic a compound leaf. Most species are dioecious.

Important genera

Well-known members include Podocarpus (yellowwoods), Dacrydium (rimu), Dacrycarpus (kahikatea), Prumnopitys, Afrocarpus, and the parasitic New Caledonian genus Parasitaxus, the only known parasitic conifer. Several podocarps are major timber trees in New Zealand, South Africa, and Southeast Asia, and some are popular in horticulture.
