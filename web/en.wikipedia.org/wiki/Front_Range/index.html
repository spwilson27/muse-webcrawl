Front Range

The Front Range is a mountain range of the Southern Rocky Mountains in North America, located in the central part of the U.S. state of Colorado and the southeastern part of Wyoming. It is the first, easternmost range encountered when travelling west across the Great Plains and is the most accessible and best-known part of the Colorado Rockies.

Geography

The range runs roughly north–south for about 480 km (300 miles), from near Casper, Wyoming, to Pueblo, Colorado. Its highest summits include Grays Peak, Mount Evans (Mount Blue Sky) and Longs Peak, all above 14,000 feet (4,300 m). The foothills rise abruptly from the plains at about 1,500–1,800 m elevation.

Settlement

Most of Colorado's population lives in the urban corridor along the range's eastern foot — the Front Range urban corridor — including Denver, Boulder, Colorado Springs and Fort Collins. The abrupt mountain front, formed by uplift along major faults, shelters recreation areas, water reservoirs and research institutions such as those in Boulder.
