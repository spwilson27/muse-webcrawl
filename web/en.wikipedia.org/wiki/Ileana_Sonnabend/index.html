Ileana Sonnabend (born Ileana Schapira; October 28, 1914 – October 21, 2007) was a Romanian-born American art dealer and gallerist, one of the most influential figures in the post-war art world. Through her galleries in Paris and New York, she introduced European audiences to American Pop art and introduced American audiences to European movements such as Arte Povera.

Life and career

Born in Bucharest to a wealthy industrialist family, she married the art dealer and theorist Michael Sonnabend in 1932. The couple collected modern art in Rome and, after emigrating to the United States, in New York. In 1962 she opened the Galerie Ileana Sonnabend in Paris, showing American artists including Robert Rauschenberg, Jasper Johns, Andy Warhol, Roy Lichtenstein, Claes Oldenburg, and Jim Dine to a European public; Rauschenberg's 1962 show there was his first European exhibition.

In 1971 she opened a gallery in New York's SoHo district, at 420 West Broadway, in the same building as the Leo Castelli Gallery (Castelli had been her husband before Michael Sonnabend). Her New York programme championed Minimalism, Conceptual art, and Arte Povera artists such as Mario Merz, Jannis Kounellis, and Gilberto Zorio, as well as later figures such as Jeff Koons.

Legacy

Sonnabend was nicknamed the "mom of Pop". Her estate's collection, assembled with Michael Sonnabend, became notable in its own right. She died in New York in 2007, aged 92.
