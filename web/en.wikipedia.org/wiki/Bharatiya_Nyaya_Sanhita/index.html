Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita

EnactedParliament of India, December 2023
In force1 July 2024
ReplacesIndian Penal Code, 1860

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) is the official criminal code of India. It replaced the colonial-era Indian Penal Code (1860) and came into force on 1 July 2024, as part of a package of three new criminal laws alongside the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (replacing the Code of Criminal Procedure) and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (replacing the Indian Evidence Act).

Changes

The BNS reorganises offences into 358 sections (compared with 511 in the IPC). Among its notable provisions: terrorism is defined and made an offence within the general penal code; organised crime and petty organised crime are newly addressed; sedition as a named offence is removed, though acts endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India are criminalised; community service is introduced as a punishment for certain petty offences; and penalties for offences such as mob lynching and hit-and-run driving are revised. A provision on sexual intercourse by deceitful means, including false promises of marriage, attracted particular debate.

Reception

The government presented the code as shedding colonial legacies — hence the Hindi title, meaning "Indian Code of Justice". Critics among lawyers, opposition parties and civil liberties groups raised concerns over expanded police powers, certain broadly worded offences, and the speed of the code's passage through Parliament.
