NaV1.7

ProteinSodium channel protein type 9 subunit alpha
GeneSCN9A
TypeVoltage-gated sodium channel
LocationPeripheral nervous system, dorsal root ganglia

NaV1.7 is a voltage-gated sodium channel encoded in humans by the SCN9A gene. It is expressed predominantly in the peripheral nervous system, especially in nociceptive neurons of the dorsal root ganglia, where it acts as a threshold channel that amplifies small depolarising inputs and helps trigger action potentials that signal pain.

Clinical significance

Mutations in SCN9A produce striking disorders of pain perception. Gain-of-function mutations cause inherited erythromelalgia and paroxysmal extreme pain disorder, conditions marked by episodes of intense burning pain, while loss-of-function mutations cause congenital insensitivity to pain, in which affected individuals cannot feel physical pain. Mutations have also been associated with some cases of small fibre neuropathy.

Drug target

Because loss of NaV1.7 function abolishes pain without apparently major effects on most other senses, the channel has been an intensely studied target for new analgesic drugs. Pharmaceutical companies have developed selective NaV1.7 blockers, although translating the genetic findings into effective clinical painkillers has proved difficult, and several candidate drugs have failed in trials.
