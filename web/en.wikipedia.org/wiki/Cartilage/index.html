Cartilage is a resilient and smooth type of connective tissue. In tetrapods, it covers and protects the ends of long bones at the joints, and is a structural component of many body parts including the rib cage, the limbs, the ear, the nose, the intervertebral discs and the bronchial tubes. It is softer and much more flexible than bone. Cartilage is composed of specialised cells called chondrocytes that produce a large amount of extracellular matrix composed of collagen fibres, proteoglycans and elastin fibres; unlike other connective tissues, cartilage contains no blood vessels or nerves, so its cells are nourished by diffusion and it heals slowly.

Types

Hyaline cartilage

The most widespread type, hyaline cartilage has a glassy, bluish appearance and contains fine type II collagen fibrils. It forms the articular cartilage covering bone ends in synovial joints, the costal cartilages of the ribs, the nasal septum, and much of the larynx, trachea and bronchi, and provides the template for the developing skeleton in the embryo.

Elastic cartilage

Elastic cartilage contains a dense network of elastin fibres, making it flexible and resilient; it forms the external ear (pinna), the epiglottis and parts of the larynx.

Fibrocartilage

Fibrocartilage, rich in type I collagen, is tough and resistant to compression and tension. It forms the intervertebral discs, the menisci of the knee, the pubic symphysis and the glenoid and acetabular labra.

Growth and development

Cartilage grows by interstitial growth (chondrocyte division within the matrix) and appositional growth (new layers at the surface, from the perichondrium). In endochondral ossification, most of the skeleton begins as hyaline cartilage models that are progressively replaced by bone; the epiphyseal growth plates that lengthen long bones are cartilage until skeletal maturity.

Clinical significance

Because it is avascular, injured cartilage (such as a torn meniscus or damaged articular surface) repairs poorly. Degeneration of articular cartilage underlies osteoarthritis, one of the commonest causes of disability. In sharks and rays (class Chondrichthyes), cartilage forms the entire adult skeleton.
