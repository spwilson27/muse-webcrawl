The Academy Honorary Award — instituted in 1950 and replacing the previous "Special Award" — is given by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences "to honor extraordinary distinction in lifetime achievement, exceptional contributions to the state of motion picture arts and sciences, or for outstanding service to the Academy". Unlike the competitive Academy Awards, recipients are chosen by the Academy's Board of Governors.

History

Special and honorary awards were given from the first Academy Awards ceremony in 1929, when Warner Bros. was honoured for The Jazz Singer and Charlie Chaplin received a special award for The Circus. Since 2009 the honorary awards have been presented at the separate Governors Awards ceremony held in the autumn before the main Oscars telecast.

Notable recipients

Recipients have included Charlie Chaplin, Walt Disney, Orson Welles, Akira Kurosawa, Satyajit Ray, Federico Fellini, Alfred Hitchcock (also recipient of the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award), James Stewart, Sidney Poitier, Robert Redford, Angela Bassett, and Mel Brooks. The award usually takes the form of a standard Oscar statuette, though early special awards included plaques and other forms.

Related awards

The Board of Governors also selects recipients of the Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award and the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award, honoring humanitarian work and distinguished producing careers respectively.
