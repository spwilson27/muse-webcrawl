The Penn World Table provides purchasing-power-parity-adjusted income comparisons across countries and time.

The Penn World Table (PWT) is a widely used database of national-account variables expressed in internationally comparable, purchasing-power-parity (PPP) terms, designed for comparing real income, output and productivity across countries and over time. It originated in the 1980s in work by Robert Summers and Alan Heston (later joined by Bettina Aten) at the University of Pennsylvania, building on the UN International Comparison Program.

Content

The dataset reports variables such as real GDP (in output- and expenditure-side variants), capital stock, employment, hours worked, labour share and total factor productivity for about 183 countries from 1950 onward. Since version 8 (2013), the table has been maintained by the Groningen Growth and Development Centre at the University of Groningen in the Netherlands.

Use and criticism

PWT is a standard source in growth empirics and development economics—many famous cross-country growth regressions rely on it. Revisions between versions can substantially change measured incomes, a phenomenon ("revisionism") that has prompted methodological debate about PPP benchmarks and the reliability of growth comparisons.
