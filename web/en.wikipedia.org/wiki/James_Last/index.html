James Last (born Hans Last; 17 April 1929 – 9 June 2015) was a German composer and big band leader whose lush orchestral arrangements of popular music made him one of the best-selling recording artists in Europe. His trademark "happy sound" — strings, brass, chorus, and a prominent rhythm section playing medleys of hits, classical themes, and folk melodies — sold an estimated 100–200 million records worldwide.

Career

Last began as a jazz bassist in post-war Bremen, leading the Last-Becker Ensemble and playing with the Radio Bremen dance orchestra. In the 1960s, as an arranger and producer at Polydor, he developed his orchestral pop style; the 1965 album Non Stop Dancing, a continuous medley of current hits, was a breakthrough, and dozens of follow-up albums in series such as Classics Up to Date, Trumpet à Go Go, and Beachparty followed. He was particularly successful in the United Kingdom, where he placed dozens of albums on the charts, and his orchestra toured internationally for decades, with regular appearances at London's Royal Albert Hall. His composition "Games That Lovers Play" became an international standard, and he wrote music for films and television. Last performed farewell tours in the 2010s and died in Florida in 2015.
