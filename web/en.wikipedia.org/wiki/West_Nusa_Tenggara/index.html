West Nusa Tenggara (Indonesian: Nusa Tenggara Barat, NTB) is a province of Indonesia comprising the western part of the Lesser Sunda Islands, principally the islands of Lombok and Sumbawa. Its capital and largest city is Mataram, on Lombok. The province lies between Bali to the west and East Nusa Tenggara to the east; the deep Lombok Strait, marking the Wallace Line between Asian and Australasian fauna, separates it from Bali.

Geography and people

Lombok is dominated by Mount Rinjani (3,726 m), Indonesia's second-highest volcano, and is home mainly to the Muslim Sasak people, with a Balinese Hindu minority in the west. Sumbawa, larger but more sparsely populated, is divided between Sumbawa speakers in the west and Bima speakers in the east around the town of Bima and the volcano Tambora, whose 1815 eruption was the largest in recorded history and caused the "Year Without a Summer".

Economy and events

Agriculture (rice, tobacco, coffee), gold and copper mining on Sumbawa (the Batu Hijau mine), and tourism — centred on the Gili Islands, Senggigi and Kuta Lombok — are the main economic activities. The Mandalika resort area on Lombok hosts the Mandalika International Street Circuit, which has staged MotoGP and World Superbike rounds since 2021–22. In August 2018 a series of powerful earthquakes struck Lombok, killing more than 500 people and causing widespread destruction in the north of the island.
