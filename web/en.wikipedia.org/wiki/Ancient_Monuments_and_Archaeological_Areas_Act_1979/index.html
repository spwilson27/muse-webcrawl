The Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Areas Act 1979 (c. 46) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that provides the principal legal protection for archaeological sites and ancient monuments in Great Britain. It consolidated a line of legislation going back to the Ancient Monuments Protection Act 1882, the first British statute to protect prehistoric sites.

Provisions

Scheduling

Part I of the Act empowers the Secretary of State (advised by Historic England in England, Cadw in Wales and Historic Environment Scotland in Scotland) to maintain a Schedule of monuments of national importance. It is a criminal offence to carry out works — demolition, damage, alteration, repair or development — affecting a scheduled monument without prior scheduled monument consent. There are roughly 20,000 scheduled entries in England alone, covering sites from prehistoric barrows and Roman remains to industrial and 20th-century military structures.

Areas of archaeological importance

Part II designates "areas of archaeological importance" — historically Canterbury, Chester, Exeter, Hereford and York — within which developers must give notice of operations, allowing time for archaeological investigation before ground disturbance.

Guardianship and offences

The Act also provides for monuments to be taken into state guardianship, regulates the use of metal detectors on protected sites (an offence without consent), and controls archaeological investigation of scheduled land.
