Dry fly fishing

Dry fly fishing is an angling technique in which the lure, an artificial fly, floats on the surface of the water and does not sink below it. Developed originally for use in still or slow-moving rivers for trout, it imitates adult aquatic insects — such as mayflies, caddisflies and stoneflies — as well as terrestrial insects falling onto the water.

Technique

The angler casts upstream and allows the fly to drift naturally with the current without drag, watching for a rise as a fish takes the fly from the surface. Flies are tied with buoyant materials and treated with floatant to resist waterlogging. Pioneers of the method on English chalk streams included Frederic M. Halford in the late 19th century.

Flies

Classic dry flies include the Adams, Royal Wulff, Elk Hair Caddis and Parachute Adams patterns.
