Visual kei

Visual kei (Japanese: ヴィジュアル系, "visual style") is a movement among Japanese musicians characterised by striking, flamboyant visual presentation — elaborate costumes, heavy make-up, dramatic hairstyles, and androgynous aesthetics — paired with musical styles that most often draw on rock, metal, and pop. It emerged in Japan in the mid-1980s with pioneering bands such as X Japan, whose drummer and leader Yoshiki is credited with popularising the term, along with Buck-Tick, D'erlanger, and Color.

History

The movement grew out of the influence of Western glam rock, punk, and heavy metal (Kiss, David Bowie, Mötley Crüe) on Japanese underground rock. X Japan's explosive success in the late 1980s and 1990s — alongside Luna Sea, L'Arc-en-Ciel, Malice Mizer, Dir en Grey, and Kuroyume — brought visual kei to mainstream attention. In the 1990s some acts toned down their appearance for commercial success ("soft visual"), while others developed sub-styles: oshare kei (bright, pop-oriented), nagoya kei (darker, Nagoya-based), and angura kei (drawing on Japanese underground theatre aesthetics).

Characteristics and culture

There is no single visual kei sound; what unites the scene is the primacy of visual identity — each band cultivates a distinctive concept, and members frequently blur gender presentation. The fan culture is distinctive too: predominantly female fans (bangya) develop strong loyalties, attend lives in coordinated fashion, and participate in fan practices such as furitsuke (synchronised hand movements). Since the 2000s, acts including the GazettE, Alice Nine, and Versailles have toured internationally, and visual kei aesthetics have fed into global alternative fashion and cosplay.
