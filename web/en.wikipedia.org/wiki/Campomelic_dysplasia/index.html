Campomelic dysplasia is a rare, often lethal skeletal dysplasia characterised by bowing of the long bones (from the Greek kampos, "bent", and melos, "limb"), especially the tibiae and femora, together with distinctive facial features and abnormalities of the spine, pelvis and respiratory tract. A related form without limb bowing is called acampomelic campomelic dysplasia.

Features

Typical findings include bowed lower legs with pretibial skin dimples, short limbs, eleven pairs of ribs, a narrow chest, dislocated hips, club feet, cleft palate and a flat face with a small chin (Pierre Robin sequence). The trachea and bronchi are soft (tracheobronchomalacia) because of defective cartilage, and respiratory failure is the usual cause of death in the newborn period.

Genetics

The condition is caused by heterozygous loss-of-function mutations in, or chromosomal rearrangements around, the SOX9 gene on chromosome 17q24. SOX9 is a transcription factor essential for cartilage formation (chondrogenesis) and for testis development. Accordingly, about two-thirds of affected XY individuals have partial or complete male-to-female sex reversal or ambiguous genitalia. Most cases arise from new (de novo) mutations.

Management and prognosis

Many affected infants die in the neonatal period from respiratory insufficiency, but with intensive support some survive; reported survivors may have hearing loss, short stature, scoliosis and developmental difficulties. Care is supportive, including airway management, orthopaedic treatment and monitoring of the cervical spine. Prenatal ultrasound may detect the bowed limbs and other anomalies.
