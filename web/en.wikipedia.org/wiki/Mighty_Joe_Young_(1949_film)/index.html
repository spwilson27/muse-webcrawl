Mighty Joe Young (1949 film)

Mighty Joe Young is a 1949 American fantasy adventure film released by RKO Radio Pictures. It was produced by the team behind King Kong (1933): director Ernest B. Schoedsack, producer-director Merian C. Cooper, screenwriter Ruth Rose, and stop-motion animator Willis O'Brien, who won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects for this film. The model animation was executed largely by the young Ray Harryhausen, in his first major feature credit.

Plot

Showman Max O'Hara (Robert Armstrong, in a role echoing Carl Denham) travels to Africa seeking acts for his Hollywood nightclub. There he meets Jill Young (Terry Moore), who has raised an enormous but gentle gorilla, Joe, from infancy. Persuaded to bring Joe to America, Jill watches him humiliated as a nightclub attraction; provoked by drunken patrons, Joe rampages. Sentenced to be shot, Joe redeems himself by rescuing children from a burning orphanage, and is allowed to return home to Africa with Jill and cowboy Gregg (Ben Johnson).

Production

The film used elaborate stop-motion models integrated with live action and full-scale mechanical arms and heads. It was made in black and white; the climactic orphanage fire sequence was tinted. Though not a commercial success on first release, it became a beloved classic and a landmark of stop-motion effects.

Remake

Disney released a remake in 1998, directed by Ron Underwood and starring Charlize Theron and Bill Paxton, using a combination of animatronics and early CGI.
