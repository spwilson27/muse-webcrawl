Honda Super Cub

The Honda Super Cub (or Honda Cub) is an underbone motorcycle manufactured by Honda since 1958. With cumulative production exceeding 100 million units in 2017, it is the most produced motor vehicle in history. Its 50–125 cc four-stroke single-cylinder engine and step-through frame made it accessible to riders worldwide.

History

Conceived by Soichiro Honda and Takeo Fujisawa, the Super Cub was designed to be simple, durable and easy to ride one-handed, so delivery riders in Japan could carry goods such as soba noodles. Its enclosed chain, automatic centrifugal clutch and plastic leg shields were distinctive innovations.

American campaign

In the United States the Cub was sold by American Honda from 1959. The 1963 advertising campaign "You meet the nicest people on a Honda" — one of the most celebrated in advertising history — repositioned motorcycles for the general public. The Beach Boys' 1964 song "Little Honda" further boosted its image.

Impact

The Super Cub motorised much of Asia and remains in production in updated forms, including the Super Cub C125 reintroduced in 2018. Its design has been widely imitated, and its reliability and efficiency made it a cultural icon of postwar mobility.
