Crane (machine)

A crane is a type of machine, generally equipped with a hoist rope, wire ropes or chains, and sheaves, that can be used both to lift and lower materials and to move them horizontally. It is mainly used for lifting heavy things and transporting them to other places. The device uses one or more simple machines to create mechanical advantage and thus move loads beyond the normal capability of a human.

History

Cranes were first invented by the ancient Greeks and were powered by men or animals such as donkeys. The Roman polyspaston, worked by treadwheels, could lift several tonnes. In the Middle Ages, harbour and treadwheel cranes were used to load ships and build cathedrals. Steam power transformed cranes in the 19th century, and modern cranes are driven by diesel engines or electric motors and controlled hydraulically or electronically.

Types

  • Tower crane — a fixed or climbing crane with a horizontal jib, ubiquitous on high-rise construction sites; its height and reach make it efficient for building work, and it is often assembled on site and increases in height as the building rises.
  • Mobile crane — mounted on crawlers or rubber-tyred carriers, including truck-mounted and rough-terrain cranes, which can travel between sites.
  • Overhead (bridge) crane — runs on elevated rails in factories and warehouses, spanning the width of the building.
  • Gantry crane — an overhead crane supported by legs, used in shipyards and container ports; the largest container cranes load and unload ships.
  • Jib crane — a crane with a horizontal member (jib) supporting a movable hoist.
  • Floating crane — mounted on a barge or specially built vessel for harbour and offshore work.

Mechanics and safety

Cranes achieve stability through counterweights and outriggers, and safe operation depends on respecting load charts that relate load weight to radius: the further a load is from the mast, the less the crane can safely lift. Overloading and wind are leading causes of crane accidents, so operators are licensed and lifts are planned, especially for tandem or heavy lifts.
