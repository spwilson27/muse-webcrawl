Projective linear group

In mathematics, the projective linear group, written PGL(n, F), is the group of automorphisms of a projective space of dimension n − 1 over a field F. It is obtained from the general linear group GL(n, F) by identifying matrices that differ by a nonzero scalar multiple: PGL(n, F) = GL(n, F) / F×, where the scalars are quotiented out because they act trivially on projective space.

Properties

The projective linear group acts faithfully and transitively on projective space, and its elements are exactly the projectivities (collineations induced by linear maps). There is a related group PSL(n, F), the projective special linear group, obtained from the special linear group SL(n, F). For most parameters, PSL(n, F) is a finite simple group — a fact important in the classification of finite simple groups — with small exceptions such as PSL(2, 2) and PSL(2, 3).

Low-dimensional examples

PGL(2, F) is the group of Möbius transformations of the projective line, z ↦ (az + b)/(cz + d). Over the complex numbers, PGL(2, ℂ) is the group of conformal automorphisms of the Riemann sphere. PGL(2, ℝ) relates to the isometries of the hyperbolic plane. PSL(2, 5) is isomorphic to the alternating group A5, and PSL(2, 7), of order 168, is the automorphism group of the Klein quartic.
