Alfred Müller-Armack (28 June 1901 – 16 March 1978) was a German economist and sociologist who coined the term social market economy (Soziale Marktwirtschaft) and, as Ludwig Erhard's closest collaborator and state secretary, helped give West Germany's post-war economic order its intellectual and political shape.

Academic career

Born in Essen, Müller-Armack studied economics at Giessen, Freiburg, Munich and Cologne, taking his doctorate in 1926 and his habilitation in 1934. His early work treated business cycles and the sociology of religion and economic styles (Wirtschaftsstile), exploring how cultural and spiritual orientations shape economic orders — an interest he pursued throughout his life. He became professor at Münster (1938) and then Cologne (1950).

The social market economy

In his 1947 book Wirtschaftslenkung und Marktwirtschaft ("Economic Control and Market Economy") Müller-Armack introduced "social market economy" to describe a "socially conscious guidance" of a competitive market order: free prices and competition as the engine, combined with a strong state framework, social balancing, anti-monopoly policy and what he later called the system's "social irenics" — the deliberate reconciliation of social groups. As head of the policy-planning staff and then State Secretary in Erhard's Economics Ministry (from 1952, with responsibility especially for European integration), he translated the concept into legislation and negotiated the German position in the formation of the European Economic Community.

Later work

After leaving the ministry in 1963 he returned to Cologne, writing on cultural theory, religion and the genealogy of economic styles, including Religion und Wirtschaft (1959) and Genealogie der Sozialen Marktwirtschaft (1974). He died in Cologne in 1978.
