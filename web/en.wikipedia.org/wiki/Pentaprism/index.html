Pentaprism

TypeOptical prism
FunctionDeviates a light beam by a constant 90°
FacesFive (two reflecting, with mirrored coatings)

A pentaprism is a five-sided reflective prism used to deviate a beam of light by a constant 90°, even if the entry beam is not at 90° to the prism. The beam reflects twice inside the prism, which reverses neither handedness nor orientation in the way a mirror pair otherwise would: the image is neither flipped vertically nor reversed left-to-right, and the deviation angle is independent of small errors in the prism's orientation, because it depends only on the fixed angle between the two reflecting faces.

Optical principle

The two reflecting surfaces are inclined at 22.5° to the optical axis and to the entrance and exit faces, and the angle between the two reflective faces is 45°. Because the angle of incidence on the reflective faces is below the critical angle for total internal reflection in glass, the two faces must be coated with a reflective metal layer, traditionally silver or aluminium. The entrance and exit faces are usually anti-reflection coated.

In cameras

The most familiar application is the roof pentaprism (or pentaprism with a "roof") used in single-lens reflex (SLR) and digital SLR viewfinders. Light from the focusing screen is reflected through the prism to the photographer's eye, producing an upright, laterally correct image rather than the reversed image seen in a simple waist-level finder. Cheaper cameras substitute a lighter pentamirror — an assembly of mirrors in the same geometry — which is dimmer but much less expensive and lighter than solid glass.

Other uses

Pentaprisms are used in surveying instruments, rangefinders, optical plummets, and precision alignment equipment, anywhere a stable, exact right-angle deviation is required. In surveying, a double pentaprism allows an observer to sight simultaneously in two opposite directions.
