Nîmes

Nîmes is a city in the Occitanie region of southern France, the prefecture of the Gard department. It is renowned for some of the best-preserved Roman monuments in Europe, legacies of the Roman colony of Nemausus. Its population is about 150,000.

History

Nîmes grew from a settlement of the Volcae Arecomici around a sacred spring dedicated to the god Nemausus. Under Augustus it became a prosperous Roman colony, encircled by walls and embellished with temples and an amphitheatre. The nearby Pont du Gard aqueduct supplied its water. In the Middle Ages the city declined but later prospered through textiles — denim takes its name from "serge de Nîmes".

Landmarks

Major monuments include the Arena of Nîmes, a Roman amphitheatre still used for concerts and bullfights; the Maison Carrée, an exceptionally intact Roman temple inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2023; the Tour Magne; and the Jardins de la Fontaine. The modern Musée de la Romanité opened in 2018.

Culture

The city's férias — notably the Feria de Pentecôte — draw large crowds for bullfighting, music and street festivals.
