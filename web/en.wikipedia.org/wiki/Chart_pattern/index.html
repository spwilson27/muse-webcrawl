Chart pattern

A chart pattern is a pattern within a chart used in technical analysis of financial markets. Analysts who study price charts believe that recognisable shapes formed by price movements — such as trends, channels, triangles and head-and-shoulders formations — can help predict future price behaviour, because the patterns reflect the psychology of market participants.

Types of patterns

Chart patterns are conventionally divided into reversal patterns, which suggest a change in the prevailing trend, and continuation patterns, which suggest a pause before the trend resumes. Well-known reversal patterns include the head and shoulders (and its inverse), double tops and double bottoms, triple tops and bottoms, and the saucer or rounding bottom. Common continuation patterns include triangles (ascending, descending and symmetrical), flags and pennants, wedges, rectangles and cup-and-handle formations.

Use and criticism

Traders typically combine pattern recognition with support and resistance levels, trendlines and volume. Academic opinion on chart patterns is mixed: some statistical studies have found limited predictive value in particular patterns, while proponents of the efficient-market hypothesis argue that apparent patterns arise from randomness and hindsight. Nevertheless, chart patterns remain a central tool of practising technical analysts and are built into most charting software.
