Norwegian language conflict

Map of Norway's municipalities by official written standard, Bokmål or Nynorsk.

The Norwegian language conflict (Norwegian: målstriden, "the language struggle") is an ongoing controversy within Norwegian culture concerning the two official written standards of the Norwegian language: Bokmål and Nynorsk.

History

After Norway's separation from Denmark in 1814, written Norwegian remained essentially Danish. In the mid-19th century, linguist Ivar Aasen constructed Landsmål (later Nynorsk) from rural dialects, while Knud Knudsen promoted the gradual Norwegianisation of Danish that became Bokmål. Parliament recognised both standards as equal in 1885.

Samnorsk and beyond

Twentieth-century reforms attempted to merge the two into a common standard, Samnorsk, but the policy was effectively abandoned by the 1960s–2000s. Today Bokmål is used by roughly 85–90% of Norwegians, while Nynorsk remains official in several western counties and is taught in all schools.
