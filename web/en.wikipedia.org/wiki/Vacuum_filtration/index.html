Vacuum filtration

Vacuum filtration is a technique for separating a solid product from a liquid reaction mixture. The liquid is drawn through filter paper in a funnel by applying a vacuum beneath it, leaving the solid collected on the paper. It is much faster than gravity filtration and is a standard operation in chemistry laboratories.

Apparatus

The usual apparatus consists of a Büchner funnel (or a Hirsch funnel for small quantities) fitted with a rubber adapter into a heavy-walled filter flask (Büchner flask) with a side arm connected to a vacuum source, commonly a water aspirator or an electric pump. A trap is often placed between the flask and the vacuum source to prevent backflow.

Procedure

Filter paper is placed in the funnel, wetted with solvent and sucked flat. The mixture is poured in; the vacuum draws the mother liquor through while the solid accumulates on the paper. The solid can then be washed with small portions of cold solvent and dried by drawing air through it.
