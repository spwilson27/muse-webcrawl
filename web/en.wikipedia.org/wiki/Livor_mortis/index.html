Livor mortis

Also called lividity, hypostasis, or post-mortem staining; one of the signs of death.

Livor mortis (Latin: livor — bluish colour, mortis — of death), also known as lividity or hypostasis, is a post-mortem sign in which blood settles by gravity into the dependent parts of the body after the heart stops, producing a purplish-red discoloration of the skin.

Timeline and characteristics

Lividity typically begins within 20 minutes to 3 hours after death, becomes maximal around 6–12 hours, and initially blanches under pressure and shifts if the body is moved. After roughly 8–12 hours it becomes "fixed" as blood haemolyses and stains tissues, no longer moving with repositioning. Contact pallor occurs where pressure prevents settling.

Forensic use

Forensic pathologists use livor mortis, together with rigor mortis and algor mortis, to estimate the post-mortem interval and to detect whether a body was moved after death. Colour can hint at cause of death — for example cherry-red lividity in carbon monoxide poisoning.
