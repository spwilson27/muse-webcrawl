The Dassault Falcon 20 is a French business jet developed and manufactured by Dassault Aviation. It was the first of the Falcon family of business jets, derived from the Mystère fighter line; the prototype, initially called the Mystère 20, first flew on 4 May 1963.

Development and service

The type attracted a large order from Pan American World Airways for its business-jet distribution arm, and was marketed in the United States as the Fan Jet Falcon. Federal Express founder Fred Smith used early Falcon 20s to launch his overnight delivery service in 1973, and the company later became a major operator of freighter conversions. The United States Coast Guard operated the HU-25 Guardian variant for search and rescue and maritime patrol from 1982 until 2014.

Variants

The improved Falcon 200, with more powerful engines and greater range, followed in the 1980s. Many Falcon 20s were re-engined under the Falcon 20-5 programme with Garrett TFE731 engines, improving economy and performance. A total of 473 Falcon 20s and 35 Falcon 200s were built before production ended in 1988.
