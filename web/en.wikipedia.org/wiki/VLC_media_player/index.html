VLC media player
Developer: VideoLAN project
Initial release: 1 February 2001
Written in: C, C++, Objective-C, others
Platform: Cross-platform
License: GPL / LGPL (free and open-source)

VLC media player (previously the VideoLAN Client and commonly known simply as VLC) is a free and open-source, cross-platform media player and streaming framework developed by the VideoLAN project. It plays most multimedia files and discs, and supports numerous streaming protocols, on desktop and mobile operating systems including Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS.

History

VLC began in 1996 as an academic project at the École Centrale Paris, where students developed it to stream videos across the campus network. It was rewritten and released under the GPL on 1 February 2001. The project's mascot, an orange traffic cone, comes from a cone collection assembled by the students' association; the cone remains one of the most recognisable icons in open-source software. VLC has been downloaded billions of times and is consistently among the most-used media players in the world.

Features

VLC's defining strength is its built-in codec support: it plays an unusually wide range of formats — including MPEG-2, MPEG-4, H.264, H.265, VP9, AV1, MKV, MP3, FLAC and DVDs — without requiring additional codec packs. Beyond playback it can stream, transcode and record, apply audio and video filters and effects, synchronise subtitles, play incomplete or damaged files, and serve as a lightweight streaming server. Its modular design, largely written in C with the libVLC core, underpins ports and derivative applications.

Versions

VLC 1.0 ("Goldeneye") arrived in 2009; the 2.x series added Blu-ray support (experimental) and new interfaces; VLC 3.0 "Vetinari" (2018) added HDR, 360-degree video, Chromecast support and hardware decoding. The project is maintained by the non-profit VideoLAN organisation, based in France.
