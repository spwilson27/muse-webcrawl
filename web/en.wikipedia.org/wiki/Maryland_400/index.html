Maryland 400

The Maryland 400 were a contingent of about 400 soldiers from the 1st Maryland Regiment of the Continental Army who, during the Battle of Brooklyn (also called the Battle of Long Island) on August 27, 1776, repeatedly charged a vastly superior British force to buy time for the rest of the American army to escape. The action, though costly, is credited with saving the Continental Army from destruction early in the American Revolutionary War.

The Battle of Brooklyn

On August 27, 1776, British forces under General William Howe outflanked Washington's army on Long Island. As the American line collapsed, General William Alexander (Lord Stirling) ordered a rear-guard action at the Cortelyou House, later known as the Old Stone House. The Marylanders launched a series of assaults against some 2,000 British and Hessian troops armed with artillery, while the remainder of the American force withdrew across the Gowanus Creek and the marshes toward Brooklyn Heights.

Casualties and legacy

Of the Marylanders engaged, only about a dozen returned; the rest were killed or captured, including the regiment's commander, Mordecai Gist. Their sacrifice allowed Washington to evacuate his army to Manhattan by night on August 29–30. The "Maryland 400" is commemorated at Prospect Park, Brooklyn, where a memorial marks the site, and the state's line "The Old Line State" is said to derive from the bravery of its troops on that day.
