Adaptation Fund

The Adaptation Fund is an international climate fund established under the Kyoto Protocol of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) to finance concrete adaptation projects and programmes in developing countries that are particularly vulnerable to the adverse effects of climate change. It was established in 2001 and began approving projects in 2010.

Financing

The Fund was originally financed primarily by a 2 percent share of proceeds from Certified Emission Reduction (CER) credits issued under the Kyoto Protocol's Clean Development Mechanism, supplemented by voluntary contributions from governments and other donors. As CER prices collapsed, donor contributions became the dominant source of revenue. Under decisions taken at the Katowice climate conference in 2018, the Fund also serves the Paris Agreement.

Direct access

The Fund pioneered a "direct access" modality: developing countries can nominate National Implementing Entities (NIEs) which, once accredited, receive and manage funds directly rather than through multilateral intermediaries. This model was later adopted by the Green Climate Fund. Projects have included coastal protection, climate-resilient agriculture, water management and early-warning systems, typically capped in their early years at US$10 million per country.
