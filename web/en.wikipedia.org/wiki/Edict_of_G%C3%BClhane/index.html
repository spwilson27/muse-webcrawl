The Edict of Gülhane (Turkish: Gülhane Hatt-ı Şerifi, "Supreme Edict of the Rosehouse") was a proclamation by Ottoman Sultan Abdulmejid I on 3 November 1839 that launched the Tanzimat period of reforms and reorganization in the Ottoman Empire. It was read aloud in the Gülhane (Rosehouse) garden of Topkapı Palace in Istanbul before an assembly of dignitaries and foreign diplomats.

Contents

The edict guaranteed the security of life, honour and property of all the Sultan's subjects, regardless of religion; promised a regular system of taxation to replace tax farming; and established fair, regular methods of recruiting soldiers and fixed terms of military service. It also pledged public trials and equality before the law.

Context and significance

The edict was largely the work of the reformist foreign minister Mustafa Reşid Pasha. Issued in the midst of the crisis with Muhammad Ali of Egypt, it was intended in part to win the support of the European powers and to undercut separatist sentiment among the Empire's non-Muslim populations by promising them legal equality. It was followed by further reforms, including the Reform Edict of 1856 and the Ottoman constitution of 1876.
