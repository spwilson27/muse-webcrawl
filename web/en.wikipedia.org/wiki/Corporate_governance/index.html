Corporate governance
Key frameworks: OECD Principles, Sarbanes–Oxley Act (2002), UK Corporate Governance Code

Corporate governance is the system of rules, practices and processes by which a company is directed and controlled, balancing the interests of shareholders, management, customers, suppliers, financiers, government and the community.

Principles

Core principles include accountability, transparency, fairness, responsibility and risk management. The OECD Principles of Corporate Governance (1999, revised 2004, 2015 and 2023 with sustainability provisions) are the international benchmark.

Structures

Models differ by country: the Anglo-American unitary board with independent directors, the German two-tier board with employee codetermination, and Japanese keiretsu-influenced boards. Mechanisms include audit and remuneration committees, external audits, shareholder voting, and disclosure rules.

Debates

Agency theory focuses on aligning managers with shareholders; stakeholder theory broadens duties. Scandals at Enron and WorldCom prompted the Sarbanes–Oxley Act; the 2008 financial crisis spurred reforms on risk oversight and executive pay. ESG investing has expanded governance's scope to environmental and social issues.
