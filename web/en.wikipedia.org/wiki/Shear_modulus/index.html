Shear modulus

In materials science, the shear modulus or modulus of rigidity, denoted by G, is a measure of the elastic shear stiffness of a material. It is defined as the ratio of shear stress to shear strain:

G = τ / γ

where τ is the shear stress and γ is the shear strain. The shear modulus is one of several quantities for measuring the stiffness of materials; together with Young's modulus and the bulk modulus, it fully describes the elastic behaviour of an isotropic material.

Common symbolsG
SI unitpascal (Pa)
DerivationsG = E / (2(1 + ν))

Explanation

The shear modulus describes a material's response to shear stress — forces that act parallel to a surface, tending to deform the material by sliding one part relative to another without changing its volume. In the elastic regime, the deformation is proportional to the applied stress and is recovered when the stress is removed. Fluids have a shear modulus of zero in the static sense, since they cannot sustain a static shear stress; their resistance to shearing is described instead by viscosity.

Relation to other moduli

For an isotropic, homogeneous material, the shear modulus is related to Young's modulus E and Poisson's ratio ν by:

G = E / (2(1 + ν))

Typical values for steel are around 79 GPa, for aluminium about 26 GPa, and for glass roughly 26 GPa. Rubber has a very low shear modulus, on the order of megapascals.

Waves

The speed of transverse shear waves (S-waves) in a solid depends on the shear modulus and the density ρ of the material: vs = √(G/ρ). This relationship is central to seismology, where the absence of S-waves in the Earth's outer core demonstrates that it is liquid.
