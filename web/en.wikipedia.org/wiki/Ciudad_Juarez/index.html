Ciudad Juarez

Ciudad Juárez (often shortened to Juárez) is a city in the Mexican state of Chihuahua, on the south bank of the Rio Grande, directly across the border from El Paso, Texas. Together the two cities form one of the largest binational metropolitan areas in the world, with a combined population of well over two million.

History

The settlement was founded in 1659 as El Paso del Norte ("the pass of the north") by Franciscan missionaries, who built the Guadalupe Mission, whose church still stands. It was renamed in 1888 in honour of President Benito Juárez, who had taken refuge there during the French intervention in Mexico. During the Mexican Revolution the city was the scene of major battles in 1911 and 1913–14 involving forces loyal to Francisco Madero and Pancho Villa.

Economy and society

Juárez became a major manufacturing centre under the maquiladora programme, hosting hundreds of assembly plants linked to the United States market. In the 1990s and 2000s the city drew grim international attention for a long series of murders of women (femicides), and from 2008 to about 2012 it was among the most violent cities in the world as cartels fought over smuggling routes; violence has since fallen substantially, though it remains a concern.
