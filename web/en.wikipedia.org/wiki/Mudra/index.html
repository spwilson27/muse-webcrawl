Mudra

OriginIndian religions — Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism
Meaning"Seal", "mark", "gesture" (Sanskrit)

A mudra (Sanskrit: मुद्रा, "seal", "mark", or "gesture") is a symbolic or ritual hand gesture used in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. While some mudras involve the whole body, most are performed with the hands and fingers. Mudras are used in religious iconography, ritual, yoga, meditation, and classical Indian dance.

In Buddhist art

Statues of the Buddha display a canon of mudras, each conveying a specific meaning: the bhumisparsha (earth-touching) mudra symbolises the Buddha's enlightenment and his calling of the earth goddess to witness it; the dhyana mudra signifies meditation; the abhaya mudra denotes peace and freedom from fear; the dharmachakra mudra represents the turning of the wheel of the law; and the vitarka and karuna mudras indicate discussion and compassion respectively.

In Hindu practice and yoga

In Hindu ritual and iconography, deities are depicted with characteristic mudras, and gestures accompany worship and temple dance, where elaborate hand vocabularies express narrative meaning. In hatha yoga, mudras are practices — including hand gestures and internal "locks" involving the breath and muscles — said to direct the flow of prana (vital energy) in the body. Well-known hand mudras include the jnana mudra (thumb and index finger joined) used in meditation.
