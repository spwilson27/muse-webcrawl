Graduation

[Image placeholder: University graduation ceremony]

Graduation is the awarding of a diploma to a student by an educational institution, and the ceremony at which students receive their degrees or diplomas. The date of the graduation ceremony is often called graduation day. The graduation ceremony itself is also called commencement, convocation or congregation, depending on the country and institution.

History

Ceremonies of academic completion date to the first European universities of the twelfth century, where scholars were admitted to the guild of masters. Many modern customs — academic dress of gowns, hoods and caps — descend from medieval university practice, in which degrees were licensed by the church and the universities.

Ceremony

A typical ceremony involves a procession of academic staff and graduands, speeches by officials and sometimes a guest speaker or valedictorian, and the calling of each graduate's name as they cross a stage to receive a diploma or have a hood placed over their shoulders. In the United States, graduates traditionally wear a cap and gown, move the tassel of the mortarboard from right to left upon conferral, and celebrate by throwing their caps into the air.

By level

Graduation marks completion at many levels: secondary school (high school), undergraduate bachelor's degrees, and postgraduate master's and doctoral degrees. Professional qualifications and vocational programmes also hold graduation ceremonies. In some countries, ceremonies exist even for kindergarten and primary school transitions, though these are largely ceremonial.

Related customs

Associated traditions include the graduation party, class rings, yearbook signing, senior proms in North American high schools, and the giving of diplomas in a scroll tied with ribbon. Honorary degrees may be conferred on distinguished guests at the same ceremony.
