Thomas Percy (Gunpowder Plot)

Thomas Percy
Born: c. 1560
Died: 8 November 1605, Holbeche House, Staffordshire
Known for: Gunpowder Plot conspirator

Thomas Percy (c. 1560 – 8 November 1605) was a member of the group of provincial English Catholics led by Robert Catesby who planned the failed Gunpowder Plot of 1605, an attempt to blow up the House of Lords during the State Opening of Parliament and kill King James I.

Background

Percy was a descendant of the powerful Percy family, Earls of Northumberland, and served as a constable at Alnwick Castle for his kinsman Henry Percy, 9th Earl of Northumberland. A tall, imposing man known to be "wild" in his youth, he converted to Catholicism and became deeply committed to the Catholic cause, delivering messages between English Catholics and the exiled community abroad.

Role in the plot

Percy was one of the five original conspirators sworn in May 1604. His position and name were used to rent the undercroft beneath the House of Lords into which the plotters moved 36 barrels of gunpowder, and a house in Lambeth where supplies were stored. His prominent movements shortly before 5 November drew attention, and after the plot was discovered he fled London with Catesby and others.

Death

Percy was killed on 8 November 1605 at Holbeche House in Staffordshire, reportedly by the same musket shot that killed Catesby, fired by John Streete, a marksman in the sheriff's posse. The Earl of Northumberland, though not involved, was imprisoned in the Tower of London for years on suspicion of complicity.
