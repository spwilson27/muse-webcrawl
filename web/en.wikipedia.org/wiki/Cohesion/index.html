Cohesion (chemistry)

Cohesion (from Latin cohaesio, "act of sticking") is the stickiness between molecules of the same type or substance. Cohesive forces are frequently caused by hydrogen bonding and Van der Waals forces. Liquids that have noticeable cohesive properties are known as cohesive liquids. A related concept is adhesion, the attraction between unlike molecules or between a molecule and a surface.

Cause

The cohesive forces between molecules share some macroscopic characteristics with tension in the surface of a liquid. Surface tension is a net downward pulling force that occurs at the surface of a liquid because the topmost layer of molecules is not bonded on all sides. Surface tension contributes to capillary action, which is when the liquid surface rises up in a narrow tube, as in a thermometer.

Water

A water molecule has four hydrogen bond donors and acceptors in total, two of each, and thus the structure of liquid water is a vast three-dimensional network of intermolecular hydrogen bonds, collectively termed the hydrogen bond network. The cohesion of water causes droplets to bead up on hydrophobic surfaces and gives water its high surface tension.
