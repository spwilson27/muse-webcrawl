American Jewish Committee

The American Jewish Committee (AJC) is a Jewish advocacy organisation founded in New York in 1906. Created partly in response to pogroms against Jews in the Russian Empire, it works on combating antisemitism, supporting Israel's security, interreligious relations, immigration and democratic civil rights.

History

Early AJC leaders favoured quiet diplomacy and legal defence. The organisation documented Nazi persecution, assisted refugees within the limits and controversies of the period, supported the creation of Israel, and took part in American civil-rights coalitions. It helped fund and encourage research and litigation connected with desegregation and religious liberty.

Contemporary work

AJC maintains offices and partnerships internationally, produces the journal Commentary's historical sibling tradition through earlier publications, and issues surveys of American and Israeli Jewish opinion. It convenes diplomatic forums and campaigns against extremism. Like other communal organisations, it sometimes debates strategy with more progressive or more hawkish Jewish groups.
