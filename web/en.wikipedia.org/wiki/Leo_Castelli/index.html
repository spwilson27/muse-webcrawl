Leo Castelli (born Leo Krausz; 4 September 1907 – 21 August 1999) was an Italian-American art dealer who became the most influential gallerist of postwar American art. His Castelli Gallery in New York, opened in 1957, launched or sustained the careers of Jasper Johns, Robert Rauschenberg, Roy Lichtenstein, Andy Warhol, James Rosenquist, and many others, and helped shift the centre of the art world from Paris to New York.

Early life

Castelli was born in Trieste, then part of Austria-Hungary, to a Hungarian Jewish banking family. He studied law at the University of Milan and worked in insurance and banking in Bucharest and Paris before fleeing Europe at the outbreak of the Second World War, settling in New York in 1941. He became a United States citizen and served in the US Army.

Career

In 1951 Castelli helped organise the landmark "Ninth Street Show" of Abstract Expressionist artists. He opened his own gallery on East 77th Street in 1957. In February 1958 he gave Jasper Johns his first solo exhibition, which was a sensation; Alfred Barr bought several works for the Museum of Modern Art. Castelli went on to champion Pop art, Minimalism, and Conceptual art, paying his artists monthly stipends — an innovation that professionalised the dealer–artist relationship.

Legacy

Castelli's archive and library were donated to the Smithsonian Institution's Archives of American Art. He is widely regarded as the dean of postwar art dealers, credited with creating the modern market for contemporary American art.
