Donation of Pepin

The Donation of Pepin, made in 756, granted the pope control of a large territory in central Italy, forming the legal basis of the Papal States, which endured until 1870.

Donation of Pepin
Date: 756
Donor: Pepin the Short, King of the Franks
Recipient: Pope Stephen II
Result: Creation of the Papal States

Background

In 751 the Lombards under Aistulf seized Ravenna, ending Byzantine rule in central Italy, and threatened Rome. Pope Stephen II crossed the Alps in 753–754 — the first pope to do so — and appealed to Pepin the Short, who had been anointed king with papal approval. At Quierzy in 754 Pepin promised to restore the seized lands to the pope rather than to Byzantium.

The donation

After two Frankish campaigns (755 and 756) defeated the Lombards, Pepin fulfilled his promise: the keys of some twenty-two cities of the former Exarchate of Ravenna and the Pentapolis were laid on the tomb of St Peter. Charlemagne confirmed and enlarged the donation in 774. The grant made the pope a temporal ruler and bound the Papacy to the Frankish — later Carolingian — alliance for centuries.
