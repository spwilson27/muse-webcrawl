Euphorbiaceae
Kingdom:Plantae
Order:Malpighiales
Family:Euphorbiaceae Juss.
Genera:c. 218 genera, c. 6,700 species

Euphorbiaceae, the spurge family, is a large family of flowering plants. In common English, the family is sometimes called the euphorbias, which is also the name of a genus in the family. Most spurges, such as Euphorbia paralias, are herbs, but some, especially in the tropics, are shrubs or trees, such as Hevea brasiliensis. Some, like Euphorbia pulcherrima (poinsettia), are succulent and resemble cacti because of convergent evolution.

Description

Members of the family often contain a milky latex sap that can be irritating or toxic. The leaves are generally alternate and simple, with stipules. The flowers are unisexual, and in the genus Euphorbia they are reduced and grouped into a specialised inflorescence called a cyathium, which mimics a single flower.

Distribution

The family is essentially cosmopolitan in distribution, with the greatest diversity in the tropics, particularly in the Neotropics and tropical Africa.

Uses

The family includes several economically important plants: the rubber tree (Hevea brasiliensis), the castor oil plant (Ricinus communis), cassava (Manihot esculenta), physic nut (Jatropha curcas), and numerous ornamentals such as poinsettia and croton.
