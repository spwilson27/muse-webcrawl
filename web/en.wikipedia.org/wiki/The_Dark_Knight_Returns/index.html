The Dark Knight Returns

Batman: The Dark Knight Returns is a 1986 four-issue comic book miniseries written and drawn by Frank Miller, inked by Klaus Janson, and coloured by Lynn Varley, published by DC Comics. It tells the story of a 55-year-old Bruce Wayne who comes out of retirement to fight crime in a dystopian Gotham City, culminating in a confrontation with Superman at the behest of the United States government.

Plot

A decade after Batman's retirement, Gotham is terrorised by a gang called the Mutants. Wayne returns as Batman, joins forces with a new Robin — thirteen-year-old Carrie Kelley — and defeats the Mutant Leader in a public mud-pit fight. A rehabilitated Two-Face and a catatonic Joker are drawn back into conflict; the Joker kills himself in a way that frames Batman for murder. As Cold War tensions escalate, Superman diverts a Soviet nuclear missile but is weakened by the blast and the ensuing nuclear winter; ordered to arrest Batman, he fights him in Crime Alley, where Batman, using powered armour, kryptonite and allies, prevails but fakes his own death. Wayne thereafter devotes himself to training an underground army.

Reception and legacy

One of the most acclaimed and influential superhero comics ever published, the series — alongside Watchmen — helped redefine the genre for adult readers in the 1980s and popularised the collected graphic-novel format through its best-selling trade paperback. Its grim vision of Batman shaped nearly all subsequent portrayals. Miller wrote sequels, The Dark Knight Strikes Again (2001) and The Dark Knight III: The Master Race (2015), and a two-part animated film adaptation appeared in 2012–2013.
