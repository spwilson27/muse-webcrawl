Domra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The domra (Russian: домра) is a long-necked lute with a round body, a member of the balalaika family of Russian and Ukrainian folk instruments. It has three or four metal strings and is played with a plectrum, producing a bright, tremolo-rich sound. In Russian folk orchestras the domra carries the principal melodic line, much as the violin does in a symphony orchestra.

History

The domra was played in Russia in the 16th and 17th centuries by skomorokhi, itinerant minstrels, but was suppressed and largely disappeared. In 1896 the musician Vasily Andreyev, founder of the modern Russian folk orchestra, reconstructed the three-string domra from an instrument found in Vyatka province, working with luthier Semyon Nalimov. A four-string version, tuned like a violin, was developed later in Moscow.

Types

Domras are built in a family of sizes matching orchestral voices: piccolo, prima (the most common), alto, tenor, bass, and contrabass. The prima domra typically has a scale length of about 60–63 cm. Three-string instruments are tuned in fourths (E–A–D), while four-string domras are tuned in fifths like the violin or mandolin family.
