Euro NCAP
Formation:1997
Type:Voluntary vehicle safety rating system
Headquarters:Leuven, Belgium
Region served:Europe

The European New Car Assessment Programme (Euro NCAP) is a European voluntary car safety performance assessment programme based in Leuven, Belgium. Formed in 1997 and backed by several European governments, motoring and consumer organisations, Euro NCAP publishes safety reports on new cars and awards an overall rating based on the car's performance in crash tests and its safety technologies, graded from zero to five stars.

History

Euro NCAP published its first results in February 1997. Initially resented by some manufacturers, whose models scored poorly, the programme became influential as consumers began to demand higher-rated cars, and ratings steadily improved. The assessment was expanded over the years to include pedestrian protection, child occupant protection, and active safety systems such as autonomous emergency braking.

Testing

The test programme includes frontal impact, side impact, pole impact, and whiplash tests, together with assessments of vulnerable road user protection and safety assist systems. Since 2009 an overall star rating combines scores in four areas: adult occupant, child occupant, vulnerable road users, and safety assist. Protocols are regularly updated and become progressively more demanding.

Comparable programmes

Euro NCAP is a member of Global NCAP and works alongside similar programmes in other markets, including programmes in North America, Australia, Japan, China, Latin America, and Southeast Asia.
