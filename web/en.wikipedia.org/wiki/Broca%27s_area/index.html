Broca's area

Broca's area
LocationPosterior inferior frontal gyrus, dominant hemisphere
ComponentsPars opercularis (BA 44), pars triangularis (BA 45)
FunctionSpeech production, language processing
Named afterPaul Broca (1861)

Broca's area is a region in the frontal lobe of the dominant hemisphere, usually the left, of the brain with functions linked to speech production. It comprises the pars opercularis and pars triangularis of the inferior frontal gyrus, corresponding to Brodmann areas 44 and 45.

Discovery

The region is named for the French physician Paul Broca, who in 1861 reported impairments in two patients who had lost the ability to speak after injury to the posterior inferior frontal gyrus. His most famous patient, Louis Victor Leborgne, nicknamed "Tan" for the only syllable he could utter, became a landmark case in the localisation of brain function.

Function and damage

Broca's area is involved in speech production, syntactic processing and aspects of language comprehension. Damage produces Broca's aphasia (expressive aphasia), characterised by slow, effortful, telegraphic speech with relatively preserved comprehension. Modern imaging shows the area also participates in action understanding and working memory, and it communicates with Wernicke's area via the arcuate fasciculus.
