The United States Army Research Laboratory (ARL) is the United States Army's corporate research laboratory, conducting fundamental and applied research in the sciences underpinning land warfare. Formed in 1992 by consolidating seven existing Army laboratories—including the historic Ballistic Research Laboratory at Aberdeen Proving Ground—ARL became the Army's central organization for basic research.

Research areas

ARL organizes its work into competencies spanning computational sciences, network and information sciences, sensors and electron devices, weapons and materials research (including armour and energetic materials), vehicle technology, survivability and lethality analysis, human research and engineering, and atmospheric sciences, based at sites including White Sands Missile Range. Its supercomputing facilities form part of the Department of Defense High Performance Computing Modernization Program.

Open campus model

Since the mid-2010s ARL has pursued an "open campus" model, embedding researchers at universities and industry sites through regional outposts (ARL West in California, ARL South in Texas, ARL Central in Illinois, and ARL Northeast in Massachusetts) and collaborative research alliances with academic consortia. Notable historical products of ARL and its predecessors include contributions to ballistics science, the BRL-CAD solid modelling system, and early work in computing carried out for the Ballistic Research Laboratory, which commissioned the ENIAC.
