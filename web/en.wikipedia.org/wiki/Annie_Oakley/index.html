Annie Oakley (born Phoebe Ann Mosey; 13 August 1860 – 3 November 1926) was an American sharpshooter and exhibition shooter who became an international star in Buffalo Bill's Wild West show. Nicknamed "Little Sure Shot" — a name said to have been given to her by the Lakota leader Sitting Bull — she was celebrated for feats such as splitting a playing card edge-on and shooting cigarettes from her husband's lips.

Early life

Oakley was born in a log cabin in Darke County, Ohio. After her father's death she spent part of her childhood in poverty and in an orphanage, but she learned to trap and shoot game, selling it to support her family. At fifteen she won a shooting match against the travelling marksman Frank E. Butler, whom she married in 1876.

Career

Oakley and Butler joined Buffalo Bill's Wild West show in 1885, and she remained one of its headline acts for some seventeen seasons, performing before royalty in Europe, including Queen Victoria. She advocated for women's self-defence and marksmanship, teaching thousands of women to shoot. She was badly injured in a train wreck in 1901 and in a car accident in 1922, but continued to set shooting records into her sixties.

Legacy

Her life inspired the Irving Berlin musical Annie Get Your Gun (1946), loosely based on her story, and numerous films and television series.
