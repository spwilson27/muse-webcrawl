Soprano saxophone

The soprano saxophone is a higher-register variety of the saxophone, a woodwind instrument invented by Adolphe Sax in the 1840s. It is pitched in B♭, one octave above the tenor saxophone, and is the third smallest member of the saxophone family in regular use, after the sopranino and soprillo. Most soprano saxophones are straight-bodied, resembling a clarinet, though curved and saxello-shaped variants exist.

Characteristics

The soprano saxophone is notoriously difficult to play in tune, owing to its small mouthpiece and sensitivity to embouchure. Its range is typically from written A♭ below middle C up to two and a half or three octaves above. Its tone is bright and penetrating, sometimes described as oboe-like.

Use in music

Although a standard member of the concert band and saxophone quartet (where it takes the highest part), the soprano found its most famous exponents in jazz: Sidney Bechet in the 1920s, Johnny Hodges, and later John Coltrane, whose 1961 recording of "My Favorite Things" revived the instrument's popularity among jazz musicians. Wayne Shorter, Steve Lacy, Branford Marsalis and Kenny G — the best-selling soprano player of the smooth jazz era — are also closely associated with it. In classical repertoire it features in Ravel's Boléro and orchestral works by Milhaud and others.
