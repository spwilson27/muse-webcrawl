Henry Steel Olcott

Henry Steel Olcott (2 August 1832 – 17 February 1907) was an American military officer, journalist, lawyer, and the co-founder and first President of the Theosophical Society. He is remembered especially for his work in the revival of Buddhism in Ceylon (Sri Lanka) and for founding Buddhist schools there.

Life

Olcott served as a colonel in the Union Army during the American Civil War and afterwards worked as a lawyer and journalist investigating spiritualist phenomena. With Helena Blavatsky, William Quan Judge, and others, he founded the Theosophical Society in New York in 1875. The headquarters moved to India — eventually to Adyar, Madras — and Olcott travelled widely in Asia promoting Theosophy and Asian religions.

Buddhist revival

Arriving in Ceylon in 1880, Olcott and Blavatsky publicly took the Buddhist refuges and precepts. Olcott campaigned for Buddhist civil rights under British colonial rule, founded the Buddhist Theosophical Society, established schools including Ananda College and Dharmaraja College, wrote a widely used Buddhist Catechism (1881), and helped design the international Buddhist flag. His efforts earned him lasting honour in Sri Lanka, where he is regarded as a hero of the Buddhist revival. He died at Adyar in 1907.
