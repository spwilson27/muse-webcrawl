Aulus Vitellius (24 September AD 15 – 20 December AD 69) was Roman emperor from April to December AD 69, the third of the four emperors who ruled in the "long year" after Nero's death — the Year of the Four Emperors. Commander of the legions of Germania Inferior, he was proclaimed emperor by his troops, defeated Otho at the First Battle of Bedriacum, and was himself overthrown and killed by the advancing forces of Vespasian.

Life and rise

Son of Lucius Vitellius, a three-times consul and censor under Claudius, Vitellius cultivated the courts of Tiberius, Caligula, Claudius, and Nero — Suetonius's biography is scathing about his sycophancy and gluttony, though modern historians discount some of the invective. He was consul in AD 48 and later governor of Africa. Galba appointed him to command in Lower Germany late in AD 68, where his generosity with donatives and discipline made him popular with the legions.

Emperor

On 2 January AD 69 the legions of Upper Germany refused the oath to Galba; those of Lower Germany proclaimed Vitellius emperor (he took the name Germanicus). His generals Caecina and Valens marched on Italy, defeating Otho's forces at Bedriacum (near Cremona) on 14 April; Otho committed suicide, and the Senate recognised Vitellius. His eight-month reign, per Tacitus and Suetonius, was marked by extravagance, purges, and a vain attempt at military credibility. Meanwhile, on 1 July the eastern legions proclaimed their general Vespasian, whose forces under Antonius Primus invaded Italy, won the Second Battle of Bedriacum in October, and fought their way into Rome in December amid fierce street fighting — the Temple of Jupiter on the Capitoline burned.

Death

Vitellius attempted to abdicate but was prevented by his supporters; when Vespasian's troops took the city on 20 December AD 69, he was dragged from hiding, tortured and killed on the Gemonian stairs, and his body thrown into the Tiber. His brother and son were also killed. Whatever his failings — ancient sources stressed his gluttony and indolence — his fall closed Rome's first imperial civil war and inaugurated the Flavian dynasty.
