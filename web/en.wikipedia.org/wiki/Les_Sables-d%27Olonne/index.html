Les Sables-d'Olonne (French pronunciation: [le sabl dɔlɔn], "the sands of Olonne") is a seaside town and commune in the Vendée department in the Pays de la Loire region in western France. On 1 January 2019 it merged with the neighbouring communes of Château-d'Olonne and Olonne-sur-Mer, forming an enlarged commune of the same name.

Sailing

The town is internationally famous as the start and finish port of the Vendée Globe, the solo non-stop round-the-world yacht race held every four years, first run in 1989–90. Its marina, Port Olona, hosts the race fleet, and the town also hosts the start of other major ocean races.

Geography and history

Situated on the Atlantic coast, the town developed as a fishing port — historically important for cod and later tuna and sardine fishing — and from the 19th century as a bathing resort, with a long sandy beach (the Grande Plage) facing south into the bay. The older quarter of La Chaume, across the harbour channel, is the traditional sailors' district, protected by the Arundel tower lighthouse. During the Second World War the town formed part of the German Atlantic Wall defences.
