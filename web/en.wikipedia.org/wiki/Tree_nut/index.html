Tree nut

Tree nuts are the nuts of trees, as a culinary and regulatory category. They include almonds, Brazil nuts, cashews, chestnuts, hazelnuts (filberts), macadamia nuts, pecans, pine nuts, pistachios, and walnuts. Botanically, several of these are not true nuts — almonds and cashews are seeds of drupes, for example — but they are grouped together in food labelling and allergy regulation. Peanuts, by contrast, are legumes grown underground and are categorised separately.

Production and use

Tree nuts are energy-dense foods rich in unsaturated fats, protein, fibre, vitamins, and minerals, and are eaten raw, roasted, or salted, and used in confectionery, baking, nut butters, and oils. Major producers include the United States (which dominates almond and pistachio production, largely in California), Turkey (hazelnuts), and Iran (pistachios).

Allergy

Tree nut allergy is one of the most common food allergies and a frequent cause of anaphylaxis; it is usually lifelong. In the United States, tree nuts are among the major food allergens that must be declared on labels under the Food Allergen Labeling and Consumer Protection Act (2004) and the FASTER Act (which added sesame). Coconut is treated as a tree nut in US labelling practice despite being a drupe.
