4X

A grand-strategy map typical of the 4X genre: explore, expand, exploit, exterminate.

4X is a subgenre of strategy video games and board games in which players control an empire and must "explore, expand, exploit, and exterminate". The term was coined by game journalist Alan Emrich in his 1993 preview of Master of Orion for Computer Gaming World, adapting the "XXX" rating pun to describe empire-building games descended from Sid Meier's Civilization (1991) and Reach for the Stars (1983).

The four X's

Explore — players begin with a small foothold in a largely unknown world and send scouts to reveal the map, find resources, rivals, and good sites for expansion. Expand — players claim territory by founding new settlements or conquering those of others. Exploit — players harness the resources of controlled territory, developing infrastructure, economies, and technology. Exterminate — players attack and eliminate rival factions, though most 4X games also offer diplomatic, economic, scientific, or cultural paths to victory.

Characteristics

4X games are typically long and complex, featuring technology trees, diplomacy, resource management, and the administration of many settlements and military units. They may be turn-based, as in the Civilization series, Master of Orion, and Age of Wonders, or real-time, as in Sins of a Solar Empire and Stellaris. Settings range from history and fantasy to space opera. Board games such as Twilight Imperium and Eclipse are also described as 4X. The genre differs from grand strategy games mainly in its emphasis on starting small and building an empire from scratch, and from real-time strategy games in its slower pace, larger scale, and economic depth.
