Histone acetylation and deacetylation

Histone acetylation
Enzymes: Histone acetyltransferases (HATs)
Removed by: Histone deacetylases (HDACs)
Effect: Generally activates transcription

Histone acetylation and deacetylation are epigenetic modifications in which lysine residues on the N-terminal tails of core histones are acetylated and deacetylated as part of gene regulation. Acetylation is catalysed by histone acetyltransferases (HATs) and reversed by histone deacetylases (HDACs).

Mechanism

Adding an acetyl group neutralises the positive charge of lysine, weakening the interaction between histones and negatively charged DNA and producing a more open chromatin structure (euchromatin) accessible to transcription machinery. Acetyl marks are also read by bromodomain-containing proteins. Deacetylation restores the charge and favours repression.

Significance

Dysregulated acetylation is implicated in cancer and neurological disorders; HDAC inhibitors such as vorinostat are approved anticancer drugs, and the balance of HAT/HDAC activity is important in memory formation.

References: Kouzarides, "Chromatin Modifications and Their Function", Cell (2007).
