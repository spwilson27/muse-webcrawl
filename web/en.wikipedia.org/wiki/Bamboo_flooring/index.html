Bamboo flooring

MaterialBamboo (typically Moso, Phyllostachys edulis)
FormsHorizontal, vertical, strand-woven, engineered
UsesResidential and light commercial flooring
HardnessUp to ~5,000 lbf (Janka) for strand-woven

Bamboo flooring is a type of flooring manufactured from the bamboo plant. The majority of today's bamboo flooring products originate in China and other portions of Asia. Moso bamboo (Phyllostachys edulis) is the species most commonly used for flooring because of its size, density, and rapid growth.

Characteristics

Because bamboo is a grass rather than a wood, it reaches maturity in roughly three to five years, far faster than hardwood trees. Strips of the culm are cut, dried, laminated under pressure, and milled into planks. Strand-woven bamboo, produced by compressing shredded bamboo fibres with resin under extreme pressure, is among the hardest flooring materials available, exceeding many hardwoods on the Janka hardness scale.

Types

Horizontal flooring shows the wide, flat face of the bamboo strip with visible nodes. Vertical flooring turns the strips on edge, giving a finer, more uniform grain. Strand-woven products have a denser, more wood-like appearance. Engineered bamboo flooring bonds a bamboo wear layer to a plywood or fibreboard core for improved dimensional stability.

Environmental aspects

Bamboo's rapid renewability has made it popular with environmentally conscious consumers. However, concerns include formaldehyde emissions from some adhesives, transportation impacts from Asian manufacturing, and the conversion of natural forests to bamboo monocultures in some regions.

Installation and maintenance

Planks may be nailed, glued, or floated like comparable laminate and engineered wood products. Bamboo flooring can be refinished a limited number of times depending on wear-layer thickness, and it is sensitive to excessive moisture.
