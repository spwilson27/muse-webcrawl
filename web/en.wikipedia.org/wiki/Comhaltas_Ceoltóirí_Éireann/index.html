Comhaltas Ceoltóirí Éireann (pronounced roughly "COAL-tus kyol-TOR-ee AIR-un"; Irish for "Society of the Musicians of Ireland") is the primary Irish organisation dedicated to the promotion and preservation of Irish traditional music, song, dance, and the Irish language. Founded in 1951 in Mullingar, it grew from a small circle of traditional musicians and enthusiasts into a worldwide movement with several hundred branches.

A session at the Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann, the All-Ireland music festival run by Comhaltas.

Activities

Comhaltas organises classes in traditional instruments (fiddle, tin whistle, uilleann pipes, button accordion, concertina, harp, banjo, and others), céilí dancing, and Irish language. Its network of fleadhanna (festivals) culminates in the Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann, the All-Ireland Fleadh, which draws hundreds of thousands of visitors and competitors each August. It also runs the SCT examination system in traditional music, publishes recordings and the magazine Treoir, operates cultural centres such as Cultúrlann na hÉireann in Monkstown, Dublin, and maintains an archive of traditional music.

Influence

Comhaltas is widely credited with helping to rescue Irish traditional music from mid-20th-century decline and carries significant cultural and political influence in Ireland. It has occasionally been criticised for standardising regional styles through its competition system, a tension debated within the traditional music community.
