Tehsildar

In Bangladesh, India, and Pakistan, a tehsildar, also spelled tahsildar, is a revenue and administrative officer in charge of a tehsil (also called a taluka or mandal in some regions), a sub-district administrative unit. Tehsildars are typically gazetted officers recruited through state civil service examinations, and they form a key link between the district administration, headed by the District Collector, and local village-level officials.

Duties

The tehsildar's functions vary somewhat between states and provinces, but commonly include:

  • Collection and assessment of land revenue and maintenance of land records, including records of rights and crop inspection
  • Executive magistracy within the tehsil, including powers relating to public order and preventive proceedings
  • Issuing certificates such as caste, income, domicile, and solvency certificates
  • Conducting elections and census operations within the tehsil
  • Relief and rehabilitation work during floods, droughts, and other natural calamities
  • Supervising subordinate revenue staff such as naib tehsildars, revenue inspectors (kanungos), and village accountants (patwaris or lekhpals)

Terminology and hierarchy

The word derives from tehsil, an Arabic-origin term for revenue collection adopted into Persian and Urdu administrative usage. In the administrative hierarchy, the tehsildar works under the Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO) or Assistant Collector and above the naib tehsildar. In some Indian states the equivalent officer is called a mamlatdar (Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Goa) or a mandal revenue officer (MRO) in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. The office has roots in the revenue systems of the Mughal Empire and was formalised under British colonial administration.
