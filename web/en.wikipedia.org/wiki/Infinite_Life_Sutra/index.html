Infinite Life Sutra

The Infinite Life Sutra (Sanskrit: Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra, the "Longer" Sukhāvatīvyūha) is one of the principal scriptures of Pure Land Buddhism and of Mahāyāna Buddhism generally. It describes the Pure Land of Sukhāvatī, the Buddha Amitābha, and the forty-eight vows by which the bodhisattva Dharmākara — who became Amitābha — pledged to create a realm where beings could easily attain enlightenment.

Content

The sutra opens with the Buddha Śākyamuni preaching on Vulture Peak to a great assembly. It recounts how, in a past age, the monk Dharmākara, inspired by the Buddha Lokeśvararāja, made forty-eight vows describing the qualities of the pure land he aspired to create and the means by which beings could be born there — above all, sincere faith and recitation of Amitābha's name. The text then describes Sukhāvatī in lavish detail: jewelled trees, lotus ponds, and the absence of suffering, and closes with exhortations, the story of Ajātaśatru, and instructions for preserving the sutra.

History and translations

The sutra was composed in India, probably in the 1st–2nd century CE, and translated into Chinese several times; the version attributed to Saṅghavarman (康僧鎧) in the 3rd century became standard in East Asia. It is the longest of the three principal Pure Land sutras, the others being the Amitābha Sutra (the "Shorter" Sukhāvatīvyūha) and the Contemplation Sutra. Together they form the doctrinal basis of Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese Pure Land traditions, including Jōdo-shū and Jōdo Shinshū in Japan.

Significance

The eighteenth vow — promising birth in the Pure Land to those who sincerely aspire to it and call Amitābha's name even ten times — became the scriptural foundation for the nembutsu practice central to Japanese Pure Land Buddhism.
