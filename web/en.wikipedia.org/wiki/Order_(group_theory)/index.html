Order (group theory)

Order

Field: Group theory, abstract algebra

Notation: |G|, ord(a)

In group theory, the order of a group is the number of its elements, that is, its cardinality. The order of an element of a group is the smallest positive integer m such that raising the element to the power m gives the identity element; if no such m exists, the element is said to have infinite order.

Order of a group

For a finite group G, the order is written |G| or ord(G). A group of order 1 contains only the identity and is called the trivial group. Groups of prime order have a particularly simple structure: every group of prime order p is cyclic, generated by any of its non-identity elements.

Lagrange's theorem states that for any finite group G, the order of every subgroup H divides the order of G. An immediate corollary is that the order of every element of a finite group divides the order of the group, and hence that a|G| = e for every element a. This result underlies Euler's theorem in number theory and the correctness of the RSA cryptosystem.

Order of an element

The order of an element a, written ord(a), equals the order of the cyclic subgroup generated by a. In the additive group of integers modulo n, for example, the element 1 has order n, while in the multiplicative group of units modulo n the order of a unit is the smallest m with am ≡ 1 (mod n).

Elements of finite order are called torsion elements; a group in which every element has finite order is a torsion group. Infinite groups may mix elements of finite and infinite order, as does the group of rational numbers under addition, in which every non-zero element has infinite order.

Counting results

The possible orders of finite groups are the subject of deep restrictions. The Sylow theorems guarantee subgroups of order pk for each prime power dividing |G|, and the Feit–Thompson theorem shows that every group of odd order is solvable. The classification of finite simple groups gives a complete list of the building blocks from which all finite groups are assembled.
