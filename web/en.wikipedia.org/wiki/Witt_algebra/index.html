Witt algebra

In mathematics, the Witt algebra, named after Ernst Witt, is the Lie algebra of meromorphic vector fields on the Riemann sphere that are holomorphic except at two fixed points (conventionally 0 and ∞). Equivalently, it is the complexification of the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields on a circle, and the Lie algebra of derivations of the ring C[z, z−1] of Laurent polynomials. The Witt algebra is infinite-dimensional and is the classical (non-centrally-extended) algebra underlying two-dimensional conformal field theory; its unique central extension is the Virasoro algebra.

Definition

A basis is given by the vector fields

Ln = −zn+1 ∂/∂z,    n ∈ Z,

which satisfy the commutation relations

[Lm, Ln] = (m − n) Lm+n.

The subalgebra spanned by L−1, L0 and L1 is isomorphic to sl2(C), corresponding to the globally defined (Möbius) vector fields on the Riemann sphere and to the generators of translations, dilations and special conformal transformations.

History and applications

Witt introduced the algebra in 1937 in the context of Lie algebras over fields of positive characteristic, where finite-dimensional analogues (the Witt algebras W(1; n)) became central examples of simple Lie algebras in the classification of simple Lie algebras in characteristic p. In characteristic zero the algebra resurfaced in physics as the symmetry algebra of the classical string and of two-dimensional conformal field theory, where the Virasoro algebra — the central extension with the famous cocycle term (m3 − m)δm+n,0c/12 — governs representations through the central charge.

Related algebras

Generalised Witt algebras are defined as derivation algebras of Laurent polynomial rings in several variables, and super-analogues include the Neveu–Schwarz and Ramond superconformal algebras. Over the real numbers, the Witt algebra is the complexification of the algebra of smooth vector fields on the circle, whose continuous version is sometimes also called the Witt (or Virasoro, after completion) algebra of diffeomorphisms of the circle.
