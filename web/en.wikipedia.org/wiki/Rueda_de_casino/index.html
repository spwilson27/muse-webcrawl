Rueda de casino

Rueda de casino (or simply rueda, Spanish for "casino wheel") is a style of Cuban salsa dancing in which pairs of dancers form a circle and perform synchronised figures in response to calls made by one person, the cantante (caller). Dancers frequently exchange partners as part of the called moves, giving the dance its dynamic, communal character and its resemblance to a wheel turning.

Origins

Rueda developed in Havana in the 1950s alongside casino, the Cuban partner dance that evolved into salsa. At beach clubs and dance halls such as the Casino Deportivo, groups of friends began dancing in circles, executing the same turns and partner changes together. The form is usually credited to the milieu of Havana's casinos and to callers who standardised hand signals—useful where the music was loud—alongside spoken calls in Spanish.

Structure and calls

Couples maintain salsa's basic step (guapea) in a circle while the caller names figures such as dame ("give me one"—pass to the next partner), enchufa, dile que no, vacila, sombrero and setenta. Complex ruedas add structures—multiple rings, dancers moving to the centre, or subgroups splitting off—plus comic routines and stops. Because the caller can improvise the sequence, no two ruedas are identical; the challenge is in synchronisation and quick responses, including calls specific to local scenes.

Spread

Rueda spread internationally with the salsa boom of the 1990s and 2000s, especially after Cuban dancers and instructors emigrated to Miami, Europe and beyond. It is now a fixture of salsa congresses and social dancing worldwide, and mass-participation "flash mob" ruedas are staged annually in many cities.
