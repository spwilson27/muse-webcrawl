Diana Ross

Diana Ross (born March 26, 1944) is an American singer and actress. Known as the "Queen of Motown Records", she was the lead singer of the vocal group the Supremes, who became Motown's most successful act during the 1960s and one of the world's best-selling girl groups of all time.

The Supremes

Ross, with Mary Wilson and Florence Ballard, signed to Motown in 1961. The group scored twelve number-one US singles, including "Where Did Our Love Go", "Baby Love", "Stop! In the Name of Love" and "You Can't Hurry Love".

Solo career

Launching her solo career in 1970, Ross reached number one with "Ain't No Mountain High Enough". Her hits include "Touch Me in the Morning", "Love Hangover", the theme from Mahogany ("Do You Know Where You're Going To"), "Upside Down", "Endless Love" (with Lionel Richie) and "I'm Coming Out". As an actress she was Oscar-nominated for playing Billie Holiday in Lady Sings the Blues (1972) and starred in Mahogany and The Wiz.

Honours

Ross received the Kennedy Center Honors in 2007, the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2012, and the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016. In 2021 she released Thank You, her first studio album in 15 years.
