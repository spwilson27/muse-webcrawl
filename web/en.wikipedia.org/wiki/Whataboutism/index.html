Whataboutism

Whataboutism (also spelled whataboutery) is a variant of the tu quoque logical fallacy that attempts to discredit an opponent's position by charging them with hypocrisy without directly refuting or disproving their argument. The term derives from constructions of the form "What about...?" used to deflect criticism.

History

The technique was associated particularly with the Soviet Union during the Cold War. When criticised by the Western world, Soviet officials frequently responded with charges of the form "And in the United States, they lynch Negroes," redirecting attention to the critics' own failings. The phrase entered wider English-language use in the late 1970s and 1980s, and gained renewed prominence in the 2010s in commentary on Russian and American political discourse.

Analysis

Commentators have noted that whataboutism differs from legitimate demands for consistency: rather than answering a charge, it changes the subject to the alleged misdeeds of the accuser. Critics argue this erodes moral discourse, while others caution that the label can itself be used to deflect legitimate criticism of double standards.
