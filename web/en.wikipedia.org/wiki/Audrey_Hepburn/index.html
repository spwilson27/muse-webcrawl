Audrey Hepburn (born Audrey Kathleen Ruston; 4 May 1929 – 20 January 1993) was a British actress. Recognised as a film and fashion icon, she has been ranked by the American Film Institute as the third-greatest female screen legend from the Classical Hollywood cinema. She was inducted into the International Best Dressed List Hall of Fame and is one of few entertainers to have won Academy, Emmy, Grammy and Tony Awards (EGOT).

Hepburn in a 1950s studio portrait.

Early life

Born in Ixelles, Brussels, to a Dutch baroness and a British-Austrian banker, Hepburn spent parts of her childhood in Belgium, England and the Netherlands. During the German occupation of the Netherlands she studied ballet in Arnhem and performed in — and carried messages and money for — the Dutch resistance; she witnessed the deportation of Dutch Jews and suffered malnutrition during the Dutch famine of 1944–45. After the war she continued ballet training in Amsterdam and London and began acting in small film and stage roles.

Career

Hepburn's breakthrough came with the title role in the Broadway play Gigi (1951), followed by her first major film, Roman Holiday (1953), for which she won the Academy Award for Best Actress. She went on to star in Sabrina (1954), Funny Face (1957), The Nun's Story (1959), Breakfast at Tiffany's (1961) — her portrayal of Holly Golightly is considered one of cinema's most iconic — Charade (1963), My Fair Lady (1964) and Wait Until Dark (1967). Her collaboration with designer Hubert de Givenchy defined her enduring style. She acted less frequently from the late 1960s, making her final film appearance in Always (1989).

Humanitarian work and death

From 1988 Hepburn worked as a UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador, travelling to Ethiopia, Sudan, Somalia, Vietnam and Bangladesh; she was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1992. She died of appendiceal cancer at her home in Tolochenaz, Switzerland, in January 1993.
