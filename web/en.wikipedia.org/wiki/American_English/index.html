American English (AmE) is the set of varieties of the English language native to the United States. English is the most widely spoken language in the US and the de facto common language used by the federal government, though the country has no official language at the federal level. American English differs from British English in pronunciation, vocabulary, spelling, and some grammar, and its global influence has grown with American media and technology.

History

English was brought to North America by colonists from 1607 onward. After independence, Noah Webster deliberately standardised a distinct American spelling — color rather than colour, center rather than centre, defense rather than defence — through his "Blue-backed Speller" (1783) and An American Dictionary of the English Language (1828).

Phonology and dialects

Most American accents are rhotic (pronouncing /r/ after vowels). Major dialect regions include the Inland North (site of the Northern Cities Vowel Shift), the Midland, the South, the West, and Eastern New England, beside ethnic and social varieties such as African-American English and Chicano English. "General American" describes the relatively neutral accent used in national broadcasting.

Vocabulary and spelling

American English borrowed terms from Native American languages (moose, skunk), Dutch (cookie, boss), Spanish (canyon, ranch), and many immigrant languages, and coined words such as sidewalk, truck (for lorry), apartment, and fall (for autumn). Spelling conventions include -ize endings, single consonants in traveled, and forms like aluminum.
