The American-British-Dutch-Australian (ABDA) Command, or ABDACOM, was a short-lived joint Allied command established in January 1942 to coordinate land, sea and air forces defending the "Malay Barrier" against Japanese expansion in South-East Asia at the start of the Pacific War.

Formation

ABDACOM was agreed at the Arcadia Conference in Washington and formally activated on 15 January 1942 under British General Sir Archibald Wavell, with American, British, Dutch and Australian deputies for the naval (Admiral Thomas C. Hart), air (Air Chief Marshal Sir Richard Peirse) and ground components. Its area stretched from Burma through Malaya and the Dutch East Indies to northern Australia.

Operations and collapse

The command inherited a hopeless strategic situation: the Royal Navy had lost HMS Prince of Wales and Repulse, Singapore's garrison was overwhelmed, and Japanese air superiority was overwhelming. Allied naval forces under Dutch Rear Admiral Karel Doorman fought delaying actions in the Makassar Strait and the Badung Strait. Singapore surrendered on 15 February 1942. With the position untenable, ABDACOM was dissolved on 25 February; two days later Doorman's striking force was destroyed at the Battle of the Java Sea, and Java itself surrendered in March.

Assessment

Historians regard ABDACOM as an improvised experiment in coalition warfare: it revealed the difficulties of multinational command, incompatible equipment and communications, and divergent national priorities, lessons later applied in the more successful South West Pacific Area and South East Asia Command structures.
