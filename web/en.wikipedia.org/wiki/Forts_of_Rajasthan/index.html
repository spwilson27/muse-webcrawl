Forts of Rajasthan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The forts of Rajasthan are the great strongholds built by the Rajput kingdoms of northwestern India, typically on the hills of the Aravalli Range. Combining massive defensive walls with palaces, temples, and water reservoirs, they embodied Rajput ideals of kingship and warfare from roughly the 7th to the 19th centuries.

Hill Forts of Rajasthan (UNESCO)

In 2013, UNESCO inscribed six forts collectively as the "Hill Forts of Rajasthan" World Heritage Site: Chittorgarh Fort, Kumbhalgarh Fort, Ranthambore Fort, Gagron Fort, Amber Fort, and Jaisalmer Fort. The listing recognises their architecture, which adapted to hill terrain and synthesised defensive and residential functions, as well as the Rajput culture they preserve.

Other notable forts

Other major forts of the state include Mehrangarh Fort at Jodhpur, Junagarh Fort at Bikaner, Nahargarh and Jaigarh above Jaipur, Taragarh at Ajmer, and the Lohagarh Fort at Bharatpur. Many remain major tourist attractions and house museums and hotels.
