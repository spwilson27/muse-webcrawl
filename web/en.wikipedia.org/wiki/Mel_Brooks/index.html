Mel Brooks (born Melvin James Kaminsky; June 28, 1926) is an American actor, comedian, and filmmaker. With a career spanning over seven decades, he is known as a writer and director of a variety of successful broad farces and parodies. He is one of the few entertainers to have won an Emmy, a Grammy, an Oscar, and a Tony Award (the EGOT).

Brooks began his career as a comic and writer for Sid Caesar's Your Show of Shows, working alongside Neil Simon, Carl Reiner, and Larry Gelbart. With Reiner he created the "2000 Year Old Man" comedy act. He co-created the television series Get Smart with Buck Henry.

Film career

Brooks's directorial debut, The Producers (1968), won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay. In 1974 he released two of the most successful comedies of the decade, Blazing Saddles and Young Frankenstein. His other films include The Twelve Chairs (1970), Silent Movie (1976), High Anxiety (1977), History of the World, Part I (1981), Spaceballs (1987), and Robin Hood: Men in Tights (1993). Through his production company Brooksfilms he also produced serious works such as The Elephant Man (1980).

Stage

Brooks adapted The Producers into a Broadway musical (2001) that won a record 12 Tony Awards, and later adapted Young Frankenstein for the stage (2007). His memoir All About Me! was published in 2021.
