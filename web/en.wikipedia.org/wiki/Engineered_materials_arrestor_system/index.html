Engineered materials arrestor system

An engineered materials arrestor system, engineered materials arresting system (EMAS), or arrestor bed is a bed of engineered materials built at the end of a runway to reduce the severity of the consequences when an aircraft overruns the end of a runway. Engineered materials means high energy absorbing materials of selected strength, which will reliably and predictably crush under the weight of an aircraft. While the surface is similar in appearance to road safety gravel pits, its purpose is distinct.

Design

An EMAS bed is typically made of lightweight, crushable cellular cement or foamed glass blocks, set in a bed at the end of the runway within the runway safety area. When an aircraft enters the bed, its landing gear sinks into the material, which crushes and creates drag that decelerates the aircraft to a stop over a short distance, with minimal damage to the aircraft. EMAS beds are designed to stop an aircraft travelling at up to about 70 knots (130 km/h) on exiting the runway.

History and use

The technology was developed in the 1990s through research sponsored by the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and was first installed at John F. Kennedy International Airport in New York in 1996. EMAS installations have since been placed at more than one hundred airports in the United States and internationally, and have safely arrested numerous overrunning aircraft, from small business jets to wide-body airliners, with no fatalities in EMAS arrestments.
