The Siege of Constantinople in 626 by the Pannonian Avars, assisted by Slavic, Bulgar, and Gepid auxiliaries, together with a Sasanian Persian army encamped across the Bosporus at Chalcedon, was the climax of the Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628. While Emperor Heraclius campaigned deep in Persian territory, the defense of the capital was left to Patriarch Sergius I and the magister militum Bonus.

The siege

The Avar khagan arrived before the Theodosian Walls in late June 626 with a vast host and sophisticated siege equipment, including siege towers and catapults. The Persians, however, could not cross the Bosporus, which was controlled by the Byzantine fleet, so the two allies could never effectively combine. The main assault, launched on 7 August, combined land attacks on the walls with a naval attack by Slavic monoxyla (dugout boats) in the Golden Horn; the boats were ambushed and destroyed by Byzantine ships, and the land assault failed. The khagan burned his siege engines and withdrew.

Aftermath

The failed siege broke Avar power and marked the turning point of the war: Heraclius went on to invade Persia and win the decisive Battle of Nineveh (627), leading to the overthrow of Khosrow II and a peace restoring the pre-war borders. Byzantine tradition attributed the city's salvation to the intercession of the Theotokos (the Virgin Mary), whose icon was carried along the walls; the deliverance is commemorated in the Feast of the Akathist.
