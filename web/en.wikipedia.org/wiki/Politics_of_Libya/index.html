Politics of Libya

Tripoli, Libya's capital and seat of the internationally recognised government

The politics of Libya have been in flux since the overthrow of Muammar Gaddafi in the 2011 civil war. Libya has since experienced rival governments, renewed civil war from 2014 to 2020, and an ongoing, fragile UN-backed political process aimed at reunifying the country and holding national elections.

The Jamahiriya

From 1969, when Gaddafi seized power in a coup, until 2011, Libya was officially governed under Gaddafi's "Third Universal Theory" as the Great Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriya, a system of nominal direct democracy through people's congresses and committees in which political parties were banned and real power rested with Gaddafi and his inner circle.

Post-2011 transition

After Gaddafi's fall, the National Transitional Council handed power in 2012 to an elected General National Congress. Disputes between rival factions erupted into civil war in 2014, splitting the country between a government in Tripoli and authorities based in the east, where Field Marshal Khalifa Haftar's Libyan National Army became the dominant force. A UN-brokered agreement in 2021 installed a Government of National Unity under Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh in Tripoli, but planned elections were postponed, and a rival Government of National Stability was appointed by the eastern-based House of Representatives in 2022, perpetuating the division.

Economy and oil

Libya's politics are dominated by competition over its oil wealth, administered through the National Oil Corporation, and by the influence of armed groups and foreign backers, including Turkey, Russia, Egypt, and the United Arab Emirates.
