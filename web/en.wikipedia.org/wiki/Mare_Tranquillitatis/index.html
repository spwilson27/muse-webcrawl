The Mare Tranquillitatis ("Sea of Tranquility") is a lunar mare located in the Tranquillitatis basin on the Moon. It is best known as the landing site of Apollo 11, where on 20 July 1969 humans first walked on the Moon. The mare material within the basin consists of basalt formed by ancient volcanic eruptions; the surrounding basin itself was formed by a very large impact during the early history of the Moon.

Formation and characteristics

The Tranquillitatis basin was created during the Nectarian period, and the mare basalts that fill it were emplaced over hundreds of millions of years. Unlike most other maria, the Mare Tranquillitatis has an irregular shape and lacks a distinct mascon (mass concentration). Its margins merge with the Mare Serenitatis to the northwest and the Mare Fecunditatis to the southeast. Samples of basalt returned by Apollo 11 were about 3.6 billion years old and were notable for their high titanium content.

Exploration

The first spacecraft to land on the Moon in the Mare Tranquillitatis was the robotic Ranger 8 probe in February 1965, which returned close-up photographs before impact. Surveyor 5 landed in the mare in September 1967 and conducted a chemical analysis of the soil. On 20 July 1969, the Apollo 11 Lunar Module Eagle, carrying Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin, landed in the southwestern part of the mare, at a site subsequently named Tranquility Base. Armstrong's first words on the surface included "Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed."

Naming

The name Mare Tranquillitatis, like those of the other lunar maria, dates to the nomenclature of the Italian astronomer Giovanni Battista Riccioli in 1651, who named the dark plains after seas and states of mind.
