River gunboat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A shallow-draught river gunboat of the type used on the Yangtze in the early 20th century

A river gunboat is a type of gunboat adapted for river operations. River gunboats are usually the largest watercraft operable on a given river, specially designed with a shallow draught so that they can operate in shallow, inland waters. They are lightly armed and armoured, carrying guns and machine guns for use against targets ashore and small craft, and are intended to protect river traffic, support land forces, and project military and diplomatic presence far inland.

History

River gunboats came to prominence in the 19th century as European powers and the United States expanded into the interiors of continents along great rivers. During the American Civil War, both Union and Confederate forces operated armoured river gunboats — the Union's "Pook turtles" of the City class — on the Mississippi and its tributaries.

British, French, German, Russian and American gunboats patrolled the rivers of China, most famously the Yangtze, under treaty rights obtained in the 19th century; the United States Navy maintained the Yangtze Patrol until 1941. River gunboats were used on the Nile during the Sudan campaigns, on the Tigris and Euphrates in Mesopotamia during the First World War, on the Danube by Austria-Hungary and Romania (whose river monitors were powerful examples of the type), and on the rivers of Indochina, where the French Dinassaut units and later US "brown-water navy" forces fought during the Vietnam War.

Design

Because rivers are shallow and obstructed, river gunboats typically draw only a few feet of water, have flat bottoms and twin screws in tunnels, and may be built in sections for transport and reassembly upriver. Larger river craft such as the Austro-Hungarian and Romanian monitors carried turret-mounted guns of substantial calibre; smaller patrol craft emphasised machine guns and grenade launchers for close support of troops.

Modern use

Riverine warfare continues in countries with major river systems: Colombia operates heavily armed riverine support craft ("nodriza" gunboats) on its jungle rivers, Brazil patrols the Amazon, and various navies and coast guards operate river patrol boats for border security and counter-insurgency.
