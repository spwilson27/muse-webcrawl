Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Westminster

The Archdiocese of Westminster is a Latin archdiocese of the Catholic Church in England. It covers much of London north of the Thames and west of the Lea, the county of Hertfordshire and the borough of Spelthorne in Surrey. Its cathedral is Westminster Cathedral in Victoria, London, and its archbishop is traditionally regarded as the leader of the Catholic Church in England and Wales and is usually made a cardinal.

History

The archdiocese was erected in 1850 when Pope Pius IX restored the Catholic hierarchy in England and Wales by the bull Universae Ecclesiae, ending the system of vicars apostolic. Nicholas Wiseman became its first archbishop and cardinal. His successors included Henry Edward Manning, Herbert Vaughan (founder of Westminster Cathedral, consecrated 1903), Arthur Hinsley, William Godfrey, John Heenan, Basil Hume, Cormac Murphy-O'Connor and Vincent Nichols.

Organisation

Westminster is the metropolitan see of a province including the suffragan dioceses of Brentwood, East Anglia, Northampton and Nottingham. It serves a Catholic population of several hundred thousand through more than 200 parishes and is the largest diocese in England and Wales by Catholic population.
