DIY ethic

The DIY ethic is the ethos of self-sufficiency associated with the "do it yourself" (DIY) movement: the belief that individuals and communities should create, repair, and organize things themselves rather than relying on corporations, professionals, or established institutions. As an ethic, it goes beyond practical home improvement to express values of independence, anti-consumerism, and self-reliance.

Punk culture

The DIY ethic is most strongly associated with punk rock culture of the late 1970s onward. Rejecting the major-label music industry, punk bands self-recorded and self-released records, booked their own tours, and produced fanzines (zines) with photocopiers. Labels such as Dischord Records, venues run as all-ages collectives, and the maxim "DIY or die" embodied the approach, which spread to hardcore punk, riot grrrl, and indie music generally.

Wider applications

The ethic underpins maker culture and hackerspaces, open-source software, craft and repair movements (including repair cafés and right-to-repair advocacy), independent publishing, guerrilla gardening, and aspects of environmental and anarchist politics. Critics note tensions when DIY aesthetics are commercialized or when self-reliance shifts burdens onto individuals.
