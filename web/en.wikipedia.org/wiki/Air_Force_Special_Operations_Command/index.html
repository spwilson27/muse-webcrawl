Air Force Special Operations Command

Headquarters: Hurlburt Field, Florida
Established: 22 May 1990
Parent: United States Air Force; component of U.S. Special Operations Command

Air Force Special Operations Command (AFSOC), headquartered at Hurlburt Field, Florida, is the special operations component of the United States Air Force and the Air Force component of United States Special Operations Command (USSOCOM). Established as a major command on 22 May 1990, AFSOC organises, trains, and equips airmen for special operations missions worldwide.

Missions and forces

AFSOC's core missions include precision strike, special operations intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance, infiltration and exfiltration of special operations forces, combat search and rescue support, and aviation foreign internal defense. Its distinctive aircraft include the AC-130 gunship, MC-130J Commando II, CV-22 Osprey, MQ-9 Reaper, and U-28A Draco. Its ground forces include Special Tactics airmen — combat controllers, pararescuemen, special operations weather technicians, and tactical air control party specialists — among the most highly trained in the U.S. military.

History

Air Force special operations trace to the air commandos of the Second World War and operations in Korea and Vietnam. After the lessons of the failed 1980 Iran rescue mission (Operation Eagle Claw) and the creation of USSOCOM in 1987, Air Force special operations forces were consolidated under AFSOC. The command has supported operations in Panama, the Gulf War, Somalia, the Balkans, Afghanistan, and Iraq.
