Delta wing

The delta wing is a wing shaped in the form of a triangle. It is named for its similarity in shape to the Greek uppercase letter delta (Δ). Although long studied, it did not find significant application until the Jet Age, when it proved suitable for high-speed subsonic and supersonic flight. At the other end of the speed scale, the Rogallo flexible wing proved to be a practical design for the hang glider and other ultralight aircraft. The delta wing form has unique aerodynamic characteristics and structural advantages.

The delta wing planform, as used on aircraft such as the Mirage 2000 and Concorde.

General characteristics

A delta wing has a sharply swept leading edge and a fairly straight trailing edge. The angle of sweep is typically around 50–60 degrees. The planform offers a large wing area for its span, a stiff and light structure, and good high-speed performance. Its disadvantages include high induced drag at low speed and a high angle of attack on landing.

History

The triangular planform was pioneered in Germany before and during the Second World War, notably by Alexander Lippisch, whose work influenced later designs. Post-war, the delta was adopted widely: the British Avro Vulcan bomber, the French Dassault Mirage series, the Swedish Saab 35 Draken, and the Anglo-French Concorde all used versions of the delta planform.

Aerodynamics

At high angles of attack, a delta wing generates vortex lift: airflow separates at the sharp leading edge and rolls up into stable vortices over the upper surface, producing additional lift. This allows delta-winged aircraft to fly at very high angles of attack without a conventional stall, at the cost of substantial drag.

Variants

Cropped delta

The wingtips are cut off, reducing drag and structural loads; used on many modern fighters.

Ogee planform

The ogee (ogival) planform, with a smoothly curved S-shaped leading edge, was developed for Concorde to balance low-speed and supersonic performance.

Tailless delta

Many delta designs omit a separate horizontal stabiliser, relying on elevons at the trailing edge for pitch and roll control.
