Phosphocreatine

Phosphocreatine, also known as creatine phosphate, is a phosphorylated molecule that serves as a rapidly mobilisable reserve of high-energy phosphates in skeletal muscle, the heart and the brain. It can anaerobically donate a phosphate group to ADP to form ATP during the first seconds of intense muscular effort, through a reaction catalysed by the enzyme creatine kinase.

The phosphocreatine system — sometimes called the ATP-PCr or phosphagen system — buffers cellular ATP concentrations and also acts as an intracellular energy shuttle between mitochondria and sites of ATP use.
