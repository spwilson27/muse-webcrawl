Purushartha

Purushartha (Sanskrit: पुरुषार्थ, puruṣārtha) literally means the "object of human pursuit". It is a key concept in Hinduism, referring to the four proper goals or aims of a human life: dharma (righteousness, duty), artha (prosperity, wealth), kama (pleasure, love) and moksha (liberation, release).

The four aims

The four purusharthas are traditionally listed in the order dharma, artha, kama and moksha. Dharma is considered the foundational aim that regulates the pursuit of artha and kama, while moksha is regarded as the ultimate goal, liberation from the cycle of rebirth (samsara).

Origins

The concept developed over centuries in Indian thought. Early texts emphasised a triad (trivarga) of dharma, artha and kama; moksha was added later as the fourth and transcendent aim, particularly in the Upanishads and in the soteriological traditions.

Balance of aims

Hindu philosophy generally teaches that the aims should be pursued in balance: wealth and pleasure are legitimate when governed by dharma, and the householder stage of life is the setting in which artha and kama are properly enjoyed before renouncing them in pursuit of moksha.
