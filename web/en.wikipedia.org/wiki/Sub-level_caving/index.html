Sub-level caving

Sub-level caving (SLC) is an underground mining method used to extract massive, steeply dipping ore bodies. In SLC, ore is drilled and blasted from a series of sub-levels developed at regular vertical intervals within the ore body, while the overlying waste rock (the hanging wall) is allowed to cave in and fill the void left by the extracted ore.

Method

Development consists of drifts driven on each sub-level, typically 8 to 15 metres apart vertically. Production rings of holes are drilled upward in a fan pattern and blasted against the caved material; broken ore is mucked out from the drift until dilution from the caved waste becomes excessive. The process proceeds top-down, and the surface above the mine commonly subsides as caving propagates upward. Large-scale SLC operations include the Kiruna and Malmberget iron ore mines in Sweden, operated by LKAB.

Advantages and disadvantages

SLC offers relatively low operating costs, high mechanisation, and early production, but it suffers from high ore dilution, some ore loss in the caved zone, and surface subsidence that precludes its use beneath sensitive infrastructure.
