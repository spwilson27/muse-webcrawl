The St Leger Stakes is a Group 1 flat horse race in Great Britain open to three-year-old thoroughbred colts and fillies. It is run at Doncaster over a distance of 1 mile, 6 furlongs and 115 yards (2,921 metres), and is scheduled to take place each year in September. Established in 1776, it is the oldest of Britain's five Classics and the final leg of the English Triple Crown, following the 2000 Guineas and the Derby, and of the Fillies' Triple Crown with the 1000 Guineas and the Oaks.

History

The race was devised by Lieutenant-Colonel Anthony St Leger, an army officer and politician who lived near Doncaster, and was first run in 1776 at Cantley Common, moving to Doncaster's Town Moor course two years later. It was named after its founder at a dinner following the second running. Initially run over two miles, the distance was shortened over the centuries to its present trip, which makes the St Leger the longest and most stamina-testing of the Classics. The event anchors Doncaster's four-day St Leger Festival, the highlight of the town's social and racing calendar since the 18th century.

Records and notable winners

Leading jockey honours belong to Bill Scott, who rode nine winners in the 19th century, while trainer John Scott saddled a record 16 winners. Notable winners include Nijinsky (1970), the last horse to complete the English Triple Crown, and champion stayers such as Oh So Sharp, who completed the Fillies' Triple Crown in 1985. Fillies carry a weight allowance against colts and have a long record of success in the race.
