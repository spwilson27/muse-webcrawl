Christian IV (12 April 1577 – 28 February 1648) was King of Denmark and Norway from 1588 until his death in 1648. His reign of nearly 60 years is the longest of any Danish monarch, and of any Scandinavian monarch. A member of the House of Oldenburg, he is remembered as a energetic builder-king and reformer, though his interventions in the Thirty Years' War ended in failure.

Reign

The son of Frederick II, Christian came to the throne as a minor and was crowned in 1596. He modernised the navy, encouraged trade and manufactures, and founded numerous towns, including Christiania (now Oslo) in Norway and Christianshavn in Copenhagen. His building projects transformed Copenhagen: the Round Tower (Rundetaarn), the Børsen (Stock Exchange), Rosenborg Castle and the rebuilt Frederiksborg Castle date from his reign.

Wars

Christian won the Kalmar War against Sweden (1611–1613), but his intervention in the Thirty Years' War as a Protestant champion ended in defeat at the Battle of Lutter (1626) and the Peace of Lübeck (1629). In the Torstenson War (1643–1645) Denmark lost territory to Sweden; the king famously lost an eye from splinters at the naval Battle of Colberger Heide in 1644 while commanding his flagship Trefoldigheden.

Family

He married Anne Catherine of Brandenburg in 1597; after her death he married Kirsten Munk morganatically, with whom he had many children before their acrimonious separation. He fathered at least 24 children in total. He died at Rosenborg Castle in 1648 and was succeeded by Frederick III.
