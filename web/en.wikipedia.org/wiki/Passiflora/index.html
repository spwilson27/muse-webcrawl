Passiflora

Passiflora, known commonly as the passion flowers or passion vines, is a genus of about 550 species of flowering plants in the family Passifloraceae. Most are climbing vines native to the Americas, with others in Asia, Australia, and the Pacific.

Description

The flowers have a distinctive structure with five sepals, five petals, a conspicuous corona of filaments, five stamens, and three stigmas — features Spanish missionaries associated with the Passion of Christ, giving the genus its name. The fruit, the passion fruit, is a berry containing many seeds.

Ecology and uses

Passiflora species are host plants for many butterfly larvae, including Heliconius species, in a well-studied coevolutionary relationship involving cyanogenic glycosides. Several species, notably Passiflora edulis, are cultivated commercially for fruit; others are grown as ornamentals, and P. incarnata is used in herbal medicine.
