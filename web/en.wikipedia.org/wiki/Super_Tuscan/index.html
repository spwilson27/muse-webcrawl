Super Tuscan

Super Tuscan wines such as Sassicaia and Tignanello rank among Italy's most prized

Super Tuscan (or Supertuscan) is an unofficial term for a style of high-end red wine from Tuscany, Italy, made outside the rules of the traditional DOC(G) appellations — typically by blending the native Sangiovese grape with international varieties such as Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc and Merlot, or by using those French varieties alone. Because the wines originally could only be labelled as lowly vino da tavola (table wine), producers and critics coined "Super Tuscan" to signal their prestige. Many became among the most expensive and sought-after wines in the world.

History

The movement's roots lie at Tenuta San Guido in Bolgheri, where Marchese Mario Incisa della Rocchetta planted Cabernet Sauvignon in the 1940s; the resulting wine, Sassicaia, was released commercially from the 1968 vintage and won international acclaim. In 1971 the Marchesi Antinori released Tignanello, a Sangiovese–Cabernet blend aged in small French barriques and made without the white grapes then required in Chianti Classico — a deliberate break with DOC rules. Wines such as Ornellaia, Solaia and Masseto followed, centred on the Bolgheri coast and Chianti hills.

Regulatory response

The success of the Super Tuscans exposed the rigidity of Italian wine law. In 1992 Italy created the Indicazione Geografica Tipica (IGT) category — notably "Toscana IGT" — giving the wines a respectable legal home. Sassicaia went further, gaining its own appellation, Bolgheri Sassicaia DOC, in 1994 — the only Italian wine from a single estate to hold one — and Bolgheri itself was reformed to legitimise Bordeaux-style blends.

Style and reputation

Super Tuscans are typically full-bodied, concentrated and aged in new French oak, combining Tuscan acidity and savoury character with the structure of Bordeaux varieties. They reshaped the global image of Italian wine, forced reform of the Chianti regulations (which now permit the blends the pioneers were punished for), and remain benchmarks of modern Italian winemaking.
