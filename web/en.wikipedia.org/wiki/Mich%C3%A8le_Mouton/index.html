Michèle Mouton (born 23 June 1951) is a French former rally driver. Competing in the World Rally Championship for Audi, she became the first — and remains the only — woman to win a round of the WRC, taking four victories in the Group B era and finishing runner-up in the 1982 world championship.

Career

Mouton began rallying in the early 1970s and won the French women's rally championship before joining Fiat France. Signed by Audi to drive the revolutionary four-wheel-drive Quattro in 1981, she scored a landmark victory at the Sanremo Rally that year. In 1982 she added wins in Portugal, Greece (Acropolis) and Brazil, mounting a title challenge against Walter Röhrl that ended with her second place in the championship after a difficult Ivory Coast rally undertaken shortly after her father's death.

She later won the German rally championship, the 1985 Pikes Peak International Hill Climb outright (setting a record), and co-founded the Race of Champions in 1988 with Fredrik Johnsson, in memory of Henri Toivonen. Mouton later served as manager of the World Rally Championship and as president of the FIA's Women in Motorsport Commission.

Legacy

Mouton is widely regarded as the most successful female rally driver in history, and her performances against the leading male drivers of the Group B era remain a benchmark in motorsport.
