Patio

A patio (from Spanish patio, "courtyard") is an outdoor area adjoining a residence, generally paved, used for dining, leisure, or recreation. Traditionally the term referred to the central open courtyard of a Spanish or Spanish-style house, surrounded by the building on all sides; in modern usage — particularly in Britain, North America, and Australia — it commonly denotes a paved terrace at ground level at the back or front of a house, similar to a deck but typically constructed of stone, brick, tile, or concrete rather than timber.

History

The enclosed courtyard house has ancient roots in Roman atrium houses and in the domestic architecture of the Islamic world, from which it passed into Spain during the period of Muslim rule. In Andalusia the patio became the social and climatic heart of the house: shaded walls, fountains, and plants cooled the interior during hot summers. The patios of Córdoba are especially celebrated; its Festival of the Patios, held each May since the early 20th century, was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2012.

Spanish colonial architecture carried the courtyard patio to Latin America and the American Southwest. In the 20th century, suburban development in the United States and Australia popularised the rear patio as a standard leisure space attached to detached houses.

Design and materials

Patios are typically paved with concrete slabs, natural stone, brick, terracotta, or porcelain tiles, laid on a bed of sand or mortar. Common features include:

  • Pergolas and shade structures — open-roofed frames, often planted with vines
  • Outdoor furniture — tables, chairs, and loungers for dining and relaxation
  • Planting — pots, raised beds, and climbing plants to soften the paved surface
  • Water features and fire pits — fountains in traditional courtyards; fire bowls in contemporary designs
  • Outdoor kitchens and barbecues — common in North American and Australian patios

Patio doors

The sliding glass "patio door" became a standard architectural element in post-war housing, linking the interior living space to the patio. Folding and French doors serve the same function in many modern designs, blurring the boundary between indoor and outdoor living areas.
