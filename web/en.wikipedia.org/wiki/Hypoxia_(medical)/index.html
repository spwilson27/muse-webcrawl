Hypoxia (medical)

Hypoxia is a condition in which the body or a region of the body is deprived of an adequate oxygen supply at the tissue level. It is distinct from hypoxemia, which refers specifically to low oxygen in the arterial blood; hypoxemia is one common cause of tissue hypoxia. Generalized hypoxia affects the whole body, while local hypoxia (ischemia) affects a limited region.

Classification

Traditional classification divides hypoxia into four types: hypoxemic (low arterial oxygen, as at high altitude or with lung disease), anaemic (reduced oxygen-carrying capacity, as in anaemia or carbon monoxide poisoning), stagnant/circulatory (inadequate blood flow, as in heart failure or shock), and histotoxic (cells unable to use oxygen, as in cyanide poisoning).

Signs and effects

Symptoms include breathlessness, rapid heart rate, headache, restlessness, impaired judgment, and in severe cases loss of consciousness. The brain is especially vulnerable: prolonged oxygen deprivation causes neuronal death within minutes. Chronic hypoxia, as in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease or at altitude, prompts compensations such as increased red blood cell production (polycythemia). A bluish discoloration of the skin and mucous membranes, cyanosis, may be present when deoxygenated haemoglobin is high.
