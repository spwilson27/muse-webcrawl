Housing

Housing refers to the provision of shelter in dwellings — houses, apartments and other residential buildings — in which people live. Housing encompasses the physical structures themselves, the markets and finance that produce and allocate them, and the public policies that regulate them. The right to adequate housing is recognised in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights.

Types and tenure

Dwellings range from detached single-family houses and terraced (row) houses to apartment blocks, condominiums, public (social) housing, cooperative housing and informal settlements. Tenure arrangements include owner-occupation, private renting, social renting and cooperative ownership; the balance differs greatly between countries, with homeownership exceeding 90% in some states and large rental sectors, including regulated social housing, in others.

Housing markets and affordability

Because housing supply adjusts slowly and demand is tied to land, incomes and credit, housing markets are prone to cycles and bubbles. In many major cities, house prices and rents have risen much faster than incomes since the late 20th century, producing an affordability crisis marked by overcrowding, long commutes, homelessness and delayed household formation. Policy responses include zoning reform, public housing construction, rent regulation, housing subsidies and taxation of vacant or investment properties.

Construction and sustainability

Housing is a major sector of construction and employment and a large source of energy use and emissions, driving interest in insulation, heat pumps, prefabrication and denser, transit-oriented development. Standards for health, safety and accessibility are enforced through building codes in most jurisdictions.
