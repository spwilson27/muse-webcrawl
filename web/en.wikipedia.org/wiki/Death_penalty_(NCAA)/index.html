Death penalty (NCAA)

The death penalty is the popular name for the most severe sanction available to the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA): the termination of a member school's team in a given sport for at least one year. Formally part of the "repeat violator" rule adopted in 1985, it applies to programmes that commit a second major violation within five years of a previous major penalty.

SMU and the death penalty in practice

The sanction is famous chiefly for its application to the SMU Mustangs football programme in 1987. Southern Methodist University was found to have maintained a slush fund to pay players even while already on probation. The NCAA cancelled SMU's entire 1987 season and most of its 1988 home games, banned postseason play and live television appearances, and stripped scholarships. SMU voluntarily cancelled 1988 as well. The devastation was so complete — the programme took decades to recover — that the NCAA has never again imposed the death penalty on a Division I football or basketball programme, and officials have acknowledged its collateral damage made them reluctant to use it.

Other applications

The penalty has been imposed a handful of times, mainly on smaller programmes: for example on Kentucky basketball in the 1950s (a de facto one-season ban following a point-shaving scandal), on Southwestern Louisiana basketball in 1973, and on Morehouse College soccer in 2003. In each case the affected programme was sidelined for one or more seasons.
