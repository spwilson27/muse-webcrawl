Melanie Richards Griffith (born 9 August 1957) is an American actress. The daughter of actress Tippi Hedren, she began her career as a child model and teenage film actress in the 1970s, rose to prominence in the 1980s with Body Double (1984), Something Wild (1986) and Working Girl (1988) — the last earning her a Golden Globe Award and an Academy Award nomination — and remained a prominent leading lady through the 1990s.

Early life

Griffith was born in Manhattan to advertising executive Peter Griffith and model-actress Tippi Hedren. She grew up in New York and Los Angeles, modelling as a child, and grew up around the big cats her mother kept for the film Roar (1981), in which Griffith also appeared and was badly injured by a lion.

Career

After early roles in Night Moves (1975) and The Drowning Pool (1975), Griffith's career breakthrough came with Brian De Palma's Body Double (1984), winning a National Society of Film Critics Award. Jonathan Demme's Something Wild (1986) confirmed her as a major star, and Mike Nichols's Working Girl (1988), in which she played ambitious secretary Tess McGill, brought her the Golden Globe for Best Actress in a Comedy and an Oscar nomination for Best Actress.

In the 1990s she starred in Pacific Heights (1990), Paradise (1991), Shining Through (1992), Born Yesterday (1993), Milk Money (1994), Nobody's Fool (1994), Two Much (1995) — on which she met Antonio Banderas — and Lolita (1997). She voiced characters in Stuart Little 2 (2002) and made her Broadway debut as Roxie Hart in Chicago in 2003.

Personal life

Griffith was married twice to actor Don Johnson, with whom she has a daughter, the actress Dakota Johnson. She has a son with actor Steven Bauer and a daughter with Antonio Banderas, whom she married in 1996; they divorced in 2015. She has spoken publicly about her struggles with addiction and her recovery.
