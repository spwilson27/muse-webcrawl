Sign stealing

In baseball, sign stealing is the act of observing the signs given by the opposing catcher to the pitcher and relaying them to the batter so that he knows the type of pitch about to be thrown. Stealing signs by players and coaches on the field of play, using only the naked eye, is traditionally considered acceptable gamesmanship; using mechanical or electronic aids to do so is prohibited.

Legality and custom

A runner on second base has a direct view of the catcher's signs and may relay them by body movements — a practice long embedded in the game's unwritten rules. Pitchers and catchers counter with sequences of decoy signs. By contrast, Major League Baseball rules forbid the use of electronic equipment to steal signs, a restriction reaffirmed in a 2017 memorandum and a broader 2019 directive restricting video-room access during games.

Houston Astros scandal

The practice became infamous through the Houston Astros sign stealing scandal, revealed in November 2019, in which the Astros had used a centre-field camera during their 2017 championship season (and part of 2018) to decode catchers' signs and relay them to batters by banging on a trash can. Commissioner Rob Manfred's investigation led to suspensions for manager A. J. Hinch and general manager Jeff Luhnow (both subsequently fired), a $5 million fine, and the forfeiture of draft picks; related penalties fell on Alex Cora and Carlos Beltrán. The players were granted immunity in exchange for testimony, a decision widely criticised.

Response

From 2022 MLB permitted the PitchCom system, allowing catchers to request pitches electronically and pitchers to receive them through an earpiece, reducing dependence on visible signs.
