George of Kunštát and Poděbrady (23 April 1420 – 22 March 1471) was King of Bohemia from 1458 to 1471, the only Hussite to sit on the Bohemian throne and the last king of native Czech stock before the Habsburgs.

Reign

A leader of the moderate Utraquist party in the aftermath of the Hussite Wars, George governed Bohemia as regent before his election as king in 1458. He upheld the Compactata of Basel, which allowed communion in both kinds, putting him at odds with the papacy; Pope Paul II excommunicated him in 1466 and proclaimed a crusade, and Matthias Corvinus of Hungary invaded and had himself crowned rival king in 1469.

Peace project

George is remembered for his visionary proposal (1462–1464) for a league of European Christian rulers — with a common assembly, court, and renunciation of war among members — sometimes cited as an early forerunner of European integration. He arranged for the crown to pass to the Polish Jagiellonian dynasty, and Vladislaus II succeeded him in 1471.
