Nahe (wine region)

Nahe is one of the thirteen German wine regions (Anbaugebiete) for quality wine, situated along the Nahe river in Rhineland-Palatinate, southwest of the Rheingau and west of Rheinhessen. It takes its name from the river, a tributary of the Rhine that it joins at Bingen.

Geography and climate

The region covers about 4,200 hectares of vineyards. Although one of Germany's smaller quality-wine regions, the Nahe is celebrated for an extraordinary diversity of soils compressed into a small area — the legacy of ancient volcanic activity. More than 180 distinct soil types have been identified, including porphyry, melaphyre, slate, loess and quartzite, which producers credit for the wines' finely etched mineral character.

Wines

Riesling is the dominant grape, accounting for roughly 29 per cent of plantings, followed by Müller-Thurgau and Dornfelder. Nahe Rieslings are often described as combining the elegance of the Mosel with some of the body of the Rheingau. Renowned estates include Dönnhoff, Schäfer-Fröhlich and Emrich-Schönleber, and famous vineyard sites lie around Oberhausen, Niederhausen and Monzingen.
