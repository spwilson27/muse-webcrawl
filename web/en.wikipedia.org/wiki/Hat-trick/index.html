Hat-trick

A hat-trick is the achievement of a generally positive feat three times in a match, or another achievement based on the number three. The term first appeared in 1858 in cricket, to describe H. H. Stephenson taking three wickets with three consecutive deliveries, after which fans raised a collection and presented him with a hat bought with the proceeds.

Scoring three goals in a match is the best-known form of hat-trick.

Association football

In association football (soccer), a player scores a hat-trick by netting three goals in a single game — not necessarily consecutively, and in any competition period. A "perfect hat-trick" consists of one goal with each foot and one with the head. By convention, the player who scores a hat-trick traditionally takes home the match ball. A variant, the "flawless" or "Gordie Howe hat trick" in ice hockey, combines a goal, an assist, and a fight.

Other sports

The term is used in cricket for a bowler taking three wickets in consecutive balls, in motorsport for pole position, fastest lap, and race win on the same weekend, and in many other sports for three successes in a single event. It has also spread to general usage for any triple achievement, such as three consecutive election victories or championship titles.
