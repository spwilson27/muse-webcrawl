The Commission for Historical Clarification (Spanish: Comisión para el Esclarecimiento Histórico, CEH) was a truth commission established by the Oslo Accord of 23 June 1994 between the government of Guatemala and the Unidad Revolucionaria Nacional Guatemalteca (URNG) to investigate human rights violations committed during Guatemala's 36-year internal armed conflict. Chaired by the German jurist Christian Tomuschat with Guatemalan commissioners Otilia Lux de Cotí and Alfredo Balsells Tojo, it delivered its final report, Guatemala: Memory of Silence (Guatemala: Memoria del silencio), on 25 February 1999.

Findings

The CEH documented more than 42,000 victims from testimonies and estimated that over 200,000 people were killed or disappeared during the war. It attributed 93% of the violations it documented to state forces and related paramilitary groups, and 3% to the guerrillas. It concluded that the army's counterinsurgency campaigns against Maya communities in regions including El Quiché, Huehuetenango and Alta Verapaz between 1981 and 1983 constituted acts of genocide, recording 626 massacres and the destruction of hundreds of villages, within a framework shaped by racism against the indigenous population and an anti-communist national security doctrine.

Recommendations and legacy

The commission recommended official acknowledgment and apology, reparations (later embodied in the National Reparations Program, created 2003), exhumations, educational reform and measures against impunity. President Álvaro Arzú received the report but the military rejected its conclusions. The report and the parallel church-led REMHI report (Guatemala: Nunca Más, 1998 — whose coordinator, Bishop Juan Gerardi, was murdered two days after its release) became key evidentiary foundations for later prosecutions, including the 2013 genocide trial of former head of state Efraín Ríos Montt.
