Endec

An endec (a portmanteau of encoder/decoder) is a device or functional unit that both encodes and decodes signals or data, most commonly converting between analogue and digital forms or preparing data for transmission over a communication channel and recovering it at the receiving end.

Usage

The term is used in telecommunications and computing for hardware that pairs the functions of an encoder and a decoder in one package. A codec performs a similar paired function for compression, while a modem pairs a modulator and demodulator; "endec" is most often used for line-coding and physical-layer conversion devices.

Examples

  • The Emergency Alert System (EAS) in the United States uses SAME (Specific Area Message Encoding) endecs at broadcast stations, which decode incoming alert headers, decide whether to relay them, and re-encode alerts for transmission.
  • Serial data interfaces use endecs to convert parallel data to serial form for transmission and back again at the receiver.
  • Storage devices use endecs implementing run-length limited (RLL) or similar codes to write data to magnetic media and read it back reliably.
