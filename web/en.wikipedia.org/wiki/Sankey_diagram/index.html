Sankey diagram

Sankey diagrams are a type of flow diagram in which the width of the arrows is proportional to the flow rate. The illustration shows a Sankey diagram representing all the primary energy that flows into a region or process; losses appear as flows exiting the system.

History

Sankey diagrams are named after Irish Captain Matthew Henry Phineas Riall Sankey, who used this type of diagram in 1898 in a classic figure showing the energy efficiency of a steam engine. While the first charts of this kind go back to Charles Joseph Minard's famous 1869 diagram of Napoleon's Russian campaign of 1812, Sankey's name became attached to the form through its use in engineering.

Applications

Sankey diagrams are often used in fields such as energy management and production technology, to visualize energy inputs, useful output, and wasted output. They are also used to visualize material flows, costs, website traffic migration, and voting transfers between rounds of elections. The United States Energy Information Administration and the Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory publish Sankey diagrams of national energy production and consumption annually.

Software

Sankey diagrams can be produced with general data-visualization tools; the D3.js Sankey plugin is widely used for web-based diagrams, and libraries exist for Python (Plotly, matplotlib via extensions) and R.
