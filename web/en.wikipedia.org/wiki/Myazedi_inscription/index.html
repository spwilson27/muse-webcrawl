Myazedi inscription

One face of the Myazedi inscription, Bagan, Myanmar.

The Myazedi inscription (Burmese: မြစေတီ ကျောက်စာ), also known as the Rajakumar inscription, is a stone inscription dated to about 1113 CE found at the Myazedi pagoda in Bagan (Pagan), Myanmar. It is inscribed in four languages — Burmese, Pyu, Mon and Pali — on the four faces of a stone pillar, and is famous as the earliest dated evidence for written Burmese and as a "Rosetta Stone" that enabled scholars to decipher the Pyu language.

Content

The inscription records a donation by Prince Rajakumar (Yazakumar), son of King Kyansittha of the Pagan dynasty. On his deathbed, the king had given his beloved queen's golden Buddha statue to Rajakumar rather than to the throne; the prince, though passed over as heir in favour of the king's grandson Alaungsithu, dedicated the statue, the Myazedi ("emerald") pagoda and villages of slaves to its upkeep, praying that the new king would approve the gift and that all beings would attain enlightenment. The four versions tell the same story, allowing linguists — notably Charles Otto Blagden, who worked on it in the 1910s — to unlock Pyu, the language of an earlier civilisation of the Irrawaddy valley whose script had been unread.

Significance

Two copies of the pillar exist: one still at the Myazedi pagoda and another at the nearby Kubyaukgyi temple; the Myazedi stone is the better preserved. The inscription is held in a protective shelter at the site. In 2015 it was added to UNESCO's Memory of the World register. See also Bagan and Pyu language.
