Biryani

Biryani is a mixed rice dish originating in South Asia, made with rice, meat or vegetables, and a blend of spices including saffron, cardamom, cumin, coriander, and bay leaves. It is typically prepared by layering partly cooked rice over marinated meat and cooking the sealed pot slowly — the dum pukht method — so the ingredients steam in their own juices. Biryani is served at celebrations across India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh and throughout the South Asian diaspora.

History

The name derives from the Persian birinj (rice) or biryan (fried/roasted). Layered rice dishes developed in the kitchens of the Mughal Empire, combining Persian pilaf traditions with South Asian spices, and spread with Mughal and later regional courts to Hyderabad, Lucknow, and Calcutta. Exact origins are debated and wrapped in legend, including tales of the dish being created for soldiers or popularised by Mumtaz Mahal.

Preparation

In kacchi biryani, raw marinated meat is layered with partly cooked rice and cooked together; in pakki biryani the components are cooked separately before layering. The pot is sealed with dough or a tight lid. Kolkata biryani, influenced by exiled Awadhi nawab Wajid Ali Shah's court, is distinctive for its large potato and often a boiled egg.

Regional varieties

Hyderabadi biryani is fiery and served with mirchi ka salan; Sindhi and Karachi versions are sour and spicy; Malabar biryani uses short-grain rice and coastal flavours; Tamil Nadu versions from Dindigul and Ambur use seeraga samba rice. Vegetarian, egg, fish, and prawn biryanis are common, though purists debate whether meatless versions deserve the name.
