The Saab 37 Viggen (Swedish for "the tufted duck" and "thunderbolt") is a single-seat, single-engine, short-medium range multirole combat aircraft developed and manufactured by the Swedish company Saab. It was designed to replace the Saab 35 Draken and the Saab 32 Lansen in the Swedish Air Force, and remained in service from 1971 until 2005. It was succeeded by the Saab JAS 39 Gripen.

The Viggen was notable for its distinctive double-delta wing with a close-coupled canard foreplane, its short take-off and landing (STOL) capability — intended to allow operations from dispersed road bases under Sweden's Bas 90 system — and its powerful Volvo RM8 turbofan engine, a licence-built derivative of the Pratt & Whitney JT8D fitted with a Swedish-designed afterburner and a thrust reverser enabling very short landing runs.

Development

Development began in the early 1960s under the Swedish "aircraft system 37" requirement. The design emphasised versatility: a single airframe was to perform strike, reconnaissance, maritime attack and interception missions through specialised variants. The first prototype flew on 8 February 1967, and the first production AJ 37 attack aircraft was delivered in June 1971.

Design

The Viggen's main wing is a cropped delta; ahead of it sits a smaller delta canard with flaps, generating vortices that improve lift at high angles of attack. The structure makes extensive use of bonded honeycomb panels. The type was designed for a maximum speed of Mach 2.1 at altitude and supersonic speed at low level. Its electronics included the Ericsson PS-37/A radar in attack variants, and the aircraft was the first production type to use a digital central computer (the CK 37) in its navigation and weapons system.

Variants

  • AJ 37 — primary strike variant with a secondary interceptor role.
  • SF 37 — photographic reconnaissance variant.
  • SH 37 — maritime reconnaissance and strike variant.
  • SK 37 — two-seat operational conversion trainer.
  • JA 37 Jaktviggen — dedicated interceptor with a more powerful RM8B engine, PS-46/A pulse-Doppler radar, and Skyflash missiles; entered service in 1979.

Operational history

All 329 Viggens served exclusively with the Swedish Air Force; the type was never exported, despite interest from several countries, partly because of Swedish export restrictions and US controls on the engine's technology. The last Viggens were retired on 25 November 2005, with the final flight by a display aircraft in 2007.
