Pourquoi story

Pourquoi stories explain, for example, how the leopard got its spots

A pourquoi story (from the French pourquoi, "why") is a type of folk tale or origin myth that explains why something in the world is the way it is — why the giraffe has a long neck, why the tides come in, why the moon changes shape, or how a particular animal acquired its markings, habits, or call. Such tales, known in folkloristics as etiological tales, are found in virtually every oral tradition and are especially prominent in stories told to children.

Characteristics

Pourquoi stories typically begin in a time when the world was different ("Long ago, when animals could talk...") and end with a transformation that permanently fixes the trait in question, often as a consequence of a character's actions, a trick played by a trickster figure, or a lesson about behaviour. Unlike scientific explanations, they work through narrative logic: a moral or whimsical cause produces the observed effect, and the tale is frequently humorous.

Famous examples

Rudyard Kipling's Just So Stories (1902) is the most famous literary collection of pourquoi tales, including "How the Whale Got His Throat", "How the Camel Got His Hump", and "How the Leopard Got His Spots". Kipling adapted the form from traditional sources, including Indian and African storytelling. Indigenous traditions worldwide contain such tales: Aboriginal Australian Dreaming stories explain landforms and animal features, West African tales explain why the sun and moon live in the sky, and Anansi and other trickster cycles often end with an etiological twist. The term is also used in education, where children are encouraged to write their own pourquoi stories as an introduction to creative writing and folklore.
