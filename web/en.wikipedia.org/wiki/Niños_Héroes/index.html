Niños Héroes

The Niños Héroes (Spanish for "Boy Heroes") were six Mexican military cadets who died defending Chapultepec Castle in Mexico City during the Battle of Chapultepec on 13 September 1847, in the Mexican–American War. They are honoured in Mexico as national martyrs.

The cadets

The six were Juan de la Barrera (aged 19), Juan Escutia, Agustín Melgar, Vicente Suárez, Fernando Montes de Oca and Francisco Márquez (aged 13). According to the famous account, as United States forces stormed the castle — then the seat of Mexico's Heroic Military Academy — the cadets refused orders to retreat, and Juan Escutia wrapped himself in the Mexican flag and leapt from the castle walls to prevent its capture. Historians debate details of the story, but the cadets' commemoration is deeply embedded in Mexican patriotic tradition.

Commemoration

13 September is a civic holiday in Mexico, and the six are remembered by the Altar a la Patria monument at the entrance to Chapultepec Park, unveiled in 1952. A Mexico City Metro station, streets and schools throughout the country bear the name Niños Héroes. The story contrasts with the fate of the Irish San Patricio battalion, commemorated in Mexico on the same date.
