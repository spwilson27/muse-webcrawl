Holden Commodore

Manufacturer: Holden (General Motors)
Production: 1978–2020
Class: Mid-size / full-size car
Layout: Front-engine, rear-wheel drive (1978–2017); front-wheel drive (2018–2020)

The Holden Commodore is a car that was produced by Australian manufacturer Holden from 1978 to 2020. The first three generations were based on Opel platforms with Australian engineering changes, while the fourth generation (VE, 2006) was an all-Australian design. The final ZB Commodore (2018–2020) was a rebadged, imported Opel Insignia.

First generation (1978–1988)

The VB Commodore of 1978 was derived from the Opel Rekord and Senator, strengthened for Australian conditions and offered with four-cylinder, six-cylinder, and V8 engines. It was smaller and more fuel-efficient than the Kingswood it replaced. Successive updates — VC, VH, VK, and VL — followed; the VL (1986) used a Nissan-derived RB30 six-cylinder engine, including a popular turbocharged version.

Second and third generations (1988–2006)

The VN (1988) was a larger car based on the Opel Omega, introducing the Australian-built 3.8-litre Buick-derived V6. The VR, VS, VT, VX, VY, and VZ series followed; the VT (1997) was a major export success, sold in the Middle East and adapted as the Pontiac GTO in the United States (as the Monaro coupé). V8 Supercars racing made the Commodore a fixture of Australian motorsport culture.

Fourth generation and end (2006–2020)

The VE Commodore, developed at a cost of about A$1 billion on the GM Zeta platform, was Australia's best-selling car for much of its life, but the market shifted to SUVs and smaller cars. The updated VF (2013) was the last Australian-built Commodore; production at Elizabeth ended on 20 October 2017. The imported ZB of 2018, front-wheel drive and without a V8, sold poorly, and the Commodore nameplate was discontinued in 2020.
