Operation Defensive Shield (Hebrew: מבצע חומת מגן, Mivtza Homat Magen) was a large-scale military operation conducted by the Israel Defense Forces in 2002 during the Second Intifada. It was the largest military operation in the West Bank since Israel captured the territory in the 1967 Six-Day War.

The operation was launched on 29 March 2002, two days after the Passover massacre, a suicide bombing at the Park Hotel in Netanya that killed 30 Israelis. The government of Prime Minister Ariel Sharon stated the operation's goal was to capture Palestinian militants, destroy their infrastructure, and confiscate weapons following a wave of suicide attacks against Israeli civilians.

The operation

Some 30,000 reservists were called up as Israeli forces re-entered nearly all major Palestinian cities in the West Bank, including Ramallah, Nablus, Jenin, Bethlehem, Tulkarm and Qalqilya, imposing curfews and conducting house-to-house searches. Palestinian Authority chairman Yasser Arafat was confined to his compound in Ramallah. Heavy fighting occurred in the Jenin refugee camp and in Nablus's old city, and a standoff developed at the Church of the Nativity in Bethlehem, where armed Palestinians took refuge for 39 days.

Aftermath and controversy

Israel reported hundreds of militants killed or captured and the seizure of weapons laboratories; Palestinian sources and human rights organisations reported extensive destruction of civilian infrastructure and deaths among non-combatants. The operation drew international criticism and UN scrutiny, particularly regarding events in Jenin, though a UN report rejected claims of a massacre while criticising both sides. Israel credited Defensive Shield with sharply reducing suicide bombings in the following years, and the operation led into the construction of the West Bank separation barrier.
