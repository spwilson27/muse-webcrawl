Winchester College is a public school (an independent fee-charging boarding school) in Winchester, Hampshire, England. It was founded by William of Wykeham in 1382 and has existed in its present location ever since, making it one of the oldest continuously operating schools in England. Originally established for 70 poor scholars, it was designed as a feeder to New College, Oxford, which Wykeham also founded. Pupils are known as Wykehamists, and alumni as Old Wykehamists.

History

Wykeham, Bishop of Winchester and Chancellor of England, obtained a papal bull in 1382 and a royal licence for his college of Saint Mary at Winchester; the foundation stone was laid in 1387 and the first scholars entered in 1394. The school's original statutes provided for 70 scholars, a warden, fellows, chaplains, and a headmaster, together with provision for "commoners"—fee-paying pupils—who over time came to outnumber the scholars. The medieval buildings, including the chapel, Chamber Court, and the gatehouse, survive in use.

The school was one of nine schools investigated by the Clarendon Commission in the 1860s and subsequently regulated by the Public Schools Act 1868. Long an all-boys school, Winchester admitted girls into its sixth form from 2022 and day pupils for the first time in its history.

Traditions and life

Winchester retains many distinctive traditions and its own vocabulary ("notions"). Its motto, Manners makyth man, was shared with New College, Oxford. The school's own game, Winchester College football ("Win Co Fo"), is played on distinctive pitches with ropes and nets. Scholars still wear gowns and live in the medieval College chambers; other pupils live in boarding houses in the surrounding streets.

Notable alumni

Old Wykehamists include the poet Matthew Arnold, the soldier and writer T. E. Lawrence's contemporaries, statesman Lord Grey of Fallodon, broadcaster and politician Kenneth Clark, and cricketer Douglas Jardine, among many figures in public life, academia, and the arts.
