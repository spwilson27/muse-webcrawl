Table setting

Table setting (laying a table) or place setting refers to the way to set a table with tableware — such as eating utensils and dishware — for serving and eating. The arrangement for a single diner is called a place setting. It is also the layout in which the utensils and ornaments are positioned.

Informal setting

In an informal setting, the dinner plate is placed in the centre of the setting. The fork is placed to the left of the plate and the knife to the right, with its blade facing the plate; the spoon, if needed, goes to the right of the knife. Glasses are placed above the knife. A napkin is placed on the plate, under the fork, or to the left of the fork.

Formal setting

Formal settings include additional pieces: a service plate or charger, separate forks for salad and main course, a fish knife and fork where fish is served, soup spoon, butter plate and knife, and multiple glasses for water, red wine and white wine arranged in the order of use. Cutlery is arranged so that pieces used first are on the outside, and diners work inward with each course. Dessert utensils may be placed horizontally above the plate.

Variations

Conventions differ by country. In France, forks are traditionally placed with the tines facing down, while in England the tines face up, often so that a crest or monogram on the handle remains visible. In Japan and China, chopsticks are placed on a rest in front of or beside the bowl. Etiquette guides also cover the placement of name cards, menus, and finger bowls at formal banquets.
