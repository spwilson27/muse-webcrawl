Alan Shepard

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Freedom 7 lifts off, 5 May 1961.

Alan Bartlett Shepard Jr. (18 November 1923 – 21 July 1998) was an American astronaut and naval aviator. On 5 May 1961, piloting the Freedom 7 spacecraft, he became the first American — and the second person, after Yuri Gagarin — to travel into space. His Mercury-Redstone flight was a 15-minute suborbital hop reaching 187 km altitude.

Grounded for most of the 1960s by an inner-ear disorder (Ménière's disease), Shepard became chief of the Astronaut Office until an experimental operation restored him to flight status. He then commanded Apollo 14 in 1971, landing in the Fra Mauro highlands — the site originally targeted by Apollo 13 — and famously hit two golf balls on the lunar surface with a makeshift club. He is the only Mercury Seven astronaut to walk on the Moon, and at 47 was the oldest person to do so.

Later life

Shepard retired from NASA and the Navy in 1974, became a successful businessman, and helped found the Mercury Seven Foundation (now the Astronaut Scholarship Foundation) to support science students.
