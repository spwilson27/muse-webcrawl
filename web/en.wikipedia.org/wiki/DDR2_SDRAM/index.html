DDR2 SDRAM

DDR2 SDRAM (double data rate 2 synchronous dynamic random-access memory) is a double data rate (DDR) synchronous dynamic random-access memory (SDRAM) interface. It is a JEDEC standard (JESD79-2), first published in September 2003. DDR2 succeeded the original DDR SDRAM specification, and was itself succeeded by DDR3 SDRAM in 2007.

In addition to double pumping the data bus as in DDR SDRAM (transferring data on the rising and falling edges of the bus clock signal), DDR2 allows higher bus speed and requires lower power by running the internal clock at half the speed of the data bus. The two factors combine to produce a total of four data transfers per internal clock cycle.

Overview

With data being transferred 64 bits at a time, DDR2 SDRAM gives a transfer rate of (memory bus clock rate) × 2 (for bus clock multiplier) × 2 (for dual rate) × 64 (number of bits transferred) / 8 (bits/byte). Thus with a bus frequency of 100 MHz, DDR2 SDRAM gives a maximum transfer rate of 3200 MB/s.

Key improvements over DDR

  • Lower operating voltage: 1.8 V instead of 2.5 V, reducing power consumption and heat.
  • 4-bit prefetch buffer (twice the 2-bit prefetch of DDR).
  • Off-chip driver calibration (OCD) and on-die termination (ODT) for improved signal integrity.
  • Higher clock speeds, from 200 MHz to 533 MHz (DDR2-400 to DDR2-1066).

Backward compatibility

DDR2 DIMMs are not backward compatible with DDR DIMMs. The notch on DDR2 DIMMs is in a different position from DDR DIMMs, and the pin density is slightly higher at 240 pins (versus 184 pins for DDR DIMMs in desktop packages).
