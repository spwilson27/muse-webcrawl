Inishowen
Irish: Inis Eoghain
Location: County Donegal, Ireland
Largest town: Buncrana
Highest point: Slieve Snaght (615 m)

Inishowen (Irish: Inis Eoghain, "Eoghan's island/peninsula") is a peninsula in County Donegal, Ireland, and the largest peninsula on the island of Ireland. Bounded by Lough Swilly to the west, Lough Foyle to the east, and the Atlantic Ocean to the north, it contains Malin Head, the most northerly point of mainland Ireland. Its principal towns include Buncrana, Carndonagh, Moville, and Greencastle.

History

The peninsula takes its name from Eoghan mac Néill, a son of Niall of the Nine Hostages, and was the historic territory of the Mac Lochlainn and Ó Dochartaigh (O'Doherty) clans. The Grianan of Aileach, a restored stone ringfort near Burt, is traditionally associated with the kings of Aileach. In the early 17th century Inishowen passed to Sir Arthur Chichester as part of the Plantation of Ulster, and the O'Doherty revolt of 1608 began here with the burning of Derry.

Geography and tourism

The interior is mountainous, dominated by the Slieve Snaght range, while the coast features sandy beaches such as Culdaff and Pollan Bay. The "Inishowen 100" scenic drive loops around the peninsula past sites including Dunree Fort, the Glenevin waterfall, and the ancient cross at Carndonagh. Fishing, agriculture, and tourism are the mainstays of the local economy.
