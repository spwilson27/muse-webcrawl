Samurai Warriors, known in Japan as Sengoku Musō (戦国無双), is a series of hack and slash video games developed by Omega Force and published by Koei (now Koei Tecmo). A sister series to Dynasty Warriors, it transposes the "one warrior against a thousand" battlefield gameplay of that series from Three Kingdoms China to Japan's Sengoku period, following famous warlords and retainers such as Oda Nobunaga, Toyotomi Hideyoshi, Tokugawa Ieyasu, Sanada Yukimura, and Date Masamune through dramatized versions of historical battles.

The series dramatizes the campaigns of the Sengoku period with large rosters of playable officers.

Gameplay

The player controls a single officer on a large battlefield crowded with hundreds of enemy soldiers, completing objectives such as defeating enemy generals, escorting allies, and capturing bases. Characters fight with period weapons — katana, spears, bows, and firearms — using normal and charge attacks and a screen-clearing Musou attack. Compared with Dynasty Warriors, the series adds features such as character-specific story modes, castle sieges, and (in later entries) mid-battle character switching and "hyper attacks" for crowd control.

Games

  • Samurai Warriors (2004) and Samurai Warriors: Xtreme Legends
  • Samurai Warriors 2 (2006), with Empires and Xtreme Legends expansions
  • Samurai Warriors 3 (2009)
  • Samurai Warriors 4 (2014) and 4-II; chronicle spin-offs for handhelds
  • Samurai Warriors 5 (2021), a soft reboot with a cel-shaded art style focusing on Nobunaga and Akechi Mitsuhide

Spin-offs include the strategy-inflected Empires titles, Samurai Warriors: Chronicles, and crossovers such as the Warriors Orochi series, which combines the Samurai and Dynasty Warriors casts.

Reception

The series has sold several million copies worldwide, with its strongest following in Japan. Reviews typically praise the large rosters and power fantasy while criticizing repetition and technical shortcomings; Samurai Warriors 5 was noted for its visual overhaul and tighter narrative focus.
