Lewis's trilemma

Lewis's trilemma is an apologetic argument traditionally attributed to C. S. Lewis, who popularised it in a 1942 BBC radio broadcast and in his book Mere Christianity (1952). It poses three options regarding Jesus of Nazareth: that he was a liar, a lunatic, or Lord — "mad, bad, or God".

The argument

Lewis argued that, given the claims Jesus makes about himself in the Gospels, one cannot reasonably regard him as merely a great moral teacher: a man who said the things Jesus said would either be deluded, deceptive, or genuinely what he claimed to be. The argument is aimed at the view, common in liberal theology, that Jesus can be accepted as a teacher while his divinity is rejected.

Criticism

Critics add a fourth option — "legend" — questioning whether the Gospel accounts reliably record Jesus's words, and note that the trilemma presupposes the accuracy of those accounts and a particular reading of his sayings. Philosophers and theologians continue to debate its force.
