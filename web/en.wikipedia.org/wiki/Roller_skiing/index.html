Roller skiing

Roller skiing is an off-snow equivalent to cross-country skiing, in which short skis fitted with small wheels at each end are used on paved surfaces. Skiers use poles and the same classic (diagonal stride) or skate techniques as on snow.

Originating in the 1930s in Italy and northern Europe as summer training for cross-country skiers, roller skiing developed into a competitive sport in its own right. The International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS) sanctions a Roller Ski World Cup and World Championships, with sprint, mass start and distance races.

Equipment includes roller skis with aluminium or composite shafts, rubber or polyurethane wheels (with speed classes regulated in racing), ski boots and bindings identical to winter gear, and mandatory helmets in competition. It is popular in Scandinavia, Italy, Germany and Russia both for elite training and recreation.
