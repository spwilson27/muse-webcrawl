Ergoline

Formula: C14H16N2
Class: Tetracyclic alkaloid scaffold

Ergoline is a chemical compound whose structural skeleton is contained in a diverse range of alkaloids, known as ergoline derivatives or ergoline alkaloids. Ergoline alkaloids are produced by fungi of the genus Claviceps (ergot) and by some related fungi, and also occur in certain morning glory seeds.

Uses and pharmacology

Ergoline derivatives include the uterotonic and vasoconstrictive drug ergotamine, used in migraine treatment; the prolactin inhibitors bromocriptine and cabergoline used in Parkinson's disease and hyperprolactinaemia; and lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD), a semisynthetic ergoline with potent psychedelic effects discovered by Albert Hofmann at Sandoz in 1943. Many ergolines act at serotonin, dopamine, and adrenergic receptors, which underlies both their therapeutic actions and the symptoms of ergotism (St Anthony's fire).
