Kateri Tekakwitha

Saint Kateri Tekakwitha (1656 – 17 April 1680), known as the "Lily of the Mohawks", was a Mohawk woman who converted to Catholicism in New France. Canonized by Pope Benedict XVI in 2012, she was the first Native American woman to be recognized as a saint by the Roman Catholic Church.

Life

Tekakwitha was born at the Mohawk village of Ossernenon to a Mohawk father and an Algonquin mother who had been raised Christian. A smallpox epidemic in 1660 killed her parents and brother and left her face scarred and her eyesight impaired; her name, Tekakwitha, is often translated "she who bumps into things". Under the peace after the French campaigns against the Mohawk, Jesuit missionaries were admitted to the villages, and in 1676 she was baptized by Father Jacques de Lamberville, taking the name Kateri (Catherine).

Her conversion estranged her from much of her community; she refused an arranged marriage and practised severe penances. In 1677 she moved to the Jesuit mission village of Kahnawake near Montreal, where she lived among other Indigenous converts, took a vow of perpetual virginity in 1679, and died the following Holy Week, aged 24. Her final words are reported as "Jesus, I love you."

Veneration

Accounts of wonders at her tomb circulated immediately, and she was venerated by French colonists and Indigenous Catholics alike. The cause for her canonization, championed for centuries by Native Catholics, advanced when Pope John Paul II beatified her in 1980. A miracle attributed to her intercession — the recovery of a Lummi boy, Jake Finkbonner, from necrotizing fasciitis in 2006 — was recognized in 2011, leading to her canonization in October 2012. Shrines honour her at Kahnawake, at Auriesville in New York, and at the Tekakwitha Conference of Indigenous Catholics.
