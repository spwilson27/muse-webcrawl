Schwartz's, also known as the Charcuterie Hébraïque de Montréal (Montreal Hebrew Delicatessen), is a delicatessen restaurant in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. Founded in 1928 by Reuben Schwartz, a Jewish immigrant from Romania, it is one of the oldest delis in Canada and is widely regarded as the definitive home of Montreal-style smoked meat.

History

Reuben Schwartz opened the deli in 1928 on Saint-Laurent Boulevard — "The Main" — in what was then Montreal's Jewish immigrant quarter. The restaurant built its reputation on smoked meat prepared according to Schwartz's own method: beef brisket marinated for ten days in a blend of spices including coriander, black pepper, garlic and mustard seed, then smoked and steamed before being sliced by hand to order.

The business passed through several hands over the decades, including long-time ownership associated with Maurice Zbriger and later the Nakis and Aintabi families, while remaining in its original, famously cramped location. In 2012 a group of investors that included singer Céline Dion and her husband René Angélil acquired the business, prompting public debate in Montreal about the institution's future; the original restaurant continued operating with its menu and staff intact, and Schwartz's branded products later appeared in grocery stores.

The restaurant

Schwartz's occupies a small, no-frills dining room with communal tables and counter seating, and long queues outside are a fixture of the boulevard. The menu centres on smoked meat sandwiches — ordered lean, medium, medium-fat or fat — served on rye bread with yellow mustard, alongside fries, pickles, coleslaw and black cherry soda. Other staples include smoked meat platters, steak, liver steak and poutine topped with smoked meat.

Reputation and cultural significance

Schwartz's is a landmark of Montreal food culture, routinely listed among the city's essential restaurants and a pilgrimage site for visitors. Its rivalry and comparison with other smoked-meat institutions such as Lester's and the Main Deli are a staple of local debate. The deli has been the subject of books and documentaries, including Chez Schwartz (2007), and the stage musical Schwartz's: The Musical, based on Bill Brownstein's book about the restaurant.
