The Second Temple was the Jewish temple in Jerusalem that stood from about 516 BC, when returning exiles from Babylon rebuilt the destroyed First (Solomonic) Temple, until Rome destroyed it in 70 AD. Massively enlarged by Herod the Great — its retaining walls, including the Western Wall, still define the Temple Mount — it was the centre of pilgrimage festivals, sacrifice and teaching in the time of Jesus, and its loss transformed Judaism from a temple cult into the rabbinic, synagogue-centred religion of the diaspora.

Destruction and legacy

Titus's legions burned the Temple during the siege of Jerusalem in the First Jewish–Roman War (9th of Av, by tradition the same date as the First Temple's fall). The Arch of Titus in Rome depicts its menorah carried in triumph (the menorah now emblems the State of Israel). Judaism still mourns the destruction on Tisha B'Av, and prayers for restoration are woven through its liturgy.
