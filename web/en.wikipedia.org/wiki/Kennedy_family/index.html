Kennedy family

The Kennedy family at their Hyannis Port compound, a centre of American political life.

The Kennedy family is an American political family of Irish descent that has produced a president, attorneys general, senators, representatives and ambassadors, and has been prominent in United States politics for more than a century. They are sometimes described as American royalty or the closest thing the United States has to a political dynasty.

Origins

The family's American story begins with Patrick J. Kennedy (1858–1929), a Boston businessman and politician, and his son Joseph P. Kennedy Sr. (1888–1969), a financier who served as the first chairman of the Securities and Exchange Commission and as ambassador to the United Kingdom. Joseph and his wife Rose Fitzgerald Kennedy had nine children.

Prominent members

  • John F. Kennedy (1917–1963) — 35th President of the United States, assassinated in office.
  • Robert F. Kennedy (1925–1968) — Attorney General and U.S. Senator, assassinated while campaigning for the presidency.
  • Ted Kennedy (1932–2009) — U.S. Senator from Massachusetts for 47 years, the "Lion of the Senate".
  • Eunice Kennedy Shriver — founder of the Special Olympics.
  • Later generations include Caroline Kennedy, former ambassador to Japan and Australia, and Robert F. Kennedy Jr., Secretary of Health and Human Services from 2025.

"Kennedy curse"

Popular commentary speaks of a "Kennedy curse" because of the family's many premature deaths, including the assassinations of John and Robert Kennedy, Joseph P. Kennedy Jr.'s death in the Second World War, and the 1999 plane crash that killed John F. Kennedy Jr.
