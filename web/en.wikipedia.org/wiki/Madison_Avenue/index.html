Madison Avenue

Madison Avenue is a north–south avenue in the borough of Manhattan in New York City, running from Madison Square at 23rd Street to the Harlem River at 142nd Street. The avenue is famous as a historic centre of the American advertising industry, so much so that "Madison Avenue" became a metonym for advertising itself.

History

The avenue was created in 1836 as part of the street plan for Manhattan and named after President James Madison. In the 1920s, major advertising agencies began clustering there, and by the mid-twentieth century the Midtown stretch housed most of the industry's great firms.

Advertising

The association with advertising was cemented in popular culture by novels, films and the television series Mad Men. Although many agencies have since dispersed, Madison Avenue retains its symbolic status, and its northern Midtown section is also known for luxury retail, including flagship boutiques.

Layout

Unlike most Manhattan avenues, Madison carries one-way traffic northbound and is paralleled by Park Avenue to the east and Fifth Avenue to the west.
