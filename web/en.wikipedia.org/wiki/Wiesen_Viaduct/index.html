Wiesen Viaduct

Wiesen Viaduct
German: Wiesener Viadukt
Location: Wiesen, Davos, Graubünden, Switzerland
Carries: Davos–Filisur railway (Rhaetian Railway)
Total length: 204 m (669 ft)
Height: 88.9 m (292 ft)
Main span: 55 m (180 ft)
Opened: 1909

The Wiesen Viaduct (German: Wiesener Viadukt) is a single-track railway viaduct on the Davos to Filisur line of the Rhaetian Railway in the canton of Graubünden, Switzerland. Built between 1906 and 1909, it spans the Landwasser gorge south-west of the village of Wiesen and is the largest stone viaduct of the Rhaetian Railway.

Structure

The viaduct is 204 metres long and rises 88.9 metres above the river. Its main span of 55 metres — one of the largest masonry arch spans in Switzerland — is flanked by a series of smaller arches, two of 20 metres on one side and four on the other. It is constructed of concrete blocks faced to resemble natural stone, with some 35,000 cubic metres of concrete used. A pedestrian walkway attached to the bridge, added in 1926, allows walkers to cross the gorge and view the structure.

Heritage

The viaduct forms part of the Rhaetian Railway network whose Albula and Bernina lines were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008. It is a well-known landmark on the route to Filisur and Davos and a popular subject for railway photography; a viewing platform on the south side provides views of trains crossing the arch.
