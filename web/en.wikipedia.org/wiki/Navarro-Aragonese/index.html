Navarro-Aragonese

Navarro-Aragonese was a Romance language once spoken in a large part of the Ebro River valley, south of the central Pyrenees, in the medieval kingdoms of Navarre and Aragon in what is now Spain. It is the medieval ancestor of the modern Aragonese language and also influenced the Spanish spoken in La Rioja and Navarre.

History

The language developed from the Vulgar Latin spoken in the middle Ebro basin. It was used in royal chanceries, legal documents (fueros) and literature from roughly the 11th to the 15th centuries. The Glosas Emilianenses, once thought to be the earliest written Spanish, contain glosses that many scholars now attribute to Navarro-Aragonese (or a Riojan variety of it).

Decline

From the 15th century onward, and especially after the dynastic union of Aragon with Castile, Castilian Spanish progressively displaced Navarro-Aragonese in writing and administration. Its spoken descendants retreated northward into the Pyrenean valleys of Aragon, where modern Aragonese survives, heavily influenced by Spanish.

Features

Characteristic features included preservation of initial Latin F- (where Castilian often lost it or changed it to h-), outcomes of Latin clusters such as -CT- developing differently from Castilian, and diphthongisation patterns distinct from both Castilian and Catalan.
