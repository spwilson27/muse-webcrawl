Diana camera

The Diana is a plastic box camera introduced in the 1960s by the Great Wall Plastic Factory of Hong Kong. Made almost entirely of plastic, including its lens, it uses 120 roll film and produces square 4×4 cm images. It was sold under many names and often given away as a promotional item.

Characteristics

The Diana's simple meniscus lens produces soft, dreamlike images with pronounced vignetting, light leaks and distortion. Once considered defects of a cheap toy camera, these qualities were later embraced by photographers seeking a lo-fi aesthetic, and the camera became a cult object in the Lomography movement.

Legacy

The Diana inspired the Holga and numerous modern reproductions, including the Diana+ and Diana F+ produced by Lomography from 2007, which added features such as interchangeable lenses and pinhole operation.
