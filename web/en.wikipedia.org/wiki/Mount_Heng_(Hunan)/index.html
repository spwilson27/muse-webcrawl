Mount Heng (Hunan)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Grand Temple of Mount Heng at the mountain's foot.

Mount Heng (Chinese: 衡山; pinyin: Héng Shān), also known as Nanyue, is a mountain range in Hunan Province, China. It is the southernmost of the Five Sacred Mountains of Chinese tradition and has been a site of religious pilgrimage for more than two thousand years. Its highest point, Zhurong Peak, reaches 1,300 metres (4,265 ft).

Religious significance

Mount Heng is unusual in being sacred to Buddhism, Taoism, and Confucianism alike; temples of all three traditions stand alongside one another on its slopes. The Grand Temple of Mount Heng (Nanyue Damiao), at the northern entrance to the scenic area, is one of the largest temple complexes in southern China, combining halls in the style of the Forbidden City with both Buddhist and Taoist shrines. Emperors of successive dynasties sent officials to offer sacrifices here, and the mountain is addressed as the Great Emperor of the Southern Peak.

The scenic area

The Hengshan scenic area covers 72 peaks stretching some 80 km through Hengyang prefecture. Pilgrim and tourist paths climb past the Martyrs' Shrine, the Sutra Collection Hall, and Fangguang Temple to Zhurong Peak, named for the ancient god of fire, where a small hall crowns the summit above a sea of clouds.
