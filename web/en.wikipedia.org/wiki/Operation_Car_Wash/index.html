Operation Car Wash

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Operation Car Wash (Portuguese: Operação Lava Jato) was a massive anti-corruption investigation conducted by the Federal Police of Brazil, initially led by a task force of prosecutors in Curitiba under judge Sergio Moro. Beginning in March 2014, it uncovered a vast scheme in which executives of the state oil company Petrobras, construction conglomerates, and politicians exchanged inflated contracts for bribes and illegal campaign funding. It became one of the largest corruption investigations in history, reshaping Brazilian politics and spreading to more than a dozen other countries.

Origins and scheme

The investigation began as a probe into money laundering through a car wash and petrol station in Brasília — hence the name — and expanded via the arrested money launderer Alberto Youssef to Petrobras director Paulo Roberto Costa. Investigators uncovered that a cartel of major construction firms (including Odebrecht, OAS, Camargo Corrêa, and Andrade Gutierrez) colluded on Petrobras contracts, overcharging by several percent, with the surplus distributed as bribes to Petrobras executives and to politicians and parties of the governing coalition. Odebrecht maintained a dedicated "Department of Structured Operations" to administer bribe payments worldwide.

Course and outcomes

Using plea-bargaining (delação premiada) extensively, prosecutors secured convictions of senior business figures — notably Odebrecht CEO Marcelo Odebrecht — and of politicians across the spectrum. Petrobras wrote off billions in corruption-related losses and, in a 2018 settlement with U.S. and Brazilian authorities, agreed to pay about $2.95 billion. Odebrecht's 2016 leniency agreement, covering its bribery in 12 countries, was at the time the largest global anti-corruption settlement ever. Hundreds of people were convicted and billions of reais were recovered or pledged.

Political impact and controversy

The operation fed the crisis that culminated in President Dilma Rousseff's impeachment (2016, formally on fiscal grounds) and led to the 2017 corruption conviction of former president Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, barring him from the 2018 election; Moro then became justice minister in the government of election winner Jair Bolsonaro. In 2019, leaked messages published by The Intercept Brasil ("Vaza Jato") suggested improper coordination between Moro and prosecutors. In 2021 Brazil's Supreme Federal Court annulled Lula's convictions on jurisdictional grounds and declared Moro biased in his case; Lula returned to win the presidency in 2022. The Curitiba task force was disbanded in 2021. Assessments of Lava Jato remain deeply polarised between those who credit it with breaking impunity and those who criticise its methods and political consequences.
