Percopsiformes

The Percopsiformes are a small order of freshwater ray-finned fishes native to North America. The order contains only a handful of species in three families: the trout-perches (Percopsidae), the pirate perch (Aphredoderidae), and the cavefishes and swampfishes (Amblyopsidae).

Characteristics

These are small fishes, mostly under 20 cm in length. The trout-perch (Percopsis omiscomaycus) combines features of trout-like and perch-like fishes, with an adipose fin but spiny fin rays. The pirate perch is noted for its anus migrating forward to the throat region in adults. The Amblyopsidae include blind, depigmented cave species such as the northern cavefish, as well as sighted swamp-dwelling forms.

Classification

Percopsiformes are placed within the superorder Paracanthopterygii in traditional classifications, alongside orders such as the Gadiformes (cods). Molecular studies have adjusted their position among the acanthomorph fishes, but the group of three families remains recognized.
