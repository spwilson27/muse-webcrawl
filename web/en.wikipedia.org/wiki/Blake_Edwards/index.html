Blake Edwards (born William Blake Crump; July 26, 1922 – December 15, 2010) was an American film director, producer, screenwriter, and actor. One of the most successful comedy directors of his era, he is best known for Breakfast at Tiffany's (1961), The Pink Panther series with Peter Sellers, Days of Wine and Roses (1962), The Party (1968), 10 (1979), and Victor/Victoria (1982).

Early life and career

Edwards was born in Tulsa, Oklahoma, and grew up in Los Angeles, where his stepfather Jack McEdwards was a film production manager and his grandfather J. Gordon Edwards had been a silent-film director. He served in the U.S. Coast Guard during the Second World War and began in film as an actor and radio writer, co-creating the hard-boiled radio drama Richard Diamond, Private Detective. In the 1950s he created the influential television series Peter Gunn, which introduced Henry Mancini's jazz scoring to TV drama.

Major films

Operation Petticoat (1959) was a major hit. Breakfast at Tiffany's (1961), adapted from Truman Capote's novella and starring Audrey Hepburn, became a cultural touchstone. Days of Wine and Roses (1962) was a stark drama of alcoholism. The Pink Panther (1963) introduced Peter Sellers as the bumbling Inspector Clouseau and led to a long series of sequels through the 1970s, including A Shot in the Dark (1964), often regarded as the best. The Party (1968) built extended sequences of silent-film-style slapstick. After career setbacks, 10 (1979) made Edwards commercially potent again, and Victor/Victoria (1982), starring his wife Julie Andrews, earned him his only competitive Academy Award nomination, for adapted screenplay.

Later life and honours

Edwards's later films included S.O.B. (1981), a bitter satire of Hollywood, Skin Deep (1989), and Son of the Pink Panther (1993). In 2004 he received an Honorary Academy Award for his body of work. He married Julie Andrews in 1969 and they remained together until his death from complications of pneumonia in 2010.
