The United States Army Rangers are an elite light infantry special operations force of the United States Army. The modern force is centered on the 75th Ranger Regiment, headquartered at Fort Benning (renamed Fort Moore), Georgia, with three Ranger battalions and supporting units. Rangers specialize in direct-action raids, airfield seizures, special reconnaissance, and personnel recovery, typically as part of joint special operations task forces.

Soldiers who complete the 61-day U.S. Army Ranger School earn the Ranger Tab, though Ranger School graduates (who may serve in any unit) are distinguished from members of the 75th Ranger Regiment, who wear the tan beret and regimental scroll and complete the Ranger Assessment and Selection Program (RASP).

History

Ranger units trace their lineage to colonial-era companies such as Rogers' Rangers of the French and Indian War. Ranger battalions fought in the Second World War — at Pointe du Hoc on D-Day, in North Africa and Italy, and in the Pacific, where the 6th Ranger Battalion conducted the Raid at Cabanatuan. Ranger companies served in Korea, and Long Range Reconnaissance Patrol (LRRP) and Ranger companies in Vietnam. The modern regiment was built from battalions activated in 1974 and fought at Grenada (1983), Panama (1989), Mogadishu (1993), and extensively in Iraq and Afghanistan.

Ranger Creed and motto

The Ranger Creed, recited by all Rangers, begins "Recognizing that I volunteered as a Ranger..." The motto "Rangers lead the way!" derives from Brigadier General Norman Cota's words on Omaha Beach on 6 June 1944: "Lead the way, Rangers!"
