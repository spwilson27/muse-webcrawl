Winter rallying

A rally car driving on snow at Rally Sweden

Winter rallying refers to the practice of rallying — the motor sport in which production-based cars are raced against the clock on closed special stages — conducted on roads covered in snow and ice, typically during the winter months. Winter events are a distinctive discipline within rallying because the low-grip surfaces demand special tyres, techniques, and preparation, and they allow very high speeds due to the use of studded tyres and the support of snow banks lining the roads.

Characteristics

Cars competing on snow use narrow tyres fitted with metal studs, which bite into the ice beneath the snow to provide surprisingly high levels of grip. Drivers use the snowbanks that line the stages to help guide the car through corners, "leaning" the car against the bank — a technique that can also end a rally if the bank is soft or hides a ditch. Stages are often ploughed or graded before the event, and repeated passage of cars can wear ice into deep ruts that the later runners must follow.

Winter rallies place particular demands on crews and machinery. Visibility can be reduced by blowing snow and by the low sun of northern winters; fogging windscreens and frozen components are common problems. Teams require heated service facilities, and engines, transmissions and dampers must be prepared to operate in severe cold.

Major events

The most famous winter rally is Rally Sweden, held in the forests of Värmland (and more recently based in Umeå), which was for many years the only true snow rally on the World Rally Championship calendar. Rally Finland, although a summer event, shares some characteristics with high-speed winter rallying. Other notable snow events include the Monte Carlo Rally, whose stages in the French Alps frequently run on snow and ice, Norway's rallies, the Arctic Lapland Rally in Finland, and national championship rounds in countries such as Canada, Russia, and the Baltic states. The Monte Carlo Rally historically began with a concentration run in winter conditions across Europe.

History

Scandinavian drivers pioneered winter rallying technique, and Finns and Swedes dominated snow events for decades, developing skills such as the Scandinavian flick to rotate the car into corners. The "Flying Finns" became famous for their mastery of loose-surface driving born of practice on frozen lakes and forest roads. With the spread of four-wheel drive in the Group B era and afterwards, snow rallies became among the fastest events in the championship, with average speeds rivalling those of any gravel rally.
