Flags of the U.S. states and territories

The flags of the U.S. states, territories, and the District of Columbia exhibit a wide variety of regional influences and local histories. Since the early 20th century, most state flags have consisted of the state seal on a blue field — a design tradition often criticized by vexillologists as indistinct, sometimes called "seal on a bedsheet". Several states, however, use strikingly distinctive designs.

Notable designs

  • Hawaii — the only state flag to include the Union Jack, reflecting British influence in the islands.
  • Texas — the "Lone Star Flag", dating to the Republic of Texas (1839).
  • California — the "Bear Flag", derived from the 1846 Bear Flag Revolt.
  • New Mexico — a red Zia sun symbol on gold, adopted 1925, frequently rated among the best-designed flags.
  • Maryland — based on the heraldic arms of the Calvert and Crossland families.
  • South Carolina — an indigo field with a white palmetto tree and crescent.

Recent redesigns

In the 21st century, several states have redesigned their flags. Mississippi adopted a new flag in 2021, replacing a design that incorporated the Confederate battle flag with one featuring a magnolia. Utah adopted a new "Beehive Flag" in 2024, and Minnesota adopted a new design in 2024 replacing its previous seal-centred flag.

Territories

The five inhabited U.S. territories — Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa, and the Northern Mariana Islands — each have their own official flags, as does the District of Columbia, whose flag is based on the coat of arms of George Washington's family.
