Halomonas titanicae is a Gram-negative, halophilic species of bacteria discovered in 2010 in samples of rusticles collected from the wreck of the RMS Titanic. Researchers from Dalhousie University in Halifax, Canada, isolated the bacterium from material recovered by the Mir submersibles in 1991 and formally described it in the International Journal of Systematic and Evolutionary Microbiology.

Description

The bacterium is rod-shaped, motile, and thrives in saline conditions similar to seawater, tolerating a range of pressures and low temperatures typical of the deep ocean floor at about 3,800 metres. It adheres to steel surfaces and metabolises iron, contributing to the corrosion of the wreck.

Significance

H. titanicae is one of many microbial species feeding on the Titanic wreck, consuming its iron and accelerating its disintegration. Studies of the species and the rusticle ecosystem suggest the wreck will be largely consumed by bacteria within decades, and researchers note such iron-eating bacteria pose a risk to other submerged steel structures, including oil rigs and pipelines — though they might also be harnessed to dispose of derelict vessels.
