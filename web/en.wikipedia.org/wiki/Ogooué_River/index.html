Ogooué River

Native name: Ogooué

Countries: Republic of the Congo, Gabon

Length: about 1,200 km (750 mi)

Basin size: about 223,000 km²

Mouth: Gulf of Guinea, near Port-Gentil

The Ogooué (sometimes spelled Ogowe) is the principal river of Gabon and the fifth-largest river in Africa by discharge. Roughly 1,200 kilometres long, it rises in the Republic of the Congo near the Batéké Plateau, crosses Gabon from southeast to northwest, and empties into the Gulf of Guinea just south of Port-Gentil, forming a broad delta. Its watershed, about 223,000 square kilometres, covers nearly the whole of Gabon and parts of Congo and Cameroon and is largely cloaked in equatorial rainforest.

Course and tributaries

The Ogooué's main tributaries include the Ivindo, which joins it from the northeast and passes the Kongou Falls near Makokou; the Ngounié, the second-largest tributary, joining from the south; and the Okano, Lolo, and Offoué rivers. Rapids interrupt navigation along parts of the middle course, notably near Booué and the Lopé region, but the river is navigable for much of its lower course and historically served as the main artery for trade in timber, ivory, and rubber from the Gabonese interior. The town of Lambaréné, where Albert Schweitzer founded his hospital in 1913, stands on the Ogooué.

Ecology

The basin supports exceptional biodiversity: forest elephants, western lowland gorillas, and diverse freshwater fish faunas inhabit its forests and floodplains. The lower Ogooué's lakes, wetlands, and delta are recognized as a Ramsar wetland of international importance, and national parks including Lopé, Ivindo, and Loango protect portions of the watershed. The Ogooué is unusual among large African rivers in remaining free of major dams on its main stem.

History

Nineteenth-century European explorers, including Paul Du Chaillu and Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza, used the Ogooué as a route into the African interior, and the river gave its name to several Gabonese provinces, including Ogooué-Maritime, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Ivindo, and Haut-Ogooué.
