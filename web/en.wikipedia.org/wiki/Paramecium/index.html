Paramecium

DomainEukaryota
PhylumCiliophora
ClassOligohymenophorea
GenusParamecium
Well-known speciesP. caudatum, P. aurelia, P. bursaria

Paramecium is a genus of single-celled ciliates, commonly studied as a model organism in biology. Paramecia are widespread in freshwater, brackish and marine environments and are often abundant in stagnant ponds and basins. Cells are slipper-shaped (hence "slipper animalcule"), ranging from about 50 to 330 micrometres long, and are covered in thousands of hair-like cilia used for swimming and for sweeping bacteria and other food into the oral groove.

Biology

Like other ciliates, a paramecium has two kinds of nuclei: a large macronucleus that controls everyday metabolism and a small micronucleus used in reproduction. It reproduces asexually by binary fission and sexually by conjugation, in which two cells exchange micronuclear material. Food is digested in food vacuoles that circulate through the cell, and excess water is expelled by contractile vacuoles. Defensive trichocysts can be discharged from the cell surface. Paramecium bursaria famously lives in symbiosis with green algae, which photosynthesise inside it.

In research and education

Paramecia are classic laboratory organisms: G. F. Gause used competing Paramecium species in foundational experiments on competitive exclusion in the 1930s, and the genus continues to be used in ecology, genetics and cell biology teaching.
