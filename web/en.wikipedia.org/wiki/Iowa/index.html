Iowa is a state in the Midwestern region of the United States, bordered by the Mississippi River to the east and the Missouri River and Big Sioux River to the west. It is named after the Iowa River, which in turn took its name from the Ioway, a Native American tribe. Iowa is known for its fertile rolling plains and is a leading producer of corn, soybeans and pork.

History

Iowa was acquired by the United States in the Louisiana Purchase of 1803. European settlement accelerated after the Black Hawk War (1832) and the removal of Native peoples to reservations further west. Iowa Territory was organised in 1838, and Iowa became the 29th state on 28 December 1846. During the American Civil War the state strongly supported the Union. In the late 19th and 20th centuries Iowa developed into one of the nation's agricultural heartlands, its politics shaped by farm economics and, in the 1980s, by a major farm crisis.

Geography and climate

Iowa's landscape was shaped by glaciers, producing rich soils over most of the state; the northeast "Driftless Area" is hillier, with bluffs along the Mississippi. The state has a humid continental climate with hot summers, cold winters and precipitation concentrated in spring and summer. Major cities include Des Moines, Cedar Rapids, Davenport, Sioux City and Iowa City, home to the University of Iowa.

Economy

Agriculture remains central: Iowa is typically the largest U.S. producer of corn and a top producer of soybeans, hogs and eggs, and a major producer of ethanol. Manufacturing (food processing, agricultural machinery), insurance and financial services centred on Des Moines, and a growing wind-energy sector — Iowa generates a very large share of its electricity from wind — are also important.

Politics

Iowa has traditionally held the first contest of the U.S. presidential nominating season, the Iowa caucuses, giving the state an outsized role in national politics since the 1970s.
