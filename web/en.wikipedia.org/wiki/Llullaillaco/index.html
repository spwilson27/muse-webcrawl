Llullaillaco

Elevation6,739 m (22,110 ft)
LocationArgentina–Chile border
RangeAndes, Puna de Atacama
TypeStratovolcano
Last eruption1877

Llullaillaco is a dormant stratovolcano on the border between Argentina and Chile. At 6,739 metres (22,110 ft) it is the second-highest active volcano in the world after Ojos del Salado and one of the highest volcanoes on Earth. It lies in the Puna de Atacama, a region of very dry climate and high volcanic peaks in the Andes.

Geology

Llullaillaco is a stratovolcano built of dacitic lava flows and pyroclastic deposits. It has erupted in historical times, with activity reported in 1854, 1866 and 1877, though it is presently dormant. Young lava flows extend down its flanks, and snow and small glaciers persist near the summit despite the extreme aridity of the Atacama region.

Archaeology

The summit of Llullaillaco hosts the highest archaeological site in the world. In 1999, an expedition led by Johan Reinhard recovered the mummified bodies of three Inca children — known as the Children of Llullaillaco — who had been offered as sacrifices around the year 1500 as part of the capacocha ritual. The extreme cold and dryness preserved them remarkably; they are now displayed at the Museum of High Altitude Archaeology in Salta, Argentina.

Climbing

The mountain is usually climbed from the Chilean side or from Argentina, with approaches across the high desert of the Puna. First recorded ascent was in 1952, though Inca priests had reached the summit centuries earlier.
