Flag of France

Use: National flag
Proportion: 2:3
Adopted: 15 February 1794 (first); restored 1830
Design: A vertical tricolour of blue, white and red

The national flag of France is a tricolour featuring three vertical bands coloured blue (hoist side), white, and red. It is known as the Tricolore (French: drapeau tricolore). It traces its origins to the French Revolution, combining the colours of Paris (blue and red) with the royal white; the design was adopted as the national flag on 15 February 1794.

Emmanuel Macron, France's president, changed the blue stripe to a darker navy blue on flags flown at the Élysée Palace in July 2020, reverting to the shade used before 1976.

History

The tricolour originated in July 1789, shortly after the storming of the Bastille, when a cockade of blue, white and red was adopted by the National Guard, including the Marquis de Lafayette. Tradition holds that the blue and red were the colours of Paris, and white the colour of the House of Bourbon, symbolising the union of the people with the monarchy under a constitutional settlement.

The flag was suppressed after the Bourbon Restoration of 1815, when the white flag of the monarchy returned, and restored following the July Revolution of 1830 under Louis-Philippe. It survived the revolutions of 1848 and the Paris Commune, although the red flag was briefly proposed as the national emblem. During the Second World War, the Vichy regime kept the tricolour but added Marshal Pétain's personal insignia to some flags, while the Free French used the tricolour with the Cross of Lorraine.

Symbolism

The motto of the Republic — Liberté, égalité, fraternité ("liberty, equality, fraternity") — is popularly associated with the three bands, though the association is unofficial. The flag's design has influenced the flags of many other nations, particularly in Europe, francophone Africa and the Americas.
