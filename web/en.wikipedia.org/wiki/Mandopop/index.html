Mandopop

Mandopop (Mandarin popular music; Chinese: 華語流行音樂) is Mandarin-language popular music produced mainly in Taiwan, mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore and Malaysia. It emerged in the 1930s from Shanghai's shidaiqu — a fusion of Chinese folk melodies with Western jazz — and developed into the dominant form of Chinese popular music, with Taiwan as its main production centre for much of the late 20th century.

History

After 1949, when the Shanghai recording industry largely relocated to Hong Kong and then Taiwan, Mandopop ballads flourished. Teresa Teng became its first pan-Asian superstar in the 1970s, her songs heard across East and Southeast Asia despite being long restricted in mainland China. The campus folk movement in 1970s Taiwan encouraged singer-songwriters such as Lo Ta-yu and Li Jianfu.

The 1990s and 2000s brought globalised production and a wave of idols and auteurs — Faye Wong, A-mei, Jay Chou, Jolin Tsai and Leehom Wang — whose styles absorbed R&B, hip-hop and electronic music. Jay Chou's fusion of Chinese musical elements with Western pop ("China wind") was especially influential.

Industry today

Since the 2010s the centre of gravity has shifted toward mainland China, the largest market, with streaming platforms and televised idol competitions driving production, while Taiwan and Singapore remain important creative hubs. Mandopop sits alongside Cantopop (Cantonese) and Taiwanese Hokkien pop as one of the main streams of C-pop.
