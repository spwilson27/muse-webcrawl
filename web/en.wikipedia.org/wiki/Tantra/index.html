Tantra

[Image: A painted Tibetan thangka showing a multi-armed tantric deity in union with consort, surrounded by a mandala of attendant figures]

Tantra (Sanskrit: तन्त्र, "loom, warp; system, doctrine") is an esoteric tradition of Hinduism and Buddhism that developed in India from the middle of the first millennium CE. Tantric traditions emphasise initiation (dīkṣā) by a guru, the use of mantra, mandala, mudra and visualisation, ritual identification with a deity, and texts called Tantras, in contrast (or as a supplement) to the older Vedic corpus.

History and traditions

Tantra spread through Shaiva, Shakta and Vaishnava Hindu traditions and, in Buddhist form (Vajrayana), to Tibet, Nepal, China and Japan, where it survives as Shingon. Shakta Tantra centres devotion on the Goddess (Devi) and the energy principle shakti; Kashmiri Shaivism developed sophisticated non-dual philosophies around Shiva-consciousness. Tantric practice typically maps the divine onto the body through channels (nāḍī) and energy centres (chakras), and includes kundalini yoga techniques.

Concepts and reception

Some lineages deliberately transgress purity norms — the so-called left-hand path (vāmācāra) — using ritual elements normally forbidden, which practitioners interpret symbolically or as methods of transcending dualities. In the modern West, "Tantra" is popularly associated mainly with sexual practices, a narrowing scholars trace to colonial-era accounts and twentieth-century popularisation; historically sexuality is a small part of a large ritual and philosophical corpus.
