German Historical Museum

The German Historical Museum (German: Deutsches Historisches Museum, DHM) is a museum in Berlin, Germany, dedicated to German history from the Middle Ages to the present day. It is housed in the Zeughaus, the former arsenal on the boulevard Unter den Linden, together with a modern exhibition hall designed by I. M. Pei. The museum's permanent exhibition presents some 7,000 exhibits from its collections of roughly a million objects, and it is one of the most visited museums in Berlin.

History

The museum was founded in 1987 on the initiative of Chancellor Helmut Kohl and the city of West Berlin, on the occasion of the 750th anniversary of Berlin, and was intended as a national history museum for the Federal Republic of Germany. The project was controversial, with critics fearing a nationalist presentation of German history. After German reunification in 1990, the new museum took over the Zeughaus — the oldest building on Unter den Linden, built between 1695 and 1730 — together with the collections of the East German Museum for German History (Museum für Deutsche Geschichte), which had been housed there since 1952. The combined museum opened its permanent exhibition, "German History in Images and Testimonies", in 2006.

Buildings and collections

The baroque Zeughaus, with its celebrated sculptures of dying warriors by Andreas Schlüter in the courtyard, houses the permanent exhibition. Behind it stands the glass-and-steel exhibition hall added in 2003, designed by I. M. Pei, used for temporary exhibitions. The collections cover everyday culture, documents, film, photography, militaria, posters, prints and works of art; the museum also runs the LeMO (Living Museum Online) portal and, in cooperation with the Haus der Geschichte in Bonn, provides extensive online resources on twentieth-century German history.
