John Gilmore (September 28, 1931 – August 19, 1995) was an American jazz tenor saxophonist best known for his long association with the bandleader Sun Ra and his Arkestra. A powerful and distinctive soloist, Gilmore was widely admired by other saxophonists — John Coltrane is said to have studied with him — yet he remained relatively obscure to the wider public because he spent nearly his entire career within Sun Ra's eccentric, self-contained musical universe.

Early life

Gilmore was born in Summit, Mississippi, and grew up in Chicago. He began playing the clarinet at age 14 and took up the tenor saxophone while serving in the United States Air Force. After his discharge he played with pianist Earl Hines in the early 1950s before joining Sun Ra's Arkestra around 1953.

Career with Sun Ra

Gilmore became the Arkestra's featured tenor soloist and, in effect, its musical anchor for four decades. Sun Ra allowed him considerable freedom, and Gilmore's solos — raw, blues-drenched and capable of sudden flights into the altissimo register — became a signature of the band's sound on albums such as Jazz in Silhouette (1959), The Heliocentric Worlds of Sun Ra (1965) and Atlantis (1969). After Sun Ra's death in 1993, Gilmore briefly led the Arkestra before ill health forced him to hand leadership to alto saxophonist Marshall Allen in 1995.

Work outside the Arkestra

Although he rarely recorded away from Sun Ra, Gilmore made notable appearances on Art Blakey's Golden Boy (1963), McCoy Tyner's Today and Tomorrow (1963–64), Andrew Hill's Compulsion (1965), and Pete La Roca's Turkish Women at the Bath (1967). He co-led one album under his own name, Blowing in from Chicago (1957), with fellow tenorist Clifford Jordan.

Death and legacy

Gilmore died of emphysema in Philadelphia on August 19, 1995. Critics and fellow musicians have frequently cited him as one of the most under-recognized tenor saxophonists in jazz; his fiery, rhythmically charged style influenced generations of avant-garde players.
