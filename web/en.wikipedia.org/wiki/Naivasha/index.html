Naivasha is a town in Nakuru County, Kenya, lying about 92 km north-west of Nairobi in the Great Rift Valley. It takes its name from the Maasai word Enaiposha, meaning "that which heaves" or rough water, referring to Lake Naivasha, the freshwater lake on whose shores the town stands.

Economy

Naivasha is a major centre of Kenya's horticulture and floriculture industries: large commercial flower farms around the lake produce roses and other cut flowers exported mainly to Europe, making Kenya one of the world's leading flower exporters. The area is also significant for geothermal energy — the Olkaria geothermal power stations near the town supply a substantial share of Kenya's electricity — and for tourism.

Tourism and nature

Nearby attractions include Lake Naivasha itself, known for hippos and rich birdlife; Hell's Gate National Park, noted for its gorges and cycling; Crescent Island; Elsamere, the former home of Joy Adamson; and Mount Longonot, a dormant volcano. Since 2021 Naivasha has hosted the Safari Rally Kenya round of the World Rally Championship.

History

Naivasha grew up around the lake and the Uganda Railway in the colonial period. The town and its surroundings were part of the so-called "White Highlands" settled by European farmers; the Happy Valley set of the 1920s–40s was centred partly in the area. The Lake Naivasha accords and other peace processes have also used the town as a venue, including Sudanese peace negotiations.
