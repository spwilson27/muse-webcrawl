The Tale of Kiều

AuthorNguyễn Du (1765–1820)
Original titleTruyện Kiều
Form3,254 lines in lục bát verse
LanguageVietnamese (chữ Nôm)

The Tale of Kiều (Vietnamese: Truyện Kiều) is an epic poem by Nguyễn Du, widely regarded as the greatest work of Vietnamese literature. Written in the lục bát (six-eight) metre in chữ Nôm script, its 3,254 lines retell an older Chinese prose tale, transforming it into a meditation on fate, talent and suffering.

Plot

Thúy Kiều, a beautiful and gifted young woman, meets the scholar Kim Trọng and pledges herself to him. When her father is falsely accused and imprisoned, Kiều sells herself into marriage to save her family, beginning fifteen years of hardship: she is deceived into a brothel, escapes, is betrayed again, serves in servitude, and is finally rescued and reunited with her family and Kim Trọng — whom she persuades to marry her younger sister, offering him a bond of gratitude rather than a wife's union.

Significance

The poem's famous opening — "Within the span of a hundred years of human existence, talent and destiny are apt to feud" — frames its enduring theme: the conflict between tài (talent) and mệnh (destiny). Memorised by generations of Vietnamese schoolchildren and quoted in everyday speech and fortune-telling practice (bói Kiều), it occupies a place in Vietnamese culture comparable to Shakespeare in English.
