May 18th National Cemetery

The May 18th National Cemetery (Korean: 국립5·18민주묘지) is a national cemetery in Gwangju, South Korea, dedicated to the victims of the Gwangju Uprising of May 18–27, 1980, in which citizens protesting martial law were killed by military forces. It serves as a central site of commemoration of South Korea's democratization movement.

History

After the uprising, victims were originally buried in a section of Mangwol-dong Cemetery, which became a pilgrimage site for democracy activists despite official attempts to downplay the event. Following democratization, the government constructed the new national cemetery, and in 1997 the victims' remains were reinterred there; May 18 was designated a national memorial day. The cemetery was elevated to national cemetery status in 2002.

Site

The grounds include burial areas arranged with graves of the victims, a memorial tower approximately 40 meters high symbolizing rebirth and democracy, a memorial hall and museum documenting the uprising, sculptures, and commemorative spaces. Annual memorial ceremonies are held each May 18, attended by national leaders and citizens. The cemetery, together with the old Mangwol-dong site, forms part of the historical landscape of the May 18 Democratic Uprising, whose archives are inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World Register.
