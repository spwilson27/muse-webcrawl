Mood disorder

SpecialtyPsychiatry, clinical psychology
SymptomsPersistently depressed or elevated mood, anhedonia, changes in sleep, energy and appetite
TreatmentPsychotherapy, medication (antidepressants, mood stabilizers)

A mood disorder, also known as an affective disorder, is a group of mental health conditions in which a disturbance of a person's mood is the main underlying feature. Mood disorders are broadly divided into elevated mood disorders, such as mania or hypomania; depressed mood disorders, of which the best-known and most researched is major depressive disorder (MDD); and disorders that alternate between mania and depression, known as bipolar disorder.

Classification

Depressive disorders include major depressive disorder, persistent depressive disorder (dysthymia), premenstrual dysphoric disorder, and disruptive mood dysregulation disorder. Bipolar and related disorders include bipolar I disorder, bipolar II disorder, and cyclothymic disorder. Mood disorders may also be induced by substances or be due to another medical condition, such as hypothyroidism.

Causes

The causes of mood disorders are multifactorial, involving genetic vulnerability, neurobiological factors, early life stress, trauma, and psychosocial circumstances. Abnormalities in neurotransmitter systems and in the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis have been implicated.

Treatment

Treatment typically combines psychotherapy, such as cognitive behavioural therapy, with pharmacotherapy. Antidepressants are used for depressive disorders, while mood stabilizers such as lithium, and certain anticonvulsants and antipsychotics, are used in bipolar disorder.
