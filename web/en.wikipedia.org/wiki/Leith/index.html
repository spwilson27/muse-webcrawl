Leith (Scottish Gaelic: Lìte) is a port area in the north of Edinburgh, Scotland, founded at the mouth of the Water of Leith and lying on the southern shore of the Firth of Forth. Historically governed as a separate burgh, it was merged with Edinburgh in 1920. Leith served as the port of Edinburgh for centuries and remains Scotland's historic gateway for trade with the Baltic and the Low Countries.

History

Leith's harbour is recorded from the fourteenth century; the town was sacked during the Rough Wooing in the 1540s, and Mary of Guise moved her court there in 1560, when it was besieged during the Scottish Reformation. Leith later became a major centre of shipbuilding, whaling, glassmaking, rope- and sail-making and the wine and whisky trades — Leith merchants pioneered the export of Scotch whisky, and great bonded warehouses lined the Shore. In 1833 Leith became an independent burgh, an arrangement ended by a contested plebiscite and amalgamation with Edinburgh in 1920.

Modern Leith

After the decline of the docks in the later twentieth century, Leith underwent extensive regeneration: the Ocean Terminal shopping centre opened beside the former Royal Yacht Britannia, which is moored there as a visitor attraction, and the waterfront filled with offices, hotels and housing. The Shore and Leith Walk became known for pubs, restaurants — including Michelin-starred establishments — and a strong local identity, captured in the "Persevere" motto of the old burgh arms and in popular culture such as Irvine Welsh's novel Trainspotting (1993). The Edinburgh Trams extension to Newhaven through Leith opened in 2023.
