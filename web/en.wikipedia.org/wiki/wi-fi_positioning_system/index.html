Wi-Fi positioning system

Wi-Fi positioning system (WPS, also abbreviated to WiPS or WFPS) is a geolocation system that uses the characteristics of nearby Wi-Fi access points and other wireless access points to discover where a device is located. It is used where satellite navigation such as GPS is inadequate due to various causes including multipath and signal blockage in indoor environments, though it can complement GPS outdoors in urban areas where access points are dense.

Techniques

The most common technique is based on measuring the intensity of the received signal (RSSI) and "fingerprinting" it against a database of known access-point locations and their observed signal patterns. Systems either build databases by wardriving and crowdsourcing, or — as with large commercial databases operated by platform vendors — continuously update them from devices that report both their satellite-derived position and the access points they can hear.

Accuracy and applications

Accuracy is typically on the order of a few tens of metres in dense urban areas. Wi-Fi positioning underlies location services in smartphones, asset tracking in warehouses and hospitals, and indoor navigation in large venues. Privacy concerns have arisen over the collection of access-point locations without the owners' knowledge. See also indoor positioning system.
