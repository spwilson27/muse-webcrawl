Kolmogorov–Smirnov test

In statistics, the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test (K–S test or KS test) is a nonparametric test of the equality of continuous, one-dimensional probability distributions. It can be used to test whether a sample came from a reference probability distribution (one-sample K–S test), or to test whether two samples came from the same distribution (two-sample K–S test).

Statistic

The test statistic is the supremum (maximum vertical distance) between the empirical distribution functions of the sample(s) and the reference cumulative distribution function. Under the null hypothesis, the distribution of the statistic follows the Kolmogorov distribution, which Andrey Kolmogorov derived in 1933; Nikolai Smirnov extended the work to the two-sample case.

Properties

The test is distribution-free for continuous distributions and sensitive to differences in both location and shape of the distributions, but it is less powerful than specialised tests for some alternatives and requires adjustment when parameters are estimated from the data (the Lilliefors test).
