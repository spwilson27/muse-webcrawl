Victory in Europe Day

Also calledVE Day, V-E Day
Date8 May 1945
SignificanceAcceptance of Nazi Germany's unconditional surrender ending World War II in Europe
Related toVictory over Japan Day (V-J Day)

Victory in Europe Day is the day celebrating the formal acceptance by the Allies of World War II of Nazi Germany's unconditional surrender of its armed forces on 8 May 1945. It marked the official end of World War II in Europe in the Eastern Front, with the last known active battles in Europe during the Second World War ending shortly after, and overall ending the war.

The German instrument of surrender was first signed in Reims, France, on 7 May 1945, and ratified in Berlin on the night of 8/9 May. News of the surrender spread on 8 May, and spontaneous celebrations broke out across Europe and North America; in London, crowds massed in Trafalgar Square and outside Buckingham Palace, where King George VI and Prime Minister Winston Churchill appeared on the balcony. Many countries observe 8 May as a public holiday or day of remembrance; Russia and several post-Soviet states observe Victory Day on 9 May, reflecting the time-zone difference at the Berlin signing.

Commemorations

Major anniversaries — the 50th in 1995, 70th in 2015, and 75th in 2020 — were marked by national services, flypasts, street parties, and moments of silence. In the United Kingdom, the 75th anniversary was made a bank holiday, and in 2025 the 80th anniversary was commemorated with four days of events including a military procession and the lighting of beacons.
