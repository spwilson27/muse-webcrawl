Handwriting

Handwriting
ImplementsPen, pencil, brush, stylus
StylesPrint, cursive, calligraphy
StudyPalaeography, graphology

Handwriting is the writing of language by hand using a writing instrument such as a pen or pencil. It includes both printed and cursive styles and is distinguished from formal calligraphy and type. Each person's handwriting is distinctive, and forensic document examiners can compare samples to assess authorship.

History

Handwriting has been the principal means of recording language since the earliest scripts. Roman cursive, Carolingian minuscule and later italic hands successively shaped Western writing. Standardised copybook styles were taught in schools from the 19th century, including the Palmer Method in the United States. With the spread of typewriters and computers, everyday handwriting declined, and many school systems reduced or dropped cursive instruction, though it remains required in a number of countries and US states.

Motor and cognitive aspects

Writing by hand engages fine motor control coordinated between the hand, eyes and brain. Research suggests handwriting practice aids letter recognition and early literacy. Difficulties with handwriting are associated with developmental dysgraphia. The study of historical handwriting is palaeography; graphology, the claim that character can be inferred from handwriting, is considered a pseudoscience.
