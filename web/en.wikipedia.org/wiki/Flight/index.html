Flight

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A bird and an airliner sharing the sky at sunset, silhouettes]
Flight, natural and mechanical.

Flight is the process by which an object moves through the atmosphere (or beyond) without support from a surface. Birds, insects and bats evolved flight; humans achieved it with kites, balloons (1783), gliders, and finally powered aeroplanes in 1903. Flight depends on balancing four forces: lift, weight, thrust and drag.

Principles

A wing moving through air generates lift because of its shape and angle of attack, deflecting air downwards. Engines provide thrust to overcome drag, and control surfaces — ailerons, elevators and rudder — govern roll, pitch and yaw, the three-axis system pioneered by the Wright brothers.

Animal flight

Powered flight evolved four times: in insects, pterosaurs, birds and bats. Birds remain the most diverse fliers, from hovering hummingbirds to the wandering albatross, whose dynamic soaring crosses oceans with barely a wingbeat.

Human flight

The Montgolfier balloon (1783) began human flight; the 20th century added airliners, supersonic flight, helicopters and spaceflight beyond the atmosphere. Modern aviation moves over four billion passengers a year (pre-2020 peak), with air traffic control managing the crowded skies.
