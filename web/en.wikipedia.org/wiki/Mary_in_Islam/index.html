Mary in Islam

Mary (Arabic: مريم, romanized: Maryam) is the only woman named in the Quran, and the nineteenth chapter of the Quran, Surah Maryam, is named after her. She is revered in Islam as the mother of Jesus (ʿĪsā), who is considered one of the most important prophets, and as one of the most righteous women in creation. Mary is mentioned more times in the Quran than in the New Testament.

Quranic account

According to the Quran, Mary's mother, the wife of Imran, vowed to dedicate her unborn child to the service of God. Mary was raised in the sanctuary under the care of Zechariah. Angels announced to her the birth of a son, Jesus, through a miraculous virgin conception, describing him as a word from God and a spirit from Him. The Quran emphasises that Mary was chosen and purified "above the women of the worlds" (Quran 3:42).

Mary withdrew to a remote place to give birth, beneath a palm tree, and later returned carrying the infant Jesus, who spoke from the cradle in defence of his mother. Islamic tradition holds that Mary never married and remained devoted to worship throughout her life.

Veneration

In hadith literature, Mary is counted among the best of women, along with Asiya (the wife of Pharaoh), Khadija, and Fatimah. Muslims regard her as a model of chastity, obedience and devotion. Her tomb is traditionally located in the Valley of Jehoshaphat in Jerusalem, at the site of the Church of the Tomb of the Virgin Mary.
