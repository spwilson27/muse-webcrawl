The Sukhishvili Georgian National Ballet (Georgian: სუხიშვილები) is a professional folk dance company based in Tbilisi, Georgia. Founded in 1945 by the husband-and-wife team Iliko Sukhishvili (1907–1985) and Nino Ramishvili (1910–2000), it was the first professional state dance company in Georgia and became the country's best-known cultural ambassador, credited with bringing Georgian folk dance to world attention.

Repertoire and style

The company's choreography is built on the traditional dances of Georgia's diverse regions — the martial, leaping khorumi; the men's dance mkhedruli; the graceful gliding women's dance samaya; dances performed on the points of the toes in soft leather boots, sword-and-shield dances, and dances over knives or on kneeling platforms. Male dancers are famous for dancing on their toes and executing high leaps and spins landing on their knees, while female dancers glide with tiny steps so smoothly that they appear to float. Performances are accompanied by traditional instruments and polyphonic singing, and the dancers wear ornate national costumes.

International recognition

The ensemble was among the first Soviet folk companies to tour the West, appearing at the Metropolitan Opera and Madison Square Garden in New York, La Scala in Milan, the Royal Albert Hall in London and other leading venues. Critics in the Western press praised its athletic precision and theatricality. After Georgia's independence the company continued as a family enterprise: it has been directed by the founders' son Tengiz Sukhishvili and his wife Inga Tevzadze, and subsequently by the third generation, with Iliko Sukhishvili Jr. as chief choreographer — making it a rare example of a major dance company led by one family across three generations.
