Common Cause is an American nonpartisan citizens' lobby and pressure group founded in 1970 by John W. Gardner, a former Secretary of Health, Education, and Welfare. Describing itself as "the people's lobby," it advocates for open, honest, and accountable government, and has been a leading campaigner for campaign finance reform, ethics rules, voting rights, and an end to partisan gerrymandering.

History

Gardner launched Common Cause in 1970 with newspaper advertisements inviting citizens to join a lobby for the public interest; membership rapidly grew into the hundreds of thousands. In its early years the organisation supported the successful campaign for the Twenty-sixth Amendment (lowering the voting age to 18), sued to open the Democratic Party's delegate selection, and pushed for the Federal Election Campaign Act amendments and ethics legislation that followed the Watergate scandal. It later campaigned for the Bipartisan Campaign Reform Act of 2002 (McCain–Feingold).

Programmes

The organisation works through a national office and state chapters. Major issue areas include:

  • Money in politics — disclosure of political spending, limits on contributions, and public financing proposals, especially following Citizens United v. FEC (2010).
  • Voting and elections — automatic and same-day voter registration, early voting, and opposition to restrictive voter ID laws.
  • Redistricting — independent redistricting commissions to replace partisan map-drawing.
  • Ethics and accountability — lobbying disclosure, judicial recusal, and protection of inspectors general.

Structure

Common Cause operates alongside the affiliated Common Cause Education Fund, a 501(c)(3). Its national governing board and state organisations coordinate litigation, lobbying, and grassroots mobilisation campaigns.
