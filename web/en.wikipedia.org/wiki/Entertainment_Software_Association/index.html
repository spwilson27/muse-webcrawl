The Entertainment Software Association (ESA) is the trade association of the video game industry in the United States. It was formed in 1994 as the Interactive Digital Software Association (IDSA) and renamed in 2003. Most of the top publishers in the video game world are members of the ESA.

Activities

The ESA conducts business and consumer research, lobbies federal and state governments on issues such as intellectual property, content regulation, and trade, and runs public-relations programmes promoting the industry. In 1994 it helped establish the Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB), the self-regulatory body that assigns age and content ratings to video games in North America, partly in response to congressional hearings over violent games such as Mortal Kombat and Night Trap.

E3

The ESA owned and operated the Electronic Entertainment Expo (E3), for decades the industry's flagship trade show, held annually in Los Angeles from 1995. After cancellations during the COVID-19 pandemic and the withdrawal of major exhibitors, the ESA announced in December 2023 that E3 would not return.
