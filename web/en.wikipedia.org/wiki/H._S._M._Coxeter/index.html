Harold Scott MacDonald "Donald" Coxeter (9 February 1907 – 31 March 2003) was a British-born Canadian geometer, regarded as one of the greatest geometers of the twentieth century. He worked for more than sixty years at the University of Toronto.

Life and career

Coxeter was educated at Marlborough College and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he took his doctorate in 1931. After fellowships and a period at Princeton, he moved in 1936 to the University of Toronto, where he remained for the rest of his career, becoming a full professor in 1948. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada and of the Royal Society of London, and was made a Companion of the Order of Canada in 1997.

Work

Coxeter is best known for his work on regular polytopes and higher-dimensional geometry, summarised in his classic book Regular Polytopes (1948). The reflection groups he studied are now called Coxeter groups, and Coxeter–Dynkin diagrams are a standard tool in geometry and theoretical physics. His other books include Introduction to Geometry (1961), Regular Complex Polytopes (1974) and, with S. L. Greitzer, Geometry Revisited (1967).

Coxeter maintained a long friendship with the Dutch artist M. C. Escher, whose tessellations and the woodcut Circle Limit III were inspired by Coxeter's illustrations of hyperbolic tilings. He also influenced Buckminster Fuller, who dedicated his book Synergetics to Coxeter.

Death

Coxeter died in Toronto on 31 March 2003, aged 96.
