Columnist

A columnist is a writer for a newspaper, magazine or website who writes a regular recurring piece or article, called a column. Columns appear on a schedule (daily, weekly or monthly), usually in the same section and format, and typically express the writer's personal point of view, distinguishing them from straight news reporting.

Types of columnists

  • Political columnists comment on public affairs and policy, often from a recognizable ideological position.
  • Humour columnists write comic pieces about everyday life; celebrated examples include Art Buchwald, Russell Baker and Dave Barry.
  • Gossip columnists report on celebrities and society life, a tradition associated with figures such as Walter Winchell and Hedda Hopper.
  • Advice columnists answer readers' letters, as in the long-running Dear Abby and Ann Landers columns.
  • Sports, food, film and music columnists combine criticism with commentary in their fields.

Syndication and the op-ed page

Successful columnists are often syndicated, with their columns appearing in dozens or hundreds of papers. Opinion columns traditionally appear on the editorial and op-ed ("opposite editorial") pages. Since the rise of online media, many columnists have migrated to newsletters and independent platforms while retaining the column form.

History

The newspaper column developed in the 19th century and flourished in the 20th, when columnists such as Walter Lippmann and H. L. Mencken became major public voices. In American journalism, the most influential columnists have at times shaped political debate and presidential fortunes.
