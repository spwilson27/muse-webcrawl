Akyn

An akyn is an improvisational poet and singer in Kazakh and Kyrgyz culture. Akyns compose and perform verses on the spot, accompanying themselves on the dombra in Kazakhstan or the komuz in Kyrgyzstan, and are best known for the competitive song-duels called aitys.

RolesPoet, singer, storyteller, social commentator

Tradition

Akyns were central figures of the nomadic steppe: entertainers at feasts and celebrations, chroniclers of genealogies and epic deeds, and licensed critics of the powerful through satire. Their art demanded a prodigious memory, command of proverb and epic, quick wit and poetic fluency — skills honed through apprenticeship to a master (ustat). Female performers, akyn kyzdar, have long been part of the tradition.

Notable akyns

Celebrated historical akyns include the Kazakh masters Birzhan Sal, Akan Sery, Jayau Musa and Zhambyl Zhabayev, whose verses were transcribed and canonised in the Soviet era, and the Kyrgyz akyn Toktogul Satylganov, regarded as a founding figure of Kyrgyz written literature. The Kyrgyz manaschi, reciters of the Epic of Manas, are closely related performers, though devoted to a single monumental epic.

Modern era

Under Soviet rule, akyns were organised into official ensembles and competitions; since independence their improvisational contests have been revived and televised, and the art of the akyn has been recognised within UNESCO intangible-heritage listings both for aitys (2015) and Kyrgyz akyn performance traditions.
