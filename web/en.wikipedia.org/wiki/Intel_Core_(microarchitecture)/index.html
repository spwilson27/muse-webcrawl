Intel Core (microarchitecture)

The Intel Core microarchitecture is a multi-core processor microarchitecture unveiled by Intel in 2006, succeeding the NetBurst designs of the Pentium 4 era. It emphasised performance per watt and was derived in part from the Pentium M lineage.

Features

Core introduced a 14-stage pipeline, wide dynamic execution with macro-fusion of instructions, a shared L2 cache between cores, and improved power management. Processors based on it included the Core 2 Duo and Core 2 Quad families, manufactured first at 65 nm and later at 45 nm (the Penryn refresh).

Legacy

The microarchitecture restored Intel's performance lead over AMD in desktop and mobile computing and laid the groundwork for the later Nehalem design and the Core i3/i5/i7 branding.
