Master of Wine (MW) is a qualification — rather than an academic degree — issued by the Institute of Masters of Wine in the United Kingdom. It is generally regarded as one of the highest standards of professional knowledge in the wine trade, and the examination is notorious for its low pass rate.

The examination

The MW study programme has three stages, culminating in an exam held annually in London, the United States, and Australia. The final examination combines theory papers on viticulture, winemaking, the business of wine, and contemporary issues with three blind tastings of twelve wines each, in which candidates must identify grape varieties, origins, and quality levels. Successful candidates then complete a research paper on an original topic before being admitted to membership and signing the Institute's code of conduct.

History and membership

The first examination was held in 1953 for members of the British wine trade; twenty-one candidates sat and six passed. Membership was opened beyond the United Kingdom in the 1980s, and Masters of Wine now work in around thirty countries as winemakers, merchants, journalists, educators, and consultants. Well-known MWs include the writer Jancis Robinson.
