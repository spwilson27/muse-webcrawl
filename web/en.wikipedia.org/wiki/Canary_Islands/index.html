Canary Islands

The Canary Islands are a Spanish autonomous community and archipelago in the Atlantic Ocean, in Macaronesia, about 100 kilometres off the northwestern coast of Africa. The seven main islands are Tenerife, Fuerteventura, Gran Canaria, Lanzarote, La Palma, La Gomera and El Hierro, with capitals shared between Santa Cruz de Tenerife and Las Palmas de Gran Canaria.

The islands are volcanic: Tenerife's Teide (3,715 metres) is Spain's highest peak. Known since antiquity (the 'Fortunate Isles') and home to the Indigenous Guanches before Spanish conquest in the 15th century, the Canaries became a provisioning stop for Columbus and the Spanish treasure fleets. Today they are one of Europe's top tourist destinations (over 15 million visitors a year), a free-port economy, and home to major astronomical observatories on La Palma and Tenerife.
