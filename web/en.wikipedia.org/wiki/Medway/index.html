Medway

Medway is a unitary authority area in Kent, South East England. It takes its name from the River Medway, which flows through it to the Thames Estuary, and comprises the historic towns of Rochester, Chatham, and Gillingham, together with Rainham, Strood, and surrounding villages.

History

The Medway towns grew around the river's strategic crossing at Rochester — home to a Norman castle and the second-oldest cathedral in England, founded in 604 — and around Chatham Dockyard, established in the Tudor period and for centuries one of the Royal Navy's principal bases. The dockyard, where many famous warships were built, closed in 1984 and is now preserved as a historic site. The area is also associated with Charles Dickens, who lived at Gad's Hill near Rochester and set parts of his novels in the locality.

Governance

Medway Council became a unitary authority in 1998, when the boroughs of Rochester-upon-Medway and Gillingham merged and left Kent County Council's jurisdiction for most local services, though Medway remains part of the ceremonial county of Kent. A long-running campaign has sought city status for the conurbation as a whole under the name "City of Medway", without success to date.

Economy and transport

The economy mixes public services, education (including the Universities at Medway campus at Chatham Maritime), logistics, and commuting to London. Fast rail services from Strood, Rochester, Chatham, Gillingham, and Rainham reach London St Pancras in about 35–45 minutes via High Speed 1 from nearby Ebbsfleet.
