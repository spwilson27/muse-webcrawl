Han (cultural)

Han (Korean: 한) is a Korean concept often described as a feeling of unresolved resentment, grievance, grief, regret, and sorrow. It is considered a defining element of Korean identity and culture, expressed in traditional music, literature, film, and art.

Han is generally understood as an accumulated emotion arising from historical suffering, injustice, poverty, and personal loss that cannot be fully discharged. Rather than being purely negative, it is often paired with endurance, hope, and the creative transformation of sorrow into art.

Origins and interpretation

Scholars trace han to Korea's history of invasions, colonial occupation, war, and division, as well as to social hierarchies and personal hardship. Some modern critics argue that han as a national characteristic was partly shaped or popularised during the Japanese colonial period and later cultural discourse.

Expression in the arts

Han is frequently invoked to explain the emotional character of pansori, folk songs such as "Arirang", Korean cinema, and contemporary literature. It is sometimes contrasted with heung, a sense of joy and exuberance, and jeong, deep affection and attachment.
