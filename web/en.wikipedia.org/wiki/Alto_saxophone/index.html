Alto saxophone

Alto saxophone
Classification: Woodwind, single-reed aerophone
Key: E♭ (transposing)
Range: D♭4 to A5 (written), sounding a major sixth lower
Inventor: Adolphe Sax, 1840s
Related: Soprano, tenor, baritone saxophones

The alto saxophone is a member of the saxophone family of woodwind instruments. Saxophones were invented by the Belgian instrument designer Adolphe Sax in the 1840s; the alto is the second most common saxophone, after the tenor, and the most common for beginners.

Description

The alto saxophone is a transposing instrument in E♭: music written C sounds E♭ a major sixth below. It is smaller than the tenor and larger than the soprano, with a curved neck and upturned bell, usually made of brass with a lacquered or plated finish, played with a single reed on a beak-shaped mouthpiece.

Uses

The alto is central to concert bands, jazz big bands (typically two altos in a five-sax section, the lead alto being a prominent chair), small-group jazz, classical saxophone repertoire and popular music. In military and marching bands it often carries melodic lines.

Notable players

Classical: Marcel Mule, Sigurd Raschèr, Eugene Rousseau. Jazz: Johnny Hodges, Charlie Parker — the defining bebop altoist — Cannonball Adderley, Phil Woods, Ornette Coleman and Kenny Garrett. Parker's virtuosity made the alto a lead solo voice in modern jazz.

Repertoire

Important classical works include Glazunov's Concerto in E-flat (1934), Ibert's Concertino da camera and pieces by Debussy, Milhaud and Creston.
