Verbal noun

A verbal noun or gerundial noun is a noun formed from or otherwise corresponding to a verb. Different languages have different types of verbal noun, and many behave in part like nouns and in part like verbs. In English, the term is often used for nouns ending in -ing (the gerund in its noun-like uses) and for deverbal nouns such as destruction (from destroy) or refusal (from refuse).

In English

English -ing forms span a spectrum: in "Swimming is fun", swimming behaves as a pure noun, while in "Swimming the channel took two hours", it retains verbal properties such as taking a direct object. Traditional grammar reserves "gerund" for the verbal end of this spectrum. Other suffixes forming verbal nouns include -tion, -ment, -al and -age, and some verbal nouns are formed by conversion, as in a run or a call.

In other languages

In Celtic languages such as Irish and Welsh, the verbal noun is the basic dictionary form of the verb and is used in many constructions where other languages use infinitives or participles. In Semitic languages, the Arabic maṣdar functions as a verbal noun. Japanese and Korean similarly make extensive use of verbal nouns combined with a "do" verb.
