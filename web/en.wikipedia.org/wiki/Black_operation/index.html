Black operation

Black operations are conducted so that the sponsoring government can plausibly deny involvement

A black operation or black op is a covert or clandestine operation by a government agency, a military unit, or a paramilitary organization in which the identity of the sponsor is concealed, and may involve activities that are illegal, controversial, or outside of standard military protocol. A key characteristic of a black operation is that it is carried out with great secrecy and often with plausible deniability — the ability of the responsible officials to deny knowledge of or responsibility for the action.

Terminology

The term "black" is used in intelligence communities to denote secrecy: black operations are funded by "black budgets," and their records may be classified at the highest levels. A black operation differs from a merely covert operation in the degree of deception: in a covert action the sponsor is concealed, while a black operation may additionally employ false flag methods, disguising the operation as the work of another party entirely.

Black sites and black propaganda

Related concepts include "black sites" — secret facilities operated by intelligence services, such as the detention and interrogation sites run by the CIA after 2001 — and "black propaganda," material purporting to come from a source other than its true one, used to deceive an adversary. Disinformation, sabotage, assassination, support for coups d'état, and clandestine paramilitary raids are among the activities commonly described as black operations.

Examples

Historical examples frequently cited include the British and American special operations of the Second World War, Cold War-era covert actions by the CIA and KGB, and operations by special forces units conducted without insignia. The extent and oversight of such operations remain subjects of political controversy: in the United States, covert actions generally require a presidential finding and notification of congressional intelligence committees, a requirement established after the revelations of the Church Committee in the 1970s.

In popular culture

Black operations are a staple of espionage fiction, thrillers, and video games, where "black ops" teams typically perform deniable missions with state-of-the-art equipment and no official recognition.
