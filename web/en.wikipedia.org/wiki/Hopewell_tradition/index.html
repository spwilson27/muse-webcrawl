Hopewell tradition

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hopewell tradition, also called the Hopewell culture or Hopewellian people, describes a network of prehistoric Native American cultures that flourished in the northeastern and midwestern United States along rivers in the Middle Woodland period, roughly from 100 BCE to 500 CE. It was not a single culture or society but a web of related peoples connected by a shared religious and trading system, known to archaeologists as the Hopewell Interaction Sphere.

Characteristics

Hopewell peoples built large earthen enclosures and burial mounds — vast geometric earthworks of circles, squares and octagons, some covering many hectares, such as those at Newark, Ohio, and at the Hopewell site in Ross County, Ohio (from the farm of Mordecai Hopewell, which gave the tradition its name). Burials of important persons in log-lined tombs were accompanied by elaborate grave goods crafted from exotic materials obtained over enormous distances: copper from the Great Lakes, mica from the southern Appalachians, obsidian from the Rocky Mountains (present-day Yellowstone), marine shells from the Gulf Coast, and shark teeth and silver from elsewhere. Crafted objects include copper earspools and breastplates, mica cut-outs, effigy pipes and finely made ceremonial blades.

Society and subsistence

Hopewell communities lived in small dispersed settlements and farmsteads, gathering periodically at the earthwork centres for ceremonies, feasting, exchange and burial rites. They practised horticulture of indigenous seed crops of the Eastern Agricultural Complex — including sunflower, squash, maygrass and knotweed — combined with hunting, fishing and gathering; maize was present but not yet the staple it later became.

Decline

Around 400–500 CE the building of great earthworks and the long-distance exchange of prestige goods faded, the bow and arrow and more intensive maize farming spread, and populations aggregated into different settlement patterns in the Late Woodland period. The descendants of Hopewell peoples include historic tribes of the Eastern Woodlands.

Preservation

Major sites are protected at Hopewell Culture National Historical Park in Ohio; in 2023 the Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
