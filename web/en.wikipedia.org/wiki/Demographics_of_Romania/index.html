Romania has a population of about 19 million, according to the 2021 census, down from a peak of over 23 million in the early 1990s. The decline reflects low fertility, high mortality, and large-scale emigration since Romania's accession to the European Union in 2007; several million Romanians live and work abroad, principally in Italy, Spain, Germany, and the United Kingdom.

Ethnic groups

Ethnic Romanians form about 89 per cent of the population. The largest minorities are Hungarians (about 6 per cent, concentrated in Transylvania, especially the Székely Land counties of Harghita and Covasna) and Roma (officially about 3 per cent, though estimates run considerably higher). Smaller historic minorities include Ukrainians, Germans (Transylvanian Saxons and Banat Swabians, much reduced by emigration), Turks, Tatars, Russians (Lipovans), Serbs, Slovaks, Bulgarians, and Jews, whose once-substantial community was devastated by the Holocaust and subsequent emigration.

Language and religion

Romanian, a Romance language, is the sole official language and the mother tongue of the great majority. Hungarian is co-official locally in areas where Hungarians exceed 20 per cent of the population. About 85 per cent of Romanians identify as Orthodox Christians, with Roman Catholic, Reformed, Greek Catholic, and Pentecostal minorities.

Urbanisation

About 54 per cent of the population lives in urban areas. Bucharest, the capital, dominates with about 1.7 million inhabitants; other major cities include Iași, Cluj-Napoca, Timișoara, Constanța, and Craiova.
