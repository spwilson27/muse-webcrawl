Bride

A bride is a woman who is about to be married or who is a newlywed. The term comes from Old English brȳd. The male counterpart is the bridegroom (often shortened to groom). Brides are central figures in wedding ceremonies worldwide, and customs surrounding their clothing, attendants, and role vary greatly between cultures.

Attire

In many Western cultures, brides traditionally wear a white wedding dress, a custom popularised by Queen Victoria's wedding in 1840. In other traditions, brides may wear red (common in China and India), elaborate kimono in Japan, or regionally specific garments and jewellery. Brides commonly carry a bouquet and may wear a veil.

Attendants

Brides are often accompanied by bridesmaids, a maid or matron of honour, and flower girls. Rituals involving the bride include the exchange of vows and rings, and in some cultures the giving of a dowry or bride price.
