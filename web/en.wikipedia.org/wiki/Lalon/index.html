Lalon

Lalon, also known as Lalon Shah or Fakir Lalon Shah (c. 1772 – 17 October 1890), was a Bengali spiritual leader, saint, mystic, songwriter and social reformer. An icon of Bengali folk culture, he inspired poets including Rabindranath Tagore and Kazi Nazrul Islam.

Life

Little is certain about Lalon's origins or religious background — he deliberately rejected caste and creed distinctions, describing himself simply as a human being. After surviving smallpox as a young man, he founded an ashram (akhra) at Chheuriya in Kushtia, in present-day Bangladesh, where he gathered disciples from Hindu and Muslim backgrounds alike.

Philosophy and songs

Lalon composed an estimated several thousand songs exploring the body as a microcosm of the divine ('the unknown bird in the cage' is among his best known), humanism, and the rejection of religious orthodoxy. His Baul songs are inscribed in oral tradition and celebrated at the Lalon festival held annually in Kushtia. UNESCO recognised the Baul tradition connected with him as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.
