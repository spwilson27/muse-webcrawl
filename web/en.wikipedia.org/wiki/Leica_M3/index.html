The Leica M3 is a 35mm rangefinder camera manufactured by Ernst Leitz in Wetzlar, Germany, from 1954 to 1966. It was the first model of the Leica M system and is widely regarded as one of the finest mechanical cameras ever made, combining a combined bright-line viewfinder/rangefinder, a new bayonet lens mount, and a rapid film-advance lever in a body of exceptional build quality.

Features

The M3 introduced several innovations. Its viewfinder, at 0.91× magnification — the highest of any M camera — displays illuminated frame lines for 50, 90, and 135mm lenses automatically as each lens is mounted; fitting special "goggled" 35mm lenses brings up the 35mm frame via an additional 50mm frame-line set. The bayonet mount allowed faster lens changes than the previous screw mount. Other refinements included a single-stroke advance lever (double-stroke on early examples), an automatically resetting frame counter, a hinged back door supplementing the removable baseplate for easier loading, and shutter speeds set on a single non-rotating dial from 1 to 1/1000 second plus bulb.

Use and legacy

Some 226,000 M3s were built, making it the best-selling M model until the M6 era. It became the archetypal photojournalist's camera, used by generations of documentary photographers, and remained in some professional use for decades. Because no later M matched the M3's viewfinder magnification, it retains a devoted following among 50mm shooters, and well-preserved examples command high prices.
