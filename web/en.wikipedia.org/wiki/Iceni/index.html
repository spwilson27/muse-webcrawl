Iceni

The Iceni or Eceni were an ancient Brythonic tribe who inhabited the area that now comprises the modern English counties of Norfolk and Suffolk (roughly the region of East Anglia) during the late Iron Age and early Roman era. Their territory bordered that of the Corieltauvi to the west and the Catuvellauni and Trinovantes to the south.

Roman period

The Iceni are best known for their revolt against Roman rule in AD 60/61 under their queen Boudica. Her husband, King Prasutagus, had ruled as an ally of Rome and willed his kingdom jointly to his daughters and the Roman emperor; on his death the Romans annexed the kingdom outright, seized property, flogged Boudica and assaulted her daughters. The ensuing uprising destroyed the Roman towns of Camulodunum (Colchester), Londinium (London) and Verulamium (St Albans) before Suetonius Paulinus defeated the Britons at the Battle of Watling Street, ending the revolt.

Culture and archaeology

The Iceni produced distinctive coinage, often bearing a stylised horse, and fine metalwork, including the great gold torcs of the Snettisham Hoard. Their settlements included the major oppidum at Thetford and the later Roman town of Venta Icenorum (Caistor St Edmund), whose name means "market town of the Iceni". Earlier, in AD 47, the tribe had been disarmed after unrest, and during the invasion period they had not been part of the resistance to Claudius's invasion of AD 43.
