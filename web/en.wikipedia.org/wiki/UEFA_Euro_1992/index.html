The 1992 UEFA European Football Championship, commonly referred to as Euro 92, was the ninth European Championship, held in Sweden from 10 to 26 June 1992. Eight teams contested the tournament, which was won in one of football's greatest upsets by Denmark, who defeated world champions Germany 2–0 in the final in Gothenburg.

Background

Denmark had not originally qualified: they finished second in their qualifying group behind Yugoslavia, but Yugoslavia was excluded shortly before the tournament because of the Yugoslav Wars and United Nations sanctions. Denmark were invited to take their place, with many of their players recalled from holiday. Yugoslavia's expulsion, the participation of the CIS team representing the former Soviet Union, and Germany's first appearance as a reunified nation reflected the continent's post-Cold War upheaval.

Tournament summary

Denmark drew with England, lost to Sweden, and then beat France to reach the semi-finals, where they eliminated the defending champion Netherlands on penalties after a 2–2 draw. In the final, goals from John Jensen and Kim Vilfort sealed a 2–0 victory over Germany, completing the famous "Danish fairy tale". It was the last European Championship with eight teams and the last in which goalkeepers could pick up back-passes — the back-pass rule was changed in direct response to the time-wasting seen at the tournament.
