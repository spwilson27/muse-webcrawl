Superman (1978 film)

Superman (marketed as Superman: The Movie) is a 1978 superhero film directed by Richard Donner, based on the DC Comics character. It stars Christopher Reeve as Superman, alongside Marlon Brando as Jor-El, Gene Hackman as Lex Luthor, and Margot Kidder as Lois Lane. The film tells Superman's origin story from the destruction of Krypton to his battles against Luthor in Metropolis. It was a major critical and commercial success, grossing over $300 million worldwide, winning a Special Achievement Academy Award for its visual effects, and is widely credited with establishing the modern superhero film genre.

Director: Richard Donner
Starring: Christopher Reeve, Marlon Brando, Gene Hackman, Margot Kidder
Music: John Williams
Released: 15 December 1978

Production

Producers Alexander and Ilya Salkind shot the film back-to-back with Superman II, a decision that contributed to a troubled production and Donner's eventual dismissal from the sequel. Mario Puzo, David and Leslie Newman, Robert Benton and Tom Mankiewicz contributed to the screenplay. John Williams composed the score, whose main theme remains iconic. The marketing tagline "You'll believe a man can fly" reflected the film's groundbreaking flying effects, achieved with a combination of wire work, front projection and optical compositing.

Legacy

Superman was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 2017. It spawned three sequels with Reeve, and its tone and structure influenced generations of comic-book adaptations, including the later DC and Marvel films.
