Solanales


Clade: Asterids (lamiids)
Families: 5 (APG IV)
Includes: Solanaceae, Convolvulaceae

The Solanales are an order of flowering plants, included in the asterid group of dicotyledons. Well-known members include the nightshades, morning glories, and sweet potato.

Classification

Under the APG IV system, the order contains five families: Solanaceae (nightshades, including potato, tomato, and tobacco), Convolvulaceae (morning glories and sweet potato), Montiniaceae, Sphenocleaceae, and Hydroleaceae. Older systems, such as Cronquist's, placed the group within the subclass Asteridae and sometimes included additional families. Molecular phylogenetics places Solanales within the lamiid clade of asterids, sister to the Lamiales.

Characteristics

Members commonly have alternate, simple leaves without stipules, and flowers with fused petals (sympetalous corollas), often funnel-shaped or tubular. Many produce alkaloids or other distinctive secondary compounds.
