Forum theatre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Forum theatre is a form of interactive theatre created by the Brazilian director Augusto Boal as part of his Theatre of the Oppressed. In forum theatre a short play depicting a situation of oppression is performed; it is then performed again, and any member of the audience — a "spect-actor" — may call "stop", step into the scene, replace the oppressed protagonist and try out strategies to change the outcome. A facilitator, the "joker", mediates between the stage and the audience.

Origins

Boal developed the technique in Brazil in the 1970s, influenced by the pedagogy of Paulo Freire. Legend holds that the form emerged after a performance in which an audience member, frustrated that the actor would not follow her suggestion, was invited on stage to enact it herself. Exiled by Brazil's military dictatorship, Boal developed his methods in Peru, Argentina and Europe, and set out the theory in his book Theatre of the Oppressed (1974).

Practice

The initial scene, the "anti-model", must end badly for the protagonist and present a concrete, changeable situation rather than fate. Interventions are tested against the other characters, played by actors who maintain the oppressive logic of the scene. The audience collectively evaluates which strategies might work in real life: forum theatre is thus a "rehearsal for reality" and for social action. Boal later extended the approach into legislative theatre while serving as a city councillor in Rio de Janeiro, using forum techniques to gather policy proposals, some of which became law.
