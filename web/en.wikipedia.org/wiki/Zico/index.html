Zico

Arthur Antunes Coimbra (born 3 March 1953), better known as Zico, is a Brazilian football coach and former player who played as an attacking midfielder. Renowned for his vision, passing and free kicks, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time and the finest player of the late 1970s and early 1980s, earning the nickname the "White Pelé."

Club career

Zico spent most of his career at Flamengo, leading the club's golden era and scoring more than 500 goals for it. With Flamengo he won the Copa Libertadores and the Intercontinental Cup in 1981 — scoring twice against Liverpool in Tokyo — plus multiple Brazilian and Rio state championships. A two-year spell at Udinese in Italy (1983–85) showcased him in Serie A before he returned to Flamengo. He finished his playing career in Japan with Kashima Antlers, becoming a founding figure of the professional J.League.

International career

Zico starred for Brazil at the 1978, 1982 and 1986 World Cups. The 1982 team, with Sócrates, Falcão and Éder, is considered one of the greatest sides never to win the tournament, losing 3–2 to Italy in a celebrated match. Zico scored 48 goals in 71 internationals, despite injuries limiting his 1986 contribution.

Coaching career

As coach, Zico managed Japan to the 2004 Asian Cup title and the 2006 World Cup, and later coached Fenerbahçe (reaching the 2008 Champions League quarter-finals), CSKA Moscow, Olympiacos, Al-Gharafa and FC Goa, and served as Iraq's national coach. He also founded the Zico Football Centre (CFZ) in Rio and served as Brazil's Minister of Sport in 1990–91.
