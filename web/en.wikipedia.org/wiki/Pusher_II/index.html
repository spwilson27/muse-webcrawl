Pusher II (Danish: Pusher II: With Blood on My Hands) is a 2004 Danish crime film written and directed by Nicolas Winding Refn. It is the second instalment of the Pusher trilogy, following Pusher (1996) and preceding Pusher 3 (2005). The film centres on Tonny, the dim, tattooed sidekick of the first film — played by Mads Mikkelsen in a breakthrough role — newly released from prison.

Plot

Tonny leaves prison determined to earn the respect of his father, "the Duke", a feared car thief and fence who treats him with open contempt. He learns that an ex-girlfriend, Charlotte, has had a son she claims is his, and he is confronted with a fatherhood he is wholly unprepared for. Employed reluctantly in his father's chop-shop operation, Tonny bungles a Ferrari theft, accumulates debts, and is humiliated by everyone around him. His desperate attempts to prove himself escalate until, in the film's climax, he turns violently on the father whose approval he craved, and flees Copenhagen with the baby.

Production and themes

Refn made the two sequels in quick succession to rescue his production company from bankruptcy after the commercial failure of Fear X (2003). The film retains the first instalment's handheld, naturalistic style and chronological shooting. Beneath the crime narrative it is a study of toxic masculinity, paternal rejection and the wish for respect: its English subtitle, "With Blood on My Hands", refers both to Tonny's tattoo ("RESPECT" is inked across the back of his head) and to his trajectory.

Reception

Pusher II is frequently singled out as the strongest of the trilogy and was a critical breakthrough for Mikkelsen, whose performance was widely praised in Denmark and abroad. The trilogy consolidated Refn's reputation and remains a reference point for European crime cinema.
