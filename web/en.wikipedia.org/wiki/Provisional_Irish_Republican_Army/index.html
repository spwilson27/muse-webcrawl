Provisional Irish Republican Army

The Provisional Irish Republican Army (Provisional IRA), officially the Irish Republican Army (IRA; Irish: Óglaigh na hÉireann), was an Irish republican paramilitary force that sought to end British rule in Northern Ireland, facilitate Irish reunification and bring about an independent, socialist republic encompassing all of Ireland. It was the largest and most active republican paramilitary group during the Troubles.

Origins

The IRA split in December 1969 into the Provisional IRA and the Official IRA, following disagreement over abstentionism, socialism and the failure to defend Catholic neighbourhoods during the 1969 riots. The Provisionals, led initially by figures including Seán Mac Stíofáin, launched an armed campaign in 1970 against the British Army and Royal Ulster Constabulary.

The Troubles

During the conflict the IRA was responsible for the deaths of around 1,700 people, including some 1,000 members of the security forces and about 640 civilians. It carried out bombings in Northern Ireland and England — including the Brighton hotel bombing of 1984 and the City of London Bishopsgate and Baltic Exchange bombs — and assassinations, robberies and punishment attacks. It received funding and weapons from Irish-American supporters and, in the 1980s, substantial arms shipments from Libya. The IRA itself lost about 275 members killed.

Ceasefires and decommissioning

The IRA declared a ceasefire in 1994, broke it with the 1996 Docklands bombing, and restored it in 1997, enabling Sinn Féin — its political wing — to enter the talks that produced the 1998 Good Friday Agreement. On 28 July 2005 the IRA announced the end of its armed campaign, and in September 2005 international monitors confirmed that it had decommissioned its weapons.
