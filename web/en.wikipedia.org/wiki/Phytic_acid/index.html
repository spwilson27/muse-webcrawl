Phytic acid

Phytic acid is an organic compound with the formula C6H18O24P6. It is the principal storage form of phosphorus in many plant tissues, especially bran and seeds, where its salt form, phytate, stores phosphate and cations for the developing seedling.

Chemistry and occurrence

Phytic acid is myo-inositol hexakisphosphate (IP6): a six-carbon inositol ring esterified with six phosphate groups. It is abundant in cereals, legumes, nuts, and oil seeds. Because humans lack significant intestinal phytase, phytate can chelate dietary minerals such as iron, zinc, and calcium and reduce their absorption, leading to its description as an antinutrient.

Food processing and health

Soaking, germination, and fermentation activate endogenous phytases and reduce phytate content. Despite its effects on mineral absorption, phytic acid and its lower inositol phosphates have also been studied for antioxidant and possible anticancer properties.

Agriculture and environment

Phytate in animal feed passes largely undigested into manure, contributing to phosphorus runoff and eutrophication; supplemental phytase enzymes are widely added to poultry and swine diets to improve phosphorus availability.
