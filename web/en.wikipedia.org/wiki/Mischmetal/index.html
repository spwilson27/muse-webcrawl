Mischmetal

Mischmetal alloy

Mischmetal (from German: Mischmetall — "mixed metal") is an alloy of rare-earth elements, typically consisting of about 55% cerium, 25% lanthanum, 15–18% neodymium and other rare earths, with traces of iron and magnesium. It is produced directly from rare-earth ores such as monazite and bastnäsite without separating the individual elements.

Uses

Because the rare earths are chemically similar, mischmetal serves as a cheap source of their combined properties. Its best-known use is in lighter flints and ferrocerium fire starters, since it is pyrophoric. It is also used as a deoxidiser and desulfuriser in steelmaking, in vacuum tubes as a getter material, and as the starting material from which individual rare earths are separated.

History

The alloy was developed by Carl Auer von Welsbach, who also pioneered the separation of rare-earth elements and gas mantles.
