Booker T. & the M.G.'s were an American instrumental R&B and soul band formed in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1962. As the house band at Stax Records, they played on hundreds of recordings by artists including Otis Redding, Sam & Dave, Wilson Pickett, Carla Thomas and Albert King, and released hit instrumentals of their own, most famously "Green Onions" (1962).


The classic line-up

Career

The band grew out of the Mar-Keys, the Stax studio group. Keyboardist Booker T. Jones, guitarist Steve Cropper, bassist Lewie Steinberg and drummer Al Jackson Jr. recorded "Green Onions" almost by accident during studio downtime in 1962; the record, with Jones's Hammond organ riff, reached No. 1 on the R&B chart and No. 3 on the pop chart. Donald "Duck" Dunn replaced Steinberg in 1964, completing the classic line-up — notably one of the first racially integrated rock groups, with two Black and two white members.

As the Stax house band the M.G.'s defined the Memphis soul sound: tight, economical grooves behind virtually the label's entire catalogue. Their own hits included "Hip Hug-Her" (1967), "Soul Limbo" (1968), "Hang 'Em High" (1968) and "Time Is Tight" (1969). The band broke up in the early 1970s amid Stax's decline; Jackson was murdered in 1975, ending hopes of a full reunion of the classic line-up, though Jones, Cropper and Dunn later toured together, famously as the backing band for Bob Dylan, Neil Young and others, and at the 1992 Bob Dylan 30th Anniversary Concert.

Legacy

Booker T. & the M.G.'s were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1992 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2007. "Green Onions" remains one of the most recognisable instrumentals in popular music.
