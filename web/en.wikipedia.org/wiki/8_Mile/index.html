8 Mile is a 2002 American drama film directed by Curtis Hanson and written by Scott Silver. Set in Detroit in 1995, it stars Eminem in his film debut as Jimmy "B-Rabbit" Smith Jr., a working-class young rapper struggling to make a name for himself in the city's hip-hop battle scene while dealing with family hardship. The film's title refers to 8 Mile Road, the highway that forms the boundary between Detroit and its wealthier northern suburbs.

Plot

Jimmy, a metal-factory worker living in a trailer with his alcoholic mother Stephanie (Kim Basinger) and young sister, chokes during an opening rap battle at a local club. Encouraged by his friend Future (Mekhi Phifer) and love interest Alex (Brittany Murphy), he confronts his fears and defeats rival Papa Doc in a climactic battle, pre-empting his opponent's insults by rapping about his own failures first.

Reception and legacy

The film was a commercial and critical success, grossing nearly $243 million worldwide. Eminem's song "Lose Yourself," written for the film, won the Academy Award for Best Original Song—the first hip-hop song to win the award—and became one of his signature hits. Eminem's performance was widely praised, though he did not pursue a major acting career afterward.
