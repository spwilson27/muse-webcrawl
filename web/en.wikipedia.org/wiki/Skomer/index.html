Skomer

LocationPembrokeshire, Wales
Area2.92 km² (292 ha)
DesignationsNational Nature Reserve, SSSI, SPA

Skomer (Welsh: Ynys Sgomer) is an island off the coast of Pembrokeshire, on the west coast of Wales. It is well known for its wildlife: around half the world's population of Manx shearwaters nest there, and the island hosts one of the largest Atlantic puffin colonies in southern Britain. Skomer is a National Nature Reserve, a Site of Special Scientific Interest, and a Special Protection Area, managed by the Wildlife Trust of South and West Wales.

Wildlife

Skomer is home to roughly 350,000 breeding pairs of Manx shearwaters — among the largest colonies in the world — which return to their burrows only at night. Some 40,000 Atlantic puffins breed on the island in spring and early summer. Other residents include razorbills, guillemots, storm petrels, choughs, short-eared owls, and the endemic Skomer vole, a subspecies of bank vole found nowhere else. Grey seals breed in the surrounding waters, and porpoises and dolphins are regularly seen.

History

The island preserves one of the best-surviving prehistoric landscapes in Britain, with Iron Age field systems, hut circles, standing stones, and a defended promontory settlement. It was farmed into the twentieth century; the old farm buildings now serve as visitor accommodation. The name Skomer derives from Old Norse Skalmey, "cleft island," referring to the rocky inlets dividing its headlands.

Access

Day visitors reach Skomer by boat from Martin's Haven, weather permitting, during the breeding season from spring to late summer. Numbers are limited daily to protect the wildlife, and overnight stays are available in converted farm buildings.
