Gene duplication

Gene duplication (or chromosomal duplication, or gene amplification) is a major mechanism through which new genetic material is generated during molecular evolution. It can be defined as any duplication of a region of DNA that contains a gene. Gene duplications can arise as products of several types of errors in DNA replication and repair machinery as well as through fortuitous capture by selfish genetic elements.

Mechanisms

Common sources of gene duplications include ectopic recombination (unequal crossing over), retrotransposition, aneuploidy, and whole genome duplication (polyploidy). Unequal crossing over between misaligned homologous chromosomes can produce one chromosome with a duplication and one with a deletion. In retrotransposition, an mRNA transcript is reverse-transcribed and inserted into the genome, producing a processed copy lacking introns.

Fates of duplicated genes

Because the duplicated copy is initially redundant, one copy is free to accumulate mutations. The possible fates of the two copies include:

  • Nonfunctionalisation: one copy accumulates disabling mutations and becomes a pseudogene.
  • Neofunctionalisation: one copy evolves a new, beneficial function while the other retains the original function.
  • Subfunctionalisation: the two copies partition the functions of the ancestral gene between them, so both are retained.

Role in evolution

Gene duplication is regarded as the most important evolutionary force in generating new genetic material, a view developed extensively by Susumu Ohno in his 1970 book Evolution by Gene Duplication. Duplication followed by divergence has produced large gene families such as the globins and the Hox genes, and whole-genome duplication events in yeast, plants, and early vertebrates have had major evolutionary consequences.

Role in disease

Duplications of genes and genomic regions can also cause disease. Copy number variation arising from duplication is implicated in disorders such as Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease type 1A (duplication of the PMP22 gene) and some cases of developmental and neurological disorders.
