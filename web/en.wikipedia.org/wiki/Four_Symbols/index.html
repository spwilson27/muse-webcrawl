Four Symbols

The Four Symbols (Chinese: 四象; pinyin: Sì Xiàng, literally "four images") are four mythological creatures appearing among the Chinese constellations along the ecliptic, and viewed as the guardians of the four cardinal directions. They are the Azure Dragon of the East, the Vermilion Bird of the South, the White Tiger of the West, and the Black Tortoise (also called "Black Warrior") of the North.

Correspondences

Each symbol governs a direction, a season, and seven of the twenty-eight lunar mansions of Chinese astronomy. The Azure Dragon represents east, spring, and the colour green/blue; the Vermilion Bird represents south, summer, and red; the White Tiger represents west, autumn, and white; and the Black Tortoise — often depicted entwined with a snake — represents north, winter, and black. In five-phase (wuxing) theory they correspond to wood, fire, metal and water respectively, with the centre and earth sometimes represented by a fifth creature, the Yellow Dragon or Qilin.

History and spread

The system developed in Chinese astronomy by the late Warring States and Han periods; tiles depicting the four creatures decorated Han dynasty tombs and palace roofs. The symbols spread with Chinese culture to Korea (where they appear in Goguryeo tomb murals), Japan (the Shijin), and Vietnam, and remain common motifs in feng shui, martial arts, literature and popular culture.
