Szczecin (German: Stettin) is the capital and largest city of the West Pomeranian Voivodeship in north-western Poland. Located on the Oder river about 57 km south of the Baltic Sea, close to the German border, it is Poland's seventh-largest city and, with nearby Świnoujście, forms one of the country's principal seaport complexes.

History

A Slavic Pomeranian stronghold existed here by the 8th–9th centuries. The settlement received town rights in 1243 and joined the Hanseatic League. After centuries under Pomeranian dukes, the city passed to Sweden in 1648, then to Prussia in 1720, developing into a major German port and shipbuilding centre as Stettin. Heavily bombed in the Second World War, it was transferred to Poland in 1945 under the Potsdam Agreement; its German population was expelled and replaced by Poles, many displaced from former eastern territories. Shipyard workers in Szczecin were central to the protests of 1970 and the Solidarity strikes of 1980, and the August 1980 Szczecin Agreement preceded the better-known Gdańsk Agreement.

Geography and economy

The city straddles the Oder and surrounds Lake Dąbie. Its economy centres on the port, maritime services, ship repair (the Szczecin Shipyard, restructured after 2009), chemicals and food processing, with a growing IT sector. Landmarks include the Ducal Castle, the Chrobry Embankment terraces, Szczecin Cathedral and the modern Philharmonic hall (2014), which won the EU Mies van der Rohe Award.
