Tequila Sunrise (cocktail)

The Tequila Sunrise is a cocktail made of tequila, orange juice and grenadine syrup, served unmixed in a tall glass. The grenadine sinks, creating a gradient of red through orange that recalls a sunrise. It is an IBA official cocktail.

History

An earlier version appeared in the 1930s at the Arizona Biltmore Hotel in Phoenix, made with tequila, crème de cassis, lime juice and soda water. The modern version was created in the early 1970s by bartenders Bobby Lozoff and Billy Rice at the Trident restaurant in Sausalito, California. At a 1972 private party there, Mick Jagger tasted it; the Rolling Stones adopted it on their 1972 tour, which they nicknamed the "Cocaine and Tequila Sunrise Tour". The Eagles' 1973 song "Tequila Sunrise" cemented its fame.

Preparation

Pour 45 ml tequila and 90 ml orange juice over ice in a highball glass, then add 15 ml grenadine, which sinks to the bottom; garnish with an orange slice and a cherry. Variants substitute other spirits or juices (e.g. a Tequila Sunset using grapefruit juice or blackberry brandy).
