Book

A book is a medium for recording information in the form of writing or images. Books are typically composed of many pages, bound together and protected by a cover. Modern books are usually printed on paper, but they are also distributed in electronic form as e-books and as audiobooks. The technical term for the physical arrangement of a book is the codex, the form that replaced the scroll in late antiquity.

History

Writing systems developed in Mesopotamia and Egypt used clay tablets and papyrus scrolls. The codex — folded sheets bound along one edge — emerged in the Roman world and spread during the first centuries AD, made from parchment and later paper. Paper, invented in China, reached Europe via the Islamic world. The invention of movable-type printing by Johannes Gutenberg around 1440 dramatically lowered the cost of books and helped drive the spread of literacy, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution.

Structure

A typical book comprises a cover, front matter (title page, copyright page, table of contents, preface), the body text divided into chapters, and back matter (appendices, index, bibliography). Books are classified into fiction and non-fiction, and into genres; libraries organise them with classification systems such as the Dewey Decimal Classification.

Modern publishing

The book industry today includes trade, academic, and educational publishing, print-on-demand, and digital formats. An ISBN uniquely identifies each edition of a book. The largest number of new titles are published in China, the United States, and the United Kingdom.
