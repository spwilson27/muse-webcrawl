Joseph Lanner

Joseph Lanner (12 April 1801 – 14 April 1843) was an Austrian dance-music composer and violinist, regarded together with Johann Strauss I as one of the founders of the Viennese waltz. His lyrical, elegant dances raised popular dance music to the level of art and made the waltz the emblematic music of Biedermeier Vienna.

Life and career

Lanner was born in the Viennese suburb of St. Ulrich, the son of a glove-maker. Largely self-taught on the violin, he began playing in tavern ensembles, including that of Michael Pamer, and by about 1819 had formed his own small group, which grew into one of Vienna's most popular dance orchestras. Among his early musicians was the young Johann Strauss, who played viola in Lanner's orchestra before leaving in 1825 to found a rival band — beginning one of music's famous friendly rivalries, since the two men remained personally close.

Lanner's orchestra was in demand at the great suburban dance halls and coffee houses of Vienna, and he toured with it in Austria and beyond. In 1829 he became musical director of the Redoute balls at the Hofburg. He died of typhoid fever in 1843, aged forty-two, at the height of his fame.

Music

Lanner composed more than 200 works — waltzes, ländler, galops, quadrilles, and potpourris. His most famous waltzes include Die Schönbrunner, Die Werber, Die Mozartisten, and the melancholy Abend-Sterne. Where Strauss's manner was rhythmic and brilliant, Lanner's was softer and more singing; together they fixed the form of the Viennese waltz — a chain of short waltz sections with introduction and coda — that the younger Johann Strauss II would carry to perfection.
