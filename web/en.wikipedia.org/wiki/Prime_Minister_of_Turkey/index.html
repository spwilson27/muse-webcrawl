Prime Minister of Turkey

Prime Minister of Turkey (Başbakan)
Formation: 1920 (Government of the Grand National Assembly)
First holder: Mustafa Kemal Atatürk
Final holder: Binali Yıldırım
Abolished: 9 July 2018

The Prime Minister of Turkey (Turkish: Başbakan) was the head of government of the Republic of Turkey from 1920 until 2018, when the office was abolished following the 2017 constitutional referendum and the transition to a presidential system.

History

The premiership originated with the Government of the Grand National Assembly during the Turkish War of Independence, with Mustafa Kemal Atatürk as its first holder. After the proclamation of the Republic in 1923, İsmet İnönü became the first prime minister of the republican era. For nearly a century the prime minister led the Council of Ministers and conducted the day-to-day business of government, while the president was head of state.

Abolition

The constitutional amendments approved in the April 2017 referendum replaced the parliamentary system with a presidential one. After the June 2018 elections, executive power passed entirely to the President of Turkey, and the office of prime minister ceased to exist; Binali Yıldırım was its last holder.

Role under the parliamentary system

The prime minister was appointed by the president, traditionally the leader of the largest party in the Grand National Assembly, and the government required the assembly's confidence. The prime minister chaired the cabinet, coordinated ministries, and could request a vote of confidence.
