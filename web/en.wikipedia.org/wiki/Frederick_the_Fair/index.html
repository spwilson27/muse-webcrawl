Frederick the Fair

Frederick the Fair (German: Friedrich der Schöne; 1289 – 13 January 1330), from the House of Habsburg, was Duke of Austria and Styria from 1308, and from 1314 also rival King of Germany (as Frederick III) in opposition to Louis IV, Holy Roman Emperor.

Life

Frederick was the second son of King Albert I of Germany. After his father's assassination in 1308 he ruled Austria jointly with his brother Leopold I. On the death of Emperor Henry VII, Frederick was elected king by part of the electors in 1314, while others chose Louis of Bavaria, producing a disputed succession. The rivals were cousins and former friends; the conflict was decided at the Battle of Mühldorf in 1322, where Frederick was defeated and captured.

Imprisoned at Trausnitz Castle, Frederick was eventually released under the Treaty of Trausnitz (1325) and, remarkably, agreed to share rule with Louis — a co-kingship approved in the Treaty of Munich. He withdrew largely to Austria and died in 1330 at Gutenstein.
