General Atomics

General Atomics (GA) is an American energy and defense corporation headquartered in San Diego, California, specializing in research and technology development including nuclear energy, unmanned aerial vehicles, and advanced electromagnetic systems. Founded in 1955 as a division of General Dynamics, it became an independent, privately held company in 1986 when acquired by Neal and Linden Blue.

History

General Atomics was created in 1955 to explore peaceful uses of nuclear energy. Early work included the TRIGA (Training, Research, Isotopes, General Atomics) research reactor, first critical in 1958, more than sixty of which were sold worldwide, and development of high-temperature gas-cooled reactors. Frederic de Hoffmann was its first president, and physicist Edward Teller was among its early advocates.

Business areas

Through General Atomics Aeronautical Systems, Inc. (GA-ASI), the company produces the Predator family of remotely piloted aircraft, including the MQ-1 Predator and MQ-9 Reaper, widely used by the U.S. Air Force and allied militaries. General Atomics Electromagnetic Systems develops electromagnetic aircraft launch systems (EMALS) and advanced arresting gear for U.S. Navy carriers, as well as railgun and pulsed-power research. The parent company continues nuclear work, including fusion research at the DIII-D National Fusion Facility, which it operates for the U.S. Department of Energy.
