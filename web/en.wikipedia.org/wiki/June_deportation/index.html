The June deportation (Estonian: juuniküüditamine; Latvian: jūnija deportācijas; Lithuanian: birželio trėmimai) was a mass deportation carried out by the Soviet Union on 14 June 1941, a year after the Soviet occupation of the Baltic states, Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina, and shortly before the German invasion of the USSR.

Background

Following the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact and the annexations of 1940, Soviet security organs under the NKVD compiled lists of "anti-Soviet elements" — former officials, military officers, police, landowners, industrialists, members of national organisations, teachers, clergy and their families. The deportation was planned under instructions associated with Ivan Serov, deputy head of the NKVD.

The operation

In the night of 13–14 June 1941, families were arrested without warning, given minutes to pack, and loaded into freight and cattle wagons. Men were generally separated from their families and sent to Gulag labour camps, where most perished; women, children and the elderly were exiled to remote settlements in Siberia, Kazakhstan and the far north.

Approximately 10,000 people were deported from Estonia, some 15,000 from Latvia, and around 17,000–18,000 from Lithuania; parallel operations in Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina (present-day Moldova and Ukraine) deported tens of thousands more. Figures vary by source and by which operations are included.

Aftermath and memory

Many deportees died of hunger, cold and disease; survivors in exile were generally allowed to return only after Stalin's death in the mid-1950s, though some were never permitted to return home. The June deportation was the first of several mass deportations from the Baltic region, including the larger Operation Priboi of March 1949.

14 June is commemorated annually in Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania as a day of mourning and remembrance, with ceremonies and moments of silence for the victims.
