Ozymandias (Watchmen)


Real name: Adrian Alexander Veidt. Created by: Alan Moore and Dave Gibbons. First appearance: Watchmen #1 (1986).

Ozymandias (real name Adrian Veidt) is a fictional character in the graphic novel Watchmen, created by writer Alan Moore and artist Dave Gibbons and published by DC Comics in 1986–1987. A former costumed adventurer turned billionaire industrialist, Veidt is regarded within the story as "the smartest man on the planet."

Character history

Born in 1939 to wealthy German immigrant parents, Veidt gave away his inheritance at seventeen and retraced the route of Alexander the Great through the Mediterranean and Middle East. Inspired by Alexander and by the Egyptian pharaoh Ramesses II — whose Greek name, Ozymandias, he adopted — he became a masked crime-fighter before retiring in 1975 to build a business empire under the Veidt Enterprises banner.

Role in Watchmen

Veidt is eventually revealed as the architect of the novel's central conspiracy: to avert nuclear war between the United States and the Soviet Union, he engineers a catastrophic event — in the comic, a genetically engineered creature teleported into New York City — that kills millions but unites the world against a perceived common threat. When confronted by Rorschach and Nite Owl, he reveals he acted "35 minutes ago," making the plan irreversible. His moral calculus, and the heroes' uneasy silence, form the novel's unresolved ending.

Reception and portrayals

Ozymandias is frequently ranked among the greatest comic-book villains and most complex antagonists in the medium. Matthew Goode portrayed him in Zack Snyder's 2009 film adaptation, and Jeremy Irons played an older Veidt in the 2019 HBO television sequel series.
