Sukhumi (Georgian: სოხუმი, Sokhumi; Abkhaz: Аҟәа, Aqwa; Russian: Сухум, Sukhum) is a city on the Black Sea coast and the capital of Abkhazia, a breakaway republic internationally recognised as part of Georgia by most countries. It has a population of roughly 65,000, much reduced from before the war of 1992–93.

History

The site was settled by Greek colonists in the 6th century BC as Dioscurias, an important trading port. It later came under Roman and Byzantine rule (as Sebastopolis), the medieval kingdom of Georgia, and the Ottoman Empire, which built a fortress there in the 1720s. The city became part of the Russian Empire in 1864 and grew into a popular subtropical resort during the Soviet period, known for its sanatoria, botanical garden (founded 1840), and a primate research institute famous for its monkey nursery.

War and aftermath

Sukhumi was heavily damaged in the War in Abkhazia (1992–93), at the end of which Abkhaz separatist forces captured the city on 27 September 1993; the Georgian population fled or was expelled, and many buildings in the centre remain derelict. Since then the city has slowly rebuilt as the de facto capital of Abkhazia, whose independence is recognised by Russia and a small number of other states.

Geography and climate

The city sits on a wide bay on the eastern Black Sea, backed by the foothills of the Greater Caucasus. It has a humid subtropical climate with warm summers and mild winters, supporting palms and other subtropical vegetation along its seafront promenade, the Makhajirs' Quay.
