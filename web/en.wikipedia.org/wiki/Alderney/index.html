Alderney (French: Aurigny) is the northernmost of the inhabited Channel Islands. It is part of the Bailiwick of Guernsey, a British Crown Dependency. It is 3 miles (5 km) long and 1.5 miles (2.4 km) wide. The island's area is 3 square miles (8 km²), making it the third-largest island of the Channel Islands, and the second largest in the bailiwick.

History

Alderney was heavily fortified in the Victorian era against a feared French invasion, and its ring of forts remains a prominent feature. During the Second World War the entire population was evacuated before German forces occupied the island in 1940; Alderney hosted German labour camps, including Lager Sylt, the only Nazi concentration camp on British soil. The islanders returned after liberation in 1945.

Government and life

The island is governed by the States of Alderney, which sends representatives to the States of Guernsey. Its only town is Saint Anne. Alderney is known for its wildlife, including a colony of blonde (leucistic) hedgehogs, its railway — the only working railway in the Channel Islands — and its strong tidal races, long considered a potential source of tidal power.
