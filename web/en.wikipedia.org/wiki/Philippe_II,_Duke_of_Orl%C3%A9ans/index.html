Philippe II, Duke of Orléans

Philippe II, Duke of Orléans (2 August 1674 – 2 December 1723) was a French prince, soldier, and statesman who served as Regent of the Kingdom of France from 1715 to 1723, during the minority of King Louis XV. The period is known as the Régence.

FatherPhilippe I, Duke of Orléans

Early life

The only son of Philippe I and Elizabeth Charlotte of the Palatinate, he distinguished himself as a cavalry commander in the Nine Years' War and the War of the Spanish Succession, and pursued wide interests in chemistry, music, and painting.

The Régence

On Louis XIV's death in 1715, Orléans had the late king's will annulled by the Parlement of Paris and assumed full regency powers. His rule saw the polysynody experiment in government, John Law's financial System and its collapse in the Mississippi Bubble, and a liberalization of court culture.

Death

He died at Versailles in December 1723, shortly after Louis XV's majority had been declared. His son Louis became the next Duke of Orléans.
