Desso GrassMaster

Desso GrassMaster is a sports playing field surface composed of natural grass combined with artificial fibres. The artificial fibres are injected 20 centimetres (7.9 in) deep and extend about 2 centimetres (0.79 in) above the soil surface, anchoring the natural grass roots and stabilising the pitch. Approximately 20 million synthetic fibres are stitched into a typical pitch by computer-controlled machines.

History and use

The system was developed by the Dutch company Desso (later part of Tarkett Sports). The first pitch was installed at Huddersfield's stadium in 1993 (the then McAlpine Stadium), and the technology came to prominence when it was used for the redeveloped Wembley Stadium in London. GrassMaster surfaces have been installed at many major venues, including stadiums in the Premier League, the NFL, and FIFA World Cup venues in South Africa (2010) and Brazil (2014).

Characteristics

Because the surface is about 3% artificial fibre by volume, it plays and looks like natural grass but is significantly more durable, recovering faster after heavy use and resisting divots and wear in high-traffic areas such as goal mouths. Critics initially raised concerns about player injuries and hardness, though studies have generally found injury rates comparable to natural grass.
