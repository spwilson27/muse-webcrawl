Cleon

Cleon (died 422 BC) was an Athenian general and demagogic politician during the Peloponnesian War, the leading figure in Athens after the death of Pericles in 429 BC until his own death at Amphipolis.

EraClassical Athens

Career

A wealthy tanner by trade, Cleon rose as a speaker in the Assembly, championing the common citizens against the aristocracy. After the revolt of Mytilene in 427 BC he urged the execution of its male population — a decree reversed the next day after the speech of Diodotus reported by Thucydides. In 425 BC he claimed credit for the victory at Sphacteria, where Spartan hoplites surrendered on the island near Pylos.

Death and reputation

Sent to Thrace in 422 BC, Cleon was killed outside Amphipolis in a battle won by the Spartan general Brasidas, who also died of his wounds. Hostile sources — the historian Thucydides and the comic playwright Aristophanes, who savaged him in The Knights — portray him as a vulgar warmonger; modern historians note this class bias and credit him as an effective, if ruthless, democratic leader.
