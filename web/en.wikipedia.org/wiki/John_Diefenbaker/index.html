John Diefenbaker

John George Diefenbaker (September 18, 1895 – August 16, 1979) was a Canadian politician who served as the 13th prime minister of Canada from 1957 to 1963. The only Progressive Conservative leader between 1930 and 1979 to lead his party to an election victory, he won one of the largest majorities in Canadian history in 1958.

Prime ministership

A Saskatchewan criminal lawyer famous for his courtroom oratory, Diefenbaker became party leader in 1956 and upset the long-governing Liberals in 1957. His government appointed the first Indigenous Canadian senator and the first woman cabinet minister, extended the federal vote to First Nations people, and passed the Canadian Bill of Rights in 1960. His "Northern Vision" promised development of Canada's Arctic and resource frontier.

Controversies and fall

Diefenbaker's cancellation of the Avro Arrow interceptor in 1959 remains controversial, and his strained relations with the Kennedy administration — especially his hesitation over accepting nuclear warheads during and after the Cuban Missile Crisis — contributed to his government's defeat in 1963. He remained party leader until 1967 and sat in Parliament until his death in 1979, an MP for nearly forty years.
