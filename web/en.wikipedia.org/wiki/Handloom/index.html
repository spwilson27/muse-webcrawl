Handloom

A handloom is a simple machine used for weaving, operated manually without the aid of electric power. Weaving on a handloom is done by intertwining two sets of yarn — the warp (lengthwise) and the weft (crosswise) — held under tension on a frame. Handlooms range from small frame and lap looms to large floor looms with shedding mechanisms operated by treadles and heddles.

Types

Common types include the pit loom, frame loom, backstrap (body-tension) loom, and the throw-shuttle and fly-shuttle looms. The flying shuttle, patented by John Kay in 1733, greatly increased weaving speed and paved the way for mechanised power looms during the Industrial Revolution.

Modern role

Although industrial production dominates textile manufacturing, handloom weaving survives as a craft and as a livelihood for millions of weavers, notably in India, where handloom cloth such as khadi carries cultural and political significance and is protected by legislation and geographic-indication marks.
