Packet (information technology)

In information technology, a packet is a formatted unit of data carried by a packet-switched network. A packet consists of control information (a header, and sometimes a trailer) and user data, known as the payload. Control information provides data for delivering the payload — for example, source and destination network addresses, error detection codes, and sequencing information.

Packet switching

In packet switching, data is divided into packets that are routed independently through a network and reassembled at the destination. This contrasts with circuit switching, in which a dedicated path is reserved for the duration of a communication. Packet switching, pioneered in the 1960s by Paul Baran and Donald Davies, underlies the Internet, where packets are formatted according to the Internet Protocol (IP).

Structure

A typical packet comprises:

  • Header — protocol version, header and total length, source and destination addresses, time-to-live, and checksum fields.
  • Payload — the actual data being carried, often itself a packet of a higher-layer protocol (encapsulation).
  • Trailer — in some protocols (e.g., Ethernet frames), a trailing frame check sequence for error detection.

Network devices forward packets based on header information: switches forward frames within a local network, while routers forward packets between networks using the destination IP address.

Terminology

Precise terminology varies by layer of the OSI model. A packet is the protocol data unit of the network layer (layer 3); at the data link layer (layer 2) the unit is a frame, and at the transport layer (layer 4) TCP uses segments while UDP uses datagrams. In common usage, however, "packet" is applied generically to all of these.

Packet loss and fragmentation

Packets may be delayed, reordered, duplicated, or lost in transit; reliable protocols such as TCP detect and retransmit lost packets. When a packet exceeds the maximum transmission unit (MTU) of a link, it may be fragmented into smaller pieces and reassembled at the destination.
