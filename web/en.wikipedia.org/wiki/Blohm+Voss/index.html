FoundersHermann Blohm, Ernst Voss

Blohm+Voss (historically Blohm & Voss) is a German shipbuilding company founded in Hamburg in 1877 by Hermann Blohm and Ernst Voss. Based on the Steinwerder island in the port of Hamburg, the yard became one of Germany's most important shipbuilders, constructing warships, ocean liners and, in the modern era, some of the world's largest private superyachts.

History

The company built numerous vessels for the Imperial German Navy and the Kriegsmarine, including the battleship Bismarck, launched in 1939, and many U-boats during the Second World War. Its aircraft subsidiary, Hamburger Flugzeugbau, later Blohm & Voss's aviation division, produced unusual designs under chief designer Richard Vogt, such as the asymmetrical BV 141 reconnaissance aircraft and the BV 238 flying boat, the heaviest aircraft of the war.

Modern era

After the war the yard resumed merchant shipbuilding and specialised in naval vessels — including MEKO-type frigates and corvettes for the German Navy and export customers — and in superyacht construction and refit. Famous yachts built or refitted there include Roman Abramovich's Eclipse and the historic Savarona. The company passed through the ownership of ThyssenKrupp and later private investors; its naval business was separated, and the Hamburg yard now focuses on yacht new builds, refits and ship repair under the Blohm+Voss name within the Lürssen group.
