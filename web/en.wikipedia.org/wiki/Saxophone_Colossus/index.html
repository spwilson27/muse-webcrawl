Saxophone Colossus is a studio album by the American jazz saxophonist Sonny Rollins, recorded on 22 June 1956 and released on Prestige Records in 1957. It is widely regarded as Rollins's masterpiece and one of the classic albums of the hard bop era.

Music

Rollins is accompanied by pianist Tommy Flanagan, bassist Doug Watkins, and drummer Max Roach. The album opens with "St. Thomas", a calypso-flavoured piece inspired by a Virgin Islands melody Rollins's mother sang, which became his signature tune. It also includes the standards "You Don't Know What Love Is" and "Strode Rode", Rollins's "Blue 7" — famous for its motivic improvisation, analysed in a well-known essay by Gunther Schuller — and a reading of "Moritat" (Mack the Knife) from The Threepenny Opera.

Reception

The album has been praised for the coherence and wit of Rollins's improvising. It was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and selected for preservation in the United States National Recording Registry by the Library of Congress in 2016 as "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant."
