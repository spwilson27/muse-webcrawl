Cheonggyecheon

Cheonggyecheon (Korean: 청계천) is a 10.9-kilometre-long (6.8 mi) stream and public recreation space in downtown Seoul, South Korea. A natural stream originating from the valley between Mount Inwang and Mount Bukak, it flows from Gwanghwamun to the Han River. The stream was covered by concrete during the post-Korean War development era and an elevated highway was built over it. In 2003, a major urban renewal project removed the highway and restored the stream; the restored Cheonggyecheon reopened in October 2005 and is now a landmark of urban regeneration in Seoul.

History

The stream was originally known as Gaecheon ("open stream") and was the subject of drainage and embankment works under the Joseon dynasty, notably under King Taejong and King Yeongjo. After the Korean War, impoverished settlements lined its banks, and the stream became polluted. Between 1958 and 1976 it was progressively covered over, and the Cheonggye Elevated Highway was completed above it in 1971, becoming a symbol of South Korea's rapid industrialisation.

Restoration

The restoration was a flagship project of Seoul mayor Lee Myung-bak. Beginning in July 2003, the elevated highway was demolished and the stream daylighted over a 5.8 km central section, with the work completed in September 2005 at a cost of over ₩386 billion. The stream is now flanked by walkways, stepping stones, waterfalls and public art, and is crossed by 22 bridges. Water is pumped from the Han River and groundwater to maintain flow.

Effects and criticism

The project is widely credited with cooling the surrounding urban area, improving air quality, encouraging public transport use and drawing millions of visitors annually. Critics have raised concerns about the cost, the artificial nature of the pumped water supply, the displacement of vendors and businesses, and the loss of the highway-era heritage. The project nevertheless became a global model cited in urban renewal debates.
