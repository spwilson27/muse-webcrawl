Palos de la Frontera (commonly called Palos) is a town and municipality located in the southwestern Spanish province of Huelva, in the autonomous community of Andalusia. It is famous throughout the world as the port from which Christopher Columbus set sail on his first voyage to the Americas on 3 August 1492.

History

The official foundation date of Palos is 1322, though the area was inhabited earlier. The town grew as a seafaring and fishing community on the estuary of the Tinto and Odiel rivers. In 1479 it passed to the Catholic Monarchs, Ferdinand and Isabella.

In 1492 the town was ordered to supply two caravels for Columbus's expedition. The local Pinzón brothers — Martín Alonso Pinzón, who captained the Pinta, and Vicente Yáñez Pinzón, who captained the Niña — were instrumental in recruiting crews from Palos and Moguer. Columbus departed from Palos on 3 August 1492 and returned there on 15 March 1493.

Columbian places

Palos forms part of the Lugares Colombinos (Columbian Places) together with Moguer and the monastery of La Rábida, where Columbus planned his voyage with Franciscan friars. Sites include the church of San Jorge, where the royal decree was read; the Fontanilla fountain, traditionally held to have supplied the ships with water; and the Muelle de las Carabelas, a museum quay with full-size replicas of the Niña, Pinta and Santa María.

Economy

The modern economy is dominated by agriculture, especially the intensive cultivation of strawberries exported across Europe, alongside fishing and industry connected with the port of Huelva.
