Former Qin (350–394) was a dynastic state of the Di ethnic group during the Sixteen Kingdoms period of Chinese history. Founded by Fu Jiàn in the Guanzhong region with its capital at Chang'an, it rose under his grandson Fu Jiān (Emperor Xuanzhao, reigned 357–385), advised by the Han Chinese statesman Wang Meng, to unify all of northern China by 376 — the only Sixteen Kingdoms state to do so — conquering Former Yan, Former Liang, and Dai, and seizing territory from the Eastern Jin.

Battle of Fei River and collapse

In 383 Fu Jiān launched a massive invasion of the Eastern Jin in the south, aiming to reunify China. His huge army was routed by a much smaller Jin force at the Battle of Fei River, one of the most famous battles in Chinese history. The defeat triggered rebellions by subject peoples (including the Xianbei of Later Yan and the Qiang of Later Qin); Fu Jiān was captured and killed in 385. Remnant regimes in the northwest persisted until 394, when the last Former Qin emperor was defeated by Later Qin and Western Qin.
