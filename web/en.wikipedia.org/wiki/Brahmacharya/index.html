Brahmacharya

Brahmacharya
Sanskritब्रह्मचर्य
Literal meaning"Conduct consistent with Brahman"; the path of Brahman
ContextHinduism, Buddhism, Jainism
RolesFirst ashrama (student stage); a yama in Yoga; a virtue of self-restraint

Brahmacharya (Sanskrit: ब्रह्मचर्य, "conduct of Brahman") is a concept in Indian religions signifying a lifestyle of disciplined self-restraint in pursuit of knowledge and liberation. In Hinduism it names the first of the four ashramas — the celibate student stage of life spent studying the Vedas under a guru. In Yoga it is one of the five yamas (restraints) of Patañjali, and in Jainism one of the five great vows (mahavratas) of monks. In its broadest modern usage, especially following Gandhi, it denotes continence and control of the senses in thought, word and deed.

As a stage of life

In the ashrama system, a twice-born boy entered brahmacharya with the upanayana (sacred thread) ceremony and lived in his teacher's household (gurukula), begging alms, tending the sacred fire and memorising scripture while practising celibacy, austerity and obedience. The stage traditionally lasted until about age 25, when the student might marry and become a householder (grihastha). Lifelong students, naiṣṭhika brahmacharins, sometimes remained celibate permanently.

In Yoga and Jainism

As a yama in the Yoga Sutras, brahmacharya is traditionally interpreted as celibacy for renunciates and fidelity and moderation for householders; attaining it, Patañjali says, yields vigour (vīrya). For Jain monks it is an absolute vow of chastity; Jain laypeople observe it as fidelity within marriage and contentment with one's spouse. Buddhism has a cognate concept — the holy life (brahmacariya) of the monastic sangha, including complete celibacy.

Gandhi

Mohandas Gandhi took a vow of brahmacharya in 1906 and made it central to his philosophy of self-rule, arguing that mastery over desire was the foundation of moral and political strength. His experiments and writings on the subject, including his autobiography, brought the term to worldwide attention.
