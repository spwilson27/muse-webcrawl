French Cathedral, Berlin

LocationGendarmenmarkt, Berlin, Germany
Built1701–1705; tower 1780–1785
DenominationOriginally Calvinist (Reformed)

The French Cathedral (German: Französischer Dom) is one of the two churches on the Gendarmenmarkt, a square in central Berlin, facing the German Cathedral across the square with the Konzerthaus between them. Despite its name it is a church, not a cathedral — it was never the seat of a bishop. It was built by and for Berlin's French-speaking Huguenot community, refugees who had fled religious persecution in France following the revocation of the Edict of Nantes in 1685 and were welcomed to Brandenburg by the Great Elector's Edict of Potsdam.

History

The church was constructed between 1701 and 1705, modelled on the destroyed Huguenot church of Charenton-Saint-Maurice near Paris. The domed tower, designed by Carl von Gontard, was added between 1780 and 1785 under Frederick the Great as part of his embellishment of the Gendarmenmarkt, giving the church its cathedral-like silhouette. The building was badly damaged in the Second World War and reconstructed by the East German government between 1977 and 1981.

Today

Since 1987 the church has housed the Huguenot Museum, documenting the history of the French Protestants in Berlin and Brandenburg. A viewing platform in the tower offers panoramic views over central Berlin, and the building hosts exhibitions and events; services are held by the French Reformed congregation in an associated chapel.
