Congress of People's Deputies of the Soviet Union

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Congress of People's Deputies of the Soviet Union (Russian: Съезд народных депутатов СССР) was the supreme governing body of the Soviet Union from 1989 until its dissolution in 1991. It was created by the constitutional amendments adopted in December 1988 as part of Mikhail Gorbachev's perestroika reforms, and was the first Soviet legislature to be chosen in a (partially) free and competitive election.

Composition and elections

The Congress consisted of 2,250 deputies: 750 elected from territorial districts, 750 from national-territorial districts, and 750 nominated by approved public organisations, including the Communist Party, trade unions, and the Academy of Sciences. The elections of March 1989 produced notable defeats for senior Communist officials running unopposed and brought reformers and nationalists into the legislature. From its membership the Congress elected a reformed, standing Supreme Soviet of 542 members, and in 1989 it elected Gorbachev as its chairman and head of state; in March 1990 it created the executive presidency and elected him the first (and only) President of the Soviet Union.

Sessions and dissolution

The Congress held five sessions, notable for televised debates watched by enormous audiences. It abolished the Communist Party's constitutionally guaranteed leading role in March 1990. However, its authority was progressively undermined by the assertiveness of the republics and by rival legislatures. On 5 September 1991, after the failed August coup, the Congress effectively dissolved itself, transferring power to a transitional State Council and Supreme Soviet; the Soviet Union ceased to exist in December 1991.
