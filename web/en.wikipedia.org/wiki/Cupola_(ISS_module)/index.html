Cupola (ISS module)

Launch date8 February 2010 (STS-130)
DockedTranquility (Node 3), nadir port
Mass1,880 kg
Diameter2.95 m
Height1.5 m

The Cupola is an ESA-built observatory module of the International Space Station (ISS). Its name derives from the Italian word cupola, which means "dome". It has seven windows, the largest of which is an 80 cm (31 in) circular window — the largest window ever flown in space at the time of launch.

Purpose

The Cupola provides an observation point for operating the station's robotic arms, viewing Earth, and monitoring visiting vehicles during docking and berthing operations. It houses one of the two robotic workstations from which the Canadarm2 is controlled. Its windows, fitted with shutters to protect against micrometeoroids, have become a favourite place for crew photography of Earth.

Construction and launch

The module was built by Thales Alenia Space in Turin, Italy, under contract to the European Space Agency. It was launched aboard Space Shuttle Endeavour on mission STS-130 in February 2010 together with the Tranquility node, to whose Earth-facing (nadir) port it is attached.

Specifications

  • Six side windows arranged around one central top window, each with fused silica and borosilicate glass panes.
  • Forged aluminium frame, about 2.95 m in diameter and 1.5 m high.
  • External shutters, opened and closed by the crew from inside.
