Xilinx, Inc. was an American technology and semiconductor company that primarily supplied programmable logic devices. The company is best known for inventing the field-programmable gate array (FPGA), a type of integrated circuit that can be configured by a customer after manufacturing.

History

Xilinx was founded in 1984 by Ross Freeman, Bernard Vonderschmitt, and James V. Barnett II. Freeman, an engineer at Zilog, wanted to create chips that acted like blank tapes, allowing users to program the technology themselves. In 1985 the company shipped the XC2064, the first commercially viable FPGA, containing 64 logic blocks. Xilinx operated as a fabless semiconductor company, outsourcing manufacturing to partners such as TSMC.

Products

The company's product lines included the Virtex and Kintex families of FPGAs, the Zynq system-on-chip platforms combining ARM processors with programmable logic, and the Versal adaptive compute acceleration platform. Its Vivado Design Suite software was used to design and implement circuits on its devices. Xilinx parts were widely used in telecommunications, automotive, aerospace, data centres, and industrial equipment.

Acquisition by AMD

In October 2020, AMD announced it would acquire Xilinx in an all-stock transaction valued at roughly $35 billion. The deal closed in February 2022, after which Xilinx's products were folded into AMD's embedded and adaptive computing businesses.
