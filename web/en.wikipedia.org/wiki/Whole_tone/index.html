Whole tone

In music, a whole tone (also whole step or major second) is an interval spanning two semitones — the distance from C to D, or from a note to the next note of the same kind two keys away on a piano. It is one of the basic building blocks of scales in Western music: the major scale is composed of whole tones and semitones in the pattern W–W–H–W–W–W–H.

Size in different tunings

In twelve-tone equal temperament, the whole tone is exactly 200 cents — one sixth of an octave. In just intonation there are two sizes of whole tone: the major tone of ratio 9:8 (about 204 cents) and the minor tone of ratio 10:9 (about 182 cents); their difference is the syntonic comma. In Pythagorean tuning only the 9:8 tone appears, and in quarter-comma meantone the whole tone is uniform at about 193 cents, halfway between the two just tones.

Terminology

The term "tone" for the interval derives from the Greek tonos. British usage favours "tone" and "semitone" where American usage has "whole step" and "half step". A scale made of nothing but whole tones is the whole-tone scale used by Debussy and others, and the tritone — three whole tones — splits the octave in half.
