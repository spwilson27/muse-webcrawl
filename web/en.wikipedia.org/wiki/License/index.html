License

A license (American English) or licence (British English) is an official permission or permit from an authority to own or use something, do a particular thing, or carry on a trade. A person who holds a license is a licensee; the party granting it is the licensor. Licenses may be issued by governments, private parties (especially for intellectual property), or other organisations, and may involve fees, examinations or conditions.

Types

Governments license activities in the interest of public safety and revenue: examples include driving licences, professional licences for doctors and lawyers, broadcasting licences, liquor licences and business permits. In private law, an intellectual property license authorises use of a patent, trademark, copyrighted work or software under stated terms while the licensor retains ownership — common forms include software licences, Creative Commons licences and free-software licences such as the GNU General Public License.

Legal nature

In property law a licence traditionally confers a personal permission that does not create an interest in land, unlike a lease or easement, though modern statutes regulate many licences extensively. Operating without a required licence, or beyond its scope, can result in fines, injunctions or criminal penalties.
