The Nordic Council is the official body for formal inter-parliamentary co-operation among the Nordic countries. Formed in 1952, it consists of 87 representatives from Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden as well as from the autonomous areas of the Faroe Islands, Greenland and Åland. The representatives are members of parliament in their respective countries and are nominated by those parliaments.

History

The Council was established in 1952 following proposals for closer Scandinavian cooperation after the Second World War. Early achievements included the Nordic Passport Union (1954), which abolished passport controls between member states, and a common labour market. Finland joined in 1955. The Helsinki Treaty of 1962 formalised the framework of Nordic co-operation, and in 1971 the Nordic Council of Ministers was created as the corresponding intergovernmental body. The Council's secretariat is in Copenhagen.

Work

The Council holds an annual Ordinary Session in the autumn, hosted in rotation by member countries, and a Theme Session in the spring. Its committees deal with culture and education, welfare, citizenship and consumer rights, the environment and natural resources, and business and industry. Recommendations adopted by the Council are addressed to the Nordic Council of Ministers and to national governments. The Council also awards five well-known annual prizes: for literature, film, music, children's and young people's literature, and the environment.
