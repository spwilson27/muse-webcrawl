Frederik's Church

LocationCopenhagen, Denmark
DenominationChurch of Denmark (Lutheran)
Construction1749–1894
ArchitectNicolai Eigtved; Ferdinand Meldahl

Frederik's Church (Danish: Frederiks Kirke), popularly known as The Marble Church (Marmorkirken), is an Evangelical Lutheran church in Copenhagen, Denmark. The church was designed by the architect Nicolai Eigtved in 1740 and was commissioned by King Frederick V as part of the Frederiksstaden district, built to commemorate the 300th anniversary of the House of Oldenburg's accession to the Danish throne.

Architecture

The church has the largest dome in Scandinavia, with a span of 31 metres, resting on twelve columns. Its design was inspired by St. Peter's Basilica in Rome. Construction began in 1749, but spiralling costs — the building was to be made entirely of Norwegian marble — led to work being halted in 1770, leaving the church an unfinished ruin for more than a century.

Completion

The industrialist Carl Frederik Tietgen financed the church's completion in the 1870s under architect Ferdinand Meldahl, this time using Danish limestone with marble reserved for the interior. The church was consecrated on 19 August 1894. Statues of Danish church figures, including Søren Kierkegaard and N. F. S. Grundtvig, surround the building. Today the Marble Church is one of Copenhagen's most visited churches and is used for services, concerts and weddings, including that of Crown Prince Frederik and Mary Donaldson in 2004.
