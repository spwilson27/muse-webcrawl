Minocher Rustom "Minoo" Masani (20 November 1905 – 27 May 1998) was an Indian politician, author, and a leading liberal voice in post-independence India. A veteran of the freedom movement who moved from socialism to classical liberalism, he co-founded the free-market Swatantra Party in 1959 and served as a Member of Parliament and as India's ambassador to Brazil.

Early life and politics

Masani was educated in Bombay and at the London School of Economics, where he was influenced by Harold Laski. He joined the Indian National Congress, helped found the Congress Socialist Party in 1934, and was imprisoned during the freedom struggle. Disillusioned with socialist central planning, he broke with the Congress after independence and became a prominent critic of the "permit-licence raj."

Swatantra Party and later life

In 1959 Masani joined C. Rajagopalachari in founding the Swatantra Party, which advocateded free enterprise, limited government, and individual liberty; it became the largest opposition party in Parliament after the 1967 elections. Masani edited the journal Freedom First, founded the Indian Liberal Group, and wrote many books, including Our India (1940), a bestseller for children, and his autobiography Bliss Was It in That Dawn (1977). He also led the world's first fact-finding mission into Tibet after the Chinese occupation, reporting for the International Commission of Jurists.
