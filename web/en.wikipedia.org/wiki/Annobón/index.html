Annobón is an island province of Equatorial Guinea in the Gulf of Guinea in the Atlantic Ocean. It is the southernmost and most remote part of the country, lying about 350 kilometres west of the mainland and just south of the equator. The island's capital is San Antonio de Palé.

Geography

Annobón is a volcanic island of about 17 square kilometres. Its terrain is rugged, with a crater lake, Lago A Pot, occupying the centre of the island. The climate is equatorial, and the island is covered in lush vegetation including breadfruit, banana and palm trees.

History

The island was uninhabited when it was encountered by Portuguese explorers on 1 January 1473, giving it its name from the Portuguese "Ano Bom" ("Good Year"). Portugal ceded Annobón to Spain under the Treaty of El Pardo in 1778. It became part of Equatorial Guinea upon that country's independence in 1968.

People and language

The Annobonese people speak Fa d'Ambô, a Portuguese-based creole language, alongside Spanish. Fishing, particularly for tuna and whales, has traditionally been central to the island's economy.
