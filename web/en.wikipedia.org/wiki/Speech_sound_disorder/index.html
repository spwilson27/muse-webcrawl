Speech sound disorder

A speech sound disorder is a type of communication disorder in which a person's production of speech sounds is persistently difficult or inaccurate for their age, reducing intelligibility. The umbrella term covers articulation disorders, which involve motor production of individual sounds, and phonological disorders, which involve patterns of sound errors reflecting the organisation of the sound system.

Development and symptoms

Young children normally simplify speech sounds as they learn to talk, for example replacing "rabbit" with "wabbit" (gliding) or omitting final consonants. Such processes are developmentally typical at some ages; a disorder is identified when errors persist beyond the age at which most children have mastered them, or when error patterns are atypical.

Causes

Many cases have no clear cause. Known contributors include hearing loss, structural problems such as cleft palate, neurological conditions, and childhood apraxia of speech, a motor planning disorder. Speech sound disorders may co-occur with language disorders and are associated with later literacy difficulties.

Treatment

Speech-language pathologists assess which sounds and patterns are in error and provide therapy targeting sound production and phonological contrasts, often through play-based practice for young children.
