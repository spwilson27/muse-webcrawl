Metacharacter

A metacharacter is a character that has a special meaning to a computer program, in the same way that a printer's or typewriter's control characters do. Within computing, the word is used primarily in the context of regular expressions and Unix shells.

Regular expressions

In regular expressions, characters such as . * + ? ( ) [ ] { } ^ $ | and \ act as operators rather than literals: for example, . matches any character, and * means "zero or more of the preceding element". To match a metacharacter literally it is usually escaped with a backslash, as in \. for a full stop.

Shells

In the Unix shell, characters such as * and ? trigger filename globbing, | creates a pipeline, > redirects output, and $ begins variable expansion. Quoting or escaping these characters suppresses their special meaning.
