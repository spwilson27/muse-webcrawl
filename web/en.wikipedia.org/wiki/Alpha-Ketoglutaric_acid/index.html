Alpha-Ketoglutaric acid

α-Ketoglutaric acid (also called 2-oxoglutaric acid) is a keto acid with the formula C5H6O5. Its anion, α-ketoglutarate (AKG), is one of the most important intermediates of cellular metabolism.

Role in metabolism

In the citric acid cycle, α-ketoglutarate is formed from isocitrate by oxidative decarboxylation and is itself converted to succinyl-CoA by the α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase complex, generating NADH and CO2. It is also the central hub of nitrogen metabolism: it accepts ammonia to form glutamate, and through transamination reactions links the metabolism of amino acids with that of carbohydrates. Glutamate derived from it is the precursor of glutamine, proline and arginine.

Other functions

α-Ketoglutarate is a co-substrate for a large family of dioxygenase enzymes, including prolyl hydroxylases that regulate the hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) and epigenetic enzymes that demethylate DNA and histones. It is sold as a dietary supplement (often as calcium or ornithine salts) and studied in ageing research, and it is used in clinical nutrition and in tests of liver function.
