Bibimbap (Korean: 비빔밥, literally "mixed rice") is a Korean rice dish. The term "bibim" means "mixing" and "bap" refers to cooked rice. It is served as a bowl of warm white rice topped with namul (sautéed and seasoned vegetables), gochujang (chilli pepper paste), soy sauce or doenjang, and commonly a raw or fried egg and sliced meat, and is stirred together thoroughly just before eating.

History

The dish is thought to have developed from the custom of mixing leftover side dishes with rice, and from ritual foods prepared for ancestral rites. It appears in Joseon-era cookbooks under names such as goldongban ("rice mixed with assorted ingredients"); the name bibimbap is attested from the late 19th century. The city of Jeonju is especially famous for its version.

Varieties

Dolsot-bibimbap is served in a sizzling hot stone bowl, so that the rice at the bottom crisps to a golden crust (nurungji) and the egg cooks on contact. Hoedeopbap uses raw seafood in place of cooked meat. Regional versions include Jeonju bibimbap, cooked in beef broth with bean sprouts, and Jinju bibimbap with seasoned raw beef. The colourful arrangement of ingredients is often said to represent the five cardinal colours of Korean obangsaek tradition: white, black, red, green/blue and yellow.

International popularity

Bibimbap became one of the most internationally recognised Korean dishes during the global spread of Korean cuisine in the 21st century; it has been served as an in-flight meal on airlines and was promoted in international advertising campaigns by the South Korean government.
