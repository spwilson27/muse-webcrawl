Green River Formation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Green River Formation is an Eocene geologic formation that records the sedimentation in a group of intermountain lakes across what is now northern Colorado, eastern Utah, and southwestern Wyoming in the United States. Deposited roughly 53.5 to 48.5 million years ago, it is famed for its exceptionally preserved fossils.

Fossils

The formation is a classic Lagerstätte: fine-grained laminated limestone and oil shale preserve complete fish (such as Knightia and stingrays), turtles, crocodiles, birds, bats (including Icaronycteris), early horses, insects, and palm leaves. Fossil Butte National Monument in Wyoming protects a portion of the deposits.

Oil shale

The Green River Formation contains the world's largest known deposits of oil shale, estimated to hold the equivalent of up to three trillion barrels of shale oil, with perhaps a trillion barrels potentially recoverable. Commercial extraction has repeatedly been attempted but has generally proved uneconomic. The formation also yields trona (natural soda ash) in Wyoming's Green River Basin.
