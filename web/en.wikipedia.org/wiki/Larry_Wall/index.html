Larry Wall

Larry Arnold Wall (born 27 September 1954) is an American computer programmer, linguist and author, best known as the creator of the Perl programming language, first released in 1987.

Trained in linguistics, Wall designed Perl as a practical text-processing and "glue" language, guided by the principles he summarised as laziness, impatience and hubris — the "three virtues of a programmer". His annual State of the Onion talks charted Perl's development, and he later led the design of Perl 6, eventually renamed Raku.

Wall also wrote the patch utility and the Usenet reader rn, and is co-author of Programming Perl (the "Camel Book").
