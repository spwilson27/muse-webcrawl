Robert De Niro


Born: August 17, 1943, New York City, U.S.
Occupation: Actor, film producer
Awards: Two Academy Awards

Robert Anthony De Niro (born August 17, 1943) is an American actor and film producer, widely regarded as one of the greatest actors of his generation. Known for his intense method acting and his long collaboration with director Martin Scorsese, he has won two Academy Awards.

Career

De Niro rose to prominence in Bang the Drum Slowly and Scorsese's Mean Streets (both 1973). He won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor as the young Vito Corleone in The Godfather Part II (1974), and gave defining performances in Taxi Driver (1976), The Deer Hunter (1978) and Raging Bull (1980) — winning Best Actor for the latter after famously gaining 60 pounds to play the older Jake LaMotta. His Scorsese collaborations continued with Goodfellas (1990), Casino (1995), The Irishman (2019) and Killers of the Flower Moon (2023).

He also succeeded in comedies such as Midnight Run (1988), Analyze This (1999) and Meet the Parents (2000). In 1989 he co-founded Tribeca Productions, and in 2002 the Tribeca Film Festival, created to help revitalise lower Manhattan after the September 11 attacks.
