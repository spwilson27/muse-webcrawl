Kibbeh

Kibbeh (Arabic: كبة, also spelled kubba) is a family of dishes found in Levantine and Middle Eastern cuisine, based on spiced ground meat, onions and grain, most commonly bulgur wheat. It is widely considered a national dish of Lebanon and is popular in Syria, Jordan, Palestine, Iraq, Turkey (as içli köfte), and in parts of Latin America where Levantine immigrants settled.

Preparation

The classic form consists of a shell made from fine bulgur, minced onions and ground lean meat pounded to a paste, stuffed with cooked spiced ground meat, pine nuts and onions, then shaped into a torpedo or football form and deep-fried. Other varieties are baked in a tray (kibbeh bi-siniyyeh), served raw (kibbeh nayyeh, similar to steak tartare), or simmered in a yogurt sauce (kibbeh labaniyyeh).

Regional varieties

In Iraq, versions made with rice flour are common. In Aleppo, numerous variations with different fillings and sauces are prepared. In Brazil and Colombia, the dish is known as quibe or kibi and is a common snack.
