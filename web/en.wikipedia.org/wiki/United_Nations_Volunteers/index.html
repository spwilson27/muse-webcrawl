The United Nations Volunteers (UNV) programme is a United Nations organization that contributes to peace and development through volunteerism worldwide. Established by the United Nations General Assembly in 1970, UNV is administered by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) and reports to the UNDP/UNFPA/UNOPS Executive Board.

UNV works in partnership with UN entities, governments, civil society and the private sector to integrate qualified, highly motivated and supported volunteers into development programming and promote the value and global recognition of volunteerism. Its headquarters are in Bonn, Germany, with field units and country teams in around 60 countries.

History

The programme was created by General Assembly resolution 2659 (XXV) of 7 December 1970, following an initiative proposed by the Shah of Iran, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, at the International Labour Organization conference in 1966, who suggested a "peace corps" of young volunteers for development. UNV began operations in 1971 and moved its headquarters from Geneva to Bonn in 1996.

UN Volunteers

Each year, UNV mobilizes thousands of volunteers who serve in some 130 countries. Volunteers are recruited under categories that include international UN Volunteers, national UN Volunteers, UN Youth Volunteers and online volunteers. In a typical year, around 7,000 to 8,000 people serve on assignments supporting peacekeeping, humanitarian response, health, education and sustainable development, while the UNV Online Volunteering service connects organizations with volunteers working remotely.

International Volunteer Day

UNV is the designated focal point in the UN system for International Volunteer Day, observed annually on 5 December since it was mandated by the General Assembly in 1985. The day recognizes the contribution of volunteers worldwide. UNV also led the observance of the International Year of Volunteers in 2001 and publishes the periodic State of the World's Volunteerism Report.
