Bernard Lawrence Madoff (29 April 1938 – 14 April 2021) was an American financier who operated the largest Ponzi scheme in history, defrauding thousands of investors of billions of dollars over decades. He was a former chairman of Nasdaq and founder of Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities, whose legitimate market-making business masked a secret investment-advisory operation run from a separate floor of his office.

The scheme

Madoff promised steady, moderate returns purportedly from a "split-strike conversion" strategy. In reality, client funds were deposited in a single bank account and redemptions were paid from new investors' money; statements were fabricated. Account statements showed about $65 billion including fictitious profits, while actual principal losses were estimated at roughly $18 billion. Whistleblower Harry Markopolos repeatedly warned the SEC, which failed to uncover the fraud in several examinations.

Collapse and trial

During the 2008 financial crisis, redemption requests of about $7 billion exposed the scheme. Madoff confessed to his sons on 10 December 2008 and was arrested the next day. He pleaded guilty in March 2009 to 11 federal felonies and was sentenced to 150 years in prison. A court-appointed trustee, Irving Picard, recovered over $14 billion for victims through clawback suits. Madoff died in prison at Butner, North Carolina, in April 2021.
