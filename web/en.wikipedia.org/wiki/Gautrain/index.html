The Gautrain is an 80-kilometre (50-mile) rapid transit railway system in the Gauteng province of South Africa, linking Johannesburg, Pretoria, Ekurhuleni, and O. R. Tambo International Airport. Trains travel at speeds of up to 160 kilometres per hour (99 mph), and the journey between Johannesburg and Pretoria takes about 42 minutes. The name "Gautrain" is a portmanteau of "Gauteng" and "train".

History

The project was announced by the Gauteng provincial government in 2000 and built as a public–private partnership. The first section, between Sandton and the airport, opened on 8 June 2010, just in time for the 2010 FIFA World Cup hosted by South Africa. The remaining sections to Johannesburg Park Station and Pretoria–Hatfield opened in 2011 and 2012.

Network

The system has ten stations: Hatfield, Pretoria, Centurion, Midrand, Marlboro, Sandton, Rosebank, Park Station, Rhodesfield, and O. R. Tambo International Airport. Uniquely for South Africa, it uses standard gauge track rather than the country's usual Cape gauge. Trains are built by Bombardier and based on the Electrostar design used in the United Kingdom.
