Leonidas I

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Bronze statue of Leonidas at Thermopylae, spear and shield raised]

Leonidas I (c. 540 – 480 BC) was king of Sparta from about 489 BC until his death at the Battle of Thermopylae in 480 BC, where he led the Greek rearguard that died holding the pass against Xerxes' invasion. One of the Agiad royal house, half-brother of his predecessor Cleomenes I (whose daughter Gorgo he married), Leonidas is known almost entirely for his last battle: Herodotus' account made him the exemplar of Spartan obedience and self-sacrifice ('Come and take them', his reputed reply to the demand to surrender his arms, is Sparta's most famous laconic phrase). His body, recovered forty years later, was reburied at Sparta with honours and commemorated in annual games.

Thermopylae

When the Greek allies resolved to hold the pass of Thermopylae in 480 BC, Leonidas took an advance guard of some 7,000 men, including his 300 picked Spartiates — all, Herodotus notes, men with living sons. Delay was expected (the main Spartan army was held by the Carneia festival and the Olympic truce, in Herodotus' telling). After two days of repulsing Persian assaults, the outflanking by the Anopaia path doomed the position; Leonidas dismissed the bulk of the force and remained with the Spartans, Thespians and Thebans, dying in the melee over which the Persians and Greeks fought for possession of his corpse. Xerxes had the body crucified and beheaded — an act Herodotus calls un-Persian in its spite.

Legacy

Simonides' epitaph at the pass — 'Go tell the Spartans...' — began a legend retold in every age: Plutarch's sayings, the paintings of David, the 1962 film The 300 Spartans and the 1998 graphic novel and 2006 film 300, which carried Leonidas worldwide while taking great liberties. Sparta raised a bronze statue to him in 1955 at Thermopylae; modern scholarship insists on the wider Greek force and the strategic (delaying) purpose, without diminishing the stand's deliberate self-sacrifice — the core fact that made the legend.
