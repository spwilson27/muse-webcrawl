Palace Square

Palace Square (Russian: Дворцовая площадь, Dvortsovaya ploshchad) is the central square of Saint Petersburg, Russia, linking Nevsky Prospekt with Palace Bridge and the Neva River. Bounded to the north by the Baroque Winter Palace (now the main building of the Hermitage Museum) and to the south by the semicircular General Staff Building with its triumphal arch, the square is considered one of the finest ensembles of monumental urban architecture in Europe and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage site of the historic centre of Saint Petersburg.

Layout and buildings

The square took its shape between the 1750s and the 1830s. Its northern side is the green-and-white Winter Palace built for Empress Elizabeth to designs by Bartolomeo Rastrelli (1754–62). Opposite, Carlo Rossi's General Staff Building (1819–29) sweeps in a great arc, pierced at its centre by a triumphal arch crowned with a chariot of Victory, commemorating Russia's triumph over Napoleon. In the centre of the square stands the Alexander Column (1830–34), designed by Auguste de Montferrand: a single polished red-granite monolith, 25.6 m tall and surmounted by an angel, erected to commemorate Emperor Alexander I and the Russian victory of 1812; remarkably, it is held in place only by its own weight.

Historical events

Palace Square has repeatedly been the stage of Russian history. On Bloody Sunday, 9 (22) January 1905, troops fired on a peaceful procession of workers led by Father Georgy Gapon as it marched to present a petition at the Winter Palace, an event that ignited the Revolution of 1905. In October (November) 1917 the Provisional Government was overthrown when Bolshevik forces entered the Winter Palace across the square — the "storming" later dramatised far beyond its modest reality in Soviet commemoration and in Eisenstein's film October.

Today the square hosts concerts, military parades on Victory Day, the Scarlet Sails festival and other civic events, and serves as the principal public space of the city centre.
