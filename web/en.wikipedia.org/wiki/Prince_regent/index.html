Prince regent

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A prince regent or princess regent is a prince or princess who, due to their position in the line of succession, rules a monarchy as regent, while the incumbent monarch is indisposed — for reasons such as minority, absence, incapacity, or (in a case such as the Regency era of the United Kingdom) mental illness. Although the term can be applied in generic terms, it is mainly used for specific periods, most famously the British Regency era, during which George, Prince of Wales, ruled as regent for his father George III, who was incapacitated by illness.

Regency in the United Kingdom

The Regency Act 1811 formally appointed the Prince of Wales as regent after George III's final descent into madness. The period from 1811 to the death of George III in 1820 is known as the Regency era, and is associated with distinctive trends in British architecture, literature, fashion, and politics. Upon his father's death, the Prince Regent became King George IV.

Other examples

  • France: Philippe II, Duke of Orléans, was regent for the young Louis XV from 1715, a period known as the Régence.
  • Germany: Prince Luitpold of Bavaria served as Prince Regent (Prinzregent) of Bavaria from 1886, first for Ludwig II and then for Otto, giving his name to the Prinzregentenzeit.
  • Liechtenstein: Hereditary Prince Alois has served as regent for his father Hans-Adam II since 2004.
  • China: Dorgon and Zaifeng, Prince Chun, both held the title of Prince Regent (Shezheng Wang) during the Qing dynasty.
