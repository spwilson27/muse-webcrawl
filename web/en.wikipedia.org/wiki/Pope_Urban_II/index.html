Pope Urban II

[Image: 19th-century painting of Urban II preaching the crusade at the Council of Clermont, 27 November 1095]

Pope Urban II (c. 1035 – 29 July 1099), born Odo of Châtillon, was head of the Catholic Church from 1088 to his death. A former prior of Cluny and a product of the Gregorian Reform, he is best known for convening the Council of Clermont in 1095, at which he preached the expedition that became the First Crusade.

Pontificate

Elected at Terracina while Rome was held by the antipope Clement III, Urban spent years patiently recovering his position against Emperor Henry IV, using councils, legates and alliances — including the marriage of Matilda of Tuscany to Welf of Bavaria — to isolate his enemies. By the mid-1090s he was again master of Rome and extending papal influence in Spain, Sicily and the reform of the French church.

Clermont and the crusade

In March 1095, at Piacenza, envoys of the Byzantine emperor Alexios I Komnenos asked for military aid against the Seljuk Turks. On 27 November 1095, at Clermont, Urban addressed a great crowd in a field outside the city: in the versions reported by chroniclers he called on the knights of the West to cease fighting one another, liberate the eastern churches and free Jerusalem, promising remission of sins to those who died on the expedition. The cry of Deus vult! ("God wills it") spread the appeal across Europe, and the response — tens of thousands taking the cross — far exceeded expectations. Urban spent the following year preaching the crusade in France and organising its leadership.

Death and legacy

Urban died in Rome on 29 July 1099, two weeks after the crusaders captured Jerusalem but before the news reached him. He was beatified in 1881. His pontificate strengthened the reform papacy, and his Clermont sermon is one of the most consequential speeches of the Middle Ages.
