Bryan Singer

Bryan Jay Singer (born September 17, 1965) is an American filmmaker. He rose to prominence with the crime thriller The Usual Suspects (1995) and became a major figure in superhero cinema by directing X-Men (2000) and X2 (2003).

Career

Singer's early film Public Access won a prize at the Sundance Film Festival. The Usual Suspects, written by Christopher McQuarrie, was a critical and commercial success. His X-Men films helped launch the modern era of comic-book movies. He later directed Superman Returns (2006), Valkyrie (2008), Jack the Giant Slayer (2013), X-Men: Days of Future Past (2014), and X-Men: Apocalypse (2016), and was a producer on the television series House.

Bohemian Rhapsody and controversies

Singer was the original director of the Queen biopic Bohemian Rhapsody (2018) but was fired during production and replaced by Dexter Fletcher, though he retained the director credit. His career has been shadowed by repeated allegations of sexual misconduct, which he has denied; reporting on the allegations in 2019 led to his removal from further projects.
