Three-body problem

In physics, specifically classical mechanics, the three-body problem is the problem of taking the initial positions and velocities of three point masses that orbit each other in space and calculating their subsequent trajectories using Newton's laws of motion and Newton's law of universal gravitation. Unlike the two-body problem, the three-body problem has no general closed-form solution: the system is chaotic for most initial conditions, so its behaviour can only be predicted numerically or through approximate methods.

History

The problem occupied Newton, whose work on the Moon's motion was frustrated by the Sun's perturbing influence. In 1887 Heinrich Bruns showed that no additional algebraic integrals of motion exist, and Henri Poincaré, competing for a prize offered by King Oscar II of Sweden, proved that the problem could not be solved by the methods then available — work that founded the modern theory of dynamical systems and chaos. In 1912 Karl Sundman found a convergent series solution of theoretical but no practical value.

Special solutions

Although the general problem is chaotic, particular families of periodic solutions exist. Euler and Lagrange found configurations in which the three bodies maintain fixed relative positions (along a line, or at the vertices of an equilateral triangle, corresponding to the Lagrange points). In 1993 Christopher Moore discovered the "figure-eight" orbit, in which three equal masses chase each other along the same figure-of-eight path; many further periodic solutions have since been found numerically. Restricted versions of the problem, in which one mass is negligible, are fundamental to celestial mechanics and spacecraft trajectory design.

Applications

The three-body problem arises in modelling the Earth–Moon–Sun system, triple star systems, the dynamics of globular clusters (generalised as the n-body problem), and gravitational-wave astronomy, where chaotic three-body interactions can form tight black-hole binaries. It has also entered popular culture as the title of Liu Cixin's science fiction novel The Three-Body Problem (2008).
