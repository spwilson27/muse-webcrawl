Moodle

Moodle is a free and open-source learning management system (LMS) written in PHP and distributed under the GNU General Public License. It is used for blended learning, distance education, flipped classrooms, and other online learning projects in schools, universities, workplaces, and other sectors, with hundreds of millions of users worldwide.

History

Moodle was created by Australian computer scientist Martin Dougiamas, who released version 1.0 in 2002, based on his research into social constructionist pedagogy. The name is an acronym for Modular Object-Oriented Dynamic Learning Environment (and "moodle" is also a verb meaning to tinker or daydream productively). Development is coordinated by Moodle HQ in Perth, Australia, supported by a network of commercial Moodle Partners.

Features

Courses combine resources (files, pages, books, SCORM packages, external tools via LTI) with activities such as assignments, quizzes with large question banks, forums, wikis, glossaries, databases, workshops for peer assessment, and H5P interactive content. Moodle offers role-based permissions, competency frameworks, learning analytics, a mobile app, and more than a thousand community plugins. Its constructivist design emphasises collaborative activities and discussion.

Adoption

Moodle is the most widely used LMS in the world by number of installations, common in higher education and in countries where institutions host their own servers rather than buying proprietary cloud services.
