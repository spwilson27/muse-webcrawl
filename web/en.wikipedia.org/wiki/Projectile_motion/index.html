Projectile motion

Projectile motion is a form of motion experienced by an object or particle (a projectile) that is projected in a gravitational field, such as from the surface of the Earth, and moves along a curved path under the action of gravity only. In the idealized treatment, air resistance is neglected, and the trajectory is a parabola.

Kinematics

The motion can be analyzed by decomposing it into independent horizontal and vertical components. Horizontally, with no forces acting, the velocity is constant: x = v0 cos θ · t. Vertically, the projectile undergoes constant acceleration −g: y = v0 sin θ · t − ½gt². Eliminating t shows the path is parabolic.

Range, height, and time of flight

For a projectile launched and landing at the same height, the time of flight is T = 2v0 sin θ / g; the maximum height is H = (v0 sin θ)² / 2g; and the horizontal range is R = v0² sin 2θ / g, maximized at a launch angle of 45°. Complementary launch angles give the same range.

Real-world effects

In reality, aerodynamic drag, wind, the variation of gravity with altitude, and the Coriolis effect modify trajectories, matters of central importance in ballistics. Galileo first gave the correct parabolic analysis of projectile motion in the early seventeenth century, superseding the medieval impetus theory.
