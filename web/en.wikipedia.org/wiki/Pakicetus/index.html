Pakicetus is an extinct genus of amphibious cetacean of the family Pakicetidae, endemic to Pakistan during the Ypresian (early Eocene) period, about 50 million years ago. It was a wolf-sized animal and one of the earliest known members of the whale lineage, occupying the transition between land mammals and fully aquatic cetaceans.

Discovery

The first fossils were found in 1979 in the Kuldana Formation of northern Pakistan — hence the name, "whale of Pakistan". The initial material, described by Philip Gingerich and Donald Russell in 1981, consisted mainly of skull fragments, including a distinctive thickened ear bone (the involucrum) characteristic of cetaceans. More complete skeletons reported in 2001 showed that Pakicetus was primarily a land animal.

Description

Pakicetus had a long skull, sharp carnivorous teeth, hooved toes, and a long tail. Its ankle bones show the double-pulley astragalus that links whales to even-toed ungulates (artiodactyls), supporting the now well-established view that whales evolved from artiodactyl ancestors. Its dense bones and eye placement suggest it waded in shallow fresh water, perhaps ambushing prey at the water's edge, but it was not a deep swimmer.

Significance

Together with later forms such as Ambulocetus, Pakicetus documents the early stages of the land-to-sea transition in whale evolution along the margins of the ancient Tethys Sea.
