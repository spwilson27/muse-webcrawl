Draco is a constellation in the far northern sky. Its name is Latin for dragon, and it was one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy; it remains one of the 88 modern constellations. The long, winding figure of the dragon circles the north celestial pole, lying between Ursa Major and Ursa Minor, and is circumpolar — visible all year — for observers in northern latitudes.

Mythology

Draco is commonly identified with Ladon, the dragon that guarded the golden apples of the Hesperides and was slain by Heracles as one of his twelve labours. Other traditions associate it with the dragon thrown into the sky during the Gigantomachy, or with the serpent Python.

Notable features

The brightest star is Eltanin (Gamma Draconis). Thuban (Alpha Draconis) was the pole star around 3000–1900 BC, at the time the Egyptian pyramids were built; owing to precession it will again be the pole star around AD 21000. Draco contains the Cat's Eye Nebula (NGC 6543), a well-known planetary nebula, and the Tadpole Galaxy (UGC 10214), whose long tidal tail results from an interaction with another galaxy. The north ecliptic pole lies within Draco, as does the deep-field region of the Draco Dwarf Spheroidal galaxy, a satellite of the Milky Way.
