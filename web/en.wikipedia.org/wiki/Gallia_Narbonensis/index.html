Gallia Narbonensis (Latin for "Gaul of Narbonne") was a Roman province located in what is now Occitania and Provence, in Southern France. It was also known as Provincia Nostra ("Our Province"), because it was the first Roman province north of the Alps, and as Gallia Transalpina ("Transalpine Gaul"). It became a province in the late Roman Republic, following campaigns in 125–121 BC and the foundation of the colony Narbo Martius (Narbonne) in 118 BC. Its name survives, transformed, in the modern region of Provence.

History

Rome intervened in southern Gaul to protect its ally Massalia (Marseille) against Ligurian and Gaulish tribes. Victory over the Salluvii, Allobroges and Arverni opened the land route from Italy to Spain along what became the Via Domitia. Because Narbonensis was Rome's earliest transalpine province and the gateway to Hispania, it was heavily colonised by veterans and thoroughly Romanised; cities like Arelate (Arles), Nemausus (Nîmes) and Arausio (Orange) acquired amphitheatres, aqueducts (including the Pont du Gard) and temples such as the Maison Carrée. Pliny the Elder remarked that it was "more Italy than a province".

Administration

Unusually for the late Republic, Narbonensis was governed by a proconsul of praetorian rank; under Augustus it became a senatorial province in 22 BC. Under Diocletian's reforms it was divided into Narbonensis Prima and Narbonensis Secunda. After the fall of the Western Empire, the region passed to the Visigoths and later the Franks, becoming the march of Septimania and the county of Provence.
