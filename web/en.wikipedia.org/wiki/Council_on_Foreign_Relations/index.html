Council on Foreign Relations

The Council on Foreign Relations (CFR) is an American think tank specialising in U.S. foreign policy and international affairs, founded in 1921. Headquartered in New York City, with an office in Washington, D.C., it publishes the journal Foreign Affairs and is widely considered one of the most influential foreign-policy organisations in the United States.

History

The CFR grew out of discussions among American and British delegates at the 1919 Paris Peace Conference. Its War and Peace Studies project during the Second World War advised the State Department and helped shape postwar planning, including ideas behind the United Nations. Membership is by invitation and includes former officials, academics, journalists and business leaders; its meetings are known for the "Chatham House rule"-style nonattribution convention.

Influence and criticism

Many U.S. secretaries of state, national security advisers and diplomats have been CFR members. Critics, across the political spectrum, argue the council embodies an insular foreign-policy establishment and promotes interventionist or globalist policies; it is also a frequent subject of conspiracy theories, which historians generally dismiss while acknowledging its real elite influence.
