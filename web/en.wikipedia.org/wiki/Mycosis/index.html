Mycosis

Specialty: Infectious disease, dermatology
Causes: Pathogenic fungi
Treatment: Antifungal medication

Mycosis, also known as a fungal infection, is an infectious disease caused by pathogenic fungi. Mycoses are common and a variety of environmental and physiological conditions can contribute to their development. Inhalation of fungal spores or localised colonisation of the skin may initiate persistent infections; therefore, mycoses often start in the lungs or on the skin.

Classification

Mycoses are classified according to the tissue levels initially colonised. Superficial mycoses affect the outer layers of skin and hair and include conditions such as athlete's foot, ringworm and pityriasis versicolor. Cutaneous mycoses extend deeper into the epidermis and affect hair and nails. Subcutaneous mycoses involve the dermis and underlying tissues. Systemic mycoses spread to internal organs and are most serious in immunocompromised patients.

Causes and risk factors

Many fungi are opportunistic, causing disease only when the immune system is weakened by conditions such as HIV/AIDS, cancer treatment, or immunosuppressive drugs. Others, including Histoplasma, Coccidioides and Blastomyces, can infect healthy hosts.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the nature of the infection and may include topical or systemic antifungal drugs such as azoles, polyenes (including amphotericin B) and echinocandins. Drug-resistant fungal infections are an increasing clinical concern.
