Meade Instruments

Meade Instruments Corporation was an American multinational company that was founded in 1972 by John Diebel. Headquartered in Irvine, California, Meade was one of the largest manufacturers of telescopes in the world, serving principally the amateur astronomy market. The company's products included Schmidt-Cassegrain telescopes (SCTs), refractors, reflectors, and computerized "GoTo" telescope mounts.

History

John Diebel founded Meade in 1972 after working for the aerospace industry. The company began as a mail-order retailer of small refracting telescopes imported from Japan. Through the 1970s and 1980s Meade grew into a major manufacturer, introducing popular Schmidt-Cassegrain models that competed directly with Celestron.

In 1999 Meade acquired Coronado Instruments, a maker of solar telescopes. In the 2000s the company faced financial difficulties, and in 2013 it merged with Ningbo Sunny Electronic Co., a Chinese manufacturer. In November 2019 Meade filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection following an antitrust judgment involving its competitor Orion Telescopes & Binoculars. In 2021 the company's assets were acquired by Orion, and the Meade brand continued under new ownership.

Products

Meade's best-known product lines included:

  • LX series — Schmidt-Cassegrain telescopes, including the LX90 and the advanced LX200 with computerized GoTo pointing.
  • ETX series — compact portable Maksutov-Cassegrain telescopes aimed at beginners.
  • LightBridge — truss-tube Dobsonian reflector telescopes.
  • Coronado PST — personal solar telescopes for hydrogen-alpha viewing of the Sun.
