Britpop was a British music and cultural movement of the mid-1990s that emphasised British identity and guitar-based pop rock, in reaction against American grunge and domestic shoegazing. Its leading bands — Blur, Oasis, Pulp and Suede — drew on the British Invasion, glam rock and punk, and became the musical wing of the wider "Cool Britannia" phenomenon.

Rise

Suede's debut (1993) and Blur's Parklife (1994) set the template: sharply observed songs about British life sung in regional accents. Oasis's Definitely Maybe (1994) and (What's the Story) Morning Glory? (1995) brought the movement mass popularity. In August 1995 the tabloid-fuelled "Battle of Britpop" pitted Blur's "Country House" against Oasis's "Roll with It" in a chart race won by Blur, while Oasis's two nights at Knebworth in 1996 drew 250,000 fans.

Decline and legacy

By 1997 overexposure, bloated third albums (Oasis's Be Here Now) and a shift towards post-Britpop acts such as Radiohead, The Verve and Travis ended the movement's dominance. Britpop is credited with reviving British guitar music and the UK charts' commercial fortunes, though critics debate its musical conservatism and the narrowness of its "lad culture" image.
