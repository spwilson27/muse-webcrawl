August Ferdinand Möbius (17 November 1790 – 26 September 1868) was a German mathematician and theoretical astronomer. He is best known for his discovery of the Möbius strip, a non-orientable two-dimensional surface with only one side, and for foundational work in projective geometry and number theory.

Möbius, and the one-sided strip that bears his name.

Life

Möbius was born at Schulpforta, Saxony, the only child of a dancing teacher who died when he was three. He studied law briefly at Leipzig before switching to mathematics, astronomy and physics, taking his doctorate under Johann Friedrich Pfaff — himself a student of Gauss, with whom Möbius also studied at Göttingen. In 1816 he became professor of astronomy at Leipzig and director of its observatory, a post he held for the rest of his life. Quiet and unassuming, he published much of his best work late and was little recognised outside Germany during his lifetime.

Work

His Der barycentrische Calcul (1827) introduced homogeneous (barycentric) coordinates, a cornerstone of projective geometry. In number theory he introduced the Möbius function μ(n) and the Möbius inversion formula relating arithmetic functions. In 1858, at age 68, he described the Möbius strip and the concept of orientability (independently, Johann Benedict Listing published the strip a few months earlier). The Möbius transformation of complex analysis and the Möbius plane of incidence geometry also bear his name. As an astronomer he wrote on celestial mechanics and supervised the rebuilding of the Leipzig observatory.

Legacy

The crater Möbius on the Moon and the asteroid 28516 Möbius are named for him. The Möbius strip has become a universal symbol of infinity and recycling and a staple of popular mathematics.
