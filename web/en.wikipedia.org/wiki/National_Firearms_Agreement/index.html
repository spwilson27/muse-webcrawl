National Firearms Agreement

The National Firearms Agreement (NFA) is a 1996 agreement concerning gun control in Australia, adopted by the Commonwealth, state, and territory governments in response to the Port Arthur massacre, in which 35 people were killed in Tasmania in April 1996.

Provisions

The agreement banned certain semi-automatic rifles and shotguns, imposed a 28-day waiting period for purchases, required firearms registration, and set uniform licensing and safe-storage requirements across all states and territories. It was accompanied by a federally funded buyback scheme that purchased and destroyed more than 650,000 firearms, financed by a one-off increase in the Medicare levy.

Background and legacy

The reforms were introduced by the government of Prime Minister John Howard, who had taken office only weeks before the massacre. The agreement was reaffirmed in 2017. Australia has had no mass shootings on the scale of Port Arthur in the decades since, and the agreement is frequently cited internationally in debates over gun control.
