New York City Ballet

New York City Ballet (NYCB) is a ballet company founded in 1948 by choreographer George Balanchine and impresario Lincoln Kirstein. Based at the David H. Koch Theater at Lincoln Center in New York City, it is one of the foremost dance companies in the world, with a repertory of more than 400 works.

History

Balanchine and Kirstein had earlier founded the School of American Ballet (1934) and several short-lived companies. In 1946 they established Ballet Society, which in 1948 became the resident company of New York City Center and was renamed New York City Ballet. The company moved to Lincoln Center in 1964. Balanchine created a distinctly American neoclassical style — plotless, musical, and virtuosic — in works such as Serenade, Concerto Barocco, Agon and Jewels. Jerome Robbins, the company's other founding choreographer, contributed works including Fancy Free and Dances at a Gathering.

Repertory and company

The company's annual production of Balanchine's The Nutcracker has been a holiday fixture since 1954. After Balanchine's death in 1983, Peter Martins directed the company until 2019, when Jonathan Stafford became artistic director, with Wendy Whelan as associate artistic director. The company employs roughly 90 dancers and maintains close ties to the School of American Ballet.
