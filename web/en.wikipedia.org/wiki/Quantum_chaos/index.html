Quantum chaos

Quantum chaos is a branch of physics that studies how chaotic classical dynamical systems can be described in terms of quantum theory. Its central question is how the fingerprints of classical chaos — sensitive dependence on initial conditions, ergodic trajectories — appear in quantum systems, whose unitary evolution is linear and quasi-periodic.

Spectral statistics

The field's main diagnostic is energy-level statistics. For systems whose classical counterparts are integrable, levels follow Poisson statistics (Berry–Tabor conjecture); for classically chaotic systems, level spacings follow the Wigner–Dyson distributions of random matrix theory (Bohigas–Giannoni–Schmit conjecture). Gutzwiller's trace formula connects quantum spectra to classical periodic orbits.

Other topics

Quantum chaos also covers dynamical localization (as in the kicked rotor), scars of periodic orbits in eigenfunctions, and quantum ergodicity, formalized in the Shnirelman theorem. Applications range from atomic and nuclear physics to mesoscopic electronics and quantum computing.
