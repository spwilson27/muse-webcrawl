Brushback pitch

SportBaseball
TypePitching tactic
PurposeMove the batter away from home plate
RelatedBeanball; chin music

In baseball, a brushback pitch is a pitch thrown deliberately high and inside, close to the batter, in order to force the batter to move back from the plate and concede the inside part of the strike zone to the pitcher. It is a long-standing, if controversial, part of pitching strategy.

Purpose and practice

Batters who "crowd the plate" gain an advantage in covering outside pitches. A brushback re-establishes the pitcher's claim to the inside corner. Skilled pitchers aim near the batter's upper body without intending to hit them; a pitch that actually strikes the batter is a hit by pitch, and one thrown at the head is a "beanball," which can result in warnings, ejections and retaliation.

Rules and controversy

Umpires may warn both benches or eject a pitcher judged to be throwing at a batter intentionally. The tactic has been criticised as dangerous, and its use has declined in modern baseball as leagues have tightened enforcement and players have adopted protective equipment such as helmet extensions.
