Coffin Texts

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Coffin Texts are a collection of ancient Egyptian funerary spells inscribed on coffins and other burial equipment, dating principally to the Middle Kingdom (c. 2100–1650 BC). They are a key source for Egyptian beliefs about the afterlife and a bridge between the earlier Pyramid Texts and the later Book of the Dead.

Content

More than a thousand spells are known. Written mainly in cursive hieratic, they were intended to protect the deceased, provide for them in the next world, and guide them through the dangers of the Duat (underworld). A famous component is the Book of Two Ways, a map-like guide to the afterlife routes by land and water, painted on coffin floors from el-Bersha — arguably the earliest illustrated "map" of the otherworld.

Significance

Whereas the Pyramid Texts had been reserved for royalty, the Coffin Texts appear on the coffins of officials and wealthy commoners, reflecting the so-called "democratisation of the afterlife" in the Middle Kingdom. Many spells were later adapted into the New Kingdom Book of the Dead. The standard modern edition is Adriaan de Buck's seven-volume The Egyptian Coffin Texts (1935–1961).
