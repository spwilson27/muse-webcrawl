Donna Tartt

Donna Louise Tartt (born December 23, 1963) is an American novelist. She is the author of three novels: The Secret History (1992), The Little Friend (2002) and The Goldfinch (2013), the last of which won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction in 2014. She is known for her classical, densely allusive prose style, and for the long intervals — about a decade — between her books. Time magazine included her in its 2014 list of the 100 most influential people.

Life

Tartt was born in Greenwood, Mississippi, and raised in nearby Grenada. She enrolled at the University of Mississippi in 1981, where her writing caught the attention of the writer-in-residence Willie Morris, who helped arrange her transfer to Bennington College in Vermont. At Bennington she studied classics and was part of a prominent literary circle including Bret Easton Ellis and Jonathan Lethem. She began the novel that became The Secret History while still a student.

Works

The Secret History (1992), narrated by a member of an elite group of classics students at a Vermont college who murder one of their own, became a bestseller and is credited with launching the "dark academia" aesthetic in later popular culture. The Little Friend (2002), set in Mississippi, follows a young girl investigating her brother's unsolved childhood murder. The Goldfinch (2013), a Dickensian novel about a boy who survives a terrorist bombing at the Metropolitan Museum of Art and steals Carel Fabritius's painting The Goldfinch, won the Pulitzer Prize and was adapted into a 2019 film. Tartt is notably private, rarely giving interviews about work in progress.
