Walking Liberty

Walking Liberty most commonly refers to the Walking Liberty half dollar, a silver 50-cent piece issued by the United States Mint from 1916 to 1947. The coin was designed by Adolph A. Weinman, whose obverse design shows Liberty striding toward the sun, draped in the Stars and Stripes and carrying branches of laurel and oak. The reverse depicts a bald eagle rising from a mountaintop perch.

The Walking Liberty design is widely regarded as one of the most beautiful in American coinage. When the United States Mint began issuing the American Silver Eagle bullion coins in 1986, it adopted Weinman's Walking Liberty obverse for them.


Weinman's Walking Liberty design (1916)

Design and production

Weinman, a sculptor who had studied under Augustus Saint-Gaudens, won the 1916 competition to redesign the dime, quarter and half dollar; his Mercury dime and Walking Liberty half dollar resulted. The half dollar was struck at the Philadelphia, Denver and San Francisco mints. The design proved difficult to strike up fully, and weakly struck pieces are common. Production ended in 1947, when the coin was replaced by the Franklin half dollar.

Collecting

Key dates in the series include the 1916-S, the 1921 issues from all three mints, and the 1938-D. The design's enduring popularity led the Mint to revive it for the American Eagle programme and again for a centennial gold issue in 2016.
