DynamIQ is a CPU cluster technology developed by Arm Holdings and announced in 2017 as the successor to big.LITTLE. It allows up to eight CPU cores of differing designs to be combined in a single cluster — a "DynamIQ Shared Unit" (DSU) — sharing an L3 cache and power domain, with much finer-grained flexibility than big.LITTLE's pairs of clusters.

Features

In big.LITTLE, high-performance and high-efficiency cores lived in separate clusters, limiting combinations and incurring migration costs. DynamIQ places cores in one cluster where each core can have its own voltage and frequency, be powered down independently, and share the cluster's L3 cache with low latency. Designers may configure, for example, a single large "prime" core alongside medium and small cores, a pattern widely adopted in smartphone system-on-chips.

Cores and adoption

The first DynamIQ-capable cores were the Cortex-A75 and Cortex-A55, and subsequent Arm performance and efficiency cores — including the Cortex-X series — are built around the concept. DynamIQ clusters underpin most modern Android flagship processors, including Qualcomm Snapdragon, Samsung Exynos and MediaTek Dimensity lines.
