Lanzhou beef noodles

Lanzhou beef noodles (Chinese: 兰州牛肉面; pinyin: Lánzhōu niúròumiàn), also called Lanzhou lamian, are a Chinese noodle soup originating in Lanzhou, the capital of Gansu province. The dish consists of hand-pulled wheat noodles in a clear, seasoned beef broth, topped with sliced beef, white radish, red chilli oil, coriander and garlic sprouts (scallions).

Characteristics

The classic presentation is summarised as "one clear, two white, three red, four green, five yellow" (一清、二白、三红、四绿、五黄): clear broth, white radish, red chilli oil, green herbs and yellow noodles. The broth is simmered from beef and beef bones with spices; the noodles are made from high-gluten wheat dough, traditionally softened with penghui, an alkaline water derived from ashes, and pulled by hand to order in thicknesses ranging from hair-thin to wide flat ribbons.

History and spread

The dish is generally traced to the Hui Muslim community of Lanzhou in the late Qing dynasty; a frequently credited figure is Ma Baozi, who in the early 20th century popularised hot beef-noodle soup in the city. Lanzhou-style noodle shops — most of them run by Hui and Salar families from Qinghai — are now found in virtually every Chinese city and increasingly abroad, though a long-running naming dispute over whether shops outside Gansu may call themselves "Lanzhou" has led many to trade as "Qinghai-style" or simply "hand-pulled noodles". The dish is eaten at any hour but is especially a breakfast staple.
