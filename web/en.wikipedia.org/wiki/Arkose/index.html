Arkose is typically pinkish or reddish owing to its abundant feldspar.

Arkose (also called arkosic sandstone) is a detrital sedimentary rock, specifically a type of sandstone containing at least 25% feldspar. It is usually poorly sorted, coarse-grained, and pink, red, or grey in colour. Arkosic sand accumulates where feldspar-rich source rocks, such as granite, are eroded and the sediment is deposited rapidly enough that the feldspar does not fully decompose by chemical weathering.

Composition and formation

Besides feldspar, arkose contains quartz and lithic fragments, often with a matrix of clay or iron oxide that imparts reddish colours. Because feldspar is chemically less stable than quartz, abundant feldspar in a sandstone implies either an arid or cold climate at the source, very rapid erosion and burial, or both. Arkoses are therefore common in fault-bounded (rift) basins, alluvial fans, and braided-stream deposits near uplifted granitic terrains.

Occurrences

Well-known arkosic formations include the Fountain Formation of Colorado (which forms the Flatirons and Red Rocks), the Permian and Triassic sandstones of several European basins, and many Precambrian and Phanerozoic rift fills worldwide. The term itself is thought to derive from an old name for feldspar-rich rocks in France's Auvergne region.
