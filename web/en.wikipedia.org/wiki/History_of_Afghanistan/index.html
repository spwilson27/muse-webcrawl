History of Afghanistan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Afghanistan sits at the crossroads of Central, South and West Asia.

The history of Afghanistan spans from prehistoric settlement along the Oxus and Helmand valleys through successive empires — Achaemenid, Hellenistic, Kushan, Islamic and Mongol — to the emergence of an Afghan state in the 18th century and the conflicts of the 19th, 20th and 21st centuries. Landlocked and mountainous, the region was a corridor for trade and invasion between Persia, Central Asia and India.

Ancient and medieval periods

Archaeological sites such as Mundigak show urban settlement by the third millennium BCE. The region formed satrapies of the Achaemenid Empire before Alexander the Great's conquest in 330 BCE; the Greco-Bactrian kingdom and then the Kushan Empire made it a centre of Silk Road commerce and Buddhist art, exemplified by the Buddhas of Bamiyan. Arab armies brought Islam from the 7th century; the Ghaznavids and Ghurids later ruled empires from Afghan bases. Genghis Khan's invasion in 1221 devastated irrigation and cities, and the region was later contested by Timurids, Mughals and Safavids.

The Afghan empires and the Great Game

In 1747 Ahmad Shah Durrani founded the Durrani Empire, conventionally the origin of the modern Afghan state. The 19th century saw the country become a buffer in the "Great Game" between the British and Russian empires; three Anglo-Afghan wars (1839–42, 1878–80, 1919) ended with Afghanistan controlling its own foreign policy after 1919 under Amanullah Khan, whose reform programme provoked civil war.

Kingdom, republic and communist period

The kingdom of Zahir Shah (1933–1973) gave way to Daoud Khan's republic, then to the Saur Revolution of 1978 and a communist government whose reforms triggered revolt. The Soviet invasion of December 1979 began a nine-year war against mujahideen factions backed by the United States, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia.

Civil war, Taliban, and after 2001

After the Soviet withdrawal in 1989 and the fall of Najibullah in 1992, factional civil war destroyed much of Kabul. The Taliban, emerging in 1994, controlled most of the country by 1998 and imposed a strict interpretation of Islamic law. Following the September 11 attacks, a US-led invasion in late 2001 toppled the Taliban; the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan, backed by international forces, lasted until the Taliban retook Kabul in August 2021 as US forces withdrew, restoring the Islamic Emirate.
