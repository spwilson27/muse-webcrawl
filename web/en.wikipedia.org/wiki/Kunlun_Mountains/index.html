Kunlun Mountains

The Kunlun Mountains form one of the longest mountain chains in Asia, extending about 3,000 kilometres across western China from the Pamirs eastward along the northern edge of the Tibetan Plateau, separating it from the Tarim Basin and Taklamakan Desert in Xinjiang. Their highest peak is Kongur Tagh at 7,649 metres (in the eastern Pamirs, sometimes counted separately).

In Chinese mythology the Kunlun were the dwelling of the Queen Mother of the West and a pillar of heaven; the range is associated with the mythical Kunlun paradise and with jade from Khotan. Geologically the Kunlun were uplifted by India's collision with Asia. The Qinghai-Tibet railway crosses the range at the Kunlun Pass, and the 2001 Kunlun earthquake left a 400-kilometre surface rupture.
