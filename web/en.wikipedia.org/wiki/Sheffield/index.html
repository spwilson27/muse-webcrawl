Sheffield is a city in South Yorkshire, England, lying in the foothills of the Pennines at the confluence of the River Don and its tributaries. Known worldwide as the "Steel City", Sheffield grew from a market town famous for cutlery into a major centre of steelmaking during the Industrial Revolution, aided by local innovations such as crucible steel and stainless steel. With a population of over half a million it is one of England's largest cities.

History

A settlement existed at the crossing of the Don by the time of the Norman Conquest, and a castle was built in the 12th century. Sheffield's cutlery trade, regulated by the Company of Cutlers from 1624, exploited fast-flowing streams for water-powered grinding wheels, along with local coal, ironstone and millstone grit. In the 18th century Benjamin Huntsman's crucible steel process transformed quality, and in 1913 Harry Brearley developed stainless steel in the city. The steel industry contracted sharply in the 1980s, and Sheffield has since redeveloped around education, services, culture and advanced manufacturing.

Geography

Sheffield is famously built on hills — local tradition says seven — and about a third of the city lies within the Peak District National Park, making it one of England's greenest cities, with more trees per person than any other European city by some estimates. Its rivers — the Don, Sheaf (from which the city takes its name), Rivelin, Loxley and Porter — run through steep valleys lined with former mill sites.

Culture and sport

The city is home to the Crucible Theatre (host of the World Snooker Championship), the Millennium Gallery and a celebrated music scene that produced acts such as Arctic Monkeys, Pulp and Def Leppard. In football, Sheffield FC, founded in 1857, is recognised as the world's oldest football club; Sheffield United and Sheffield Wednesday are the city's professional clubs, and Bramall Lane and Hillsborough are historic grounds. The city has two universities: the University of Sheffield and Sheffield Hallam University.
