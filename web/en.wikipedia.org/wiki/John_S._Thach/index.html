John S. Thach

John Smith Thach (April 19, 1905 – April 15, 1981) was a United States Navy naval aviator and admiral during World War II. He invented the Thach Weave, a combat flight formation that allowed American fighters to counter the superior manoeuvrability of the Japanese Mitsubishi Zero.

Early career

Thach was born in Pine Bluff, Arkansas, and graduated from the United States Naval Academy in 1927. He became a naval aviator, test pilot and aerial gunnery expert, and was a member of the Navy's aerobatic team.

World War II

At the outbreak of war Thach commanded Fighting Squadron Three (VF-3), flying the Grumman F4F Wildcat from the carrier Lexington and later Yorktown. Working out the "beam defence position" — later named the Thach Weave — in his spare time using matchsticks on a table, he first used it in combat at the Battle of Midway in June 1942, where it proved highly effective. Later he served as operations officer for the Fast Carrier Task Force and planned strikes against Japan, and he was present at the Japanese surrender aboard USS Missouri.

Later career

Thach commanded the carrier USS Sicily during the Korean War and later the supercarrier Forrestal. He rose to the rank of admiral and served as Deputy Chief of Naval Operations for Air and as Commander in Chief of U.S. Naval Forces Europe before retiring in 1967. The frigate USS Thach was named in his honour.
