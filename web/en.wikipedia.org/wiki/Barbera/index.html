Barbera

Barbera is a red Italian wine grape variety that, as of 2000, was the third most-planted red grape variety in Italy, after Sangiovese and Montepulciano. It is the signature grape of Piedmont in the northwest, especially around Asti, Alba and Alessandria, and produces wines with deep ruby colour, low tannin and characteristically high natural acidity, with flavours of red cherry, blackberry and spice.

History and regions

Barbera is documented in the Monferrato hills of Piedmont from the thirteenth century and is thought to be much older. Long regarded as an everyday drinking wine, its quality rose markedly from the 1980s as producers reduced yields and aged wines in small French oak barriques. The principal appellations are Barbera d'Asti (promoted to DOCG in 2008), Barbera del Monferrato and Barbera d'Alba. The grape is also planted in Emilia-Romagna and Lombardy, and outside Italy in Argentina, Australia and California, where Italian immigrants introduced it and it is grown notably in the Sierra Foothills and Central Valley.

Viticulture

Barbera is vigorous and adaptable, ripening late and retaining acidity even in warm climates, which makes it valued for blending as well as varietal wines. Its thin skins and soft tannins produce food-friendly wines that are often drunk young, though the best examples age well.
