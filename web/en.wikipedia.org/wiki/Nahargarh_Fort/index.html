Nahargarh Fort

Nahargarh Fort stands on the edge of the Aravalli Hills, overlooking the city of Jaipur in the Indian state of Rajasthan. Along with Amer Fort and Jaigarh Fort, Nahargarh once formed a strong defence ring for the city. Originally named Sudarshangarh, it became known as Nahargarh, meaning 'abode of tigers'.

LocationJaipur, Rajasthan, India
Built1734
Built byMaharaja Sawai Jai Singh II
TypeHill fort and palace

History

The fort was built mainly in 1734 by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II, founder of Jaipur, as a retreat and defensive stronghold. Legend holds that construction was disturbed by the spirit of a Rathore prince, Nahar Singh Bhomia, placated by dedicating the fort to him. The fort was extended in 1868 under Sawai Ram Singh and in the 1880s–90s under Sawai Madho Singh, who built the Madhavendra Bhawan — a suite of identical apartments for the maharaja and his queens, connected by corridors with fine frescoes. The fort figured in treaties with Maratha forces in the 18th century and sheltered Europeans during the 1857 revolt.

Today

Popular for panoramic views of Jaipur, the fort houses a wax museum, a sculpture park (installed from 2017), restaurants and the Padao viewpoint. It has featured in Bollywood films including Rang De Basanti and Shuddh Desi Romance.
