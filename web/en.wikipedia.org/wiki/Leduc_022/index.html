Leduc 022

The Leduc 022 was a French experimental interceptor aircraft designed by René Leduc in the 1950s, powered by a combination of a ramjet and a turbojet. It was the final and most advanced of Leduc's series of ramjet-propelled aircraft and was intended as a supersonic interceptor for the French Air Force.

Design and development

René Leduc had pioneered ramjet aircraft with the Leduc 010, the first aircraft to fly powered solely by a ramjet (in 1949, after being launched from a carrier aircraft), followed by the 016 and 021 research aircraft. The 022 was designed as a practical military interceptor. Because a ramjet cannot produce thrust at standstill, the 022 combined the ramjet duct — which formed the aircraft's fuselage — with a SNECMA Atar turbojet mounted inside the duct to provide power for takeoff, climb, and landing. The pilot sat in a glazed nose capsule at the front of the large circular intake.

The aircraft was a mid-wing design with swept wings and a cruciform tail. Intended armament and equipment were to include air-to-air missiles and radar, reflecting its planned role as a point-defence interceptor capable of speeds approaching Mach 2.

Testing and cancellation

The 022 made its first flight under turbojet power in late December 1956. During 1957 it accumulated a number of test flights and lit its ramjet in flight, reaching transonic speeds. However, the programme was cancelled at the end of 1957 amid French defence budget cuts and official preference for conventional designs such as the Dassault Mirage III. Two prototypes had been built; the surviving example is preserved at the Musée de l'Air et de l'Espace at Le Bourget near Paris.

Specifications (approximate)

  • Crew: 1
  • Powerplant: 1 × SNECMA Atar 101 turbojet plus 1 × Leduc ramjet
  • Maximum speed: designed for about Mach 2 (ramjet power); demonstrated speeds lower in testing
  • Configuration: single-seat experimental interceptor
