Patreon

Patreon (pronounced /ˈpeɪtriɒn/) is an American membership platform that provides business tools for content creators to run a subscription service and sell digital products. It helps creators and artists earn a monthly income by receiving payments directly from their fans, or "patrons", in exchange for exclusive content and benefits. The company is headquartered in San Francisco, California.

History

Patreon was founded in 2013 by musician Jack Conte, who was frustrated with the low revenue he earned from YouTube advertising despite having millions of views, together with developer Sam Yam. The platform grew rapidly among podcasters, video creators, musicians, illustrators, and game developers. By the late 2010s it was paying out hundreds of millions of dollars annually to creators, and in 2020–2021 it raised large venture funding rounds that valued it at over $4 billion.

Business model

Creators set up tiered membership levels with different rewards. Patreon takes a percentage of creators' earnings — under its standard plans, 8 or 12 percent, plus payment processing fees. In 2023 the company consolidated its offering around a standard 10 percent fee for new creators and expanded into community features and digital product sales.

Controversies

Patreon has periodically faced criticism over content moderation, including the removal of controversial creators, which prompted some users to migrate to rival platforms, and over changes to its fee structure — a planned 2017 fee change was reversed after a backlash from small creators.
