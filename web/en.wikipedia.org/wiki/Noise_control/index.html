Noise control

Noise control or noise mitigation is a set of strategies to reduce noise pollution or to reduce the impact of that noise, whether outdoors or indoors. The main areas are transportation noise control, architectural acoustics, workplace noise reduction and vibration control.

Source, path and receiver

Noise control measures are conventionally grouped by where they act. Controlling the source — quieter machinery, low-noise road surfaces, mufflers — is usually most effective. Controlling the path includes distance, enclosures, noise barriers and sound-absorbing treatments. Protecting the receiver includes building insulation and personal hearing protection such as earplugs and earmuffs.

Occupational noise

Regulations in many countries require employers to limit workers' exposure, commonly to an average of 85 dB(A) over eight hours, with a hierarchy that prefers engineering controls over administrative controls and hearing protection. Prolonged excessive exposure causes noise-induced hearing loss.

Transportation

Road traffic is the most widespread source of environmental noise. Mitigation includes traffic management, quieter tyres and road surfaces, barriers, and land-use planning that separates housing from major routes. Aircraft noise is addressed through quieter engines, operational procedures and airport planning rules.
