The Septinsular Republic (Greek: Ἑπτάνησος Πολιτεία, Heptanēsos Politeia; Italian: Repubblica Settinsulare) was an island republic that existed from 1800 to 1807 under nominal Ottoman suzerainty and Russian protection, encompassing the seven principal Ionian Islands off the western coast of Greece: Corfu, Paxoi, Lefkada, Cephalonia, Ithaca, Zakynthos (Zante), and Kythira (Cerigo).

History

After the fall of the Republic of Venice in 1797, the Ionian Islands passed to Revolutionary France. A joint Russo-Ottoman fleet under Admiral Fyodor Ushakov expelled the French in 1798–1799. By the Treaty of Constantinople (1800), the islands were constituted as a single federal republic — the first self-governing Greek state entity of modern times — paying tribute to the Ottoman Sultan while enjoying autonomy guaranteed by Russia.

The republic adopted constitutions in 1800 and 1803; the latter made Greek the official language and recognised Orthodoxy, though political power remained concentrated in the islands' aristocracy. The "Byzantine Constitution" of 1800 was notably conservative, restricting the franchise to the nobility.

Dissolution

By the Treaty of Tilsit (1807), Russia ceded the islands back to Napoleonic France. They subsequently came under British occupation, and in 1815 were reorganised as the United States of the Ionian Islands, a British protectorate, before their eventual union with the Kingdom of Greece in 1864.
