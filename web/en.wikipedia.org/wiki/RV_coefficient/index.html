RV coefficient

In statistics, the RV coefficient is a multivariate generalization of the squared Pearson correlation coefficient (because the RV coefficient takes values between 0 and 1). It measures the closeness of two sets of points that may each be represented in a matrix of data and can be thought of as a generalization of the Pearson correlation coefficient to multivariate data.

Definition

Given two matrices of observations, the RV coefficient is defined as the ratio of the scalar product between the two cross-covariance-like matrices to the geometric mean of the scalar products of each matrix with itself. Formally, for two centered data matrices, one constructs the matrices of scalar products between individuals and computes the trace of the products of these matrices. The RV coefficient is given by:

RV(X, Y) = tr(XX′YY′) / √[tr(XX′)² · tr(YY′)²]

where tr denotes the trace of a matrix. When each matrix consists of a single variable, the RV coefficient reduces exactly to the squared Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient.

Properties

The RV coefficient always lies between 0 and 1. It is invariant to rotation and reflection of the configurations of points, and to changes of scale of either configuration. A value of 1 indicates that the two configurations of points are similar up to rotation and dilation, while a value near 0 indicates a lack of relationship between them.

Applications

The RV coefficient is widely used in multivariate data analysis, including principal component analysis, sensory analysis, and genomics, where researchers need to compare configurations of observations arising from different sets of measurements on the same individuals. Permutation tests are commonly used to assess the statistical significance of an observed RV coefficient.
