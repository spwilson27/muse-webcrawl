Sun sensor

A fine sun sensor on a small satellite

A sun sensor (also known as a sun detector) is a navigational instrument used by spacecraft to detect the position of the Sun. Sun sensors are used for attitude control and solar array pointing, and as part of an attitude and orbit control system (AOCS) they provide a reference vector toward the Sun with accuracies ranging from several degrees down to arcseconds, depending on the design.

Types

Coarse sun sensors are simple photovoltaic cells or photodiodes mounted on different faces of a spacecraft. By comparing the currents produced by cells facing different directions, the control system obtains a rough estimate of the Sun direction; several cells glued on a sphere or cube can give full-sky coverage. They are cheap, robust and widely used for safe-mode sun acquisition.

Fine (digital) sun sensors image the Sun through a slit, pinhole or coded mask onto a CCD or CMOS detector. The position of the Sun's image on the focal plane yields two-axis angle measurements, typically accurate to arcminutes or better. Analog fine sensors use quadrant photodiodes, measuring the displacement of a light spot from the cell boundaries.

Applications

Sun sensors keep solar arrays aimed at the Sun for power, orient antennas and instruments, and provide the primary attitude reference during spacecraft safe modes, when the vehicle must hold a power-positive, thermally safe orientation. Because they are useless in eclipse, they are combined with magnetometers, Earth sensors and star trackers for complete attitude determination. Sun sensors are also used on sounding rockets, high-altitude balloons and some terrestrial solar-tracking installations.
