Ramstein air show disaster

The Ramstein air show disaster occurred on Sunday, 28 August 1988, during the Flugtag '88 airshow at Ramstein Air Base near Kaiserslautern, West Germany. Three aircraft of the Italian Air Force display team Frecce Tricolori collided in mid-air, and one of the crashing jets, along with debris and burning fuel, struck the crowd of spectators. Seventy people died, including the three pilots, and nearly 1,000 were injured, making it one of the deadliest air show accidents in history.

The disaster killed 70 people and injured almost 1,000.

Accident

The Frecce Tricolori were performing their "pierced heart" (cardioide) manoeuvre, in which two groups of jets cross in front of the crowd while a solo aircraft flies through the heart-shaped formation. The solo pilot, Lt. Col. Ivo Nutarelli, misjudged the timing and altitude and collided with the two lead aircraft of one group. His Aermacchi MB-339 fell onto the runway area near the crowd, skidded and disintegrated; its fuel ignited in a fireball that engulfed spectators. The other two jets crashed away from the main crowd, one striking near a Black Hawk helicopter and an ice-cream van. Nutarelli and the other two pilots, Lt. Col. Mario Naldini and Capt. Giorgio Alessio, were killed.

Aftermath

Emergency response was hampered by confusion between German and American rescue services, incompatible equipment and delayed access to the base; criticism of the response led to reforms in German disaster medicine, including improvements in triage, burn treatment organisation and psychological support for survivors and responders — the accident is regarded as a formative event in modern German emergency management. Many survivors suffered severe burns and post-traumatic stress.

Following the disaster, West Germany banned air shows for three years; when they resumed, strict new rules applied, including minimum distances between aircraft manoeuvres and spectators and bans on manoeuvres directed toward crowds. The accident is also remembered for the experiences of the survivors' association and is frequently referenced in discussions of air show safety.
