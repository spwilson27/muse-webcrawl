Richard Bachman

Richard Bachman is a pen name used by American author Stephen King for a series of novels published between 1977 and 1984, and revived once more in 1996. Writing as Bachman, King published darker, more pessimistic works including Rage, The Long Walk, Roadwork, The Running Man and Thinner. The pseudonym was exposed in 1985 by a bookstore clerk, Steve Brown, who noticed similarities between the two authors' styles and found King's name on a copyright form.

Origin

In the late 1970s, publishers generally believed an author should release only one book a year, and King also wished to test whether his success was due to talent or luck. He took the name from crime writer Richard Stark and the band Bachman–Turner Overdrive. The Bachman books were issued in paperback by Signet Books with a mock biography claiming Bachman was a New Hampshire chicken farmer.

Exposure and aftermath

When Thinner (1984) sold far better than the earlier Bachman titles, scrutiny increased; Brown's research confirmed the identity in 1985. King announced that Bachman had died of "cancer of the pseudonym." The four early novels were collected as The Bachman Books (1985). King later returned to the name for The Regulators (1996), published as a "lost" Bachman novel, and referenced the persona in The Dark Tower series and Blaze (2007).

Bibliography

Rage (1977), The Long Walk (1979), Roadwork (1981), The Running Man (1982), Thinner (1984), The Regulators (1996), Blaze (2007).
