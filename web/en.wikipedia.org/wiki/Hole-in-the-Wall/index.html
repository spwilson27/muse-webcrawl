Hole-in-the-Wall

Hole-in-the-Wall is a remote pass and outlaw hideout in the Big Horn Mountains of Johnson County, Wyoming, United States. The "hole" is a narrow gap in a red sandstone escarpment (the Red Wall) through which a trail passes; the surrounding valley, sheltered and well-watered with abundant game, was nearly inaccessible to pursuing lawmen.

Outlaw era

From the 1880s to the early 1900s, the Hole-in-the-Wall country sheltered a loose network of rustlers and robbers, including the Hole in the Wall Gang associated with Butch Cassidy, the Sundance Kid, and other members of the Wild Bunch. Cabins in the valley allowed outlaws to rest stolen livestock and evade posses, who had to approach through a few easily watched passes. Other notorious hideouts linked in the "Outlaw Trail" included Robbers Roost in Utah and Brown's Park in Colorado.

Today

The site lies on private ranch land and is accessible to organised tours and by arrangement; a preserved outlaw cabin was moved to the grounds of the Jim Gatchell Museum in Buffalo, Wyoming. The area is managed in part with public access via Bureau of Land Management land nearby, and the Hole-in-the-Wall remains a famous symbol of the American outlaw West, frequently referenced in film and literature.
