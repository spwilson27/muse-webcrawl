Alvin Ira Goldman (born 1938) is an American philosopher known for his work in epistemology and the philosophy of mind. He taught at the University of Michigan and the University of Arizona before moving to Rutgers University, where he became a Board of Governors Professor. Goldman is a central figure in the turn toward naturalised and externalist epistemology and is widely regarded as the founder of modern social epistemology.

Reliabilism and the theory of knowledge

In a famous 1967 paper, "A Causal Theory of Knowing", Goldman proposed that a belief counts as knowledge when it is caused in an appropriate way by the fact that makes it true — challenging purely internalist accounts in the wake of the Gettier problem. He went on to develop reliabilism: a belief is justified if it is produced by a reliable cognitive process. His books Epistemology and Cognition (1986) and Liaisons: Philosophy Meets the Cognitive and Social Sciences (1992) argue that epistemology should be informed by psychology and the empirical study of belief formation.

Social epistemology and other work

Goldman's Knowledge in a Social World (1999) founded "veritistic" social epistemology, evaluating social practices — science, law, education, the media, and later the internet — by how well they promote true belief. He has also contributed to philosophy of mind with a simulation-based account of mindreading, to metaphysics with A Theory of Human Action (1970), and to philosophical methodology through work on intuitions and experimental philosophy.
