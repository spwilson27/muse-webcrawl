George Eastman

George Eastman
Born: July 12, 1854, Waterville, New York, U.S.
Died: March 14, 1932, Rochester, New York, U.S.
Occupation: Entrepreneur, inventor, philanthropist
Known for: Founding Eastman Kodak Company

George Eastman (July 12, 1854 – March 14, 1932) was an American entrepreneur who founded the Eastman Kodak Company and helped bring photography to the mass market. He invented roll film and the simple box camera marketed with the slogan "You press the button, we do the rest."

Career

Eastman, a former bank clerk in Rochester, New York, began experimenting with dry photographic plates in the late 1870s. He patented a roll-film system and, in 1888, launched the Kodak camera, pre-loaded with film for 100 exposures and returned to the factory for processing. In 1900 the one-dollar Brownie camera made photography accessible to children and amateurs. His company, reorganised as Eastman Kodak in 1892, dominated the film market for most of the 20th century, supplying film stock to the early motion picture industry and pioneering colour processes and employee welfare schemes including profit-sharing and a wage dividend.

Philanthropy

Eastman was one of the great philanthropists of his era, giving away more than $100 million, chiefly to the University of Rochester, the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, and dental clinics in Rochester, London and other European cities. He founded the Eastman School of Music and the Eastman Theatre in Rochester, supported Tuskegee and Hampton institutes, and built a mansion on East Avenue that is now the George Eastman Museum of photography and film.

Death

Suffering from a progressive spinal disorder and chronic pain, Eastman took his own life on March 14, 1932, leaving a note reading "My work is done – Why wait?" He never married and left much of his estate to the University of Rochester.
