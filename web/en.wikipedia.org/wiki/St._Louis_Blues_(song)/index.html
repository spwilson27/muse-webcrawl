"Saint Louis Blues" (often "St. Louis Blues") is a popular American song composed by W. C. Handy in the blues style and published in September 1914. It is one of the first blues songs to succeed as a pop song and remains a cornerstone of the jazz repertoire; the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame included it among the "500 Songs That Shaped Rock and Roll".

Composition

Handy said he was inspired by a chance meeting with a woman distraught over her husband's absence, who lamented "Ma man's got a heart like a rock cast in de sea". The song blends a habanera-like tango rhythm in its middle section with the twelve-bar blues, a hallmark of Handy's style. Its opening lines — "I hate to see the evening sun go down" — are among the most recognisable in American music.

Recordings and influence

Early recordings included those by Prince's Band (1915) and Marion Harris (1920). Bessie Smith's 1925 recording, featuring Louis Armstrong on cornet, is considered a classic. Benny Goodman, Glenn Miller, and countless jazz and rock musicians have recorded it. African troops in both world wars were said to hum the song, and it was adopted as a signature tune by the St. Louis Blues NHL team, which took its name from the song.

Lyrics and themes

The lyrics tell of a woman lamenting her lover's infidelity with a "St. Louis woman" with her "diamond rings". The song's fusion of African American folk blues with published song form helped establish the commercial blues idiom.
