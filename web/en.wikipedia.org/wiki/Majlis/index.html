Majlis

Majlis (Arabic: مجلس, "a place of sitting"; plural majālis) is an Arabic term used for a council, assembly or gathering, and for the reception room in a private house where guests are received and seated. The word has been borrowed into Persian, Turkish, Urdu and other languages of the Islamic world.

Uses

In politics, majlis forms part of the official name of legislative bodies in several countries, such as Iran's Islamic Consultative Assembly (Majles-e Shura-ye Eslami), and the parliaments or councils of a number of Arab and Central Asian states; Bahrain, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE have consultative councils called Majlis al-Shura or similar. In a religious context, a majlis may denote a gathering for remembrance or mourning, particularly the Shia assemblies commemorating the martyrdom of Husayn during Muharram. In domestic architecture, especially in the Arabian Peninsula, the majlis is a formal sitting room, traditionally furnished with cushions and carpets along the walls, serving as the social heart of the house and a venue for discussion and hospitality.

Cultural significance

The majlis as a social space for dialogue and community decision-making was inscribed by UNESCO in 2015 on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, jointly for the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Oman and Qatar. Historically, the ruler's majlis functioned as a court of audience where subjects could present petitions directly.
