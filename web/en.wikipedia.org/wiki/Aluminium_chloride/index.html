Aluminium chloride

Aluminium chloride is an inorganic compound with the formula AlCl3. It is the principal industrial compound of aluminium and chlorine and is best known as a powerful Lewis acid used in Friedel–Crafts alkylation and acylation reactions. The anhydrous material is highly reactive with water, releasing heat and hydrogen chloride.

Structure and properties

In the solid state aluminium chloride forms layered structures at lower temperatures and dimeric Al2Cl6 units in the vapour and in nonpolar solvents. The hexahydrate, AlCl3·6H2O, is a different, ionic hydrate and does not behave like the anhydrous Lewis acid in organic synthesis.

The compound is colourless when pure but often appears yellow because of iron(III) chloride contamination. It fumes in moist air and must be handled with care because it is corrosive.

Production and uses

Industrially, aluminium chloride is prepared by chlorinating aluminium metal or alumina in the presence of carbon. Besides Friedel–Crafts chemistry, it is used in the production of anthraquinone, detergents, ethylbenzene and some pharmaceuticals, and as a catalyst or reagent in polymerisation and hydrocarbon processing.
