French Basque Country

The French Basque Country, or Northern Basque Country (Basque: Iparralde, "the Northern Region"; French: Pays basque français; Spanish: País Vasco francés), is a region in the western Pyrenees that forms the western part of the French department of the Pyrénées-Atlantiques. It comprises three historical provinces: Labourdin the west, Lower Navarre in the centre, and Soule in the east. Together with the Southern Basque Country in Spain, it forms the Basque Country as a cultural region.

Geography

The region extends from the Atlantic coast — with towns such as Bayonne, Anglet, Biarritz and Saint-Jean-de-Luz — inland to the foothills of the Pyrenees. Bayonne is the largest urban centre and economic capital, though the traditional capital is often considered to be Ustaritz in Labourd. The population is around 300,000. The coastal area is heavily French-speaking and urbanised, while Basque (Euskara) remains more widely spoken in the rural interior.

History

The Basque provinces north of the Pyrenees were incorporated into the Kingdom of France over the course of the Middle Ages and early modern period, with Lower Navarre joining in personal union through Henry IV in 1589 and formally in 1620. After the French Revolution the three provinces were merged with Béarn into the department of Basses-Pyrénées. Basque nationalist movements in Iparralde have historically been weaker than in Spain, though cultural activism, Basque-language schools (ikastolak) and the Seaska federation have grown since the 1960s.

Institutions

In 2017 the Basque communes formed the Communauté d'agglomération du Pays Basque, a single intercommunal body covering the whole French Basque Country — the first institution corresponding to the territory since the Revolution. Demands for a separate Basque department have been repeatedly raised but not adopted by the French state.
