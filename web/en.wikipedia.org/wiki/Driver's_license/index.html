Driver's license

A driver's license (American English) or driving licence (British English) is an official document authorising a specific individual to operate one or more types of motorised vehicles — such as a motorcycle, car, truck or bus — on a public road. In most jurisdictions it is issued after the recipient has passed a driving test and met age, residency, medical and identity requirements.

History

Compulsory licensing was introduced in the United Kingdom by the Motor Car Act 1903 and in parts of the United States and continental Europe in the early 20th century. Modern licences are typically plastic cards showing the holder's photograph, name, date of birth, address, licence number, vehicle categories and expiry date.

Categories and identification

Licences are divided into categories by vehicle weight and type. In many countries the licence also serves as a primary form of photo identification. International Driving Permits allow holders to drive in other countries under conventions signed in 1949 and 1968.
