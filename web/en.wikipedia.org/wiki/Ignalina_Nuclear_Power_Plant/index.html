Ignalina Nuclear Power Plant

The Ignalina Nuclear Power Plant is a decommissioned two-unit nuclear power station in Visaginas Municipality, Lithuania, near the borders with Latvia and Belarus. It housed two RBMK-1500 water-cooled, graphite-moderated reactors — the most powerful variants of the design used at Chernobyl. At its peak the plant supplied more than 70 percent of Lithuania's electricity. As a condition of Lithuania's accession to the European Union, both units were shut down, Unit 1 in 2004 and Unit 2 in 2009, and decommissioning is under way with EU financial support.

History

Construction began in the late 1970s, and the new town of Visaginas was built to house the plant's largely Russian-speaking workforce. Unit 1 entered service in December 1983 and Unit 2 in August 1987; a third unit was under construction but was cancelled after protests following the Chernobyl disaster of 1986, which involved a smaller RBMK-1000 reactor of the same family. Originally rated at 1,500 MWe each — the most powerful reactors in the world at the time — the units were derated to about 1,360 MWe and later 1,185 MWe after safety upgrades.

Closure and decommissioning

Because the RBMK design lacks a full containment structure, Lithuania committed in its EU accession treaty to close Ignalina early. Unit 1 shut down on December 31, 2004, and Unit 2 on December 31, 2009, making Lithuania dependent on electricity imports and on the Elektrėnai gas-fired plant. Decommissioning, funded largely by the EU through the Ignalina International Decommissioning Support Fund managed by the EBRD, involves defuelling, dismantling the reactor graphite stacks, and managing spent fuel in an on-site interim storage facility; the project is expected to run into the late 2030s.

Replacement plans and cultural impact

Lithuania, with Latvia, Estonia, and Poland, planned a replacement station (the Visaginas Nuclear Power Plant with a Hitachi ABWR reactor), but a 2012 advisory referendum rejected the project and it stalled. The plant gained new fame as the principal filming location for the 2019 HBO miniseries Chernobyl, since its RBMK reactors closely resemble those of the Ukrainian plant, drawing increased tourism to Visaginas.
