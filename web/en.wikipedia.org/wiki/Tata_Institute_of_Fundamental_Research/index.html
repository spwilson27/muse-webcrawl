Tata Institute of Fundamental Research

The Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR) is India's premier research institution for basic sciences, located in Mumbai. It was founded on 1 June 1945 by the physicist Homi J. Bhabha, with support from the Sir Dorabji Tata Trust and J. R. D. Tata, and is funded by the Government of India through the Department of Atomic Energy. TIFR is a deemed university and the cradle of India's atomic energy programme and much of its modern science.

History

Bhabha persuaded the Tata trust to fund an institute for fundamental research in physics and mathematics before Indian independence. TIFR began in the Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore, then moved to Bombay, first to Kenilworth on Pedder Road and in 1962 to its purpose-built campus at Navy Nagar, Colaba, at the southern tip of the city — a landmark modernist complex designed by Chicago architect Helmuth Bartsch. Bhabha used TIFR as the nucleus from which the Atomic Energy Establishment at Trombay (later BARC) grew.

Research

The Institute works in three broad areas: mathematics (algebra, geometry, number theory, analysis), natural sciences (theoretical and experimental physics, astronomy, chemistry and biology, including the National Centre for Biological Sciences in Bengaluru) and technology and computer science. TIFR built India's first computer, TIFRAC (commissioned 1960), pioneered cosmic ray and balloon-borne astronomy from Hyderabad, and operates major national facilities including the Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope near Pune, the National Facility for Neutron Beam Research, and a pelletron accelerator in Mumbai. Centres include TIFR Hyderabad, the International Centre for Theoretical Sciences in Bengaluru, and the Homi Bhabha Centre for Science Education.

Academics

TIFR runs an integrated PhD and MSc graduate programme admitting students through a national entrance examination. Its alumni and faculty include many of India's most decorated scientists, and its mathematics school is internationally renowned.
