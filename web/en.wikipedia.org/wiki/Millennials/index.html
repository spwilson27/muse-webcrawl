Millennials, also known as Generation Y or Gen Y, are the demographic cohort following Generation X and preceding Generation Z. Researchers and popular media typically use the early 1980s as starting birth years and the mid-1990s to early 2000s as ending years; the U.S. Pew Research Center defines millennials as those born from 1981 to 1996. The generation is generally marked by its familiarity with the internet, mobile devices, and social media from a young age.

Characteristics

Millennials came of age during the spread of the World Wide Web, instant messaging, and smartphones, and their adolescence and early adulthood were shaped by the September 11 attacks, the Iraq and Afghanistan wars, and — for the younger half of the cohort — entering the workforce during or after the Great Recession. Studies describe millennials as, on average, more educated than previous generations but burdened by higher student debt, delayed home ownership, marriage, and childbearing relative to earlier cohorts at the same age.

Politically, millennials in many Western countries lean more liberal than older generations on social issues, and they are the most ethnically and racially diverse adult generation in United States history. They have been the subject of extensive marketing and workplace commentary, including both admiring accounts of their technological fluency and stereotypes about entitlement and job-hopping, which researchers often find overstated.

Economic impact

Because the Great Recession struck as many millennials were starting their careers, the cohort experienced higher unemployment and slower wage growth early on, with measurable long-term effects on earnings and wealth accumulation. In the United States, millennials surpassed baby boomers in 2019 as the nation's largest living adult generation, and they became the largest generation in the U.S. labour force in the mid-2010s.

Terminology

The term "millennials" was coined by authors William Strauss and Neil Howe, whose generational theory described them as the cohort coming of age around the turn of the third millennium. Alternative names, including "Generation Y" and "echo boomers", preceded its general adoption.
