The Prime Minister of Greece (Greek: Πρωθυπουργός της Ελλάδας) is the head of government of the Hellenic Republic and the leader of the cabinet. The prime minister is appointed by the President of Greece and is almost always the leader of the party holding a majority in the Hellenic Parliament.

Appointment and powers

After a general election, the president appoints as prime minister the leader of the party with an absolute majority of seats; if no party has a majority, the president gives exploratory mandates to the leaders of the largest parties to attempt to form a coalition. The government must win a vote of confidence in parliament. The prime minister recommends the appointment and dismissal of ministers, directs government policy, and represents Greece in the European Council and internationally, making the office the most powerful in the Greek political system.

History

The office dates to 1833, when Spyridon Trikoupis became the first head of government of the independent Greek state. In the 19th century the title of the office changed between "President of the Council of Ministers" and other forms before "Prime Minister" became standard. Notable holders include Charilaos Trikoupis, Eleftherios Venizelos — the dominant figure of early 20th-century Greek politics — Konstantinos Karamanlis, Andreas Papandreou, Konstantinos Mitsotakis, Costas Simitis, and Alexis Tsipras. Kyriakos Mitsotakis of New Democracy became prime minister in 2019 and was re-elected in 2023.

Residence

The official seat and office of the prime minister since 1982 is the Maximos Mansion (Megaro Maximou) in central Athens, adjacent to the Presidential Mansion and the National Garden.
