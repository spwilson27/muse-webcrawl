Avionics

Avionics (a portmanteau of aviation and electronics) are the electronic systems used on aircraft. Avionic systems include communications, navigation, the display and management of multiple systems, and the hundreds of systems that are fitted to aircraft to perform individual functions. These can be as simple as a searchlight for a police helicopter or as complicated as the tactical system for an airborne early warning platform. Avionics account for a large fraction — often around a third — of the cost of a modern aircraft.

History

The term became common in the early 1950s as military aircraft acquired radar, radio navigation and electronic countermeasures. Airborne radio communication began in the First World War era, and radio navigation aids such as the low-frequency radio range spread in the 1930s. Post-war development added VOR and ILS navigation, weather radar, transponders and inertial navigation. From the 1980s, CRT and then LCD "glass cockpits" replaced mechanical instruments, flight management systems automated navigation, and fly-by-wire flight controls were integrated with the avionics suite.

Major systems

Communication systems include VHF and HF radios, satellite communications and controller–pilot data link. Navigation systems comprise GNSS (GPS), inertial reference systems, VOR/DME and instrument landing systems, increasingly managed through a flight management system. Monitoring and safety systems include the ground proximity warning system (EGPWS), traffic collision avoidance system (TCAS), weather radar, flight data recorders and the electronic flight instrument system (EFIS). In modern architectures, functions are hosted on shared computing modules — integrated modular avionics — and exchange data over standard buses such as ARINC 429 and AFDX (ARINC 664).

Certification

Because failures can be catastrophic, avionics are developed under rigorous certification standards: DO-178C governs software, DO-254 complex hardware, and system safety is assessed under ARP4754A. Development assurance levels A (most critical) to E scale the rigour of design and verification.

Military avionics

Military aircraft add radar and electro-optical targeting, electronic warfare and self-protection suites, secure communications, sensor fusion and stores management. The mission systems of an aircraft such as an airborne early warning platform are among the most complex electronic systems ever flown.
