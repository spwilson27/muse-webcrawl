Ornithischia

Ornithischia is an extinct order of mainly herbivorous dinosaurs characterised by a "bird-like" pelvis, in which the pubis points backward alongside the ischium. The name, coined by Harry Seeley in 1888, means "bird-hipped", though birds themselves descend from the other great dinosaur order, the Saurischia.

Characteristics

Ornithischians share several distinctive traits: a predentary bone at the front of the lower jaw supporting a beak, cheek pouches implied by ridged, leaf-shaped teeth suited to cropping vegetation, and ossified tendons stiffening the back and tail in many forms. All known members were herbivores, and many lived in herds.

Major groups

The order includes the armoured thyreophorans (stegosaurs and ankylosaurs), the horned and frilled ceratopsians, the dome-headed pachycephalosaurs, and the ornithopods — ranging from small bipeds to the great duck-billed hadrosaurs; Iguanodon is an early, historically important ornithopod. Ornithischians thrived worldwide from the Early Jurassic until the end-Cretaceous extinction 66 million years ago.
