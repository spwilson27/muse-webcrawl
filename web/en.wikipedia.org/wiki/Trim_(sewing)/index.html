Trim (sewing)

In sewing and dressmaking, trim or trimming refers to decorative or functional materials applied to a garment or other textile item, usually at its edges. Trims include lace, ribbon, braid, fringe, piping, binding, rickrack, sequins, appliqué, tassels, and galloon. The term also covers notions such as buttons and zips when considered as finishing elements of a design.

Uses

Trims finish raw edges, reinforce stress points, and provide visual accent; historically, elaborate trimmings signalled wealth and status and were listed among the most costly parts of a garment in sumptuary accounts. Military uniforms make extensive use of braid and lace trim to denote rank, while folk costumes and liturgical vestments preserve distinctive regional trimming traditions. In home sewing and upholstery, trims such as gimp and brush fringe finish curtains, cushions, and furniture.

Industry

Trimmings are sold by the metre from dedicated notions departments and specialist shops, and a substantial manufacturing sector — historically centred in regions such as St. Etienne in France for ribbon — produces both machine-made and hand-made trims. Vintage and antique trims are collectible and are used in costume conservation and reproduction clothing.
