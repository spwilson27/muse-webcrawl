Preadditive category

In mathematics, specifically category theory, a preadditive category is a category that is enriched over the category of abelian groups. That is, every hom-set is equipped with the structure of an abelian group, and composition of morphisms is bilinear. Preadditive categories are also sometimes called Ab-categories.

Examples

The category of abelian groups itself is preadditive, as is the category of modules over a ring and the category of vector spaces over a field. Any category with finite products and coproducts in which the hom-sets carry a natural abelian group structure is an example; a one-object preadditive category is exactly a ring.

Additive categories

A preadditive category with a zero object and finite biproducts (objects that are simultaneously products and coproducts) is called an additive category. Additive categories are the usual setting for homological algebra, and an additive category admitting kernels and cokernels of all morphisms with suitable properties is an abelian category.
