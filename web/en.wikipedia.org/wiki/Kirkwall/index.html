Kirkwall

Kirkwall is the largest town and capital of the Orkney Islands, an archipelago off the north coast of mainland Scotland. Situated on the northern coast of the island of Mainland, Kirkwall is the administrative centre of the Orkney Islands Council and a busy ferry port linking Orkney to Aberdeen, Shetland and the other islands.

History

The name Kirkwall comes from the Old Norse Kirkjuvágr, meaning "church bay". The town is first mentioned in the Orkneyinga saga around 1046, and a church dedicated to Saint Olaf stood here in the 11th century. Kirkwall was granted royal burgh status by King James III in 1486, shortly after Orkney was annexed to the Scottish crown. The town grew around its harbour and the magnificent St Magnus Cathedral.

St Magnus Cathedral

Founded in 1137 by Earl Rognvald Kolsson in honour of his uncle, Saint Magnus Erlendsson, the cathedral is built of red and yellow sandstone and is one of the finest surviving examples of Romanesque architecture in Scotland. It is owned not by any church but by the people of Orkney, having been gifted to the burgh by James III in 1486.

Landmarks and culture

Other landmarks include the Bishop's Palace and the ruined Earl's Palace, the Orkney Museum in Tankerness House, and Highland Park distillery, founded in 1798. Each year on Christmas Day and New Year's Day the town hosts the Ba' Game, a mass street football contest between the "Uppies" and the "Doonies". Kirkwall Airport provides air links to mainland Scotland and the other islands.
