Lamiaceae (/ˌleɪmiˈeɪsiː, -iː/), also known as the mint family or deadnettle family, is a family of flowering plants in the order Lamiales. It is one of the largest families of flowering plants, comprising an estimated 7,500 to 7,700 species in about 230 to 236 genera. The family has a cosmopolitan distribution.

The plants are frequently aromatic in all parts and include many widely used culinary herbs such as basil, mint, rosemary, sage, savory, marjoram, oregano, hyssop, thyme, lavender, and perilla. Some species are shrubs or trees, such as teak, or, rarely, vines. Many members of the family are widely cultivated, not only for their aromatic qualities, but also for their ease of cultivation; these species are among the easiest plants to propagate by stem cuttings.

Characteristics

Members of Lamiaceae are characterised by square stems, opposite leaves, and bilaterally symmetrical flowers with five united petals and five united sepals. The flowers are typically two-lipped (bilabiate), with an upper lip of two lobes and a lower lip of three. The ovary is superior and composed of two carpels that are divided into four chambers by a false septum; the fruit is usually a schizocarp of four nutlets. Many species bear glandular hairs that secrete volatile oils, accounting for the family's characteristic aromas.

Taxonomy

The family name Lamiaceae derives from the genus Lamium (the deadnettles), itself from the Greek laimos, meaning mouth or throat, in reference to the gaping shape of the flowers. The alternative name Labiatae, referring to the lipped corolla, was historically used and is still permitted as an alternative family name. Modern molecular phylogenetics has greatly expanded the family: many genera formerly placed in Verbenaceae, such as teak (Tectona) and Vitex, are now included within Lamiaceae.

Uses

Besides culinary herbs, the family provides important sources of essential oils, ornamentals such as coleus and salvia, and timber, most notably teak. Many species are significant nectar sources for pollinators and feature prominently in traditional medicine.
