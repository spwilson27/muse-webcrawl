The New York Public Library (NYPL) is a public library system in New York City. With nearly 53 million items and 92 locations, it is the second-largest public library in the United States (behind the Library of Congress) and the fourth-largest in the world. It is a private, non-governmental, independently managed non-profit corporation operating with both private and public financing.

History

The library was formed in 1895 by combining the collections of the Astor Library (founded 1849), the Lenox Library (founded 1870), and the Tilden Foundation, whose bequest from former governor Samuel J. Tilden provided funds for a free library for the city. Philanthropist Andrew Carnegie later donated $5.2 million to build 65 branch libraries across the city. The main branch — the Stephen A. Schwarzman Building on Fifth Avenue and 42nd Street, guarded by the marble lion statues nicknamed Patience and Fortitude — opened on May 23, 1911, exactly sixteen years after the agreement creating the library.

Collections and services

The NYPL comprises research libraries, including the Schwarzman Building, the Library for the Performing Arts at Lincoln Center, the Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture in Harlem, and the Science, Industry and Business Library (now part of the Stavros Niarchos Foundation Library), alongside dozens of circulating branch libraries in Manhattan, the Bronx, and Staten Island. (Brooklyn and Queens have separate library systems.) Its holdings include a Gutenberg Bible, the first book printed in the English colonies, Jefferson's handwritten copy of the Declaration of Independence, and extensive manuscript and photograph collections. The library also provides free classes, exhibitions, digital lending through its SimplyE app, and one of the most-used public Wi-Fi and computer networks in the city.
