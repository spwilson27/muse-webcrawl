Smokey Bear

Smokey Bear is an American campaign and advertising icon of the U.S. Forest Service in the Wildfire Prevention Campaign, which is the longest-running public service announcement campaign in United States history. The Ad Council, the United States Forest Service (USFS), and the National Association of State Foresters (NASF), in partnership with creative agency FCB, employ Smokey Bear to educate the public about the dangers of unplanned human-caused wildfires.

History

The campaign began in 1944, prompted by wartime fears that enemy shelling or sabotage could ignite wildfires in western forests. After Walt Disney's Bambi was loaned to the campaign for one year, the Forest Service needed its own mascot, and artist Albert Staehle drew the first Smokey — a bear in jeans and a campaign hat pouring water on a campfire. The slogan became "Only you can prevent forest fires" in 1947 (changed to "wildfires" in 2001).

The living Smokey

In 1950, a young American black bear cub was rescued from the Capitan Gap fire in New Mexico's Lincoln National Forest after climbing a tree to escape the flames, suffering burns to his paws and legs. The cub, first nicknamed "Hotfoot Teddy", became the living symbol of Smokey Bear and spent the rest of his life at the National Zoo in Washington, D.C. When he died in 1976 he was buried at what is now Smokey Bear Historical Park in Capitan, New Mexico.

Legacy

Smokey Bear is protected by a 1952 Act of Congress, and his image is administered by the Forest Service. He remains one of the most recognizable advertising icons in the United States.
