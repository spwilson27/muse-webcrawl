Internationalization and localization

In computing, internationalization and localization (American) or internationalisation and localisation (British) are means of adapting computer software to different languages, regional peculiarities, and the technical requirements of a target locale. The terms are frequently abbreviated to the numeronyms i18n (for internationalization, where 18 is the number of letters between the first i and the last n) and L10n (for localization).

Definitions

Internationalization is the design and development process that ensures a product can be adapted to various languages and regions without engineering changes: separating translatable text from code, supporting Unicode, and allowing for differing date, number, and currency formats. Localization is the subsequent adaptation of the internationalized product for a specific region or language, including translation, imagery, and cultural conventions.

Scope

Beyond language, localization addresses units of measurement, address and name formats, legal requirements, colour symbolism, and reading direction — right-to-left scripts such as Arabic and Hebrew require mirrored layouts. Globalization is sometimes used for the combined business process, abbreviated g11n.

Challenges

Text expansion (German and French translations are often longer than English), plural rules that vary by language, sorting orders, and context-free string fragments all complicate localization. Modern frameworks provide message-format systems that handle plurals, gender, and variable interpolation.
