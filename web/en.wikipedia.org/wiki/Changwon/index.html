Changwon (Korean: 창원; Hanja: 昌原) is the capital and largest city of South Gyeongsang Province, South Korea. A planned industrial city on the southeastern coast, it is a major centre of the country's machinery, defence, and heavy industries, and is noted for its broad boulevards, parks, and bicycle infrastructure.

History

The area of modern Changwon has been inhabited since prehistoric times. The modern city was founded in 1974 as a planned city intended to serve as a hub for South Korea's machinery industry and as a potential emergency wartime capital, with wide straight roads designed for rapid evacuation. On 1 July 2010, Changwon merged with the neighbouring cities of Masan and Jinhae, creating a unified city of more than one million people.

Economy

Changwon is home to major Korean industrial firms, including defence and aerospace manufacturers, machinery producers, and electronics plants. The Changwon National Industrial Complex hosts hundreds of companies. The city also has a significant port function through nearby Masan and Jinhae, the latter famous for its naval base and annual cherry blossom festival (Jinhae Gunhangje).

Culture and transport

The city operates the Nubija public bicycle system, one of the first of its kind in South Korea. Changwon hosted matches of the shooting events and is home to sports teams including the NC Dinos baseball club. It is connected to the rest of the country by expressways and the Gyeongjeon Line railway, with KTX services at Changwon Station and Changwonjungang Station.
