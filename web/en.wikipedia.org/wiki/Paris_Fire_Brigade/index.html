Paris Fire Brigade

The Paris Fire Brigade (French: Brigade de sapeurs-pompiers de Paris, BSPP) is a French Army unit that serves as the primary fire and rescue service for Paris and the neighbouring departments of the petite couronne. Founded by Napoleon in 1811 after a disastrous fire at the Austrian embassy, it is one of only two military fire brigades in France, alongside the naval fire battalion of Marseille.

Organisation

The brigade employs roughly 8,500 military personnel and operates dozens of fire stations across Paris, Hauts-de-Seine, Seine-Saint-Denis and Val-de-Marne. It responds to hundreds of thousands of calls each year, most of them medical emergencies rather than fires.

History

Napoleon created the battalion of sapeurs-pompiers by imperial decree on 18 September 1811, militarising firefighting in the capital. The brigade became famous internationally for its gymnastics team and for its role in fighting the fire at Notre-Dame Cathedral in April 2019, when its firefighters saved the structure and its treasures.
