Madeleine Jana Korbel Albright (born Marie Jana Körbelová; May 15, 1937 – March 23, 2022) was an American diplomat and political scientist who served as the 64th United States secretary of state from 1997 to 2001 under President Bill Clinton — the first woman to hold the office, making her at the time the highest-ranking woman in the history of the US government. She previously served as US ambassador to the United Nations (1993–1997).

Early life

Born in Prague to Czech diplomat Josef Korbel, she fled with her family to Britain after the Nazi occupation in 1939, returned briefly after the war, and emigrated to the United States in 1948 when her father sought asylum from Czechoslovakia's communist coup — he became a noted professor at the University of Denver, where Condoleezza Rice was among his students. Albright learned only in the 1990s that her parents were Jewish and that three of her grandparents had been murdered in the Holocaust. She graduated from Wellesley College in 1959, became a US citizen in 1957, and earned a PhD from Columbia University in 1975 while raising three daughters with her husband, newspaper heir Joseph Albright (whom she divorced in 1982).

Career

Albright worked for Senator Edmund Muskie and as Zbigniew Brzezinski's chief legislative assistant on the National Security Council under President Carter, then taught at Georgetown University and advised Democratic presidential campaigns. As UN ambassador she was a forceful advocate of an assertive multilateralism — she clashed with Secretary-General Boutros Boutros-Ghali and helped block his re-election. Her tenure is shadowed by the Clinton administration's failure over the Rwandan genocide and by her 1996 remark on 60 Minutes that the price of sanctions on Iraq — reportedly half a million child deaths — was "worth it", a statement she later called a mistake.

As secretary of state she championed NATO enlargement to include Poland, Hungary, and the Czech Republic, pushed the 1999 NATO intervention in Kosovo, worked on (unsuccessful) US ratification of the Kyoto Protocol and the Comprehensive Test Ban Treaty, and in 2000 became the first US secretary of state to visit North Korea, meeting Kim Jong Il. After leaving office she founded the Albright Stonebridge Group consultancy, wrote bestselling memoirs including Madam Secretary (2003) and the anti-authoritarian warning Fascism: A Warning (2018), and was known for communicating through her collection of brooches — chronicled in Read My Pins. She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2012 and died of cancer in 2022.
