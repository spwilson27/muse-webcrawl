Protected areas of Georgia (country)

The protected areas of Georgia cover a wide range of habitats in the Caucasus, one of the world's biodiversity hotspots. Georgia's first nature reserve, Lagodekhi, was established in 1912, making it among the oldest in the former Russian Empire. The system is administered by the Agency of Protected Areas of the Ministry of Environmental Protection and Agriculture, and covers roughly 7–8% of the country's territory, with plans for expansion.

National parks

  • Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park — one of the largest national parks in Europe, protecting Caucasian mountain forests.
  • Tusheti National Park — high mountain landscapes in the Greater Caucasus.
  • Vashlovani National Park — semi-desert "badlands" in the southeast.
  • Kolkheti National Park — Black Sea coastal wetlands and relict Colchic forests, part of a UNESCO World Heritage listing (2021).
  • Mtatsminda, Algeti, Kazbegi, Javakheti, Machakhela, Pshav-Khevsureti, Tbilisi, Kintrishi, and Erusheti national parks, among others.

Other categories

Georgian law recognizes strict nature reserves, national parks, natural monuments, managed reserves, protected landscapes, and multiple-use areas. Notable reserves include Lagodekhi, Tusheti, Batsara-Babaneuri, Vashlovani, and the Colchic rainforests and wetlands. Protected areas are home to species such as the Caucasian leopard, East Caucasian tur, chamois, lynx, and brown bear.
