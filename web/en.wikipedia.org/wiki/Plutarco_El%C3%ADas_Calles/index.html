Plutarco Elías Calles (25 September 1877 – 19 October 1945) was a Mexican politician and general who served as President of Mexico from 1924 to 1928. A leading figure of the Mexican Revolution's Sonoran dynasty, he remained the dominant power in Mexican politics during the period known as the Maximato (1928–1935), ruling through puppet presidents.

Presidency

Calles pursued land reform, infrastructure, and education programmes, founded the Bank of Mexico, and sought to subordinate the military and the Catholic Church to the revolutionary state. His enforcement of anticlerical provisions, including the "Calles Law" of 1926 restricting religious practice, provoked the Cristero War (1926–1929), a widespread Catholic rebellion.

Maximato and exile

After the assassination of president-elect Álvaro Obregón in 1928, Calles founded the National Revolutionary Party (PNR), the ancestor of the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) that governed Mexico for most of the 20th century. His behind-the-scenes rule ended in 1936 when President Lázaro Cárdenas exiled him to the United States; he was allowed to return in 1941.
