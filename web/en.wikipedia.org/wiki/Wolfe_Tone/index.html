Wolfe Tone

Theobald Wolfe Tone (20 June 1763 – 19 November 1798), posthumously known as Wolfe Tone, was a leading Irish revolutionary and a founding member of the United Irishmen. He is regarded as a father of Irish republicanism.

Life

Born in Dublin and educated at Trinity College, Tone argued for Catholic emancipation and parliamentary reform, then for Irish independence from Britain. He helped found the Society of United Irishmen in Belfast in 1791 and sought French military support for an Irish rising.

Rebellion of 1798 and death

Tone accompanied French expeditions to Ireland in 1796 and 1798. Captured after the Battle of Tory Island in October 1798, he was court-martialled in Dublin and sentenced to hang; he died from a self-inflicted wound on 19 November 1798. His grave at Bodenstown, County Kildare, became a place of annual republican commemoration.
