Laboratory school

A laboratory school or demonstration school is an elementary or secondary school operated in association with a university, college, or other teacher education institution. It serves the training of teachers, the testing of educational innovations, and research into child development and pedagogy.

History

The most famous laboratory school was founded by John Dewey at the University of Chicago in 1896. The University of Chicago Laboratory Schools became a model for progressive education, where Dewey developed his ideas about learning by doing. Many teachers colleges established "model schools" or "training schools" in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Purpose

Student teachers observe and practise teaching in the school, while faculty use it as a setting for educational research. Laboratory schools often pioneer curricula and teaching methods before they are adopted more widely, though their selective admissions can make them unrepresentative of ordinary schools.
