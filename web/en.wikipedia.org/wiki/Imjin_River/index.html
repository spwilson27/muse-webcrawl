Imjin River

Length254 km (158 mi)
SourceMount Hoemul, North Korea
MouthHan River estuary, Yellow Sea
CountriesNorth Korea, South Korea

The Imjin River (Korean: 임진강; in South Korea written 임진강, in North Korea 림진강) is a river in the Korean Peninsula that flows from North Korea across the Demilitarized Zone into South Korea, joining the lower course of the Han River near Paju before emptying into the Yellow Sea. At about 254 kilometres (158 mi) long, it drains a basin of some 8,138 square kilometres and is the seventh-longest river on the peninsula.

The Imjin River crosses the Demilitarized Zone between the two Koreas.

Course

The Imjin rises in the Rangrim Mountains of North Korea's Kangwon Province and flows southwest through Kaesong's outskirts, crossing the DMZ near Panmunjom. In South Korea it passes Paju and Yeoncheon, receives tributaries such as the Hantan River, and merges with the Han River downstream of Gimpo. Because its estuary lies within a military-controlled zone, the lower Imjin remains unusually undeveloped and serves as habitat for migratory birds, including cranes and spoonbills.

History

The river takes its name from Imjin ford, a historic crossing. It gave its name to the Imjin War (1592–1598), the Japanese invasions of Korea launched under Toyotomi Hideyoshi, though the name derives from the year of the invasion (imjin in the sexagenary cycle) rather than from the river itself. During the Korean War, the Battle of the Imjin River (April 1951) saw the British 29th Infantry Brigade, especially the Gloucestershire Regiment, hold up a major Chinese offensive toward Seoul — one of the most celebrated defensive actions of the war.

Floods and cooperation

The Imjin is prone to monsoon flooding, and its transboundary character has produced friction and occasional cooperation: in 2009 an unannounced release from North Korea's Hwanggang Dam caused a flash flood that killed six South Korean campers, prompting agreements — imperfectly kept — on advance notification of dam discharges.
