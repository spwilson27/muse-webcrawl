Cruyff turn

The Cruyff turn is a dribbling move in association football named after Dutch player Johan Cruyff. It is an evasive feint in which a player appears to pass or cross the ball, but instead drags it behind their standing leg with the inside of the foot, turns 180 degrees, and accelerates away in the opposite direction.

Origin

Cruyff performed the move most famously during the 1974 FIFA World Cup in West Germany, in the Netherlands' group match against Sweden on 19 June 1974 in Dortmund. Marked closely by Swedish defender Jan Olsson near the corner flag, Cruyff shaped to cross, then flicked the ball behind himself and spun away, leaving Olsson wrong-footed. The moment became one of the tournament's enduring images and the move entered coaching manuals worldwide.

Technique

The player approaches the ball as if to deliver a pass, plants the non-kicking foot beside the ball, and with the inside of the kicking foot pulls the ball behind the planted leg while pivoting sharply away. The deception relies on the defender committing to block the apparent pass. The Cruyff turn remains a staple skill taught in youth academies. See also Johan Cruyff and Dribbling.
