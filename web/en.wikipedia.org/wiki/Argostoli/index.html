Argostoli

Argostoli (Greek: Αργοστόλι) is a town and a municipality on the island of Kefalonia, Ionian Islands, Greece. It has been the capital and administrative centre of Kefalonia since 1757, following a population shift down from the old capital of Agios Georgios, about 9 km to the southeast. Argostoli developed into one of the busiest ports in Greece, leading to prosperity and growth.

History

Argostoli became the island's capital in 1757. The town was almost completely destroyed by the 1953 Ionian earthquake, which devastated Kefalonia and neighbouring islands, and it was rebuilt in a more modern style, though several neoclassical buildings survive or were restored.

During the Second World War the island was occupied first by Italian and then by German forces; the massacre of the Italian Acqui Division in September 1943, dramatised in the novel and film Captain Corelli's Mandolin, took place on the island.

Geography

The town stands on a hillside across from Lixouri across the Gulf of Argostoli, on the western side of the island. The Koutavos Lagoon, a shallow body of water at the head of the gulf, is a nature reserve and a feeding ground for birds, including mute swans. The De Bosset Bridge (Drapano Bridge), built in 1813 during the British protectorate, crosses the lagoon and is one of the longest stone bridges over the sea; an obelisk at its midpoint commemorates its construction.

Sights

  • The Archaeological Museum of Argostoli, with finds from Mycenaean tombs on the island.
  • The Korgialenio History and Folklore Museum and Library.
  • The Katavothres sinkholes, where seawater disappears into fissures in the rock near the Fanari lighthouse.
  • The lighthouse of Saint Theodore (Agioi Theodoroi), a circular Doric-style structure on the Fanari peninsula.

Transport

Argostoli is served by Kefalonia International Airport, a few kilometres south of the town, and by ferry services to Lixouri across the gulf and to the mainland port of Kyllini.
