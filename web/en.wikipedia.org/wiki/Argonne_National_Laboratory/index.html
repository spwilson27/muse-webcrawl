Argonne National Laboratory is a federally funded research and development centre in Lemont, Illinois, operated by UChicago Argonne LLC for the United States Department of Energy. Founded in 1946 out of the Manhattan Project's Metallurgical Laboratory at the University of Chicago, it is the largest national laboratory by size and scope in the Midwest.

History

Argonne traces its origins to Enrico Fermi's team, which achieved the first self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction (Chicago Pile-1) in 1942. In 1943 the experimental reactors were moved to a forest preserve site outside Chicago (Site A), and in 1946 the laboratory was formally chartered. Early work centred on reactor physics: Argonne researchers produced the first electricity from nuclear energy at its Idaho site (EBR-I, 1951) and developed the designs behind most commercial power reactors, as well as the reactors of the U.S. Navy's nuclear fleet.

Facilities and research

Today Argonne's work spans energy storage and grid science, materials, chemistry, environmental research, and high-performance computing. Its major user facilities include the Advanced Photon Source (APS), a synchrotron X-ray source upgraded in 2023–2024 to the APS-U; the Center for Nanoscale Materials; and the Argonne Leadership Computing Facility, home to the exascale supercomputer Aurora, delivered in the 2020s. Argonne also played a notable role in lithium-ion battery research and in the rapid structural analysis of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein at the APS.
