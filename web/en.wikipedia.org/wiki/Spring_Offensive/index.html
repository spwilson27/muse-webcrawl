Spring Offensive

German spring offensive, 1918
Date21 March – 18 July 1918
LocationNorthern France and West Flanders, Belgium
ResultGerman operational failure; Allied strategic victory

The German spring offensive, also known as the Kaiserschlacht ("Kaiser's Battle") or Ludendorff offensive, was a series of German attacks along the Western Front from March to July 1918 during the First World War. Freed by Russia's exit from the war, Germany transferred some fifty divisions west and staked everything on defeating Britain and France before American forces could arrive in strength.

Operations

The offensive opened on 21 March 1918 with Operation Michael, in which infiltration tactics by stormtroopers, preceded by a massive short artillery bombardment, drove the British Fifth Army back up to 65 kilometres — the largest advance on the Western Front since 1914. Successive operations (Georgette in Flanders, Blücher-Yorck toward the Marne, and others) gained ground but failed to achieve a decisive breakthrough, and German casualties among elite assault units were irreplaceable.

Outcome

By July the offensive had exhausted itself. German forces had outrun their supplies, occupied exposed salients, and suffered roughly a million casualties. The Allied counterstroke at the Second Battle of the Marne in July 1918, followed by the Hundred Days Offensive, reversed the gains and led to the Armistice of 11 November 1918.
