The Hungary national football team represents Hungary in men's international association football and is administered by the Hungarian Football Federation. Hungary was a dominant force in world football in the first half of the 20th century, reaching two World Cup finals (1938 and 1954) and producing the legendary "Golden Team" (Aranycsapat) of the 1950s, led by Ferenc Puskás.

History

Early years

Hungary played its first international in 1902 against Austria and was among Europe's strongest sides in the interwar period, finishing runners-up to Italy at the 1938 World Cup in France under coach Alfréd Schaffer, with György Sárosi among its stars.

The Golden Team

Coached by Gusztáv Sebes, the team of Puskás, Sándor Kocsis, Nándor Hidegkuti, Zoltán Czibor and József Bozsik went unbeaten in 31 matches between 1950 and 1954, won Olympic gold at Helsinki 1952, and famously beat England 6–3 at Wembley in 1953 and 7–1 in Budapest in 1954 — the "Match of the Century". They were shock losers 3–2 to West Germany in the 1954 World Cup final, the "Miracle of Bern". The team dispersed after the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, when several stars, including Puskás, emigrated.

Later decades

Hungary won further Olympic golds in 1964 and 1968 and reached World Cup quarter-finals, but declined from the 1970s; its last World Cup appearance of the 20th century was 1986. After a 30-year absence from major tournaments, Hungary qualified for UEFA Euro 2016, reaching the round of 16, and also appeared at Euro 2020 and Euro 2024, playing home matches at the Puskás Aréna in Budapest.

Records

Ferenc Puskás is Hungary's all-time top scorer with 84 goals in 85 internationals. József Bozsik was the first Hungarian to reach 100 caps. Balázs Dzsudzsák is among the most-capped players of the modern era.
