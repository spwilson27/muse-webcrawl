Serotonin–norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor

SNRIs block the serotonin and norepinephrine transporters at the presynaptic terminal.

Drug class
ExamplesVenlafaxine, duloxetine, desvenlafaxine, milnacipran, levomilnacipran
UsesDepression, anxiety disorders, neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia
MechanismInhibition of serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake

Serotonin–norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) are a class of antidepressant medications used to treat major depressive disorder, anxiety disorders, and some chronic pain conditions. They act by inhibiting the reuptake of the neurotransmitters serotonin and norepinephrine at the presynaptic membrane, increasing their availability in the synaptic cleft and in descending pain-modulating pathways.

Medical uses

Venlafaxine and duloxetine are widely prescribed for depression and generalised anxiety disorder. Duloxetine is also approved for diabetic peripheral neuropathic pain and fibromyalgia; milnacipran and levomilnacipran are used for fibromyalgia and depression respectively.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include nausea, dry mouth, sweating, insomnia, and sexual dysfunction. SNRIs can raise blood pressure, particularly at higher doses of venlafaxine, and abrupt discontinuation may cause a discontinuation syndrome with dizziness and "brain zap" sensations. Combination with other serotonergic agents carries a risk of serotonin syndrome.
