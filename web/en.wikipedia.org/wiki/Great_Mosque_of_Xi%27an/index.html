LocationMuslim Quarter, Xi'an, Shaanxi, China
Founded742 AD (Tang dynasty)
StyleChinese temple architecture
AreaOver 12,000 m²

The Great Mosque of Xi'an (西安大清真寺) is one of the largest and oldest mosques in China. Located in the Muslim Quarter of Xi'an, it was founded in 742 AD during the Tang dynasty, with most of the present buildings dating from the Ming dynasty. Unlike most mosques in the Middle East, it is built entirely in the style of a Chinese temple complex, without domes or minarets in the Islamic style.

Architecture

The mosque is arranged along an east–west axis through five courtyards, entered through a series of gateways, arches and pavilions adorned with Chinese calligraphy and Arabic inscriptions. The prayer hall, at the western end facing Mecca, can accommodate about 1,000 worshippers and is roofed with glazed tiles supported by wooden beams.

History

Xi'an, the eastern terminus of the Silk Road, had a large Muslim community descended from Arab and Persian traders. The mosque was renovated under the Song, Yuan, Ming and Qing dynasties, and remains an active place of worship for the city's Hui Muslims as well as a popular tourist site.
