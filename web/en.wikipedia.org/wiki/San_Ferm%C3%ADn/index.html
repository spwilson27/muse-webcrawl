San Fermín is a week-long festival held annually in Pamplona, Spain, from 6 to 14 July in honour of Saint Fermin, co-patron of Navarre. It is one of Spain's most famous festivals, internationally known for the encierro, the running of the bulls.

Festival

The festival opens at noon on 6 July with the chupinazo, a rocket fired from the city hall balcony, and crowds in white clothing with red scarves and sashes fill the old town. Each morning at 8 a.m., six fighting bulls and steers run an 875-metre course through the streets to the bullring, pursued by thousands of runners; bullfights take place in the afternoon. Ernest Hemingway's 1926 novel The Sun Also Rises made the fiesta famous worldwide.

Traditions

Other events include the procession of Saint Fermin on 7 July, the giants and big-heads parade, fireworks competitions, and the closing Pobre de mí candlelit ceremony at midnight on 14 July.
