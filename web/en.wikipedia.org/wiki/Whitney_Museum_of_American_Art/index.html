The Whitney Museum of American Art, known informally as "The Whitney", is a museum of modern and contemporary American art located in the Meatpacking District of Manhattan, New York City. It was founded in 1930 by the sculptor and arts patron Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney, after the Metropolitan Museum of Art declined her offer of a collection of some 500 works by contemporary American artists.

History

The museum opened in 1931 on West 8th Street in Greenwich Village, growing out of the Whitney Studio Club (founded 1918) that had championed living American artists. It subsequently moved to West 54th Street (1954) and then to the distinctive granite-clad building designed by Marcel Breuer on Madison Avenue at 75th Street (1966–2014). In 2015 the Whitney returned downtown to a new building at 99 Gansevoort Street, designed by Renzo Piano, beside the southern entrance of the High Line.

Collection and exhibitions

The Whitney's collection includes works by Edward Hopper (the museum holds the largest collection of his work), Georgia O'Keeffe, Jackson Pollock, Jasper Johns, Andy Warhol, and many others. Its signature exhibition is the Whitney Biennial, founded as an annual in 1932 and held every two years since 1973, which surveys current developments in American art and frequently provokes critical debate.
