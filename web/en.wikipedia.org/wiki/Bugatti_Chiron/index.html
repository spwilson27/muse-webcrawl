The Bugatti Chiron is a mid-engine two-seater sports car developed and manufactured in Molsheim, France, by Bugatti Automobiles. It is the successor to the Bugatti Veyron and was first shown at the Geneva Motor Show in March 2016. The car is named after the Monegasque racing driver Louis Chiron, who raced for Bugatti in the 1920s and 1930s.

Specifications and performance

The Chiron is powered by an updated version of the Veyron's 8.0-litre quad-turbocharged W16 engine, producing 1,500 PS (1,103 kW; 1,479 hp) and 1,600 Nm of torque. Power is delivered to all four wheels through a seven-speed dual-clutch transmission. Bugatti quotes acceleration from 0-100 km/h in about 2.4 seconds and an electronically limited top speed of 420 km/h (261 mph), restricted for tyre safety. The car is built around a carbon-fibre monocoque with adaptive chassis and aerodynamics.

Variants

Numerous derivatives were produced, including the track-focused Divo, the one-off La Voiture Noire, the Centodieci (a homage to the EB110), and the Chiron Super Sport 300+, a version of which in 2019 became the first production-based car to exceed 300 mph, reaching 304.77 mph (490.48 km/h) at the Ehra-Lessien test track. The Bolide concept explored an extreme lightweight track interpretation of the W16 platform.

Production

Production of the Chiron family was limited to 500 units of the base coupe lineage, with final examples completed in 2024 as Bugatti transitioned toward its next-generation model under the Bugatti Rimac joint venture. Prices for the original Chiron started at around EUR 2.4 million before options and taxes.
