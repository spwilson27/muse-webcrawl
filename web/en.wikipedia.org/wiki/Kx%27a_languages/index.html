Kx'a languages

The Kx'a languages are a language family spoken in the Kalahari region of Botswana and Namibia in southern Africa. The family was proposed in the 2010s, linking the Ju languages (such as Juǀ'hoan) with the moribund ǂ'Amkoe language.

Classification

The Kx'a family consists of two branches: the Ju branch, a dialect cluster including Juǀ'hoan, ǃKung, and related varieties, and ǂHoan (ǂ'Amkoe), spoken by a small number of older people in Botswana. Like other "Khoisan" languages, Kx'a languages are notable for their extensive inventories of click consonants.
