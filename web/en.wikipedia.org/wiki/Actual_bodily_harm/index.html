Actual bodily harm

Assault occasioning actual bodily harm
StatuteOffences against the Person Act 1861, s. 47
JurisdictionEngland and Wales; similar offences elsewhere
Maximum penalty5 years' imprisonment

Assault occasioning actual bodily harm (often abbreviated to ABH) is a statutory offence of aggravated assault in English criminal law, created by section 47 of the Offences against the Person Act 1861. It is frequently known simply as actual bodily harm.

[Image: Scales of justice]

Elements

The offence requires an assault or battery which causes actual bodily harm to the victim. "Actual bodily harm" has been interpreted as any injury that is more than merely transient or trifling — including bruises, grazes, and scratches — and can include recognised psychiatric injury. The prosecution need not prove the defendant intended or foresaw the injury; it suffices that they intended or were reckless as to the assault itself.

Sentencing and mode of trial

The offence is triable either way, in the magistrates' court or the Crown Court, and carries a maximum sentence of five years' imprisonment (seven years where racially or religiously aggravated). It sits between common assault and the more serious offence of grievous bodily harm under sections 18 and 20 of the 1861 Act.
