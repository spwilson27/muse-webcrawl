Counterfeit

Counterfeiting: making imitations with intent to deceive.

To counterfeit means to imitate something authentic, with the intent to steal, destroy, or replace the original, for use in illegal transactions, or otherwise to deceive individuals into believing that the fake thing is of equal or greater value than the real thing. Counterfeit products are fakes or unauthorised replicas of the real product, and the word is also used for forged documents, currency, and works of art.

Counterfeit money

Counterfeiting of currency is one of the oldest forms of the crime, historically punished as treason in many jurisdictions because it attacked the sovereign's prerogative. Modern banknotes incorporate security features—watermarks, security threads, microprinting, colour-shifting inks, and polymer substrates—to resist imitation. Central banks and law-enforcement bodies such as the United States Secret Service, founded in 1865 in part to combat counterfeiting, monitor and suppress fake currency.

Counterfeit goods

Counterfeit consumer goods—including luxury clothing, watches, pharmaceuticals, electronics, and software—form a large global trade, estimated by the OECD to amount to hundreds of billions of dollars annually. Fake medicines are a particular public-health concern, as they may contain wrong or no active ingredients. Trademark holders employ authentication technologies such as holograms, serialisation, and RFID tags, and customs authorities seize infringing shipments at borders.

Documents and art

Forged documents include identity papers, certificates, and signatures, while art forgery produces works falsely attributed to famous artists. Detection relies on forensic examination of materials, provenance research, and expert connoisseurship.
