Scallion

Scallions (also known as green onions, spring onions, or salad onions) are versatile vegetables belonging to various species in the genus Allium. Scallions generally have a milder taste than most onions and are eaten both raw and cooked for their hollow green leaves and slender white bases. Unlike the mature bulb onion, scallions are harvested before a bulb has fully formed, or from varieties that never form true bulbs.

Types and cultivation

Most scallions sold commercially come from the Welsh onion (Allium fistulosum), which never forms a bulb, or from young common onions (Allium cepa) pulled early. Related forms include the Japanese negi and the ramsons-adjacent tree onion. Scallions grow quickly in most temperate climates and can be sown in succession through the season.

Culinary use

Scallions are fundamental to East Asian cooking — sliced over noodles, stir-fries, pancakes (such as Chinese scallion pancakes and Korean pajeon) and dipping sauces — and are widely used elsewhere as a garnish for soups, salads, baked potatoes and grilled dishes. Both the green tops and white parts are used; the greens are milder and often added raw at the end of cooking. In many regional dialects of English, "scallion" and "spring onion" distinguish subtly different growth stages, though usage varies.
