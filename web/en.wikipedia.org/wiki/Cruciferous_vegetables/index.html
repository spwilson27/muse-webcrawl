Cruciferous vegetables

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cruciferous vegetables are vegetables of the family Brassicaceae (also called Cruciferae, after the cross-shaped arrangement of their four petals). Many are cultivars of a single species, Brassica oleracea, bred over centuries for different parts of the plant — a striking example of artificial selection. Crucifers are among the most widely eaten vegetables in the world and are studied extensively for their nutritional value and characteristic sulphur compounds.

Cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli and kale are all cultivars of Brassica oleracea.

Common cruciferous vegetables

  • From Brassica oleracea: cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, kale, Brussels sprouts, kohlrabi, collard greens, Savoy cabbage, Romanesco.
  • From other Brassica species: bok choy, napa cabbage, turnip, rutabaga (swede), rapeseed/canola, mustard greens, Chinese broccoli (gai lan).
  • Other genera: watercress, garden cress, rocket (arugula), radish, daikon, horseradish, wasabi.

Nutrition and chemistry

Cruciferous vegetables are generally low in calories and rich in vitamin C, vitamin K, folate, carotenoids and fibre. Their distinctive pungency comes from glucosinolates, sulphur-containing compounds that, when the plant tissue is chopped or chewed, are converted by the enzyme myrosinase into isothiocyanates such as sulforaphane (from broccoli) and indoles such as indole-3-carbinol. These breakdown products have been widely researched for possible protective effects against cancer in laboratory and epidemiological studies; evidence in humans is suggestive but not conclusive, and health authorities generally recommend eating crucifers as part of a varied diet rather than as supplements.

Considerations

Very high intakes of raw crucifers can supply goitrogens (notably progoitrin), which interfere with iodine uptake by the thyroid; cooking reduces this effect, and the risk is negligible at normal dietary intakes where iodine is adequate. The high vitamin K content of leafy crucifers is relevant for people taking warfarin, who are advised to keep vitamin K intake consistent.

Cultivation

Most crucifers are cool-season crops of temperate regions. They share important pests and diseases — including clubroot (Plasmodiophora brassicae), cabbage white butterflies and flea beetles — so crop rotation is standard practice. China and India are the largest producers of cauliflower and broccoli; cabbages are produced worldwide, with China the leading grower.
