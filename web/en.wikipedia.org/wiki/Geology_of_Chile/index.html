Geology of Chile

The geology of Chile is dominated by the subduction of the Nazca Plate (and, in the far south, the Antarctic Plate) beneath the South American Plate along the Peru–Chile Trench. This process has built the Andes, the backbone of the country, and makes Chile one of the most seismically and volcanically active countries in the world.

Chile's major longitudinal units are the Coastal Range, the Central Valley and the Andes proper. Subduction-related magmatism produced the great porphyry copper deposits of the north, making Chile the world's largest copper producer.

Hazards

The largest earthquake ever recorded, the magnitude 9.5 Valdivia earthquake of 1960, occurred off southern Chile. Active volcanoes such as Villarrica and Láscar are monitored by the national geology service, SERNAGEOMIN.
