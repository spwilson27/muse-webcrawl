Dubna (Russian: Дубна́) is a town in Moscow Oblast, Russia, about 125 km (78 mi) north of Moscow on the Volga River, at the confluence with the Dubna River and near the Ivankovo Reservoir and the Moscow Canal. Dubna is a purpose-built science city (naukograd), home to the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research (JINR), an international particle-physics centre; the chemical element dubnium (atomic number 105) is named after the town.

History

The site grew around the Soviet nuclear research programme: in 1946 Igor Kurchatov's programme sited a secret laboratory here, where the synchrocyclotron—then the world's largest particle accelerator—came online in 1949 under the direction of scientists including Bruno Pontecorvo. On 26 March 1956 the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research was founded as an international collaboration of (originally socialist) member states, and Dubna received city status the same year. Georgy Flyorov's Laboratory of Nuclear Reactions at JINR became famous for synthesizing new superheavy elements—dubnium (105), flerovium (114), moscovium (115), livermorium (116), tennessine (117), and oganesson (118) among them. The town was officially opened to foreigners only in the early 1990s.

Landmarks and geography

Dubna straddles the Volga: the older scientific district lies on the right bank, and a monumental 37-metre statue of Vladimir Lenin (1937), one of the two tallest in the world, faces a formerly occupied pedestal across the Moscow Canal where a matching Stalin statue was demolished in 1961. The town is notable for its low-rise, pine-forest setting, its river beaches and sailing, and a giant wooden chair sculpture installed by a local furniture factory. In 2005 Dubna was designated a special economic zone for technology development, adding IT and applied-science firms to the physics base.
