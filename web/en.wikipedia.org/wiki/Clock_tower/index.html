Clock tower

TypeTower with clock faces
Famous examplesBig Ben (London), Zytglogge (Bern)

A clock tower is a tower built with one or more clock faces on its upper walls, often forming part of a church, town hall or other civic building, or standing free as a landmark.

History

Public striking clocks appeared in Europe in the 14th century, and towers bearing clocks became symbols of civic pride. Earlier timekeeping towers included Chinese and Islamic examples. Famous clock towers include the Elizabeth Tower (Big Ben) in London, the Zytglogge in Bern, and the Makkah Royal Clock Tower in Mecca, one of the tallest buildings in the world.

Mechanisms

Tower clocks historically used weight-driven pendulum mechanisms, often with a separate bell struck on the hour; many have since been electrified. Some clock towers, such as astronomical clocks, display additional information.
