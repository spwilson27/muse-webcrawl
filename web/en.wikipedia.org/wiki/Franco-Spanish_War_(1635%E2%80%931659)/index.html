Franco-Spanish War (1635–1659)
Date: 19 May 1635 – 7 November 1659
Belligerents: France (with England, Savoy, others) vs Spain
Result: Treaty of the Pyrenees; France gains territory

The Franco-Spanish War (1635–1659) was fought between France and Spain, with the participation of a variety of other European powers. France, under Cardinal Richelieu and later Cardinal Mazarin, declared war on Spain in 1635 during the Thirty Years' War, seeking to break Habsburg encirclement of French territory.

Course

Though the Peace of Westphalia (1648) ended the Thirty Years' War, fighting between France and Spain continued for another eleven years, through the Fronde civil wars in France and the Catalan Revolt in Spain. Commanders included the Prince of Condé and Turenne for France. The war ended with the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659), by which France gained Roussillon, Cerdanya and parts of the Spanish Netherlands, marking Spain's decline and France's emergence as Europe's dominant power.
