Finlaggan

Finlaggan is a historic site on the island of Islay in Argyll and Bute, Scotland, centred on two islands in Loch Finlaggan — Eilean Mòr ("the large island") and Eilean na Comhairle ("the council isle"). In the later Middle Ages it was the administrative and ceremonial centre of the Lordship of the Isles, the semi-independent Gaelic sea-kingdom ruled by Clan Donald, whose chiefs styled themselves Lords of the Isles and, at their height, treated with the kings of England and France as near-equals.

History

The loch was occupied from prehistoric times, with crannogs and evidence of early metalworking and a possible early Christian chapel. From the 13th century the site became the chief stronghold of the MacDonald Lords of the Isles. Eilean Mòr carried the lord's great hall, chapel, houses for retainers and workshops; a causeway led to Eilean na Comhairle, where the Council of the Isles — some fourteen island chiefs and magnates — met to administer justice and issue judgments recorded by hereditary judges (the Morrison brieves of Lewis). Inauguration of a new lord took place at Finlaggan, where by tradition he stood in a carved footprint stone.

After the forfeiture of the Lordship of the Isles to James IV in 1493, Finlaggan declined, though the buildings were occupied into the 16th century before abandonment.

Archaeology and visitor centre

Excavations in the 1990s, led by archaeologist David Caldwell, revealed the hall, chapel, and a range of high-status medieval buildings, together with grave slabs including that of a lady of the kindred. Finds included a fine 13th-century processional cross and evidence of feasting and craftworking. The Finlaggan Trust maintains a visitor centre near the site, displaying artefacts and interpreting the Lordship's history; a wooden bridge now links the shore to Eilean Mòr.
