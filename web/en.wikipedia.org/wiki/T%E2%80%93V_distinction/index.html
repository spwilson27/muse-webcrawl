T–V distinction

The T–V distinction is the use of different pronouns to convey levels of formality, politeness or social distance between speakers. The term comes from the Latin pronouns tu (singular, familiar) and vos (plural, later respectful singular).

Usage across languages

Many languages maintain the distinction: French tu vs vous, German du vs Sie, Spanish tú vs usted, Russian ty vs vy. The familiar form signals intimacy, solidarity or lower social distance; the formal form signals respect, distance or higher status of the addressee. English formerly had the distinction between thou and you, but standard English lost it by the 18th century.

Sociolinguistics

The classic analysis by Roger Brown and Albert Gilman (1960) described the semantics of "power" and "solidarity" governing pronoun choice. Usage has shifted in many societies toward the familiar form, reflecting egalitarian norms, though the formal form remains standard in professional and polite contexts.
