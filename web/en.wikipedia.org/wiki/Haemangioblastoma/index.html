Haemangioblastoma

Haemangioblastomas (also spelled hemangioblastomas) are benign, highly vascular tumours of the central nervous system, most commonly arising in the cerebellum, brainstem, and spinal cord. They are composed of stromal cells and abundant capillary networks and are classified as WHO grade I tumours.

Association with von Hippel–Lindau disease

Haemangioblastomas may occur sporadically or as part of von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) disease, an autosomal dominant disorder caused by mutation of the VHL tumour suppressor gene on chromosome 3p. In VHL, multiple haemangioblastomas may develop alongside retinal angiomas, renal cell carcinomas, and phaeochromocytomas. Tumours frequently secrete erythropoietin, which can cause secondary polycythaemia.

Presentation and treatment

Symptoms depend on location and include headache, ataxia, and raised intracranial pressure, often related to an associated cyst with a mural nodule. Imaging typically shows a cystic lesion with an enhancing mural nodule. Complete surgical resection is generally curative for solitary sporadic tumours; stereotactic radiosurgery may be used for lesions that are difficult to access.
