Polar decomposition

In linear algebra, the polar decomposition of a matrix is a factorisation of the form A = UP, where U is a unitary matrix and P is a positive semi-definite Hermitian matrix. It is the matrix analogue of writing a complex number z as r·eiθ: a "modulus" part P that stretches, and a "phase" part U that rotates. Intuitively, any linear transformation can be decomposed into a rotation (possibly with reflection) combined with a scaling along orthogonal axes.

A linear map decomposed into a rotation and a symmetric stretch.

Properties

P is always uniquely determined as the positive square root of A*A; if A is invertible, P is positive definite and U is also unique (U = AP−1). The decomposition is closely related to the singular value decomposition A = WΣV*: one may take P = VΣV* and U = WV*. For real matrices, U is orthogonal and P is symmetric positive semi-definite.

Applications

Polar decomposition is used in continuum mechanics, where the deformation gradient F = RU separates local rotation R from the right stretch tensor U; in computer graphics and robotics for interpolating rotations and correcting matrix drift; and in quantum mechanics and signal processing. The orthogonal factor U is the nearest unitary (or orthogonal) matrix to A in the Frobenius norm, a fact exploited in orthogonal Procrustes problems.
