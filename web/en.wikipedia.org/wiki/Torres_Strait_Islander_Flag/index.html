The Torres Strait Islander Flag is the flag that represents Torres Strait Islander people. It was designed in 1992 by Bernard Namok, a resident of Thursday Island, as the winning entry in a design competition held by the Island Coordinating Council. In 1995 it was officially recognised by the Australian Government under the Flags Act 1953 as a flag of significance to the Australian nation.

Design and symbolism

The flag has three horizontal panels: green at the top and bottom, representing the land; a narrow black panel across the middle, representing the Torres Strait Islander people; and blue below the black, representing the waters of the Torres Strait. The panels are separated by thin white lines. In the centre is a white dhari, a traditional ceremonial headdress, beneath which is a white five-pointed star. The five points of the star represent the five island clusters of the Torres Strait, and the star itself symbolises navigation and the seafaring traditions of the Islanders.

Designer

Bernard Namok (1971–2012) designed the flag at the age of 20. Following his death, copyright in the flag was held by his estate, and the flag is administered in consultation with Torres Strait Islander bodies.
