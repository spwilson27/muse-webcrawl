Christopher McQuarrie (born June 12, 1968) is an American filmmaker. He won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay for the crime thriller The Usual Suspects (1995) and later became known as the principal writer-director of the Mission: Impossible film series, directing Rogue Nation (2015), Fallout (2018), Dead Reckoning (2023) and The Final Reckoning (2025). He is a frequent collaborator of Tom Cruise, whom he also directed in Jack Reacher (2012).

[Image: Christopher McQuarrie on a film set]

Early life

McQuarrie was born in Princeton, New Jersey, and attended West Windsor-Plainsboro High School. Before entering the film industry he worked in Australia and as an assistant to a detective agency.

Career

His screenwriting breakthrough came with The Usual Suspects, directed by his childhood friend Bryan Singer, whose intricate flashback structure and twist ending won wide acclaim. He made his directorial debut with the crime drama The Way of the Gun (2000). After a period of uncredited script work, he wrote Valkyrie (2008) and co-wrote Edge of Tomorrow (2014) and Top Gun: Maverick (2022), the latter earning him an Academy Award nomination for Best Adapted Screenplay.

Mission: Impossible

McQuarrie became the first director to helm more than one Mission: Impossible film, and, with Cruise, the franchise's principal creative voice. Fallout is frequently cited among the finest action films of its decade.
