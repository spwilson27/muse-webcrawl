Battle of Mons Lactarius

The Battle of Mons Lactarius (also known as the Battle of Mount Vesuvius) was fought in 552 or 553 near Mount Vesuvius in Italy during the Gothic War. The East Roman (Byzantine) army under Narses destroyed the last Ostrogothic field army; its king Teias was killed and organised Gothic resistance in Italy effectively ended.

Background

After Narses defeated and killed King Totila at Taginae in 552, the Goths elected Teias as their last king. Teias gathered the remaining Gothic forces and treasure and marched to relieve his brother Aligern at Cumae, taking position at Mons Lactarius ("Milk Mountain") near the river Sarno, supplied by sea.

Battle

For about two months the armies skirmished across the river. When the Byzantine fleet cut off Gothic sea supply, starvation forced Teias to descend and fight on foot. Procopius describes Teias fighting heroically at the front, shield after shield filled with spears, until he was killed while changing shields. The Goths fought on until night and again the next day before negotiating surrender.

Aftermath

The surviving Goths were allowed to leave Italy. Some Gothic garrisons and a Frankish-Alamannic invasion prolonged the war a little longer, but the Ostrogothic kingdom was finished and Italy was restored — briefly — to imperial rule under Justinian.
