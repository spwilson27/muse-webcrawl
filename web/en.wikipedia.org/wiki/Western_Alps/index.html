Western Alps

The Western Alps are the western part of the Alpine mountain range, lying in France, Italy, Switzerland and Monaco. They are conventionally separated from the Central Alps at the Great St Bernard Pass and the Splügen Pass line. The Western Alps contain the highest peaks of the entire range, including Mont Blanc (4,808 m), Monte Rosa and the Matterhorn.

Subdivisions

Traditional groupings include the Maritime Alps near the Mediterranean coast, the Cottian Alps, the Dauphiné Alps (Écrins massif), the Graian Alps (including Mont Blanc and Gran Paradiso), the Pennine Alps (Matterhorn, Monte Rosa) and the Bernese Alps at the northern edge. The region contains major national parks — Écrins, Vanoise, Gran Paradiso and Mercantour — and famous ski resorts such as Chamonix, Val d'Isère, Courchevel and Zermatt.

Geology and geography

The Western Alps form a great arc curving from the Mediterranean to Lake Geneva. They are drained by the Rhône, Po, Isère and Durance rivers. The Mont Blanc Tunnel and the Great St Bernard and Simplon passes provide major transalpine routes.
