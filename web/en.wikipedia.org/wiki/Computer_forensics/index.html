Computer forensics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A forensic workstation used to image digital storage media

Computer forensics (also known as computer forensic science) is a branch of digital forensic science pertaining to evidence found in computers and digital storage media. The goal of computer forensics is to examine digital media in a forensically sound manner with the aim of identifying, preserving, recovering, analyzing and presenting facts and opinions about the digital information.

Although it is most often associated with the investigation of a wide variety of computer crime, computer forensics may also be used in civil proceedings. The discipline involves similar techniques and principles to data recovery, but with additional guidelines and practices designed to create a legal audit trail.

Overview

Evidence from computer forensics investigations is usually subjected to the same guidelines and practices of other digital evidence. It has been used in a number of high-profile cases and is becoming widely accepted as reliable within primary judicial systems in the US, UK and other major judiciaries.

In the early 1980s personal computers became more accessible to consumers, leading to their increased use in criminal activity (for example, to help commit fraud). At the same time, several new "computer crimes" were recognized (such as cracking). The discipline of computer forensics emerged during this time as a method to recover and investigate digital evidence for use in court. Since then several major catastrophes in the field of computer forensics have influenced the overall development of the discipline.

Forensic process

The forensic process typically follows standard phases: acquisition or imaging of exhibits, analysis, and reporting. Investigators use write blockers to ensure that no data on a suspect drive is altered during imaging, and they verify forensic images using cryptographic hash functions such as MD5 and SHA-1. Analysis may involve recovering deleted files, examining file system metadata, searching for keywords, and reconstructing a timeline of user activity.

Tools

Well-known forensic tools include EnCase, FTK (Forensic Toolkit), X-Ways Forensics, and open source suites such as The Sleuth Kit and Autopsy. Mobile device forensics and network forensics are closely related subfields.
