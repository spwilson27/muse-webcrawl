Jamnalal Bajaj (4 November 1889 – 11 February 1942) was an Indian industrialist and philanthropist. He founded the Bajaj Group of companies in the 1920s and was a close associate and adopted "fifth son" of Mahatma Gandhi, financing and participating in the Indian independence movement.

Early life and business

Born into a poor family in a village near Sikar, Bajaj was adopted as a grandson by a wealthy Wardha merchant, Seth Bachhraj, whose trading business he inherited and expanded. He founded what became the Bajaj Group in 1926, with interests that later grew to include sugar, steel, and other industries.

Role in the freedom movement

Bajaj met Gandhi in 1915 and became one of his most devoted followers and the chief financier of Congress activities. He took part in the Non-Cooperation Movement (1921), the Nagpur flag satyagraha (1923), and the Salt Satyagraha (1930), enduring imprisonment by the British authorities. He served as treasurer of the Indian National Congress for many years and gave up the British-granted title of Rai Bahadur.

At Gandhi's suggestion he opened the Lakshmi Narayan temple in Wardha to Dalits in 1928, among the earliest such acts by a prominent industrialist. He settled at Wardha, where Gandhi established his ashram at Sevagram, and supported khadi, village industries, and cow protection causes.

Legacy

Bajaj died at Wardha in 1942. The Jamnalal Bajaj Award, instituted in 1978, honours work in Gandhian constructive programmes; the Jamnalal Bajaj Institute of Management Studies in Mumbai and the locality of Bajaj Nagar are named after him. His sons Kamalnayan and Ramkrishna Bajaj carried the business and its philanthropic tradition forward.
