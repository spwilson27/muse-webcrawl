Félibrige
Founded21 May 1854, Fontvieille, France
FoundersFrédéric Mistral, Joseph Roumanille, Théodore Aubanel, and four others
PurposeRevival and promotion of the Occitan (Provençal) language and culture
HeadquartersArles (Museon Arlaten)

The Félibrige (Occitan: lou Felibrige) is a literary and cultural association founded in 1854 by seven young Provençal poets — Frédéric Mistral, Joseph Roumanille, Théodore Aubanel, Jean Brunet, Paul Giéra, Anselme Mathieu and Alphonse Tavan — to defend, restore and promote the Occitan language (in its Provençal dialect) and the culture of the south of France. The name, coined by Mistral, derives from an old Occitan word whose exact origin is debated.

The seven founders met at the Château de Fontvieille on 21 May 1854, the feast day of Saint Estelle, whom they adopted as the movement's patron saint; its symbol is a seven-pointed star. Members are called félibres, and the association is headed by a capoulié (president); Mistral served as the first from 1876 to 1888.

Activities

The Félibrige promoted a standardised Provençal orthography (the Mistralian norm), published almanacs such as the Armana Prouvençau, dictionaries — above all Mistral's Lou Tresor dóu Felibrige — and literary works, and organised festivals including the annual Santo Estello congress held each year in a different southern town. Mistral's 1904 Nobel Prize in Literature crowned the movement's literary achievements. The association influenced regional revival movements elsewhere, including in Catalonia, and still exists today, advocating for Occitan language teaching and culture.
