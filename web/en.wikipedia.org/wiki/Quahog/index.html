Quahog

The quahog or hard clam (Mercenaria mercenaria) is a species of edible marine bivalve mollusc native to the eastern coasts of North and Central America, from Prince Edward Island in Canada to the Yucatán Peninsula in Mexico. It is one of the most commercially important shellfish of the Atlantic coast of the United States.

Names and grades

The word "quahog" derives from the Narragansett poquauhock. In the seafood trade, hard clams are graded by size: the smallest legal clams are littlenecks, followed by topnecks, cherrystones and the largest quahogs or chowder clams. Smaller clams are tender and eaten raw on the half shell or steamed; larger ones are tougher and typically chopped for chowders, clam cakes and stuffed clams ("stuffies"). A related but distinct species is the ocean quahog (Arctica islandica), one of the longest-lived animals known.

Biology

Hard clams live buried in sediment, feeding by filtering phytoplankton from the water. They grow slowly, adding annual rings to their thick, heavy shells, and may live several decades. Like some other bivalves they are protandrous hermaphrodites, beginning life as males and many later becoming female. The species has been introduced to the Pacific coast of North America and to parts of Europe.

Wampum and cultural significance

The purple-coloured portion of the quahog shell was prized by Native American peoples of the Northeast for making wampum beads, used in ornaments, records and ceremonial belts; white beads were made from whelk shells. The quahog is the state shell of Rhode Island. In popular culture, the fictional Rhode Island city of Quahog is the setting of the animated series Family Guy.
