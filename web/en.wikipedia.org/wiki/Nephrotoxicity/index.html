Nephrotoxicity

Nephrotoxicity is toxicity in the kidneys. It is a poisonous effect of some substances, both toxic chemicals and medications, on renal function. Nephrotoxins are substances with the potential to damage the kidneys, which are especially vulnerable because they receive a large share of cardiac output and concentrate substances in the tubular fluid as urine is formed.

Types and mechanisms

Nephrotoxicity can take several forms, depending on the site and mechanism of injury:

  • Acute tubular necrosis — direct toxic injury to tubular epithelial cells, as with aminoglycoside antibiotics or cisplatin.
  • Acute interstitial nephritis — an inflammatory, often allergic reaction in the interstitium, classically associated with beta-lactam antibiotics and proton pump inhibitors.
  • Reduced glomerular filtration — haemodynamic effects, as with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), which impair prostaglandin-dependent afferent arteriolar dilation.
  • Crystal nephropathy — intratubular precipitation of drugs such as acyclovir or methotrexate.
  • Chronic interstitial damage — as seen with long-term lithium use, calcineurin inhibitors, and aristolochic acid.

Common nephrotoxins

Important nephrotoxic agents include aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, cisplatin and other chemotherapy agents, iodinated contrast media, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers in susceptible patients, calcineurin inhibitors, certain antivirals, and heavy metals such as lead, mercury and cadmium. Plant and fungal toxins, including aristolochic acid found in some herbal remedies, are significant causes in certain populations.

Diagnosis and prevention

Nephrotoxicity is usually detected by rising serum creatinine and falling estimated glomerular filtration rate, changes in urine output, or abnormal urinary sediment; biomarkers such as cystatin C and urinary NGAL and KIM-1 are also studied. Prevention centres on dose adjustment for renal function, adequate hydration, avoiding combinations of nephrotoxic agents, and monitoring drug levels where appropriate.
