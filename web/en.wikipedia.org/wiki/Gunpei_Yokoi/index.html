Gunpei Yokoi (横井 軍平, Yokoi Gunpei; 10 September 1941 – 4 October 1997) was a Japanese video game designer and inventor. A longtime employee of Nintendo, he is best known as the creator of the Game & Watch handheld series and the Game Boy, the inventor of the cross-shaped D-pad control, and the producer of the Metroid and Kid Icarus games.

Career at Nintendo

Yokoi graduated from Doshisha University with a degree in electronics and joined Nintendo in 1965, initially maintaining the assembly line machines used to manufacture hanafuda cards. His first major invention was the Ultra Hand (1966), an extending grabber toy that sold over a million units and helped pull Nintendo out of financial difficulty. He went on to create the Ultra Machine, the Love Tester, and the Beam Gun series of light-gun toys.

Leading Nintendo's Research & Development 1 division, Yokoi created the Game & Watch series (1980–1991), which introduced the D-pad, and in 1989 the Game Boy, which sold over 118 million units together with its Color successor and became one of the best-selling game systems of all time. His design philosophy, "Lateral Thinking with Withered Technology", favoured using mature, cheap technology in novel ways rather than chasing cutting-edge specifications.

Virtual Boy and departure

Yokoi designed the Virtual Boy, a tabletop 3D console released in 1995 that was a commercial failure. He left Nintendo in 1996, insisting his departure was planned and unrelated to the Virtual Boy, and founded Koto Laboratory, where he helped develop Bandai's WonderSwan handheld. On 4 October 1997, Yokoi was killed in a road accident on the Hokuriku Expressway after stepping out to inspect damage from a minor collision.

Legacy

Yokoi is widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in gaming history. He mentored Shigeru Miyamoto, whose first game Donkey Kong Yokoi supervised, and received a posthumous Lifetime Achievement Award at the Game Developers Choice Awards in 2003.
