Leiomyosarcoma

Leiomyosarcoma (LMS) is a malignant smooth muscle tumor and a type of soft tissue sarcoma. It is one of the more common types of soft tissue sarcoma in adults, arising most often in the uterus, the retroperitoneum, and the soft tissues of the limbs, though it can develop wherever smooth muscle is found, including the walls of blood vessels and the gastrointestinal tract.

Signs and symptoms

Leiomyosarcomas often grow silently and may present as a painless mass. Depending on the site, symptoms can include abdominal or pelvic pain, abnormal uterine bleeding, weight loss, and swelling. Because deep tumors may reach a large size before detection, many are diagnosed at an advanced stage.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on imaging (MRI or CT) followed by biopsy. Under the microscope the tumor shows intersecting fascicles of spindle-shaped cells with cigar-shaped nuclei; immunohistochemistry typically shows positivity for smooth muscle markers such as smooth muscle actin, desmin and h-caldesmon. Tumors are graded by differentiation, mitotic rate and necrosis, which guide prognosis.

Treatment

The mainstay of treatment for localized disease is complete surgical resection with wide margins, sometimes combined with radiotherapy. Chemotherapy regimens based on doxorubicin, often with ifosfamide, and later-line agents such as gemcitabine with docetaxel, trabectedin or pazopanib are used for advanced disease. Uterine leiomyosarcoma is notably resistant to hormonal manipulation compared with endometrial stromal tumors.
