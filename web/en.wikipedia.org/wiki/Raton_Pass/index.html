Raton Pass
Elevation: 7,834 ft (2,388 m)
Location: Colorado–New Mexico border, USA
Traversed by: Interstate 25; BNSF Railway
Designation: National Historic Landmark

Raton Pass is a 7,834 ft (2,388 m) mountain pass on the Colorado–New Mexico border in the United States, at the eastern foot of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains. It was a critical choke point on the Mountain Branch of the Santa Fe Trail, and is crossed today by Interstate 25 and the BNSF Railway (used by Amtrak's Southwest Chief).

History

The pass — named Ratón, Spanish for "mouse" — was used by Spanish explorers and later by traders on the Santa Fe Trail from 1821. In 1866 "Uncle Dick" Wootton improved the route and charged a toll. During the American Civil War era and the railroad race of 1878, the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway battled the Denver and Rio Grande Railroad for control of the pass; the Santa Fe won and built through it in 1879. The difficult grades later led freight traffic to shift to other routes, but the pass remains a passenger rail and highway corridor.

Designation

Raton Pass was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1960 for its role in westward expansion and the Santa Fe Trail.
