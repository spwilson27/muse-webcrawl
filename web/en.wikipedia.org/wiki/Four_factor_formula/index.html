Four factor formula

The four factor formula, used in nuclear engineering, describes the neutron multiplication factor of an infinite nuclear reactor in terms of four physical factors. In an infinite system the effective multiplication factor is:

k∞ = η · f · p · ε

The factors

  • η (eta), the reproduction factor: the number of fission neutrons produced per neutron absorbed in the fuel.
  • f, the thermal utilization factor: the fraction of thermal neutrons absorbed that are absorbed in the fuel rather than in moderator, coolant, or structure.
  • p, the resonance escape probability: the fraction of neutrons that are slowed down past the resonance absorption energies of U-238 without being captured.
  • ε (epsilon), the fast fission factor: the ratio of total fission neutrons to those produced by thermal fission alone, accounting for fast fission mainly in U-238.

Typical values in a light-water reactor are η ≈ 1.65, f ≈ 0.71, p ≈ 0.87, and ε ≈ 1.02. For a finite reactor, the four factor result is combined with fast and thermal non-leakage probabilities to give the six factor formula for keff.
