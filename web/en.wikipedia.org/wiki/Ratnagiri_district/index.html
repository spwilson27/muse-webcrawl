Ratnagiri district is one of the 36 districts of Maharashtra state in western India. The district headquarters are at the port town of Ratnagiri. The district forms part of the Konkan region, lying between the Sahyadri (Western Ghats) mountain range to the east and the Arabian Sea to the west.

Geography

Ratnagiri district has a long coastline of about 237 km dotted with beaches, creeks, and historic sea forts such as Jaigad, Ratnadurg, and Vijaydurg. The terrain is hilly, with lateritic plateaus and fertile river valleys. The district experiences heavy monsoon rainfall.

Economy

The district is famous for its Alphonso (Hapus) mangoes, which enjoy a Geographical Indication tag, as well as cashews, coconuts, and rice. Fishing and fish processing are major industries, along with mining of bauxite and ilmenite and a growing chemical and power sector around Lote and Dabhol.

History

The region was ruled successively by the Mauryas, Satavahanas, Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, the Maratha Empire, and the British. Ratnagiri was the birthplace of Lokmanya Bal Gangadhar Tilak and the place of exile of Vinayak Damodar Savarkar and Burma's King Thibaw.
