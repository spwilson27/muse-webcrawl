Carlo Gambino (24 August 1902 – 15 October 1976) was an Italian-American crime boss of the Gambino crime family of New York City. After the 1957 murder of Albert Anastasia, Gambino took control of the family — renamed for him — and became the most powerful figure in the American Mafia, regarded as the "Boss of Bosses" of the Five Families, while maintaining an unusually low public profile.

Life

Born in Palermo, Gambino entered the United States as a stowaway in 1921 and rose through the ranks during Prohibition as a bootlegger, serving under bosses including Salvatore D'Aquila, Al Mineo, Frank Scalice and Vincent Mangano. He survived the Castellammarese War and the purges that followed, and became underboss to Anastasia before allegedly arranging his murder in a Park Avenue barbershop in 1957. As boss he expanded the family's interests in labour racketeering, construction, trucking and loansharking, while shunning narcotics (at least officially) and flamboyant violence.

Death and legacy

Gambino died of a heart attack in 1976, having never served a significant prison sentence, and controversially passed the leadership to his brother-in-law Paul Castellano rather than his underboss Aniello Dellacroce. He is often cited as a partial model for the character Vito Corleone in The Godfather.
