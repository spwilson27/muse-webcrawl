Falstaff is a comic opera in three acts by the Italian composer Giuseppe Verdi. The libretto by Arrigo Boito is adapted from William Shakespeare's play The Merry Wives of Windsor and scenes from Henry IV, Parts 1 and 2. The work premiered on 9 February 1893 at La Scala, Milan, and was Verdi's last opera, completed when he was nearly 80 years old.

Synopsis

The aging, corpulent knight Sir John Falstaff, short of money, sends identical love letters to two wealthy married women, Alice Ford and Quickly, hoping to seduce them. The women compare letters and plot his humiliation: he is induced to hide in a laundry basket and is dumped into the Thames. In a final masquerade in Windsor Great Park, Falstaff is tormented by "fairies" before admitting his folly. The opera closes with a famous fugue, "Tutto nel mondo è burla" — everything in the world is a jest.

Significance

Verdi's second comedy, after the youthful Un giorno di regno, Falstaff is admired for its sparkling orchestration, continuous musical flow, and ensemble writing, eschewing the set-piece arias of earlier Italian opera. It is a staple of the repertoire and is generally regarded as one of the greatest comic operas.
