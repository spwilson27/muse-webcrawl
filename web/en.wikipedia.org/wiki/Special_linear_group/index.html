Special linear group

In mathematics, the special linear group SL(n, F) of degree n over a field F is the set of n × n matrices with determinant 1, with the group operations of ordinary matrix multiplication and matrix inversion. It is the normal subgroup of the general linear group GL(n, F) given by the kernel of the determinant homomorphism.

Elements of SL(2, R) preserve area (and orientation): shears and rotations are typical examples.

Properties

Because the determinant is a surjective homomorphism GL(n, F) → F×, the special linear group is normal, and the quotient is isomorphic to the multiplicative group F×. Over a finite field with q elements, |SL(n, q)| = |GL(n, q)| / (q − 1). SL(n, F) is generated by elementary transvections (shear matrices) except in a few small cases, and it is a perfect group — equal to its own commutator subgroup — except for SL(2, 2) and SL(2, 3).

Projective special linear group

The quotient of SL(n, F) by its centre (the scalar matrices of determinant 1) is the projective special linear group PSL(n, F), one of the four families of finite simple groups of Lie type: with few exceptions, PSL(n, q) is simple. Famous examples include PSL(2, 5) ≅ A5 and PSL(2, 7), a simple group of order 168 acting on the Fano plane and the Klein quartic.

Lie group structure

Over the real and complex numbers, SL(n, R) and SL(n, C) are Lie groups of dimensions n2 − 1 and 2(n2 − 1) respectively; their Lie algebra consists of traceless matrices. SL(2, R) is the universal double cover of the identity component of the Lorentz group SO(2,1) and underlies the theory of modular forms through its discrete subgroups such as the modular group PSL(2, Z). Volume-preserving flows in geometry and dynamics are described by maps into SL(n, R).
