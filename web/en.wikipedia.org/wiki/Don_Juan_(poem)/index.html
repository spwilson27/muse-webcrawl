Don Juan (poem)

Don Juan is a satirical epic poem by Lord Byron, based on the legend of Don Juan. It was published in instalments between 1819 and 1824; Byron died before completing it, leaving sixteen cantos and a fragment of a seventeenth. In Byron's version, Don Juan is not a cruel seducer but a passive young man who is frequently seduced by the women he encounters.

Structure and verse form

The poem is written in ottava rima, an eight-line stanza rhyming ABABABCC, which Byron had used in Beppo. The form allowed him to move rapidly between heroic narrative, comedy, and digressive commentary. The poem runs to more than 16,000 lines.

Plot summary

Juan is born in Seville to Donna Inez and Don José. As a youth he is discovered in an affair with Donna Julia and is sent abroad. His adventures include a shipwreck and love affair with Haidée on a Greek island; capture and sale into slavery in Constantinople; service, in female disguise, in the harem of the Sultana; participation in the Siege of Izmail in the Russo-Turkish war; a stay at the court of Catherine the Great; and a final episode in England as a young diplomat amid aristocratic society.

Publication and reception

The first cantos appeared anonymously in 1819 and were immediately controversial for their satire of contemporary society, politics, and Byron's estranged wife and her circle. Byron's publisher John Murray issued early cantos; later ones were published by John Hunt. Although denounced as immoral by many reviewers, the poem was enormously popular and is now regarded as one of Byron's masterpieces and a landmark of English Romantic poetry.
