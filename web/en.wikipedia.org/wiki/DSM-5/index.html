The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5) is the 2013 update to the American Psychiatric Association's standard classification of mental disorders used by clinicians, researchers, insurers and policymakers in the United States and widely abroad. It provides diagnostic criteria, codes (harmonised with the ICD), and descriptive text for several hundred disorders, organised developmentally across the lifespan. A text revision, the DSM-5-TR, appeared in March 2022.

Development

Development began in 1999–2000 with a research agenda jointly pursued by the APA and the National Institute of Mental Health. Thirteen work groups drafted revisions, and draft criteria were posted publicly for comment in 2010–2012, drawing tens of thousands of responses. Field trials tested the reliability of new criteria in academic medical centres; results were mixed, with some common diagnoses (such as major depressive disorder) showing only modest inter-rater reliability. The APA Board of Trustees approved the final text in December 2012.

Major changes from DSM-IV

  • The multiaxial system (Axes I–V) was eliminated, and the Global Assessment of Functioning scale dropped.
  • Asperger syndrome, autistic disorder and other pervasive developmental disorders were unified into a single diagnosis, autism spectrum disorder.
  • The bereavement exclusion for major depression was removed, one of the most contested changes.
  • New diagnoses included disruptive mood dysregulation disorder, hoarding disorder, binge eating disorder (formerly in an appendix) and premenstrual dysphoric disorder.
  • "Mental retardation" was renamed intellectual disability; "gender identity disorder" became gender dysphoria; dementia and amnestic disorder were folded into major neurocognitive disorder.
  • A dimensional approach was encouraged through cross-cutting symptom measures and an alternative model for personality disorders in Section III.

Organisation

Section I covers use of the manual; Section II contains the diagnostic criteria and codes for 20 disorder chapters (neurodevelopmental, schizophrenia spectrum, bipolar, depressive, anxiety, obsessive-compulsive, trauma- and stressor-related, dissociative, somatic symptom, feeding and eating, sleep-wake, sexual dysfunctions, gender dysphoria, disruptive/impulse-control, substance-related and addictive, neurocognitive, personality, paraphilic, and other disorders); Section III includes emerging measures, cultural formulation, and conditions for further study such as attenuated psychosis syndrome and suicidal behaviour disorder.

Criticism

The DSM-5 attracted criticism for allegedly lowering diagnostic thresholds and medicalising normal distress (the bereavement controversy and grief debates), for the secrecy and conflicts of interest of panel members with pharmaceutical ties, and for weak field-trial reliability for some diagnoses. In 2013 the NIMH under Thomas Insel announced it would reorient research away from DSM categories toward its own Research Domain Criteria (RDoC), arguing the manual's symptom-based categories lack biological validity. Defenders respond that the DSM remains clinically indispensable for communication, treatment planning and reimbursement even if its categories are provisional constructs.
