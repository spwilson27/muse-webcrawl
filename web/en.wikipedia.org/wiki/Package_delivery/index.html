Package delivery or parcel delivery is the delivery of shipping containers, parcels, or high-value mail as a single shipment. The service is provided by most postal systems, express mail, private courier companies, and less-than-truckload shipping carriers. Package delivery differs from freight shipping in that items are generally handled individually, are subject to weight and size limits, and are tracked end-to-end by machine-readable labels and barcodes or RFID.

How it works

A typical parcel moves through a hub-and-spoke network: it is picked up or dropped off, inducted at a local depot, transported to a regional or national sortation hub where automated systems read its label and divert it by destination, carried by truck or air line-haul to the destination hub, sorted again to a delivery route, and carried the "last mile" to the recipient's door. Carriers offer tiers from economy ground service to overnight and same-day delivery, with services such as tracking, delivery confirmation, insurance and returns built in. The last mile is disproportionately expensive — often estimated at half the total delivery cost — and it drives experimentation with parcel lockers, pickup points, gig-economy couriers, delivery vans optimized by route software, and (experimentally) delivery drones and sidewalk robots.

History and industry

Organized parcel post began with postal reforms of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries — the U.S. Parcel Post service launched in 1913 — while private carriers United Parcel Service (founded 1907) and later Federal Express (1973), DHL and others built express networks for businesses. The rise of e-commerce from the 1990s transformed the industry: volumes grew from business documents to billions of consumer parcels annually, with China becoming the largest parcel market by volume. Peak-season surges, warehouse automation, and vertical integration — retailers such as Amazon building their own logistics networks — define the modern industry, alongside debates about labor conditions, vehicle emissions and packaging waste.
