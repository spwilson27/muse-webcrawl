Marbled meat

Marbled meat is meat, especially red meat, that contains various amounts of intramuscular fat, giving it an appearance similar to a marble pattern. The fat appears as fine white flecks or streaks distributed through the lean muscle.

Factors affecting marbling

Marbling is influenced by genetics, breed, feed, and the length of time an animal is fed a high-energy diet. Breeds such as Wagyu (including Kobe beef) and Angus are noted for a greater genetic propensity to deposit intramuscular fat. Grain feeding generally produces more marbling than grass feeding.

Grading

In the United States, the USDA grades beef carcasses in part on the degree of marbling in the ribeye at the twelfth rib; the top grade, Prime, has abundant marbling, followed by Choice and Select. In Japan, the Beef Marble Standard (BMS) scale runs from 1 to 12, with higher numbers indicating more extensive marbling.

Culinary qualities

Marbling is generally considered desirable because the melting fat contributes to flavor, juiciness, and tenderness during cooking. Highly marbled cuts command premium prices, and excessive leanness is associated with drier, tougher meat.
