Saint Petersburg

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Saint Petersburg
Founded: 1703, by Peter the Great
Former names: Petrograd (1914–24); Leningrad (1924–91)
Population: about 5.6 million

Saint Petersburg is Russia's second-largest city and its former imperial capital, founded by Peter the Great in 1703 on the marshy delta of the Neva River at the head of the Gulf of Finland. Conceived as Russia's "window to Europe", it is a planned city of canals, palaces and grand prospects, and its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Built at immense human cost by conscripted labour, the city became the seat of the Russian Empire for two centuries and the stage of its culture — Pushkin, Gogol, Dostoevsky and Tchaikovsky, whose conservatory trained generations. The Hermitage, housed in the Winter Palace, is among the world's greatest art museums, in the company of the Met.

Renamed Petrograd in 1914 and Leningrad in 1924, the city endured the 872-day Siege of Leningrad (1941–44), in which around a million civilians died of starvation and shelling — one of the deadliest sieges in history. It recovered its historic name by referendum in 1991. Today it is a major port, industrial centre and Russia's cultural capital, its white nights in June drawing visitors from around the world.
