The Tomb of Salim Chishti is a mausoleum in the courtyard of the Jama Masjid in Fatehpur Sikri, Uttar Pradesh, India. Built by the Mughal emperor Akbar in 1580–81, it enshrines the burial place of the Sufi saint Salim Chishti (1478–1572), a descendant of Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti of Ajmer. The tomb is considered one of the finest examples of Mughal architecture and a jewel of the Fatehpur Sikri complex, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Akbar, who was without a male heir, visited Sheikh Salim Chishti at Sikri and was blessed with the prophecy of three sons. In gratitude, Akbar built his new capital at Sikri and named his first son Salim (later the emperor Jahangir) after the saint. The tomb was originally built of red sandstone and was later embellished with white marble during the reign of Jahangir.

Architecture

The square tomb stands on a raised platform in the mosque courtyard. Its walls are of finely carved white marble, with perforated stone screens (jalis) of intricate geometric and floral designs. A broad projecting eave (chhajja) supported on serpentine brackets shades the chamber, and the interior walls are inlaid with mother-of-pearl and inscribed with verses from the Quran. The saint's cenotaph is covered by a carved wooden canopy inlaid with mother-of-pearl.

Pilgrimage

The tomb remains an active place of pilgrimage. Devotees, particularly women seeking children, tie threads to the marble screens of the tomb in the belief that their wishes will be granted.
