Free-to-play (F2P or FtP) video games are games that give players access to a significant portion of their content without paying or do not require paying to continue playing. Free-to-play is distinct from traditional commercial software distributed for a price, and from freeware, which is entirely free. The model is closely related to freemium, in which users pay for premium features or to remove limitations.

History

The model emerged from multiplayer online games in South Korea and China in the early 2000s, notably titles published by Nexon, which sold virtual items rather than subscriptions. It spread to Western markets through casual and social games in the late 2000s, and then to mobile games, where it became the dominant revenue model. Major successes include League of Legends (2009), Team Fortress 2 (converted to free-to-play in 2011), Fortnite Battle Royale (2017) and Genshin Impact (2020).

Mechanics and monetisation

Free-to-play games typically monetise through an in-game store selling cosmetic items (skins, emotes), convenience (experience boosts, extra storage), additional content, randomised items such as loot boxes or gacha mechanics, and season or "battle" passes. A small proportion of highly engaged players — colloquially called "whales" — often account for a disproportionate share of revenue, while the large non-paying player base provides matchmaking pools and an audience.

Criticism

The model has been criticised for manipulative design patterns, "pay-to-win" advantages, gambling-like loot boxes (restricted or banned in some jurisdictions), and mechanics designed to frustrate players into paying, sometimes summarised as "pay to skip". Regulators in several countries have examined whether loot boxes constitute gambling and whether children require special protection.
