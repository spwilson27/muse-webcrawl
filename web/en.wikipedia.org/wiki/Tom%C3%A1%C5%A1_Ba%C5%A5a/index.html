Tomáš Baťa

Tomáš BaťaBorn: 3 April 1876, Zlín, Austria-Hungary
Died: 12 July 1932, Otrokovice, Czechoslovakia
Occupation: Founder of the Bata shoe company

Tomáš Baťa (3 April 1876 – 12 July 1932) was a Czech entrepreneur and founder of the Bata shoe company, one of the world's largest footwear manufacturers and retailers. He transformed the Moravian town of Zlín into a company town organised around his factories.

Early life

Baťa was born in Zlín into a family of shoemakers whose craft stretched back generations. He trained as a cobbler and, with his siblings Antonín and Anna, founded the T. & A. Baťa shoe company in 1894.

Growth of the company

Inspired by American industrial methods, Baťa introduced mechanised production, conveyor systems and a system of semi-autonomous workshop units. During the First World War the firm supplied footwear to the Austro-Hungarian army, and in the interwar years it expanded internationally, building factory towns abroad and selling affordable shoes in company stores worldwide.

Death and legacy

Baťa died in an aeroplane crash near Otrokovice in 1932. His half-brother Jan Antonín Baťa succeeded him. The Bata company remains a global footwear business, and Baťa's management ideas and the functionalist architecture of Zlín are widely studied.
