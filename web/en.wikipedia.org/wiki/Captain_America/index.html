Captain America

Captain America (Steve Rogers) is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by cartoonists Joe Simon and Jack Kirby, the character first appeared in Captain America Comics No. 1 (cover dated March 1941) from Timely Comics, Marvel's predecessor. Designed as a patriotic supersoldier who fought the Axis powers during the Second World War, Captain America wears a costume modelled on the American flag and wields a nearly indestructible shield of vibranium-steel alloy, which he throws as a projectile.

Publication history

Captain America was among the most popular Timely characters of the war years, outselling even Superman titles at his peak. After the war his popularity waned and the series ended in 1950. Marvel revived him in The Avengers No. 4 (March 1964), retconning that Rogers had been frozen in ice since 1945 and thawed in the modern era, allowing him to serve as the Avengers' leader. His solo series explored his role as a "man out of time" and addressed contemporary American politics and disillusionment, notably in the 1970s and again in storylines of the 2000s.

Fictional biography

Steve Rogers, a frail young artist rejected by the Army, volunteered for Operation: Rebirth and was transformed by Dr. Abraham Erskine's Super-Soldier Serum and Vita-Rays into the peak of human physical perfection. After Erskine's assassination by a Nazi spy, Rogers fought alongside his sidekick Bucky Barnes until both were lost in an explosion in 1945; Barnes survived as the brainwashed Winter Soldier.

In other media

Chris Evans portrayed Captain America in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, beginning with Captain America: The First Avenger (2011) and continuing through Avengers: Endgame (2019), after which Anthony Mackie's Sam Wilson took up the shield.
