David Dixon Porter (8 June 1813 – 13 February 1891) was a United States Navy admiral and one of the most distinguished naval officers of the American Civil War. The son of Commodore David Porter, he rose from midshipman to become the second officer in US history to hold the rank of full admiral.

Porter commanded Union naval forces on the Mississippi and in the capture of Fort Fisher.

Early career

Porter first went to sea as a boy with his father, serving briefly in the Mexican navy before entering the US Navy as a midshipman in 1829. He saw service in the Mediterranean and in the Mexican–American War, and by 1861 was an experienced commander known for energy and ambition.

Civil War

In 1862 Porter commanded the mortar schooner flotilla that bombarded the forts below New Orleans in support of his foster brother David Farragut's capture of the city. Given command of the Mississippi River Squadron later that year, he cooperated closely with Ulysses S. Grant in the Vicksburg campaign, running his gunboats past the Confederate batteries and ferrying troops across the river. In 1864 he led the naval side of the failed Red River campaign, escaping only with difficulty as river levels fell. In January 1865 his fleet supported the successful second assault on Fort Fisher, North Carolina, closing the Confederacy's last major port.

Later life

After the war Porter served as superintendent of the US Naval Academy, reforming its curriculum, and became the Navy's senior officer, attaining the rank of admiral in 1870. He wrote extensively on naval subjects, including a history of the Navy in the Civil War. He died in Washington in 1891.
