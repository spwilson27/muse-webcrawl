Earthsea is a fictional world created by American author Ursula K. Le Guin, the setting of a series of fantasy books beginning with A Wizard of Earthsea (1968) and two short stories published earlier in the 1960s. Earthsea is an archipelago of hundreds of islands surrounded by an unexplored ocean; there are no large continents. Magic is a central part of life, governed by the knowledge of things' true names in the Old Speech, taught to wizards at the school on the island of Roke.

The books

The first trilogy follows Ged, a proud young wizard who unleashes a shadow creature by reckless spell-casting (A Wizard of Earthsea), frees the priestess Tenar from the dark Tombs of Atuan, and journeys with the young prince Arren to repair a draining of magic from the world (The Farthest Shore). Decades later Le Guin returned to the series with Tehanu (1990), which re-examined the world's gender politics, the story collection Tales from Earthsea (2001), and the closing novel The Other Wind (2001).

Themes

Le Guin drew on Taoism, Jungian psychology, and anthropology. Recurring themes include balance and equilibrium, the power of names and language, coming of age, death, and the danger of denying one's shadow self. Notably for its era, most of Earthsea's inhabitants have brown or copper skin.

Adaptations

The series was adapted, loosely, in the 2004 television miniseries Legend of Earthsea and the 2006 Studio Ghibli film Tales from Earthsea, directed by Gorō Miyazaki; Le Guin was publicly critical of both adaptations.
