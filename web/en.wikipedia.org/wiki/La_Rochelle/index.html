La Rochelle (French: [la ʁɔʃɛl]) is a coastal city and commune in western France and the prefecture of the Charente-Maritime department, on the Bay of Biscay. Founded in the tenth century and chartered in 1199, it grew into one of France's great Atlantic trading ports, dealing in salt, wine and later colonial trade with Canada and the Caribbean.

History

In the sixteenth century La Rochelle became a stronghold of French Protestantism (the Huguenots). After the St. Bartholomew's Day tensions and the city's alliance with England, Cardinal Richelieu besieged it in 1627–28: the famous Siege of La Rochelle, in which a seawall was built to starve the city, ended with its capitulation and the loss of its privileges, a turning point in the consolidation of royal power. The harbour entrance is still guarded by medieval towers — the Tour Saint-Nicolas, Tour de la Chaîne and Tour de la Lanterne.

The modern city

Today the Vieux-Port (Old Port) is lined with cafés and pleasure craft, and the adjoining marina at Les Minimes is one of the largest in Europe. The city is known for its aquarium, its university (founded 1993), the Francofolies music festival (since 1985), and its pioneering urban ecology policies, including one of France's first public bicycle-sharing schemes (1976). A bridge connects the mainland to the nearby Île de Ré.
