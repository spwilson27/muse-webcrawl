Council Tax

[Image: Council Tax]

Council Tax is a local taxation system used in England, Scotland and Wales. It is a tax on domestic property introduced in 1993 by the Local Government Finance Act 1992, replacing the short-lived and unpopular Community Charge (or "poll tax").

How it works

Each dwelling is allocated to one of eight valuation bands (A to H) based on its capital value at 1 April 1991 prices in England and Scotland (2003 in Wales). The local authority sets an annual charge for Band D properties, and the bills for other bands are set as proportions of that amount — from two-thirds for Band A to twice for Band H.

Liability and discounts

The person liable is normally the resident owner or tenant; a hierarchy of liability determines who pays when several people could be responsible. Single occupants receive a 25% discount, and various properties and people — including students and the severely mentally impaired — are exempt or disregarded. Council Tax funds local services such as policing, fire services, refuse collection and libraries, alongside central government grants and business rates.

Criticism

The tax has been criticised for being based on outdated 1991 property values and for being regressive, since the ratio between the highest and lowest bands is capped regardless of actual property values. Successive governments have considered but not implemented revaluation in England.
