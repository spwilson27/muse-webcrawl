Foot Locker, Inc. is an American sportswear and footwear retailer headquartered in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, and operating in 40 countries. It is one of the world's largest retailers of athletic footwear and apparel, selling products from brands such as Nike, Adidas, Jordan, New Balance, and Puma. The company's employees traditionally wear uniforms resembling those of sports referees — black-and-white striped shirts — a trademark look dating to the chain's founding.

History

The company's roots lie in the F. W. Woolworth Company, which in 1963 acquired the Kinney Shoe Corporation. In 1974 Woolworth launched Foot Locker as a specialty athletic shoe store within Kinney; the first store opened at the Puente Hills Mall in City of Industry, California. Foot Locker expanded rapidly through American shopping malls during the running and sneaker booms of the 1980s, and Woolworth added related chains including Lady Foot Locker (1982) and Kids Foot Locker (1987), and acquired Champs Sports in 1984.

As Woolworth's variety stores declined, the company closed its last U.S. Woolworth stores in 1997 and renamed itself Venator Group, then, in 2001, Foot Locker, Inc., reflecting that the athletic business had become the whole company. On its remaining Woolworth legacy, it retained the historic Woolworth Building in New York as its former headquarters before relocating.

Operations

As of the mid-2020s Foot Locker operates roughly 2,500 to 2,900 stores across its banners: the flagship Foot Locker chain, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, the Japanese sneaker boutique chain atmos (acquired in 2021), and WSS (acquired 2021), a footwear retailer serving largely Latino communities on the U.S. West Coast. It also licenses stores in the Middle East and Asia and sells through its websites and apps. Nike has historically accounted for roughly 70 percent or more of the company's merchandise purchases, a dependence Foot Locker has sought to reduce by expanding other brands and its own private labels.

The company has faced the pressures on mall-based retail, the shift to direct-to-consumer sales by brands, and competition from e-commerce. In 2023 it announced a "Lace Up" turnaround plan under CEO Mary Dillon (who took the role in 2022), involving new store formats, a revamped loyalty programme (FLX Rewards), and a reset of its Nike relationship. In 2025, Dick's Sporting Goods agreed to acquire Foot Locker for about $2.4 billion, in a deal announced in May 2025; shareholders and regulators considered the transaction later that year.

Marketing and culture

Foot Locker is a central institution of sneaker culture: its stores host release-day launches of limited-edition shoes, and the chain has partnered with brands and athletes on exclusive colourways. Its annual "Week of Greatness" campaign and the referee-stripe staff uniform are widely recognized brand signatures.
