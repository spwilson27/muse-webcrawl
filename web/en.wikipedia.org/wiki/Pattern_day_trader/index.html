Pattern day trader

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A pattern day trader (PDT) is a regulatory designation in United States securities law, created by rules of the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) and approved by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), for a brokerage customer who executes four or more "day trades" within five business days in a margin account, provided that the number of day trades represents more than six percent of the customer's total trades in the account during that window.

Requirements

Once an account is flagged as a pattern day trading account, the customer must maintain a minimum equity of US$25,000 in the account at the start of any day on which day trading occurs. The requirement, set out in FINRA Rule 4210 (formerly NYSE Rule 431 and NASD Rule 2520), must be met before the customer begins day trading and must be maintained at all times. If the account falls below $25,000, the customer will not be permitted to day trade until the balance is restored; brokers typically freeze day-trading privileges and issue a day-trading minimum equity call.

Pattern day traders are afforded additional buying power: their day-trading buying power is generally four times the maintenance margin excess as of the close of the previous business day (so-called "4x leverage"), rather than the standard 2:1 margin for overnight positions.

Scope and exceptions

The rule applies only to margin accounts at US broker-dealers. Cash accounts are not subject to the PDT designation, although trading with unsettled funds in a cash account can trigger separate "good faith violation" restrictions. Futures and options accounts are treated under different margin regimes, and the rule does not apply in many non-US jurisdictions. Brokers may impose their own stricter definitions of day trading and may designate an account as a pattern day trading account based on a reasonable belief that the customer is a pattern day trader even before the four-trade threshold is reached.

Criticism and reform debate

The $25,000 minimum, introduced in 2001 in response to the day-trading boom of the dot-com era, has been criticised for effectively excluding small retail traders from day trading in margin accounts and for pushing activity into cash accounts, futures or offshore brokers. FINRA has repeatedly studied the rule; in 2024–2025 the regulator advanced proposals to replace the fixed $25,000 threshold with a lower, risk-based intraday margin standard, though the details and timing of reform have been debated among exchanges, brokers and investor advocates.
