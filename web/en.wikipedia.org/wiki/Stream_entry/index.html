Stream entry

Entering the stream of the noble path

Stream entry (Pali: sotāpatti) is the first of the four stages of awakening in Buddhism. A practitioner who has attained stream entry is called a stream-enterer or stream-winner (sotāpanna) and is said to have "entered the stream" that leads inevitably to full enlightenment (arahantship). According to the texts, a stream-enterer will attain liberation within at most seven more rebirths and can no longer be reborn in the lower realms—the hells, the animal realm, or the realm of hungry ghosts.

The three fetters abandoned

Stream entry is defined by the abandonment of the first three of the ten fetters: (1) identity view (sakkāya-diṭṭhi), the belief in a permanent self among the five aggregates; (2) doubt (vicikicchā) in the Buddha, Dhamma and Sangha; and (3) clinging to rites and rituals (sīlabbata-parāmāsa) as ends in themselves.

Qualities and factors

The traditional qualities of a stream-enterer include unshakeable confirmed confidence in the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Sangha, and virtue "dear to the noble ones"—the five precepts kept without breach. The four "factors of stream entry" given in the Pali Canon are association with good people, hearing the true Dhamma, careful attention (yoniso manasikāra), and practice in accordance with the Dhamma.

The four stages

The remaining stages are once-returner (sakadāgāmi), who will be reborn in the human or heavenly world at most once more; non-returner (anāgāmi), who will not return to the sense-sphere world; and arahant, the fully awakened one in whom all ten fetters are destroyed.
