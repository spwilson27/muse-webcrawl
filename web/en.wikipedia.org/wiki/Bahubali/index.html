Bahubali

Bahubali (image placeholder)

Bahubali (literally "one with strong arms"), also called Gommateshwara, is a revered figure in Jainism. He was the son of Rishabhanatha, the first Jain Tirthankara, and the younger brother of Bharata Chakravartin.

Legend

According to Jain tradition, Bahubali fought his elder brother Bharata in a contest over the kingdom, winning three non-violent duels. Rather than accept worldly rule after victory, Bahubali renounced the world, gave the kingdom to Bharata, and meditated standing motionless for a year in the Kayotsarga posture, with vines growing around his legs and anthills rising at his feet. Despite this austerity, he was troubled by lingering pride, but after overcoming it he attained Kevala Jnana (omniscience) and ultimately moksha (liberation).

Statues

Bahubali is famous for the colossal monolithic statues erected in his honour. The most celebrated is the 57-foot (17-metre) statue at Shravanabelagola in Karnataka, carved around 981 CE under the patronage of the Ganga minister Chamundaraya. It is the largest free-standing monolithic statue in the world and the site of the Mahamastakabhisheka festival held every 12 years. Other large statues stand at Karkala, Dharmasthala, and Venur.
