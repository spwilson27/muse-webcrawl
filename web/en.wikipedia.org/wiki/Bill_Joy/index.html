William Nelson Joy (born November 8, 1954) is an American computer engineer and venture capitalist. He co-founded Sun Microsystems in 1982 along with Scott McNealy, Vinod Khosla, and Andy Bechtolsheim, and served as chief scientist and CTO at the company until 2003.

Early work on Unix

While a graduate student at the University of California, Berkeley, Joy was a major contributor to the Berkeley Software Distribution (BSD) of Unix. He wrote the vi text editor and the C shell (csh), and implemented much of the TCP/IP networking stack that shipped in BSD, work recognized by the Grace Murray Hopper Award in 1986.

Sun Microsystems

At Sun, Joy designed the Network File System (NFS) and the SPARC microprocessor architecture, and authored early design papers for the Java programming language. His 2000 Wired essay "Why the Future Doesn't Need Us" warned about the dangers of genetic engineering, nanotechnology and robotics, and drew wide public attention.

Later career

After leaving Sun in 2003, Joy became a partner at the venture capital firm Kleiner Perkins, investing in green technology and later in companies addressing climate and pandemic risk. He has served on defense and technology advisory panels and is a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.
