Pembrolizumab

Trade name: Keytruda
Type: Monoclonal antibody
Target: PD-1

Pembrolizumab, sold under the brand name Keytruda, is a humanized monoclonal antibody used in cancer immunotherapy. It works by blocking the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) receptor on T cells, thereby preventing cancer cells from evading the immune system and allowing the immune system to attack the tumour.

Pembrolizumab was initially approved in the United States in 2014 for the treatment of advanced melanoma. It has since been approved for many other cancers, including non-small cell lung cancer, head and neck cancer, Hodgkin lymphoma, urothelial carcinoma, colorectal cancer, cervical cancer, and triple-negative breast cancer, among others.

The drug is administered by intravenous infusion. Common side effects include fatigue, rash, itching, diarrhoea, and nausea; because it stimulates the immune system, it can also cause immune-related adverse events affecting organs such as the lungs, colon, liver, and endocrine glands.

Pembrolizumab is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines. It was developed by Merck & Co. (MSD outside the United States and Canada).
