Gurdwara

Religion: Sikhism

Meaning: "Door to the Guru"

Features: Darbar Sahib (prayer hall), langar (community kitchen), Nishan Sahib flag

A gurdwara (Punjabi: ਗੁਰਦੁਆਰਾ, "door to the Guru") is a place of assembly and worship for Sikhs. Its central focus is the Guru Granth Sahib, the Sikh holy scripture, which is installed in the main prayer hall (Darbar Sahib) where hymns are sung and scripture is read.

Features

Gurdwaras traditionally have four doors, symbolising openness to all, and fly the Nishan Sahib, a saffron triangular flag. Every gurdwara maintains a langar, a free community kitchen serving vegetarian meals to all visitors regardless of faith or background, embodying the Sikh values of service (seva) and equality. The most revered gurdwara is the Golden Temple (Harmandir Sahib) in Amritsar.
