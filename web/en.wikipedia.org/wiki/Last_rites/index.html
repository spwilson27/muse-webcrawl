Last rites

A priest anointing a dying person.
TraditionRoman Catholic and other Christian churches
ComponentsPenance, Anointing of the Sick, Viaticum

In Catholicism, the last rites, also known as the Commendation of the Dying, are the final prayers and ministrations given to an individual of faith shortly before death. They traditionally comprise the sacraments of Penance (confession), the Anointing of the Sick, and the reception of the Eucharist as Viaticum ("food for the journey"), together with the Apostolic Blessing.

Practice

The rites are intended to prepare the dying person's soul for death, granting forgiveness of sins and spiritual strength. Although popularly associated with the moment of death, the Anointing of the Sick is properly a sacrament for the seriously ill, not reserved to the dying. Similar ministries to the dying — prayers, scripture and commendation — exist in other Christian traditions, though the term "last rites" is most closely associated with Catholic (and some Anglo-Catholic and Orthodox) practice.
