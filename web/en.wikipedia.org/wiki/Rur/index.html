Rur

The Rur (German; in Dutch: Roer) is a major river in Germany, Belgium and the Netherlands, and a right-bank (eastern) tributary of the Meuse (Maas). It rises in the Hautes Fagnes (High Fens) in Belgium, at 660 metres above sea level, and flows for about 165 kilometres through the Eifel hills and the Aachen region before joining the Meuse at Roermond in the Netherlands. It should not be confused with the Ruhr, a much larger tributary of the Rhine further east in Germany.

Course

From its source in the Botrange area of the High Fens, the Rur flows through the German towns of Monschau, Heimbach, Nideggen, Düren, Jülich and Linnich, then crosses the Dutch border and passes through territory near Vlodrop before reaching its confluence with the Meuse at Roermond. In the Eifel the river has been dammed to form a chain of reservoirs, notably the Rurtalsperre Schwammenauel, one of Germany's largest drinking-water reservoirs, together with the Obersee and Eiserbach dams.

History

The river's name is of Celtic origin. In the final phase of the Second World War the lower Rur formed a significant defensive line: in February 1945 the Allied Operation Grenade required crossing the Rur after German forces had released water from the Schwammenauel dams, flooding the valley and delaying the advance toward the Rhine by about two weeks. The hard-fought battles in the Hürtgen Forest took place on the hills west of the upper Rur valley.

Dams and lakes

The Eifel-Rur reservoir system, managed by the Wasserverband Eifel-Rur, provides drinking water for the Aachen–Düren region, flood protection and hydroelectric power. Lakes include the Rursee (Lake Rur), Obersee near Einruhr, and the reservoirs at Heimbach and Obermaubach.
