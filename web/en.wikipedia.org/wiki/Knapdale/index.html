Knapdale forest and Loch Sween

Knapdale (Scottish Gaelic: Cnapadal) is a rural district of Argyll and Bute in the Scottish Highlands, bounded by the Sound of Jura to the west, Loch Fyne to the east, the Kintyre peninsula to the south and the Crinan Canal to the north. Much of it is designated a National Scenic Area, one of forty in Scotland.

Geography and nature

The district is a landscape of low, parallel rocky ridges and lochans, cloaked in commercial forestry and remnants of Atlantic oakwood. Its deeply indented coast is centred on Loch Sween and Loch Caolisport. Knapdale was chosen for the Scottish Beaver Trial (2009–2014), the first licensed reintroduction of a mammal to the United Kingdom, in which families of Eurasian beavers from Norway were released into lochs in the Knapdale forest; the trial succeeded and beavers were later granted protected status in Scotland. The area also supports otters, red squirrels, sea eagles and important populations of lichens and bryophytes in its temperate rainforest fragments.

History

Knapdale was part of the Gaelic kingdom of Dál Riata and later a lordship contested by the MacMillans, Campbells and Lords of the Isles; Castle Sween, among Scotland's earliest stone castles, guards Loch Sween. The carved stones and chapel at Kilmory Knap are notable medieval remains. In the 19th century the Crinan Canal, opened in 1801, cut across Knapdale's northern edge, linking Loch Fyne with the Sound of Jura at the fishing village of Ardrishaig.
