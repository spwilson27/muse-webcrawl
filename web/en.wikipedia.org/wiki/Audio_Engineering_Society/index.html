Audio Engineering Society

The Audio Engineering Society (AES) is a professional organisation for engineers, scientists and others with an interest in the audio industry, founded in 1948 in the United States. It is the only professional society devoted exclusively to audio technology, and is best known for its technical standards, its peer-reviewed Journal of the Audio Engineering Society, and its international conventions and conferences.

History

The AES was founded in 1948, with its first convention held in New York in 1949. From the outset it provided a forum for the rapidly developing fields of disc recording, magnetic tape and broadcasting. It grew into an international body with sections in dozens of countries and a membership drawn from recording, broadcast, live sound, acoustics, consumer electronics and audio research.

Standards

The AES publishes widely used technical standards for professional audio, most famously AES3, the serial digital audio interface standard commonly known as AES/EBU, as well as standards for audio networking (such as AES67 for high-performance audio-over-IP interoperability), loudspeaker measurement, digital audio synchronisation and file interchange.

Publications and events

The Society's journal has been published since 1953 and is a principal archival record of audio research. Its conventions — historically held twice yearly, in North America and Europe — combine technical paper sessions, workshops and a trade exhibition, and its specialist conferences cover topics such as spatial audio, automotive audio, game audio and audio forensics.
