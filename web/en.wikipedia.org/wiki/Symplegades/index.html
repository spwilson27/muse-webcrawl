The Symplegades (Greek: Συμπληγάδες), also known as the Clashing Rocks or Cyanean Rocks, are a pair of mythical rocks at the entrance of the Euxine Sea (the Black Sea) which, according to Greek legend, clashed together randomly, crushing any ship that attempted to pass between them.

The Argonauts

The most famous account comes from the voyage of Jason and the Argonauts, told in Apollonius of Rhodes' Argonautica. On the advice of the blind seer Phineus, the Argonauts first released a dove between the rocks; the rocks clashed and clipped the dove's tail feathers, and as they recoiled the ship Argo rowed through at full speed, losing only part of its stern ornament. Thereafter the rocks stood rooted in place forever, as it had been decreed that if a ship passed through them they would clash no more.

Related legends

Homer mentions similar wandering rocks, the Planctae ("Wanderers"), in the Odyssey: Circe warns Odysseus that only the Argo has ever passed them alive, and he chooses the route past Scylla and Charybdis instead. Ancient and modern writers have associated the Symplegades with the entrance to the Bosphorus, where currents and rocky islets made the passage genuinely dangerous; some scholars treat the myth as an exaggerated memory of those hazards.
