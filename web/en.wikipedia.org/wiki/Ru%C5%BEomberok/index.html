Ružomberok (Hungarian: Rózsahegy; German: Rosenberg) is a town in northern Slovakia, in the Žilina Region at the confluence of the Váh and Revúca rivers, surrounded by the Veľká Fatra, Choč Mountains and Low Tatras. It has around 27,000 inhabitants.

History

First mentioned in 1233, the town grew wealthy on trade routes between Poland and Hungary and became a centre of crafts and, later, of the paper and textile industries. Ružomberok was an important centre of the Slovak national movement; the priest and politician Andrej Hlinka, leader of the Slovak People's Party, was born in nearby Černová and served as the town's parish priest.

Sights and economy

Nearby Vlkolínec, a remarkably preserved village of wooden folk architecture, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The surrounding mountains support skiing at Malinô Brdo and hiking in the Veľká Fatra National Park. The town's economy is dominated by the Mondi SCP paper mill, one of the largest in Central Europe, and by tourism.
