Rare Breeds Survival Trust

The Rare Breeds Survival Trust (RBST) is a conservation charity whose purpose is to secure the future of rare breeds of farm livestock in the United Kingdom. It was founded in 1973 by Joe Henson, and its headquarters are at Stoneleigh Park in Warwickshire.

The trust maintains a watchlist of rare and native breeds of cattle, sheep, pigs, goats, horses and poultry, categorising them by rarity according to the number of breeding females. It works with breed societies, farmers and gene banks to conserve genetic diversity in British livestock.

Activities

RBST accredits farm parks that keep and breed rare breeds, supports conservation grazing projects, and operates a gene bank storing frozen semen and embryos from rare breeds as insurance against their extinction. Its patron is the King, who as Prince of Wales was a long-standing supporter of rare breed conservation on his own farms.

History

The trust was established after concern grew in the post-war decades that traditional British livestock breeds were being lost as farming intensified and a small number of commercial breeds came to dominate. Since its foundation, no British native breed has become extinct, an achievement often credited in part to the RBST's work.
