Pysanka

A pysanka (Ukrainian: писанка; plural pysanky) is a Ukrainian Easter egg, decorated with traditional folk designs using a wax-resist (batik) method. The word comes from the verb pysaty, "to write" or "to inscribe", since the designs are written onto the egg with beeswax rather than painted on.

Technique

The artist uses a stylus called a kistka to apply hot beeswax to a raw (or emptied) egg, then dips the egg in a series of dyes from lightest to darkest, adding wax at each stage to preserve the colours already laid down. When the final, darkest dye bath is complete, the wax is melted off by holding the egg beside a candle flame, revealing the layered design. Traditional dyes were made from natural materials — onion skins for yellow, for example — though aniline dyes are now common.

Symbolism

Pysanka motifs carry symbolic meanings inherited from pre-Christian Slavic tradition and later Christian interpretation: suns and stars for life and good fortune, deer and horses for wealth and prosperity, flowers for love and beauty, endless lines (meanders) for eternity, and triangles variously read as the Holy Trinity or the elements. Colours are likewise symbolic — red for life and joy, black for remembrance and the earth, yellow for harvest and light.

History and spread

Decorated eggs in the region date to prehistoric times; ceramic pysanky have been found in medieval contexts, including a well-preserved example discovered in Lviv in 2013. The craft was suppressed in Soviet Ukraine but survived and was carried abroad by the Ukrainian diaspora, especially to Canada and the United States, where giant pysanka monuments stand at Vegreville, Alberta, and elsewhere. Interest in pysankarstvo revived strongly after Ukraine's independence in 1991.
