Artur Schnabel (7 April 1882 – 15 August 1951) was an Austrian-born classical pianist, composer, and teacher, widely regarded as one of the greatest pianists of the twentieth century. He is especially celebrated for his performances and recordings of the works of Beethoven and Schubert, composers whose piano music had been comparatively neglected as virtuoso fare.

Life

Born in Kunzendorf, then in Austrian Silesia, Schnabel gave his first concert at the age of seven and studied in Vienna with Theodor Leschetizky, the era's most celebrated piano teacher, from 1891 to 1897. He settled in Berlin in 1900, building a career across Europe and forming notable chamber partnerships, including with the violinist Bronisław Huberman and the cellist Pablo Casals. From 1925 to 1933 he taught masterclasses at the Berlin Hochschule für Musik.

Being Jewish, Schnabel left Germany when the Nazis came to power in 1933. He lived in England and Italy before emigrating to the United States in 1939, becoming an American citizen in 1944. After the war he resumed touring in Europe but never again performed in Germany or Austria. He died in Switzerland in 1951.

Recordings

Between 1932 and 1937, Schnabel recorded the complete cycle of Beethoven's 32 piano sonatas for His Master's Voice (EMI) — the first complete recording of the set — together with the five piano concertos. His recordings of Schubert's late sonatas, made when much of that music was little known, helped establish them in the repertoire. Although Schnabel disliked recording and famously described himself as a musician for whom the gramophone could never capture the essential experience, the Beethoven sonata cycle remains in print and is considered a landmark of recorded music.

Teaching and compositions

Schnabel's pupils included Leon Fleisher, Clifford Curzon, and Rudolf Firkušný; his son Karl Ulrich Schnabel was also a pianist. He also composed throughout his life — symphonies, chamber music, and songs — in a modernist idiom quite unlike his classical performing repertoire, though these works were long neglected. His book My Life and Music (1961) was published posthumously.
