Christianity in Egypt

Christianity in Egypt dates to the apostolic age; tradition holds that Saint Mark brought the faith to Alexandria in the 1st century. Egypt's Christians, mostly Coptic Orthodox, form the largest Christian community in the Middle East, commonly estimated at around 5–15% of Egypt's population.

Christianity in Egypt
Largest church: Coptic Orthodox Church
Founder (tradition): Saint Mark
Centres: Alexandria, Cairo

History

Alexandria became a leading centre of theology (Catechetical School, Origen, Athanasius) and monasticism began in the Egyptian desert with Anthony the Great and Pachomius. After the Council of Chalcedon (451), the Coptic Church separated from the imperial church. Christianity remained the majority faith for centuries after the Muslim conquest of 641, gradually becoming a minority through conversion and Arabisation.

Churches today

Besides the Coptic Orthodox Church (led by the Pope of Alexandria), there are Coptic Catholic, Evangelical (Presbyterian), Greek Orthodox, and other communities.
