Brauer–Manin obstruction

[Image placeholder: Brauer–Manin obstruction]

In number theory, the Brauer–Manin obstruction, named after Richard Brauer and Yuri Manin, is a phenomenon in which a variety defined over a number field may have points in every completion of the field (satisfying the local conditions) yet possess no rational point, the obstruction lying in the Brauer group.

Exposition

For a variety X over a number field k, the Brauer–Manin set is the subset of the adelic points of X orthogonal to the Brauer group Br(X) under the Brauer–Manin pairing. Rational points must lie in this set; if the adelic points are non-empty but the Brauer–Manin set is empty, the Hasse principle fails for X, as Manin showed using examples such as certain Châtelet surfaces and (earlier, ad hoc) the Lind–Reichardt curve. Whether the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to the Hasse principle is a major open question for broad classes of varieties, resolved affirmatively for some families and refuted for others.
