Deadpan

Deadpan, also called dry humour or dry wit, is a form of comic delivery in which jokes are presented with a deliberately emotionless, matter-of-fact expression and tone, as though the speaker does not realise anything funny has been said. The contrast between the absurdity of the material and the flatness of the delivery is the source of much of the comedy.

History and practitioners

The word "deadpan" (from dead + pan, slang for face) emerged in American theatre and film slang in the 1920s. Buster Keaton built his screen persona on a stoic, unsmiling expression in the face of catastrophe. Later masters include Jack Benny, Bob Newhart, Steven Wright, Mitch Hedberg, and the British tradition of dry wit exemplified by figures from P. G. Wodehouse's Jeeves to Rowan Atkinson's Blackadder (in his more sardonic register). Deadpan is a hallmark of mockumentaries and of performers such as Aubrey Plaza, and the Finnish director Aki Kaurismäki has made deadpan acting a signature of his films.

Technique

Deadpan delivery typically avoids smiling, changes in pitch, or signalling a punchline, and often involves understatement. Because the audience must recognise the joke unaided, deadpan is risky in live performance but can produce an unusually strong response when it lands. It is closely related to irony and to the "straight man" role in double acts.
