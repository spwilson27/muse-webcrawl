Pukehinahina — Gate Pā, 1864.

The Battle of Gate Pā (Māori: Pukehinahina) was fought on 29 April 1864 near Tauranga during the New Zealand Wars. A force of about 230 Māori warriors of Ngāi Te Rangi and their allies, fighting from a purpose-built fortified pā designed by Pene Taka Tuaia, repulsed an assault by a much larger British force under Lieutenant-General Duncan Cameron. It is remembered as one of the most notable Māori victories of the wars.

Background

After the invasion of the Waikato, Tauranga Māori who had supported the Kīngitanga were targeted by a British expedition. The defenders constructed Gate Pā on the Pukehinahina ridge, with deep trenches, covered bunkers and concealed firing positions designed to withstand artillery.

Battle

Cameron's guns — including heavy Armstrong artillery — bombarded the pā for some ten hours, the heaviest bombardment of the New Zealand Wars, breaching the palisade. A storming party of about 300 men entered the pā in the afternoon, only to be met by devastating fire from hidden trenches; the survivors fled, leaving over 100 casualties, including many officers. During the night the defenders evacuated the pā. The conduct of Hēnare Taratoa, who is credited with drafting a code of conduct for the battle and with giving water to wounded British officer Colonel Booth, became legendary.

Aftermath

British casualties were about 35 killed and 80 wounded; Māori losses were around 25–30 killed. The defeat shocked colonial opinion. Two months later the British gained a victory at Te Ranga, where Rāwiri Puhirake was killed.
