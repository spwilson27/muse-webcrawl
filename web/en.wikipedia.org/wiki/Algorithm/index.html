Algorithm

[Image: a flowchart on paper — boxes and arrows stepping through a sorting procedure, with a pencil beside it]

In mathematics and Computer science, an algorithm is a finite sequence of precise instructions, typically used to solve a class of specific problems or to perform a computation. Algorithms are used as specifications for performing calculations, data processing, automated decision-making and much else; they range from the long-division procedure learned at school to the ranking systems that order search results and social-media feeds.

The word derives from the Latinised name of the ninth-century Persian mathematician Al-Khwarizmi (Algoritmi), whose works introduced systematic computational procedures to the West.

Properties and analysis

Algorithms are studied by their correctness — whether they produce the required result for all inputs — and their efficiency: the time and memory they consume as input grows, expressed in big-O notation. The theory of computational complexity, with its classes such as P and NP, classifies problems by the algorithms available for them.

Foundational algorithms include Euclid's algorithm for greatest common divisors (from his Elements, c. 300 BC) — often called the oldest non-trivial algorithm — quicksort and mergesort for ordering, Dijkstra's algorithm for shortest paths, and the Fourier transform family underlying signal processing.

Algorithms in society

As software came to mediate commerce, media and public services, 'the algorithm' entered everyday language, usually meaning opaque recommendation and ranking systems. Debates about algorithmic bias, accountability and transparency are now central to technology policy.
