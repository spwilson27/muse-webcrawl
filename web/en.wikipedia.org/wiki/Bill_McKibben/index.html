William Ernest McKibben (born December 8, 1960) is an American environmentalist, author, and journalist who has written extensively on the impact of global warming. He is the Schumann Distinguished Scholar at Middlebury College and leader of the climate campaign group 350.org. He has written a dozen books about the environment, including his first, The End of Nature (1989), about climate change.

The End of Nature is regarded as the first book for a general audience about climate change, and has been published in 24 languages. McKibben's subsequent books include The Age of Missing Information, Enough, Deep Economy, Oil and Honey, and Falter. He is a frequent contributor to various magazines including The New Yorker, The New York Review of Books, and Rolling Stone.

Early life and education

McKibben was born in Palo Alto, California, and grew up in Lexington, Massachusetts. He attended Harvard University, where he was president of The Harvard Crimson newspaper. After graduating in 1982, he joined The New Yorker as a staff writer, writing the "Talk of the Town" column from 1982 to 1987.

Environmental activism

In 2007, McKibben co-founded 350.org, an international climate movement named for the 350 parts per million of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere that scientists identified as a safe upper limit. The organisation has coordinated thousands of demonstrations across the globe. McKibben was a leading opponent of the Keystone XL pipeline and a prominent advocate of fossil fuel divestment. In 2021 he founded Third Act, an organisation mobilising Americans over sixty for action on climate and democracy.

Honours

McKibben has received numerous awards, including the Gandhi Peace Award in 2013 and the Right Livelihood Award in 2014. Foreign Policy magazine named him to its inaugural list of the 100 most important global thinkers.

Personal life

McKibben lives in Vermont with his wife, the writer Sue Halpern. He is a Methodist and a Sunday school teacher, and has written about the intersection of faith and environmental stewardship.
