Cryphonectria parasitica

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Fungi
Phylum: Ascomycota
Order: Diaporthales
Family: Cryphonectriaceae
Genus: Cryphonectria
Species: C. parasitica

Cryphonectria parasitica is a parasitic fungus, formerly known as Endothia parasitica, that causes chestnut blight. Native to East Asia, where local chestnut species are resistant to it, the fungus was introduced to North America and Europe with devastating effects.

Chestnut blight

The fungus infects the bark of chestnut trees through wounds, producing cankers that girdle branches and trunks, killing the tree above the canker. First noticed in 1904 at the Bronx Zoo in New York, the blight spread through the range of the American chestnut (Castanea dentata) within decades, killing an estimated four billion trees and transforming eastern North American forests. In Europe it attacked the European chestnut (Castanea sativa) from 1938 onward, though with less severity.

Hypovirulence

In Europe, many fungal strains are infected with a virus (Cryphonectria hypovirus 1) that reduces their virulence, a phenomenon called hypovirulence; infected cankers heal and trees survive. Hypovirulence has been exploited as a biological control, though its spread in North America has been limited by the diversity of fungal vegetative compatibility groups.
