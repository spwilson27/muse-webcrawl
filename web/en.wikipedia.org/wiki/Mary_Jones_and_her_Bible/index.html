Mary Jones and her Bible

Mary Jones and her Bible is the story of Mary Jones (16 December 1784 – 28 December 1864), a Welsh girl from Llanfihangel-y-Pennant, who at the age of fifteen walked barefoot some 26 miles (42 km) across the countryside to Bala in order to buy a copy of the Welsh Bible from Thomas Charles, a prominent Methodist minister and member of the Religious Tract Society. The story became famous in Britain and beyond and is often credited with inspiring the foundation of the British and Foreign Bible Society in 1804.

Background

Mary Jones was born at Llanfihangel-y-Pennant, near Dolgellau in Merionethshire, Wales, the daughter of Jacob Jones, a weaver, and his wife Molly. She became a Christian at the age of eight after attending a Sunday school run by the Calvinistic Methodists. She learned to read at a school opened in the village by Thomas Charles, and became determined to own a Bible of her own. Welsh Bibles were scarce and expensive at the time, and Jones saved money for six years — from selling eggs, knitting, and doing small jobs — until she had enough to buy one.

The journey

In 1800, learning that Thomas Charles in Bala had Bibles for sale, the fifteen-year-old Mary walked the whole distance to Bala — some 26 miles over rough mountain terrain, reportedly barefoot — only to find that Charles had sold all his copies except a few reserved for others. After hearing her story and her plea, Charles sold her one of the reserved copies, and, deeply moved, gave her additional Bibles. The incident convinced Charles of the desperate need for cheap Bibles in Wales and elsewhere.

Legacy

Thomas Charles recounted Mary's story to the committee of the Religious Tract Society in London. The story is traditionally credited with directly prompting the founding of the British and Foreign Bible Society on 7 March 1804, which in turn inspired the formation of Bible societies around the world. Mary Jones herself married a weaver, Thomas Lewis, and lived the rest of her life in the area, dying in 1864. Her Bible is preserved and her grave at Llanfihangel-y-Pennant remains a place of pilgrimage. In 2020 the Bible Society opened a visitor centre at Bala called Byd Mary Jones (Mary Jones World).
