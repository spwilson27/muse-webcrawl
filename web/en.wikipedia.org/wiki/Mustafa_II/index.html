Mustafa II

Mustafa II (Ottoman Turkish: مصطفى ثانى, Muṣṭafā-yi sānī; 6 February 1664 – 29/30 December 1703) was the sultan of the Ottoman Empire from 1695 to 1703. His reign saw the disastrous conclusion of the Great Turkish War: the Ottoman defeat at Zenta (1697) forced the empire to sign the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699), its first major cession of territory in Europe.

Early life

Mustafa was born at Edirne Palace, the son of Sultan Mehmed IV and Emetullah Rabia Gülnuş Sultan, who became a powerful Valide Sultan during his reign. Unlike many later princes he received a good education and spent part of his father's reign at Edirne. He succeeded his uncle Ahmed II in February 1695.

Reign

Determined to recover territory lost to the Holy League, Mustafa personally led campaigns in 1695 and 1696, retaking Chios and defeating Habsburg and Venetian forces on several occasions, while Ottoman armies also campaigned against Russia, which captured Azov in 1696. The turning point came on 11 September 1697, when Prince Eugene of Savoy destroyed the Ottoman army as it crossed the Tisza river at the Battle of Zenta; the Grand Vizier Elmas Mehmed Pasha was killed and the sultan's treasury and seals were captured.

The defeat compelled the Ottomans to accept mediation, and on 26 January 1699 they signed the Treaty of Karlowitz, ceding Hungary and Transylvania to Austria, Podolia to Poland, and the Morea and parts of Dalmatia to Venice; a separate treaty with Russia followed in 1700. Karlowitz is generally regarded as the beginning of Ottoman military decline in Europe.

Deposition and death

Mustafa spent increasing time at Edirne and relied heavily on his tutor, the Şeyhülislam Feyzullah Efendi, whose nepotism and the sultan's attempt to curb the Janissaries provoked the revolt known as the Edirne Event in 1703. The rebels marched on the capital, and Mustafa was deposed on 22 August 1703 in favour of his brother Ahmed III. He died in Istanbul a few months later, in December 1703. Two of his sons, Mahmud I and Osman III, later became sultans.
