Public Citizen is an American nonprofit, progressive consumer rights advocacy group and think tank based in Washington, D.C. It was founded in 1971 by activist and lawyer Ralph Nader. Public Citizen advocates before the three branches of the United States federal government on issues including consumer protection, product safety, health care, energy policy, government ethics, campaign finance, and corporate accountability.

History and structure

Nader founded Public Citizen as an umbrella for his public-interest work; its early campaigns helped secure the recall of unsafe products and strengthened federal safety regulation. The organisation includes divisions such as the Health Research Group (long led by Sidney Wolfe), the Litigation Group, which argues public-interest cases before appellate courts including the Supreme Court, the Congress Watch lobbying arm, and the Energy Program. Public Citizen does not accept funding from corporations or governments, relying on individual members and foundations.

Campaigns

Public Citizen has campaigned against corporate influence in politics, for single-payer health care ("Medicare for All"), for stronger regulation of prescription drugs through its Worst Pills, Best Pills newsletter, and against trade agreements it views as harmful to consumers and workers. Its Litigation Group helped establish important precedents in administrative and constitutional law, including litigation over government secrecy and agency rulemaking.
