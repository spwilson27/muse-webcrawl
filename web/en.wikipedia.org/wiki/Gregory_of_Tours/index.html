Gregory of Tours

Gregory of Tours (30 November c. 538 – 17 November 594) was a Gallo-Roman historian and Bishop of Tours, during which period he was a leading prelate of the area now comprising France. Born Georgius Florentius, he took the name Gregory in honour of his maternal great-grandfather, Gregory of Langres. His Decem Libri Historiarum ("Ten Books of History"), better known as the History of the Franks, is the principal contemporary source for Merovingian history.

Life

Gregory was born in Clermont to a senatorial family with long ties to the episcopate. After his father's death he was raised by his uncle Gallus, Bishop of Clermont, and later by Avitus of Clermont. Following a pilgrimage to the tomb of Saint Martin of Tours, where he was cured of an illness, he was ordained deacon. In 573 he succeeded his cousin Eufronius as Bishop of Tours, a post he held for twenty-one years, frequently clashing with Merovingian kings such as Chilperic I, whom he called "the Nero and Herod of our time."

Works

The History of the Franks begins with the Creation and proceeds through Frankish history to Gregory's own day; its vivid accounts of Clovis's conversion, the feuds of Fredegund and Brunhilda, and Merovingian court intrigue make it indispensable to historians. Gregory also wrote extensive hagiography, including the Glory of the Martyrs, Glory of the Confessors, and the Life of the Fathers, promoting the cult of the saints, especially Saint Martin.

Reputation

Writing in a Latin far removed from classical standards — which he acknowledged and apologized for — Gregory remains a lively, well-connected witness to the transition from Roman Gaul to the Frankish kingdoms. He is venerated as a saint, with a feast day of 17 November.
