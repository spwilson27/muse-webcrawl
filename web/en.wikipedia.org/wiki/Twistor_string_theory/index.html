Twistor string theory

Twistor string theory is an equivalence between N = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory and a perturbative topological string theory in twistor space, introduced by Edward Witten in 2003. It reformulates scattering amplitudes in gauge theory as being supported on curves in the projective twistor space CP3|4.

Background

Twistor theory, developed by Roger Penrose, encodes spacetime points as complex lines in twistor space. Witten showed that a topological B-model string on supertwistor space reproduces the tree-level maximally helicity violating (MHV) amplitudes of Yang–Mills theory, which in twistor space localise on degree-one curves (lines), explaining the MHV diagram (Cachazo–Svrček–Witten) method for computing amplitudes.

Impact

Although twistor string theory did not by itself yield a complete theory of quantum gravity, it catalysed major advances in the study of scattering amplitudes, including the amplituhedron programme and modern on-shell methods.
