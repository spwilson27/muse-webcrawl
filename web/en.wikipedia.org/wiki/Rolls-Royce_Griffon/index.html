Rolls-Royce Griffon

The Rolls-Royce Griffon is a British 37-litre (2,240 cu in) capacity, 60-degree V-12, liquid-cooled aero engine designed and built by Rolls-Royce Limited. In keeping with company convention, it was named after a bird of prey, the griffon vulture. The Griffon was the last in the line of V-12 aero engines to be produced by Rolls-Royce, with production ceasing in 1955.

Design and development

Derived from the Merlin but with a larger displacement of 36.7 litres, the Griffon produced up to about 2,350 hp in late marks with a two-stage, two-speed supercharger. Unlike the Merlin, the Griffon rotated its propeller in the opposite direction and drove accessories from the front of the engine.

Applications

The Griffon powered late marks of the Supermarine Spitfire (from the Mk XII onward) and the Seafire, the Fairey Firefly, and, in postwar service, the Avro Shackleton maritime patrol aircraft, whose Griffons drove contra-rotating propellers. Griffon engines also enjoyed a long afterlife in unlimited hydroplane racing and tractor pulling.
