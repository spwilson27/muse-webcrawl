The Grenadier Guards (GREN GDS) is the most senior infantry regiment of the British Army, being at the top of the Infantry Order of Precedence. It is one of the five regiments of Foot Guards in the Household Division.

History

The regiment was raised in 1656 by Lord Wentworth in Bruges, in the Spanish Netherlands, as the Guards Regiment of the exiled Charles II. It was renamed the First Regiment of Foot Guards in 1665 and adopted the name Grenadier Guards in 1815 after defeating the grenadiers of the French Imperial Guard at the Battle of Waterloo.

The Grenadier Guards have served in nearly every major conflict involving the British Army, including the American War of Independence, the Crimean War, both World Wars, and more recent operations in Iraq and Afghanistan. The regiment fought at Oudenaarde, Dettingen, Inkerman, Tel el-Kebir, and in the trenches of the Western Front.

Role and traditions

Today the regiment alternates between operational light infantry duties and ceremonial public duties in London and Windsor, including mounting the King's Guard and participating in Trooping the Colour. Grenadiers wear a red tunic and tall bearskin cap, with a white plume on the left side of the bearskin — a distinction associated with their position of honour on the right of the line.

The regimental quick march is "The British Grenadiers" and the slow march is "Scipio". The monarch serves as Colonel-in-Chief of the regiment.
