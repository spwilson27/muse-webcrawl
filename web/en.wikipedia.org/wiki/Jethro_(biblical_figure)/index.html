Jethro (biblical figure)

Jethro (Hebrew: יִתְרוֹ, Yitro) is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, described as a priest of Midian and the father-in-law of Moses. In Exodus 2 he first appears under the name Reuel (Raguel); biblical scholars and traditional commentators have long debated whether Reuel is a personal name, a clan name, or an alternative tradition. Jethro is called "priest of Midian" and is also associated with the Kenites.

Narrative

After Moses killed an Egyptian overseer and fled Pharaoh, he came to Midian, where he defended Jethro's seven daughters at a well and was taken into the household, marrying Jethro's daughter Zipporah. Moses tended Jethro's flocks when he encountered the burning bush at Mount Horeb.

Following the Exodus from Egypt, Jethro met Moses in the wilderness (Exodus 18), bringing Zipporah and Moses' sons with him. On hearing what God had done for Israel, Jethro offered sacrifices and confessed the greatness of the Lord. The next day, seeing Moses judging the people's disputes single-handedly from morning to evening, Jethro gave him the Bible's classic advice on delegation: appoint capable, trustworthy men as rulers of thousands, hundreds, fifties and tens, reserving only the hardest cases for Moses himself. Moses accepted the counsel, and Jethro returned to his own land.

Traditions

In Jewish tradition Jethro is honoured as a righteous gentile and the Torah portion Yitro, which contains the giving of the Ten Commandments, is named after him. In Islam he is commonly identified with the prophet Shuayb, sent to the people of Midian. The Druze revere Jethro (Nabi Shu'ayb) as their chief prophet, and his tomb near Hittin in Galilee is their holiest pilgrimage site.
