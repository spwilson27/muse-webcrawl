Superclub

Superclubs are very large nightclub venues, often holding several thousand people.

A superclub is a very large or superior-quality nightclub, often with several rooms and a capacity in the thousands. Many superclubs are internationally known and attract leading DJs and clubbers from around the world. The term is strongly associated with the dance-music culture of Ibiza and with large British venues of the 1990s.

Characteristics

Superclubs typically feature multiple arenas playing different styles of electronic dance music, elaborate lighting and sound installations, and residencies by famous DJs. They often operate as global brands, selling branded merchandise and compilation albums and staging tours and satellite events in other cities.

Notable examples

Famous superclubs include Amnesia, Pacha, Privilege and Ushuaïa on Ibiza — Privilege was for many years recognized as the world's largest club, with a capacity of around 10,000 — as well as Ministry of Sound and Fabric in London, Berghain in Berlin, and venues such as Space and DC-10. In the 1990s, British brands such as Cream (Liverpool) and Gatecrasher (Sheffield) were central to the superclub phenomenon.

Cultural impact

The superclub era reshaped dance music into a global entertainment industry, elevating DJs to celebrity status and turning clubbing destinations such as Ibiza into major tourist attractions.
