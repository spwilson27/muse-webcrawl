Jack Edmonds

Jack R. Edmonds (born April 5, 1934) is an American-born and educated computer scientist and mathematician who lived and worked in Canada for much of his career. He is one of the founders of combinatorial optimisation.

Contributions

Edmonds gave the first polynomial-time algorithm for matching on general graphs — the "blossom algorithm" — in 1965, and helped characterise P versus NP through the Cobham–Edmonds thesis, the idea that polynomial time captures feasible computation. He also made fundamental contributions to matroid theory, including the matroid intersection theorem and the Edmonds matrix.

Career

He worked at the US National Bureau of Standards and later for many years at the University of Waterloo in Ontario.
