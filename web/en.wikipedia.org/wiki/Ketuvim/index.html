The Ketuvim (Hebrew: כְּתוּבִים, "Writings") is the third and final section of the Tanakh, the Hebrew Bible, following the Torah ("Teaching") and the Nevi'im ("Prophets"). In English translations it is usually called the Writings or Hagiographa.

Contents

In the Masoretic tradition the Ketuvim comprises eleven books:

  • Poetic books: Psalms (Tehillim), Proverbs (Mishlei), and Job (Iyov) — these three use a special system of cantillation accents.
  • The Five Scrolls (Hamesh Megillot), read at festivals: Song of Songs (Passover), Ruth (Shavuot), Lamentations (Tisha B'Av), Ecclesiastes (Sukkot), and Esther (Purim).
  • Historical and other books: Daniel, Ezra–Nehemiah (counted as one book), and Chronicles.

Canonisation

The order and even the contents of the Ketuvim varied in antiquity: in the Septuagint the books are dispersed among poetic and historical sections, and in the Talmud (Bava Batra 14b) a slightly different order appears, with Ruth before Psalms and Lamentations before Ecclesiastes. Most scholars hold that the Ketuvim was the last part of the canon to be fixed, reaching its final form by about the 2nd century CE, although many of its books are much older and some — such as Daniel — are among the youngest in the Bible, dating to the Hellenistic period.

Liturgical use

Beyond the festival readings of the Five Scrolls, the Psalms form a central part of Jewish daily prayer, and verses from across the Ketuvim pervade the liturgy.
