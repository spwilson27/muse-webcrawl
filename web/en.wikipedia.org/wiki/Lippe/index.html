Lippe is a district and a historic territory in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. Its capital is Detmold. For more than 800 years Lippe was a state of the Holy Roman Empire and later of the German Confederation and German Empire — successively a lordship, a county (from 1528/1529) and a principality (from 1789), and after 1918 a free state of the Weimar Republic — before being incorporated into North Rhine-Westphalia in 1947.

History

The House of Lippe traces its origins to Bernhard I, documented in 1123. The counts acquired towns including Lemgo and Detmold, which became the residence. Count Simon VI converted the county to Calvinism in the early 17th century, making Lippe one of few Reformed territories in the Empire. The last prince, Leopold IV, abdicated in November 1918. British occupation authorities dissolved the state in 1947; its symbol, the red Lippe rose (Lippische Rose) on gold, survives in the arms of North Rhine-Westphalia.

Geography

The district lies between the Teutoburg Forest to the west and the river Weser to the east. The Hermannsdenkmal (Hermann monument) near Detmold, commemorating Arminius's victory over Rome in the Teutoburg Forest, stands on former Lippe soil.
