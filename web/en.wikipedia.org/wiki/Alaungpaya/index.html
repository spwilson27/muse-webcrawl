Alaungpaya

Alaungpaya (Burmese: အလောင်းဘုရား)
Reign: 29 February 1752 – 11 May 1760
Successor: Naungdawgyi
Born: 24 August 1714, Moksobo (Shwebo)
Died: 11 May 1760, Kinywa
Dynasty: Konbaung (founder)

Alaungpaya (24 August 1714 – 11 May 1760) was the founder and first king of the Konbaung dynasty of Burma (Myanmar). By the time of his death in 1760, this former chief of a small village in Upper Burma had reunified the country, founded the city of Yangon (Rangoon), and driven out the French and British interests that had supported his Mon rivals. He is considered one of the three greatest kings in Burmese history, alongside Anawrahta and Bayinnaung.

Rise and reunification

Born Aung Zeya in Moksobo (later Shwebo), Alaungpaya refused to submit when the Mon-speaking Restored Hanthawaddy Kingdom conquered Ava in 1752. Taking the name Alaungpaya — "the Embryo Buddha", a future Buddha-king — he rallied the Burmese of the Mu valley, retook Ava in 1753, and campaigned down the Irrawaddy. He captured the port of Syriam, founded Yangon in 1755, and took Pegu in 1757, destroying the Hanthawaddy kingdom and reunifying Burma after two decades of fragmentation. In 1759 he crushed a rebellion in Manipur and intervened in the Arakanese succession.

Death

In 1760 Alaungpaya invaded Siam (Thailand), besieging Ayutthaya, but fell ill during the campaign — possibly wounded by an exploding cannon — and died at Kinywa during the retreat. His dynasty ruled Burma until the Third Anglo-Burmese War of 1885.
