KingdomPlantae
CladeAngiosperms; eudicots; rosids
OrderSapindales

Sapindales is an order of flowering plants containing about 460 genera and some 5,700 species. Members of the order are mostly woody plants — trees, shrubs, and lianas — distributed worldwide, especially in the tropics. The order includes many economically important plants, including citrus fruits, mahogany, lychee, rambutan, maple, and cashew.

Families

In the APG IV classification system, Sapindales comprises nine families, including:

  • Sapindaceae — the soapberry family, including maples, lychee, and horse chestnut
  • Rutaceae — the rue or citrus family
  • Meliaceae — the mahogany family
  • Anacardiaceae — the cashew and mango family
  • Simaroubaceae — the quassia family
  • Burseraceae — the frankincense and myrrh family

Characteristics

Plants in Sapindales commonly have pinnately compound leaves and flowers with a nectar disc. Many species produce resinous compounds, aromatic oils, or bitter triterpenoids. The order belongs to the malvid clade of the rosids, and its member families have been substantially rearranged by molecular phylogenetic studies; for example, the maples, formerly placed in their own family Aceraceae, are now included in an enlarged Sapindaceae.
