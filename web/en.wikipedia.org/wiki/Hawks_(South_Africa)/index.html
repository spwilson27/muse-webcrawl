Hawks (South Africa)

The Hawks, officially the Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation (DPCI), is the branch of the South African Police Service responsible for investigating organised crime, economic crime, corruption, and other serious offences. It was established in 2008 under President Kgalema Motlanthe, replacing the independent Scorpions unit of the National Prosecuting Authority, and began operating in 2009.

The DPCI — the "Hawks" — investigates priority crimes in South Africa.

History and controversies

The replacement of the Scorpions by a unit housed within the police was politically contentious, as the Scorpions had pursued high-profile corruption cases against senior politicians. In 2011 the Constitutional Court ruled in Glenister v President that the legislation creating the Hawks did not provide adequate independence from political interference, and Parliament amended the law in response. The unit's first head was Anwa Dramat, succeeded in 2015 (in an acting capacity, then permanently in 2018) by Berning Ntlemeza and later by Godfrey Lebeya. The Hawks have handled major investigations including corruption prosecutions arising from the state-capture era examined by the Zondo Commission.

Mandate

The DPCI investigates crimes including offences under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act, serious corruption, and crimes selected nationally as priorities. Its head is appointed by the Minister of Police and accounts to the police commissioner and Parliament.
