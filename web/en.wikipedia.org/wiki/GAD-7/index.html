GAD-7

The Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) is a seven-item self-report questionnaire used to screen for generalised anxiety disorder (GAD) and to measure symptom severity. It was developed by Robert L. Spitzer, Kurt Kroenke, Janet B. W. Williams, and Bernd Löwe in 2006.

Respondents rate how often they have been bothered by anxiety symptoms over the past two weeks. Each item is scored from 0 to 3: "not at all" (0), "several days" (1), "more than half the days" (2), and "nearly every day" (3), giving a total possible score from 0 to 21.

Items

The seven items cover feeling nervous, anxious or on edge; not being able to stop or control worrying; worrying too much about different things; trouble relaxing; being so restless it is hard to sit still; becoming easily annoyed or irritable; and feeling afraid as if something awful might happen.

Scoring

ScoreSeverity
0–4Minimal anxiety
5–9Mild anxiety
10–14Moderate anxiety
15–21Severe anxiety

A score of 10 or higher has a sensitivity of 89% and specificity of 82% for generalised anxiety disorder. The GAD-7 is widely used in primary care, in trials, and along with the PHQ-9 depression questionnaire. It is a screening tool, not a diagnosis.
