The Pennsylvania Dutch (Pennsylvania German: Pennsilfaanisch-Deitsche) are a cultural group formed from German-speaking immigrants who settled in Pennsylvania and neighbouring states in the 17th and 18th centuries. "Dutch" here derives from Deutsch (German), not from the Netherlands.

History

Beginning with Germantown in 1683 and the Palatine migration of 1709 onwards, settlers from the Rhineland, Switzerland and south-western Germany farmed the rich lands of south-eastern Pennsylvania. They divide broadly into the "Plain" Anabaptist sects — Amish and many Mennonites, who keep distinctive dress and limit technology — and the "Fancy" or church Dutch (Lutheran and Reformed).

Culture

Pennsylvania Dutch culture is known for barn stars (hex signs), quilts, fraktur folk art, and a hearty cuisine of chicken pot pie, shoofly pie, scrapple and chow-chow. The Deitsch dialect is still spoken in Old Order communities, while tourism centres on Lancaster County.
