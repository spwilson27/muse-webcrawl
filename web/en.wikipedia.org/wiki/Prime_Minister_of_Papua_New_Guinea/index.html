Prime Minister of Papua New Guinea

Appointer: Governor-General of Papua New Guinea

Term length: At the Governor-General's pleasure

Formation: 16 September 1975

First holder: Michael Somare

The prime minister of the Independent State of Papua New Guinea is Papua New Guinea's head of government, consequent on being the leader of the party or coalition with majority support in the National Parliament. The prime minister serves as the head of the executive branch under the country's Westminster-style system of government.

History

The office was established when Papua New Guinea gained independence from Australia on 16 September 1975. Michael Somare, who had been Chief Minister under Australian administration, became the country's first prime minister and is widely regarded as the "father of the nation". Somare served several separate terms, becoming the country's longest-serving prime minister.

Role and selection

Following a general election, the Governor-General invites the member of Parliament who appears most likely to command majority support to form a government. The prime minister appoints the cabinet (the National Executive Council) and can be removed by a vote of no confidence in Parliament, subject to constitutional grace periods that restrict such motions early and late in a parliamentary term.

Papua New Guinea's politics is characterised by fluid party allegiances and frequent changes of coalition, and several prime ministers have left office through no-confidence votes or political crises, including the 2011–12 constitutional crisis, during which rival claimants to the office disputed its occupancy.
