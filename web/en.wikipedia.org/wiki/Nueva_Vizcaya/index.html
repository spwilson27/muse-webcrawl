Nueva Vizcaya is a landlocked province in the Philippines located in the Cagayan Valley region of northern Luzon. Its capital is Bayombong. The province is named after Vizcaya (Biscay) in Spain.

Geography

Nueva Vizcaya is largely mountainous and forested, bounded by the Sierra Madre to the east, the Cordillera Central to the west and the Caraballo Mountains to the south. The Magat River system drains much of the province. It borders Ifugao and Isabela to the north, Quirino and Aurora to the east, Nueva Ecija and Pangasinan to the south, and Benguet to the west.

People and economy

The population comprises Ilocano settlers alongside indigenous peoples including the Isinai, Gaddang, Ilongot (Bugkalot), Ibaloi and Kankanaey. Agriculture dominates, with rice, corn and citrus — the province is sometimes called the citrus capital of the north — and mining has been controversial, notably the Didipio gold-copper mine in Kasibu.
