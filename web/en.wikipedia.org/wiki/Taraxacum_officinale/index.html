Taraxacum officinale

Taraxacum officinale, the common dandelion, is a flowering herbaceous perennial plant in the aster family Asteraceae, native to Europe and Asia and now naturalised worldwide. It is famous both as a lawn weed and as a traditional edible and medicinal herb.

Description

The plant forms a basal rosette of toothed leaves — its name comes from the French dent-de-lion, "lion's tooth" — and hollow stems bearing single yellow composite flower heads. After flowering, the head closes and reopens as a spherical seed head ("clock") of silky parachuted achenes dispersed by wind. Many dandelions reproduce asexually by apomixis, producing large complexes of microspecies.

Uses

All parts are edible: the leaves in salads, the flowers in wine and fritters, and the roasted root as a coffee substitute. Dandelion has a long history in herbal medicine as a diuretic and digestive remedy. It is also an important early-season nectar and pollen source for bees and other insects.
