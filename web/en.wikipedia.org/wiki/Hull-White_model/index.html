Hull–White model

The Hull–White model is a model of future interest rates used in mathematical finance. In its most common formulation it is a one-factor model in which the short rate follows a mean-reverting stochastic process, and it generalises the Vasicek model by allowing the drift to depend on time so that the model can be fitted exactly to the initial term structure of interest rates.

The model

Under the risk-neutral measure, the short rate r satisfies the stochastic differential equation

drt = [θ(t) − a·rt] dt + σ dWt

where a and σ are constants (mean-reversion speed and volatility), W is a Wiener process, and the time-dependent function θ(t) is chosen so that the model reproduces the market's initial yield curve. When a = 0 the model reduces to a version of the Ho–Lee model; with θ constant it reduces to the Vasicek model.

Bond pricing

Because the short rate is Gaussian, zero-coupon bond prices have a closed affine form, P(t,T) = A(t,T)·exp(−B(t,T)·rt), which makes the model tractable for pricing interest-rate derivatives such as caps, floors, swaptions and bond options. Trinomial tree implementations, popularised by John C. Hull and Alan White in papers from 1990 onward, are widely used in practice.

Two-factor extension

Hull and White also proposed a two-factor extension in which a second stochastic factor drives the long-run mean of the short rate, allowing richer correlation structures between rates of different maturities.
