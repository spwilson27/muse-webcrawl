Polylactic acid

Polylactic acid, also known as poly(lactic acid) or PLA, is a thermoplastic polyester derived from renewable resources such as corn starch or sugarcane. It is the most widely consumed bioplastic in the world and is biodegradable under industrial composting conditions.

Production

PLA is made by fermenting plant sugars to lactic acid, which is then polymerised — industrially, most often by converting the acid to the cyclic dimer lactide and ring-opening polymerising it with metal catalysts. The stereochemistry of the monomer (L- or D-lactic acid) determines crystallinity and properties: pure poly-L-lactide is semi-crystalline with a melting point around 170–180 °C and a glass transition near 60 °C.

Uses

PLA is used in food packaging, disposable tableware, fibres and textiles, and in medicine as resorbable sutures, screws and drug-delivery implants. It is the most popular filament material for desktop fused-deposition 3D printing because it is easy to print and has low warping and odour. Limitations include brittleness, low heat resistance and slow degradation outside industrial composters — in landfill or the ocean it persists for long periods. Blends and copolymers are used to improve toughness. Major producers include NatureWorks (Ingeo) and TotalEnergies Corbion (Luminy). See also bioplastic.
