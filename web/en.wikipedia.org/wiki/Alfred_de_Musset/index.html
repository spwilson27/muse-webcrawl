Alfred de Musset

Alfred Louis Charles de Musset-Pathay (11 December 1810 – 2 May 1857) was a French dramatist, poet, and novelist, one of the leading figures of French Romanticism. He is best known for his plays Lorenzaccio and On ne badine pas avec l'amour, the autobiographical novel La Confession d'un enfant du siècle, and his celebrated love affair with the novelist George Sand.

Life and career

Born into an aristocratic family in Paris, Musset abandoned studies in law and medicine to join Victor Hugo's romantic circle, the Cénacle. His first collection, Contes d'Espagne et d'Italie (1830), made his reputation at nineteen. The failure of his play La Nuit vénitienne drove him to write "armchair theatre" — plays meant to be read — including the masterpiece Lorenzaccio (1834), a drama about Lorenzo de' Medici.

George Sand and the Confession

His tempestuous liaison with George Sand, begun in 1833 and broken off after their ill-fated trip to Venice, furnished the material for his novel La Confession d'un enfant du siècle (1836), the classic statement of the mal du siècle — the melancholy of a generation raised amid Napoleonic glory and left idle by the Restoration. His lyric poems, including the Nuits cycle, are among the most admired in French.

Later years

Musset was elected to the Académie française in 1852. Increasingly dependent on alcohol and opium, he died in Paris in 1857 and is buried in Père Lachaise Cemetery.
