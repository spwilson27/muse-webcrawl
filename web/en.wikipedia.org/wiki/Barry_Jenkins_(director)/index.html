Barry Jenkins (born 19 November 1979) is an American filmmaker. He gained wide acclaim for Moonlight (2016), which won the Academy Award for Best Picture — in the famous envelope mix-up at the 89th Academy Awards — and earned Jenkins and Tarell Alvin McCraney the Oscar for Best Adapted Screenplay.

Career

Born in Miami and raised partly in the Liberty City neighbourhood that Moonlight depicts, Jenkins studied film at Florida State University. His debut feature, Medicine for Melancholy (2008), was a low-budget San Francisco romance praised for its visual lyricism. After years in advertising and short films, Moonlight — told in three chapters about one man's childhood, adolescence, and adulthood — made him a leading voice in American cinema.

He followed it with If Beale Street Could Talk (2018), adapted from James Baldwin's novel, and the acclaimed Amazon limited series The Underground Railroad (2021), based on Colson Whitehead's novel. In 2024 he directed the photorealistic animated prequel Mufasa: The Lion King.
