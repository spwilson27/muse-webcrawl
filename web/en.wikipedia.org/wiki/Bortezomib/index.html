Bortezomib, sold under the brand name Velcade among others, is an anti-cancer drug used to treat multiple myeloma and mantle cell lymphoma. It was the first therapeutic proteasome inhibitor to be approved for use in humans.

Mechanism of action

Bortezomib is a modified dipeptidyl boronic acid that binds reversibly and with high affinity to the chymotrypsin-like site of the 26S proteasome. By blocking the proteasome — the cell's machinery for degrading unneeded or damaged proteins — it disrupts multiple signaling cascades. Myeloma cells, which produce large quantities of immunoglobulin protein, are especially sensitive to proteasome blockade and undergo apoptosis.

History

The drug was synthesized in 1995 and developed by Millennium Pharmaceuticals. It was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2003 for relapsed multiple myeloma, unusually quickly after phase II trials showed responses in patients with refractory disease. Julian Adams, a key figure in its development, championed the compound through clinical trials.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include peripheral neuropathy, fatigue, nausea, thrombocytopenia, and herpes zoster reactivation, the risk of which is reduced by antiviral prophylaxis. Subcutaneous administration was shown to reduce the incidence of neuropathy compared with intravenous infusion.
