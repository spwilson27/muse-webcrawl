Watercolor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A luminous watercolour landscape: washes of blue and green suggesting hills, water and a pale sky]

Watercolor (American English) or watercolour (British English) is a painting method in which the paints are made of pigments suspended in a water-based solution, traditionally bound with gum arabic. The medium is known for its transparency and luminosity: light passes through the pigment layers and reflects off the white of the paper support.

Watercolour has roots in ancient Egyptian and Chinese brush painting and became a major medium in Europe from the Renaissance (Dürer's studies) through its golden age in 18th- and 19th-century Britain, where artists such as J. M. W. Turner made it a vehicle for ambitious landscape. It remains popular for sketching, illustration and botanical art.
