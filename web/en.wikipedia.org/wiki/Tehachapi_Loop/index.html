Tehachapi Loop

The Tehachapi Loop is a 0.73-mile (1.17 km) long spiral (helix) on the Union Pacific Railroad Mountain Subdivision through Tehachapi Pass in Kern County, California. It is one of the most famous engineering feats of American railroading: on the Loop, trains climb a 2% grade by circling a hill, with the track passing over itself, so that a sufficiently long train crosses above its own rear cars.

Construction

The line was built by the Southern Pacific Railroad between 1874 and 1876 to connect Los Angeles with San Francisco and the Central Valley. Chief engineer William Hood designed the Loop to gain elevation — about 77 feet (23 m) — within a short horizontal distance, keeping grades manageable for steam locomotives. Construction required thousands of workers, including many Chinese labourers, and involved numerous tunnels and cuts through difficult terrain.

Operation

The Tehachapi Pass route remains a busy freight corridor shared by Union Pacific and BNSF Railway, carrying roughly 40 trains a day. The Loop is a popular railfan viewing spot, with a viewing area and monument near the site. It is designated a California Historical Landmark and a National Historic Civil Engineering Landmark.

Passenger service over the Loop is rare: Amtrak's Coast Starlight occasionally detours via Tehachapi when the coastal route is closed, since regular passenger trains are normally prohibited on the line.
