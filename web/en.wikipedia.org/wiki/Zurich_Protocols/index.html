Zurich Protocols

The Zurich Protocols were two protocols signed between the Republic of Armenia and the Republic of Turkey in Zurich, Switzerland, on 10 October 2009. Their purpose was to establish diplomatic relations between the two countries and to open their common border, which Turkey had closed in 1993 during the First Nagorno-Karabakh War.

Contents

The two documents were the Protocol on the Establishment of Diplomatic Relations and the Protocol on the Development of Bilateral Relations. They envisaged recognition of the existing border, establishment of diplomatic ties, the opening of the frontier within two months of entry into force, and a joint historical commission to study the events of 1915.

Failure of ratification

The protocols required ratification by both parliaments. In Armenia, the Constitutional Court's ruling on the protocols drew objections from Turkey, while in Turkey ratification became linked to progress in the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict with Azerbaijan. In April 2010, Armenian president Serzh Sargsyan suspended the ratification process. Armenia formally annulled the protocols in 2018, and the border remained closed, though normalisation talks between the two countries resumed in 2021–2022.
