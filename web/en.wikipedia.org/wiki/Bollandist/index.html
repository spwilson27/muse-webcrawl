Bollandist

The Bollandists are a society of scholars, originally Jesuits, devoted to the critical study of hagiography—the lives of the saints. They are named after Jean Bolland (1596–1665), the Flemish Jesuit who in 1643 published the first volumes of the Acta Sanctorum, a monumental critical edition of saints' lives arranged by feast day. The Bollandist enterprise effectively founded the modern scholarly discipline of hagiography.

History

The project was conceived by Heribert Rosweyde, who collected manuscripts of saints' lives; after his death Bolland took over, applying critical method—comparing manuscripts, questioning legends, and printing sources in full. Collaborators such as Godfrey Henschen and Daniel van Papenbroeck (Papebroch) travelled Europe's libraries copying manuscripts. Papenbroeck's critical skepticism about the Carmelite order's claimed origins provoked controversy with the Spanish Inquisition, which condemned volumes of the work in 1695 (the ban was later lifted).

The society was dissolved with the suppression of the Jesuits in 1773, briefly continued by other hands, and refounded in 1837 at Brussels, where the Société des Bollandistes remains active, based at the Collège Saint-Michel. The Acta Sanctorum reached 68 folio volumes by the 1920s; the Bollandists also publish the journal Analecta Bollandiana (since 1882) and reference works such as the Bibliotheca Hagiographica Latina.
