The Mongol conquest of Western Xia was a series of conflicts between the Mongol Empire and the Tangut-led Western Xia dynasty in north-western China between 1205 and 1227. It was the first campaign of conquest undertaken by Genghis Khan, begun even before the formal proclamation of the Mongol Empire in 1206, and it ended with the complete destruction of Western Xia and the death of Genghis Khan himself during the final campaign.

The Mongol invasions of Western Xia, 1205–1227.

Background

Western Xia, founded in 1038 by the Tangut people, controlled the Hexi Corridor and the trade routes between China and Central Asia from its capital at Xingqing (modern Yinchuan). It balanced between the Jin dynasty and the Song dynasty, and its armies were experienced and well fortified. Genghis Khan regarded its submission as essential: it would secure his flank before the planned conquest of the Jin dynasty and provide supplies, craftsmen and experience in siege warfare against Chinese fortifications.

The early campaigns (1205–1210)

Probing raids in 1205 and 1207 were followed by a full-scale invasion in 1209 in which the Mongols defeated a Tangut army, captured the general Gao Lianghui, and advanced to the gates of Xingqing. According to the sources, the Mongols attempted to divert the Yellow River to flood the capital — a plan that backfired when the dikes broke and flooded the Mongol camp. Emperor Li Anquan nevertheless sued for peace, gave a daughter in marriage to Genghis Khan, and accepted Mongol overlordship; Western Xia thereafter supplied troops for Mongol campaigns and refused aid to the Jin.

The final campaign (1226–1227)

When the last Xia emperor refused to send troops for the Mongol campaign against the Khwarazmian Empire, and Xia officials insulted Mongol envoys, Genghis Khan — though past sixty — personally led a massive punitive expedition in 1226. Mongol columns systematically reduced the cities of the Hexi Corridor and the Tangut heartland; Khara-Khoto and Suzhou, Ganzhou and Liangzhou fell, and by early 1227 the capital was besieged. The last emperor, Li Xian (Emperor Modi), surrendered in the autumn of 1227 and was executed.

Genghis Khan died in August 1227 during the campaign, from causes still debated (wounds, illness, or malaria). In accordance with his final orders — and, in Tangut and later accounts, out of vengeance — the Mongols massacred much of the population, destroyed cities and irrigation works, and effectively erased the Tangut state: its script, literature and people largely disappeared from history until modern archaeology recovered its remains.

Aftermath

The conquest gave the Mongols the wealth, siege engineers and Chinese administrative experience used against the Jin dynasty (conquered by 1234). The former Western Xia territories became part of the Mongol — later Yuan — administrative system, and the region was eventually organised as Ningxia and Gansu provinces.
