Dynamic braking

Dynamic braking is a method of slowing a vehicle or machine by using its electric traction motors as generators. The kinetic energy of the moving vehicle drives the motors, which generate electrical energy that either is dissipated as heat in resistor grids (rheostatic braking) or returned to the power supply or an energy storage system (regenerative braking).

Principle

An electric motor operated as a generator produces a torque opposing its rotation, in proportion to the current drawn from it. By controlling the electrical load connected to the motors, the driver or control system can vary the braking effort smoothly, with no wear to friction components.

Railway use

Dynamic braking is standard on diesel-electric and electric locomotives, where it is especially valuable on long descents: it holds train speed without overheating the friction brakes. On classic diesel-electric locomotives the generated power is burned off in large roof-mounted resistor grids cooled by fans. Electric multiple units and metros commonly feed the energy back into the third rail or catenary for use by other trains.

Other applications

Dynamic braking is used in electric and hybrid road vehicles, trams, trolleybuses, elevators, cranes and industrial motor drives. In small motors, simply short-circuiting the terminals or disconnecting the supply provides a related form of braking.

Limitations

Braking effort fades at low speed because the generated voltage falls; dynamic brakes therefore cannot hold a vehicle stationary and are blended with friction brakes for final stops and parking.
