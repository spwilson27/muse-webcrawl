Battle of Megiddo (609 BC)

The Battle of Megiddo was fought in 609 BC between the Kingdom of Judah under King Josiah and the forces of Pharaoh Necho II of Egypt at Megiddo, in the Jezreel Valley. Josiah was killed in the battle — an event remembered as a national catastrophe in Judah — as he attempted to block the Egyptian march north to aid the remnants of Assyria against Babylon.

Background

With the Neo-Assyrian Empire collapsing before Babylonian and Median attack, Pharaoh Necho II marched north in 609 BC to support the last Assyrian forces holding out at Harran. His route took him along the Via Maris through Judah's territory. King Josiah — whose religious reforms had made him the righteous king of the biblical account — chose to intercept the Egyptians at the Megiddo pass, whether to assert independence, to favour Babylon, or for reasons the sources do not fully explain.

The battle

The biblical accounts (2 Kings 23; 2 Chronicles 35) say Josiah refused Necho's warning that the pharaoh had no quarrel with him, disguised himself, and joined battle at Megiddo, where he was mortally wounded by Egyptian archers. He was carried to Jerusalem, where he died and was buried amid great lamentation — the prophet Jeremiah is said to have composed laments for him. Details of the fighting itself are not preserved.

Aftermath

Necho proceeded north but failed to save Assyria. On his return he deposed Josiah's son Jehoahaz, deported him to Egypt, and installed Jehoiakim as a vassal king, making Judah an Egyptian tributary until Babylon's victory at Carchemish (605 BC) reversed the balance. Megiddo's name — via "Armageddon" (Har Megiddo, "mount of Megiddo") — later became a byword for a climactic final battle.
