Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park

Brimstone Hill Fortress
Location: Saint Kitts, Saint Kitts and Nevis
UNESCO World Heritage Site (1999)

Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, a well-preserved fortress on a hill on the island of St. Kitts in the Federation of St. Christopher (St. Kitts) and Nevis in the Eastern Caribbean. It was designed by British military engineers and built and maintained by enslaved Africans. It is one of the best preserved historical fortifications in the Americas.

History

The first cannon were mounted on Brimstone Hill in 1690, as the English and French contested control of the island. Over the following century the fortress expanded into a massive citadel of volcanic stone and limestone, sometimes called the "Gibraltar of the West Indies". In 1782 a large French force besieged and captured the fortress, but it was returned to Britain under the Treaty of Versailles the following year. The British garrison withdrew in 1854 and the fortress fell into decay before restoration began in the 20th century.

Description

Rising roughly 240 m above the sea, the fortress complex includes the Fort George citadel at its summit, bastions, barracks, magazines, and a hospital, all built with remarkable polygonal masonry. The national park covers about 15 hectares and offers views toward several neighbouring islands, including Sint Eustatius, Saba, and Nevis.

Heritage status

Inscribed on the World Heritage List in 1999, the site is managed by the Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park Society and is St. Kitts and Nevis's principal historic attraction.
