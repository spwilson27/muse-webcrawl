Hamilton, New Zealand

Hamilton (Māori: Kirikiriroa)
Country: New Zealand
Region: Waikato
Population: about 185,000 (urban, 2024 estimate)
Founded: 1864
Named for: Captain John Fane Charles Hamilton
Known for: Hamilton Gardens, University of Waikato, dairy farming

Hamilton (Māori: Kirikiriroa, "long stretch of gravel") is an inland city in the North Island of New Zealand. Located in the Waikato region on the banks of the Waikato River, it is the country's fourth most populous city, with an urban population of roughly 185,000, and the seat of the Waikato Regional Council. Hamilton is a major service and research centre for the surrounding dairy-farming region, the most intensive in New Zealand.

History

The area was originally settled by Māori, with numerous pā and settlements along the river; Kirikiriroa was a village on the west bank. During the Invasion of the Waikato in 1863–64, the region was conquered by British and colonial forces, and Māori land was confiscated (raupatu). In August 1864 the 4th Waikato Militia established a settlement named Hamilton in honour of Captain John Fane Charles Hamilton, a Royal Navy officer killed at the Battle of Gate Pā. The town grew slowly as a farming service centre and rail junction; it became a borough in 1877 and a city in 1945. Some confiscated land has been returned or compensated under the Waikato Raupatu Settlement of 1995 between the Crown and Waikato-Tainui.

Geography and economy

Hamilton sits astride the Waikato River, which divides it into Hamilton East and Hamilton West. The surrounding Waikato basin is rich agricultural land — the city hosts the AgResearch and Plant & Food Research institutes and is near the dairy research centre at Ruakura. The National Agricultural Fieldays at Mystery Creek, the largest agricultural show in the southern hemisphere, is held nearby each June. The city is home to the University of Waikato (founded 1964), Wintec polytechnic and Waikato Hospital, a major regional trauma centre.

Attractions

The city's best-known attraction is the Hamilton Gardens, a 58-hectare public garden on the riverbank built largely on a former rubbish dump; its themed Paradise, Productive, Fantasy and Cultivar collections have won international awards, including Garden of the Year at the International Garden Tourism Awards in 2014. Other landmarks include the Waikato Museum, Hamilton Zoo, the Seddon Park cricket ground and FMG Stadium Waikato for rugby. Six bridges cross the river within the city, and long river-path walkways and cycleways line both banks.
