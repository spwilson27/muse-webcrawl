Eponychium

The eponychium is the thickened layer of skin at the base of a human fingernail or toenail, just proximal to the nail plate. Together with the cuticle it forms a protective seal that shields the nail matrix and the nail bed from bacteria, moisture, and other contaminants.

Anatomy

Strictly, the eponychium is the living skin tissue at the nail's base, while the cuticle (pterygium) is the thin, non-living layer of tissue it sheds that adheres to the nail plate. In common usage the two terms are often conflated. Beneath the eponychium lies the germinal matrix, whose cells divide to produce the growing nail. The pale half-moon visible through the nail, the lunula, is the distal portion of this matrix.

Clinical significance

Inflammation or infection of the eponychium and surrounding nail folds is called paronychia. Damage to the eponychial seal — from biting, aggressive manicuring, or occupational exposure — predisposes to such infections. In horses, the eponychium refers to the soft deciduous hoof capsule (the "fetal slipper") that covers a newborn foal's hooves and protects the mare's uterus during gestation.
