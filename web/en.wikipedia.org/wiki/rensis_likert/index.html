Rensis Likert

BornAugust 5, 1903, Cheyenne, Wyoming, U.S.
DiedSeptember 3, 1981, Ann Arbor, Michigan, U.S.
FieldsPsychology, organizational behavior

Rensis Likert (1903–1981) was an American organizational psychologist known principally for developing the Likert scale, a psychometric scale used in questionnaire research, and for his work on management styles.

Career

Likert studied at the University of Michigan and Columbia University. During World War II he directed survey work for the U.S. Department of Agriculture and the Strategic Bombing Survey. In 1946 he founded the Survey Research Center at the University of Michigan, which became the Institute for Social Research.

Likert scale

His 1932 doctoral work introduced a method for measuring attitudes: respondents rate agreement with a series of statements on a symmetric scale (typically five points, from "strongly disagree" to "strongly agree"), and the summed scores indicate attitude direction and intensity. The format became standard in social science surveys.

Management systems

In works such as New Patterns of Management (1961), Likert described four management systems — exploitative authoritative, benevolent authoritative, consultative, and participative — arguing that participative management improves performance and satisfaction.
