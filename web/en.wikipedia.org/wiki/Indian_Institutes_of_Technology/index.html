The Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs) are a group of 23 autonomous public engineering and research institutes across India, established by the Government of India to train scientists and engineers and declared "Institutes of National Importance" under the Institutes of Technology Act, 1961. They are among the most selective universities in the world and anchor India's higher technical education system.

History

The concept originated with a 1946 committee chaired by Nalini Ranjan Sarkar, which recommended institutions modelled on the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. The first IIT was founded at Kharagpur in 1951 on the site of the former Hijli detention camp. Four more followed in quick succession, each established with foreign collaboration: IIT Bombay (1958, with Soviet/UNESCO assistance), IIT Madras (1959, West German), IIT Kanpur (1959, American) and IIT Delhi (1961, British). IIT Guwahati was added in 1994, and the University of Roorkee became IIT Roorkee in 2001. A major expansion in 2008–2009 created eight new IITs; several more followed in 2015–2016, bringing the total to 23, including IIT (BHU) Varanasi and IIT (ISM) Dhanbad, converted from older institutions.

Admissions and academics

Undergraduate admission is through the Joint Entrance Examination: candidates first sit JEE Main, and the top qualifiers take JEE Advanced, which the IITs conduct jointly; acceptance rates are around 1–2%. Postgraduate admission to M.Tech programmes uses the GATE examination, while PhD and MBA admissions use GATE, CAT and institute-level tests. The IITs award B.Tech, M.Tech, MSc and PhD degrees across engineering, sciences, design, humanities and management, and each institute is fully residential, with students living in hostels (halls) on campus. Governance is headed by the President of India as Visitor, with the IIT Council coordinating policy across institutes.

Reputation and alumni

The IITs consistently top Indian rankings such as the NIRF and appear in global rankings (QS, THE), with IIT Bombay, Delhi and Madras usually highest placed. Alumni occupy leadership roles in global technology and business — including, prominently, Sundar Pichai (CEO of Alphabet/Google) — in academia, in the Indian civil services and in entrepreneurship, and an extensive alumni diaspora contributes to the institutions through funding and mentorship. The system's intensity and the coaching industry built around the JEE are also subjects of criticism and debate within India.
