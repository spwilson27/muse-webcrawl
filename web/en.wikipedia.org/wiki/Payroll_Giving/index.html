Payroll Giving, also known as Give As You Earn or workplace giving, is a scheme that enables employees to donate to charity directly from their pay, before income tax is deducted. Because donations are made from gross pay, the donor receives full tax relief immediately at their highest rate of tax: a donation of £10 costs a basic-rate taxpayer £8, and a higher-rate taxpayer £6.

United Kingdom

Payroll Giving was introduced in the United Kingdom in 1987. Any employer can operate the scheme by registering with an HM Revenue and Customs-approved Payroll Giving agency, which distributes the donations to the employees' chosen charities for a small administration fee. Employees may give any amount, to any registered charity, and can stop or change donations at any time. Unlike Gift Aid, Payroll Giving gives tax relief to the donor rather than the charity, and it is the only way for higher-rate taxpayers to obtain the full relief automatically without completing a tax return.

Take-up in the UK has remained relatively modest compared with the United States: only a minority of employers offer the scheme and around a few per cent of employees participate, though those who do give regularly, and the total raised annually exceeds £100 million. The government has periodically promoted the scheme, including through Payroll Giving Awards and quality marks for employers.

Other countries

In the United States, workplace giving campaigns — most prominently the Combined Federal Campaign for federal employees and the campaigns coordinated by United Way — raise billions of dollars annually through payroll deduction, though donations there are generally made from post-tax income and claimed as deductions on the employee's tax return. Similar workplace giving arrangements operate in Canada and Australia.
