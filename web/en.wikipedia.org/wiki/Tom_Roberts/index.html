Thomas William Roberts (8 March 1856 – 14 September 1931) was a British-born Australian artist and a key member of the Heidelberg School art movement, also known as Australian impressionism. He is regarded as one of the foundational figures of Australian landscape painting.

Early life

Roberts was born in Dorchester, Dorset, England, and emigrated with his family to Australia in 1869, settling in Melbourne, Victoria. He worked as a photographer's assistant while studying art at night at the National Gallery School under Eugene von Guérard and Louis Buvelot. From 1881 to 1885 he studied in London at the Royal Academy Schools, travelling also in Europe, where he was influenced by the plein air naturalism of Jules Bastien-Lepage.

Heidelberg School

Returning to Melbourne, Roberts organised painting camps at Box Hill and Heidelberg with Frederick McCubbin, Arthur Streeton and Charles Conder. The group's 9 by 5 Impression Exhibition of August 1889 — named for the cigar-box lids on which many works were painted — was a landmark in Australian art. Roberts painted iconic works of Australian life including Shearing the Rams (1890), A break away! (1891), Bailed Up (1895, depicting the bushranger Captain Thunderbolt) and The Opening of the First Parliament of the Commonwealth of Australia (1903), an enormous commissioned canvas of the 1901 ceremony in Melbourne's Royal Exhibition Building, often called "the big picture".

Later life and legacy

Roberts spent periods in London during and after the First World War, in which he served with a hospital unit, before returning to Australia in 1923. He settled at Kallista in the Dandenong Ranges, where he died in 1931. His work is held by major Australian galleries, and his paintings are considered defining images of the young Australian federation.
