Passenger Rail Agency of South Africa (PRASA)
Founded: 1990 (as SARCC); PRASA from 2008
Headquarters: Johannesburg, South Africa
Parent: Department of Transport
Services: Metrorail, Shosholoza Meyl, Premier Classe, Autopax

The Passenger Rail Agency of South Africa (PRASA) is a South African state-owned enterprise responsible for most passenger rail services in the country. Its subsidiaries and divisions include Metrorail, which operates commuter rail in metropolitan areas; Shosholoza Meyl, which runs long-distance intercity trains; Premier Classe, a luxury train service; and Autopax, a bus operator.

History

PRASA's origins lie in the South African Rail Commuter Corporation (SARCC), created in 1990 to manage commuter rail. In 2008 the SARCC was renamed PRASA and absorbed the long-distance services of the former South African Railways, consolidating passenger rail under one agency reporting to the national Department of Transport.

Challenges

PRASA has faced major difficulties, including chronic underinvestment, vandalism and theft of infrastructure such as copper cables, derailments, and governance problems. A widely publicised procurement scandal involved the purchase of Afro 4000 locomotives from Spain that proved too tall for parts of the South African network. Service levels on both Metrorail and Shosholoza Meyl declined sharply during the 2010s and early 2020s, and recovery programs have sought to restore corridors, rolling stock, and security.
