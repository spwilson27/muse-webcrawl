Merriweather Post Pavilion is an outdoor concert venue in Columbia, Maryland, located in the planned community's Symphony Woods. Opened in 1967 and designed by a young Frank Gehry, it was originally the home of the National Symphony Orchestra's summer performances and was a gift to the community from cereal heiress Marjorie Merriweather Post.

History

The pavilion was developed by James Rouse as part of his new town of Columbia. Marjorie Merriweather Post donated funds for its construction, and it was named in her honour. Over the decades it has hosted legendary rock, folk, and pop acts, and festivals including several editions of the HFStival and Virgin Festival. The experimental rock band Animal Collective named their acclaimed 2009 album Merriweather Post Pavilion after the venue, where members had seen concerts growing up.

Renovation

A major multi-year renovation completed in the late 2010s raised the pavilion roof, added a rotating stage and new seating, and modernised backstage facilities, while preserving the venue's wooded setting. The venue is owned by the Downtown Columbia Arts and Culture Commission and operated by I.M.P., the company behind Washington, D.C.'s 9:30 Club.
