Otto, Crown Prince of Austria (20 November 1912 – 4 July 2011), known in later life as Otto von Habsburg, was the last crown prince of Austria-Hungary and, from 1922 until his renunciation in 1961, the head of the House of Habsburg and pretender to its thrones. He became a prominent advocate of European integration, serving for twenty years as a German member of the European Parliament.

Early life and exile

Otto was born at Villa Wartholz in Reichenau an der Rax, the eldest son of Archduke Charles — who became Emperor Charles I in 1916 — and Princess Zita of Bourbon-Parma. Following the collapse of the monarchy in 1918, the family went into exile in Switzerland and then Madeira, where Charles died in 1922, making the nine-year-old Otto the Habsburg pretender. He was educated in Belgium, earning a doctorate in political and social sciences from the University of Leuven in 1935.

Opposition to Nazism and the war years

Otto was an outspoken opponent of the Nazi annexation of Austria and of Hitler, who personally detested him; the Nazi codename for the operation against him was "Operation Otto". When Germany invaded Belgium and France in 1940, he fled to the United States, where he lobbied in Washington for Austrian independence and against a harsh peace — the 1943 Moscow Declaration's treatment of Austria as a victim of aggression owed something to such advocacy.

European politician

Barred for decades from Austria by the Habsburg Law, Otto formally renounced his dynastic claims in 1961 and was finally permitted to visit Austria in 1966. Settled at Pöcking in Bavaria, he became a German citizen and was elected to the European Parliament for the Christian Social Union in 1979, serving until 1999 and becoming its longest-serving and oldest member. A longtime president of the International Paneuropean Union, he championed the enlargement of the European Union to Central and Eastern Europe and was present at the Pan-European Picnic on the Austrian–Hungarian border in August 1989, an event that helped open the Iron Curtain.

Death and legacy

Otto died on 4 July 2011 at the age of 98 — he had been Europe's longest-lived former heir apparent. His funeral in Vienna's St. Stephen's Cathedral, followed by burial in the Imperial Crypt, drew heads of state and royalty from across Europe; his heart was buried separately at Pannonhalma Archabbey in Hungary, symbolising his dual Austrian and Hungarian inheritance. He was succeeded as head of the house by his son Karl.
