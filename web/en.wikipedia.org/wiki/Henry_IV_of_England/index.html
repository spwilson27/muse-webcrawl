Henry IV (April 1367 – 20 March 1413), also known as Henry Bolingbroke, was King of England from 1399 to 1413, the first king of the House of Lancaster.

Early life

Son of John of Gaunt, Duke of Lancaster, Henry was exiled by Richard II in 1398 and disinherited on Gaunt's death. He invaded England in 1399 while Richard was in Ireland, forced Richard's deposition, and had Parliament proclaim him king.

Reign

His reign was beset by rebellions — the Percy revolt defeated at Shrewsbury (1403), Owain Glyndŵr's Welsh revolt, and plots linked to his contested title — as well as conflict with Parliament over finance and chronic ill health in later years.

Death and legacy

He died in the Jerusalem Chamber at Westminster Abbey, fulfilling a prophecy he would die in Jerusalem, and was buried at Canterbury Cathedral. He was succeeded by his son Henry V. Shakespeare devoted two plays, Henry IV, Part 1 and Part 2, to his reign.
