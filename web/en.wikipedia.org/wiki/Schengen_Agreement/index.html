Schengen Agreement

Schengen Agreement
Signed: 14 June 1985
Location: Near Schengen, Luxembourg
Original signatories: Belgium, France, Luxembourg, Netherlands, West Germany

The Schengen Agreement is a treaty signed on 14 June 1985 near the village of Schengen in Luxembourg, on board the river cruiser Princesse Marie-Astrid on the Moselle River near the tripoint of France, Germany, and Luxembourg. The original signatories — Belgium, France, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, and West Germany — agreed to gradually abolish controls at their common borders.

Implementation

The agreement was supplemented by the Schengen Convention of 1990, which set out the practical measures for abolishing internal border checks, together with common rules on external borders, visas, police cooperation, and the Schengen Information System. The system came into effect on 26 March 1995 for the first group of countries.

The Schengen Area today

The Schengen Area has grown to include most European Union member states plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, and Liechtenstein, covering over 400 million people who can travel across internal borders without passport controls. The Schengen rules were incorporated into EU law by the Treaty of Amsterdam in 1999. Member states may temporarily reintroduce internal border controls in exceptional circumstances, as several did during the 2015 migration situation and the COVID-19 pandemic.
