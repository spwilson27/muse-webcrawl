Daum (studio)

Daum is a crystal studio based in Nancy, France, founded in 1878 by Jean Daum. It became one of the most important producers of Art Nouveau and Art Deco glass, working in partnership with artists and designers including Émile Gallé's successors in the École de Nancy, Jacques Grüber, and later Salvador Dalí and César Baldaccini.

History

The Daum family took over a glassworks in Nancy in settlement of a debt and expanded production under Jean Daum's sons Auguste and Antonin. Daum mastered techniques of coloured and cameo glass — acid-etched, enamelled and intercalated decoration — winning a Grand Prix at the 1900 Paris Exposition. After the First World War the studio turned to Art Deco forms and pioneered modern pâte de verre (glass paste) casting, a process in which crushed glass is moulded and refired, for which Daum remains renowned.

Today

Daum continues to produce limited-edition crystal pieces in pâte de verre and pâte de cristal, collaborating with contemporary artists, and its historic works are held in the Musée de l'École de Nancy and the Musée des Beaux-Arts de Nancy, which holds several hundred Daum pieces.
