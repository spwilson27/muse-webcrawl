Andrew Zachary Fire (born 27 April 1959) is an American biologist and professor of pathology and genetics at the Stanford University School of Medicine. He shared the 2006 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Craig Mello "for their discovery of RNA interference — gene silencing by double-stranded RNA".

Career and discovery

Fire graduated in mathematics from UC Berkeley at 19 and completed his Ph.D. in biology at MIT in 1983 under Phillip Sharp. After postdoctoral work with Sydney Brenner in Cambridge, England, he joined the Carnegie Institution's Department of Embryology in Baltimore in 1986, before moving to Stanford in 2003.

Working with Mello, Fire investigated why injecting antisense RNA silenced genes in the nematode worm Caenorhabditis elegans. In their landmark 1998 Nature paper, Fire, Mello and colleagues showed that double-stranded RNA — containing both sense and antisense strands — was far more potent than either strand alone, and that a few molecules per cell sufficed, implying a catalytic, amplified mechanism. RNA interference became a standard laboratory tool for switching off genes one at a time and the basis of an entire class of RNA-based medicines; the first RNAi drug, patisiran, was approved in 2018. The prize followed the discovery with unusual speed, after only eight years.
