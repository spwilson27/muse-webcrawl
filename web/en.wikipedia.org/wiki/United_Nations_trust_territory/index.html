United Nations trust territory

United Nations trust territories were the successors of the remaining League of Nations mandates and came into being when the League of Nations ceased to exist in 1946. All trust territories were administered through the United Nations Trusteeship Council under the UN Charter's International Trusteeship System, whose stated aim was to promote the political, economic, and social advancement of the inhabitants and their progressive development towards self-government or independence.

Map showing the eleven UN trust territories.

System

Under Chapter XII of the UN Charter, territories could be placed under trusteeship if they had been League mandates, had been detached from enemy states after the Second World War, or were voluntarily placed under the system. Administering powers reported to the Trusteeship Council, which could receive petitions from inhabitants and send visiting missions — a stronger supervision than under the League mandate system. One territory, the Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands (administered by the United States), was designated a "strategic" trust, supervised by the Security Council instead.

List of territories

Eleven territories were placed under trusteeship, administered by Australia, Belgium, France, Italy, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, and the United States. They included British and French Togoland and the Cameroons, Ruanda-Urundi, Tanganyika, Italian Somaliland, New Guinea, Nauru, Western Samoa, and the Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands (the Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Palau, and the Northern Mariana Islands).

End of the system

All trust territories attained self-government or independence, the last being Palau, which became independent in 1994 after the termination of the U.S. trusteeship. The Trusteeship Council suspended operations on 1 November 1994; it remains in existence formally and can meet if required.
