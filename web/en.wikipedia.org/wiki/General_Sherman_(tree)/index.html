General Sherman is a giant sequoia (Sequoiadendron giganteum) tree located at an elevation of about 2,109 metres (6,919 ft) in the Giant Forest of Sequoia National Park in Tulare County, California. By volume, it is the largest known living single-stem tree on Earth, with an estimated bole (trunk) volume of 1,487 cubic metres (52,500 cu ft).

Dimensions

The tree stands 83.8 metres (274.9 ft) tall, with a diameter of 7.7 metres (25 ft) at breast height and a maximum base diameter of 11.1 metres (36.5 ft). Its largest branch is nearly 2.1 metres (6.8 ft) in diameter, and the crown spreads about 32.5 metres (106.5 ft). It is neither the tallest tree (a title held by the coast redwood Hyperion) nor the widest, but its total trunk volume exceeds every other known single-stem tree.

History

The tree was named in 1879 by naturalist James Wolverton, who had served as a lieutenant under General William Tecumseh Sherman in the American Civil War. In 1931, following comparisons with the nearby General Grant tree, General Sherman was established as the largest tree in the world by volume. During the KNP Complex Fire of 2021, firefighters wrapped the tree's base in aluminium-based burn-resistant material to protect it from the flames.

Age and growth

Estimates place the tree's age between 2,300 and 2,700 years. Despite its age, General Sherman continues to grow, adding wood each year equivalent to a sizeable ordinary tree — roughly one cubic metre of new wood annually in recent measurements.
