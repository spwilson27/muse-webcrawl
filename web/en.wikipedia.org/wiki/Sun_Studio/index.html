Sun Studio

Sun Studio, 706 Union Avenue, Memphis, Tennessee

Sun Studio is a recording studio opened by rock-and-roll pioneer Sam Phillips at 706 Union Avenue in Memphis, Tennessee, on 3 January 1950. It was originally called Memphis Recording Service, sharing the building with the Sun Records label business. Reputedly the birthplace of rock and roll, the studio recorded landmark sessions by Elvis Presley, Johnny Cash, Carl Perkins, Jerry Lee Lewis, Roy Orbison and Howlin' Wolf.

Early years

Phillips, a former radio engineer, opened the studio in a former auto repair shop, with the motto "We Record Anything, Anywhere, Anytime". In 1951 he recorded what is often called the first rock and roll song, "Rocket 88" by Jackie Brenston and his Delta Cats (actually Ike Turner's Kings of Rhythm). He also made early recordings of B.B. King and Howlin' Wolf before launching Sun Records in 1952.

The Elvis breakthrough

In the summer of 1954, a young truck driver named Elvis Presley, who had earlier paid to cut a personal acetate at the studio, was invited to record. During a break on 5 July 1954 he launched into a fast, bluesy version of Arthur Crudup's "That's All Right", and Phillips captured the spontaneous birth of rockabilly. Presley's contract was sold to RCA in 1955, but the studio's legend was sealed — including the impromptu "Million Dollar Quartet" jam of Presley, Cash, Perkins and Lewis on 4 December 1956.

Legacy

Phillips moved his operations to a larger studio in 1959. The 706 Union building later housed an auto shop before being restored and reopened as a studio and museum in 1987. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 2003 and remains both a working recording studio and one of America's most visited music shrines.
