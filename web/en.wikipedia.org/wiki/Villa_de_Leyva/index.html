Villa de Leyva

Villa de Leyva, also called Villa de Leiva, is a touristic colonial town and municipality in the Boyacá Department of Colombia. It is located in a highland valley at about 2,149 metres (7,051 ft) above sea level, roughly 40 kilometres west of Tunja and about 180 kilometres north of Bogotá. The town was founded on 12 June 1572 by Hernán Suárez de Villalobos, on the orders of Andrés Díaz Venero de Leiva, the first president of the Royal Audiencia of Santa Fe, after whom the town is named.

History

The area was inhabited in pre-Columbian times by the Muisca people and contained a Muisca observatory. After the Spanish conquest, a first settlement was established in 1572 near the Muisca observatory but was moved to its present site in 1582 because the indigenous population objected to the takeover of their ceremonial ground. Villa de Leyva served as a retreat for Spanish military officers, clergy and nobility, and was declared a national monument in 1954, which helped preserve its colonial character.

The town

Villa de Leyva is renowned for its huge main plaza (Plaza Mayor), measuring about 14,000 square metres, which is claimed to be one of the largest town squares in the Americas; it is paved with cobblestones and surrounded by Spanish colonial buildings with whitewashed walls and wooden balconies. The parish church on the plaza dates from the 17th century. The town's largely unaltered colonial fabric makes it a popular weekend destination from Bogotá and a frequent filming location.

Surroundings and festivals

The dry landscape around the town is notable for fossils from the Cretaceous period, when the area lay beneath a sea; the nearly complete skeleton of a kronosaurus found nearby in 1977 is displayed in the Museo El Fósil. Other attractions include the Muisca observatory site known as El Infiernito, the Casa Terracota (a large house built entirely of fired clay), and Pozos Azules, a set of vividly blue artificial ponds. Annual events include a kite festival in August, an astronomy festival, and a gastronomy festival.
