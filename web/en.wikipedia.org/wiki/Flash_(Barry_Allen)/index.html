Barry Allen is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. He is the second character known as the Flash, succeeding Jay Garrick. Created by writers Robert Kanigher and John Broome and artist Carmine Infantino, he debuted in Showcase #4 (October 1956), a debut widely credited with launching the Silver Age of Comic Books.

Fictional character biography

Barry Allen is a forensic chemist with the Central City Police Department, notorious for being slow and frequently late. One night, while working late, a lightning bolt shatters a case of chemicals that douse him, granting him superhuman speed and reflexes. Inspired by his childhood comic book hero, Jay Garrick, he adopts the name the Flash and fights crime in Central City, facing a rogues' gallery including Captain Cold, Mirror Master, Gorilla Grodd, and his nemesis, the time-travelling Reverse-Flash, Eobard Thawne.

Barry married Iris West, and his nephew Wally West later became Kid Flash. In the 1985–86 crossover Crisis on Infinite Earths, Barry sacrificed himself to destroy the Anti-Monitor's anti-matter cannon, running so fast that he disintegrated — a death that stood for over two decades, during which Wally West carried the mantle. Barry returned to life in Final Crisis (2008) and was restored as the primary Flash in The Flash: Rebirth (2009). His attempt to undo his mother's murder in Flashpoint (2011) rewrote DC continuity, launching The New 52.

Powers and abilities

Barry draws his power from the Speed Force, an extradimensional energy source. He can run at speeds exceeding light, vibrate through solid matter, generate whirlwinds, read at super-speed, and travel through time and between dimensions, usually aided by the Cosmic Treadmill.

In other media

Grant Gustin portrayed Barry Allen in The CW's The Flash (2014–2023) and the wider Arrowverse. Ezra Miller played the character in the DC Extended Universe films, including the solo film The Flash (2023).
