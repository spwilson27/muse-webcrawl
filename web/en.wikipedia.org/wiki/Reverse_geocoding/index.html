Reverse geocoding

Reverse geocoding is the process of converting geographic coordinates (latitude and longitude) into a human-readable address or place name. It is the inverse of geocoding, which converts addresses into coordinates. Reverse geocoding services typically return the nearest known address, road, or named feature to a given point, often with a structured breakdown (street number, street, city, region, postal code, country) and an indication of the precision of the result.

Methods

Algorithms for reverse geocoding search spatial databases indexed with structures such as R-trees or geohashes to find nearby features. For a point near a road, the system may snap the point to the nearest road segment and interpolate a house number from the address ranges recorded for that segment. When no address is nearby, systems fall back to the nearest named place, neighborhood, or administrative area. "Reverse geocoding" can also refer to assigning a point to the area features (such as a county or watershed) that contain it — a point-in-polygon lookup.

Applications

Reverse geocoding is used in location-based services: labeling where a photograph was taken from its embedded GPS metadata, describing the location of vehicles and assets in fleet tracking, emergency services, ride-hailing and delivery apps, and turning raw GPS logs into meaningful place histories. Because reverse geocoding of precise coordinates can reveal sensitive information about individuals, its use is subject to privacy considerations.
