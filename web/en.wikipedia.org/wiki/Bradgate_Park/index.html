Bradgate Park

Bradgate Park is a public park in Charnwood Forest, in Leicestershire, England, northwest of Leicester. It covers 830 acres (340 hectares) and was once a medieval deer park. The park is home to the ruins of Bradgate House, the childhood home of Lady Jane Grey, who was queen of England for nine days in 1553.

History

The park was first mentioned in 1241 as a hunting ground. Bradgate House was built around 1520 by Thomas Grey, 2nd Marquess of Dorset, and is one of the earliest unfortified brick great houses in England. The Old John Tower, a folly on the park's highest hill, was built in 1784.

Geology and wildlife

Bradgate Park contains some of the oldest rocks in England, of Precambrian age, part of the Charnian Supergroup; fossils of early multicellular life, including Charnia, occur in the wider area. The park supports herds of red and fallow deer and is designated a Site of Special Scientific Interest.
