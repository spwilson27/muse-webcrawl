Slumdog Millionaire is a 2008 British drama film directed by Danny Boyle, written by Simon Beaufoy, and produced by Christian Colson, loosely adapted from the novel Q & A (2005) by Indian author Vikas Swarup. Set and filmed in India, it tells the story of Jamal Malik, an 18-year-old from the Juhu slums of Mumbai who appears on the Indian version of Who Wants to Be a Millionaire? and exceeds expectations, arousing the suspicions of the game show host and of law enforcement.

Plot

Interrogated on suspicion of cheating, Jamal recounts his life through flashbacks: each quiz question corresponds to an episode of his childhood — the death of his mother in communal riots, survival with his brother Salim among street children exploited by the gangster Maman, his love for Latika, and his work as a chai-wallah at a call centre. His answers come not from education but from experience. Released in time for the final 20-million-rupee question — the name of the third Musketeer, the one thing he was never taught — he guesses correctly after phoning Latika, and the brothers' stories resolve in tragedy and reunion: "It is written."

Reception and awards

The film premiered at Telluride and became a surprise worldwide hit, grossing over $378 million against a $15 million budget. At the 81st Academy Awards it won eight Oscars from ten nominations, including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Original Score and Song ("Jai Ho") for A. R. Rahman. In India it drew both celebration and criticism, including debate over its title and its portrayal of poverty.
