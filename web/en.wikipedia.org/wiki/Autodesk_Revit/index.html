Autodesk Revit is a building information modelling (BIM) software for architects, structural engineers, mechanical, electrical, and plumbing (MEP) engineers, and contractors. The software allows users to design a building and structure and its components in 3D, annotate the model with 2D drafting elements, and access building information from the building model's database. Revit is 4D BIM capable with tools to plan and track various stages in the building's lifecycle, from concept to construction and later demolition.

History

Revit was originally developed by Charles River Software, founded in 1997 by Leonid Raiz and Irwin Jungreis, key developers of Pro/ENGINEER. The name "Revit" is a contraction of "Revise Instantly", reflecting the software's parametric change engine: a change made anywhere in the model is propagated to all views and schedules. The first version was released on 5 April 2000. Autodesk acquired the company (renamed Revit Technology Corporation) in 2002 for US$133 million and has developed and marketed the product since.

Features

Revit models buildings as assemblies of intelligent parametric objects—walls, doors, windows, ducts, pipes, structural members—organised into "families" that can be customised. Because every plan, section, elevation, and schedule is a view of the same underlying database, edits update all documentation automatically, reducing coordination errors. Discipline-specific toolsets address architecture, structures (including reinforcement modelling), and MEP systems. Multi-user collaboration is supported through worksharing on a central model, and interoperability is provided via formats such as IFC, DWG, and links to analysis tools.

Versions and licensing

Revit is released annually and is offered by subscription, commonly through Autodesk's AEC Collection. Separate editions for architecture, structure, and MEP were merged into a single "One Box" Revit product in 2013. A free but feature-limited version, Revit LT, was introduced for smaller practices.

Reception and use

Revit became the dominant BIM authoring platform in many markets, particularly North America and the United Kingdom, where government BIM mandates accelerated adoption. Criticisms have included its Windows-only availability, performance with very large models, and the cost and complexity of family creation.
