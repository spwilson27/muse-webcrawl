Soviet–Japanese War

The Soviet–Japanese War, known in Mongolia as the Liberation War of 1945, was a campaign of the Second World War that began with the Soviet invasion of Japanese-occupied Manchuria on 9 August 1945. The Soviet Union, having denounced the Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact, declared war on the Empire of Japan in accordance with commitments made at the Yalta Conference, and within days Soviet and Mongolian forces overran Manchukuo, Mengjiang, northern Korea, southern Sakhalin, and the Kuril Islands.

LocationManchuria, Inner Mongolia, Korea, Sakhalin, Kuril Islands

Background

The Soviet Union and Japan had clashed in border conflicts in the late 1930s, culminating in the Soviet victory at Khalkhin Gol in 1939, but signed a Neutrality Pact in April 1941 that held for most of the war. At Yalta in February 1945, Stalin promised to enter the war against Japan within three months of Germany's surrender, in exchange for territorial concessions including southern Sakhalin and the Kuril Islands. Germany surrendered on 8 May 1945 (Moscow time); exactly three months later the USSR attacked.

The campaign

Operation August Storm employed more than 1.5 million Soviet troops, battle-hardened from the war in Europe, against the Kwantung Army, which had been stripped of many of its best units for the Pacific and home-islands defence. Coordinated offensives from Transbaikal, the Amur region, and the Maritime provinces encircled Manchukuo within days. The speed of the advance stunned the Japanese command; Emperor Hirohito's surrender broadcast came on 15 August, though fighting continued in places until early September. Soviet amphibious operations seized the Kuril Islands and southern Sakhalin in late August.

Consequences

The war contributed decisively to Japan's decision to surrender by destroying hopes that Moscow might mediate a negotiated peace. Hundreds of thousands of Japanese soldiers became prisoners, many held in Siberian labour camps for years. The Soviet occupation of northern Korea and the American occupation of the south established the division of Korea. Japanese civilians in Manchuria suffered mass displacement and death during the collapse and repatriation. The Soviet annexation of the Kuril Islands remains the basis of the unresolved Kuril Islands dispute between Russia and Japan, which has prevented the signing of a formal peace treaty.
