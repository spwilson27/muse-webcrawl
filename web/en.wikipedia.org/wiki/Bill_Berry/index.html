William Thomas Berry (born July 31, 1958) is an American musician best known as the drummer of the alternative rock band R.E.M. from its formation in Athens, Georgia, in 1980 until his departure in 1997. Berry was also a significant songwriter in the band, contributing to songs such as "Everybody Hurts", "Perfect Circle", "Man on the Moon" and "Driver 8", and played guitar, bass and piano on some recordings.

Biography

Born in Duluth, Minnesota, Berry moved with his family to Macon, Georgia, where he met future bandmate Mike Mills in high school. At the University of Georgia in Athens he met Michael Stipe and Peter Buck, and the four formed R.E.M. in 1980.

In March 1995, Berry collapsed on stage in Lausanne, Switzerland, from a ruptured brain aneurysm; he recovered after surgery and rejoined the band for the completion of the Monster tour and the album New Adventures in Hi-Fi. In October 1997, Berry announced his departure from R.E.M., saying he was ready for a change; he became a farmer on his hay farm in Watkinsville, Georgia. R.E.M. continued as a trio until its amicable disbandment in 2011.

Later work

Berry largely withdrew from the music industry but has performed occasionally, including R.E.M.'s induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2007 and later appearances with the supergroup the Baseball Project and Howl Owl Howl. In June 2024, Berry performed on stage with his three former bandmates at the Songwriters Hall of Fame induction — their first performance together in many years.
