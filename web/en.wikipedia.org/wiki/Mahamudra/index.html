Mahamudra

Mahamudra, the "Great Seal", points directly to the nature of mind.

Mahāmudrā (Sanskrit: महामुद्रा, "great seal") literally means the totality of existence as sealed by emptiness; in Tibetan Buddhism it denotes a system of meditation and view focused on directly realising the nature of mind. It is central to the Kagyu schools of Tibetan Buddhism and is also taught in the Gelug tradition as the Gelug–Kagyu tradition of Mahamudra.

Practice

Mahamudra instruction is often summarised as ground, path, and fruit. Practitioners first calm the mind with śamatha (calm abiding), then investigate its nature through vipaśyanā (insight), resting in non-dual awareness. The Indian mahāsiddha Tilopa's Ganges Mahamudra and his "Six Words of Advice" ("Don't recall, don't imagine, don't think, don't examine, don't control, rest") are classic instructions; his lineage passed through Naropa and Marpa to Milarepa and Gampopa, whose "Four Dharmas" organise the path.

Interpretations

Mahamudra is taught in sutra (essence) and tantra forms; it is often compared and combined with Dzogchen, the analogous direct approach of the Nyingma school, both emphasising the innate luminosity and emptiness of awareness.
