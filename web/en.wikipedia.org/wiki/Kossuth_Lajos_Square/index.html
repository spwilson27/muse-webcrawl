Kossuth Lajos Square

The square in front of the Hungarian Parliament Building

Kossuth Lajos Square (Hungarian: Kossuth Lajos tér) is a major city square in the Lipótváros district of Budapest, Hungary, on the bank of the Danube. Named after the Hungarian statesman Lajos Kossuth, it is dominated by the Hungarian Parliament Building and is one of the symbolic centres of Hungarian public life, frequently used for state ceremonies, protests and national commemorations.

History

The square took shape in the nineteenth century as land was reclaimed along the Danube embankment. It was officially named after Kossuth in 1927, when a monument group to him was unveiled. During the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, a mass demonstration in the square on 25 October 1956 turned into a massacre when unidentified gunmen fired on the crowd. The square was comprehensively reconstructed in 2012–2014, restoring its pre-1944 appearance and removing parking in favour of pedestrian space.

Monuments and buildings

Besides the Parliament Building (completed 1904), the square features the equestrian statue of Francis II Rákóczi, the Kossuth Memorial, the monument to Count István Tisza, and the restored statue of Attila József seated by the embankment. Nearby stand the Ministry of Agriculture and the Ethnographic Museum building, and a memorial flame and markers commemorate the victims of 1956.

Transport

The square is served by Kossuth Lajos tér station on line M2 of the Budapest Metro, as well as tram line 2 along the Danube.
