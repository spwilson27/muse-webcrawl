Guinea

Guinea, officially the Republic of Guinea, is a country on the Atlantic coast of West Africa. It borders Guinea-Bissau and Senegal to the north, Mali to the northeast, Côte d'Ivoire to the southeast, and Liberia and Sierra Leone to the south. Its capital and largest city is Conakry, and its population is about 14 million.

The territory was part of a succession of West African empires and kingdoms, including the imamate of Futa Jallon, before becoming the French colony of French Guinea. Guinea gained independence from France in 1958 under Ahmed Sékou Touré, whose authoritarian rule lasted until 1984. Subsequent decades saw military coups, contested elections, and, in 2021, another coup that installed a transitional junta.

Guinea is sometimes called the "water tower of West Africa" because the Niger, Senegal and Gambia rivers rise in its Futa Jallon highlands. It is rich in bauxite — holding some of the world's largest reserves — as well as iron ore, gold and diamonds, yet remains one of the world's poorer countries.
