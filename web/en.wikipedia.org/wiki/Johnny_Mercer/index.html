John Herndon Mercer (18 November 1909 – 25 June 1976) was an American lyricist, songwriter and singer, and a co-founder of Capitol Records. He wrote the lyrics to more than 1,500 songs, including many standards of the Great American Songbook, and won four Academy Awards for Best Original Song.

Career

Born in Savannah, Georgia, Mercer moved to New York in the late 1920s, acting briefly and singing with bands before his songwriting took off. In Hollywood from 1935, he collaborated with composers including Harold Arlen ("Blues in the Night", "That Old Black Magic", "One for My Baby"), Hoagy Carmichael ("Skylark"), Harry Warren, and later Henry Mancini.

His Oscar-winning songs were "On the Atchison, Topeka and the Santa Fe" (1946), "In the Cool, Cool, Cool of the Evening" (1951), "Moon River" (1961, from Breakfast at Tiffany's) and "Days of Wine and Roses" (1962). Other famous lyrics include "Hooray for Hollywood", "Jeepers Creepers", "Fools Rush In", "Autumn Leaves" (English lyric) and "Satin Doll" (with Billy Strayhorn and Duke Ellington). Mercer's own recordings as a singer also sold in the millions.

Capitol Records and legacy

In 1942 Mercer co-founded Capitol Records with Glenn Wallichs and Buddy DeSylva, building the label into a major company and signing artists such as Nat King Cole, Peggy Lee and Stan Kenton. He was a founding president of the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 1969. His hometown of Savannah honours him with a statue and the Johnny Mercer Theatre.
