Grigory Yevseyevich Zinoviev (born Ovsei-Gershon Aronovich Radomyslsky; 23 September 1883 – 25 August 1936) was a Bolshevik revolutionary and Soviet politician. A close associate of Vladimir Lenin in exile, he became head of the Petrograd soviet and party organisation, chairman of the Communist International (Comintern) from 1919 to 1926, and, after Lenin's death, one of the "triumvirate" with Joseph Stalin and Lev Kamenev that initially ruled the Soviet Union. Defeated in the succession struggle, he was expelled from the party, recanted, and was eventually executed after the first Moscow show trial.

Revolutionary career

Zinoviev joined the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party in 1901 and sided with the Bolsheviks in 1903, becoming one of Lenin's closest collaborators during years of exile in Switzerland. Returning to Russia after the February Revolution of 1917, he and Kamenev publicly opposed the planned October insurrection, fearing it premature — a lapse Lenin later held against them. After the revolution Zinoviev took charge of Petrograd, presiding over the city during the Civil War, and in 1919 became the first chairman of the Comintern, the organisation coordinating world communist parties.

Succession struggle and fall

During Lenin's final illness Zinoviev joined Stalin and Kamenev in blocking Leon Trotsky, but by 1925 he broke with Stalin and formed first the "Leningrad" and then the United Opposition with his former rivals. Stalin crushed the oppositions using the party apparatus; Zinoviev lost the Comintern chairmanship in 1926 and was expelled from the party in 1927. He repeatedly capitulated and was readmitted, holding minor posts, but after the Kirov assassination (1934) he was arrested, tried for "moral complicity" in 1935, and in August 1936 made the principal defendant, with Kamenev, in the first great Moscow show trial — the "Trial of the Sixteen". Convicted on fabricated charges of leading a terrorist centre, he was shot on 25 August 1936. He was rehabilitated by the Soviet Supreme Court in 1988.
