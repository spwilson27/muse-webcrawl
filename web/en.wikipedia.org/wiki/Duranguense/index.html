Duranguense

Duranguense is a genre of regional Mexican music. It is a fusion of technobanda and tamborazo, with strong influences from norteño and banda, and is named after the Mexican state of Durango. Although its roots lie in Durango, the genre was popularised and commercialised in large part by Mexican immigrant communities in the United States, particularly in Chicago, Illinois, in the early 2000s.

Characteristics

The Duranguense sound is built around synthesizer keyboards, saxophones, trumpets, and the fast, driving beat of the tambora drum, typically in a brisk polka-like rhythm that supports the dance style known as the pasito duranguense. Groups wear matching outfits with cowboy hats and boots. Leading acts include Grupo Montez de Durango — whose albums brought the genre mainstream success in the United States — K-Paz de la Sierra, Los Horóscopos de Durango, Patrulla 81, and Alacranes Musical.

At its commercial peak, roughly 2003–2008, Duranguense songs charted highly on Billboard's Latin charts and filled dance venues across the U.S. and Mexico. The genre later declined in popularity as banda and other regional styles reasserted themselves, though its groups continue to tour.
